# LIBERO-PRO: Towards Robust and Fair Evaluation of Vision-Language-Action Models Beyond Memorization

> [!summary] Summary
> State-of-the-art Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, despite reporting high success on the standard LIBERO benchmark, exhibit a severe lack of generalization. The LIBERO-PRO benchmark introduces systematic perturbations, revealing that these models rely on rote memorization and their performance collapses to near 0% under even minor environmental or instructional variations.

## Problem

- Existing VLA models achieve artificially high success rates (often exceeding 90%) on the standard LIBERO benchmark, which can be misleading regarding their true capabilities.
- Current evaluation protocols promote rote memorization of action sequences and environment layouts rather than genuine task understanding, environmental perception, or generalization.
- The lack of robust evaluation hinders fair comparison between VLA models and impedes progress towards building truly adaptable and generalizable embodied AI agents.

## Method

![[2510.03827-method.png]]
*LIBERO-PRO benchmark task overview extending LIBERO's four original task categories with object attribute, initial position, instruction, and environmental perturbations.*

- Introduced **LIBERO-PRO**, an ==extended evaluation suite== that systematically applies ==controlled perturbations== across four orthogonal dimensions: ==object attributes==, ==initial object positions==, ==natural language instructions==, and ==environment context==.
- Formalized the critique of the standard ==LIBERO protocol== by demonstrating how its inherent homogeneity allows models to succeed via ==memorization== due to minimal differences between training and test initial states.
- Conducted rigorous evaluation of three state-of-the-art VLA models (**OpenVLA**, **pi0**, **pi0.5**) against these diverse perturbations to expose their generalization and robustness limitations.

## Results

- All tested state-of-the-art VLA models experienced a catastrophic performance collapse, achieving nearly **0.0%** success rates under the generalized settings introduced by **LIBERO-PRO**, despite over **90%** success on the standard benchmark.
- Models consistently produced nearly identical, memorized action trajectories even when critical task components like target objects were replaced, removed, or instructions were semantically corrupted, indicating a profound lack of adaptation.
- High sensitivity to object placement was observed, with success rates plummeting to zero when displacement exceeded **0.2 units** for most models, demonstrating a severe lack of spatial generalization.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Current VLA models primarily rely on rote memorization of action sequences and environment layouts, with their apparent understanding and robustness being superficial.
> - Apparent robustness to linguistic variations is often an artifact of visual context reliance and memorized trajectories, rather than genuine semantic comprehension of instructions.
> - VLA models exhibit critical deficiencies in spatial reasoning, adaptability to minor object placement changes, and the ability to perform compositional tasks, leading to failure when these elements are perturbed.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models represent a paradigm in embodied artificial intelligence, aiming to integrate visual perception, natural language understanding, and physical action execution. The development of standardized benchmarks is a critical factor in advancing this field, as they enable reproducible, quantitative, and comparable evaluation of various methods. Several benchmarks exist, including RLBench, CALVIN, RoboCasa, and BridgeData, each providing environments for task execution guided by vision and language.

Among these, LIBERO has gained significant adoption, becoming a widely recognized standard for evaluating VLA models. Its influence extends to shaping research directions and claims of progress within the VLA community. However, a growing body of related work has begun to raise concerns regarding the validity of LIBERO's evaluation protocol. Observations from other researchers suggest that the high performance reported on LIBERO often does not translate to reliable behavior under even minor task variations. This has led to questions about whether VLA models are truly learning generalizable skills or are instead exhibiting a form of memorization. Some studies have explored stronger algorithms or proposed stress tests to encourage more robust VLA systems, but a systematic and standardized framework for robust evaluation specifically within the LIBERO context has been identified as a deficiency.

This research addresses these concerns by directly examining the limitations of the current LIBERO evaluation protocol. It positions itself as a response to the need for a more rigorous and fair assessment framework for VLA models, particularly those that are widely reported as high-performing on LIBERO. By systematically introducing perturbations and analyzing model behavior, the paper aims to provide a diagnostic tool that clarifies the true capabilities of VLA systems beyond in-distribution performance. The introduction of LIBERO-PRO is presented as a plug-and-play extension designed to integrate into existing LIBERO workflows, fostering a more accurate understanding of model generalization and robustness in embodied AI.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this work stems from the observation that the widely adopted LIBERO benchmark, despite its utility, possesses fundamental limitations in its training and evaluation settings. These limitations are reported to lead to inflated performance estimates and impede equitable comparison among VLA models. The core problem identified is that models often achieve high success rates on LIBERO by relying on rote memorization of action sequences and environment layouts from the training set, rather than developing genuine task understanding or robust environmental perception.

The authors detail several specific issues that highlight this problem:
- **Minimal Distributional Shift:** The standard LIBERO evaluation tasks are reported to be nearly identical to the training tasks, with only marginal and often visually imperceptible perturbations in initial object states. This narrow scope of variation enables models that overfit to the training distribution to achieve near-perfect scores, even if their performance collapses under minor changes.
- **Empirical Evidence of Memorization:** Through preliminary qualitative experiments, the authors observed consistent failure patterns suggesting overfitting. These patterns include:
 - **Visual Perceptual Hallucinations:** Models attempting grasping actions on unrelated objects when the target is replaced, or "grasping the air" when the target object is entirely removed. This suggests a failure to bind instructions to correct visual entities and an over-reliance on memorized trajectories.
 - **Instruction Understanding Hallucinations:** Models executing the original action sequence even when provided with meaningless or corrupted instructions (e.g., "fdsgfdsgsd"). This indicates insensitivity to linguistic semantics and a reliance on visual context to trigger memorized visuomotor patterns.
 - **Location Perception Hallucinations:** Models failing when objects are displaced from their training positions, consistently moving the end-effector towards a memorized location rather than the object's actual, perturbed position. This suggests a lack of online spatial reasoning.
 - **Lack of Task Understanding/Compositionality:** Models successfully performing individual subtasks (e.g., "pick up bowl," "turn on stove") but failing to execute them when combined into a single, multi-step instruction (e.g., "pick up bowl, place on stove, and then turn on stove"). This suggests an inability to compose learned behaviors or understand temporal/causal structures.

Based on these observations, the key objective of this research is to introduce LIBERO-PRO, an extended and more rigorous evaluation suite. LIBERO-PRO is designed to systematically evaluate VLA models by introducing reasonable, controlled perturbations across four distinct dimensions: manipulated objects, initial states, task instructions, and environments. The motivation is to construct evaluation scenarios that better approximate the diversity of real-world conditions and to expose the actual generalization capabilities of VLA models, moving beyond assessments that may primarily reflect memorization. The broader goal is to advocate for evaluation protocols that incorporate structured perturbations, thereby aligning reported progress with the requirements of robust and generalizable embodied intelligence.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology of this research is structured around empirically demonstrating the limitations of the existing LIBERO benchmark and subsequently introducing a new, perturbation-based framework for more rigorous evaluation.

#### 3.1. Observing Overfitting Issues (Qualitative Analysis)

The authors initially conduct qualitative experiments on existing VLA models (including OpenVLA, Pi0, and Pi0.5) to identify systematic failure patterns on LIBERO tasks. These observations form the empirical basis for the proposed benchmark.
- **Visual Perceptual Hallucinations:**
 - **Object Replacement:** The model was tasked to "Pick up salad dressing and place it in the basket." When the salad dressing bottle was replaced with an unrelated item (e.g., alphabet soup), the model still reached for and grasped the alphabet soup, indicating a failure to bind the instruction to the correct visual entity and an over-reliance on scene context and familiar trajectories.
 - **Object Removal:** When the target object (salad dressing) was entirely absent from the scene, the model still performed the grasping motion towards the location where the object typically appeared ("grasping the air"), suggesting a lack of online visual verification of object existence.
- **Instruction Understanding Hallucinations:** When the original instruction was replaced with a meaningless character sequence (e.g., "fdsgfdsgsd" or "xxx"), the model still executed a trajectory similar to the original task. This implied insensitivity to linguistic semantics and a reliance on visual context to trigger memorized visuomotor patterns.
- **Location Perception Hallucinations:** Qualitative and quantitative results (Figure 4) demonstrated high sensitivity to the object's initial position. Even small displacements from training coordinates led to consistent failure, with the end-effector moving towards the original, memorized location. For OpenVLA and Pi0, task success collapsed to zero for displacements exceeding 0.2 units, while Pi0.5 showed slightly more robustness but also degraded to zero at higher displacements.
- **Lack of Task Understanding:** A multi-step task involving "pick up the bowl, place it on the stove, and then turn on the stove" was used. While models successfully executed individual subtasks ("pick up the bowl and place it on the stove" and "turn on the stove"), they failed when these subtasks were combined. This indicated a lack of compositional reasoning, with models replaying partial or spurious trajectories instead of forming a coherent execution plan.

These observations led to the conclusion that VLA models, despite high in-distribution performance, rely on superficial regularities and shortcut feature-action correlations rather than robust visuomotor understanding.

#### 3.2. Formal Problem Definition

The paper formalizes a VLA task instance as T = (l, e, G), where `l` is a natural-language instruction, `e` is an environment instance (comprising visual context `W`, interactive objects `O`, and initial spatial configuration `R0`), and `G` is a success predicate. It then formalizes the current LIBERO evaluation protocol, highlighting that test tasks `T'` are typically constructed with `l'=l`, `W'=W`, `O'=O`, `G'=G`, and only minor perturbations to `R0`. This limited distributional shift is identified as the source of overestimation.

#### 3.3. Proposed Perturbation-Based Evaluation Framework (LIBERO-PRO)

To address the limitations, the authors introduce LIBERO-PRO, a framework that generates perturbed task variants along four orthogonal dimensions:
- **Object Attribute Perturbation (k = O):** Modifies non-essential visual attributes (color, texture, size) while preserving semantic equivalence. `T^(O) = (l, e^(O), G)` where `e^(O) = (W, O^(O), R0)`.
- **Initial Configuration Perturbation (k = R):** Alters absolute and relative positions of objects, maintaining physical plausibility. `T^(R) = (l, e^(R), G)` where `e^(R) = (W, O, R0^(R))`.
- **Instruction Perturbation (k = L):** Includes semantic rephrasing (`l^(L_sem)`) and task-level variations. `T^(L_sem) = (l^(L), e, G)`.
- **Task Perturbation (k = T):** Modifies the target object or action within the training distribution. `T^(T) = (l, e, G^(T))`.
- **Environment Perturbation (k = E):** Modifies visual environment context (background, lighting, texture) without altering task feasibility. `T^(E) = (l, e^(E), G)` where `e^(E) = (W^(E), O, R0)`.

#### 3.4. Perturbation Constraints

To ensure logical coherence and executability of perturbed tasks, two types of constraints are applied:
- **Bounded Perturbation:** `x^(k) ∈ N_k(x), dist_k(x, x^(k)) ≤ δ_k`, where `δ_k` controls magnitude.
- **Non-trivial Separation:** `d_TV(T, T^(k)) > ϵ`, ensuring meaningful behavioral changes.

#### 3.5. LIBERO-PRO Evaluation Benchmark

The LIBERO-PRO benchmark integrates these perturbations, allowing for single- and multi-dimensional combinations. Task-level perturbations are excluded from cross-type compositions to avoid infeasible scenarios, while other types can be combined. Specific implementations include:
- **Object Variation:** Changes in object color, texture, and scale.
- **Configuration Variation:** Altered object placements.
- **Instruction Variation:** Paraphrased task descriptions.
- **Task Variation:** New object sets and goals, while remaining executable.
- **Environment Variation:** Changes in backgrounds and lighting.

#### 3.6. Experimental Settings

Experiments were conducted on LIBERO-PRO, evaluating three VLA models: OpenVLA, Pi0, and Pi0.5. OpenVLA was assessed using four official checkpoints trained on different LIBERO suites, while Pi0 and Pi0.5 used a single official checkpoint. Each task was evaluated with 50 episodes.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted using the LIBERO-PRO evaluation suite revealed consistent patterns of performance degradation across the tested Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models when subjected to controlled perturbations. The findings contradict the high success rates typically reported on the standard LIBERO benchmark.

**Overall Performance Collapse:**
A central finding is that while existing VLA models often achieve success rates exceeding 90% under the standard LIBERO evaluation protocol, their performance collapses significantly under the generalized settings introduced by LIBERO-PRO. For instance, in some generalized settings, performance dropped to 0.0% success rate, indicating a near-complete failure to adapt to even reasonable changes. This discrepancy suggests a reliance on rote memorization of training data rather than genuine task understanding or robust environmental perception.

**Specific Observations Across Perturbation Dimensions:**
The evaluation of OpenVLA, Pi0, and Pi0.5 across different perturbation dimensions yielded the following results (summarized in Figure 7 of the paper):

1. **Failure under Simple Perturbations:**
 - Models exhibited sharp performance degradation when subjected to changes in object positions or minor task modifications, even when these modifications were constructed from components present in the training distribution. This indicates a limited capacity for generalizing beyond tightly constrained initial states.
 - The models' tendency to execute grasping actions even when the target object was replaced with irrelevant items, or when the object was entirely absent, provided qualitative evidence of a failure in visual grounding and object recognition.

2. **Illusory Robustness:**
 - Apparent stability against some object and instruction perturbations was attributed to overfitting rather than genuine task understanding. For example, models continued to execute similar action trajectories even when given corrupted or nonsensical instructions, suggesting that the instruction input had minimal impact on the decision-making process. This behavior indicated that the models often rely on retrieving a memorized visuomotor pattern based on visual context, rather than genuinely parsing and grounding linguistic semantics.

3. **Model-Specific Sensitivity:**
 - Environmental perturbations were observed to affect models differently. The degree of performance degradation varied depending on the extent of environmental divergence from the training data and the specific model's reliance on memorized environmental contexts. This suggests heterogeneity in how different VLA architectures process and generalize visual background information.

4. **Subtle Capability Differences Masked by Standard Benchmarks:**
 - While most models demonstrated widespread failures under various perturbations, some subtle differences in capabilities were revealed. For example, in the "libero-goal" task under position changes, Pi0.5 achieved a success rate of 0.38. In contrast, OpenVLA and Pi0 both registered 0.0% success in this specific scenario. This particular finding indicates that while the overall generalization capabilities remain limited, there can be measurable differences between models that are otherwise obscured by high, undifferentiated scores on the standard LIBERO benchmark.

In summary, the results consistently demonstrated that current VLA models, despite their reported high performance on the original LIBERO benchmark, lack true robustness and generalization capabilities when evaluated under systematically perturbed conditions. The identified issues predominantly point to a significant reliance on memorization of training configurations and trajectories.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research carries several significant implications for the field of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models and embodied AI, potentially impacting future research directions, evaluation practices, and model development.

**Exposing Fundamental Flaws in Current Evaluation:** The most immediate significance of this work is its empirical demonstration that the widely adopted LIBERO benchmark, in its current configuration, substantially overestimates the competence of VLA models. By revealing that high reported success rates largely stem from rote memorization of training configurations rather than genuine understanding, the paper fundamentally challenges the validity of a de facto standard in the field. This exposure is critical for preventing researchers from pursuing misleading avenues based on inflated performance metrics.

**Highlighting the Memorization Problem:** The study clearly articulates and provides evidence for the critical problem of VLA models relying on "shortcut" learning and memorization. The qualitative and quantitative results, such as models grasping absent objects, ignoring meaningless instructions, and failing to compose subtasks, underscore that current systems often do not achieve robust language grounding, perceptual reasoning, or compositional understanding. This finding serves as a diagnostic tool, pointing to specific areas where current VLA architectures are deficient.

**Introducing a Robust and Fair Evaluation Standard:** LIBERO-PRO is presented as a direct solution to the identified evaluation shortcomings. By introducing a systematically designed, plug-and-play benchmark that incorporates controlled perturbations across multiple dimensions (objects, initial states, instructions, environments), the paper offers a principled framework for assessing true generalization and comprehension. This new benchmark has the potential to become a more rigorous and reliable standard for evaluating VLA models, promoting fairer comparisons and more accurate reporting of capabilities.

**Guiding Future Research and Development:** By systematically revealing the limitations of current VLA models under various perturbations, LIBERO-PRO provides clear directions for future research. It implicitly calls for the development of architectures and training methodologies that prioritize:
- **Robust Perceptual Grounding:** Models that can accurately identify and interact with objects regardless of minor visual variations or positional shifts.
- **Semantic Understanding:** Systems that genuinely interpret language instructions rather than simply correlating keywords with memorized actions.
- **Compositional Reasoning:** Agents capable of breaking down complex tasks into subtasks and reassembling them into coherent, novel plans.
- **Generalization to Novel Environments:** Policies that can adapt to new visual contexts beyond those seen in training.
The insights from LIBERO-PRO can therefore guide the design of more capable and reliable embodied AI systems.

**Fostering a Shift in the Research Community:** The paper concludes with an explicit "call on the community to abandon misleading methodologies in favor of robust assessments." This strong recommendation, backed by empirical evidence, aims to catalyze a shift in how VLA research evaluates progress. If widely adopted, LIBERO-PRO could significantly influence which models are considered "state-of-the-art" and reshape the criteria for defining success in embodied AI, ultimately accelerating the development of truly intelligent and generalizable robotic agents.
