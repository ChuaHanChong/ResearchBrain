# ASTRO: Teaching Language Models to Reason by Reflecting and Backtracking In-Context

> [!summary] Summary
> AI at Meta and University of Washington researchers introduce ASTRO, a three-stage framework that teaches large language models to reason by reflecting and backtracking in-context. It instills search-like reasoning via synthetic data generation, supervised fine-tuning, and reinforcement learning, leading to absolute performance gains of up to 26.9% on challenging mathematical reasoning benchmarks.

## Problem

- Open-source large language models (LLMs) often lack the advanced search-like reasoning capabilities (e.g., self-reflection, backtracking) observed in state-of-the-art proprietary models.
- Current methods for enhancing LLM reasoning often rely on distillation from already strong models or external inference-time scaffolds, rather than enabling LLMs to internalize and generate complex search procedures themselves.
- It has been unclear how to robustly enhance the reasoning capabilities of general-purpose LLMs, such as the Llama 3 family, which may not inherently demonstrate such advanced behaviors.

## Method

- Generates a synthetic dataset of high-quality 'long Chain-of-Thoughts' (CoTs) that encode diverse search behaviors, including ==self-reflection and backtracking==, derived from ==Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS) trajectories== and linearized into natural language.
- Applies Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) to a target LLM (Llama-3.1-70B-instruct) on this curated dataset for a single epoch to instill ==autoregressive search priors==.
- Utilizes a modified ==Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)== for Reinforcement Learning (RL) to further refine and optimize the SFT-tuned model's reasoning capabilities, leveraging verifiable binary rewards.

## Results

- Llama-3.1-70B-ASTRO-RL achieved significant absolute performance gains on mathematical benchmarks: **81.8%** on MATH-500 (**+16.0%** over baseline), **64.4%** on AMC 2023 (**+26.9%**), and **30.0%** on AIME 2024 (**+20.0%**).
- ASTRO-RL models consistently outperformed strong baselines, including Llama-3.3-70B-Instruct and other Llama-3.1-70B-Instruct variants fine-tuned with Step-KTO and SPOC.
- Control experiments confirmed that explicitly infused search priors from SFT are essential, as ASTRO-RL models consistently and significantly outperformed 'Direct-RL' models across all benchmarks, which lacked these priors.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly teaching search priors, such as self-reflection and backtracking, during Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) is crucial for the efficacy and performance ceiling of subsequent Reinforcement Learning (RL) in reasoning tasks.
> - The models learn to generate longer, more detailed Chain-of-Thought (CoT) traces and increase their frequency of backtracking during RL training, which directly correlates with improved problem-solving performance.
> - The framework enables LLMs to internalize complex search procedures, allowing them to autoregressively generate full search trajectories, including recoveries from errors.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of sophisticated reasoning capabilities in large language models (LLMs) represents a critical frontier in artificial intelligence. Recent advancements, particularly through reinforcement learning (RL), have led to the emergence of powerful "reasoning models" such as OpenAI o1, DeepSeek-R1, and Gemini 2.5. A distinguishing feature of these state-of-the-art models is their ability to engage in iterative refinement, exhibiting behaviors akin to search algorithms, including self-reflection and backtracking.

However, the authors identify a crucial gap in the current research landscape: while open-source replications of these advanced reasoning models have shown success, they often rely on methods like distillation from already highly capable proprietary models or direct RL applied to LLMs that inherently possess some degree of reflective behavior and strong reasoning capabilities (e.g., strong base models like Qwen). This raises a fundamental scientific question: how can reasoning capabilities, particularly the desirable structured search behavior, be instilled *ab initio* into other general-purpose LLMs that do not pre-exhibit such characteristics, such as the Llama 3 family of models? This is the void that ASTRO ("Autoregressive Search-Taught Reasoner") aims to fill.

ASTRO positions itself at the intersection of several growing research areas:
- **Reinforcement Learning for Reasoning:** A major trend where RL is used to fine-tune LLMs for complex tasks, often leveraging verifiable rewards to improve accuracy. ASTRO builds upon this by using GRPO with verifiable rewards, but critically, it *primes* the model with search priors first, addressing limitations of "spurious performance gains from incorrect or noisy reward signals during RL" observed in some prior work.
- **Self-Correction and Iterative Refinement:** Prior research has explored training models to self-correct by learning from erroneous steps and their corrections (e.g., Setlur et al., 2024; Ye et al., 2025; Kumar et al., 2025). ASTRO significantly extends this by not just correcting errors, but by explicitly teaching the *process* of reflection and backtracking as part of a broader search strategy, making it an inherent part of the model's autoregressive generation.
- **Learning to Search/Algorithm Distillation:** This field focuses on teaching neural networks to imitate or internalize search algorithms. Works like Algorithm Distillation (Laskin et al., 2022) and Procedure Cloning (Yang et al., 2022) aim to distill expert computational histories into models. Xiang et al. (2025) specifically proposed applying procedure cloning to LLM search traces for math reasoning as a proof-of-concept. ASTRO concretizes and scales this idea by systematically generating complex MCTS trajectories and converting them into natural language CoTs that embed the full search procedure, making the LLM itself the search algorithm rather than relying on external scaffolds at inference time.

By systematically infusing search-like behavior through a synthetic dataset derived from MCTS and then refining this behavior with RL, ASTRO offers a principled and end-to-end approach to imbue open-source LLMs with robust, iterative reasoning capabilities. It addresses the challenge of building reasoning models from a broader base of LLMs, moving beyond reliance on pre-existing strong reasoning foundations.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to introduce ASTRO, the "Autoregressive Search-Taught Reasoner," a novel framework designed to train language models to emulate the reasoning processes of search algorithms. This explicitly involves enabling LLMs to leverage self-reflection, backtracking, and systematic exploration directly within their generated outputs.

The motivation behind developing ASTRO stems from several critical observations and unmet needs in the current landscape of large language model reasoning:
- **Bridging the Performance Gap in Open LLMs:** While cutting-edge reasoning models (e.g., OpenAI o1, DeepSeek-R1) exhibit impressive capabilities, including search-like iterative refinement, their development often starts from models that already possess strong intrinsic reasoning or reflective behaviors. It remains unclear how to robustly enhance the reasoning capabilities of other widely used open-source LLMs, such as the Llama 3 family, which may not inherently demonstrate such advanced behaviors prior to specialized training. ASTRO seeks to provide a clear recipe for boosting these models.
- **Internalizing Search Procedures:** Current approaches often rely on external search scaffolds (e.g., beam search) during inference. A key motivation for ASTRO is to enable LLMs to *internalize* the search procedure, allowing them to generate entire search trajectories—complete with reflections and backtracks—in a single, autoregressive inference pass. This makes the model itself the "reasoner," fostering a deeper and more integrated form of intelligence.
- **Improving Robustness and Iterative Correction:** Complex problems, particularly in mathematical reasoning, frequently require iterative correction and recovery from initial missteps. ASTRO aims to train models to exhibit improved reasoning by consistently re-evaluating their solutions and backtracking when necessary, thereby increasing their confidence in the final answer.
- **Generating Structured Reasoning Traces:** By teaching models to produce outputs that capture both successful paths and recoveries from failure, ASTRO seeks to generate structured reasoning traces. These traces can be mapped to directed graphs, offering a richer and more interpretable understanding of the model's thought process, which is crucial for debugging and analyzing complex reasoning.
- **Providing Principled Priors for RL:** The authors motivate ASTRO as a method to provide beneficial "priors" for reinforcement learning. By systematically injecting self-reflection and backtracking patterns into synthetic training data, ASTRO bootstraps models with a rich prior for exploration, which is then further optimized through verifiable-reward RL. This sequential approach addresses concerns about the effectiveness of RL when initialized from models lacking desirable reasoning behaviors.

In essence, ASTRO is motivated by the desire to systematically instill advanced, search-inspired reasoning capabilities into open LLMs, making them more robust, interpretable, and effective at tackling challenging, multi-step problems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

ASTRO is a sophisticated, three-stage framework designed to imbue large language models with autoregressive search capabilities, mimicking human-like reflection, backtracking, and exploration during problem-solving.

**1. Search Trajectory Generation (Procedure Cloning in Language):**
This initial stage focuses on creating a high-quality synthetic dataset of "long Chain-of-Thoughts" (CoTs) that explicitly encode search behaviors.
- **Problem Formulation:** The problem-solving process is framed as a Markov Decision Process (MDP) where the LLM acts as the policy ($\Pi_{LM}$), generating steps (actions) to navigate a solution space. Rewards are binary (+1 for correct, -1 for incorrect) and obtained from a verifier.
- **Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS):** To generate diverse and high-quality solution traces, MCTS is employed. MCTS systematically explores the solution space for each math problem, balancing exploration and exploitation.
 - **Selection:** The PUCT (Predictor+Upper Confidence bounds applied to Trees) algorithm is used to select the most promising next step, considering both the estimated quality (Q-value) and visit counts.
 - **Expansion:** From a selected state, $\Pi_{LM}$ samples *k* potential next steps. For each action, *M* rollouts (full solutions) are sampled, and a verifier assigns a binary correctness score. These scores are averaged to compute the reward for the new states.
 - **Backpropagation:** Rewards are propagated back up the tree to update the Q-values and visit counts of ancestor nodes, guiding future search.
 - *Implementation Details:* Llama-3.3-70b-instruct is used as the policy, generating k=8 actions with M=16 rollouts, for 32 iterations and a max depth of 50.
- **Search Tree Linearization:** The complex MCTS search tree, containing multiple branches and potentially incorrect paths, is converted into a linear sequence of nodes (L).
 - The linearization ensures two invariants: the sequence always ends with a high-quality correct answer, and no two nodes in the same sequence contain the same incorrect answer (to prevent repetitive errors).
 - This involves a Depth-First Search (DFS) to identify correct and incorrect terminal nodes. A high-quality correct terminal node is sampled, along with a subset of *k* distinct incorrect terminal nodes. Paths from the root to these nodes are then sequentially merged, creating a trajectory that explicitly includes *backtracking* instances (where a subsequent node is an ancestor of the previous one).
- **Procedure Cloning in Language:** The linearized node sequence is then transformed into a natural language Chain-of-Thought (CoT). This is the "procedure cloning" step, where the abstract search steps are verbalized.
 - For standard sequential steps, the LLM is prompted to rewrite the reasoning step into a smooth continuation of the CoT.
 - For backtracking instances, specific hard-coded self-reflection phrases ("But wait, are we solving the problem correctly so far? Hmm...") are inserted. If the backtrack is to a non-root node, a phrase like "Let’s go back to where we..." is generated. If it's a full restart, "Let’s restart our solution from the beginning..." is used.
 - For intermediate nodes leading to high Q-values or after the final correct answer, "Our solution seems to be correct so far." is inserted to teach confidence.
- **Dataset Composition:** Approximately 36.1K high-quality CoT solutions are curated from 14K valid search trees, spanning MATH-train and NuminaMath datasets (AMC/AIME, AoPS Forum). This dataset includes both "direct" solutions (without backtracking) and "search" solutions (with self-reflection and backtracking).

**2. Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT):**
- **Objective:** To instill the autoregressive search priors (self-reflection and backtracking patterns) generated in Stage 1 into the target LLM.
- **Process:** Llama-3.1-70b-instruct is fine-tuned on the curated 36.1K search trajectories for only one epoch. The entire CoT sequence (prompt + generated steps) is used for training without masking. This brief SFT period is crucial to inject the priors without overfitting.

**3. Reinforcement Learning (RL):**
- **Objective:** To further refine and improve the reasoning and search capabilities of the SFT-tuned model.
- **Dataset Curation for RL:** Problems are selected from diverse open-source math datasets (AIME 1983-2023, MATH-4500, MATH-train, NuminaMath). To ensure efficient learning, only problems of "moderate to high difficulty" for the SFT policy are chosen (pass rate between 1% and 75% for N=64 generations).
- **Training Algorithm:** A modified version of Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO, similar to Dr. GRPO) is used. The objective function maximizes the policy's ability to generate correct answers, leveraging the self-reflection and backtracking priors. The advantage is computed by subtracting the prompt-level mean reward and removing standard deviation, encouraging the policy to generate correct solutions that outperform its average performance on a given prompt.
- **Reward Mechanism:** Verifiable rewards are used, meaning a binary (+1 for correct, -1 for incorrect) score is assigned based on direct comparison of the predicted answer with the ground truth. This provides a stable and reliable signal for optimization.

This multi-stage approach systematically builds complex reasoning behaviors: first, by cloning expert-derived search procedures into natural language examples, then by teaching the LLM to imitate these examples via SFT, and finally, by using RL to reinforce the efficacy of these learned search behaviors in solving novel problems.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research paper presents compelling evidence for the efficacy of the ASTRO framework in significantly enhancing the mathematical reasoning capabilities of large language models, particularly the Llama 3 family.

**1. Impact of Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) with Search Priors:**
- Even with a single epoch of SFT on the synthetically generated search trajectories, the model (Llama-3.1-70B-ASTRO-SFT) demonstrated improved performance compared to the base Llama-3.1-70B-Instruct. For instance, on MATH-500, Astro-SFT achieved 69.6% pass@1, surpassing the baseline's 65.8%. Similar gains were observed on AMC 2023 (51.9% vs 37.5%) and AIME 2024 (16.3% vs 10.0%).
- Qualitative analyses revealed that this initial SFT stage successfully instilled explicit search capabilities, including observable self-reflection and backtracking behaviors, into the language model.

**2. Significant Gains from Reinforcement Learning (RL):**
- The full ASTRO framework, which combines SFT with subsequent RL (Llama-3.1-70B-ASTRO-RL), yielded substantial absolute performance improvements. The model achieved 81.8% on MATH-500 (+16.0% absolute gain), 64.4% on AMC 2023 (+26.9% absolute gain), and 30.0% on AIME 2024 (+20.0% absolute gain) compared to the base Llama-3.1-70B-Instruct.
- These results demonstrate Astro's particular effectiveness on challenging problems that necessitate iterative correction and robust reasoning.

**3. Performance Against Strong Baselines:**
- Llama-3.1-70B-ASTRO-RL outperformed Llama-3.3-70B-Instruct (which is a stronger base model often trained with online RL), as well as other Llama-3.1-70B-Instruct baselines fine-tuned with Step-KTO and SPOC (spontaneous self-correction via Pair-SFT and RL).
- Notably, ASTRO-RL even performed comparably or better than Step-KTO and SPOC models based on the more powerful Llama-3.3-70B-Instruct, specifically achieving superior results on MATH-500 and comparable performance on AIME 2024.

**4. Training Dynamics and Behavior Evolution:**
- **Generation Length:** During RL training, the model's Chain-of-Thought (CoT) generation length initially increased steeply (from ~1,600-1,800 tokens for the SFT policy), and then continued to grow more slowly but steadily, eventually averaging around 6,000 tokens. This indicates the model learned to produce more extensive and detailed reasoning traces.
- **Reward Scores:** The reward scores assigned to the policy (binary +1 for correct, -1 for incorrect) consistently increased throughout RL training, demonstrating that the policy learned to solve a higher percentage of problems correctly (from less than 30% initially to over 60%).
- **Evaluation Metrics:** Performance on all three evaluation benchmarks (MATH-500, AMC 2023, AIME 2024) showed a steady and consistent improvement as RL training progressed, validating the efficacy of the RL stage.

**5. Crucial Role of Search Priors:**
- To isolate the impact of the search priors, a control experiment was conducted comparing ASTRO-trained models with "Direct" models (Llama-3.1-70B-Direct-SFT/RL). The Direct models were trained on the exact same problem sets and CoT solutions, but the CoT solutions did *not* contain the explicit self-reflection and backtracking phrases.
- While Llama-3.1-70B-Direct-SFT showed some gains over the base model, Llama-3.1-70B-ASTRO-SFT generally performed better (except AIME 2024).
- Critically, after RL, Llama-3.1-70B-ASTRO-RL consistently and significantly outperformed Llama-3.1-70B-Direct-RL across all benchmarks. This directly demonstrates that the explicit search priors (self-reflection and backtracking) infused during SFT are essential for improving reasoning capabilities via RL, suggesting better training efficacy and a higher performance ceiling.
- **Correlation with Backtracking:** A strong positive correlation (Pearson's coefficients ranging from 0.816 to 0.854) was found between the number of backtracks performed by the model at test time and its evaluation performance. This reinforces the hypothesis that learning to self-reflect and backtrack more frequently is a key mechanism for solving challenging math problems.

**6. Qualitative Evidence:**
- Example outputs from Llama-3.1-70B-ASTRO-RL vividly illustrate the model's ability to intermittently reflect on its solution, backtrack from uncertain or incorrect paths, and iteratively refine its reasoning until arriving at the correct answer. The generated CoTs can be mapped to directed graphs, providing transparent insights into the model's search process.

In summary, ASTRO successfully imparts complex search-like reasoning behaviors into LLMs through a systematic SFT and RL pipeline, leading to state-of-the-art mathematical reasoning performance and clearly demonstrating the critical importance of explicitly teaching self-reflection and backtracking priors.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The ASTRO framework represents a significant advancement in the field of large language model reasoning, offering a principled and effective method for instilling robust, search-like intelligence. Its significance and potential impact extend across several dimensions:

- **Systematic Instillation of Robust Reasoning:** ASTRO provides a clear and systematic recipe for training open-source LLMs to reason like search algorithms, a capability previously observed more strongly in closed, highly optimized models or those already possessing significant inherent reasoning abilities. This addresses a fundamental challenge: how to reliably imbue models like Llama 3 with advanced problem-solving strategies from the ground up, moving beyond mere imitation or fine-tuning on existing reasoning data.
- **Bridging to System 2 Reasoning:** By explicitly teaching self-reflection, backtracking, and exploration, ASTRO moves LLMs closer to "System 2" cognition—deliberate, step-by-step, and iterative reasoning—as opposed to System 1's fast, intuitive responses. This is crucial for tackling complex, multi-step problems where initial attempts may fail, and a methodical corrective process is required. The ability to identify and recover from errors in-context is a hallmark of intelligent behavior.
- **Enhanced Mathematical Problem Solving:** The impressive performance gains on challenging math benchmarks (MATH-500, AMC 2023, AIME 2024) highlight ASTRO's immediate practical impact in a domain that rigorously tests logical and procedural reasoning. This is particularly valuable for educational tools, scientific research, and any application requiring precise numerical or symbolic manipulation.
- **Improved Interpretability and Debuggability:** The framework trains models to generate structured reasoning traces that explicitly show self-reflection and backtracking points. This is a considerable step towards making LLM reasoning more transparent. The ability to map CoTs to directed graphs allows researchers and developers to better understand the model's decision-making process, identify where it went wrong, and potentially debug its reasoning, which is a major challenge in current LLM research.
- **Foundation for Broader Reasoning Tasks:** While demonstrated on mathematical reasoning, the core principles of ASTRO—generating search trajectories, procedure cloning, and RL with verifiable rewards—are highly generalizable. This framework could potentially be adapted to imbue LLMs with similar structured reasoning capabilities for a wide range of other complex tasks, such as scientific discovery, logical inference, coding, or even strategic planning.
- **Optimizing RL for Reasoning:** The control experiment definitively demonstrates that explicitly infusing search priors during SFT significantly boosts the efficacy and performance ceiling of subsequent RL training. This finding is significant for the broader RL for LLMs community, indicating that carefully designed pre-training or SFT data can prime models for more effective and stable reinforcement learning, leading to superior final performance. It suggests that *how* the initial reasoning capabilities are introduced profoundly impacts the ultimate success of RL.
- **Efficiency in Data Utilization:** Achieving substantial gains with a relatively small, high-quality synthetic dataset (36.1K trajectories) suggests that the method is data-efficient in terms of human-annotated data, relying more on algorithmic generation of diverse, instructive traces.

In conclusion, ASTRO offers a powerful and systematic methodology for empowering LLMs with robust reasoning, self-correction, and explorative capabilities. Its impact lies in advancing the fundamental understanding of how to build more intelligent and reliable AI systems that can effectively navigate complex problem spaces and learn from their own "mistakes" in an interpretable manner.
