# LocateAnything: Fast and High-Quality Vision-Language Grounding with Parallel Box Decoding

> [!summary] Summary
> LocateAnything is a vision-language framework that introduces Parallel Box Decoding (PBD) to enable the simultaneous generation of entire bounding box units, overcoming the sequential decoding bottleneck in visual grounding. This method achieves a greater than 10x speedup in decoding throughput and improves localization accuracy, for example, by 3.8% in mean F1-score on LVIS over previous methods, setting new benchmarks across diverse visual perception tasks.

## Problem

- Existing vision-language models (VLMs) serialize 2D bounding box coordinates into 1D token sequences, leading to a practical inference bottleneck due to strictly sequential (Next-Token Prediction) decoding.
- Serializing 2D geometric objects into 1D tokens disassociates the intrinsically coupled coordinates of a bounding box, potentially hindering the model's ability to capture structural correlations and reducing localization accuracy.
- Generic Multi-Token Prediction (MTP) methods, while faster for language models, are structure-agnostic, risking incoherent predictions, amplified error propagation, and compromised accuracy when applied to spatial coordinates.

## Method

- Introduces Parallel Box Decoding (PBD), a novel method within the **LocateAnything** framework that treats entire bounding boxes (or points) as atomic units to be predicted in parallel, replacing token-by-token coordinate generation.
- Employs a dual-formulation training strategy that jointly optimizes a standard sequential representation (NTP) and a block-wise parallel representation (MTP), using a specialized attention mask for both causal flow between blocks and bidirectional attention within blocks.
- Develops on-demand inference modes (Fast, Slow, Hybrid) to balance throughput and robustness, with the default Hybrid Mode adaptively switching to sequential decoding when parallel outputs are deemed unreliable due to format irregularity or spatial ambiguity.
- Curated **LocateAnything**-Data, a large-scale, multi-domain dataset comprising over 12 million images and 138 million queries, with 785 million annotated bounding boxes, to provide extensive supervisory signals for generalized, high-precision localization.

## Results

- LocateAnything-3B achieved a decoding throughput of 12.7 Boxes Per Second (BPS) in Hybrid Mode, representing a greater than **10x** speedup over textual-based VLMs and a **2.5x** speedup over quantized-based methods.
- Improved mean F1-scores on general detection benchmarks by **3.8%** on LVIS and **1.8%** on COCO compared to Rex-Omni-3B, and demonstrated superior performance in dense and tiny object detection, achieving 39.9 mean F1 on VisDrone.
- Set new benchmarks in GUI Grounding (60.3 mean F1 on ScreenSpot-Pro) and point-based localization (e.g., 83.9 F1@Point on COCO), showcasing precise open-world localization capabilities across diverse tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Aligning multi-token prediction with the intrinsic coupled structure of 2D bounding boxes, by treating full boxes as atomic units, is more effective for visual grounding than generic structure-agnostic MTP approaches.
> - Jointly optimizing both sequential (NTP) and parallel (MTP) generation during training allows VLMs to achieve both high localization accuracy and decoding throughput without sacrificing causal reasoning.
> - Dynamic switching between parallel and sequential decoding modes can effectively mitigate issues like 'format irregularity' and 'spatial ambiguity' that arise from high-throughput parallel generation, ensuring robust performance in real-world scenarios.
