# LocateAnything: Fast and High-Quality Vision-Language Grounding with Parallel Box Decoding

> [!summary] Summary
> LocateAnything is a vision-language framework that introduces Parallel Box Decoding (PBD) to enable the simultaneous generation of entire bounding box units, overcoming the sequential decoding bottleneck in visual grounding. This method achieves a greater than 10x speedup in decoding throughput and improves localization accuracy, for example, by 3.8% in mean F1-score on LVIS over previous methods, setting new benchmarks across diverse visual perception tasks.

## Problem

- Existing vision-language models (VLMs) serialize 2D bounding box coordinates into 1D token sequences, leading to a practical inference bottleneck due to strictly sequential (Next-Token Prediction) decoding.
- Serializing 2D geometric objects into 1D tokens disassociates the intrinsically coupled coordinates of a bounding box, potentially hindering the model's ability to capture structural correlations and reducing localization accuracy.
- Generic Multi-Token Prediction (MTP) methods, while faster for language models, are structure-agnostic, risking incoherent predictions, amplified error propagation, and compromised accuracy when applied to spatial coordinates.

## Method

- Introduces Parallel Box Decoding (PBD), a novel method within the **LocateAnything** framework that treats entire bounding boxes (or points) as atomic units to be predicted in parallel, replacing token-by-token coordinate generation.
- Employs a dual-formulation training strategy that jointly optimizes a standard sequential representation (NTP) and a block-wise parallel representation (MTP), using a specialized attention mask for both causal flow between blocks and bidirectional attention within blocks.
- Develops on-demand inference modes (Fast, Slow, Hybrid) to balance throughput and robustness, with the default Hybrid Mode adaptively switching to sequential decoding when parallel outputs are deemed unreliable due to format irregularity or spatial ambiguity.
- Curated **LocateAnything**-Data, a large-scale, multi-domain dataset comprising over 12 million images and 138 million queries, with 785 million annotated bounding boxes, to provide extensive supervisory signals for generalized, high-precision localization.

## Results

- LocateAnything-3B achieved a decoding throughput of 12.7 Boxes Per Second (BPS) in Hybrid Mode, representing a greater than **10x** speedup over textual-based VLMs and a **2.5x** speedup over quantized-based methods.
- Improved mean F1-scores on general detection benchmarks by **3.8%** on LVIS and **1.8%** on COCO compared to Rex-Omni-3B, and demonstrated superior performance in dense and tiny object detection, achieving 39.9 mean F1 on VisDrone.
- Set new benchmarks in GUI Grounding (60.3 mean F1 on ScreenSpot-Pro) and point-based localization (e.g., 83.9 F1@Point on COCO), showcasing precise open-world localization capabilities across diverse tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Aligning multi-token prediction with the intrinsic coupled structure of 2D bounding boxes, by treating full boxes as atomic units, is more effective for visual grounding than generic structure-agnostic MTP approaches.
> - Jointly optimizing both sequential (NTP) and parallel (MTP) generation during training allows VLMs to achieve both high localization accuracy and decoding throughput without sacrificing causal reasoning.
> - Dynamic switching between parallel and sequential decoding modes can effectively mitigate issues like 'format irregularity' and 'spatial ambiguity' that arise from high-throughput parallel generation, ensuring robust performance in real-world scenarios.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Vision-language models (VLMs) have emerged as versatile backbones for various interactive and embodied systems, requiring the ability to localize task-relevant entities from natural language queries with both accuracy and low latency. This capability, known as visual grounding and detection, is central to enabling VLMs to interact with the physical and digital world.

Current VLM approaches frequently formulate visual grounding and detection as a coordinate-token generation problem. In this paradigm, a 2D bounding box (typically defined by four coordinates) is serialized into a 1D sequence of tokens. This token-by-token generation, often following a next-token prediction (NTP) strategy, introduces an inherent inference bottleneck due to its strictly sequential nature. This serialization also disassociates the intrinsically coupled geometric units of a bounding box, potentially hindering the model's ability to capture and utilize strong structural correlations among the coordinates.

Attempts to accelerate language model inference, such as Multi-Token Prediction (MTP) and Diffusion Language Models (DLMs), have been explored. These methods aim to predict multiple tokens or blocks simultaneously to reduce decoding steps. However, existing MTP formulations for language models are often structure-agnostic, treating sequences as generic token streams. When applied to spatial coordinates, this approach may fail to preserve geometric coherence, potentially leading to irregular predictions, spurious correlations, amplified error propagation, and consequently, reduced accuracy and reliability despite potential speed gains. For instance, such methods might erroneously combine coordinate tokens across different bounding box boundaries or even object categories.

This work addresses these limitations by introducing a novel approach that specifically aligns multi-token prediction with the structured nature of geometric entities. Instead of generating coordinates as an unstructured token stream, LocateAnything treats entire bounding boxes (or points) as atomic units to be predicted in parallel. This methodology positions the work as an advancement in visual grounding and detection within VLMs, aiming to overcome the accuracy-latency trade-off observed in previous generative approaches by integrating structural coherence directly into the decoding process.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a unified framework for VLM-based visual detection and grounding, termed LocateAnything, that can achieve both high decoding throughput and improved localization accuracy simultaneously. The motivation stems from several limitations observed in existing vision-language grounding paradigms:

1. **Addressing the Inference Bottleneck of Sequential Decoding**: Current VLMs typically serialize 2D bounding box coordinates into 1D token sequences, leading to a token-by-token, strictly sequential generation process (Next-Token Prediction, NTP). This sequential decoding is identified as a practical bottleneck, resulting in high latency and low inference throughput. The work aims to fundamentally redesign the decoding mechanism to enable parallel generation, thereby enhancing speed.

2. **Preserving Intra-Box Geometric Coherence**: The serialization of 2D geometric objects into 1D token streams often treats individual coordinate tokens as largely independent entities. This approach disregards the inherent coupled structure of bounding box geometry (e.g., the four coordinates of a box are intrinsically related). This structural mismatch can reduce localization accuracy and reliability. The objective is to devise a method that respects and utilizes these geometric correlations by decoding bounding boxes as atomic, coherent units.

3. **Overcoming Limitations of Generic Multi-Token Prediction (MTP)**: While MTP techniques exist to accelerate language models, they are generally structure-agnostic, treating inputs as arbitrary token chunks. Applying such generic MTP to geometric coordinates can lead to "unstructured" and "non-coherent" patterns, as the model may infer unreliable cross-boundary token combinations. This results in spurious correlations, sacrifices structured decoding benefits, and can amplify error propagation, ultimately compromising both accuracy and reliability. A specific motivation is to align MTP with the intrinsic structure of geometric units (e.g., a full bounding box) to ensure structured and accurate predictions.

4. **Developing Robust and Flexible Inference for Real-World Applications**: High-throughput decoding can sometimes introduce unreliability, particularly in complex visual scenes with multiple instances or dense arrangements, leading to "format irregularity" or "spatial ambiguity." A practical system requires a mechanism to detect and mitigate such failures without completely sacrificing speed. This motivates the development of a flexible inference design with different modes to balance throughput and robustness dynamically.

5. **Enhancing Localization Precision through Data Diversity and Scale**: Achieving high-precision localization across a wide array of visual grounding and detection tasks (e.g., object detection, GUI grounding, document analysis, referring expression comprehension) necessitates extensive and diverse training data. The authors aim to curate a large-scale, multi-domain dataset to provide the necessary supervisory signals for a model capable of high-precision, generalized localization.

In summary, the key motivation is to reconcile the often conflicting requirements of high decoding throughput and high localization accuracy in VLMs, enabling more efficient and precise visual grounding for real-time and interactive applications.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

LocateAnything is a unified framework designed for VLM-based visual detection and grounding, fundamentally built upon Parallel Box Decoding (PBD). The methodology encompasses a specialized model architecture, a dual-formulation training strategy, on-demand inference modes, and a large-scale curated dataset.

**Model Architecture and Output Formulation:**
The framework utilizes a native-resolution VLM, comprising a Moon-ViT vision encoder and a Qwen2.5 language decoder, connected by an MLP projector. The vision encoder extracts fine-grained visual tokens from an input image, which are then processed by the language model to generate spatial predictions.
To enable PBD, the standard Next-Token Prediction (NTP) approach for coordinate generation is replaced. Continuous coordinates are normalized (to), discretized into tokens, and then grouped into fixed-length "blocks." Each block serves as an atomic unit, typically of length 6, capable of accommodating four quantized bounding box coordinates and two structural tokens (e.g., `<box>`, `</box>`). Unused positions within a block are filled with `<null>` tokens to maintain uniform tensor shapes. Four functional block types are defined: Semantic (for linguistic identity), Box (for bounding box coordinates), Negative (for object absence), and End (for generation termination). The model formulates localization as generating a sequence of these blocks.

**Training Design (Dual-Formulation Strategy):**
The training strategy is designed to enable parallel generation while preserving the VLM's causal reasoning abilities. It involves jointly optimizing two aligned representations: a standard NTP sequence and a block-wise Multi-Token Prediction (MTP) formulation. A single concatenated input sequence (`x_all = x_vis ⊕ x_q ⊕ x_ntp ⊕ x_blk`) is constructed, where `x_vis` (visual features) and `x_q` (text query) provide shared context. `x_ntp` represents the ground truth in a token-level autoregressive format, while `x_blk` represents the same ground truth in a block-level MTP format.
For `x_blk`, the `x_ntp` sequence is traversed, split, and padded into blocks. Within each block, the first token acts as the prediction context, and subsequent tokens are replaced with `[mask]` tokens, prompting the model to predict all masked tokens in parallel.
A specialized attention mask manages information flow during training:
- **Causal Attention for NTP**: The shared context and `x_ntp` adhere to a causal attention mask, where tokens only attend to preceding tokens, and crucially, are prevented from attending to `x_blk` to avoid data leakage.
- **Causal Flow Across Blocks (MTP)**: For `x_blk`, attention across different blocks is causal, meaning an active block can attend to the shared context and all prior blocks, but not future ones. This helps in learning dependencies and mitigating duplicate or missing boxes.
- **Bidirectional Intra-Block Attention (MTP)**: Tokens *within* the same `x_blk` block share bidirectional attention, enabling the model to capture internal geometric relationships and resolve all internal tokens simultaneously.
The overall objective is to minimize a joint cross-entropy loss combining both NTP and MTP losses (`ℒ = ℒ_ntp + ℒ_mtp`).

**On-Demand Inference Modes:**
To balance throughput and robustness, LocateAnything offers three inference modes, addressing potential issues like "Format Irregularity" (malformed syntax) and "Spatial Ambiguity" (low IoU in dense scenes) that can arise from parallel decoding:
1. **Fast Mode (MTP)**: Predicts full box-aligned blocks in parallel for maximum throughput, suitable for latency-sensitive applications.
2. **Slow Mode (NTP)**: Decodes coordinate tokens autoregressively, equivalent to traditional NTP, providing maximum stability and precision for accuracy-critical tasks.
3. **Hybrid Mode (Adaptive Decoding)**: This default mode uses Fast Mode (MTP) by default but dynamically switches to Slow Mode (NTP) when parallel outputs are deemed unreliable. An "ambiguity trigger" is activated if the top-1 coordinate token's probability falls below 0.7 and the max-min difference among the top-5 coordinate tokens exceeds 80 within the normalized coordinate space. If triggered, the problematic block is discarded, NTP re-decodes that specific block, and then the model reverts to MTP.
During inference, a block-causal attention mask is used, similar to training. KV cache management ensures consistency by truncating the cache to committed tokens after each MTP step.

**LocateAnything-Data Construction:**
A large-scale, multi-domain dataset, LocateAnything-Data, was curated to train the model, comprising over 12 million unique images and 138 million natural language queries, with 785 million annotated bounding boxes. This corpus is categorized into six tasks: general object detection, GUI grounding, natural language referring comprehension, text localization, document/scene layout grounding, and point-based localization.
The dataset is constructed by leveraging existing open-source data and a multi-target grounding data engine. This engine synthesizes grounding annotations from:
- **Detection Datasets**: Category labels of ground-truth bounding boxes are used to prompt Qwen3-VL to generate detailed object-centric queries. Molmo then predicts candidate points, which are retained if they fall within the ground-truth boxes.
- **Unlabeled Images**: Qwen3-VL generates diverse queries from unlabeled images (e.g., Unsplash, SA-1B). These queries prompt Molmo for point predictions, which are converted to bounding boxes by SAM 3, or directly prompt Rex-Omni for box predictions.
All generated boxes undergo a post-verification step by Qwen3-VL to ensure quality. Negative samples (queries for non-existent objects) are explicitly included to mitigate hallucination. Task-specific prompt designs accommodate diverse output formats and query types. The default box output order for training is X-Y Corner Order.

**Training Details:**
The LocateAnything model undergoes a two-stage supervised fine-tuning (SFT) process on a base VLM (Moon-ViT, Qwen2.5) that was initially trained for world-knowledge alignment. Stage 1 incorporates the 138M queries for comprehensive grounding and detection. Stage 2 focuses on dense detection by reducing general data proportion and increasing data with many objects per image. Ablation studies use models trained solely on the COCO dataset. System-level optimizations, such as Stream Packing and MagiAttention, are employed to manage variable sequence lengths and heterogeneous attention masks efficiently across distributed training infrastructure.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of LocateAnything across various visual perception tasks, with a focus on its default Hybrid Mode, demonstrated improvements in both localization quality and decoding throughput.

**High-Quality Multi-Object Detection:**
- On general detection benchmarks, LocateAnything-3B improved the mean F1-score by 3.8% on LVIS and 1.8% on COCO compared to Rex-Omni-3B, despite having an identical model size (Table 1).
- For dense and tiny object scenarios, it achieved a 39.9 mean F1 on VisDrone, surpassing Rex-Omni's 35.8. On Dense200, it reached a competitive 58.7 mean F1, indicating enhanced boundary delineation and instance separation in crowded environments (Table 2).

**Precise Open-World Localization Ability:**
- In GUI Grounding tasks, LocateAnything-3B achieved a mean F1-score of 60.3 on ScreenSpot-Pro, outperforming both larger generalist VLMs (e.g., Qwen3-VL-30B-A3B) and specialized UI models (e.g., GUI-Owl-32B) (Table 3).
- For document understanding, the model achieved 76.8 and 70.1 mean F1 on DocLayNet and M6Doc, respectively, showing substantial improvements over Rex-Omni (Table 4).
- In complex referring expression tasks, LocateAnything-3B demonstrated robust alignment of linguistic intents with visual regions, achieving 78.7 mean F1 on HumanRef and remaining competitive on RefCOCOg (Table 5).
- For point-based localization, LocateAnything-3B set new benchmarks, scoring 83.9 F1@Point on COCO, 87.6 F1@Point on Dense200, 84.7 F1@Point on HumanRef, and 91.0 F1@Point on RefCOCOg test set (Table 11, Appendix C.1).

**Superior Decoding Speed:**
- LocateAnything-3B achieved a decoding throughput of 12.7 Boxes Per Second (BPS) in its default Hybrid Mode (Table 1).
- This represents a greater than 10x speedup compared to textual-based models like Qwen3-VL (1.1 BPS) and a 2.5x speedup over quantized-based Rex-Omni (5.0 BPS).
- Ablation studies confirmed that the Parallel Box Decoding method exhibited significantly less increase in generation time as the number of target boxes increased, translating to a 2x to 6x speedup over traditional NTP methods in dense scenes (Figure 7, right).

**Ablation Study Insights:**
- **Coordinate Representation**: The box-aligned PBD formulation (Slow Mode) yielded a 52.1 mean F1 on COCO, surpassing traditional Textual (49.1) and Quantized (50.1) NTP representations, indicating the benefit of structured supervision for spatial reasoning (Table 6a).
- **MTP Formulation**: PBD's box-aligned MTP (Fast Mode) demonstrated a superior balance of accuracy and throughput (49.6 F1 at 16.9 BPS) compared to structure-agnostic MTP formulations like SDLM (e.g., 46.1 F1 at 5.5 BPS), highlighting the advantage of aligning parallel blocks with geometric units (Table 6b).
- **Decoding Modes and Losses**: Joint training with both NTP and block-level MTP losses (`ℒ_ntp + ℒ_blk`) improved the Slow Mode's F1-score from 50.1 to 52.1. The Hybrid Mode successfully maintained most of the speed gains (13.2 BPS) while achieving robust localization (51.6 F1-score), validating its design for balancing speed and accuracy (Table 6c).
- **Box Output Order**: Sorting bounding boxes by X-Y Corner Order yielded the highest F1-score among tested strategies (Figure 7, left).

**Backbone Generalization:**
The benefits of Parallel Box Decoding were observed to generalize across different VLM backbones. When applied to Qwen3-VL-4B, PBD improved its COCO F1 from 50.8 to 52.0 and increased throughput from 2.8 to 9.4 BPS (Table 13, Appendix C.3).

**Qualitative Results:**
Qualitative assessments (Figures 8, 12, 13, 14) indicated that LocateAnything consistently performed well in compositional grounding, showed robustness to varying instance counts and scales, and maintained reliable localization in cluttered or occluded environments. The Hybrid Mode specifically contributed to output stability in challenging multi-instance generation scenarios.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The LocateAnything framework represents an advancement in vision-language grounding and detection within the VLM paradigm. Its introduction of Parallel Box Decoding (PBD) and associated methodologies carries several significant implications and potential impacts:

1. **Breaking the Inference Bottleneck**: By enabling the parallel decoding of geometric elements as atomic units, LocateAnything directly addresses the long-standing latency issue inherent in sequential, token-by-token generation methods. The demonstrated speedup of up to 2.5x over competitive methods and more than 10x over traditional textual-based VLMs indicates a practical route towards real-time visual perception, which is critical for many applications.

2. **Enhanced Localization Accuracy and Geometric Coherence**: The core insight of aligning multi-token prediction with the intrinsic coupled structure of 2D bounding boxes (rather than treating coordinates as arbitrary 1D token streams) contributes to higher localization accuracy. This structural coherence leads to improved high-IoU localization quality, particularly in complex scenarios like dense object detection and fine-grained UI element grounding, where precise boundary delineation is crucial.

3. **Unified and Versatile Perception Framework**: LocateAnything presents a single, general-purpose VLM that unifies diverse visual grounding and detection tasks, including object detection, GUI grounding, document layout analysis, text localization, and referring expression comprehension. Its ability to achieve competitive or state-of-the-art results across these varied benchmarks, often surpassing both generalist VLMs and domain-specific specialized models, suggests a move towards more capable and comprehensive visual AI systems.

4. **Robustness and Adaptability for Production Environments**: The development of on-demand inference modes, especially the Hybrid Mode with its dynamic fallback mechanism, provides a practical solution for deploying VLMs in real-world production pipelines. This mode balances the speed benefits of parallel decoding with the robustness and precision required to handle complex or ambiguous scenes, reducing worst-case failures and ensuring consistent output quality.

5. **Enabling Advanced Embodied AI and Interactive Agents**: The combination of high speed and high precision in visual grounding is a foundational requirement for latency-sensitive applications such as embodied robotics, autonomous agents, and intelligent interactive systems (e.g., GUI automation). LocateAnything's capabilities could facilitate the more effective and responsive integration of general-purpose VLMs into these domains, allowing agents to perceive and act in environments with greater efficiency and reliability.

6. **Influence on Future Multi-Token Prediction Designs**: This work demonstrates the effectiveness of structure-aligned multi-token prediction beyond traditional text generation, specifically for non-linguistic, structured outputs like geometric coordinates. This could inform and inspire future research into designing MTP strategies that account for the inherent structure of various data types, potentially extending the benefits of parallel decoding to other complex, structured output generation problems in AI.
