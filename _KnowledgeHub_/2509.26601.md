# MENLO: From Preferences to Proficiency -- Evaluating and Modeling Native-like Quality Across 47 Languages

> [!summary] Summary
> Meta Superintelligence Labs developed MENLO, a framework and dataset for evaluating and improving the native-like quality of Large Language Model (LLM) responses across 47 language varieties. The work demonstrates that reinforcement learning (RL) trained LLM judges can effectively guide policy models to enhance native-like proficiency, with human evaluation confirming improvements in output quality.

## Problem

- Large Language Models (LLMs) need to produce high-quality, native-like responses across many languages, incorporating cultural and linguistic nuances, to be globally useful.
- Existing multilingual LLM evaluation benchmarks often rely on translated content, introducing "translationese" biases, or focus on general naturalness/task-specific metrics, failing to capture comprehensive native-like proficiency.
- Scaling human evaluation for many languages is expensive and time-consuming, while LLMs as zero-shot judges often lack the reliability required for consistent multilingual assessment.

## Method

- Introduced **MENLO** (Multilingual Evaluation of ==Native-Like Output==), a framework that defines and measures native-like quality across four dimensions: Fluency, Tone, Localized Tone, and Localized Factuality, using audience-design inspired prompts.
- Created a large human-annotated dataset comprising 6,423 prompt-response preference pairs and 81,014 annotations across 47 typologically diverse language varieties, achieving high inter-annotator agreement.
- Developed and fine-tuned LLM judges using supervised fine-tuning (SFT) and reinforcement learning (RL) with a composite reward signal, demonstrating that RL-trained judges can effectively serve as generative reward models to directly improve the native-like proficiency of policy LLMs.

## Results

- The MENLO dataset achieved an average Krippendorff’s α of **0.84**, demonstrating high inter-annotator agreement in assessing native-like quality across 47 language varieties.
- RL-trained LLM judges consistently outperformed SFT models; for example, Qwen3-**4B**-RL-Judge improved Macro-F1 by **4.0%** and Preference accuracy by **2.9%** over its SFT counterpart, surpassing frontier API models like gpt-4.1.
- Post-training a base Qwen3-**4B** policy model using an RL-trained judge resulted in consistent quality gains, with automated evaluation showing average score improvements of +**0.80** to +1.16 and human validation confirming a **55.7%** win rate with +**10.9%** average score improvement in a 10-language subset.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Pairwise evaluation consistently and significantly outperforms pointwise scoring for LLM judges in assessing native-like quality, with detailed rubrics further enhancing performance.
> - Reinforcement learning (RL) with reward shaping proved more effective than SFT for training LLM judges, achieving higher agreement with human annotators and surpassing frontier API models in certain metrics.
> - Localized Factuality remains a persistent challenge for LLM judges, showing limited benefits from RL training and suggesting a need for external tools or retrieval mechanisms.
> - While RL-trained LLM judges can effectively improve policy model quality, they tend to overestimate the magnitude of these improvements compared to human evaluators, indicating a potential for stylistic "caricature" without careful calibration.
