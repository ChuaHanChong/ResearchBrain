# FSD-VLN: Fast-Slow Dual-System Modeling for Aerial Long-Horizon Vision-Language Navigation

> [!summary] Summary
> FSD-VLN, a fast-slow dual-system framework, balances deep semantic reasoning with low-latency flight control for aerial long-horizon Vision-Language Navigation. This approach significantly improves navigation accuracy and execution efficiency for UAVs in simulated urban environments.

## Problem

- Long-horizon aerial Vision-Language Navigation (VLN) systems struggle with a fundamental conflict between computationally intensive global semantic understanding and the demand for low-latency, high-frequency motion control.
- Existing VLN methods often lead to unstable or oscillating flight trajectories due to instantaneous observations, or incur high inference latency that hinders real-time deployment on agile aerial platforms.
- Training models for long-horizon sequential decision processes frequently encounter issues such as gradient oscillation and error accumulation, impacting learning stability.

## Method

- The **FSD-VLN** framework employs a ==fast-slow dual-system== architecture, with a Slow System extracting high-level multimodal semantic priors and a Fast System generating temporally conditioned actions.
- The Slow System uses a Vision-Language Model (VLM) to generate and buffer stable semantic features, while the Fast System utilizes a ==Diffusion Transformer (DiT)== to iteratively refine and generate action sequences.
- The method integrates ==Global Adaptive Normalization== and a ==Time-Weighted Mean Squared Error (TW-MSE)== objective to stabilize multimodal training and optimize long-horizon action prediction.

## Results

- FSD-VLN achieved up to a **2.6x** improvement in Success Rate (SR) and **3x** in Success Weighted by Path Length (SPL) in unseen environments compared to baseline methods, along with a substantial reduction in navigation error.
- The framework reduced per-action inference latency by approximately **56%** (from **402 ms** to **176 ms**) and overall task execution time by **53%** in simulated environments.
- Ablation studies demonstrated that the Time-Weighted MSE loss led to smoother convergence and reduced oscillation during training, and a shorter action horizon (**H=1**) yielded the best navigation performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly decoupling high-level semantic reasoning from low-level, high-frequency flight control via a coordinated fast-slow dual-system can effectively resolve the structural tension in aerial VLN.
> - Formulating action prediction as a temporally conditioned generative process, rather than relying on instantaneous observations, improves global trajectory alignment and mitigates myopic perception biases.
> - A time-weighted optimization strategy can stabilize gradient propagation and alleviate error amplification in long action sequences, leading to more robust and effective learning for long-horizon tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Unmanned Aerial Vehicles (UAVs) are employed in various missions, including environmental monitoring and emergency response, necessitating robust decision-making capabilities. Traditionally, UAV navigation has relied on predefined maps or manually engineered control strategies, which limit adaptability in dynamic, semantically complex, and open environments. The emergence of large-scale foundation models has initiated a shift in robotic intelligence, enabling more sophisticated multimodal reasoning.

Vision-Language Navigation (VLN) represents a paradigm that allows embodied agents, such as UAVs, to interpret and execute natural language instructions using real-time visual perception and sequential decision-making. This approach enhances the flexibility and scalability of UAVs in interactive tasks, moving towards autonomous aerial capabilities.

Despite advancements in large multimodal models for semantic alignment and zero-shot instruction following, long-horizon aerial VLN presents distinct challenges. These tasks require UAVs to continuously reconcile high-level semantic understanding with high-frequency motion control. This introduces a structural tension: while global reasoning benefits from computationally intensive multimodal perception using large models, stable flight necessitates low-latency, non-blocking decision-making.

Existing VLN methodologies generally fall into two categories: reactive action prediction based on instantaneous vision-language features or large-scale autoregressive inference models that directly generate control commands. Reactive models can exhibit instability in long-horizon navigation due to vulnerability to short-term perceptual biases. Conversely, large-scale inference models improve semantic consistency but incur significant inference latency, which hinders their applicability in real-time aerial deployment. Furthermore, many traditional action predictors process each timestep independently, overlooking critical temporal action dependencies that contribute to flight stability and mitigate error accumulation over long navigation sequences.

Inspired by the dual-process theory (System 1, System 2), dual-system architectures have been explored in autonomous systems, particularly in autonomous driving. These frameworks typically involve a "System 2" that performs high-level reasoning (often leveraging large language models) and a "System 1" that handles real-time perception and control. Similar hierarchical or mixture-of-experts structures have appeared in embodied robotics for decoupling planning from continuous control. While these approaches aim to improve adaptability and computational efficiency through asynchronous coordination, a relatively loose coupling between high-level planning and low-level execution can lead to temporal misalignment and suboptimal coordination. For aerial VLN, flight dynamics are particularly sensitive to motion continuity, and misaligned reasoning and control can result in trajectory instability. This research introduces a framework designed to explicitly address these challenges by providing a coordinated fast-slow dual-system, aiming to balance deep semantic reasoning with stable, efficient, low-level flight control.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this work is to address the inherent challenges in long-horizon aerial Vision-Language Navigation (VLN), specifically the conflict between detailed global semantic understanding and the demand for low-latency, temporally coherent flight control. Existing approaches often struggle with this trade-off, leading to unstable flight trajectories and significant decision latency, especially over extended navigation tasks in unfamiliar environments.

The key motivations are:
1. **Resolving Structural Inconsistency:** Current VLN systems for UAVs often suffer from a fundamental inconsistency. High-level semantic reasoning, necessary for interpreting complex natural language instructions and understanding the environment, typically requires computationally intensive processing by large vision-language models. However, the generation of real-time, low-latency flight commands, crucial for maintaining aerial stability and responsiveness, requires rapid decision-making. The goal is to explicitly decouple these two aspects to allow both deep reasoning and fast control to operate effectively without compromising each other.
2. **Enhancing Trajectory Stability and Consistency:** Reactive action prediction methods, common in VLN, tend to focus on instantaneous observations. This can lead to short-term perceptual biases and, consequently, unstable or oscillating trajectories over long navigation horizons. The motivation is to integrate mechanisms that capture cross-temporal action dependencies, thereby improving trajectory consistency and mitigating error accumulation throughout long flights.
3. **Reducing Decision Latency and Execution Time:** The use of large-scale autoregressive inference models for VLN, while potentially improving semantic consistency, often introduces substantial inference latency. This latency makes real-time deployment on agile aerial platforms challenging. A critical motivation is to reduce both the single-action inference latency and the overall task execution time, making UAV-VLN systems more efficient and practical for real-world applications.
4. **Improving Long-Horizon Training Stability:** Training models for long-horizon sequential decision processes often encounters issues such as gradient oscillation and error accumulation. The researchers aim to develop a training strategy that stabilizes optimization, particularly for sequences of actions, to achieve more robust and effective learning.

In summary, the work aims to propose a principled solution, FSD-VLN, that efficiently balances the requirements of comprehensive semantic reasoning and real-time, stable flight control, thereby improving navigation accuracy, robustness, and operational efficiency for long-horizon aerial VLN tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The FSD-VLN framework addresses long-horizon aerial VLN by formulating it as a sequential decision process conditioned on visual observations and language instructions. The core approach involves a fast-slow dual-system decomposition to handle the structural tension between reasoning depth and control frequency.

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation

The objective is to model the conditional distribution $p(a_{1:T} | I_{1:T}, L)$, where $L$ is the natural language instruction, $\{I_t\}_{t=1}^T$ is a sequence of visual observations, and $\{a_t\}_{t=1}^T$ is the sequence of flight actions. This is factorized as $p(a_1 | I_1, L) \prod_{t=2}^T p(a_t | a_{1:t-1}, I_{1:t}, L)$, emphasizing the temporal dependencies. The method argues against a monolithic architecture due to the need for both semantic consistency and temporal coherence.

#### 3.2. Fast-Slow Dual-System Architecture

The FSD-VLN framework consists of two asynchronously operating, coordinated systems: a Slow System for high-level semantic priors and a Fast System for temporally conditioned action generation.

#### 3.3. Slow System

The slow system is responsible for extracting high-level multimodal semantic representations.
- **Input Processing:** Each image frame $I_t$ is processed by a vision encoder to produce visual tokens. These tokens are then fused with textual embeddings from the language instruction $L$.
- **Vision-Language Model (VLM):** A pretrained VLM integrates these fused features to generate multimodal semantic features, denoted as $z_t = f_{VLM}(I_t, L)$. This semantic feature serves as a global contextual prior.
- **Semantic Buffer (VLSF):** The generated semantic features ($V L_{sf}$) are continuously written into a Vision-Language Semantic Feature buffer (VLSF). This buffer is updated whenever new visual observations arrive, ensuring a stable and temporally consistent semantic representation of the environment, which guides subsequent action generation.

#### 3.4. Fast System

The fast system is designed for low-latency, temporally conditioned action generation.
- **Architecture:** It comprises a Diffusion Transformer (DiT) model and three distinct multilayer perceptrons (MLPs): a State Encoder (SED), an Action Encoder (AED), and an Action Decoder (ADD). The DiT architecture, inspired by Peebles et al., features alternating cross-attention and self-attention layers.
- **State and Action Encoding:** The SED encodes the UAV's current state (position, yaw) into a state sequence representation ($S_{seq}$). The AED embeds the historical action sequence into an action sequence representation ($A_{seq}$).
- **Conditioned Action Generation:** The DiT processes these encoded sequences along with the visual-language semantic features from the slow system. If the VLSF has been updated, the most recent feature ($V L_{up_{sf}}$) is used; otherwise, the current cached feature ($V L_{sf}$) is adopted.
- **Diffusion Model for Action Sequences:** The DiT models the conditional distribution of an action sequence of horizon $H$, $a_{t:t+H} \sim p_0(a_{t:t+H} | z_t, s_t, a_{t-1})$. During training, Gaussian noise is progressively added to ground-truth actions, and the DiT learns to denoise this corrupted action sequence, conditioned on the semantic priors ($z_t$), current UAV state embeddings ($s_t$), and previous action embeddings ($a_{t-1}$).
- **Action Decoding:** The DiT outputs a latent representation ($d_f$), which the ADD then uses to generate the updated action ($A_{up}$). This iterative refinement process continues until convergence.
- **Discrete Action Representation:** The model uses a predefined discrete action set of 8 flight primitives (e.g., Forward (3m, 6m, 9m), Turn Left (30°)). While the diffusion model operates in a continuous embedding space, its outputs are mapped to these discrete action intervals via predefined thresholds, balancing generative flexibility with interpretable control commands.

#### 3.5. Dual-System Parallel Execution

The slow and fast systems operate in parallel and asynchronously. The slow system continuously updates the semantic buffer with new visual observations, while the fast system iteratively optimizes action sequences under semantic conditioning. A new RGB observation is acquired and fed to the slow system when the UAV reaches a target waypoint, triggering another round of semantic update and action refinement. This closed-loop design ensures stable and semantically consistent navigation.

#### 3.6. Training Method

- **Global Adaptive Normalization:** To stabilize multimodal training across heterogeneous state and action scales, dataset-level statistical normalization is applied. Each dimension $d$ of input data $x_d$ is normalized using its mean $\mu_d$ and standard deviation $\sigma_d$, and outliers are clipped. This ensures numerical stability for long-horizon diffusion training, with inverse normalization applied during inference.
- **Time-Weighted Optimization (TW-MSE):** To counteract gradient oscillation and error accumulation in long-horizon action prediction, a time-weighted Mean Squared Error (MSE) objective is introduced: $L_{TW-MSE} = \frac{1}{\sum_{b,t} m_{b,t}} \sum_{b=0}^B \sum_{t=0}^T \sum_{h=0}^H w_t \cdot m_{b,t} \cdot \| \hat{a}_{b,t,h} - a_{b,t,h} \|^2$. Here, $w_t$ is a weight coefficient assigned to each timestep, typically assigning larger weights to later timesteps. This strategy encourages the model to maintain long-range consistency while preserving short-term accuracy, stabilizing gradient propagation and improving convergence robustness.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The FSD-VLN framework was evaluated in large-scale simulated low-altitude urban environments.

#### 4.1. Evaluation Protocol

Navigation performance was assessed using four standard metrics:
- **Navigation Error (NE):** Euclidean distance between the final UAV position and the target.
- **Success Rate (SR):** Percentage of trajectories ending within 20 meters of the target.
- **Oracle Success Rate (OSR):** Percentage of trajectories reaching within 20 meters of the target at any timestep.
- **Success Weighted by Path Length (SPL):** A combined metric of success and path efficiency.

#### 4.2. Datasets and Implementation Details

The approach was evaluated using data sampled from AirVLN-S, OpenFly, and a Guangzhou urban rendering scene. The dataset comprised over 30,000 navigation trajectories across diverse urban settings. Trajectory lengths were concentrated between 50-150 meters, and action counts mostly ranged from 20-50 after merging "move forward 3m" actions. Models were initialized with a GR00T N1 pre-trained backbone. During fine-tuning, only the DiT decision module, state encoder, and action decoder were updated, freezing the visual-language encoder. The slow system utilized 16 DiT blocks with specific dimension settings. Optimization employed AdamW and LoRA for 200K steps on 4 RTX 4090 GPUs, requiring approximately 320 GPU hours.

#### 4.3. Main Results

**Navigation Performance Comparison (Table 1):**
FSD-VLN demonstrated improved performance compared to representative baselines, including Random, Seq2Seq, Naid, AerialVLN, and OpenFly, in both seen and unseen environments.
- **Unseen Environments:** FSD-VLN achieved an SR of 13.6% (compared to OpenFly's 5.1%), an SPL of 10.7% (compared to OpenFly's 3.5%), and an NE of 78 (compared to OpenFly's 198). This indicates an improvement of up to 2.6x in SR and 3x in SPL for unseen environments, alongside a substantial reduction in navigation error.
- **Seen Environments:** FSD-VLN maintained a high SR of 26.7% (compared to OpenFly's 18.5%) and an SPL of 22.8% (compared to OpenFly's 12.2%), with an NE of 76 (compared to OpenFly's 115).
The results suggest that formulating action prediction as a temporally conditioned generative process, rather than relying solely on instantaneous observations, improves global trajectory alignment and stability by mitigating myopic perception bias and suppressing oscillatory behaviors.

**Action Generation Efficiency (Table 2 and 3):**
- **Single-Action Generation Latency:** FSD-VLN reduced per-action inference latency from 402 ms (OpenFly) to 176 ms, demonstrating a reduction of approximately 56%.
- **Overall Task Execution Time:** On 214 randomly sampled trajectories, FSD-VLN decreased the total execution time from 307.6 seconds to 144.7 seconds, representing a 53% reduction. The average number of executed actions was also reduced by approximately 10%. When evaluating 10 identical successful trajectories for both methods, FSD-VLN showed a 56% reduction in execution time (from 52.71s to 22.78s). These efficiency gains are attributed to both reduced per-step inference overhead and enhanced trajectory-level consistency, which minimizes redundant corrective maneuvers.

**Qualitative Example (Figure 4):**
A qualitative example illustrated successful navigation where the agent used landmark-based visual information (e.g., "two grey buildings with many black windows," "gray building with fan-shaped windows") to sequentially adjust its heading and locate the target. This supports the framework's ability to integrate visual semantic information with low-level flight control.

#### 4.4. Ablation Study

**Effect of Time-Weighted Optimization (Figure 5):**
The introduction of Time-Weighted MSE (TW-MSE) loss during fine-tuning, which assigns temporally adaptive weights, resulted in a smoother convergence behavior, reduced oscillation amplitude, and a lower final loss compared to standard MSE. This suggests that the temporal reweighting mechanism stabilizes gradient propagation and alleviates error amplification in long sequences, enhancing the model’s ability to balance short-term action precision and long-term trajectory planning.

**Effect of Action Horizon (Table 4):**
The parameter $H$ (action horizon) controls the number of future actions generated per inference step. Performance was evaluated for $H \in \{1, 2, 4\}$.
- $H=1$ achieved the best SR (20.13%), SPL (18.06%), and lowest NE (88).
- Performance consistently degraded as $H$ increased.
This indicates a trade-off: while longer horizons could offer more foresight, they amplify prediction bias due to perceptual latency and environmental non-stationarity, leading to reduced trajectory stability. Maintaining short-horizon reactivity while leveraging structured temporal modeling appears to be a more stable strategy for long-distance aerial navigation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents FSD-VLN, a fast-slow dual-system framework, as a solution to critical challenges in long-distance, low-altitude UAV vision-language navigation. The work demonstrates a method to explicitly coordinate high-level semantic reasoning with low-level action generation, addressing the structural inconsistency that often leads to high decision latency and unstable trajectories in existing approaches.

The proposed architecture provides a unified solution for autonomous navigation in complex, open environments by integrating multimodal perception, temporally structured decision modeling, and real-time control. The fast-slow decomposition offers a principled approach to balancing global planning consistency with real-time responsiveness in UAV-VLN.

The quantitative results highlight several significant impacts:
- **Improved Navigation Accuracy and Robustness:** FSD-VLN demonstrates an improvement of up to 2x in success rate for navigation in unseen environments compared to prior art, along with a notable reduction in navigation error. This indicates enhanced global trajectory alignment and stability, which are crucial for reliable autonomous operation.
- **Enhanced Decision Efficiency:** The framework significantly reduces single-action inference latency by over 50% and decreases overall task execution time by more than 50%. This efficiency gain is critical for real-time deployment on agile aerial platforms, bridging the gap between computational complexity and practical operational requirements.
- **Stable Long-Horizon Learning:** The introduction of a time-weighted optimization strategy contributes to smoother training convergence and reduced gradient oscillations, improving the robustness and effectiveness of learning over long action sequences.

These advancements collectively contribute to the development of more capable and efficient UAV-VLN systems. The work provides a foundation for the deployment of intelligent aerial autonomy in diverse applications, ranging from environmental monitoring to emergency response, where intuitive human-machine interaction and adaptability are paramount.

Future work outlined by the authors, such as scaling the framework with larger multimodal foundation models, exploring multi-UAV coordination, and transitioning to real-world deployment, suggests continued potential for broader impact and practical application beyond simulated environments.
