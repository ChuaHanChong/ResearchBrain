# RoboOmni: Proactive Robot Manipulation in Omni-modal Context

> [!summary] Summary
> RoboOmni, developed by Fudan University and NUS, introduces an end-to-end omni-modal framework for robots to proactively infer and verify user intentions from human speech, environmental sounds, and visual cues. The system achieved an 85.6% success rate on cross-modal contextual instructions, outperforming baselines like NORA (25.9%), and reduced inference latency by 0.49x compared to ASR-based approaches.

## Problem

- Robotic manipulation systems largely rely on explicit commands, failing to understand implicit user intentions conveyed through natural human communication.
- Current Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models lack systematic ways to infer user intent from 'cross-modal contextual instructions' where intent is derived, not explicitly given.
- ASR-based approaches for speech instructions discard critical paralinguistic cues and cannot interpret environmental sounds, hindering proactive and intuitive human-robot interaction.

## Method

- A Perceiver-Thinker-Talker-Executor architecture forms an end-to-end omni-modal LLM framework, unifying speech, environmental audio, vision, and robotic actions.
- The central 'Thinker' component, based on an LLM backbone, autoregressively generates text tokens, speech representations, and action tokens for seamless reasoning and control.
- The large-scale OmniAction dataset was created with 141,162 multimodal episodes, including diverse contextual instruction categories and simulated real-world acoustic conditions, to address data scarcity.

## Results

- RoboOmni achieved an 85.6% success rate on cross-modal contextual instructions in simulation (OmniAction-LIBERO-TTS), significantly surpassing the strongest baseline (NORA at 25.9%).
- On real human-recorded speech (OmniAction-LIBERO-Real), RoboOmni demonstrated robust performance with a 76.6% average success rate, outperforming text-based VLAs and showing resilience to acoustic variability.
- The framework achieved 0.49x lower inference latency per step compared to ASR + OpenVLA, demonstrating improved runtime efficiency by eliminating the ASR bottleneck.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Direct, end-to-end processing of raw audio (speech and environmental sounds) with visual input and robotic actions is essential for robustly inferring implicit intentions, outperforming modular or ASR-dependent systems.
> - Unified autoregressive modeling across perception, language, and robotic control successfully integrates intention recognition, verbal confirmation, and action execution within a single framework.
> - A purpose-built, multimodal dataset like OmniAction, rich in contextual and acoustic cues, provides the necessary training ground for models to develop proactive assistance capabilities.
