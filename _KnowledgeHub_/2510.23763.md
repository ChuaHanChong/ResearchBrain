# RoboOmni: Proactive Robot Manipulation in Omni-modal Context

> [!summary] Summary
> RoboOmni, developed by Fudan University and NUS, introduces an end-to-end omni-modal framework for robots to proactively infer and verify user intentions from human speech, environmental sounds, and visual cues. The system achieved an 85.6% success rate on cross-modal contextual instructions, outperforming baselines like NORA (25.9%), and reduced inference latency by 0.49x compared to ASR-based approaches.

## Problem

- Robotic manipulation systems largely rely on explicit commands, failing to understand implicit user intentions conveyed through natural human communication.
- Current Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models lack systematic ways to infer user intent from 'cross-modal contextual instructions' where intent is derived, not explicitly given.
- ASR-based approaches for speech instructions discard critical paralinguistic cues and cannot interpret environmental sounds, hindering proactive and intuitive human-robot interaction.

## Method

- A Perceiver-Thinker-Talker-Executor architecture forms an end-to-end omni-modal LLM framework, unifying speech, environmental audio, vision, and robotic actions.
- The central 'Thinker' component, based on an LLM backbone, autoregressively generates text tokens, speech representations, and action tokens for seamless reasoning and control.
- The large-scale OmniAction dataset was created with 141,162 multimodal episodes, including diverse contextual instruction categories and simulated real-world acoustic conditions, to address data scarcity.

## Results

- RoboOmni achieved an 85.6% success rate on cross-modal contextual instructions in simulation (OmniAction-LIBERO-TTS), significantly surpassing the strongest baseline (NORA at 25.9%).
- On real human-recorded speech (OmniAction-LIBERO-Real), RoboOmni demonstrated robust performance with a 76.6% average success rate, outperforming text-based VLAs and showing resilience to acoustic variability.
- The framework achieved 0.49x lower inference latency per step compared to ASR + OpenVLA, demonstrating improved runtime efficiency by eliminating the ASR bottleneck.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Direct, end-to-end processing of raw audio (speech and environmental sounds) with visual input and robotic actions is essential for robustly inferring implicit intentions, outperforming modular or ASR-dependent systems.
> - Unified autoregressive modeling across perception, language, and robotic control successfully integrates intention recognition, verbal confirmation, and action execution within a single framework.
> - A purpose-built, multimodal dataset like OmniAction, rich in contextual and acoustic cues, provides the necessary training ground for models to develop proactive assistance capabilities.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation has seen significant advancements with the advent of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, often leveraging Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs). These models aim to enable robots to understand human instructions and execute physical tasks. The current landscape is largely dominated by two categories:
1. **End-to-end models:** These directly map vision-language inputs to motor actions, offering fluidity but often limited by explicit command structures.
2. **Modular Brain-Cerebellum models:** These use LLMs/VLMs for high-level planning, decomposing tasks into sub-goals for low-level controllers, which can suffer from fragmentation and interface constraints.

RoboOmni positions itself within the end-to-end VLA paradigm, seeking to overcome two fundamental limitations of existing approaches:

- **Instruction Type:** Most VLA models operate on explicit instructions, ranging from simple direct commands to more complex but still explicitly stated forms. While some recent work has started exploring inferential text-based instructions, systematic studies on how robots can proactively infer intent from context are scarce. RoboOmni introduces a novel setting of "cross-modal contextual instructions" where intent is *derived* rather than *given*, reflecting how humans naturally communicate.
- **Instruction Source:** Current systems predominantly rely on textual instructions or speech transcribed via Automatic Speech Recognition (ASR). ASR-based approaches suffer from a critical flaw: they discard vital paralinguistic cues (tone, intonation, emotion) and cannot interpret environmental sounds, which are crucial in real-world human interaction. This limitation prevents robots from understanding implicit intentions and reacting proactively. While some efforts have begun to integrate direct speech commands, they still largely convert existing textual commands and neglect environmental audio entirely.

RoboOmni directly addresses these gaps by proposing an end-to-end omni-modal framework that integrates human speech, environmental sounds, and visual cues to proactively infer, confirm, and execute user intentions. This moves beyond merely following explicit commands to understanding and anticipating user needs, which is a significant step toward more natural and intuitive human-robot interaction. It extends the concept of omni-modal LLMs (which typically output text or audio) into the embodied domain by generating physical actions and interactive speech.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for RoboOmni stems from the observation that human-robot collaboration in real-world settings is severely hampered by robots' inability to understand implicit user intentions. Humans rarely issue direct, explicit commands in daily interactions; instead, intentions are often conveyed through a rich tapestry of spoken dialogue, emotional tones, environmental sounds, and visual cues.

The key research question driving this work is: **Can a robot integrate cross-modal context, including speech, environmental audio, and visual observations, to proactively infer and verify user intent?**

To answer this, RoboOmni sets forth the following objectives:

- **Introduce a New Paradigm:** Define and establish the "cross-modal contextual instructions" setting for robotic manipulation, where latent user intent is inferred from a fusion of auditory (speech, environmental sound) and visual cues, rather than explicit commands.
- **Develop an End-to-End Omni-modal Framework:** Propose **RoboOmni**, a unified framework that seamlessly integrates intention recognition, interaction confirmation (through speech), and action execution within a single, end-to-end omni-modal Large Language Model. This aims to overcome the limitations of modular or ASR-dependent pipelines.
- **Support Direct Speech Interaction:** Enable robots to process human speech directly, capturing paralinguistic cues and avoiding the information loss and latency associated with ASR-based transcription.
- **Incorporate Environmental Acoustics:** Fuse environmental sounds with speech and visual information for robust intent recognition, mirroring how humans use auditory context to understand situations.
- **Address Data Scarcity:** Create a large-scale, diverse dataset, **OmniAction**, specifically designed for proactive intention recognition in robotic manipulation, complete with multimodal cues and inferential instructions. This dataset also includes a simulation benchmark, **OmniAction-LIBERO**, for standardized evaluation.
- **Demonstrate Emerging Cognitive Intelligence:** Validate RoboOmni's capabilities in both simulation and real-world environments, showcasing its ability to exhibit proactive assistance, superior intention recognition, and improved inference efficiency compared to existing baselines.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

RoboOmni adopts an **end-to-end omni-modal LLM framework** structured around a **Perceiver–Thinker–Talker–Executor** architecture. This design unifies speech, environmental audio, vision, and robotic actions into a single autoregressive model.

#### 3.1. Architecture Components

- **Perceiver (Multimodal Input Encoding):** This component is responsible for processing heterogeneous input modalities—text, audio, and visual—into a unified embedding space. It leverages a pipeline similar to Qwen2.5-Omni, generating visual embeddings from images and audio embeddings from raw audio segments. These, along with textual context, form the unified input representation for the Thinker.
- **Thinker (Omni-Modal Reasoning):** As the central reasoning engine, the Thinker is built upon an LLM backbone. It processes the unified multimodal representations from the Perceiver and generates contextually appropriate outputs. A key innovation is its ability to autoregressively produce sequences that seamlessly interleave text tokens, speech representations, and action tokens, enabling unified reasoning across perception, language, and robotic control.
- **Talker (Speech Generation):** This hierarchical component allows the system to generate natural speech responses. It receives high-level semantic representations and text tokens from the Thinker and converts them into speech waveforms, facilitating seamless voice interaction with users.
- **Executor (Action Generation):** To integrate robotic control, the Thinker's vocabulary is extended with 2048 discrete action tokens, utilizing the FAST+ tokenizer. This approach represents continuous action vectors (e.g., 7-DoF control) as short sequences of discrete symbols. The Thinker autoregressively generates these action tokens, which the Executor then decodes back into executable robot commands.

#### 3.2. Dual-Mode Generation & Training Paradigms

RoboOmni supports both conversational responses and robotic actions through a unified autoregressive objective.

- **Text and Speech Generation:** For conversational interactions, the Thinker generates text tokens, which can optionally be converted to speech by the Talker module, based on the multimodal context.
- **Action Generation:** For robotic control, the Thinker predicts discrete action tokens in chunks, which are then decoded into continuous actions by the Executor.

The model is trained using a unified autoregressive maximum-likelihood objective. This objective learns to predict either conversational replies (text tokens) or action sequences (action tokens) from the multimodal input, effectively treating both as outputs in a shared token space.

#### 3.3. OmniAction Dataset Construction

To overcome the critical challenge of data scarcity for proactive intention recognition, the authors constructed **OmniAction**, a large-scale multimodal dataset.

- **Overview:** OmniAction comprises 141,162 multi-modal episodes covering 112 skills and 748 objects. It features over 5,000 distinct speaker timbres, 2,482 non-verbal sound events, and 640 environmental backgrounds. It introduces **six contextual instruction categories**: Sentiment Cues, Overlapping Voices, Non-Verbal Cues, Identity Cues, Dyadic Dialogue, and Triadic Dialogue.
- **Construction Process:** A three-stage pipeline was used:
 1. **Textual Scripting:** Tasks and trajectories from Open-X Embodiment datasets were transformed into contextual instructions using GPT-4o. This involved filtering low-information samples, rewriting instructions into multi-turn household dialogues spanning the six categories, extending dialogues with human-robot interaction, and validating intent consistency.
 2. **Auditory Realization:** Dialogues were converted into audio reflecting real household conditions. This involved speech synthesis using high-fidelity TTS engines (MOSS-TTSD, CosyVoice, Gemini-TTS) with voice cloning, multi-speaker simulation (including controlled overlaps via CTC-based methods), insertion of contextual non-verbal sound events, and mixing with diverse environmental backgrounds at varying Signal-to-Noise Ratios (SNRs).
 3. **Verification:** Manual evaluation ensured that task intent was reliably recoverable from the dialogues (98.7% agreement).
- **Simulation Dataset: OmniAction-LIBERO:** A simulation benchmark was built upon LIBERO, with two variants: OmniAction-LIBERO-TTS (synthetically generated audio) and OmniAction-LIBERO-Real (real human-recorded speech from 10 volunteers).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

RoboOmni's performance was rigorously evaluated in both simulation and real-world settings against various baselines, demonstrating significant improvements.

#### 4.1. Evaluation on Cross-modal Contextual Instructions (OmniAction-LIBERO-TTS)

- **Superior Performance:** RoboOmni achieved an overall 85.6% success rate, dramatically outperforming the strongest baseline (NORA, 25.9%) and other cascaded methods (all below 10%).
- **Cruciality of End-to-End Auditory Integration:** Text-only models (whether ground-truth or ASR-transcribed) consistently failed to capture paralinguistic cues and contextual information, with the best textual baseline scoring only 25.9%. RoboOmni's direct audio processing enabled it to achieve over 76% success across all instruction types.
- **Robust Intent Recognition:** RoboOmni maintained high performance (Goal: 85.8%, Object: 84.0%) even in challenging suites with semantic ambiguity (multiple objects/actions), where baselines struggled (averaging 16.3% and 13.8% for best baselines).
- **Varying Cognitive Demands:** Dyadic and Overlapping tasks were relatively easier for end-to-end models (~88% success), while Non-Verbal instructions proved hardest (~82%), requiring complex integration of non-speech audio with visual and speech cues.

#### 4.2. Evaluation on Real Human Audio Direct Instructions (OmniAction-LIBERO-Real)

- **Robustness to Real-World Acoustics:** RoboOmni achieved the highest average performance (76.6%) on real human-recorded speech, surpassing strong text-based VLAs like π₀ (73.8%), OpenVLA (40.1%), and NORA (17.4%).
- **ASR Limitations:** ASR-based VLAs suffered from acoustic variability (accents, noise), leading to recognition errors that degraded performance. RoboOmni's direct speech processing bypassed this bottleneck, benefiting from pretraining on diverse speakers and sounds.

#### 4.3. Real-World Experiments

- **Successful Transfer:** Fine-tuning on a real WidowX 250S robot arm with volunteer-recorded speech demonstrated RoboOmni's capabilities beyond simulation.
- **Key Real-World Competencies:** RoboOmni showed strong intent recognition (e.g., identifying objects based on audio and visual cues), effective interaction (proactively asking clarifying questions like "should I...?", awaiting confirmation), and reliable execution (locating and manipulating correct objects).

#### 4.4. Evaluation of Proactive Assistance Capabilities

- **Superior Intent Recognition Accuracy:** RoboOmni achieved 88.9% accuracy in recognizing user intent under contextual instructions, significantly outperforming Qwen2.5-Omni (3B/7B) and ASR+GPT-4o, confirming the advantage of end-to-end speech-action modeling in preserving paralinguistic cues and dialogue context. GPT-4o, despite ASR noise, showed stronger reasoning than smaller omni models, highlighting the need for both acoustic modeling and robust reasoning.
- **Enhanced Interaction Capability:** Qualitatively, RoboOmni excelled in proactive clarification of ambiguous instructions, multi-modal integration (combining speech and environmental sounds), and maintaining natural, collaborative dialogue flows, in contrast to baselines that often failed or gave irrelevant responses.

#### 4.5. Further Analysis

- **Improved Training Efficiency via Pretraining:** Pretraining on OmniAction led to rapid convergence (nearly 90% accuracy within 2k steps) on downstream tasks, significantly outperforming training from scratch (∼30% after 20k steps), highlighting the strong generalizable priors provided by the dataset.
- **Advantage Over Cascaded Pipelines:** RoboOmni consistently outperformed cascaded planner-controller pipelines (Qwen2.5-Omni as planner + text-based VLAs as controllers). This demonstrated that end-to-end speech-action learning avoids semantic drift, lossy interfaces, and better preserves intent fidelity compared to modular approaches, especially in handling paralinguistic cues like speaker identity.
- **Enhanced Inference Efficiency:** RoboOmni achieved 0.49x lower latency per inference compared to ASR + OpenVLA (1.0x baseline), indicating that end-to-end audio-action modeling effectively eliminates the ASR bottleneck and substantially improves runtime efficiency.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The RoboOmni project makes several significant contributions that collectively advance the field of human-robot interaction and embodied AI:

- **Pioneering a New Paradigm for HRI:** By introducing "cross-modal contextual instructions," RoboOmni shifts the paradigm from robots passively awaiting explicit commands to actively inferring and verifying user intent from a rich multimodal context. This is a crucial step towards making human-robot collaboration more natural, intuitive, and efficient, mirroring human-human communication.
- **Robust End-to-End Omni-Modal Framework:** The proposed Perceiver-Thinker-Talker-Executor framework based on end-to-end omni-modal LLMs represents a powerful architectural advancement. It unifies perception, reasoning, interaction, and action within a single model, overcoming the limitations of modular pipelines (e.g., fragmentation, latency, loss of paralinguistic cues). This integrated approach leads to higher success rates, faster inference, and more robust performance in complex, ambiguous scenarios.
- **Addressing Data Scarcity with OmniAction:** The creation of the large-scale OmniAction dataset, along with its simulation benchmark OmniAction-LIBERO, is a monumental contribution. This dataset is meticulously designed to include diverse contextual instruction types, speakers, environmental sounds, and visual cues, filling a critical gap in existing resources for proactive intention reasoning in robotics. Its availability will undoubtedly accelerate future research in this domain.
- **Demonstrating Emerging Cognitive Intelligence:** RoboOmni's ability to interpret implicit intentions, engage in proactive clarification dialogues, and fuse complex multimodal signals (speech, environmental audio, vision) for action execution signifies a tangible step towards robots exhibiting higher levels of cognitive intelligence. This capability allows robots to act as more intelligent and collaborative partners rather than mere command followers.
- **Practical Implications:** The advancements presented by RoboOmni have profound practical implications. Robots equipped with such capabilities could find applications in a wide range of settings, from assisting in homes (e.g., smart kitchens, elderly care) and workplaces (e.g., manufacturing, logistics) to public spaces. Imagine a robot noticing a user’s sigh of frustration while looking at a cluttered desk and proactively offering to organize it, or anticipating a need based on environmental sounds like a phone ringing. This level of proactive assistance could significantly enhance productivity, safety, and user experience in human-robot co-existence.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** RoboOmni provides a strong foundation for future research in several directions, including further enhancing reasoning capabilities, exploring more complex social cues, adapting to novel environments, and developing more sophisticated interaction strategies to make robots truly indispensable and intelligent assistants. The open-source nature of the project (OpenMOSS, GitHub, Hugging Face) further promises to foster community engagement and accelerate innovation.
