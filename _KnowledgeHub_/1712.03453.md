# Single-Shot Multi-Person 3D Pose Estimation From Monocular RGB

> [!summary] Summary
> A single-shot fully-convolutional method estimates 3D pose for all people in a monocular RGB image. Occlusion-robust pose-maps encode each joint's 3D location redundantly at torso and limb pixel locations, so a fixed number of output maps covers any number of people, with part affinity fields giving person association and a hierarchical torso-then-limb read-out. It reaches 65.0 3DPCK on the new MuPoTS-3D benchmark.

## Problem

- Existing monocular 3D pose methods assume a single un-occluded subject, so they fail badly under inter-person and object occlusion in natural cluttered scenes.
- Prior multi-person 3D approaches decompose scenes into single-person bounding-box instances, needing redundant proposals plus fusion post-processing, and detectors break under strong occlusion.
- No large multi-person training set of real images carried 3D annotations, and no in-the-wild benchmark existed with more than two interacting annotated subjects.

## Method

- ==Occlusion-robust pose-maps== store each joint's 3D coordinate at torso pixels (neck, pelvis) and at every 2D joint of its own limb, giving selective redundancy with a fixed channel count.
- Hierarchical read-out takes a base full-body pose at neck or pelvis, then refines each limb at its extremity or, when occluded, walks up the kinematic chain; part affinity fields handle person association.
- MuCo-3DHP composites segmented single-person MPI-INF-3DHP frames into depth-ordered multi-person images with background and shading-aware clothing augmentation; MuPoTS-3D adds a filmed markerless-mocap test set.

## Results

- On MuPoTS-3D the method reaches **65.0** 3DPCK versus **53.8** for LCR-net and **61.1** for VNect on ground-truth crops, detecting **93%** of annotated subjects versus **86%**.
- Under occlusion it stays ahead: **48.7** versus **42** 3DPCK on occluded MuPoTS-3D joints, and **62.8-64.0** versus **53.2** for VNect on synthetically occluded MPI-INF-3DHP joints.
- Limb refinement adds about **10** 3DPCK over torso-only read-out (**75.2** versus **65.6** single-person; **73.4** versus **63.6** multi-person), and Human3.6M error is **69.9mm** versus **87.7mm** for LCR-net.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Redundant encoding of structured output at multiple spatially distinct read-out sites buys occlusion robustness without variable-size outputs or instance detection.
> - Redundancy must be selective: allowing full-body read-out at every joint creates supervision conflicts between overlapping people, while limb-scoped redundancy confines conflicts to like joints.
> - Poses read at torso centers regress toward the training mean, so coarse-to-fine read-out near articulated extremities recovers detail that a single global estimate loses.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The problem of 3D human pose estimation from monocular RGB input is a fundamental and challenging task in computer vision with widespread applications, including activity recognition, human-computer interaction, robotics, and content creation for graphics. While significant progress has been made in single-person 2D and 3D pose estimation, the research landscape for *multi-person* 3D pose estimation, especially in cluttered and occluded "in-the-wild" scenes, remains largely unexplored and significantly more complex.

Prior work in multi-person pose estimation largely falls into two categories:
1. **Multi-person 2D Pose Estimation:** Methods like OpenPose have successfully tackled 2D multi-person pose by first localizing joints and then associating them to individuals (bottom-up), or by detecting individuals first and then estimating their pose (top-down).
2. **Single-Person 3D Pose Estimation:** Numerous methods exist for single-person 3D pose from monocular RGB, often relying on deep learning to regress 3D joint coordinates or fit 3D human models. However, these methods typically assume an un-occluded subject and struggle with partial visibility.

The critical gap addressed by this paper lies at the intersection of these two areas: *multi-person 3D pose estimation from a single image under significant occlusions*. Existing attempts at multi-person 3D often adopt a "top-down" approach, where individual persons are first detected using bounding boxes, and then single-person 3D pose estimation is applied within each box. This strategy, however, has severe limitations: person detectors may fail under strong occlusions, and the computational cost increases with the number of detected persons, making it inefficient for dense scenes. Furthermore, these methods often struggle to fuse redundant or conflicting predictions for occluded parts.

This paper tackles these limitations head-on by proposing a "single-shot" (bottom-up) approach, meaning it processes the entire image once to jointly infer 2D and 3D joint locations for all people, without needing explicit bounding box proposals. This aligns with the shift seen in 2D multi-person pose estimation towards bottom-up methods (e.g., Part Affinity Fields) which are inherently more robust to occlusions and scale better with scene complexity.

Crucially, the broader research landscape also suffers from a severe lack of suitable training and evaluation datasets for multi-person 3D pose. Existing 3D datasets are predominantly single-person, while multi-person datasets mostly offer only 2D annotations. The few exceptions are limited in terms of number of people, interaction complexity, and background diversity. This paper directly addresses this data scarcity by introducing two new datasets: MuCo-3DHP (for training) and MuPoTS-3D (for evaluation), significantly advancing the benchmarks available for this challenging problem.

In summary, this work represents a significant leap forward by proposing a novel, occlusion-robust, single-shot method that aims to be robust and efficient for multi-person 3D pose estimation in unconstrained real-world environments, simultaneously pushing the boundaries of available training and evaluation data.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a novel, single-shot method capable of accurately estimating the 3D pose of multiple individuals from a single monocular RGB camera in general, cluttered scenes. This encompasses the challenging scenarios involving strong partial occlusions by other people and objects within the scene.

The motivation behind this ambitious objective stems directly from the significant limitations observed in the existing state-of-the-art methods:

- **Addressing Occlusion Challenges:** Current 3D pose estimation methods, even highly effective ones, are predominantly designed for single, un-occluded subjects. Natural human activities, however, frequently involve multiple people interacting, leading to both self-occlusions and significant inter-person occlusions. These occlusions cause drastic failures in existing single-person methods, highlighting the need for an inherently occlusion-robust approach.
- **Overcoming Limitations of Top-Down Approaches:** Many multi-person 3D pose estimation attempts decompose the problem into multiple single-person instances, relying on initial person detection (bounding box prediction). This "top-down" strategy is prone to failure when person detectors struggle under heavy occlusion, and it can be computationally expensive and redundant in dense crowd scenarios, necessitating complex post-processing to filter and fuse predictions. The authors are motivated to bypass this by proposing a "single-shot" (bottom-up) approach that reasons about all people jointly in one forward pass, thereby increasing robustness and efficiency.
- **Tackling Data Scarcity:** A major bottleneck in advancing multi-person 3D pose estimation is the lack of large-scale, diverse, and well-annotated training and testing datasets. While 2D multi-person datasets exist, and some 3D single-person datasets are available, there is a critical absence of real multi-person datasets with accurate 3D ground truth, especially ones capturing complex human–human interactions and varied backgrounds. Synthetic datasets often lack realism. This paper is motivated to address this by introducing new, high-quality datasets to facilitate research and provide robust benchmarks.
- **Improving Articulation and Generalizability:** The authors observed that prior multi-person 3D methods might produce "conservative" pose estimates, tending towards neutral poses and lacking the full extent of human articulation, particularly in limbs. They aim to improve the accuracy and articulateness of pose predictions, especially for extremities, even under challenging conditions.

In essence, the research aims to move beyond idealized single-person scenarios to enable practical and accurate 3D pose estimation for multiple people in unconstrained real-world settings, driven by the need for more robust algorithms and comprehensive evaluation benchmarks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of the proposed methodology is a novel "Occlusion-Robust Pose-Map (ORPM)" formulation, integrated into a single-shot, fully convolutional neural network. This approach distinguishes itself by jointly reasoning about all people in a scene during a single forward pass, eliminating the need for explicit bounding box predictions.

#### 3.1. Network Architecture

The network architecture builds upon a ResNet-50 backbone. It splits into two main streams:
- **2DPose+Affinity Stream:** This stream is responsible for predicting 2D heatmaps (confidence maps for 2D joint locations) based on the MS-COCO joint set, and Part Affinity Fields (PAFs). PAFs encode the direction and magnitude of vectors connecting anatomically related joints, crucial for associating individual joints to specific persons. This stream's output resolution is $W/8 \times H/8$.
- **3DPose Stream:** This stream predicts 3D Occlusion-Robust Pose-Maps (ORPMs) and 2D heatmaps for the MPI-INF-3DHP joint set. ORPMs are central to encoding 3D joint locations. This stream outputs at a resolution of $W/4 \times H/4$.
The network is designed to predict a fixed number of output maps (heatmaps, pose-maps, PAFs) regardless of the number of people in the scene, which is a key characteristic of its single-shot nature.

#### 3.2. Occlusion-Robust Pose-Maps (ORPMs)

ORPMs are an extension of the "location-map" concept from prior work. Instead of simply storing a 3D coordinate at a single 2D joint pixel location, ORPMs incorporate redundancy to enable robust pose inference even under strong occlusions. The body is decomposed into a torso, four limbs, and a head.
The redundancy is structured in multiple levels:
- **Base Pose Read-out:** The complete base pose (a representation of the entire body's 3D pose) can be read out at either the neck or pelvis 2D joint locations. These torso joints are generally less prone to occlusion and provide a reliable starting point.
- **Limb Refinement:** The base pose can be refined by reading out the pose for individual limbs (or the head) at any of their respective 2D joint locations. For example, the pose of an arm can be read out from the wrist, elbow, or shoulder 2D locations. This allows capturing full articulation.
- **Multi-Person Encoding:** ORPMs store information for all people in the scene within the same map channel for a given joint type. If read-out locations for the *same joint type* (e.g., two right wrists) of different people overlap, the map encodes information for the person closer to the camera. However, due to the built-in redundancy (i.e., information for the occluded person's limb might still be available at their elbow or shoulder), a complete pose estimate can still be obtained. This selective redundancy avoids conflicts that would arise if the *entire* pose of a person was encoded at *every* joint location.

#### 3.3. Pose Inference (Read-Out Process)

After the network predicts the heatmaps, ORPMs, and PAFs, a two-step inference process extracts the 3D poses:
1. **2D Joint Detection and Association:** The 2D heatmaps and PAFs are used to infer 2D joint locations for all people in the image and associate them to individual identities, similar to the method described by Cao et al..
2. **3D Pose Read-out:** For each detected person, the 3D pose is inferred from the ORPMs using a hierarchical, occlusion-aware strategy:
 - First, a "base pose" for the entire person is read from the ORPMs at the 2D pixel location of the neck (or pelvis if the neck is invalid). This provides a default, albeit potentially averaged, pose.
 - Then, for each limb (and head), the process attempts to refine the base pose. It starts by checking the most "extremity" joint (wrist, ankle, head). If this extremity joint's 2D detection is "valid" (un-occluded and sufficiently isolated from other people's same-type joints), the complete limb pose is read out from the ORPMs at that extremity's 2D location and replaces the corresponding part of the base pose.
 - If the extremity joint is invalid (e.g., occluded), the process walks up the kinematic chain (e.g., from wrist to elbow, or ankle to knee) to find the first valid 2D joint location for that limb. If a valid location is found, the limb pose is read out there.
 - If no valid 2D joint location can be found for an entire limb, the base pose for that limb (read out from the torso) is retained. This ensures that even heavily occluded body parts still have a 3D pose estimate, albeit less articulated.

#### 3.4. Dataset Contributions

A significant part of the methodology involves developing new datasets:
- **MuCo-3DHP (Multi-person Composited 3D Human Pose):** This is a large-scale training dataset created by compositing real single-person images from MPI-INF-3DHP (which includes 3D ground truth and segmentation masks) into multi-person scenes. The compositing is 3D-aware, ensuring correct depth ordering and realistic occlusions. Background augmentation and shading-aware foreground augmentation are applied to increase diversity and realism, simulating a wide range of inter-person overlaps and activities.
- **MuPoTS-3D (Multi-person Pose Test Set in 3D):** This is a new, real-world evaluation dataset consisting of 20 filmed sequences (indoor and outdoor) with up to three subjects. Ground-truth 3D pose is obtained using a multi-view marker-less motion capture system. It features challenging occlusions, interactions, and diverse backgrounds, making it a robust benchmark for methods generalizing to real scenes.

#### 3.5. Training Details

The network is trained in stages. The core ResNet-50 and the 2DPose+Affinity branch are first pre-trained on MS-COCO (and MPI, LSP as done in). The 3DPose stream is then trained with either the single-person MPI-INF-3DHP or the multi-person MuCo-3DHP datasets. Losses include per-pixel L2 loss for heatmaps and PAFs, and a weighted per-pixel L2 loss for ORPMs in the neighborhood of valid read-out locations.

This comprehensive approach combining a novel pose representation, a single-shot network architecture, and extensive new datasets addresses the core challenges of multi-person 3D pose estimation in complex scenes.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents compelling evidence that its proposed method significantly advances the state-of-the-art in multi-person 3D pose estimation, particularly in challenging scenarios involving occlusions. The evaluation is rigorous, using both newly introduced multi-person benchmarks and established single-person datasets.

#### 4.1. Multi-Person Pose Performance on MuPoTS-3D

- The method demonstrates a substantial improvement over the only comparable multi-person 3D pose estimation method at the time, LCR-net.
- On the new MuPoTS-3D test set, the proposed method achieves an overall 3DPCK (Percentage of Correct Keypoints) of **65.0**, significantly outperforming LCR-net's **53.8**.
- Even when considering only successfully detected subjects (ignoring cases where a person was missed entirely), the proposed method still outperforms LCR-net (69.8 3DPCK vs 62.4 3DPCK, and 132.5mm vs 146mm MPJPE).
- The method also exhibits a higher subject detection rate (93% vs 86% for LCR-net), which is crucial for multi-person scenarios.
- Qualitative results further highlight LCR-net's tendency to predict more "neutral poses" with limited limb articulation and its greater susceptibility to detection failures under occlusion, in contrast to the proposed method's robustness.

#### 4.2. Single-Person Pose Performance

- Despite being designed for the harder multi-person task, the proposed method demonstrates competitive performance on single-person benchmarks, proving the generalizability and efficacy of the ORPM formulation.
- **MPI-INF-3DHP Dataset:** The multi-person trained variant achieves 73.4 3DPCK, which is comparable to leading single-person methods like VNect (76.6 3DPCK) and Mehta et al. (75.7 3DPCK), and significantly better than LCR-net (59.7 3DPCK) and Zhou et al. (69.2 3DPCK). The single-person trained variant performs even better at 75.2 3DPCK.
- Notably, the method shows superior performance for activities with significant self- and object-occlusion (e.g., "Sit on Chair," "Crouch/Reach"), indicating the inherent robustness of the ORPM formulation.
- **Human3.6m Dataset:** The proposed method achieves an average MPJPE of 69.6mm, which is a considerable improvement over LCR-net (87.7mm) and the original VNect (80.5mm). This places its performance on par with recent state-of-the-art single-person methods.

#### 4.3. Occlusion Robustness

- Through controlled experiments with synthetic occlusions on MPI-INF-3DHP, the method shows a marked advantage in estimating occluded joints. Both the single-person and multi-person variants significantly outperform VNect for occluded joints (62.8/64.0 3DPCK vs 53.2 3DPCK).
- On the real-world MuPoTS-3D dataset, with natural self- and inter-personal occlusions (≈23.7% of joints occluded), the proposed method maintains its lead over LCR-net for both occluded (48.7 3DPCK vs 42 3DPCK) and un-occluded (70.0 3DPCK vs 57.5 3DPCK) joints, confirming its practical robustness to varied occlusion types.

#### 4.4. Ablation Study: Importance of Limb Refinement

- An ablation study explicitly validates the benefits of the proposed hierarchical read-out strategy with limb refinement.
- Reading out the full pose with limb refinement ("Full" read-out) consistently yields about a **10 3DPCK** advantage over simply using the base pose read out from the torso ("Torso-only" read-out). This confirms that incorporating detailed articulation information from limb-specific read-out locations is crucial for accurate and expressive pose estimation, moving beyond mean-pose predictions.

In summary, the main findings highlight the effectiveness of the ORPM formulation and the hierarchical read-out strategy in providing robust, accurate, and articulated multi-person 3D pose estimates under real-world occlusion challenges, outperforming prior multi-person approaches and remaining competitive with single-person methods.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research paper presents several significant contributions that collectively have a substantial impact on the field of computer vision, particularly in human pose estimation.

#### 5.1. Algorithmic Advancement: Occlusion-Robust Pose-Maps (ORPMs)

The introduction of Occlusion-Robust Pose-Maps (ORPMs) is a major algorithmic innovation. By incorporating structured redundancy and allowing for hierarchical pose read-out (from torso for base pose, and from specific limb joints for refinement), the method effectively addresses the critical challenge of occlusions in multi-person scenarios. This departs from the less robust top-down approaches, paving the way for more robust and efficient "single-shot" multi-person 3D pose estimation. The fixed number of output channels, irrespective of the number of people, is a crucial design choice that contributes to computational efficiency and scalability, making it suitable for real-time applications. This new representation fundamentally improves the ability of CNNs to learn and represent complex 3D human poses under partial visibility.

#### 5.2. Dataset Contributions: MuCo-3DHP and MuPoTS-3D

The development and public release of two novel datasets, MuCo-3DHP and MuPoTS-3D, are arguably equally impactful as the algorithmic contribution. The scarcity of large-scale, diverse, and accurately annotated multi-person 3D pose datasets has been a significant bottleneck for research.
- **MuCo-3DHP** offers an ingenious solution to generate large volumes of training data by compositing existing single-person 3D datasets. This 3D-aware compositing technique, combined with appearance augmentations, enables the creation of realistic multi-person scenes with complex interactions and occlusions, which are extremely difficult and costly to capture with traditional multi-view marker-less motion capture systems. This dataset empowers researchers to train more robust models for occluded multi-person scenarios.
- **MuPoTS-3D** provides a much-needed, high-quality, real-world test benchmark. Its diverse indoor/outdoor settings, challenging interactions, and accurate 3D ground truth (from marker-less MoCap) make it an invaluable resource for rigorously evaluating the generalizability and performance of multi-person 3D pose estimation methods in unconstrained environments.

The public availability of these datasets, along with the associated code, is critical for fostering further research and allowing the community to build upon and compare against this work.

#### 5.3. Practical Implications and Applications

The ability to accurately estimate multi-person 3D pose from a single monocular camera in real-time, even under occlusions, has profound practical implications across various domains:
- **Human-Computer Interaction:** More natural and intuitive interfaces for gaming, virtual reality (VR), and augmented reality (AR) applications, where multiple users might interact within a shared space.
- **Activity Recognition and Sports Analytics:** Enhanced analysis of group activities, team sports, and complex social interactions, leading to better insights into human movement dynamics.
- **Robotics:** Improving robots' understanding of human presence, posture, and intent in dynamic, multi-person environments, enabling safer and more collaborative human-robot interaction.
- **Surveillance and Security:** More sophisticated analysis of crowd behavior and identification of anomalous activities.
- **Content Creation:** Streamlining the creation of animations and digital human models for film, games, and virtual production.

#### 5.4. Future Research Directions

The paper also provides valuable insights into limitations and avenues for future work, such as addressing sharp transitions in ORPMs for extremely close joints, improving robustness to 2D joint mis-predictions, and moving beyond root-relative 3D pose estimation to estimate relative sizes of people. Further investigation into generating "true" person-person interaction data at scale beyond compositing could also be a next step.

In conclusion, this research marks a significant step forward in the challenging field of multi-person 3D pose estimation. By introducing an innovative algorithmic framework that inherently handles occlusions and by providing essential new datasets, it addresses critical gaps in the research landscape, opening up numerous possibilities for both academic advancements and real-world applications.
