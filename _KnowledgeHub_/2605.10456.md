# Learning Point Cloud Geometry as a Statistical Manifold: Theory and Practice

> [!summary] Summary
> A principled mathematical formulation represents point cloud geometry as a statistical manifold induced by Gaussian distributions, with a self-supervised deep learning framework (POLI) estimating per-point local covariance matrices. This approach consistently improves performance in object pose estimation, LiDAR odometry, and scan augmentation, demonstrating robustness across diverse sparsity levels and sensor types.

## Problem

- LiDAR point clouds are inherently sparse and non-uniform, providing incomplete observations of underlying geometries.
- Traditional hand-crafted methods for geometry estimation are sensitive to observation conditions and neighborhood selection, limiting accuracy and scalability.
- Existing learning-based approaches often require extensive, accurately labeled datasets and may lack explicit geometric inductive biases, leading to inconsistent representations.

## Method

- Point cloud geometry is modeled as a ==statistical manifold==, where each point's local geometry is represented by a 3D Gaussian distribution's covariance matrix.
- A probabilistic framework is developed to estimate these per-point covariance matrices, allowing for ==self-supervised learning== from point cloud pairs and noisy relative pose measurements.
- A deep neural network (**POLI**) is trained with a certifiably correct, ==differentiable optimization layer== (==SDPRLayer==) to predict covariance matrices, enabling end-to-end learning of local geometry.

## Results

- POLI+FPFH achieved **higher registration success rates** in object pose estimation, demonstrating generalization to unseen objects and varying sparsity levels.
- POLI+GICP **consistently outperformed classical ICP methods** in LiDAR odometry, matching or exceeding dedicated LiDAR odometry systems on unseen test environments.
- POLI-based scan augmentation improved both LiDAR odometry and global registration across multiple datasets and sensor resolutions, **outperforming PCA and state-of-the-art upsampling methods like TULIP**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Modeling local point cloud geometry as a statistical manifold provides a mathematically principled and quantitative representation of properties like planarity and anisotropy.
> - A self-supervised learning paradigm, leveraging a differentiable optimization layer, can effectively infer underlying geometric uncertainty without requiring dense ground-truth geometry or extensive annotations.
> - Explicitly embedding geometric inductive biases through probabilistic modeling and maximum likelihood estimation yields consistent and robust geometric representations applicable across various robotic perception tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Point clouds serve as a fundamental data representation in various robotic perception tasks, including localization, mapping, and object pose estimation. However, point clouds acquired by LiDAR sensors are characterized by sparsity and non-uniformity, providing incomplete observations of underlying geometries. This inherent data characteristic can impede robust geometric reasoning, leading to a degradation in performance for downstream perception tasks.

Prior research has addressed these limitations primarily through two categories of approaches:
1. **Hand-crafted neighborhood statistics:** These methods approximate local surface geometry using statistical techniques, such as Principal Component Analysis (PCA), applied to local point distributions within a neighborhood. Examples include point-to-plane ICP, GICP, VGICP for registration, and PFH, FPFH, SHOT, RoPS for correspondence estimation. A limitation of these approaches is their dependence on the observation process (e.g., sensor resolution, viewpoint) and sensitivity to neighborhood selection, which can restrict their accuracy and scalability.
2. **Learning-based approaches:** These methods often utilize deep neural networks to estimate point-wise surface normals, densify point clouds, or reconstruct complete surface geometry. While these approaches can offer improved scalability, they commonly rely on end-to-end supervised training, necessitating large volumes of accurately annotated data. This dependence on extensive labeling and the implicit delegation of geometric reasoning to neural networks can limit the incorporation of explicit geometric inductive biases and the consistency of learned representations. Furthermore, reconstructed geometries in unobserved regions may be inaccurate or inconsistent.

This work addresses the identified limitations by proposing a principled mathematical formulation for point cloud geometry estimation. It aims to bridge the gap between model-driven and data-driven methods, offering a self-supervised learning framework that integrates strong geometric inductive biases, thereby reducing the reliance on extensive labeled data and improving the consistency and scalability of geometric representations for robotic perception.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to explicitly model and estimate point cloud geometry under a principled mathematical formulation, thereby addressing the challenges posed by sparse and non-uniform LiDAR-acquired point clouds. The work is motivated by the observation that the absence of an explicit, principled model for point cloud geometry limits the accuracy and scalability of existing estimation methods, whether hand-crafted or deep learning-based.

Specifically, the key objectives and motivations are:

- **Develop a Principled Mathematical Formulation:** To represent point cloud geometry as a statistical manifold induced by a family of Gaussian distributions. Each Gaussian distribution is intended to capture the local geometry associated with a specific point, providing an expressive representation that is less reliant on dense observations.
- **Design a Probabilistic Framework for Estimation:** Based on the statistical manifold formulation, to construct a probabilistic model that predicts per-point local geometry in the form of a Gaussian distribution's covariance matrix. This framework aims to enable a quantitative representation of local geometry, such as local planarity and anisotropy.
- **Enable Self-Supervised Learning:** To instantiate the theoretical framework using a deep neural network, creating an estimator (named Point-to-Ellipsoid, POLI) capable of learning the mapping between point cloud observations and their underlying geometry in a self-supervised manner. This objective is crucial for overcoming the limitations of supervised methods that require extensive, accurately labeled training data, or hand-crafted methods sensitive to observation conditions. The self-supervised approach aims to remove the reliance on dense scans or explicit ground-truth geometry.
- **Integrate Strong Geometric Inductive Biases:** To embed geometric principles within the learning process, ensuring that the neural network's predictions of local geometry are consistent and informative. This is achieved through the explicit probabilistic modeling and the loss function derived from maximum likelihood estimation.
- **Achieve Seamless Integration into Robotic Perception Pipelines:** To ensure that the learned geometric representations (e.g., covariances, normals, or augmented point clouds) can be directly integrated into existing robotic perception systems (e.g., ICP-based registration, local feature descriptors) without requiring architectural modifications, thereby enhancing their performance.
- **Demonstrate Robustness and Generalizability:** To validate the proposed method's ability to accurately and consistently estimate point cloud geometry across diverse point clouds and its capability to improve performance across a wide range of robotic perception tasks, including object pose estimation, LiDAR odometry, and scan augmentation, even in unseen environments or varying sensor configurations.

In summary, the motivation is to move beyond heuristic approaches and data-hungry supervised learning by establishing a solid theoretical foundation for point cloud geometry estimation, leading to a practical, self-supervised learning system that delivers accurate, consistent, and readily integrable geometric representations for robotics applications.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology integrates a theoretical mathematical formulation of point cloud geometry with a practical deep learning-based estimation framework.

#### 3.1. Theory: Modeling Point Cloud Geometry

The theoretical foundation models point cloud geometry as a statistical manifold, denoted $M_g$, induced by a family of three-dimensional (3D) Gaussian distributions. Each Gaussian distribution is parameterized by a mean ($\bar{x}_g_i$) and a covariance matrix ($C_g_i$), encoding the local geometry around a point. The covariance matrix $C_g_i$ quantifies the local geometric properties, such as local planarity.

The paper outlines the following propositions and lemmas:
- **Statistical Manifold Representation from Different Sensor Frames (Proposition 1):** It establishes how the parameters of local statistical manifolds ($M_p$, $M_q$) in different sensor frames ($T_p$, $T_q$) are related to the global model ($M_g$) through rigid-body transformations. It shows that two scans P and Q from the same underlying world surface, related by a relative transformation T, share identical local geometry ($C_g_i$). The displacement $d_i = q_i - Tp_i$ for corresponding points is modeled as a zero-mean Gaussian distribution $N(0, 2C_q_i)$.
- **Re-parameterization (Lemma 1):** The conditional likelihood of a target point $q_i$ given a source point $p_i$ is shown to be equivalent to the likelihood of the residual $d_i$, $p(q_i | p_i, T, C_q_i) \equiv p(d_i | T, C_q_i)$.
- **Factorized Correspondence Likelihood (Proposition 2):** The total conditional likelihood of the target set Q given the source set P, transformation T, and covariances $C_q$ factorizes as a product of individual point likelihoods, based on the Gaussian model of the residuals.

A **Probabilistic Modeling (Assumption 1)** framework is then introduced to estimate the covariances $C_q$ from observed data $D=(Q, \tilde{T})$, where $\tilde{T}$ is a noisy measurement of the true relative pose $T$. A marginal likelihood $p(D | P, C_q)$ is formulated by integrating over the latent pose $T$. This involves:
- Assuming the latent pose $T$ is independent of $(P, C_q)$ and has a non-informative prior.
- Modeling the observed pose $\tilde{T}$ as dependent solely on $T$ (e.g., Gaussian noise $\xi(T)$).
- Defining an **Energy Function (Definition 2)** $\Phi(T; C_q)$ as the negative log-probability of $p(Q | P, T, C_q) p(\tilde{T} | T)$. This function includes terms for correspondence residuals and pose regularization.
- **Laplace Approximation (Proposition 3):** Since the integral for marginal likelihood is intractable, a Laplace approximation is used. This approximates the integral by fitting a Gaussian to the integrand around its mode $\hat{T}$, which is the pose minimizing $\Phi(T; C_q)$. This results in a closed-form, differentiable surrogate objective.
- **Geometry Estimation (Corollary 1):** The maximum likelihood estimate for covariances $\hat{C}_q$ is obtained by minimizing an objective function that includes $\Phi(\hat{T}; C_q)$ and a term related to the Hessian of $\Phi$ at $\hat{T}$. This formulation enables self-supervised learning, requiring only reasonably accurate relative pose measurements and consistent correspondences, without dense point clouds or explicit ground-truth geometry.

#### 3.2. Practice: Learning Point Cloud Geometry (POLI)

To address the high-dimensional, non-convex optimization problem of estimating $C_q$, the problem is reformulated as learning a mapping from input point cloud Q to $C_q$ using a neural network.

- **Covariance Estimator:** A deep neural network, inspired by PointNet++ architecture, is designed to predict per-point covariance matrices. It incorporates permutation invariance, local feature extraction (using farthest point sampling and ball queries), and handles variable-sized point sets. For each point $q_i$, the network predicts six parameters forming a lower-triangular matrix $L_i$. The covariance $C_q_i$ is then constructed as $L_i L_i^\top$, ensuring positive semi-definiteness.
- **Certifiably Correct Differentiable Optimization Layer:** The mode pose $\hat{T}$ (Eq. 8 in the paper) needs to be estimated within the learning loop. To enable end-to-end differentiation, the original optimization problem is relaxed to minimize only the correspondence residual term (Eq. 14). This relaxed problem is then solved using SDPRLayer, which provides certifiably globally optimal solutions via semidefinite programming (QCQP to SDP relaxation) and supports differentiation through the optimizer using the Implicit Function Theorem (IFT). The relaxation is justified by the implicit constraint provided by correspondence construction based on a reference pose.
- **Overall Training and Inference Procedure:**
 - **Training (Algorithm 1):** The neural network predicts lower-triangular matrices $L$, forming covariances $C_q$. SDPRLayer then estimates the relative transformation $\hat{T}$ and its gradients. The network parameters are updated by back-propagating the loss (from Corollary 1) through this differentiable optimization layer. The process is self-supervised, relying on point cloud pairs and noisy relative pose measurements.
 - **Inference (Algorithm 2):** The trained network directly predicts the lower-triangular matrices $L$ for an input point cloud, from which the point-wise covariance matrices $C_q$ are constructed. These covariances then serve as local geometry information for downstream tasks.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy of POLI was evaluated by integrating it into various robotic perception tasks and comparing its performance against established baselines.

#### 4.1. Object Pose Estimation

- **Setup:** Experiments were conducted on the Stanford 3D Scanning Repository. POLI-estimated normals (eigenvector corresponding to the smallest eigenvalue of $C_q$) were incorporated into an FPFH-based local descriptor pipeline within Open3D, with inlier selection via ROBIN. Baselines included KISS-Matcher, TEASER, and RPM-Net. Evaluations were performed across varying point cloud sparsity levels and on unseen objects (Dragon, Happy Buddha).
- **Results:** POLI+FPFH achieved higher registration success rates (defined as rotation error $\le 10^\circ$) across most settings and sparsity levels. The method also demonstrated generalization to unseen objects, indicating that accurate geometry estimation contributes to robust object pose estimation.

#### 4.2. LiDAR Odometry

- **Setup:** Experiments utilized the HeLiPR dataset, with sequences partitioned for training and testing, notably without spatial overlap between splits. Both Velodyne VLP-16 and Ouster OS2-128 LiDAR sensors were used, with various voxel grid downsampling resolutions. POLI-predicted covariances were integrated into a GICP framework, refining correspondences via Mahalanobis distance minimization. Baselines included classical ICP variants (Point-to-Point, Point-to-Plane, GICP, VGICP) and LiDAR odometry systems (A-LOAM, F-LOAM, KISS-ICP, GenZ-ICP).
- **Results:** POLI+GICP consistently outperformed classical ICP-based approaches across all sequences and achieved performance comparable to dedicated LiDAR odometry systems. It demonstrated consistent performance on unseen test environments (DCC and KAIST), highlighting its generalizability and the benefit of its local geometry estimation for scan matching. Performance was evaluated using relative pose error (RPE).

#### 4.3. Scan Augmentation

- **Setup:** POLI models point cloud geometry probabilistically, allowing for principled scan augmentation through sampling from the estimated covariances. This capability was evaluated on LiDAR odometry and global registration tasks using cross-dataset experiments (HeLiPR and KITTI). The POLI network was trained on HeLiPR VLP-16 data. For augmentation, points were sampled within a certain $\sigma$ region of each Gaussian distribution. Augmented scans were then fed into KISS-ICP (for odometry) and KISS-Matcher/TEASER (for global registration). Baselines included PCA-based augmentation and TULIP (a state-of-the-art LiDAR upsampling algorithm). Performance was also assessed under varying LiDAR resolutions (16, 32, 64 beams on KITTI) using a single POLI model, and its plug-and-play capability was tested by integrating into FAST-LIO and GLIM systems.
- **Results:**
 - **LiDAR Odometry & Global Registration:** POLI-based augmentation consistently improved performance on both HeLiPR and KITTI datasets for both LiDAR odometry (lower RPE) and global registration (higher success rate), outperforming PCA and TULIP. POLI demonstrated robustness to variations in sensor type, field of view, and mounting configuration, while TULIP showed performance degradation on unseen data.
 - **Resolution Robustness:** POLI improved global registration performance across most LiDAR resolutions on the KITTI dataset, with more pronounced gains in low-resolution settings (16-beam), suggesting effective mitigation of information loss in sparse sensing.
 - **Plug-and-Play Integration:** Integrating POLI into existing LiDAR localization and mapping systems (FAST-LIO, GLIM) without system-specific tuning resulted in substantial performance improvements in terms of RPE and Absolute Pose Error (APE).

#### 4.4. Limitations

The paper acknowledges several limitations:
- **Out-of-Distribution (OOD) Scenarios:** While POLI shows improvements in OOD settings, the performance gains can be smaller compared to in-domain scenarios.
- **Local Low-Order Geometric Representation:** The 3D Gaussian covariance primarily captures second-order moments (anisotropy, tangential extent), lacking explicit encoding of higher-order differential invariants like surface curvature.
- **Global Optimum for Pose Estimation:** Finding the certifiably global optimum of the full pose estimation problem (Eq. 13) in a differentiable manner remains challenging, necessitating a relaxation (Eq. 14) which does not include the explicit pose regularization term during the inner optimization.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a unified theoretical and practical framework for point cloud geometry estimation, offering several significant contributions and potential impacts on robotic perception:

- **Principled Geometric Modeling:** By formalizing point cloud geometry as a statistical manifold induced by Gaussian distributions, the work introduces a mathematically grounded approach that moves beyond heuristic methods and implicit learning. This provides a robust and consistent representation of local surface structure and uncertainty.
- **Self-Supervised Learning with Inductive Biases:** The developed framework enables self-supervised learning, reducing the dependency on large, accurately labeled datasets that are often expensive and difficult to acquire in robotics. Integrating strong geometric inductive biases within the neural network's design, along with the principled loss function, allows the network to learn more meaningful and consistent geometric representations.
- **Enhanced Robotic Perception Performance:** The experimental results demonstrate consistent performance improvements across diverse robotic perception tasks, including object pose estimation, LiDAR odometry, and scan augmentation. This indicates that accurate and consistent estimation of local geometry is fundamental to improving the reliability and accuracy of these systems.
- **Seamless Integration and Versatility:** The POLI estimator, by producing point-wise Gaussian covariances, offers a versatile output that can be seamlessly integrated into existing perception pipelines. These covariances can be utilized directly, or derived products like surface normals or augmented point clouds can be leveraged, enhancing various downstream algorithms without requiring extensive architectural modifications or retraining.
- **Robustness to Sparsity and Resolution Changes:** The ability of POLI to improve performance, particularly in low-resolution and sparse sensing conditions, suggests its utility in mitigating information loss inherent in LiDAR data. This can lead to more robust operation across different sensor types and environmental conditions.
- **Bridge Between Modeling and Learning:** The work bridges the gap between explicit geometric modeling and deep learning-based inference. This unified perspective could inspire further research in developing hybrid approaches that combine the strengths of both paradigms for more robust and scalable 3D perception in robotics.

Overall, the proposed methodology offers a theoretically sound and practically effective approach to a fundamental problem in robotic perception, with the potential to improve the performance, reliability, and generalizability of a wide range of autonomous systems.
