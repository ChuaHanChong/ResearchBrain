# Sample-efficient and Scalable Exploration in Continuous-Time RL

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at ETH Zürich developed COMBRL, a continuous-time model-based reinforcement learning algorithm that achieves scalable and sample-efficient exploration by integrating uncertainty-aware intrinsic rewards. The method yields improved asymptotic returns and superior generalization on control tasks while reducing computational cost by approximately three times compared to prior optimistic continuous-time approaches.

## Problem

- Most reinforcement learning algorithms operate in discrete time, which misaligns with inherently continuous-time real-world systems, leading to potential information loss and control precision limitations.
- Existing continuous-time model-based RL methods often lack robust, principled exploration mechanisms or are computationally prohibitive due to intensive co-optimization of policies and plausible dynamics.
- A unified framework is needed to address both reward-driven and unsupervised exploration effectively in continuous-time environments, balancing task performance with global model accuracy.

## Method

- Introduces **COMBRL** (==Continuous-Time Optimistic== Model-Based RL), an episodic algorithm that uses probabilistic models (e.g., Gaussian Processes, Bayesian Neural Networks) to learn unknown ODE dynamics and quantify epistemic uncertainty.
- Employs an optimistic planning objective where policies maximize a weighted sum of extrinsic reward and an intrinsic bonus directly derived from model uncertainty, guided by a tunable weight

## Results

- Achieved higher asymptotic returns on continuous control tasks (Pendulum, MountainCar) with Gaussian Process dynamics and iCEM planning, matching or exceeding OCORL's performance at approximately three times lower computational cost.
- Demonstrated superior zero-shot generalization to unseen downstream tasks in the unsupervised setting, indicating a more globally accurate system model by prioritizing uncertainty reduction.
- Integrated with the time-adaptive control framework (TaCoS), COMBRL-TaCoS achieved competitive or superior returns with fewer environment interactions compared to other time-adaptive baselines.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - COMBRL, a continuous-time model-based reinforcement learning (MBRL) algorithm, significantly improves both sample efficiency and scalability for systems governed by ordinary differential equations (ODEs).
> - Its optimistic planning objective incorporates an intrinsic reward directly derived from model uncertainty, allowing flexible control between exploitation, balanced exploration, and purely unsupervised exploration.
> - Theoretical guarantees include sublinear regret for reward-driven tasks and the first known sample complexity bound for unsupervised continuous-time reinforcement learning, validating its efficiency.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Reinforcement Learning (RL) has demonstrated efficacy across domains such as robotics, gaming, medicine, and energy. However, most contemporary RL algorithms are formulated for discrete-time dynamics, where agents select actions at fixed intervals and the system evolves in discrete steps. Many real-world control systems, including physical robots and biological processes, are inherently continuous in time, governed by ordinary differential equations (ODEs). Discretizing these systems can lead to loss of information regarding temporal behaviors and can limit control precision. The development of continuous-time RL addresses this misalignment, allowing for models that better reflect real-world physics and sensing/actuation capabilities.

Within continuous-time model-based RL (MBRL), existing approaches like those by Yildiz et al. (2021) typically focus on greedy maximization of reward, which can lead to suboptimal exploration and limited generalization of learned dynamics. More advanced methods, such as OCORL by Treven et al. (2023), introduce optimism by optimizing over plausible dynamics to encourage exploration in uncertain regions. While theoretically sound, such approaches often involve computationally intensive co-optimization, hindering their scalability to higher-dimensional systems or those requiring control from raw sensor data like pixels.

Another significant challenge in RL is efficient exploration, particularly in settings where external reward signals are absent or sparse (unsupervised RL) or when the primary goal is system identification. Traditional intrinsic motivation techniques (e.g., based on prediction error, novelty, information gain) have been employed in discrete-time settings to encourage exploration, but their continuous-time counterparts are less developed. The paper positions its work within this context by proposing a scalable and sample-efficient continuous-time MBRL algorithm that addresses both reward-driven and unsupervised exploration by leveraging epistemic uncertainty. The proposed method aims to bridge the gap between principled exploration mechanisms and practical scalability in continuous-time environments, offering a unified framework for balancing task performance and model fidelity. The work builds upon discrete-time concepts such as optimism-in-the-face-of-uncertainty and information gain maximization, adapting them for the specific challenges and opportunities presented by continuous-time dynamics.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a sample-efficient and scalable exploration algorithm for continuous-time model-based reinforcement learning. This objective is pursued within two distinct but related problem settings:
1. **Reward-driven RL:** The goal is to solve a specific task by maximizing an extrinsic reward function, which requires the agent to learn the underlying system dynamics with sufficient accuracy in task-relevant regions.
2. **Unsupervised RL:** The objective is to learn the underlying nonlinear ODE dynamics globally and as accurately as possible, without relying on an extrinsic reward signal. This setting is particularly relevant for system identification and pre-training stages where general understanding of the system is prioritized over immediate task performance.

The motivation for these objectives stems from several challenges and limitations in current RL paradigms:

- **Mismatch with Real-World Dynamics:** Many real-world control systems operate continuously in time and are naturally described by Ordinary Differential Equations (ODEs). However, most RL algorithms operate in discrete time. Discretization can lead to an approximation error, potentially obscuring key temporal behaviors and restricting the flexibility of control. A continuous-time formulation aligns better with the intrinsic nature of these systems.
- **Limitations of Existing Continuous-Time RL Methods:** Prior continuous-time MBRL approaches often lack robust exploration mechanisms or are computationally prohibitive. Methods that solely optimize for extrinsic reward tend to explore only high-reward regions, leading to incomplete models and poor generalization. Optimistic continuous-time methods, while theoretically sound, often require complex co-optimization over policies and plausible dynamics, making them unscalable for higher-dimensional problems.
- **Need for Principled Exploration:** Effective learning in unknown environments requires a balance between exploiting current knowledge to maximize reward and exploring uncertain regions to improve the model. Existing heuristic-based exploration strategies are often suboptimal. There is a need for a principled, uncertainty-aware exploration mechanism that can be seamlessly integrated into continuous-time models.
- **Addressing Unsupervised Learning:** In many practical scenarios, such as system identification or pre-training for diverse tasks, an extrinsic reward signal may be unavailable or irrelevant. In such cases, the agent must explore to build an accurate model of the environment dynamics. Current continuous-time methods are primarily designed for reward-driven tasks and lack dedicated mechanisms for unsupervised exploration.
- **Scalability and Flexibility:** The researchers aim to develop an algorithm that is scalable to complex, high-dimensional systems and flexible enough to integrate various probabilistic models (e.g., Gaussian processes, Bayesian neural networks) for uncertainty quantification, as well as different trajectory planners and measurement selection strategies.

By addressing these motivations, the research seeks to provide a more effective, robust, and versatile framework for continuous-time reinforcement learning that can adapt to different learning objectives and real-world system constraints.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed algorithm, **COMBRL (Continuous-Time Optimistic Model-Based RL)**, operates in an episodic, continuous-time setting. It iteratively collects data, updates an uncertainty-aware dynamics model, and then selects policies by balancing task performance with model exploration.

#### 3.1. Model Learning

COMBRL leverages probabilistic models, such as Gaussian Processes (GPs) or Bayesian Neural Networks (BNNs), to learn the unknown underlying ODE dynamics. These models provide not only a mean estimate of the dynamics, $\mu_n(z)$, but critically, also quantify the epistemic uncertainty, $\sigma_n(z)$, about the true dynamics $f^*(z)$ (where $z = (x,u)$ is a state-action pair). This uncertainty is crucial for guiding exploration. The paper states that the model is designed to be an "all-time well-calibrated statistical model," meaning that the true dynamics $f^*$ are contained within a confidence set defined by $\mu_n$ and $\sigma_n$ with high probability. This makes COMBRL model-agnostic, supporting various uncertainty-quantifying estimators.

#### 3.2. Optimistic Planning Objective

In each episode $n$, COMBRL selects a policy $\pi_n$ by maximizing an objective function that combines extrinsic reward and intrinsic motivation derived from model uncertainty. Specifically, the policy is chosen to maximize a weighted sum of the expected extrinsic reward integral and the expected epistemic uncertainty integral over a time horizon $T$:
$$ \pi_n = \text{arg max}_{\pi \in \Pi} \int_0^T \left( r(x'(s), u(s)) + \lambda_n \cdot ||\sigma_{n-1}(x'(s), u(s))||_1 \right) ds $$
subject to the dynamics $\dot{x}'(t) = f_n(x'(t), u(t))$, where $u(t) = \pi(x'(t))$.
Here, $f_n$ is a model of the dynamics chosen from a confidence set $M_{n-1}$ (in practice, often the mean model $\mu_n$ is used). The term $\lambda_n$ is a scalar weight that controls the trade-off between maximizing extrinsic reward and exploring uncertain regions. This objective embodies the "optimism-in-the-face-of-uncertainty" principle, encouraging the agent to visit parts of the state-action space where the model is least confident.

#### 3.3. Scalability Enhancement

A key aspect of COMBRL's design is its improved scalability compared to prior continuous-time optimistic methods like OCORL. OCORL achieves optimism by jointly optimizing over policies and "optimistic" dynamics chosen from a set of plausible models, which is computationally expensive. COMBRL simplifies this by incorporating optimism directly into the reward function as an intrinsic bonus. This avoids the computationally prohibitive co-optimization step, making it more feasible for higher-dimensional systems.

#### 3.4. The Internal Reward Weight $\lambda_n$

The parameter $\lambda_n$ allows for flexible agent behavior across different scenarios:
- **Greedy ($\lambda_n = 0$):** Pure exploitation of the current model's mean prediction, akin to prior continuous-time MBRL methods that prioritize reward-seeking behavior.
- **Balanced ($0 < \lambda_n < \infty$):** The agent balances task reward with model uncertainty reduction, leading to goal-directed yet exploratory behavior. Strategies for setting $\lambda_n$ include static values, scheduled annealing (decreasing over time), or auto-tuning (adaptively based on information-theoretic criteria like mutual information gain).
- **Unsupervised ($\lambda_n \to \infty$):** The agent disregards extrinsic reward and acts solely to reduce model uncertainty. This effectively becomes an active learning or unsupervised RL setting, driving the agent to explore the most uncertain regions of the state-action space.

#### 3.5. Measurement Selection Strategy (MSS)

In continuous-time RL, agents must decide not only which actions to take but also *when* to observe the system. COMBRL integrates with an MSS, $S = (S_n)_{n \geq 1}$, which specifies the time points $t_{n,i}$ at which measurements are taken during an episode $n$. This allows for flexible, event-driven sampling and control, in contrast to fixed-rate discrete-time methods.

#### 3.6. Theoretical Guarantees

The paper provides theoretical results for COMBRL:
- **Sublinear Regret (Reward-driven):** In the reward-driven setting, COMBRL achieves sublinear regret, $R_N \le O(\sqrt{I_N^3(f^*, S) N})$, indicating that the average reward converges to the optimal reward over time. The regret bound explicitly depends on the "model complexity" $I_N(f^*, S)$, which quantifies how hard it is to learn the true dynamics and how efficiently the MSS collects informative measurements. For common kernels and MSSs (e.g., RBF kernel with equidistant sampling), $I_N$ is sublinear (e.g., poly log(N)), ensuring policy convergence.
- **Sample Complexity Bound (Unsupervised):** In the unsupervised setting ($\lambda_n \to \infty$), COMBRL provides a sample complexity bound demonstrating that the maximum epistemic uncertainty over the state-action space decreases at a rate of $O(\sqrt{I_N^3(f^*, S)/N})$. This formalizes the efficiency of uncertainty-driven exploration in continuous time.

#### 3.7. Experimental Setup

Experiments evaluate COMBRL in both standard and time-adaptive settings across OpenAI/Farama Gymnasium and DeepMind Control Suite environments. Dynamics are modeled using Gaussian Processes (for low-dimensional tasks) and Probabilistic Ensembles (for deep RL tasks), with policies trained using Soft Actor-Critic (SAC) and trajectory optimization performed with iCEM (Improved Cross-Entropy Method). Time-adaptive control utilizes the TaCoS framework. Continuous-time dynamics are emulated by fine-grained discretization, and derivatives are approximated via finite differences.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research provides empirical evidence supporting the theoretical claims and practical advantages of COMBRL across various continuous-time reinforcement learning tasks.

#### 4.1. Scalability and Sample Efficiency (Figure 1)

On classic continuous control tasks like Pendulum Swing-up and MountainCar, using Gaussian Process (GP) dynamics and iCEM planning, COMBRL consistently achieved higher asymptotic returns compared to baselines like PETS and Mean Planning. Crucially, COMBRL matched or exceeded the performance of the state-of-the-art continuous-time RL algorithm OCORL, but with approximately three times lower computational cost. This finding validates COMBRL's improved scalability and computational efficiency without compromising performance, addressing a key limitation of prior optimistic continuous-time methods.

#### 4.2. Effect of Intrinsic Rewards on Learning (Figure 2, Figure 6, Figure 7)

Evaluating COMBRL with a non-zero, auto-tuned intrinsic reward weight ($\lambda_n$) against PETS and Mean Planning (which use $\lambda_n=0$) across multiple Gym and DeepMind Control (DMC) environments (using Probabilistic Ensembles and SAC), the study observed that COMBRL achieved higher asymptotic returns. This performance gain was particularly pronounced in sparse-reward or underactuated tasks such as MountainCar and CartPole, demonstrating that optimism-driven exploration significantly accelerates learning in challenging scenarios. In higher-dimensional environments like HalfCheetah, Hopper, and Humanoid, COMBRL also showed performance improvements by encouraging exploration of uncertain regions, leading to the discovery of more effective behaviors. The auto-tuning mechanism for $\lambda_n$ (Figure 6) demonstrated a tendency to emphasize exploration early in training by assigning a higher weight to uncertainty, then gradually reducing it as the model and policy stabilized. Furthermore, visualizations (Figure 7) showed that COMBRL's intrinsic reward steers the agent towards regions of higher epistemic uncertainty, confirming that exploration is guided towards informative parts of the state-action space, while overall uncertainty still decreases over time.

#### 4.3. Performance in Unsupervised RL and Generalization (Figure 3)

The research investigated COMBRL's ability to generalize to unseen downstream tasks, particularly in the unsupervised RL setting ($\lambda_n \to \infty$). While standard COMBRL (balanced $\lambda_n$) generally performed best on the primary training task, its unsupervised variant excelled at zero-shot generalization to diverse downstream tasks (e.g., "push away from target" instead of "push to target"). This finding suggests that exploration purely driven by model uncertainty, without extrinsic reward bias, encourages the agent to cover a more diverse state-action space, leading to a more globally accurate system model that generalizes better to novel objectives. This highlights a trade-off between task-focused optimization and broad exploration for model fidelity.

#### 4.4. Ablation of the Internal Reward Weight $\lambda_n$ (Figure 4)

An ablation study on the $\lambda_n$ parameter for tasks like HalfCheetah, Hopper, Reacher, and Pusher demonstrated that increasing non-zero values of $\lambda_n$ generally improved downstream generalization while maintaining strong performance on the primary task. However, excessively large $\lambda_n$ values could overly favor exploration, potentially degrading performance on the primary task. This indicates the existence of an optimal intermediate range for $\lambda_n$ that effectively balances goal-directed behavior with comprehensive model uncertainty reduction, achieving a synergistic outcome between task performance and generalization capabilities.

#### 4.5. Performance in the Time-Adaptive Setting (Figure 5, Figure 8)

A key advantage of continuous-time RL is the flexibility to deploy policies at varying control frequencies. When integrated with the time-adaptive control & sensing (TaCoS) framework, **COMBRL-TaCoS** achieved competitive or superior returns compared to other time-adaptive baselines (OTaCoS, Mean-TaCoS, PETS-TaCoS) and even outperformed its fixed-rate counterpart. Importantly, it did so while requiring fewer interactions with the environment. This demonstrates COMBRL's applicability and sample efficiency in settings where sensing and control actions incur costs, further validating its flexibility regarding measurement selection strategies.

In summary, the main findings consistently indicate that COMBRL offers a scalable, sample-efficient, and flexible approach to continuous-time MBRL, demonstrating strong performance in both reward-driven and unsupervised exploration scenarios, with empirical results aligning with its theoretical underpinnings.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The work presented in this paper, introducing COMBRL, contributes significantly to the field of reinforcement learning and has several potential impacts:

#### 5.1. Advancement in Continuous-Time Reinforcement Learning

COMBRL offers a scalable and sample-efficient approach to continuous-time model-based RL, which is a crucial step towards more realistic and high-fidelity control of real-world systems. By explicitly modeling dynamics as ODEs, it overcomes limitations inherent in discrete-time approximations, which can be critical for applications in robotics, autonomous systems, and process control where precise temporal behaviors matter.

#### 5.2. Unified Framework for Exploration

The algorithm provides a flexible and principled mechanism to unify reward-driven (extrinsic) and uncertainty-driven (intrinsic/unsupervised) exploration through a single, tunable internal reward weight ($\lambda_n$). This enables practitioners to systematically balance task performance and model fidelity, which is a long-standing challenge in RL. The ability to transition seamlessly between greedy exploitation, balanced exploration, and pure unsupervised exploration (for system identification) adds significant versatility.

#### 5.3. Enhanced Scalability and Computational Efficiency

By reformulating the optimism-in-the-face-of-uncertainty principle into an intrinsic reward, COMBRL avoids the computationally expensive co-optimization over dynamics models that characterizes prior continuous-time optimistic methods like OCORL. This design choice makes COMBRL more scalable to complex, high-dimensional environments, which is essential for deep RL applications involving high-dimensional state-action spaces or raw sensor inputs.

#### 5.4. Strong Theoretical Guarantees

The provision of sublinear regret bounds for the reward-driven setting and sample complexity bounds for the unsupervised setting offers a robust theoretical foundation for COMBRL. These guarantees, explicitly linking learning performance to model complexity and measurement selection strategy, advance the theoretical understanding of continuous-time RL. The novelty of the sample complexity bound for unsupervised continuous-time RL is particularly noteworthy.

#### 5.5. Improved Generalization and Model Fidelity

The empirical results demonstrate that unsupervised COMBRL, which prioritizes reducing epistemic uncertainty, leads to a more globally accurate dynamics model. This enhanced model fidelity translates into superior zero-shot generalization capabilities to unseen downstream tasks, an important factor for building adaptable and robust agents. This has implications for pre-training agents in simulation or real-world environments before deploying them for specific, potentially novel, tasks.

#### 5.6. Flexibility and Model Agnosticism

COMBRL's design is agnostic to the specific probabilistic dynamics model (supporting GPs, BNNs, ensembles) and trajectory planner, as well as the measurement and control strategy (fixed-rate or time-adaptive). This modularity allows for integration with various existing tools and future advancements in these areas, increasing its potential impact and applicability across diverse research and engineering contexts. The successful integration with the TaCoS framework for time-adaptive control further highlights this flexibility.

In summary, COMBRL represents a significant methodological and theoretical contribution to continuous-time RL. Its focus on scalable, uncertainty-aware exploration, combined with strong empirical performance and theoretical guarantees, positions it as a promising framework for developing more intelligent, adaptable, and robust control systems for complex real-world continuous-time environments.
