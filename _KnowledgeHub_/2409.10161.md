# SplatSim: Zero-Shot Sim2Real Transfer of RGB Manipulation Policies Using Gaussian Splatting

> [!summary] Summary
> SplatSim enables zero-shot Sim2Real transfer of RGB manipulation policies by integrating 3D Gaussian Splatting with physics simulators to generate photorealistic synthetic training data. This method achieves an 86.25% average success rate across four contact-rich manipulation tasks, reducing data collection effort from 20.5 hours to as little as 0-3 hours.

## Problem

- RGB images suffer from a significant Sim2Real domain shift, making policies trained on synthetic RGB data often fail in the real world despite RGB's rich visual information.
- Traditional Sim2Real methods for RGB, such as domain randomization or adaptation, are often labor-intensive, requiring extensive real-world data collection or complex environment engineering.
- Collecting sufficient real-world robotic demonstration data for training robust manipulation policies is prohibitively time-consuming and expensive.

## Method

- SplatSim replaces traditional mesh rendering in physics simulators with 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) to generate photorealistic synthetic data.
- It segments real-world Gaussian Splats for rigid bodies (robot links, objects) and applies physics-derived transformations from the simulator to render them in novel poses.
- Behavior cloning, utilizing a Diffusion Policy, trains on these photorealistic simulated trajectories, with image augmentations applied to enhance robustness against residual Sim2Real discrepancies.

## Results

- SplatSim achieved an average success rate of 86.25% for zero-shot Sim2Real transfer across four contact-rich manipulation tasks, approaching the 97.5% success rate of Real2Real policies.
- The method drastically reduced human effort for data collection, from approximately 20.5 hours for real-world demonstrations to 0-3 hours using automated simulation.
- Quantitative evaluation of robot renderings yielded an average PSNR of 22.62 and SSIM of 0.7845, indicating close visual fidelity between simulated and real-world images.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating Gaussian Splatting with physics simulators can effectively bridge the Sim2Real gap for RGB-based manipulation by generating high-fidelity visual inputs.
> - The ability to segment and transform individual Gaussian splat components allows for dynamic scene rendering aligned with physics simulations, crucial for manipulation tasks.
> - Image augmentations play a critical role in building policy robustness against subtle remaining domain differences, improving zero-shot transfer performance from 21% to 86.25%.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in "SplatSim" addresses the fundamental and long-standing **Sim2Real problem** in robotics, which involves successfully transferring control policies learned in simulated environments to real-world settings. This problem is central to scaling up robot learning, as simulation offers unparalleled advantages in terms of data generation, parallelization, cost-efficiency, and safety, avoiding the prohibitive costs and dangers of real-world data collection.

**Current State of Sim2Real:** Significant progress has been made in Sim2Real transfer, particularly for robotic tasks relying on perception modalities with a relatively *low Sim2Real gap*. These include depth, tactile sensing, and point cloud inputs. Such modalities have enabled impressive feats like quadruped locomotion on rough terrains, in-hand object rotation, and grasping of novel objects. The success in these areas reinforces the notion that "policies trained on modalities that can be simulated well, can be transferred well."

**The RGB Challenge:** In stark contrast, **RGB images** have historically presented a major hurdle for Sim2Real transfer. Despite their richness in visual details like color, texture, lighting, and surface reflectivity (which are crucial for complex tasks like discerning fruit ripeness), RGB images suffer from a significant "domain shift" between synthetic and real-world distributions. This makes "vision Sim2Real an out-of-domain generalization problem," a notoriously difficult challenge in machine learning. Consequently, RGB images are rarely used as the primary sensing modality in zero-shot robot learning applications, often requiring extensive real-world data collection or elaborate domain adaptation techniques.

**Prior RGB Sim2Real Approaches:** Previous attempts to bridge the RGB Sim2Real gap have largely relied on **domain randomization**. While somewhat effective, these methods often demand task-specific tuning and labor-intensive environment engineering within traditional physics simulators, which can be difficult to achieve accurately. Other approaches like **domain adaptation** frequently necessitate extensive offline collection of real-world object interaction data, counteracting some benefits of simulation. SplatSim directly confronts these limitations by seeking to generate highly photorealistic synthetic data that inherently reduces the domain shift, thereby minimizing the need for such adjustments or real-world data.

**Emergence of Neural Radiance Fields (NeRFs) and Gaussian Splatting (GS):** The broader computer vision and graphics community has seen a revolution with Neural Radiance Fields (NeRFs) and their derivatives, which can synthesize novel views of complex scenes with remarkable photorealism. More recently, **3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS)** has emerged as a state-of-the-art rendering technique. 3DGS models scenes using explicit 3D Gaussian primitives, offering superior real-time rendering capabilities and an explicit, point cloud-like structure that is more amenable to manipulation than implicit NeRF representations.

**Gaussian Splatting in Robotics:** The robotics community has quickly recognized the potential of 3DGS, applying it to areas like language-guided manipulation, object grasping, and deformable object manipulation. SplatSim differentiates itself from related works by specifically targeting *RGB-based manipulation policies* for *zero-shot Sim2Real transfer* by integrating GS with *physics simulators*. For example, RialTo uses a Real2Sim2Real approach but relies on point clouds for policy execution, and Maniwhere also requires depth at test time. Embodied Gaussians learns forward models directly from real-world data, demanding new data for each robot/object, whereas SplatSim offloads dynamics to a physics engine and generates synthetic data for policy deployment. RoboStudio uses GS with simulation for system identification, not policy deployment. Finally, applies GS to navigation, where the agent doesn't interact with the environment, unlike the manipulation tasks SplatSim addresses.

**SplatSim's Unique Contribution:** SplatSim positions itself at the intersection of photorealistic rendering (Gaussian Splatting) and physics-based simulation. By leveraging the visual fidelity of GS while retaining the scalability and interactive capabilities of simulators, it proposes a novel and systematic method to drastically reduce the Sim2Real gap specifically for RGB images in manipulation, enabling truly zero-shot transfer without significant real-world data collection or arduous domain engineering.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of SplatSim is to **reduce the Sim2Real gap for RGB-based manipulation policies**, thereby enabling **zero-shot transfer** of policies trained entirely within a simulated environment to real-world robotic systems.

The motivation for this objective stems from several critical challenges and opportunities in robot learning:

1. **Addressing the RGB Domain Shift:** The primary motivation is to overcome the significant "vision Sim2Real" domain shift between synthetic and real-world RGB images. While RGB images contain rich visual information (color, texture, lighting, reflectivity) vital for complex understanding and manipulation, this domain gap makes policies trained on synthetic RGB data often fail in the real world.
2. **Unlocking RGB for Manipulation:** Current successful Sim2Real approaches largely rely on modalities like depth or point clouds due to their smaller domain gap. However, these modalities may not always provide sufficient information for tasks requiring nuanced visual cues (e.g., distinguishing ripe from unripe fruit). By enabling effective Sim2Real for RGB, SplatSim aims to unlock the full potential of high-fidelity visual perception for a broader range of manipulation tasks.
3. **Maintaining Simulator Advantages:** Robotic simulators offer immense benefits: parallelizable data generation, cost and time efficiency, and safety. However, their traditional mesh-based rendering often lacks the photorealism needed for effective RGB Sim2Real. The motivation is to enhance visual realism *without sacrificing* these core advantages of simulation.
4. **Minimizing Real-World Data and Effort:** Existing RGB Sim2Real methods (domain randomization, domain adaptation) often require extensive real-world data collection, task-specific tuning, or complex environment engineering. SplatSim seeks to eliminate these labor-intensive requirements, proposing a method that requires only an initial video of the static scene to construct Splat models, and then relies solely on simulated demonstrations for policy training. This significantly reduces human effort and deployment costs.
5. **Leveraging Gaussian Splatting's Capabilities:** The explicit, point cloud-like structure of Gaussian Splatting, combined with its photorealistic rendering capabilities, motivates its use as a superior rendering primitive compared to traditional meshes. This allows for easier manipulation of individual scene components (robot links, objects) within the rendering pipeline, making it suitable for dynamic physics simulations.

In essence, SplatSim is motivated by the desire to merge the best of both worlds: the robust physics and scalability of simulators with the unparalleled visual fidelity of Gaussian Splatting, to create a data generation framework that allows robots to learn complex RGB-based manipulation skills in simulation and deploy them seamlessly in the real world.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

SplatSim proposes a novel framework that integrates Gaussian Splatting (GS) as the primary rendering primitive within existing physics simulators to generate photorealistic synthetic data for robot manipulation. The core idea is to replace traditional mesh-based rendering with GS for scene components while leveraging the simulator for accurate physics and dynamics.

The methodology can be broken down into several key steps:

1. **Core Premise: Rigid Body Transformation of Gaussian Splats:** The method hinges on the ability to accurately segment each rigid body (robot links, objects) in a real-world Gaussian Splat representation and identify its corresponding homogeneous transformation relative to the simulator. This enables rendering these rigid bodies in novel poses within the photorealistic GS environment.

2. **Problem Formalization:**
- `S_real`: Gaussian Splat of the entire real-world scene (including the robot), captured from multiple RGB viewpoints.
- `S_k_obj`: Splat of the k-th object, captured separately.
- Goal: Generate photorealistic renderings `I_sim` from a robot operating in a physics simulator (e.g., PyBullet).
- Expert `E` generates trajectories `τ_E = {(s_1, a_1)..., (s_T, a_T)}`, where `s_t` includes robot joint angles (`q_t`) and object poses (`p_k_t`, `R_k_t`), and `a_t` is the end-effector pose.
- `I_sim` derived from `s_t` is used to train policy `π_I`, which then operates solely on real-world RGB images `I_real` at test time.

3. **Coordinate Frame Definitions:**
- `F_real`: Real-world coordinate frame (primary reference).
- `F_sim`: Simulator coordinate frame, aligned with `F_real` (robot base same in both).
- `F_robot`: Real-world robot frame, also aligned with `F_real`.
- `F_splat`: Splat coordinate frame (of the robot base in `S_real`), which has a different origin/orientation from `F_real`. A transformation `T_F_splat_F_robot` accounts for this.
- `F_k-obj,sim`: k-th object frame in the simulator (initialized at origin).
- `F_k-obj,splat`: k-th object frame in its Gaussian splat `S_k_obj`. `T_F_k-obj,splat_F_k-obj,sim` aligns these.

4. **Robot Splat Models (Rendering Robot at Novel Poses):**
- **Alignment:** Manually segment 3D Gaussians of the robot from `S_real`. Use Iterative Closest Point (ICP) algorithm to align the point cloud formed by these Gaussians with the ground truth point cloud of the robot from the simulator, yielding `T_F_splat_F_robot`.
- **Link Segmentation:** Utilize ground truth bounding boxes from the robot's CAD model to isolate 3D Gaussians belonging to each link (`S_l_real`).
- **Forward Kinematics Transformation:** For any given robot joint angles `q_t` from the simulator, use PyBullet's forward kinematics to get the transformation `T_l_fk` for each link `l`. A combined transformation `T = (T_F_splat_F_robot)^-1 · T_l_fk · T_F_splat_F_robot` is applied to the mean positions (`μ`) and covariance matrices (`Σ`) of each link's Gaussians (Eq. 1 & 2).
- **Rendering:** The transformed 3D Gaussians are then rendered using the standard Gaussian Splatting framework.

5. **Object Splat Models (Rendering Objects at Novel Poses):**
- Similar to robot links, ICP is used to align each object's 3D Gaussians (`S_k_obj`) with its simulated ground truth point cloud, obtaining `T_F_k-obj,splat_F_k-obj,sim`.
- The simulated object pose (`p_k_t`, `R_k_t`) provides `T_k-obj_fk`.
- A combined transformation `T = (T_F_splat_F_robot)^-1 · T_k-obj_fk · T_F_k-obj,splat_F_k-obj,sim` is applied to the object's Gaussians, and they are rendered.

6. **Articulated Object Handling (e.g., Grippers):** For objects where CAD bounding boxes are insufficient for segmentation (e.g., gripper jaws), a K-Nearest Neighbor (KNN) classifier is trained on labeled simulator point clouds. This classifier infers the link class for each 3D Gaussian, enabling precise segmentation.

7. **Rendering Simulated Trajectories:** By applying the above processes for each state `s_t` in an expert-generated simulator trajectory `τ_E`, SplatSim generates a photorealistic demonstration trajectory `τ_G = {(I_sim_1, a_1)..., (I_sim_T, a_T)}`.

8. **Policy Training and Deployment:**
- **Policy Architecture:** The state-of-the-art **Diffusion Policy** is used for behavior cloning to learn from the generated `τ_G` dataset.
- **Image Augmentations:** To address residual Sim2Real discrepancies (e.g., lack of dynamic shadows, flexible components), image augmentations like Gaussian noise, random erasing, and brightness/contrast adjustments are applied during training. These augmentations are crucial for enhancing policy robustness.
- **Zero-Shot Deployment:** The trained policy is frozen and directly deployed to the real-world setup, relying solely on real-world RGB images `I_real` at test time.

This comprehensive approach systematically combines the strengths of physics simulation for dynamics with the visual fidelity and manipulability of Gaussian Splatting for rendering, specifically tailored to bridge the RGB Sim2Real gap.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted with SplatSim demonstrate its effectiveness in achieving zero-shot Sim2Real transfer for RGB-based manipulation policies.

1. **Overall Zero-Shot Sim2Real Performance:**
- SplatSim achieved an **average success rate of 86.25%** for zero-shot Sim2Real transfer across four contact-rich manipulation tasks.
- This performance is remarkably close to policies trained directly on real-world data (**Real2Real success rate of 97.5%**).
- For comparison, the Sim2Sim performance (training and testing entirely within SplatSim) was 95.62%, indicating that the majority of the performance gap is bridged by the framework.

2. **Efficiency of Data Collection:**
- A significant finding is the drastic reduction in human effort for data collection. Real-world demonstration collection required approximately **20.5 hours** for the tasks.
- In contrast, generating the same demonstrations in SplatSim, leveraging privileged information-based motion planners, took only **3 hours** of human effort (for tasks like T-Push, where teleoperation was used). For tasks like Pick-Up-Apple, Orange-On-Plate, and Assembly, the process was fully automated, requiring **0 hours** of human intervention. This highlights the scalability and cost-efficiency of the SplatSim approach.

3. **Task-Specific Results:**
- **T-Push Task (non-prehensile manipulation):** Achieved a 90% success rate (36/40 trials) in zero-shot Sim2Real transfer, demonstrating effectiveness in handling dynamics and contact forces. This was comparable to 100% in Sim2Sim and Real2Real.
- **Pick-Up-Apple Task (grasping and full 3D pose manipulation):** Achieved an impressive 95% success rate (38/40 trials), showcasing the robot's ability to grasp and manipulate objects accurately based on simulated RGB training.
- **Orange-On-Plate Task (pick-and-place):** Achieved a 90% success rate (36/40 trials), validating the method for common pick-and-place scenarios.
- **Assembly Task (precise placement):** Achieved a 70% success rate (28/40 trials). While lower than other tasks and Real2Real (90%) / Sim2Sim (95%), this is still a respectable performance for a task requiring high precision, indicating its challenging nature and highlighting the remaining subtle domain gaps for extreme accuracy.

4. **Quantitative Evaluation of Robot Renderings:**
- The visual fidelity of the robot renderings was quantitatively assessed by comparing synthetic images with real-world images across 300 different robot joint angles.
- The renderings achieved an average **Peak Signal-to-Noise Ratio (PSNR) of 22.62** and a **Structural Similarity Index Measure (SSIM) of 0.7845**. These metrics indicate that the simulated RGB images closely approximate the visual quality of real-world observations, underpinning the success of the Sim2Real transfer.

5. **Impact of Data Augmentations:**
- Experiments confirmed the critical role of image augmentations (Gaussian noise, Color Jitter, random erasing). Without these augmentations, the Sim2Real performance dropped dramatically to **21%**.
- With augmentations, performance jumped to **86.25%**. This demonstrates that while Gaussian Splatting significantly reduces the domain gap, augmentations are vital for building robustness against residual discrepancies like missing dynamic shadows, reflections, or proper rendering of flexible components (e.g., robot cables) that are not perfectly captured by the rigid-body GS models.

In summary, SplatSim successfully demonstrates that by leveraging Gaussian Splatting for photorealistic rendering in conjunction with physics simulators, it is possible to achieve high-fidelity synthetic data generation that enables effective zero-shot Sim2Real transfer for RGB-based manipulation policies, drastically cutting down on real-world data collection efforts.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

SplatSim represents a significant advancement in the field of robot learning and Sim2Real transfer, with profound implications for how robots are trained and deployed.

1. **Breaking the RGB Barrier for Sim2Real:** The most significant impact is SplatSim's success in effectively **bridging the Sim2Real gap for RGB-based manipulation policies**. For years, RGB images, despite their rich information content, have been a major bottleneck due to the substantial domain shift between simulation and reality. SplatSim's demonstration of an 86.25% zero-shot success rate against a 97.5% real-world baseline proves that photorealistic synthetic RGB data can indeed be a reliable basis for learning, opening up a wealth of complex manipulation tasks that rely on subtle visual cues (e.g., color, texture) where depth-based modalities might be insufficient.

2. **Enabling Zero-Shot Transfer:** The ability to perform **zero-shot transfer** of policies from simulation to the real world without any real-world fine-tuning is a monumental achievement. This drastically reduces the time, cost, and expertise required to deploy new robotic skills. It accelerates research cycles, democratizes access to advanced robotics, and makes practical applications more feasible for industries that cannot afford extensive real-world data collection.

3. **Scalable and Cost-Efficient Data Generation:** SplatSim offers a **scalable and cost-efficient framework for generating high-quality synthetic datasets**. By integrating Gaussian Splatting with physics simulators, it retains all the traditional benefits of simulation—parallelization, safety, and reduced cost—while elevating visual realism. The finding that simulated data collection can reduce human effort from over 20 hours to just 3 hours (or even 0 for automated tasks) is transformative. This addresses one of the primary bottlenecks in robot learning: the scarcity and expense of diverse, labeled real-world demonstration data.

4. **Foundation for More Capable Robots:** By allowing robots to learn from photorealistic visual inputs, SplatSim lays the groundwork for developing **more capable and versatile robots** that can perceive and interact with complex, unstructured environments in ways more akin to human perception. This is crucial for tasks requiring fine-grained visual discrimination or adaptation to changing environmental conditions.

5. **Future Research and Applications:**
- **Complex Manipulation Tasks:** The framework has immediate potential for applications in various industries. The authors specifically mention **agricultural tasks** like pruning and harvesting, which are challenging due to the difficulty of obtaining real-world data under diverse field conditions. SplatSim could revolutionize training robots for these domains.
- **Reinforcement Learning (RL) Integration:** The authors plan to combine SplatSim with RL-based methods to acquire more dynamic skills, suggesting an expansion beyond behavior cloning. This could lead to robots learning highly adaptive and intelligent behaviors.
- **Beyond Rigid Bodies:** The current limitation to rigid body manipulation indicates a clear path for future work, extending the framework to handle deformable objects, liquids, or other complex materials, which would further broaden its applicability.
- **Embodied AI and World Models:** By creating a visually rich and physically accurate simulated environment, SplatSim contributes to the broader goal of building more effective embodied AI agents and sophisticated world models for robotics.

In conclusion, SplatSim represents a significant leap forward in robot learning, effectively harmonizing the fidelity of advanced rendering with the practicality of simulation. Its ability to enable zero-shot Sim2Real transfer for RGB-based policies promises to unlock new frontiers in robotic capabilities and accelerate the deployment of intelligent autonomous systems across diverse applications.
