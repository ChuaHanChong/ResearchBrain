# Lance: Unified Multimodal Modeling by Multi-Task Synergy

> [!summary] Summary
> The Intelligent Creation Lab at ByteDance developed Lance, a lightweight unified multimodal model integrating image and video understanding, generation, and editing within a single framework. Leveraging multi-task synergistic training, the model achieves competitive performance across diverse benchmarks, including an 11.3% relative improvement on MVBench for video understanding and top-tier scores in image and video generation and editing.

## Problem

- Existing multimodal models were specialized, with understanding models (MLLMs) excelling at semantics but not visual synthesis, and generative models producing high-fidelity visuals but lacking integrated reasoning.
- Prior Unified Multimodal Models (UMMs) struggled with misaligned visual representation requirements, as understanding tasks benefit from high-level semantics while generation tasks need low-level continuous representations.
- Current UMMs often had limited task coverage, being confined to text-image domains or partial task combinations, with diverse generation-oriented tasks like editing typically treated as downstream fine-tuning rather than systematically integrated.

## Method

- Introduces **Lance**, a lightweight native unified multimodal model featuring a dual-expert architecture, initialized from Qwen2.5-VL 3B, with dedicated streams for understanding (LLM_UND) and generation (LLM_GEN).
- Employs a unified interleaved multimodal sequence by tokenizing text, semantic visual inputs (from a ViT encoder), and ==VAE== latent tokens (for generation), all processed through a single context modeling framework.
- Utilizes Modality-Aware Rotary Positional Encoding (MaPE) to reduce positional ambiguity among heterogeneous visual token groups and implements a four-stage multi-task training paradigm with capability-oriented objectives and adaptive data scheduling.

## Results

- Achieved an overall score of 0.90 on GenEval for image generation and the highest Total Score of 85.11 on VBench among unified models with 3B parameters for video generation, demonstrating strong compositional ability and semantic fidelity.
- Secured the highest Avg/G_O score (7.30) on GEdit-Bench for multimodal editing, producing visually coherent edits for images and accurate multi-attribute modifications with preserved motion dynamics for videos.
- Attained the highest overall score (62.0) on MVBench for multimodal understanding, representing an approximate **11.3%** relative improvement over the second-best unified model, and surpassed most specialized understanding models with fewer parameters.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Multi-task synergistic training promotes significant positive transfer across diverse image and video understanding, generation, and editing tasks, leading to mutual enhancement of capabilities.
> - A balanced architectural approach, combining unified interleaved context modeling with decoupled capability pathways (dual-stream experts), effectively reconciles task-specific requirements for semantic reasoning and visual synthesis.
> - Modality-Aware Rotary Positional Encoding (MaPE) is crucial for stabilizing training and improving performance by distinguishing and correctly aligning heterogeneous visual token groups within the shared multimodal sequence.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of multimodal artificial intelligence is progressing towards native unified models that integrate diverse capabilities such as understanding, reasoning, and generation across various modalities. Historically, advancements in vision-language models have largely followed two separate trajectories: understanding models, primarily Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs), focus on semantic reasoning and instruction following, while generative models (e.g., diffusion- and flow-based models) prioritize visual synthesis and spatiotemporal dynamics.

Existing MLLMs, such as Flamingo, LLaVA, Qwen-VL, and proprietary models like GPT-4V and Gemini, excel at semantic abstraction and cross-modal alignment for image and video understanding, but are typically not optimized for native visual synthesis. Conversely, visual generative models like Stable Diffusion, FLUX, and HunyuanVideo have achieved high-fidelity image and video synthesis but lack integrated understanding and reasoning capabilities.

Recent efforts aim to bridge this gap by developing Unified Multimodal Models (UMMs). These UMMs can be broadly categorized into:
- **Fully autoregressive formulations:** Models like Chameleon and Emu3/Emu3.5 cast both understanding and generation as next-token prediction, offering a unified interface but potentially facing trade-offs in reasoning ability, visual fidelity, and generation efficiency.
- **Autoregressive-diffusion hybrid formulations:** Approaches like Transfusion, Show-o/Show-o2, and BAGEL combine language modeling for text with diffusion or flow-based modeling for visual generation. Within this category, some models explore decoupled representations or specialized expert capacities to mitigate the inherent misalignment between understanding (benefiting from high-level semantic features) and generation (requiring low-level continuous representations).

However, current UMMs often exhibit limitations in task coverage, frequently being confined to text-image domains or partial task combinations. While some have extended to video, they usually cover limited subsets of the full image-video task space, and diverse generation-oriented tasks like editing and subject-driven generation are often introduced as downstream fine-tuning rather than systematically integrated. This suggests an unexplored potential for multi-task learning to promote transfer across modalities and task formulations.

Lance contributes to this landscape by proposing a lightweight native unified model that systematically integrates joint learning across image/video understanding, generation, and editing tasks within a single framework. It aims to address the limitations of current UMMs by balancing unified context modeling with decoupled capability pathways and by emphasizing multi-task synergy for enhanced cross-task transfer and emergent generalization.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop Lance, a lightweight native unified multimodal model that supports image and video understanding, generation, and editing within a single framework. The model aims to achieve this by exploring a practical paradigm for unified multimodal modeling through collaborative multi-task training, rather than relying solely on model capacity scaling or text-image-dominant designs.

The motivation for Lance stems from two fundamental limitations observed in prior unified multimodal models:

1. **Misaligned Visual Representation Requirements:** Understanding tasks typically benefit from high-level semantic features that align well with language, whereas generation tasks require low-level continuous representations to preserve texture, geometry, and temporal dynamics.
 - Existing approaches either use a single unified visual representation, which can struggle to balance semantic reasoning and generation quality, or adopt decoupled semantic and generative representations, leading to increased architectural and optimization complexity. Lance aims to address this by maintaining decoupled visual representations for understanding and generation, while integrating them within a shared interleaved multimodal sequence.

2. **Limited Task Coverage and Training Formulation:** Most prior unified models are primarily focused on text-image domains or limited task combinations, leaving the full spectrum of image-video understanding and generation largely unexplored.
 - Diverse generation-oriented tasks, such as editing and subject-driven generation, are often treated as downstream fine-tuning rather than being systematically optimized within a unified multi-task training process. The authors observe that broader task coverage correlates with emergent generalization on unseen tasks. This motivates Lance to systematically integrate a comprehensive set of image and video understanding, generation, and editing tasks from the outset, viewing multi-task learning as a mechanism for promoting transfer across modalities and task formulations.

To achieve these objectives, Lance is designed around two core principles:
- **Unified Context Modeling:** This principle is implemented through shared interleaved multimodal sequence representations and multi-task collaborative optimization, enabling all tasks to interact within a common context.
- **Decoupled Capability Pathways:** This principle acknowledges that different tasks impose distinct requirements. Lance employs a dual-stream mixture-of-experts architecture, allocating dedicated capacity for semantic reasoning (understanding) and visual synthesis (generation), thereby mitigating interference among heterogeneous objectives.

The development of Lance is driven by the goal of demonstrating that a lightweight, resource-efficient unified multimodal model can achieve competitive performance across a broad range of tasks by strategically leveraging multi-task synergy during training.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Lance is designed with a dual-expert architecture operating on a shared interleaved multimodal sequence, grounded in the principles of unified context learning and decoupled capability pathways.

**Overall Framework:**
The model processes interleaved inputs of text, images, and videos. These inputs are converted into task-appropriate token representations and organized into a shared interleaved multimodal sequence. A dual-expert architecture, initialized from Qwen2.5-VL 3B, then processes this sequence.
- **Understanding Expert (LLMUND):** Primarily operates on text and semantic visual tokens for multimodal reasoning and text generation. It uses an autoregressive language modeling head.
- **Generation Expert (LLMGEN):** Operates on VAE latent tokens for visual synthesis and editing. Its hidden states are projected into the latent space and passed to a flow prediction head.

This design enables cross-task interaction through the shared context while allowing task-specific specialization in terms of representations, parameters, and objectives.

**Unified Context Learning:**
Lance constructs a unified interleaved multimodal sequence by tokenizing inputs from various modalities:
1. **Text Inputs:** Embedded using the language embedding layer of Qwen2.5-VL.
2. **Understanding-Oriented Visual Inputs:** Processed by the Qwen2.5-VL ViT encoder, which uses spatio-temporal patching and spatial merging to produce compact semantic visual tokens (e.g., 14× spatial, 2× temporal). These tokens provide language-aligned semantics for reasoning.
3. **Generation-Oriented Visual Inputs:** Encoded into continuous latent representations using the Wan2.2 3D causal VAE encoder. This VAE supports both images and videos (16× spatial, 4× temporal downsampling for videos), preserving low-level appearance and temporal structure required for high-fidelity visual generation. These latent features are projected into the generation backbone via an MLP connector.

The resulting sequence combines text tokens, ViT semantic tokens, clean VAE latent tokens, and noisy VAE latent tokens, enabling a single context modeling framework for understanding, generation, and mixed interleaved samples. Generalized 3D causal attention is applied, partitioning the sequence into modality-specific segments that attend to preceding clean segments. Text tokens use causal attention, while visual tokens use bidirectional attention within their segments to capture spatio-temporal structure.

**Decoupled Capability Pathways:**
While sharing a unified input sequence, Lance employs specialized pathways for understanding and generation:
- **LLMUND :** Processes text and semantic visual tokens. It autoregressively predicts target text tokens for multimodal understanding tasks, optimized with a standard next-token prediction loss ($L_{UND}$).
- **LLMGEN :** Processes VAE latent tokens and generates hidden states conditioned on the multimodal context. These states are projected to the latent space and fed to a flow prediction head. Generation is optimized using a flow-matching objective, predicting the velocity field ($v_{θGEN}$) for interpolated latents ($x_t$) between clean ($x_1$) and noisy ($x_0$) VAE latents ($L_{GEN}$).

The overall objective is a weighted sum of these two losses: $L = λ_u L_{UND} + λ_g L_{GEN}$. Each attention block in both LLM
UND
 and LLM
GEN
 is equipped with QK-Norm modules for training stabilization.

**Modality-Aware Rotary Positional Encoding (MaPE):**
To address potential interference among heterogeneous visual token groups (ViT semantic, clean VAE, noisy VAE) within the shared sequence, MaPE is introduced. Standard 3D-RoPE encodes spatiotemporal layouts but does not distinguish functional roles. MaPE applies a fixed offset step (Δt) along the temporal dimension to the base 3D-RoPE coordinates for each modality group. This separates different visual token groups in the global positional space while preserving their intrinsic spatial layouts and temporal coherence within a video.

**Staged Multi-Task Training Paradigm:**
Lance adopts a four-stage training strategy with capability-oriented objectives and adaptive data scheduling:
1. **Pre-Training Stage (PT):** Establishes basic image/video understanding and generation. Frozen VAE and ViT encoders are used, optimizing the multimodal backbone, QK-Norms, and MLP connectors. Data includes ~1B image-text pairs and ~140M video-text pairs for paired captioning and conditional generation. A progressive resolution curriculum (192p → 360p → 480p) and an image:video sampling ratio of ~1:4 are used.
2. **Continual Training Stage (CT):** Expands the task space to unified multi-task multimodal learning. It introduces richer interleaved multimodal data (~2.73M understanding samples, ~2.8M image editing, ~2.6M video editing, ~3.6M subject-driven image, ~1M subject-driven video samples) and diverse input-output mappings. A progressive data-mixture strategy gradually increases the ratio of challenging tasks. Task-specific system prompts (Figures 8 & 9) are used to guide task interpretation.
3. **Supervised Fine-Tuning Stage (SFT):** Refines instruction following, visual fidelity, editing accuracy, and identity consistency using curated high-quality, task-aligned supervision data (~190K image captioning, ~5K video captioning, 2.73M interleaved understanding, 190K image generation, 84K image editing, 5K video generation, 9K video editing, 5.5K subject-driven video generation).
4. **Reinforcement Learning Stage (RL):** Further refines image generation, specifically text rendering accuracy, image-text correspondence, and prompt compositional adherence. It uses Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) with 20K image generation prompts, where PaddleOCR serves as a reward model to evaluate consistency with textual constraints.

Classifier-free guidance (CFG) is used throughout training and inference, with specific drop probabilities for conditions during different stages.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

Lance demonstrates competitive performance across multimodal understanding, generation, and editing benchmarks, often outperforming or matching larger unified models with fewer activated parameters.

#### 4.1. Image Generation

- **Quantitative Results:** On GenEval, Lance achieves an overall score of 0.90, matching the top-tier performance among unified models and showing strong compositional ability in counting, colors, and spatial position. On DPG-Bench, Lance exhibits competitive overall performance and excels in relation modeling, indicating robust semantic consistency. These results are achieved with only 3B activated parameters.
- **Qualitative Results:** Comparisons against 7B Bagel, 1.7B InternVL-U, 20B Qwen-Image, and Nano Banana (commercial closed-source) show Lance producing images with higher visual aesthetics and improved image-text alignment, particularly for complex instructions (e.g., specific object counts, attribute relationships). Lance’s quality is comparable to the 20B Qwen-Image and Nano Banana.

#### 4.2. Video Generation

- **Quantitative Results:** Lance achieves the highest Total Score of 85.11 on VBench among unified models with 3B activated parameters. It demonstrates strong performance across quality-oriented and semantic-oriented dimensions, including visual quality, object grounding, color consistency, spatial relationships, scene understanding, and temporal style, confirming its ability for compositional video generation and text-video alignment.
- **Qualitative Results:** Generated videos exhibit strong semantic fidelity, coherent motion, and appealing visual quality. Lance accurately follows complex prompts involving human interactions or explicit camera transitions, producing videos with stable visual texture and consistent temporal evolution.

#### 4.3. Multimodal Editing

- **Quantitative Results:** On GEdit-Bench, Lance achieves the highest Avg/G_O score (7.30) among unified models. It shows strong performance in categories such as background change, material modification, motion change, portrait beautification, subject removal, replacement, and tone transfer. However, performance on text modification is noted as an area for improvement.
- **Qualitative Results:** For image editing, Lance produces visually coherent edits with preserved structures and realistic textures. For video editing, it performs accurate multi-attribute modifications while maintaining natural motion dynamics and temporal consistency.

#### 4.4. Multimodal Understanding

- **Quantitative Results:** On MVBench, a benchmark for temporal perception and video-centric understanding, Lance achieves the highest overall score (62.0) among existing unified multimodal models, representing an approximately 11.3% relative improvement over the second-best unified model, Show-o2 7B. Lance also surpasses most specialized understanding models with significantly fewer parameters.
- **Qualitative Results:** Lance handles diverse understanding tasks, including OCR, knowledge-grounded reasoning, multi-image motion analysis, detailed video captioning, and action counting, demonstrating its ability to recognize fine-grained visual details, reason over static images, and capture temporal dynamics in videos.

#### 4.5. Training Dynamics Analysis

- **Quantitative:** Image and video generation performance (DPG-Bench, VBench) shows rapid gains in the early Pre-Training (PT) stage, followed by slower growth. The Continual Training (CT) stage further enhances generation capabilities, including multi-task data like editing, indicating positive transfer from multi-task synergy.
- **Qualitative:** As training tokens increase from 0.5T to 1.5T, Lance progressively improves prompt alignment, visual fidelity, text rendering, and temporal coherence, evolving from coarse semantics to faithful compositions and coherent multi-object dynamics.

#### 4.6. Effect of Cross-Task Data Synergy

- **Understanding Data:** Introducing understanding-oriented data improves both image and video generation when used at an appropriate ratio (Gen.:Und. = 8:2), suggesting useful semantic grounding for visual synthesis.
- **Multi-task Data:** Multi-task generation data (e.g., editing, subject-driven generation) enhances base generation capabilities, with the Gen.:MT-Gen. = 6:4 mixture achieving the best overall results. These data also improve video understanding, indicating mutual reinforcement across tasks.

#### 4.7. Effect of Modality-Aware Rotary Positional Encoding (MaPE)

- Removing MaPE consistently degrades performance across generation, editing, and understanding tasks. The improvement is particularly noticeable in image editing, where joint reasoning over visual conditions and generation targets is crucial. This indicates MaPE's role in reducing positional ambiguity among heterogeneous visual token groups, leading to improved cross-task contextual alignment and stable visual synthesis.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of Lance represents a contribution to the field of unified multimodal AI, particularly in demonstrating the efficacy of multi-task synergy within a lightweight, native unified model. Its significance and potential impact include:

- **Advancement in Unified Multimodal Modeling:** Lance systematically integrates a comprehensive range of image and video understanding, generation, and editing tasks into a single framework. This extends the scope of unified modeling beyond traditional text-image domains and partial task coverage, moving towards more general-purpose multimodal intelligence.
- **Validation of Multi-Task Synergy:** The research provides evidence that multi-task learning can promote transfer across modality and task boundaries, leading to mutual enhancement of capabilities rather than simple aggregation. This suggests a valuable paradigm for unlocking the potential of unified models.
- **Resource Efficiency:** Achieving competitive performance across diverse benchmarks with only 3 billion activated parameters and a 128-GPU training budget demonstrates that capable unified multimodal models can be built in a resource-efficient manner. This is significant for broader accessibility and deployment of advanced AI.
- **Architectural and Training Innovations:** The dual-stream mixture-of-experts architecture, combined with unified interleaved context modeling, provides a balanced approach to reconcile task-specific requirements with integrated learning. The introduction of Modality-Aware Rotary Positional Encoding (MaPE) addresses a specific challenge of heterogeneous visual tokens, improving cross-task contextual alignment. The staged multi-task training paradigm, with capability-oriented objectives and adaptive data scheduling, offers a structured approach to progressively develop and balance multimodal capabilities.
- **Broadening Multimodal AI Applications:** By integrating understanding, generation, and editing for both images and videos, Lance provides a foundation for more versatile and practical multimodal applications, potentially simplifying the development of systems that require complex visual-linguistic interactions.

Future work directions identified by the authors, such as more comprehensive video-aware reward models, dedicated text-rendering data curation, further model scaling, incorporation of broader modalities (audio, 3D), and streaming multimodal interaction, indicate avenues for further enhancing the capabilities and utility of such unified models.
