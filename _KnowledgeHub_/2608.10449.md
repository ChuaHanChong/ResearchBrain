# PBD-AG: Persistent Baseline-Delta Active Graphs with Uncertainty-Aware Inspection for Long-Horizon Service Robots

> [!summary] Summary
> PBD-AG is a persistent scene-graph framework for long-horizon service robots. Frontier exploration bootstraps a frozen baseline of verified structural fixtures; fine objects attach to them and change via typed event deltas. Reliability-weighted states plus a geometric visibility gate block false deletions under occlusion, and a graph-uncertainty policy schedules inspection views. It reaches 0.868 coarse F1 and 0.833 IDF1 with zero identity switches.

## Problem

- Online remapping accumulates localization and observation error, fragmenting object identity and forcing inefficient full semantic-map rebuilds on every revisit of the environment.
- Open-vocabulary maps and object-centric scene graphs are evaluated as static reconstructions, so they cannot represent objects that appear, move, or transfer between support surfaces.
- Dynamic memories treat a missed detection as absence, unable to separate real removal from occlusion, bad viewpoint, or detector failure; they also need a preloaded initial graph.

## Method

- Robot-bootstrapped **baseline-delta graph**: ==frontier exploration== plus ==SigLIP voxel features== and ==DBSCAN clustering== form fixture tracks, cross-batch consensus freezes an immutable baseline, later changes append typed audit events.
- ==Visibility-gated existence log-odds== fuse class, parent, geometry, and appearance evidence; negative evidence applies only when 3D box samples are in-frustum, unoccluded, and depth-supported.
- ==Graph-uncertainty inspection policy== scores fixtures by six normalized uncertainties plus proximity, then ranks feasible viewpoints by coverage, standoff, travel distance, and obstacle clearance before ==VLM-plus-Grounding-DINO-plus-SAM2 grounding==.

## Results

- Under shared evidence, coarse fixture F1 of **0.868** beats DynaMem-adapted by **11.1** points and ConceptGraphs-adapted by **17.2**, cutting count MAE from **0.716** to **0.356**.
- Dynamic protocol: IDF1 **0.833** with **zero** identity switches and **11 of 12** events recovered, versus **0.702** IDF1 and **8 of 12** for the strongest last-observation control.
- Ablations: removing the visibility gate raises false absence from **0.014** to **0.262** and drops event recall to **6/12**; removing persistent IDs collapses IDF1 to **0.070** with **43.33** switches.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Split a world model by rate of change: slow structural fixtures deserve a frozen verified baseline, fast object state belongs in revisable typed deltas.
> - Absence should be a geometric claim, not a detection statistic; only admit negative evidence when the object was provably observable from that viewpoint.
> - Delaying publication until cross-frame confirmation costs presence F1 but buys identity continuity and event recall, a trade worth making for cross-session planners.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotics is continuously advancing towards developing autonomous agents capable of performing complex tasks over extended durations in dynamic, unstructured environments. A fundamental requirement for such "long-horizon service robots" is the ability to construct and maintain a persistent, reliable world model. This model must not only capture the static layout of an environment but also track changes in task-relevant objects, including their appearance, disappearance, movement, and changes in support relationships.

Existing approaches to world modeling for robots exhibit several limitations that PBD-AG aims to address. Traditional online mapping methods often accumulate localization and observation errors over time, leading to inconsistencies in long-term representations. Static scene representations, by their nature, cannot account for persistent changes in object states. Holistic vision-language prediction systems, while offering flexible semantic grounding, frequently lack verifiable 3D geometric evidence to support their predictions, making them less robust for robotic manipulation tasks requiring precise spatial understanding.

More recent developments in semantic mapping and scene graph construction, such as VLMaps, ConceptFusion, ConceptGraphs, HOV-SG, Hydra, and Clio, focus on fusing language-aligned features into metric maps or organizing hierarchical object abstractions. However, these systems are primarily evaluated as static reconstructions or emphasize semantic querying, and typically do not explicitly address the challenge of distinguishing persistent real-world changes from unreliable or occluded observations over extended operational periods. They may struggle with identity continuity when objects move or temporarily disappear.

Similarly, dynamic robot memory systems like DynaMem and DovSG provide online state updates from posed observations. DynaMem constructs a mutable spatio-semantic voxel memory but does not explicitly maintain persistent object identities or hierarchical support relations, nor does it record object changes as typed events. DovSG updates affected subgraphs during long-term manipulation but typically begins from an initialized global scene graph, rather than bootstrapping from an empty, unknown environment. These approaches can suffer from "identity fragmentation" where a moved object is assigned a new identity, or "false deletions" where an occluded object is mistakenly considered removed.

In the realm of active perception, frontier exploration is a standard technique for exploring unknown space. Learned exploration policies and methods like VLFM and SCOUT integrate semantic information or uncertainty to guide exploration. PBD-AG differentiates itself by using conventional frontier exploration for unknown-space coverage, while employing a distinct mechanism for directing sensing towards already discovered fixtures whose relational state is unresolved. This separation prevents attributing semantic content to unexplored regions, which can be prone to errors.

PBD-AG positions itself within this landscape by introducing a framework that explicitly decouples stable structural "fixtures" from dynamic "object events," providing a robust mechanism for autonomous bootstrapping, evidence-gated state revision, and uncertainty-aware active inspection. This approach seeks to provide a verifiable, traceable, and persistent world model that maintains object identities and support relations over long horizons, directly addressing the identified shortcomings of prior work.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a persistent world model for long-horizon service robots that can be autonomously constructed in novel environments and reliably updated as task-relevant objects change over time. The motivation stems from the limitations of existing robotic perception systems, which often fail to maintain accurate, persistent object identities and states in dynamic environments, leading to issues like identity fragmentation, inefficient global re-mapping, and misinterpretation of observations.

The key motivations and objectives underpinning the PBD-AG framework are:

1. **Autonomous Bootstrapping of World Models:** To enable service robots to operate in unseen environments without requiring pre-loaded maps or semantic graphs. The system should autonomously discover and confirm stable structural elements (fixtures) through onboard exploration and observation.
2. **Decoupling Stable and Dynamic Information:** To separate information about relatively stable structural fixtures from rapidly changing fine-grained objects. This separation is intended to make the world model more robust and efficient, as different rates of change and evidence requirements can be applied. The aim is to represent stable fixtures as a versioned "baseline" and subsequent object changes as "typed event deltas."
3. **Robust Persistence of Object States and Identities:** To maintain reliability-weighted object states, encompassing geometry, semantics, identity, existence, and support relations, over extended periods. A crucial aspect is to prevent false deletions of objects due to temporary occlusion, poor viewpoints, or sensor/detector failures. This requires a mechanism to critically evaluate negative evidence.
4. **Evidence-Gated State Revision:** To implement a systematic approach for updating object states based on incoming observations, where negative evidence (e.g., a missed detection) is only considered valid under specific geometric visibility conditions. This "visibility gate" aims to ensure that an object's existence belief is reduced only when it was genuinely observable but not detected.
5. **Active and Adaptive Inspection:** To develop a strategy for active sensing that intelligently directs the robot's attention towards unresolved or uncertain parts of the persistent graph. This involves interleaving general frontier-based exploration with targeted, close-range inspection of discovered fixtures. The inspection viewpoint selection policy should balance factors like target coverage, travel cost, collision risk, and observation redundancy.
6. **Traceable Event-Based Memory:** To record object lifecycle changes (e.g., appearance, movement, disappearance, re-parenting) as an ordered audit trail of typed events. This provides provenance for changes in the world model, enhancing the robot's reasoning and planning capabilities.
7. **Rigorous Evaluation:** To design specific controlled evaluation protocols that isolate the effects of representation, temporal memory, and active acquisition strategies, allowing for a detailed understanding of the framework's contributions.

By addressing these objectives, PBD-AG aims to provide a more reliable, efficient, and robust world model foundational for long-horizon autonomous tasks in dynamic environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

PBD-AG (Persistent Baseline-Delta Active Graphs) is a framework designed to construct and maintain a persistent world model for service robots. Its methodology is based on decoupling stable environmental fixtures from dynamic object events and using an active perception strategy guided by graph uncertainty.

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation

The robot's input at time *t* consists of posed RGB-D observations (image, depth, camera intrinsics, camera-to-world transform) and range measurements for a 2D occupancy map. The system maintains an online state comprising a canonical-fixture view (*eB*), a persistent runtime track table (*M*), an ordered audit-event stream (*E*), and the occupancy map (*A*). A published graph (*G*) is derived from *eB* and *M*. A key concept is the "baseline package" (*B(0)*), an immutable representation of the structural baseline finalized after initial bootstrapping.
Each persistent track *i* in *M* stores a state vector including class and existence beliefs, a 3D center and its covariance, support-parent belief, an optional appearance embedding, observation time, a persistent identity (*h*i), and a lifecycle state (*slife*i).

#### 3.2. Robot-Bootstrapped Baseline and Grounded Observations

The process begins with an empty structural memory and an unknown occupancy map.
- **Frontier Exploration:** The robot navigates using standard frontier exploration, incrementally expanding the known regions of the occupancy map using range sensing.
- **Coarse Fixture Discovery:** As RGB-D observations accumulate, SigLIP-encoded open-vocabulary features are aggregated in a voxel map. Map points are retained if their class similarity exceeds a threshold and have a sufficient margin over competing classes. DBSCAN clusters these points in metric space, and geometrically implausible clusters are discarded.
- **Persistent Candidate Tracks:** Resulting fixture proposals are associated across semantic updates based on class consistency, center distance, and footprint overlap, forming persistent candidate tracks (*H*t).
- **Fixture Confirmation:** A candidate track requires support from multiple temporally distinct observation batches to be published. Stable, high-margin tracks are promoted to confirmed fixtures (*C*t). Near-threshold or conflicting tracks undergo a coarse VLM verification. Only canonical fixtures whose class is within a predefined inspection taxonomy and whose close-range fine observation is unresolved are added to an inspection queue (*Q*t).
- **Fine-Grained Object Grounding:** When the robot reaches a fixture-centered viewpoint for an item in *Q*t, an independent VLM query verifies the target fixture's class. A VLM-based multimodal inventory then proposes task-relevant fine categories and counts. Grounding DINO localizes these phrases, and SAM 2 refines their masks. Valid depth from the masks is back-projected to produce proposal centers. SigLIP provides proposal-level appearance evidence. Non-maximum suppression, cross-class overlap suppression, and support constraints form a local fine graph within the primary RGB-D view. Verified child nodes and support edges are attached to the existing fixture node.

#### 3.3. Evidence-Gated Persistent Update

PBD-AG uses type-specific association front ends for coarse fixture clusters and dynamic proposals, followed by a shared persistent lifecycle update.
- **Type-Specific Association:**
 - **Coarse Fixtures:** Greedily associated using same-class planar proximity, expanded-footprint coverage, and Intersection over Union (IoU). Residual clusters may attach to matched fixtures to mitigate fragmentation.
 - **Dynamic Proposals:** Require class agreement, 3D center distance below 0.8m, and SigLIP cosine similarity above 0.78 (appearance gate omitted if embedding unavailable). Greedy one-to-one matching, with a unique-class fallback for large motion recovery.
- **Persistent State Fusion:** For matched pairs, accumulated class and parent evidence are normalized. Ordinary observations add unit evidence, while cached VLM verification contributes a weight of 3. Dynamic geometry uses the latest accepted RGB-D proposal, while coarse fixture centers use an exponential moving average. Covariance summarizes recent centers. Appearance embeddings are averaged and ℓ2-normalized.
- **Visibility-Gated Existence:** Object existence is maintained using log-odds (ℓt_i). The log-odds are updated based on positive observations, cached confirmations, unmatched tracks, and cached rejections. A critical component is the visibility gate *Vi(t)*: a missed detection (*unmatched track*) reduces existence belief *only if* the object was geometrically observable. Observability is determined by projecting samples from the object's 3D bounding box; samples must be within camera range, inside image boundaries, have valid depth in a 5x5 window, and be unoccluded (measured depth + 0.15m ≥ expected depth). If at least two samples pass these checks, *Vi(t)* is 1, allowing negative evidence to be applied. Otherwise, an unmatched but out-of-view or occluded track does not receive negative evidence.
- **Lifecycle Transitions and Audit Events:** The system tracks lifecycle states with events such as `add` (hypothesis creation), `confirm` (graph publication after cross-batch confirmation), `update` (positive match), `move` (motion > 0.35m), `reparent` (support change), and `remove` (demotion based on existence log-odds after multiple visibility-admissible misses). Removed tracks retain their persistent ID for potential reassociation. All events are stored in an audit trail.

#### 3.4. Graph-Uncertainty-Guided Fixture-Centered Inspection

This component guides the robot's active sensing.
- **Inspection Queue (Q*t*):** Contains confirmed canonical fixtures that require unresolved close-range inspection.
- **Fixture Uncertainty:** An uncertainty score (*u*t_i) is computed for each fixture, combining normalized existence, class, parent, position, age, and confirmation uncertainties with fixed weights.
- **Target Selection:** The scheduler selects the next fixture for inspection by maximizing a score that balances its uncertainty and proximity to the robot. The selected target is locked until completion or failure.
- **Candidate View Selection:** Feasible views for inspecting the selected fixture are identified based on standoff distance, map reachability, line-of-sight, predicted coverage, and image margin constraints. Among these, the controller minimizes a cost function that considers distance to a preferred standoff (0.90m), robot-to-view travel distance, collision clearance, and predicted support-surface coverage.
- **Fine-Object Grounding:** At the selected pose, an RGB-D sweep provides a primary view for fine-object grounding. Failed views are excluded, and frontier exploration resumes if no fixture is reachable or all queued targets are addressed.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluates PBD-AG across several protocols, focusing on autonomous scene construction, coarse association, and dynamic object persistence.

#### 4.1. Autonomous Scene Construction and Coarse Comparison

- **Overall Autonomous Performance:** PBD-AG was evaluated in nine integrated rollouts across three distinct environments (Gates bedroom, Hotel suite, Benevolence) using the OmniGibson/BEHAVIOR simulator. The system achieved a coarse-fixture F1 score of 0.866 ± 0.102 and a fine-grained object F1 score of 0.713 ± 0.167.
- **Environment-Specific Performance:** In the "Gates bedroom" environment, PBD-AG demonstrated its strongest joint recovery, attaining 0.961 coarse F1 and 0.878 fine F1. In the "Hotel suite," it maintained high fine-grained precision (0.963) but exhibited lower recall, suggesting that fine errors were primarily due to missed objects rather than spurious insertions. Performance showed a decrease in the "Benevolence" environment, which the authors attribute to challenges posed by repeated fixture geometry and restricted viewpoints, leading to increased cross-instance ambiguity.
- **Controlled Coarse Comparison (Shared Evidence):** To isolate the effects of instance association and memory updates, PBD-AG was compared against adapted controls derived from DynaMem and ConceptGraphs, all consuming identical proposal-derived fixture observations. PBD-AG obtained the highest nine-run fixture F1 of 0.868 ± 0.102. This represents an improvement of 11.1 points over DynaMem-adapted and 17.2 points over ConceptGraphs-adapted. PBD-AG also reduced the class-wise count Mean Absolute Error (MAE) to 0.356, compared to 0.716 for the stronger DynaMem-adapted control. Matched-center errors were found to be similar across all methods, indicating that the performance differences primarily stemmed from instance association, consolidation, and retention strategies.

#### 4.2. Dynamic Persistence and Component Analysis

- **Persistent-Memory Results:** PBD-AG's dynamic memory capabilities were tested using a controlled protocol on three Hotel sequences (300 frames each) with 12 scheduled object-state events (appearance, within-support motion, disappearance, cross-support transfer). PBD-AG achieved the highest IDF1 score of 0.833 ± 0.029, successfully recovered 11 out of 12 scheduled events, and incurred no identity switches (IDS = 0). Immediate-update controls, which published single observations without cross-frame confirmation, generally achieved higher presence F1 but suffered from identity fragmentation. PBD-AG's approach, which trades immediate publication for identity and event continuity, improved IDF1 by 13.1 points and event recall from 8/12 to 11/12 compared to the strongest immediate-update control.
- **Component Ablations:**
 - **Without Visibility Gate:** When the geometric visibility gate was removed, the false absence rate (objects considered absent but physically present) significantly increased from 0.014 to 0.262, and event recall dropped from 11/12 to 6/12. This result indicates the importance of the visibility gate in mitigating false deletions due to occlusion or out-of-view observations.
 - **Without Persistent IDs:** Removing persistent identity tracking led to a substantial decrease in IDF1 from 0.833 to 0.070, and the number of identity switches (IDS) rose dramatically to 43.33 ± 1.15. This confirms that persistent identity is crucial for maintaining continuity across object changes.
 - **Other Baselines:** Online remapping also exhibited a loss of identity continuity. "Append-only" and "static-only" baselines avoided false absences by never resolving disappearance events, highlighting their failure to accurately model dynamic changes. These ablations collectively underscore the specific contributions of the geometric visibility gate and the persistent identity mechanism within PBD-AG.

#### 4.3. Qualitative Physical-Robot Demonstration

A qualitative demonstration of the complete perception-to-graph pipeline was performed on a physical mobile robot. The robot autonomously explored, built a working structural memory using onboard RGB-D and LiDAR, performed fixture-centered inspections, and published a hierarchical graph while preserving canonical fixture identities. This demonstration provided qualitative evidence of the system's integration and transferability to physical hardware in previously unseen indoor environments.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The PBD-AG framework addresses a critical challenge in long-horizon robotics: enabling service robots to build and maintain robust, dynamic, and persistent world models in evolving environments. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Enhanced Reliability in Dynamic Environments:** By explicitly decoupling stable fixtures from dynamic object events and employing an evidence-gated update mechanism, PBD-AG offers a more reliable approach to tracking object states. The geometric visibility gate is a notable innovation that mitigates common failure modes like false object deletions due to occlusion, which can lead to costly errors in robotic tasks. This directly improves the robot's ability to accurately perceive its environment over extended operational periods.

2. **Autonomous Bootstrapping and Adaptability:** The robot-bootstrapped baseline approach means that PBD-AG does not require pre-loaded semantic graphs or prior knowledge of an environment. This capability is essential for deploying service robots in novel or continuously changing settings, reducing setup time and increasing autonomy.

3. **Improved Identity Continuity:** The framework’s emphasis on persistent identities for objects, even through motion or temporary disappearance, addresses the problem of identity fragmentation prevalent in many dynamic mapping systems. Maintaining consistent identities is crucial for downstream robotic tasks such as long-term manipulation, human-robot interaction (e.g., referring to "the red cup" even after it's moved), and task planning that relies on object histories.

4. **Efficient and Targeted Perception:** The graph-uncertainty-guided active inspection strategy enables the robot to prioritize sensing efforts towards unresolved or uncertain elements of the world model. This focused approach is more efficient than exhaustive exploration or constant global re-mapping, allowing robots to gather necessary information while minimizing travel cost and redundant observations, thereby extending operational longevity and improving task performance within bounded resource budgets.

5. **Traceable World Model:** The generation of an ordered audit trail of typed events (e.g., add, move, remove, reparent) provides a detailed provenance for every change in the world model. This traceability is valuable for debugging, analysis, and for advanced robot reasoning systems that might need to understand *why* an object's state changed.

6. **Broader Impact on Robotic Applications:** The advancements offered by PBD-AG have the potential to significantly impact various service robotics applications. These include:
 - **Household Robotics:** Enabling domestic robots to understand and adapt to changes in home environments (e.g., moved furniture, new items on a table).
 - **Industrial Automation:** Improving the robustness of robots in manufacturing or logistics settings where item locations and configurations can frequently change.
 - **Exploration and Surveillance:** Providing more accurate and persistent maps for long-term monitoring or reconnaissance in dynamic areas.
 - **Human-Robot Collaboration:** Facilitating more natural and effective collaboration by ensuring robots have a consistent understanding of objects and their states in shared workspaces.

By combining robust scene representation, intelligent active perception, and meticulous state management, PBD-AG represents a notable step towards developing more capable, autonomous, and reliable long-horizon service robots that can truly operate within the complexities of real-world dynamic environments.
