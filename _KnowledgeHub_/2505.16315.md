# Incentivizing Dual Process Thinking for Efficient Large Language Model Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> This paper introduces Adaptive Cognition Policy Optimization (ACPO), a reinforcement learning framework that enables Large Reasoning Models (LRMs) to dynamically adjust their reasoning depth based on task complexity, inspired by human dual process thinking. ACPO reduces reasoning token usage by up to 60.5% on challenging tasks like AIME 2024, while maintaining or improving accuracy and achieving a better balance between accuracy and computational cost.

## Problem

- Large Reasoning Models (LRMs) suffer from 'overthinking,' generating excessively long and redundant reasoning paths regardless of task difficulty, leading to inefficient inference and increased computational costs.
- Existing LRM reasoning strategies are often fixed or rely on offline length estimations, lacking the real-time adaptability to adjust cognitive effort to varying task complexities.
- Prior methods for CoT compression or length penalization can lead to 'overcompression' for complex tasks (sacrificing accuracy) or insufficient compression for simpler ones.

## Method

- **ACPO**, a reinforcement learning framework, enables LRMs to dynamically switch between ==fast and slow thinking== modes by introducing explicit system-aware reasoning tokens (e.g., ``).
- It integrates online difficulty estimation and token length budgeting directly into the RL framework, using a task's sampling success rate as a proxy for difficulty.
- A two-stage training strategy involves initial Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) using GPT-4-annotated data to teach explicit thinking patterns, followed by Reinforcement Learning (RL) with a composite reward function that balances accuracy, token length budget adherence, and appropriate system pattern usage.

## Results

- ACPO significantly reduces the average number of tokens per response across benchmarks, achieving up to a **60.5%** reduction on AIME 2024 (e.g., from 16,894 to 6,670 tokens for DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-**1.5B**).
- This efficiency gain is achieved with minimal or improved accuracy; for instance, ACPO-**1.5B** improved accuracy from **28.9%** to **30.0%** on AIME 2024 while drastically cutting tokens.
- ACPO demonstrates adaptive behavior by dynamically adjusting reasoning length based on problem difficulty and increasing the proportion of 'slow thinking' for harder tasks, validating the dual process approach.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Operationalizing cognitive science's dual process theory allows LRMs to mimic human-like adaptive cognition, applying more deliberate thought to harder problems and faster thinking to easier ones.
> - Online estimation of task difficulty and token length budgeting, directly within the RL loop, facilitates real-time adaptive control over reasoning length, moving beyond static or offline strategies.
> - Explicit system-aware reasoning tokens make the model's internal cognitive processes transparent and interpretable, offering fine-grained control and monitoring of its thinking modes.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper addresses a critical challenge in the field of Large Reasoning Models (LRMs): the phenomenon of "overthinking." While LRMs have shown remarkable capabilities in complex reasoning tasks, often relying on generating detailed, step-by-step reasoning processes like Chain-of-Thought (CoT) (Ref), this can lead to redundancy, inefficient inference, and increased computational costs. Most existing LRMs employ fixed reasoning strategies, failing to adapt their cognitive effort to the varying difficulty of tasks.

The broader research landscape for efficient LRM reasoning can be categorized into several approaches:
- **CoT Compression:** Some efforts focus on shortening CoT reasoning paths through supervised fine-tuning with concise, high-quality exemplars (Refs).
- **Reinforcement Learning with Length Penalties:** Another line of work integrates length penalties into reinforcement learning (RL) reward functions to explicitly discourage overly long reasoning trajectories during training (Refs). Examples include THINKPRUNE (Ref), which imposes fixed token limits, and L1 (Ref), which introduces exact or maximum length penalties.
- **Difficulty-Adaptive Approaches:** More recently, methods like DAST (Ref) have attempted to estimate length budgets offline based on task difficulty and then train models on sampled trajectories.

However, existing methods face limitations. Fixed-length constraints or uniform treatment of all problems often lead to "overcompression" for complex tasks (sacrificing accuracy) or insufficient compression for simple ones (still inefficient). Offline strategies, while adaptive to difficulty, rely on precomputed budgets and fixed preference data, which restricts their real-time adaptability and scalability.

Inspired by the **dual process theory in cognitive science** (Ref), which posits humans engage in fast, intuitive (System 1) and slow, deliberate (System 2) thinking, this paper seeks to enable LRMs to dynamically switch between these modes. This inspiration connects to other recent works exploring fast and slow thinking in LLMs, such as System1.x (Ref), FaST (Ref), HaluSearch (Ref), and Dyna-Think (Ref). These approaches often involve external controllers, uncertainty estimation, or are training-free.

This paper's contribution, **Adaptive Cognition Policy Optimization (ACPO)**, distinguishes itself by:
1. **Explicitly representing thinking modes** using special system-aware tokens, making the cognitive process transparent.
2. **Integrating online difficulty estimation and token length budgeting directly into an RL framework**, allowing for real-time, adaptive control over reasoning length.
3. **Developing an end-to-end learning strategy** for adaptive cognition, contrasting with methods that rely on external controllers or are entirely training-free.

By addressing the rigidity and inefficiency of prior LRM reasoning methods through an adaptive, learning-based approach inspired by human cognition, ACPO aims to bridge the gap towards more efficient, flexible, and interpretable reasoning in LLMs.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind this research stems from the observation that while Large Reasoning Models (LRMs) have achieved impressive performance on complex tasks, they frequently suffer from "overthinking." This phenomenon involves generating excessively long and redundant reasoning paths, irrespective of the task's inherent difficulty. Such overthinking leads to significant inefficiencies in terms of computational resources (higher inference costs) and slower response times.

Against this backdrop, the authors pose a fundamental research question: **"Can LRMs learn to dynamically switch between fast and slow thinking modes based on task complexity, enabling more efficient and adaptive reasoning?"** This question is directly inspired by the dual process theory in cognitive science (Ref), which describes human cognition as involving two systems: a fast, intuitive System 1, and a slow, deliberate System 2. The goal is to imbue LRMs with a similar adaptive cognitive capability.

The key objectives of this work are thus multifaceted:
- **To develop a reinforcement learning framework, ACPO, that enables LRMs to achieve efficient reasoning.** This overarching objective aims to optimize the balance between accuracy and computational efficiency.
- **To facilitate adaptive cognitive allocation and dynamic system switch in LRMs.** This means equipping models with the ability to determine when to engage in brief, fast thinking (for simpler problems) and when to commit to more extensive, slow thinking (for complex problems).
- **To introduce system-aware reasoning tokens to explicitly represent thinking modes.** This objective aims to make the internal cognitive processes of LRMs transparent and interpretable, allowing for explicit control and monitoring of their reasoning strategies.
- **To integrate online difficulty estimation and token length budgeting into the reinforcement learning process.** This is crucial for enabling real-time, adaptive adjustments to the reasoning process, moving beyond static or offline methods.
- **To design a novel reward function** that effectively guides the model towards difficulty-aware length control and appropriate switching between fast and slow thinking modes.
- **To empirically demonstrate that ACPO can effectively reduce redundant reasoning without significant sacrifice in accuracy,** thereby achieving a robust balance between efficiency and performance across varying task complexities.

In essence, the motivation is to make LRMs "think smarter, not just harder," by fostering a more human-like, adaptive, and resource-efficient reasoning paradigm.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of this work is **Adaptive Cognition Policy Optimization (ACPO)**, a reinforcement learning (RL) framework built upon Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) (Ref). ACPO is designed to enable dynamic and adaptive system switching in LRMs to improve reasoning efficiency and adaptability to task complexity. The methodology involves two main components and a two-stage training strategy.

**Key Components of ACPO:**

1. **System-Aware Reasoning Tokens:**
 - Inspired by the dual process theory, ACPO introduces four special tokens: `<fast_think>`, `</fast_think>`, `<slow_think>`, and `</slow_think>`.
 - These tokens explicitly wrap segments of reasoning to indicate whether the model is engaged in a fast, intuitive thinking mode or a slow, deliberate thinking mode.
 - **Purpose:** To make the model's internal thinking modes transparent and observable, and to provide a fine-grained mechanism for controlling and monitoring its cognitive dynamics, allowing for optimization of cognitive allocation based on task difficulty.

2. **Online Difficulty Estimation and Token Length Budget (TLB):**
 - This component guides adaptive system switching and reasoning by estimating task difficulty and an appropriate token length budget in real-time during RL training.

**Two-Stage Training Strategy:**

**Stage 1: Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) for Cold Start**
The first stage focuses on equipping the model with the foundational ability to generate explicit thinking processes using the introduced system-aware tokens.
- **Data Construction:**
 - A training dataset is constructed using the LIMO dataset (Ref) for complex mathematical reasoning tasks.
 - **Candidate Response Sampling:** The DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-32B model (Ref) is prompted using strategies from TOPS (Ref) to generate multiple candidate responses with varying lengths for each question.
 - **Response Comparison and Annotation:** GPT-4 (Ref) acts as an evaluator to compare the longest and shortest correct responses.
 - Reasoning steps present in *both* short and long responses are deemed essential and annotated with `<slow_think>...</slow_think>`.
 - Steps present in the long response but omitted or summarized in the short response are considered trivial and labeled with `<fast_think>...</fast_think>`.
 - The resulting dataset consists of 745 annotated samples, where reasoning trajectories are interleaved with explicit fast and slow thinking steps.
- **Supervised Fine-Tuning:** The models are fine-tuned on this dataset using a standard cross-entropy loss, enabling them to align their generated outputs with the explicit thinking patterns and learn to switch between modes.

**Stage 2: Reinforcement Learning with ACPO**
This stage refines the model's adaptive cognitive allocation and system switching capabilities through interaction and exploration, overcoming potential overfitting from SFT.
- **Underlying RL Algorithm:** GRPO (Group Relative Policy Optimization) is adopted. For each training query, a group of `N` candidate responses is sampled from the current policy model.
- **Reward Design (Composite Reward):** For each sampled response `y_i`, a composite reward `R_i` is computed as a weighted combination of three components:
 1. **Accuracy Reward (R_acc,i):** A simple binary reward: `+1` if `y_i` is correct, `-1` otherwise (Eq. 4).
 2. **Online Token Length Budget (TLB) Reward (R_TLB,i):**
 - Unlike offline methods, the token length budget `L_budget` is estimated *online* during training. The sampling success rate `p = c/N` (where `c` is the number of correct responses among `N` samples) serves as an online proxy for task difficulty.
 - `L_budget` is calculated as `p * L_r + (1-p) * L_max` (Eq. 3), where `L_r` is the average length of correct responses and `L_max` is the maximum token length among all responses.
 - The reward `R_TLB,i` is based on the deviation `λ_i = (L_i - L_budget) / L_budget`, smoothed by `tanh`. It rewards responses that are accurate and close to the budget, and penalizes inaccurate or excessively long/short responses (Eq. 5). This promotes difficulty-aware length control.
 3. **System Pattern Reward (R_think,i):**
 - This reward encourages appropriate system switching based on the task difficulty proxy `p`.
 - If `p > p_thresh` (indicating an easier task), the model is incentivized to use more fast thinking, rewarding `ρ_fast,i` (proportion of fast thinking tokens).
 - If `p <= p_thresh` (indicating a harder task), the model is encouraged to use more slow thinking, rewarding `ρ_slow,i` (proportion of slow thinking tokens) (Eq. 6).
 - **Final Reward (R_i):** `R_i` is a weighted sum of `R_acc,i`, `R_TLB,i`, and `R_think,i` (Eq. 7). It's clipped to be strictly positive for correct answers and strictly negative for incorrect ones, ensuring clear guidance.
- **Optimization:** The GRPO objective (Eq. 2) is then optimized using this composite reward function.

**Experimental Setup:**
- **Datasets:** Training on DeepScaleR-Preview-Dataset (Ref). Evaluation on MATH 500 (Ref), AIME 2024 (challenging math), and GSM8K (Ref) (simpler math).
- **Models:** DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B, 7B, and DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Llama-8B (Ref).
- **Metrics:** Pass@1 accuracy, average number of tokens, and Accuracy per Computation Unit (ACU) (Ref).
- **Baselines:** DeepScaleR-1.5B-Preview (Ref), THINKPRUNE (Ref), L1 (L1-Exact, L1-Max) (Ref), and various SimPO (Ref) variants from DAST (Ref).
- **Hyperparameters:** `p_thresh = 0.5`, reward weights `w_acc = 0.6, w_len = 0.3, w_think = 0.1`.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental results demonstrate that ACPO effectively addresses the overthinking problem in LRMs by enabling adaptive cognitive allocation and dynamic system switching, leading to efficient hybrid reasoning while maintaining competitive accuracy.

**Overall Performance Across Datasets (Tables 1 and 2):**
- **Efficient Reasoning:** ACPO consistently achieves significant reductions in the average number of tokens generated per response across various reasoning benchmarks. For challenging datasets like AIME 2024, ACPO-1.5B reduced token usage by 60.5% (from 16,894 to 6,670 tokens) compared to the base DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B model. Similar trends were observed for other models and datasets (e.g., DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B on MATH 500 saw a reduction from 3,977 to 1,405 tokens).
- **Accuracy Preservation:** Crucially, this token reduction is achieved with minimal, and sometimes even improved, accuracy. For instance, ACPO-1.5B on AIME 2024 improved accuracy from 28.9% to 30.0% while drastically cutting tokens. On the simpler GSM8K dataset, ACPO often improved accuracy while also reducing tokens (e.g., ACPO-1.5B from 79.9% to 81.3% accuracy with tokens reduced from 643 to 572).
- **Superior Efficiency-Accuracy Balance (ACU):** ACPO consistently yields higher Accuracy per Computation Unit (ACU) scores compared to baselines, indicating a better trade-off between reasoning accuracy and computational cost.
- **Comparison to Baselines:**
 - Unlike methods like L1-Exact and L1-Max, which achieve substantial token compression but suffer noticeable accuracy drops, ACPO maintains higher accuracy, particularly on complex tasks.
 - For GSM8K, ACPO's online token length budget estimation shows adaptability: for models already concise, it maintains length and improves accuracy, avoiding overcompression. For more verbose baselines (e.g., DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Llama-8B), it still effectively compresses.

**Ablation Analysis (Table 3):**
- Comparing ACPO to plain GRPO (without ACPO's specific reward design) reveals that ACPO consistently achieves *more significant length compression* (often exceeding 50% reduction).
- While GRPO might show a slight accuracy advantage on MATH 500, ACPO demonstrates *comparable or even better accuracy on the more challenging AIME 2024 dataset* while being considerably more efficient. This highlights that ACPO's multi-component reward function (accuracy, TLB, system pattern) effectively balances these objectives, leading to a superior overall performance trade-off.

**Difficulty Adaptability Analysis (Figures 2 and 3):**
- **Adaptive Length Control (Figure 2):** On the MATH 500 dataset across five difficulty levels, ACPO-1.5B exhibits a dynamically adjusted response length. It shortens responses for simpler problems but preserves necessary reasoning length for more complex ones, resulting in minimal accuracy loss at higher difficulty levels. This contrasts with fixed-length approaches like L1, which apply uniform constraints regardless of difficulty.
- **Adaptive Cognitive Allocation (Figure 3):** Analysis of fast and slow thinking ratios for ACPO-7B on MATH 500 confirms adaptive behavior. As problem difficulty increases, the proportion of fast thinking decreases, and the proportion of slow thinking increases. This trend aligns well with the dual process theory, showing the model effectively allocates more deliberate thought to harder problems.

**Case Study (Figure 4):**
- An example from MATH 500 vividly illustrates ACPO's effectiveness. On a number theory problem, a GRPO-trained DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B model exhibited cautious, exhaustive reasoning (1555 tokens), including redundant self-verification.
- In contrast, the ACPO-trained model efficiently identified constraints and converged to the correct answer in just 476 tokens. The explicit system-aware tokens showed clear switching: slow thinking for initial problem analysis and fast thinking for verification once a solution was identified, providing transparent and interpretable cognitive control.

In summary, the results strongly support ACPO's ability to imbue LRMs with a human-inspired adaptive reasoning capability, leading to significant efficiency gains without compromising accuracy, especially on complex tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Adaptive Cognition Policy Optimization (ACPO) framework proposed in this paper represents a significant step forward in the development of efficient and intelligent Large Reasoning Models (LRMs). Its impact can be evaluated across several dimensions:

- **Addressing a Core LRM Inefficiency:** The paper directly tackles the pervasive problem of "overthinking" in LRMs. By effectively reducing redundant reasoning, ACPO makes LRMs more practical for real-world deployment, where inference speed and computational cost are critical considerations. This directly translates to lower operational expenses and faster response times for applications relying on complex LLM reasoning.

- **Bridging Cognitive Science and AI:** ACPO successfully operationalizes the dual process theory from cognitive science within an LLM framework. This biologically inspired approach allows models to emulate human-like adaptive cognition, dynamically allocating mental effort based on task complexity. This not only enhances performance but also offers a more intuitive and understandable paradigm for LRM behavior.

- **Enhanced Adaptability and Flexibility:** Unlike previous methods that rely on fixed reasoning strategies or offline length budgeting, ACPO's online difficulty estimation and dynamic system switching provide unprecedented flexibility. The model can adapt its reasoning depth and style in real-time, making it robust across a wide spectrum of tasks, from simple arithmetic to complex mathematical challenges. This capability is crucial for general-purpose AI systems that encounter diverse problem types.

- **Transparency and Interpretability:** The introduction of explicit system-aware reasoning tokens (`<fast_think>`, `<slow_think>`) is a crucial methodological innovation. These tokens make the model's internal cognitive process transparent, allowing researchers and users to observe and understand *how* the model is thinking. This interpretability is vital for debugging, auditing, and building trust in complex AI systems, especially in high-stakes reasoning applications.

- **Improved Resource Allocation:** By optimizing the balance between reasoning length and accuracy, ACPO maximizes the "Accuracy per Computation Unit" (ACU). This metric highlights the framework's ability to achieve high performance with minimal computational overhead, pushing the boundaries of what is achievable with resource-constrained models or environments.

- **Foundation for Future Research:** ACPO opens several avenues for future work:
 - **Generalization of Difficulty Estimation:** The current online difficulty estimation relies on verifiable data. Future research could explore more generalized and domain-agnostic difficulty estimation methods, potentially using uncertainty quantification or self-assessment mechanisms, to extend ACPO's applicability to open-domain tasks.
 - **Refined Reward Engineering:** While effective, the reward function could be further refined to achieve an even finer-grained balance between efficiency and correctness, potentially incorporating more nuanced penalties or incentives for specific reasoning patterns.
 - **Human-in-the-Loop Reasoning:** The transparent thinking modes could enable more effective human-AI collaboration, allowing humans to monitor the model's cognitive state and intervene when necessary, or even provide feedback directly on the chosen thinking mode.
 - **Beyond Mathematical Reasoning:** The core principles of adaptive cognitive allocation and dynamic system switching are transferable to other complex reasoning domains, such as scientific discovery, legal analysis, or strategic planning.

In conclusion, ACPO offers a robust, flexible, and interpretable framework for intelligent reasoning in LRMs. By teaching models to think adaptively and efficiently, much like humans do, it contributes significantly to both the theoretical understanding of AI cognition and the practical development of more capable and cost-effective large language models.
