# DINO-WM: World Models on Pre-trained Visual Features enable Zero-shot Planning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at NYU and Meta AI introduce DINO-WM, a task-agnostic world model that leverages frozen DINOv2 pre-trained visual features to enable zero-shot planning. The model demonstrates high-quality future state predictions and superior planning success rates across diverse robotic manipulation and navigation tasks from offline data.

## Problem

- Current robotics and embodied AI systems struggle with generalization, as feed-forward policies often require extensive prior exposure to diverse scenarios and lack runtime adaptability.
- Existing world models are either online, requiring continuous interaction and retraining, or offline but reliant on strong auxiliary information (rewards, expert demonstrations) which limits their generality.
- Developing a world model that can be trained efficiently on offline data and support flexible, task-agnostic behavior optimization at test time remains a challenge.

## Method

![[2411.04983-method.png]]
*Figure 1: DINO-WM framework overview. (a) Training uses DINOv2 features to learn dynamics in latent space. (b) At test time, the model plans by optimizing actions to reach goal states. (c) Performance comparison showing superior results on manipulation tasks.*

- ==DINO-WM== utilizes a frozen, pre-trained ==DINOv2== model as its observation encoder, converting raw RGB images into compact, spatially rich patch embeddings.
- A Vision Transformer (ViT) acts as a ==transition model==, predicting future DINOv2 latent states based on past latent states and actions, trained with a ==latent consistency loss== without pixel reconstruction.
- Zero-shot planning is performed at test time using ==Model Predictive Control (MPC) with CEM== to optimize action sequences that minimize the distance between predicted and goal latent states.

## Results

- DINO-WM produces superior future world predictions, showing up to a **56%** improvement in LPIPS scores (e.g., **0.007** on PushT) compared to prior state-of-the-art models.
- It achieves high success rates for zero-shot planning in diverse environments, outperforming prior work in manipulation tasks by an average of **45%** (e.g., **0.90** success rate on PushT vs. **0.32** for IRIS).
- The model demonstrates promising generalization to novel environment configurations within task families (e.g., **0.82** SR in WallRandom, **0.63** Chamfer Distance in GranularRandom).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Pre-trained, general-purpose visual features (like DINOv2 patch embeddings) are essential for building robust and consistent world models, especially in complex manipulation tasks.
> - Decoupling the world model's dynamics learning from pixel-level reconstruction via latent space prediction improves performance and computational efficiency, while still enabling high-fidelity rollouts for planning.
> - A truly task-agnostic world model trained on offline data can enable effective zero-shot planning for arbitrary visual goals, without needing task-specific rewards or expert demonstrations.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work is positioned at the intersection of several rapidly evolving fields: **world models**, **reinforcement learning (RL)**, **embodied AI/robotics**, and **self-supervised visual representation learning**.

Traditionally, robotics and embodied AI have made significant strides through imitation learning and reinforcement learning, allowing agents to learn complex behaviors. However, a major bottleneck remains **generalization**. Existing policies often operate in a feed-forward manner, requiring prior exposure to diverse scenarios during training to generalize effectively. This approach is inherently inefficient and often unfeasible for the vast array of potential tasks and environments a robot might encounter in the real world.

World models, which learn to predict future environmental states given actions, offer a promising alternative by enabling **runtime optimization** of behavior. Instead of learning a policy for every task, a robust world model can allow agents to reason and plan for novel tasks on the fly. While world models have a long history in control theory, their application to raw sensory data (especially images) in deep learning has gained traction with works like Dreamer and PlaNet.

The paper identifies two main paradigms for learning world models: **online** and **offline**. Online world models, though powerful for specific tasks, require continuous interaction with the environment and retraining for new tasks. Offline world models, trained on pre-collected datasets, offer task-agnostic potential but often rely on strong auxiliary information (e.g., expert demonstrations, dense rewards, inverse models), which limits their generality.

DINO-WM directly addresses this limitation by proposing a truly **task-agnostic** offline world model. It departs from the common practice of learning visual representations from scratch or relying on reward signals, which inherently ties the world model to specific tasks. Instead, it leverages **pre-trained, general-purpose visual features** (specifically DINOv2's patch features) as its observation space. This choice is critical as DINOv2 is a self-supervised vision transformer trained on vast amounts of unlabelled image data, providing rich, spatially aware, and semantically meaningful representations without any task-specific supervision.

By predicting future states in this pre-trained latent space, DINO-WM aims to decouple the perception component from the dynamics modeling, allowing for greater generality. This approach aligns with the broader trend of using large-scale pre-trained "foundation models" in various AI domains, adapting them for downstream tasks rather than training models from scratch. The work contributes to the vision of developing universal world models that can facilitate broad generalization and zero-shot planning in complex physical environments, moving beyond the limitations of task-specific policies and data-hungry online learning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to develop a **task-agnostic world model** that can enable **zero-shot planning** from offline, pre-collected trajectories, without reliance on expert demonstrations, reward modeling, or pre-learned inverse models.

The authors are motivated by the inherent challenges of generalization in current robotics and embodied AI systems. They argue that existing approaches, which train feed-forward policies for specific tasks, are neither feasible nor efficient for learning solutions to all possible future scenarios. They propose that a more promising path lies in fitting a dynamic model to training data and optimizing task-specific behavior at runtime.

To unlock the full potential of world models, the authors identify three key desiderata:
1. **Trainability on offline, pre-collected trajectories:** This removes the need for costly online interaction and continuous data collection during training, making the approach more scalable and applicable to large, diverse datasets.
2. **Support for test-time behavior optimization:** This allows for flexible adaptation to novel tasks and goals at deployment time, rather than relying on pre-trained policies that cannot adapt.
3. **Facilitation of task-agnostic reasoning:** The world model should capture general environmental dynamics rather than being specialized for a particular task or reward function. This is crucial for true zero-shot planning to arbitrary goals.

The core motivation for using DINOv2's pre-trained patch features stems from the hypothesis that a strong, general-purpose visual representation can significantly improve the robustness and consistency of world modeling. This reduces the necessity for task-specific data coverage during world model training, pushing towards a more versatile and generalizable predictive model of the world.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

DINO-WM models environmental dynamics in a **latent space**, specifically leveraging pre-trained patch features from the DINOv2 model. The system comprises three main components: an Observation Model, a Transition Model, and an optional Decoder Model.

- **Observation Model:**
 - This is a **frozen, pre-trained DINOv2 model**. Crucially, it remains fixed during both training and testing of the world model.
 - It encodes raw RGB image observations ($o_t$) into compact patch embeddings ($z_t \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times E}$), where $N$ is the number of patches and $E$ is the embedding dimension.
 - The rationale behind using DINOv2 is its independence from specific tasks and environments, along with its ability to capture rich spatial information essential for navigation and manipulation due to its self-supervised training on large-scale internet data. This choice eliminates the need to learn an observation model from scratch, which is often inefficient.

- **Transition Model:**
 - This model uses a **Vision Transformer (ViT) architecture** adapted as a decoder-only transformer. It processes the patch embeddings from the observation model.
 - It takes a history of past latent states ($z_{t-H:t-1}$) and actions ($a_{t-H:t-1}$) as input.
 - It predicts the latent state at the next time step ($z_t$).
 - To ensure proper temporal dependencies, a **causal attention mechanism** is implemented. This means predictions for the current frame can only attend to past observations, preventing the model from "cheating" by observing future frames during training (which are unavailable during test-time planning). The prediction occurs at a "frame level" (predicting all patches of the next frame simultaneously) rather than "token level" (patch-by-patch), which the authors argue better captures global structure.
 - The agent's actions (and optionally proprioceptive information) are incorporated by concatenating their MLP-encoded representations to each patch vector, allowing the model to condition its predictions on control inputs.
 - The transition model is trained using a **latent consistency loss** ($L_{pred} = ||p_\theta(enc(o_{t-H:t}), \phi(a_{t-H:t})) - enc(o_{t+1})||^2$), meaning it directly predicts future DINOv2 embeddings. This training is performed entirely in latent space, *without* requiring reconstruction of original pixel images, which reduces computational costs.

- **Decoder Model (Optional):**
 - This component consists of a stack of transposed convolution layers.
 - Its sole purpose is to decode the latent patch representations back into image pixels ($q_\theta(z_t) \to o_t$) for **visualization and interpretability**.
 - Crucially, its training (with a simple reconstruction loss $L_{rec} = ||q_\theta(z_t) - o_t||^2$) is **entirely independent** of the transition model training. This ensures that the world model's learning of dynamics is not coupled with or biased by pixel-level reconstruction objectives, which might not be relevant for task completion.

- **Visual Planning at Test Time:**
 - Once trained, DINO-WM facilitates **zero-shot planning** for arbitrary observational goals.
 - Given a current observation ($o_0$) and a goal observation ($o_g$), both are encoded into latent states ($enc(o_0)$ and $enc(o_g)$).
 - Planning is formulated as searching for an action sequence that minimizes the Mean Squared Error (MSE) between the predicted final latent state and the goal latent state: $C = ||\hat{z}_T - z_g||^2$.
 - The optimization is performed using **Model Predictive Control (MPC)** with the **Cross-Entropy Method (CEM)**. CEM iteratively samples action sequences, evaluates them using the differentiable world model (predicting latent trajectories), selects the best-performing samples, and refines the distribution of actions. While gradient descent (GD) was also explored, CEM empirically performed better.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

DINO-WM demonstrates strong performance across six diverse environments, outperforming prior state-of-the-art world models.

- **High-Quality World Modeling:** DINO-WM produces superior future world predictions, even when measured by visual reconstruction metrics (LPIPS). On challenging tasks, it achieves up to a 56% improvement over prior state-of-the-art in LPIPS scores (e.g., LPIPS of 0.007 on PushT for DINO-WM vs. 0.045 for R3M and 0.046 for AVDC). Qualitatively, its open-loop rollouts are "visually indistinguishable from the ground truth observations," despite the world model itself not being trained on pixel reconstruction.

- **Effective Zero-Shot Planning:** The learned latent world models enable high success rates for reaching arbitrary goals. DINO-WM significantly outperforms prior work in manipulation environments (e.g., PushT, Rope, Granular) where precise control and understanding of object dynamics are crucial, showing an average improvement of 45% in success rate (e.g., 0.90 SR on PushT vs. 0.32 for IRIS, 0.30 for DreamerV3, and 0.00 for TD-MPC2; 0.41 CD on Rope vs. 1.11 for IRIS). On simpler navigation tasks (Maze, Wall), it performs on par with the best baselines (0.98-0.96 SR).

- **Importance of Pre-trained Visual Representations:** Ablation studies confirm that DINOv2's patch-based embeddings are critical for performance, especially in complex manipulation tasks. While global feature encoders (R3M, ResNet, DINO CLS) perform adequately on simple tasks, their performance drops significantly as environmental complexity increases. DINO-WM, leveraging DINOv2's patch features, consistently outperforms these alternatives, indicating that detailed spatial information is essential.

- **Generalization to Novel Environment Configurations:** DINO-WM shows promising generalization capabilities to unseen configurations within task families (WallRandom, PushObj, GranularRandom).
 - In **WallRandom**, it significantly outperforms baselines (0.82 SR vs. 0.76 for DreamerV3), indicating effective learning of general concepts like walls and doors, independent of specific layouts.
 - In **GranularRandom** (varying particle counts), DINO-WM achieves the lowest Chamfer Distance (0.63 vs. 0.86 for IRIS), suggesting better scene understanding even with out-of-distribution particle numbers, likely due to its patch-feature encoding.
 - **PushObj** (novel object shapes) remains challenging for all methods, including DINO-WM, though it still performs better than baselines (0.34 SR vs. 0.18 for DreamerV3), highlighting the difficulty in inferring precise physical parameters for entirely novel objects.

- **Computational Efficiency:** DINO-WM's inference time (0.014s per step) is significantly faster than traditional simulation rollouts (3.0s per step for deformable environments), enabling efficient planning, particularly beneficial for computationally intensive tasks.

- **Ablation on Causal Attention and Decoder Loss:** The causal attention mask is shown to be crucial for models with longer history, preventing "cheating" and improving performance. Training the world model's predictor independently of a pixel reconstruction loss (as in DINO-WM) is also found to slightly outperform coupling them, further emphasizing the benefit of disentangling feature learning from pixel reconstruction.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

DINO-WM represents a significant step towards developing more **general purpose and autonomous AI agents** capable of operating in diverse and novel environments. Its core contribution lies in demonstrating that a robust, task-agnostic world model can be trained entirely from offline data by leveraging powerful, pre-trained visual features.

The **zero-shot planning** capability, achieved without any task-specific supervision (rewards, expert demonstrations) or inverse models at test time, is a major breakthrough. This means a single trained DINO-WM model could potentially be deployed to solve a multitude of tasks within an environment, simply by providing a visual goal. This moves away from the traditional paradigm of training specialized policies for each task, dramatically improving efficiency and scalability in robotics and embodied AI.

The emphasis on **latent space dynamics** and the decoupling of perception from pixel reconstruction is impactful. By operating on DINOv2's high-level patch features, DINO-WM avoids the computational cost and potential information loss associated with pixel-space prediction, while still retaining sufficient spatial and semantic detail for fine-grained manipulation. This approach aligns with the growing trend of leveraging large-scale self-supervised models as powerful perception backbones for downstream robotic control.

The ability to **generalize to novel environment configurations** (e.g., varying wall positions, particle counts) further underscores the model's potential for real-world deployment, where exact training configurations are rarely encountered. This suggests a path towards more robust and adaptive robotic systems.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Reduced Data Requirements:** By leveraging pre-trained vision models and operating on offline data, DINO-WM could significantly reduce the need for extensive, task-specific data collection and online interaction, which are major bottlenecks in robotics.
- **Faster Deployment:** The zero-shot planning capability means that new tasks can be solved instantly at test time, without additional training or fine-tuning, accelerating the deployment of robotic systems in new scenarios.
- **Towards General AI:** This work contributes to the long-term goal of building generalist AI agents that understand and interact with the physical world, moving beyond narrow task-specific solutions.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** DINO-WM provides a strong baseline and framework for future work in world modeling, particularly in exploring how to combine powerful pre-trained perception with efficient dynamics learning and planning.

**Limitations and Future Work:**
The authors acknowledge several limitations:
1. **Reliance on Sufficient Offline Data Coverage:** While task-agnostic, DINO-WM still requires offline datasets with sufficient state-action coverage. Obtaining such diverse datasets for highly complex real-world environments remains challenging. Future work could integrate DINO-WM with exploration strategies to continually update the model.
2. **Dependence on Ground Truth Actions:** The current model assumes access to ground truth actions from agents in the offline dataset. This might not be feasible for training with vast, unlabelled internet video data.
3. **Current Planning in Action Space:** While effective, the current planning is directly in action space. An extension could involve a hierarchical structure, integrating high-level planning with low-level control policies for more fine-grained control and complex, long-horizon tasks.

Overall, DINO-WM represents a compelling demonstration of how state-of-the-art self-supervised visual features can be effectively harnessed to build generalizable world models, paving the way for more intelligent and adaptable robotic agents.
