# RL Is Neither a Panacea Nor a Mirage: Understanding Supervised vs. Reinforcement Learning Fine-Tuning for LLMs

> [!summary] Summary
> This research uses spectral analysis to reveal that Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) causes out-of-distribution (OOD) generalization loss in Large Language Models primarily through rotations of singular vectors, while Reinforcement Learning Fine-Tuning (RL-FT) acts as a restorative mechanism for these rotations, recovering up to 99% of lost OOD performance in some cases. The study identifies that restoring singular vector directions in low-rank components or early layers can recover 70-80% of OOD performance, offering efficient alternatives to full RL-FT.

## Problem

- The precise parameter-level dynamics of how SFT and RL-FT impact LLM performance, particularly out-of-distribution (OOD) generalization, were not well understood.
- Existing literature lacked a mechanistic explanation for SFT's tendency to improve in-distribution (ID) performance while often degrading OOD generalization.
- The fundamental role of RL-FT in LLMs, specifically whether it creates new generalization abilities or merely restores those compromised by SFT, remained unclear.

## Method

- Conducted two-stage fine-tuning (SFT followed by PPO-based RL) on Llama and Qwen LLMs using a controlled out-of-distribution (OOD) variant of the GeneralPoints card game.
- Employed ==spectral analysis== via ==Singular Value Decomposition (SVD)== and ==Principal Angles Between Subspaces (PABS)== to track parameter-level dynamics and geometric shifts in weight matrices during fine-tuning.
- Performed targeted ablation studies by selectively restoring ==singular vector directions== layer-wise and rank-wise to causally investigate their impact on OOD performance.

## Results

- Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) caused significant OOD performance degradation, with Llama-3.2-11B dropping from **17.52%** to **8.97%** and Qwen-2.5-7B experiencing a **13%** decline.
- Reinforcement Learning Fine-Tuning (RL-FT) substantially restored OOD performance, recovering up to **99%** for Qwen-2.5-7B and **85%** for Llama-3.2-11B.
- Targeted restoration of singular vector directions in the top **20%** of singular values or the first **25%** of layers successfully recovered **70-80%** of a model's OOD performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Reinforcement Learning Fine-Tuning (RL-FT) primarily functions as a 'memory restoration' mechanism, counteracting the out-of-distribution (OOD) performance decay caused by Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT).
> - Changes in OOD generalization are predominantly driven by rotations of singular vectors within weight matrices, rather than significant alterations in singular value magnitudes.
> - Restoring singular vector directions in low-rank components (e.g., top 20% of singular values) or early layers (e.g., first 25% of layers) is surprisingly effective for OOD recovery.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of Large Language Models (LLMs) has profoundly reshaped the field of AI. Training these models from scratch is increasingly resource-intensive and impractical, making post-training fine-tuning methods indispensable. Among these, Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) and Reinforcement Learning Fine-Tuning (RL-FT), particularly methods like Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO), have emerged as central paradigms for adapting pre-trained LLMs to specific tasks and aligning them with human preferences.

This paper positions itself at the heart of a significant ongoing debate: understanding the precise roles and mechanisms of SFT and RL-FT in shaping an LLM's capabilities, particularly concerning in-distribution (ID) performance versus out-of-distribution (OOD) generalization. Prior research has offered various high-level observations:
- Some studies suggest SFT tends to "memorize" training distributions, leading to strong ID performance but potentially poor OOD generalization, while RL is credited with improving OOD generalization (Kirk et al., Chu et al.).
- Others emphasize that KL-regularized RL acts as a counteracting force, preventing SFT from drifting too far from the base model's pre-trained knowledge (Fu et al.).
- Specialized RL techniques, such as rule-based or structure-aware RL, have been shown to enhance reasoning abilities (Xie et al.).
- There's also an active discussion on the optimal sequencing or integration of SFT and RL, with some proposing unified training stages to overcome the limitations of a rigid SFT-then-RL pipeline (Liu et al., Huang et al.).

However, as the authors astutely point out, much of this prior work has been "largely observational and lacked a deep, mechanistic account of the parameter-level dynamics." The existing insights, while valuable, often remained correlational, offering limited actionable guidance for practitioners. This paper steps in to fill this critical gap. By employing a controlled benchmark (an OOD variant of the 24-point card game) and novel spectrum-based diagnostics (Singular Value Decomposition and Principal Angles Between Subspaces), Jin et al. aim to move beyond surface-level observations to provide a mechanistic, parameter-level understanding of how SFT and RL fundamentally reshape the internal representations of LLMs. This detailed analysis allows the paper to reconcile seemingly contradictory findings in the literature and offer concrete recommendations for fine-tuning strategies.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to provide a deeper, mechanistic understanding of how Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) and Reinforcement Learning Fine-Tuning (RL-FT) impact the internal representations and performance of Large Language Models (LLMs), particularly concerning their ability to generalize to out-of-distribution (OOD) tasks.

The key motivations driving this study are:

1. **Bridging the Mechanistic Gap:** Despite the widespread adoption of SFT and RL-FT, the precise parameter-level dynamics that govern their effects on LLM performance, especially OOD generalization, remain poorly understood. Previous studies offered high-level observations but lacked a granular, mechanistic account. The authors aim to fill this gap by analyzing how these fine-tuning stages reshape the model's weight matrices.
2. **Disentangling SFT's Role in Memorization vs. Generalization:** It is commonly observed that SFT improves in-distribution (ID) performance but can degrade OOD generalization. The paper seeks to understand *why* this happens and *how* RL subsequently interacts with these changes.
3. **Clarifying RL's Contribution:** A critical question is whether RL-FT endows LLMs with fundamentally new reasoning or generalization capabilities, or if its primary role is to restore abilities compromised during SFT. The authors are motivated to empirically investigate this distinction.
4. **Providing Actionable Guidance:** By uncovering the underlying mechanisms, the research aims to offer practitioners more informed strategies for balancing SFT and RL, potentially identifying "inexpensive recovery knobs" that could achieve similar benefits to costly RL-FT in certain scenarios.
5. **Reconciling Existing Literature:** The paper seeks to provide a coherent framework that reconciles prior reports of RL's superior OOD performance, explaining the parameter-level shifts that underpin these observations.

In essence, the motivation is to move beyond "what happens" to "how and why it happens" at a fundamental level within LLM architectures during post-training, thereby enabling more effective and principled fine-tuning practices.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research employs a rigorous experimental design combining a controlled evaluation environment with advanced spectral analysis techniques to probe the internal dynamics of LLMs during two-stage fine-tuning.

#### 3.1. Models and Fine-Tuning Pipeline

- **Base Models:** The study utilizes two popular open-source LLMs: Llama-3.2-11B (an 11-billion parameter model) and Qwen-2.5-7B (a 7-billion parameter model). These models provide diverse architectural and training backgrounds, enhancing the generalizability of findings.
- **Two-Stage Fine-Tuning:** The standard post-training paradigm is followed:
 1. **Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT):** Models are initially warmed up with SFT, minimizing the negative log-likelihood on a labeled dataset.
 2. **Reinforcement Learning Fine-Tuning (RL-FT):** After SFT reaches a certain checkpoint, Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO), a widely used RL algorithm, is applied. The objective is to maximize a scalar reward signal that evaluates the quality of generated actions.

#### 3.2. Evaluation Environment and OOD Variant

- **GeneralPoints Game:** The GeneralPoints environment (a variant of the 24-point card game) is used as the primary benchmark. This environment is designed to assess arithmetic reasoning and generalization. Given four cards, the model must output an equation equaling 24.
- **Out-of-Distribution (OOD) Variation:** To rigorously test OOD performance, the standard rule for face cards (J, Q, K = 10) is altered in the OOD evaluation. Instead, J, Q, and K are interpreted as 11, 12, and 13 respectively. This change creates a clear distribution shift, allowing the disentanglement of learned format alignment from genuine arithmetic reasoning and generalization abilities.
- **Performance Metrics:** Both in-distribution (ID) and OOD performance are continuously tracked using accuracy and loss curves throughout SFT and RL phases.

#### 3.3. Mechanistic Analysis Tools

- **Spectral Analysis via Singular Value Decomposition (SVD):** SVD is applied to key weight matrices within the transformer architecture (specifically, the Query (Q), Key (K), and Value (V) matrices in self-attention layers, as well as input embedding and output head matrices). SVD decomposes a matrix into singular values (quantifying representational mode importance) and singular vectors (defining directional modes). This allows tracking of how internal representations evolve.
- **Principal Angles Between Subspaces (PABS):** To quantify the geometric shifts in parameter space, PABS is used. This method measures the "tilt" or rotation between the dominant singular directions (singular vectors) of a weight matrix before and after a fine-tuning stage. It provides a fine-grained measure of subspace rotation, with angles closer to 0 indicating preservation and angles closer to π/2 indicating maximal misalignment.

#### 3.4. Experimental Manipulations for Causal Inference

- **Singular Vector Directional Re-alignment:** To test the hypothesis that SFT-induced vector rotations cause generalization loss, the researchers perform ablation studies:
 - **Layer-wise Restoration:** Singular vector directions of specific top-K layers from the post-SFT model are replaced with those from the original pre-trained model. This helps identify which layers are crucial for ID specialization vs. OOD generalization.
 - **Rank-wise Restoration:** Within each layer, singular vector directions corresponding to the top-K ranks are selectively restored, investigating the role of different singular components.
- **Causal Validation of RL's Rotational Recovery:** The feature space of an RL-tuned model (known for good OOD) is forced to adopt the geometric orientation (singular vector directions) of a poorly-generalizing SFT model. This causal intervention assesses whether RL's OOD benefits are indeed tied to its specific re-alignment of singular vectors.

The combination of controlled task evaluation, dynamic performance monitoring, detailed spectral analysis, and targeted causal manipulations allows the authors to move beyond correlation and offer compelling evidence for the mechanisms governing SFT and RL fine-tuning.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The study yields several crucial insights into the interplay between SFT and RL fine-tuning for LLMs, particularly regarding their impact on OOD generalization:

1. **SFT Forgetting, RL Recovery, with Limits:**
 - **SFT's Dual Effect:** Supervised fine-tuning initially improves OOD generalization but then causes a significant degradation (catastrophic forgetting) as training progresses and the model overfits to the in-distribution (ID) data. For instance, Llama-3.2-11B's OOD performance dropped from 17.52% (early SFT) to 8.97% (full SFT), while Qwen-2.5-7B saw a 13% drop. Simultaneously, ID accuracy monotonically increases throughout SFT.
 - **RL as Restoration:** The subsequent RL fine-tuning stage substantially mitigates this OOD decay. It restores a significant portion of the lost OOD performance—up to 99% for Qwen-2.5-7B (from 17.09% to 19.66%) and 85% for Llama-3.2-11B (from 8.97% to 16.24%). This restoration comes at a slight cost to the peak ID performance achieved by aggressive SFT.
 - **Limits to RL's Efficacy:** RL's restorative power has boundaries. If SFT pushes the model into a regime of *severe* overfitting, leading to a marked distribution shift in its representation, RL-FT can no longer fully recover the lost OOD performance.
 - **Conclusion:** RL primarily functions as "memory restoration" or "generalization recovery" by counteracting SFT-induced forgetting, rather than discovering fundamentally new solutions or capabilities.

2. **Singular Values Remain Stable, Singular Vector Rotations are Key:**
 - **Singular Value Stability:** Contrary to some previous studies emphasizing singular value changes, the singular value distributions of the Query, Key, and Value (Q, K, V) matrices (and other key matrices) remain remarkably stable during both SFT and RL. The changes in singular value magnitudes are minor, low-magnitude, and noisy across the spectrum. This suggests that the "transformation energy" or overall amplification capacity of these matrices is largely preserved from the pre-trained state.
 - **Directional Shifts Govern Performance:** Instead, OOD performance degradation during SFT and its subsequent recovery by RL correlate almost entirely with *directional shifts*—rotations of the singular vectors. Principal angles between singular vectors before and after fine-tuning show significant rotations, particularly for the extremal components (corresponding to the largest and smallest singular values), while the bulk spectrum remains relatively intact. This implies that fine-tuning primarily re-orients the model's high-dimensional weight space rather than altering its intrinsic capacity.

3. **Low-Rank and Shallow Recovery is Effective:**
 - **Layer-wise Recovery:** Restoring the singular vector directions of "head" (early) and "tail" (late) layers (e.g., layers 0-9 and 20+ for Llama/Qwen) in an SFT-tuned model is most effective for recovering OOD performance, often with less detrimental impact on ID accuracy. This indicates that general functional alignment is maintained by outer layers, while intermediate layers specialize during SFT.
 - **Rank-wise Recovery:** Reversing rotations in the top-rank singular directions (e.g., top 512 for Qwen) within weight matrices has the most significant positive impact on OOD accuracy. This suggests that generalizable, foundational knowledge is primarily encoded in these principal components, while task-specific knowledge is more broadly distributed. Restoring just the top 20% of singular values' directions or the first 25% of layers can recover 70-80% of a model's OOD performance.

4. **Causal Validation of RL's Rotational Recovery:**
 - Direct causal evidence supports the importance of RL's specific vector directions. When the feature space of a high-performing RL-tuned model is forced to adopt the geometric orientation of a poorly-generalizing SFT model, its OOD accuracy plummets. This confirms that the distinct vector directions found by RL are essential for its success in restoring generalization.

5. **"Better In, Better Out":**
 - The quality of the initial SFT checkpoint significantly influences RL's effectiveness. A stronger (less overfitted) SFT checkpoint allows RL to more faithfully rescue OOD abilities. Conversely, highly overfitted SFT checkpoints are harder to restore, even with aggressive directional manipulation.

In summary, the study demonstrates that RL's primary benefit is to mitigate SFT-induced catastrophic forgetting by re-aligning singular vectors, especially in key layers and high-rank components, rather than fundamentally creating new OOD generalization abilities.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research offers profound implications for both the theoretical understanding of Large Language Model fine-tuning and the practical strategies employed by AI practitioners.

#### 5.1. Reconciling and Deepening Mechanistic Understanding

The paper provides a crucial mechanistic explanation for previously observed phenomena. It reconciles conflicting reports regarding RL's superior OOD performance by clarifying that RL primarily acts as a *restorative mechanism* against SFT-induced "catastrophic forgetting" of general capabilities, rather than inventing entirely new solutions. This shift from "creation" to "restoration" is a significant conceptual refinement, providing a more accurate understanding of the sequential fine-tuning pipeline. By attributing OOD performance changes almost entirely to *rotations of singular vectors* and not singular value magnitudes, the study refines the lens through which model adaptation is viewed, moving beyond superficial metrics to parameter-level dynamics.

#### 5.2. Actionable Insights for Practitioners and Cost Reduction

Perhaps the most immediate impact lies in its practical recommendations:
- **Early Intervention:** The finding that RL cannot restore OOD performance after severe SFT overfitting emphasizes the importance of monitoring OOD metrics *during* SFT and intervening earlier, or choosing appropriate SFT durations, to prevent irreversible degradation.
- **Inexpensive Recovery Knobs:** The revelation that "low-rank and shallow recovery is surprisingly effective" provides direct, cost-saving alternatives to full RL fine-tuning. Practitioners might be able to recover 70-80% of OOD performance by selectively restoring singular vector directions in:
 - The *top 20% of singular values* within weight matrices (low-rank UV merging).
 - The *first 25% of layers* (shallow-layer resets).
 These targeted interventions are likely much less computationally expensive than full-parameter RL fine-tuning, offering a powerful avenue for optimizing the resource allocation in LLM development.
- **"Better In, Better Out" Principle:** The confirmation that RL is more effective when starting from a stronger (less overfitted) SFT checkpoint reinforces the need for thoughtful SFT practices as a foundational step.

#### 5.3. Guiding Future Research

The work opens several new avenues for theoretical and empirical exploration:
- **Optimal Stopping Criteria:** The insights into SFT's OOD degradation can inform the development of more sophisticated stopping criteria for SFT, moving beyond purely ID-focused metrics.
- **Unified Fine-tuning Approaches:** Understanding the specific directional conflicts between SFT and RL could inspire novel unified fine-tuning algorithms that inherently balance specialization and generalization by managing these parameter shifts.
- **Generalizability Across Tasks:** The authors explicitly mention plans to extend this analysis to other complex reasoning tasks (e.g., advanced math, code generation), which will be crucial for establishing the universality of these findings.
- **Theoretical Development:** The provided theoretical proof (Appendix A), showing why optimizers prefer to rotate singular vectors rather than change singular values under certain conditions, provides a strong foundation for further theoretical investigations into the learning dynamics of deep neural networks.

In conclusion, "RL Is Neither a Panacea Nor a Mirage" offers a nuanced and mechanistically grounded perspective on LLM fine-tuning. By dissecting the internal workings of SFT and RL, it provides critical insights that can lead to more efficient, robust, and generalizable LLMs, thereby significantly advancing the field.
