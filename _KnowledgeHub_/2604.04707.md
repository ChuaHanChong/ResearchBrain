# OpenWorldLib: A Unified Codebase and Definition of Advanced World Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Peking University, Kuaishou Technology, and other institutions developed OpenWorldLib, a unified inference framework for world models, alongside a standardized definition clarifying their scope and capabilities. This work provides a common codebase for interactive video generation, 3D generation, multimodal reasoning, and Vision-Language-Action tasks, facilitating structured development and comparison within the research community.

## Problem

- The field of world models lacked a universally accepted, clear, and unified definition, leading to diverse interpretations and a fragmented research landscape.
- Ambiguity in defining world model scope hindered systematic research, comparison of methods, and integration of diverse capabilities in AI.
- A practical, unified inference framework was needed to standardize the invocation and integration of various world model-related tasks, accelerating development and application.

## Method

- Proposed a standardized definition for ==world models== as models centered on building ==internal representations== from perception, equipped with ==action-conditioned simulation== and ==long-term memory==.
- Introduced **OpenWorldLib**, a comprehensive, modular inference framework with five core components (==Operator==, ==Synthesis==, ==Reasoning==, ==Representation==, ==Memory==) and a top-level ==Pipeline module==.
- Clarified the scope of world model tasks, distinguishing legitimate tasks (e.g., ==interactive video generation==, ==3D generation==, ==VLA==) from others that do not involve complex physical world understanding.

## Results

- OpenWorldLib successfully integrated and evaluated various interactive video generation models, showing that Hunyuan-WorldPlay achieved superior visual performance for navigation videos, while Cosmos offered higher quality and physical realism for complex interactive operations compared to WoW.
- The framework's Reasoning module demonstrated the capacity for high-level cognitive tasks like spatial and general multimodal reasoning by interpreting diverse perceptual inputs (images, video, audio) to derive verifiable conclusions.
- Evaluation of 3D generation revealed that while methods like VGGT and InfiniteVGGT could generate scenes from various views, challenges remained in maintaining geometric consistency and sharp details with significant camera movement; FlashWorld improved speed but faced similar quality trade-offs.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A clear, standardized definition of world models is crucial for unifying research efforts and distinguishing genuine world modeling capabilities from other generative tasks.
> - Modular architecture within OpenWorldLib, including dedicated modules for Operator, Synthesis, Reasoning, Representation, and Memory, enables robust integration and efficient reuse of diverse world model components.
> - The framework's support for tasks like interactive video generation, 3D scene reconstruction, multimodal reasoning, and Vision-Language-Action (VLA) demonstrates a practical approach to advancing AI systems capable of perceiving, understanding, and interacting with the physical world.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

World models represent a significant direction in artificial intelligence, aiming to enable models to learn from, understand, and predict the dynamics of complex environments. The emergence of large language models (LLMs) and intelligent agents has increased the urgency for AI systems to transition from virtual-world applications to real-world interactions. Historically, the concept of world models, introduced by Ha and Schmidhuber, has evolved to encompass tasks such as next-frame prediction in video generation and 3D generation. Despite growing research interest and numerous surveys, a consistent and unified definition of what constitutes a "world model" and its core capabilities has remained elusive. This lack of standardization has led to ambiguity regarding which tasks truly align with the objectives of world models and which are merely conflated with the term.

This paper addresses these gaps by proposing a clear, standardized definition of world models and presenting OpenWorldLib, a unified inference framework. OpenWorldLib aims to structure and standardize research by integrating models across different tasks—such as interactive video generation, 3D generation, multimodal reasoning, and vision-language-action (VLA)—within a coherent framework. By clarifying the scope and providing a common codebase, this work seeks to facilitate efficient reuse of components, collaborative inference, and more consistent evaluation, thereby advancing the development of AI systems capable of interacting with the physical world. The work also distinguishes between tasks considered relevant to world models (e.g., interactive video generation, multimodal reasoning, VLA, 3D/simulator integration) and those that are often mistakenly categorized as such (e.g., text-to-video generation without complex perception, code generation, entertainment-focused avatar generation).

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind this research stems from the observed lack of a clear and unified definition for "world models" despite their increasing prominence in artificial intelligence research, particularly with the progression of large language models (LLMs) and agents towards real-world applications. The authors note that existing literature often presents diverse interpretations and applications, leading to a fragmented understanding of the field.

The key objectives of this work are articulated as follows:

1. **Standardized Definition of World Models:** To propose a precise and broadly acceptable definition of a world model. This definition positions a world model as a system centered on building internal representations from perception, equipped with action-conditioned simulation and long-term memory capabilities, intended for understanding and predicting the dynamics of a complex world. This objective seeks to clarify which tasks genuinely contribute to developing world model capabilities.
2. **Systematic Categorization of Capabilities:** To systematically categorize the essential capabilities that a world model should possess, moving beyond task-specific implementations. This includes aspects such as perception, interaction, and long-term memory in complex environments.
3. **Development of OpenWorldLib:** To introduce OpenWorldLib, a comprehensive and standardized inference framework. This framework aims to provide a unified codebase for integrating diverse world model-related tasks, including interactive video generation, 3D generation, multimodal reasoning, and Vision-Language-Action (VLA), under a single, coherent structure.
4. **Facilitating Research Efficiency:** To enable efficient reuse of model components and promote collaborative inference within the world model research community. By standardizing interfaces and module designs, OpenWorldLib seeks to reduce redundancy and accelerate development cycles.
5. **Future Direction Analysis:** To offer additional reflections and analyses on potential future directions for world model research, contributing to ongoing discussions about the evolution and challenges of the field.

Collectively, these objectives are motivated by the need to provide a clearer foundation for world model research, enabling more focused development, consistent evaluation, and ultimately, the creation of AI systems that can effectively perceive, interact with, and remember the complexities of the physical world.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology centers on defining world models and then designing a unified, modular inference framework called OpenWorldLib to operationalize this definition. The proposed definition of a world model is "a model or framework centered on building internal representations from perception, equipped with action-conditioned simulation and long-term memory capabilities, for understanding and predicting the dynamics of a complex world." This definition emphasizes perception, interaction, and long-term memory as core capabilities, irrespective of specific tasks or architectures.

Based on this definition, OpenWorldLib is designed with five core modules and an overarching Pipeline module to orchestrate their interactions:

1. **Operator Module:** This module serves as the interface between raw user or environmental inputs and the core execution modules. Its primary functions are:
 - **Validation:** Ensuring that diverse input data (e.g., text prompts, images, continuous control actions, audio signals) conform to the required formats, shapes, and types.
 - **Preprocessing:** Transforming raw signals into standardized tensor representations or structured formats, making them compatible with downstream models. A unified `BaseOperator` template is provided for consistency.

2. **Synthesis Module:** This module is responsible for the generative processes, producing multimodal outputs (visual, auditory, and embodied actions) based on internal learned dynamics and standardized conditioning. It embodies the model's "implicit representation" of the world.
 - **Visual Synthesis:** Covers image and video generation, converting structured conditioning into raster outputs (frames, clips) to predict how scenes evolve. It supports both checkpoint-driven pipelines and hosted-service wrappers.
 - **Audio Synthesis:** Focuses on continuous waveform generation from text, video features, and timing information, providing the auditory component of multimodal outputs.
 - **Other Signal Synthesis (e.g., VLA):** Primarily emphasizes Vision-Language-Action (VLA) signal generation for embodied tasks. It loads specialized physical policies and maps action representations to unified interfaces compatible with simulators or robotic hardware, translating multimodal contexts into executable physical commands. A unified `BaseSynthesis` template is defined.

3. **Reasoning Module:** This module endows the world model with structured understanding capabilities, enabling it to interpret multimodal evidence and produce explicit, verifiable conclusions. It is crucial for inferring spatial relationships, integrating multimodal contexts, and generating grounded semantic interpretations.
 - **General Reasoning:** Utilizes Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) to process text, images, audio, and video for broad instruction following and multimodal understanding.
 - **Spatial Reasoning:** Specialized models for 3D spatial understanding and object localization from visual observations.
 - **Audio Reasoning:** Models dedicated to interpreting and reasoning over auditory signals. A unified `BaseReasoning` template standardizes its integration.

4. **Representation Module:** Distinct from the Synthesis module, this module handles "explicit representations," such as 3D meshes or human-defined simulators. It aims to build structured, verifiable environments where physical rules can be consistently followed over long-term interactions.
 - **3D Reconstruction:** Transforms input data into explicit 3D outputs (e.g., point clouds, depth maps, camera poses).
 - **Simulation Support:** Creates manual environments for testing reasoning and validating actions within a coordinate system.
 - **Service Integration:** Supports local inference and cloud-based APIs for exporting explicit representations to external physics engines. A `BaseRepresentation` template ensures consistent API.

5. **Memory Module:** Essential for maintaining long-term contextual awareness, this module manages the multimodal interaction history, including historical observations, reasoning chains, and interaction states.
 - **Historical Storage:** Records structured information (text, visual features, action trajectories, scene states).
 - **Context Retrieval:** Selects relevant history for consistent reasoning and generation.
 - **State Update:** Records new interaction results after each pipeline execution.
 - **Session Management:** Supports independent memory for different tasks and sessions. A unified `BaseMemory` template is provided.

6. **Pipeline Module:** This is the top-level orchestrator, integrating all other modules into a cohesive system. It manages model initialization, data flow, module invocation, memory interaction, and result post-processing. It offers both single-turn inference (`__call__`) and multi-turn interactive execution (`stream`) with automatic memory handling. A `BasePipeline` template ensures framework-wide consistency.

The approach emphasizes modularity, standardized APIs, and the ability to integrate both implicit (generative) and explicit (simulatory) representations of the world, fostering a more structured and comprehensive research ecosystem for world models.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The paper primarily introduces OpenWorldLib as a unified framework and definition, rather than presenting novel state-of-the-art performance results from a single model. The "Evaluation" section demonstrates how OpenWorldLib provides a standardized environment for testing and comparing existing world model-related methods across various tasks. Experiments were conducted using NVIDIA A800 (80GB VRAM) and H200 (141GB VRAM) GPUs.

The evaluation highlights the framework's capability to integrate and analyze different methods within the defined world model scope:

- **Interactive Video Generation:**
 - **Navigation Video Generation:** The framework supports the evaluation of models like Matrix-Game-2, which offered fast generation but exhibited color shifting issues over longer sequences. More recent models such as Lingbot-World, Hunyuan-GameCraft, and YUME-1.5 showed improved high-quality navigation video generation, with Hunyuan-WorldPlay achieving notable visual performance. The evaluation emphasizes the importance of video generation in assessing a world model's understanding and memory, especially its ability to produce videos that align with precise visual evolution required by interactive prompts (visual conditions with textual instructions, movement, and camera controls).
 - **Interactive Video Generation:** For more complex interactive operations, Wan-IT2V demonstrated basic generation capabilities but struggled with physical consistency. WoW supported a diverse range of functionalities, yet its generation quality and physical realism were lower compared to Cosmos, which exhibited better performance in these aspects. This category underscores the need for models to accurately understand physical rules and interact with the world to produce consistent and realistic future states.

- **Multimodal Reasoning:** The Reasoning module within OpenWorldLib facilitates the grouping of high-level cognitive tasks. This includes evaluating spatial reasoning capabilities (e.g., geometry-centric queries, object relations, spatial deductions) and omni/general reasoning over mixed modalities (text, images, audio, video). Inputs typically consist of instructions or questions paired with perceptual signals, yielding natural-language responses and sometimes generated audio. The framework's ability to integrate these reasoning tasks is critical for translating observations and memory into actionable decisions, explanations, and plans, guiding subsequent generation or control.

- **3D Generation:** OpenWorldLib supports 3D scene reconstruction, which is deemed crucial for robustly representing real-world environments and ensuring physical consistency during long-term interactions. The evaluation showed that methods like VGGT and InfiniteVGGT could generate 3D scenes from different views, but limitations such as geometric inconsistency and texture blurring arose with significant camera movements or in complex areas. FlashWorld offered faster generation speeds, but maintaining a balance between stable shapes and sharp details remained a challenge. This module underscores the framework's capacity to integrate techniques essential for creating verifiable environments where physical rules are strictly followed, supporting realistic physical simulation.

- **Vision-Language-Action (VLA) Generation:** The framework incorporates simulation environments, specifically AI2-THOR (for embodied video generation and photorealistic interaction) and LIBERO (for physically grounded manipulation), to evaluate VLA capabilities. This involves assessing how world models couple semantic understanding with physical dynamics and fine-grained action planning. The evaluation supports methods such as π₀ and π₀.₅, which utilize vision-language backbones augmented with mixture-of-experts action heads for multi-task generalization. LingBot-VA was also evaluated for its generative approach, modeling visual future predictions and continuous action synthesis through a video diffusion architecture. These evaluations highlight OpenWorldLib's role in assessing closed-loop interactive capabilities in complex embodied scenarios, including robotic arm manipulation and autonomous driving.

In summary, the evaluation section demonstrates OpenWorldLib's utility as a unified framework for integrating, testing, and analyzing a range of existing world model-related methods across different modalities and tasks, providing a structured approach to comparing their respective strengths and limitations in the context of the proposed world model definition.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of OpenWorldLib represents a significant step towards standardizing and advancing research in the field of world models. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

**Standardization and Clarity:**
The most immediate significance is the provision of a standardized definition for "world models." By explicitly delineating the core capabilities (perception, interaction, long-term memory for understanding and predicting a complex world) and distinguishing world model-related tasks from other generative AI applications, OpenWorldLib helps to clarify the research landscape. This clarity can mitigate confusion, focus research efforts, and ensure that contributions genuinely align with the objectives of developing comprehensive world models.

**Unified Framework and Codebase:**
OpenWorldLib offers a unified, modular inference framework that integrates diverse world model-related tasks such as interactive video generation, 3D scene reconstruction, multimodal reasoning, and Vision-Language-Action (VLA). This unified codebase standardizes the interfaces for multimodal perceptual inputs and various interaction controls. This modularity facilitates:
- **Efficient Reuse:** Researchers can more easily reuse and combine components from different sub-modules, accelerating the development of new world model architectures.
- **Collaborative Inference:** A common framework promotes collaboration by providing a shared infrastructure for researchers to test and integrate their models.
- **Fair Comparison:** By offering a standardized evaluation pipeline, OpenWorldLib enables more equitable and consistent comparisons of different world model approaches, fostering robust progress in the field.

**Advancing World Model Capabilities:**
The framework's design inherently encourages a holistic view of world models, emphasizing the necessity of integrating multiple modalities and capabilities. This approach is poised to:
- **Promote Holistic Development:** Move research beyond isolated task performance towards models that can truly perceive, interact, and remember across complex environments.
- **Inform Architectural Design:** The discussion section suggests that existing Large Language Models (LLMs) might serve as foundational backbones for world models, capable of encompassing multimodal reasoning and generation. OpenWorldLib provides a platform to explore and validate such hypotheses, guiding future architectural designs.
- **Guide Hardware and Model Structure Evolution:** The paper identifies limitations in current hardware and token-based Transformer architectures for tasks like efficient next-frame prediction. By highlighting these bottlenecks, OpenWorldLib's work implicitly calls for and can help guide future hardware iterations and fundamental changes in model structures to better support the unique demands of world models.

**Impact on Data-Centric AI:**
The authors acknowledge the increasing role of data-centric methodologies in strengthening foundational models for world models. OpenWorldLib's emphasis on comprehensive interaction with complex worlds necessitates robust data strategies, including multimodal data synthesis, domain-specific data augmentation, dynamic training, and rigorous training data quality evaluation. The framework can serve as a testbed for developing and applying these data-centric approaches, ultimately leading to more robust and capable world models.

**Enabling Real-World AI Agents:**
Ultimately, OpenWorldLib aims to promote the development of world models so that AI can better assist humans in complex environments. By providing the tools and definitions for building AI systems that can understand and interact with the physical world, the framework contributes directly to the realization of more capable and intelligent embodied AI agents, impacting fields ranging from robotics and autonomous driving to general AI assistants.

In conclusion, OpenWorldLib serves as a practical reference for the research community, providing both conceptual clarity and engineering infrastructure to facilitate future explorations and fair comparisons in world model research, with the potential to significantly accelerate the development of AI agents that are truly grounded in and capable of interacting with the physical world.
