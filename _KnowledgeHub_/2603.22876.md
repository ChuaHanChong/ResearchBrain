# Grounding Sim-to-Real Generalization in Dexterous Manipulation: An Empirical Study with Vision-Language-Action Models

> [!summary] Summary
> An empirical study systematically investigates factors influencing zero-shot Sim-to-Real generalization for Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models in dexterous manipulation, leveraging over 10,000 real-world trials. It reveals that spatial domain randomization and frame-wise perturbations are crucial for transferability, while showing reinforcement learning can boost real-world success rates from a supervised learning baseline of 5.6% to 42.8% when combined with comprehensive randomization.

## Problem

- Acquiring large-scale, diverse real-world datasets for training generalist VLA models for dexterous manipulation is prohibitively expensive and time-consuming.
- Policies trained exclusively in simulation often suffer significant performance degradation when deployed in physical environments, known as the Sim-to-Real gap, due to discrepancies in visual appearance, physics, and sensor characteristics.
- There is a lack of systematic, factorized analysis on how individual and combined Sim-to-Real techniques impact the transferability of complex VLA models in dexterous manipulation tasks.

## Method

- Conducted a factorized empirical study using OpenVLA-OFT, a state-of-the-art VLA model, examining the influence of multi-level domain randomization, photorealistic rendering, physics-realistic modeling, and reinforcement learning updates on zero-shot Sim-to-Real performance.
- Utilized the RoboTwin 2.0 simulator to collect 100 demonstration trajectories per task, comparing episode-wise and frame-wise randomization strategies and three levels of rendering fidelity (low, medium, high) and physics realism.
- Developed and executed a comprehensive real-world evaluation protocol involving over 10,000 trials on a physical Piper robot, systematically varying backgrounds, lighting, objects, distractors, and spatial layouts to quantify policy robustness.

## Results

- Spatial perturbations (camera pose, table height) delivered the largest individual performance gains in both Sim-OOD and real-world evaluations, showing their dominance over appearance-level changes like background and lighting.
- Frame-wise domain randomization improved real-world success rates by approximately 3–8% for camera pose and up to about 13% for background randomization compared to episode-wise strategies.
- RL fine-tuning, combined with comprehensive domain randomization, increased the average real-world success rate from a 5.6% supervised fine-tuning baseline to 42.8%, and achieved 70.8% in Sim-OOD evaluation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Spatial domain randomization (e.g., camera pose and table height) is the most critical factor for Sim-to-Real generalization, yielding greater performance gains than appearance-level randomization.
> - Frame-wise application of domain randomization (re-sampling factors at every simulation step) consistently outperforms episode-wise randomization, indicating that dynamic, within-episode variations are more effective.
> - Reinforcement Learning (RL) fine-tuning significantly enhances policy robustness to distribution shifts, acting as a complementary strategy that, when combined with domain randomization, further amplifies Sim-to-Real transferability.
