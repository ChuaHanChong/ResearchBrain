# Grounding Sim-to-Real Generalization in Dexterous Manipulation: An Empirical Study with Vision-Language-Action Models

> [!summary] Summary
> An empirical study systematically investigates factors influencing zero-shot Sim-to-Real generalization for Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models in dexterous manipulation, leveraging over 10,000 real-world trials. It reveals that spatial domain randomization and frame-wise perturbations are crucial for transferability, while showing reinforcement learning can boost real-world success rates from a supervised learning baseline of 5.6% to 42.8% when combined with comprehensive randomization.

## Problem

- Acquiring large-scale, diverse real-world datasets for training generalist VLA models for dexterous manipulation is prohibitively expensive and time-consuming.
- Policies trained exclusively in simulation often suffer significant performance degradation when deployed in physical environments, known as the Sim-to-Real gap, due to discrepancies in visual appearance, physics, and sensor characteristics.
- There is a lack of systematic, factorized analysis on how individual and combined Sim-to-Real techniques impact the transferability of complex VLA models in dexterous manipulation tasks.

## Method

- Conducted a factorized empirical study using OpenVLA-OFT, a state-of-the-art VLA model, examining the influence of multi-level domain randomization, photorealistic rendering, physics-realistic modeling, and reinforcement learning updates on zero-shot Sim-to-Real performance.
- Utilized the RoboTwin 2.0 simulator to collect 100 demonstration trajectories per task, comparing episode-wise and frame-wise randomization strategies and three levels of rendering fidelity (low, medium, high) and physics realism.
- Developed and executed a comprehensive real-world evaluation protocol involving over 10,000 trials on a physical Piper robot, systematically varying backgrounds, lighting, objects, distractors, and spatial layouts to quantify policy robustness.

## Results

- Spatial perturbations (camera pose, table height) delivered the largest individual performance gains in both Sim-OOD and real-world evaluations, showing their dominance over appearance-level changes like background and lighting.
- Frame-wise domain randomization improved real-world success rates by approximately 3–8% for camera pose and up to about 13% for background randomization compared to episode-wise strategies.
- RL fine-tuning, combined with comprehensive domain randomization, increased the average real-world success rate from a 5.6% supervised fine-tuning baseline to 42.8%, and achieved 70.8% in Sim-OOD evaluation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Spatial domain randomization (e.g., camera pose and table height) is the most critical factor for Sim-to-Real generalization, yielding greater performance gains than appearance-level randomization.
> - Frame-wise application of domain randomization (re-sampling factors at every simulation step) consistently outperforms episode-wise randomization, indicating that dynamic, within-episode variations are more effective.
> - Reinforcement Learning (RL) fine-tuning significantly enhances policy robustness to distribution shifts, acting as a complementary strategy that, when combined with domain randomization, further amplifies Sim-to-Real transferability.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of precise and scalable robotic manipulation policies is a key area of research for achieving artificial general intelligence (AGI). Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models represent an approach in this field, aiming to create general-purpose robotic agents capable of mapping visual observations and language instructions to continuous motor actions. These models typically adapt multimodal foundation models and fine-tune them on robotic demonstrations, enabling compositional reasoning and multi-task manipulation across diverse environments.

A significant challenge in VLA model development is the reliance on large-scale datasets, which are costly and difficult to acquire in real-world settings due to hardware specificity and inherent scarcity. Simulation offers a scalable alternative for generating synthetic data; however, policies trained in simulation often exhibit performance degradation when deployed in physical environments, a phenomenon known as the "Sim-to-Real gap."

Existing Sim-to-Real techniques fall into categories such as domain randomization, domain adaptation, photorealistic or physics-realistic rendering, and reinforcement learning (RL) fine-tuning. While these methods have been applied to general robotics tasks or control models, there has been limited principled research specifically examining their individual and combined effects on the Sim-to-Real transferability of VLA models in dexterous manipulation. This gap in understanding limits the systematic optimization of Sim-to-Real strategies and hinders the diagnosis of VLA model failures in real-world deployment. This work addresses this limitation by providing an empirical, factorized analysis of how various Sim-to-Real techniques influence the zero-shot generalization of VLA models. It focuses on isolating the contributions of different simulation design factors to provide a mechanistic understanding.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the inherent difficulties in training generalist VLA models for dexterous manipulation. Real-world robotic data collection is resource-intensive, time-consuming, and specific to particular hardware and environments, rendering large-scale data acquisition impractical. While simulation offers a scalable solution for generating training data, policies trained exclusively in simulated environments often fail to generalize effectively to the physical world due to discrepancies in visual appearance, physics, and sensor characteristics—the Sim-to-Real gap.

Although various Sim-to-Real techniques exist, their efficacy and specific contributions to the generalization of complex VLA models in dexterous manipulation tasks remain underexplored. Previous studies often evaluate these techniques in isolation or on simpler control policies, leaving a gap in understanding their impact on VLA models, which integrate visual perception, language grounding, and long-horizon decision-making. This lack of a systematic and mechanistic understanding impedes the principled development and diagnosis of robust VLA policies for real-world deployment.

In response to these motivations, the key objectives of this study are:

1. **Empirical Examination of Sim-to-Real Determinants:** To conduct a factorized empirical analysis of the zero-shot Sim-to-Real performance of VLA models. This involves systematically examining how specific simulation design factors influence generalization. The study focuses on four dimensions: multi-level domain randomization, photorealistic rendering, physics-realistic modeling, and reinforcement learning updates.
2. **Development of a Comprehensive Evaluation Protocol:** To design and implement a standardized evaluation protocol for quantifying the real-world performance of manipulation tasks. This protocol is designed to account for key variations in environmental factors, including background, lighting, distractors, object types, and spatial features, enabling controlled assessment of policy robustness.
3. **Derivation of Critical Insights:** To process and analyze data from over 10,000 real-world trials, deriving actionable insights into the specific factors that contribute to or hinder Sim-to-Real transfer for VLA models.
4. **Establishment of a Standardized Benchmark:** To release the developed robotic platforms and the comprehensive evaluation protocol for public access. This aims to facilitate independent verification of results and establish a realistic and standardized benchmark for future studies on dexterous manipulation policies, thereby fostering reproducible research and advancing the field.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research adopts a zero-shot Sim-to-Real paradigm, where VLA models are trained entirely within a simulated environment and subsequently deployed directly to real-world tasks without any real-world data for fine-tuning. This approach isolates the effectiveness of simulation-side techniques in bridging the reality gap.

**VLA Model Architecture:**
The study bases its analysis on **OpenVLA-OFT**, a state-of-the-art Vision-Language-Action model. OpenVLA-OFT builds upon OpenVLA by incorporating optimized designs for efficient and robust control, including:
- A fused visual encoder (SigLIP and DINOv2 vision transformers) and a Llama2 7B language backbone.
- Parallel decoding with action chunking for single-pass prediction of multiple future actions.
- Continuous action representation using L1 regression, predicting low-level control commands (e.g., joint targets and gripper states).
- FiLM modules to enhance language grounding by modulating visual features with task language embeddings.
For Reinforcement Learning (RL) fine-tuning experiments, the action prediction head is modified to generate discrete action tokens using the original LLaMA2 output head, optimized with a cross-entropy loss, consistent with approaches like SimpleVLA-RL.

**Simulation Environment and Data:**
All policies are trained using **RoboTwin 2.0**, a physics-based manipulation simulator that supports configurable lighting, camera pose, table height, background textures, and distractor objects. Five bimanual manipulation tasks from the RoboTwin 2.0 benchmark are used, covering varying horizon lengths and spatial complexity (e.g., "Stack Bowls Two," "Beat Block Hammer," "Pick Dual Bottles," "Click Bell," "Place Empty Cup"). For each task and training setting, 100 demonstration trajectories are collected in simulation. The simulated robot embodiment is the Cobot Magic platform, matching the real-world deployment platform. Visual input is from a single forward-facing Intel RealSense D435 RGB camera at 640x480 resolution.

**Sim2Real Techniques Investigated:**
The study factorizes Sim2Real techniques into four main categories:

1. **Domain Randomization (DR):**
 - **Factorized Components:** DR is decomposed into five structured components:
 - **Background (BG):** Randomizes wall and table textures from an image set.
 - **Lighting (LT):** Randomizes light color, type, intensity, and position.
 - **Table Distractor (TD):** Samples number, mesh, pose, and texture of irrelevant objects.
 - **Camera Pose (CP):** Applies random translational offsets to the camera position.
 - **Table Height (TH):** Samples table height from a predefined range.
 - **Randomization Granularity:** Comparison of **episode-wise** (factors sampled once per episode) and **frame-wise** (factors resampled at every simulation step) strategies for BG, LT, and CP. Distractor placement and table height remain episode-wise to preserve task stability.

2. **Rendering Fidelity:**
 - **Photorealism:** Three levels are compared:
 - **Low:** Standard (non-ray-tracing) renderer with low samples per pixel (spp) and path depth, denoiser disabled.
 - **Medium:** Ray-tracing (RT) enabled, with low spp and path depth, denoiser disabled.
 - **High:** Ray-tracing enabled, with increased spp and path depth, OIDN denoising enabled.
 - **Physical Realism:** Comparison between standard physics settings and an intentionally distorted dynamics setting (e.g., reduced friction, increased restitution, lowered gravity) to assess sensitivity to physical parameter mismatches.

3. **Reinforcement Learning Fine-Tuning (RFT):**
 - After Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) using behavioral cloning, RFT is optionally performed in simulation. The study uses Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), which optimizes the policy by comparing relative returns within sampled trajectories.
 - Three training variants are compared: (1) SFT in a clean simulation, (2) SFT followed by RL fine-tuning with rollouts in a clean simulation, and (3) SFT followed by RL fine-tuning with rollouts under domain randomization.

**Evaluation Protocols:**

1. **Simulation OOD (Out-of-Distribution) Evaluation:** Policies are tested in simulation environments with unseen variations along individual factors (e.g., unseen textures, distractors, perturbed table heights, camera poses, lighting disturbances) to serve as a controlled proxy for real-world generalization.

2. **Real-World Evaluation:** Conducted on a physical Piper robot platform equipped with a RealSense D435 RGB camera. The evaluation involves controlled variations across multiple real-world factors:
 - **Background Variation:** Three different table textures (wood, blue fabric, yellow grid).
 - **Lighting Variation:** Three distinct light positions with varying illumination colors.
 - **Object Variation:** Both seen and unseen object instances within the same task category.
 - **Distractor Presence:** Task-irrelevant objects placed in the scene at three difficulty levels (2, 4, or 8 distractors) from a separate object set.
 - **Spatial Generalization:** Evaluation on unseen positions within a predefined 3x3 grid layout.
 For each configuration, the task is evaluated under multiple object positions with small random shifts. Performance is measured by success rate, with over 10,000 real-world trials conducted in total.

**Benchmark Platform:**
An online system, inspired by RoboChallenge, is developed to offer open access to standardized protocols, experimental settings, and real-world deployment interfaces for bimanual robots. This platform allows researchers to train policies locally and then upload them for evaluation on real robots at the study's centralized site, ensuring reproducibility and consistency.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The empirical study yields several insights into Sim-to-Real generalization for VLA models:

- **Spatial Features are Dominant for Generalization:** Training with clean simulation data consistently resulted in low success rates in both Sim-OOD and real-world environments. When individual domain randomization factors were introduced, spatial perturbations, specifically **camera pose (CP)** and **table height (TH) randomization**, provided the largest performance gains. These factors significantly improved success rates in both Sim-OOD and real-world evaluations. In contrast, appearance-level changes such as background textures (BG) and lighting (LT) yielded smaller individual improvements. This indicates that variations affecting geometric relationships and viewpoint are more critical for robust policy learning than purely visual changes.

- **Complementary Benefits of Appearance Randomization:** While appearance randomization factors (BG, LT) showed modest individual gains, their combination with spatial randomization factors further enhanced performance. Configurations like "TH + CP + BG" and "TH + CP + LT" consistently outperformed single-factor settings across most tasks. The highest success rates in both Sim-OOD and real-world evaluations were achieved when all domain randomization factors (BG, LT, TD, CP, TH) were applied simultaneously. This suggests that appearance-level randomization helps address additional visual discrepancies when integrated with spatial perturbations.

- **Importance of Randomization Granularity:** Applying domain randomization at the frame level consistently outperformed episode-wise randomization across tasks. Frame-wise perturbations, where factors like background textures, lighting, and camera position are re-sampled at every simulation step, introduced continuous variation within each rollout. This approach improved real-world success rates by approximately 3–8% for camera pose and up to about 13% for background randomization compared to episode-wise strategies, with similar gains observed under Sim-OOD evaluation. Lighting perturbations showed smaller improvements (0–2%) with frame-wise randomization. This indicates that dynamic, within-episode variations are more effective in preventing overfitting to static scene configurations.

- **Simulation Fidelity Enhances Sim2Real Transfer:** Increasing photorealism in rendering consistently improved real-world success rates, indicating that visually realistic observations aid the policy in learning transferable representations. However, the magnitude of improvement diminished beyond the medium fidelity setting, suggesting that a moderate level of photorealism captures most of the essential visual cues for transfer. Furthermore, improving physical realism also led to gains in Sim2Real performance for VLA models. However, these improvements were less significant compared to those obtained from increasing photorealism, indicating a more dominant role for visual fidelity in facilitating Sim2Real transfer.

- **Reinforcement Learning Augments Robustness:** Reinforcement Learning (RL) fine-tuning significantly enhanced policy robustness to distribution shifts. Even when RL was trained under clean simulation conditions (without domain randomization), it substantially improved performance over the Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) baseline, increasing the average real-world success rate from 5.6% to 33.4%. This performance level approached that of SFT combined with comprehensive domain randomization. When domain randomization was incorporated during RL rollouts (SFT + RL + DR), performance further improved to an average of 42.8% in real-world evaluation and 70.8% in Sim-OOD evaluation. This indicates that RL itself contributes to generalization beyond supervised imitation and can effectively leverage diverse training experiences provided by domain randomization to amplify Sim2Real robustness.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of robotic manipulation by systematically investigating the factors influencing Sim-to-Real generalization for Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. The findings address a gap in current literature by providing a factorized empirical analysis of Sim-to-Real techniques, offering a mechanistic understanding that extends beyond previous studies focused on general robotics tasks or simpler control models.

The study's insights have several potential impacts:

1. **Informing Policy Optimization:** The identified dominance of spatial domain randomization and the benefits of frame-wise randomization provide guidance for designing more effective simulation training strategies. Researchers can prioritize variations in geometric relationships and viewpoint, and implement dynamic randomization within episodes, to improve VLA model robustness.
2. **Guiding Simulation Development:** The findings regarding rendering fidelity suggest that while higher photorealism is beneficial, the marginal gains diminish beyond a certain threshold. This information can inform developers of robotic simulators on resource allocation, suggesting that moderate photorealism might offer a good balance between realism and computational cost. The comparative impact of visual versus physical realism also helps focus efforts in simulator development.
3. **Enhancing VLA Model Development:** The demonstration that reinforcement learning fine-tuning significantly improves robustness, even in clean simulation environments, highlights its potential as a complementary strategy to supervised learning. When combined with structured domain randomization, RL further amplifies generalization, providing a path towards more resilient VLA policies.
4. **Establishing a Standardized Benchmark:** The development and public release of the robotic platforms and comprehensive evaluation protocol constitute a significant contribution. This standardized benchmark enables reproducible research, facilitates independent verification of new methods, and allows for systematic comparison of Sim-to-Real performance across different VLA models and techniques. This infrastructure can accelerate progress in developing generalist robotic agents.
5. **Advancing Scalable Robotics:** By improving the ability to transfer policies trained in simulation to the real world, this work moves closer to realizing scalable and practical robotic systems. Reducing the reliance on costly real-world data collection and fine-tuning is crucial for the widespread deployment of autonomous manipulation capabilities, contributing to the broader goal of artificial general intelligence in embodied systems.
