# Causal Reinforcement Learning: A Survey

> [!summary] Summary
> This survey paper systematically organizes and defines the emerging field of Causal Reinforcement Learning (Causal RL), illustrating how incorporating causal principles addresses fundamental limitations of traditional RL. It categorizes existing approaches by the challenges they solve and provides a roadmap for future research, aiming to build more robust, efficient, and ethical AI systems.

## Problem

- Traditional Reinforcement Learning (RL) agents often lack a fundamental understanding of world dynamics, leading to poor sample efficiency and difficulty generalizing policies to new environments.
- RL methods are susceptible to learning spurious correlations from observational data, making them brittle and unreliable in real-world scenarios.
- Classical RL lacks inherent mechanisms for explainability, fairness, and safety, which are crucial for deployment in sensitive or high-stakes applications.

## Method

- The authors formally define Causal RL by linking ==Partially Observable Markov Decision Processes (POMDPs)== to ==Structural Causal Models (SCMs)==, integrating causal assumptions and knowledge into the RL framework.
- A problem-oriented taxonomy is proposed, categorizing Causal RL methods based on how they address specific challenges in traditional RL, such as generalizability, spurious correlations, sample efficiency, and ethical concerns.
- The survey systematically reviews and evaluates diverse Causal RL techniques, including ==causal representation learning==, ==do-calculus==, ==counterfactual reasoning==, and causal graph applications, across these problem categories.

## Results

- Causal RL approaches demonstrate enhanced generalizability to irrelevant variables, varying dynamics, and different tasks by focusing on invariant causal mechanisms.
- Causal methods effectively address spurious correlations by de-biasing policy learning and evaluation, particularly in offline/off-policy settings, and show promise in data fusion strategies.
- Improvements in sample efficiency are observed through compact causal representations, directed exploration based on causal influence, and powerful counterfactual data augmentation, which generates 'what-if' scenarios without real-world interaction.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Causality provides a principled framework for RL agents to learn invariant mechanisms of the environment, enabling robust generalization and knowledge transfer beyond mere statistical correlations.
> - Causal inference tools, such as do-calculus and instrumental variables, can effectively identify and correct biases arising from unobserved confounders and selection bias in RL data.
> - Integrating causal reasoning empowers RL with capabilities like counterfactual explanations, formal characterization of fairness, and proactive identification of safety threats, extending agent intelligence beyond return maximization.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Reinforcement Learning (RL) has emerged as a powerful paradigm for solving complex sequential decision-making problems under uncertainty, achieving remarkable successes in domains from game playing (e.g., AlphaGo) to robotics. However, despite these advancements, applying traditional RL methods to real-world scenarios remains challenging. The authors identify several core limitations: RL agents often lack a fundamental understanding of the world, necessitating extensive trial-and-error learning; they struggle with sample efficiency, requiring vast amounts of data; they face difficulties in generalizing learned policies to new environments or tasks; they are susceptible to spurious correlations; and they often lack explainability, fairness, and safety guarantees, which are critical for deployment in sensitive applications.

Concurrently, Causal Inference offers a rigorous mathematical framework for formalizing cause-and-effect relationships, providing tools to understand the data generation process, reason about interventions, and perform counterfactual analysis. This field has seen successful applications in various machine learning domains, including computer vision, natural language processing, and recommender systems, demonstrating its potential to address issues related to robustness, generalizability, and interpretability.

The paper posits that **Causal Reinforcement Learning (Causal RL)** is an "emerging field" that seeks to bridge the gap between these two powerful domains. It represents a "subfield of reinforcement learning that seeks to enhance existing algorithms by incorporating causal relationships into the learning process." The fundamental premise is that by equipping RL agents with a causal understanding of their environment, they can move beyond merely learning superficial statistical correlations to grasping the invariant mechanisms that govern the world.

This survey addresses a crucial gap in the rapidly evolving landscape of AI research. While individual works have explored various facets of integrating causality into RL, the field has lacked a "clear and consistent conceptualization of causal assumptions and knowledge" and suffered from "disparate terminologies and techniques." This incoherence makes it difficult for researchers, particularly newcomers, to grasp the essence, implications, and opportunities within Causal RL. By providing the "first comprehensive survey of causal RL," the authors aim to consolidate these diverse advancements, establish meaningful connections, and foster a cohesive understanding, thereby accelerating research and application in this critical area. The work positions itself as a foundational resource for navigating the complexities and harnessing the potential of Causal RL for building more intelligent, robust, and ethical AI systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind this survey stems from the inherent limitations of traditional Reinforcement Learning (RL) when confronted with the complexities of real-world applications. Despite its remarkable achievements, classical RL suffers from fundamental challenges that hinder its widespread deployment. Specifically, traditional RL agents often lack a deep, fundamental understanding of the underlying world dynamics, forcing them to learn from scratch through numerous, often costly, trial-and-error interactions. This leads to issues such as poor sample efficiency, difficulty in generalizing acquired knowledge to novel situations, susceptibility to spurious correlations, and a general inability to provide transparent explanations for their decisions or ensure fairness and safety.

In response to these challenges, the authors recognize the profound potential of **Causality**. Causal inference, with its systematic framework for formalizing knowledge and leveraging invariance for effective knowledge transfer, offers a principled approach to overcoming many of RL's shortcomings. The convergence of these two fields has led to the emergence of Causal Reinforcement Learning (Causal RL), a promising area dedicated to integrating causal principles into the RL learning process.

Against this backdrop, the authors outline several key objectives for their survey:

1. **Comprehensive Review and Definition:** To provide a thorough review of the literature in Causal RL, clearly defining the field, exploring its motivations, and articulating how it fundamentally improves upon traditional RL approaches. This includes a concise overview of foundational concepts in both causality research and RL.
2. **Problem-Oriented Taxonomy:** To systematically identify and categorize the key challenges in RL that can be effectively addressed or improved by explicitly considering causality. This involves proposing a novel problem-oriented taxonomy to facilitate a deeper understanding of the benefits of causality-aware techniques.
3. **Comparative Analysis:** To conduct a detailed comparative analysis of existing Causal RL approaches, examining their diverse methodologies, the specific causal concepts they employ (e.g., SCMs, interventions, counterfactuals), and their inherent limitations. The evaluation focuses on how these methods enhance sample efficiency, generalizability, knowledge transfer, mitigate spurious correlations, and promote explainability, fairness, and safety.
4. **Identification of Research Gaps and Future Directions:** To shed light on the limitations of current research in Causal RL and to outline promising avenues for future exploration. This includes advancing theoretical analyses, establishing robust benchmarks, and tackling specific, complex learning problems to propel the application of Causal RL in real-world scenarios.

By achieving these objectives, the authors aim to consolidate the diverse advancements within Causal RL, establish meaningful connections between different works, clarify disparate terminologies, and ultimately provide a cohesive and accessible resource for the research community. This effort is motivated by the conviction that a clearer understanding of Causal RL will foster its continuous development and accelerate its success in building more intelligent, trustworthy, and effective AI systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors adopt a structured and problem-oriented approach to surveying the vast and interdisciplinary field of Causal Reinforcement Learning. Their methodology can be broken down into several key stages:

1. **Foundational Background:** The survey begins by establishing a common ground, providing a concise yet comprehensive introduction to the fundamental concepts of both causality and reinforcement learning.
 - **Causality:** It delves into Structural Causal Models (SCMs) as the primary framework, explaining key components like endogenous/exogenous variables, structural equations, and causal graphs. Crucially, it elaborates on the "Ladder of Causation" (correlation, intervention, counterfactuals) and introduces essential causal operators like `do`, emphasizing the distinction between observational conditioning and interventional probabilities. Mediation analysis, causal discovery, and causal representation learning are also briefly introduced.
 - **Reinforcement Learning:** It defines Markov Decision Processes (MDPs), value functions, and Bellman equations. It also outlines common categorizations of RL methods (e.g., policy-based, value-based, model-based, on-policy, off-policy, offline).
 - **Formalizing Causal RL:** A crucial step is taken to formally define Causal RL by illustrating how a Partially Observable Markov Decision Process (POMDP) can be cast into an SCM. This theoretical link underpins the entire survey's premise, allowing for a principled discussion of causality within RL contexts. The definition emphasizes incorporating "additional assumptions or prior knowledge" about causal mechanisms.

2. **Problem-Oriented Taxonomy:** Instead of categorizing methods by their algorithmic families, the authors propose a novel taxonomy based on the *challenges faced by traditional RL that causality can address*. This problem-centric view helps readers understand the *why* behind integrating causal concepts. The four main categories are:
 - Advancing Generalizability and Knowledge Transfer
 - Addressing Spurious Correlations
 - Enhancing Sample Efficiency
 - Promoting Explainability, Fairness, and Safety (beyond maximizing returns)

3. **Systematic Evaluation within Categories:** For each of the four identified challenges:
 - The authors first describe the *nature of the problem* in traditional RL (e.g., the "issue of generalizability," "issue of spurious correlation").
 - They then explain *how causality helps* to mitigate or solve this problem, leveraging the foundational concepts introduced earlier (e.g., invariant mechanisms for generalization, do-calculus and causal graphs for spurious correlations, counterfactuals for sample efficiency and explainability).
 - A detailed review of *existing methods* is provided, often with illustrative examples and tables summarizing representative papers, their techniques, settings (e.g., online, offline, imitation), and environments/tasks. This comparative analysis highlights diverse methodologies and their specific contributions.
 - Techniques discussed include causal representation learning, causal dynamics learning, intervention, domain randomization, counterfactual reasoning, instrumental variables, backdoor/frontdoor adjustment, sensitivity analysis, inverse probability weighting, mediation analysis, and various applications of causal graphs.

4. **Discussion of Limitations and Future Directions:** The survey concludes by acknowledging the inherent limitations of current Causal RL approaches, such as the reliance on domain knowledge, challenges in causal representation learning from raw data, and scalability issues. It then articulates promising future research directions, including deeper exploration of causal learning (discovery, representation, mechanisms), causality-aware multitask and meta-RL, human-in-the-loop learning, theoretical advances (especially beyond bandit problems), and the development of robust benchmarks for real-world Causal RL.

By adopting this structured, problem-oriented approach, the authors effectively synthesize a complex body of literature, clarify foundational concepts, provide a coherent framework for understanding the field, and lay a roadmap for future research in Causal RL.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The survey systematically synthesizes the current landscape of Causal Reinforcement Learning (Causal RL) by examining how causal principles address critical limitations of traditional RL. The main findings and results are organized around the core challenges discussed:

#### 4.1. Advancing Generalizability and Knowledge Transfer

The survey finds that causality offers a principled framework to tackle RL's generalization issues. By focusing on **invariant causal mechanisms**—stable relationships that persist across different environments or tasks—Causal RL agents can learn policies that are robust to distributional shifts. This is in contrast to traditional RL, which can easily overfit to specific training environments.
- **Generalization to Irrelevant Variables:** Methods leverage causal representation learning to identify and disregard irrelevant features (e.g., background color), allowing policies to generalize to diverse observation spaces. Techniques like invariant prediction help extract causal representations robust to such nuisances.
- **Generalization to Varying Dynamics:** Causal dynamics learning, often involving self-supervised experimental behavior or interventions, helps agents infer and categorize causal factors in the environment (e.g., physical properties), leading to better adaptation to new dynamics. Counterfactual trajectories can be generated to train agents on diverse object properties.
- **Generalization to Different Tasks:** By explicitly modeling how different tasks correspond to alterations in the reward mechanism or its causal parents, agents can learn adaptive policies using disentangled causal features, allowing efficient transfer to new tasks. This aligns with the "sparse mechanism shift hypothesis," where only a subset of causal mechanisms change between tasks.

#### 4.2. Addressing Spurious Correlations

Spurious correlations arise from **unobserved confounders** (common causes) leading to confounding bias, or **collider nodes** (common effects) leading to selection bias. Causal RL provides the tools to identify and correct these biases.
- **Confounding Bias:** Techniques like **do-calculus, backdoor/frontdoor adjustments, instrumental variables, and proxy variables** are employed to de-bias policy learning or evaluation from confounded observational data. This is particularly crucial in offline/off-policy RL or Dynamic Treatment Regimes (DTRs) in healthcare. The trend of **data fusion** (combining observational and experimental data) is highlighted as a powerful approach.
- **Selection Bias:** Causal methods, such as **inverse probability weighting**, are used to correct for biases introduced when observed data samples do not accurately represent the target population, as seen in hindsight experience replay in goal-conditioned RL or offline RL scenarios where uncertainty can be spuriously correlated with rewards.

#### 4.3. Enhancing Sample Efficiency

Causality can significantly reduce the amount of data required for RL agents to learn optimal policies.
- **Representation Learning:** Learning compact, informative causal representations that abstract away unnecessary details helps agents focus on relevant features, improving learning efficiency. Methods identify action-sufficient state representations or task-independent abstractions.
- **Directed Exploration:** Causal knowledge can guide exploration by prioritizing actions that have a high causal influence on the outcome or by designing experiments that efficiently uncover causal factors (e.g., One-Factor-at-A-Time interventions to maximize "causal curiosity").
- **Counterfactual Data Augmentation:** This powerful technique uses an estimated causal model (SCM) to generate "what-if" scenarios (counterfactual trajectories) that were not observed in the real world. This artificial data, derived from a three-step reasoning process (abduction, action, prediction), allows agents to learn from diverse situations without costly real-world interaction, leading to substantial sample efficiency gains.

#### 4.4. Promoting Explainability, Fairness, and Safety

Causal RL extends beyond purely maximizing returns, addressing ethical and reliability concerns critical for real-world deployment.
- **Explainability:** Causal insights enable RL agents to provide natural, human-understandable explanations for their decisions. **Counterfactual explanations** (e.g., "why did I do X instead of Y?") are highlighted as a key method, helping humans understand the minimal changes required to achieve a different outcome. This also aids in **credit assignment** in multi-agent RL.
- **Fairness:** Causal modeling helps formally characterize and understand the intricate interplay between decision-making and various factors, including sensitive attributes. Concepts like **counterfactual individual fairness** and the decomposition of effects (direct, indirect, spurious) allow for systematic analysis and mitigation of discrimination, turning fairness into a constrained optimization problem.
- **Safety:** Causal frameworks help identify potential safety threats and develop preventive solutions. For instance, **counterfactual policy evaluation** can assess the safety implications of different policies before deployment (e.g., in autonomous driving). Causal influence diagrams can also help understand and prevent issues like **reward tampering**, ensuring agent behavior aligns with intended objectives.

#### 4.5. Limitations and Future Directions

The survey also highlights crucial limitations: the strong reliance on accurate **domain knowledge** (e.g., causal graphs), the difficulty of **causal representation learning** from raw, unstructured data without strong assumptions, and **scalability challenges** in complex environments. Furthermore, **identifiability issues** and computational complexity associated with counterfactual reasoning remain significant hurdles.

These limitations lead directly to the proposed **future directions**:
- **Causal Learning:** Emphasizing the need for advancements in causal representation learning, causal discovery (especially with interventional data), and causal mechanism learning from raw data.
- **Causality-aware Multitask and Meta RL:** Leveraging causal structures to improve knowledge sharing and adaptation across tasks, potentially leading to better lifelong learning.
- **Human-in-the-loop Learning and RL from Human Feedback:** Integrating human causal knowledge and feedback to improve agent learning and align with human values.
- **Theoretical Advances:** Extending theoretical analyses beyond Multi-Armed Bandit problems to complex MDPs, focusing on regret bounds and identifiability.
- **Benchmarking Causal RL:** Developing transparent, multi-faceted benchmarks that expose causal mechanisms, allowing for comprehensive evaluation of Causal RL algorithms on diverse metrics beyond just return.
- **Real-world Causal RL:** Bridging the gap between academic research and practical deployment by addressing real-world challenges like limited samples, high-dimensional inputs, safety, and explainability.

In essence, the survey strongly advocates for a "causal thinking" mindset in RL research, demonstrating its transformative potential across a wide spectrum of critical challenges.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This survey by Deng et al. represents a highly significant contribution to the fields of Reinforcement Learning (RL) and Artificial Intelligence more broadly. Its primary significance lies in being the "first comprehensive survey of causal RL" that systematically consolidates the disparate advancements and contributions within this rapidly emerging domain. Before this work, researchers, particularly those new to the intersection of causality and RL, faced a fragmented landscape of terminology, techniques, and problem formulations. This survey addresses this critical need for coherence and clarity.

The potential impacts of this work are multi-faceted:

1. **Structuring and Unifying the Field:** By providing a clear definition of Causal RL, introducing foundational concepts from both causality and RL, and proposing a novel problem-oriented taxonomy, the survey offers a much-needed organizational framework. This structured understanding will significantly lower the barrier to entry for new researchers, enabling faster assimilation of existing knowledge and fostering more targeted and impactful future research. It creates a common language and understanding, which is crucial for interdisciplinary fields.

2. **Accelerating Research Progress:** By systematically identifying key challenges in RL that can be addressed by causality and reviewing the diverse approaches, the survey highlights both successful strategies and persistent limitations. This comprehensive overview can inspire new research directions, guide the development of more principled algorithms, and encourage researchers to explore underexplored areas such as causal learning from raw data, theoretical advancements in MDPs, and robust benchmarking. The emphasis on future directions provides a concrete roadmap for the field's evolution.

3. **Enhancing RL Capabilities for Real-World Applications:** The core value proposition of Causal RL, as elucidated by the survey, is to move beyond superficial correlations to a deeper, more robust understanding of cause-and-effect. This is paramount for deploying RL systems in high-stakes real-world environments.
 - **Robustness and Generalizability:** By highlighting how causality improves generalization to unseen environments and tasks and mitigates spurious correlations, the survey points to a path toward building more reliable and adaptable RL agents. This is crucial for applications like autonomous systems and robotics that operate in dynamic, unpredictable settings.
 - **Efficiency:** The discussion on sample efficiency, particularly through causal representation learning and counterfactual data augmentation, offers strategies to reduce the prohibitive data requirements of traditional RL, making it more feasible for real-world scenarios where data collection is costly or unsafe.
 - **Trustworthiness and Ethics:** The dedicated section on explainability, fairness, and safety is particularly impactful. As AI systems become more integrated into society, their ethical implications are paramount. Causal RL provides tools to make AI decisions transparent, equitable, and safe, addressing public and regulatory concerns in domains like healthcare, finance, and criminal justice. This contributes directly to the development of trustworthy AI.

4. **Promoting Interdisciplinary Collaboration:** The survey intrinsically bridges machine learning (specifically RL) and statistics/philosophy (causal inference). By clearly articulating the connections and benefits, it encourages greater collaboration between these communities, leading to cross-pollination of ideas and methodologies that can push the boundaries of AI. The discussions on data fusion, human-in-the-loop learning, and RL from human feedback further underscore the value of integrating diverse forms of knowledge.

In conclusion, "Causal Reinforcement Learning: A Survey" is more than just a summary of existing work; it is a foundational document that shapes the discourse, clarifies the landscape, and charts the future course for a critical subfield of AI. Its significance lies in its potential to accelerate the development of more intelligent, adaptable, efficient, explainable, fair, and safe autonomous agents, ultimately fostering the responsible and effective deployment of AI in the real world.
