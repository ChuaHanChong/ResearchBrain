# RoboGrasp: A Universal Grasping Policy for Robust Robotic Control

> [!summary] Summary
> RoboGrasp introduces a framework that enhances diffusion-based visuomotor policies by integrating an auxiliary grasping-box detection model to provide explicit spatial guidance for robotic grasping. This approach achieved up to 34% higher grasp success rates in few-shot and prompt-based tasks compared to a standard Diffusion Policy baseline, improving generalization and adaptability.

## Problem

- Existing Behavior Cloning (BC) methods rely on raw sensor data (e.g., RGB images), leading to overfitting and poor generalization to unseen objects or cluttered environments.
- Current policies often learn implicit patterns without explicit task guidance, limiting their robustness and adaptability to novel scenarios.
- Large-scale BC models are computationally expensive, and point-based affordance representations lack actionable information for effective grasping.

## Method

- The **RoboGrasp** framework augments the ==Diffusion Policy (DP)== by integrating a Grasp Detection Module for explicit grasp-specific information.
- A fine-tuned YOLOv11-m model serves as the Grasp Detection Module, predicting the 2D coordinates (center, width, height) of grasping boxes for observed visual data.
- The policy's Observation Encoder combines visual data, robot arm state, and these explicit grasping box features to condition a diffusion-based action prediction head.

## Results

- RoboGrasp achieved up to **33.75%** higher Task Success Rate (TSR) in the PickBig task and **28.7%** higher TSR in the PickGoods prompt-based task compared to the Diffusion Policy baseline.
- In the PickCup task, RoboGrasp demonstrated **100%** TSR and Grasp Success Rate (GSR) for new cup instances with limited demonstrations, outperforming DP's lower success rates (e.g., **70%** TSR for green mug).
- The framework successfully performed prompt-based grasping for specific retail items (e.g., Meiji Chocolate Bar, Tissue) with **100%** TSR and high GSRs, using grasping boxes as explicit goal prompts.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating explicit grasping box detections as structured visual guidance significantly improves the generalization, precision, and stability of diffusion-based robotic grasping policies.
> - Leveraging pretrained vision models to provide task-relevant information allows robotic policies to focus on pertinent objects and regions, mitigating overfitting to raw sensor data.
> - Grasping-based affordances facilitate robust few-shot learning for novel objects and enable goal-oriented, promptable grasping actions, reducing the need for extensive training data.
