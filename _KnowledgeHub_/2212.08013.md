# FlexiViT: One Model for All Patch Sizes

> [!summary] Summary
> FlexiViT, developed by Google Research, introduces a method to train a single Vision Transformer model capable of effective performance across a wide range of patch sizes, eliminating the need for retraining for different speed/accuracy trade-offs. The model achieves this flexibility while often matching or exceeding the performance of fixed-patch-size ViTs on various computer vision tasks.

## Problem

- Standard Vision Transformers (ViTs) are typically trained and optimized for a single, fixed patch size, making them inflexible for deployment under varying computational budgets.
- The choice of patch size in ViTs introduces a rigid trade-off between model speed (computational cost) and accuracy, requiring costly re-training for each desired operating point.
- Current ViT deployment strategies necessitate maintaining multiple specialized models for different computational constraints, increasing development and management overhead.

## Method

- The model is trained by ==uniformly sampling patch sizes randomly== from a predefined set during each training step.
- A novel =='PI-resize' method== is introduced for adaptively resizing patch embedding weights on-the-fly, along with bilinear interpolation for position embeddings, to maintain embedding integrity.
- ==Knowledge distillation==, coupled with initializing the student FlexiViT with a pre-trained fixed-patch-size teacher, is used to boost performance, especially at larger patch sizes.

## Results

- FlexiViT models achieve robust performance across patch sizes (e.g., **8x8** to **48x48**), matching or outperforming standard ViTs trained for specific fixed patch sizes.
- The method enables resource-efficient transfer learning, allowing models to be fine-tuned cheaply with large patches and deployed with small patches for higher performance without additional training.
- FlexiViT models perform comparably to, and often slightly better than, fixed-patch ViTs across a diverse set of downstream tasks including classification, object detection, and segmentation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Randomizing patch sizes during training, combined with principled embedding resizing, enables a single ViT model to learn robust representations adaptable to diverse patch granularities.
> - The PI-resize method effectively preserves the magnitude and meaning of patch embeddings across different scales, which is crucial for generalized performance.
> - FlexiViT models learn consistent internal representations across varying patch sizes, indicating a shared understanding of visual features regardless of input granularity.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Vision Transformers (ViTs) represent a paradigm shift in computer vision, moving away from traditional convolutional neural networks (CNNs) by processing images as sequences of patches. This "patchification" procedure is central to ViTs' operation, but the choice of patch size introduces a rigid trade-off between model speed and accuracy. Smaller patches lead to more tokens, higher accuracy, but also significantly higher computational costs, scaling quadratically with sequence length. Crucially, standard ViTs are typically trained and perform optimally at a *single, fixed patch size*, meaning adapting a model to different compute budgets or deployment scenarios traditionally requires costly re-training for each desired patch size.

FlexiViT addresses this fundamental inflexibility. The broader research landscape for improving ViT efficiency includes several concurrent avenues:
- **Token Pruning/Removal:** Methods like randomized or structured token dropping (e.g., Masked Autoencoders, DynamicViT) aim to reduce computational load by selectively discarding less important tokens during or after training. FlexiViT *contrasts* with these by always keeping all tokens, focusing instead on the initial patchification.
- **Input Resolution Scaling:** Some works explore changing input image resolution during training to accelerate pre-training or for data augmentation. However, these models generally operate at a single final resolution, unlike FlexiViT, which aims for a single model flexible across *multiple* patch sizes at inference.
- **Neural Architecture Search (NAS) and Supernets:** NAS approaches, such as "supernets," train a single large model from which various sub-architectures can be extracted. While conceptually similar in aiming for a single flexible model, NAS often involves complex architectural modifications and searching across many dimensions. FlexiViT offers a *sharpened focus* solely on the patch size, providing a simpler, more direct, and easily integrable solution. SuperViT is a closely related work that also leverages multiple scales and token dropping, but FlexiViT maintains a simpler, drop-in approach.
- **Matryoshka Representation Learning:** This complementary approach focuses on training models where output vectors contain meaningful sub-vectors at different granularities, enabling adaptive deployment by truncating representations.

FlexiViT distinguishes itself by offering a **simple, drop-in improvement** to existing ViT training procedures, requiring minimal architectural changes to achieve significant flexibility. It doesn't aim to invent a new architecture but rather to "flexify" existing standard ViTs, making them inherently adaptable to varying patch sizes and, consequently, different speed/accuracy trade-offs, without the need for multiple specialized models or complex token management strategies. This positions FlexiViT as a practical and efficient enhancement to the mainstream ViT ecosystem.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core objective of the FlexiViT project is to develop a single Vision Transformer model that can perform effectively across a wide spectrum of patch sizes without requiring dedicated retraining for each specific size. This aims to overcome the inherent inflexibility of standard ViT models, where the chosen patch size during training dictates the optimal performance and computational cost during inference.

The motivations driving this research are multi-faceted:
- **Addressing the Speed/Accuracy Trade-off:** The patch size in ViTs directly controls the computational cost (speed) and predictive performance (accuracy). Smaller patches lead to higher accuracy but are computationally expensive, while larger patches are faster but less accurate. Current ViTs are locked into a single point on this trade-off curve, making it difficult to adapt them to diverse deployment environments with varying computational budgets.
- **Enabling Compute-Adaptive Deployment:** In real-world applications, models often need to operate under different latency, memory, or throughput constraints. A single FlexiViT model capable of dynamically adjusting its patch size at inference time can seamlessly cater to these varying compute budgets, offering flexibility without the burden of maintaining and deploying multiple specialized models.
- **Reducing Training and Development Costs:** Retraining a ViT model for every desired patch size is computationally intensive and time-consuming. FlexiViT aims to significantly reduce these costs by allowing a single training run to produce a model capable of handling multiple patch sizes, thereby streamlining the model development and deployment pipeline.
- **Generalizing ViT Utility:** By making ViTs inherently flexible, FlexiViT enhances their general utility as backbones for a wide range of downstream tasks, allowing researchers and practitioners to easily integrate compute-adaptive capabilities into their applications without complex modifications.
- **Improving Transfer Learning:** The work explores how this flexibility can be leveraged to introduce more resource-efficient transfer learning strategies, such as fine-tuning cheaply at a large patch size and then deploying at a smaller patch size for high performance.

In essence, FlexiViT seeks to make Vision Transformers truly "flexible," unlocking their potential for broader and more efficient application in diverse computational settings.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology behind FlexiViT is centered on a straightforward yet effective idea: **randomizing the patch size during training**. This seemingly simple change, combined with specific parameter adaptation techniques, enables a single ViT model to generalize across various patch sizes.

The core components of the FlexiViT approach are:

1. **Randomized Patch Size Training:**
 - During each training step, the patch size is uniformly sampled at random from a predefined set of possible patch sizes (e.g., 8x8 to 48x48 pixels for a 240x240 input image).
 - This ensures that the model is exposed to a wide range of sequence lengths and patch granularities throughout training, fostering its ability to handle varied inputs.

2. **Adaptive Patch and Position Embedding Resizing:**
 - Standard ViTs have fixed patch embedding weights ($\omega$) and position embeddings ($\pi$) tied to a specific patch size. FlexiViT introduces an "underlying parameter shape" for these embeddings (e.g., 32x32 for patches, 7x7 for position embeddings).
 - For each sampled patch size during training, these underlying parameters are adaptively resized "on-the-fly" as part of the model's forward pass to match the current patch configuration.
 - **Position Embeddings:** Bilinear interpolation is used for resizing position embeddings, a technique already established in the original ViT paper for fine-tuning at higher resolutions.
 - **Patch Embeddings: PI-resize (Pseudoinverse Resize):** This is a key technical innovation. Unlike simple bilinear interpolation, PI-resize is designed to *preserve the magnitude and meaning of patch embeddings* even when resizing the input image and the patch embedding weights. The method leverages the pseudoinverse of the bilinear resize operation to adapt the embedding weights. This ensures that the dot product between the patch and the (resized) embedding weight remains consistent with the original patch embedding, minimizing information loss and preventing dramatic changes in token norms. This inductive bias is crucial for learning a single, robust model.

3. **Knowledge Distillation with Teacher Initialization:**
 - To achieve strong performance, FlexiViT employs knowledge distillation, a technique where a student model learns from a more powerful teacher model.
 - A critical aspect is initializing the FlexiViT student with the weights of a high-performing, fixed-patch-size ViT teacher (e.g., a ViT-B/8 model). During initialization, the teacher's patch embedding weights are PI-resized to the student's underlying 32x32 shape, and position embeddings are bilinearly resampled to 7x7.
 - The student is then trained by minimizing the KL-divergence between its predictions (with randomized patch sizes) and the teacher's predictions (at its fixed, optimal patch size). This initialization helps alleviate the challenging optimization problem of distillation, leading to significantly better student performance, especially at larger patch sizes.

4. **Minimal Architectural Changes:**
 - A notable strength of FlexiViT's approach is its simplicity. The core ViT architecture remains largely unchanged, making it a "drop-in improvement" for existing ViT-based training setups. The main modifications are limited to the patchification layer and the management of embedding parameters.

This combined methodology enables FlexiViT to learn a unified set of weights that perform well across diverse patch sizes, effectively decoupling the model parametrization from the patch size used at inference.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research paper presents a comprehensive evaluation of FlexiViT, highlighting its efficacy across various tasks and settings:

1. **Inherent Flexibility:** FlexiViT models (FlexiViT-S, -B, -L) demonstrate robust performance across a wide range of patch sizes (e.g., 8x8 to 48x48), matching or even outperforming standard ViT models trained specifically at a single patch size (Figure 2, 3). This effectively eliminates the need for retraining for different speed/accuracy trade-offs.

2. **Effectiveness of PI-resize:** The proposed PI-resize method for patch embedding weights is shown to be superior to other heuristics (e.g., vanilla bilinear resize, normalization-based methods) in maintaining performance when adapting a pre-trained model to different patch sizes, especially when upscaling (Figure 4). It ensures a more principled preservation of embedding information.

3. **Benefits of Knowledge Distillation with Teacher Initialization:** Initializing the FlexiViT student model with weights from a powerful ViT teacher, followed by distillation, significantly boosts performance, particularly for larger patch sizes. This approach leads to higher accuracy and better alignment with the teacher's output compared to random initialization or supervised training alone (Figure 5).

4. **Consistent Internal Representations:** Analysis using Centered Kernel Alignment (CKA) reveals that FlexiViT learns internal representations that are largely similar across different patch sizes, especially in deeper layers and for CLS tokens (Figure 6). This suggests that the model develops a shared understanding of visual features regardless of patch granularity, contributing to its flexibility.

5. **Strong Performance on Downstream Tasks:** When used as a backbone, a single pre-trained FlexiViT model performs comparably to, and often slightly better than, fixed-patch ViTs across a diverse set of downstream computer vision tasks. These include various image classification benchmarks (SUN397, Food101, etc.), image-text retrieval (LiT), open-world object detection (OWL-ViT), panoptic segmentation (UViM), and semantic segmentation (Segmenter) (Figure 7, 18). This validates FlexiViT's generalizability and utility beyond simple classification.

6. **Resource-Efficient Transfer Learning:** FlexiViT enables a novel transfer learning paradigm. Flexibility is largely retained even after fine-tuning at a *fixed* patch size. This means models can be fine-tuned cheaply with large patches (small sequence length) but then deployed with small patches (large sequence length) for significantly higher performance, achieving accuracy gains with no additional training cost (Figure 8, 19).

7. **Flexifying Existing Training Setups:** The randomization of patch sizes can also be effectively applied *during transfer learning* or multimodal training (e.g., FlexiLiT, FlexiCLIP). This "flexified transfer" of even initially inflexible models yields surprisingly good results, further enhancing adaptability (Figure 9, 10, 20, 21).

8. **Accelerated Pre-training:** A patch size curriculum, where larger patches are used initially and gradually transition to smaller ones, can accelerate the pre-training of fixed-size ViTs, achieving similar accuracy with reduced computational budget (Figure 12).

9. **Attention and Token Analysis:** Attention relevance patterns are observed to change with patch size, concentrating into more numerous, smaller areas for finer granularities. However, token representations show clear correspondences across scales, indicating the model's ability to relate visual information across different patch resolutions (Figure 13, 24, 28, 32).

10. **Superiority over Alternatives:** FlexiViT's approach of varying patch size offers a more favorable compute-accuracy trade-off compared to alternative methods of introducing flexibility, such as varying patch embedding stride or model depth (Figure 14).

In summary, the findings consistently demonstrate that FlexiViT successfully achieves its objective of creating a single, highly adaptable ViT model that performs robustly across diverse patch sizes and tasks, often outperforming its fixed-patch counterparts.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

FlexiViT represents a significant advancement in the practical deployment and efficient training of Vision Transformers, with several profound implications for the field of computer vision and beyond:

1. **Unlocking Compute-Adaptive AI:** The most direct impact of FlexiViT is enabling a single ViT model to dynamically adjust its performance and computational cost based on real-time deployment needs. This is crucial for applications requiring flexible resource allocation, such as edge devices, mobile platforms, or cloud environments with variable latency and throughput demands. Instead of needing multiple specialized models, one FlexiViT can serve diverse scenarios.

2. **Substantial Cost Reduction in Development and Deployment:** By eliminating the necessity to retrain distinct models for each desired patch size, FlexiViT drastically reduces the computational resources (GPU/TPU hours), time, and engineering effort associated with ViT development and deployment. This cost efficiency democratizes access to powerful ViT models, making them more accessible for researchers and organizations with limited compute budgets.

3. **Simplified Model Management:** A single, flexible backbone simplifies the entire model lifecycle, from training and version control to deployment and maintenance. This reduces complexity and potential for errors compared to managing a suite of fixed-size models.

4. **Enhanced Transfer Learning Paradigms:** FlexiViT introduces novel and more efficient strategies for transfer learning. The ability to fine-tune models cheaply with large patch sizes (faster training) and then deploy them at smaller patch sizes (higher accuracy) without further training is a powerful new capability. This can accelerate research cycles and improve the performance of downstream tasks.

5. **"Drop-in" Improvement for Existing ViT Architectures:** The minimal architectural changes required for FlexiViT mean it can be easily integrated into existing ViT-based workflows and build upon current and future ViT advancements. This high compatibility ensures that the benefits of FlexiViT can be widely adopted without disrupting established practices.

6. **Foundation for Future Research:** The success of FlexiViT in making patch size a flexible parameter opens up new avenues for exploration. Future work could investigate combining FlexiViT with other efficiency techniques (e.g., token pruning), extending flexibility to other architectural dimensions (though initial explorations in depth/stride were less promising), or exploring its impact on tasks beyond those already studied. It also inspires deeper analysis into how neural networks learn robust representations across varying input granularities.

In conclusion, FlexiViT is not just an incremental improvement but a foundational one that makes Vision Transformers inherently more adaptable, cost-effective, and practical for real-world applications. Its simplicity and effectiveness are likely to influence how ViT models are designed, trained, and deployed moving forward.
