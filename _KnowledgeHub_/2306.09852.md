# Actor-Critic Model Predictive Control: Differentiable Optimization meets Reinforcement Learning for Agile Flight

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at the University of Zurich developed Actor-Critic Model Predictive Control (AC-MPC), a framework integrating a differentiable MPC solver into an actor-critic reinforcement learning architecture, improving sample efficiency and robustness for agile quadrotor flight. The system successfully transfers from simulation to real-world, achieving high-speed drone racing performance comparable to pure model-free methods while better handling disturbances and dynamic changes.

## Problem

- Model-free Reinforcement Learning (RL) suffers from high sample inefficiency, lacks robustness to out-of-distribution scenarios, and provides few safety guarantees.
- Traditional Model Predictive Control (MPC) requires extensive manual engineering of cost functions and parameters, leading to conservative assumptions and suboptimal performance at extreme limits.
- Combining the strengths of RL and MPC while mitigating their individual limitations for agile, robust, and generalizable flight in dynamic environments remains a challenge.

## Method

- Introduced **Actor-Critic** ==Model Predictive Control== (AC-MPC), embedding a differentiable MPC solver as the actor policy within a Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) reinforcement learning framework.
- A "Neural Cost Map" learns the quadratic cost function coefficients for the MPC solver, providing explicit prior knowledge of quadrotor dynamics and control constraints.
- Proposed Model Predictive Value Expansion (MPVE) to enhance critic training and sample efficiency by reusing short-horizon state-action predictions from the differentiable MPC.

## Results

- AC-MPC demonstrated superior robustness to strong wind gusts (**83.33%** success rate vs. **6.5%** for AC-MLP) and out-of-distribution initial conditions (**90.37%** vs. **74.78%** SR for AC-MLP).
- The framework exhibited high robustness to significant variations in quadrotor mass (up to **+27%**), inertia (up to 17%**80%**), and body rate limits (up to 17%**40%**) without requiring retraining.
- Achieved successful zero-shot sim-to-real transfer on complex drone racing tracks, matching state-of-the-art model-free RL performance at speeds up to 21 m/s while maintaining predictive properties.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Constraining the MPC's quadratic cost matrix (Q) to be diagonal was empirically crucial for successful learning, outperforming more complex matrix representations.
> - The critic's learned value function strongly correlates with the learned MPC cost terms, suggesting the critic effectively learns an approximation of the infinite-horizon cost-to-go, which informs local MPC optimization.
> - Embedding prior knowledge through the differentiable MPC significantly reduces the system's sensitivity to exploration hyperparameters, allowing AC-MPC to learn effectively with less exploration compared to pure model-free RL.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper addresses a fundamental challenge in robotic control: combining the strengths of model-free Reinforcement Learning (RL) and Model Predictive Control (MPC).
**Model-based control (e.g., MPC)** has excelled in robotics for its reliability, online replanning capabilities, and ability to handle complex dynamics, as seen in industrial processes and legged robots. For agile drone flight, MPC has achieved notable successes, enabling decisions that optimize future states over a short horizon. However, traditional MPC requires extensive manual engineering of cost functions, parameter tuning, and planning strategies for each task, often leading to conservative assumptions and suboptimal performance when systems are pushed to their limits. Its modular structure can also lead to error accumulation.

In contrast, **data-driven and model-free RL approaches** have recently shown remarkable success, achieving "superhuman" performance in tasks like drone racing, Go, and Starcraft II. RL can directly optimize task-level objectives, handle sparse rewards, and learn complex feedback policies without explicit intermediate representations or handcrafted cost terms, making it flexible and scalable. However, model-free RL suffers from significant drawbacks: it is data-intensive (requiring millions of interactions), lacks generalizability and robustness to out-of-distribution (OOD) scenarios, struggles with sparse rewards in complex settings, and provides few safety guarantees, making its application in safety-critical domains challenging.

This paper positions itself as a **bridge between these two paradigms**, aiming to leverage their complementary strengths. The concept of combining MPC and learning is not new; previous works have explored learning cost functions, dynamics models,–, or both,– for MPC. Additionally, approaches leveraging differentiability through optimizers have gained traction for tuning controllers, planning, and control pipelines. Specifically, differentiable MPC,– allows learning cost or dynamics end-to-end, but prior works primarily demonstrated this in *imitation learning* contexts, which are limited by reliance on expert demonstrations.

The current work distinguishes itself by integrating a *differentiable MPC with model-free reinforcement learning*—specifically, an actor-critic framework. It extends the authors' prior work by introducing Model Predictive Value Expansion (MPVE), analyzing the relationship between the critic's value function and MPC cost, and conducting more extensive ablation studies and real-world validations. By doing so, it tackles the limitations of both pure RL and MPC, aiming for a system that combines the robustness and prior knowledge benefits of model-based control with the flexibility and learning capabilities of RL, ultimately advancing robust control for agile robotics.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to introduce and rigorously evaluate **Actor-Critic Model Predictive Control (AC-MPC)**, a novel framework that integrates a differentiable Model Predictive Control (MPC) module within an actor-critic Reinforcement Learning (RL) architecture.

The core motivations behind AC-MPC stem from the limitations of both pure model-free RL and traditional model-based MPC in achieving agile, robust, and generalizable flight in dynamic and uncertain environments, specifically for challenging tasks like quadrotor racing:

1. **Leverage Complementary Strengths:** Combine the flexibility and task-level generality of model-free RL (for end-to-end learning and long-term exploration) with the structure, online replanning capabilities, and predictive nature of MPC (for short-term optimal control and feasibility guarantees).
2. **Address RL Limitations:** Overcome model-free RL's shortcomings, such as high sample inefficiency (requiring millions of interactions), lack of robustness to out-of-distribution (OOD) scenarios, poor generalizability, and the absence of explicit prior knowledge (like physics or dynamic models).
3. **Mitigate MPC Limitations:** circumvent the need for extensive manual engineering of cost functions and parameters, conservative assumptions leading to sub-optimal performance in extreme conditions, and the cascading error accumulation inherent in hierarchical model-based approaches. AC-MPC aims to directly optimize non-differentiable task-level objectives without relying on proxy objectives or reference trajectories.
4. **Enhance Robustness and Generalizability:** Develop a control system that performs reliably even when the quadrotor's dynamics change or when encountering unforeseen external disturbances, without requiring retraining.
5. **Improve Sample Efficiency:** Accelerate the learning process by embedding prior knowledge (system dynamics) directly into the controller's architecture.
6. **Increase Interpretability:** Provide a deeper understanding of the interplay between the learned value function of the RL critic and the cost function of the MPC, shedding light on how these two components cooperate.
7. **Achieve Real-world Superhuman Performance:** Validate the framework in demanding real-world drone racing tasks, matching or exceeding the performance of state-of-the-art model-free RL while retaining MPC's predictive properties.

Beyond the initial AC-MPC architecture presented in, this paper specifically extends the work by:
- Introducing **Model Predictive Value Expansion (MPVE)** to improve the critic's learning and sample efficiency by reusing MPC's short-horizon predictions.
- Conducting a systematic analysis of the **relationship between the critic’s learned value function and the MPC's learned cost function**, revealing a concrete connection.
- Performing a detailed **exploration analysis** to show how AC-MPC leverages prior knowledge for better training performance.
- Presenting a **broad set of ablations and comparisons** to demonstrate robustness to dynamic parameter changes, analyze different matrix representations for the cost map, and compare against adaptive robust controllers like L1-MPC.
- Providing **extended real-world validation** on complex drone racing tracks, showcasing zero-shot sim-to-real transfer and superhuman speeds.
- **Open-sourcing the AC-MPC module** to facilitate further research and adoption.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core methodology of this work is the **Actor-Critic Model Predictive Control (AC-MPC)** framework, which integrates a differentiable Model Predictive Control (MPC) solver into the actor policy of an actor-critic Reinforcement Learning (RL) algorithm (specifically, Proximal Policy Optimization, PPO).

1. **Differentiable MPC Integration:**
 - **Actor Policy:** Unlike traditional actor-critic setups where the actor is a simple neural network (e.g., MLP), in AC-MPC, the actor's final module is a differentiable MPC solver. This means the control commands for the environment are generated by solving an MPC problem at each time step.
 - **Prior Knowledge:** The differentiable MPC explicitly incorporates the quadrotor's dynamics model and control constraints, providing the agent with valuable prior knowledge even before any training data is collected. This ensures that generated actions are inherently feasible and adhere to system limits.
 - **Quadratic Program (QP) Formulation:** The paper proposes to directly learn the matrix coefficients (Q_k and p_k) of a general quadratic cost function (J_QP) for the MPC problem, instead of hand-crafting complex non-linear costs or relying on reference trajectories.

2. **Neural Cost Map:**
 - **Learning the Cost Function:** A deep neural network, termed the "Cost Map," is introduced as a component of the actor. This network takes observations as input and outputs the Q_k and p_k terms for the MPC's quadratic cost function.
 - **Structure:** The Cost Map consists of two hidden layers with ReLU activations and a sigmoid non-linearity at the output layer to bound the learned cost coefficients (e.g., Q and p values between 0.1 and 100000.0), ensuring positive semi-definiteness for Q and positive definiteness for R.
 - **Dimensionality Reduction:** For tractability, the Q matrix is constrained to be diagonal, significantly reducing the number of learnable parameters. The paper empirically validates this choice, showing that more complex representations (Cholesky, Full Matrix) lead to learning failures.
 - **Differentiability:** The differentiable MPC allows gradients from the RL objective to be propagated *through* the MPC solver and back to the neural cost map, enabling end-to-end learning of the cost function parameters.

3. **Actor-Critic RL Framework (PPO):**
 - **Long-term Reasoning:** The critic network learns a state-value function V(s) to estimate the expected future rewards, providing long-term reasoning capabilities, while the MPC focuses on short-term optimal actions.
 - **Exploration:** During training, control inputs are sampled from a Gaussian distribution whose mean is the output of the MPC block. The PPO algorithm manages the variance for exploration. During deployment, the direct output of the deterministic MPC is used.
 - **Task-level Objectives:** RL allows AC-MPC to optimize sparse, non-differentiable reward functions (e.g., minimizing crash, maximizing lap times), which are difficult for traditional MPC.

4. **Model-Predictive Value Expansion (MPVE):**
 - **Enhanced Critic Training:** This extension re-uses the short-horizon state-action predictions (sequences of states and actions) generated by the differentiable MPC during its forward pass.
 - **TD k-trick:** To mitigate distribution mismatch between states encountered during training and predicted states, MPVE incorporates the TD k-trick, training the value function on k-step returns derived from MPC predictions. This improves the critic's accuracy and sample efficiency, especially for lower TD(λ) values.

5. **Experimental Setup:**
 - **Platform:** Quadrotor system in both simulation (simple and high-fidelity BEM simulator) and real-world experiments.
 - **Tasks:** Agile drone racing through gates (horizontal, vertical, circular, SplitS tracks). SplitS track is particularly challenging due to its complex maneuver.
 - **Observations:** Quadrotor's linear velocity, rotation matrix, and relative positions of the four corners of the next two target gates.
 - **Action Space:** 4-dimensional vector of mass-normalized collective thrust and body rates ([c, ωx, ωy, ωz]), a choice previously shown effective for agile flight.
 - **Reward Function:** Sparse, task-oriented rewards: collision penalty, gate passing bonus, race finish bonus, and a penalty on body rates to encourage smooth flight.
 - **Sim-to-Real Transfer:** Utilizes techniques from, including domain randomization (thrust map, mass, latency, drag, initial state) and control action smoothing for robust real-world deployment.
 - **Comparisons:** Evaluated against vanilla PPO (AC-MLP), a standard tracking MPC, and an adaptive L1-MPC controller.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The paper presents a comprehensive set of experiments and analyses, highlighting the benefits and characteristics of the AC-MPC framework:

1. **Improved Performance and Sample Efficiency:**
 - AC-MPC achieved better asymptotic training performance and significantly improved sample efficiency compared to the vanilla actor-critic PPO (AC-MLP), especially in challenging tasks like Vertical and SplitS tracks (Figure 5).
 - The embedded MPC module, with its explicit knowledge of platform dynamics, enabled AC-MPC to consistently utilize control input saturation limits, leading to more effective and consistent control actions than AC-MLP (Figure 3).
 - The value function's rapid shift to guide the drone towards the next gate after passing one demonstrates a form of discrete mode switching, a challenging behavior for traditional MPC.

2. **Impact of Cost Matrix Parameterization:**
 - Empirical analysis demonstrated that constraining the quadratic cost matrix (Q) to be **diagonal** was crucial for learning. Both Cholesky and Full Matrix representations, despite being theoretically more expressive, failed to learn the required behavior within the same sample budget (Figure 4). This highlights the tractability benefits of the diagonal constraint in high-dimensional learning problems.

3. **Robustness to External Disturbances:**
 - AC-MPC exhibited superior robustness to strong wind gusts (1.5x platform weight) in the Vertical track compared to AC-MLP, standard tracking MPC, and even an adaptive L1-MPC controller. AC-MPC achieved an 83.33% success rate at 10.76 m/s, significantly outperforming AC-MLP (6.5% SR) and L1-MPC (41.66% SR) (Figure 6A, Table I).
 - It also showed higher success rates in handling out-of-distribution initial conditions (3m cube randomization) in the Horizontal track compared to AC-MLP (90.37% vs. 74.78% SR) (Figure 6B, Table I).

4. **Robustness to Changes in Dynamics:**
 - AC-MPC proved significantly more robust to variations in quadrotor mass (up to +27%), XY inertia (up to ±80%), and body rate limits (up to ±40%) without retraining, maintaining high success rates (Figure 7). This robustness is directly attributed to the differentiable MPC's embedded dynamic model, which allows for adaptive control in response to perturbed dynamics.

5. **Benefits of Model-Predictive Value Expansion (MPVE):**
 - MPVE, which re-uses MPC predictions for critic training, substantially improved sample efficiency, particularly for lower values of the temporal difference parameter λ (Figure 8, Figure 9). This indicates that MPVE is most advantageous when the critic relies more on immediate rewards (TD(0)-like updates), efficiently injecting predictive information from the model.

6. **Interpretability of Internal Workings:**
 - A systematic analysis revealed a strong empirical correlation between the Hessian and gradient of the critic's learned value function V(x) and the learned MPC cost terms (Q_N, p_N) (Figure 10). A linear probe could predict Q_N and p_N with high accuracy from the Hessian and gradient of V(x). This suggests that the critic effectively learns an approximation of the infinite-horizon cost-to-go, which directly informs the MPC's local quadratic cost landscape.

7. **Sensitivity to Exploration Hyperparameters:**
 - AC-MPC was found to be less sensitive to the initial policy standard deviation (exploration parameter) and performed well even with very low exploration noise (Figure 11). This demonstrates its ability to leverage prior dynamic knowledge, requiring less exploration compared to AC-MLP, which needs higher exploration to find optimal solutions.

8. **Real-World Deployment and Sim-to-Real Transfer:**
 - AC-MPC achieved successful zero-shot sim-to-real transfer on a high-performance racing drone, navigating complex tracks (Circular, SplitS) in the real world at speeds up to 21 m/s (Figure 13).
 - It demonstrated comparable lap times and success rates to state-of-the-art model-free RL (AC-MLP) in both high-fidelity simulation and real-world drone racing (Table IV). This indicates that the benefits of AC-MPC do not come at the expense of peak performance.

9. **Computational Cost:**
 - AC-MPC has a higher computational cost for both training (factor of ~30 for N=2) and inference due to the batched optimization required by the differentiable MPC (Table V). However, the inference time (13.5ms for N=2) was well within the 20ms control loop budget for real-world deployment. The authors suggest that optimized C/C++ implementations could further reduce these times.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work presents a significant advancement in autonomous control, particularly for agile robotic systems operating in dynamic and uncertain environments. The introduction of **Actor-Critic Model Predictive Control (AC-MPC)** offers a robust framework that successfully bridges the gap between model-free reinforcement learning (RL) and model predictive control (MPC), leveraging their complementary strengths while mitigating their individual limitations.

The **significance** of this research is multi-faceted:

1. **Hybrid Control Paradigm:** AC-MPC demonstrates a powerful fusion of learning-centric and model-based control. By embedding a differentiable MPC within an actor-critic RL framework, the system gains the flexibility and task-level generality of RL for long-term learning and exploration, coupled with the structure, online replanning, and feasibility guarantees of MPC for short-term optimal control.
2. **Enhanced Robustness and Generalizability:** The empirical results conclusively show that AC-MPC surpasses pure model-free RL (AC-MLP) in terms of robustness to external disturbances and adaptability to changes in system dynamics. This is a critical factor for real-world deployment, especially in safety-critical applications where learned policies often struggle with out-of-distribution scenarios.
3. **Improved Sample Efficiency:** The introduction of Model Predictive Value Expansion (MPVE) significantly boosts the sample efficiency of the critic's learning, particularly in scenarios where immediate reward feedback is crucial. This helps alleviate one of the major bottlenecks in scaling RL to complex tasks.
4. **Interpretability and Understanding:** The discovery of a strong correlation between the Hessian and gradient of the critic's learned value function and the learned MPC cost parameters provides valuable insights into the internal workings of such hybrid controllers. This interpretability fosters a deeper understanding of how RL learns to inform and shape model-based optimization, opening avenues for more principled design.
5. **Achieving Superhuman Performance in Real-World:** The successful zero-shot sim-to-real transfer and demonstration of superhuman performance in agile drone racing (up to 21 m/s) on par with state-of-the-art model-free RL validates the practical applicability and effectiveness of AC-MPC in a highly challenging benchmark. This showcases that the gains in robustness do not come at the cost of peak performance.
6. **Open-Source Contribution:** Releasing the AC-MPC module's source code is a commendable contribution to the research community, lowering the barrier for others to build upon and extend this promising architecture.

The **potential impact** of this work is substantial and could extend beyond agile quadrotor flight:

1. **Advancement in Real-World Robotics:** AC-MPC paves the way for the development of more robust, reliable, and efficient AI-driven control systems for a wider range of robotic applications, particularly those requiring high-performance, adaptive control in uncertain environments. This includes industrial automation, autonomous driving, and complex manipulation tasks.
2. **Safer Learned Systems:** By inherently incorporating physical models and constraints, AC-MPC offers a step towards building safer learned controllers, addressing a major concern in deploying RL in safety-critical domains.
3. **Motivation for Differentiable Solvers:** The work highlights the practical value of further research into differentiable MPC solvers, particularly those that can handle state constraints and scale efficiently to high-dimensional systems, which are currently limitations.
4. **Inspiration for Hybrid Architectures:** This research serves as a strong testament to the power of modular solutions that strategically combine model-based reasoning with data-driven learning. It encourages the robotics and AI communities to explore more sophisticated hybrid architectures that exploit prior knowledge more effectively.
5. **Broader AI for Robotics:** By addressing core challenges in generalizability, robustness, and sample efficiency, AC-MPC contributes positively towards the broader goal of advancing AI for real-world robotics, making autonomous systems more capable and trustworthy.

While the computational cost remains a consideration and current limitations exist regarding state constraints and scalability to very high-dimensional systems, this paper represents a significant step towards developing more intelligent and resilient robotic control solutions that can learn complex behaviors while adhering to physical principles.
