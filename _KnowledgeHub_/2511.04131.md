# BFM-Zero: A Promptable Behavioral Foundation Model for Humanoid Control Using Unsupervised Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> BFM-Zero introduces a promptable behavioral foundation model for humanoid control, utilizing unsupervised reinforcement learning to train a generalist policy capable of robust sim-to-real transfer. This model successfully deploys zero-shot on a real Unitree G1 robot, demonstrating natural recovery from severe disturbances and achieving efficient few-shot adaptation for complex tasks like balancing a 4kg payload.

## Problem

- Humanoid whole-body control policies were traditionally task-specific, lacking versatility and requiring retraining for new tasks.
- Existing methods struggled with efficient adaptation or composition for novel tasks, and often lacked a unified, explainable interface.
- A scarcity of large-scale actuator-level action labels for humanoids made direct behavior cloning challenging for generalist policy learning.

## Method

- The **BFM-Zero** framework employs an online off-policy unsupervised ==reinforcement learning== approach, extending the Forward-Backward Contrastive Policy Representation (FB-CPR) algorithm.
- It integrates asymmetric training, massive parallel environments, domain randomization, and auxiliary reward regularization to enable robust sim-to-real transfer.
- The system learns a structured, objective-centric latent space for whole-body motions, allowing for promptable control via various inputs like motions, goals, or rewards.

## Results

- BFM-Zero achieved zero-shot deployment on a real Unitree G1 humanoid, performing diverse tasks like motion tracking, goal reaching, and reward optimization.
- The robot demonstrated strong robustness to severe physical disturbances (e.g., kicks, pushes) and exhibited natural, human-like recovery behaviors.
- Few-shot adaptation enabled the robot to maintain single-leg balance for over 15 seconds with a 4kg payload, a task where the zero-shot policy destabilized within 5 seconds.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Off-policy unsupervised reinforcement learning can be successfully adapted to bridge the sim-to-real gap and manage dynamic disturbances for real-world humanoid control.
> - A well-structured and smooth latent space for behaviors allows for versatile zero-shot inference, efficient few-shot adaptation, and meaningful interpolation of skills.
> - Combining unsupervised skill learning with techniques like asymmetric training and extensive domain randomization is crucial for developing robust generalist policies for physical robots.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of "foundation models" has revolutionized fields like natural language processing and computer vision, and the robotics community is actively exploring their potential. This paper positions "Behavioral Foundation Models (BFMs)" as a key paradigm for humanoid robots, aiming to create a single, promptable generalist policy capable of unifying diverse control tasks.

Traditionally, humanoid whole-body control has relied heavily on physics-based simulations and Reinforcement Learning (RL) for training policies before transferring them to hardware. However, these methods, often using on-policy policy gradient algorithms like PPO, have several limitations:
1. **Task Specificity:** Policies are typically trained for a single task (e.g., motion tracking, specific gaits), lacking versatility.
2. **Non-Adaptiveness:** Trained policies are difficult to fine-tune or compose for novel tasks.
3. **Lack of Unified Interface:** Specifying goals or composing behaviors often lacks a clear, explainable interface.

Current efforts in robotics foundation models, particularly Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, have shown promise in robotic manipulation by leveraging human demonstrations (behavior cloning). However, the authors correctly identify a fundamental mismatch for humanoid whole-body control: a scarcity of large-scale actuator-level action labels or teleoperation datasets, making direct behavior cloning challenging.

BFM-Zero addresses these gaps by proposing an *off-policy unsupervised RL* approach. This is a significant departure from the prevalent on-policy methods in real-world humanoid control. The paper builds upon recent advancements in unsupervised RL and Forward-Backward (FB) models, specifically extending the FB-CPR algorithm. Unlike prior works that either operate exclusively in simulation or are task-specialized (e.g., tracking), BFM-Zero aims for real-world deployment of a generalist policy. Recent works (mostly from 2025 as cited in the paper, indicating a rapidly evolving field) have started to explore multi-task and generalist humanoid control, often involving two-stage training (e.g., tracking policies distilled into multi-task policies). BFM-Zero distinguishes itself by directly learning a promptable multi-task policy using off-policy RL, promising a richer and more structured skill space not limited by the quality of a specific motion dataset. It represents a "first-of-its-kind" model for real humanoids, demonstrating that unsupervised RL can indeed bridge the sim-to-real gap and handle dynamic disturbances for robust real-world deployment.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop and validate **BFM-Zero**, a framework for learning a promptable Behavioral Foundation Model for real-world humanoid control using unsupervised reinforcement learning. This overarching goal is driven by several specific motivations:

- **Unify Diverse Control Tasks:** To move beyond task-specific policies, enabling a single, generalist policy to perform a wide array of whole-body control tasks (motion tracking, goal reaching, reward optimization, etc.) without requiring retraining for each new task. This aims for a "promptable" interface where tasks can be specified via various inputs (motions, goals, rewards).
- **Enable Zero-shot and Few-shot Adaptation:** To allow the humanoid robot to execute new tasks immediately (zero-shot inference) or adapt efficiently with minimal additional interaction (few-shot adaptation), thereby increasing the robot's versatility and reducing deployment costs.
- **Address Sim-to-Real Gap Challenges for Unsupervised RL:** To investigate and prove that off-policy unsupervised RL, a technique commonly used for multi-task policies in simulation, can be successfully adapted to handle the complexities of the sim-to-real gap and robustly manage dynamic disturbances in real-world humanoid deployment—a proposition previously lacking evidence.
- **Leverage Unsupervised RL for Skill Learning:** To exploit the benefits of unsupervised RL (specifically Forward-Backward models) in learning a well-structured, objective-centric, explainable, and smooth latent representation of whole-body motions. This structured latent space is crucial for versatility and interpolation of skills.
- **Improve Robustness and Naturalness:** To develop policies that can naturally recover from perturbations and exhibit human-like compliance and resilience, rather than rigid, fiercely reactive behaviors often seen in traditional control.
- **Scalability for Humanoid Control:** To establish a scalable approach for training generalist policies, drawing inspiration from advances in large-batch off-policy RL.

In essence, the motivation is to unlock the full potential of humanoid robots by endowing them with general intelligence and adaptability, much like foundation models have done for other AI domains, specifically focusing on the challenging domain of whole-body physical interaction.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

BFM-Zero proposes an online off-policy unsupervised reinforcement learning framework, built upon the concept of Forward-Backward (FB) representations. The methodology is divided into three main stages: unsupervised pre-training, zero-shot inference, and few-shot adaptation.

**Core Unsupervised RL Framework (Forward-Backward Representations):**
BFM-Zero extends FB-CPR (Forward-Backward Contrastive Policy Representation) [Tirinzoni et al., 2025], which itself combines the FB method for zero-shot RL [Touati and Ollivier, 2021] with online training and policy regularization using motion-capture data. This framework learns:
- **Latent Task Feature (φ):** Embeds observations (states) into a compact, low-dimensional vector.
- **Policy (πz):** A generalist policy conditioned on a latent vector `z`.
- **Latent-conditioned Successor Features (Fz):** Encodes the expected discounted sum of latent task features under the policy `πz`.
The FB framework learns forward (F) and backward (B) mappings that approximate long-term policy dynamics. Crucially, the learned representation `φ` defines a latent task space where a policy `πz` optimizes a linear combination of `φ` (i.e., `r_z = φ^T z`). This means the reward functions are not predefined but learned, representing a wide range of tasks. FB-CPR further introduces a latent-conditioned discriminator to regularize the learning process towards human-like demonstrated behaviors from a motion dataset.

**BFM-Zero Pre-training for Humanoid Control:**
The pre-training phase involves online interaction in a simulated environment. Key design choices are integrated to enable robust sim-to-real transfer:
- **Asymmetric Training:** The policy (actor) observes history-dependent proprioceptive states (`o_t,H`), while the critics (value functions) have access to privileged information (`o_t,H`, `s_t`, including full state). This enhances policy robustness under partial observability in the real world while using richer information for accurate value estimation during training.
- **Scaling to Massively Parallel Environments:** Inspired by FastTD3 [Seo et al., 2025], training is scaled across thousands of parallel environments with large replay buffers and high update-to-data ratios. This is critical for efficient and stable unsupervised training of a diverse family of policies for humanoid pre-training.
- **Domain Randomization (DR):** Key physical parameters (link masses, friction, joint offsets, COM) and environmental disturbances (perturbations, sensor noise) are randomized during training. This prevents overfitting to specific simulation dynamics and promotes robust policies for real hardware deployment.
- **Reward Regularization:** Auxiliary penalty rewards are incorporated to ensure learned behaviors adhere to safety and operational constraints of the physical robot (e.g., joint limits, action rates, self-contact, feet orientation, ankle roll, feet slip).

The training algorithm employs an off-policy actor-critic scheme:
- **Forward (F) and Backward (B) Map Training:** Minimize a temporal difference loss derived from the Bellman equation for successor measures, effectively learning the latent task space.
- **Auxiliary Critic (Q_R) Training:** Uses a standard Bellman residual loss to incorporate `N_aux` penalty rewards for safety and physical feasibility.
- **Discriminator Critic (Q_D) Training:** Leverages a GAN-style objective and variational Jensen-Shannon divergence to regularize towards human-like behaviors based on a motion dataset `M`. A style critic `Q_D` is fitted with a Bellman residual loss.
- **Actor (π) Loss:** The final actor loss combines the objective from the FB representation (`F(s, a, z)^T z`) with weighted terms from the discriminator critic (`Q_D`) and auxiliary critics (`Q_R`), encouraging policies that optimize desired latent tasks while being human-like and safe.

**Zero-shot Inference:**
At test time, the pre-trained BFM-Zero can solve tasks without additional learning:
- **Reward Optimization:** For a given reward function `r(s)`, the corresponding latent `z_r` is estimated by integrating `r(s)` with the `B(s)` mapping over a sample of states (`z_r = 1/N Σ r(s_i)B(s_i)`).
- **Goal Reaching:** For a target state `s_g`, the latent `z_g` is simply `B(s_g)`.
- **Motion Tracking:** For a motion trajectory `τ = {s_1..., s_n}`, a sequence of latents `z_t` is obtained by summing `B(s_t')` over a look-ahead horizon (`z_t = Σ B(s_t')`).

**Few-shot Adaptation:**
For tasks not fully covered by zero-shot inference, BFM-Zero allows for efficient adaptation by optimizing directly in the latent space `Z` using online interaction:
- **Single-Pose Adaptation:** Uses the Cross-Entropy Method (CEM) to refine an initial zero-shot latent `z_0` (from `B(s_g)`) to maximize a task-specific objective (e.g., single-leg balance with payload).
- **Trajectory-level Adaptation:** Employs zero-order, sampling-based trajectory optimization over a sequence of latent prompts (`z_t:t+H-1`) with a dual-loop annealing schedule (similar to DIAL-MPC) to reduce tracking error or stabilize challenging segments.

The architecture primarily utilizes residual neural networks for the actor and critics, incorporating layer normalization and Mish activation functions, while the discriminator and backward map are standard MLPs. The training is conducted in IsaacLab at 200 Hz, with a control frequency of 50 Hz, using the LAFAN1 dataset (retargeted to Unitree G1) for motion regularization.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

BFM-Zero demonstrates remarkable versatility, robustness, and adaptability both in simulation and on a real Unitree G1 humanoid robot.

#### 4.1. Zero-shot Validation in Simulation

- **Asymmetric Learning and Domain Randomization:** BFM-Zero trained with domain randomization (Sim DR) performed slightly worse than its privileged, non-randomized counterpart (BFM-Zero-priv) across tracking, reward, and pose reaching tasks (2.47% to 25.86% performance drop). This indicates that the algorithmic changes for sim-to-real were effective, maintaining satisfactory performance despite increased complexity. Reward tasks showed a larger drop, potentially due to their sparse nature and the brittleness of inference with randomized data.
- **Sim-to-Sim Performance:** The model exhibited strong robustness when tested in a different physics simulator (Mujoco vs. IsaacLab), with performance differences being less than 7%. This highlights the effectiveness of domain randomization and history-dependent components in promoting robustness and adaptivity.
- **Out-of-Distribution Tasks:** BFM-Zero successfully generalized to tracking and pose-reaching tasks from the AMASS dataset, which was not part of the training data. This demonstrates the model's ability to handle novel motions and poses, even when dynamics were also shifted (Mujoco).

#### 4.2. Zero-shot Validation on the Real Robot (Unitree G1)

BFM-Zero was successfully deployed zero-shot on a real Unitree G1, showcasing:
- **Tracking:** The robot could track various motions, including dynamic dances, walking, fighting, and sports. Crucially, it demonstrated natural and safe recovery from instability or falls, seamlessly continuing the motion. This "human-like" recovery is attributed to off-policy training, GAN-based reward (encouraging human-likeness), and regularization, enabling it to draw upon a rich skill library. It was also robust to lower-quality, occluded motion data from real videos.
- **Goal Reaching:** The robot consistently converged to natural poses that approximated target poses, even infeasible ones. Transitions between successive, discontinuous targets or from arbitrary poses to a T-pose were smooth and natural, validating the smoothness of the learned skill space.
- **Reward Optimization:** With simple reward definitions, the robot faithfully executed commands for locomotion (base velocities, angular velocities), arm movements (wrist height), and pelvis height (sitting, crouching). Composite skills (e.g., backward locomotion while raising arms) could be derived from linear combinations of rewards, showing controllable skill-level interpolability and an intuitive interface for human users. Different latent variables from averaged mini-batches also yielded diverse optimal behaviors for a given reward.
- **Disturbance Rejection:** The policy demonstrated strong compliance and robustness to severe disturbances (kicks, pushes, being dragged down). The robot recovered naturally and human-like, automatically adopting dynamic recovery poses and returning to the original task (e.g., T-pose tracking) without explicit re-planning or policy modification.

#### 4.3. Efficient Adaptation for BFM-Zero

- **Single-Pose Adaptation:** Few-shot adaptation in simulation, using CEM to refine the latent, allowed the robot to maintain single-leg balance for over 15 seconds while carrying a 4 kg payload in the real world, whereas the zero-shot policy destabilized within 5 seconds. This highlights that prompt-level optimization can compensate for significant dynamics shifts without fine-tuning model parameters.
- **Trajectory Adaptation:** Trajectory optimization using dual-annealing improved the tracking accuracy of a leaping motion under altered ground friction by approximately 29.1%, demonstrating effective reduction of tracking error through adaptation.

#### 4.4. The Latent Space Structure of BFM-Zero

- **Visualization:** t-SNE projections revealed a well-organized latent space where semantically similar trajectories clustered, demonstrating a shared task-centric structure.
- **Motion Interpolation:** Spherical Linear Interpolation between latent vectors generated semantically meaningful intermediate skills in a zero-shot manner on both simulated and real robots, showing the inherent smoothness and compositional capability of the learned skill space.

**Ablation Study (Additional Results):**
- **Data and Model Size:** Increasing model capacity generally improved tracking performance, with residual architectures outperforming simple MLPs. Training with the LAFAN1 dataset (even partially) was found important for reward inference. The model used in the main paper (`ResNet 6-block, 2048dim`) represented a strong configuration among tested architectures.
- **Booster T1 Application:** BFM-Zero's generality was further validated by its successful application on a Booster T1 humanoid robot, enabling natural walking and expressive dancing motions with the same hyperparameters as G1.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

BFM-Zero represents a significant leap forward in humanoid robotics, particularly by demonstrating the viability of unsupervised reinforcement learning for real-world whole-body control. Its "first-of-its-kind" status establishes a new benchmark and opens several avenues for future research and application.

**Key Significance:**
- **Paradigm Shift:** Successfully applies off-policy unsupervised RL, specifically an extension of FB-CPR, to real humanoid robots, challenging the conventional reliance on on-policy methods and task-specific learning. This offers a path towards more data-efficient and flexible training.
- **Generalist and Promptable Policy:** Creates a single policy capable of handling diverse tasks (tracking, goal-reaching, reward optimization) through a unified, promptable latent interface, significantly enhancing robot versatility and reducing the need for retraining or designing task-specific controllers.
- **Robustness and Naturalness:** Achieves unprecedented levels of robustness and natural recovery from severe perturbations on a physical robot. The human-like adaptation and compliance, rather than rigid reactions, are crucial for safe and intuitive human-robot interaction in unstructured environments.
- **Structured Latent Space:** The learned latent space is not just effective but also structured, smooth, and interpretable, enabling zero-shot inference, efficient few-shot adaptation, and even meaningful skill interpolation. This interpretability is vital for debugging, understanding, and controlling complex robot behaviors.
- **Reduced Sim-to-Real Gap:** The effective integration of asymmetric training, massive parallel environments, domain randomization, and reward regularization provides a powerful recipe for bridging the notorious sim-to-real gap, a critical barrier in physical robotics research.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Accelerated Humanoid Development:** By providing a foundation model, BFM-Zero could significantly accelerate the development and deployment of humanoid robots in various sectors, from manufacturing and logistics to healthcare and personal assistance, by making them more adaptable and easier to program for novel tasks.
- **Enhanced Human-Robot Interaction:** The natural and robust behaviors, combined with a promptable interface, could lead to more intuitive and safer interactions between humans and humanoid robots, fostering greater trust and integration in shared environments.
- **Foundation for Higher-Level Cognition:** A robust and versatile low-level behavioral foundation model can serve as a strong base for integrating higher-level planning, reasoning, and language understanding modules, moving closer to truly intelligent and autonomous humanoid agents.
- **Scalability and Efficiency:** The off-policy and unsupervised nature of the learning approach hints at scalability to larger datasets and more complex behaviors, potentially unlocking capabilities that were previously unattainable with task-specific, on-policy methods.

**Acknowledged Limitations (and future impact areas):**
The authors openly discuss limitations, which also highlight areas for future impact:
1. **Scope of Behaviors:** The performance is tied to the quality and quantity of training motion data. Investigating scaling laws for motion datasets, architectures, and performance is critical for future iterations.
2. **Online Adaptation:** While few-shot adaptation is demonstrated, better algorithms for online adaptation are needed for reliably expressing more complex and truly novel movements.
3. **Thorough Adaptation Understanding:** A deeper understanding of fast adaptation and fine-tuning mechanisms is necessary to broaden practical applicability.

Despite these limitations, BFM-Zero represents a monumental step towards truly generalist, promptable, and robust humanoid control, laying critical groundwork for the future of embodied AI.
