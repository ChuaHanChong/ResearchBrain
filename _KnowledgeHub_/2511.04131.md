# BFM-Zero: A Promptable Behavioral Foundation Model for Humanoid Control Using Unsupervised Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> BFM-Zero introduces a promptable behavioral foundation model for humanoid control, utilizing unsupervised reinforcement learning to train a generalist policy capable of robust sim-to-real transfer. This model successfully deploys zero-shot on a real Unitree G1 robot, demonstrating natural recovery from severe disturbances and achieving efficient few-shot adaptation for complex tasks like balancing a 4kg payload.

## Problem

- Humanoid whole-body control policies were traditionally task-specific, lacking versatility and requiring retraining for new tasks.
- Existing methods struggled with efficient adaptation or composition for novel tasks, and often lacked a unified, explainable interface.
- A scarcity of large-scale actuator-level action labels for humanoids made direct behavior cloning challenging for generalist policy learning.

## Method

- The **BFM-Zero** framework employs an online off-policy unsupervised ==reinforcement learning== approach, extending the Forward-Backward Contrastive Policy Representation (FB-CPR) algorithm.
- It integrates asymmetric training, massive parallel environments, domain randomization, and auxiliary reward regularization to enable robust sim-to-real transfer.
- The system learns a structured, objective-centric latent space for whole-body motions, allowing for promptable control via various inputs like motions, goals, or rewards.

## Results

- BFM-Zero achieved zero-shot deployment on a real Unitree G1 humanoid, performing diverse tasks like motion tracking, goal reaching, and reward optimization.
- The robot demonstrated strong robustness to severe physical disturbances (e.g., kicks, pushes) and exhibited natural, human-like recovery behaviors.
- Few-shot adaptation enabled the robot to maintain single-leg balance for over 15 seconds with a 4kg payload, a task where the zero-shot policy destabilized within 5 seconds.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Off-policy unsupervised reinforcement learning can be successfully adapted to bridge the sim-to-real gap and manage dynamic disturbances for real-world humanoid control.
> - A well-structured and smooth latent space for behaviors allows for versatile zero-shot inference, efficient few-shot adaptation, and meaningful interpolation of skills.
> - Combining unsupervised skill learning with techniques like asymmetric training and extensive domain randomization is crucial for developing robust generalist policies for physical robots.
