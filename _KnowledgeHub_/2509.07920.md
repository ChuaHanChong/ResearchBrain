# ScoreHOI: Physically Plausible Reconstruction of Human-Object Interaction via Score-Guided Diffusion

> [!summary] Summary
> ScoreHOI reconstructs 3D human-object interaction from a monocular image by treating a score-based diffusion model as an optimizer: an affordance-aware regressor gives initial SMPL-H and object pose, DDIM inversion plus score-guided sampling refines them under contact, floor and penetration gradients, and a contact-driven iterative refinement updates contact masks each step, yielding state-of-the-art accuracy and far faster inference.

## Problem

- Optimization-based interaction reconstruction lacks prior knowledge of plausible interaction patterns, is slow, and over-weights physical terms at the expense of image evidence.
- Regression-based methods predict human and object parameters in one forward pass, so they stay brittle under severe occlusion and monocular depth ambiguity.
- Diffusion priors were applied to human mesh recovery or interaction point-cloud generation, never to refining joint human plus object pose parameters under physics.

## Method

- ==Affordance-aware regressor== fuses image features with ==PointNeXt== geometry features from an object template to initialize SMPL-H body-hand pose, shape, and object rotation and translation.
- **ScoreHOI**'s ==score-guided DDIM==: initial estimate is ==DDIM inversion== to noise level tau, then denoised with the conditional score shifted by gradients of human-object contact, object-floor contact, and ==SDF penetration== losses.
- ==Contact-driven iterative refinement== (CDIR) re-predicts human, object, and floor contact masks each loop, while an **IG-Adapter** injects image and geometry conditions into the ==U-Net cross-attention==.

## Results

- On BEHAVE and InterCap the method leads most metrics, with about **9%** higher contact F-Score on BEHAVE plus best Chamfer distance for human and object meshes.
- Inference runs **one to two orders of magnitude** faster in FPS than optimization baselines such as CHORE and VisTracker, while still scoring higher.
- Cutting refinement to **two** iterations gives a **sevenfold** speedup at minimal accuracy loss; removing image or geometry conditioning degrades reconstruction in ablations.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A diffusion model can serve as an optimizer, not just a generator: learned priors supply the interaction knowledge that hand-written physical objectives alone cannot.
> - Contact targets should be re-estimated during refinement, since guidance computed from stale contact masks stops matching the geometry it is steering.
> - Physical terms split by role: contact losses drive recall of true interactions, penetration losses drive precision, so both are needed together.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The reconstruction of 3D human-object interaction (HOI) from monocular images or videos is a challenging yet critical problem in computer vision, with far-reaching applications in robotics, virtual/augmented reality (VR/AR), and game development. This field builds upon significant advancements in Human Mesh Recovery (HMR), which focuses on reconstructing human body mesh and pose. However, HOI reconstruction goes a step further by requiring an understanding and accurate modeling of the intricate spatial and physical relationships between humans and their surrounding objects.

Historically, existing HOI reconstruction methodologies can be broadly categorized into two main approaches:
1. **Optimization-based methods:** These approaches (e.g.,) typically start with an initial estimate and iteratively refine it by minimizing a loss function that incorporates physical objectives such as contact and collision constraints. While effective at enforcing physical realism, these methods often struggle with a lack of prior knowledge about diverse interaction patterns. They can become computationally intensive and, in some cases, overemphasize physical constraints to the detriment of image-level features, leading to reconstruction inaccuracies.
2. **Regression-based methods:** These techniques (e.g.,) directly predict human and object parameters, often employing contact-aware mechanisms or dual-branch networks. While generally faster, their single-step forward refinement process can lack robustness, particularly in challenging scenarios characterized by severe occlusion or depth ambiguity.

The emergence of **diffusion models** has significantly reshaped the landscape of generative AI. These models excel at learning complex data distributions and generating high-quality samples through a denoising process guided by the gradient of the data distribution (score). Their ability to capture rich implicit priors and facilitate controlled sampling has made them highly successful in tasks like text-to-image generation. More recently, diffusion models have been explored for 3D human mesh recovery (HMR) tasks, where they demonstrate efficacy in refining estimations with observational guidance. However, their application to the more complex domain of joint human-object interaction reconstruction, particularly for refining interaction *parameters* and ensuring physical plausibility, has remained largely unexplored. Some recent works, such as, have integrated diffusion models into HOI reconstruction but primarily focused on point cloud generation rather than directly refining human and object interaction parameters with rich physical priors.

ScoreHOI addresses this critical gap by leveraging diffusion models as a robust optimizer. It explicitly integrates the generative power and prior knowledge of diffusion models with specific physical constraints, offering a novel hybrid approach. This work positions itself as a significant advancement by overcoming the limitations of both traditional optimization-based (lack of prior knowledge, inefficiency) and regression-based (lack of robustness) methods, thereby enhancing the accuracy, physical plausibility, and computational efficiency of joint 3D HOI reconstruction.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of ScoreHOI is to achieve **physically plausible and accurate reconstruction of 3D human-object interactions** from a single monocular image. The authors identify and address two core challenges inherent in this task:

1. **Integrating human-object interaction prior knowledge into the optimization process:** Traditional optimization methods often operate without explicit high-level prior knowledge about how humans typically interact with objects, leading to suboptimal or implausible results. ScoreHOI aims to inject this rich implicit knowledge, learned by diffusion models, into the refinement process.
2. **Supervising the sampling procedure with plausible physical constraints:** While diffusion models offer powerful generative capabilities, their raw output might not inherently adhere to physical laws. The paper seeks to effectively guide the denoising (sampling) process with explicit physical constraints (e.g., contact, collision avoidance) to ensure the reconstructed interactions are realistic and physically sound.

The motivation for ScoreHOI stems from several critical observations:
- The inherent limitations of prior optimization-based methods, which are often computationally expensive and can produce physically implausible results due to a lack of comprehensive prior knowledge.
- The robustness issues of regression-based methods, especially in challenging real-world scenarios like occlusion or depth ambiguity, which lead to unreliable single-step refinements.
- The untapped potential of score-based diffusion models as robust optimizers capable of inferring implicit data distributions and leveraging guided sampling. This capability is particularly attractive for the ill-posed problem of 3D reconstruction from 2D inputs.
- The significant demand for high-fidelity 3D human-object interaction data in various application domains, including robotics (for learning dexterous manipulation), VR/AR (for immersive and realistic virtual environments), and game development (for dynamic character interactions). By improving reconstruction accuracy and physical plausibility, ScoreHOI directly supports these applications.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

ScoreHOI introduces a two-stage framework that combines an initial affordance-aware regression with a contact-driven iterative refinement process powered by a score-guided diffusion model.

#### 3.1. Affordance-Aware Regressor

The first stage involves generating an initial, coarse estimation of human and object parameters. Given an input RGB image, human and object segmentation masks, and a coarse object template, the system aims to estimate:
- Human body-hand pose (θ) and shape parameters (β) using the SMPL-H model.
- Object rotation (R_o) and translation (t_o).
To enhance generalization beyond predefined object shapes, the regressor incorporates an "affordance-aware" network (PointNeXt, pre-trained on ModelNet40). This network extracts geometric features and prior knowledge about how an object can be used, which is then combined with image features. Two separate heads process these features to produce the initial human and object parameters.

#### 3.2. Optimization with Physical Guidance (Contact-Driven Iterative Refinement - CDIR)

The core of ScoreHOI lies in its iterative refinement strategy, which uses a score-based diffusion model as a robust optimizer.
- **Parameter Representation:** The human and object parameters ({θ, β, R_o, t_o}) are concatenated into a single vector `x`, representing the optimization objective.
- **DDIM Inversion:** The initial estimate (`x_init`) from the regressor is inverted into a noisy latent representation (`x_τ`) using the DDIM (Denoising Diffusion Implicit Models) inversion process, where `τ` denotes the noise level. This allows the diffusion model to start its refinement from a "noised" version of the initial prediction.
- **Score-Guided DDIM Sampling:** The noisy latent `x_τ` is then denoised back to a refined `x_0` through a DDIM sampling loop. Crucially, this denoising process is *guided* by physical constraints.
 - **Conditional Score Modification:** Instead of relying solely on the diffusion model's inherent score (∇_x_t log p(x_t|c)), ScoreHOI incorporates physical objectives `P` by modifying the score based on Bayes' rule: ∇_x_t log p(x_t|c, P) ≈ ∇_x_t log p(x_t|c) + ∇_x_t log p(P|c, x_0(x_t)). This means the model's predicted noise is adjusted by the gradient of the physical loss, steering the reconstruction towards physically plausible states.
 - **Physical Objectives (L_P):** The physical guidance is composed of three main terms:
 - **Human-Object Contact (L_ho):** Enforces contact plausibility by minimizing the Euclidean distance between human and object meshes at predicted contact regions.
 - **Object-Floor Contact (L_of):** Ensures that parts of the object intended to be on the floor maintain zero height, reflecting ground contact.
 - **Penetration Avoidance (L_pt):** Prevents unreasonable overlaps or interpenetrations between the human and object meshes using a penalty based on the Signed Distance Function (SDF).
 - **Contact-Driven Iterative Refinement of Contact Masks:** A key innovation is the iterative updating of contact masks (M_h for human-object, M_o for object-human, M_f for object-floor) within the refinement loop. After each optimization iteration, a dedicated "contact predictor" updates these masks based on the current human and object meshes and features, ensuring that the physical guidance becomes increasingly accurate and relevant as the reconstruction improves.
- **Diffusion Model Architecture (IG-Adapter):** The diffusion model, typically a U-Net with cross-attention layers, is augmented with an "IG-Adapter." This adapter integrates two types of conditions to enhance controllability:
 - **Image Feature Condition (c_I):** Derived from average pooling of the extracted image features.
 - **Geometry Feature Condition (c_G):** Extracted from the pre-trained affordance-aware network, providing geometric prior knowledge.
 This dual conditioning imbues the model with both observational and geometric awareness, making the generation process more controlled and context-aware.
- **Final Refinement:** After `N` iterations of the DDIM loop, a dual-branch masked transformer further refines the output meshes, similar to techniques used in prior work.

#### 3.3. Training Details

The training is performed in two stages. First, the image backbone, contact predictor, and vertex refinement modules are trained on BEHAVE and InterCap datasets. Second, the diffusion model is trained using image features from the frozen backbone, additionally leveraging the IMHD dataset to enhance its generative capabilities. Adam optimizer is used, with data augmentation applied to both stages.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

ScoreHOI demonstrates significant improvements over existing state-of-the-art methods across various quantitative and qualitative metrics.

- **Superior Reconstruction Accuracy:**
 - **Quantitative Metrics:** On standard benchmarks like BEHAVE and InterCap, ScoreHOI achieves the best performance in most metrics, including Chamfer Distance for human (CD_human) and object (CD_object) meshes, and crucially, Contact Precision, Recall, and F-Score.
 - **Contact F-Score:** A notable achievement is a 9% improvement in the Contact F-Score on the BEHAVE benchmark, directly indicating superior reconstruction of contact interactions. This metric balances precision and recall, highlighting the model's ability to accurately identify and represent actual contact regions.
- **Enhanced Physical Plausibility:**
 - **Qualitative Results:** Visual comparisons (Figure 4) clearly show that ScoreHOI reconstructs human and object meshes with greater rationality and physical plausibility. It effectively addresses the "ill-posed problem" of 3D reconstruction from monocular images, particularly in challenging side-view perspectives where previous methods often struggle. The diffusion model's inherent prior knowledge helps in generating more consistent and realistic 3D poses and interactions.
- **Improved Inference Efficiency:**
 - ScoreHOI significantly surpasses the inference efficiency of previous optimization-based methods (e.g., CHORE, VisTracker). It achieves one to two orders of magnitude faster Frames Per Second (FPS) while simultaneously delivering superior performance.
 - A more expedited configuration (N=2 iterations in the iterative refinement loop) shows a substantial seven-fold increase in efficiency with only a minimal compromise in reconstruction performance, demonstrating practical applicability for real-time or near-real-time scenarios.
- **Comprehensive Ablation Studies:**
 - **Effectiveness of Optimizer Modules:** The diffusion sampling loop and the Contact-Driven Iterative Refinement (CDIR) are both crucial. The CDIR, particularly with its dynamic updating of contact masks, significantly contributes to improved reconstruction accuracy and contact quality.
 - **Effectiveness of Conditions:** Both the Image Guidance (c_I) and Geometry Guidance (c_G) through the IG-Adapter are essential. Their absence leads to a decrement in reconstruction performance, confirming their role in providing observational and geometric awareness for controlled generation.
 - **Effectiveness of Physical Guidance:** All three physical constraints (L_ho, L_of, L_pt) are vital for realistic interactions. L_ho (human-object contact) is critical for contact recall, ensuring more interactions are correctly identified. L_pt (penetration avoidance) is crucial for contact precision, preventing unrealistic overlaps. L_of (object-floor contact) ensures proper grounding of objects.
 - **Hyper-parameter Analysis:** Detailed studies on iteration times (N), intermediate noise level (τ), and DDIM step size (∆t) confirm that more refinement steps, a balanced initial noise level, and finer DDIM sampling steps generally lead to better contact F-Score, highlighting the system's sensitivity and tunability.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

ScoreHOI represents a significant step forward in the field of 3D human-object interaction reconstruction, offering several impactful contributions:

- **Novel Paradigmatic Shift:** The paper introduces a novel and highly effective paradigm for integrating score-based diffusion models as robust optimizers for a complex 3D reconstruction task. This extends the utility of diffusion models beyond pure generation, showcasing their power in iterative refinement guided by domain-specific constraints. This approach offers a powerful alternative to traditional optimization and regression methods.
- **Addressing Fundamental Limitations:** ScoreHOI successfully mitigates the key drawbacks of existing methods:
 - It overcomes the lack of prior knowledge in traditional optimization-based techniques by embedding rich generative priors from diffusion models.
 - It enhances the robustness of reconstruction, particularly in challenging visual conditions like occlusion, which regression-based methods struggle with, by leveraging the iterative, noise-resilient nature of diffusion.
 - It significantly improves computational efficiency compared to computationally intensive iterative optimization methods.
- **Enhanced Realism and Physical Plausibility:** By meticulously integrating a contact-driven iterative refinement strategy with multiple physical constraints (contact, penetration, object-floor interaction), ScoreHOI produces highly accurate and physically plausible human-object interactions. This level of realism is crucial for meaningful applications.
- **Broad Practical Applications:** The advancements offered by ScoreHOI have direct implications for several high-impact domains:
 - **Robotics:** Provides a robust method for generating high-quality 3D data of human-object interactions, which is essential for training robots in object manipulation, collaborative tasks, and understanding human intent.
 - **VR/AR and Game Development:** Enables the creation of more realistic and interactive virtual characters and environments, enhancing immersion and user experience.
 - **Digital Human Modeling:** Contributes to the development of more sophisticated digital human models capable of realistic interactions within virtual scenes.
 - **Synthetic Data Generation:** Can serve as a powerful tool for generating synthetic, physically consistent HOI datasets, which can be invaluable for training other vision models, especially when real-world data collection is challenging.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The work opens up exciting avenues for future research, particularly in tackling the limitation of generalizing to template-free or unseen objects with undefined canonical poses. Extending ScoreHOI to handle arbitrary objects would significantly broaden its applicability and impact. It also encourages further exploration of how generative priors can be effectively combined with physical constraints in other complex 3D perception tasks.

In conclusion, ScoreHOI not only pushes the boundaries of 3D human-object interaction reconstruction but also demonstrates the immense potential of diffusion models as versatile, powerful optimizers for complex, constraint-driven computer vision problems.
