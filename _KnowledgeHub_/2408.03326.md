# LLaVA-OneVision: Easy Visual Task Transfer

> [!summary] Summary
> LLaVA-OneVision is an open Large Multimodal Model that achieves state-of-the-art performance among open LMMs across single-image, multi-image, and video tasks. This model narrows the performance gap with proprietary models like GPT-4V/o and demonstrates robust cross-modal task transfer, enabling diverse emergent visual understanding capabilities.

## Problem

- A performance gap exists between proprietary state-of-the-art Large Multimodal Models (LMMs) and open-source alternatives, particularly in their versatility across single-image, multi-image, and video scenarios.
- Existing open-source LMMs often specialize in individual modalities, struggling to provide comprehensive multi-modal capabilities without compromising performance.
- There is a need for a single, unified open-source model capable of simultaneously pushing performance boundaries across diverse visual inputs and leveraging task transfer efficiently.

## Method

- A minimalist architecture is employed in **LLaVA-OneVision**, combining the ==Qwen-2 LLM==, ==SigLIP vision encoder==, and a simple 2-layer MLP projector.
- An enhanced ==AnyRes== strategy is introduced with bilinear interpolation and modality-specific token allocation to flexibly represent single-image, multi-image, and video inputs, ensuring balanced visual representations.
- A 'quality over quantity' data strategy is implemented, utilizing high-quality synthetic data for knowledge learning and curated mixed multimodal instruction tuning data (==OneVision Data==) for fine-tuning.

## Results

- LLaVA-OneVision models achieve state-of-the-art performance among open-source LMMs across single-image, multi-image, and video benchmarks.
- The **LLaVA-OneVision-72B** model consistently performs between GPT-4V and GPT-4o on most benchmarks, significantly surpassing GPT-4V on tasks like chart, diagram, document, and multi-image reasoning.
- The model demonstrates emerging capabilities through task transfer, including joint diagram and chart understanding, GUI interaction for agents, and visual prompting in video.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Strong image understanding can effectively transfer to enhance multi-image and video comprehension, demonstrating that capabilities learned in one modality can yield emergent behaviors in others.
> - Careful design of visual representations and the curation of high-quality, mixed multimodal instruction data are crucial for fostering cross-modal knowledge transfer and enabling novel emergent capabilities.
> - The 'cost-efficient recipe' of connecting pre-trained vision encoders with strong large language models, when combined with strategic data and training, can achieve state-of-the-art performance for open multimodal models.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Artificial Intelligence is increasingly moving towards building general-purpose AI assistants, with Large Multimodal Models (LMMs) at the forefront of this aspiration. LLaVA-OneVision directly contributes to this grand vision by extending the highly successful LLaVA research line, which began with LLaVA and evolved through LLaVA-1.5 and LLaVA-NeXT. The LLaVA series is known for its cost-efficient recipe, primarily involving connecting pre-trained vision encoders with large language models (LLMs) using simple connection modules, enabling impressive multimodal chat abilities.

Within the LMM landscape, a significant gap exists between proprietary state-of-the-art (SOTA) models—such as OpenAI's GPT-4V/o, Google's Gemini, and Anthropic's Claude-3.5—and open-source alternatives. While proprietary LMMs demonstrate remarkable versatility across diverse visual scenarios, including single-image, multi-image, and video, open-source efforts have often focused on optimizing performance for individual modalities. Many open models excel in single-image understanding, but few comprehensively address multi-image or video scenarios without compromising image performance.

LLaVA-OneVision directly aims to fill this gap. It represents a critical step towards creating a *single, open-source model* that not only achieves competitive SOTA performance across single-image, multi-image, and video tasks but also explicitly leverages *task transfer* capabilities. This "one vision" approach challenges the traditional paradigm of developing specialized models for each visual modality, instead proposing a unified framework where capabilities learned in one scenario can inherently benefit others. The paper's emphasis on consolidating insights from the extensive LLaVA-NeXT blog series underscores a systematic approach to identifying key factors (data, models, visual representations, training strategies) that drive LMM performance and generalization. By open-sourcing its assets, LLaVA-OneVision aims to accelerate collective progress towards truly general-purpose visual assistants for the broader AI community.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core objectives and motivations behind the development of LLaVA-OneVision are clearly articulated:

- **Primary Objective: Performance Boundaries Across Modalities:** The foremost goal is to develop a family of open Large Multimodal Models, LLaVA-OneVision, capable of simultaneously pushing the performance boundaries of open LMMs across three critical computer vision scenarios: single-image, multi-image, and video. This aims to match the versatility seen in proprietary SOTA models, which currently outperform open alternatives in multi-modal comprehensive understanding.

- **Key Objective: Emerging Capabilities with Task Transfer:** A central design principle is to enable strong transfer learning across different modalities and scenarios. The researchers aim to demonstrate that by carefully designing modeling and data representations, capabilities learned from one modality (e.g., images) can effectively transfer and yield new, emergent capabilities in other modalities (e.g., strong video understanding through task transfer from images). This seeks to prove the efficiency and power of a unified multimodal learning approach.

- **Motivation for General-Purpose Assistants:** At a higher level, the work is driven by the fundamental aspiration in AI to build general-purpose assistants using LMMs. LLaVA-OneVision continues the LLaVA lineage, which has consistently aimed to create large vision-and-language assistants capable of following diverse instructions to complete various computer vision tasks in real-world settings.

- **Cost-Efficient Recipe Advancement:** Building upon the "cost-efficient recipe" established by previous LLaVA models, the motivation includes demonstrating that this approach can be effectively scaled with larger datasets and stronger LLMs to achieve SOTA performance while remaining accessible to the open research community.

- **Consolidation of Insights and Open-Source Contribution:** LLaVA-OneVision is motivated by consolidating the empirical insights gained from the extensive LLaVA-NeXT blog series. Furthermore, a strong motivation is to contribute back to the open research community by releasing generated multimodal instruction data, codebase, model checkpoints, and a visual chat demo. This open-source commitment aims to accelerate collective research and foster broader development of general-purpose visual AI.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

LLaVA-OneVision adopts a minimalist and modular design, characteristic of the LLaVA series, while integrating novel strategies for visual representation and data curation.

#### 3.1. Network Architecture

The architecture (Figure 1) prioritizes leveraging the pre-trained strengths of its components and facilitating strong scaling behavior:
- **Large Language Model (LLM):** Qwen-2 is chosen for its strong language capabilities and availability across various model sizes (0.5B, 7B, 72B parameters). This choice is informed by prior LLaVA-NeXT insights that stronger LLMs tend to supercharge multimodal capabilities.
- **Vision Encoder:** SigLIP serves as the visual encoder. It encodes input images into visual features. The selection of SigLIP is based on empirical observations from LLaVA-NeXT that it yields higher LMM performance among open vision encoders.
- **Projector:** A simple 2-layer MLP projects the vision encoder's features into the LLM's word embedding space, creating a sequence of visual tokens. This simple connection module remains a hallmark of the LLaVA design.

#### 3.2. Visual Representations (Key Innovation for Versatility)

The representation of visual signals is crucial for handling diverse modalities. LLaVA-OneVision extends the "AnyRes" strategy to enable flexible visual input across scenarios:
- **Higher AnyRes with Bilinear Interpolation:** This improved strategy divides images into `a × b` crops, each suitable for the vision encoder. To manage the total number of visual tokens (L), a threshold `τ` is introduced. If L exceeds `τ`, bilinear interpolation is used to reduce tokens per crop, prioritizing resolution over an excessive number of tokens. This balances performance with computational cost, particularly for high-resolution images.
- **Modality-Specific Token Allocation (Figure 3):**
 - **Single-Image:** Employs a large maximum spatial configuration for images (e.g., 384x{1x1..., 6x6}) and allocates a large number of visual tokens per image (up to 7290 tokens). This long sequence representation mimics video input, facilitating smoother capability transfer from image to video understanding.
 - **Multi-Image:** Uses a simple padding strategy. Each image is resized to a base resolution (384x384) with zero-padding and fed into the encoder. Up to 12 images are supported, resulting in a maximum of 12 × 729 = 8748 tokens.
 - **Video:** Each frame is resized to the base resolution, processed by the encoder, and then down-sampled to 196 tokens per frame using bilinear interpolation. This allows for a larger number of frames (up to 32 frames), leading to a maximum of 32 × 196 = 6272 tokens. The design ensures that the maximum number of tokens across different modalities is similar, promoting balanced visual representations and cross-scenario capability transfer.

#### 3.3. Data Strategy ("Quality over Quantity")

The paper emphasizes high-quality data curation, acknowledging the limitations of web-scale low-quality data.
- **High-Quality Knowledge Learning (4M samples):** Primarily relies on synthetic data (99.8%):
 - **Re-Captioned Detailed Description Data (3.5M):** Generated by LLaVA-NeXT-34B for images from COCO118K, BLIP558K, and CC3M. This represents a form of self-improvement AI.
 - **Document / OCR Data (1.1M):** Derived from UReader's Text Reading subset and SynDOG EN/CN.
 - **Chinese and Language Data (0.5M):** Includes GPT-4V generated Chinese captions for ShareGPT4V images and samples from Evo-Instruct for language balance.
- **Visual Instruction Tuning Data:** Crucial for LMM capability, categorized by vision input, language instruction, and response type. Manual review and correction ensure high quality and consistency with LLaVA-1.5 prompting.
 - **Single-Image Data (3.2M samples):** A large, balanced collection of single-image instructions for foundational multimodal learning.
 - **OneVision Data (1.6M mixed samples):** Used for fine-tuning, comprising multi-image (560K from M4-Instruct), video (350K from various sources re-annotated with GPT-4o), and carefully sampled high-quality single-image (800K) data. This mixture is key for expanding capabilities and fostering cross-scenario knowledge transfer.

#### 3.4. Training Strategies (Curriculum Learning)

The model is trained progressively through a curriculum learning approach with increasing difficulty:
- **Stage-1: Language-Image Alignment:** Focuses on aligning visual features with the LLM's embedding space. Only the projector is updated, using base image representation (384 resolution, 729 tokens).
- **Stage-1.5: High-Quality Knowledge Learning:** Injects new knowledge using the curated high-quality synthetic data. The full model is updated, employing AnyRes with up to 5 times more visual tokens.
- **Stage-2: Visual Instruction Tuning:** Teaches the LMM to solve diverse visual tasks with preferred responses.
 - **Phase 1: Single-Image Training:** Model is trained on 3.2M single-image instructions for strong foundational performance.
 - **Phase 2: OneVision Training:** Model is fine-tuned on the 1.6M mixed data (video, single-image, multi-image). This crucial phase expands capabilities to new scenarios and facilitates knowledge transfer, yielding emergent abilities.

Training hyperparameters, such as batch sizes and learning rates (vision encoder LR is 5x smaller than LLM/projector), are meticulously detailed to ensure effective progression.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

LLaVA-OneVision demonstrates significant advancements in open large multimodal models, showcasing superior performance across single-image, multi-image, and video benchmarks, and highlighting impressive emerging capabilities through task transfer.

#### 4.1. Overall Performance and Comparison to SOTA Models

- **State-of-the-Art (SOTA) in Open LMMs:** LLaVA-OneVision models (0.5B, 7B, 72B) achieve SOTA performance among publicly available LMMs across all three tested modalities.
- **Closing the Gap with Proprietary Models:** The largest model, LLaVA-OneVision-72B, consistently yields performance that places it between GPT-4V and GPT-4o on most benchmarks. This indicates the proposed recipe's effectiveness and a promising path for further scaling. While a gap remains in complex visual chat scenarios, the overall competitive performance is a notable achievement for an open model.

#### 4.2. Single-Image Benchmarks (Table 3)

- **Chart, Diagram, and Document Understanding:** LLaVA-OneVision significantly surpasses GPT-4V on benchmarks like AI2D, ChartQA, and DocVQA, approaching or even surpassing GPT-4o in some cases (e.g., AI2D). This demonstrates strong capabilities in handling structured OCR data.
- **Perception and Multi-discipline Reasoning:** On benchmarks like MathVerse, MathVista, MMBench, MME, and MMMU, LLaVA-OneVision substantially outperforms GPT-4V and often achieves performance comparable to GPT-4o (e.g., MathVista). This confirms its superiority in complex visual perception and reasoning tasks.
- **Real-world Understanding and Visual Chat:** While still having room for improvement compared to GPT-4V/o in general real-world chat scenarios (e.g., LLaVA-Bench-Wilder, Vibe-Eval), LLaVA-OneVision-72B shows competitive performance against other open-source models and excels on MM-LiveBench, which evaluates understanding of real-world internet content, suggesting broad world knowledge and generalization abilities.

#### 4.3. Multi-Image Benchmarks (Table 4)

- **Strong Baseline Performance:** Even LLaVA-OV (SI) (trained only on single images) consistently outperforms existing multi-image LMMs, indicating that the strong image-only training provides a robust foundation.
- **Significant Improvement with OneVision Training:** After additional fine-tuning on multi-image and video data (LLaVA-OV), the models show a marked improvement, often surpassing GPT-4V by significant margins in complex tasks like multi-image reasoning, spot-the-difference, and 3D environment understanding.
- **Cross-Modal Transfer Validation:** Consistent performance enhancements are observed after the OneVision training stage, especially on multi-view benchmarks, demonstrating the efficacy of the proposed "one-vision paradigm" in empowering LMMs with comprehensive visual capabilities that extend beyond their initial training modality.

#### 4.4. Video Benchmarks (Table 5)

- **Competitive with Larger Open Models:** LLaVA-OneVision achieves comparable or superior results to previous open-source models, even those with much larger LLMs, demonstrating the efficiency of its training recipe.
- **Superiority in Complex Video Tasks:** Its strength is particularly evident in benchmarks requiring significant reasoning, such as EgoSchema and VideoMME.
- **Competitive with Proprietary Video Models:** LLaVA-OneVision performs competitively with GPT-4V on ActivityNet-QA, MLVU, and VideoMME.
- **Insights into Modality Transfer:** The smallest performance difference in PerceptionTest (minimal gain when scaling LLM from 0.5B to 7B) suggests that perception capabilities heavily rely on the vision module. Conversely, reasoning-intensive tasks like EgoSchema benefit substantially from a larger LLM. The strong performance of LLaVA-OV (SI) on ActivityNet-QA (where many questions can be answered from a single frame) highlights the effective transfer of image understanding to video, reducing the need for extensive video-specific training for certain tasks.

#### 4.5. Emerging Capabilities with Task Transfer (Section 7)

The paper showcases several qualitative examples of LLaVA-OneVision's emerging abilities, demonstrating task transfer and composition:
- **Joint Diagram and Chart Understanding (S1):** Transferred from single-image understanding to multi-image interpretation.
- **GUI for Multi-modal Agent (S2):** Leverages single-image OCR and multi-image relational reasoning for task automation.
- **Set-of-Mark Prompting (S3):** A novel emergent capability for open LMMs, composed from visual referring and OCR.
- **Image-to-Video Editing Instruction (S4):** Generalized from image-to-image editing and video detailed description.
- **Video-to-Video Difference (S5):** Extends multi-image "spot the difference" to video sequences.
- **Multi-camera Video Understanding in Self-driving (S6):** Combines multi-panel comprehension, video description, and spatial-temporal reasoning.
- **Composed Sub-video Understanding (S7):** Generalizes multi-image sequence comprehension to vertical videos with sub-scenes.
- **Visual Prompting in Video (S8):** Transfers single-image visual prompting and OCR capabilities directly to video.
- **Visual Referring in Image in Video Understanding (S9):** A new capability to relate an image query to video content, possibly due to strong base single-image training.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

LLaVA-OneVision represents a significant leap forward for open-source large multimodal models, with profound implications for the research community and real-world applications:

- **Setting a New Standard for Open LMMs:** By demonstrating state-of-the-art performance across single-image, multi-image, and video modalities with a *single unified model*, LLaVA-OneVision establishes a new benchmark for general-purpose open LMMs. It significantly narrows the performance gap with proprietary models like GPT-4V/o, making highly versatile multimodal AI more accessible.

- **Validation of Unified Multimodal Learning:** The core contribution of LLaVA-OneVision is its empirical validation of effective task transfer and capability composition across modalities. The observation that strong image understanding can directly enable or enhance video understanding, and that skills like OCR or relational reasoning can compose into new, emergent behaviors (e.g., GUI understanding or set-of-mark prompting), is highly significant. This suggests a more efficient and scalable paradigm for training future LMMs, moving away from modality-specific architectures towards truly unified representations.

- **Cost-Efficient Development Pathway:** The LLaVA series' emphasis on a "cost-efficient recipe" combined with the effective use of high-quality, often synthetic, data provides a practical blueprint for researchers and developers with limited computational resources to build powerful LMMs. This approach makes advanced multimodal AI development more democratic and widespread.

- **Accelerating Real-World Applications:** A single model capable of handling diverse visual inputs simplifies deployment and integration into various applications. This has direct implications for fields like:
 - **Autonomous Driving:** Multi-camera video understanding (S6) is critical for perceiving complex environments.
 - **Robotics:** GUI understanding (S2) and visual prompting in video (S8) could enable more intuitive human-robot interaction and task automation.
 - **Content Creation & Editing:** Image-to-video editing instruction generation (S4) could revolutionize creative workflows.
 - **Education and Accessibility:** Enhanced document, chart, and diagram understanding can improve learning tools and aid visually impaired users.

- **Empowering Future Research:** The open-sourcing of LLaVA-OneVision's generated multimodal instruction data, codebase, and model checkpoints is a massive contribution. It provides a robust foundation for researchers to:
 - Build specific applications tailored to their needs.
 - Further scale the models with additional data and larger LLMs.
 - Explore novel training strategies, such as preference learning for more sophisticated visual chat scenarios.
 - Investigate the underlying mechanisms of cross-modal transfer and emergent capabilities.

In conclusion, LLaVA-OneVision marks a pivotal moment in the development of open LMMs, pushing the boundaries of what a single model can achieve across the spectrum of visual understanding tasks. Its emphasis on unified learning, task transfer, and open-source contributions positions it as a foundational work for the next generation of general-purpose visual AI assistants.
