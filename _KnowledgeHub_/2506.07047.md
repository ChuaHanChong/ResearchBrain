# Mathesis: Towards Formal Theorem Proving from Natural Languages

> [!summary] Summary
> Mathesis, developed by Huawei's Noah's Ark Lab and The Chinese University of Hong Kong, is an end-to-end framework for theorem proving from natural language inputs. It integrates online reinforcement learning for autoformalization in Lean and introduces a nuanced evaluation metric, achieving superior autoformalization and end-to-end proving results on the new Gaokao-Formal benchmark and MiniF2F.

## Problem

- Mathematical problems are often expressed in natural language, while automated theorem provers require formal language, necessitating a time-consuming and error-prone autoformalization step.
- Existing autoformalization methods largely rely on supervised fine-tuning with static datasets and lack the ability to learn dynamically from feedback on correctness or semantic faithfulness.
- Current evaluation metrics for autoformalization are inadequate, typically using binary compilation checks that fail to capture nuanced semantic errors or the degree of faithfulness to the original natural language.

## Method

- **Mathesis** introduces a three-stage pipeline for end-to-end theorem proving: ==autoformalization==, validation, and proving from natural language inputs.
- The Mathesis-Autoformalizer employs online reinforcement learning with Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) and Hierarchical Preference Optimization (HPO), using a composite reward function that combines semantic equivalence and verification rewards.
- LeanScorer, a novel evaluation framework, assesses semantic faithfulness of formalizations by decomposing problems, applying a three-tier evaluation, and aggregating scores with a Sugeno Fuzzy Integral.

## Results

- Mathesis-Autoformalizer with HPO achieved **71%** LC+LSC@6 on Gaokao-Formal, an absolute improvement of **22%** over the previous state-of-the-art baseline for autoformalization.
- The complete Mathesis pipeline achieved an **18.0%** success rate (pass@32) on the challenging Gaokao-Formal benchmark and **64.3%** on MiniF2F for end-to-end theorem proving.
- Integrated into Huawei Celia, Mathesis increased accuracy on 95 proof-related Gaokao problems from a baseline of **65.3%** (informal reasoning) to **84.2%**, demonstrating practical enhancement of AI assistants.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Autoformalization quality is a primary bottleneck for end-to-end theorem proving, showing disproportionately large impact on overall performance compared to prover enhancements.
> - Applying online reinforcement learning, specifically with a composite reward function and hierarchical preference optimization, effectively improves autoformalization accuracy in Lean.
> - Nuanced evaluation of autoformalization through tools like LeanScorer, which uses subtask decomposition and fuzzy logic aggregation, is crucial for assessing semantic faithfulness beyond simple syntactic checks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Introduction

Mathematical reasoning represents one of the most challenging frontiers in artificial intelligence, requiring precise logical thinking and rigorous proof construction. While automated theorem provers (ATPs) have achieved remarkable success in formal verification systems like Lean, Isabelle, and Coq, they face a critical limitation: they require problems to be expressed in formal mathematical language rather than natural language.

This paper introduces Mathesis, a comprehensive framework that bridges the gap between natural language mathematical problems and formal theorem proving. Developed by researchers from Huawei's Noah's Ark Lab, Celia Team, and The Chinese University of Hong Kong, Mathesis represents the first system to apply online reinforcement learning to autoformalization in Lean, enabling end-to-end theorem proving directly from natural language inputs.

### 2. Background and Problem Context

The field of automated theorem proving has experienced significant advances with the emergence of large language model (LLM)-based systems like DeepSeek-Prover-V2, Kimina-Prover, and Goedel-Prover. These systems have achieved state-of-the-art results on formal benchmarks such as MiniF2F and ProofNet. However, a fundamental disconnect exists between these capabilities and real-world applications.

Mathematical problems in textbooks, competitions, and scientific literature are predominantly expressed in natural language. Converting these informal statements into formal mathematical language—a process called autoformalization—has traditionally required expert manual effort that is time-consuming, error-prone, and requires specialized knowledge of formal systems.

Existing autoformalization approaches primarily rely on supervised fine-tuning with static natural language to formal language pairs. These methods lack the ability to learn dynamically from feedback about the correctness and semantic faithfulness of their generated formalizations. Moreover, current evaluation methods for autoformalization quality are inadequate, typically relying on binary compilation checks that miss nuanced semantic errors.

### 3. The Mathesis Framework

Mathesis introduces a three-stage pipeline for end-to-end theorem proving from natural language:

#### 3.1. Stage 1: Autoformalization

The Mathesis-Autoformalizer converts natural language problem statements into multiple candidate formal Lean 4 statements. This component employs Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) with a composite reward function that considers both syntactic validity and semantic faithfulness.

#### 3.2. Stage 2: Validation

Generated formal statements undergo two-step validation:
- **Syntactic Check**: The Lean compiler verifies syntactic correctness
- **Semantic Assessment**: LeanScorer evaluates semantic faithfulness to the original natural language

The formal statement that passes compilation and receives the highest semantic score is selected for proving.

#### 3.3. Stage 3: Proving

The Mathesis-Prover generates complete, machine-verifiable proofs in Lean 4 from the selected formal statement.

### 4. Technical Innovations

#### 4.1. Reinforcement Learning for Autoformalization

Mathesis-Autoformalizer introduces several key innovations:

**Composite Reward Function**: The system learns from direct feedback through rewards that combine:
- Semantic Equivalence Reward (R_sem): Binary reward based on semantic preservation
- Verification Reward (R_ver): Binary reward for syntactic correctness

The overall reward is: `r_i = R_sem + R_ver`

**Hierarchical Preference Optimization (HPO)**: After GRPO training, the system undergoes Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) to align with the global objective of successful proof generation. This creates a hierarchical approach where local syntactic/semantic objectives are balanced with downstream proof success.

#### 4.2. LeanScorer: Advanced Evaluation Framework

LeanScorer addresses the limitations of binary evaluation methods through:

1. **Subtask Decomposition**: Problems are broken into discrete components (premises, conclusions)
2. **Three-Tier Evaluation**: Each subtask receives ratings of "Perfectly Match," "Minor Inconsistency," or "Major Inconsistency"
3. **Sugeno Fuzzy Integral**: A robust aggregation method that provides continuous scoring from 0 to 1, with progressive penalties for inconsistencies

#### 4.3. Gaokao-Formal Benchmark

The authors introduce Gaokao-Formal, a challenging dataset of 488 mathematical problems from China's National Higher Education Entrance Examination. This benchmark addresses limitations in existing datasets by:
- Including diverse mathematical domains (geometry, combinatorics, analysis)
- Retaining complex natural language expressions that are difficult to formalize
- Providing human-expert formalizations for validation

### 5. Experimental Results

#### 5.1. Autoformalization Performance

Mathesis-Autoformalizer with HPO achieves state-of-the-art performance:
- **Gaokao-Formal**: 71% LC+LSC@6 score (22% absolute improvement over previous best)
- **MiniF2F**: Consistent improvements across all metrics
- The results demonstrate the effectiveness of dynamic learning from reinforcement signals

#### 5.2. End-to-End Theorem Proving

The complete Mathesis pipeline achieves:
- **MiniF2F**: 64.3% success rate (pass@32)
- **Gaokao-Formal**: 18.0% success rate (pass@32), representing state-of-the-art performance

A critical finding reveals that autoformalization quality has disproportionately large impact on end-to-end performance. On MiniF2F, autoformalizer improvements yielded 29.5% gains versus 11.5% from prover enhancements, highlighting autoformalization as the primary bottleneck.

### 6. Real-World Applications

Mathesis was successfully integrated into Huawei Celia, demonstrating practical impact:
- **Baseline accuracy**: 65.3% using DeepSeek-R1 for informal reasoning
- **With Mathesis integration**: 84.2% accuracy on 95 proof-related Gaokao problems
- **Direct end-to-end pipeline**: 69.4% accuracy

This integration showcases how formal reasoning capabilities can augment informal LLM reasoning, leading to more reliable AI assistants for complex mathematical problem-solving.

### 7. Significance and Future Directions

Mathesis represents a significant advancement in automated formal reasoning with several key contributions:

**Methodological Innovation**: The first application of online reinforcement learning to autoformalization in Lean, with hierarchical preference optimization representing a sophisticated approach to aligning local and global objectives.

**Evaluation Advancement**: LeanScorer provides the field with a much-needed tool for nuanced assessment of autoformalization quality, moving beyond simplistic binary metrics.

**Practical Impact**: The successful deployment in Huawei Celia demonstrates immediate real-world applicability, showing how formal reasoning can enhance AI assistants.

**Research Foundation**: The Gaokao-Formal benchmark establishes a new standard for evaluating end-to-end formal reasoning systems, while the finding about autoformalization being the primary bottleneck provides clear direction for future research.

The work opens several promising research directions:
- Integration of autoformalization, validation, and proving into unified models
- Exploration of how formal reasoning capabilities enhance general mathematical reasoning
- Extension to broader domains beyond competition mathematics
- Development of more sophisticated reward functions for reinforcement learning in formal reasoning

Mathesis demonstrates that the gap between natural language mathematical problems and formal verification is bridgeable through sophisticated machine learning techniques, potentially democratizing access to formal mathematical reasoning tools and advancing the field of AI-assisted mathematics.
