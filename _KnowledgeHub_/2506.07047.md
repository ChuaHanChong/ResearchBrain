# Mathesis: Towards Formal Theorem Proving from Natural Languages

> [!summary] Summary
> Mathesis, developed by Huawei's Noah's Ark Lab and The Chinese University of Hong Kong, is an end-to-end framework for theorem proving from natural language inputs. It integrates online reinforcement learning for autoformalization in Lean and introduces a nuanced evaluation metric, achieving superior autoformalization and end-to-end proving results on the new Gaokao-Formal benchmark and MiniF2F.

## Problem

- Mathematical problems are often expressed in natural language, while automated theorem provers require formal language, necessitating a time-consuming and error-prone autoformalization step.
- Existing autoformalization methods largely rely on supervised fine-tuning with static datasets and lack the ability to learn dynamically from feedback on correctness or semantic faithfulness.
- Current evaluation metrics for autoformalization are inadequate, typically using binary compilation checks that fail to capture nuanced semantic errors or the degree of faithfulness to the original natural language.

## Method

- **Mathesis** introduces a three-stage pipeline for end-to-end theorem proving: ==autoformalization==, validation, and proving from natural language inputs.
- The Mathesis-Autoformalizer employs online reinforcement learning with Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) and Hierarchical Preference Optimization (HPO), using a composite reward function that combines semantic equivalence and verification rewards.
- LeanScorer, a novel evaluation framework, assesses semantic faithfulness of formalizations by decomposing problems, applying a three-tier evaluation, and aggregating scores with a Sugeno Fuzzy Integral.

## Results

- Mathesis-Autoformalizer with HPO achieved **71%** LC+LSC@6 on Gaokao-Formal, an absolute improvement of **22%** over the previous state-of-the-art baseline for autoformalization.
- The complete Mathesis pipeline achieved an **18.0%** success rate (pass@32) on the challenging Gaokao-Formal benchmark and **64.3%** on MiniF2F for end-to-end theorem proving.
- Integrated into Huawei Celia, Mathesis increased accuracy on 95 proof-related Gaokao problems from a baseline of **65.3%** (informal reasoning) to **84.2%**, demonstrating practical enhancement of AI assistants.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Autoformalization quality is a primary bottleneck for end-to-end theorem proving, showing disproportionately large impact on overall performance compared to prover enhancements.
> - Applying online reinforcement learning, specifically with a composite reward function and hierarchical preference optimization, effectively improves autoformalization accuracy in Lean.
> - Nuanced evaluation of autoformalization through tools like LeanScorer, which uses subtask decomposition and fuzzy logic aggregation, is crucial for assessing semantic faithfulness beyond simple syntactic checks.
