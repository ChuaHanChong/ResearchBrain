# Steerable Scene Generation with Post Training and Inference-Time Search

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers introduce a framework for generating diverse, physically feasible, and task-specific SE(3) scenes for robotic simulation. This approach trains a diffusion-based model on a dataset of over 44 million procedurally generated scenes, then employs post-training and inference-time search to create highly cluttered and task-aligned environments, ensuring physical plausibility.

## Problem

- Generating diverse, large-scale, and task-specific 3D scenes with full 6-DOF object poses (SE(3)) for robotics training is challenging and costly.
- Existing generative models often struggle with ensuring physical feasibility (non-penetration, static equilibrium) and typically optimize for data likelihood rather than specific robotic objectives.
- Manual scene curation or rule-based procedural generation lacks the scalability and flexibility needed for diverse and adaptive robotic learning scenarios.

## Method

- A unified ==diffusion==-based generative model is trained on a massive dataset of 44 million procedurally generated SE(3) scenes to predict both object identities and 6-DOF poses.
- A two-step post-processing pipeline ensures physical feasibility, involving ==nonlinear optimization== for collision resolution and physics simulation for static equilibrium.
- The framework integrates three steering mechanisms: ==RL-based post-training== for distributional shifts, text-conditioned and inpainting-based conditional generation, and a novel inference-time ==Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS)== for goal-directed scene optimization.

## Results

- The proposed model produced scenes with improved visual quality and inherent physical plausibility, achieving lower Classifier Accuracy and significantly reduced Median Total Penetration compared to baseline methods.
- RL-based post-training successfully shifted scene distributions, generating scenes with object counts and clutter levels exceeding the pretraining data (e.g., taller steamer tray stacks).
- Conditional generation demonstrated strong adherence to text prompts and effective scene rearrangement/completion, while inference-time MCTS optimized scenes to contain maximum physically feasible objects, such as **34** objects in a Dimsum scene, from a training mean of **17.1**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Distilling large-scale procedural data into a diffusion model creates a flexible, physically-grounded prior for diverse SE(3) scene synthesis, suitable for robotic applications.
> - Robust post-processing for collision resolution and static equilibrium is essential for translating generative model outputs into simulation-ready 3D environments.
> - Pretrained generative models can be effectively 'steered' through reinforcement learning or inference-time search to meet specific, external task objectives, adapting scene generation beyond the original training data distribution.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses the increasing demand for diverse and task-specific 3D scenes for training and evaluating robots in simulation. Modern robotics, particularly with the advent of foundation models, relies heavily on large-scale datasets, which are challenging and costly to acquire or curate manually, especially for complex scenarios such as high-clutter environments or specific object interaction tasks.

Historically, scene creation has employed various approaches. Classical methods involve procedural modeling, using rule sets or grammars to define object relationships. More recent developments integrate priors from large language models (LLMs) or vision-language models (VLMs) to guide scene generation, or aim to reconstruct 3D scenes directly from 2D image data. Another line of research focuses on training generative models that learn object relationships directly from scene data, rather than relying on handcrafted rules. However, many of these generative models typically operate in SE(2) (two dimensions with rotation in the plane), focusing on floor-aligned layouts and large static furniture items. This limits their applicability for robotic manipulation tasks that require cluttered SE(3) (three dimensions with full 6-DOF pose) scenes involving small, manipulable objects, vertical translation (e.g., placing objects on shelves), and full 3D rotation (e.g., standing cutlery).

Prior work like PhyScene has attempted to encourage physical feasibility in generated scenes through classifier-based guidance, but samples may still exhibit violations. Furthermore, existing generative models often optimize for likelihood under the training data distribution, which may not align with specific downstream objectives such as maximizing robot performance or human preferences.

This paper positions itself by combining elements from procedural modeling and data-driven generative models. It utilizes large-scale procedurally generated data as a source for training a unified generative model, effectively distilling the procedural output into a flexible prior. The key advancements include targeting cluttered SE(3) scenes, explicitly guaranteeing physical feasibility through post-processing, and introducing novel methods for *steering* the generative model towards task-specific objectives that may lie outside the original training data distribution. These steering methods—reinforcement learning-based post training, conditional generation, and a novel inference-time Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS)—address the challenge of adapting scene generation to specific robotic task requirements without extensive manual re-curation or retraining of the entire model.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the increasing reliance of robotic learning on vast and diverse datasets, which are often expensive and time-consuming to create manually. Training robots in simulation offers a scalable alternative, but requires physically plausible 3D scenes that accurately reflect the specific challenges of real-world tasks. Such task-specific scenes—for instance, highly cluttered environments or those demanding interaction with particular object categories—are difficult to procure.

The overarching objective of this work is to develop a scalable and flexible framework for generating diverse, physically feasible, and task-specific SE(3) scenes suitable for robotic simulation.

The key objectives are detailed as follows:
1. **Develop a Unified Generative Model:** To train a single, diffusion-based generative model that can predict both the identity (from a fixed asset library) and the full SE(3) poses (translation and rotation) of objects within a scene. This model is intended to serve as a flexible scene prior.
2. **Leverage Procedural Data for Scale:** To utilize large-scale procedurally generated scene data as a training source, recognizing its efficiency compared to manual curation, while ensuring the generative model can distill and generalize from this data.
3. **Ensure Physical Feasibility:** To implement robust post-processing mechanisms that guarantee the physical feasibility of generated scenes, specifically addressing non-penetration and static equilibrium constraints, which are critical for realistic robotic interactions.
4. **Enable Goal-Directed Scene Synthesis (Steering):** To introduce and evaluate multiple complementary strategies for adapting or "steering" a pretrained scene generative model towards specific downstream objectives. These strategies include:
 - **Reinforcement Learning (RL)-based Post Training:** To fine-tune the generative model using task-specific rewards to align its output with desired properties (e.g., increased clutter).
 - **Conditional Generation:** To allow scene synthesis guided by external inputs such as natural language descriptions or partial scene configurations, enabling flexible control over generated content.
 - **Inference-Time Search:** To develop a novel Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS) procedure that optimizes scene properties during the generation process itself, without requiring model retraining.
5. **Contribute to Dataset Availability:** To release a large-scale dataset of over 44 million unique SE(3) scenes across diverse environments, providing a benchmark and resource for future research in SE(3) scene generation.
6. **Demonstrate Simulation Readiness:** To validate that the generated scenes are directly usable for robotic data collection and interaction in simulation, exemplifying their utility for real-world robotics challenges.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology involves a multi-stage pipeline: data generation, generative model training, physical feasibility post-processing, and flexible steering mechanisms.

#### 3.1. Data Generation

The foundation of the approach is the use of procedurally generated scenes. The authors utilize a probabilistic context-free scene grammar framework to create a large dataset of (object ID, SE(3) pose) tuples. This method supports varied scene compositions by defining rules for object types and production rules, implicitly encoding hierarchical structures. The system generates 44,790,942 unique SE(3) scenes across five diverse scene types: Breakfast Table (low- and high-clutter), Dimsum Table, Living Room Shelf, Pantry Shelf, and Restaurant (low- and high-clutter). These scenes contain up to 125 objects. Textual annotations for these scenes are also generated using a rule-based system, covering object counts, names, and spatial relationships. The choice of procedural generation allows for scalability, though the framework is designed to be agnostic to the data source, allowing future integration of real-world scenes.

#### 3.2. SE(3) Scene Diffusion

- **Scene Representation:** A scene is represented as an unordered set of objects $X = \{o_i\}$, where $N$ is an upper bound on the number of objects. Each object $o_i$ is defined by its SE(3) pose (a 3D translation $p \in \mathbb{R}^3$ and a 9D rotation vector $R \in \mathbb{R}^9$, which is projected to SO(3) at sample time), and a one-hot vector $c$ indicating its asset category from a fixed library of $C$ assets. An 'empty' object category is included to accommodate variable scene sizes.
- **Training Objective:** The model employs a mixed discrete-continuous diffusion framework. Continuous diffusion is applied to the translation ($p$) and rotation ($R$) components, while discrete diffusion is used for the object category ($c$). These components are conditioned on each other during the generation process.
- **Model Architecture:** The denoising model is built upon the Flux architecture, specifically its image branch, adapted to operate on object sets by omitting positional encodings to maintain object-order equivariance. Input and output MLP projections are added to handle the mixed discrete-continuous data types. A frozen BERT-Base encoder processes text prompts for conditional generation, and sinusoidal embeddings are used for timesteps.

#### 3.3. Physical Feasibility Post Processing

Generative models, even when trained on valid data, can produce physically infeasible scenes (e.g., inter-object penetrations, unstable objects). To address this, a two-step post-processing pipeline is applied:
1. **Collision Resolution:** Continuous object translations are adjusted by solving a nonlinear optimization problem that minimizes the squared difference between optimized and original translations, subject to non-penetration constraints ($d(i,j) \ge 0$). Object orientations are kept fixed during this step. The SNOPT solver is used for this optimization.
2. **Simulation for Static Equilibrium:** After collision resolution, the scene is simulated in Drake for a short duration. This allows unstable objects to settle under gravity, ensuring static equilibrium. Simulation is applied after projection to prevent numerical instability from deep penetrations.

#### 3.4. Steering Scene Generative Models Toward Downstream Objectives

The core contribution of the paper lies in its exploration of three complementary strategies for steering the pretrained scene model:

- **4.4.1 Post Training with Reinforcement Learning (RL)**
 The differentiable nature of the diffusion model enables fine-tuning with RL. The method adopts DDPO, a reinforcement learning approach for diffusion models, applying it to a continuous DDPM-based scene model. An object count reward is used as a proof-of-concept for adapting the model beyond its original training distribution to generate scenes with increased clutter. Regularization is applied to stabilize training.

- **4.4.2 Conditional Generation**
 This strategy allows flexible control over scene content using external conditions.
 - **Text-conditioned generation:** Text prompts, encoded by BERT, are injected into the conditional branch of the Flux-based architecture. Random masking of conditioning information during training enables classifier-free guidance (CFG) at inference time, allowing interpolation between conditional and unconditional outputs.
 - **Scene completion and re-arrangement:** Inpainting is performed directly within the structured scene representation. A binary mask indicates which parts of the scene (e.g., specific objects, object poses) to regenerate. Masked components are initialized with noise and updated via reverse diffusion, while unmasked parts are clamped to their original values, enabling consistent completion or rearrangement of existing scenes.

- **4.4.3 Inference-Time Search via MCTS**
 A novel Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS) procedure is introduced to incrementally construct and refine a scene during inference, guided by a task-specific reward function.
 - **Tree Structure:** Each node represents a partially completed scene and an associated inpainting mask (e.g., identifying unstable or empty objects).
 - **Search Process:** Follows standard MCTS phases: Selection (using UCT to choose nodes), Expansion (sampling multiple completions by inpainting masked objects with different noise initializations), Rollout (scoring a completed scene with a task-specific reward, e.g., number of physically feasible objects), and Backpropagation (updating reward estimates and visit counts).
 - **Modularity:** The mask generator (determining which objects to re-inpaint) and the reward function (evaluating scene quality) are modular, allowing adaptation to diverse objectives without retraining.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation focused on generative quality using image-based metrics, physical feasibility, and the effectiveness of steering mechanisms across five diverse scene types.

#### 4.1. Unconditional Generation

- **Metrics:** Fréchet Inception Distance (FID), Classifier Accuracy (CA), KL divergence between object category distributions, and Median Total Penetration (MTP) before post-processing.
- **Performance:** The proposed model consistently achieved lower CA (closer to 50%), indicating that its generated scenes were harder to distinguish from real training scenes, suggesting higher realism. It also obtained competitive FID scores and significantly lower MTP scores compared to baselines (DiffuScene and MiDiffusion), demonstrating improved visual quality and inherent physical plausibility prior to explicit post-processing. While KL divergence values were low across all methods, the proposed approach showed competitive results.
- **Qualitative Results:** Samples from various datasets (Breakfast Table, Dimsum Table, Living Room Shelf, Pantry Shelf, Restaurant) showed the model's ability to generate diverse and visually realistic scenes, including successful cotraining across all scene types.

#### 4.2. Post Training with Reinforcement Learning

- **Objective:** Increase object count (clutter).
- **Performance:** RL-based post training successfully adapted the pretrained model to generate scenes with object counts substantially exceeding the distribution observed during pretraining. For instance, in the Dimsum Table dataset, the model learned to produce taller steamer tray stacks, and in the Living Room Shelf dataset, it generated denser scenes by utilizing expanded object capacity. This demonstrated that RL can effectively shift and reshape scene distributions toward specific task goals.

#### 4.3. Conditional Generation

- **Text-Conditioned Generation:** The model demonstrated strong adherence to text prompts, achieving higher Average Prompt-Following accuracy (APF) and competitive FID and CA scores compared to baselines. Qualitative examples showed the model capturing both large-scale layouts and fine-grained object details as specified by prompts (e.g., "A scene with four tables, a shelf, a steamer bottom, and a tea bottle").
- **Scene Rearrangement and Completion:** The model successfully performed scene rearrangement by regenerating object poses while maintaining object categories, including complex 3D rotations (e.g., moving cutlery from a crock to a table). It also effectively completed scenes by synthesizing both categories and poses for empty object slots, adding new objects consistently with the existing scene context.
- **Cross-Dataset Interpolation:** Cotraining on Living Room Shelf and Pantry Shelf datasets enabled the model to generate interpolated scenes that combined elements unique to each dataset when prompted with mixed object descriptions, indicating it learned a meaningful joint distribution.

#### 4.4. Inference-Time Search via MCTS

- **Objective:** Maximize the number of physically feasible objects.
- **Performance:** MCTS successfully optimized the number of physically feasible objects in scenes, reaching the maximum possible object count (e.g., 34 objects in a Dimsum scene, up from a mean of 17.1 in the training data). This demonstrated that inference-time search can push scene complexity and adherence to objectives well beyond the typical training distribution without requiring model retraining. The search process also exhibited an ability to exploit learned inductive biases, such as incrementally building realistic and feasible steamer stacks. Qualitative results illustrated how MCTS could refine initial samples into more physically robust and object-rich configurations.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a framework for steerable SE(3) scene generation that offers several significant contributions and potential impacts, particularly for the field of robotics.

**Significance:**
1. **Scalable and Flexible Scene Synthesis:** The work demonstrates that distilling large-scale procedurally generated data into a unified, physically grounded diffusion model provides a robust prior for 3D scene synthesis. This approach bypasses the limitations of manual scene curation and offers a more scalable solution for generating diverse environments.
2. **Addressing Robotic Data Needs:** By focusing on SE(3) scenes with small, manipulable objects and ensuring physical feasibility, the generated scenes are directly relevant and usable for robotic tasks. This directly addresses the increasing demand for high-quality, diverse training data for modern data-intensive robot learning methods, including foundation models in robotics.
3. **Novel Steering Mechanisms:** The introduction of RL-based post training and, notably, a Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS) procedure for inference-time steering of diffusion models for scene generation are key methodological advancements. These mechanisms enable adaptation to task-specific objectives that may not be well-represented in the original training data, providing flexible control over the generation process without requiring full model retraining or explicit hand-crafting of rules for every new objective.
4. **Physical Grounding:** The guaranteed physical feasibility of generated scenes through a combination of nonlinear projection and physics simulation is crucial for their utility in robot simulators. This ensures that the generated environments are stable and interactable, preventing costly simulation errors or unrealistic behaviors.
5. **Dataset Contribution:** The release of a massive dataset comprising over 44 million unique SE(3) scenes, each with full 6-DOF object poses, represents a valuable resource for the research community. This dataset significantly surpasses the scale of prior SE(2) scene datasets and can serve as a benchmark for future work in SE(3) scene generation and robotic learning.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Accelerated Robot Training:** By providing a scalable source of diverse, task-specific, and physically plausible scenes, the framework can significantly accelerate the training and evaluation of robotic manipulation policies in simulation, leading to more robust and capable robots.
- **Reduced Manual Effort:** It offers a pathway to reduce the labor-intensive and expensive manual curation of simulation environments, freeing up resources for other aspects of robot development.
- **Advanced Embodied AI:** The ability to steer scene generation towards complex, reward-driven objectives could enable the creation of highly challenging and specific environments for embodied AI research, pushing the boundaries of autonomous agents.
- **Bridging Sim-to-Real Gap:** Generating physically realistic scenes is a crucial step in reducing the sim-to-real gap, ensuring that policies learned in simulation transfer more effectively to physical robots. The demonstration of teleoperated interaction in generated scenes provides initial validation of their simulation readiness.
- **Foundation Models for Robotics:** The framework aligns with the trend towards foundation models in robotics by enabling the generation of vast datasets required for pre-training large-scale robotic models.

While the paper acknowledges limitations such as reliance on procedural data not fully capturing real-world complexity, and challenges in applying RL to mixed discrete-continuous models, the established foundation for steerable, physically-grounded SE(3) scene generation marks a significant step towards more autonomous and data-efficient robotic development.
