# SigLoMa: Learning Open-World Quadrupedal Loco-Manipulation from Ego-Centric Vision

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers developed SigLoMa, a fully onboard, ego-centric vision-based framework that enables dynamic quadrupedal robots to perform loco-manipulation by distilling visual information into sparse geometric features. The system achieved precise floating-base maneuvers in real-world pick-and-place tasks, demonstrating end-to-end success rates comparable to expert human teleoperation and converging efficiently in simulation.

## Problem

- Existing quadrupedal loco-manipulation methods struggle with sample inefficiency and sim-to-real gaps due to dense visual inputs and reliance on external infrastructure like motion capture or off-board computation.
- Onboard open-vocabulary visual object detectors typically operate at low frequencies, creating a temporal mismatch with the high-frequency demands of dynamic floating-base control for precise manipulation.
- Severe ego-motion of a walking quadruped combined with visual latency leads to rapid target state estimation drift, often requiring the robot to stop for manipulation.

## Method

- Introduces **SigLoMa**, a hierarchical framework that uses sparse '==Sigma Points==' as an efficient, geometry-aware representation of target objects, derived from onboard open-vocabulary visual tracking.
- Implements an ==ego-centric Kalman Filter== that operates in the camera frame, utilizing visual odometry to compensate for ego-motion and bridge the frequency gap between low-rate visual observations and high-frequency floating-base control.
- Leverages a reinforcement learning policy with Proximal Policy Optimization (**PPO**), incorporating trajectory shaping through 'hint poses' and an '==Active Sampling Curriculum==' for efficient learning of complex, long-horizon manipulation behaviors.

## Results

- The framework achieved policy convergence in approximately 6 hours on a single GPU, demonstrating sample efficiency in simulation, and exhibited emergent active perception behaviors.
- Ablation studies confirmed that 'hint poses' and 'Active Sampling Curriculum' improved long-axis grasping success rates from 12-**16%** to 83-**87%**, while a Temporal Memory Network (TCN) was critical for robustness against visual occlusions.
- Real-world deployment on a Unitree Go2 quadruped demonstrated precise floating-base loco-manipulation, with the ego-centric Kalman Filter and simulated blind-spot noise proving indispensable for high success rates comparable to expert human teleoperation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Distilling dense visual information into sparse 'Sigma Points' offers an efficient, sim-to-real invariant geometric representation crucial for robust perception and reducing computational overhead in RL.
> - Compensating for ego-motion within an ego-centric Kalman Filter effectively transforms low-frequency semantic observations into high-frequency, low-latency target state estimates, enabling dynamic and precise floating-base control.
> - Augmenting RL training with 'hint poses' for trajectory shaping, an 'Active Sampling Curriculum,' and simulated Kalman Filter drift during blind spots significantly improves learning efficiency and policy robustness for complex loco-manipulation tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The integration of manipulation capabilities with quadrupedal locomotion represents a significant area of research aimed at enabling robots to operate in unstructured, human-centric environments. Existing work in this domain faces several interconnected challenges.

Traditional reinforcement learning (RL) frameworks for legged robots often rely on exteroceptive sensing, such as dense visual inputs (e.g., depth maps or point clouds). However, these methods typically suffer from substantial sample inefficiency in simulation due to high memory overhead from rendering dense visuals, which also limits environment parallelism during training. Furthermore, deploying such vision-based policies in the real world often introduces a profound sim-to-real gap, necessitating techniques like privileged two-stage distillation. While some pipelines incorporate visual object detectors to gain semantic understanding, running open-vocabulary trackers on resource-constrained onboard computers typically yields control frequencies too low for dynamic manipulation tasks.

Current perception pipelines aiming for high-frequency state estimation frequently depend on external infrastructure, such as high-performance off-board computation nodes or motion capture systems. These infrastructure-heavy setups, while effective for specialized tasks like robotic sports, restrict the autonomy and mobility of legged systems in everyday environments. Moreover, applying filtering algorithms directly on a walking quadruped for low-frequency visual signals is complicated by the severe ego-motion of the floating base, which can lead to rapid target state estimation drift. This challenge has led many frameworks to avoid continuous floating-base control during manipulation, instead requiring the robot to stop and perform quasi-static arm operations.

Loco-manipulation architectures generally fall into implicit end-to-end learning frameworks or modular decoupled designs. Implicit approaches, such as end-to-end RL or behavior cloning, map observations directly to actions but often suffer from sample inefficiency and heavy reliance on teleoperation. Modular systems typically involve outfitting quadrupedal robots with dedicated robotic arms. While this transfers accuracy burdens to the manipulator, it incurs significant hardware cost, payload constraints, and power overhead. An alternative is mounting a gripper directly onto the base, which demands continuous, highly precise base regulation. Existing systems utilizing this arm-free morphology are often restricted to task-specific dynamic interceptions or rely on global external cameras and low-frequency perception.

SigLoMa is positioned within this landscape by addressing these challenges through an exclusive focus on fully onboard, ego-centric vision for dynamic, floating-base loco-manipulation. It aims to eliminate the need for external motion capture, off-board computation, and additional robotic arms, while simultaneously bridging the perception-control frequency gap and mitigating the sim-to-real transfer issues associated with visual perception.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this work is to develop a fully onboard, ego-centric vision-based framework, named SigLoMa, that enables dynamic quadrupedal loco-manipulation in unstructured, open-world environments. This overarching goal is motivated by several specific challenges and aims:

1. **Eliminate External Dependencies:** A core motivation is to free quadrupedal robots from reliance on expensive external motion capture systems and high-performance off-board computational nodes. This enables true autonomy and expanded deployment in diverse, uninstrumented environments.
2. **Bridge Perception-Control Frequency Gap:** Onboard open-vocabulary visual object detectors typically operate at low frequencies (e.g., 5 Hz), which fundamentally conflicts with the high-frequency demands (e.g., 50 Hz) of precise floating-base control on a dynamic quadruped. The system aims to bridge this temporal divide to enable real-time, responsive manipulation.
3. **Manage Visual Latency and Ego-Motion:** Visual tracking inherently introduces latency. For a robot with a floating base, this latency, combined with severe ego-motion, can lead to significant state estimation errors and grasp failures. The objective is to provide robust, low-latency target state estimation despite the robot's own movement.
4. **Enhance Sim-to-Real Transfer for Vision:** Traditional dense visual inputs in simulation are computationally expensive and prone to large sim-to-real gaps. The work seeks a lightweight, robust visual representation that facilitates efficient simulation training and generalizes well to real-world deployment with sparse semantic information.
5. **Improve RL Sample Efficiency and Learning Robustness:** Long-horizon loco-manipulation tasks often present sparse rewards and sample inefficiency in RL. The aim is to develop a learning framework that accelerates convergence and enables the policy to acquire robust, object-specific manipulation strategies without extensive reward engineering.
6. **Address Visual Blind Spots:** During close-range manipulation, especially grasping, target objects can move out of the camera's field of view, leading to temporary visual occlusion and potential drift in state estimation. The system aims to maintain manipulation precision through these blind spots.
7. **Support Open-Vocabulary Perception:** The framework is designed to interface with high-level Vision Language Models (VLMs) for prompt-driven, open-vocabulary target detection, enabling the robot to interact with a wide variety of everyday items specified by natural language.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

SigLoMa is structured as a hierarchical and adaptive framework designed to address the challenges of onboard, ego-centric vision-based loco-manipulation. The pipeline consists of three core pillars: an efficient exteroceptive state representation, an ego-centric state estimation mechanism, and a robust reinforcement learning framework.

#### 3.1. Hierarchical System Overview

The process begins with user language commands processed by a Vision Language Model (VLM) to identify pick and place targets. These targets are then fed to an onboard open-vocabulary tracking model, which generates low-frequency semantic masks. These raw masks are then distilled into a sparse set of geometric features called Sigma Points. A Kalman Filter (KF) processes these sparse features to provide high-frequency, low-latency target state estimates, which are subsequently routed to an RL policy network. The policy generates continuous velocity and posture commands for the robot's base, which are executed by a pre-trained low-level controller.

#### 3.2. Exteroceptive State Representation via Sigma Points

To achieve computational efficiency and effective object geometry representation, SigLoMa introduces **Sigma Points**. This representation is a sparse, mathematically derived set of features designed to encapsulate an object's bounding volume and spatial orientation.
- **Extraction:** In simulation, dense object point clouds are first processed to extract a "visible point set" by applying back-face culling and Field-of-View (FoV) constraints. A geometry-aware weight is assigned to each visible point to emulate real-world camera pixel distribution, accounting for differential solid angles. Weighted Principal Component Analysis (PCA) is then applied to this weighted point set to compute the centroid and spatial covariance matrix.
- **Representation:** From the eigendecomposition of the covariance matrix, eigenvalues and eigenvectors define the principal axes. Sigma Points are generated by sampling two points along each principal axis, in addition to the geometric centroid, resulting in a set of seven 3D points.
- **Sim-to-Real Alignment:** The weighted PCA procedure ensures that the statistical distribution of Sigma Points generated in simulation aligns with those derived from real-world pixel-level observations (back-projected semantic masks), contributing to the sim-to-real invariance of the downstream RL policy.

#### 3.3. Ego-Centric State Estimation via Kalman Filtering

To bridge the frequency gap between low-rate visual observations (e.g., 5 Hz from object trackers) and high-frequency control demands (e.g., 50 Hz), an **ego-centric Kalman Filter (KF)** is employed. This filter operates locally within the camera frame, explicitly compensating for camera ego-motion using Visual Odometry (VO).
- **State Definition:** For each Sigma Point, the system state is defined as a 6D vector in the current camera frame, comprising its 3D position and relative velocity.
- **Prediction Step:** The state transition is decoupled into point motion prediction and camera ego-motion compensation. A linear kinematic approximation predicts the short-term displacement of each Sigma Point relative to the previous camera frame. Simultaneously, the filter rectifies shifts caused by the robot's base movement by transforming the predicted point states into the new camera perspective using the relative rotation and translation provided by VO.
- **Measurement Update:** When a new visual observation (i.e., newly computed Sigma Points from PCA) becomes available, it serves as a measurement update to correct the predicted states. The measurement model is linear, isolating the positional subspace of the state. The measurement covariance matrix is dynamically scaled based on the estimated Z-axis depth of the observed point, degrading trust in more distant and thus noisier observations. This approach ensures robust, low-delay geometric state estimations without relying on a rigid global coordinate system.

#### 3.4. Policy Formulation and Robust Learning Framework

The high-level RL policy maps observations to actions at a 50 Hz control rate, utilizing Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO).
- **Task Taxonomy:** The problem is formulated across a discrete task space, including picking operations for long-axis objects (requiring an orthogonal approach, `τ_long`), short-axis objects (`τ_short`), and a universal placing operation (`τ_release`).
- **Observation Space:** Consists of proprioceptive state (base-projected gravity, linear/angular velocities, previous action, task phase flag) and exteroceptive state (Sigma Points). The Sigma Points are processed by a dual-horizon architecture: a short-horizon encoder (for high-frequency history to combat noise) and a long-horizon encoder (for low-frequency history to provide memory during blind spots).
- **Action Space:** The Actor network outputs continuous base velocities (v_x, v_y, ω_z) and body pitch commands (θ_y), which are then executed by a pre-trained low-level controller.
- **Trajectory Shaping and Active Sampling:**
 - **Hint Poses:** To address sparse rewards and poor constraint of approach trajectories, intermediate "hint poses" are introduced. These orientational waypoints, strategically placed midway between the initial state and the optimal terminal pose, implicitly regularize policy rollouts and guide the robot towards object-specific approach trajectories.
 - **Active Sampling Curriculum (ASC):** This curriculum dynamically shifts training focus. Initially, objects are spawned in close proximity to the optimal pose ("near-optimal initialization"), providing dense rewards to bootstrap learning. As the policy's success rate increases, this "feeding" mechanism exponentially decays, and the curriculum transitions towards "failure-replay sampling," where past failed episodes are prioritized. This mechanism helps overcome the exploration barrier and aggressively targets bottleneck behaviors.
- **Memory-Augmented Blind-Spot Handling:**
 - **Simulated KF Drift:** To prepare the policy for real-world visual blind spots (when the target moves out of FoV during grasping), a cumulative, clamped random-walk noise is injected into the true Sigma Points during simulation whenever visual tracking is lost. This simulates the open-loop drift of the KF.
 - **Temporal Memory Network:** A Temporal Convolutional Network (TCN) processes a lightweight 2-second history of down-sampled (5 Hz) perception memory. This TCN effectively encapsulates spatiotemporal context, allowing the policy to maintain stable posture and precise manipulation even when the target is temporarily occluded. The TCN's feedforward structure is considered more robust to non-stationary RL data than recursive networks like GRUs.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy of SigLoMa was evaluated through extensive simulation studies and real-world hardware deployments.

#### 4.1. Simulation Setup and Ablation Studies

The RL policy was trained using Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) in Isaac Gym, leveraging 4096 parallel environments. The training incorporated a diverse set of objects, including geometric primitives and 27 items from the YCB dataset, with randomized initial 6-DoF poses for both the robot and objects. The system achieved policy convergence in approximately 6 hours on a single NVIDIA RTX 4090 GPU, indicating sample efficiency.

Ablation studies were conducted to assess the contribution of SigLoMa's core algorithmic components (Table 1):
- **Hint Poses and Active Sampling Curriculum (ASC):** Removing hint poses ("No Hint") or the ASC ("No ASC") significantly degraded performance, especially for long-axis grasping (e.g., water bottle, conditioner, shampoo). Without these, the policy struggled to learn and execute the required perpendicular approach trajectories, resulting in success rates as low as 12-16% for long-axis objects, compared to SigLoMa's 83-87%. This indicates that these mechanisms are critical for learning complex, long-horizon manipulation behaviors.
- **Temporal Memory Network (TCN):** Ablating the TCN encoder ("No TCN") or replacing it with an MLP ("Enc-MLP") or a GRU ("Enc-GRU") demonstrably reduced grasping success rates. The "No TCN" variant showed a drop of about 6-8% in success for various tasks, while "Enc-GRU" performed substantially worse, particularly for long-axis objects (e.g., 67-72% success vs. SigLoMa's 83-87%). This finding supports the importance of retaining historical visual observations and the TCN's robustness in providing effective temporal representations, especially in the context of non-stationary RL data and managing structural blind spots.
- **Emergent Behaviors:** During simulation testing, the agent was observed to exhibit active perception behaviors, such as maintaining a "head-up" pitch at a distance to maximize its field of view, performing granular lateral corrections when in proximity to the target, and executing a smooth deceleration into the visual blind spot to minimize internal Kalman Filter drift.

#### 4.2. Real-World Hardware Deployment

SigLoMa was deployed on a Unitree Go2 quadruped equipped with a RealSense D435i camera, a custom low-cost gripper, and an onboard NVIDIA Jetson Orin NX. Visual Odometry (VO) was provided by Isaac ROS Visual SLAM at 60 Hz. The perception stack utilized the Qwen 3.5 VLM API for zero-shot semantic mask extraction, which then initialized Cutie for continuous onboard target tracking at 5 Hz.

Real-world experiments involved three pick-and-place tasks: ball-to-bin (short-axis pick), toy-to-hamper (short-axis pick), and water bottle-to-box (long-axis pick), each followed by a release task. Performance was documented over 10 trials per setting, recording first-attempt end-to-end success counts (Figure 3).
- **Ego-Centric Kalman Filter (KF):** Ablation studies confirmed the indispensability of the ego-centric KF in real-world deployments. It effectively compensated for visual processing latency and bridged the low-frequency vision backbone, ensuring real-time locomotion reactivity and stability.
- **Blind-Spot Random-Walk Noise (BS-Noise):** Injecting random-walk noise during simulation training was found to be crucial. This augmentation modeled the open-loop prediction drift of the KF during visual occlusions, explicitly reducing the sim-to-real gap and enabling robust policy execution even within visual blind spots during grasping.
- **Overall Performance:** The fully onboard SigLoMa autonomous pipeline achieved highly precise floating-base maneuvers. Its end-to-end success counts for the tested tasks were comparable to those achieved through expert human teleoperation, demonstrating the practical effectiveness of the proposed framework.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research presented in SigLoMa introduces a framework for quadrupedal loco-manipulation that addresses several critical limitations in robotic autonomy. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Enabling Infrastructure-Free Autonomy:** By eliminating the need for external motion capture systems and off-board high-performance computing, SigLoMa advances the goal of truly autonomous robots. This expands the operational envelope of quadrupedal robots, allowing deployment in a wider range of unstructured environments (e.g., homes, warehouses, disaster sites) where such infrastructure is unavailable or impractical.
2. **Overcoming Vision-Control Latency:** The ego-centric Kalman Filter is a notable contribution that effectively bridges the inherent frequency gap between slow, onboard semantic perception and the high-frequency demands of dynamic floating-base control. This enables responsive and precise manipulation actions despite the latency and low update rates of vision systems.
3. **Enhanced Sim-to-Real Transfer:** The introduction of Sigma Points as a lightweight, category-agnostic geometric representation, combined with geometry-aware weighting and the simulation of Kalman Filter drift through random-walk noise, provides robust sim-to-real alignment. This allows for efficient training in simulation and reliable transfer to complex real-world conditions.
4. **Robust Learning for Complex Tasks:** The Active Sampling Curriculum and hint poses address the challenges of sparse rewards and sample inefficiency in long-horizon RL tasks. These learning mechanisms enable the policy to acquire complex, object-specific manipulation strategies, such as adapting approach trajectories for long-axis objects, with reduced engineering effort.
5. **Cost-Effective Solution:** By relying solely on onboard computation, a low-cost gripper, and the robot's existing vision system (e.g., a RealSense D435i camera), SigLoMa presents a comparatively low-cost hardware solution for advanced manipulation capabilities on quadrupedal platforms. This reduces the barrier to entry for deploying such systems.
6. **Broader Applications:** The capabilities demonstrated, such as dynamic pick-and-place with open-vocabulary object recognition, suggest potential applications in various fields, including logistics, domestic assistance, search and rescue, and inspection tasks where interaction with diverse objects is required.
7. **Foundation for Future Work:** The explicit identification of limitations, such as current constraint to flat surfaces, lack of long-horizon navigation, and susceptibility to perception instability, provides clear directions for future research. Addressing these could further extend the framework's capabilities towards more general and robust real-world deployment.
