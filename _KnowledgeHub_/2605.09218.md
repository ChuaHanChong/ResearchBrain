# Flame3D: Zero-shot Compositional Reasoning of 3D Scenes with Agentic Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Carnegie Mellon University researchers developed Flame3D, a training-free framework that enables agentic language models to perform compositional reasoning on 3D scenes using a structured visual-textual memory and dynamically generated spatial operations. This approach achieves competitive performance on ScanQA and successfully addresses complex multi-hop 3D queries on the new Compose3D benchmark, demonstrating spatial understanding without 3D-specific training.

## Problem

- Current 3D scene understanding methods struggle with compositional reasoning, including understanding free space, hypothetical object insertions, and integrating external knowledge.
- Finetuned 3D Large Multimodal Models (3D-LMMs) require extensive and costly paired 3D–language training data, leading to limited generalization and potential catastrophic forgetting.
- Existing zero-shot approaches primarily address object grounding, failing to meet the demands of open-ended and complex queries about 3D environments.

## Method

- A structured visual-textual 3D scene memory is constructed from raw RGB-D scans, incorporating 3D instance segmentation, spatial attributes, and VLM-generated textual descriptions.
- This memory is exposed to a tool-calling Multimodal Large Language Model (MLLM) via composable spatial tools (e.g., Search, Distance) and a meta-tool ("Execute program") for dynamic code synthesis.
- An MLLM performs multi-hop compositional reasoning in a ReAct-style loop, chaining tool calls and integrating external knowledge to provide grounded answers to natural language queries.

## Results

- Flame3D achieved competitive performance on the ScanQA benchmark, matching or exceeding several trained 3D-LMMs in metrics like BLEU-4 and METEOR.
- The framework demonstrated robust multi-hop 3D reasoning on Compose3D, a benchmark designed to prevent reliance on language priors, successfully addressing queries about free space and hypothetical object insertions.
- Ablation studies confirmed the necessity of the "Execute program" meta-tool for open-ended queries and showed that direct visual input is often not required for spatial reasoning due to detailed scene memory captions.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Genuine 3D understanding does not strictly require training on 3D–language data, as zero-shot frameworks can achieve comparable performance.
> - Expressive meta-tools that enable dynamic synthesis of spatial and logical operations are essential for handling open-ended 3D queries, as fixed toolsets are insufficient.
> - The framework's capabilities scale naturally with improvements in underlying general-purpose Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs), without requiring 3D-specific architectural changes.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in "Flame3D" addresses the field of 3D scene understanding, particularly focusing on compositional reasoning and the integration of diverse information sources to answer complex natural language queries about 3D environments. This area is critical for advancements in robotics, augmented reality, and intelligent assistants.

Existing approaches to 3D scene understanding broadly fall into several categories. One significant line of research involves finetuned 3D Large Multimodal Models (3D-LMMs). These models treat 3D data, often represented as tokenized point clouds, as a new modality aligned into the latent space of a large language model (LLM). While aiming for broad generalization, this approach typically requires extensive and expensive paired 3D–language training data. Concerns have been raised regarding their limited compositional generalization beyond the training distribution and the potential for catastrophic forgetting. Recent studies have also questioned the extent to which point-cloud tokens genuinely enhance LLM spatial reasoning or if finetuned 3D-LMMs truly comprehend 3D spatial relationships.

Another approach utilizes visual feature fields, which project pretrained embeddings, such as those from CLIP, into 3D space. While providing 3D representations, a limitation of these methods is that the semantics are largely frozen at construction time, resulting in restricted editability of 3D semantics—a feature often necessary in practical applications.

A growing trend involves zero-shot methods that leverage off-the-shelf multimodal large language models (MLLMs). These approaches typically avoid 3D-specific training by presenting the scene through 2D views (e.g., keyframes, bird’s-eye maps, active camera manipulation) or by orchestrating pre-defined 3D operations (e.g., hand-coded geometric operators, scene graphs). However, these zero-shot methods have primarily concentrated on object grounding and object-centric reasoning, often falling short of broader 3D scene understanding requirements. The paper argues that comprehensive 3D understanding necessitates reasoning about free space, hypothetical object insertions, complex geometric relationships, and seamless integration with external knowledge sources.

Flame3D positions itself within this landscape as a training-free, zero-shot framework that aims to achieve broad generalization and handle open-ended queries without the need for 3D-specific training or the limitations of existing zero-shot methods. It seeks to bridge the gap by extending the compositional reasoning capabilities of modern MLLMs to complex 3D scenes through a structured, editable scene memory and dynamically synthesizable spatial operations. The work explores whether a rich, explicit scene memory and strong compositional abstractions can serve as an effective alternative to scaling 3D-native architectures for achieving advanced 3D scene understanding.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for Flame3D stems from the observed limitations of current 3D scene understanding methods in addressing the full spectrum of practical queries. Real-world 3D scene understanding tasks frequently demand compositional reasoning, combining spatial relationships, physical layouts, visual attributes, and external knowledge. For example, a query such as "Suggest a bookshelf that matches my interior and fits next to the TV" requires synthesizing information about spatial positioning ("next to the TV"), available volume ("free space"), visual aesthetics ("matches my interior"), and external product catalogs ("suggest a bookshelf"). Crucially, such reasoning often extends beyond merely identifying existing objects to encompassing free spaces for potential object insertions or modifications.

The paper identifies several key challenges that existing 3D understanding methods struggle to address:
- **Compositional Reasoning:** Chaining multiple inferences across different types of information (spatial, semantic, physical, external).
- **Free-Space Reasoning:** Understanding and utilizing empty volumes within a scene, for tasks like navigation or accommodating new objects.
- **Hypothetical Object Insertions:** Imagining and reasoning about new spatial configurations with objects not currently present in the scene.
- **Complex Geometric Relationships:** Moving beyond simple object-centric relations to more intricate geometric analyses.
- **External Knowledge Integration:** Seamlessly incorporating information from sources outside the immediate scene data, such as shopping catalogs, compliance databases, or personal preferences.
- **Cost and Generalization of 3D-LMMs:** The high cost of acquiring paired 3D–language training data, the limited compositional generalization of finetuned models beyond their training distribution, and the risk of catastrophic forgetting.

Given these challenges, the central question driving this research is whether the compositional reasoning capabilities inherent in modern large language models, proficient in visual and textual domains, can be effectively leveraged and extended to 3D scene reasoning without requiring 3D-specific training.

Based on this motivation, the key objectives of the Flame3D project are:
1. **Develop a training-free framework:** Propose a zero-shot approach that achieves broad generalization for 3D scene understanding at inference time, obviating the need for expensive 3D-specific training.
2. **Represent scenes as editable visual-textual 3D memories:** Create a structured scene representation that is accessible to off-the-shelf MLLMs and allows for dynamic updates or additions of external data without retraining.
3. **Expose memory through composable spatial tools:** Design a systematic set of spatial and visual abstractions (tools) that enable an MLLM to interact with the structured scene memory.
4. **Enable agentic synthesis of custom spatial programs:** Provide meta-tools that allow the MLLM to generate and execute custom code at inference time, thereby extending the framework's capabilities to open-ended and long-tail queries involving layouts, empty space, and hypothetical objects.
5. **Evaluate compositional 3D reasoning:** Assess the framework's performance on both standard 3D QA benchmarks (ScanQA) and a newly curated benchmark (Compose3D) specifically designed to test multi-hop, complex spatial reasoning that cannot be resolved by language priors alone.
6. **Investigate the necessity of agent-synthesized operations:** Determine if fixed toolsets are sufficient for open-ended 3D queries or if the agent's ability to synthesize spatial operations dynamically is essential.
7. **Challenge conventional wisdom:** Explore whether explicit, zero-shot frameworks with rich scene memories and strong compositional abstractions can effectively address the need for broad generalization and open-ended queries in 3D scene understanding, potentially offering an alternative or complement to purely 3D-native architectural scaling.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Flame3D operates as a training-free framework that processes posed RGB-D frames of an indoor scene and a natural-language query to produce a grounded answer. The methodology is structured into three main stages: constructing a structured visual-textual scene memory, exposing this memory via spatial and visual abstractions, and facilitating agentic reasoning using a tool-calling MLLM.

#### 3.1. Structured Visual-Textual Scene Memory

The first stage converts raw RGB-D scans into a persistent, queryable representation without 3D-specific fine-tuning.
1. **3D Reconstruction:** Standard techniques are applied to generate a 3D reconstruction of the scene from posed RGB-D frames.
2. **3D Instance Segmentation (Appendix A):**
 - **Object Inventory:** An off-the-shelf vision-language model (VLM, e.g., GPT-4o-mini) is prompted to identify tangible objects in each RGB frame, generating a per-frame list of labels.
 - **Label Normalization:** Labels undergo lemmatization and are embedded using OpenCLIP. Agglomerative clustering with cosine distance groups synonymous labels, and a canonical representative is chosen for each cluster. Temporal hole filling is applied to ensure label consistency across frames.
 - **SAM3 Video Segmentation:** For each normalized object label within its contiguous frame subsequences, SAM3 (a text-prompted video segmentation model) is used to generate temporally consistent 2D instance masks with tracking IDs.
 - **2D-to-3D Mask Association:** The generated 2D masks are projected into 3D using the COLMAP model built from the RGB-D input. 3D feature points are associated with masks, and DBSCAN is applied to remove spatial outliers from the accumulated 3D point IDs for each instance.
 - **Mask Connectivity Graph:** A graph is constructed to merge duplicate observations of the same physical entity appearing across different labels or temporal segments. Nodes represent unique instance triples (sequence, object-slug, SAM3 obj-id) with their 3D points. Edges are formed between nodes if their 3D voxel-occupancy Jaccard similarity exceeds a threshold and their OpenCLIP image embeddings are visually similar (CLIP semantic guard). A constrained union-find prevents merging explicitly distinct SAM3 instances.
 - **Component Cleaning:** A final DBSCAN pass refines the 3D points for each connected component, discarding noise and optionally splitting components with multiple distinct clusters.
3. **3D Spatial Database:** For each resulting persistent 3D component, the following attributes are computed and stored:
 - Axis-aligned 3D bounding box and centroid coordinates.
 - Representative image crops (selected from viewpoints with best visibility).
 - A textual description generated by captioning the selected crops with an off-the-shelf VLM. These captions capture object identity, visual attributes (color, material), and local spatial context.
 The components and their attributes are then organized into a PostGIS spatial database, indexed by both 3D coordinates and textual annotations. This textual nature allows arbitrary non-visual attributes (e.g., price, compliance) to be appended without retraining.

#### 3.2. Spatial and Visual Abstractions

Flame3D exposes the structured scene memory to an MLLM through a set of composable tool-call abstractions, each encapsulating a specific operation.
1. **Spatial Tools:**
 - `Search`: Retrieves components whose textual descriptions match a natural-language query using BM25.
 - `Distance`: Computes the Euclidean distance between the 3D centroids of two specified components.
 - `Navigation distance`: Calculates the traversable path length between two components on the reconstructed floor plane, avoiding obstacles.
 - `Vicinity search`: Returns all components within a specified radius of a given component.
 - `Get image`: Retrieves representative image crops for a specified component for visual inspection by the MLLM.
 - `Execute program` (Meta-tool): This is a crucial meta-abstraction allowing the agent to write and execute arbitrary Python code. This enables dynamic synthesis of novel spatial or logical operations at inference time, such as verifying object insertion fits or computing precise geometric relations (e.g., projections).
2. **External Tools:** Flame3D can interface with external data sources (e.g., compliance databases, machine profiles, pricing catalogs) by integrating their attributes as text into the scene memory, allowing the MLLM to reason over them using the same abstractions.

#### 3.3. Agentic Reasoning

A tool-calling MLLM performs multi-hop compositional reasoning using a ReAct-style loop.
1. **System Prompt:** The MLLM receives the natural-language query along with a system prompt detailing the available tools, their signatures, and the structure of the scene memory. The prompt encourages strategic planning of tool calls.
2. **Iterative Tool Use:** In each step, the MLLM either generates a tool call or provides a final answer. The results of tool calls are appended to the agent's context, enabling complex multi-hop reasoning chains (e.g., searching for outlets, then querying their vicinity for combustible materials, then computing distances).
3. **Grounded Output:** The final answer explicitly references specific 3D scene components using their unique identifiers (e.g., `<component_ID>text</component_ID>`), grounding the response in the 3D environment and enabling downstream visualization.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of Flame3D was conducted using two primary datasets: ScanQA and a newly introduced benchmark, Compose3D.

#### 4.1. Datasets

- **ScanQA:** A standard 3D question answering benchmark based on real-world indoor RGB-D scans from ScanNet. It contains diverse questions about object attributes, locations, counts, and relationships, with answers grounded in 3D object annotations.
- **Compose3D:** A new, curated benchmark developed by the authors to specifically evaluate complex, multi-hop 3D reasoning. It was designed to prevent models from relying solely on learned language priors, which is a known issue with existing benchmarks. Compose3D questions are authored on ScanNet++ validation scenes, with answers strictly grounded in 3D space by tagging entities. Questions are categorized into: Entity Search, Spatial Relations, Affordance, Functionality, and Physics/Safety.

#### 4.2. Qualitative Examples

Figure 5 illustrates Flame3D's ability to answer diverse spatial, visual, and multi-hop queries by actively chaining spatial tools, visual inspection, and external knowledge retrieval. Examples include:
- **Precise Geometric Queries:** Using the `Execute program` tool to determine the fractional position of a safety valve along a superheater outlet or verify if sprinkler spacing meets a safety code.
- **Contextual Reasoning:** Chaining `Search` and `Vicinity search` to find a resting spot near first-aid supplies for an injured person.
- **External Knowledge Integration:** Querying the web for a robotic arm's length and comparing it against computed 3D distances between objects to determine if it can reach a whiteboard.
- **Visual Verification and Compliance:** Using the `Get image` tool to visually inspect the state of a trash bin or retrieving regulations to determine waste sorting rules.

#### 4.3. Quantitative Results and Insights

**Performance on ScanQA:**
Table 1 and 2 present Flame3D's performance on the ScanQA validation set, comparing it against expert 3D QA models, 3D LMMs, and other zero-shot 2D LMM-based methods.
- Flame3D achieved competitive performance, often matching or exceeding methods specifically trained or fine-tuned on 3D-language data (e.g., CVP, LEO, Video-3D-LLM) in several metrics. Notably, its BLEU-4 and METEOR scores were significantly higher than other methods, while CIDEr, ROUGE, and EM scores were competitive.
- The paper notes that Flame3D often generates contextually valid answers that are penalized due to ambiguity in the ScanQA ground truth, suggesting its true capacity for 3D understanding might be stronger than indicated by some quantitative metrics.

**Insights from Compose3D and Ablation Studies:**

1. **Genuine 3D understanding may not require training on 3D–language data:** Flame3D's competitive performance on ScanQA, a benchmark typically addressed by trained 3D-LMMs, indicates that zero-shot methods can perform comparably, suggesting that explicit 3D-specific training might not be a strict prerequisite for genuine 3D spatial understanding.

2. **Fixed toolsets are insufficient for open-ended 3D queries; expressive meta-tools are essential:** Ablation studies (Figure 8) show incremental performance gains with the progressive addition of spatial tools (`No Tools` -> `Search Only` -> `+Distance` -> `+Vicinity Search` -> `+Image` -> `+Exec`). This demonstrates that a predefined, fixed set of tools is inadequate for the diversity of multi-hop 3D queries. The `Execute program` meta-tool, enabling dynamic synthesis of custom geometric and logical operations, was found to be essential.

3. **Visual input is often not required during spatial reasoning:** For a majority of spatial queries, direct visual input was not strictly necessary. This is attributed to the detailed captions generated offline for the scene memory, which capture sufficient semantic attributes. However, visual access remains important for specific long-tail or rare queries requiring fine-grained visual inspection (e.g., identifying specific plant species).

4. **Sole reliance on language priors fails for genuine 3D reasoning:** The "No Tools" baseline, where the agent relies only on language priors without spatial tools, performed poorly on Compose3D (Figure 8). This contrasts with observations on traditional benchmarks like ScanQA, where language priors can sometimes suffice for answering questions. Compose3D's design effectively required explicit spatial memory and reasoning.

5. **Meta tool is surprisingly powerful but not sufficient:** An "Execute Only" baseline, where the agent relied solely on the `Execute program` meta-tool (which implicitly has access to other spatial tools except `Get Image`), showed strong performance (Figure 10). This indicates the high expressiveness of the meta-tool in subsuming many geometric operations. However, the full suite of tools still yielded better results, suggesting that a hybrid approach reduces cognitive load, minimizes programming errors, and covers functionalities like image retrieval that the meta-tool cannot directly replicate. Practical considerations regarding privacy and security also favor a hybrid toolset.

6. **Scaling foundation models improves spatial reasoning:** Evaluations across different underlying foundation models (Figure 9) demonstrated that larger and more modern MLLMs consistently lead to superior spatial reasoning and object grounding capabilities. This trend is advantageous for Flame3D, as its performance scales naturally with advancements in general-purpose foundation models without requiring 3D-specific architectural changes or retraining.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Flame3D framework introduces a zero-shot, training-free approach to compositional 3D scene understanding that offers several significant contributions and potential impacts:

1. **Challenging Conventional Wisdom:** Flame3D demonstrates that complex, open-ended, and long-tailed reasoning over 3D scenes can be effectively achieved without specialized 3D-specific training. This finding challenges the prevailing paradigm that often necessitates expensive data collection and dedicated architectural fine-tuning for 3D-native models. Instead, it suggests that leveraging existing, powerful off-the-shelf MLLMs with a carefully designed scene representation and toolset is a viable and competitive alternative.

2. **Enhanced Generalization and Open-ended Reasoning:** By relying on agentic language models and their inherent compositional reasoning abilities, Flame3D enables a broad range of queries, including those involving free space, hypothetical object insertions, and complex geometric relationships that extend beyond object-centric grounding. This capability for open-ended reasoning is crucial for real-world applications where queries are often diverse and unstructured.

3. **Flexible and Editable Scene Representation:** The structured visual-textual scene memory is inherently editable. This means that scene information, including semantic and non-visual attributes (e.g., compliance status, prices), can be updated or corrected without requiring any retraining or re-encoding of the scene. This flexibility contrasts with feature field approaches where semantics are often frozen at construction time and finetuned 3D-LMMs that require re-training for significant updates.

4. **Scalability with Foundation Model Improvements:** A notable advantage of Flame3D is its inherent scalability. As the underlying general-purpose MLLMs improve, Flame3D's capabilities naturally enhance without any architectural changes or 3D-specific updates. This allows the framework to benefit directly from ongoing advancements in large language models.

5. **Practical Tool-Augmented AI:** The framework's emphasis on composable spatial and meta-tools, particularly the `Execute program` abstraction, highlights the power of agentic LLMs in synthesizing custom logic at inference time. This capability is critical for addressing long-tail queries and highly specific geometric or logical operations that cannot be covered by a fixed set of predefined tools. It showcases a robust paradigm for tool-augmented AI in complex perceptual tasks.

6. **Potential for Broader Applications:** The ability to perform fine-grained spatial reasoning, integrate external knowledge, and handle hypothetical scenarios opens doors for applications in various domains. These include:
 - **Embodied AI and Robotics:** Enabling robots to understand complex spatial instructions, reason about navigation, and manipulate objects within dynamic environments.
 - **Augmented Reality (AR):** Providing intelligent context and interaction capabilities for AR applications, such as interior design (e.g., "will this furniture fit here?"), maintenance, or guided assembly.
 - **Intelligent Assistants:** Developing more sophisticated AI assistants that can answer spatial questions about a user's environment, provide safety recommendations, or help with inventory management.
 - **Safety and Compliance:** Automatically assessing scenes for safety hazards or compliance with regulations by reasoning about spatial relationships and integrating external databases.

7. **Future Research Direction:** The findings suggest that future progress in 3D scene understanding may benefit significantly from focusing on developing richer explicit scene memories and stronger compositional abstractions for zero-shot reasoning. This perspective encourages exploring hybrid approaches that might combine these explicit memory and tool-use strategies with advancements in 3D-native architectures, rather than exclusively pursuing the latter.

**Limitations:** The primary limitation identified is the dependency of the overall performance on the initial scene memory construction process. If the underlying 2D vision-language models and segmentation tools fail to accurately detect or segment certain entities during this stage, the agent will be unable to reason about them. However, the authors anticipate continuous rapid development in these foundational 2D vision-language models and segmentation tools, which would naturally mitigate this limitation over time.

In conclusion, Flame3D offers a compelling demonstration of how sophisticated 3D spatial reasoning can be achieved through a training-free, agentic framework. Its emphasis on structured memory, composable tools, and inference-time program synthesis provides a robust and flexible approach that challenges existing paradigms and points toward new avenues for advancing 3D scene understanding.
