# Continual World: A Robotic Benchmark For Continual Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> The "Continual World" benchmark introduces a computationally accessible suite of robotic manipulation tasks for Continual Reinforcement Learning (CL-RL). This work demonstrates that while most existing CL methods effectively mitigate catastrophic forgetting, they largely fail to achieve positive forward transfer, with PackNet achieving the highest average performance of 0.80 on the CW20 sequence while maintaining positive transfer.

## Problem

- Deep learning models exhibit catastrophic forgetting when continuously learning new tasks in dynamic, non-stationary environments, a critical limitation for autonomous agents like robots.
- Existing continual reinforcement learning (CL-RL) benchmarks are either computationally prohibitive or fail to adequately capture the complexities and inter-task relationships needed to study knowledge transfer effectively.
- The CL research community has overly emphasized catastrophic forgetting prevention, often overlooking or failing to measure positive forward transfer—the ability to leverage prior knowledge to learn new tasks more efficiently.

## Method

- Introduces **Continual World**, a new benchmark for ==continual reinforcement learning== based on 10 or 20 robotic manipulation tasks from **Meta-World**, designed to be computationally accessible.
- The benchmark includes specific ==task sequences== (CW10, CW20, Triplets) ordered to assess varying levels of ==positive and negative transfer== between tasks.
- Provides a standardized evaluation framework with implementations of ==seven diverse continual learning methods== integrated with the ==Soft Actor-Critic (SAC)== algorithm, making the benchmark and code publicly available.

## Results

- PackNet achieved the highest average performance, with a **0.80** success rate on the CW20 benchmark, outperforming all other continual learning methods and even multi-task learning approaches.
- Most regularization-based methods (e.g., L2, EWC, MAS, VCL) effectively mitigated catastrophic forgetting (F ≈ **0.00-0.02**) but concurrently exhibited substantial negative forward transfer, meaning prior learning actively hindered subsequent task acquisition.
- Only Fine-tuning (**0.20**) and PackNet (**0.19**) demonstrated positive forward transfer on CW20, highlighting a significant gap as both remain considerably below the estimated optimal Reference Transfer (**0.46**).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Existing continual learning methods, while effective at preventing catastrophic forgetting, generally fail to achieve positive forward transfer in robotic reinforcement learning settings, often leading to negative transfer.
> - Regularizing the critic network is detrimental to performance in continual reinforcement learning; allowing the critic to adapt freely to the current task is crucial, even if it means 'forgetting' past value functions.
> - The performance of replay-based methods like A-GEM and Perfect Memory, which are strong in supervised CL, significantly diminishes in RL-CL contexts due to factors such as high-variance gradients and replay buffer management challenges.
