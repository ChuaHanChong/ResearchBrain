# GameFactory: Creating New Games with Generative Interactive Videos

> [!summary] Summary
> GameFactory is a framework that generates interactive game videos by leveraging large-scale pre-trained video diffusion models. It enables action-controllable and scene-generalizable game content creation, moving beyond style-locked game-specific engines.

## Problem

- Existing generative game engines lack scene generalization, often overfitting to specific game styles and physics, limiting the creation of diverse new games.
- Achieving precise action controllability, particularly for both discrete keyboard inputs and continuous mouse movements, within generated videos is a core technical challenge.
- The impracticality of collecting large-scale, unbiased, action-annotated datasets for diverse open-domain game environments hinders generalizable model training.

## Method

- A transformer-based ==latent video diffusion model== forms the core, enhanced with a specialized ==Action Control Module== to integrate granular user inputs.
- The **GF-Minecraft** dataset, custom-collected from Minecraft, provides unbiased action distributions and diverse scene descriptions to facilitate robust action control learning.
- A ==multi-phase training strategy== combined with a ==LoRA domain adapter== decouples game style from action control, allowing for generalization to open-domain scenes by preserving the base model's generative priors.

## Results

- **GameFactory** achieved superior action following and preserved the generative priors of the base model in open-domain scenarios, outperforming one-phase fine-tuning by maintaining higher generation quality and text alignment.
- The GF-Minecraft dataset enabled the model to correctly execute rare or complex actions that models trained on human-biased datasets often failed to perform.
- The framework successfully generated long, continuous interactive videos exceeding **300 frames**, demonstrated implicit collision detection, and showed transferability of learned control to different game types like racing.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decoupling game-specific visual styles from action control mechanisms is critical for enabling generative models to create interactive content in diverse, open-domain scenes.
> - Training data with an unbiased distribution of atomic actions, rather than human-biased gameplay, leads to superior and more robust action following, especially for less frequent actions.
> - Different integration methods for action control module—cross-attention for discrete inputs and concatenation for continuous inputs—optimize the model's ability to interpret and respond to varied user actions.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in "Creating New Games with Generative Interactive Videos" is situated at the intersection of generative artificial intelligence, computer vision, and interactive media, specifically game development.

The broader research landscape has witnessed a rapid acceleration in **video diffusion models**, which have demonstrated impressive capabilities in generating highly realistic and diverse video content. This progress has naturally led researchers to explore their potential as **generative game engines**. Traditional game development is highly labor-intensive, relying on artists and designers to create every asset and scene. Generative AI promises to automate content creation, reduce development costs, and enable "unlimited game content" for players.

Prior research in generative game engines, as highlighted by the authors, primarily focused on **game-specific approaches**. Examples include models trained on DOOM, Atari, CS:GO, Super Mario Bros, or Minecraft. While these efforts established the feasibility of AI-driven game content, they suffered from a critical limitation: **lack of scene generalization capability**. Models trained on specific game environments often overfit to the visual style and physics of those games, failing to generate content beyond their narrow training domain. This severely limits their utility for creating *new* and *diverse* games. Recent works like Genie 2 and Matrix have begun to address control generalization, but often with simpler action spaces or extensive data collection, leaving room for improvement in broader scene generalization with complex controls.

GameFactory directly tackles this **scene generalization challenge**. Instead of relying on vast, impossible-to-collect open-domain action-annotated video datasets, the authors leverage the **generative priors** embedded in large-scale pre-trained video diffusion models (which are trained on abundant open-domain internet videos). The core idea is to combine the powerful open-domain generative capabilities of these models with the action control knowledge learned from a *small-scale*, *unbiased* game-specific dataset. This approach allows the system to transfer learned controls to entirely new, imagined scenes, thereby enabling the creation of novel game experiences beyond fixed styles. This work distinguishes itself by validating on a comprehensive action space (forward/backward/left/right movement, jumping, acceleration/deceleration, mouse movement) and demonstrating effective generalization across diverse open-world scenarios, a significant step towards truly generalizable generative game engines.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind the GameFactory framework are multifaceted, aiming to bridge significant gaps in the current landscape of AI-driven content generation for games:

1. **Develop an Action-Controlled, Scene-Generalizable Game Video Generation Framework:** The overarching goal is to create a system that can generate videos of new game environments that are not only visually diverse and novel but also fully interactive and responsive to player inputs (keyboard and mouse). This capability would fundamentally change how games are developed and played.
2. **Overcome Limitations of Game-Specific Generative Models:** Existing generative game engines often produce content locked into the style and mechanics of the games they were trained on. GameFactory aims to break this dependency, enabling the generation of entirely *new* games with diverse visual styles and environments.
3. **Address the Challenge of Action Controllability:** A core technical challenge in creating interactive game videos is precisely mapping user actions to visual outputs. The framework seeks to enable fine-grained control over both discrete keyboard inputs and continuous mouse movements within generated video sequences.
4. **Leverage Open-Domain Generative Priors:** Recognizing the impracticality of collecting large-scale, action-annotated datasets for every conceivable game scene, the authors are motivated to harness the rich generative capabilities of existing large-scale, pre-trained video diffusion models. The challenge then becomes integrating action control without sacrificing the model's open-domain generalization.
5. **Disentangle Game Style from Action Control:** A crucial motivation is to prevent action control learning from becoming inextricably linked to a specific game's visual style. By decoupling these two aspects, the framework can generalize action control to any new scene generated by the base diffusion model, free from domain-specific stylistic biases.
6. **Enable Unlimited and Autoregressive Game Content Generation:** Traditional video generation models often produce fixed-length outputs. For practical game applications, continuous, unlimited-length video streams are essential. The framework aims to support autoregressive generation, allowing for prolonged interactive experiences.
7. **Reduce Game Development Manual Labor and Foster Creativity:** Ultimately, the motivation extends to revolutionizing game development by significantly reducing the manual effort involved in content creation, thereby empowering developers and players to realize imaginative game scenarios at minimal cost.

In essence, GameFactory is motivated by the vision of a generative game engine that is truly creative, flexible, and interactive, moving beyond mere content replication to facilitate the creation of entirely novel playable worlds.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

GameFactory’s methodology is built upon a transformer-based latent video diffusion model and introduces several key innovations to achieve action-controlled, scene-generalizable game video generation.

#### 3.1. Backbone Model

The framework utilizes a **transformer-based latent video diffusion model** as its core generative engine. This choice is due to the superior quality and longer-duration video generation capabilities of such models, which are believed to implicitly understand real-world physical rules. An encoder compresses video sequences into latent representations, and a decoder reconstructs videos from these latents. During training, noise is added to clean latents, and the model learns to predict this noise, conditioned on prompts and, crucially, action inputs.

#### 3.2. GF-Minecraft Dataset: Addressing Data Challenges

To provide the necessary action-annotated data, the authors developed **GF-Minecraft**, a custom dataset collected from the Minecraft platform. This dataset was designed to overcome limitations of existing human-collected datasets (like VPT), which exhibit human behavioral biases and lack diverse scene descriptions.
- **Accessibility:** Minecraft's comprehensive API facilitated cost-effective, large-scale data collection.
- **Unbiased Actions:** Unlike human gameplay, GF-Minecraft features an unbiased distribution of atomic keyboard and mouse actions, including rare combinations, to ensure robust action control learning.
- **Diverse Scenes with Textual Descriptions:** Videos were captured across varied biomes, weather conditions, and times of day. An efficient multimodal LLM (MiniCPM) was used to annotate these clips with textual descriptions, enabling scene-specific physical dynamics learning.

#### 3.3. Action Control Module: Granular Interaction

The core innovation for action controllability is the **Action Control Module**, seamlessly integrated into the transformer blocks of the video diffusion model. This module differentiates between continuous mouse movements and discrete keyboard inputs:
- **Grouping Actions with a Sliding Window:** To resolve the granularity mismatch between the high-frequency action sequences (rn frames) and the lower-frequency latent features (n+1 frames) due to temporal compression, actions are grouped using a sliding window. This mechanism allows the model to capture the delayed effects of actions (e.g., a jump command affecting multiple subsequent frames).
- **Mouse Movement Control:** Grouped continuous mouse actions are reshaped, repeated, and concatenated with the intermediate features in the transformer, followed by MLP and temporal self-attention layers for integration.
- **Keyboard Action Control:** Grouped discrete keyboard action embeddings undergo positional encoding and are integrated via cross-attention, similar to how text prompts condition the model. This is found to be more effective for categorical control.

#### 3.4. Autoregressive Generation: Enabling Playability

To move beyond fixed-length video outputs and enable continuous, unlimited gameplay, GameFactory employs an **autoregressive generation approach**:
- The model allows for **varying noise levels** across frames, with later frames having more noise but conditioned on earlier, less noisy frames.
- During training, loss computation focuses **only on predicted frames**, treating initial frames as conditions without noise.
- During inference, the model iteratively selects the most recent `k+1` generated frames as conditions to generate `N-k` new frames, thereby enabling **multi-frame generation per step** and continuous video streams.

#### 3.5. Open-Domain Generalization: Style-Action Decoupling and Multi-Phase Training

This is the most critical aspect for achieving scene-generalizable action control. The authors recognized that directly fine-tuning a pre-trained open-domain model with game data would embed the game's visual style, compromising scene generalization. Their solution involves **disentangling game style from action control** using a **domain adapter** and a **multi-phase training strategy**:
- **Style-Action Decoupling with Domain Adapter:** A LoRA (Low-Rank Adaptation) module acts as an independent domain adapter to learn game-specific visual styles. LoRA’s design allows it to be plugged in or removed without altering the original model's open-domain generative priors.
- **Multi-Phase Training Strategy:**
 - **Phase #0: Model Pretraining:** An internal 1B-sized transformer-based text-to-video diffusion model, pre-trained on open-domain data, serves as the powerful foundation.
 - **Phase #1: Tune LoRA to Fit Game Videos:** The pre-trained model is fine-tuned with LoRA on GF-Minecraft to adapt to the game's visual style. This phase focuses solely on style adaptation.
 - **Phase #2: Tune Action Control Module:** With the pre-trained parameters and LoRA frozen, only the action control module is trained using game videos and action signals. By this point, style adaptation has been handled, allowing the model to focus purely on learning style-independent action control.
 - **Phase #3: Inference on Open Domain:** For open-domain generalization, the LoRA weights (responsible for game style) are *removed*. Only the action control module parameters are retained, enabling controlled video generation in diverse, newly generated scenes while preserving the original model's open-domain capabilities.

This meticulously designed methodology allows GameFactory to generate action-controllable videos across a wide range of open-domain scenes, moving beyond the limitations of prior game-specific approaches.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental results validate GameFactory's effectiveness in action-controlled, scene-generalizable video generation, showcasing its advancements over existing methods.

#### 4.1. Action Controllability Performance

- **Ablation Study on Control Mechanisms:** Experiments compared cross-attention and concatenation for discrete keyboard and continuous mouse inputs.
 - For **discrete keyboard inputs**, cross-attention outperformed concatenation, suggesting that similarity-based attention is better suited for categorical signals. Concatenation led to poor action following for keys.
 - For **continuous mouse movements**, concatenation was more effective than cross-attention. This is attributed to cross-attention potentially diminishing the magnitude of continuous control signals.
- **Impact of Mouse Movements:** Metrics like Cam (camera pose distance) and Flow (optical flow) indicated that mouse movements have a larger visual impact on the generated video than keyboard inputs, especially for large movements.
- **Consistent Semantic and Quality Metrics:** CLIP (semantic relevance), FID, and FVD (generation quality) showed minor differences between control methods due to the multi-phase training strategy decoupling style learning, ensuring consistent visual quality across variants.

#### 4.2. Scene Generalization Effectiveness

- **Comparison of Training Strategies:** The multi-phase training strategy was compared against a one-phase approach (direct fine-tuning).
 - **Superior Action Following:** Multi-phase training achieved significantly better action following (lower Cam and Flow scores) in open-domain scenarios, closer to the in-domain baseline, demonstrating effective transfer of control.
 - **Preservation of Generative Priors:** The multi-phase strategy maintained a domain closer to the original pre-trained model (higher Dom metric), preventing "style leakage" where the generated content would adopt the training game's style.
 - **Higher Quality Generation:** It also yielded superior CLIP, FID, and FVD scores, indicating better text-alignment and overall generation quality, with fewer visual artifacts compared to the one-phase approach.
- **Qualitative Demonstrations:** Figure 1 showcases diverse, newly generated game videos in open-domain scenes (cherry blossom forest, minimalist room, snowy mountain, Renaissance palace), all responding to keyboard and mouse inputs. This qualitatively confirms the model's ability to create interactive experiences beyond predefined game environments.

#### 4.3. GF-Minecraft Dataset Advantages

- **Unbiased Action Distribution:** The GF-Minecraft dataset's design, which eliminates human behavioral biases by uniformly distributing atomic actions, proved critical.
- **Improved Action Following for Rare Actions:** Models trained on GF-Minecraft demonstrated superior action control for rarely performed human actions (e.g., jumping in place, moving backward) compared to models trained on the human-biased VPT dataset. The VPT-trained model often failed to execute these actions correctly, defaulting to common behaviors.

#### 4.4. Long Video Generation Capability

- **Optimized Loss Computation:** Ablation studies on long video generation showed that computing losses only on frames requiring prediction (rather than all frames) led to better performance (lower Cam/Flow, higher CLIP, lower FID/FVD). This highlights the efficiency and effectiveness of the proposed autoregressive training approach.
- **Extended Playability:** The model successfully generated long videos exceeding 300 frames, demonstrating its potential for continuous, extended interactive gameplay.

#### 4.5. Qualitative Demonstrations and Insights

- **Collision Detection:** The model exhibited an implicit ability to detect collisions and provide appropriate interaction feedback, causing the agent to stop when encountering obstacles, even with continued input. This suggests learned physical understanding from the dataset.
- **Generalization to Different Game Types (Racing Game):** A compelling example showed the yaw control learned in Minecraft seamlessly transferring to steering control in a racing game scenario. Furthermore, irrelevant actions (e.g., backward movement, sideways movement, pitch adjustments) automatically diminished, demonstrating intelligent adaptation of the action space. This finding suggests a latent, versatile action space.

In summary, the results strongly support GameFactory's core contributions: robust action control, effective scene generalization through style-action decoupling, and practical long video generation, all underpinned by a carefully designed dataset and training strategy.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

GameFactory represents a significant leap forward in the field of generative AI for interactive content and holds profound implications across several domains:

#### 5.1. Advancements in Game Generation and Development

- **Revolutionizing Game Development:** By automating the creation of diverse, interactive game environments, GameFactory has the potential to drastically reduce the manual labor and costs associated with traditional game development. This can democratize game creation, enabling smaller teams or even individual creators to bring complex and imaginative game worlds to life.
- **Enabling Unlimited Game Content:** The framework can generate virtually endless variations of game levels and scenarios, offering unprecedented replayability and continuous novelty for players. This moves beyond static, pre-designed game worlds to dynamic, evolving interactive experiences.
- **New Forms of AI-Driven Gaming:** GameFactory paves the way for a new genre of games where players or AI agents can interact with entirely novel, dynamically generated worlds based on simple text prompts and real-time controls. This could lead to highly personalized and adaptive gaming experiences.

#### 5.2. Towards Generalizable World Models

- **Generalizable Physical Knowledge:** The most significant broader impact lies in the concept of GameFactory as a "Generalizable World Model." By learning action control and physical dynamics from a relatively small, biased-free game dataset and transferring this knowledge to open-domain generative priors, the model demonstrates an ability to understand and simulate real-world physics in diverse, unseen scenarios.
- **Data Production:** This generalizable world model can act as a powerful **data producer**. It can generate unlimited, diverse, and action-annotated synthetic data that closely approximates real-world complexity. This is invaluable for fields struggling with the scarcity or cost of large-scale labeled datasets.
- **Simulators for Real-World Tasks:** Beyond game development, the framework's ability to create controllable, diverse environments positions it as an effective **simulator** for training AI agents for real-world tasks.
 - **Autonomous Driving:** The paper explicitly mentions applications in autonomous driving, where the model could generate diverse driving scenarios, including rare or dangerous situations difficult to capture in real-world data. This could significantly enhance the robustness and generalizability of autonomous driving policies.
 - **Embodied AI and Robotics:** For embodied AI agents and robots, GameFactory offers a playground for learning complex interactions and navigation strategies in varied environments. It can facilitate the development of policy models that are exposed to a wide range of interactions, thereby improving their robustness and aiding in overcoming the challenges of sim-to-real transfer.

#### 5.3. Future Directions and Challenges

The authors candidly acknowledge that GameFactory marks a "first effort" in this ambitious field, and significant challenges remain:
- **Diverse Level and Gameplay Design:** Generating coherent and engaging game logic, level design, and varied gameplay mechanics beyond just visual and movement control.
- **Player Feedback Systems:** Incorporating sophisticated player feedback mechanisms to guide the generative process and create truly enjoyable experiences.
- **In-Game Object Manipulation:** Enabling more complex interactions with objects within the generated environments (e.g., picking up, using, crafting).
- **Long-Context Memory:** Improving the model's ability to maintain long-term memory and coherence in extremely long interactive sequences.
- **Real-time Game Generation:** Achieving truly real-time, low-latency generation for seamless interactive gameplay.

Despite these challenges, GameFactory lays a robust foundation by demonstrating that powerful generative video models, coupled with intelligent decoupling of style and control, can effectively create interactive, scene-generalizable content. Its potential to accelerate both creative industries and foundational AI research is substantial.
