# MoDA: Modulation Adapter for Fine-Grained Visual Grounding in Instructional MLLMs

> [!summary] Summary
> MoDA (Modulation Adapter) enhances Multimodal Large Language Models by dynamically refining pre-aligned visual features through instruction-guided modulation, improving fine-grained visual grounding and reducing hallucinations. Integrating MoDA into the LLaVA-1.5 pipeline boosted MMVP accuracy by 12.0 percentage points and achieved competitive or superior performance against recent MLLMs on various benchmarks.

## Problem

- MLLMs often struggle with fine-grained visual grounding due to entangled semantic elements within patch-based visual encoder representations.
- Poor visual grounding leads to frequent hallucinations, where MLLMs generate text inconsistent with image semantics.
- Existing solutions to these issues typically incur substantial computational overhead, require large-scale retraining, or introduce architectural complexity.

## Method

- ==MoDA==, a lightweight, ==plug-and-play module==, refines pre-aligned visual features using instruction-guided modulation.
- It employs a ==cross-attention mechanism== to estimate a soft modulation mask, dynamically weighting visual features based on the input language query.
- MoDA integrates into the standard LLaVA two-stage training pipeline and is fine-tuned jointly with the LLM during the instruction-tuning phase without additional supervision.

## Results

- MoDA integration improved LLaVA-1.5's accuracy on the MMVP hallucination benchmark by **12.0 percentage points**, from **24.0%** to **36.0%**.
- LLaVA-MoRE SigLIP-S2 + MoDA achieved **65.4%** on GQA and **81.9%** on ScienceQA, outperforming Qwen-2.5-VL by **+4.0** and **+11.7 percentage points** respectively, while matching its POPE score of **87.7%**.
- Ablation studies confirmed cross-attention without L1 regularization as the optimal MoDA architecture, and demonstrated that applying MoDA only at the beginning of the LLM is more effective and efficient than layer-wise application.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Dynamically modulating visual features based on language instructions effectively disentangles semantic elements and enhances fine-grained visual grounding in MLLMs.
> - A lightweight, modular adapter can significantly improve MLLM reliability by reducing hallucinations without requiring major architectural overhauls or extensive retraining.
> - The effectiveness of instruction-guided feature modulation is amplified when combined with richer base visual features (e.g., SigLIP-S2) and more powerful LLM backbones (e.g., LLaMA 3.1).

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) have demonstrated capabilities in instruction-following tasks by integrating pretrained visual encoders with large language models (LLMs). This integration typically involves lightweight adapter modules that align visual features with the language embedding space. Despite advancements, existing MLLMs frequently encounter limitations in fine-grained visual understanding. This includes challenges in tasks requiring precise localization, detailed reasoning about specific visual elements, and a propensity for generating outputs that contradict actual image semantics, a phenomenon referred to as hallucination.

Prior research has identified the patch-based representations generated by visual encoders, particularly CLIP-based architectures, as a contributing factor to these limitations. Semantic entanglement within individual patches, where multiple distinct visual elements are combined into a single embedding, can impede a model's ability to focus on instruction-relevant details.

Current approaches to address these limitations involve several strategies:
- **Specialized Visual Encoders:** Some works incorporate multiple visual encoders or fine-tune existing ones (e.g., Tong et al., 2024b; Kar et al., 2024; Villa et al., 2025). These methods can introduce substantial computational overhead or require extensive retraining.
- **Attention Masking:** Techniques adapted from Natural Language Processing (NLP) apply attention masking (e.g., Fan et al., 2021; Tang et al., 2021; Lin & Joe, 2023). However, these methods often operate on token-level sparsity rather than channel-wise feature refinement and typically lack instruction-guided conditioning. Layer-wise adaptive masking (Barrios & Jin, 2024) can also introduce significant overhead for deep models.
- **Cross-Modal Attention:** Modern MLLMs, such as InstructBLIP (Dai et al., 2023) and Q-Former (Li et al., 2023a), utilize cross-attention for multimodal integration. These methods primarily operate on discrete token interactions, selecting or reweighting entire tokens.
- **Adapter Architectures:** Models in the LLaVA family (Liu et al., 2023a; Cocchi et al., 2025; Chen et al., 2024a) employ lightweight adapters for initial cross-modal alignment, but they primarily focus on mapping visual features into the language space rather than dynamic, instruction-conditioned refinement.
- **Feature Refinement Across Pipelines:** Other efforts explore visual feature refinement at different stages, such as encoder-level pre-training (Villa et al., 2025), dynamic layer selection (Li, 2025), or per-layer transformations within the LLM (Bi et al., 2024; Wang et al., 2023).

This work positions MoDA within this landscape by introducing a lightweight module that performs instruction-guided channel-wise multiplicative modulation of pre-aligned visual features. MoDA differs from existing cross-attention methods by operating at a finer granularity (channel-level), applying multiplicative modulation rather than additive feature selection, and operating post-alignment as a complement to existing adapters, rather than replacing them. Its single-pass operation after the adapter stage is designed to avoid the scaling issues associated with layer-wise approaches, while explicitly incorporating language guidance to dynamically adapt visual attention based on specific queries.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation for this research stems from the observed difficulties MLLMs face in tasks requiring fine-grained visual understanding. Despite their overall success in instruction-following, state-of-the-art MLLMs frequently struggle with queries that necessitate precise localization, detailed reasoning about specific visual elements, and maintaining factual consistency with image content, often leading to visual hallucinations.

The paper identifies semantic entanglement within visual patch representations as a key underlying cause. Visual encoders, particularly those based on the CLIP architecture, segment images into fixed-size patches. An example provided in the paper illustrates this: a single patch might contain parts of a dog's torso, a stuffed toy, and a cushioned bed. This forces the encoder to blend distinct shapes, textures, and semantic concepts into individual embeddings, where feature dimensions may encode multiple unrelated meanings. Consequently, when presented with a query such as "What color is the dog's ear?", the model struggles to disentangle these mixed visual representations and focus on the instruction-relevant details, potentially leading to inaccurate or unreliable responses.

This challenge leads to the central research question posed by the authors: "How can we enable MLLMs to dynamically focus on instruction-relevant visual details for better visual understanding without architectural modifications or computational overhead?"

To address this question, the key objectives of this work are:
1. **Develop a Lightweight Module:** Design a lightweight module, termed MoDA (Modulation Adapter), capable of enhancing visual grounding.
2. **Instruction-Guided Channel-wise Modulation:** Implement this module to perform channel-wise modulation of pre-aligned visual features, guided by natural language instructions. This is intended to enable selective suppression of irrelevant embedding dimensions and emphasis on relevant ones.
3. **Post-Alignment Operation:** Integrate MoDA to operate on already-projected visual features, complementing existing adapter architectures rather than replacing them, to maintain compatibility with standard MLLM designs.
4. **Minimal Overhead:** Ensure that MoDA introduces minimal architectural modifications, no additional supervision or training data beyond the standard LLaVA protocol, and negligible computational overhead (< 1% FLOPs, 3.7% parameters).
5. **Generalizability:** Evaluate MoDA's effectiveness across a diverse set of benchmarks and distinct MLLM architectures to confirm its consistent performance gains and generalization capabilities.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

MoDA (MODulation Adapter) is presented as a lightweight module designed to post-process visual embeddings output by an MLLM's adapter. Its core function is to leverage the alignment between visual and language embedding spaces to dynamically select and emphasize the most relevant visual features based on the input language query.

**MoDA Design and Mechanism:**
The module operates by assigning individual weights to visual features through a cross-attention mechanism, where the language instruction guides the weighting process. These weights are encoded in a soft modulation mask. The modulation is applied element-wise across the embedding dimensions of the visual features using a Hadamard product, expressed as:
`e_V_aligned = V_aligned ⊙ σ(W · F(T, V_aligned))`
Here, `V_aligned` represents the language-aligned visual features from the adapter, `T` denotes the language token embeddings from the initial LLM layers, `⊙` signifies the Hadamard product, `σ` is the sigmoid activation function, and `W` is a learnable linear projection. `F(T, V_aligned)` is the modulation function.

The `F(·)` function is implemented as a stack of Transformer Layers. In this setup, the language-aligned visual features (`V_aligned`) serve as the target sequence, while the language token embeddings (`T`) act as the memory input for the multi-head cross-attention mechanism. This design allows each visual token to attend to relevant parts of the language input. Following the Transformer stack, the output is passed through the linear projection `W` and then a sigmoid activation `σ(·)`, which constrains the mask values to the range. This output effectively functions as a channel-wise mask that re-weights each feature channel in the visual embedding, emphasizing instruction-relevant dimensions and de-emphasizing less relevant ones. Each Transformer layer also incorporates a feed-forward network, residual connections, and layer normalization to promote stable training.

**MLLM Architecture Integration:**
MoDA is strategically integrated into the MLLM pipeline as an interface between the pre-trained visual-language adapter and the LLM. The vision encoder first extracts patch-level visual features from the input image. These features are then projected into the language embedding space by the standard adapter module. MoDA subsequently processes these aligned visual features, estimates the instruction-guided channel-wise modulation weights, and passes the modulated features (`e_V_aligned`) to the LLM. These refined visual embeddings are then used as prefix tokens for the LLM, which integrates them with the input query tokens to autoregressively generate a natural language response.

**Training Procedure:**
The training of MLLMs incorporating MoDA follows a two-stage protocol, consistent with the LLaVA framework:
1. **Stage 1 (Pre-training):** In this initial stage, only the original visual adapter is trained. The vision encoder and the LLM remain frozen. Training is performed using a large corpus of alt-text image-caption pairs (e.g., 558K pairs) and supervised by an autoregressive language modeling objective.
2. **Stage 2 (Instruction Tuning):** The MoDA module is introduced in this stage. MoDA's parameters are initialized using Xavier initialization, while the visual adapter retains the weights learned in Stage 1. Both MoDA and the LLM are jointly fine-tuned on high-quality visual instruction datasets. The objective for both stages remains the minimization of the autoregressive cross-entropy loss, where the model learns to predict the next token given previous tokens, modulated visual features (`e_V_aligned`), and the tokenized instruction.

Crucially, MoDA's integration does not require additional supervision or specialized training data beyond the standard LLaVA protocol, indicating that it enhances the utilization of existing evidence rather than requiring new data or labels.

**Computational Efficiency:**
The design of MoDA prioritizes computational efficiency. It introduces minimal overhead, adding less than 1% to the total FLOPs and approximately 3.7% to the total parameters compared to a LLaVA-MoRE (8B) baseline. In multi-turn dialogue scenarios, visual features are cached after the initial processing, with only the modulation recomputation required for subsequent queries, further limiting computational cost.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy of MoDA was evaluated across 12 diverse benchmarks, categorized into visual question answering (VQA), vision-centric reasoning, and hallucination detection. These benchmarks include recent datasets from 2024 and 2025 such as MMVP, CV-Bench, MMStar, and RealWorldQA. The evaluation was conducted on three distinct MLLM architectures: LLaVA-1.5, LLaVA-MoRE, and Qwen3-VL, representing various visual encoders and LLM backbones.

**Key Findings:**

1. **Consistent Performance Gains Across Architectures:** MoDA consistently delivered improvements across all three MLLM families, indicating its generalizability.

2. **Visual Question Answering (VQA):**
 - **LLaVA-MoRE Family:** When paired with the SigLIP-S2 encoder, MoDA improved ScienceQA scores by 4.8 points (from 77.1 to 81.9). It achieved the highest LLaVA-MoRE scores on all five VQA benchmarks: GQA (65.4), ScienceQA (81.9), MMBench (72.4), RealWorldQA (58.2), and ChartQA (18.1).
 - **Qwen3-VL:** MoDA enhanced performance on GQA by 3.8 points (from 59.4 to 63.2), ScienceQA by 4.9 points (from 79.3 to 84.2), RealWorldQA by 4.1 points (from 64.7 to 68.8), and MMBench by 0.9 points (from 86.5 to 87.4).

3. **Vision-Centric Tasks:**
 - **LLaVA-MoRE Family:** With OpenAI CLIP, MoDA achieved the highest LLaVA-Wild score (73.9). With SigLIP-S2, it recorded the strongest LLaVA-family results on MM-Star (38.5), V*Bench (44.8), and CV-Bench (62.2).
 - **LLaVA-1.5 Family:** For the compact Vicuna-7B model, MoDA led to a peak score on MM-Vet (29.9).
 - MoDA-enhanced models surpassed ShareGPT-4V (a 13B baseline trained on larger data) on several benchmarks, including LLaVA-Wild (73.9 vs 72.6), MMStar (38.5 vs 33.0), and CV-Bench (62.2 vs 61.8).

4. **Hallucination Detection:** This is where MoDA demonstrated some of its most substantial gains for CLIP-family encoders.
 - **LLaVA-1.5 (Vicuna-7B):** MMVP scores improved by 12.0 points (from 24.0 to 36.0).
 - **LLaVA-MoRE (SigLIP-S2):** MoDA achieved the strongest LLaVA-MoRE scores on both POPE (87.7) and MMVP (42.7), surpassing the LLaVA 1.5 13B baseline (POPE 85.9, MMVP 24.7).
 - **Qwen3-VL:** POPE scores improved by 0.5 points (from 89.4 to 89.9).

5. **Generalization to Non-CLIP Encoders:** MoDA's benefits extended to Qwen3-VL-2B-Instruct, an MLLM utilizing a ViT backbone that diverges from the CLIP family. While most VQA and vision-centric tasks showed gains, slight regressions were observed on ChartQA and V*Bench. This observation suggests that MoDA's impact is most pronounced when the encoder's representations may underutilize task-relevant channels, leaving less headroom for channel-wise refinement in encoders already specialized for specific tasks like chart reading or high-resolution visual search.

**Ablation Studies:**

- **Cross-Attention vs. Alternatives:** Cross-Attention, as implemented in MoDA, outperformed simpler linear modulation (MLP) and self-attention mechanisms. With SigLIP-S2, Cross-Attention yielded an average performance of 69.4, compared to 67.3 for Linear MLP and 68.0 for Self-Attention. This difference was particularly noticeable in reasoning tasks (ScienceQA: 81.9 for Cross-Attention vs. 77.8 for Linear MLP) and hallucination detection (MMVP: 42.7 for Cross-Attention vs. 40.0 for Linear MLP), attributed to its ability to dynamically weight channels based on instruction semantics.
- **Architecture vs. Capacity:** The performance patterns indicated that architectural design choices, particularly MoDA's channel-wise modulation, were more impactful than simply increasing parameter count. Gains were task-specific rather than uniform, consistent across different LLM backbones, and amplified when paired with stronger visual encoders (e.g., SigLIP-S2 significantly boosted MoDA's effectiveness compared to CLIP).
- **Component Synergy:** The benefits of instruction-guided modulation were found to scale with the quality of the visual encoder. SigLIP-S2, providing richer features, amplified MoDA's effectiveness (+5.1 points over CLIP). L1 regularization consistently degraded Cross-Attention performance.
- **Comparison with Masking Approaches:** MoDA achieved superior performance (69.4 average) compared to token-level Learnable Masking (68.5) and Sparse Masking (66.5), particularly on fine-grained tasks like MMVP (42.7) and ScienceQA (81.9). This was attributed to MoDA's continuous, instruction-guided channel-wise refinement versus discrete token-level operations.
- **Computational Efficiency:** MoDA was shown to introduce minimal computational overhead, adding only 3.7% parameters and less than 1% MACs/FLOPs to the LLaVA-MoRE (8B) model. This supports the conclusion that performance gains arise from architectural innovation rather than increased model capacity.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of MoDA represents an advancement in addressing the fine-grained visual understanding and hallucination challenges inherent in Multimodal Large Language Models. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Enhanced Visual Grounding:** By proposing a novel instruction-guided channel-wise modulation approach, MoDA directly tackles the problem of semantic entanglement within visual patch representations. This allows MLLMs to more effectively disentangle mixed visual elements and focus on instruction-relevant details, thereby improving the precision and reliability of visual-language understanding. The observed improvements in hallucination detection benchmarks underscore this enhanced grounding.

- **Architectural Innovation with Efficiency:** MoDA is a lightweight module that operates as a post-processing step between the visual adapter and the LLM. It achieves substantial performance gains across diverse benchmarks while introducing minimal computational overhead (<1% FLOPs and 3.7% parameters). This efficiency allows for performance improvements without necessitating significantly larger models or increased computational infrastructure, potentially democratizing access to more reliable vision-language systems for researchers and practitioners with limited resources.

- **Broad Generalizability:** The consistent performance gains demonstrated across various benchmarks and distinct MLLM architectures (LLaVA-1.5, LLaVA-MoRE, and Qwen3-VL), including those with non-CLIP-based encoders, validate MoDA's generalizability. This suggests that the channel-wise modulation mechanism is broadly applicable as a post-alignment refinement strategy, rather than being a specific remedy for a particular encoder type.

- **Improved Reliability and Factual Consistency:** The significant improvements observed in hallucination detection benchmarks, such as a 12.0-point increase on MMVP for the LLaVA-1.5 family, indicate that MoDA can reduce the tendency of MLLMs to generate incorrect or inconsistent information not present in the visual input. This contributes to more trustworthy and factually aligned model outputs, a crucial aspect for real-world applications.

- **Potential Positive Societal Applications:** More reliable visual understanding can positively impact several domains:
 - **Accessibility Tools:** Improved accuracy in image descriptions can benefit visually impaired users.
 - **Education:** Enhanced visual reasoning capabilities could be leveraged in science tutoring and for interpreting diagrams.
 - **Medical Imaging Analysis:** While requiring extensive validation, reduced hallucinations in describing radiological findings could be beneficial in clinical settings.

- **Future Research Directions:** The paper also identifies several avenues for future work, including:
 - Exploring sparsity-inducing objectives to achieve more explicit channel selection.
 - Further characterizing the relationship between MoDA's benefit and the degree to which visual encoders underutilize task-relevant channels across a wider range of backbones.
 - Extending MoDA's application to multi-image reasoning and video understanding tasks.

- **Responsible Deployment Considerations:** The authors acknowledge the potential risks associated with advanced vision-language models, such as their misuse for generating misleading visual descriptions or manipulating image-based content. They advocate for responsible deployment practices, including appropriate safeguards and content verification mechanisms. The reliance solely on publicly available datasets for training and evaluation also aligns with ethical data practices.
