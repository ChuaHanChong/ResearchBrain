# GLOVER++: Unleashing the Potential of Affordance Learning from Human Behaviors for Robotic Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from HKUST develop GLOVER++, a framework that learns actionable affordances from human demonstrations using a novel global-to-local decoding approach, while introducing HOVA-500K - a large-scale dataset of 500,000 human-object interaction images with precise affordance annotations for training robotic manipulation systems.

## Problem

- Limited availability of large-scale, precisely annotated affordance datasets for training robotic systems
- Difficulty in effectively transferring affordance knowledge from human demonstrations to robotic manipulation
- Challenge of developing generalizable manipulation capabilities across novel objects and tasks

## Method

- ==Created HOVA==-500K dataset with semi-automatic annotation pipeline for affordance labeling
- Developed **GLOVER++** framework using vision-language models and global-to-local decoding
- Implemented KLD-based optimization to align predicted and ground truth affordance distributions

## Results

- Achieved state-of-the-art performance on HOVA-500K benchmark metrics
- Demonstrated successful zero-shot manipulation of novel objects
- Enabled complex behaviors like multi-task imitation learning and bimanual manipulation
- Showed improved performance in downstream robotic manipulation tasks through explicit affordance learning

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Global-to-local decoding effectively balances semantic context with fine-grained affordance learning
> - Explicit affordance representations improve performance compared to implicit policy learning
> - Large-scale human demonstration data enables better generalization to novel scenarios

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work sits at the intersection of several active research areas:

- **Affordance Learning:** This is a core area. Affordance learning aims to enable robots to understand the potential interactions an object offers (e.g., a handle affords grasping). GLOVER++ builds upon existing affordance-based methods, including 3D radiance field modeling, object retrieval, and vision-language model reasoning. The paper directly addresses the limitations of current methods in distilling actionable affordance knowledge from human videos and demonstrating effective transfer to diverse manipulation tasks.

- **Robotics Manipulation from Human Demonstrations:** Learning from human demonstrations (LfD) is a promising way to teach robots complex tasks. This work leverages human videos and images to train robots to understand object affordances from how humans interact with them. The use of HOVA-500k dataset, which consists of human-object interaction, is an integral element of this research objective. This approach has been a subject of interest to researchers for the past years, and this work represents the expansion of research efforts in this field.

- **Vision-Language Models (VLMs) for Robotics:** VLMs are becoming increasingly important in robotics. They allow robots to understand natural language instructions and ground them in the visual world. GLOVER++ utilizes VLMs to encode multi-modal inputs (images and language) and reason about affordances in an open-vocabulary manner.

- **Robotic Datasets and Benchmarks:** The creation of large-scale, high-quality datasets is essential for advancing machine learning research. The HOVA-500K dataset is a key contribution of this work, addressing the lack of large-scale datasets with precise affordance annotations. It also provides a standardized benchmarking suite for multi-modal affordance reasoning, facilitating comparisons between different methods.

- **Zero-Shot Learning:** GLOVER++ demonstrates the ability to perform zero-shot manipulation, meaning it can manipulate objects in novel scenes without prior training on those specific objects or environments. This is a significant step towards more generalizable robotic systems.

This research builds upon previous works that focus on policy pretraining, affordance representation, and language-guided manipulation. In contrast to policy pretraining methods, GLOVER++ emphasizes explicit and generalizable affordance representations. It also advances existing VLM-based affordance methods by addressing data scarcity with HOVA-500K and providing an effective fine-tuning framework (GLOVER++) to leverage large-scale data.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind this research are:

- **Addressing the Lack of Large-Scale Affordance Datasets:** The researchers recognize that the limited availability of large-scale, precisely annotated affordance datasets hinders the development of generalizable robotic manipulation systems. The creation of HOVA-500K directly addresses this gap.

- **Improving Affordance Transfer from Human Demonstrations to Robots:** The researchers aim to develop a framework that can effectively transfer actionable affordance knowledge from human demonstrations to robots, enabling them to manipulate objects in a more human-like manner.

- **Enhancing Generalizability and Robustness of Robotic Manipulation:** The researchers want to create robotic systems that can generalize to new scenes, modalities (e.g., different types of images), and tasks, rather than being limited to specific, pre-trained scenarios.

- **Bridging the Gap Between Human Demonstrations and Robotic Manipulation:** The overarching goal is to develop a system that can effectively learn from human behaviors and translate that knowledge into robotic manipulation capabilities, ultimately making robots more useful and adaptable in real-world environments.

- **Explicit Actionable Affordances:** The researchers aim to move away from implicit policy learning and towards explicit representations of affordances, making the learning process more interpretable and allowing for better transfer to different tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research methodology involves several key components:

- **Dataset Creation (HOVA-500K):** The researchers compiled a large dataset of 500,000 images from various sources, including human demonstration videos, object-part segmentation masks, and existing affordance datasets. They developed a semi-automatic annotation pipeline to precisely label affordance points in the images.

- **Framework Development (GLOVER++):** The researchers designed an end-to-end framework that learns to predict executable affordance points. This framework utilizes a vision-language model (VLM) to encode multi-modal inputs (images and language instructions) and a global-to-local decoding policy to balance global semantic perception and local affordance learning.

- **Global-to-Local Affordance Tuning:** This is the key innovative aspect of the model. The process balances global semantic context from images/text with local fine-grained reasoning. This policy is implemented through global decoding, which generates a semantic logits map that captures contextual relationships, and localized decoding, where the semantic map acts as a mask prompt to focus on relevant regions.

- **Experiments and Evaluation:** The researchers conducted extensive experiments in both simulation and the real world to evaluate the performance of GLOVER++. They tested it on various tasks, including zero-shot manipulation, multi-task imitation learning, long-horizon manipulation, and bimanual manipulation. They used several metrics to evaluate the performance of the model, including KLD, SIM, NSS, and SIMpart. Ablation studies were also performed to assess the contribution of key components.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The main findings and results of the research are:

- **State-of-the-Art Performance on HOVA-500K:** GLOVER++ achieved state-of-the-art performance on the HOVA-500K benchmark, demonstrating its effectiveness in affordance reasoning.

- **Strong Generalization Across Diverse Tasks:** GLOVER++ exhibited strong generalization across diverse downstream robotic manipulation tasks, including zero-shot manipulation, multi-task imitation learning, long-horizon manipulation, and bimanual manipulation.

- **Effectiveness of Global-to-Local Decoding:** The global-to-local decoding policy was shown to be effective in balancing global semantic perception and local affordance learning, leading to improved performance.

- **Importance of KLD-Based Optimization:** The Kullback-Leibler Divergence (KLD) loss was found to be important for aligning the predicted affordance distribution with the ground truth, further improving performance.

- **Explicit Affordance Representation Improves Imitation Learning:** The experiments in RLBench demonstrated that using explicit affordance representation as a prior can significantly improve the performance of imitation learning models.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The significance and potential impact of this research are substantial:

- **Advances the Field of Affordance Learning:** The HOVA-500K dataset and GLOVER++ framework provide valuable resources for researchers working on affordance learning, enabling them to develop more powerful and generalizable robotic manipulation systems.

- **Enables More Human-Like Robotic Manipulation:** By learning from human demonstrations, GLOVER++ can enable robots to manipulate objects in a more intuitive and human-like manner, making them more useful and adaptable in real-world environments.

- **Facilitates Zero-Shot Learning:** The ability to perform zero-shot manipulation opens up new possibilities for robotic systems to operate in unstructured and unpredictable environments.

- **Promotes Collaboration Between Humans and Robots:** By enabling robots to understand and respond to natural language instructions, GLOVER++ can facilitate more seamless collaboration between humans and robots.

- **Broad Applications in Robotics:** The findings of this research have potential applications in various fields of robotics, including manufacturing, healthcare, logistics, and assistive robotics.

- **Foundation for Future Research:** The paper identifies several limitations of the current work, providing directions for future research in areas such as dynamic interactions, bias mitigation, and trajectory planning. This research lays a strong foundation for future work in this area.
