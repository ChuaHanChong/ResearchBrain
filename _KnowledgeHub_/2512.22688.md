# Autoregressive Flow Matching for Motion Prediction

> [!summary] Summary
> Autoregressive Flow Matching (ARFM) presents a generalized framework for probabilistic motion prediction, capable of forecasting future point track locations across diverse human and robotic video datasets. The method significantly improves performance in downstream tasks like robotic manipulation and human-object interaction synthesis, demonstrating strong zero-shot generalization and computational efficiency by predicting sparse point tracks.

## Problem

- Existing narrow-domain motion prediction models lack generality, being optimized for specific contexts like human or vehicle motion.
- Large-scale video generation models struggle with accurately modeling complex, physically consistent motion over long horizons and are computationally expensive for inference.
- There was a lack of a generalized, efficient, and probabilistically robust method for predicting motion across domains by focusing on sparse point tracks rather than full pixel-level video synthesis.

## Method

- **ARFM** (Autoregressive Flow Matching) employs a ==Feature Fusion Module== that aggregates multi-modal past input information (video frames, past tracks, language) into a compact feature vector using foundation models like ==SAMv2== and ==CLIP==.
- A ==Flow Matching Predictor== then performs ==conditional probabilistic prediction== of relative shifts between track points, leveraging a ==flow matching objective== to generate diverse and coherent future trajectories.
- The system efficiently predicts ==sparse point tracks== and includes an ==online horizon updating mechanism== for dynamic adaptation, allowing for controlled updates as new frames become available.

## Results

- ARFM demonstrated superior track prediction on UCF-101 (human motion) and CALVIN ABC→D (robotics), significantly outperforming all baselines and exhibiting strong zero-shot generalization.
- Conditioning downstream models with ARFM-predicted tracks substantially improved human motion prediction in interaction synthesis and enhanced robotic task success rates on the challenging CALVIN benchmark.
- Ablation studies confirmed that the flow matching objective significantly outperforms a simple squared regression objective, and pre-training data composition (e.g., including human motion) did not negatively impact robotics-specific performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Generalized motion prediction is effectively achieved by modeling sparse point tracks across diverse video data rather than focusing on pixel-level generation, integrating multi-modal context from established foundation models.
> - Probabilistic modeling through flow matching is crucial for capturing the inherent uncertainty in future motion, enabling the generation of multiple plausible trajectories essential for decision-making systems.
> - An architectural design that separates past input conditioning from the denoising process, combined with autoregressive prediction, enhances computational efficiency and mitigates train-test time mismatches common in generative models.
