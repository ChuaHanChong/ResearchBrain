# Autoregressive Flow Matching for Motion Prediction

> [!summary] Summary
> Autoregressive Flow Matching (ARFM) presents a generalized framework for probabilistic motion prediction, capable of forecasting future point track locations across diverse human and robotic video datasets. The method significantly improves performance in downstream tasks like robotic manipulation and human-object interaction synthesis, demonstrating strong zero-shot generalization and computational efficiency by predicting sparse point tracks.

## Problem

- Existing narrow-domain motion prediction models lack generality, being optimized for specific contexts like human or vehicle motion.
- Large-scale video generation models struggle with accurately modeling complex, physically consistent motion over long horizons and are computationally expensive for inference.
- There was a lack of a generalized, efficient, and probabilistically robust method for predicting motion across domains by focusing on sparse point tracks rather than full pixel-level video synthesis.

## Method

- **ARFM** (Autoregressive Flow Matching) employs a ==Feature Fusion Module== that aggregates multi-modal past input information (video frames, past tracks, language) into a compact feature vector using foundation models like ==SAMv2== and ==CLIP==.
- A ==Flow Matching Predictor== then performs ==conditional probabilistic prediction== of relative shifts between track points, leveraging a ==flow matching objective== to generate diverse and coherent future trajectories.
- The system efficiently predicts ==sparse point tracks== and includes an ==online horizon updating mechanism== for dynamic adaptation, allowing for controlled updates as new frames become available.

## Results

- ARFM demonstrated superior track prediction on UCF-101 (human motion) and CALVIN ABC→D (robotics), significantly outperforming all baselines and exhibiting strong zero-shot generalization.
- Conditioning downstream models with ARFM-predicted tracks substantially improved human motion prediction in interaction synthesis and enhanced robotic task success rates on the challenging CALVIN benchmark.
- Ablation studies confirmed that the flow matching objective significantly outperforms a simple squared regression objective, and pre-training data composition (e.g., including human motion) did not negatively impact robotics-specific performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Generalized motion prediction is effectively achieved by modeling sparse point tracks across diverse video data rather than focusing on pixel-level generation, integrating multi-modal context from established foundation models.
> - Probabilistic modeling through flow matching is crucial for capturing the inherent uncertainty in future motion, enabling the generation of multiple plausible trajectories essential for decision-making systems.
> - An architectural design that separates past input conditioning from the denoising process, combined with autoregressive prediction, enhances computational efficiency and mitigates train-test time mismatches common in generative models.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Motion prediction is a fundamental problem in artificial intelligence, critical for applications ranging from autonomous driving and robotics to human-computer interaction and virtual reality. Historically, research in motion prediction has branched into two main directions, each with distinct strengths and limitations.

Firstly, a significant body of work has focused on **narrow-domain motion prediction**. These models are meticulously trained and optimized for specific contexts, such as human motion analysis, self-driving vehicle trajectory forecasting, or robot manipulation planning. While highly successful within their specialized domains, these approaches often rely on domain-specific knowledge, datasets, and inductive biases, making them lack generality. A model trained for predicting human gait, for instance, would likely fail to predict the motion of a robot arm or a car.

Secondly, recent advancements in **large-scale video generation** have demonstrated impressive capabilities in synthesizing visually realistic and diverse videos. These models, often trained on vast internet datasets, excel at producing visually plausible content. However, as the authors highlight, they frequently struggle to accurately model complex, physically consistent motion over long horizons. Their primary focus is on pixel-level realism, leading to prohibitive computational expense at inference time, and they often lack the precise understanding of underlying physics or agent intent necessary for accurate motion forecasting.

This paper positions "Autoregressive Flow Matching (ARFM)" as a crucial bridge between these two paradigms. The authors identify a critical gap: the lack of a **generalized motion prediction** method that combines the scalability and flexibility of large-scale video models with the efficiency and structural accuracy of motion-centric prediction. ARFM aims to address this by:
- **Generalizability**: Moving beyond narrow domains to predict motion across a wide range of video types (human and robot motion).
- **Efficiency**: Focusing on predicting sparse point tracks rather than full pixel-level video synthesis, significantly reducing computational cost.
- **Probabilistic Modeling**: Explicitly addressing the irreducible uncertainty inherent in future motion by modeling multiple plausible outcomes, which is vital for decision-making in dynamic environments.
- **Multi-modal Conditioning**: Incorporating vision and natural language inputs, enabling richer control and understanding of predicted motions.

By tackling these challenges, ARFM seeks to establish a "foundation model" for motion prediction, capable of zero-shot generalization to unseen environments and improving downstream task performance in diverse applications. This work contributes to the broader trend of developing large, general-purpose models that can perform multiple tasks and generalize across domains, akin to the success of foundation models in natural language processing and static image generation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a novel, generalized method for **probabilistic modeling of sequential continuous data**, specifically focusing on the prediction of future point track locations over long horizons in diverse video datasets. This overarching goal is broken down into several key sub-objectives:

1. **Develop a Generalized Motion Predictor**: The authors aim to create a single model capable of predicting future motion across a wide variety of video domains, moving beyond the limitations of domain-specific models. This involves enabling vision-language conditioning to enhance predictive capabilities.
2. **Achieve Probabilistic Motion Modeling**: Recognizing that future motion often has irreducible uncertainty, the model must be capable of generating multiple plausible future trajectories rather than a single deterministic outcome. This is crucial for applications requiring robust decision-making under uncertainty.
3. **Ensure Computational Efficiency**: Unlike pixel-level video generation models which are computationally expensive, the proposed method should efficiently predict sparse point tracks, making it practical for real-time and resource-constrained applications.
4. **Facilitate Online and Adaptive Prediction**: The model needs to efficiently update its predictions as new frames become available, maintaining temporal consistency and adaptability for dynamic tasks like robotics.
5. **Demonstrate Downstream Task Improvement**: A crucial objective is to show that the predicted future tracks generated by the model can significantly enhance the performance of downstream AI tasks, such as robotic action prediction and human interaction synthesis.
6. **Create Training and Evaluation Benchmarks**: Given the novelty of generalized motion prediction, the authors aim to establish new benchmarks, metrics, and a scalable data pipeline to train and evaluate models in this new setting.

The motivation for these objectives stems from several observations:
- **Human Intuition vs. Machine Capability**: Humans can effortlessly infer plausible future motions from minimal visual cues, integrating knowledge of agent behavior and physics. Machines, however, struggle to replicate this generalized understanding.
- **Limitations of Prior Work**: Existing motion prediction models are either too specialized (lacking generality) or too focused on visual realism (lacking motion accuracy and computational efficiency). There is a clear need for a scalable, flexible, and accurate motion-centric prediction framework.
- **Scalability Challenges**: Training on pixel-level data for video prediction is computationally intensive and often fails to capture fine-grained motion dynamics accurately, especially over long horizons.
- **Applicability in AI**: Accurate and generalizable motion prediction is a critical component for building more intelligent and adaptable AI systems that can interact with and understand complex dynamic environments, whether in robotics, virtual agents, or human behavior analysis.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of the proposed method is **Autoregressive Flow Matching (ARFM)**, a novel approach designed for probabilistic modeling of sequential continuous data. The methodology can be broken down into several interconnected components:

#### 3.1. Data Pipeline

To train a generalized motion predictor, ARFM leverages a large, unlabeled video database.
- **Pseudo-labeling**: An off-the-shelf, high-accuracy point tracker (BootsTAP) is used to extract pseudo-label point tracks for a large number of query points across all frames of the videos. This technique enables scalable training without manual annotation.
- **Diverse Datasets**: The training data comprises a concatenation of Kinetics-400 (human actions), Motion-X (3D human motion), and various robotics datasets from Open-X-Embodiment (e.g., Language Table, BridgeDataV2, Maniskill, MimicGen). Downstream evaluation datasets are excluded from pre-training for zero-shot testing.
- **Track Sub-sampling**: During training, tracks are filtered based on minimum visibility ratio and then sampled using movement-weighted sampling to focus on informative motions.
- **Relative Shift Prediction**: Instead of predicting absolute future coordinates, the model is trained to predict the relative shifts between consecutive track points (T_T = T[:, 1:] - T[:,:-1]), which is more stable and robust for dynamic predictions.

#### 3.2. Autoregressive Flow Matching (ARFM) Model

ARFM separates the conditioning on past inputs from the denoising process, offering efficiency and avoiding train-test time mismatches common in some generative models.

**a. Feature Fusion Module**: This module aggregates multi-modal past input information into a compact feature vector for each future track prediction location.
- **Input Modalities**: Video frames, past point tracks, and optional natural language captions.
- **Foundation Models for Feature Extraction**:
 - **Local Image Features**: SAMv2 (a Segment Anything Model variant) extracts hierarchical feature maps, capturing high-frequency image features and positional information. Input tracks are used to spatio-temporally index these maps.
 - **Global Image Features**: The image branch of CLIP extracts low-resolution global image features, allowing each query point to attend to full image context.
 - **Language Embeddings**: The text branch of CLIP encodes natural language captions.
- **Spatiotemporal Transformer**: These extracted multi-modal features are processed by a transformer architecture that alternates between causal attention along the time axis (for autoregressive prediction) and bidirectional attention along the spatial axis (for joint point interactions). This design allows for efficient key-value caching during inference.

**b. Flow Matching Predictor**: This module takes the track-level features from the Feature Fusion Module and performs conditional probabilistic prediction.
- **Flow Matching Objective**: Inspired by continuous normalizing flows, the model optimizes an objective to predict target relative shifts. It samples a denoising timestep *t* and Gaussian noise *ϵ*, creating a noised target (tT_T + (1-t)ϵ). The model is trained to predict the difference (T_T - ϵ) from this noised target.
- **Joint Prediction**: The predictor conditions on other point tracks and their relative shifts (T_C), ensuring coherent motion for groups of points (e.g., points on a rigid object moving together).
- **Inference**: Denoising is performed at each video timestep using the current track-level features. The fully denoised tracks are then used as conditioning for generating features for the next timestep, repeating the process for the desired horizon. The spatial-only attention in this module allows for efficient per-timestep denoising.

#### 3.3. Query Information Predictor

To handle scenarios without pre-defined query points, a lightweight model predicts the expected future movement magnitude for a large number of random query points. This allows for movement-weighted sampling at inference time, mirroring the training data distribution.

#### 3.4. Updating Horizon Prediction

For tasks requiring a constantly updated prediction horizon (e.g., online robotics), ARFM proposes an efficient method:
- Instead of regenerating the entire horizon, a new point is tracked by an online point tracker.
- Previously predicted horizon tracks are partially noised and then re-denoised from an intermediate timestep (t_e ∈). This allows for controlled updates, balancing between retaining past predictions and incorporating new information, maintaining continuity and consistency.
- A new point at the end of the horizon is then fully denoised.

#### 3.5. Track Conditioning Applications

To integrate predicted tracks into downstream tasks:
- **Track Feature Extraction**: Raw predicted tracks are encoded into a compact conditioning vector. This involves combining 2D Fourier features of initial track locations with flattened track shift features, processed by a ViT-like bidirectional transformer.
- **Downstream Model Training**: During training, downstream application models (e.g., for robotics or interaction synthesis) are conditioned on *ground truth* future tracks. This strategy ensures that the application model is robust and can be used with any track prediction model that produces accurate tracks, without requiring re-training of the application model itself. At inference, ARFM's predicted tracks are used.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrate ARFM's strong performance and utility across diverse domains, both in zero-shot and fine-tuned settings.

#### 4.1. Track Prediction Evaluation

ARFM was evaluated on UCF-101 (human motion) and CALVIN ABC→D (robotics), using metrics like `<δ_x` (ratio of points within *x* pixels of ground truth) and Average Displacement Error (ADE).
- **Superior Performance**: ARFM significantly outperformed all baselines, including simple no-movement, time-series forecasting (Chronos), and video generation models (LARP, iVideoGPT, Cosmos Video2World).
- **Zero-shot Generalization**: ARFM showed strong zero-shot prediction capabilities on unseen datasets, indicating its generalizability.
- **Fine-tuning Benefits**: Fine-tuning ARFM on downstream datasets further improved performance, especially on CALVIN, suggesting adaptation to domain-specific rendering or dynamics.
- **Baseline Insights**: Notably, a simple "no movement" baseline sometimes outperformed other complex baselines at very low error thresholds, highlighting the struggle of video generation models to accurately predict still backgrounds or maintain temporal consistency.

#### 4.2. Interaction Synthesis Evaluation

ARFM's predicted tracks were used to condition CHOIS, an interaction synthesis model, on the FullBodyManipulation dataset.
- **Significant Improvement**: Conditioning CHOIS with ARFM-predicted tracks substantially improved human motion prediction results across various metrics.
- **Oracle Performance**: Using ground truth tracks yielded the best results, confirming that better future tracks directly translate to higher quality interaction synthesis. This validates the utility of ARFM's output.

#### 4.3. Robotics Evaluation

ARFM's predicted tracks were used to guide the GR-1 robotics model on the challenging CALVIN benchmark (ABC→D and 10% data splits).
- **Enhanced Task Success**: Conditioning GR-1 with ARFM-predicted tracks led to significant improvements in task success rate across different chained task lengths for both evaluation settings.
- **Robustness with Limited Data**: The performance gains were particularly evident in the 10% data setting, suggesting that ARFM can help robotics models learn more effectively even with limited task-specific demonstrations.
- **Comparison with Other Baselines**: ARFM also demonstrated improvements over other strong robotics baselines like RT-1, HULC, and MT-R3M.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies

- **Pre-training Data**: An ablation study showed that including human motion data in the pre-training dataset did not significantly impact robotics performance. This suggests that the generalized motion representation learned by ARFM is modular and domain-specific knowledge might not negatively interfere when domains are distinct.
- **Prediction Objective**: Comparing flow matching against a simple squared regression objective, flow matching was found to significantly outperform regression across all metrics. This validates the choice of flow matching for its ability to model uncertainty and produce diverse, plausible predictions.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a significant advancement in the field of motion prediction, with several profound implications and potential impacts:

1. **Towards General-Purpose AI for Motion**: ARFM represents a critical step towards developing foundation models for motion understanding. By demonstrating zero-shot generalization across vastly different domains like human motion and robotics, it opens the door for more versatile and broadly applicable AI systems that can reason about and interact with dynamic environments. This moves beyond the current paradigm of highly specialized models.

2. **Bridging the Gap Between Generative Models and Task-Specific Predictors**: The work successfully combines the scalability of large-scale video models with the accuracy and efficiency demanded by motion-centric tasks. By predicting sparse point tracks instead of full pixel frames, ARFM achieves a practical balance between visual realism and motion fidelity, making it suitable for real-world applications where computational resources are a concern.

3. **Robustness via Probabilistic Modeling**: The adoption of flow matching for probabilistic prediction is crucial. It allows ARFM to explicitly model the inherent uncertainty in future events, generating multiple plausible motion trajectories. This capability is vital for decision-making systems in autonomous agents (robots, self-driving cars) that need to account for unpredictable futures and plan accordingly.

4. **Tangible Downstream Impact**: The most compelling evidence of ARFM's significance is its ability to substantially improve the performance of state-of-the-art models in key downstream applications:
 - **Robotics**: Enhanced task success rates on challenging robot manipulation benchmarks indicate that ARFM can provide critical future context, enabling robots to act more intelligently and robustly, even in novel environments or with limited training data. This could accelerate the development of more capable and general-purpose robotic systems.
 - **Human-Object Interaction Synthesis**: Improved quality of synthesized human motions in interaction scenarios suggests that ARFM can generate more realistic and contextually appropriate behaviors for virtual agents, character animation, and human-computer interaction applications.

5. **Novel Methodological Contributions**: The introduction of Autoregressive Flow Matching itself is a significant methodological contribution for modeling sequential continuous data. The architectural design that separates past input conditioning from denoising not only improves efficiency but also addresses potential train-test time mismatches, setting a new standard for such models. The efficient online horizon updating mechanism is also highly practical for real-time applications.

6. **Enabling Future Research**: By publicly releasing code, models, and new evaluation benchmarks, the authors are fostering an ecosystem for future research in generalized motion prediction. This allows other researchers to build upon ARFM, compare new methods effectively, and explore its limitations.

While the paper acknowledges limitations (e.g., inheriting point tracker flaws, unreliability with extreme camera motion), these do not diminish the overall significance. Instead, they highlight clear avenues for future research to further refine and expand the capabilities of generalized motion prediction. ARFM represents a foundational step towards building AI systems that possess a more comprehensive and adaptable understanding of the dynamic world.
