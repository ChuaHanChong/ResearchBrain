# Divide, Harmonize, Then Conquer It: Shooting Multi-Commodity Flow Problems with Multimodal Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> PRAM, a framework from Zhejiang University and Hefei University of Technology, addresses large-scale Multi-Commodity Flow (MCF) problems by integrating multimodal language models (MLMs) and problem decomposition. This approach achieves near-optimal allocations while significantly reducing computation time, demonstrating up to 100x speedup over traditional LP solvers on large topologies and robust performance against network failures and demand fluctuations.

## Problem

- Traditional Linear Programming (LP) solvers for Multi-Commodity Flow (MCF) problems suffer from poor scalability and high computational cost (O(d^(2.3729))) for large networks, making them impractical for real-time applications.
- Existing machine learning (ML)-based MCF solutions often require extensive retraining, specialized Deep Neural Network (DNN) architectures, and struggle with generalization to unseen conditions due to the 'curse of dimensionality' in representing flow allocations.
- Service providers face a trade-off between obtaining computationally prohibitive optimal solutions and fast heuristic solutions that compromise significantly on quality for large-scale allocation systems.

## Method

- **PRAM** decomposes large MCF problems into smaller, source-node-specific ==subproblems==, reducing model complexity and enabling parallel processing.
- A shared Multimodal Language Model (MLM) backbone acts as the core 'agent,' processing subgraphs (as visual inputs) and demand data (as textual prompts) to determine commodity flow configurations.
- A lightweight multi-agent adaptation framework uses LoRA (Low-Rank Adaptation) for internal communication and learnable global context embeddings for external guidance, fine-tuning the MLM with Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning (MARL) and counterfactual reasoning to achieve coordinated decision-making.

## Results

- PRAM achieved near-optimal allocation quality, often ranking second only to ideal LP solvers with perfect foresight, and occasionally outperforming predictive LP by **21%** (CERNET) to **45%** (GÉANT) in minimizing MLU.
- The framework demonstrated strong scalability on large datasets, completing flow allocation in under 25 seconds for the largest Kdl topology (754 nodes), achieving **5x** to **100x** speedups over POP, LP-top, and LP respectively, while retaining over **90%** of LP's performance.
- PRAM exhibited robustness to unseen situations, showing less than **10%** performance degradation during multiple link failures and less than **15%** degradation under high demand fluctuations (α=2) without re-adaptation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - MLMs possess emergent reasoning and generalization abilities, allowing them to effectively process both visual network topologies and textual demand data, reducing the need for extensive manual feature engineering in combinatorial optimization.
> - Decomposing the MCF problem into smaller subproblems and employing a shared MLM significantly improves scalability, transforming the complexity from O(|V|^2) to O(|V|).
> - PRAM's MARL adaptation, supported by theoretical proofs of convergence, effectively guides the MLM to simulate gradient descent, enabling it to learn near-optimal solutions for convex/concave MCF objectives.
