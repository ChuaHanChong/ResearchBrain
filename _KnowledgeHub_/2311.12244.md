# Provable Representation with Efficient Planning for Partial Observable Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from the University of Alberta, UT Austin, The Chinese University of Hong Kong, Shenzhen, and Google DeepMind introduce the Multi-step Latent Variable Representation (μLV-Rep) framework for partially observable reinforcement learning. This framework provides a provably efficient, linear representation of value functions for L-decodable POMDPs, enabling efficient planning and achieving strong empirical performance on complex robotic manipulation tasks and continuous control environments.

## Problem

- Reinforcement Learning (RL) typically assumes full observability (MDPs), which often doesn't hold in real-world partially observable scenarios (POMDPs), leading to suboptimal policies.
- POMDPs suffer from significant computational and statistical intractability due to exponentially growing belief state spaces, complex belief calculation, and challenges in representing high-dimensional value functions.
- Existing theoretical approaches for structured POMDPs (e.g., L-decodability) often reduce statistical complexity but lack practical, computationally tractable planning and exploration algorithms, relying on idealized oracles.

## Method

- Introduces the Multi-step Latent Variable Representation (μLV-Rep) to transform L-decodable POMDPs into a tractable form, bypassing explicit belief state computation by leveraging a finite L-step memory window.
- Derives a provably exact and tractable linear representation for value functions by combining L-step Bellman expansions with a 'moment matching policy' to resolve overlapping dependencies in multi-step observations.
- Develops an efficient planning algorithm that utilizes least squares policy evaluation for Bellman backups and a variational learning approach for the μLV-Rep, integrating optimism for effective exploration.

## Results

- Theoretically, the proposed algorithm achieves polynomial sample complexity (e.g., poly(C, H, |A|^L, L, ε, log(|M|/δ)) episodes), providing PAC guarantees for an ε-optimal policy and avoiding exponential dependence on horizon length.
- Empirically, μLV-Rep achieved success rates above **90%** on 33 out of 50 Meta-world visual robotic manipulation tasks, outperforming DreamerV2 and MWM while demonstrating faster training speed (21.3 vs 8.1 steps/second).
- On partially observable continuous control benchmarks (MuJoCo/DeepMind Control Suites), μLV-Rep delivered competitive or superior performance, often closely matching the performance of algorithms operating with full observations.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - L-decodability allows the belief state and Q-function to be effectively represented by a finite L-step observation history window, simplifying the problem without needing the entire history.
> - The introduction of a 'moment matching policy' enables a linear factorization of the multi-step Q-function, transforming complex belief-dependent value functions into a linearly tractable form for efficient learning.
> - By formulating the Bellman backup as a least squares regression problem and learning the latent representation variationally, the framework eliminates the need for intractable belief calculations and integrations.
