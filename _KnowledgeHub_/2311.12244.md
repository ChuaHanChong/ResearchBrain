# Provable Representation with Efficient Planning for Partial Observable Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from the University of Alberta, UT Austin, The Chinese University of Hong Kong, Shenzhen, and Google DeepMind introduce the Multi-step Latent Variable Representation (μLV-Rep) framework for partially observable reinforcement learning. This framework provides a provably efficient, linear representation of value functions for L-decodable POMDPs, enabling efficient planning and achieving strong empirical performance on complex robotic manipulation tasks and continuous control environments.

## Problem

- Reinforcement Learning (RL) typically assumes full observability (MDPs), which often doesn't hold in real-world partially observable scenarios (POMDPs), leading to suboptimal policies.
- POMDPs suffer from significant computational and statistical intractability due to exponentially growing belief state spaces, complex belief calculation, and challenges in representing high-dimensional value functions.
- Existing theoretical approaches for structured POMDPs (e.g., L-decodability) often reduce statistical complexity but lack practical, computationally tractable planning and exploration algorithms, relying on idealized oracles.

## Method

- Introduces the Multi-step Latent Variable Representation (μLV-Rep) to transform L-decodable POMDPs into a tractable form, bypassing explicit belief state computation by leveraging a finite L-step memory window.
- Derives a provably exact and tractable linear representation for value functions by combining L-step Bellman expansions with a 'moment matching policy' to resolve overlapping dependencies in multi-step observations.
- Develops an efficient planning algorithm that utilizes least squares policy evaluation for Bellman backups and a variational learning approach for the μLV-Rep, integrating optimism for effective exploration.

## Results

- Theoretically, the proposed algorithm achieves polynomial sample complexity (e.g., poly(C, H, |A|^L, L, ε, log(|M|/δ)) episodes), providing PAC guarantees for an ε-optimal policy and avoiding exponential dependence on horizon length.
- Empirically, μLV-Rep achieved success rates above **90%** on 33 out of 50 Meta-world visual robotic manipulation tasks, outperforming DreamerV2 and MWM while demonstrating faster training speed (21.3 vs 8.1 steps/second).
- On partially observable continuous control benchmarks (MuJoCo/DeepMind Control Suites), μLV-Rep delivered competitive or superior performance, often closely matching the performance of algorithms operating with full observations.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - L-decodability allows the belief state and Q-function to be effectively represented by a finite L-step observation history window, simplifying the problem without needing the entire history.
> - The introduction of a 'moment matching policy' enables a linear factorization of the multi-step Q-function, transforming complex belief-dependent value functions into a linearly tractable form for efficient learning.
> - By formulating the Bellman backup as a least squares regression problem and learning the latent representation variationally, the framework eliminates the need for intractable belief calculations and integrations.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Reinforcement Learning (RL) commonly relies on the Markov Decision Process (MDP) framework, which assumes full observability of the environment's state. While MDP-based RL has achieved notable successes in various applications, this full observability assumption often does not hold in real-world scenarios, such as chatbot learning or video game control, where agents only perceive partial information (e.g., dialogue exchanges, images). This discrepancy can lead to performance degradation for standard MDP algorithms.

The Partially Observable Markov Decision Process (POMDP) framework extends MDPs to account for partial observability, making it more applicable to real-world problems. However, POMDPs introduce significant computational and statistical complexities. The optimal policy in a POMDP is dependent on the entire history of observations and actions, leading to an exponentially growing belief state space. Consequently, computing an optimal policy for a POMDP is PSPACE-complete, and sample complexity for learning can grow exponentially with the horizon.

Existing approaches to mitigate these challenges include:
1. **Heuristic Extensions:** Many practical RL algorithms extend MDP-based methods by using recurrent neural networks (RNNs) or maintaining a history window to encode policy or value functions. These methods have shown empirical success in complex visual or text-based tasks but often lack theoretical guarantees regarding their efficiency and optimality.
2. **Structured POMDPs:** Theoretical research has explored specific structures within POMDPs, such as L-decodability and $\gamma$-observability. These structures aim to reduce complexity by ensuring that the latent state can be recovered or approximated from a finite window of past observations. While these assumptions promise reduced statistical complexity, most prior work relies on the existence of ideal computational oracles for planning, which are often intractable in practice (e.g., tabular settings only, reliance on integration oracles for large observation spaces). This gap between theoretical guarantees and practical implementation remains a significant challenge.

This paper positions itself within this landscape by seeking to bridge the gap between statistically efficient theoretical structures in POMDPs and computationally tractable, practical RL algorithms. It aims to develop an approach that exploits natural structures like L-decodability to achieve both statistical and computational efficiency, thereby advancing reliable RL for practical applications.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this work stems from the inherent difficulties of applying traditional RL algorithms to real-world scenarios characterized by partial observability. Standard MDP formulations break down when the true state is not fully perceived, leading to suboptimal policies. While POMDPs offer a robust theoretical framework for partial observability, their computational and statistical intractability presents a major barrier to practical implementation.

The paper identifies three major difficulties in learning with POMDPs:
1. **Belief Calculation:** Belief states, which represent probability distributions over latent states, depend on the entire history of observations and actions. Their calculation involves marginalization and renormalization, which is computationally expensive for discrete state spaces and intractable for continuous ones.
2. **Value Function Representation:** Representing the value function over belief states is challenging due to the high-dimensional and continuous nature of the belief space. The optimal value function is piecewise linear and convex, but the number of pieces can grow exponentially with the horizon, making compact representation difficult, especially for continuous state spaces.
3. **Expectation Calculation:** The Bellman equation for POMDPs requires computing expectations over future observations, which involves integration over belief distributions. This is generally intractable and approximations can lead to compounded errors over time.

Prior work on structured POMDPs (L-decodability, $\gamma$-observability) has primarily focused on reducing statistical complexity but has largely overlooked the computational tractability of planning and exploration. Algorithms often assume idealized oracles that are infeasible in practice, such as being limited to tabular settings or requiring integration oracles for nonlinear structures. Furthermore, effective exploration strategies for POMDPs, particularly those based on principles like optimism in the face of uncertainty (OFU), are difficult to implement without explicit model or value function uncertainty estimates.

Given these challenges, the key objectives and motivations of this research are to:
- **Provide an Affirmative Answer:** Determine if efficient and practical RL algorithms can be designed for partial observations by exploiting natural structures in POMDPs. "Efficient" here refers to statistical complexity avoiding exponential dependence on history length, and "practical" means all components (learning, planning, exploration) are easily implementable.
- **Reveal a Sufficient Representation:** Identify a latent variable representation, termed Multi-step Latent Variable Representation (μLV-Rep), that enables an exact and tractable linear representation of value functions in L-decodable POMDPs. This is aimed at breaking fundamental computational barriers.
- **Design a Computationally Efficient Planning Algorithm:** Develop a planning method that leverages the learned μLV-Rep to implement principles of optimism (for online RL) and pessimism (for offline RL) without explicit belief calculation or intractable integration.
- **Conduct Theoretical Analysis:** Provide a rigorous analysis of the proposed algorithm's sample complexity to justify its statistical efficiency in balancing exploration and exploitation.
- **Demonstrate Empirical Superiority:** Empirically compare the proposed algorithm against state-of-the-art RL methods for POMDPs across various benchmarks to demonstrate its performance advantages and practical applicability.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of the proposed methodology, named Multi-step Latent Variable Representation (μLV-Rep), centers on transforming the POMDP problem into a tractable form by leveraging the L-decodability assumption. The approach bypasses explicit belief state computation and develops a linear representation for value functions, facilitating efficient planning and exploration.

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation and Initial Challenges

The paper defines a POMDP as P = (S, A, O, r, H, ρ_0, P, O), with S (state), A (action), O (observation), r (reward), H (horizon), ρ_0 (initial state distribution), P (transition kernel), and O (emission kernel). The non-Markovian dependence in observations necessitates history-dependent policies. While POMDPs can be reformulated as an equivalent MDP over belief states, this often results in a continuous and high-dimensional belief space, leading to the computational and statistical intractability highlighted in Section 3.
The work focuses on L-decodable POMDPs, where the belief state b(τ_h) can be recovered from an L-step window of past observations and actions x_h = (o_{h-L+1}, a_{h-L+1}..., o_h). The related concept of $\gamma$-observability is noted as being approximable by L-decodability.

#### 3.2. Key Observations and μLV-Rep Derivation

The methodology is built upon a series of key observations:

1. **Belief Elimination via L-decodability:** L-decodability simplifies the problem by allowing the belief state b_h to be represented by a finite L-step memory x_h, rather than the entire history τ_h. This reparametrizes the Q-function as Q^π_h(x_h, a_h) and the transition dynamics as P(o_{h+1}|x_h, a_h), avoiding explicit belief computation.

2. **Addressing Overlapping Dependencies in L-step Bellman:** A direct L-step Bellman expansion, Q^π_h(x_h, a_h) = E[Σ_{i=h}^{h+L-1} r(o_i, a_i) + V^π_{h+L}(x_{h+L})], still faces issues because x_{h+L} (which is (o_{h+1}, a_{h+1}..., o_{h+L})) overlaps with x_h = (o_{h-L+1}, a_{h-L+1}..., o_h). This overlap breaks a simple linear structure for the Q-function.

3. **Moment Matching Policy (ν^π):** To resolve the overlapping dependence, the authors introduce the concept of a "moment matching policy" (ν^π). This policy is constructed such that, while it implicitly depends on the L-step history, it *conditions on a sufficient latent variable to generate the same expected observation dynamics* as the original policy π, but *is independent of history older than L steps*. This allows for a factorization of the multi-step observation dynamics P(x_{h+L}|x_h, a_h) as an inner product ⟨p(·|x_h, a_h), P_{ν^π}(x_{h+L}|·)⟩, where p(·|x_h, a_h) represents the latent variable distribution given the current history window. Importantly, the latent variable z need not be the ground truth state, avoiding identifiability assumptions.

4. **Linear Representation of Q^π:** By combining the L-step Bellman equation with the moment matching policy, the Q-function can be represented linearly: Q^π_h(x_h, a_h) = ⟨p(·|x_h, a_h), w^π(·)⟩. Here, p(·|x_h, a_h) acts as a feature map of the observation history window and action, while w^π(·) is a weight vector or function that captures the policy-dependent expected cumulative rewards and future values. This linear structure addresses the difficulty of value function representation.

#### 3.3. Efficient Policy Evaluation and Learning

1. **Least Squares Policy Evaluation:** With the linear representation, the Bellman backup step for dynamic programming can be formulated as a least squares regression problem:
 min_{w^π_h} E [⟨w^π_h, p(·|x_h, a_h)⟩ - (Σ_{i=h}^{h+L-1} r(o_i, a_i) + ⟨w^π_{h+L}, p(·|x_{h+L}, a_{h+L})⟩)]^2.
 This optimization problem is efficiently solvable by stochastic gradient descent, replacing intractable integration with tractable regression.

2. **Variational Learning of μLV-Rep:** Since the latent variable representation p(·|x_h, a_h) is generally unknown, it is learned online. The authors use a variational learning approach based on maximizing the Evidence Lower Bound (ELBO) for the latent variable model p(o_{h+1:h+l}|x_h, a_h), where l is a chosen prediction horizon (e.g., l=1). This involves fitting a generative model for observations and an inference model for latent variables.

3. **Practical Parametrization of Q^π:** For continuous latent variables, Q^π_h(x_h, a_h) = E_{p(z|x_h)} [w^π_h(z)] is approximated using Monte-Carlo methods or random feature quadrature, where w^π_h(z) is implemented by a neural network. This allows for scalable learning in continuous state and action spaces.

#### 3.4. Planning and Exploration

For continuous action spaces, the learned μLV-Rep (as the critic) is integrated with policy gradient methods like Soft Actor-Critic (SAC).
For exploration, the principle of optimism in the face of uncertainty (OFU) is implemented by adding an ellipsoid bonus to the reward function. This bonus is computed using the random feature representation, allowing the agent to favor states/actions where there is high uncertainty in the value function estimate. A similar approach can be adapted for pessimism in offline RL settings.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents both theoretical guarantees and empirical demonstrations of the effectiveness of the μLV-Rep framework.

#### 4.1. Theoretical Findings

- **Linear Representation Provability:** The paper formally establishes that an L-decodable POMDP admits a sufficient representation, the Multi-step Latent Variable Representation (μLV-Rep), which supports an *exact and tractable linear representation of the value functions* for an arbitrary policy. This finding is central, as it breaks down the long-standing computational barriers associated with POMDPs by converting complex belief-dependent value functions into a linearly factorizable form.
- **Sample Complexity Guarantees:** The proposed algorithm, when combined with an appropriately chosen exploration bonus, is shown to achieve an ε-optimal policy with high probability. The theoretical analysis provides a PAC (Probably Approximately Correct) guarantee for the sample complexity, demonstrating a **polynomial dependence** on key parameters such as horizon H, effective action space size |A|^L, L, the inverse of optimality gap 1/ε, and logarithmic terms related to the model class size and confidence delta (e.g., poly(C, H, |A|^L, L, ε, log(|M|/δ)) episodes). Critically, this polynomial dependence avoids the exponential growth with horizon length that characterizes general POMDPs, confirming the statistical efficiency of the approach under the L-decodability assumption. The specific polynomial bounds vary depending on the eigendecay properties of the kernel used for the latent variable representation (finite spectrum, polynomial decay, or exponential decay).

#### 4.2. Empirical Results

The empirical evaluation of μLV-Rep was conducted on two main sets of benchmarks: Meta-world visual robotic manipulation tasks and partially observable continuous control problems based on OpenAI gym MuJoCo / DeepMind Control Suites.

- **Meta-world Visual Robotic Manipulation Tasks:**
 - **Performance:** μLV-Rep demonstrated superior performance compared to strong baselines, DreamerV2 and Masked World Model (MWM), across various tasks. This included faster convergence and achieving higher final success rates.
 - **Quantitative Success:** On 50 Meta-world tasks, μLV-Rep achieved a success rate above 90% on 33 tasks after 1 million environment steps. It performed better than or comparably to (within a 10% difference) the best baseline on 41 out of 50 tasks.
 - **Computational Efficiency:** μLV-Rep exhibited a training speed of 21.3 steps per second, which was notably faster than MWM (8.1 steps per second), indicating computational advantages despite its sophisticated representation learning.
 - **Ablation Studies:** Experiments on Meta-world (and DMC) demonstrated the importance of representation dimension and the optimal window size L. For instance, L=3 was found to be sufficient for learning in the test domains, and increasing representation dimension generally improved performance.

- **Partially Observable Continuous Control (MuJoCo/DeepMind Control Suites):**
 - **Partial Observability Setup:** Tasks were made partially observable by masking velocities in observations, a common technique.
 - **Comparative Performance:** μLV-Rep delivered competitive or superior outcomes across all tested domains (e.g., HalfCheetah, Humanoid, Walker, Ant, Hopper, Cheetah-run, Walker-run, Hopper-run, Humanoid-run, Pendulum) compared to model-based baselines (Dreamer, SLAC) and model-free baselines (SAC-MLP, neural PSR).
 - **Near Full-Observation Performance:** In many domains, μLV-Rep's performance nearly matched that of algorithms operating with *full observations* (Best-FO), confirming its ability to extract effective representations for decision-making even in partially observable settings.

Overall, the main findings demonstrate that the μLV-Rep framework provides a theoretically grounded and empirically effective solution for partially observable reinforcement learning, overcoming significant computational and statistical hurdles.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents several significant advancements with notable potential impacts on the field of reinforcement learning and its applications.

#### 5.1. Theoretical Significance

- **Bridging Theory and Practice for POMDPs:** The most significant theoretical contribution is the explicit revelation of a *linear representation for value functions* within structured (L-decodable) POMDPs. This is a crucial breakthrough, as it directly bridges the gap between theoretical results on the statistical efficiency of structured POMDPs and the development of computationally tractable algorithms. Previously, despite theoretical guarantees for reduced statistical complexity, practical planning in POMDPs often relied on intractable oracles or heuristics.
- **Overcoming Computational Barriers:** The μLV-Rep framework provides a mechanism to bypass the long-standing computational difficulties of POMDPs, such as the intractable belief state calculation and the exponential complexity of value function representation. By reducing the Bellman backup to a least-squares regression problem and allowing for efficient gradient-based optimization, it makes complex POMDPs amenable to modern deep learning techniques.
- **Generalization of Linear MDPs:** The work extends the insights from linear MDPs, which have been instrumental in developing provably efficient RL algorithms in fully observable settings, to the more complex partially observable domain. This extension, achieved through the novel use of "moment matching policies" and L-step Bellman expansions, demonstrates that linear structures can exist even when observation dynamics are policy-dependent.
- **Provable Efficiency with Partial Observability:** The provision of PAC guarantees for sample complexity, demonstrating polynomial dependence on key parameters, provides strong theoretical backing for the algorithm's efficiency. This is distinct from many heuristic methods that lack such guarantees.

#### 5.2. Practical Impact

- **Enabling Robust RL in Real-World Scenarios:** The development of a practical and provably efficient algorithm for POMDPs has direct implications for real-world applications where full state observability is rare. This includes robotics, autonomous systems, natural language interaction, and complex game environments, where agents must infer latent states from partial sensory inputs.
- **Improved Sample Efficiency and Performance:** Empirically, μLV-Rep consistently outperforms or matches state-of-the-art model-based and model-free baselines in various visual robotic manipulation and continuous control tasks. This improved sample efficiency and higher final performance can accelerate the development and deployment of RL systems.
- **Computational Tractability:** The demonstrated computational efficiency (e.g., faster training speed than MWM) makes μLV-Rep a viable option for large-scale problems, which is critical for real-world adoption.
- **Unified Framework for Learning, Planning, and Exploration:** The framework coherently integrates representation learning, policy evaluation (planning), and exploration (via optimism/pessimism bonuses) into a single, tractable algorithmic approach. This holistic design enhances its practicality.

#### 5.3. Potential Future Directions

The work lays a foundation for future research. Potential impacts could include:
- Exploring the applicability of μLV-Rep to a wider range of structured POMDPs beyond L-decodability and $\gamma$-observability.
- Investigating how this linear representation can be combined with other advanced representation learning techniques (e.g., contrastive learning) to further enhance performance.
- Applying the framework to more diverse and challenging real-world tasks, potentially leveraging larger-scale datasets and more complex neural architectures.
- Further refining exploration strategies within the μLV-Rep framework, particularly for very sparse reward settings.

In conclusion, this research marks a substantial step forward in making principled reinforcement learning in partially observable environments both theoretically sound and practically feasible, offering a robust foundation for future developments in the field.
