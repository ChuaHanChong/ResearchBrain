# Phys4DGen: Physics-Compliant 4D Generation with Multi-Material Composition Perception

> [!summary] Summary
> Phys4DGen, developed by researchers at Xiamen University, enables the generation of physically compliant 4D content from a single image or 3D model by perceiving multi-material compositions and internal structures. It utilizes multimodal large language models to rapidly identify material properties, resulting in realistic dynamics and fine-grained control over object deformation.

## Problem

- Existing 4D generation methods often produce physically unrealistic motions due to their reliance on data-driven priors without enforcing physical laws.
- Prior physics-integrated approaches require either manual, expert-level specification of material properties or computationally expensive and often inaccurate iterative optimization processes.
- Current methods largely assume homogeneous, single-material objects and lack robust mechanisms for modeling internal structures, limiting the realism of deformations for complex, heterogeneous objects.

## Method

- A framework is introduced that segments 3D ==Gaussian Splatting== (3DGS) representations into distinct material groups and discovers internal physical structures within the object's volume.
- Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs), such as GPT-4o, are leveraged to automatically infer accurate material properties (density, Young's modulus, Poisson's ratio) for each perceived material component.
- The ==Material Point Method== (==MPM==) is integrated for robust physical simulation, iteratively updating particle states and reconstructing deformed 3DGS representations to generate dynamic 4D content.

## Results

- Phys4DGen demonstrated superior physical realism (PR scores up to 7.87) and overall quality (OQ scores up to 7.97) compared to state-of-the-art image-to-4D and 3D-to-4D generation methods.
- Material property identification was achieved in approximately 14.88 seconds, representing a drastic efficiency improvement over prior methods that required hours (5688.79s to 7273.32s).
- Qualitative results show realistic, localized deformations for multi-material objects and stable dynamics, effectively preventing structural collapse seen in baselines lacking internal structure modeling.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The explicit perception of multi-material compositions and the discovery of internal physical structures are crucial for generating stable and physically plausible deformations in complex objects.
> - MLLMs can serve as efficient 'multimodal physics experts,' providing rapid and accurate material property estimations, thus significantly reducing computational overhead and expert dependency.
> - The particle-based nature of 3D Gaussian Splatting aligns effectively with continuum mechanics simulations like MPM, facilitating seamless integration for physics-driven 4D content generation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The generation of 4D content, defined as dynamically evolving 3D representations, has become a pivotal area in computer animation, interactive gaming, and virtual reality. Historically, 4D generation has seen significant advancements through data-driven generative models, particularly video diffusion models. These models excel at producing high-quality, temporally consistent visual priors, enabling automated content creation. However, a fundamental limitation of these data-driven approaches is their inherent lack of physical modeling constraints, often leading to motions that violate physical laws and exhibit inconsistencies.

To address this, the research landscape has shifted towards incorporating physical priors into 4D generation. Early attempts like PhysGaussian pioneered the integration of continuum mechanics (specifically the Material Point Method, MPM) with 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) representations, enabling more realistic physical dynamics. However, these methods typically required manual specification of material properties, demanding significant physics expertise from users. Subsequent efforts, such as PAC-NeRF and GIC, attempted to estimate physical properties from multi-view videos, but their reliance on extensive and often hard-to-obtain multi-view data limited practical applicability.

More recently, methods like PhysDreamer, DreamPhysics, and Physics3D sought to automate material property estimation by leveraging dynamic priors from pre-trained video diffusion models. While these approaches reduced the strict input constraints by working with 3DGS models rather than multi-view videos, they introduced new challenges. The stochastic nature and non-physical dynamic priors embedded in video diffusion models could often mislead material property estimation, resulting in inaccuracies. Furthermore, their iterative optimization processes were computationally expensive and highly sensitive to initial material property settings, still posing a barrier for general users without domain-specific knowledge.

Phys4DGen directly addresses these critical limitations, positioning itself as a significant advancement in the following ways:
1. **Multi-Material Composition:** Existing methods largely assume objects are monolithic, made of a single material. Phys4DGen acknowledges the real-world complexity of multi-material objects, tackling heterogeneous compositions for the first time in this context.
2. **Internal Structure Modeling:** While 3DGS excels at surface representation, it lacks interior modeling, which is crucial for realistic deformation under stress. Phys4DGen explicitly addresses this by discovering and modeling internal physical structures.
3. **Efficient and Automated Material Identification:** Unlike methods relying on manual input or slow, potentially misleading video diffusion priors, Phys4DGen leverages the vast, embedded physical knowledge of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) like GPT-4o for rapid and automatic material property identification. This greatly enhances user-friendliness and efficiency.

By integrating multi-material composition perception, internal structure discovery, MLLM-guided material identification, and robust physical simulation (MPM), Phys4DGen aims to bridge the current gaps, enabling fast, user-friendly, and physically plausible 4D content generation without the need for extensive manual input or specialized physics expertise. It pushes the boundary of generative models towards greater physical fidelity and practical utility in diverse applications.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of Phys4DGen is to create a robust and user-friendly framework for generating high-fidelity, dynamically evolving 3D content (4D content) that adheres to physical laws. This framework is designed to operate efficiently from a single input image or an existing 3D representation.

The motivation for developing Phys4DGen stems from several critical limitations observed in prior 4D content generation methodologies:

1. **Lack of Multi-Material Handling:** A significant challenge in current 4D generation is the prevalent assumption that objects are uniform entities composed of a single material. In reality, most objects are heterogeneous, featuring multiple materials with distinct physical properties. This disparity limits the realism of simulations, as it prevents accurate modeling of localized deformation behaviors. Phys4DGen aims to overcome this by enabling the perception and handling of multi-material compositions, ensuring more accurate and nuanced physical responses.

2. **Inadequate Internal Structure Modeling:** Representations like 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS), while effective for surface geometry and appearance, inherently lack detailed internal structural information. This deficiency is particularly problematic for physical simulations, as the interior composition significantly influences an object's mechanical behavior, especially under large deformations. Without proper internal modeling, simulations are prone to structural collapse or unrealistic deformations. The motivation here is to develop a mechanism for discovering and representing an object's internal physical structure to enhance simulation fidelity and stability.

3. **Inefficient and Expert-Dependent Material Property Identification:** Existing physics-integrated 4D generation methods either require users to manually specify material properties (which demands specialized physics expertise) or rely on iterative optimization guided by video diffusion models. The latter approach is often computationally expensive, slow, and susceptible to the non-physical biases of diffusion models, frequently leading to inaccurate material property estimations. Furthermore, its performance is highly sensitive to initial parameter settings, again requiring expert knowledge. Phys4DGen is motivated to provide an automatic, rapid, and user-friendly method for identifying accurate material properties, thereby democratizing physically plausible 4D content generation for a broader audience.

To address these motivations, Phys4DGen sets out to achieve the following specific objectives:
- To develop a 3D Material Grouping module that can accurately partition the surface of 3D representations into distinct material regions using semantic segmentation from vision foundation models.
- To design an Internal Physical Structure Discovery module that effectively models the mechanical structure of object interiors and assigns appropriate material groups to internal particles.
- To leverage multimodal large language models (MLLMs) as a "multimodal physics expert" to distill physical prior knowledge and automatically identify material properties (density, Young's modulus, Poisson's ratio) for both surface and internal structures efficiently.
- To seamlessly integrate these perception capabilities with physical simulation algorithms, specifically the Material Point Method (MPM), to generate physically plausible and temporally coherent 4D dynamics.
- To demonstrate superior performance over state-of-the-art methods in terms of physical realism, spatio-temporal consistency, and computational efficiency on both synthetic and real-world datasets.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Phys4DGen introduces a novel, physics-driven framework for 4D generation that integrates multi-material composition perception with physical simulation. The overall pipeline, as illustrated in the paper's Figure 2, comprises four main stages: Static 3D Gaussians Generation, Material Grouping and Internal Discovery, MLLMs-Guided Material Identification, and 4D Dynamics Generation.

#### 3.1. Static 3D Gaussians Generation

The process begins with an input, either a single image or an existing 3D model. For a single image, Phys4DGen leverages state-of-the-art image-to-3D generation methods, specifically LGM, to produce a static 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) representation. 3DGS is chosen for its explicit representation of 3D objects as a collection of anisotropic Gaussian kernels, which can be interpreted as particles. This particle-based nature is highly advantageous for direct integration with particle-based physical simulation algorithms. Each Gaussian kernel is defined by its center position, covariance matrix, opacity, and color.

#### 3.2. 3D Material Grouping

Recognizing that real-world objects are often composite, this module is designed to assign a unique material group to different regions of the 3D Gaussians.
- **Pre-Process:** The generated static 3D Gaussians are rendered from multiple viewpoints to create an image sequence and corresponding depth maps. SAM2, a powerful vision foundation model known for video segmentation, is then applied to this sequence to generate consistent 2D mask maps across views. Each unique mask index is treated as a material group label.
- **Projection and Aggregation:** Each 3D Gaussian kernel is projected into 2D space onto the multi-view mask maps. Visibility is checked using the 3DGS-estimated depth. For each visible Gaussian, its material group is recorded from the corresponding mask. A majority voting scheme across all views determines the final material group for each 3D Gaussian kernel, effectively partitioning the object's surface into distinct material regions.

#### 3.3. Physical Internal Structure Discovery (IPSD)

This module addresses the limitation of 3DGS representations, which primarily describe surfaces, by modeling the object's interior.
- **Particle Initialization and Filtering:** Grid particles are uniformly sampled within the bounding box of the 3D Gaussians. These particles are then projected onto the multi-view mask images and depth maps. Particles are filtered out if their projected depth significantly differs from the rendered depth or if they fall outside the foreground mask, ensuring they represent the object's volume.
- **Surface and Internal Classification:** The filtered grid particles are categorized into surface and internal particles. Surface particles are those within a predefined Mahalanobis distance from any existing 3D Gaussian kernel. The outermost layer of grid particles is also designated as surface particles to ensure boundary closure. All other particles are classified as internal particles.
- **Material Group Assignment:** Material groups from the surface particles are propagated to the internal particles to infer their composition. Each surface particle inherits the material group of its nearest Gaussian kernel. For each internal particle, rays are cast along the six principal axes, and the material groups of the intersected surface particles are collected. The most frequently observed material group is then assigned to the internal particle. Finally, the initial 3D Gaussians are merged with these filled internal particles, creating a unified continuum particle representation `P` that includes material groups for both surface and interior.

#### 3.4. MLLMs-Guided Material Identification

This module leverages the extensive physical knowledge of multimodal large language models (MLLMs) to automatically infer material properties.
- **Material Information Reasoning:** The user-input image, treated as a canonical view, along with sub-images of segmented material components, is fed into GPT-4o, an advanced MLLM. Specific prompts (detailed in Appendix A.2) guide GPT-4o to infer the material types (e.g., plastic, fabric) and key physical properties (Density, Young's modulus, Poisson's ratio) for both the surface and internal parts of each material region.
- **CLIP Fusion:** To ensure consistency, the MLLM-inferred material properties are aligned with the material groupings of the continuum particles. This is achieved by extracting CLIP embeddings for the segmented sub-images from the canonical view and a rendered front view of the 3D object. The similarity between these CLIP features is used to establish correspondences between material groups, allowing the inferred surface and internal material properties to be accurately assigned to the corresponding continuum particles. This forms a complete material continuum representation `~P` that includes spatial positions, material groups, and physical properties.

#### 3.5. 4D Dynamics Generation

The final stage simulates the dynamics using a particle-based physical simulation algorithm.
- **MPM Simulation:** Phys4DGen integrates the Material Point Method (MPM), which is well-suited for modeling the motion and deformation of continuum materials under external forces (details in Appendix C). Each particle `p` in `~P` carries properties like mass, density, volume, Young's modulus, Poisson's ratio, velocity, and deformation gradient. MPM iteratively transfers these properties between Lagrangian particles and an Eulerian grid to compute forces and update particle states.
- **3DGS Reconstruction:** At each time step of the MPM simulation, the updated particle positions and deformation gradients are used to reconstruct the deformed 3DGS representation. The local deformation described by the deformation gradient is applied as an affine transformation to the covariance matrix of each Gaussian kernel.
- **Output and Control:** The sequence of deformed 3DGS representations over time constitutes the final 4D content, exhibiting physically plausible dynamics. A key advantage of the multi-material grouping is the ability to precisely apply external forces (e.g., gravity, pushes, pulls) to specific material regions, enabling fine-grained control without manual selection.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

Phys4DGen demonstrates significant advancements in 4D content generation, particularly in achieving physical realism, multi-material perception, and efficiency. The findings are supported by both qualitative and quantitative comparisons on synthetic and real-world datasets.

#### 4.1. Qualitative Results

- **Multi-Material Composition Perception:** As shown in Figure 3, Phys4DGen effectively discerns and models the distinct physical attributes of different materials within a single object. Examples include differentiating the petals and stems of carnations or the fabric surface and polyester fiberfill interior of a doll. This capability leads to localized and varied deformation behaviors that align with real-world physics.
- **Physical Realism in Open-World Scenarios:** The generated 4D content consistently exhibits high fidelity and adherence to physical laws across diverse scenarios (e.g., orange roses swaying, sandcastle collapsing, rubber duck being pressed – Figure 9). The space-time slices (Figure 5 and Figure 10) illustrate that Phys4DGen's dynamics, including oscillation frequency and intensity, more closely resemble real captured videos compared to baselines.
- **Superiority over Image-to-4D Baselines:** In qualitative comparisons against state-of-the-art image-to-4D methods like DG4D and STAG4D (Figure 4), Phys4DGen generates motions that are significantly more physically plausible and controllable. For instance, while DG4D might show a balloon and wooden block moving upwards against gravity, Phys4DGen produces consistent, gravity-obeying dynamics. This highlights the limitations of purely data-driven methods that lack physical constraints.
- **Improved 3D-to-4D Dynamics:** When compared to physics-incorporating 3D-to-4D methods such as PhysGaussian, PhysDreamer, and Physics3D (Figure 5, Figure 10), Phys4DGen produces dynamics that are more stable, exhibit better structural preservation, and show more realistic material responses, especially under large external forces. Baselines often suffer from over-distorted or unstable dynamics.

#### 4.2. Quantitative Results

- **Image-to-4D Generation (Table 1):** Phys4DGen significantly outperforms baselines in both objective and subjective metrics. It achieves the highest scores for:
 - **Physical Realism (PR):** 7.50 (vs. 5.90-6.30 for baselines).
 - **Overall Quality (OQ):** 7.72 (vs. 5.97-6.70 for baselines).
 - **CLIP-T (Spatio-temporal consistency):** 0.99459 (vs. 0.98813-0.99242).
 - **CLIP-T-other (Consistency across views):** 0.99409 (vs. 0.98492-0.99275).
 These metrics confirm that Phys4DGen generates 4D content that is not only visually consistent but also convincingly adheres to physical laws.
- **3D-to-4D Generation (Table 2):** For tasks starting with 3D content, Phys4DGen maintains its lead:
 - **Physical Realism (PR):** 7.87 (highest).
 - **Overall Quality (OQ):** 7.97 (highest).
 - **CLIP-T:** 0.99925 (highest).
 Crucially, Phys4DGen achieves material property estimation in a remarkable **14.88 seconds**, a drastic improvement over the **hour-level computation times** (5688.79s to 7273.32s) required by PhysDreamer and Physics3D. This highlights the efficiency gained from MLLM-guided material identification.
- **MLLM Reliability (Table 3 and 4):** Ablation studies on MLLM estimations for objects like a snowman revealed that material predictions exhibit strong preference patterns and a high degree of consistency across different MLLMs (GPT-4o, GPT-o3, Gemini 2.5 Pro). This suggests that MLLMs have internalized substantial material property knowledge, making them reliable estimators for downstream physical simulations.

#### 4.3. Ablation Analysis (Figure 6 and 7)

- **Importance of Material Grouping and IPSD:** The ablation study clearly demonstrates the synergistic necessity of both the Material Grouping module and Internal Physical Structure Discovery (IPSD).
 - Without Material Grouping, objects deform uniformly, contradicting physical reality for heterogeneous materials (e.g., bun and beef deforming similarly).
 - Without IPSD, the lack of internal structural support leads to structural collapse under large deformations, despite capturing material differences.
 - The complete model, with both modules enabled, shows ideal simulation performance, maintaining structural stability and exhibiting appropriate localized deformations.
- **Material Editability:** Material Grouping enables fine-grained control and localized material editing. An example with a snowman (Figure 7) shows that melting can be simulated specifically on the snowman's body without affecting the scarf, which would be impossible if treated as a uniform material. This underscores the practical utility of multi-material perception for interactive applications.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

Phys4DGen represents a significant leap forward in the field of 4D content generation, addressing several long-standing challenges and opening new avenues for realistic and user-friendly dynamic content creation. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Enhanced Physical Realism:** By explicitly modeling multi-material compositions and internal structures, Phys4DGen generates 4D content that adheres much more closely to real-world physics than previous data-driven or partially physics-informed methods. This high degree of physical plausibility is crucial for applications where visual fidelity and believable interactions are paramount.

2. **Increased Efficiency and Accessibility:** The integration of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) for material property identification drastically reduces the computational overhead from hours to mere seconds. This efficiency, combined with the automation of a typically expert-dependent step, makes advanced 4D content generation accessible to a broader user base, including designers, artists, and general users who lack specialized physics knowledge. This democratization of complex simulation tasks is a major impact.

3. **Fine-Grained Control and Editability:** The proposed 3D Material Grouping module enables users to apply external forces and manipulate properties on specific regions of an object. This fine-grained control allows for highly customized and interactive 4D dynamics, such as simulating localized melting or targeted deformation, which was previously challenging or impossible without extensive manual segmentation and property assignment.

4. **Robustness for Complex Objects:** By effectively tackling heterogeneous materials and internal structures, Phys4DGen moves beyond simplistic single-material assumptions, offering a robust framework for generating dynamic content for real-world objects that inherently possess complex compositions. This expands the scope of objects that can be realistically animated.

5. **Foundation for Future Research:** Phys4DGen establishes a modular framework that can readily integrate with evolving image-to-3D generation techniques, new MLLMs with enhanced physical reasoning capabilities, and various particle-based physical simulators. This makes it a strong foundation for future advancements in generalizable and physically intelligent content creation.

6. **Broad Application Potential:** The advancements offered by Phys4DGen have far-reaching implications across multiple domains:
 - **Computer Animation and Film:** Creation of more believable character and object interactions, leading to more immersive visual storytelling.
 - **Interactive Gaming and Virtual Reality:** Enabling highly realistic environmental interactions and object deformations, enhancing user immersion and gameplay realism.
 - **Product Design and Engineering Simulation:** Providing faster and more intuitive ways to visualize how product prototypes might deform or respond to forces, aiding in design iterations.
 - **Education and Training:** Developing interactive simulations for scientific or engineering principles, making complex concepts more tangible.

While currently designed for single-object 4D generation, the authors acknowledge extending it to multi-object scenarios as an exciting future direction. This suggests that the core principles of Phys4DGen could eventually contribute to the creation of entire dynamic scenes with physically coherent interactions between multiple complex entities, further amplifying its potential impact.
