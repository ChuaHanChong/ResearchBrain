# InEKFormer: A Hybrid State Estimator for Humanoid Robots

> [!summary] Summary
> The InEKFormer, a hybrid state estimator developed by researchers at the University of Bremen and DFKI, integrates an Invariant Extended Kalman Filter (InEKF) with a Transformer network to enhance floating base state estimation for the RH5 humanoid robot. It demonstrates superior estimation accuracy over KalmanNet in short-sequence tests and benefits from increased training data, while also providing a new open-source dataset for humanoid robotics research.

## Problem

- Classical state estimators like Kalman filters require extensive manual tuning of noise parameters and can suffer from linearization errors or compromised theoretical properties with state augmentations.
- Purely data-driven methods for high-dimensional robotic state estimation often demand large, high-quality datasets and considerable computational effort.
- Existing internally coupled hybrid state estimators have primarily been tested on simplified systems, lacking application to complex, full-size humanoid robots and their high-dimensional state spaces.

## Method

- The **InEKFormer** integrates a model-based ==Invariant Extended Kalman Filter (InEKF)== with a Transformer network to implicitly learn and predict the Kalman gain matrix.
- The state representation is augmented to include continuous left and right foot contact states, allowing the estimator to handle alternating bipedal locomotion.
- The gain estimator utilizes five specific input features derived from observation and state differences, along with contact states, processed by a Transformer encoder-decoder architecture.

## Results

- InEKFormer models achieved significantly smaller estimation errors (up to magnitudes lower) than KalmanNet models in baseline tests with short, unseen sequences.
- Increased training data generally improved InEKFormer's filter performance, with models trained on real data outperforming simulation-only trained models by approximately 100 times in RMSE for multiple trajectories.
- The model-based InEKF maintained superior estimation in z-direction velocity compared to the best-performing InEKFormer model, suggesting areas for further dataset refinement.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Transformer networks are effective for learning complex statistical information required for Kalman gain prediction in high-dimensional humanoid robot state estimation, outperforming RNN-based approaches like KalmanNet.
> - Autoregressive training, though challenging to stabilize for high-dimensional regression tasks, consistently yields superior performance compared to teacher-forcing mode.
> - A significant sim-to-real gap exists, as models trained solely on simulated data perform substantially worse on real robot data, highlighting the necessity of real-world data or improved simulation fidelity for diverse motions.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Humanoid robots are increasingly deployed in real-world environments, necessitating robust and stable locomotion capabilities across various terrains. A critical component for achieving such performance is state estimation, which provides rapid and accurate feedback regarding the robot's floating base state to its motion controller. Traditional state estimation methods, such as Kalman filters, are widely employed but often demand extensive expert knowledge for the manual tuning of noise parameters. Furthermore, their performance can be affected by linearization errors in the case of Extended Kalman Filters (EKF), or by loss of theoretical properties when state elements like sensor biases are incorporated in methods like Invariant Extended Kalman Filters (InEKF).

The broader research landscape for state estimation algorithms can be categorized into model-based, data-driven, and hybrid approaches. Recent trends indicate that hybrid methods, which integrate elements from both model-based and data-driven techniques, are demonstrating performance advantages over purely model-based or data-driven alternatives.

Within hybrid methods, two main categories exist:
- **Externally coupled approaches** combine model-based and data-driven algorithms in parallel or succession without altering their internal structures. Examples in legged robotics often involve enhancing InEKF performance by providing learned inputs (e.g., inertial odometry, contact events, leg odometry) or refining its output. However, many of these applications have been limited to quadruped robots and have not explored humanoid robot use cases.
- **Internally combined approaches** involve data-driven models taking over internal functionalities of model-based algorithms. KalmanNet, for instance, utilizes a Recurrent Neural Network (RNN) to predict the Kalman gain for an EKF, addressing nonlinearities and model mismatches. Extensions to KalmanNet have been proposed, including unsupervised and uncertainty-aware versions. KalmanFormer also uses a Transformer to learn the Kalman gain in an EKF. A limitation of these internally combined approaches, however, is their primary application to canonical examples with reduced state spaces (e.g., simple pendulum, 3D Lorenz attractor, planar robots), rather than complex, high-dimensional systems like full-size humanoid robots. Moreover, the model-based components in these methods often rely on standard Kalman filters, which the InEKF has demonstrated to outperform in specific state estimation contexts.

This paper addresses a specific research gap by proposing an internally coupled hybrid state estimation method that integrates the InEKF, known for its robustness and convergence properties, with a Transformer network. Crucially, this method is applied and evaluated on a full-size humanoid robot, the RH5, a scenario not widely explored in the existing literature for such hybrid architectures.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The development of the InEKFormer is motivated by several challenges and limitations inherent in current state estimation practices for humanoid robots.
The primary challenge for humanoid robots lies in achieving stable and dynamic bipedal locomotion across diverse and potentially unstructured environments. The ability to execute such movements safely and robustly depends critically on fast and accurate estimations of the robot's floating base position, orientation, and velocity. These estimates must be derived from noisy and often incomplete sensor data, including Inertial Measurement Units (IMUs), leg kinematics, and contact measurements.

The motivations and objectives of this work are detailed as follows:

- **Addressing Limitations of Classical State Estimators:** Conventional model-based state estimators, such as Kalman filters, require significant expert intervention for manual tuning of noise covariance matrices, which is complex and time-consuming. Furthermore, filters like the Extended Kalman Filter (EKF) can suffer from linearization errors, potentially leading to inaccurate estimates or divergence. While the Invariant Extended Kalman Filter (InEKF) mitigates linearization issues by exploiting Lie group symmetries, its theoretical properties can be compromised when additional state elements like IMU biases are introduced. The motivation is to overcome these limitations by incorporating a data-driven component that can implicitly learn these complex noise characteristics.

- **Overcoming Deficiencies of Purely Data-Driven Approaches:** Although deep learning methods, including RNNs and Transformers, have shown promise in processing sequential data, their application to high-dimensional state estimation in robotics presents challenges. These include the requirement for large volumes of high-quality training data, which can be difficult to acquire in robotics, and the substantial computational effort needed for high-frequency estimation as the state space size increases. The objective is to leverage the strengths of data-driven models while mitigating these data and computational demands through a hybrid design.

- **Exploiting Synergies through Hybrid Methods:** The research is motivated by the observed trend that hybrid approaches, combining model-based and data-driven techniques, often surpass the performance of purely model-based or data-driven methods. The goal is to harness the benefits of both: leveraging known system dynamics and kinematics from model-based filters, while using data-driven intelligence to compensate for unmodeled parameters, sensor noise, and model mismatches.

- **Developing an Internally Coupled Hybrid State Estimator for Humanoids:** A specific gap in the literature is identified in the application of internally coupled hybrid state estimators to full-size humanoid robots. Previous internally combined methods, like KalmanNet and KalmanFormer, have primarily been tested on simplified systems with reduced state spaces and often relied on standard EKF structures rather than the more robust InEKF. The key objective is to address this gap by proposing the InEKFormer, a novel hybrid architecture that fully integrates the InEKF with a Transformer network. The Transformer's role is to predict the Kalman gain, thereby implicitly learning to adjust the filter's behavior to better account for real-world complexities.

- **Application to a Full-Size Humanoid Robot:** The work aims to validate the proposed InEKFormer on the RH5 humanoid robot, moving beyond the canonical examples typically used for such methods. This involves handling the high-dimensional state space and the complexities of bipedal locomotion, including alternating foot contact states.

- **Creation and Publication of a Dedicated Dataset:** To facilitate the training and evaluation of the InEKFormer on a humanoid robot, a new dataset is created. This dataset includes motion capture ground truth alongside proprioceptive measurements from the RH5 robot performing various motions. A further objective is to make this dataset openly available to the research community.

- **Comprehensive Performance Evaluation:** A significant objective is to rigorously compare the InEKFormer's performance against established baselines, specifically the model-based InEKF and the hybrid KalmanNet, to objectively assess its estimation accuracy and efficiency.

In summary, the core motivation is to develop a robust, accurate, and adaptable state estimator for humanoid robots by synergistically combining the theoretical rigor of InEKF with the learning capabilities of Transformer networks, thereby enhancing performance in challenging, real-world scenarios.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The InEKFormer is designed as an internally combined hybrid state estimation algorithm. It integrates the model-based Invariant Extended Kalman Filter (InEKF) with a data-driven Transformer network. Unlike KalmanNet, which typically uses Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs) to estimate Kalman gain, the InEKFormer utilizes a Transformer model, citing its contextual interpretation capabilities and scalability for larger problem sizes. The InEKF's state propagation and correction steps, along with its Lie group-based state matrix computations, form the foundation of the model-based component.

**A. Invariant Extended Kalman Filter (InEKF) Principles:**
The InEKF propagates the current state estimate using a strapdown IMU model and IMU measurements. The state correction step computes a refined state using the Lie exponential function, incorporating the Kalman gain matrix and an observation vector derived from leg odometry. The InEKFormer's hybrid nature leads to the omission of explicit state covariance matrix propagation and correction, as the necessary uncertainty information is implicitly learned by the Transformer.

**B. Hybrid State Estimation Algorithm Details:**
The InEKFormer estimates the Kalman gain by implicitly learning statistical information from a history of state and observation differences, rather than relying on hand-crafted noise covariance matrices. This design aims for adaptability across various robotic platforms subject to model mismatch and sensor noise.

For application on the RH5 humanoid robot, which exhibits alternating foot contacts, the state representation is augmented. The Transformer network requires a fixed input token dimension, so the state is permanently augmented with both left and right foot contacts. This results in an SE4(3) Lie group state matrix, X, which includes the robot's floating base rotation (W_B R), velocity (W_v B), and position (W_p B) in world coordinates, as well as the world positions of the left (W_c L) and right (W_c R) foot contact frames. Foot contacts are modeled as point contacts due to the RH5's flat feet.

Right-invariant observation vectors (B_Y_L and B_Y_R) are computed for each foot, representing the position of the respective contact in the floating base coordinates based on joint angles (forward kinematics).

Contact state information ($\mu_c = [\mu_L, \mu_R]^T$) is provided to the gain estimator at each time step. This is derived by applying a logistic sigmoid function to the z-direction contact force, yielding continuous scalar values between 0 (no contact) and 1 (stable contact). This continuous representation allows for more nuanced contact estimation.

The gain estimator model uses five input features, adapted from KalmanNet to align with the InEKF's Lie group structure:
1. **F1: Observation difference ($\Delta \tilde{Y}$):** The difference between the current and previous observation vectors.
2. **F2: Innovation difference ($\Delta Y$):** The innovation vector, which is the difference between the propagated state and the observation in the Lie group space.
3. **F3: Forward evolution difference ($\Delta \tilde{X}$):** A measure of how the state evolves from one corrected estimate to the next.
4. **F4: Forward update difference ($\Delta \hat{X}$):** A measure of the difference between the corrected state and the propagated state.
5. **F5: Contact states ($\mu_c$):** The calculated continuous contact state for each foot.

Features F1-F4 are computed in world coordinates, ensuring they are right-invariant differences consistent with the robot's state representation. F1 and F2 are concatenated into a single vector before being processed.

**C. Gain Estimator Model Architecture:**
The gain estimator model employs a Transformer network with an encoder-decoder architecture, utilizing scaled dot-product attention to predict the Kalman gain matrix (K).

- **Input Preprocessing:** Input features F1-F4 are scaled using a robust scaler to a specific quantile range, a hyperparameter for optimization.
- **Feature Embedding and Encoding:**
 - F1 and F2 (observation-related statistical information) are concatenated into a 12-dimensional vector. This vector is then processed by a learned fully-connected embedding layer ($\Phi_Y$) and fed into a Transformer encoder ($\Psi_Y$), yielding a latent representation of the observation noise ($Z_N$). This process is conceptually analogous to the InEKF's computation of the observation covariance matrix.
 - F3 and F4 (system noise-related statistical information) are flattened, concatenated into a 42-dimensional vector, embedded by another fully-connected layer ($\Phi_X$), and fed into a Transformer decoder ($\Psi_X$). This produces a latent representation of estimation uncertainty ($Z_K$), mirroring the InEKF's covariance propagation step.
- **Preliminary Kalman Gain Estimation:** A fully connected decoder ($\Phi_K$) computes a preliminary Kalman gain matrix ($\bar{K}$).
- **Final Kalman Gain (with Contact Integration):** To integrate the contact information (F5) without making the Transformer's size dependent on the contact state representation, the preliminary Kalman gain $\bar{K}$ is post-processed. The columns of $\bar{K}$ corresponding to the left and right foot contacts are multiplied by their respective continuous contact states ($\mu_L$ and $\mu_R$) to obtain the final estimated Kalman gain ($\hat{K}$).

**D. Robot Dataset:**
A custom dataset was collected using the RH5 humanoid robot, encompassing both simulated (RobotDART) and real-world experiments. The RH5 is a 32-DoF humanoid equipped with IMU (400 Hz), joint encoders (~150 Hz average), and 6-axis force/torque (FT) sensors at the ankles (1 kHz). Kinematics and dynamics are handled by the HyRoDyn library. The dataset includes 30 real and 14 simulated trajectories, featuring motions like walking, squatting, single-leg balancing, and waist movements. All data was resampled or downsampled to 150 Hz. Ground truth data for the floating base and foot poses in real-world experiments was obtained from a Qualisys motion capture system, tracked at 300 Hz and smoothed with a Butterworth filter. The dataset comprises 575,258 samples over 50 minutes and is openly available.

**E. Optimization and Training:**
The gain estimator model is trained in a supervised manner using a Mean Squared Error (MSE) loss function. The loss is computed as the Frobenius norm of the difference between the corrected state (derived using the predicted Kalman gain $\hat{K}$) and the motion capture ground truth state. Gradients are backpropagated using truncated backpropagation through time with scheduled sampling (TBPTT-SS), which transitions from teacher-forcing to fully autoregressive training. The Adam optimizer with a OneCycleLR learning rate scheduler is used. Hyperparameter optimization is performed using a Tree-structured Parzen Estimator (TPE) sampler. Model selection is based on the filter's performance within the integrated loop, quantified by the Root Mean Square Error (RMSE) of the absolute state estimation error.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of the InEKFormer method involved comparisons with the model-based InEKF and the hybrid KalmanNet across three categories of tests: baseline, single trajectory, and multiple trajectories. Various InEKFormer (labeled $\Omega$) and KalmanNet (labeled $\Gamma$) models were trained under different conditions (simulation/real data, teacher-forcing/autoregressive mode).

**i) Baseline Tests (Short Sequences):**
- **Overfitting and Generalization:** InEKFormer models ($\Omega_1, \Omega_2$) demonstrated the ability to overfit to short training sequences (10 samples) and then gradually deviate on subsequent unseen samples (10 samples), suggesting that the models learned general motion patterns beyond simple reproduction of training data.
- **Comparison with KalmanNet:** KalmanNet ($\Gamma_1$) exhibited oscillations around the training samples, indicating that its RNN-based approach was less effective for the high-dimensional data of the RH5 humanoid robot compared to the Transformer-based InEKFormer.
- **Training Mode Impact:** Autoregressive training consistently yielded superior performance compared to teacher-forcing mode, with RMSE values up to three orders of magnitude lower for InEKFormer models ($\Omega_2, \Omega_4$) and KalmanNet ($\Gamma_4$).
- **Relative Performance:** InEKFormer models achieved significantly smaller estimation errors (magnitudes lower) than KalmanNet models, establishing InEKFormer's superiority in these baseline tests.
- **Dataset and Feature Validation:** This category of tests confirmed that the developed dataset and selected input features provided the necessary causal information for accurate Kalman gain prediction.

**ii) Single Trajectory Tests (Generalization and Sim-to-Real Transfer):**
- **Training Conditions:** Models ($\Omega_5, \Omega_6, \Gamma_5, \Gamma_6$) were trained on 75% of a single simulated or real-world walking trajectory in teacher-forcing mode (autoregressive training was not successful for these larger tasks). They were then tested on the remaining 25% of the trajectory, operating in a one-timestep-ahead prediction mode with ground truth feedback.
- **InEKFormer Performance:** Model $\Omega_6$ (trained on real data) generally outperformed $\Omega_5$ (trained on simulation data) across most state dimensions, although $\Omega_5$ showed better performance for x and z-axis rotation.
- **Comparison with InEKF:** Both InEKFormer models were outperformed by the model-based InEKF in terms of orientation, velocity, and z-axis position estimation.
- **KalmanNet Limitations:** KalmanNet models ($\Gamma_5, \Gamma_6$) demonstrated an inability to learn robot motion patterns effectively, even with teacher-forcing, further indicating their limitations with high-dimensional data.
- **Sim-to-Real Potential:** For a single motion, some potential for sim-to-real transfer learning was observed, as $\Omega_5$ (trained on simulation) outperformed $\Omega_6$ (trained on real data) in specific orientation axes. However, $\Omega_5$ also exhibited high-frequency transverse oscillations.

**iii) Multiple Trajectory Tests (Scalability and Data Quantity):**
- **Training Conditions:** Models ($\Omega_7, \Omega_8, \Omega_9$) were trained on datasets comprising multiple motion trajectories, with $\Omega_7$ trained only on simulation data, $\Omega_8$ on real robot data (excluding the test trajectory), and $\Omega_9$ on the entire dataset (excluding the test trajectory). KalmanNet training failed to converge for these larger datasets.
- **Impact of Training Data:** A clear trend was observed where increased training data generally led to improved filter performance (lower RMSE values).
- **Sim-to-Real Gap:** Model $\Omega_7$ (trained on simulation data only) performed substantially worse than models trained on real data ($\Omega_8, \Omega_9$), exhibiting heavier transversal oscillations. The RMSE values of $\Omega_8$ and $\Omega_9$ were approximately 100 times smaller than $\Omega_7$, highlighting a significant sim-to-real gap that becomes more pronounced with larger, diverse datasets.
- **Comparison with InEKF:** The InEKF continued to demonstrate superior estimation in the z-direction velocity, even outperforming the best-performing InEKFormer model ($\Omega_9$), despite the InEKF's inherent constant drift.
- **Model Efficiency:** InEKFormer models were found to be more parameter-efficient than KalmanNet, often having up to 10 times fewer trainable parameters while achieving better or comparable estimation performance. Models trained solely on simulated data (e.g., $\Omega_1, \Omega_2, \Omega_5, \Omega_7$) generally required more parameters than those trained on real robot data, further indicating the influence of the sim-to-real gap on model complexity.

**Discussion Points:**
- **Autoregressive Training Challenge:** Stable autoregressive training for Transformer-based regression tasks, especially with high-dimensional robotics data, was a significant challenge. The authors suggest that this may be due to the demanding nature of regression (requiring a single precise output) compared to classification (where multiple outputs can be considered "correct" as long as the target class probability is highest).
- **Inconsistent Sampling Frequencies:** Transversal oscillations observed in some InEKFormer models were partly attributed to the varying sampling frequencies of the robot's joint encoders (120-190 Hz around an average of 150 Hz). This inconsistency can lead to over-correction.
- **Dataset Improvement:** The superior performance of InEKF in the z-direction suggests that the current dataset may require improvements, particularly in this dimension, possibly through a more realistic simulation to reduce the sim-to-real gap.
- **Inference Speed:** Average inference times ranged from 50 to 350 ms on a 4-core i5-3320M CPU. The authors suggest that specialized processors or more efficient Transformer architectures could significantly reduce these times for real-time applications.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work presents a novel hybrid state estimation method, the InEKFormer, contributing to the field of humanoid robotics by addressing limitations of both traditional and purely data-driven approaches. The significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Advancement in Hybrid State Estimation:** The InEKFormer represents an advancement in internally coupled hybrid state estimation. By integrating a state-of-the-art model-based InEKF with a Transformer network, it moves beyond previous hybrid methods that were limited to simpler systems or less robust filter components. This approach demonstrates the potential of deep learning, specifically Transformers, to enhance the accuracy and robustness of state estimation in complex robotic systems.

- **Improved Humanoid Locomotion:** Accurate and fast state estimation is foundational for stable and dynamic bipedal locomotion. By providing more reliable feedback on the robot's floating base state, the InEKFormer could enable humanoid robots to perform complex maneuvers and navigate varied, unstructured environments with increased safety and stability. This has direct implications for the deployment of humanoids in industrial, domestic, healthcare, and search-and-rescue applications where robust and reliable movement is paramount.

- **Application to High-Dimensional Systems:** A key contribution is the application and evaluation of such an internally coupled hybrid method on a full-size, high-dimensional humanoid robot (RH5). This moves the research beyond canonical examples and provides insights into the practical challenges and performance of these advanced estimators in real-world robotics. The outperformance of KalmanNet on high-dimensional data, in particular, highlights the InEKFormer's scalability potential.

- **Open Dataset Contribution:** The creation and public release of a new dataset for humanoid robot state estimation, featuring motion capture ground truth alongside proprioceptive measurements, is a valuable resource for the research community. This dataset can facilitate further research, benchmarking, and the development of new state estimation algorithms for complex robotic platforms.

- **Insights into Sim-to-Real Transfer and Training Challenges:** The study provides important insights into the challenges of sim-to-real transfer learning and the complexities of stable autoregressive training for Transformer-based regression models in robotics. The observed sim-to-real gap for multiple motions and the difficulties with autoregressive training highlight critical areas for future research and development, which could lead to more robust learning strategies for real-world robotic applications.

- **Path for Future Research and Optimization:** The findings indicate clear directions for future work, including the need for advanced regularization techniques and Lie group-aware Transformer architectures to achieve stable autoregressive training, enabling online execution on robots. Addressing the sim-to-real gap through improved simulation fidelity and optimal data blending strategies is also identified as a crucial next step. The potential for generalizing the InEKFormer to other types of robots (quadrupeds, aerial, wheeled) further broadens its impact. Additionally, exploring smaller, more efficient Transformer models could significantly improve real-time performance, making the InEKFormer more suitable for onboard robot computation.

In conclusion, the InEKFormer represents a significant step towards developing more intelligent and adaptive state estimators for humanoid robots, with the potential to enhance their capabilities and expand their operational domains.
