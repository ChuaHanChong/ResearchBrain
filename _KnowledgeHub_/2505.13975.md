# DRP: Distilled Reasoning Pruning with Skill-aware Step Decomposition for Efficient Large Reasoning Models

> [!summary] Summary
> The DRP framework enables small-scale Large Reasoning Models to achieve efficient and accurate reasoning by using a powerful teacher model to prune and refine the student's own verbose reasoning trajectories, notably reducing token usage by 64% while improving GSM8K accuracy from 91.7% to 94.1%. This process is enhanced by skill-aware step decomposition, which creates precise, functionally aligned reasoning units for more effective distillation.

## Problem

- Large Reasoning Models (LRMs) tend to "overthink," producing excessively verbose and often redundant reasoning steps, leading to high inference overhead and computational costs.
- Existing inference-time pruning methods often risk prematurely halting reasoning, causing a degradation in accuracy for complex tasks.
- Traditional distillation methods face a "learnability gap" when attempting to transfer concise reasoning from a teacher to a student model that naturally generates verbose explanations, hindering effective knowledge transfer.

## Method

- The ==Distilled Reasoning Pruning (DRP)== framework is introduced, employing a three-stage process to refine student model reasoning: ==Skill-Based Step Decomposition==, ==Step-Level Pruning==, and Supervised Fine-Tuning.
- A powerful teacher model (e.g., GPT-4o) is used to segment the student's initial verbose reasoning into fine-grained, skill-labeled steps, ensuring semantic coherence.
- The teacher then performs step-level revision actions (keep, delete, rewrite, merge) on these decomposed steps to create a concise, refined reasoning trace, which is used to fine-tune the student model.

## Results

- DRP achieved substantial token reductions, such as a **64%** decrease on GSM8K (from **917** to **328** tokens), while consistently maintaining or improving Pass@1 accuracy across various benchmarks, including an increase from **91.7%** to **94.1%** on GSM8K.
- The framework significantly outperformed prior methods like TALE and CoT-Valve in both compression and accuracy, particularly on out-of-domain tasks, and showed competitive performance with RL-based ThinkPrune with higher accuracy improvements.
- Ablation studies confirmed the superiority of skill-based decomposition for robust generalization and demonstrated DRP's effectiveness is consistent across different teacher models, with greater impact on smaller student models (e.g., **12.7%** accuracy increase for 1.5B model on GSM8K).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Effective reasoning distillation requires supervision that is not merely short but also informative and structurally consistent with the student's natural reasoning style to bridge the learnability gap.
> - Skill-based step decomposition provides a more stable and effective basis for pruning and revision than simple sentence splitting, enhancing both fine-grained signal and out-of-domain generalization.
> - Teacher-guided structural pruning of a student model's own verbose Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning is more effective than direct distillation of a teacher's concise CoT, leading to simultaneous improvements in efficiency and accuracy.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Large Reasoning Models (LRMs) have demonstrated advanced capabilities in complex reasoning tasks, such as those found in mathematics and coding. However, a prevalent challenge with these models, particularly smaller ones, is their tendency to generate verbose reasoning trajectories, often referred to as "overthinking." This verbosity leads to inefficiencies due to increased token usage and can sometimes misdirect the model toward incorrect conclusions.

Existing approaches to address this issue typically fall into two categories:
1. **Inference-time pruning**: Methods that attempt to terminate reasoning generation prematurely to avoid redundant steps. Examples include techniques that employ early-exit mechanisms or set target lengths.
2. **Distillation-based compression**: Approaches where smaller "student" models are trained on reasoning paths generated by larger "teacher" models, aiming to replicate the teacher's concise reasoning style.

Both paradigms face limitations. Inference-time pruning risks halting the reasoning process before a complete or correct solution is reached, potentially sacrificing accuracy. Distillation methods often encounter a "learnability gap," particularly when the student model's natural reasoning style (e.g., verbose Long-CoT) mismatches the teacher's concise reasoning (e.g., Short-CoT). This stylistic discrepancy can hinder effective knowledge transfer and lead to suboptimal performance. Prior research has identified this compatibility issue as a significant barrier to effective learning.

This work addresses the limitations of both pruning and distillation by proposing a hybrid framework, Distilled Reasoning Pruning (DRP), that unifies these two paradigms. DRP aims to reduce the "overthinking" problem by leveraging a teacher model to *prune* a student model's initial verbose reasoning trajectories, rather than merely distilling concise teacher-generated responses. This approach is designed to bridge the learnability gap by providing structurally similar, yet refined, reasoning paths for the student to learn from, thus improving both efficiency and accuracy without compromising the depth of reasoning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the efficiency and accuracy of Large Reasoning Models, specifically focusing on smaller-scale student models, by mitigating their tendency toward verbose and redundant reasoning.

The motivation for developing Distilled Reasoning Pruning (DRP) stems from several key observations and challenges within the field:

1. **Addressing Model Verbosity and Inefficiency**: Large Reasoning Models, while powerful, frequently generate excessively long Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning trajectories. This "overthinking" results in substantial computational overhead, increased inference costs, and can sometimes lead to reasoning drift and incorrect conclusions. The motivation is to achieve more concise and efficient reasoning without compromising solution quality.

2. **Bridging the Learnability Gap in Distillation**: Traditional knowledge distillation methods often struggle when there is a significant mismatch between the reasoning styles of the student and teacher models. Specifically, a common scenario involves a student model that naturally generates verbose, reflective reasoning (Long-CoT) attempting to learn from a teacher model that produces highly polished, compact reasoning (Short-CoT). This discrepancy can lead to inefficient knowledge transfer, as the student model may not effectively internalize the teacher's reasoning patterns. The motivation is to develop a distillation strategy that accounts for and actively manages this stylistic difference.

3. **Combining Strengths of Pruning and Distillation**: Existing pruning methods often risk accuracy loss by prematurely truncating necessary reasoning steps, while direct distillation of short CoTs can lead to a loss of critical intermediate steps required for complex problem-solving. The motivation is to create a hybrid framework that leverages the strengths of both approaches: using the guidance of a powerful teacher model to intelligently prune and refine the student's *own* reasoning, thereby retaining essential logical steps while removing redundancy.

**The key contributions of this work are explicitly stated as:**
- Proposing Distilled Reasoning Pruning (DRP), a framework that unifies step-level pruning and distillation to enhance both reasoning efficiency and accuracy in small-scale LLMs.
- Introducing a skill-based step decomposition method that segments reasoning traces into semantically coherent and functionally aligned units, which provides a robust foundation for pruning and supervision.
- Highlighting that effective training Chain-of-Thoughts (CoTs) should be both informative and structurally consistent with the student’s reasoning process. This consistency is identified as crucial for facilitating knowledge transfer and narrowing the capability gap between teacher and student models.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The Distilled Reasoning Pruning (DRP) framework is a three-stage process designed to refine verbose reasoning traces generated by a student model using guidance from a concise teacher model. The refined traces are then used to fine-tune the student.

#### 3.1. Skill-Based Step Decomposition

1. **Initial Student Response Generation**: The student model first generates an initial response R, which comprises a detailed reasoning trace T enclosed within `<think></think>` tags and a final answer summarization A.
2. **Trace Extraction and Teacher Prompting**: The trace T is extracted and provided to a teacher model. The teacher is prompted to decompose T into a sequence of non-overlapping segments.
3. **Skill-Aligned Segmentation**: Each segment, denoted as `s_i`, is paired with a corresponding functional reasoning skill `k_i` (e.g., arithmetic, logical inference, interpreting quantity). This "skill-based" segmentation is intended to yield more stable step boundaries, preserve semantic coherence, and offer finer-grained supervision signals compared to simpler methods like sentence splitting.
4. **Formal Representation**: The decomposition function `D` maps the trace `T` to a set of step-skill pairs: `D(T) → {(s_1, k_1), (s_2, k_2)..., (s_m, k_m)}`.

#### 3.2. Step-Level Pruning

1. **Teacher-Guided Revision**: For each `(s_i, k_i)` pair, the teacher model is prompted to revise the step. This revision process aims to condense the step without altering its fundamental logical intent or structure.
2. **Pruning Actions**: The teacher model selects one of four actions for each step `s_i`:
 - **Keep**: Retain the step as is.
 - **Delete**: Remove the step if it is deemed redundant or uninformative.
 - **Rewrite**: Rephrase the step into a more concise version that conveys the same logic.
 - **Merge**: Combine the step with adjacent steps if they form a single, coherent atomic reasoning unit.
3. **Revised Trace Generation**: These actions result in a revised step `ŝ_i = Revise(s_i)`, and the collection of revised steps forms a pruned reasoning trace `T̂ = {ŝ_1, ŝ_2..., ŝ_m′}`, where `m′ ≤ m`. This process reduces redundancy and increases information density.
4. **Final Coherence and Answer Alignment**: The teacher model then rewrites `T̂` into a fluent, coherent reasoning trace, maintaining the student model’s original tone and style. To ensure consistency, the teacher optionally revises the original final answer `A` to `Â`. The complete revised response `R̂ = (T̂, Â)` is then used for fine-tuning.

#### 3.3. Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT)

1. **Dataset Construction**: A training dataset is created using pairs `(x, R̂)`, where `x` is the input question and `R̂` is the complete revised response generated in the pruning stage.
2. **Training Objective**: The student model is fine-tuned using teacher-forced decoding. The training objective is to minimize the negative log-likelihood of the revised response: `L_SFT = - Σ log P_θ(y_i | x, y_<i)`, where `{y_1..., y_n}` are the tokens in `R̂`, and `θ` represents the model parameters.
3. **Goal**: This fine-tuning enables the student model to internalize the skill-aligned, token-efficient reasoning strategies derived from the teacher's pruning, thereby improving both reasoning efficiency and accuracy.

#### 3.4. Experimental Setup

- **Training Data**: The training splits of GSM8K and the full PRM12K dataset were used to generate initial reasoning paths, which were then processed by DRP.
- **Evaluation Benchmarks**: Out-of-domain mathematical reasoning tasks were used for evaluation, including MATH500, AIME24, and AMC23, to assess generalization.
- **Student Models**: DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B and DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B, which are distilled variants optimized for efficient inference.
- **Teacher Models**: GPT-4o was the primary teacher for decomposition and pruning. For ablation studies, Gemini 2.0 Flash, Deepseek V3, and ChatGPT were also explored.
- **Compared Methods**:
 - **TALE**: A prompt-based method that incorporates a soft token budget constraint.
 - **CoT-Valve**: An SFT-based method that trains models on multiple rounds of increasingly shorter CoTs.
 - **ThinkPrune**: A tuning-based method using reinforcement learning to reduce CoT length under token constraints.
- **Implementation**: Supervised fine-tuning was performed using the LLAMA-FACTORY framework with LoRA adaptation, trained for 3 epochs with a cosine learning rate schedule.
- **Evaluation Metrics**: Pass@1 accuracy (averaged over five runs) and average number of reasoning tokens generated per completion (with a 12k token cutoff to mitigate outlier long generations).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

#### 4.1. Accuracy and Token Efficiency

The DRP method consistently demonstrated improvements across various benchmarks and model sizes:
- **Token Usage Reduction**: DRP achieved substantial reductions in average token usage on both in-domain (e.g., up to 64% on GSM8K with the 7B model) and out-of-domain tasks (28%–44% reductions across MATH500, AIME24, and AMC), indicating strong generalization.
- **Accuracy Improvement**: Despite significant token reductions, DRP either preserved or improved Pass@1 accuracy on nearly all benchmarks. Notably, accuracy improved on harder datasets such as AMC and MATH500. For AIME24 with the 7B model, accuracy remained unchanged, which was attributed to the benchmark's inherent difficulty.
- **Benefits for Smaller Models**: Accuracy gains were more pronounced for the 1.5B model, with a 12.7% increase on GSM8K and 4 more problems correctly solved on AIME24 and AMC compared to the base model. This suggests DRP's effectiveness in enhancing limited model capacity through more efficient supervision.

#### 4.2. Comparison to Prior Work

DRP demonstrated superior or competitive performance compared to existing token-efficient reasoning strategies:
- **Prompt-based methods (TALE)**: While simple and moderately effective for short-answer tasks like GSM8K (with minor accuracy degradation), TALE's effectiveness diminished significantly on more challenging, complex reasoning benchmarks, often causing notable performance drops.
- **SFT-based methods (CoT-Valve)**: CoT-Valve achieved token reductions but frequently incurred accuracy trade-offs, particularly on MATH500 and AMC. DRP consistently delivered stronger compression alongside accuracy improvements across most benchmarks.
- **RL-based methods (ThinkPrune)**: ThinkPrune showed strong performance, especially for smaller models, indicating the effectiveness of pruning. However, DRP yielded higher accuracy improvements, attributed to its high-quality teacher-guided pruning, while maintaining competitive compression rates.

#### 4.3. Token Usage Distribution Shift

Analysis of token length distributions revealed that DRP effectively compressed reasoning:
- **Concise Reasoning**: The main density of token distribution shifted leftward across all benchmarks (GSM8K, MATH500, AIME24, AMC), particularly on harder out-of-domain tasks, indicating more concise reasoning.
- **Mitigation of Degenerate Reasoning**: DRP significantly reduced "long-tail" completions, which often result from verbose or degenerate reasoning. A clear drop in density near the upper token limit for AIME24 and AMC suggested that DRP helps models avoid excessively long or looping outputs, improving robustness.
- **Optimization in High-Performing Regimes**: Even on datasets where baseline models already produced relatively short completions (e.g., GSM8K, MATH500), DRP still achieved measurable compression gains, indicating its ability to optimize reasoning beyond mere verbosity reduction.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies

- **Skill-based Decomposition vs. Default Step Split**: Skill-based decomposition consistently outperformed both default step-wise splitting (without skill labels) and no decomposition. It led to higher accuracy (e.g., +2 on AMC) and more effective token reduction. This was partly attributed to finer granularity (12.6 steps vs. 8.3 steps on GSM8K). Skipping decomposition entirely resulted in significant performance degradation, underscoring the necessity of explicit step segmentation. The benefits of skill-based decomposition generalized to out-of-distribution tasks, providing more transferable supervision.
- **Structured Pruning vs. Direct Distillation**: Comparing DRP with direct distillation from GPT-4o (which produced much shorter CoTs, averaging 186 tokens vs. DRP's 330 tokens) showed that while direct distillation reduced tokens on GSM8K with a minor accuracy drop, it led to sharp accuracy drops on OOD tasks (e.g., 88.6% vs. 93.0% on MATH500). This indicates that simply providing shorter CoTs does not generalize well and can omit critical steps. DRP's approach of removing redundancy while preserving a step-wise, skill-aligned structure facilitated more robust reasoning and better generalization.
- **Influence of Teacher Model Choice**: Experiments with GPT-4o, Gemini 2.0 Flash, ChatGPT, and DeepSeek-V3 as teacher models demonstrated that all teachers consistently improved both accuracy and token efficiency over the base model, confirming the robustness of DRP. GPT-4o achieved the strongest performance, particularly in token compression. The study noted that teacher-student family alignment was not critical for DRP's effectiveness, suggesting broad applicability across different teacher models.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Distilled Reasoning Pruning (DRP) framework presents a notable advancement in addressing the efficiency and accuracy challenges associated with Large Reasoning Models, particularly for smaller-scale deployments. Its significance and potential impact are multifold:

- **Improved Efficiency and Reduced Inference Costs**: DRP directly tackles the "overthinking" problem by significantly reducing the token usage in reasoning trajectories. This translates into lower inference costs and faster response times, making the deployment of powerful reasoning capabilities more economically viable and accessible, especially for resource-constrained environments or applications requiring high throughput. The reported token reductions across diverse datasets highlight a practical benefit for real-world applications.

- **Enhanced Reasoning Accuracy for Smaller Models**: Beyond efficiency, DRP demonstrates the ability to maintain or even improve reasoning accuracy, particularly for smaller student models. This is crucial as it suggests that judicious pruning and structured distillation can compensate for limited model capacity, allowing smaller models to achieve performance levels closer to larger, more expensive counterparts without sacrificing critical reasoning steps. This can democratize access to advanced reasoning capabilities.

- **Effective Knowledge Transfer through Structural Alignment**: The introduction of skill-based step decomposition and the structured pruning process are key innovations that bridge the "learnability gap" in distillation. By refining the student's own verbose CoTs into semantically coherent, skill-aligned units, DRP provides a more effective and transferable supervision signal. This finding underscores the importance of structural consistency between training data and the student model's inherent reasoning process, offering valuable insights for future research in knowledge distillation and fine-tuning.

- **Robustness and Generalization**: DRP's consistent performance improvements across various in-domain and out-of-domain mathematical benchmarks, as well as its insensitivity to the specific choice of teacher model, indicate a robust and generalizable approach. This suggests that the principles behind DRP are broadly applicable and not overfit to specific task domains or teacher model characteristics, increasing its potential utility across different reasoning tasks.

- **Mitigation of Degenerate Reasoning**: The observed reduction in long-tail completions and the mitigation of degenerate reasoning loops are important for model stability and reliability. By preventing models from generating excessively long or looping outputs, DRP contributes to more predictable and robust inference behavior, which is critical for trustworthy AI systems.

- **Guidance for Future LRM Development**: The insights derived from DRP regarding the construction of informative and structurally consistent training CoTs can guide the development of future efficient reasoning models. This work suggests that simply having "short" CoTs is not enough; the *quality* and *structure* of those CoTs, derived from a careful refinement process, are paramount for effective learning.

While the authors acknowledge limitations regarding generalizability to other architectures and the rapidly evolving nature of LRM training paradigms, DRP represents a significant step toward developing more efficient, accurate, and robust reasoning capabilities in large language models.
