# Planning in 8 Tokens: A Compact Discrete Tokenizer for Latent World Model

> [!summary] Summary
> A compact discrete tokenizer, CompACT, allows world models to achieve up to an 80x speedup in planning latency for navigation and robotic manipulation while maintaining competitive performance. This is achieved by encoding observations into as few as 8-16 tokens, prioritizing planning-critical semantic information over photorealistic detail.

## Problem

- World models, while powerful, face a computational bottleneck due to high-fidelity visual representations (hundreds to thousands of latent tokens per image).
- Attention-based architectures, common in generative world models, scale quadratically with token count, making real-time planning prohibitively slow (e.g., minutes per episode).
- The focus on pixel-perfect image reconstruction in existing tokenizers is misaligned with the actual requirements for efficient planning, which needs compact, action-relevant representations.

## Method

- Developed **CompACT**, a ==compact discrete tokenizer== that encodes images into 8-16 discrete tokens by leveraging a ==frozen, pretrained vision encoder (DINOv3)== and a latent resampler with ==Finite Scalar Quantization (FSQ)==.
- Introduced a ==generative decoding== strategy for CompACT, which, instead of direct pixel reconstruction, learns to generate higher-fidelity latent tokens from a separate pretrained target tokenizer using ==masked generative modeling==.
- Trained action-conditioned world models directly on CompACT's compact discrete latent space using masked generative modeling to predict future states for efficient ==Model Predictive Control (MPC)==.

## Results

- Achieved competitive planning accuracy on visual navigation tasks (ATE **1.330**, RPE **0.390**) while delivering approximately a **40x speedup** in planning latency (**5.78s** vs. **178.78s**) compared to SD-VAE baselines.
- Demonstrated that using latent-space cost functions during planning provides an **~80x** overall speedup with minimal accuracy degradation.
- CompACT (16 tokens) showed a **3x lower** Action Prediction Error (APE) and was **5.2x faster** in action-conditioned video prediction compared to a 256-token baseline, indicating better preservation of action-driven dynamics.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Effective planning in world models prioritizes compact, semantic representations of decision-critical information over photorealistic perceptual details.
> - Leveraging frozen foundation models (like DINOv3) during tokenization encourages the emergence of modular, semantically meaningful latent tokens that are highly beneficial for capturing action-relevant state changes.
> - Decoupling the generation of planning-critical semantic content from the synthesis of high-frequency perceptual details allows for extreme compression without sacrificing planning performance.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated at the intersection of reinforcement learning (RL), generative modeling, and vision-based control, specifically addressing a critical challenge in the development and deployment of "world models." World models are neural networks that learn to simulate environment dynamics, enabling agents to predict future states given current observations and actions. This capability is vital for mitigating the sample inefficiency of traditional model-free RL and facilitating tasks like action planning and policy learning without extensive real-world interaction.

Recent advancements in generative models, particularly in photorealistic image and video generation, have significantly enhanced world models' ability to predict future observations. These models often capture rich perceptual details, suggesting an implicit understanding of underlying world dynamics. However, this pursuit of photorealism introduces a major computational bottleneck. Conventional image tokenizers, commonly used in these generative world models (e.g., SD-VAE), encode each observation into hundreds or even thousands of latent tokens. When combined with attention-based architectures, which scale quadratically with token count, this high dimensionality makes planning computationally prohibitive for real-time applications. For instance, state-of-the-art Navigation World Models (NWM) can require minutes of computation per episode, rendering them unsuitable for dynamic environments demanding rapid decision-making.

The paper argues that for planning tasks, agents do not require pixel-perfect recall but rather compact, action-relevant mental representations, akin to human cognitive processes. The conventional approach of increasing token count for higher fidelity is therefore misaligned with the true requirements of planning.

"Planning in 8 Tokens" directly challenges this paradigm. It proposes a novel approach to overcome the computational bottleneck by prioritizing *extreme compression* of visual information over *photorealistic reconstruction*. By drastically reducing the token count while preserving planning-critical semantics, this work aims to make world models practical for real-time control. It aligns with a growing interest in efficient and scalable RL, pushing the boundaries towards more deployable AI systems that can operate effectively within computational constraints. The paper positions itself as a practical step towards enabling widespread real-world deployment of world models in tasks like navigation and robotic manipulation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation behind this research stems from the significant computational burden imposed by current world models, which impedes their application in real-time decision-making and control. While world models offer a powerful framework for simulating environment dynamics, their reliance on high-fidelity visual representations, often involving hundreds of latent tokens per observation, leads to prohibitively slow planning. This is particularly problematic for attention-based architectures, where computational cost scales quadratically with the number of tokens. The authors highlight that state-of-the-art models like NWM can take several minutes per planning episode, making them impractical for real-world scenarios requiring responsiveness.

The key objectives of this work are driven by a bold hypothesis:
1. **Prioritize Extreme Compression:** The primary objective is to develop a novel, compact discrete tokenizer, named **CompACT**, capable of encoding each image into as few as 8 to 16 discrete tokens. This represents an aggressive compression strategy, aiming to drastically reduce the computational cost associated with world model rollouts.
2. **Preserve Planning-Critical Information:** Despite extreme compression, a crucial objective is to ensure that CompACT preserves sufficient information essential for effective planning. The hypothesis is that forcing the model to abstract away perceptual details will lead it to learn more semantically relevant, action-focused representations.
3. **Enable Efficient Real-time Planning:** The ultimate goal is to demonstrate that an action-conditioned world model built upon CompACT's compact latent space can achieve competitive planning performance with orders-of-magnitude faster planning latency. This would offer a practical pathway toward real-world deployment of world models in applications like visual navigation and robotic manipulation.
4. **Validate Semantic Abstraction:** The authors also aim to validate their underlying design philosophy: that effective planning requires compact representations of decision-critical information (e.g., object-level semantics, spatial relationships) rather than photorealistic detail.

By pursuing these objectives, the research seeks to overcome a fundamental scalability issue in world modeling, thereby expanding the applicability of these powerful RL tools to computationally constrained and time-sensitive real-world environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology revolves around two core components: the **CompACT tokenizer** and a **world model** trained to operate within its compact, discrete latent space. The overall formulation follows a latent generative model paradigm for world modeling.

#### 3.1. Latent Generative Model as World Model

The authors frame the world model as a latent generative model `f_phi: (z_t, a_t) -> p_phi(z_{t+1}|z_t, a_t)`, where `z_t` are low-dimensional latent tokens obtained from an observation `o_t`. The process involves:
1. **Tokenizer Training (Fig. 1a):** An image tokenizer (CompACT) is trained to map input images `o` to compact latent tokens `z` and reconstruct them.
2. **Latent World Model Training (Fig. 1b):** The world model `f_phi` is then trained on these compact latent tokens to predict future states, modeling the conditional distribution `p_phi(z_{t+1}|z_t, a_t)`. This is done using masked generative modeling.
3. **Decision-time Planning (Fig. 1c):** At inference, the trained world model is used for Model Predictive Control (MPC). An optimization procedure (e.g., Cross-Entropy Method, CEM) searches for a sequence of actions `a_0:H-1` that minimizes the distance `d(D(z_H), o_goal)` between the predicted final state `z_H` (decoded to `o_H`) and a goal observation `o_goal`.

#### 3.2. CompACT Tokenizer Architecture

CompACT (Compact Action-Conditioned Tokenizer) is designed to encode each image into a minimal number of discrete tokens (N=8 or 16), fundamentally reducing the computational complexity for subsequent world model operations.

- **Semantic Encoding via Frozen Features (E_compact):**
 - **Principle:** Prioritize planning-critical semantic information over high-frequency perceptual details.
 - **Mechanism:** The encoder `E_compact` leverages a **frozen, pretrained vision encoder (DINOv3)** as its backbone. DINOv3 is chosen for its ability to abstract away low-level visual details and capture rich semantic representations.
 - **Process:** Input images are patchified and encoded by frozen DINOv3 to get semantic patch representations. These representations are then processed by a **latent resampler**, which uses learnable query tokens in a transformer decoder-based architecture. These query tokens cross-attend to the DINOv3 outputs, distilling high-level semantic cues (object identities, spatial layouts, scene structure). The resampler's output is finally discretized using **Finite Scalar Quantization (FSQ)** to yield the N discrete latent tokens `z`. This design implicitly ensures that only planning-critical information is retained.

- **Generative Decoding (D_compact):**
 - **Challenge:** Directly reconstructing pixels from extremely compact (8-16) tokens is an ill-posed problem due to the immense information loss.
 - **Solution:** Instead of direct pixel reconstruction, `D_compact` learns to generate latent tokens from a **pretrained target tokenizer (MaskGIT-VQGAN)**, which typically uses hundreds of tokens (e.g., 196) and captures fine-grained perceptual details.
 - **Process:** `D_compact` takes CompACT's compact tokens `z` as conditioning. It then uses **masked generative modeling** to predict the higher-fidelity latent tokens `z_psi` from the target tokenizer. During training, a random subset of `z_psi` is masked, and `D_compact` learns to recover them using `z` and the unmasked `z_psi` (Eq. 4). During inference, `D_compact` iteratively unmasks `z_psi` based on prediction confidence. The final pixel-level reconstruction `o_hat` is then obtained by passing `z_psi` through the target tokenizer's decoder `D_psi`. This strategy transforms an intractable decompression problem into a tractable conditional generation task.

#### 3.3. World Model in CompACT Latent Space

With the CompACT tokenizer, the world model `f_phi` is trained directly on the N-token discrete latent space.
- **Training Objective:** Similar to the tokenizer's decoder, `f_phi` is trained using masked generative modeling to predict `z_{t+1}` conditioned on `z_t, a_t`, and `M(z_{t+1})` (Eq. 5).
- **World Model Architectures:**
 - **Navigation Tasks:** An **autoregressive framework** (DiT-based architecture) is used, conditioning on a fixed-length history window of latents and actions. History masking is applied during training to enhance robustness.
 - **Robotic Manipulation Tasks:** A **block-causal transformer** is employed, designed to predict multiple future frames in parallel while maintaining causal dependencies.

This modular and layered approach allows CompACT to achieve extreme compression by leveraging semantic understanding from foundation models and decoupling semantic content from perceptual detail through generative decoding, making world model planning significantly more efficient.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The paper presents compelling evidence for CompACT's effectiveness across image reconstruction, latent space characterization, and diverse planning tasks.

#### 4.1. Tokenizer Evaluation and Ablations

- **Reconstruction Performance:** CompACT (16 tokens) achieves competitive reconstruction quality on ImageNet, with an rFID of 2.40 and an IS of 209.0. This is comparable to state-of-the-art tokenizers like MaskGIT-VQGAN (rFID 1.83, IS 186.7 for 256 tokens) and significantly better than FlexTok (rFID 5.60 for 16 tokens), despite using 16 times fewer tokens than MaskGIT-VQGAN and 64 times fewer than SD-VAE (rFID 0.64 for 1024 tokens). The higher IS for CompACT suggests it better preserves perceptually relevant features.
- **Encoder Design Ablations:** Crucially, experiments demonstrate that using a *frozen pretrained vision encoder* (DINOv3-B) is key to CompACT's performance. Finetuning DINOv3-B or training from scratch leads to significantly worse rFID (e.g., 5.22 vs. 2.40 for frozen DINOv3), indicating that preserving the foundation model's semantic abstraction is vital.
- **Generative Decoding Ablations:** The generative decoding strategy is also critical. Replacing the generative decoder with a single-step feedforward decoder drastically degrades reconstruction quality (rFID 28.80), confirming its role in synthesizing fine-grained details.

#### 4.2. Characterizing CompACT Latent Tokens

- **Modular Scene Elements:** Attention visualizations show that CompACT's compact latent tokens naturally attend to distinct, semantically meaningful regions or objects within an image (e.g., individual animals, structural elements like buildings, robot end-effectors). This emergent *compositional structure* is attributed to leveraging frozen DINOv3 features.
- **Benefits for Planning:** An Inverse Dynamics Model (IDM) trained on CompACT latents (16 tokens) for RoboNet outperforms an IDM trained on MaskGIT-VQGAN latents (256 tokens) in predicting end-effector positions (L1 error 0.091 vs 0.093; R^2 0.716 vs 0.684). This indicates that CompACT's modular tokens are inherently better at capturing action-relevant state changes.

#### 4.3. Planning in CompACT Latent Space

- **Goal-Conditioned Visual Navigation:** On the RECON benchmark, an NWM (Navigation World Model) trained with CompACT (16 tokens) achieves comparable planning accuracy (ATE 1.330, RPE 0.390) to the SD-VAE baseline (ATE 1.262, RPE 0.354), while delivering an approximate **40x speedup in planning latency** (5.78s vs. 178.78s). The 8-token CompACT model is even faster (4.83s). Notably, CompACT significantly outperforms FlexTok models (16 and 64 tokens) in planning accuracy, validating the superior design of its extreme compression.
- **Qualitative Planning:** Generated planning rollouts accurately preserve planning-critical information like spatial layout and object positions, even though fine-grained visual details are synthesized.
- **Ablations on Planning:**
 - **History Masking:** Incorporating history masking during world model training improves planning accuracy.
 - **Cost Function:** While pixel-space cost (LPIPS) is slightly more accurate, using latent-space cost (L1 on FSQ outputs) offers an **~80x overall speedup** with minimal accuracy degradation.
 - **Frozen Encoder:** Similar to reconstruction, *finetuning* the vision encoder (instead of keeping it frozen) degrades planning performance, reinforcing the importance of semantic abstraction.

#### 4.4. Action-Conditioned Video Prediction

- On RoboNet, CompACT (16 tokens) achieves a **3x lower Action Prediction Error (APE)** compared to the 256-token baseline (0.1122 vs. 0.3383) and is **5.2x faster** in generation. This demonstrates that CompACT's latent representations better preserve action-driven dynamics.
- Qualitative results show that videos generated using CompACT tokens maintain consistent, action-driven end-effector movements, unlike the baseline.

#### 4.5. Closed-loop Robot Arm Manipulation

- In a preliminary closed-loop manipulation experiment on RoboMimic, CompACT (16 tokens) matches the target tokenizer (256 tokens) in success rate (56%) while requiring 17% fewer action steps, suggesting more efficient path planning.

#### 4.6. Scalability

- A larger 750M-parameter CompACT world model improved navigation accuracy (ATE 1.305) and remained 7x faster than the SD-VAE baseline, confirming that the compact latent space enables scaling up model capacity without sacrificing practical efficiency.

In summary, the main findings consistently support the paper's central hypothesis: extreme compression, when carefully designed to prioritize planning-critical semantics, can lead to world models that are both significantly more efficient and equally (or sometimes more) effective for decision-making tasks than their high-fidelity counterparts.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research presented in "Planning in 8 Tokens" marks a significant step forward in the field of world models and reinforcement learning, with profound implications for the practical deployment of AI agents. Its significance can be broken down into several key areas:

1. **Bridging the Gap to Real-time Control:** The most immediate and impactful contribution is the drastic reduction in planning latency achieved by CompACT. By demonstrating a **40x to 80x speedup** over existing state-of-the-art methods while maintaining competitive accuracy, the paper directly addresses a critical bottleneck preventing world models from being deployed in real-time, computationally constrained environments. This makes complex tasks like autonomous navigation, robotic manipulation, and other decision-making processes feasible for practical applications where immediate responses are paramount.

2. **Validation of Semantic Abstraction for Planning:** The work fundamentally challenges the prevailing notion that world models require photorealistic image generation for effective planning. By showing that *extreme compression* (as few as 8-16 tokens per image) is not only viable but can even enhance planning performance (e.g., superior IDM performance, better APE than higher token count baselines), the authors validate their hypothesis that *semantic abstraction* of decision-critical information is more important than visual fidelity. This paradigm shift could redirect research efforts towards more efficient and purpose-driven representation learning for RL.

3. **Novel Tokenization Strategy:** CompACT's tokenizer design is innovative. Its use of a **frozen pretrained vision foundation model (DINOv3)** for semantic encoding, combined with a **generative decoding strategy** that synthesizes perceptual details by conditioning on a high-fidelity target tokenizer, elegantly solves the information bottleneck problem. This approach ensures that the compact tokens capture high-level semantic features (object identities, spatial relationships) crucial for planning, while offloading the reconstruction of low-frequency visual details to a separate generative process.

4. **Emergent Modular Representations:** The observation that CompACT's latent tokens naturally learn to attend to modular, semantically coherent regions (e.g., distinct objects, robot end-effectors) without explicit supervision is a noteworthy finding. This "object-centric" or "modular" representation is highly beneficial for planning, as it inherently captures dynamic elements and state changes induced by actions, as evidenced by the superior Inverse Dynamics Model performance. This characteristic could pave the way for more interpretable and robust world models.

5. **Scalability of World Models:** The demonstration that a significantly larger world model (750M parameters) can be trained on CompACT's compact latent space and still operate much faster than smaller baseline models using conventional tokenizers highlights the potential for developing highly capable yet efficient world models. This suggests a new avenue for scaling model complexity without sacrificing real-time performance.

In conclusion, "Planning in 8 Tokens" presents a compelling case for a new design philosophy in world modeling. Its practical implications are vast, potentially accelerating the real-world adoption of advanced AI in robotics, autonomous systems, and other domains requiring intelligent decision-making under tight computational budgets. The research not only delivers a highly efficient technical solution but also offers a valuable conceptual insight into what information truly matters for effective planning in learned environment models.
