# Force Prompting: Video Generation Models Can Learn and Generalize Physics-based Control Signals

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Brown University and Google DeepMind developed "Force Prompting," a method to control video generation models using physics-based force signals like localized pokes or global wind. The approach shows that models, trained with a small synthetic dataset, can generalize to diverse real-world scenes, exhibiting an intuitive understanding of mass and yielding more realistic physical responses in generated videos compared to trajectory-based control.

## Problem

- Existing video generation models lack intuitive, physically meaningful control signals beyond text or sparse trajectories.
- Generating realistic physical interactions in videos often requires explicit 3D assets or complex physics simulators at inference time.
- Acquiring high-quality, paired force-video data from the real world is challenging.

## Method

- The method introduces "force prompts" (global wind forces and local point forces) as novel control signals for video generation models.
- It adapts a state-of-the-art video generative model (**CogVideoX**) with a **ControlNet** architecture to interpret these physics-based control signals.
- A strategy of generating limited, diverse synthetic training data from physics simulators (==Blender==, **PhysDreamer**) is employed to teach physical interactions efficiently.

## Results

- The models generalize physical control from minimal synthetic data (15k-23k videos) to diverse real-world scenes, outperforming text-only and trajectory-based controls in realism and adherence to physical instructions based on human evaluations.
- An intuitive understanding of mass is demonstrated, where perceived lighter objects move farther than heavier ones under the same force, and displacement scales linearly with force magnitude.
- The training process is computationally efficient, completing in approximately one day on four NVIDIA A100 GPUs.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Pretrained video generation models possess latent physical understanding that can be elicited and controlled with targeted conditioning.
> - Robust generalization for physics-based control can be achieved from limited, diverse synthetic data, effectively overcoming real-world data scarcity.
> - Specific text keywords during training and the inclusion of stationary distractor objects are critical for effective generalization and precise force localization.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This paper addresses a critical frontier in generative AI, particularly within the domain of video generation and world modeling: enabling physically meaningful and controllable interactions.

The broader research landscape can be segmented into several interconnected areas:

- **Advances in Video Generation Models:** The last few years have witnessed exponential growth in the quality and realism of video generation, largely driven by diffusion models. Models like Sora (Brooks et al., 2024), CogVideoX (Yang et al., 2024), and Wan 2.1 (Wang et al., 2025) have demonstrated remarkable capabilities in generating diverse, visually compelling videos, with some, notably Sora, explicitly advocating for video generators as simulators of the physical world. Gillman et al.'s work builds directly on these powerful "video priors," recognizing their inherent capacity to encode complex visual dynamics.
- **Controllable Video Generation:** As video generation advanced, so did the demand for finer control over the output. Existing efforts have explored various control signals:
 - **Camera Control:** Allowing users to dictate camera movements (He et al., 2024; Zheng et al., 2024).
 - **Motion Control:** This category is more diverse, including drag-based (Wu et al., 2024), optical flow-based (Niu et al., 2024), and trajectory-based methods (Yin et al., 2023; Chen et al., 2023; Zhang et al., 2024b). Many trajectory-based methods require dense, frame-by-frame specification, which is impractical for interactive simulation. Motion Prompting (Geng et al., 2024) introduced sparse trajectory control, but Gillman et al. highlight crucial distinctions: force control inherently captures global phenomena (e.g., wind) and mass-dependent dynamics, which are difficult or impossible to represent purely by trajectories. Force prompting offers a more intuitive and causal control mechanism compared to simply prescribing motion.
- **Interactive World Models:** The concept of "world models" (Ha and Schmidhuber, 2018) capable of simulating realistic environments has gained traction. While much research focuses on game environments (Valevski et al., 2024; Che et al., 2024), real-world applications are emerging, predominantly around camera control or text-based interactions (Bar et al., 2024; Agarwal et al., 2025). This paper significantly advances interactive world models by introducing a *physically grounded* interaction mechanism via forces, moving beyond abstract textual or visual cues.
- **Physical Simulators and Hybrid Approaches:** Traditional methods for simulating physical interactions involve explicit physics solvers and 3D assets (Chen et al., 2022; Xie et al., 2024). More recent work combines physics simulators with generative models (e.g., PhysGen (Liu et al., 2024b), PhysMotion (Tan et al., 2024)), aiming for the accuracy of simulation and the visual fidelity of generative models. However, these often require 3D geometry and are limited by the types of dynamics their physics engines can model. Gillman et al. diverge significantly by proposing a *purely neural* approach where the video generation model itself acts as the "simulator," requiring no physics engine or 3D assets at inference time. This represents a paradigm shift, exploring if large generative models can *learn* intuitive physics directly from data, rather than being explicitly programmed with physics rules.
- **Concurrent Works:** The paper acknowledges several concurrent works that also explore physics in generative models. Li et al. (2025a) focuses on object freefall; Li et al. (2025b) uses action-conditioned generation but requires a physics simulator at inference; and Wang* et al. (2025) requires learning a 3D point cloud trajectory model. Gillman et al.'s "force prompting" is distinguished by its direct force input, generalization across diverse scenarios, and independence from physics simulators during inference.

In essence, "Force Prompting" positions itself at the intersection of advanced video generation, intuitive control, and learned physical understanding, advocating for a future where generative models can not only create compelling visuals but also respond intelligently and realistically to physical stimuli.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind this research are deeply rooted in extending the capabilities of current video generation models beyond mere aesthetic output to encompass meaningful, interactive physical understanding.

The central motivating question driving this work is: **Do video generation models, which encode powerful visual and motion priors through internet-scale pretraining, possess an intuitive understanding of how objects respond to forces, and if so, how can this capability be elicited and controlled?**

Based on this, the key objectives are:

1. **Introduce Physical Forces as Control Signals:** The core objective is to propose and investigate "force prompts" as a novel control signal for video generation. This moves beyond existing text, image, or sparse trajectory inputs to allow users to interact with objects through localized point forces (e.g., poking a plant) and global force fields (e.g., wind blowing fabric).
2. **Demonstrate Realistic Response to Physical Control:** The authors aim to show that video generation models can learn to respond realistically and physically plausibly to these force prompts, leveraging their existing visual and motion priors without requiring explicit 3D assets or a physics simulator at inference time.
3. **Overcome Data Acquisition Challenges:** Recognizing the difficulty of obtaining high-quality paired force-video data in the real world (due to force signal measurement) and limitations in synthetic data (visual quality, domain diversity), a key objective is to demonstrate that even **limited, synthetically generated data from physics simulators (like Blender) can enable strong sim2real generalization.** This addresses a major practical bottleneck.
4. **Investigate Generalization Capabilities:** A significant goal is to explore the extent to which a model trained on a minimal set of synthetic examples (e.g., flags waving, balls rolling) can generalize to diverse objects, materials, geometries, settings, and affordances in real-world scenarios. This tests the hypothesis that pretrained video models already hold latent physics knowledge.
5. **Understand Sources of Generalization:** Through ablation studies, the researchers aim to understand which aspects of the training data and model configuration contribute most to the observed generalization, specifically looking at visual diversity and the role of text keywords.
6. **Assess Emergent Physical Understanding:** A specific objective is to determine if the model develops an intuitive understanding of concepts like mass, where identical forces applied to objects of different perceived masses result in different displacements.
7. **Develop an Intuitive World Model:** Ultimately, the broader motivation is to contribute towards more intuitive and expressive world models that can facilitate flexible content creation, enable interactive applications (like video games), and support intelligent agents in planning and decision-making within physically consistent environments.

By addressing these objectives, the paper seeks to bridge the gap between high-quality video generation and physically intelligent interactive systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology of "Force Prompting" hinges on adapting a state-of-the-art video generation model to interpret and respond to physics-based control signals, primarily through carefully curated synthetic training data and a specialized control architecture.

#### 3.1. Force Prompting Paradigms

The method proposes two distinct paradigms for force input:
- **Global Force Prompt:** Animates an entire scene with a directional wind force (e.g., wind strength and angle).
- **Local Force Prompt:** Enables precise interaction through localized point forces applied to specific objects or regions (e.g., force magnitude, angle, and pixel coordinates).

The goal is to generate a video `v` given a text prompt `τ`, an initial frame `ϕ`, and a physics control signal `π`.

#### 3.2. Synthetic Training Data Generation (Key Innovation)

Given the challenge of real-world force data, the authors leverage physics simulators to create perfectly annotated paired force-video data.

- **Global Force Dataset (Wind):**
 - **Simulator:** Blender.
 - **Content:** Videos of flags waving in wind.
 - **Diversity Parameters:** Randomized flag quantity (1-64), 100 flag colors, varied flag positions, camera placements, 50 HDRIs for lighting/background, wind direction (0-360 degrees), and wind speed (0-1, from none to strong).
 - **Size:** 15,000 videos.
 - **Dynamic:** Each video shows flags transitioning from stationary to wind-affected.

- **Local Force Dataset (Point):** This dataset has two components to teach different types of motion:
 - **Scenario 1: Ball Rolling (Linear Motion):**
 - **Simulator:** Blender.
 - **Content:** Videos of one ball rolling (pushed by an unseen point force) while other "distractor balls" remain stationary.
 - **Diversity Parameters:** Randomized ball quantity (2-4), varied textures (soccer vs. bowling balls), 108 ball colors, diverse ball positions, camera position, 42 ground textures, target ball selection, force angle (0-360 degrees), and force magnitude (0-1, gentle to strong).
 - **Mass Understanding:** Bowling balls were assigned four times the mass of soccer balls to implicitly teach mass-based dynamics.
 - **Size:** 12,000 videos.
 - **Scenario 2: Plant Poking (Oscillatory/Complex Motion):**
 - **Simulator:** PhysDreamer (Zhang et al., 2024a), a generative-simulator hybrid.
 - **Content:** Videos of a carnation swaying in response to an unseen poke.
 - **Diversity Parameters:** Randomized camera position, contact points, force angles, and magnitudes.
 - **Size:** 11,000 videos.
 - **Force Projection:** Forces from 3D simulator space are projected onto the 2D pixel plane using camera parameters.
 - **Text Prompts:** GPT-4o API is used to generate detailed text descriptions for each video, correlating with background and ground textures.

#### 3.3. Force Encoding Strategies

The raw force signals are converted into a tensor representation `˜π` that can be fed into the video generation model.

- **Global Force Encoding:**
 - A force `F` ∈ and an angle `θ` ∈ [0, 360) are encoded into a `f × c × h × w` tensor (`f=49` frames, `c=3` channels, `h=480, w=720`).
 - The channels represent: `(-1 + 2F)` (magnitude), `cos θ`, and `sin θ`. This provides a smooth, spatially uniform representation of the global force field over time.

- **Local Force Encoding:**
 - Pixel coordinates `(x, y)`, force magnitude `F` ∈, and angle `θ` ∈ [0, 360) are used.
 - The `˜π` tensor is a sequence of frames showing a **Gaussian blob**. This blob starts at `(x, y)` in the first frame and moves in direction `θ` at a constant velocity, for a total distance proportional to `F`. This indicates the *action* of the force, not the *trajectory* of the affected object, which is a crucial distinction from trajectory-based control.

#### 3.4. Architecture and Training

- **Base Model:** CogVideoX-5B-I2V (Yang et al., 2024), a text- and initial frame-conditioned video generative model.
- **Control Integration:** A **ControlNet** (Zhang et al., 2023) is added to integrate the physics control prompt `π`.
 - The ControlNet processes `π` through downscaling, encoding, and temporal compression.
 - It combines these features with the hidden states of the base model via a zero convolution.
 - **Partial Fine-tuning:** The ControlNet clones the first six transformer layers of CogVideoX and fine-tunes *only these layers*, keeping the much larger base model frozen. This is crucial for efficiency and leveraging the strong prior of the pretrained model.
- **Computational Resources:** Training is highly efficient, completed in approximately **one day** on four NVIDIA A100 GPUs.
- **Hyperparameters:** 5000 training steps, effective batch size of 8, bf16 mixed precision, AdamW optimizer (learning rate 1e-5), cosine-with-restarts schedule.

By combining limited, strategically diverse synthetic data with an efficient ControlNet adaptation of a powerful base model, "Force Prompting" aims to imbue video generators with a sophisticated, yet intuitive, understanding of physical interactions.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The core findings of this research highlight the remarkable ability of video generation models to learn and generalize physics-based control signals from limited synthetic data.

#### 4.1. Generalization Capabilities

- **Broad Generalization from Limited Data:** A key finding is that despite training on a minimal set of synthetic videos (15k for global forces on flags, 23k for local forces on balls/carnations), the models demonstrate surprisingly strong generalization.
 - **Local Force Model:** Trained on linear ball motion and oscillatory plant motion, it generalizes well to diverse "real-world" images involving linear movement (toy cars, hot air balloons), oscillatory movement (windmills, pendulums), and complex movements (ivy, apple trees). It even handles multiple simultaneous forces zero-shot, a capability not explicitly trained for.
 - **Global Force Model:** Trained only on flags waving (tethered motion), it generalizes effectively to aerodynamic motion (bubbles, falling leaves) and fluid dynamics (fog, smoke, snow), demonstrating control over a broader range of physical phenomena.
- **Robustness Across Diverse Visuals:** The models can simulate forces across diverse geometries, settings, and materials, suggesting they learn underlying physical principles rather than simply memorizing visual patterns from the synthetic training data.

#### 4.2. Quantitative and Qualitative Performance

- **Superior Force Adherence and Realism:** Human evaluation studies (2AFC) against text-only baselines (zero-shot and fine-tuned) and Motion Prompting (Geng et al., 2024) consistently show that Force Prompting achieves superior adherence to physical instructions and generates more realistic physics. While visual quality can sometimes be comparable, the control over physics is significantly better.
- **Outperforms Trajectory-Based Control:** Force Prompting significantly outperforms Motion Prompting, particularly because forces can describe global phenomena (like wind) and account for object properties (like mass) which trajectory models struggle with.
- **Computational Efficiency:** The models are trained efficiently in approximately one day on four A100 GPUs, making the approach practical and scalable.

#### 4.3. Emergent Physical Understanding: Mass Understanding

- The model demonstrates an intuitive understanding of mass.
 - **Quantitative Study (Balls):** Experiments show that soccer balls consistently travel farther than bowling balls when subjected to identical force magnitudes, and distance traveled increases linearly with force for both. This confirms a basic understanding of force-mass relationship.
 - **Qualitative Study (Various Objects):** Similar behavior is observed for other objects with implied mass differences (e.g., wooden vs. cast iron ornaments, empty vs. full laundry baskets, single vs. stacked books/cubes), where lighter objects move more. This understanding persists even in zero-shot multiple-object scenarios.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies: Insights into Generalization

- **Composition of Synthetic Dataset:**
 - **Visual Diversity:** Training with diverse flag quantities (1-64) prevents overfitting. A model trained on only one flag overfits, causing inappropriate cloth-like motion in non-cloth objects (e.g., a woman's arm waving like fabric). Similarly, varied backgrounds are crucial to prevent the model from failing to differentiate foreground/background elements and degrading visual quality.
 - **Distractor Objects:** For local forces, including "distractor balls" that remain stationary significantly improves force localization, preventing unintended movement of other objects when a specific one is poked.
- **Text Prompt Specificity:**
 - **Keywords During Training:** Explicitly using force-related keywords (e.g., "wind," "breeze," "blow") in text prompts *during training* is critical for generalization. Omitting them leads to significantly more failure cases (e.g., static fog, collapsing lanterns, unprompted steam).
 - **Keywords During Inference:** While less impactful than during training, using these keywords at inference time still helps produce more robust results.

#### 4.5. Emergent Phenomena (Additional Details)

- **Physical Affordances:** The models respect object-specific movement constraints, e.g., a toy train following its circular track or a windmill rotating on its axis, rather than moving in a straight line.
- **Object Atomicity:** The model treats objects holistically; poking different parts of a train results in the entire train moving, not just the poked pixels.
- **Cinematic Preservation:** The model maintains the stylistic properties of the initial image, such as depth-of-field effects, throughout the generated video.

In summary, the main findings demonstrate that video generation models, when appropriately conditioned and trained on thoughtfully designed synthetic data, can acquire a robust, generalizable, and intuitive understanding of physical forces, enabling unprecedented levels of control and interaction.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "Force Prompting" research by Gillman et al. represents a significant step forward in the field of generative AI, particularly at the intersection of video generation and physically intelligent systems. Its significance and potential impact span several key areas:

1. **Enabling Intuitive Human-AI Interaction:** The most immediate impact is the introduction of a more intuitive and expressive interface for interacting with generative video models. Moving beyond abstract text prompts or rigid motion trajectories, force prompts (like poking or wind) mimic natural human interactions with the physical world. This makes video generation more accessible and creatively flexible for users.

2. **Advancing World Models for Intuitive Physics:** The work makes a strong case for video generation models as implicit "world models" capable of simulating realistic physical environments. By demonstrating that these models can learn and generalize intuitive physics from limited synthetic data, the paper paves the way for more sophisticated AI agents that can plan, predict, and make decisions within simulated worlds that adhere to physical laws without requiring explicit physics engines. This moves closer to the long-standing AI goal of human-like intuitive physics.

3. **New Paradigm for Controllable Video Generation:** Force prompting offers a novel control mechanism that addresses limitations of prior methods. It inherently handles global phenomena (like fluid dynamics) and mass-dependent interactions, which are difficult to specify using only trajectories or optical flow. This expands the range of controllable dynamics achievable in generated videos.

4. **Efficiency in Learning Physical Priors:** The finding that robust generalization can be achieved with only a small synthetic dataset (15k-23k videos) and modest computational resources (one day on four A100 GPUs) is highly impactful. It suggests that large pretrained video models already possess strong latent physical understanding, which merely needs to be "elicited" through targeted, high-quality, synthetic conditioning data, rather than requiring massive real-world datasets or complex physics-based architectures.

5. **Applications in Creative Content Creation:** The ability to precisely control physical interactions opens up vast possibilities for creative industries. Artists, animators, and game developers could generate complex physical effects (e.g., cloth dynamics, object impacts) with simple force inputs, accelerating content creation and enabling more dynamic and interactive media. This could include generating interactive video games, dynamic movie scenes, or personalized visual effects.

6. **Implications for Embodied AI and Robotics:** For embodied AI agents and robotics, learning intuitive physics is crucial for understanding environments and planning actions. Force-conditioned video generation could serve as a powerful simulation tool for training agents to predict the consequences of their actions in a physically consistent manner, facilitating robust interaction with the real world.

7. **Guidance for Synthetic Data Design:** The ablation studies provide valuable insights into designing effective synthetic training data. The importance of visual diversity, distractor objects, and specific text keywords for generalization offers a blueprint for future research aiming to imbue models with various capabilities using synthetic sources.

**Potential Risks and Limitations (as acknowledged by the authors):**
The authors responsibly highlight that this work focuses on "intuitive physics"—motion that is *visually plausible* to humans—rather than *precise physical simulation*. This is a crucial distinction. The method is **not a substitute for high-fidelity, precise simulation** required in fields like materials science, architecture, mechanical engineering, or civil engineering, where exact numerical accuracy is paramount. The model's inherent physical understanding is still constrained by the underlying video prior's capabilities and may exhibit out-of-domain failures or hallucinations when pushed beyond its learned scope.

Despite these limitations, "Force Prompting" marks a significant advancement toward creating more interactive, intelligent, and physically aware generative AI models, opening new avenues for research and application across various domains.
