# Force Prompting: Video Generation Models Can Learn and Generalize Physics-based Control Signals

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Brown University and Google DeepMind developed "Force Prompting," a method to control video generation models using physics-based force signals like localized pokes or global wind. The approach shows that models, trained with a small synthetic dataset, can generalize to diverse real-world scenes, exhibiting an intuitive understanding of mass and yielding more realistic physical responses in generated videos compared to trajectory-based control.

## Problem

- Existing video generation models lack intuitive, physically meaningful control signals beyond text or sparse trajectories.
- Generating realistic physical interactions in videos often requires explicit 3D assets or complex physics simulators at inference time.
- Acquiring high-quality, paired force-video data from the real world is challenging.

## Method

- The method introduces "force prompts" (global wind forces and local point forces) as novel control signals for video generation models.
- It adapts a state-of-the-art video generative model (**CogVideoX**) with a **ControlNet** architecture to interpret these physics-based control signals.
- A strategy of generating limited, diverse synthetic training data from physics simulators (==Blender==, **PhysDreamer**) is employed to teach physical interactions efficiently.

## Results

- The models generalize physical control from minimal synthetic data (15k-23k videos) to diverse real-world scenes, outperforming text-only and trajectory-based controls in realism and adherence to physical instructions based on human evaluations.
- An intuitive understanding of mass is demonstrated, where perceived lighter objects move farther than heavier ones under the same force, and displacement scales linearly with force magnitude.
- The training process is computationally efficient, completing in approximately one day on four NVIDIA A100 GPUs.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Pretrained video generation models possess latent physical understanding that can be elicited and controlled with targeted conditioning.
> - Robust generalization for physics-based control can be achieved from limited, diverse synthetic data, effectively overcoming real-world data scarcity.
> - Specific text keywords during training and the inclusion of stationary distractor objects are critical for effective generalization and precise force localization.
