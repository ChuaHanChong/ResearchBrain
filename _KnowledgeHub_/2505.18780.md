# DreamPolicy: A Unified World-model Policy for Scalable Humanoid Locomotion

> [!summary] Summary
> Humanoid robots gain versatile locomotion capabilities across diverse and unseen terrains through a unified world-model policy. This approach leverages an autoregressive diffusion-based world model to guide behavior, leading to robust performance and effective zero-shot transfer to physical hardware.

## Problem

- Achieving versatile humanoid locomotion with a single, unified policy that generalizes robustly across diverse, unseen, and composite terrains.
- Limitations of policy distillation, which often averages diverse skills, preventing effective generalization and organic skill composition on novel terrains.
- The heavy reliance of learning-based locomotion methods on manually engineered reward functions and their tendency to overfit to specific training conditions.

## Method

- Collection of a diverse dataset of humanoid locomotion skills by training multiple specialized reinforcement learning policies on primitive terrains.
- Development of a terrain-aware autoregressive diffusion world model, trained on aggregated offline data, to synthesize adaptable multi-step future state trajectories.
- Formulation of unified policy learning as a ==Goal-Conditioned Reinforcement Learning== problem, where the diffusion-generated trajectories serve as dynamic guidance, coupled with a hybrid reward mechanism for kinematic tracking and motion manifold regularization.

## Results

- Demonstrated superior zero-shot generalization on four novel terrains, outperforming the strongest baseline by up to **27%** in success rate, and achieved substantial improvements (up to **37.98%** higher success rates) on complex combined terrains.
- Successful zero-shot sim-to-real transfer on a Unitree G1 humanoid robot, exhibiting robust locomotion across diverse real-world terrains like stairs, slopes, and uneven surfaces.
- Ablation studies confirmed the critical role of both the diffusion world model for generalizing to out-of-distribution states and Goal-Conditioned Reinforcement Learning for physically feasible and robust policy execution.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Diffusion models can effectively serve as terrain-aware world models, directly synthesizing multi-step future state trajectories that act as dynamic objectives for humanoid policy learning.
> - Aggregating data from specialized policies allows a diffusion world model to learn structured, terrain-conditioned motion priors, enabling dynamic skill composition and robust generalization.
> - Formulating policy learning with diffusion-guided future tracking and online motion manifold regularization substantially reduces the need for extensive, task-specific reward engineering.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of embodied intelligence has seen significant advancements, particularly in legged robotics, enabling dynamic tasks for quadrupedal robots and showcasing the potential of humanoid robots in human-centric environments. Despite these developments, robust performance of humanoid robots in complex and composite terrains, especially with a single, generalist policy, remains a substantial challenge.

Prevailing approaches to versatile locomotion often involve training multiple specialized policies for different terrains and then distilling these into a single student policy. However, this distillation process frequently compresses diverse behaviors into an average representation, limiting its capacity to organically compose skills and generalize effectively to novel composite terrains unseen during training. Many learning-based methods also tend to overfit to specific training conditions and rely on manual reward engineering, which is labor-intensive and difficult to tune for mixed environments.

Other related areas include the use of pre-trained models or human motion priors to guide policy learning, which can reduce exploration burdens but still face hurdles in handling diverse scenarios and external disturbances. Recent advancements in foundation models demonstrate the benefits of scaling data for generalization, although these primarily focus on manipulation or cross-embodiment alignment rather than unified humanoid locomotion across complex terrains.

Diffusion models have gained traction in robot learning for their generative capabilities, enabling multi-modal behavior modeling and temporal consistency. Existing applications include diffusion as an action policy, path planner, or reference motion generator. Explicit predictive world models in humanoid locomotion are less explored, with current methods often using models for latent dynamics reconstruction or policy regularization rather than as direct sources of execution-time motion guidance.

DreamPolicy addresses these gaps by proposing a data-driven framework that integrates offline data with an autoregressive diffusion-based world model. This approach aims to circumvent the limitations of distillation by directly synthesizing physically plausible future state trajectories as dynamic objectives for a conditioned policy. It positions the diffusion model not merely as a planner or action generator but as a terrain-aware world model that predicts future humanoid states, thereby unifying world model-based planning with policy learning and reducing the reliance on extensive reward engineering. This framework aims to provide a scalable solution for generalist humanoid control that can robustly transfer to unseen and composite terrains.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to achieve versatile humanoid locomotion using a single, unified policy that exhibits scalability and robust generalization across diverse, including previously unseen, terrains. The authors aim to overcome the limitations of existing approaches, particularly the "one task, one policy" bottleneck prevalent in humanoid control.

The motivation for this work stems from several key challenges identified in the current research landscape:

1. **Scalability of Unified Policies:** A critical challenge is enabling a single policy to master a wide range of locomotion tasks and adapt to various terrains. Traditional methods often rely on distilling multiple terrain-specific "teacher" policies into a unified "student" policy. However, this approach tends to capture only basic locomotion primitives and struggles to compose these skills organically, leading to poor generalization in complex, novel composite terrains.
2. **Limitations of Policy Distillation:** While policy distillation can aggregate specialized skills, it typically compresses diverse behaviors into a single, often averaged, representation. This limitation prevents it from effectively modeling the complex, multi-modal distributions of motion required for navigating varied and mixed terrains, causing failures in adaptation to distributions outside its curated training sets.
3. **Overfitting and Reward Engineering Burden:** Most learning-based approaches for humanoid locomotion tend to overfit to specific training conditions. Furthermore, they heavily rely on manually engineered, task-specific reward functions, which are difficult and time-consuming to design and fine-tune, especially for diverse or mixed environments. This process becomes increasingly unmanageable as environmental complexity grows.
4. **Lack of Adaptive Motion Priors:** There is a need for a data-driven framework capable of organically integrating terrain-conditioned motion priors. Such a framework would allow for continuous improvement through dataset expansion and leverage the inherent structure of motion data to generalize across complex terrain distributions without explicit hand-coding of behaviors.

To address these motivations, DreamPolicy sets forth specific objectives:

- To introduce a diffusion-based world model into humanoid locomotion that can directly synthesize multi-step future state trajectories. These synthesized trajectories are intended to serve as dynamic guidance for policy learning, thereby bypassing extensive manual reward engineering.
- To leverage aggregated rollouts from specialized policies to train this terrain-aware diffusion world model, enabling it to learn structured, terrain-conditioned motion priors. This capability is expected to facilitate dynamic skill composition and robust zero-shot generalization to unseen terrains.
- To demonstrate that the proposed framework benefits consistently from scalable offline data and can generalize beyond the terrains used to train its source policies, achieving superior performance on novel and combined terrains compared to existing baselines.

Ultimately, the goal is to establish a scalable, data-driven paradigm for generalist humanoid control by unifying world model-based planning and policy learning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

DreamPolicy is a unified framework designed to achieve versatile humanoid locomotion across diverse terrains. Its approach integrates offline data with a diffusion-based world model, operating in three distinct stages: data collection, world model training, and policy learning.

#### 3.1. Terrain-aware Humanoid Locomotion Skill Data Collection

The first stage involves generating a diverse dataset of humanoid locomotion skills.
- **Humanoid Locomotion Benchmarks:** A modular terrain benchmark was developed, comprising primitive terrains (e.g., Gap, Stair, Bridge) for expert policy training and challenging terrains (e.g., Balancing Beam, Slope-Bridge) for zero-shot generalization testing. These terrains can be flexibly combined to create varied scenarios. Performance is measured by success rate and completion rate.
- **Humanoid Primitive Skills Collection via Specialized Policy Learning:** Multiple specialized reinforcement learning (RL) policies (π = [π_1..., π_n]) are trained in the Isaaclab simulator. Each π_i is an expert policy for a single primitive terrain.
 - **Training Curriculum:** A two-phase progressive curriculum is used: first, a foundational locomotion policy is trained with strict regularization for basic stability; second, scenario-specific policies are refined using terrain-specific reward shaping. This decouples reward engineering for primitives from generalized locomotion.
 - **Observation Space (o_t):** Includes proprioceptive state (base angular velocity, projected gravity, joint positions and velocities), exteroceptive information (sparse scandots from egocentric height map), historical observations, user commands (desired linear velocities and yaw rate), and the previous action.
 - **Action Space (a_t):** Represents target positions for all robot joints.
 - **Data Aggregation:** The process yielded a dataset of 75 million transitions from five expert policies.

#### 3.2. Terrain-aware Autoregressive Diffusion World Model

This stage trains a diffusion model on the collected multi-expert data to predict adaptable humanoid future trajectories.
- **Autoregressive Denoising on State Space:** The humanoid state `s_t` is defined as a concatenation of proprioceptive states, user commands, and previous actions, making it invariant to global position and yaw for robust generalization.
 - The model uses a Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Model (DDPM) framework. Given a history of `K` frames of humanoid state `x_{t-1}` and current local terrain embedding `y_t`, the goal is to sample the next `H` humanoid states `x_t = [s_{t+1} ... s_{t+K}]` conditioned on `(x_{t-1}, y_t)`.
 - Noise is gradually added to the clean state trajectory `x_0t`. A transformer-based denoiser `G` is trained to predict the cleaned state trajectory `x_0t` from the noisy input, history, diffusion timestep, and terrain embedding.
- **Training:** The model is trained by minimizing the mean-squared error between predicted and true states. A scheduled sampling strategy is employed, where the probability of using ground-truth history (`p` from 1 to 0) is gradually annealed to expose the model to its own predictions, preventing divergence in long sequences. Terrain embedding is randomly masked for classifier-free guidance.
- **Inference:** After training, the denoiser `G` is used to auto-regressively generate motion imagery given history states and online terrain embeddings, using classifier-free guidance.

#### 3.3. Diffusion-guided Unified Policy Learning

This final stage formulates unified policy learning as a Goal-Conditioned Reinforcement Learning (GCRL) problem.
- **Goal Definition:** The goal `g_t` is the dynamic future trajectory `s_{t+1:t+H}` synthesized by the diffusion world model.
- **Hybrid Reward Mechanism:** A composite reward function (`r_t`) is designed, combining kinematic tracking with motion manifold regularization.
 - **Regularization Reward (r_regu):** Enforces fundamental physical constraints, penalizing excessive joint positions, velocities, accelerations, torques, and encouraging smooth base motion and contact stability.
 - **Tracking Reward (r_track):** Uses the first predicted step `s_{t+1}` from the diffusion model to define an immediate body-tracking reward. This includes tracking errors for position, orientation, linear velocity, and angular velocity, mapped through Gaussian-shaped scores.
 - **Style Reward (r_style):** An Adversarial Motion Priors (AMP)-style approach adapted for online diffusion generation. A discriminator `D` is jointly trained to differentiate between the policy's actual transitions `(s_t, s_{t+1})` and the "dreamed" transitions `(s_t, s_{t+1})` predicted by the diffusion model. This encourages the policy to produce trajectories aligned with the diffusion model's implicit knowledge of stable, adaptive locomotion.
- **Reduced Reward Engineering:** This framework significantly reduces the need for task-specific reward engineering by replacing handcrafted terrain-dependent terms with diffusion-guided future tracking and online motion-manifold regularization. The unified policy utilizes a compact set of non-shared reward terms.
- **Policy Architecture:** The policy network incorporates an RNN future encoder that processes the predicted future state sequence from the diffusion world model, conditioning the policy's actions on this dynamic guidance.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted assessed DreamPolicy's performance across various terrain scenarios, comparing it against several baselines, including specialized policies, distillation-based methods, and ablations of its core components.

#### 4.1. Performance on Single-Terrains

DreamPolicy demonstrated performance comparable to specialized expert policies on individual terrains (Flat, Gap, Stair, Bridge, Uneven). This indicates that the unified policy effectively learned fundamental locomotion skills without exhibiting significant degradation or "regression issues" often associated with distillation-based baselines, which typically average behaviors.

#### 4.2. Zero-shot Generalization on Unseen Terrains

On four novel terrains (Wave, Slope, Balancing Beam, Slope bridge), DreamPolicy exhibited superior zero-shot generalization capabilities. It outperformed the strongest baseline (Distillation) by up to 27% in success rate and completion rate. For example, on the 'Slope bridge', DreamPolicy achieved a 92.77% success rate compared to Distillation's 65.48%. This suggests the diffusion world model effectively learns transferable motion priors.

#### 4.3. Performance on Combined Terrains

When facing composite terrains that combine multiple challenges (e.g., Stair&Uneven, Bridge&Uneven), DreamPolicy significantly outperformed baselines. It achieved substantial improvements, such as 37.98% higher success rates on 'Stair&Uneven' and 22.45% on 'Bridge&Uneven' compared to the distillation method, while generally maintaining success rates above 91% on other combinations. This performance highlights its ability to dynamically compose and adapt skills in complex, multi-modal environments.

#### 4.4. Ablation Analysis

- **Without Diffusion World Model:** Removing the diffusion planner resulted in a sharp decline in performance, particularly on complex and unseen terrains. For instance, the 'w/o diffusion' variant achieved 0.0% success on 'Slope bridge' and 'Balancing Beam' where DreamPolicy achieved over 90%. This indicates that without the planner's guidance, the policy struggled to generalize to out-of-distribution (OOD) states and could not adapt to unseen complex environments.
- **Without Goal-Conditioned Reinforcement Learning (GCRL):** When the policy relied solely on the diffusion model's actions without the GCRL optimization, performance was strong on simple terrains but significantly deteriorated on complex ones (e.g., 0.0% success on 'Slope bridge' and 'Balancing Beam'). This suggests that while the diffusion model can generate optimal policies, these may not always be physically feasible or robust enough to environmental disturbances without the physics-constrained optimization provided by GCRL.

#### 4.5. Scaling Analysis

The study investigated the scalability of DreamPolicy with respect to both dataset size and diversity:
- **Dataset Size:** DreamPolicy demonstrated consistent performance gains as the size of the offline dataset increased from 5 million to 75 million transitions.
- **Dataset Diversity:** Performance also improved as the diversity of terrain types in the training dataset increased (from 1 to 5 types). The framework exhibited higher data efficiency compared to standard distillation methods.

#### 4.6. Real-world Experiments (Sim-to-Real Transfer)

DreamPolicy was successfully deployed on a Unitree G1 humanoid robot via zero-shot transfer in various real-world scenarios (stairs, slopes, gaps, uneven surfaces, and combinations).
- Despite simulation-to-real-world dynamics discrepancies, the robot exhibited robust locomotion.
- The diffusion world model's ability to synthesize terrain-adaptive reference trajectories was crucial, allowing the robot to proactively adjust torso posture and foot placement in anticipation of terrain changes, enabling continuous traversal of discrete gaps and stairs without pauses.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

DreamPolicy represents a notable advancement in humanoid locomotion control, offering a scalable and generalizable framework with several significant implications:

1. **Addressing the "One Task, One Policy" Bottleneck:** The research directly confronts the prevailing challenge of designing a single policy capable of versatile locomotion across diverse and complex terrains. By achieving robust performance on both known and unseen composite terrains with a unified policy, DreamPolicy moves beyond the paradigm of needing specialized policies for each task or environment.

2. **Novel Integration of Diffusion Models and World Models:** This work introduces a novel application of diffusion models as a terrain-aware world model for humanoid locomotion. Unlike prior uses of diffusion for action generation or path planning, DreamPolicy leverages it to directly synthesize multi-step, physically plausible future state trajectories. This provides dynamic, adaptive guidance for the policy, effectively capturing rich, multi-modal motion priors.

3. **Reduced Reward Engineering Burden:** A substantial impact of DreamPolicy is its ability to significantly reduce the dependency on extensive, manual reward engineering. By using diffusion-generated trajectories as explicit, terrain-adaptive reference objectives and integrating an online AMP-style regularization, the framework replaces numerous handcrafted, terrain-specific reward terms with a more compact and generalizable reward structure. This streamlines the policy development process and enhances its scalability to new environments.

4. **Enhanced Generalization and Zero-Shot Transfer:** DreamPolicy demonstrates superior generalization capabilities, particularly its robust zero-shot transfer to novel and composite terrains. This is attributed to the diffusion world model's capacity to learn structured, terrain-conditioned motion priors and facilitate dynamic skill composition, enabling the humanoid to adapt to unseen environmental configurations without additional training.

5. **Scalable and Data-Driven Paradigm:** The framework establishes a scalable, data-driven paradigm for humanoid control. Its performance consistently improves with increasing offline dataset size and diversity, indicating that future advancements can be driven by simply expanding the training data rather than redesigning policies for every new scenario. This aligns with trends in large-scale model development.

6. **Potential for Real-World Applications:** The successful zero-shot sim-to-real transfer of DreamPolicy to a physical Unitree G1 robot across diverse real-world terrains underscores its practical potential. This capability is critical for deploying humanoid robots in unstructured, human-centric environments, where they must navigate varied and unpredictable ground conditions.

While the computational overhead of the iterative denoising process is acknowledged as a limitation, the established framework lays groundwork for future research into lightweight sampling techniques. Overall, DreamPolicy represents a significant step towards more autonomous, adaptable, and generalist humanoid robots capable of operating effectively in complex and dynamic real-world settings.
