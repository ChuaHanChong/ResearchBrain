# HERMES: Human-to-Robot Embodied Learning from Multi-Source Motion Data for Mobile Dexterous Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> HERMES presents a comprehensive framework for enabling mobile robots with dexterous hands to perform complex, bimanual manipulation tasks in diverse real-world environments. It integrates learning from heterogeneous human motion data, robust sim2real transfer via vision-based policies, and precise navigation capabilities to achieve high success rates for a range of challenging tasks.

## Problem

- Translating diverse human hand motions from various sources (teleoperation, motion capture, raw video) into feasible robot behaviors, especially for high-dimensional dexterous hands.
- Mitigating the sim2real gap for policies to adapt to varied real-world conditions without requiring explicit state information or extensive manual tuning.
- Enabling robots to autonomously navigate and precisely align visual goals for dexterous manipulation in large, unstructured environments.

## Method

- **HERMES** employs a unified ==reinforcement learning== approach capable of transforming heterogeneous human motion data into generalizable robotic behaviors using one-shot augmentation and a task-agnostic reward design.
- An end-to-end, depth image-based sim2real transfer method for vision-based policies, incorporating tailored ==depth image augmentation== and a ==hybrid sim2real control strategy==.
- Augmentation of a navigation foundation model (ViNT) with a ==closed-loop Perspective-n-Point (PnP) localization== mechanism to ensure precise alignment of visual goals for mobile manipulation.

## Results

- Achieved an average success rate of **67.8%** across six complex real-world bimanual dexterous manipulation tasks, representing a **+54.5%** improvement over baselines.
- Demonstrated superior sample efficiency, learning policies from one-shot human demonstrations within **3 million** training steps and outperforming prior methods.
- Closed-loop PnP localization reduced translational error from **18cm** to **2.4cm** in indoor scenes, enabling high success rates (e.g., **70%** for 'Bottle Handover') for mobile manipulation tasks, even in textureless environments.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Leveraging diverse human motion data (teleop, mocap, video) combined with RL and residual action learning effectively addresses the embodiment gap for complex dexterous hands.
> - Robust sim2real transfer for dexterous manipulation is achievable through depth-image-based visual policies with specific augmentation techniques and a hybrid control approach.
> - Integrating a general navigation model with a precise closed-loop PnP localization system successfully bridges the accuracy gap between long-horizon navigation and high-precision dexterous manipulation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation is rapidly advancing, with a long-standing goal of achieving human-level dexterity and autonomy in complex, unstructured environments. HERMES positions itself at the intersection of several critical research areas: learning from human demonstrations, dexterous manipulation, sim2real transfer, and mobile manipulation.

Historically, robots have struggled with the versatility and adaptability of human hands, especially in handling diverse objects and executing intricate tasks. Early attempts to learn from human data often focused on simpler gripper-based robots or relied on extensive, costly teleoperation data. The "embodiment gap" – the kinematic and dynamic differences between human and robot bodies – has remained a significant hurdle, particularly for multi-fingered dexterous hands with their high-dimensional action spaces. HERMES directly addresses this by proposing a unified framework to convert diverse human motion data (teleoperation, motion capture, raw video) into physically plausible robot behaviors, going beyond simpler grippers to tackle dexterous hands.

Furthermore, the transition of policies from simulation to the real world (sim2real transfer) is a perpetual challenge in robotics. Many existing methods for dexterous manipulation rely on explicit state information or laborious domain randomization, limiting their adaptability to varied real-world conditions. HERMES aims for an end-to-end, vision-based sim2real approach using depth images, emphasizing robustness and generalization to unseen scenarios without extensive manual tuning.

Finally, integrating manipulation with mobile navigation to enable robots to operate in large, unconstrained environments ("mobile manipulation") is another frontier. While navigation foundation models have emerged, they often lack the precision required for subsequent manipulation tasks. HERMES bridges this gap by augmenting a navigation foundation model (ViNT) with a closed-loop Perspective-n-Point (PnP) localization mechanism, ensuring precise alignment essential for high-precision dexterous tasks in diverse, in-the-wild settings. This holistic approach distinguishes HERMES from works that focus solely on one aspect of mobile dexterous manipulation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind HERMES is to overcome the inherent challenges in endowing robots with human-level dexterity, particularly for mobile bimanual systems equipped with multi-fingered dexterous hands. The authors highlight several specific challenges that inform their objectives:
- **Translating diverse human motion:** Existing methods struggle to convert heterogeneous human hand motions from various sources into feasible robot behaviors, especially for complex dexterous hands with high-dimensional action spaces.
- **Mitigating the sim2real gap:** Policies often fail to adapt to diverse real-world environmental conditions due to discrepancies between simulation and reality, and current visual learning approaches often require explicit state extraction, limiting generalization.
- **Enabling autonomous operation in varied environments:** Robots need to precisely align visual goals and bridge navigation with dexterous manipulation for robust operation in unstructured settings.

To address these challenges, HERMES sets out three key objectives:
1. **Develop a unified reinforcement learning (RL) approach** capable of seamlessly transforming heterogeneous human hand motions from multiple sources (teleoperated simulation data, motion capture data, raw human videos) into physically plausible and generalizable robotic behaviors. This objective aims to significantly reduce the cost and effort of data collection and reward engineering.
2. **Devise an end-to-end, depth image-based sim2real transfer method** for improved generalization to real-world scenarios. This involves distilling state-based policies into robust vision-based policies capable of handling perceptual and dynamic discrepancies between simulation and reality.
3. **Augment a navigation foundation model with a closed-loop Perspective-n-Point (PnP) localization mechanism** to ensure precise alignment of visual goals. This objective is crucial for bridging autonomous navigation and dexterous manipulation, enabling autonomous operation in varied and unstructured environments.

Ultimately, HERMES aims to empower robots to execute a wide range of complex, long-horizon mobile bimanual dexterous manipulation tasks across diverse real-world environments by integrating these three capabilities.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

HERMES proposes a comprehensive four-stage pipeline for achieving mobile bimanual dexterous manipulation through sim2real transfer:

1. **Human Motion Collection:**
 - **Diverse Sources:** The framework supports three distinct human motion sources:
 - **Teleoperation in simulation:** Using Apple Vision Pro to extract hand and arm movements at 75 Hz.
 - **Motion Capture (Mocap) data:** Sourced from public datasets like OakInk2, leveraging RL to address the embodiment gap in retargeting.
 - **Extracted arm and hand poses from videos:** Utilizing WiLoR for 2D/3D hand keypoint detection and PnP for wrist translation and palm orientation. FoundationPose and ARCode are used to estimate object poses and reconstruct meshes from raw video.
 - **One-shot Augmentation:** A single human reference trajectory is augmented through randomized positions and orientations of objects and hands, enabling spatial generalization without extensive demonstrations.
 - **Initial Retargeting:** DexPilot is employed for initial mapping of human hand poses to robot hand configurations, followed by RL for refinement.

2. **RL Training & DAgger Training:**
 - **Task Formulation:** Tasks are formulated as goal-conditioned Markov Decision Processes (MDPs), where the robot learns to convert desired kinematic motion trajectories into executable behaviors.
 - **Generalizable Reward Design:** To simplify reward engineering across diverse tasks, HERMES uses a unified reward structure:
 - **Object-centric distance chain:** Tracks temporal variations of vectors between object center and robot fingertips/palms, activated only with sufficient contact points.
 - **Object trajectory tracking:** Aligns the robot's object manipulation with the reference object trajectory (position and orientation).
 - **Power penalty:** Encourages smoother policy execution by penalizing high joint forces and velocities.
 - **Residual Action Learning:** Actions are decomposed into coarse (from human trajectory) and fine (network-predicted, $\Delta a_f$) components for arms. For hands, only network-predicted actions ($a_{hand}$) are used due to retargeting inaccuracies.
 - **RL Algorithms:** DrM (off-policy) and PPO (on-policy, with MJX GPU parallel simulation) are used for training in MuJoCo/MJX. Early termination and temporary collision disabling are used to facilitate learning.
 - **DAgger Distillation:** A state-based expert policy (trained with privileged information) is distilled into a vision-based student policy for sim2real transfer.
 - **Depth Image as Visual Input:** A manipulation-tailored egocentric depth-image augmentation method is introduced, including clipping, filling missing values, Gaussian noise/blur, random pixel zeroing, and a mixup strategy with the NYU Depth Dataset to semantically align simulated and real depth images.
 - **Architecture:** ResNet-18 (first two layers) encoder with GroupNorm (instead of BatchNorm) for distributional consistency.
 - **Rollout Scheduler:** Gradually shifts from expert to student policy for data collection.
 - **Loss:** Joint L1 and L2 action loss, with noise injection into proprioception states to prevent overfitting.

3. **Navigation & Localization:**
 - **ViNT Navigation Foundation Model:** An image-goal navigation model (ViNT) is integrated, relying on RGB inputs for long-horizon, in-the-wild navigation. It builds topological maps and computes waypoints.
 - **Closed-loop PnP Localization:** To overcome ViNT's limited precision, a local refinement step is introduced. Efficient LoFTR matches features between current and goal images. These features are lifted to 3D using depth maps, and RANSAC PnP + refine PnP algorithms compute the relative rotation and translation. A PID controller then adjusts the robot's pose (sequentially for x, y, yaw) until errors are below thresholds.

4. **Sim2real Manipulation (Deployment):**
 - **Hybrid Sim2real Control:** Real-world visual observations are used to infer actions. These actions are applied in the simulation environment to compute updated joint positions, which are then transferred to the real robot. This approach leverages shared Inverse Kinematics (IK) and dynamic parameters to mitigate the sim2real gap.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

HERMES's experimental evaluation demonstrates its effectiveness across simulation and real-world settings:

- **Sample Efficiency:** HERMES consistently exhibits superior sample efficiency compared to ObjDex, especially in complex multi-object interaction tasks, successfully learning policies from one-shot human motion demonstrations within 3 million training steps. It outperforms ObjDex across all evaluated tasks.
- **Comparison with Non-learning Approaches:**
 - **Kinematic Retargeting:** Direct kinematic retargeting from mocap or video data fails to achieve any task success (0%), highlighting the critical role of reinforcement learning in modeling object interactions and physically plausible behaviors. HERMES achieves 78.1% to 88.9% success rates for these tasks.
 - **Replay Edited Trajectories (Teleoperation):** For teleoperated data, HERMES significantly outperforms direct replay of edited trajectories, achieving higher success rates (e.g., 91.9% vs. 52.2% for "Bottle Handover"), underscoring RL's ability to learn adaptive residual actions.
- **Training Wall-time:** Leveraging MJX GPU parallel simulation and the PPO algorithm, HERMES achieves reduced wall-clock training time while maintaining high sample efficiency and asymptotic performance.
- **Real-world Manipulation Evaluation:**
 - HERMES achieves successful zero-shot transfer for most bimanual dexterous manipulation tasks, with only two tasks requiring minor fine-tuning with 5 real-world trajectories.
 - It demonstrates an average success rate of 67.8% across six complex real-world tasks, a substantial improvement of +54.5% compared to a baseline using raw depth inputs (13.3% average success). This validates the efficacy of the vision-based sim2real transfer and depth image augmentation.
- **Effectiveness of Closed-loop PnP Localization:**
 - Integration of closed-loop PnP significantly reduces localization errors compared to ViNT alone. For instance, in indoor scene 1, translational error drops from 18cm (ViNT) to 2.4cm (HERMES), and orientation error from 2.57° to 1.79°.
 - Qualitative results show HERMES achieving precise semantic and point cloud alignment with target positions, crucial for downstream manipulation.
- **Localization in Textureless Scenarios:** HERMES maintains precise localization (1.26cm translational error, 2.06° orientation error) even in textureless environments where traditional RGBD-based SLAM methods like RTAB-MAP fail.
- **Mobile Manipulation Evaluation:** When combining all components, HERMES achieves high success rates for complex mobile bimanual dexterous manipulation tasks in the real world (e.g., 70% for "Bottle Handover"). In contrast, the baseline using only ViNT for navigation (without PnP) fails due to localization inaccuracies, underscoring the vital role of closed-loop PnP in bridging the navigation-manipulation gap (+54% improvement with PnP).
- **Object Generalization (Appendix):** The policy demonstrates zero-shot generalization to bottles of varying shapes in real-world settings by randomizing object geometry during training.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

HERMES represents a significant advancement in the field of mobile bimanual dexterous manipulation, addressing several core challenges that have limited the real-world deployment of advanced robotic systems. Its key contributions and potential impacts include:

- **Democratizing Robot Learning from Human Data:** By effectively leveraging diverse and readily available human motion data (teleoperation, mocap, raw videos) and transforming them into feasible robot behaviors, HERMES lowers the barrier to entry for teaching complex skills to robots. This reduces the reliance on costly and laborious expert teleoperation, making advanced robot learning more accessible.
- **Robust Sim2real Transfer for Dexterous Manipulation:** The end-to-end, vision-based sim2real transfer method, particularly with its novel depth image augmentation and hybrid control strategy, sets a new benchmark for bridging the reality gap in dexterous manipulation. This enables policies trained in simulation to generalize effectively to unstructured real-world environments with high success rates, which is crucial for practical applications.
- **Seamless Integration of Navigation and Manipulation:** The innovative closed-loop PnP localization mechanism effectively bridges the precision gap between general navigation models and the stringent requirements of dexterous manipulation. This allows robots to autonomously navigate over long distances in varied environments (both indoor and outdoor) and then execute precise manipulation tasks, moving beyond static setups.
- **Foundation for General-Purpose Mobile Manipulators:** By combining these capabilities, HERMES provides a robust framework for developing general-purpose mobile manipulators that can operate autonomously and perform a wide array of human-centric tasks in dynamic environments. This has profound implications for applications in logistics, healthcare, personal assistance, and manufacturing.
- **Advancement in Learning Algorithms:** The design of generalizable reward functions and residual action learning strategies within a reinforcement learning framework contributes to the broader understanding of how to train complex, high-degree-of-freedom robotic systems more efficiently and robustly.

While the authors acknowledge limitations such as the focus on quasi-static tasks, manual tuning of physics parameters, and calibration mismatches, HERMES provides a solid foundation. Future work on more dynamic tasks and robust hardware systems will further enhance its practical utility, paving the way for ubiquitous, highly capable mobile dexterous robots.
