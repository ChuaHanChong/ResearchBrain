# Self-Improving Language Models for Evolutionary Program Synthesis: A Case Study on ARC-AGI

> [!summary] Summary
> SOAR (Self-improving Operators for Automated program Refinements) is a framework that enables large language models (LLMs) to learn to improve their program synthesis and refinement capabilities from their own search experience. Applied to the ARC-AGI benchmark, SOAR achieves 52.00% accuracy on the public test set, establishing a new state-of-the-art for open-source LLM-based inductive methods and demonstrating continuous performance gains beyond fixed model capacities.

## Problem

- Large Language Models (LLMs) often struggle with complex program synthesis tasks, especially those requiring abstract reasoning, failing to provide correct solutions in a single attempt.
- Integrating LLMs into traditional evolutionary search methods leads to performance plateaus, as the LLM's generative and refinement capabilities remain static after initial training.
- Existing LLM-based program synthesis often relies on extensive human-curated datasets for finetuning or hand-crafted domain-specific languages (DSLs), limiting autonomy and scalability.

## Method

- ==SOAR== integrates LLMs into an ==iterative self-improving evolutionary loop== that alternates between a ==Sample&Refine phase== for program generation and a ==Learning phase== for model updates.
- The Sample&Refine phase uses an LLM to generate initial program candidates and iteratively refine them based on execution feedback within a multi-armed bandit framework (REX).
- The Learning phase fine-tunes the LLM using data generated during its own search attempts, leveraging =="hindsight relabeling"== to create synthetic training examples from both successful and unsuccessful programs.

## Results

- SOAR dramatically improves LLM performance on ARC-AGI; for instance, the Qwen-2.5-Coder-7B model's accuracy increases from **14.25%** (search-only) to **36.25%** (SOAR), a **2.5**-fold gain.
- The framework achieves a peak accuracy of **52.00%** on the public ARC-test set by ensembling solutions from various SOAR-trained open-source LLMs, setting a new state-of-the-art for open-source inductive methods.
- SOAR successfully breaks through performance ceilings observed when merely increasing LLM size or search budget with fixed models, demonstrating sustained improvements through iterative learning and test-time adaptation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Self-improvement allows LLMs to overcome performance plateaus tied to fixed model size or search budget, continuously elevating performance ceilings through adaptive learning.
> - Hindsight relabeling is an effective technique for generating diverse and relevant training data from self-generated search traces, enabling autonomous learning without relying on human-curated datasets.
> - Jointly finetuning LLMs for both program sampling and refinement capabilities (rather than separately) leads to better overall performance, indicating a synergistic relationship between these tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Program synthesis, the automatic discovery of programs from high-level specifications, represents a transformative area in human-computer interaction. It aims to enable users to express goals through constraints or examples, abstracting away implementation details. Historically, program synthesis has relied on iterative search methods, such as genetic programming or sequential Bayesian inference. These traditional approaches generate candidate programs and refine them through operations like mutation and crossover. However, their efficacy is significantly dependent on the sophistication of the underlying program generators and mutation operators. Without intelligent operators, these algorithms may exhaust substantial computational resources in a blind exploration of the solution space, leading to limited effectiveness.

The emergence of large language models (LLMs) has introduced a new paradigm in program synthesis. LLMs have demonstrated capabilities as powerful program generators, capable of solving numerous tasks in a single attempt. For more complex problems, they can serve as intelligent operators within evolutionary search frameworks, proposing targeted modifications to existing solutions. Despite these advancements, a fundamental limitation persists: the generative and refinement capabilities of the LLMs used in these search processes remain fixed. Consequently, merely increasing the number of sampled candidates or refinement attempts often yields diminishing returns, imposing a performance ceiling.

This research paper introduces SOAR (Self-improving Operators for Automated program Refinements) as a method designed to address this limitation. SOAR integrates LLMs into a self-improving evolutionary loop, allowing the model to learn and enhance its program sampling and refinement abilities from its own synthesis attempts. This positions SOAR as an advancement beyond previous LLM-based program synthesis methods that treat the LLM as a static component.

Related work includes efforts to teach LLMs to reason by training on self-generated or classical search-produced reasoning traces. SOAR builds upon this by internalizing search traces, including both successes and failures, through hindsight learning, and by explicitly learning to refine solutions for subsequent test-time evolutionary search.

The Abstract Reasoning Corpus (ARC) is a benchmark specifically designed to challenge AI systems' reasoning capabilities, requiring inference of implicit grid transformations from minimal examples. Previous LLM applications to ARC have involved both transductive approaches (direct output prediction) and inductive approaches (program synthesis). Early inductive methods often relied on human-engineered domain-specific languages (DSLs) and exhaustive search. More recent inductive approaches have utilized closed-source LLMs for Python synthesis, involving sampling and refining candidate programs.

SOAR extends the self-improvement paradigm, notably from CodeIt, by several key differentiators:
1. Synthesizing programs in general-purpose Python, rather than a hand-crafted DSL.
2. Incorporating an iterative refinement step that leverages execution feedback to repair candidate programs within the search loop.
3. Scaling the approach to larger open-weight LLMs.
4. Combining test-time training with test-time search for continuous adaptation on target tasks.

Unlike many previous methods that rely on human-engineered DSLs or human-generated solutions for fine-tuning, SOAR operates by learning exclusively from its own synthesis attempts, encompassing both successful and unsuccessful trials. This places SOAR in the landscape of self-learning AI systems that aim to bootstrap their own improvement.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a program synthesis system that transcends the inherent limitations of fixed-capability generative models by enabling continuous self-improvement. The authors propose SOAR, a framework designed to learn program synthesis by iteratively enhancing an underlying language model's abilities to sample and refine programs.

The motivation for this work stems from several challenges in the current landscape of program synthesis:
1. **Complexity of Program Synthesis Tasks:** Many program synthesis tasks, particularly those requiring abstract reasoning like the ARC-AGI benchmark, remain highly challenging. State-of-the-art language models often fail to solve these problems in a single attempt, indicating a gap between their generative capacity and the complexity of desired transformations.
2. **Limitations of Search-Based Methods:** While search-based evolutionary methods offer an effective strategy to explore solution spaces and improve performance, their efficacy is ultimately constrained by the fixed capabilities of the generative model providing candidate solutions and refinements. As a result, performance gains from simply increasing computational budget (e.g., more samples or refinements) tend to diminish, leading to a performance plateau.
3. **Dependence on Human Engineering and Data:** Many successful program synthesis approaches, especially on benchmarks like ARC, have historically relied on human-engineered domain-specific languages (DSLs) or extensive human-generated solution datasets for training and scaffolding the search process. This dependence limits scalability, generalizability, and the autonomy of such systems.
4. **Need for Autonomous Learning:** The development of AI systems capable of learning and improving from their own experience, rather than solely from external human input, is a significant frontier in artificial intelligence. Applying this paradigm to program synthesis could lead to more robust and adaptable systems.

Specifically, the paper outlines four key objectives:
1. **Develop a Self-Improving Framework:** To create a framework that iteratively enhances its evolutionary search capabilities by learning from its own search experience, without requiring human-engineered data or external datasets.
2. **Implement Test-Time Training:** To introduce a mechanism for continuous improvement on target problems during test time, where ground-truth solutions are not accessible, enabling adaptation to new tasks.
3. **Empirically Demonstrate Performance Breakthroughs:** To provide empirical evidence that iterative model improvement can effectively overcome the performance plateaus typically associated with scaling search methods or model sizes with fixed capabilities.
4. **Achieve Competitive Performance:** To achieve state-of-the-art results for program synthesis on the ARC-AGI public test set using open-source LLMs, demonstrating the efficacy of the self-improvement approach without reliance on hand-crafted data or proprietary models.

The overarching motivation is to enable program synthesis systems to bootstrap their own improvement, allowing them to continually refine their understanding of program structure and transformation patterns through experience, thereby tackling increasingly complex reasoning tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

SOAR (Self-improving Operators for Automated program Refinements) is a framework designed to enable program synthesis systems to learn and improve their capabilities through an iterative self-improvement loop. This method alternates between two principal phases: evolutionary search and a learning phase, thereby fostering a virtuous cycle of improvement.

#### 3.1. Problem Definition: The ARC Benchmark

The Abstract Reasoning Corpus (ARC) benchmark is framed as a programming by example (PBE) task. Each ARC task provides:
- 2–10 training examples: `{(x_train, y_train)}`, where `x_train` and `y_train` are colored grid pairs.
- A set of test inputs: `{x_test}`.

The objective is to synthesize a Python function `f` such that `f(x_train) = y_train` for all training examples, and `f(x_test)` correctly generates the hidden `y_test`. Grids are 2D arrays (1-30 dimensions) of integers (0-9 representing colors). The challenge is to infer the underlying transformation logic from a few examples, which often involve abstract concepts.

#### 3.2. Program Synthesis as Evolutionary Search with LLM-based Sampling and Refinement

SOAR addresses the complexity of ARC tasks by combining the generative power of LLMs with an evolutionary search process. This `Sample&Refine` search consists of two main steps:

- **Program Sampling:** An initial pool of 3,000 candidate Python programs `f` is generated by an LLM, parameterized by `θ`, conditioned on the task's test input and training examples: `f ~ P_θ(· | x_test, x_train, y_train)`. Each program is executed using a Python interpreter to produce output grids.
- **Program Refinement:** When a candidate program `f` yields incorrect outputs (`y_synth = f(x_train) ≠ y_train`), this execution feedback is leveraged to guide the LLM in refining the solution into `f+`: `f+ ~ P_θ(· | f, x_test, x_train, y_train, y_synth)`. Both successful and failed transformations are explicitly labeled in the refinement prompt. This step aims to iteratively repair programs.

**Sample&Refine Search Algorithm:**
The search procedure allocates a total budget of 6,000 attempts. The first 3,000 attempts are dedicated to initial program sampling. The remaining 3,000 attempts are used for refinement. Refinement is conceptualized as a generative multi-armed bandit problem, where each refinement creates a new "arm" that can be further refined. SOAR employs REX (Refinement through EM-based sampling), which combines Thompson sampling based on training example accuracy with an exploration bonus, to efficiently balance exploration of new program variations and exploitation of promising paths. Modifications to REX include:
- Accelerated Refinement: Sampling four completions per prompt instead of one.
- Parallelized REX: Running four independent REX instances concurrently.
- Prioritized Feedback: Removing code that generates execution errors to focus on actionable feedback.

**Ensembling with Weighted Majority Voting:**
From the 6,000 candidate programs (3,000 sampled, 3,000 refined), each is evaluated for its accuracy on ARC training examples and its output on test inputs. Programs producing identical test output grids are grouped. Each unique test output grid is assigned a score, which is the sum of the example accuracies of all programs that generated it. This weighted voting mechanism prioritizes outputs from more accurate programs and mitigates individual program errors. The process ultimately returns two candidate solutions as per the ARC benchmark rules.

#### 3.3. Learning to Search via Self-Improved Sampling and Refinement

The core of SOAR's self-improvement lies in fine-tuning the underlying LLM using data generated during the `Sample&Refine` phase.

- **Finetuning Sampling Capabilities:**
 - **Challenge:** The scarcity of perfectly correct sampled programs limits direct training data.
 - **Solution: Hindsight Relabeling:** For any program `f0` sampled during search (even if incorrect for its original task), a new synthetic task is created where `f0` is a correct solution. This is done by executing `f0` on all inputs to generate `y_synth`, forming a new `(task, solution)` pair `{(x_train, y_synth, x_test), f0}`. This strategy enables the use of all sampled programs for training.
 - **Data Curation:** From the generated `2.4M` synthetic datapoints for ARC-train (6k programs * 400 tasks), a subset of `≤ 50` examples per task is used. This subset is created by ranking solutions by their accuracy on input-output examples and test pairs, then sampling 25 top-performing solutions (greedy) and 25 bottom-performing solutions (diverse) to ensure diversity.
 - **Loss Function:** The model is fine-tuned to minimize `L = E_D'_gen_correct [- log P_θ(f | x_train, y_train, x_test)]`.

- **Finetuning Refinement Capabilities:**
 - **Data Collection:** A dataset `D_refine_correct` is formed from instances where an initially incorrect program `f` was successfully refined into a correct program `f+` during the `Sample&Refine` phase.
 - **Data Curation:** Similar to sampling, `≤ 50` examples per task are selected, balancing the sampling across different accuracy bins (0%, 1-34%, 34-98%, 100% with incorrect test outputs) of the parent program to ensure diversity.
 - **Loss Function:** The model is fine-tuned to minimize `L = E_D_refine_correct [- log P_θ(f+ | f, x_test, x_train, y_train, y_synth)]`.
- **Joint Finetuning:** The paper investigates whether a single model can learn both sampling and refinement effectively, finding that joint finetuning yields better overall performance, indicating positive synergy.

#### 3.4. Closing the Loop: Iterative Self-Improvement

- **Self-Improvement on Training Tasks:** SOAR iterates between the `Sample&Refine` phase (using `LLM_i`) and the learning phase (fine-tuning to create `LLM_i+1`). This allows the model to continuously improve, with better models generating richer training data for subsequent iterations. Solutions generated across models and iterations are deduplicated, and a subset (prioritizing shorter programs) is used to fine-tune a base model for test-time training.
- **Test-Time Training:** The self-improvement loop is adapted for target problems where ground truth is unavailable. This involves fine-tuning sampling capabilities by selecting solution examples based on their training accuracy (on input-output examples only) before applying hindsight relabeling. Refinement finetuning is not applied at test time in the current work. This enables the model to adapt continuously to new, unseen tasks.

#### 3.5. Implementation Details

- **LLMs:** The study utilizes open-source LLMs from the Qwen-2.5-Coder series (7B, 14B, 32B), Qwen-2.5-72B, and Mistral-large-2407.
- **Finetuning:** Models are fine-tuned on a single H100 GPU using RS-LoRA (for 7B, 14B) and RS-QLoRA (for larger models) via the Unsloth library. LoRA parameters are rank 256, α=32, 3 epochs, and learning rate 5e-5.
- **Inference:** SGLang is used for inference, with 50 completions sampled in parallel per task. Generation for a task is halted after 100 solutions achieve perfect training accuracy. Temperature is set to 1, and Min-p to 0.05.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several findings regarding the efficacy of SOAR in improving program synthesis capabilities on the ARC-AGI benchmark.

#### 4.1. The Necessity of Search for LLM-based Program Synthesis

Initial evaluations confirmed that state-of-the-art language models, both open-source and closed-source, exhibit limited success when attempting to solve ARC tasks in a single attempt (one-shot setting). For instance, Claude-4-Sonnet achieved 20.75%, GPT-4.1 8.00%, and Qwen-2.5-Coder models 1.00-2.25% on ARC-test. This performance indicated that direct program synthesis is challenging for current LLMs.

Implementing search strategies significantly improved performance. Comparing "single-shot sampling," "sampling 6k candidate programs" (Sample-6k), and "Sample&Refine-6k" (3k samples + 3k refinements), the Sample&Refine-6k approach consistently yielded the best results across all model sizes. This allowed smaller open-source models to surpass larger closed-source models in performance; for example, the 7B Qwen model outperformed GPT-4.1, and ≥32B Qwen models surpassed Claude-4-Sonnet. However, it was observed that search performance typically scales logarithmically with compute budget and is ultimately bounded by the fixed capabilities of the base model. This observation motivated the core premise of SOAR: to learn to search more effectively by improving the underlying model capabilities.

#### 4.2. Effectiveness of Learning to Sample and Refine Programs

The study investigated methods for extracting meaningful training signals from search attempts to fine-tune the LLM's sampling and refinement abilities.

- **Learning to Sample Programs:** Various strategies for hindsight relabeling (creating synthetic training data from all sampled programs) were evaluated. The "greedy-diverse" method, which samples 25 top-performing and 25 bottom-performing solutions (based on training and test example accuracy), performed best. This suggests that balancing learning from successful solutions with maintaining diversity in training data is beneficial, leading to an increase in sampling accuracy from 29.29% (no fine-tuning) to 36.46% (greedy-diverse) on ARC-train with 3k samples.
- **Learning to Refine Programs:** Two strategies for curating successful refinement examples were tested. The "diverse" sampling strategy (balancing examples across different accuracy bins of the parent program) marginally outperformed uniform sampling, achieving 42.88% ARC-train performance with 3k samples + 3k refinements, compared to 34.83% for no fine-tuning. This demonstrated that fine-tuning significantly improves refinement capabilities.
- **Synergy Between Sampling and Refinement:** Joint fine-tuning of a single model for both sampling and refinement tasks outperformed task-specific fine-tuning and base models. This indicates a positive synergy between these capabilities, suggesting that learning to sample aids refinement and vice versa, likely due to shared representations of program structure and transformation patterns. For example, a model jointly fine-tuned for both achieved 44.42% search accuracy, compared to 43.88% for a model fine-tuned only for refinement (when sampling was done by a fine-tuned sampling model).

#### 4.3. Impact of Iterated Self-Improvement

SOAR's iterative self-improvement loop demonstrated substantial and accumulating performance gains.

- **Self-Improvement on ARC-train Problems:** Across four training iterations, all model sizes showed significant improvements on ARC-train tasks, with gains ranging from +19% to +27% solved problems. Smaller models (e.g., 7B) exhibited steeper improvements in early iterations. The practice of pooling data from all five models and performing majority voting yielded performance superior to any single model, suggesting complementary problem-solving strategies.
- **Self-Improvement on ARC-test Problems (Test-Time Training):** After the initial training phases, two additional iterations of test-time training on ARC-test problems (without ground truth) further improved performance by an extra 5%. The combination of train-time and test-time improvements drastically increased accuracy on ARC-test. For instance, the 7B model improved from 14.25% to 36.25%, and the 14B model from 19.87% to 42.75%. The best overall performance was achieved by combining solutions from all model sizes through majority voting, reaching 52.00% on ARC-test, with an oracle performance of 57.25%.

#### 4.4. Overcoming Scaling Plateaus

The research found that simply increasing model size or search budget with fixed LLM capabilities eventually led to diminishing returns and performance plateaus. SOAR, in contrast, consistently lifted these plateaus across iterations, establishing new, higher scaling curves. This indicates that self-improvement allows models to reach performance levels that would otherwise require much larger models or significantly greater computational budgets. For example, the Qwen-2.5-7B model, after two test-time training iterations, achieved 36.25% on ARC-test, outperforming larger static systems like o3-mini and Claude-4-Sonnet.

#### 4.5. Solution Diversity

Analysis of solution diversity across iterations showed a steady decrease for problems that SOAR successfully solved, indicating convergence on effective solutions. However, for unsolved problems, diversity initially dropped after the first iteration but then stabilized. The authors suggest that hindsight relabeling might contribute to maintaining this exploratory capacity on challenging tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research demonstrates that program synthesis systems can overcome the inherent limitations of fixed-capability generative models by implementing a self-improving evolutionary loop. This finding carries several significant implications and potential impacts:

1. **Breaking Performance Plateaus:** A central significance of SOAR is its empirical demonstration that iterative self-improvement can lift the performance ceilings typically encountered when scaling either model size or search budget. Conventional approaches often hit diminishing returns; SOAR, by continuously improving the underlying LLM's sampling and refinement capabilities, establishes new, higher performance baselines. This suggests a fundamental shift from relying solely on larger models or more extensive search to enabling models to learn and adapt from their own experience.

2. **Increased Autonomy and Reduced Human Dependence:** SOAR operates without requiring human-engineered domain-specific languages (DSLs) or human-generated solution datasets for fine-tuning. It learns purely from its own synthesis attempts, including both successes and failures, through hindsight relabeling. This autonomy reduces the need for costly and labor-intensive human intervention, making the program synthesis process more scalable and less susceptible to human biases or limitations in defining optimal solution spaces.

3. **Enhanced Efficiency for Open-Source Models:** The study shows that even smaller, open-source LLMs, when integrated into the SOAR framework, can achieve performance competitive with or surpass much larger, proprietary closed-source models. This implies that self-improvement mechanisms can democratize access to high-performance program synthesis capabilities, enabling researchers and developers with limited resources to tackle complex tasks effectively.

4. **Advancement in Abstract Reasoning:** By demonstrating robust performance on the ARC-AGI benchmark, which is designed to test core reasoning capabilities and resist simple pattern matching, SOAR contributes to the development of AI systems capable of more abstract and human-like reasoning. The ability of general-purpose language models to bootstrap strong reasoning capabilities solely from their own search experience is a notable advance.

5. **Broader Applicability and Integration Potential:** The SOAR framework is described as domain-agnostic, suggesting its potential applicability beyond ARC. It could serve as a "drop-in upgrade" for existing program synthesis systems that utilize static LLMs in search loops, such as FunSearch or AlphaEvolve, enabling them to continually learn and improve. This could extend to diverse domains like software engineering, mathematical discovery, and scientific research, where automated program generation and refinement are valuable.

6. **Future Research Directions:** The work opens several avenues for future research, including:
 - **Computational Efficiency:** Addressing the current computational cost of 6,000 synthesis attempts per task per iteration, perhaps through adaptive search budget allocation or improved optimization methods.
 - **Solution Diversity:** Exploring strategies to explicitly enhance solution diversity during fine-tuning or through quality-diversity methods, to counteract potential convergence and sustain continuous progress on harder, unsolved problems.
 - **Richer Operators:** Integrating more complex evolutionary operators, such as crossover, to potentially accelerate discovery.

7. **Ethical Considerations:** The development of self-improving AI systems inherently raises safety considerations. While the observed performance improvements in this work show a slowdown rather than unbounded growth, the general principle of systems improving through self-directed learning could inspire future systems with broader, more impactful capabilities. The authors emphasize the importance of transparency through open-sourcing their code and considering potential societal impacts, advocating for responsible development.

In summary, SOAR presents a methodological innovation that allows AI systems to transcend their initial performance boundaries in program synthesis by continuously learning from their own experiences. This contributes to the development of more autonomous, capable, and efficient AI, with potential far-reaching implications across various scientific and engineering disciplines.
