# 3DGSNav: Enhancing Vision-Language Model Reasoning for Object Navigation via Active 3D Gaussian Splatting

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Zhejiang University of Technology developed 3DGSNav, a framework that integrates 3D Gaussian Splatting as a continuous environmental memory to enhance Vision-Language Model reasoning for zero-shot object navigation. The system achieved average improvements of 13.5% in success rate and 32.08% in success weighted by inverse path length across benchmarks and demonstrated 69.44% success in real-world robot experiments.

## Problem

- Existing Zero-Shot Object Navigation (ZSON) methods rely on abstracting environments into semantic maps or textual descriptions, which lose continuous spatial structure and fine-grained geometric details critical for VLM reasoning.
- Perceptual bottlenecks in current approaches limit VLMs from fully leveraging their spatial reasoning capabilities, as high-level decisions are constrained by inaccurate low-level perception.
- Current 3D Large Multimodal Models (LMMs) focus on short-horizon interactions or passive scene analysis, lacking the ability for continuous observation integration and long-horizon sequential decision-making required for ZSON.

## Method

- A 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) framework serves as a dense, continuous, and persistent environmental memory, optimized in the backend to reconstruct and update the scene from observed RGB-D frames.
- An active perception module uses opacity-based panoramic fields, DBSCAN clustering for target viewpoints, and spatial-structure adaptive frontier point clustering with guidance trajectories to efficiently acquire environmental information.
- VLM-driven planning utilizes trajectory-guided free-viewpoint optimization to generate information-dense, annotated first-person views (FPV) and bird's-eye views (BEV) for Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting, while a VLM-driven re-verification mechanism renders novel views from 3DGS memory to confirm target objects without physical robot movement.

## Results

- 3DGSNav achieved an average improvement of 13.5% in Success Rate (SR) and 32.08% in Success weighted by inverse Path Length (SPL) across HM3Dv1, HM3Dv2, and MP3D datasets, significantly outperforming state-of-the-art methods.
- Ablation studies confirmed the criticality of key components: virtual viewpoints improved SR by 10.59%, free-viewpoint optimization further enhanced performance, and the VLM-driven re-verification module alone led to a 32.81% increase in SR.
- The framework demonstrated robust real-world efficacy on a quadruped robot, achieving a 69.44% success rate across six complex scenarios in office and hotel environments.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating 3DGS as a continuous memory representation allows VLMs to access rich, spatially consistent visual observations from arbitrary viewpoints, overcoming the limitations of discrete scene abstractions.
> - Active perception strategies, including optimized viewpoint selection and comprehensive frontier clustering, are essential for efficiently providing VLMs with the information-dense visual prompts needed for robust long-horizon spatial reasoning.
> - A VLM-driven re-verification mechanism, leveraging 3DGS's novel-view synthesis, significantly improves target recognition reliability and navigation efficiency by allowing virtual exploration and confirmation without incurring costly physical robot movements.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Object navigation (ObjectNav) is a fundamental capability for embodied intelligence, requiring agents to locate and navigate to target objects in complex, often unknown, environments. A particularly challenging variant is Zero-Shot Object Navigation (ZSON), where the agent must find unseen target categories in unfamiliar environments without prior task-specific training.

The broader research landscape in ZSON has seen significant advancements with the advent of large models, especially Vision-Language Models (VLMs). These models possess strong contextual understanding and reasoning abilities, making them attractive for high-level decision-making in navigation tasks. However, Zheng et al. highlight critical limitations in existing VLM-based ZSON approaches:

- **Reliance on Scene Abstractions**: Many methods (e.g., Zhou et al., 2024; Qiao et al., 2025; Zhang et al., 2025; Yokoyama et al., 2024) abstract environments into semantic maps, textual descriptions, or 3D scene graphs. While simplifying planning, these abstractions inevitably lose continuous spatial structure and fine-grained geometric details. This "discretization of the continuous environment" restricts VLMs from fully leveraging their inherent spatial reasoning potential, forcing high-level decisions to rely on potentially inaccurate low-level perception or symbolic representations.
- **Perceptual Bottlenecks**: Even methods employing 3D scene graphs (e.g., Yin et al., 2024; Chen et al., 2023) still face bottlenecks in their perception modules, limiting the overall performance and the extent to which VLMs can truly reason about spatial relationships.
- **Limited Spatial Reasoning in Existing LMMs**: Recent advancements in 3D Large Multimodal Models (LMMs) and video-language models (e.g., Hong et al., 2023; Wang et al., 2025b; Yang et al., 2025b) have focused on tasks like 3D visual question answering or 3D grounding, where the model passively analyzes a given scene snapshot. These approaches are typically designed for short-horizon interactions or scene-level reasoning and lack the ability to perform long-horizon, sequential decision-making under partial observability, which is crucial for ZSON. They don't continuously integrate observations while preserving visual spatial consistency.

3DGSNav directly addresses these limitations by introducing 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) as a dense, continuous, and persistent environmental memory representation for VLMs. This is a significant departure from discrete semantic or textual maps. By leveraging 3DGS's efficient novel-view synthesis capabilities, 3DGSNav aims to provide VLMs with rich, spatially consistent visual observations from arbitrary viewpoints, thereby overcoming the perceptual bottleneck and enabling more sophisticated, long-horizon spatial reasoning. The paper claims to be the first to systematically incorporate 3DGS representations into VLMs for such complex, dynamic tasks. This positions 3DGSNav at the forefront of combining advanced 3D reconstruction with powerful VLM reasoning for embodied AI.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of 3DGSNav is to enhance Vision-Language Model (VLM) reasoning capabilities for Zero-Shot Object Navigation (ZSON) by providing them with a superior environmental representation and active perception mechanisms. The core motivation stems from the observed shortcomings of existing ZSON methods:

1. **Overcoming Limitations of Scene Abstractions**: Current ZSON methods often abstract environments into semantic maps or textual descriptions. While simplifying planning, this abstraction inevitably sacrifices continuous spatial structure and fine-grained geometric details. The authors are motivated to replace these coarse abstractions with a richer, more faithful representation that allows VLMs to fully exploit their inherent spatial understanding and reasoning potential.
2. **Addressing Perceptual Bottlenecks**: Even with the integration of VLMs, the accuracy of high-level decision-making in navigation is often constrained by the quality and completeness of low-level perception. The goal is to develop an active perception system that efficiently acquires comprehensive environmental information, thereby alleviating this bottleneck.
3. **Enabling Long-Horizon Spatial Reasoning**: Existing 3D LMMs typically focus on short-horizon tasks or passive scene analysis. ZSON, however, demands continuous integration of observations and long-horizon sequential decision-making under partial observability while preserving visual spatial consistency. A key objective is to design a framework that empowers VLMs to perform such complex, dynamic spatial reasoning over extended periods.
4. **Improving Efficiency and Robustness of Navigation**: Beyond enhancing VLM reasoning, the authors aim to build a navigation system that is both efficient in exploration and robust in target identification. This involves optimizing viewpoint selection, generating informative visual prompts, and implementing a reliable re-verification mechanism for detected targets.

In essence, 3DGSNav seeks to bridge the gap between powerful VLM reasoning and the rich, continuous spatial information required for effective embodied navigation by leveraging 3D Gaussian Splatting as a VLM-accessible memory, coupled with intelligent perception and prompting strategies.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

3DGSNav is a novel ZSON framework that embeds 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) as a persistent memory for VLMs, enabling enhanced spatial reasoning through active perception and intelligent prompting. The system operates in perception, planning, and re-verification stages.

#### 3.1. Perception

The perception module is responsible for constructing and updating the 3DGS environment representation.
- **3DGS Preliminaries**: The environment is represented by a set of anisotropic Gaussian primitives, each defined by its position, opacity, color, and covariance matrix. These primitives are projected onto the image plane for rendering, and the 3D map is optimized in the backend using a composite loss function (`L_g`) that minimizes differences in rendered color, structural similarity, and depth against observed RGB-D frames.
- **Active Perception**: To improve efficiency over passive rotational scanning, 3DGSNav introduces an active perception mechanism. A virtual camera renders a panoramic opacity field to estimate observation completeness. The DBSCAN algorithm clusters low-opacity regions to identify discrete target viewpoints (pitch and yaw). The real camera is then guided to these optimal viewpoints to actively acquire missing perceptual information, enhancing exploration efficiency and robustness.
- **Frontiers**: An exploration map is constructed by integrating opacity-based rendering and agent height from a top-down perspective, allowing the extraction of frontier points (boundaries between explored and unexplored regions). To ensure that the extracted frontier points lie within observed regions, their positions are further refined based on Chebyshev distance. A novel *spatial-structure adaptive frontier point clustering* method is proposed, using a distance field and watershed segmentation, to group multiple frontiers into representative centroids, thereby providing richer context for VLM rendering and reducing computational overhead.
- **Guidance Trajectory**: For each representative frontier point, a guidance trajectory is computed using the Dijkstra algorithm, employing a cost function (`C(n)`) based on distance transform and an exponential penalty for proximity to obstacles. This ensures short, obstacle-free paths, providing a principled reference for free-viewpoint optimization and spatial awareness for the VLM planner.

#### 3.2. Planning

The planning stage leverages the 3DGS memory to generate informative visual prompts for VLM-driven decision-making.
- **Virtual Viewpoint Initialization**: An initial virtual viewpoint position and orientation towards a frontier point are determined by maximizing a weighted score of curvature and distance along the guidance trajectory, ensuring a manageable and stable solution space for optimization.
- **Free-Viewpoint Optimization**: The initial virtual viewpoint cannot always provide optimal views (e.g., due to occlusions). Therefore, camera pose estimation is formulated as a multi-constraint optimization problem. It iteratively optimizes the camera's rotation and translation by minimizing a composite loss function (`L`). This loss includes:
 - `Opacity loss (L_opa)`: Balances observed and unobserved regions in the rendered First-Person View (FPV).
 - `Ray occlusion loss (L_vis)`: Ensures the frontier point is directly observed by checking depth consistency.
 - `View alignment loss (L_cos)`: Aligns the camera's gaze towards the frontier point.
 - `Guidance trajectory loss (L_traj)`: Keeps the virtual camera's position close to the pre-computed guidance trajectory, with an adaptive weight based on opacity loss. This optimization generates information-dense FPVs of candidate frontier points.
- **Annotation**: To enhance the VLM's understanding, online visual annotations are applied to both the FPVs and a Bird’s-Eye View (BEV).
 - FPV: Overlays a gaze point to highlight the region of interest around the frontier and marks unobserved regions in purple to differentiate known from unknown areas.
 - BEV: Explicitly annotates the spatial layout of the frontier, the guidance trajectory, the agent’s historical trajectory, and its current position, establishing FPV-BEV correspondence and providing global spatial context.
- **Navigation Policy**: A composite image, combining the annotated FPV and BEV, along with Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting, is fed into the VLM (Gemini3-Pro). The VLM performs spatial and semantic reasoning to select the optimal exploration target (next frontier point). The Fast Marching Method (FMM) is then used for low-level navigation to this selected goal.

#### 3.3. Re-verification

To ensure reliable target detection and avoid unnecessary physical movement, 3DGSNav incorporates a novel re-verification mechanism.
- **Initial Detection**: During navigation, a real-time object detector (YOLOE) continuously identifies potential targets.
- **VLM-driven Active Viewpoint Switching**: If the detector's results are ambiguous, an action-decision VLM (GLM-4.5v) is invoked. Instead of physically moving the robot, this VLM reasons over the environmental structure and object occlusions to select appropriate actions (e.g., small shifts, turns). These selected actions are then *projected into the 3DGS memory to render novel viewpoints* of the target. The VLM re-evaluates the target based on these new views, effectively performing target re-verification without incurring additional exploration costs in the real environment, significantly improving recognition reliability and accuracy.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

3DGSNav was extensively evaluated across three benchmarks (HM3Dv1, HM3Dv2, and MP3D datasets) in the Habitat simulator, as well as in real-world experiments on a quadruped robot. Key performance metrics were Success Rate (SR) and Success weighted by inverse Path Length (SPL).

- **Benchmark Performance**:
 - 3DGSNav demonstrated robust and competitive performance against state-of-the-art methods, achieving an average improvement of **13.5% in SR** and **32.08% in SPL** across the datasets.
 - Specifically, it significantly outperformed RL-based ZSON (Majumdar et al., 2022) with an average of 203.01% higher SR and 320.11% higher SPL on HM3Dv1 and MP3D.
 - Against BeliefMapNav (Zhou et al., 2025), which uses voxel representations, 3DGSNav achieved gains of 25.25% in SR and 51.65% in SPL, highlighting the effectiveness of 3DGS as a VLM-accessible memory over scene abstractions. On HM3Dv1, 3DGSNav achieved an impressive 80.0% SR and 51.79% SPL.

- **Ablation Studies**:
 - **VLM Performance**: A comparison of VLMs revealed that Gemini3-Pro (used in 3DGSNav) significantly outperformed open-source models like Qwen3-VL-32b-Thinking and Qwen3-VL-235b-A22B-Thinking. Gemini3 achieved a 20.14% improvement in SR and 31.25% in SPL over Qwen235b. Self-explanations showed Gemini3's superior image and spatial understanding, its ability to infer relationships between frontier points from the BEV, and strong planning capabilities, while Qwen models showed deficiencies in fundamental image understanding.
 - **Failure Analysis**: Reasoning failures accounted for only 9.5% of total failures, the lowest among all failure types, primarily due to detection errors (e.g., category ambiguity, multi-target ambiguity, or detection errors from limited local perception) and the inability to perform cross-floor navigation. This suggests the proposed paradigm effectively unlocks VLMs' potential for ZSON.
 - **Viewpoints and Annotations**:
 - Removing *virtual viewpoints* (i.e., not rendering optimized FPVs) led to a 13.25% decrease in SPL and a 10.59% drop in SR, underscoring their importance in overcoming occlusions and providing rich frontier information to the VLM.
 - *Free-viewpoint optimization* further improved SR and SPL, demonstrating its effectiveness over fixed virtual viewpoints.
 - The *annotated historical trajectory* (in BEV) reduced redundant backtracking, improving navigation efficiency.
 - *Gaze points* in FPVs effectively steered VLM attention to relevant regions, preventing misinterpretation of room types or navigation directions.
 - Representing *unobserved regions in purple* (instead of black/gray) in FPVs was crucial to prevent the VLM from misinterpreting them as obstacles or hallucinating lighting cues.
 - **Re-verification**: The VLM-driven re-verification module was critical. Removing the re-verification VLM (relying solely on the real-time detector) resulted in a substantial **32.81% drop in SR**. The ability for the VLM to select actions and render novel views from 3DGS memory for target re-verification proved effective in mitigating misrecognition.
 - **Planner CoT and Frontier Clustering**: Both were found to be essential. CoT prompting was vital for effective VLM reasoning, and *frontier point clustering* significantly improved SR by 21.87%, as it aggregated dispersed frontiers to enrich free-viewpoint rendering and provide a more comprehensive context.

- **Real-Robot Experiments**:
 - The method was deployed on a Go2 quadruped robot in real-world office and hotel environments across six increasingly difficult scenarios.
 - It achieved a **69.44% success rate** overall, demonstrating its robustness and efficacy in real-world settings.
 - Challenges included motion control issues (payload-induced trajectory deviations, vibrations causing motion blur) and visual perception limitations (glass surfaces, specular reflections, hollow structures causing RGB-D failures). High VLM API latency also caused system overheating.

- **Runtime Analysis**: The main components showed reasonable real-time performance: Free-viewpoint optimization (115ms per iteration), guidance trajectory (2.4ms per trajectory), and frontier clustering (6.3ms total).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

3DGSNav presents a significant advancement in the field of embodied AI, particularly for zero-shot object navigation, by fundamentally reimagining how Vision-Language Models interact with their environment.

- **Pioneering 3DGS as VLM Memory**: The most significant contribution is the pioneering use of 3D Gaussian Splatting as a continuous, persistent, and VLM-accessible memory representation for long-horizon navigation. This departs from traditional discrete scene abstractions (semantic maps, textual descriptions, voxel maps) that inherently lose fine-grained spatial and geometric detail. By providing VLMs with access to a rich 3D representation and the ability to synthesize arbitrary novel views, 3DGSNav empowers them to perform more sophisticated and accurate spatial reasoning.
- **Enhanced VLM Reasoning for Embodied Tasks**: The framework effectively unlocks the full potential of VLMs for dynamic, sequential decision-making in real-world scenarios. The combination of active perception, trajectory-guided free-viewpoint optimization, and annotation-empowered visual prompts (with CoT) ensures that VLMs receive optimal, information-dense visual context, addressing previous perceptual bottlenecks.
- **Robust and Efficient Navigation**: The proposed active perception strategy significantly improves sensing efficiency. The VLM-driven re-verification mechanism, which leverages 3DGS to render novel views for target confirmation *without physical robot movement*, makes the navigation process more efficient and robust, reducing costly false positives and unnecessary exploration.
- **Bridging the Gap between 3D Reconstruction and Embodied AI**: This work effectively integrates cutting-edge 3D reconstruction techniques (3DGS) with advanced large multimodal models, demonstrating a powerful synergy. It shows a pathway for embodied agents to build a high-fidelity internal model of the world that directly facilitates complex reasoning.
- **Practical Applicability**: The successful deployment and robust performance on a real-world quadruped robot, despite inherent challenges of robotic platforms, underscores the practical viability and potential impact of 3DGSNav. This move beyond simulation is crucial for validating the generalizability of embodied AI solutions.
- **Future Directions**: The identified limitations in real-world deployment (e.g., motion control, sensor failures with challenging materials, VLM latency) offer clear and impactful directions for future research. Tightly integrating manipulator-based active perception with locomotion policies and addressing inherent sensor weaknesses will further enhance the real-world robustness and versatility of such systems.

In conclusion, 3DGSNav offers a compelling paradigm shift in zero-shot object navigation, establishing 3DGS as a powerful memory for VLMs and setting a new benchmark for enabling sophisticated spatial reasoning in embodied agents. Its contributions pave the way for more intelligent, autonomous robots capable of navigating and interacting with complex environments.
