# Progressive Reinforcement Learning with Distillation for Multi-Skilled Motion Control

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at the University of British Columbia developed Progressive Learning and Integration via Distillation (PLAiD), a framework for continually acquiring and integrating multiple motion skills into a single deep neural network. This method leverages iterative transfer learning and policy distillation to mitigate catastrophic forgetting and achieve robust performance across a growing repertoire of skills in continuous control tasks like bipedal locomotion.

## Problem

- Endowing deep reinforcement learning agents with rich, multi-skilled motion repertoires for continuous control, especially when skills are acquired sequentially.
- Catastrophic forgetting, where existing methods struggle to integrate new skills without losing previously learned abilities or requiring extensive retraining.
- The challenge of combining multiple expert policies efficiently in continuous action spaces while maintaining performance on all tasks without hierarchical structures.

## Method

- The Progressive Learning and Integration via Distillation (PLAiD) framework iteratively combines deep ==reinforcement learning==, transfer learning, and policy distillation for continual policy transfer.
- A two-step process: an acquisition step fine-tunes the existing policy for a new skill, followed by an integration step using policy distillation to combine all acquired skills into a unified controller.
- An "input injection" mechanism allows augmenting existing policy networks with new perceptual features (e.g., terrain heightmaps) by initializing new weights to zero, preserving old behaviors while enabling new capabilities.

## Results

- PLAiD effectively acquired and integrated new skills, outperforming the MultiTasker baseline, which struggled with increasing task complexity, and showed less forgetting compared to the TL-Only method.
- The method demonstrated superior mitigation of catastrophic forgetting, exhibiting significantly smaller performance drops on previously learned skills (e.g., for 'steps' and 'slopes') compared to alternative approaches.
- Integrated policies developed through PLAiD achieved zero-shot generalization, successfully navigating a 'mixed' environment composed of various terrain types not explicitly trained as a combined task, and obtained the highest average reward (**0.698**) across five sequential terrain types.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - An iterative distillation strategy, sequentially learning and integrating skills, scales more effectively with increasing tasks compared to simultaneous multi-task learning, leading to faster skill combination.
> - Input injection effectively augments policies with new perceptual inputs without disrupting existing learned behaviors, enabling adaptive control for new contexts.
> - Progressive distillation is more robust at mitigating catastrophic forgetting than a single, large distillation step, preserving performance on prior skills when combining policies.
