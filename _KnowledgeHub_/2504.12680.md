# Embodied-R: Collaborative Framework for Activating Embodied Spatial Reasoning in Foundation Models via Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> A collaborative framework from Tsinghua University enables foundation models to perform embodied spatial reasoning by combining large vision-language models for perception with reinforcement learning-trained small language models for reasoning, achieving comparable performance to state-of-the-art multimodal models while reducing computational costs through key frame extraction and logical consistency rewards.

## Problem

- Current foundation models lack human-like spatial cognition abilities needed for embodied reasoning in physical environments
- Existing multimodal models face limitations with input token limits, frame redundancy, and poor generalization for embodied video understanding
- Achieving strong spatial reasoning typically requires large-scale models, creating a difficult trade-off between model size, computational cost, and deployable performance

## Method

- ==Collaborative framework== combining large VLMs for perception with small LMs for reasoning
- RL training with novel ==logical consistency reward== to improve reasoning quality, using a three-stage training schedule to optimize RL performance
- ==Key frame extractor== to reduce computational costs by eliminating redundant frames

## Results

- Achieved **comparable or better** performance than proprietary models like OpenAI-o1 and Gemini-2.5-Pro, with strong generalization on out-of-distribution datasets like EgoSchema and MVBench
- Reduced computational costs while maintaining accuracy through key frame selection
- RL training improved reasoning quality and mitigated reward hacking through logical consistency

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Small LMs can match large model performance when trained with RL and logical consistency rewards
> - Frame extraction and collaborative approach enable efficient processing of embodied video
> - RL training leads to better out-of-distribution generalization compared to supervised fine-tuning

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper directly addresses a critical gap in the development of Artificial General Intelligence (AGI): endowing foundation models with human-like embodied spatial cognition. It tackles this problem by combining VLMs and LMs to enable agents to perceive, think, and move within a 3D physical world.

Here's how it fits into the broader research landscape:

- **Embodied AI:** It builds upon the growing interest in embodied AI, which aims to develop intelligent agents that can interact with the physical world. This area is driven by the limitations of AI systems that primarily exist in the "cyber world" and the need for agents that can perform tasks in real-world environments.
- **Spatial Reasoning:** The paper targets a specific and challenging aspect of embodied AI: spatial reasoning. While progress has been made in improving the perception capabilities of foundation models, their reasoning abilities, particularly in spatial contexts, lag behind. This research addresses this disparity.
- **Video Understanding:** It contributes to the field of video understanding, particularly from an egocentric perspective. Unlike traditional video analysis, which focuses on content understanding from a broad perspective, this work emphasizes the relationship between the observer (agent) and the surrounding environment from a first-person perspective.
- **Large Language Model Reasoning:** It leverages the advancements in LLM reasoning achieved through techniques like Chain-of-Thought (CoT) and Reinforcement Learning (RL). The paper adapts these techniques to embodied spatial reasoning, introducing a "slow-thinking" mode where models reason thoroughly before responding.
- **Collaboration Between Large and Small Models:** The paper proposes a collaborative framework that leverages both large-scale VLMs (for perception) and small-scale LMs (for reasoning). This approach addresses the trade-off between model size, computational cost, and performance, offering a resource-efficient way to enhance reasoning abilities. The proposed approach is aligned with a trend toward resource-efficient and scalable AI systems.
- **Addressing Limitations of Existing MLLMs:** The research acknowledges and aims to overcome limitations of applying existing Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) to embodied videos, such as issues related to input token limits, redundancy between frames, and lack of generalization.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives of this research are:

- **Develop a collaborative framework (Embodied-R) for embodied spatial reasoning.** This framework leverages the strengths of large-scale VLMs for perception and small-scale LMs for reasoning.
- **Enhance the spatial reasoning abilities of foundation models using Reinforcement Learning (RL).** The authors aim to enable a "slow-thinking" mode in these models, where they reason thoroughly before generating responses.
- **Introduce a novel logical consistency reward in RL training.** This reward encourages alignment between the reasoning process and the generated answers, mitigating reward hacking and improving the quality of the reasoning.
- **Achieve performance comparable to state-of-the-art multimodal reasoning models (e.g., OpenAI-o1, Gemini-2.5-Pro) on embodied spatial reasoning tasks.** The authors aim to demonstrate the effectiveness of their approach by comparing it to established models.
- **Investigate research questions related to the framework's performance, the role of RL, and out-of-distribution generalization capabilities.** This includes exploring the impact of response length, training on VLMs, reward design strategies, and differences in model generalization after SFT and RL training.

The motivation behind this work stems from the need to equip AI systems with human-like spatial cognition, enabling them to perceive, reason, and act in real-world environments. The authors identify limitations in existing foundation models' spatial reasoning abilities and aim to address these limitations through a collaborative and resource-efficient framework.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology and approach can be summarized as follows:

- **Problem Formulation:** The paper clearly defines the problem of embodied spatial reasoning as taking a sequence of video frames and a spatial reasoning question as inputs and outputting a correct answer.
- **Collaborative Framework (Embodied-R):** This is the core of the approach, consisting of:

 - **Large-Scale VLM-based Perception:** A large VLM extracts semantic information from video frames, simulating real-world online reasoning and managing input token length. A key-frame extractor is introduced to reduce computational costs by retaining critical information and eliminating redundant frames.
 - **Small-Scale LM-based Reasoning:** A small LM receives the semantic information and reasoning question and outputs the reasoning process and final answer. This LM is trained using RL.
- **Reinforcement Learning (RL):** The small-scale LM is trained using RL with a novel reward system. The RL algorithm used is Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO).
- **Reward Modeling:** The reward system consists of three components:
 *Format Reward: Encourages the model to follow a "think-answer" reasoning pattern.
 *Accuracy Reward: Rewards the model for providing correct answers.
 *Logical Consistency Reward: This novel reward encourages the model to produce a reasoning process that is logically consistent with the final answer.
*Three-Stage Training Schedule: To optimize RL training, the authors use a three-stage training schedule to achieve a smooth improvement in training performance.
- **Experimental Setup:** The authors conduct experiments on embodied video datasets, including VSI-Bench and UrbanVideo-Bench. They also use out-of-distribution datasets (EgoSchema and MVBench) to evaluate generalization.
- **Baseline Comparison:** The authors compare their approach to several baselines, including proprietary models, SOTA reasoning models, open-source models, and supervised fine-tuned models.
- **Ablation Studies:** The authors conduct ablation studies to evaluate the contributions of each module (key-frame extractor, collaboration, RL training).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The main findings and results of the research are:

- **Embodied-R achieves performance comparable to or surpassing state-of-the-art multimodal large language models.** The model outperforms proprietary models as well as OpenAI-o1 and Gemini-2.5-Pro on embodied reasoning tasks.
- **The collaborative framework effectively enhances reasoning performance.** The use of a small-scale LM in conjunction with a large-scale VLM improves accuracy compared to using the VLM alone.
- **Reinforcement Learning (RL) significantly enhances the reasoning abilities of the LM.** RL training leads to significant improvements in accuracy compared to using the original LM.
- **The logical consistency reward improves the quality of the reasoning process.** The reward encourages alignment between the reasoning process and the answer, mitigating reward hacking and improving interpretability.
- **The key-frame extractor reduces computational costs without sacrificing performance.** The extractor effectively removes redundant frames, reducing training and inference time.
- **Models trained with RL exhibit stronger generalization on out-of-distribution datasets compared to those trained with SFT.** This suggests that RL training promotes more robust and adaptable reasoning abilities.
- **RL training leads to output length convergence, aligning with the requirements of the task.**
- **The reasoning upper bound of same-scale VLMs trained with RL is significantly lower than that of Embodied-R, due to inherent limitations in perception.**
