# B-cos Networks: Alignment is All We Need for Interpretability

> [!summary] Summary
> B-cos Networks introduce a method for building inherently interpretable deep neural networks by integrating a B-cos transform that promotes alignment between weights and input features. This allows models to explain their decisions through their internal structure, achieving classification accuracies comparable to conventional networks, such as 74.5% top-1 accuracy on CIFAR-10 for B-cos DenseNet-121, matching the baseline.

## Problem

- Deep neural networks are black-box models, making their decision-making processes opaque and difficult to understand, particularly in critical applications.
- Existing post-hoc explanation methods often lack faithfulness to the model's actual reasoning or produce explanations that are difficult for humans to interpret.
- The complex non-linear transformations within traditional DNNs prevent tracing how specific input features contribute to model outputs.

## Method

- Introduce the ==B-cos transform==, a drop-in replacement for standard linear transforms that incorporates a ==cosine similarity term== between weights and inputs.
- Utilize a hyperparameter =='B'== within the transform to control the pressure for weight vectors to align with task-relevant input features.
- Build B-cos networks by replacing standard linear layers with B-cos transforms, using ==MaxOut units== to maintain non-linearity while preserving interpretability through linear transform summarization.

## Results

- B-cos networks achieve classification performance comparable to conventional counterparts, for instance, B-cos DenseNet-121 reached **74.5%** top-1 accuracy on CIFAR-10, matching the baseline.
- The networks provide inherent interpretability where weights directly align with task-relevant input features, offering natural and faithful explanations for decisions.
- B-cos networks quantitatively outperform various post-hoc explanation methods in localization accuracy, as demonstrated by the "grid pointing game" metric.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Aligning weights with input features during training can enable neural networks to inherently explain their decisions through their own structure.
> - The B-cos transform's exponent 'B' allows control over the degree of weight-input alignment, making weights directly interpretable as feature templates.
> - B-cos networks allow for summarization of sequences of transforms, enabling interpretation of both output and intermediate neurons, which often represent semantic concepts.
