# Quo Vadis, World Modeling?

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers propose a re-evaluation of world modeling, introducing a framework of 'Agent-Centric Interactive World Proxies' to provide diverse, actionable feedback for continually improving AI agents. This framework outlines six functional forms of proxies and three levels of agent empowerment, aiming to accelerate learning and overcome real-world interaction bottlenecks.

## Problem

- AI agents often rely on static, offline datasets, limiting their ability to adapt and continually improve in dynamic environments.
- Direct interaction with real environments for agent training is costly, inefficient, risky, and provides limited proactive feedback.
- Traditional world models, focused solely on predicting future physical states, do not provide the diverse, actionable information required by advanced agents for planning and learning.

## Method

- A formal definition of **'Agent-Centric Interactive World Proxies'** is introduced, generalizing beyond physical state prediction to information transitions conditioned on agent-initiated interactions.
- The paper categorizes these proxies into ==six distinct functional forms== based on the type of feedback they provide: Dynamics, Spatial, Execution, Memory/Experience, Skill, and Reward/Verification Proxies.
- ==Three levels of agent empowerment== are established—Inference-Time Guidance, Training-Time Optimization, and Agent-Proxy Co-Evolution—detailing how proxies can progressively enhance agent capabilities.

## Results

- A formal definition for Agent-Centric World Proxies was established, incorporating generalized interaction steps, a broad information state space, and agent-initiated interactions.
- **Six** functional forms of world proxies were identified and characterized, providing a systematic overview of how different feedback modalities can serve agent improvement.
- **Three** progressive empowerment levels were outlined, clarifying how world proxies can provide inference-time guidance, enable training-time optimization, and facilitate agent-proxy co-evolution.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - World modeling should shift from high-fidelity physical prediction to providing 'actionable information gain' for the agent, optimizing for its planning and learning needs.
> - A structured design space, mapping six functional proxy forms against three empowerment levels, reveals both established research areas and underexplored directions in agentic AI.
> - World proxies offer a critical intermediate layer to mitigate real-environment interaction bottlenecks, potentially accelerating agent learning and development in costly or risky domains.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses a central challenge in developing highly capable artificial intelligence agents: enabling them to continually improve through interaction with their environment. Traditionally, many agents are trained using static, offline datasets, which limits their competence to the distribution of the training data and hinders their ability to adapt to novel situations or learn from active trial and error. This approach contrasts with the desired characteristic of "continually improving agents" that can explore, draw feedback, and convert experiences into future momentum.

The most direct method for continual improvement involves agents interacting directly with real environments. However, the paper identifies several critical bottlenecks associated with this approach: high cost and low training efficiency (e.g., robotic wear, computational resources for digital agents), non-rollbackable and risky interactions (e.g., physical damage, irreversible digital actions), limited forward-looking feedback (real-environment feedback is often passive and after-the-fact), and difficulty in parallelization (limited real-environment instances).

World modeling emerges as a natural intermediate solution to mitigate these issues. Classical world models primarily focus on predicting future physical states, typically formalized as `next_state = WorldModel(current_state, action)`. While useful for tasks like robotics and model-based reinforcement learning, this traditional formulation is presented as too narrow for agents requiring diverse, actionable feedback beyond raw state transitions. This paper positions itself by advocating for a fundamental shift in the conceptualization of world modeling, moving beyond mere physical state prediction to a broader paradigm centered on "Agent-Centric Interactive World Proxies" that provide versatile, agent-usable information. This reframing seeks to broaden the utility of world models to support advanced capabilities such as planning, learning, and continual evolution in complex scenarios.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to reconceptualize world modeling from a paradigm focused on physical state transitions to one centered on "Agent-Centric Interactive World Proxies" that predict agent-usable information transitions. This shift is motivated by the recognized need for continually improving agents to obtain dynamic interaction feedback beyond static supervision or narrow physical state predictions.

The detailed motivations behind this objective are multifaceted:

1. **Bridging the Gap from Static to Continual Improvement:** Existing agents are often trained on static datasets, which inherently limits their ability to adapt to new situations or continuously learn from real-world interactions. To achieve true continual improvement, agents require an ongoing, active feedback loop.
2. **Overcoming Bottlenecks of Direct Real-Environment Interaction:** Direct interaction with real environments for training and improvement is presented as impractical and inefficient at scale. Specific bottlenecks include:
 - **High Cost and Low Efficiency:** Real interactions are often expensive (e.g., device wear for robots, computational cost for digital agents) and slow, necessitating a large volume of trial and error.
 - **Irreversibility and Risk:** Actions in real environments can have non-rollbackable or dangerous consequences (e.g., physical damage, data corruption), making extensive exploration risky.
 - **Limited Forward-Looking Feedback:** Real environments typically provide feedback *after* an action is executed, making proactive planning, multi-step reasoning, and "what-if" analysis difficult.
 - **Difficulty in Parallelization:** Replicating real-world instances for parallel training is often challenging or impossible.
3. **Broadening the Scope of World Modeling:** Classical world models, focused on `Current State + Action → Future State`, are considered insufficient to provide the full spectrum of feedback required by sophisticated agents. These agents need to:
 - Simulate various interaction outcomes (e.g., API calls, tool uses).
 - Retrieve relevant experiences, memories, or failure cases.
 - Query and utilize reusable skills or sub-policies.
 - Verify the safety and feasibility of plans or actions.
 - Obtain reward, critique, or error-diagnosis signals for training.
 The paper argues that confining world modeling to next-state prediction limits its utility for these diverse agent needs.

To systematically address these motivations, the research aims to:
- **Define Agent-Centric World Proxies:** Establish a formal definition that generalizes beyond physical state and time, emphasizing agent-initiated interactions and information gain.
- **Map the Design Space:** Organize world proxies into six functional forms based on their feedback modalities (dynamics, spatial, execution, memory/experience, skill, and reward/verification proxies) to characterize the various ways world modeling can serve agent improvement.
- **Analyze Empowerment Levels:** Detail how these proxies empower agents across three progressive levels: L1 (Inference-Time Guidance), L2 (Training-Time Optimization), and L3 (Agent-Proxy Co-Evolution), illustrating an increasing depth of proxy-agent integration.
- **Establish a Roadmap:** Ultimately, the work seeks to provide a conceptual framework and roadmap for developing world proxies that enable agents to achieve better planning, faster learning, and continuous evolution. The underlying motivation is to optimize for "actionable information gain" for the agent rather than solely focusing on the fidelity or realism of world prediction.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

This paper is primarily a conceptual and theoretical work, proposing a new framework for understanding and developing world models for continually improving agents. Its methodology involves a systematic redefinition, categorization, and analysis of world modeling.

The approach can be broken down into several key components:

1. **Conceptual Shift from World Models to World Proxies:**
 - **Critique of Classical World Models:** The paper begins by analyzing the limitations of the traditional `next_state = WorldModel(current_state, action)` formulation, arguing it is too narrow for diverse agent needs.
 - **Generalization to World Proxies:** It proposes generalizing the concept to a "World Proxy," which is an intermediate mechanism, grounded in the real environment, designed to supply diverse interaction feedback that would otherwise require direct, costly real-world execution. The core idea is that a World Proxy provides information gain, not just state prediction.
 - **Formal Definition:** An "Agent-Centric World Proxy" is formally defined as "an environment-grounded proxy that models or predicts information transitions conditioned on agent-initiated interactions, aiming to provide information gain for agent improvement." This definition introduces:
 - `ℓ`: an interaction step, not limited to physical time.
 - `𝒮`: an information state space, including physical state, observation, memory, knowledge, execution results, verification, and guidance.
 - `ℱ`: a proxy function (e.g., dynamics prediction, spatial rendering).
 - `𝑢ℱℓ`: an agent-initiated query, action, or intervention.
 - `̂𝑠ℓ+1`: the returned feedback or information gain.

2. **Defining Criteria for Effective World Proxies:**
 - The paper establishes five criteria for a "good" World Proxy: Groundedness (reliance on real-environment data), Controllability (supports agent-initiated queries), Feedback Usefulness (improves agent planning/learning), Cost and Scalability (reduces real-world interaction cost), and Forward-Looking Ability (anticipates outcomes).

3. **Categorization of Agent Empowerment Levels:**
 - The research introduces a three-level hierarchy detailing *how* World Proxies improve agents:
 - **L1: Inference-Time Guidance:** The proxy provides additional context, experience, skill, or verification feedback to the agent at decision-making time, enriching its current context without altering its core parameters.
 - **L2: Training-Time Optimization:** The proxy acts as a reward model, verifier, critic, or simulator, generating training signals (e.g., rewards, critiques, synthetic rollouts) that directly optimize the agent's policy or parameters.
 - **L3: Agent-Proxy Co-Evolution:** A closed-loop system where real-environment evidence continually updates the proxy, and the refined proxy, in turn, guides and trains the agent, leading to mutual improvement and co-evolution.

4. **Identification and Characterization of Functional Forms of World Proxies:**
 - The paper systematically unpacks the `ℱ` in its formal definition into six distinct functional forms based on the type of feedback they provide:
 - **Dynamics Proxy:** The classical world model, predicting future physical states and rewards based on current state and actions.
 - **Spatial Proxy:** Generates observations from alternative viewpoints or spatial positions.
 - **Execution Proxy:** Simulates the outcomes of executable interactions in digital environments (e.g., code, commands, API calls).
 - **Memory / Experience Proxy:** Retrieves task-relevant information from past interactions, experiences, or failures.
 - **Skill Proxy:** Recommends or retrieves reusable behavioral knowledge, skills, or tool-use routines.
 - **Reward / Verification Proxy:** Evaluates agent behavior, trajectories, or plans, providing reward signals, critiques, preferences, or verification results.
 - For each functional form, the paper provides its formula, meaning, and typical examples from existing literature.

5. **Integration of Functions and Levels:**
 - The methodology culminates in mapping how each of the six functional forms can operate across the three empowerment levels (Table 6), providing a comprehensive design space for Agent-Centric World Proxies. This integrated view highlights both established research areas and underexplored directions.

In essence, the methodology is a structured theoretical decomposition and synthesis, aiming to provide a robust conceptual framework rather than presenting experimental results. It analyzes existing research to support its definitions and categorizations.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

As a conceptual paper, the "findings" are primarily the proposed theoretical framework, definitions, and categorizations, rather than empirical observations. The main results establish a reoriented perspective on world modeling:

1. **Reconceptualization of World Modeling:** The paper successfully shifts the fundamental paradigm of world modeling from "physical state transition prediction" to "agent-usable information transition prediction." This involves defining "Agent-Centric Interactive World Proxies" that focus on providing actionable feedback tailored to an agent's needs for planning, learning, and continual improvement, rather than solely aiming for high-fidelity simulation of raw physical states.
2. **Formal Definition of Agent-Centric World Proxies:** A formal definition `̂𝑠ℓ+1 = 𝒲𝒫 (𝑠ℓ, 𝑢ℱℓ)` is established, explicitly incorporating generalized interaction steps (ℓ), a broad information state space (𝒮), specific proxy functions (ℱ), and agent-initiated interactions (𝑢ℱℓ) to return diverse feedback (̂𝑠ℓ+1). This definition provides a unified language for discussing varied world model applications.
3. **Identification of Six Functional Forms:** The work systematically categorizes world proxies into six distinct functional forms, each addressing a different type of agent query and providing a different modality of feedback:
 - **Dynamics Proxy:** Predicts future states and rewards (classical world model).
 - **Spatial Proxy:** Generates observations from queried viewpoints.
 - **Execution Proxy:** Simulates outcomes of digital interactions (e.g., code, API calls, GUI clicks).
 - **Memory/Experience Proxy:** Retrieves past experiences, failures, or constraints.
 - **Skill Proxy:** Suggests or retrieves reusable behavioral knowledge and skills.
 - **Reward/Verification Proxy:** Evaluates agent behavior, plans, or trajectories to provide feedback on correctness, safety, or feasibility.
 This categorization provides a structured overview of the diverse ways world models can serve agents.
4. **Establishment of Three Empowerment Levels:** The paper outlines a progressive hierarchy of how world proxies empower agents:
 - **L1: Inference-Time Guidance:** Proxies enrich the agent's current context with relevant information (e.g., retrieved memories, simulated outcomes) to improve immediate decision-making.
 - **L2: Training-Time Optimization:** Proxies generate direct training signals (e.g., rewards, critiques, synthetic data) that optimize the agent's policy parameters, enabling it to learn and improve its capabilities.
 - **L3: Agent-Proxy Co-Evolution:** A fully closed-loop system where real-environment evidence updates the proxy, and the improved proxy in turn refines the agent, leading to a continuous, mutual evolution of both components.
 This hierarchy clarifies the varying depths of integration and impact a proxy can have on an agent.
5. **Integrated Design Space Mapping:** The paper presents a matrix (Table 6) that maps the six functional forms against the three empowerment levels, illustrating how different proxy types can operate at various stages of agent improvement. This visualization serves as a comprehensive design space, highlighting both mature areas and underexplored research directions (e.g., spatial and reward proxies at the co-evolution level).
6. **Articulation of Open Challenges:** The work identifies critical open challenges inherent in this new paradigm, including:
 - **Fidelity and Limits of Imagination:** Addressing the issue of generative models producing convincing but physically inaccurate simulations.
 - **Trust Calibration:** Developing mechanisms for agents to assess when to trust proxy feedback versus real-environment interaction.
 - **Reward Hacking and Safety:** Mitigating risks when proxies serve as reward or verification mechanisms in L2.
 - **Agent-Centric Evaluation:** The need for new benchmarks that measure the proxy's impact on agent planning, learning, and improvement, rather than just isolated proxy performance metrics like realism.

These conceptual results provide a new lens for understanding and advancing research in agentic AI and world modeling.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The paper "Quo Vadis, World Modeling? Towards Interactive World Proxies for Continually Improving Agents" carries several significances and potential impacts on the field of artificial intelligence:

1. **Reframing the Purpose of World Modeling:** The most significant contribution is the proposed paradigm shift from viewing world models primarily as "world predictors" to "agent-serving proxies." This reorientation emphasizes the functional utility of the model for an agent's continual improvement over mere high-fidelity simulation. This shift can redirect research efforts towards developing more actionable and agent-centric world models.

2. **Structured Roadmap for Agentic AI Research:** By systematically defining Agent-Centric World Proxies, categorizing them into six functional forms, and outlining three levels of agent empowerment, the paper provides a comprehensive conceptual framework. This framework serves as a roadmap for researchers, enabling them to:
 - Better classify existing work.
 - Identify unexplored research avenues (as highlighted by sparse cells in Table 6).
 - Design new world proxies with specific agent improvement goals in mind.
 - Communicate more clearly about the diverse roles of world models.

3. **Accelerating Agent Learning and Development:** World proxies offer a crucial intermediate layer that can alleviate the bottlenecks of direct real-environment interaction. By providing cheaper, faster, safer, and more parallelizable feedback, they can significantly accelerate the training, evaluation, and refinement cycles for continually improving agents. This is particularly impactful for domains like robotics, autonomous driving, and complex digital agents (e.g., web agents) where real-world interaction is costly or risky.

4. **Broadening the Definition and Application of World Models:** The paper expands the traditional understanding of world models beyond dynamics prediction to encompass a wider array of information-generating mechanisms (spatial, execution, memory, skill, reward/verification). This inclusive definition could foster interdisciplinary research and unlock new applications for world modeling in areas like cognitive architectures, explainable AI, and ethical AI by considering a broader spectrum of agent-usable information.

5. **Emphasis on Actionable Information Gain:** The recurring theme of "actionable information gain" as the ultimate criterion for a good proxy underscores a pragmatic approach to AI development. This focus contrasts with purely academic pursuits of realism, suggesting a more direct path to building useful and performant agents.

6. **Driving New Evaluation Methodologies:** The explicit identification of "Evaluation that measures information gain" as an open challenge has the potential to spur the development of new, agent-centric benchmarks. Current benchmarks often evaluate proxy realism or fidelity in isolation. A shift to evaluating how well a proxy enables an agent to plan, learn, or improve will lead to more relevant and practical evaluation metrics for agentic AI.

7. **Addressing Trust and Safety in Advanced Agents:** The framework acknowledges open challenges related to proxy fidelity, trust, and safety (e.g., reward hacking). By framing the L3 "Agent-Proxy Co-Evolution" loop as a mechanism for continuous grounding and correction by real-environment evidence, the paper implicitly contributes to the discourse on building more robust and trustworthy AI systems that can self-correct and mitigate risks associated with proxy-generated feedback.

In summary, this work is positioned to influence the trajectory of world modeling research by advocating for an agent-centric perspective, providing a structured framework for its development, and highlighting critical future directions for building truly continually improving intelligent agents.
