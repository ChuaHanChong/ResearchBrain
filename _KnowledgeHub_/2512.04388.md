# Learning to Orchestrate Agents in Natural Language with the Conductor

> [!summary] Summary
> Sakana AI introduces the "Conductor," a 7B LLM trained with end-to-end reinforcement learning to orchestrate diverse worker LLMs by generating natural language-based agentic workflows. This framework achieved state-of-the-art results on challenging reasoning benchmarks, including 83.93% on LiveCodeBench and 87.5% on GPQA-Diamond, significantly outperforming individual frontier models and prior multi-agent systems.

## Problem

- Manually designing optimal prompt engineering strategies and multi-agent workflows for complex tasks is time-consuming and challenging.
- Effectively leveraging the specialized strengths of diverse Large Language Models (LLMs) is difficult, as no single model is universally optimal.
- Existing multi-agent coordination approaches often rely on human-designed scaffolds, predefined routing, or fixed tool-use patterns, limiting flexibility and adaptivity.

## Method

- A 7B LLM, named **Conductor**, is trained using end-to-end reinforcement learning (==GRPO== algorithm) to generate agentic workflows in natural language, specifying worker LLMs, subtasks, and communication patterns.
- The Conductor orchestrates a diverse pool of seven powerful worker LLMs (both proprietary and open-source) by dynamically decomposing problems and delegating targeted subtasks with tailored instructions.
- The framework allows for the emergence of flexible communication topologies, including recursive self-invocation, enabling dynamic refinement of coordination strategies and adaptive resource allocation at test time.

## Results

- The **7B** Conductor achieved state-of-the-art performance on several challenging reasoning benchmarks, including **83.93%** on LiveCodeBench and **87.5%** on GPQA-Diamond, significantly surpassing individual frontier LLMs and previous multi-agent baselines.
- It demonstrated efficiency by constructing effective workflows with an average of approximately 3 steps, leading to competitive cost efficiency and often lower token usage compared to multi-agent baselines.
- The Conductor proved adaptable to arbitrary agent pools, maintaining high performance even when restricted to only open-source models, where it outperformed Claude Sonnet 4 by nearly **10%** in that constrained setting.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - End-to-end reinforcement learning can enable an LLM to learn highly effective, flexible, and efficient natural language orchestration strategies for coordinating other LLMs.
> - The ability to dynamically generate specific subtasks and design communication topologies is crucial for an orchestrator to effectively leverage the complementary strengths of a diverse worker pool.
> - Recursive self-invocation, where the orchestrator can call itself as a worker, provides a new mechanism for adaptive test-time scaling and further performance gains by refining solutions or adjusting strategies.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of Large Language Models (LLMs) has led to models with advanced capabilities across diverse domains. However, effectively leveraging these models, especially in complex tasks, remains challenging. This challenge is partly due to the difficulty in designing optimal prompt engineering strategies and multi-agent workflows manually, and the inherent specialization of different LLMs, meaning no single model is universally optimal.

This work builds upon two interconnected areas of contemporary LLM research:
- **Reinforcement Learning (RL) for Reasoning:** Recent advancements in LLM performance, particularly in reasoning tasks, have been significantly driven by RL-based post-training paradigms. Approaches like the DeepSeek R1 line of work utilize RL to align LLMs with self-emergent thinking capabilities, often by training them to generate reasoning traces alongside solutions. This paper extends this concept by applying RL not just to individual model reasoning but to the orchestration of multiple models.
- **Multi-Agent Coordination and Tool Use:** The increasing power of individual LLMs has spurred research into coordinating groups of agents to tackle more complex problems. Prior work in multi-agent coordination has explored various strategies, including:
 - **Hand-designed topological and prompt-based scaffolds:** These approaches manually define workflows and communication patterns for agents.
 - **Learned routing mechanisms:** Methods that learn embedding spaces to map queries to agents or predefined topologies.
 - **Learnable graphs:** Frameworks that represent collaboration as an optimizable graph structure.
 - **RL with tools:** Research has also integrated RL with tool-use, enabling LLMs to interact with external systems like code interpreters, web browsers, or specialized computation modules to enhance their capabilities.

The "Conductor" model presented in this paper positions itself as a departure from these existing multi-agent coordination approaches. Instead of relying on human-designed scaffolds, predefined routing options, or fixed tool-use patterns, the Conductor is an LLM trained with end-to-end RL to *learn* to design flexible, natural-language-based agentic workflows. This framework aims to allow powerful coordination strategies, including prompt engineering and communication topology design, to emerge organically from reward maximization, offering a higher degree of adaptivity and less reliance on human intervention in orchestration. The work proposes a new extension to the RL with tools paradigm, where the "tool" is the ability to delegate tasks to other LLMs via API calls and structure their collaboration.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind the "Conductor" research are multifaceted, addressing current limitations in LLM utilization and multi-agent systems:

- **Overcoming Manual Orchestration Challenges:** Current advanced AI applications often rely on complex, manually designed agentic workflows and sophisticated prompt engineering. This manual effort is time-consuming, requires expert knowledge, and may not optimally leverage the specific strengths of diverse LLMs. The motivation is to automate this process.
- **Leveraging Specialized LLMs:** Different LLMs are often fine-tuned for specialized domains or tasks, meaning no single model excels universally. The goal is to develop a system that can effectively identify and combine the complementary strengths of a pool of powerful, specialized worker LLMs to achieve superior performance on complex problems.
- **Dynamic Problem Decomposition and Task Delegation:** Challenging problems often benefit from decomposition into smaller, manageable subtasks. The objective is to enable an LLM to dynamically break down an input problem, intelligently delegate these targeted subtasks to the most appropriate worker agents, and provide clear natural language instructions.
- **Learning Optimal Communication Topologies:** Beyond simple delegation, effective multi-agent collaboration requires structured communication. The research aims to train the Conductor to learn to design flexible communication strategies and topologies (e.g., sequential, parallel, tree-structured) that optimize information flow and collaboration among worker agents, adapting to the specific demands of each problem.
- **Achieving State-of-the-Art Performance:** A key motivation is to demonstrate that an RL-trained orchestrator can achieve performance gains on challenging reasoning benchmarks that surpass both individual frontier LLMs and existing multi-agent baselines, indicating a new frontier in collective intelligence for language models.
- **Enhancing Adaptability and Robustness:** The study aims to make the orchestration system robust to variations in the available worker pool (e.g., a mix of open-source and closed-source models, or specific cost constraints). This includes developing methods for the Conductor to adapt its strategies to arbitrary sets of agents.
- **Introducing Dynamic Test-Time Scaling:** To explore novel ways of enhancing model capabilities during inference, the research aims to introduce a mechanism for recursive self-invocation, allowing the Conductor to dynamically refine its coordination strategy or allocate additional compute based on intermediate results, leading to a new form of adaptable test-time scaling.
- **Validating End-to-End RL for Coordination:** A broader motivation is to provide early evidence that powerful, emergent coordination strategies can arise in LLMs through a pure end-to-end reinforcement learning approach, where the "policy" is the natural language output defining the workflow.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology centers on training a specialized Large Language Model, termed the "Conductor," using reinforcement learning to orchestrate a pool of other powerful LLMs (worker agents).

**Conductor Model and Task Framing:**
- The Conductor itself is a 7B parameter LLM, initialized from a Qwen2.5 checkpoint.
- Its primary task is to solve problems by generating "agentic workflows." Each workflow is a sequence of up to five steps, outputted as three Python lists:
 - `model id`: A list of integers corresponding to the worker agents assigned to each step.
 - `subtasks`: A list of natural language instructions for each assigned worker.
 - `access list`: A list of lists specifying which previous subtask solutions (or `“all”` for all previous outputs, or `[]` for none) are included in the current worker's context.
- This natural language-based output grants the Conductor flexibility in designing tailored subtasks, defining communication patterns, and specifying various collaboration topologies (e.g., sequential, parallel, refinement loops).
- The Conductor is provided with detailed instructions in its system prompt, along with few-shot examples of expected output formats, to accelerate initial learning and ensure parseable outputs.

**Workflow Execution and Learning Dynamics:**
- Once the Conductor outputs a workflow, it is executed sequentially. Each specified worker agent is prompted with its assigned subtask, and its context is constructed based on the `access list` using a conversational template.
- **Reinforcement Learning (RL):** The Conductor is trained using a variant of the GRPO (Grouped Policy Optimization) algorithm.
 - **Reward Function:** Rewards ($r_i$) are determined by two conditions:
 1. **Conductor Format Condition:** $r_i = 0$ if the Conductor's output (Python lists) cannot be parsed.
 2. **Conductor Correctness Condition:** $r_i = 1$ if the final output from the executed agentic workflow matches the ground truth solution ($s_i$); otherwise, $r_i = 0.5$.
 - **Training Configuration:** The Conductor is trained for 200 GRPO iterations, with a small batch size (4 questions per iteration, 64 rollouts per question), using the AdamW optimizer. No KL regularization or reference model synchronization is used.
- **Worker Pool:** The Conductor orchestrates a diverse pool of seven worker LLMs, including powerful proprietary models (Gemini-2.5-Pro, Claude-Sonnet-4, GPT-5) and open-source alternatives (DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-32B, Gemma3-27B-instruct, Qwen3-32B). All workers are configured with a max completion length of 4096 tokens and a decoding temperature of 0.2.
- **Training Dataset:** The training dataset consists of 960 problems across four reasoning domains: MATH (500), MMLU, RLPR, and LiveCodeBench V1, selected for their difficulty and diversity.

**Extensions to the RL Conductor Framework:**
- **Adaptive Worker Selection:** To enhance robustness to varying worker availability, a pretrained Conductor is finetuned by restricting it to randomly sampled $k$-model subsets from the total $n$ workers for each training step. This enables generalization to custom worker pools (e.g., using only open-source models).
- **Recursive Topologies and Test-time Scaling:** The framework is extended to allow the Conductor to specify *itself* as a worker, creating recursive agentic workflows. In recursive calls, the Conductor receives its parent output and the previous agent's response, deciding whether to instantiate a new workflow or return the final solution. This capability is unlocked by finetuning the pretrained Conductor, manually instantiating a single recursion call for half the samples in each batch, and setting a maximum recursion depth to prevent infinite loops.

**Evaluation Setup:**
- **Benchmarks:** Evaluation is conducted on both in-domain test sets (unseen questions from MATH500, MMLU, RLPR, LiveCodeBench V6) and out-of-domain benchmarks (GPQA Diamond, BigCodeBench, AIME25) to assess generalization.
- **Baselines:** The Conductor's performance is compared against:
 - Individual worker LLMs (with varying context lengths and self-reflection strategies).
 - Prior multi-agent routing and coordination strategies (MAS-Router, Mixture-of-Agents (MoA), RouterDC, Smoothie).
- **Evaluation Settings:** Experiments are performed in "unconstrained" (maximum reasoning and output token budgets) and "constrained" (4096 output tokens, minimal reasoning budgets) settings.
- **Ablation Studies:** Various ablations are conducted to analyze the Conductor's properties, including the impact of Conductor scale (3B vs. 7B), task adaptivity, the necessity of subtask generation, agent selection choices (e.g., fixing to a single powerful agent), the effect of few-shot examples, and replacing the trained Conductor with a frontier LLM acting as an orchestrator.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded several key findings regarding the capabilities and performance of the RL Conductor:

- **State-of-the-Art Performance:** A 7B Conductor attained state-of-the-art results on several challenging reasoning benchmarks, including LiveCodeBench and GPQA Diamond, significantly surpassing the performance of individual frontier LLMs (e.g., GPT-5, Gemini 2.5 Pro, Claude Sonnet 4) and previous best "unconstrained" results. For example, it achieved 83.93% on LiveCodeBench and 87.5% on GPQA-Diamond, demonstrating substantial improvements across diverse domains like math, coding, and natural science.
- **Outperformance of Multi-Agent Baselines:** In controlled evaluations, the Conductor considerably outperformed established multi-turn self-reflection strategies and prior multi-agent collaboration baselines (e.g., MAS-Router, Mixture-of-Agents, RouterDC, Smoothie). This was achieved despite many baselines using a larger number of agent calls, highlighting the efficiency and effectiveness of the Conductor's learned coordination.
- **Efficiency:** The Conductor demonstrated efficiency by learning to construct effective agentic workflows with an average of approximately 3 steps, remaining well below its allocated limit without specific regularization. This often translated to lower token usage and competitive cost efficiency compared to multi-agent baselines and even popular inference-time scaling techniques like 5x consensus sampling.
- **Adaptability to Arbitrary Agent Pools:** Finetuning with randomized agent pools allowed the Conductor to generalize to custom sets of open- and closed-source workers. When restricted to only open-source models, the finetuned Conductor effectively combined their capabilities, outperforming Claude Sonnet 4 by nearly 10% in this constrained setting. It also maintained its original state-of-the-art performance when restricted to only closed-source models.
- **Emergence of Recursive Topologies and Test-time Scaling:** By enabling the Conductor to specify itself as a worker, recursive agentic workflows emerged. This recursive variant achieved additional performance gains, particularly notable on the BigCodeBench, where it surpassed the non-recursive Conductor. Analysis showed the recursive Conductor could adaptively redistribute agent selection (e.g., favoring Claude 4 and Gemini 2.5 over GPT-5 on BigCodeBench when GPT-5 performed suboptimally), demonstrating dynamic adjustment of coordination strategies.
- **Conductor Scale and Prompt Engineering:** Comparing a 7B Conductor with a 3B variant revealed that while both learned similar agent selection distributions, the 7B model achieved higher performance. This difference was attributed to the larger model's superior prompt engineering skills, enabling it to craft more intelligent and focused subtasks.
- **Task and Difficulty Adaptivity:** The Conductor exhibited emergent behavior of dynamically allocating computational resources based on problem difficulty. For complex tasks like LiveCodeBench, it designed workflows with more granular subtasks and more agents (e.g., planning, implementation, verification). For simpler tasks like MMLU, it often produced simpler, one- or two-step workflows.
- **Ablation Study Insights:**
 - Removing the ability to generate specific subtasks (i.e., using a uniform "Solve the user question" prompt) led to consistent performance drops, particularly on complex coding tasks, confirming the importance of learned prompt engineering.
 - Fixing all agents to a single powerful model (GPT-5) resulted in lower performance than the full Conductor, validating that the Conductor effectively leverages the diverse and complementary capabilities of its worker pool.
 - Replacing the RL-trained Conductor with powerful frontier models (GPT-5, Gemini 2.5 Pro) instructed to act as orchestrators showed that while these models could perform some coordination, the specifically trained Conductor still achieved superior results, underscoring the value of the RL training.
 - Providing few-shot examples from out-of-distribution tasks significantly boosted performance, suggesting that these examples encourage exploration of coordination strategies rather than mere exploitation of templates.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "Conductor" model represents a notable advancement in the field of large language model coordination and multi-agent AI, with several potential implications:

- **Paradigm Shift in Multi-Agent System Design:** The work introduces a new paradigm for designing multi-agent systems, shifting from manually engineered scaffolds and predefined routing rules to an end-to-end reinforcement learning approach where an LLM learns to orchestrate others using natural language. This could lead to more flexible, adaptive, and autonomous AI systems.
- **Unlocking Collective Intelligence of LLMs:** By effectively coordinating diverse and specialized LLMs, the Conductor demonstrates a method to amplify their individual capabilities, pushing the boundaries of what LLMs can achieve on complex reasoning tasks. This "collective intelligence" approach suggests a path for future LLM systems to tackle problems beyond the scope of any single model.
- **Enhanced Practicality and Efficiency:** The Conductor's ability to achieve state-of-the-art performance with high efficiency, often at a lower cost than many baselines, enhances the practical deployability of advanced LLM solutions. Its adaptability to varying worker pools (e.g., favoring open-source models when cost is a concern) makes it a versatile tool for diverse user requirements.
- **Novel Axis for Test-Time Scaling:** The introduction of recursive topologies provides a new, tunable axis for inference-time scaling. This allows AI systems to dynamically allocate additional computational effort and iteratively refine solutions in response to problem complexity or unexpected outcomes, potentially increasing robustness and accuracy in real-world applications.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** By open-sourcing its codebase, this work provides a framework for future research. This could include exploring larger Conductor models, more complex coordination topologies, and integrating workers with expertise in modalities beyond text (e.g., vision, robotics, scientific simulation). Such extensions could enable the Conductor to address more ambitious challenges in interdisciplinary fields.
- **Implications for Prompt Engineering:** The emergence of sophisticated prompt engineering capabilities within the RL-trained Conductor highlights the potential for AI to automate and optimize the design of instructions for other AI systems, reducing the reliance on human prompt engineers for complex tasks.
- **Ethical Considerations:** The paper acknowledges that the reliance on expensive frontier LLMs could exacerbate existing economic divides in AI access. However, the Conductor's adaptability to open-source models offers a potential mitigation strategy, allowing for high performance even with cost-constrained or custom agent pools.
