# Learning to Orchestrate Agents in Natural Language with the Conductor

> [!summary] Summary
> Sakana AI introduces the "Conductor," a 7B LLM trained with end-to-end reinforcement learning to orchestrate diverse worker LLMs by generating natural language-based agentic workflows. This framework achieved state-of-the-art results on challenging reasoning benchmarks, including 83.93% on LiveCodeBench and 87.5% on GPQA-Diamond, significantly outperforming individual frontier models and prior multi-agent systems.

## Problem

- Manually designing optimal prompt engineering strategies and multi-agent workflows for complex tasks is time-consuming and challenging.
- Effectively leveraging the specialized strengths of diverse Large Language Models (LLMs) is difficult, as no single model is universally optimal.
- Existing multi-agent coordination approaches often rely on human-designed scaffolds, predefined routing, or fixed tool-use patterns, limiting flexibility and adaptivity.

## Method

- A 7B LLM, named **Conductor**, is trained using end-to-end reinforcement learning (==GRPO== algorithm) to generate agentic workflows in natural language, specifying worker LLMs, subtasks, and communication patterns.
- The Conductor orchestrates a diverse pool of seven powerful worker LLMs (both proprietary and open-source) by dynamically decomposing problems and delegating targeted subtasks with tailored instructions.
- The framework allows for the emergence of flexible communication topologies, including recursive self-invocation, enabling dynamic refinement of coordination strategies and adaptive resource allocation at test time.

## Results

- The **7B** Conductor achieved state-of-the-art performance on several challenging reasoning benchmarks, including **83.93%** on LiveCodeBench and **87.5%** on GPQA-Diamond, significantly surpassing individual frontier LLMs and previous multi-agent baselines.
- It demonstrated efficiency by constructing effective workflows with an average of approximately 3 steps, leading to competitive cost efficiency and often lower token usage compared to multi-agent baselines.
- The Conductor proved adaptable to arbitrary agent pools, maintaining high performance even when restricted to only open-source models, where it outperformed Claude Sonnet 4 by nearly **10%** in that constrained setting.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - End-to-end reinforcement learning can enable an LLM to learn highly effective, flexible, and efficient natural language orchestration strategies for coordinating other LLMs.
> - The ability to dynamically generate specific subtasks and design communication topologies is crucial for an orchestrator to effectively leverage the complementary strengths of a diverse worker pool.
> - Recursive self-invocation, where the orchestrator can call itself as a worker, provides a new mechanism for adaptive test-time scaling and further performance gains by refining solutions or adjusting strategies.
