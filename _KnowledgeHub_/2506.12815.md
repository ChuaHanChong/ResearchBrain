# TrojanTO: Action-Level Backdoor Attacks against Trajectory Optimization Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers developed TrojanTO, an action-level backdoor attack framework that effectively injects malicious behaviors into Trajectory Optimization models for offline reinforcement learning, achieving high attack success rates with minimal data poisoning while preserving benign task performance. This work highlights a new vulnerability in TO models, which are immune to traditional reward-manipulation backdoor attacks due to their sequence modeling objective.

## Problem

- Trajectory Optimization (TO) models in offline reinforcement learning, widely used in control tasks like robotics, have largely uninvestigated security vulnerabilities.
- Existing backdoor attacks for traditional RL agents rely on reward manipulation, a method ineffective against TO models which minimize reconstruction loss rather than maximizing rewards.
- Achieving precise action-level manipulation in high-dimensional continuous action spaces for specific malicious behaviors is technically challenging.

## Method

- Introduces **TrojanTO**, an ==action-level backdoor== attack framework designed specifically for Trajectory Optimization models (e.g., Decision Transformer, Decision ConvFormer).
- Employs trajectory filtering to select high-quality data, ensuring the backdoored model maintains benign task performance.
- Uses batch poisoning to stealthily inject triggers and target actions into training data, and an alternating training scheme to co-optimize the trigger and model parameters effectively.

## Results

- TrojanTO achieved an average composite performance (CP) of **0.701**, significantly outperforming baseline attacks (Baffle **0.342** CP, IMC **0.551** CP) across various D4RL environments and TO model architectures.
- The attack demonstrated high efficiency and stealth, achieving an Attack Success Rate (ASR) of **0.719** with a poisoning rate as low as **0.3%** of trajectories while maintaining a Benign Task Performance (BTP) of **0.914**.
- Ablation studies confirmed that each component of TrojanTO (Trajectory Filtering, Batch Poisoning, Alternating Training) critically contributed to the attack's overall effectiveness, efficiency, and stealth.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Trajectory Optimization models are fundamentally immune to traditional reward-manipulation-based backdoor attacks due to their sequence modeling objective and reconstruction loss optimization.
> - The choice of target action (e.g., boundary actions) and the design of learnable triggers are critical factors for successfully implanting action-level backdoors in TO models.
> - A combination of data quality control, partial data poisoning, and alternating optimization can enable effective and stealthy backdoor implantation in continuous, high-dimensional action spaces.
