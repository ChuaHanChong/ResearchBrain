# ST-$π$: Structured SpatioTemporal VLA for Robotic Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers developed ST-π, a framework for robotic manipulation that explicitly structures spatiotemporal reasoning at both chunk-level task decomposition and step-level action generation. This approach enabled higher success rates and faster completion times on complex, long-horizon tasks across simulation benchmarks like LIBERO and real-world scenarios using the new STAR dataset.

## Problem

- Existing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models rely on implicit spatiotemporal reasoning, limiting their effectiveness in fine-grained manipulation for long-horizon, multi-stage tasks.
- Challenges in accurately modeling causal dependencies and smooth transitions between sub-tasks in complex, sequential robotic behaviors.
- A lack of explicit structured spatiotemporal modeling at both high-level planning and low-level action execution, leading to instability in extended trajectories.

## Method

- **ST-π** introduces a ==Spatiotemporal Vision-Language Model (ST-VLM)== that decomposes tasks into ==causally ordered chunk-level action prompts==, each with semantic, spatial, and temporal attributes.
- A ==Spatiotemporal Action Expert (ST-AE)== generates step-level actions using a ==dual-generator guidance mechanism==, jointly modeling spatial dependencies (==bidirectional attention==) and temporal causality (==causal attention==).
- A new real-world dataset, **STAR** (Spatiotemporal Task Annotation for Robotics), was created with detailed ==structured spatiotemporal annotations== to facilitate training and evaluation of complex manipulation policies.

## Results

- ST-π achieved the highest success rates and shortest completion times on most tasks across four LIBERO benchmark suites and demonstrated competitive performance on the SIMPLER benchmark, surpassing other VLA approaches.
- On the real-world STAR dataset, ST-π showed the highest average success rate across all evaluation suites, with performance gains becoming more pronounced in long-horizon manipulation tasks.
- Ablation studies confirmed that both the ST-VLM for structured task decomposition and the ST-AE with its dual-generator guidance contributed positively to the overall performance, and the causal attention structure for task decomposition was superior for stable planning.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly structuring spatiotemporal modeling at both the planning (chunk) and execution (step) levels significantly enhances stability and coherence for long-horizon robotic manipulation.
> - Integrating distinct spatial and temporal motion generators with complementary attention mechanisms (bidirectional for spatial, causal for temporal) effectively produces both spatially smooth and temporally stable trajectories.
> - Utilizing full 4D observations (multi-frame images + geometric features) is crucial for comprehensive spatiotemporal perception, outperforming simpler 3D or 2D modalities.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation has seen significant advancements with the emergence of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, which aim to integrate perception, language reasoning, and action generation into a unified framework. Early VLA models, often end-to-end architectures, encode images, text, and actions into shared representations, leveraging large-scale multimodal pretraining for various tasks. More recent developments include hierarchical VLA models that combine high-level reasoning with low-level control, seeking to enhance performance in complex scenarios.

However, a persistent challenge for these models lies in fine-grained spatiotemporal manipulation, particularly for long-horizon tasks composed of multiple sub-tasks with explicit spatial and temporal boundaries. Many conventional VLA frameworks predominantly encode geometric cues and underutilize temporal information. This reliance on static or primarily 3D spatial representations often leads to difficulties in capturing dynamic scene understanding and coherent action execution over time.

To address this, a subset of research has begun exploring 4D VLA models, which aim to integrate temporal dimensions into both visual inputs and action representations. Approaches like Zhang et al. (2025) augmented visual inputs with historical frame sampling to construct 4D observations, thereby enhancing spatiotemporal perception. Zhou et al. (2025) further extended temporal modeling to the action space by embedding temporal information into action representations, promoting more coherent action execution. While these 4D VLA methods introduce temporal awareness, their spatiotemporal reasoning typically remains implicit, limiting their effectiveness in multi-stage, long-horizon tasks where explicit causal dependencies and transitions between sub-tasks are crucial.

Concurrently, advances in large multimodal models (LMMs) have improved spatiotemporal reasoning over dynamic scenes, extending visual-language modeling to include geometry cues and temporal dynamics for tasks like temporally consistent grounding and cross-frame reasoning. However, these 4D LMMs primarily focus on perception-oriented understanding rather than structured reasoning for embodied robotic manipulation.

ST-𝜋 positions itself within this landscape by identifying a gap: the need for *explicitly structured* spatiotemporal modeling in VLA systems for fine-grained, long-horizon robotic manipulation. It moves beyond implicit 4D representations by proposing a framework that explicitly decomposes high-level instructions into causally ordered sub-tasks with clear spatiotemporal boundaries and refines step-level actions with explicit spatial coherence and temporal causality. This approach aims to provide a more robust and stable solution for complex, multi-stage manipulation scenarios compared to existing implicit 4D VLA or perception-focused 4D LMMs.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this work is to develop spatiotemporal models capable of performing fine-grained robotic manipulation, particularly for long-horizon tasks that involve multiple sequential behaviors with explicit spatiotemporal boundaries.

The motivation stems from the limitations observed in existing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models:
1. **Implicit Spatiotemporal Reasoning:** Current VLA methods typically embed spatiotemporal knowledge into visual and action representations and perform cross-modal mapping for step-level action prediction. However, this reasoning is largely implicit, making it difficult for the models to effectively handle complex tasks composed of multiple sequential behaviors that have distinct spatiotemporal boundaries.
2. **Limitations in Long-Horizon Tasks:** While implicit spatiotemporal representations may suffice for short-horizon coherence, they often fail to adequately model the causal dependencies and transitions required for stable and consistent execution in long-horizon scenarios. This can lead to spatiotemporal chaos in extended trajectories and instability in later stages of a task.
3. **Lack of Explicit Structure:** The paper argues that fine-grained manipulation necessitates explicit and structured spatiotemporal modeling at two distinct levels:
 - **Chunk-level Planning:** Complex tasks are viewed as sequences of sub-tasks, each possessing distinct spatial and temporal boundaries, and exhibiting causal dependencies between them. Explicitly modeling these dependencies and boundaries is crucial for structured task decomposition.
 - **Step-level Execution:** Within a single sub-task, individual action steps demonstrate inherent spatial coherence and temporal causality. Explicitly guiding action generation along both spatial and temporal dimensions is necessary to ensure spatial smoothness and temporal stability during execution.

To address these motivations, the paper sets forth several specific objectives:
- To propose ST-𝜋, a unified framework that explicitly structures both chunk-level task decomposition and step-level action generation, thereby enabling fine-grained manipulation.
- To design a Spatiotemporal Vision-Language Model (ST-VLM) for behavior planning. This ST-VLM is intended to predict causally ordered sub-tasks, represented as "chunk-level action prompts" that include semantic, spatial, and temporal attributes.
- To introduce a Spatiotemporal Action Expert (ST-AE), which utilizes a structured dual-generator guidance mechanism. This expert is designed to jointly model spatial dependencies and temporal causality, predicting step-level action parameters to ensure spatially coherent and temporally consistent action generation.
- To construct a real-world robotic dataset, named STAR, specifically designed with structured spatiotemporal annotations. This dataset is intended to facilitate the fine-tuning and evaluation of models in complex manipulation scenarios.

By explicitly modeling spatiotemporal structure at both high-level planning and low-level execution, ST-𝜋 aims to overcome the limitations of implicit reasoning, leading to more coherent, stable, and effective robotic manipulation for complex, long-horizon tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The ST-𝜋 framework is designed to address fine-grained robotic manipulation through explicit structured spatiotemporal modeling, comprising two main components: a Spatiotemporal Vision-Language Model (ST-VLM) for chunk-level task decomposition and a Spatiotemporal Action Expert (ST-AE) for step-level action generation.

**Overall Framework:**
Given a sequence of multi-view observations $I_t$ (with $W$ frames) and a high-level language instruction $L$, ST-𝜋 first uses the ST-VLM to decompose the task into a sequence of chunk-level action prompts $\{p_k\}_{k=1}^K$. Subsequently, the ST-AE generates executable action parameters conditioned on these prompts.

#### 3.1. Spatiotemporal Vision-Language Model (ST-VLM)

The ST-VLM performs structured chunk-level task decomposition by explicitly representing sub-tasks as action prompts with semantic, spatial, and temporal information, and modeling inter-chunk dependencies.

- **Structured 4D Representation Construction:**
 - Visual features ($f_v$) are extracted from the multi-view observations using a SigLIP-based vision encoder (VE).
 - Geometric features ($f_g$) are extracted using a geometry encoder (GE) adapted from VGGT. These features capture appearance semantics and spatial structure, respectively.
 - Temporal contexts are incorporated by encoding timestamps ($t$) into temporal embeddings using Fourier positional encoding ($\phi(\cdot)$).
 - A spatiotemporal fusion module (STE) combines these features: $f_{4D} = \text{STE}(f_v, f_g, t)$, creating a unified 4D representation. In practice, this is implemented as a linear layer on the concatenated features: $f_{4D} = w_F [f_v || f_g || \phi(t)]$. This 4D representation provides explicit spatiotemporal grounding for task decomposition.

- **Structured Planning with Action Prompts:**
 - The ST-VLM autoregressively predicts a sequence of chunk-level action prompts $\{p_k\}_{k=1}^K$ within a short planning horizon $K$, conditioned on $f_{4D}$ and $L$. The model follows a rolling-horizon scheme, re-planning after each sub-task completion.
 - Each action prompt $p_k$ is composed of three elements:
 - **Semantic tokens ($s_k$):** $M$ learnable query tokens capturing the semantic intent, supervised by the textual description of the sub-task.
 - **Spatial tokens ($x_k$):** Encode the target execution region (e.g., bounding box coordinates).
 - **Temporal tokens ($\tau_k$):** Encode the expected duration of the sub-task.
 - **Attention Mechanism:** All tokens (semantic, spatial, temporal) attend to the 4D representations to extract spatiotemporal context. Spatial and temporal tokens further attend to semantic tokens to explicitly model execution constraints.
 - **Causal Dependencies:** A block-wise causal attention structure models dependencies across action prompts, where semantic tokens of prompt $p_k$ attend to all preceding prompts $p_{<k}$, masking future prompts to enforce causally ordered task decomposition.

#### 3.2. Spatiotemporal Action Expert (ST-AE)

The ST-AE generates action chunks for each sub-task using a flow-matching process, conditioned on the chunk-level action prompts.

- **Action Chunk Structure:** Each action chunk $A_k$ consists of $H$ action steps, $A_k = \{a^{(i)}_k\}_{i=1}^H$. Each action step $a^{(i)}_k$ includes translational motion ($\Delta x$), rotational motion ($\Delta \theta$), gripper motion ($g$), and step duration ($\Delta t$).

- **Dual-Generator Spatiotemporal Guidance:** Action generation is guided by two complementary motion generators that share a backbone network but differ in architectural constraints and conditioning features:
 - **Spatial Motion Generator (SG):** Conditions on semantic ($s_k$) and spatial ($x_k$) tokens. It employs a *bidirectional attention constraint* to action tokens, allowing all action steps within a chunk to attend to each other. This captures global spatial structures and promotes coordinated updates for smooth trajectories, producing spatial update flows ($v_s$).
 - **Temporal Motion Generator (TG):** Conditions on semantic ($s_k$) and temporal ($\tau_k$) tokens. It uses a *causal attention constraint* to action tokens, where each step is generated conditioned only on its preceding steps. This captures step-wise temporal dependencies and enforces sequential consistency, producing temporal update flows ($v_t$).

- **Dual-Generator Action Generation:** A time-dependent fusion strategy combines the outputs of the two generators:
 - $v_\tau = \alpha_\tau v_t + (1 - \alpha_\tau)v_s$, where $\alpha_\tau = \tau/T$ (current step $\tau$, total denoising steps $T$). This strategy ensures that spatial shaping dominates in the early phase of generation (establishing coarse trajectory structure), while temporal refinement becomes dominant in the later phase (fine-tuning individual steps for causal dependencies).
 - The fused flow ($v_\tau$) guides the refinement of the action chunk $A_k$ through an iterative denoising process: $\hat{A}_k = A_k + v_\tau \Delta \tau$. Starting from an initial Gaussian noise sample, the action chunk is iteratively refined to generate spatially coherent and temporally consistent trajectories.

#### 3.3. Optimization

The framework is optimized end-to-end with a weighted sum of two objectives:
- **Structured Task Planning Loss ($L_{VLM}$):** Combines a language modeling loss for semantic tokens, an L1 spatial regression loss for spatial tokens (target region), and an L1 temporal regression loss for temporal tokens (execution duration).
- **Dual-generator Action Generation Loss ($L_{AE}$):** A flow-matching objective where the fused flow $v$ from the ST-AE is trained to match a target update flow $u_{\omega_k}$, which is derived from interpolating between a ground-truth action chunk $A^*_k$ and Gaussian noise $\omega$.

#### 3.4. Training Datasets and Pipeline

The model is trained using three complementary datasets in a three-stage procedure:
- **ScanNet series (ScanNet, ScanRefer, Scan2Cap):** Used in Stage 1 for representation alignment, focusing on spatial grounding and aligning geometric features with visual representations. Only spatial regression loss is applied, with frozen VLM backbone, vision, and geometry encoders.
- **DROID-ST:** A structured task decomposition dataset derived from DROID demonstrations, providing sub-task descriptions, bounding boxes, durations, and action segments. Used in Stage 2 for behavior planning learning (training ST-VLM with language modeling and temporal regression losses) and in Stage 3 for end-to-end fine-tuning.
- **STAR (Spatiotemporal Task Annotation for Robotics):** A newly constructed real-world dataset on a Franka Research 3 platform. It includes 30 manipulation tasks with 50 demonstrations each (~300k steps), collected via teleoperation with multi-view images, depth, language instructions, and robot proprioceptive states. Tasks are segmented into sub-tasks with semantic, spatial, and temporal annotations. Suites include Object Recognition, Sequential Goal, and Long-Horizon tasks. This dataset is crucial for real-world fine-tuning and evaluation.

The three-stage training recipe progressively enhances spatiotemporal perception, structured task planning, and robotic manipulation policy.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted demonstrate the effectiveness of ST-𝜋 across both simulation benchmarks and real-world robotic tasks, with specific findings related to its structured spatiotemporal framework, attention mechanisms, observation modalities, and the role of its dual generators.

#### 4.1. Comparison on Simulation Benchmarks (LIBERO and SIMPLER)

- **LIBERO Benchmark:** ST-𝜋 achieved the highest success rates and the shortest completion times on most tasks across four LIBERO suites (Spatial, Object Goal, Long, Average). This performance surpassed other representative VLA approaches including OpenVLA, Octo, SpatialVLA, TraceVLA, 4D-VLA, CogACT, and $\pi_{0.5}$. The results indicate that incorporating 4D spatiotemporal observations and dual-system architectures (high-level planning and low-level action generation) generally improves performance. ST-𝜋's advantage is attributed to its explicit modeling of spatiotemporal sub-task dependencies, which is critical for long-horizon manipulation.
- **SIMPLER Benchmark:** On the SIMPLER benchmark, designed for scalable evaluation and real-to-sim generalization, ST-𝜋 demonstrated competitive performance across both Visual Matching and Variant Aggregation protocols. It showed effectiveness in tasks like "Pick Coke Can," "Move Near," and "Open/Close Drawer," suggesting efficient execution in diverse simulation environments and robustness to visual distribution shifts and environment variations.

#### 4.2. Comparison on Real-World STAR Dataset

- Real-world experiments on a Franka Research 3 platform, using the newly constructed STAR dataset, validated the practical applicability of ST-𝜋. The model achieved the highest average success rate across all three evaluation suites (Object Recognition, Sequential Goal, Long-Horizon).
- The performance gap between ST-𝜋 and baselines (OpenVLA, CogACT, SpatialVLA, $\pi_{0.5}$) became more pronounced in long-horizon manipulation scenarios, where precise behavior transitions and robust task decomposition are essential for planning. This indicates that explicit structured spatiotemporal modeling significantly enhances execution stability and robustness in real-world robotic environments.

#### 4.3. Ablation Study

- **Effect of Structured Spatiotemporal Framework:** An ablation study demonstrated that both components of the ST-𝜋 framework—the Spatiotemporal VLM (ST-VLM) for structured task decomposition and the Spatiotemporal Action Expert (ST-AE) with its motion generators—contribute positively to overall performance. Combining both yielded the best results, affirming their complementary roles: ST-VLM improves long-horizon stability by modeling chunk-level dependencies, while ST-AE enhances action consistency through spatial coherence and temporal causality.
- **Ablation on High-Level Spatiotemporal Attention Structure:** The causal attention structure used in ST-VLM for sub-task decomposition was found to be superior to bidirectional attention or no inter-chunk attention. The causal structure, which enforces temporal ordering during decomposition, consistently produced the best performance, highlighting the necessity of explicitly modeling causal dependencies for stable planning and coherent long-horizon execution.
- **Ablation on Observation Modality:** The study showed that the full 4D observation (multi-frame images + geometric features) yielded the highest performance. Performance consistently degraded when observations were simplified to 3D (single-frame image + geometric features) or 2D (single-frame image only). This indicates the importance of jointly modeling appearance, geometry, and temporal context for robotic manipulation.

#### 4.4. Discussion

- **Role of Spatiotemporal Generators:** Analysis of the spatial and temporal motion generators within ST-AE revealed their distinct and complementary functions. The spatial-only variant produced smooth trajectories but with irregular step sizes, leading to unstable temporal progression. The temporal-only variant generated trajectories with consistent velocities but lacked spatial smoothness, resulting in jagged motion. The full model, integrating both generators, produced trajectories that were both spatially smooth and temporally stable, showcasing the benefit of their combined inductive biases.
- **Effect of Task Decomposition Granularity:** Varying the number of predicted sub-tasks ($K$) showed that a moderate decomposition granularity ($K=3$) achieved optimal performance. Overly coarse decomposition ($K=1$) hindered task structure, while excessively fine-grained decomposition ($K=4$) slightly decreased performance, potentially due to increased prediction noise or insufficient training for deeper decomposition levels.

In summary, ST-𝜋 demonstrates consistent and notable improvements in robotic manipulation tasks, particularly for complex, long-horizon scenarios, by explicitly integrating structured spatiotemporal reasoning into both planning and execution. The empirical results support the hypothesis that explicit modeling of sub-task dependencies and spatiotemporal action generation enhances the stability, coherence, and robustness of robotic policies.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The ST-𝜋 framework represents a significant contribution to the field of robotic manipulation, particularly for tasks requiring fine-grained spatiotemporal control over extended horizons. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Advancement in Spatiotemporal Reasoning:** The core contribution of ST-𝜋 lies in its departure from implicit spatiotemporal reasoning towards an *explicitly structured* approach. By designing distinct components for chunk-level task decomposition (ST-VLM) and step-level action generation (ST-AE) that explicitly account for spatial boundaries, temporal causality, and inter-task dependencies, the framework addresses a critical limitation of previous VLA models. This structured approach facilitates a more robust understanding and execution of complex, multi-stage manipulation sequences.

2. **Enhanced Performance and Robustness:** The consistent performance improvements demonstrated across diverse simulation benchmarks (LIBERO, SIMPLER) and, crucially, in real-world robotic experiments on the STAR dataset, underscore the practical effectiveness of ST-𝜋. Its superior success rates and reduced completion times, particularly in long-horizon tasks, indicate a heightened level of robustness and stability in execution. This is a vital step towards deploying robotic systems in unstructured and dynamic real-world environments.

3. **Novel Architectural Design:** The dual-generator guidance mechanism within the Spatiotemporal Action Expert (ST-AE), which decouples and then fuses spatial and temporal motion generation, introduces a novel method for generating coherent and stable trajectories. This architectural design, combined with the structured planning capabilities of ST-VLM, provides a principled way to manage the intricate interplay of spatial and temporal aspects in robot control.

4. **Dataset Contribution:** The introduction of the STAR (Spatiotemporal Task Annotation for Robotics) dataset is a valuable asset for the research community. By providing real-world demonstrations with fine-grained structured spatiotemporal annotations (semantic intent, target locations, and execution durations for sub-tasks), STAR fills a gap in available resources, enabling more targeted training and evaluation of advanced manipulation policies. This can accelerate future research in structured behavior learning for robotics.

5. **Implications for Long-Horizon Autonomy:** The ability of ST-𝜋 to effectively decompose and execute long-horizon tasks with causal dependencies has significant implications for increasing the autonomy of robots. Robots capable of reliably performing complex sequences of actions, such as assembly, cooking, or household chores, could see broader application in manufacturing, logistics, service robotics, and assistive technologies. The explicit planning at the chunk level provides a clearer pathway for understanding and potentially debugging robot behaviors.

6. **Future Research Directions:** The paper also identifies limitations, such as the assumption of sequential task decomposition, and suggests future work on supporting parallel or branching task structures. This indicates that the proposed framework can serve as a strong foundation upon which more sophisticated and flexible robotic intelligence can be built, further expanding the scope of manipulable tasks.

In summary, ST-𝜋 represents a methodological and empirical leap forward in robotic manipulation, moving beyond implicit reasoning to a structured, explicit understanding and execution of spatiotemporal behaviors. Its impact could be seen in the development of more capable, reliable, and autonomous robotic systems for complex real-world applications.
