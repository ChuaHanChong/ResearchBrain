# Hierarchical Optimization via LLM-Guided Objective Evolution for Mobility-on-Demand Systems

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from A*STAR and Lancaster University introduce a hybrid framework that integrates large language models (LLMs) with mathematical optimization to improve Mobility-on-Demand systems. This approach dynamically generates high-level objectives and ensures constraint satisfaction, leading to a reduction in mean passenger waiting times by over 14% on the Manhattan taxi dataset and 18% on the Chicago taxi dataset compared to current baselines.

## Problem

- Reinforcement Learning (RL) methods for Mobility-on-Demand (MoD) systems suffer from data inefficiency, unstable training, and difficulty in enforcing hard operational constraints.
- Decomposed online optimization frameworks often use static, manually designed high-level objectives that lack real-time awareness of low-level routing dynamics, leading to suboptimal decisions.
- Existing LLM applications in operational research are primarily for static optimization and lack mechanisms for iterative refinement in dynamic systems or direct integration with optimization solvers, resulting in potentially infeasible or low-quality solutions.

## Method

- A training-free, hybrid framework integrates LLMs as a '==meta-optimizer==' to adaptively generate high-level objectives for passenger-to-taxi assignment.
- Mathematical optimization solvers handle the low-level vehicle sequencing problems, ensuring spatiotemporal feasibility and rigorously enforcing operational constraints.
- A closed-loop evolutionary prompt optimization process, driven by a Harmony Search algorithm, refines the LLM-generated objectives based on real-time feedback from the dynamic environment and simulation performance.

## Results

- The hybrid LLM+Optimizer method achieved an average improvement exceeding **14%** in mean passenger waiting times on the Manhattan taxi dataset and over **18%** on the Chicago taxi dataset compared to state-of-the-art baselines.
- In large-scale, high-demand scenarios, the framework reduced mean passenger delay by over **40%** compared to a Full RL baseline, demonstrating robust performance under complex conditions.
- Ablation studies confirmed that integrating optimization model blueprints and Gurobi-compatible syntax into LLM prompts significantly reduced error rates, while dynamic environment feedback was vital for adaptive objective generation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Large language models can effectively generate adaptive, dynamic high-level objectives for complex optimization problems, bridging the gap between abstract assignments and concrete routing feasibility.
> - Explicitly grounding LLM prompts with optimization model blueprints and solver-compatible format restrictions is crucial for reducing error rates and ensuring the generation of valid and high-quality objective functions.
> - Dynamic, closed-loop feedback from the environment allows LLMs to acquire rich semantic information and adapt objectives more effectively than static, open-loop prompting.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Mobility-on-Demand (MoD) platforms, such as ride-hailing services, operate as complex urban transportation infrastructures. A core challenge for these platforms involves dynamically balancing supply and demand across spatially heterogeneous and evolving urban environments, primarily through efficient order dispatch and vehicle routing. Existing research to address this problem generally falls into three categories: reinforcement learning (RL) approaches, decomposed online optimization methods, and recent applications of large language models (LLMs).

Reinforcement learning methods are designed to learn policies that account for long-term rewards, considering future trajectories of drivers and passengers. However, these approaches often require extensive training data and interactions, leading to data inefficiency and unstable training. Furthermore, enforcing hard operational constraints is challenging, and learned policies can exhibit unpredictable behavior when faced with data distributions outside their training set.

Decomposed online optimization frameworks typically segment the problem into a high-level stage focusing on supply-demand balancing (e.g., batch matching via Mixed-Integer Linear Programming, MILP) and a low-level stage for dispatching or routing (e.g., graph-based search). While this decomposition reduces complexity, a significant limitation is that high-level objectives are frequently manually designed. These static objectives often lack real-time awareness of low-level routing dynamics and operational constraints, potentially resulting in suboptimal decisions such as increased passenger waiting times or inefficient vehicle utilization. This disconnect can lead to misaligned objectives between the hierarchical layers.

More recently, large language models have been explored for their potential in combinatorial reasoning and urban mobility contexts. Current applications of LLMs in operational research primarily focus on static optimization settings, such as automating mathematical model formulation or generating algorithmic code. When extended to dynamic ride-hailing systems with real-time state transitions, these LLM-only methods encounter two main limitations: the absence of iterative refinement aligned with evolving system states and a lack of direct integration with optimization solvers. This can lead to proposed solutions that violate operational constraints or degrade solution quality.

This paper positions itself as the first integration of LLMs with mathematical optimization specifically for dynamic sequential decision-making systems in the MoD context. It aims to overcome the data inefficiency of RL, the suboptimality of manually designed objectives in decomposed optimization, and the static nature and solver-agnosticism of current LLM applications.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the efficiency and adaptability of Mobility-on-Demand systems by addressing the limitations of existing decision-making paradigms for order dispatch and vehicle routing. The authors identify several key motivations:

1. **Overcoming Data Inefficiency and Instability in RL:** Traditional reinforcement learning methods require vast amounts of interaction data and computational resources for training. The resulting policies can be unstable and struggle to incorporate hard operational constraints effectively, leading to unpredictable outcomes in dynamic real-world scenarios. The motivation is to develop a training-free framework that bypasses these requirements while still capturing dynamic system behaviors.

2. **Mitigating Suboptimality from Myopic, Handcrafted Objectives in Decomposed Optimization:** Current hierarchical optimization approaches often rely on high-level objectives that are manually specified. These objectives, while simplifying the problem, typically lack granular awareness of downstream, low-level routing dynamics and real-time operational constraints. This disconnect can lead to misaligned decisions that result in increased passenger waiting times, inefficient vehicle repositioning, or suboptimal resource utilization across the entire system. The research seeks to dynamically align high-level assignment decisions with low-level routing feasibility and performance.

3. **Extending LLM Capabilities to Dynamic Optimization with Constraint Enforcement:** While LLMs possess considerable reasoning capabilities and implicit knowledge, their application in operational research has largely been limited to static problem settings. When applied to dynamic, real-time systems like ride-hailing, existing LLM-only approaches lack mechanisms for iterative refinement in response to evolving system states and integration with robust solvers to ensure constraint satisfaction. A key motivation is to leverage LLM's generative and reasoning abilities to adaptively generate problem objectives, while integrating them with mathematical optimizers to ensure proposed solutions are feasible and of high quality.

To address these motivations, the authors propose a novel hybrid framework designed to:
- Be training-free, thus eliminating the need for extensive interaction data required by RL.
- Leverage LLMs as a "meta-optimizer" to adaptively generate high-level objectives, thereby bridging the cognitive limitations associated with problem decomposition and manual objective design.
- Integrate mathematical optimization solvers to enforce operational constraints and execute real-time decisions, ensuring numerical rigor and feasibility.
- Facilitate a closed-loop evolutionary process for LLM-generated heuristics, continuously refining objectives based on feedback from the optimization layer and simulator performance.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework integrates large language models with mathematical optimization within a dynamic hierarchical system for Mobility-on-Demand (MoD) services. This hybrid approach decomposes each decision step into two levels and employs a closed-loop evolutionary mechanism for objective refinement.

**Dynamic Hierarchical Optimization Problem:**
The overall problem is hierarchically decomposed to manage complexity, addressing the assignment of passengers to taxis (high-level) and the routing of individual taxis (low-level).
- **First-level Assignment Problem:** This stage focuses on assigning passengers to available taxis across the entire system at a given snapshot. Traditionally, this involves minimizing handcrafted objectives, such as a weighted sum of distance, temporal alignment, and utilization fairness. In this framework, the manual objective is replaced by a dynamically generated objective `Φt = LLM(St, Ht-1)`. Here, `St` represents the current system state (e.g., vehicle positions, pending requests), and `Ht-1` captures historical information (e.g., congestion, past assignments). Constraints ensure each passenger is assigned to at most one taxi. The LLM's role is to embed latent urban dynamics into the objective, bridging the gap between assignment and routing without explicit dynamic modeling.
- **Second-level Sequencing Problem:** Following passenger assignment by the high-level module, this stage solves an independent routing problem for each taxi. The objective is to minimize the total passenger waiting time for the assigned passengers. This problem is formulated as a Mixed-Integer Linear Program (MILP) with constraints addressing flow conservation, taxi arrival and departure times at pickup and dropoff points, and passenger request times. The formulation ensures spatiotemporal feasibility for each taxi's assigned sequence.

**LLM-Optimizer Interaction Protocol:**
The interaction between the LLM and the optimizer is structured to enable adaptive objective generation and constraint-aware decision-making.
- **Scenario Prompt Setup (P_sce):** The LLM is provided with contextual knowledge through a structured prompt:
 - `P_sys`: Defines the LLM's role as an adaptive objective designer, specifying that its outputs must align with solver-compatible templates.
 - `P_geo`: Encodes geospatial semantics of the operational area, such as Manhattan or Chicago zone graphs and Origin-Destination (OD) matrices.
 - `P_model`: Offers class blueprints for the first-level assignment model variables and structure, ensuring the LLM generates valid and solver-compatible objective formulations.
- **Dynamic Environment Feedback (P_dyn):** The framework operates in a closed-loop control system. At each time step `t` within a simulation run, the latest environment state (`P_t_dyn = {(veh_t, pass_t)}`) is incorporated into the prompt context. This allows the LLM to generate objectives dynamically based on real-time feedback, making it context-aware and adaptive to evolving conditions.
- **Evolutionary Prompt Optimization:** To refine the LLM-generated objectives, a Harmony Search (HS) algorithm is employed. The HS algorithm, a meta-heuristic inspired by musical improvisation, iteratively optimizes a population of prompts. Each prompt is treated as an evolving entity, and its fitness is evaluated by the overall system performance (e.g., total passenger waiting time) obtained from simulation-based inference.
 - **HS Mechanism:** The evolution is governed by the Harmony Memory Considering Rate (HMCR) and Pitch Adjustment Rate (PAR). HMCR determines whether to explore new solutions or exploit existing ones. If exploitation is chosen, PAR controls the probability of local adjustments.
 - **Three Novel Operators:** The framework introduces three prompt-refinement operators for LLM-driven objective generation, selected probabilistically based on HS parameters:
 - **Random Inference (W1):** The LLM generates a new objective function from scratch, without direct influence from previous prompts, promoting exploration.
 - **Heuristic Improvement (W2):** The LLM refines an existing "parent prompt" by incorporating specific instructions aimed at incremental enhancements (e.g., temporal alignment, resource weighting).
 - **Innovative Generation (W3):** The LLM formulates a novel prompt by drawing inspiration from a "parent prompt" but with instructions for more significant, paradigm-level innovations (e.g., multi-horizon optimization, dynamic weight adaptation).
 - **Prompt Composition:** Each prompt given to the LLM is a combination of the scenario block (`P_sce`), dynamic constraint block (`P_dyn`), the selected operator's instruction block, and a format restriction block (`P_restriction`) which enforces Gurobi-compatible syntax for the objective function.
 - **Token Management:** To mitigate the growing token size of prompts over long simulation horizons, a `TOKENSELECT` function retains only the most critical components (the objective part and its fitness) from parent prompts for subsequent evolutionary steps.

This training-free methodology is designed to leverage the semantic reasoning of LLMs to generate adaptive high-level objectives, which are then rigorously enforced and executed by mathematical optimizers, all within a continuous feedback loop.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The authors conducted extensive experiments on scenarios derived from the New York taxi dataset (Downtown Manhattan) and the Chicago taxi dataset (Central Chicago), evaluating their approach against various baselines across nine progressively complex scenarios for each city. The primary evaluation metric was the mean passenger waiting time (minutes).

**Baseline Comparisons on Manhattan Taxi Dataset:**
- The proposed hybrid LLM+Optimizer method outperformed the best baseline (FunSearch) in 7 out of 9 scenarios, achieving an average improvement exceeding 14% across all scenarios.
- In large-scale scenarios (P130_C80_T1200 and P200_C100_T1200), the method reduced mean passenger delay by over 40% compared to the Full RL baseline.
- **Manual Objective Limitations:** Manually designed composite objectives (e.g., Distance × Temporal × Utilization) showed declining effectiveness as problem scale increased, indicating limited scalability.
- **RL-based and LLM-only Methods:** RL methods performed well in low-complexity settings but exhibited struggles with reward sparsity and exploration inefficiencies in larger scenarios. LLM-based methods like EoH struggled with dynamic adaptation, while FunSearch showed better generalization. However, both LLM-only approaches lacked dynamic, per-time-interval feedback and a low-level optimizer, leading to suboptimal performance in larger cases.
- **Hybrid LLM+Optimizer Strength:** The hybrid framework consistently delivered performance gains, particularly under high-demand, long-horizon conditions (e.g., 4.01 min vs. 6.82 min for Full RL in Scenario 9), demonstrating the benefit of combining LLM adaptability with optimizer precision.

**Baseline Comparisons on Chicago Taxi Dataset:**
- The framework maintained its superiority on Chicago's conditions, which feature larger spatial extents and more imbalanced ride request distributions, leading to higher average OD travel times and overall waiting times.
- It reduced mean passenger waiting times by an average of 18% in large-scale scenarios compared to baselines.
- Compared to FunSearch, it outperformed in 8 of 9 scenarios, with an average improvement exceeding 18% across all cases.
- **Manual Objective Degradation:** Composite objectives degraded severely under scale and imbalanced distributions, with delays escalating significantly (e.g., 25.01 min in Scenario 9 vs. 15.37 min for the proposed approach). Single-objective variants performed very poorly under high-demand conditions (e.g., 145.14 min in Scenario 8 for Distance-only).
- **RL-based and LLM-only Inconsistencies:** Full RL outperformed Default+RL-Seq but remained inferior to the proposed method. LLM-only methods exhibited inconsistent adaptation, with FunSearch excelling in smaller scenarios and EoH in larger ones, but both lacked dynamic feedback and fine-grained optimization for solution quality.
- **Hybrid LLM+Optimizer Robustness:** The proposed framework delivered robust performance across all Chicago scenarios, showing substantial gains in several cases (e.g., 8.40 min vs. 12.05 min for Full RL in Scenario 4), underscoring the importance of closed-loop adaptation in dynamic and high-demand environments.

**Ablation Studies:**
- **Scenario Prompt Composition:** Incorporating `P_model` (optimization model blueprint) significantly reduced waiting times compared to just `P_data` (variable formats), highlighting the importance of task grounding. Further integrating `P_restriction` (Gurobi-compatible constraints) led to the lowest costs and minimized error rates (invalid objective functions) across scenarios, emphasizing the necessity of formalizing constraints for reliable LLM outputs.
- **Dynamic Environment Feedback:** A dynamic closed-loop querying mechanism, where the LLM is queried at each time step, consistently outperformed a single open-loop query at the beginning of the simulation. This demonstrates that real-time feedback allows the LLM to acquire rich semantic information and adapt objectives more effectively.
- **Spatiotemporal Analysis:** Heatmaps illustrating passenger waiting times across zones and temporal bins demonstrated that the proposed method completed all passenger pickups significantly faster (e.g., within 4 time slots) compared to baselines (5 to 9 time slots), which showed accumulating delays and hotspots in later time slots. This confirmed the framework's effectiveness in mitigating delay accumulation and maintaining spatially consistent low delays.
- **Evolutionary Hyperparameter Study (Harmony Search):** A high HMCR (0.9) led to significant performance improvements, suggesting effective exploitation of high-quality parent solutions. The optimal PAR varied with HMCR, indicating that operator selection frequency is influenced more by solution quality feedback than fixed parameterization.
- **LLM Temperature Study:** An intermediate LLM temperature (τ=0.9) yielded the lowest overall costs, balancing exploitation (adherence to constraints) and exploration (sampling novel objective structures) effectively. Extreme temperatures (0.1, 1.3) resulted in higher costs due to over-constraint or excessive stochasticity.
- **Error Rate Study:** Error rates of LLM-generated objectives decreased drastically with progressive prompt enrichment (model structuring and explicit constraint formalization). Errors increased with scenario complexity for less constrained prompts, reflecting the fragility of LLM-generated objectives in high-dimensional spaces without proper grounding.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a significant advancement in addressing complex dynamic optimization problems within Mobility-on-Demand systems, particularly ride-hailing. The proposed hybrid LLM-optimizer framework offers a novel paradigm that integrates the semantic reasoning capabilities of large language models with the mathematical rigor of optimization solvers.

The key significance of this work lies in its ability to overcome several limitations inherent in existing approaches:
- **Bridging Methodological Gaps:** It successfully integrates LLMs with mathematical optimization for dynamic sequential decision-making, a combination not previously explored. This addresses the data inefficiency and training instability issues of reinforcement learning, as well as the suboptimality stemming from manually designed and static objectives in traditional decomposed optimization.
- **Enhanced Adaptability and Robustness:** The closed-loop evolutionary process, driven by harmony search and dynamic environment feedback, allows the LLM to adaptively generate high-level objectives. This makes the system more responsive to evolving real-time conditions, such as fluctuating supply and demand or changing urban congestion patterns, a critical feature for real-world MoD operations.
- **Guaranteed Feasibility and Quality:** By leveraging optimization solvers for low-level routing and constraint enforcement, the framework ensures that LLM-generated heuristics lead to feasible and high-quality solutions, addressing a common challenge with LLM-only approaches that may propose impractical strategies.
- **Training-Free Nature:** The framework's training-free design eliminates the need for extensive interaction data and lengthy training phases, making it potentially more cost-effective and faster to deploy in new environments compared to data-hungry RL methods.

The potential impact of this research is substantial, with implications for improving the efficiency and user experience of urban mobility services:
- **Reduced Passenger Waiting Times:** The demonstrated 16-18% average improvement in passenger waiting times compared to state-of-the-art baselines directly translates to better service quality and increased customer satisfaction for ride-hailing users.
- **Optimized Resource Utilization:** By generating more adaptive and context-aware objectives, the system can lead to more efficient allocation of vehicles, reducing idle times and optimizing fleet operations.
- **Foundation for Future Hybrid AI Systems:** This work provides a concrete blueprint for combining the strengths of generative AI (LLMs) with symbolic AI (optimization solvers) to tackle complex real-world decision-making problems, fostering a new direction for hybrid intelligent systems.

Future work outlined by the authors includes integrating microscopic traffic simulators like SUMO for higher-fidelity modeling and evaluation under realistic transient disruptions. Furthermore, exploring reinforcement learning for LLM-optimizer co-adaptation, where optimizer feedback directly fine-tunes the LLM's internal parameters, could enable more sample-efficient and domain-adaptive optimization. The paper also points to the potential of quantum-enhanced hierarchical optimization as quantum computing matures, which could accelerate both LLM inference and classical optimization, enabling real-time implementations in highly dynamic decision environments.
