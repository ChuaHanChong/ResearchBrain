# NaVid: Video-based VLM Plans the Next Step for Vision-and-Language Navigation

> [!summary] Summary
> NaVid is a video-based Vision-Language Model that enables navigation in continuous environments using only monocular RGB video streams. It achieves state-of-the-art performance on R2R Val-Unseen with 35.9% SPL and demonstrates a 66% success rate in real-world scenarios, eliminating reliance on depth, odometry, or maps.

## Problem

- Vision-and-Language Navigation (VLN) models face significant generalization gaps to unseen environments and struggle with Sim-to-Real transfer.
- Existing VLN methods often depend on specialized and noisy sensors like depth cameras, odometry, or pre-built maps, complicating hardware and deployment.
- Effectively encoding spatio-temporal context from continuous observations for low-level, executable action planning remains a challenge in real-world VLN.

## Method

- Developed **NaVid**, an end-to-end video-based VLM that operates solely on monocular RGB video and linguistic instructions, adapting the ==LLaMA-VID== architecture for VLN-CE.
- Implemented a multi-token visual encoding for video history, using both instruction-queried tokens for relevant features and instruction-agnostic tokens with varying densities for current versus historical frames.
- Utilized a hybrid training strategy combining oracle data with DAgger-inspired non-oracle trajectories and co-training with auxiliary tasks like instruction reasoning and video-based Question-Answering (VQA).

## Results

- Achieved state-of-the-art performance on VLN-CE R2R Val-Unseen (**35.9%** SPL, **37.4%** SR) and RxR Val-Unseen (**21.2%** SPL, **23.8%** SR) in simulation, surpassing methods that utilize depth and odometry.
- Demonstrated a **66%** overall success rate (**84%** for simple, **48%** for complex instructions) in real-world deployments across diverse indoor scenes using a physical robot, significantly outperforming prior baselines.
- Ablation studies confirmed the efficacy of the video-based history encoding, special tokens for structural input, and the hybrid training strategy, while showing that direct waypoint prediction for VLMs is challenging.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Large Vision-Language Models (VLMs) can be effectively fine-tuned for continuous, real-world Vision-and-Language Navigation, achieving high generalization using only RGB input, thereby simplifying sensor requirements.
> - A video-based representation of historical observations, incorporating rich spatio-temporal visual information, is crucial for robust low-level action planning in continuous VLN, outperforming text or simpler visual history encodings.
> - Co-training with auxiliary tasks (instruction reasoning, VQA) and collecting non-oracle trajectories are critical for enhancing a VLM's understanding, robustness, and generalization across diverse navigation scenarios.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Vision-and-Language Navigation (VLN) is a cornerstone problem in Embodied AI, requiring an agent to navigate an environment to a specified target location by interpreting natural language instructions and visual observations. The field has evolved significantly, addressing challenges such as understanding complex instructions, perceiving diverse visual information, and operating in novel, unseen environments.

Initially, much of VLN research focused on **discrete environments**, where the world is abstracted into a connectivity graph and navigation involves teleporting between predefined waypoints (e.g., R2R in MP3D simulator). While this simplified setting allowed for rapid algorithmic development, it introduced challenges related to graph construction, local navigation between landmarks, and a significant **Sim-to-Real (S2R) gap** when attempting to deploy these agents in the physical world.

More recently, the focus has shifted towards **continuous environments (VLN-CE)**, where agents predict low-level actions (e.g., move forward, turn) and can navigate freely. This approach is more realistic but faces severe generalization challenges due to data scarcity and domain gaps in model inputs. Many existing VLN-CE methods still rely on a rich array of sensory inputs, such as **RGB-D (color and depth), odometry, and pre-built maps**. These dependencies introduce their own S2R gaps: depth sensors might have different noise characteristics or failures in real-world scenarios, odometry can drift, and detailed maps are often unavailable or difficult to construct in novel real-world settings.

The recent "prosperity of large Vision Language Models (VLMs)" and Large Language Models (LLMs) has opened new avenues for VLN. LLMs have been explored as high-level planners in discrete environments, while VLMs like RT-2 have shown promise in transferring web knowledge to generalizable robotic manipulation. The core idea is to leverage the vast pre-trained knowledge in these foundational models to enhance an agent's understanding, reasoning, and generalization capabilities.

**NaVid distinguishes itself** by making the "first endeavor to capitalize on the power of foundational VLMs for generalizing VLN to the real world" in a **continuous environment setting, solely relying on monocular RGB video streams**. This is a significant departure from prior work that either used more sensor data (depth, odometry, maps) or operated in discrete environments. By mimicking human navigation (which primarily uses visual cues), NaVid aims to directly address the generalization issues arising from noisy or domain-divergent non-RGB inputs, making it easier to deploy in diverse real-world scenarios. It positions itself as a practical Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model that directly infers executable low-level actions, bridging the gap between high-level VLM understanding and robotic control.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind the NaVid project are multifaceted, aiming to address critical limitations in current Vision-and-Language Navigation (VLN) systems:

1. **Mitigate Generalization Gaps:** A central motivation is to overcome the persistent generalization challenges in VLN. This includes generalization to "out-of-distribution scenes" (novel environments not seen during training) and the crucial "Sim-to-Real (S2R) transfer" gap, which hinders the deployment of simulated agents in the real world.
2. **Eliminate Reliance on Specialized Sensors and Maps:** Existing VLN models often depend on noisy or hard-to-obtain inputs like depth maps, odometry data, or pre-built navigation graphs/maps. NaVid aims to break this dependency, showcasing that state-of-the-art navigation can be achieved "without any maps, odometers, or depth inputs." This simplifies the robot's hardware requirements and removes sources of S2R discrepancy or sensor noise that can plague more complex multi-modal systems.
3. **Mimic Human Navigation:** The authors motivate their "RGB-only" and "video-based" approach by drawing an analogy to human navigation. Humans primarily rely on monocular vision and spatio-temporal context to understand an environment and follow instructions. By formulating the problem in this way, NaVid naturally "gets rid of the problems introduced by odometer noises, and the Sim2Real gaps from map or depth inputs."
4. **Leverage Large Vision-Language Models (VLMs):** Inspired by the "unprecedented promises" and "impressive generalization capacities" of large models in various AI fields (AIGC, chatbots, autonomous driving, embodied AI like RT-2), NaVid seeks to explore if VLMs can "propel generalizable VLN" in continuous, real-world environments. The goal is to transfer the vast general-purpose knowledge acquired during large-scale pre-training to the specific task of VLN, facilitating learning and boosting generalization.
5. **Encode Spatio-Temporal Context Effectively:** The video-based approach is motivated by its ability to "effectively encode the historical observations of robots as spatio-temporal contexts for decision making and instruction following." This richer, continuous representation of past trajectories is hypothesized to be superior to discrete or textual representations used in some prior LLM-based VLN methods.
6. **Direct Low-Level Action Planning:** Unlike some generalist AGI models that perform coarse planning or LLM-based methods operating in discrete spaces, NaVid aims to be a practical Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model that directly infers "executable actions with quantitative arguments" (e.g., specific distances to move, degrees to rotate). This enables direct deployment and closed-loop control in real-world robots.

In essence, NaVid's core motivation is to simplify the input requirements for VLN while simultaneously boosting its generalization capabilities and practical deployability by harnessing the power of modern video-based VLMs in a human-inspired, end-to-end manner.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

NaVid is designed as an end-to-end video-based Large Vision Language Model (VLM) for Vision-and-Language Navigation in Continuous Environments (VLN-CE), operating solely on monocular RGB video and linguistic instructions.

#### 3.1. a. Overall Architecture

NaVid builds upon the architecture of **LLaMA-VID**, adapting it for the specific requirements of VLN-CE. The model comprises several key components:
- **Vision Encoder:** An off-the-shelf pre-trained model (EVA-CLIP) to extract visual features from RGB frames.
- **Query Generator:** A Q-Former-based component to generate instruction-aware queries.
- **LLM (Large Language Model):** A pre-trained LLM (Vicuna-7B) that serves as the central reasoning and action planning module.
- **Cross-Modality Projectors:** Modules that align the visual features with the language token space of the LLM.

The general flow involves encoding the video observations and instructions into tokens, concatenating them, and feeding them to the LLM to generate the next-step action in a linguistic format.

#### 3.2. b. VLN-CE Modeling of NaVid

**Observation Encoding:** NaVid processes a continuous video stream $O_t = \{x_0, \ldots, x_t\}$ from a monocular RGB camera. Each frame $x_t$ is represented by two types of visual tokens:

1. **Instruction-Queried Tokens ($E_Q^t$):** These tokens extract visual features specifically relevant to the given linguistic instruction $I$. A Q-Former-based query generator ($G_Q$) takes the visual embeddings $X_t$ (from the vision encoder) and the textual embeddings of the instruction $I$ to generate instruction-aware queries $Q_t$. These queries then cross-attend with $X_t$, and the result is projected and pooled to yield a single instruction-queried token per frame, focusing on instruction-relevant visual information.
2. **Instruction-Agnostic Tokens ($E_V^t$):** These tokens encode global, fine-grained visual information from the frame, irrespective of the instruction. They are obtained by applying a grid pooling operation (GridPool) to the visual embeddings $X_t$ and then projecting them. This is a crucial distinction from LLaMA-VID, which uses fewer instruction-agnostic tokens, as VLN-CE requires sufficient geometric context for executable actions.

A key design choice is the **varying number of instruction-agnostic tokens** for historical frames versus the current frame:
- **Current frame ($x_t$):** Encoded with 64 instruction-agnostic tokens, providing rich visual detail for immediate decision-making.
- **Historical frames ($x_0, \ldots, x_{t-1}$):** Each encoded with 4 instruction-agnostic tokens, providing contextual information about the trajectory without excessive computational load.
This video-based representation of historical trajectories is claimed to be more informative and adaptive than discrete or text-based encodings.

**Special Tokens for Context:** To explicitly differentiate and structure the input information for the LLM, NaVid uses special tokens:
- `<HIS>` and `</HIS>`: Demarcate tokens from historical frames.
- `<OBS>` and `</OBS>`: Demarcate tokens from the current frame.
- `<NAV>`: Prompts the LLM to begin processing the instruction and output navigation actions.

The full input format to the LLM is thus: `<HIS> {historical frames} </HIS> <OBS> {current frame} </OBS> <NAV> {instruction content}`.

**Action Planning:** NaVid generates low-level, executable actions in a linguistic form. Each output action specifies:
- **Action type:** Chosen from a discrete set {FORWARD, TURN-LEFT, TURN-RIGHT, STOP}.
- **Quantitative arguments:** For FORWARD, a specific moving distance (e.g., "75 cm"); for TURN-LEFT/RIGHT, specific rotation degrees (e.g., "30 degrees").
A regular expression parser extracts these action types and arguments for robot execution.

#### 3.3. c. The Training of NaVid

NaVid employs a **hybrid training strategy** to maximize data utilization and enhance generalization, addressing the scarcity and limited diversity of navigational simulation data.

1. **Non-Oracle Navigation Trajectories Collection:**
 - **Oracle Trajectories:** Initial training data (320k step-wise samples) are collected from the VLN-CE R2R dataset, consisting of monocular videos, instructions, and correct (oracle) actions.
 - **Non-Oracle Trajectories (Dagger-inspired):** After initial training, the NaVid agent is deployed in VLN-CE environments to generate its own navigation trajectories. These "non-oracle" trajectories (an additional 180k step-wise samples) capture the agent's behavior and errors, which are then used for further training. This process, inspired by DAgger, makes the agent more robust to its own mistakes and real-world deviations from optimal paths.

2. **Co-training of VLN-CE and Auxiliary Tasks:** To instill a deeper understanding of environments and instructions, NaVid is co-trained with two auxiliary tasks:
 - **Instruction Reasoning:** Given a video-based navigation trajectory, NaVid is prompted to deduce the corresponding human instruction. This task (10k trajectories) forces the model to learn to summarize and interpret observed trajectories, enhancing its environmental understanding.
 - **Video-based Question-Answering (VQA):** Standard VQA samples from web-scale video data (inherited from LLaMA-VID) are included. This task helps the model retain its general vision-language understanding capabilities and mitigates catastrophic forgetting of pre-trained knowledge.

#### 3.4. d. Implementation Details

NaVid is trained for approximately 28 hours on 24 NVIDIA A100 GPUs (672 GPU hours). The model uses a fixed 1 FPS sampling rate for video-caption data but retains all frames for navigation-action data (up to 300 frames). All modules (EVA-CLIP, QFormer, BERT, Vicuna-7B) are loaded with pre-trained weights, and only the trainable parameters of LLaMA and the text encoder are optimized for a single epoch, following LLaMA-VID's strategy. For real-world deployment, a remote server runs NaVid, receiving compressed RGB images and sending parsed linguistic commands to a local Turtlebot4 robot with a Kinect DK camera. Inference latency is 1.2-1.5 seconds per action, with potential for improvement via acceleration techniques.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

NaVid demonstrates state-of-the-art performance and strong generalization capabilities across various benchmarks and real-world scenarios.

#### 4.1. a. Simulated Environments

**VLN-CE R2R Val-Unseen (Cross-Split Generalizability):**
- NaVid achieves **state-of-the-art (SOTA) level performance** in terms of SPL (Success weighted by Path Length, 35.9%), NE (Navigation Error, 5.47m), and OS (Oracle Success, 49.1%) on the R2R Val-Unseen dataset, a significant benchmark for cross-split generalization.
- Crucially, NaVid achieves this **without using depth or odometry information**, relying only on RGB observations. Compared to RGB-only baselines (RGB-Seq2Seq, RGB-CMA), NaVid shows a massive improvement, increasing SR from 5.00% to 37.4% and SPL from 4.43% to 35.9%. It even outperforms the SOTA method WS-MGMap (which uses RGB-D and odometry) by 1.6% in SPL.
- The method shows **consistent performance across different instruction horizons** (short to long, 30-90+ steps), which is attributed to co-tuning with web-scale video data that includes long sequences.

**VLN-CE RxR Val-Unseen (Cross-Dataset Generalizability):**
- On the RxR Val-Unseen dataset, designed for finer granularity instructions and longer trajectories, NaVid exhibits **superior cross-dataset generalization**.
- In a zero-shot setting (trained on R2R, evaluated on RxR), NaVid significantly outperforms existing methods across all metrics, achieving 23.8% SR and 21.2% SPL. This is a substantial improvement over the previous SOTA, A2Nav (6.3% SPL), demonstrating NaVid's ability to handle the large language and visual gaps in RxR. RGB-only baselines like Seq2Seq-RGB and CMA-RGB completely fail on this task.

**Comparison with Large Foundation Models:**
- A comparative study on a 100-episode R2R Val-Unseen sub-split reveals that **vanilla large foundation models (GPT-4V, Emu, LLaVA, LLaMA-VID) perform very poorly** or fail to output valid actions when not specifically trained for navigation. Emu, LLaVA, and LLaMA-VID frequently produce irrelevant answers.
- Even with careful prompting, GPT-4V achieves only 5% SR.
- After fine-tuning with the proposed navigation data (LLaVA-Nav, LLaMA-VID-Nav), performance significantly improves, underscoring the **critical importance of task-specific navigation data** in enabling VLMs to master VLN tasks. NaVid (35.4% SPL, 38% SR) still outperforms LLaMA-VID-Nav (27.5% SPL, 29% SR), indicating the efficacy of NaVid's specific modeling choices.

**Comparison with Different Navigation History Representations:**
- An ablation study on how past trajectories are modeled shows the **superiority of NaVid's video-based approach**.
- Modeling history as **text (Text-based)**, **2D maps with text (Map-text)**, or **ego-view images with text (Ego-view-text)** all yield significantly lower SPL (dropping from 35.9% to 0%, 8.97%, and 20.8% respectively).
- This highlights that richly encoded spatio-temporal visual information is crucial for continuous VLN, and compressing it into text or simpler visual forms leads to a loss of essential geometric context. Video-based representation also offers better inference time compared to text-based methods requiring additional captioning queries.

**Comparison with LM-Nav and its Variants:**
- NaVid significantly outperforms LM-Nav, a baseline using off-the-shelf foundation models and a discrete environment setting (relying on pre-built oracle landmark graphs and shortest paths), on the RxR Val-Unseen split (21.2% SPL vs. 7.38% for LM-Nav*).
- This suggests that while LM-Nav's modular design (instruction decomposition, landmark grounding) is intuitive, it struggles with the nuances of continuous navigation and complex instructions, particularly in differentiating semantically similar objects in dense environments. NaVid's end-to-end approach demonstrates greater adaptability.

#### 4.2. b. Real-world Environment

- NaVid demonstrates **impressive robustness and generalizability in real-world environments**, performing experiments across four diverse indoor scenes (Meeting Room, Office, Lab, Lounge) with both simple and complex instructions.
- NaVid achieves an overall success rate of **about 66%**, specifically **84% for simple instructions** and **48% for complex instructions**, relying solely on RGB videos.
- This is a **significant improvement over baselines** like Seq2Seq, CMA, and WS-MGMap, which show very poor performance (often 0% SR) in the real world. The end-to-end methods (Seq2Seq, CMA) suffer greatly from Sim-to-Real gaps, even with RGB-D and odometry. WS-MGMap, while better than pure end-to-end, still lags significantly behind NaVid.
- Visual results demonstrate NaVid's ability to accurately follow forward and rotation instructions, understand specific landmarks, and differentiate between semantically similar objects based on spatial context (e.g., "chair on the right" vs. "chair on the left").

#### 4.3. c. Ablation Studies

- **Training Strategy:**
 - **No co-training (with web data):** Performance drops noticeably, confirming the importance of co-tuning with general VQA data for maintaining generalizability and preventing forgetting.
 - **No instruction reasoning samples:** Performance slightly decreases, showing the benefit of learning to summarize trajectories and enhancing environmental understanding.
 - **No non-oracle navigation samples (Dagger-like):** A minor drop in performance, suggesting that exposure to diverse, non-optimal trajectories contributes to robustness.
- **Architecture:**
 - **Removal of special tokens ([NAV], [HIS], [OBS]):** Leads to a noticeable performance drop, validating their role in structuring information and guiding the LLM.
 - **Waypoint prediction:** Directly outputting continuous location/orientation waypoints proves extremely challenging for VLMs, resulting in near-zero success rates. This justifies NaVid's approach of outputting discrete action types with quantitative arguments.
- **Numbers of Instruction-Agnostic (Visual) Tokens per frame:**
 - Using **more tokens (16)** slightly improves performance over 4 tokens (36.1% vs 35.9% SPL) but at a significant cost in inference time (2.72s vs 1.22s).
 - **4 tokens** per historical frame provides an optimal balance between visual information richness and computational efficiency. 1 token per frame is insufficient.
- **Navigation Data Scale:** Performance (SR, OS, SPL, NE) improves steadily with increasing navigation data, with a significant boost around 330k samples (pre-dagger phase). The gains slow down in the post-dagger phase, suggesting that the R2R train split's diversity might be limited for further substantial improvement through DAgger alone.

#### 4.4. d. Additional Experiments

- **Object Goal Navigation (Zero-Shot):** NaVid demonstrates competitive performance on this task (32.5% SR, 21.6% SPL) despite not being explicitly designed for it, outperforming several specialized zero-shot object goal navigation baselines. This further highlights its generalizability to sparse instructions and unseen objects.
- **Single-Step Prediction:** The model achieves a 69.4% single-step success rate, 52.1% stop success rate, 6.82-degree angle error, and 16.7cm distance error. While this doesn't directly translate to the overall task success rate (37.4%), it indicates sound low-level action prediction and that full trajectories can be completed through sequences of such actions, even if not perfectly optimal.
- **Trajectory Summary:** The model successfully generates brief textual descriptions of navigation trajectories (both simulated and real-world videos), demonstrating an understanding of its motions and the visual context, aided by the instruction reasoning auxiliary task.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The NaVid project represents a significant step forward in the field of Vision-and-Language Navigation (VLN) and embodied AI, with several key contributions and potential impacts:

1. **Paradigm Shift in Sensor Dependency:** NaVid makes a pioneering move by achieving state-of-the-art navigation performance *without* relying on depth sensors, odometers, or pre-built maps. This is a substantial simplification of the robot's hardware requirements and sensing pipeline. It challenges the long-held assumption that multi-modal inputs are strictly necessary for robust navigation, ushering in a new paradigm of "RGB-only" embodied intelligence akin to human navigation.
2. **Enhanced Generalization Capabilities:** The work directly addresses the critical and long-standing challenges of generalization to unseen environments (cross-dataset, cross-split) and the Sim-to-Real (S2R) gap. By formulating VLN as an end-to-end, video-based VLM task, NaVid leverages the vast pre-trained knowledge of foundational models, demonstrating superior transferability and robustness in real-world deployment. This makes VLN agents more practical and deployable outside of highly controlled simulation environments.
3. **Leveraging Foundation Models for Embodied AI:** NaVid effectively demonstrates the power of large vision-language models (VLMs) in a core embodied AI task. It showcases how general-purpose knowledge acquired from web-scale data can be successfully transferred and fine-tuned to enable complex robot behaviors, further solidifying the role of foundation models as backbones for future embodied agents.
4. **Practical Real-World Deployment:** The successful deployment and evaluation of NaVid on physical robots in diverse indoor scenes highlight its practical utility. Achieving a 66% success rate for complex instructions solely with RGB input in the real world is a remarkable feat that far surpasses previous baselines, indicating a viable path towards widely deployable language-guided robots.
5. **Robust Spatio-Temporal Reasoning:** The video-based approach for encoding historical observations is validated as superior to text-based or simpler visual history representations. This emphasis on spatio-temporal context, combined with instruction-aware and instruction-agnostic visual tokenization, provides the LLM with richer information for robust decision-making, particularly for low-level executable actions.
6. **Insights into Training Strategies:** The hybrid training strategy, including the collection of non-oracle (DAgger-inspired) navigation trajectories and co-training with auxiliary tasks like instruction reasoning, offers valuable insights for training robust embodied agents. It highlights the importance of diversifying training data and reinforcing core capabilities beyond just action prediction.

**Potential Impact:**
NaVid's success could inspire further research into simpler, more robust, and more human-like perception stacks for robotics. It could accelerate the development of cost-effective autonomous agents that can understand and execute human commands in unstructured environments, without the need for expensive and complex sensor suites or extensive environmental mapping. This has implications for service robotics, assistive technologies, and intelligent automation in various domains. The work lays the groundwork for future generalist embodied AI agents that can seamlessly integrate vision, language, and action planning.

**Limitations and Future Work:**
Despite its strengths, NaVid acknowledges limitations. **Computation cost and latency** are issues for real-time deployment, suggesting future work in action chunking or quantization techniques. The model also faces potential **performance drops for very long horizon instructions** due to the "long context tokens problem," compounded by a lack of high-quality long video annotation data. Future work includes exploring advanced large model backbones and utilizing larger long-video datasets. The authors also plan to **extend NaVid to other embodied AI tasks** like mobile manipulation, which would require investigating new action designs and collecting diverse annotated mobile manipulation video datasets. Finally, efforts to enhance model efficiency are crucial for broader adoption.
