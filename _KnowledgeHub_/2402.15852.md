# NaVid: Video-based VLM Plans the Next Step for Vision-and-Language Navigation

> [!summary] Summary
> NaVid is a video-based Vision-Language Model that enables navigation in continuous environments using only monocular RGB video streams. It achieves state-of-the-art performance on R2R Val-Unseen with 35.9% SPL and demonstrates a 66% success rate in real-world scenarios, eliminating reliance on depth, odometry, or maps.

## Problem

- Vision-and-Language Navigation (VLN) models face significant generalization gaps to unseen environments and struggle with Sim-to-Real transfer.
- Existing VLN methods often depend on specialized and noisy sensors like depth cameras, odometry, or pre-built maps, complicating hardware and deployment.
- Effectively encoding spatio-temporal context from continuous observations for low-level, executable action planning remains a challenge in real-world VLN.

## Method

- Developed **NaVid**, an end-to-end video-based VLM that operates solely on monocular RGB video and linguistic instructions, adapting the ==LLaMA-VID== architecture for VLN-CE.
- Implemented a multi-token visual encoding for video history, using both instruction-queried tokens for relevant features and instruction-agnostic tokens with varying densities for current versus historical frames.
- Utilized a hybrid training strategy combining oracle data with DAgger-inspired non-oracle trajectories and co-training with auxiliary tasks like instruction reasoning and video-based Question-Answering (VQA).

## Results

- Achieved state-of-the-art performance on VLN-CE R2R Val-Unseen (**35.9%** SPL, **37.4%** SR) and RxR Val-Unseen (**21.2%** SPL, **23.8%** SR) in simulation, surpassing methods that utilize depth and odometry.
- Demonstrated a **66%** overall success rate (**84%** for simple, **48%** for complex instructions) in real-world deployments across diverse indoor scenes using a physical robot, significantly outperforming prior baselines.
- Ablation studies confirmed the efficacy of the video-based history encoding, special tokens for structural input, and the hybrid training strategy, while showing that direct waypoint prediction for VLMs is challenging.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Large Vision-Language Models (VLMs) can be effectively fine-tuned for continuous, real-world Vision-and-Language Navigation, achieving high generalization using only RGB input, thereby simplifying sensor requirements.
> - A video-based representation of historical observations, incorporating rich spatio-temporal visual information, is crucial for robust low-level action planning in continuous VLN, outperforming text or simpler visual history encodings.
> - Co-training with auxiliary tasks (instruction reasoning, VQA) and collecting non-oracle trajectories are critical for enhancing a VLM's understanding, robustness, and generalization across diverse navigation scenarios.
