# WorldScape Policy 2.0: Empowering Steerable World Action Modeling with Reasoning-Augmented Memory

> [!summary] Summary
> WorldScape Policy 2.0, developed by Manifold AI, Tsinghua University, and Shanghai Jiao Tong University, advances World Action Models by incorporating reasoning-augmented long short-term memory and diverse multimodal control. This system achieved 94.3% average success across 50 bimanual simulation tasks and demonstrated improved real-world performance on long-horizon and memory-dependent robotic manipulation.

## Problem

- Existing World Action Models (WAMs) rely on limited temporal context, hindering global task understanding and performance in long-horizon tasks.
- WAMs suffer from coarse language-video-action grounding due to weak, episode-level linguistic supervision, leading to insensitivity to fine-grained action requests.
- Current WAMs are largely restricted to text-only control interfaces, limiting their applicability for users preferring visual prompts like goal images or video demonstrations.

## Method

- **WorldScape Policy 2.0** integrates a ==reasoning-augmented long short-term memory== system, combining ==causal short-term visual memory== with a Vision-Language Model (VLM)-based ==event memory== for ==latent subgoal reasoning==.
- Developed ==ManipEvent-5M==, an event-grounded multimodal dataset with ==hierarchical captioning==, to enable fine-grained language-video-action grounding.
- Implemented diverse ==multimodal control interfaces==, supporting autonomous planning from high-level instructions, fine-grained text control, goal-image conditioning, and video-demonstration-based adaptation.

## Results

- Achieved an overall average success rate of **94.3%** across **50** bimanual manipulation tasks in simulation, outperforming representative VLA baselines by **14.5%** and WAM baselines by **2.4%**.
- Demonstrated robust real-world performance on dual-arm tasks, including **75%** success on long-horizon autonomous planning (e.g., clothes folding) and **86.7%** on in-domain fine-grained instruction following.
- Ablation studies confirmed that integrating short-term visual memory, long-term event memory, and latent subgoal reasoning cumulatively improved average success, reaching **47.89%** in the clean-to-randomized setting.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Effective long-horizon controllability in WAMs necessitates complementary temporal contexts: semantic event memory for task progress and frame-level visual memory for local interaction dynamics.
> - Fine-grained grounding between event-level intent and atomic actions can be achieved through event-based supervision, overcoming the limitations of episode-level instructions.
> - Enabling multimodal prompting (visual and linguistic) expands the utility of WAMs, fostering enhanced visual-context reasoning and cross-embodiment transfer capabilities.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic learning is progressing from task-specific policies to generalist embodied models designed to interpret task intent, predict action outcomes, and adapt to new tasks based on contextual cues. Within this landscape, Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models have demonstrated the ability to map language and perception to robot actions, often focusing on direct policy prediction. World Action Models (WAMs) represent a complementary paradigm, aiming to jointly model visual state transitions and robot actions. This joint modeling is intended to provide a basis for action planning and context-conditioned adaptation.

Despite advancements, existing WAMs exhibit limitations. Current methods often rely on limited temporal context, typically conditioning on the current observation or a short history window. This can obscure global task progress, especially when different stages of a task share visually similar environments. While approaches like MemoryWAM have attempted to extend access to full visual history, they do not explicitly structure this history around task progress or translate it into a latent subgoal for autonomous planning.

Furthermore, existing WAMs frequently suffer from coarse language-video-action grounding. Embodied datasets often provide weak linguistic supervision, with entire episodes sharing the same task-level instruction, which may lead models to rely on visual shortcuts rather than learning fine-grained alignment between event-level intent, specific state transitions, and atomic actions. This can result in models generating plausible trajectories that are not robust to subtle changes in requested actions.

Finally, current WAMs largely employ text-only control interfaces. This limits their applicability in scenarios where users might naturally express intent through a goal image, a demonstration video, or an example from a different embodiment. Without native visual prompting, WAMs may not fully leverage target-state evidence, object correspondences, or latent task rules, thereby restricting visual-context reasoning and cross-embodiment transfer capabilities.

WorldScape Policy 2.0 addresses these identified limitations by integrating reasoning-augmented long short-term memory and supporting fine-grained multimodal control, positioning itself as an advancement towards more steerable and capable long-horizon robotic manipulation systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a controllable World Action Model, named WorldScape Policy 2.0, that can support both autonomous planning from high-level instructions and controllable execution from fine-grained multimodal prompts.

The motivation for this work arises from three specific challenges observed in existing World Action Models (WAMs):
- **Limited Progress-Aware Memory:** Current WAMs typically use static observations, short-term visual history, or full visual history without mechanisms for progress-aware reasoning or converting historical information into actionable latent subgoal conditions. This hinders performance in long-horizon tasks where understanding completed subgoals and remaining steps is crucial. The paper's insight is that long-horizon controllability requires two complementary forms of temporal context: semantic event memory for reasoning about task progress and frame-level visual memory for preserving local interaction dynamics.
- **Coarse Language-Video-Action Grounding:** Existing embodied datasets often provide only episode-level instructions, leading to weak linguistic supervision for the diverse atomic actions within an episode. This can prevent models from learning precise alignments between event-level intent and specific state changes or action chunks, making them insensitive to fine-grained action requests. The motivation is to establish fine-grained grounding through event-level supervision.
- **Restricted Interaction Interfaces:** Most WAMs are predominantly controlled through text. This limits their utility as users may prefer to specify intent using goal images, video demonstrations, or examples from other embodiments. The objective is to enable diverse multimodal interaction interfaces to enhance visual-context reasoning and cross-embodiment transfer.

By addressing these limitations, WorldScape Policy 2.0 aims to transform WAMs from short-horizon predictive models into controllable, long-horizon policies capable of complex manipulation tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

WorldScape Policy 2.0 is designed as a controllable World Action Model that integrates reasoning-augmented long short-term memory to facilitate autonomous planning and interactive manipulation.

#### 3.1. Model Architecture and Memory Mechanisms

The framework couples a causal short-term visual memory with a long short-term event memory.
- **Causal Short-Term Visual Memory:** This component directly feeds recent observations, represented as VAE-encoded visual latents, into the causal Diffusion Transformer (DiT) as prefill. This mechanism is intended to preserve local interaction dynamics and ensure temporal coherence in video-action prediction. Optional goal images and video demonstrations are also retained as persistent visual context within this pathway.
- **Reasoning-Augmented Long Short-Term Event Memory:** This branch, based on a Vision-Language Model (VLM), processes historical information. For each action chunk under autonomous planning, the VLM encodes the current head-view observation and a task-conditioned planning prompt. It then autoregressively generates discrete planning tokens. These are concatenated with perception tokens to form a "current reasoning latent."
 - **Event Memory Construction:** A queue (`Q_t`) stores historical chunk-level VLM outputs. Perception tokens of historical chunks are compressed into learned gist tokens, while planning tokens are retained. Three memory views are constructed:
 - **Global-History latents (`m_gh`):** Summarize the complete trajectory by fusing the high-level instruction embedding with an averaged representation of the compact full-history bank.
 - **Local-Active latents (`m_la`):** Represent the currently active event by storing full-token anchor representations of the `S_e` most recent completed chunks.
 - **Event-Boundary latents (`m_eb`):** Sparsely preserve major semantic transitions by selecting up to `S_b` chunks with the highest semantic change (cosine distance between averaged tokens) while enforcing minimum temporal separation. These also retain full-token anchor representations.
 - **Event-Memory Retrieval and Gated Fusion:** The three memory views are concatenated with the compact full-history bank. The current reasoning latent queries this concatenated memory through cross-attention. A learned, token-wise sigmoid gate controls the injection of retrieved memory into the current reasoning latent, allowing for selective use of historical context. The resulting memory-enhanced reasoning latent (`q_hat`) acts as an implicit subgoal condition for autonomous planning.

#### 3.2. Multimodal Controllability

WorldScape Policy 2.0 supports diverse interaction modes:
- **Fine-grained Instruction Following:** Segment-level subtask captions are encoded by a T5 model, and their embeddings (`s_t`) directly condition the DiT through cross-attention, providing explicit control over atomic actions.
- **Autonomous Planning:** The model receives only an episode-level high-level instruction. The VLM reasoning branch, augmented by event memory, infers the active subgoal, and the memory-enhanced VLM tokens (`q_hat`) condition the DiT.
- **Goal-Conditioned Policy Learning:** Goal images are encoded as VAE latents and serve as persistent visual prefill in the DiT, allowing the model to infer actions towards a desired visual target.
- **Demonstration-Conditioned In-Context Adaptation:** Video demonstrations are encoded as VAE latents and provided as persistent visual prefill, encouraging the model to infer object correspondences, action patterns, and task rules for cross-embodiment transfer.

#### 3.3. Action Representation and Embodiment Adapters

Robot actions are represented as 10-dimensional chunk-relative delta poses (3D position, 6D continuous rotation) and a 1D absolute gripper command per arm. For dual-arm, this becomes a 20D raw action vector. Each robot embodiment has dedicated action encoders and decoders that project raw actions into the shared DiT token space and map hidden states back to velocity fields. The causal video-action DiT backbone is shared across embodiments.

#### 3.4. ManipEvent-5M: Event-Based Multimodal Dataset

To facilitate event-grounded pretraining, the ManipEvent-5M dataset was constructed. It aggregates human-arm egocentric videos, robot-free UMI data, simulated robot trajectories, and real-robot data. Each trajectory is decomposed into an ordered sequence of events, aligning observations, actions, episode-level instructions, segment-level subtask captions, goal images, and video demonstrations.
- **Action Canonicalization and Human-Hand Retargeting:** Robot trajectories are converted to Cartesian end-effector representations. Human hand poses are retargeted to robot gripper poses to enable joint pretraining.
- **Hierarchical Captioning:** A four-stage procedure using Qwen3-VL generates text prompts:
 1. **Two-stage labeling:** Temporal structure from robot-state signals followed by VLM-based open-vocabulary semantic relabeling to assign coarse primitive priors (move, grasp/release, idle).
 2. **Hierarchical frame sampling:** Episode-level captions are generated from representative timestamps and event lists (hindsight task descriptions). Event-level captions (`step_text`) are generated from uniformly sampled timestamps within each segment, conditioned on the episode-level description and local visual evidence.
 3. **Embodied prompt conditioning:** Annotation guidelines are translated into structured prompt constraints for the VLM, encouraging semantic density (e.g., specifying acting gripper, target object, motion direction).
 4. **Multi-view conflict resolution:** VLM is instructed to prioritize gripper-view evidence over head-view evidence in case of conflicts to mitigate target-object confusion.
- **Goal-Image Prompt Construction:** First-view goal images from event terminal observations provide unambiguous visual targets. Third-view goal images from task-matched human demonstrations require abstracting away the demonstrator and viewpoint.
- **Video-Prompt Construction:** Context-target pairs for human-to-robot and robot-to-robot task transfer are created by matching global tasks, event sequences, and terminal outcomes, promoting embodiment-invariant learning.

#### 3.5. Training Objectives and Three-Stage Curriculum

The model is optimized with a joint video-action flow-matching objective (`L_act` for actions, `L_world` for future visual latents) and, when autonomous planning is active, a semantic forcing objective (`L_sem`).
- **Semantic Forcing:** During autonomous planning training, a trainable projector maps the memory-enhanced reasoning latent (`q_hat`) into the fixed T5 semantic space of the ground-truth fine-grained subtask caption (`s_t`), using cosine similarity as `L_sem`. This aligns latent subgoal reasoning with explicit subtask semantics.
The training follows a three-stage curriculum:
1. **Stage 1 (Event-grounded multimodal WAM pretraining):** The causal video-action backbone is pretrained on ManipEvent-5M using fine-grained captions, goal images, video demonstrations, visual transitions, and robot actions. Short-term causal visual memory is active. Event memory and autonomous latent subgoal reasoning are not yet introduced.
2. **Stage 2 (Memory-aware mid-training):** The VLM planning branch and long short-term event memory are introduced. The model uses high-level instructions as input, constructing `q_hat` through memory retrieval. `L_sem` aligns `q_hat` with fine-grained T5 embeddings.
3. **Stage 3 (Downstream interactive post-training):** The model is post-trained on downstream robot tasks using task-specific interaction modes (e.g., high-level instructions for autonomous planning, T5 embeddings for fine-grained control, visual prompts for adaptation).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of WorldScape Policy 2.0 included extensive experiments in both simulation and real-world environments, along with ablation studies.

#### 4.1. Simulation Benchmark Results (RoboTwin 2.0)

- **Overall Performance:** WorldScape Policy 2.0 achieved an overall average success rate of 94.3% across 50 bimanual manipulation tasks. This included 94.3% under clean conditions and 94.2% under randomized conditions, indicating robust generalization.
- **Comparison with Baselines:**
 - It outperformed representative VLA baselines like $\pi_{0.5}$ by 14.5% in average success.
 - Compared to representative WAM baselines such as Fast-WAM, it improved average success by 2.4%.
 - Against LingBot-VA 2.0, a recent natively pretrained WAM, WorldScape Policy 2.0 demonstrated further improvements of 0.7% in average success.
- **Clean-to-Randomized (C2R) Setting:** In the stricter C2R setting, where models are trained only on clean data but evaluated on both clean and randomized conditions, WorldScape Policy 2.0 achieved the highest average success rate of 47.9%, outperforming $\pi_{0}$, $\pi_{0.5}$, and Fast-WAM by 16.5%, 10.4%, and 8.8% respectively.

#### 4.2. Real-World Evaluation Results (Dual-arm PiPER platform)

Evaluations on five real-world tasks assessed four core capabilities:
- **Long-Horizon Autonomous Planning:** Achieved 75% success on both clothes folding and box folding tasks, outperforming $\pi_{0.5}$ (60-65%) and DreamZero (45-55%).
- **Memory-Dependent Visual Reasoning:** On a video-conditioned shell game task, WorldScape Policy 2.0 reached 75% success, compared to 30% for $\pi_{0.5}$ and 50% for DreamZero.
- **Cross-Embodiment Skill Transfer:** For block stacking, it achieved 60% success with goal-image conditioning and 70% with video-demonstration conditioning, significantly higher than $\pi_{0.5}$ (10-20%) and DreamZero (20%).
- **Sequential Fine-Grained Control:** Demonstrated 80% success on the full table-cleaning sequence driven by successive subtask captions, exceeding $\pi_{0.5}$ (70%) and DreamZero (60%).
- **Fine-Grained Instruction Following:** On atomic table-cleaning instructions in reset scenes, WorldScape Policy 2.0 achieved an in-domain average success rate of 86.7% and an out-of-domain average of 60.0%. The overall average was 73.3%, which was higher than $\pi_{0.5}$ (63.3%) and DreamZero (51.7%).

#### 4.3. Ablation Study (RoboTwin 2.0, clean-only training)

- **Memory and Latent Reasoning Components:**
 - Adding Short-Term Visual Memory (STM) improved the average success rate from 40.91% (no memory) to 44.67%.
 - Further adding Long-Term Event Memory (LTM) increased the average success to 46.25%.
 - Including Latent Subgoal Reasoning (LSR) led to the highest average success rate of 47.89%. This indicates the cumulative benefit of integrating these memory and reasoning components.
- **Training Stages and Semantic Forcing:**
 - Stage-1 event-grounded pretraining alone provided a notable improvement, especially under randomization.
 - Stage-2 memory-aware mid-training, even without semantic forcing, yielded further gains.
 - The complete curriculum, including semantic forcing (SF), achieved the highest average success rate, suggesting that semantic forcing effectively transfers explicit event semantics into latent subgoal reasoning.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

WorldScape Policy 2.0 contributes to the advancement of World Action Models by addressing key limitations of previous approaches. The integration of reasoning-augmented long short-term memory, which combines causal short-term visual context with event-level progress memory and latent subgoal reasoning, represents a step toward more capable robotic manipulation.

The proposed architecture enables a single video-action model to support diverse interaction paradigms, including autonomous planning from high-level instructions, fine-grained text control, goal-image conditioning, and demonstration-based in-context adaptation. This unification of control interfaces is intended to enhance the flexibility and applicability of robotic systems.

The creation of ManipEvent-5M, an event-grounded multimodal dataset with nearly five million segments, provides a scalable resource for training WAMs. This dataset, with its aligned action trajectories, hierarchical text instructions, goal images, and video demonstrations, offers a richer form of supervision that is designed to improve fine-grained language-video-action grounding and facilitate multimodal control.

The three-stage training curriculum, particularly the use of semantic forcing to transfer explicit event semantics into autonomous reasoning, provides a structured approach for developing robust and generalizable manipulation capabilities. The demonstrated performance gains in both simulation and real-world benchmarks, especially in long-horizon tasks, memory-dependent reasoning, and cross-embodiment transfer, indicate the practical utility of this framework.

The work has the potential to impact the development of embodied AI by moving WAMs beyond passive future prediction towards actively controllable, memory-grounded, and multimodally steerable manipulation systems, which could be relevant for complex, adaptive robotic applications.
