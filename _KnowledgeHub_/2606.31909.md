# CoDex: Learning Compositional Dexterous Functional Manipulation without Demonstrations

> [!summary] Summary
> CoDex, from the Robot Interactive Intelligence Lab at the University of Texas at Austin, enables robots to autonomously perform complex compositional dexterous manipulation tasks on unseen objects without human demonstrations. The framework achieved a 73% success rate across six real-world tasks by integrating Vision-Language Model semantic understanding with physics-grounded policy learning.

## Problem

- Integrating high-level semantic understanding with precise low-level physical dexterity for complex functional object manipulation (CD-FOM) tasks.
- The impracticality and labor-intensity of collecting extensive human demonstration data for multi-fingered robotic hands to actuate internal object mechanisms.
- Existing optimization-based methods lack inherent semantic awareness, requiring extensive manual reward engineering or object-specific guidance, while VLMs provide overly abstract guidance for fine-grained actions.

## Method

- A ==zero-demonstration== framework that translates ==VLM== outputs (from language task descriptions and visual observations) into explicit geometric and spatial constraints, such as actuation points and target object poses.
- Employs a two-stage learning pipeline: first, ==analytic constrained optimization== synthesizes diverse, functionally valid grasp candidates, and second, ==reinforcement learning== refines these into robust grasp-move-actuate policies.
- Utilizes a ==unified reward function== during reinforcement learning, combining continuous shaped rewards and binary stage-completion bonuses, to guide policy optimization without task-specific reward engineering.

## Results

- Achieved an overall **73%** task success rate on six previously unseen compositional dexterous functional manipulation tasks in real-world experiments.
- Significantly outperformed baselines, achieving a **40 percentage point** higher success rate than an analytical method (**33%** success) and a **73 percentage point** higher rate than an alternative VLM-guided RL approach (**0%** success).
- Policy learning improved grasp stability by over **12%** and functional actuation success by over **26%** compared to the oracle best analytical grasp candidates, demonstrating enhanced robustness.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Vision-Language Models can effectively guide complex physical manipulation by translating abstract semantic goals into concrete geometric and spatial constraints, bridging the gap between high-level understanding and low-level action.
> - A hybrid approach combining analytical grasp synthesis with reinforcement learning significantly improves robustness for dynamic dexterous manipulation, surpassing the performance of either method alone.
> - Autonomous, zero-demonstration learning for compositional tasks is viable by integrating semantic reasoning with physics-grounded policy generation, reducing reliance on human data for complex robotic skills.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The research addresses the field of Compositional Dexterous Functional Object Manipulation (CD-FOM), a class of tasks requiring a robot to both actuate an object's internal mechanism (e.g., squeezing a trigger) and control its external pose to apply its function to the environment (e.g., aiming a spray bottle at a plant). This domain presents a significant challenge in robotics due to the necessity of integrating semantic understanding with precise physical dexterity.

Historically, robotic manipulation research has explored several avenues, each with limitations regarding CD-FOM tasks:

- **Learning from Demonstration (LfD):** Methods that acquire dexterity from human experts implicitly encode semantic understanding in observed behaviors. However, learning the intricate correlations between semantics and dexterity for multi-fingered hands and internal object mechanisms requires extensive, labor-intensive data collection, often through teleoperation. Approaches using human videos,– alleviate teleoperation but face challenges due to human-robot morphological differences and limited object-specific strategies.
- **Optimization-based Approaches (Reinforcement Learning and Analytical Grasp Synthesis):** Methods like reinforcement learning (RL), and analytical grasp synthesis can achieve the physical dexterity required for CD-FOM without demonstrations. However, these methods typically lack semantic understanding and necessitate external, object-specific guidance, such as careful reward design or optimization objectives,–. This limits their autonomy and generalizability across diverse tasks.
- **Vision-Language Models (VLMs):** Large-scale pre-trained models like VLMs offer general semantic understanding. Early integrations of VLMs into robotic systems, demonstrated limitations in geometric and embodied understanding. Their guidance often remains at a coarse, abstract level, which is insufficient for the precise, coordinated hand-arm motions characteristic of CD-FOM. While some VLM-based systems show promise, they may require extensive training data or are restricted to less intricate manipulations.

The current work, CoDex, aims to bridge the gap between abstract semantic understanding provided by VLMs and the precise physical dexterity required for CD-FOM. It does so by translating VLM outputs into concrete geometric constraints that can guide both constrained optimization and reinforcement learning. This approach distinguishes itself by enabling zero-demonstration learning for complex compositional tasks, directly addressing the limitations of previous methods by integrating semantic reasoning with physics-grounded policy generation. It builds upon prior work in functional grasping,– and compositional manipulation, but seeks to overcome their reliance on demonstrations or lack of holistic arm-hand coordination for internal object actuation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a zero-demonstration framework that can autonomously discover and execute compositional dexterous functional object manipulation (CD-FOM) strategies. This encompasses tasks that require a robot to manipulate an object's internal mechanisms while simultaneously controlling its external pose to achieve a functional outcome in the environment.

The motivation for this work stems from the inherent complexity of CD-FOM tasks and the shortcomings of existing robotic manipulation paradigms:

- **Bridging Semantic Understanding and Physical Dexterity:** CD-FOM tasks demand a tight coupling of high-level semantic reasoning with low-level physical interaction. Robots must understand the object's function (e.g., "spray"), its actuation mode (e.g., "press trigger"), and the target application area (e.g., "on the plant"). Concurrently, they must execute precise physical behaviors, including stable functional grasping, coordinated arm and hand motion, and controlled force application for actuation. This integration is a significant challenge.
- **Limitations of Current Approaches:**
 - **Data Scarcity for Dexterous Manipulation:** Learning from human demonstrations, while effective for some tasks, faces severe practical difficulties for CD-FOM. Collecting sufficient high-quality data through teleoperation of complex multi-fingered hands to actuate objects with internal mechanisms is labor-intensive and often impractical.
 - **Lack of Semantic Awareness in Optimization Methods:** Optimization-based methods, including reinforcement learning and analytical grasp synthesis, excel at achieving physical dexterity. However, they typically lack an inherent understanding of task semantics. This necessitates extensive manual reward engineering or object-specific guidance, which limits their autonomy and generalizability to novel objects or tasks.
 - **Abstractness of VLM Outputs:** While Vision-Language Models offer powerful zero-shot semantic understanding, their outputs are often too abstract to directly inform precise robotic actions. They can interpret high-level goals but struggle to provide the geometric and embodied details required for intricate hand-arm motions.
- **Enabling Autonomous Skill Discovery:** The goal is to move beyond methods that require explicit human programming or demonstrations for each new task or object. By allowing robots to autonomously derive manipulation strategies from semantic inputs, the framework aims to enhance robotic versatility and reduce deployment effort.

To address these motivations, CoDex specifically aims to:
1. **Utilize Vision-Language Models (VLMs)** to infer semantic constraints from language descriptions and visual observations of the task and scene.
2. **Translate these semantic constraints into explicit geometric and spatial conditions** that can guide subsequent learning processes.
3. **Employ a two-stage learning pipeline:** first, analytic constrained optimization to generate functionally valid grasp candidates, and second, reinforcement learning to refine these candidates into robust, complete grasp-move-actuate policies.
4. **Achieve sim-to-real transfer** for learned policies, enabling real-world execution without human demonstrations.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

CoDex is a zero-demonstration framework designed to generate manipulation strategies for Compositional Dexterous Functional Object Manipulation (CD-FOM) tasks. The pipeline consists of two main stages: VLM-Generated Semantic Constraints and Constraint-Guided Policy Training.

**A. VLM-Generated Semantic Constraints**
This initial stage translates a language task description ($L$) and an RGB-D scene observation ($I$) into explicit semantic task constraints.

1. **Visual Task Parsing:**
 - The functional object is identified using open-vocabulary segmentation (LangSAM).
 - A 3D mesh ($M$) of the segmented object is reconstructed using a shape reconstruction and completion method (Tripo).
 - The reconstructed mesh is scaled to match heights derived from point clouds.

2. **Local Semantic Constraints:**
 - A VLM is queried to generate text descriptions of key task-relevant interaction points (e.g., "trigger," "nozzle").
 - These text descriptions are linked to 2D image pixels using MoLMo.
 - The 2D pixels are unprojected into 3D by aligning the image with the reconstructed mesh via an image-based tracker (FoundationPose), yielding the Actuation Point ($p_{act}$) and Function Point ($p_{fnc}$).
 - The actuation direction ($d_{act}$) is assumed to be parallel to the surface normal at $p_{act}$, pointing into the object.

3. **Global Semantic Constraints (VLM-Guided Cross-Entropy Method - VLM-CEM):**
 - This algorithm, inspired by, iteratively searches for the object's target 6D pose ($T^*$).
 - It operates for $N$ rounds, proposing $K$ new candidate poses at each round.
 - The VLM is prompted with a history ($H$) of previously scored candidate poses to propose new candidates.
 - These candidates are rendered, scored by the VLM, added to $H$, and an elite subset is retained.
 - The process converges on a pose that is functionally correct and physically grounded, representing the goal pose $T^*$.

**B. Constraint-Guided Policy Training**
This stage leverages the VLM-generated semantic constraints to learn a complete grasp-move-actuate policy ($\pi$).

1. **Analytic Constrained Optimization:**
 - This phase synthesizes a diverse set of functionally valid grasp candidates, serving as structured initializations for reinforcement learning.
 - Initial hand configurations ($q_0$) are sampled from the valid joint space using inverse kinematics, biasing a finger towards $p_{act}$.
 - These samples are refined through constrained optimization (Equation 1), which aims to maximize grasp robustness (measured by the min-weight force closure metric $l^*(q)$,).
 - The optimization is subject to various constraints:
 - Minimum force closure ($l^*(q) \geq l_{min}$).
 - Surface contact for all contact points.
 - Collision avoidance.
 - Actuation point proximity (fingertip within $\delta_{dist}$ of $p_{act}$).
 - Actuation alignment (fingerpad normal aligns with $-d_{act}$).
 - If optimization fails, $q_0$ is resampled and the process restarts. This yields a set of feasible, function-aligned grasp candidates.

2. **Constraint-Guided Reinforcement Learning (RL):**
 - The goal is to learn a dynamically robust motion sequence for the entire task. The analytic candidates significantly reduce the exploration space for RL.
 - **Policy Parametrization:** The learned policy $\pi$ receives an observation $o$ (relative hand pose, candidate finger joints, actuation finger index). It outputs an action vector $a \in \mathbb{R}^{38}$ that parameterizes a multi-stage motion primitive by defining targets relative to an input candidate $q_{cand}$. The action vector includes:
 - Target Grasp Joint Offsets ($\Delta j_{grasp} \in \mathbb{R}^{16}$).
 - Target Pre-Grasp Joint Percentages ($p_{pregrasp} \in \mathbb{R}^{16}$).
 - Residual Hand Pose Offset ($\Delta T_{hand} \in \mathbb{R}^6$).
 - **Motion Primitive:** The action parameters guide a structured, multi-stage motion primitive:
 - Move hand to a pre-contact pose near the object.
 - Transition to the final grasp pose.
 - Fingers close to secure the object.
 - Move the object towards the global goal $T^*$ while simultaneously performing actuation.
 - **Reward Function:** The policy is trained using PPO to maximize a unified reward function $R$, avoiding task-specific reward engineering. $R$ is a normalized weighted sum of:
 - **Continuous shaped rewards ($R_k$):** grasp stability (penalizing object shifting), lifting (raising object while keeping it upright), actuation alignment (minimizing distance between acting finger and $p_{act}, d_{act}$), and directional force (applying sufficient force along $d_{act}$).
 - **Binary stage-completion bonuses ($S_k$).**
 - **Final task success bonus ($S_{success}$).**
 - A penalty resets the reward to zero if significant grasp displacement occurs.
 - **Training and Deployment:** The online training process converges in approximately one hour using MANISKILL3 simulation with 2,048 parallel environments. The trained RL policy is then executed on a real robot: a Franka arm equipped with a 16-DoF LEAP Hand.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of CoDex focused on its ability to bridge high-level semantic understanding with low-level, physics-grounded execution across six CD-FOM tasks with previously unseen objects.

**Q1: Performance on Compositional Dexterous Functional Manipulation Tasks (Real World)**
- **Overall Success Rate:** CoDex achieved an overall task success rate of 73% across 7-DoF robot arm and 16-DoF multi-fingered hand experiments. A trial was considered successful if both correct object movement to the required pose and correct internal mechanism actuation were achieved, without other failures.
- **Baseline Comparison:**
 - CoDex significantly outperformed the **Analytical+VLM-CEM** baseline, which achieved 33% success. This baseline used the best analytical grasp combined with CoDex's VLM-CEM global constraints. The improvement highlights CoDex's policy learning stage for increasing robustness during actuation. Analytical grasps were often sufficient for lifting but prone to in-hand shifts that disrupted precise actuation.
 - The **RL+PIVOT** baseline achieved a 0% overall success rate. While it sometimes triggered internal mechanisms, it consistently failed to execute correct object movement because PIVOT’s generated global constraints did not meet task requirements (e.g., misaiming the spray bottle).
- **Failure Analysis:**
 - **Semantic Constraint Generation Failures (23.3% of failed trials):** Primarily related to global constraint prediction ($T^*$). Predicted goal poses could appear plausible in 2D renderings but be spatially incorrect in 3D (e.g., misaligned flashlight aim) or jointly infeasible with the grasp due to joint limits or collisions.
 - **Dexterous Functional Manipulation Failures (23.3% of trials):** Attributed to sim-to-real discrepancies (reconstructed geometry, friction, density, deformability) and actuation inaccuracy. High-precision tasks, such as pressing a small deformable button on a flashlight, consistently failed due to the robot's finger size and control limitations. Less frequently, grasp instability (e.g., salt grinder toppling) occurred.

**Q2: Quality of VLM-CEM Generated Constraints (Human Study)**
- A human study involving twenty participants evaluated the quality of goal poses generated by VLM-CEM and three baselines (VLM-CEM (Dir.), PIVOT (SE3), PIVOT (Trans.)) on a five-point scale.
- **Superiority of VLM-CEM:** Both CoDex's VLM-CEM variants (with and without directional exploration restriction) were rated significantly higher than the PIVOT baselines (Wilcoxon signed-rank test, p < 0.02).
- **Directional Constraint Benefit:** For tasks like "spray plant," VLM-CEM with directional exploration scored higher, suggesting that restricting translation along the functional axis effectively filters out functionally misaligned poses that might otherwise score well in 2D renderings.
- **Baseline Limitations:** PIVOT's image-space perturbations frequently introduced lateral or depth offsets, leading to less functionally appropriate goal poses.

**Q3: Benefits of Constraint-Guided RL Policy Learning**
- This experiment isolated the contribution of compositional policy learning by comparing its performance against direct execution of analytical grasps (average and oracle best candidates from constrained optimization).
- **Improved Grasp Stability:** Policy learning significantly improved grasp stability, exceeding the average analytical grasp performance by over 36% and surpassing the oracle best analytical grasp by over 12%.
- **Enhanced Functional Actuation:** The learned policy achieved 60% higher actuation success than the average analytical grasp candidate and over 26% higher success than the best possible initial grasp candidate (oracle).
- **Robustness for Challenging Objects:** For the challenging salt grinder, where no initial analytical grasp achieved stable actuation, CoDex's policy learning successfully discovered a stable grasp, demonstrating the value of its holistic, simulation-based refinement for dynamically robust policies.

These findings collectively indicate that CoDex's integration of VLM-derived semantic constraints with a two-stage, physics-grounded policy learning process is effective. The VLM-CEM module produces high-quality goal poses, and the subsequent constraint-guided RL significantly enhances both grasp stability and the robustness of functional actuation compared to analytical methods alone.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The work presented in "CoDex: Learning Compositional Dexterous Functional Manipulation without Demonstrations" carries several points of significance and potential impact within the field of robotics:

- **Zero-Demonstration Autonomous Skill Discovery:** A core contribution is the framework's ability to autonomously discover and execute complex dexterous manipulation strategies without requiring human demonstrations. This addresses a major bottleneck in robotic skill acquisition, as collecting high-quality demonstration data for multi-fingered hands and internal object actuations is labor-intensive and often impractical.
- **Bridging Semantic Reasoning and Physical Dexterity:** CoDex effectively integrates high-level semantic understanding from Vision-Language Models (VLMs) with low-level, physics-grounded execution. By translating abstract VLM outputs into concrete geometric constraints, the framework enables robots to interpret task goals semantically and then physically realize those goals with precise movements and forces. This represents a key step in bridging the cognitive and physical aspects of robotic intelligence.
- **Enhanced Robot Versatility and Generalization:** The method's ability to operate with previously unseen objects and tasks demonstrates a significant step towards more versatile and generalizable robotic systems. By deriving functional grasps and target poses from semantic cues rather than pre-programmed knowledge or extensive training data for each object, robots can adapt to novel situations and tools more readily.
- **Improved Robustness through Policy Learning:** The research highlights the critical role of reinforcement learning in refining statically plausible analytical grasps into dynamically robust manipulation policies. This iterative, simulation-based refinement process is shown to significantly improve grasp stability and, critically, the reliability of functional actuation in real-world scenarios, surpassing the performance of even oracle-selected analytical grasps.
- **Advancement in Functional Object Manipulation:** CoDex specifically tackles Compositional Dexterous Functional Object Manipulation (CD-FOM), a challenging class of tasks that requires coordinated control of both an object's internal and external degrees of freedom. By providing a holistic solution to the grasp-move-actuate problem, the work pushes the boundaries of what robots can achieve in tool use and object interaction.

**Potential Impact:**

- **Broadened Applications for Robotics:** The capabilities demonstrated by CoDex could open doors for robotic deployment in domains requiring complex object interaction, such as domestic assistance, industrial assembly involving tools, and even assistive robotics where functional manipulation of various objects is essential.
- **Foundation for More Intuitive Robot Programming:** By allowing task goals to be specified through natural language and visual cues, the framework contributes to a paradigm where robots can be "programmed" more intuitively by non-experts, reducing the need for specialized robotics expertise.
- **Future Research Directions:** The limitations identified, such as challenges with pinpoint contact and tasks requiring sustained coupled arm-hand motion or multi-point actuation, provide clear avenues for future research. This includes incorporating richer sensory feedback (e.g., tactile), developing trajectory-level constraints, and exploring more complex VLM dialogues for nuanced task understanding. This ongoing work will further enhance the precision and adaptability of autonomous manipulation.

In summary, CoDex represents a significant advancement by combining cutting-edge VLM capabilities with robust, physics-informed policy learning to enable autonomous, zero-demonstration functional manipulation. This integration is crucial for the development of robots that are more intelligent, versatile, and capable of operating in unstructured, dynamic environments.
