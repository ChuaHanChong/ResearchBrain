# AgentGym: Evolving Large Language Model-based Agents across Diverse Environments

> [!summary] Summary
> AGENTGYM provides a framework for evolving Large Language Model-based agents across diverse environments by integrating initial behavioral cloning with a self-evolution method called AGENTEVOL. This approach enables agents to generalize and adapt to new tasks, demonstrating improved performance and efficiency in various simulated settings compared to imitation-only baselines.

## Problem

- Existing LLM-based agent approaches largely depend on costly expert-provided trajectories for behavioral cloning, limiting scalability and scope.
- Current self-improvement methods for LLM agents are often confined to isolated environments, resulting in specialized agents that struggle to generalize across diverse tasks and real-world scenarios.

## Method

- **AGENTGYM** is introduced as a comprehensive platform for training, evaluating, and evolving LLM-based agents across diverse environments including web navigation, embodied tasks, and tool use.
- The framework employs a two-stage learning process: initially equipping agents with basic instruction-following abilities through ==behavioral cloning== from expert trajectories (==AGENTTRAJ==), followed by continuous evolution.
- **AGENTEVOL**, a new evolution method, alternates between an exploration step where agents interact with environments to generate trajectories and a learning step where policies are updated based on a ==reward-weighted objective==.

## Results

- Agents evolved with AGENTEVOL consistently outperformed those trained solely with behavioral cloning, demonstrating performance improvements across various environments.
- Evolved agents exhibited enhanced generalization, showing improved performance even in environments not explicitly used during the evolution phase.
- The evolution process led to more efficient task completion, with agents requiring fewer interaction rounds and displaying improved reasoning and action selection, as evidenced in WebShop and ALFWorld tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Combining an initial phase of behavioral cloning with a subsequent self-evolution stage enables agents to acquire basic skills and then adapt to diverse, real-world tasks.
> - AGENTEVOL, a novel evolution method, effectively handles sparse rewards and maintains stability across varied environments by leveraging a weighted negative log-likelihood objective for policy updates.
> - A comprehensive framework with standardized APIs facilitates the integration of diverse environments and efficient data collection, promoting the development of generalist LLM-based agents.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

#### 1.1. Introduction

Large Language Models (LLMs) have shown remarkable capabilities for building autonomous agents that can understand natural language instructions and interact with their environments. However, most current approaches to developing LLM-based agents face significant limitations: they either rely on costly expert-provided trajectories for behavioral cloning or focus on self-improvement in isolated environments, leading to specialized agents that struggle to generalize across diverse tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

#### 2.1. AGENTGYM's Objectives: A Two-Stage Evolution Framework

The research paper "AGENTGYM: Evolving Large Language Model-based Agents across Diverse Environments" introduces a novel framework that addresses these limitations by enabling LLM-based agents to evolve and generalize across a variety of environments. This approach draws inspiration from human learning, where individuals first acquire basic skills through imitation and then continuously adapt to new situations through interaction and experience.

As shown in Figure 1, AGENTGYM implements a two-stage learning process: first equipping agents with basic capabilities through behavioral cloning from expert trajectories (like a student learning from a teacher), then allowing them to explore and evolve across diverse "wild" environments based on environmental feedback, gradually transforming from weak to strong agents.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

#### 3.1. The AGENTGYM Framework

AGENTGYM provides a comprehensive platform for training, evaluating, and evolving LLM-based agents. The framework is built on three key components:

1. **Diverse Environments**: A collection of environments spanning different domains and difficulty levels, including web navigation, embodied tasks, text games, and tool use.

2. **Trajectory Set with Basic Capabilities**: High-quality interactive trajectories (AGENTTRAJ) to equip base agents with instruction-following abilities.

3. **Effective Evolution Method**: AGENTEVOL, a novel approach that balances exploration and learning across diverse environments.

As illustrated in Figure 2, AGENTGYM integrates a wide range of environments through a standardized API, facilitating the development of generalist agents. The platform includes web-based environments (WebShop, WebArena), embodied tasks (ALFWorld, ScienceWorld, BabyAI), games (TextCraft, MAZE, Wordle), and tool-using tasks (Weather, Todo, Academia, Movie, Sheet).

#### 3.2. AGENTEVOL: A New Evolution Method

AGENTEVOL is the core contribution of this research—a novel method for evolving LLM-based agents across diverse environments. Unlike traditional reinforcement learning approaches that can be unstable and inefficient in multi-environment settings, AGENTEVOL draws inspiration from the connection between RL and probabilistic inference.

The method consists of alternating between two key steps:

1. **Exploration Step**: The agent interacts with environments to generate trajectories and receive rewards based on task completion.

2. **Learning Step**: The agent updates its policy based on collected trajectories, using a weighted negative log-likelihood objective:

$$\mathcal{L}(\theta) = -\sum_{\tau \in \mathcal{D}} w(\tau) \log p_\theta(\tau)$$

where $w(\tau)$ is a weight function based on the trajectory's reward, and $p_\theta(\tau)$ is the probability of generating the trajectory under the current policy.

This approach has several advantages:
- It handles sparse rewards more effectively than traditional RL
- It leverages the generalization capabilities of LLMs
- It maintains stability across diverse environments with varying difficulty levels

As shown in Figure 3, performance consistently improves across iterations for most environments, though the rate of improvement varies. This demonstrates AGENTEVOL's ability to foster continual learning and adaptation.

#### 3.3. Environments and Data Collection

AGENTGYM integrates a diverse set of environments that challenge agents across different domains:

1. **Web Navigation**: Tasks like online shopping (WebShop) and general web interaction (WebArena)
2. **Embodied Tasks**: Navigating and manipulating objects in simulated environments (ALFWorld, ScienceWorld, BabyAI)
3. **Text Games**: Strategic text-based games (TextCraft, MAZE)
4. **Tool-Using Tasks**: Using tools to accomplish specific goals (Weather, Todo, Movie, Academia)
5. **Coding Tasks**: SQL generation and debugging (BIRD-SQL)

For each environment, the researchers collected and expanded instructions using crowdsourcing and AI-based methods (self-instruct, instruction evolution). They then constructed the AGENTEVAL benchmark by selecting a diverse and challenging subset of these instructions.

High-quality interactive trajectories (AGENTTRAJ and AGENTTRAJ-L) were collected using state-of-the-art models and crowdsourced annotations. These trajectories were used to train a base agent through behavioral cloning, equipping it with basic instruction-following abilities before the evolution phase.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

#### 4.1. Experimental Results

The experiments demonstrated that AGENTEVOL significantly outperforms baseline methods and achieves comparable or better results than state-of-the-art models in several environments.

Key findings include:

1. **Evolution Improves Performance**: Agents evolved with AGENTEVOL consistently outperformed those trained only with behavioral cloning.

2. **Generalization Across Environments**: The evolved agents demonstrated the ability to transfer knowledge across different environments, showing improved performance even in environments not explicitly used during training.

3. **Efficiency of Interaction**: Evolved agents required fewer interaction rounds to complete tasks successfully.

4. **Improved Reasoning and Action Selection**: The evolution process enhanced the agent's ability to reason about task requirements and select appropriate actions.

Figures 5 and 6 provide concrete examples of how evolution improves agent behavior:

In Figure 5, we see how evolution improves the agent's shopping behavior. Before evolution, the agent selects a product that fails to meet the price requirement ($63.05 vs. the required <$50.00). After evolution, the agent selects a suitable product ($16.79) and completes all required steps to purchase it.

Figure 6 shows a similar improvement in the embodied ALFWorld environment. Before evolution, the agent wanders around with repetitive "move forward" actions until reaching the maximum interaction rounds. After evolution, it efficiently checks available actions, turns left to explore, and directly picks up the green box once it's visible.

#### 4.2. Comparison with Existing Approaches

AGENTGYM and AGENTEVOL offer several advantages over existing approaches to developing LLM-based agents:

1. **Compared to Behavioral Cloning (BC)**:
 - BC relies heavily on expert demonstrations, which are costly to obtain and limited in scope
 - BC-trained agents struggle to handle situations not covered in the training data
 - AGENTEVOL uses BC only as a starting point, then evolves beyond the limitations of the initial training data

2. **Compared to Reinforcement Learning (RL)**:
 - Traditional RL methods like PPO and DPO can be unstable in multi-environment settings
 - RL often requires dense reward signals, which are not always available
 - AGENTEVOL handles sparse rewards more effectively and maintains stability across diverse environments

3. **Compared to Environment-Specific Agents**:
 - Most existing agents are specialized for specific environments
 - AGENTGYM facilitates the development of generalist agents that can operate across diverse environments
 - The framework enables knowledge transfer between environments

The paper demonstrates that while behavioral cloning with large trajectory sets (AGENTTRAJ-L) provides a strong baseline, AGENTEVOL consistently improves performance beyond what can be achieved through imitation alone.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

#### 5.1. Practical Applications and Case Studies

The evolution of LLM-based agents across diverse environments has numerous practical applications:

1. **Personal Assistants**: Evolved agents can help users with a wide range of tasks, from searching the web to managing calendars and interacting with various applications.

2. **Customer Service**: Agents can assist customers across different platforms and handle a variety of queries without requiring specialized training for each scenario.

3. **Education**: Agents can adapt to different educational contexts and provide personalized assistance to students across subjects.

4. **Research and Development**: The framework can accelerate the development and testing of AI agents in simulated environments before deployment in real-world settings.

The case studies presented in Figures 5 and 6 illustrate how evolved agents can improve at practical tasks like online shopping and object manipulation. These examples demonstrate the potential for agents to learn and adapt to user needs across different contexts.

#### 5.2. Limitations and Future Work

Despite its promising results, AGENTGYM has several limitations that point to directions for future research:

1. **Computational Requirements**: Training and evolving agents across multiple environments requires significant computational resources.

2. **Limited Environment Diversity**: While AGENTGYM includes a variety of environments, it still represents only a fraction of the diversity of real-world tasks.

3. **Evaluation Challenges**: Measuring agent performance across diverse environments with different metrics remains challenging.

4. **Environmental Complexity**: The current environments are simplified compared to the complexity of real-world scenarios.

Future work could address these limitations by:

- Incorporating more diverse and complex environments
- Developing more efficient evolution methods that require fewer computational resources
- Designing better evaluation metrics for generalist agents
- Integrating multimodal capabilities (vision, audio) into the evolution framework
- Exploring methods for continual learning that prevent forgetting of previously acquired skills

#### 5.3. Conclusion

AGENTGYM represents a significant step toward building generally capable LLM-based agents. By providing a framework for training and evolving agents across diverse environments, it addresses the limitations of existing approaches that rely on behavioral cloning or focus on isolated self-improvement.

The key innovations of AGENTGYM include:

1. A comprehensive platform integrating diverse environments with standardized APIs
2. AGENTEVOL, a novel method for agent evolution that balances exploration and learning
3. AGENTEVAL, a benchmark suite for evaluating agent performance across environments
4. High-quality trajectory datasets (AGENTTRAJ and AGENTTRAJ-L) for initial agent training

Experimental results demonstrate that agents evolved with AGENTEVOL consistently outperform those trained only with behavioral cloning and achieve comparable or better results than state-of-the-art models in several environments.

As AI continues to advance, frameworks like AGENTGYM will play a crucial role in developing generally capable agents that can assist humans across a wide range of tasks and domains. The ability to evolve and generalize across diverse environments is a key stepping stone toward more adaptable and useful AI systems.
