# Natural Humanoid Robot Locomotion with Generative Motion Prior

> [!summary] Summary
> The Generative Motion Prior (GMP) framework enables humanoid robots to achieve natural, human-like locomotion by providing fine-grained, trajectory-level guidance from a pre-trained generative model. This approach demonstrates significantly higher motion naturalness, with a JFID of 0.931 compared to 2.088 for the best adversarial baseline, while maintaining robust velocity tracking on both simulated and physical robots.

## Problem

- Existing humanoid locomotion methods (Model Predictive Control, Reinforcement Learning) often result in stiff, mechanical, and unnatural movements despite prioritizing stability.
- Adversarial Motion Prior (AMP) methods suffer from instability, training difficulties (e.g., mode collapse), and provide ambiguous scalar style rewards that lack fine-grained guidance.
- A need existed for a stable and precise supervision mechanism to guide complex whole-body movements towards detailed human-like fluidity.

## Method

- ==Developed Generative Motion Prior (GMP)==, a frozen Conditional Variational Auto-encoder (CVAE) pre-trained offline to synthesize future, fine-grained, trajectory-level reference motions for the robot.
- Integrated the GMP as an online motion generator, providing detailed guidance (joint angles, keypoint positions) to a Reinforcement Learning (RL) control policy.
- Trained the RL policy using Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) with a composite reward function combining motion guidance from GMP, task rewards for velocity tracking, and regularization.

## Results

- Achieved superior motion naturalness across all metrics, with a Joint Fréchet Inception Distance (JFID) of **0.931** and Keypoint FID (KFID) of **0.004**, significantly outperforming the best AMP baseline (JFID **2.088**, KFID **0.021**).
- Maintained competitive velocity tracking performance (Mean Episode Linear Velocity Tracking Reward of **2.449**) while generating more natural gaits compared to pure RL methods.
- Demonstrated successful sim-to-real transfer, enabling a full-sized NAVIAI humanoid robot to exhibit robust and natural locomotion, closely resembling human movement characteristics, in real-world environments.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Replacing scalar adversarial style rewards with stable, fine-grained, trajectory-level guidance from a generative model effectively overcomes instability and ambiguity in learning natural human-like movements.
> - Offline pre-training and freezing a generative model creates a robust and detailed online supervision system for RL policies, enhancing training stability and interpretability.
> - Achieving natural human-like locomotion through this method is crucial for improving human-robot interaction, social acceptance, and expanding the practical deployment of humanoid robots.
