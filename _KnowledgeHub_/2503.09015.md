# Natural Humanoid Robot Locomotion with Generative Motion Prior

> [!summary] Summary
> The Generative Motion Prior (GMP) framework enables humanoid robots to achieve natural, human-like locomotion by providing fine-grained, trajectory-level guidance from a pre-trained generative model. This approach demonstrates significantly higher motion naturalness, with a JFID of 0.931 compared to 2.088 for the best adversarial baseline, while maintaining robust velocity tracking on both simulated and physical robots.

## Problem

- Existing humanoid locomotion methods (Model Predictive Control, Reinforcement Learning) often result in stiff, mechanical, and unnatural movements despite prioritizing stability.
- Adversarial Motion Prior (AMP) methods suffer from instability, training difficulties (e.g., mode collapse), and provide ambiguous scalar style rewards that lack fine-grained guidance.
- A need existed for a stable and precise supervision mechanism to guide complex whole-body movements towards detailed human-like fluidity.

## Method

- ==Developed Generative Motion Prior (GMP)==, a frozen Conditional Variational Auto-encoder (CVAE) pre-trained offline to synthesize future, fine-grained, trajectory-level reference motions for the robot.
- Integrated the GMP as an online motion generator, providing detailed guidance (joint angles, keypoint positions) to a Reinforcement Learning (RL) control policy.
- Trained the RL policy using Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) with a composite reward function combining motion guidance from GMP, task rewards for velocity tracking, and regularization.

## Results

- Achieved superior motion naturalness across all metrics, with a Joint Fréchet Inception Distance (JFID) of **0.931** and Keypoint FID (KFID) of **0.004**, significantly outperforming the best AMP baseline (JFID **2.088**, KFID **0.021**).
- Maintained competitive velocity tracking performance (Mean Episode Linear Velocity Tracking Reward of **2.449**) while generating more natural gaits compared to pure RL methods.
- Demonstrated successful sim-to-real transfer, enabling a full-sized NAVIAI humanoid robot to exhibit robust and natural locomotion, closely resembling human movement characteristics, in real-world environments.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Replacing scalar adversarial style rewards with stable, fine-grained, trajectory-level guidance from a generative model effectively overcomes instability and ambiguity in learning natural human-like movements.
> - Offline pre-training and freezing a generative model creates a robust and detailed online supervision system for RL policies, enhancing training stability and interpretability.
> - Achieving natural human-like locomotion through this method is crucial for improving human-robot interaction, social acceptance, and expanding the practical deployment of humanoid robots.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of humanoid robots capable of natural and lifelike locomotion is a cornerstone challenge in robotics, crucial for their integration into human-centric environments and effective interaction with society. This paper addresses a significant gap in the existing literature by tackling the problem of generating natural, human-like gaits for humanoid robots, a capability often overlooked by current control methods.

Historically, humanoid locomotion has been approached through several paradigms:
- **Model Predictive Control (MPC):** These methods, such as those discussed in, rely on precise mathematical models to optimize control variables for whole-body balance. While effective for stability and fundamental movement, MPC-based approaches often result in stiff, mechanical, and unnatural movements because they typically prioritize physical constraints and stability over aesthetic or human-like fluidity.
- **Reinforcement Learning (RL):** RL-based methods, exemplified by works like, learn control policies by interacting with simulation environments and maximizing reward signals. These methods have shown remarkable success in enabling robots to perform complex manipulation and locomotion tasks, adapt to varied terrains, and recover from disturbances. However, like MPC, pure RL approaches often neglect the *naturalness* of motion, leading to characteristic "bent-leg gaits" or other robotic movements that lack human fluidity, as seen in the quantitative results for "SaW" and "PBRS" baselines in Table II.
- **Imitation Learning and Adversarial Motion Priors (AMP):** To imbue robots with human-like motion, researchers turned to imitation learning, using human motion data as a reference. A notable advancement in this area is the Adversarial Motion Prior (AMP) framework, first introduced in for physically simulated characters and then adapted for humanoid robots in works like. AMP leverages a discriminator, similar to Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs), to distinguish between real human (or retargeted human) motions and robot-generated motions. This discriminator provides a "style reward" to the RL policy, encouraging the robot to learn motions that appear human-like.

Despite the promise of AMP, it suffers from well-known drawbacks inherent to adversarial training, including **instability** (e.g., mode collapse, training difficulties where the discriminator fails to provide effective guidance) and the **ambiguity of scalar style rewards**. The discriminator provides a single scalar value indicating how "human-like" a motion segment is, which lacks the fine-grained, detailed guidance necessary to precisely shape complex whole-body movements. This makes it challenging to achieve consistent, high-quality human-like locomotion across diverse scenarios.

This paper's proposed Generative Motion Prior (GMP) directly addresses these limitations. Instead of an adversarial discriminator providing an ambiguous scalar reward, the GMP utilizes a *frozen generative model* (a CVAE) that is pre-trained to synthesize *future, fine-grained, trajectory-level reference motions* for the robot. This novel approach transforms the task from inferring "style" to tracking a dynamically generated, precise motion trajectory. By pre-training the generative model offline and keeping it frozen during RL policy training, the GMP framework achieves significantly enhanced stability and provides detailed, interpretable supervision at the motion level (joint angles, keypoint positions). This positions the work as a robust evolution beyond adversarial methods, pushing the frontier of natural humanoid locomotion towards more reliable and adaptable solutions.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to enable **natural and lifelike locomotion for humanoid robots**, a fundamental capability required for their seamless integration and effective interaction within human society. The authors are motivated by the current limitations in humanoid robot control that prevent them from exhibiting truly human-like movements.

Specifically, the key motivations and objectives can be broken down as follows:
1. **Address the lack of naturalness in existing locomotion methods:** Traditional approaches, including Model Predictive Control (MPC) and many Reinforcement Learning (RL) techniques, prioritize stability and task completion but often result in robotic movements that are mechanical, stiff, and perceptibly unnatural (e.g., "bent-leg gaits"). The authors aim to overcome this deficiency.
2. **Overcome the instability and ambiguity of Adversarial Motion Prior (AMP) methods:** While AMP-based approaches attempt to imbue human-like styles by learning from human motion data, they are plagued by the inherent instability of adversarial training, which can lead to difficulties in convergence and mode collapse. Furthermore, the scalar "style reward" provided by a discriminator lacks the granularity and interpretability needed to guide the robot's whole-body movements precisely. The authors seek a more stable and effective method for leveraging human motion data.
3. **Develop a novel supervision mechanism for RL policies:** The core motivation is to introduce a new form of guidance that provides **fine-grained, motion-level supervision** to the RL policy during training. This supervision should be precise, comprehensive, and stable, guiding the robot towards natural human-like motion patterns at the trajectory level, encompassing details like joint angles and keypoint positions.
4. **Enhance training stability and interpretability:** By utilizing a frozen, pre-trained generative model as an online motion generator, the authors aim to improve the stability of the training process, avoiding the common pitfalls of co-training an RL policy with a discriminator. The explicit trajectory-level guidance also improves the interpretability of how the robot learns natural motions.
5. **Achieve state-of-the-art performance in motion naturalness:** The ultimate goal is to demonstrate that the proposed method can achieve superior motion naturalness in both simulated and real-world environments compared to existing state-of-the-art techniques, while maintaining robust task performance (e.g., velocity tracking).

In essence, the research aims to bridge the gap between functional robot locomotion and human-like naturalness, paving the way for more socially acceptable and capable humanoid robots.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology introduces a novel framework called Generative Motion Prior (GMP) to achieve natural humanoid robot locomotion. The approach is primarily divided into three stages: whole-body motion retargeting, generative motion prior training, and motion-guided humanoid control, as illustrated in Fig. 2.

**A. Whole-body Motion Retargeting:**
The first step involves transforming natural human motion data into a format suitable for the humanoid robot. This process, extended from previous work, is formulated as an optimization problem designed to transfer human motion to the robot while preserving motion similarity and adhering to kinematic robot constraints. The objective function minimizes three types of losses:
- **Vector Similarity Loss ($L_{vec}$):** Encourages key joint vectors (e.g., shoulder-to-elbow, hip-to-knee) to maintain consistent directions between human and robot motions.
- **Foot Contact Loss ($L_{foot}$):** Ensures the robot's feet maintain the same contact relationships with the ground as the human reference, crucial for stable bipedal locomotion.
- **Motion Smoothness Loss ($L_{smo}$):** Penalizes high joint angle accelerations to ensure the retargeted robot motions are smooth and fluid.
This offline process generates a "Robot Reference Motion Dataset" from the initial "Human Motion Dataset."

**B. Generative Motion Prior (GMP) Training:**
Once the robot reference motion dataset is prepared, a generative model, referred to as the Generative Motion Prior (GMP), is trained offline. This model learns to predict future human-like robot motion trajectories.
- **Conditional Variational Auto-encoder (CVAE):** The core of the GMP is a CVAE that models the probabilistic distribution of the next-frame robot pose ($m_{t+1}$) conditioned on the current-frame robot pose ($m_t$). A robot pose $m_t$ is a comprehensive representation, including base linear/angular velocity, joint angles, keypoint positions, and keypoint velocities.
 - **Motion Encoder ($f_\theta$):** Maps the next-frame robot pose ($m_{t+1}$) and current pose ($m_t$) into a latent space (a multivariate Gaussian distribution).
 - **Motion Decoder ($f_\phi$):** Reconstructs the predicted next-frame robot pose ($\hat{m}_{t+1}$) from the latent vector ($z_{t+1}$) and current pose ($m_t$).
- **Training Objective:** The CVAE is trained to minimize a combination of motion reconstruction loss (Mean Squared Error between predicted and actual motion) and KL divergence loss (to regularize the latent space). Scheduled sampling is employed during training to enhance the generation of accurate long-sequence motions by occasionally feeding the model's own predictions as input for subsequent frames.
- **Auto-Regressive Motion Generation:** After training, the motion decoder can generate long sequences of future robot motions by iteratively feeding its own output as the "current pose" for the next prediction.
- **Command Encoder ($f_\psi$):** To enable user control over the generated motion velocity, a command encoder is introduced. This encoder takes a user velocity command ($c_t$) and the current robot pose ($m_t$) as input to generate the latent vector ($z_{t+1}$) for the motion decoder. Crucially, the motion decoder's parameters are *frozen* during the command encoder's training, ensuring that the decoder continues to produce natural motions while the encoder learns to control their velocity.

**C. Motion Guided Humanoid Control:**
The final stage involves training an RL control policy to enable the humanoid robot to perform natural locomotion while tracking user velocity commands.
- **Goal-Conditioned Reinforcement Learning:** The task is framed as a goal-conditioned RL problem, with Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) used for policy optimization.
- **State Representation ($s_t$):** The RL policy's input state comprises three components:
 1. **Proprioceptive information ($s_p_t$):** Robot's joint positions/velocities, base angular velocity, last action, and projected gravity vector.
 2. **User velocity command ($c_t$):** Desired linear and angular velocities.
 3. **Reference motion ($m_{t+1}$):** The crucial *future whole-body reference motion* predicted online by the *frozen* Generative Motion Prior. This serves as the fine-grained guidance signal.
- **Action Space:** The RL policy outputs target joint positions, which are then converted into torque commands using a PD controller.
- **Reward Function:** A composite reward function guides the policy:
 - **Motion Guidance Reward ($r_{guidance}$):** This is the key component leveraging the GMP. It includes:
 - **Joint Angle Guidance Reward ($r_{dof}$):** Encourages robot joint angles to match the reference motion's joint angles.
 - **Keypoint Position Guidance Reward ($r_{keypos}$):** Encourages robot keypoint positions (e.g., wrists, ankles, relative to the robot base) to match the reference motion's keypoint positions. Both are exponential negative squared error terms, providing stronger penalties for larger deviations.
 - **Task Reward ($r_{task}$):** Incentivizes the robot to track the commanded linear and angular velocities accurately.
 - **Regularization Reward ($r_{reg}$):** Imposes penalties for undesirable behaviors (e.g., high vertical base velocity, large angular velocities around X/Y axes, self-collision, high torque, high joint velocity/acceleration, action rate, joint deviation from default) and bonuses for desirable ones (e.g., feet air time, foot contact).
- **Experimental Setup:** Experiments are conducted on a full-size NAVIAI humanoid robot (21 active DoFs from 41 total). Training occurs in the Isaac Gym simulator, utilizing domain randomization to enhance sim-to-real transferability.

This meticulously designed methodology leverages offline data processing and generative modeling to create a stable, online expert-like guidance system, effectively addressing the limitations of previous approaches and enabling highly natural humanoid locomotion.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents compelling quantitative and qualitative results that demonstrate the effectiveness and superiority of the Generative Motion Prior (GMP) framework for natural humanoid robot locomotion.

**A. Quantitative Results (Comparison Study):**
The proposed method was rigorously compared against five representative baselines, including pure RL methods (SaW, PBRS) and various adversarial motion prior (AMP) methods (HumanMimic, SaW+AMP, PBRS+AMP). The metrics used to evaluate motion naturalness were Fréchet Inception Distance (FID) for joint angles (JFID) and keypoint positions (KFID), and Dynamic Time Warping (DTW) for joint angles (JDTW) and keypoint positions (KDTW). Lower scores indicate higher naturalness and similarity to human motion. Velocity tracking performance was measured by Mean Episode Linear Velocity Tracking Reward (MELV), where higher is better.

As shown in Table II:
- **Superior Motion Naturalness:** Our method significantly outperforms all baselines across all naturalness metrics. For instance, the JFID score for our method is 0.931, dramatically lower than the best baseline (PBRS+AMP at 2.088). Similarly, KFID is 0.004 for our method, compared to 0.021 for PBRS+AMP. JDTW (969.369) and KDTW (67.741) also show substantial improvements over baselines (e.g., JDTW of 1512.808 and KDTW of 145.697 for PBRS+AMP). This indicates that the robot's motion distribution and temporal alignment are much closer to human reference motions.
- **Robust Task Performance:** While achieving superior naturalness, our method maintains competitive velocity tracking performance (MELV of 2.449). This demonstrates that the naturalness is not achieved at the expense of the primary task objective of accurately following velocity commands. Pure RL methods like PBRS achieved slightly higher MELV (2.590) but at the cost of significantly less natural motion.

**B. Ablation Study:**
An ablation study (Table III) was conducted to quantify the contribution of key components of the GMP framework:
- **Effectiveness of Generative Motion Prior (GMP):** Removing the GMP ("Ours w/o GMP") leads to a substantial degradation in motion naturalness, with JFID jumping to 2.744 and KFID to 0.052. This clearly underscores the critical role of the generative model in providing effective guidance for learning natural locomotion.
- **Contribution of Motion Guidance Rewards:** Eliminating either the joint angle guidance reward ("Ours w/o r_dof") or the keypoint position guidance reward ("Ours w/o r_keypos") also resulted in noticeable performance drops in naturalness. This highlights that both fine-grained guidance signals (at the joint and keypoint levels) are essential for preserving the human-like quality of the generated motions.

**C. Qualitative Study:**
- **Simulation Performance (Fig. 4):** Visual comparisons in the Isaac Gym simulator show that our method generates robot postures that are notably more natural and human-like, exhibiting straighter legs and fluid motion patterns. In contrast, pure RL methods (like SaW) often produce unnatural bent-leg gaits, and even AMP-based methods (like PBRS+AMP) fail to fully capture the nuances of human-like characteristics.
- **Velocity Tracking (Fig. 6):** The results illustrate that our method achieves robust and precise tracking of user-specified velocity commands. The transition from standing to walking at 1.5 m/s is smooth, and the vertical foot contact forces demonstrate stable ground interaction, indicating good balance control.
- **Real-world Experiment (Fig. 5):** The efficacy of the method is validated on the NAVIAI full-sized humanoid robot. Snapshots from real-world experiments confirm that the robot can track user velocity commands robustly while maintaining a high degree of motion naturalness that closely resembles human movement characteristics, affirming the successful sim-to-real transfer.

In summary, the findings robustly establish that the Generative Motion Prior framework significantly advances the state-of-the-art in natural humanoid robot locomotion. It successfully provides stable, fine-grained supervision, leading to motions that are quantitatively and qualitatively more human-like, without compromising essential task performance.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "Natural Humanoid Robot Locomotion with Generative Motion Prior" paper introduces a highly significant advancement in the field of humanoid robotics, with substantial potential impact across several domains.

**A. Addressing Core Challenges in Humanoid Locomotion:**
The most immediate significance lies in its successful resolution of long-standing challenges in generating natural locomotion for humanoids. By replacing the unstable and ambiguous scalar rewards of adversarial methods with stable, fine-grained, trajectory-level supervision from a frozen generative model, the authors have found a more robust pathway to instill human-like motion. This stability and granularity are critical for enabling the learning of complex, high-dimensional, and nuanced whole-body movements. The work effectively bridges the gap between functional, stable locomotion and the aesthetic requirement of natural, human-like fluidity.

**B. Novel Integration of Generative Models and Reinforcement Learning:**
The paper pioneers a novel integration strategy between generative models and reinforcement learning. By using an offline-trained generative model as an *online motion generator* to provide real-time, detailed reference trajectories for an RL policy, it offers a powerful new paradigm. This approach can potentially be generalized beyond locomotion to other complex robot behaviors that benefit from human-like subtlety and style, such as manipulation, interaction gestures, or even expressive tasks. It demonstrates that pre-trained "expert knowledge" (in the form of natural motion generation) can be effectively and stably incorporated into an RL framework to guide policy learning.

**C. Enhanced Human-Robot Interaction and Acceptance:**
The ability of humanoid robots to move naturally is paramount for their successful integration into human society, particularly in environments like homes, hospitals, or workplaces. Robots with unnatural or jerky movements can be perceived as unsettling, inefficient, or even threatening, hindering acceptance and effective interaction. By achieving highly natural and lifelike locomotion, this research significantly contributes to making humanoid robots more intuitive, approachable, and socially acceptable. This, in turn, can accelerate their deployment in various service, assistance, and collaborative roles.

**D. Improved Training Stability and Interpretability:**
The GMP framework offers inherent advantages in training stability, bypassing the notorious difficulties associated with adversarial training. Furthermore, the explicit guidance signals (joint angles, keypoint positions) provided by the generative prior make the learning process more interpretable. Researchers can directly observe what "naturalness" means to the robot's policy at a detailed level, facilitating debugging, analysis, and further improvements.

**E. Practical Applications and Real-World Impact:**
The successful deployment of the method on a full-sized NAVIAI humanoid robot in real-world environments is a testament to its practical viability. This indicates that the learned policies are robust enough to transfer from simulation to reality, a critical step for any robotics research. This paves the way for commercial and research applications where natural bipedal locomotion is essential, such as:
- **Service Robotics:** Humanoids assisting in homes or public spaces.
- **Logistics:** Navigating complex indoor environments.
- **Education and Entertainment:** Robots for demonstrative or performance purposes.
- **Exploration:** Traversing diverse terrains with human-like agility.

In conclusion, this paper provides a robust, stable, and effective method for teaching humanoid robots to walk naturally. Its contributions are not just incremental improvements but represent a significant conceptual step forward in how generative models can be leveraged to guide complex robot learning tasks, with profound implications for the future of human-robot coexistence.
