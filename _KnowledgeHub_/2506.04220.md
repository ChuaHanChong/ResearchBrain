# Struct2D: A Perception-Guided Framework for Spatial Reasoning in MLLMs

> [!summary] Summary
> Struct2D introduces a perception-guided framework enabling Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) to perform 3D spatial reasoning using structured 2D representations, bypassing direct 3D input. The framework, along with its automatically generated instruction tuning dataset Struct2D-Set, achieves an average score of 43.6% on VSI-Bench for open-source MLLMs and competitive performance on other 3D scene understanding benchmarks.

## Problem

- Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) traditionally struggle with robust 3D spatial reasoning and typically require explicit 3D representations like point clouds as direct inputs.
- Existing 3D-input methods for MLLMs face limitations in adaptability, generalization across diverse textual queries, and architectural complexity.
- Prior 2D-based approaches for inferring 3D spatial cues often omit crucial object appearance details and explicit spatial priors (e.g., coordinates, categories), limiting their effectiveness.

## Method

- Struct2D proposes a ==perception-guided prompting strategy== that transforms raw egocentric RGB-D video inputs into structured 2D representations, including ==Bird's-Eye-View (BEV) images== with object marks, object-centric metadata (category, 3D coordinates), and intelligently selected egocentric keyframes.
- A large-scale instruction tuning dataset, ==Struct2D-Set==, is automatically generated from over 6K indoor scenes, designed for open-source MLLMs to acquire robust 3D spatial reasoning skills using only 2D projected inputs during training.
- The framework is applied in a zero-shot setting to closed-source MLLMs (e.g., GPT-o3) and also used to fine-tune an open-source MLLM (Qwen2.5VL) on the Struct2D-Set.

## Results

- In zero-shot analysis, GPT-o3 achieved high scores with Struct2D inputs on VSI-Bench (e.g., **96.5%** on relative distance and **94.4%** on relative direction with ground-truth object annotations).
- The Qwen2.5VL model fine-tuned on Struct2D-Set attained the highest average score (**43.6%**) among open-source MLLMs on VSI-Bench, with notable gains in relative direction (**45.9%**) and route planning (**35.8%**).
- The instruction-tuned model demonstrated competitive performance on standard 3D question answering and visual grounding benchmarks (ScanQA, SQA3D, Scan2Cap, ScanRefer, Multi3DRefer), often outperforming methods reliant on explicit 3D point cloud inputs.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - MLLMs can achieve strong 3D spatial reasoning capabilities by leveraging carefully constructed structured 2D inputs, even without direct 3D features as input.
> - Object-centric metadata and filtered object marks are crucial for effectively grounding spatial context and reducing ambiguity, while guide prompts and rotation-aligned BEV images are essential for complex reasoning tasks like relative direction.
> - Large-scale instruction tuning with step-by-step reasoning traces (using augmented QA pairs and `` tokens) substantially enhances an MLLM's ability to perform complex spatial reasoning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses a critical challenge at the intersection of computer vision, natural language processing, and embodied AI: enabling Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) to effectively reason about 3D space. Traditionally, intelligent agents (e.g., robots, navigation systems) rely on explicit 3D representations like point clouds or reconstructed environments to perform spatial tasks. While these methods offer geometric precision, they often face limitations in adaptability, generalization to diverse textual queries, and integration with natural language understanding due to their reliance on specialized architectures and extensive 3D-annotated datasets.

The emergence of MLLMs has brought significant advances in perception and reasoning for 2D images and videos. Extending these capabilities to 3D understanding has seen two main directions:
- **Point Cloud-based LLMs:** These models directly integrate 3D spatial features from point clouds into LLMs, enhancing spatial reasoning. However, they require point cloud features as input, demanding additional training and infrastructure, limiting flexibility and scalability.
- **2D-based Spatial Cues:** Recognizing that humans infer 3D from continuous 2D visual streams, some recent works explore leveraging cognitive maps or Bird’s-Eye View (BEV) images generated from video as 2D spatial cues. Struct2D builds on this direction but identifies limitations in prior approaches, which often omit crucial object appearance and detailed spatial priors (e.g., coordinates, categories).

Struct2D positions itself as a perception-guided framework that bridges the gap between these two approaches. It capitalizes on the strengths of MLLMs in language comprehension and 2D visual reasoning, while implicitly incorporating 3D spatial information through structured 2D representations derived from a 3D perception module. This allows MLLMs to perform 3D spatial reasoning *without requiring explicit 3D representations as direct inputs during inference or instruction tuning*, offering a more scalable and flexible alternative to direct 3D feature integration. The paper also contributes to the growing field of instruction tuning for MLLMs, particularly for spatial reasoning tasks, by introducing a large-scale, automatically generated dataset, Struct2D-Set, which focuses on 2D projected inputs.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation of "Struct2D" stems from a fundamental question: "Can MLLMs perform spatial reasoning without using explicit 3D features as direct inputs?" This inquiry is driven by the observation that humans naturally infer 3D spatial relationships from 2D visual inputs and the inherent challenges posed by direct 3D input methods for MLLMs (e.g., data requirements, architectural complexity, limited generalization).

The key objectives of this research are:
1. **To propose a novel perception-guided framework, Struct2D, that enables MLLMs to reason about 3D space using only structured 2D representations.** This involves transforming 3D perception outputs (point clouds, object detections) into informative 2D inputs, such as BEV images with object marks, object-centric metadata, and intelligently selected egocentric keyframes.
2. **To conduct an in-depth zero-shot analysis of closed-source MLLMs (e.g., GPT-o3) using Struct2D prompting.** The aim is to assess the inherent spatial reasoning capabilities of powerful pre-trained MLLMs when provided with these structured 2D inputs, identifying effective prompting strategies and their individual contributions.
3. **To construct a large-scale instruction tuning dataset, Struct2D-Set, tailored for diverse spatial reasoning tasks.** This dataset is designed to allow open-source MLLMs to acquire robust 3D spatial reasoning skills using only 2D projected inputs during training, overcoming the limitations of explicit 3D data.
4. **To fine-tune an open-source MLLM (Qwen2.5VL) on Struct2D-Set and evaluate its performance across multiple spatial reasoning and scene understanding benchmarks.** This objective aims to validate the practical applicability and effectiveness of the Struct2D framework and dataset in real-world scenarios, even under noisy perception conditions.

Ultimately, the motivation is to unlock robust, adaptable, and scalable spatial reasoning capabilities in MLLMs, crucial for enabling intelligent interaction with dynamic 3D environments, particularly for embodied AI applications.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The Struct2D framework comprises a perception-guided prompting strategy and a corresponding instruction tuning dataset.

#### 3.1. Struct2D Prompting Strategy

The core idea is to transform raw egocentric RGB-D video inputs into structured 2D representations that provide sufficient spatial context for MLLMs. This process involves:
1. **3D Perception Module:** An offline perception module (ϕ_percept_) processes the input RGB-D video to reconstruct a 3D point cloud and detect 3D objects (using methods like BundleFusion, Mask3D, UniDet). The ceiling is removed from the point cloud to ensure a clear top-down view.
2. **Structured 2D Input Generation:**
 - **Bird's-Eye-View (BEV) Image with Object Marks:** A BEV image of the scene is rendered from the reconstructed point cloud. Detected 3D objects relevant to the query are projected onto this BEV plane as distinct 2D "object marks." For tasks requiring egocentric-to-allocentric transformations (e.g., relative direction, route planning), the BEV image is rotation-aligned with the agent's facing direction.
 - **Object-centric Metadata:** Textual metadata is generated for each relevant object, including its category label and 3D coordinates (e.g., "1: sofa: [-2.4, 3.0, -0.5]"). This provides explicit spatial priors.
 - **Optional Egocentric Keyframes:** For questions requiring appearance or depth cues (e.g., object color, size), selected egocentric keyframes are incorporated. Instead of uniform sampling, these keyframes are intelligently chosen based on 3D projections, ensuring relevant objects are clearly visible and unobstructed, making them fewer and more informative.
3. **Prompt Construction:** The MLLM receives these structured 2D visual inputs (BEV image, keyframes) along with the object-centric metadata and the natural language query as textual inputs. A structured guide prompt is often used for complex reasoning tasks (e.g., outlining steps for relative direction estimation) to guide the MLLM's thought process.

#### 3.2. Zero-shot Analysis

The framework was first evaluated in a zero-shot setting using GPT-o3, a powerful closed-source MLLM, on a subset of VSI-Bench. This analysis aimed to understand the inherent spatial reasoning capabilities of MLLMs with structured 2D inputs. Ablation studies were conducted to isolate the impact of different components: object-centric metadata, filtered object marks, guide prompts, and rotation alignment of the BEV image.

#### 3.3. Struct2D-Set: Large-Scale Instruction Tuning Dataset

Based on insights from the zero-shot analysis, Struct2D-Set was developed to enable instruction tuning of open-source MLLMs.
1. **Data Sources:** 200K QA pairs generated from over 6K richly annotated indoor scenes from ARKitScenes, ScanNet, and ScanNet++.
2. **Automated Generation Pipeline:**
 - **Global Spatial Reasoning Tasks:** For tasks like spatial relation identification, egocentric navigation, and comparative reasoning (inspired by VSI-Bench), ground-truth 3D object annotations (boxes, depth maps, camera poses) are used with structured templates to generate initial QA pairs.
 - **Scene Understanding Tasks:** For tasks like object attribute identification, counting, and binary verification, QA pairs are adapted from existing 3D benchmarks (ScanQA, SQA3D, Scan2Cap, ScanRefer, Multi3DRefer).
3. **ChatGPT Augmentation:** ChatGPT is extensively used to:
 - Enrich QA pairs with natural language formulations.
 - Generate step-by-step reasoning traces (long-form answers) for complex questions, using special `<think>` and `</think>` tokens to guide the model.
 - Improve clarity and reasoning depth, and identify low-quality samples.
4. **Human-in-the-Loop:** A human review process refines and validates the dataset quality, especially for navigation routes.
5. **2D Projection Focus:** Crucially, the dataset is designed such that during training, the MLLM receives only 2D projected inputs (BEV images, object marks, metadata, selected keyframes), not raw 3D data.

#### 3.4. Instruction Tuning and Evaluation

An open-source MLLM, Qwen2.5VL, was fine-tuned using Struct2D-Set. During training, visual inputs (BEV, keyframes) are resized and efficiently batched. The model is trained to generate step-by-step reasoning for complex tasks and direct answers for simpler ones. The fine-tuned model was evaluated on:
- **VSI-Bench:** For complex spatial reasoning tasks (e.g., relative direction, route planning).
- **3D Scene Understanding Benchmarks:** ScanQA, SQA3D (3D question answering), Scan2Cap (3D dense captioning), ScanRefer, Multi3DRefer (3D visual grounding).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded several significant findings across both zero-shot analysis and instruction tuning:

#### 4.1. Zero-shot Analysis with GPT-o3

- **Strong Spatial Reasoning with Structured 2D Inputs:** GPT-o3 demonstrated surprisingly strong spatial reasoning abilities when provided with Struct2D inputs. With ground-truth object annotations, it achieved high scores: 96.5% on relative distance, 94.4% on relative direction, and 80.1% on route planning on a VSI-Bench subset. This significantly outperformed direct video-based prompting (VSI-Bench) and previous 2D spatial prompting (GPT4Scene).
- **Effectiveness of Prompt Components:**
 - **Object-centric Metadata and Filtered Marks:** Both were crucial for improved performance. Metadata significantly grounded spatial context (e.g., relative distance accuracy from 67.5% to 96.5%), and filtering marks reduced ambiguity, especially for route planning (from 50.6% to 80.1%).
 - **Guide Prompt and Rotation Alignment:** For challenging tasks like relative direction, both a structured guide prompt (outlining geometric reasoning steps) and rotation alignment of the BEV image (to match agent's viewpoint) were essential, with their combination yielding the best results (94.4%).
- **Efficiency:** The method requires only a single BEV image and lightweight metadata, making it a low-cost and robust alternative to multi-frame prompting strategies.

#### 4.2. Instruction Tuning with Struct2D-Set (Qwen2.5VL)

- **State-of-the-Art Open-source Performance on VSI-Bench:** The Qwen2.5VL model fine-tuned with Struct2D-Set achieved the highest average score (43.6%) among all open-source MLLMs evaluated on VSI-Bench. This surpassed the Struct2D prompting variant (29.4%) and standard video-based tuning baselines (33.9%), validating the full instruction tuning approach.
- **Significant Gains in Complex Spatial Reasoning:** Performance gains were particularly prominent in tasks like relative direction (45.9%) and route planning (35.8%), highlighting the model's enhanced ability to integrate geometric understanding and egocentric context.
- **Competitive Performance on Standard 3D Benchmarks:** The fine-tuned model also showed competitive performance on traditional 3D question answering (ScanQA, SQA3D), 3D dense captioning (Scan2Cap), and 3D visual grounding (ScanRefer, Multi3DRefer) benchmarks, often outperforming methods that rely on explicit 3D point cloud inputs.
- **Impact of QA Augmentation and Reasoning Tokens:**
 - **Augmented QA Pairs:** Incorporating enriched QA pairs generated by ChatGPT (with step-by-step reasoning traces) led to substantial performance improvements, especially on reasoning-heavy tasks like relative direction (42.2% vs. 14.7%), demonstrating the value of detailed supervision.
 - **`<think>` and `</think>` Tokens:** Explicitly instructing the model to reason step-by-step using these tokens improved performance on reasoning-intensive tasks (e.g., relative direction, route planning), enhancing output quality and interpretability.

#### 4.3. Efficiency and Scalability

The approach leverages fewer visual frames (e.g., 1 BEV image + optional keyframes) compared to video-based methods and does not rely on dense temporal input or direct 3D features during inference, leading to a more efficient and scalable training and inference process.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Struct2D framework represents a significant advancement in enabling MLLMs to perform 3D spatial reasoning, offering a novel and practical pathway that bypasses the need for explicit 3D input representations at inference time.

**Significance:**
- **Paradigm Shift in 3D Reasoning for MLLMs:** The core finding that structured 2D inputs, carefully crafted from 3D perception outputs, are sufficient to unlock strong 3D spatial reasoning in MLLMs is highly significant. It challenges the prevailing notion that MLLMs require direct access to 3D data for complex spatial understanding.
- **Enhanced MLLM Capabilities:** Struct2D demonstrates how existing MLLMs, even closed-source ones like GPT-o3, can be effectively prompted for advanced spatial tasks, and how open-source models can be instruction-tuned to achieve competitive performance.
- **Scalability and Efficiency:** By relying on 2D projections, the framework offers a more scalable and computationally efficient approach compared to methods that process raw point clouds or numerous video frames. This reduces training and inference costs, making robust spatial reasoning more accessible.
- **Interpretable Reasoning:** The use of guide prompts and `<think>` tokens encourages MLLMs to generate explicit, step-by-step reasoning traces, enhancing the interpretability and trustworthiness of their spatial decisions.
- **Novel Dataset Contribution:** Struct2D-Set is a valuable contribution to the research community, providing a large-scale, fine-grained instruction tuning dataset specifically designed for 3D spatial reasoning with 2D inputs. Its automated generation, ChatGPT augmentation, and human-in-the-loop validation ensure high quality and diversity.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Embodied AI and Robotics:** Struct2D can significantly advance embodied AI systems, allowing robots and intelligent agents to better understand and interact with their physical environments using natural language commands. This is crucial for tasks like navigation, object manipulation, and human-robot collaboration in homes, offices, and industrial settings.
- **Augmented Reality (AR) and Virtual Reality (VR):** The ability to reason about 3D space from 2D views can enhance AR/VR applications, enabling more natural and intuitive interactions where spatial queries can be resolved efficiently without requiring full 3D scene reconstruction at all times.
- **Accessibility Technologies:** For individuals with visual impairments, language-driven scene understanding based on minimal visual cues could lead to more effective assistive technologies that provide rich spatial descriptions of environments.
- **Future Research Directions:** Struct2D opens new avenues for research into more lightweight 3D perception modules, generalization to outdoor scenes, and exploring dynamic spatial reasoning. It encourages the development of modular AI systems where perception, reasoning, and language capabilities are more effectively decoupled and integrated.

**Limitations and Future Work:**
The authors acknowledge two main limitations:
1. **3D Preprocessing Requirements:** Struct2D still relies on a 3D perception module for initial scene reconstruction and object detection. While this happens offline and doesn't feed raw 3D data to the MLLM, it can still pose challenges in latency-sensitive or resource-constrained environments. Future work could integrate with lightweight, real-time 3D reconstruction systems.
2. **Indoor Scene Focus:** The current Struct2D-Set primarily uses indoor scenes. Generalizing to outdoor or open-world environments with diverse spatial layouts and object categories is an important next step for dataset expansion and model applicability.

Despite these limitations, Struct2D provides a compelling demonstration of how structured 2D representations can effectively bridge perception and language reasoning in MLLMs, paving a scalable path toward robust multimodal spatial understanding.
