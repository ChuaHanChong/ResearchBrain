# vla-eval: A Unified Evaluation Harness for Vision-Language-Action Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers developed `vla-eval`, an open-source evaluation harness that standardizes and accelerates the assessment of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models across various simulation benchmarks. This framework achieved up to a 47x speedup for evaluating models on benchmarks like LIBERO and successfully reproduced published scores while uncovering several undocumented evaluation pitfalls.

## Problem

- Integrating VLA models with multiple simulation benchmarks incurs significant engineering costs due to incompatible software dependencies and distinct environments.
- Evaluation protocols in published VLA research are often underspecified, making it difficult to reproduce results and conduct fair comparisons.
- Comprehensive VLA model evaluations are time-consuming, hindering iterative development and broad assessment of generalization capabilities.

## Method

- A **vla-eval** ==client-server architecture== decouples model inference from benchmark execution, utilizing ==Docker containers== for benchmarks and isolated Python environments for models to resolve dependency conflicts.
- ==Declarative YAML configurations== define evaluation parameters, ensuring reproducibility, and a standardized interface simplifies integration of new benchmarks and models.
- A two-pronged ==parallelization strategy==, combining ==episode sharding== for environments and ==batched inference== for models, significantly accelerates evaluation wall-clock time.

## Results

- `vla-eval` successfully reproduced published scores for **six VLA models** across **three benchmarks**, with deviations generally within expected variance.
- Achieved significant speedups: **47x for LIBERO** (reducing 14 hours to **18 minutes** for 2,000 episodes), **16x for CALVIN**, and **12x for SimplerEnv**.
- Identified critical undocumented pitfalls: incorrect proprioceptive state sources causing a **55 percentage point drop** and quaternion conversion errors causing **14–39 percentage point drops**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Many previously undocumented parameters and subtle implementation differences in existing benchmark setups critically impact reported VLA model performance.
> - The vast majority of existing VLA models (81%) are evaluated on only a single benchmark, highlighting a community-wide need for more comprehensive, cross-benchmark assessments.
> - Decoupling model and benchmark dependencies into isolated environments effectively transforms the integration burden from an O(N × M) to an O(N + M) cost.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models is an area of increasing research focus, aiming to develop robotic agents that can understand natural language instructions and execute corresponding actions in physical or simulated environments. A key aspect of advancing this field involves evaluating model performance across a diverse range of tasks and environments to assess generalization capabilities and robustness. As VLA models become more complex and capable, researchers frequently evaluate them on multiple simulation benchmarks.

However, the current landscape of VLA model evaluation is characterized by significant engineering challenges. Each simulation benchmark typically comes with its own set of dependencies, including specific Python versions, simulation engines (e.g., robosuite, SAPIEN, PyBullet), and unique asset requirements. These dependencies are often incompatible, making it difficult to set up a unified environment for evaluating multiple benchmarks. Furthermore, evaluation protocols are frequently underspecified in published literature, with details regarding seeds, episode counts, and crucial preprocessing steps often omitted. This lack of standardization necessitates significant effort in "reverse-engineering" evaluation setups, leading to high integration costs for each new benchmark or model.

This situation results in an O(N × M) integration burden for evaluating N models on M benchmarks, making comprehensive multi-benchmark evaluation impractical for many research teams. This burden limits the ability to conduct fair comparisons, assess model generalization across different scenarios, and reproduce results, thereby hindering progress in developing more generalist VLA agents. The work presented in this paper directly addresses these limitations by proposing a unified framework designed to streamline and standardize the evaluation process for VLA models, drawing inspiration from similar efforts in other domains such as `lm-evaluation-harness` for language models.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop and release `vla-eval`, an open-source evaluation harness that simplifies and standardizes the process of evaluating Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models across multiple simulation benchmarks. This overarching goal is motivated by several specific challenges observed in the current VLA research landscape:

1. **Eliminate Per-Benchmark Integration Cost:** A significant motivation is to address the substantial engineering effort required to integrate each new benchmark into an evaluation pipeline. This includes resolving incompatible software dependencies, managing distinct Python runtimes, and handling specific asset requirements for each simulator. The authors aim to reduce this integration effort from a quadratic O(N × M) relationship (for N models and M benchmarks) to a linear O(N + M) relationship by enabling a single integration for each model and each benchmark.

2. **Decouple Model Inference from Benchmark Execution:** The framework seeks to create a clear separation between the VLA model's inference logic and the benchmark's execution environment. This decoupling is intended to allow models and benchmarks to operate in isolated, self-contained environments, thereby preventing dependency conflicts and promoting modularity.

3. **Ensure Reproducibility and Standardize Evaluation Protocols:** The paper highlights that evaluation protocols are often underspecified in published works, leading to ambiguity and difficulties in reproducing reported results. The authors are motivated to provide canonical protocol definitions for benchmarks, including standardized task subsets, metrics, and comparability constraints, along with recording full evaluation configurations. This aims to enable exact reproduction of any evaluation run.

4. **Accelerate Evaluation Through Parallelism:** Evaluating VLA models, especially on benchmarks involving numerous episodes or long-horizon tasks, can be time-consuming. A key objective is to significantly reduce the wall-clock time required for comprehensive evaluations through efficient parallelization techniques. This acceleration is crucial for facilitating more extensive experimentation and model development.

5. **Validate Framework Fidelity and Document Pitfalls:** The authors aim to validate the reliability of their framework by reproducing published scores of existing VLA models across several benchmarks. This validation process is also motivated by the need to identify and document undocumented parameters or implementation details in existing benchmarks and model codebases that can critically affect reported performance, thereby improving transparency and accuracy in future evaluations.

6. **Aggregate and Standardize Published Results:** Beyond providing an evaluation tool, the paper aims to create a centralized resource for the VLA community. This involves aggregating published results from a wide range of papers into a VLA leaderboard with standardized metrics and protocols, thereby offering a more coherent overview of progress and enabling fair cross-paper comparisons.

In summary, the motivation stems from the recognition that the current fragmented and labor-intensive evaluation practices in VLA research impede progress. By providing a unified, efficient, and reproducible evaluation harness, the authors aim to lower barriers to comprehensive model assessment, foster better research practices, and accelerate the development of generalist robotic agents.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The `vla-eval` framework employs a client-server architecture designed to isolate and standardize the evaluation of VLA models. The core methodological components are as follows:

**A. Architecture and Decoupling:**
The framework separates model inference from benchmark execution using a client-server paradigm. Communication between the benchmark client and the model server is facilitated by a WebSocket protocol combined with msgpack for binary serialization. Each message exchanged contains essential information such as type (e.g., observation, action, episode\_start/end), a benchmark-specific payload, a sequence number, and a timestamp.

**B. Dependency Isolation:**
To address the pervasive issue of incompatible software dependencies, `vla-eval` implements robust isolation mechanisms:
- **Benchmarks:** Each benchmark is encapsulated within a dedicated Docker container. These Docker images are pre-configured with pinned dependencies and include all necessary assets (e.g., scene files, textures, robot descriptions), eliminating the need for manual, ad-hoc asset installation. These images are published to `ghcr.io` with versioned tags. Integrators of new benchmarks are required to implement a standardized four-method interface (`reset`, `step`, `make_obs`, `get_step_result`) within their Docker environment.
- **Model Servers:** Model servers declare their dependencies using PEP 723 inline metadata. When a model server is launched via `vla-eval serve`, the `uv run` tool is utilized to create an isolated Python environment automatically. This approach allows different model servers with conflicting dependencies (e.g., varying `transformers` library versions) to coexist and operate without interference. Model integrators primarily need to extend the `PredictModelServer` class and implement a `predict(obs, ctx)` method, typically requiring around 50 lines of code. The framework also supports automatic action chunking and optional batched inference.

**C. Declarative Configuration and Execution:**
Evaluations are driven by two declarative YAML configuration files: one for the benchmark and one for the model server. This allows for clear, version-controlled specification of evaluation parameters. A complete evaluation sequence involves only two commands: `vla-eval serve` to start the model server and `vla-eval run` to initiate the benchmark execution. Every evaluation run produces a structured JSON result file, which records the harness version, benchmark configuration, and per-episode metrics, facilitating exact reproduction of results.

**D. Supported Benchmarks and Models:**
The framework currently supports 14 simulation benchmarks, encompassing diverse action spaces (6D to 14D) and varying Docker image sizes (4.7 GB to 35.6 GB). These include benchmarks such as SimplerEnv, LIBERO, CALVIN, ManiSkill2, and RoboCasa. Six VLA model servers have been integrated, including CogACT, OpenVLA, OpenVLA-OFT, π0/π0-FAST, GR00T N1, and X-VLA.

**E. Parallel Evaluation Methodology:**
To enhance evaluation efficiency, `vla-eval` incorporates a two-pronged parallelization strategy:
- **Environment Parallelism:** Achieved through "episode sharding," where evaluation episodes are distributed across multiple (K) Docker containers, allowing parallel execution of benchmark environments.
- **Inference Parallelism:** Utilizes batched forward passes on the model server, processing multiple observations concurrently.
The framework employs a "demand/supply" methodology to tune parallelism. This involves measuring environment throughput (λ(K)) as a function of the number of shards and model throughput (μ(B)) as a function of batch size (B). The optimal operating point aims to ensure that environment demand (λ(K)) remains below a certain threshold (e.g., 80%) of the model's supply capacity (μ(B*)) to prevent queue buildup and maximize throughput. This approach makes the speedup model-agnostic, as the bottleneck typically shifts to the environment step rate rather than model inference.

**F. Validation Approach:**
The framework's fidelity was validated by attempting to reproduce published scores from six VLA codebases (OpenVLA, π0.5, OpenVLA-OFT, GR00T N1.6, DB-CogACT, and X-VLA) across three simulation benchmarks (LIBERO, CALVIN, and SimplerEnv). This process involved using fixed seeds and the versioned Docker images provided by `vla-eval`. Critical to this validation was the systematic comparison of intermediate values against reference implementations to uncover and document any undocumented parameters or subtle implementation differences that significantly affected results.

**G. VLA Leaderboard Curation:**
A VLA leaderboard was created by aggregating 657 published results from 1,704 papers that cited at least one of 17 tracked benchmarks. The curation process involved:
- **Canonical Protocol Definitions:** Establishing standardized task subsets, metrics, and comparability constraints for each benchmark to ensure fair comparison.
- **Automated and Human Review:** An AI agent (Claude Code with Opus 4.6) was used to extract and normalize results from papers, followed by a human operator reviewing every entry to resolve anomalies and ambiguities.
- **Provenance and Validation:** Each entry is versioned with full provenance metadata and validated against automated schema constraints, maintaining data integrity and transparency.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The development and deployment of `vla-eval` yielded several key findings and results demonstrating its efficacy and utility in the VLA research landscape:

**A. Framework Capabilities and Integration Efficiency:**
- **Broad Support:** `vla-eval` supports 14 distinct simulation benchmarks, encompassing a range of action space dimensionalities (6D to 14D) and Docker image sizes (4.7 GB to 35.6 GB). It also integrates six prominent VLA model servers, including OpenVLA, π0/π0-FAST, and CogACT.
- **Reduced Integration Burden:** The decoupled architecture and Docker-based isolation successfully reduced the integration effort from an O(N × M) dependency for N models and M benchmarks to an O(N + M) cost. This means models and benchmarks only need to be integrated once into the harness.
- **Dependency Resolution:** The system effectively manages conflicting dependencies across different models and benchmarks by isolating them within their respective environments, thereby eliminating a common integration bottleneck.

**B. Parallel Evaluation Performance:**
- **Significant Wall-Clock Speedup:** The parallel evaluation methodology, combining episode sharding and batched inference, demonstrated substantial speedups:
 - **LIBERO:** A 47x speedup was achieved, reducing the evaluation of 2,000 episodes from approximately 14 hours to about 18 minutes. This involved using 50 shards for environment parallelism and a batch size of 16 for model inference.
 - **CALVIN:** A 16x speedup was observed, completing 1,000 sequences in roughly 33 minutes.
 - **SimplerEnv:** A 12x speedup was recorded, finishing 288 episodes in approximately 8.5 minutes.
- **Bottleneck Shift:** The parallelization effectively shifted the evaluation bottleneck from model inference to the environment step rate, indicating that the model server capacity generally exceeds the demand imposed by parallel environments.

**C. Reproduction Results and Challenges:**
- **Fidelity to Published Scores:** The framework successfully reproduced published scores for six VLA codebases across three benchmarks (LIBERO, CALVIN, SimplerEnv) with deviations generally within expected variance (e.g., OpenVLA on LIBERO showed a -0.3% difference, X-VLA on LIBERO -0.7%). This validates the framework's accuracy in replicating reference implementations.
- **Identification of Undocumented Pitfalls:** The reproduction process revealed several undocumented settings and implementation details that critically impacted reported success rates, highlighting the need for standardized protocols:
 - **X-VLA on LIBERO:** Using an incorrect proprioceptive state source caused a 55 percentage point (pp) drop in success rate (from 97.8% to 42%).
 - **Action Mode Ambiguity:** Confusion between absolute and delta action modes resulted in 0% success due to accumulated positional errors.
 - **OpenVLA-OFT Quaternion Conversion:** A mismatch in quaternion-to-axis-angle conversion (lack of antipodal normalization) led to significant drops: 14 pp on LIBERO-Goal (from 97% to 83%) and 39 pp on LIBERO-Long (from 95% to 56%).
 - **OpenVLA Center Crop:** An undocumented center crop (scale = 0.9) applied during evaluation time, if omitted, resulted in approximately a 3 pp decrease in performance.
 - **GR00T on SimplerEnv:** GR00T's expectation of end-effector pose as proprioceptive input, which was only available in an internal simulator fork, led to 0% success rates in the official SimplerEnv. Implementing a patch to provide this input raised scores to 59.7%, though an 8.0 pp gap to the reported score remained.

**D. VLA Leaderboard Insights:**
- **Comprehensive Aggregation:** The VLA leaderboard aggregates 657 published results across 17 benchmarks and over 509 model configurations, providing a centralized and standardized resource.
- **Limited Cross-Benchmark Evaluation:** Analysis of the leaderboard data revealed that 81% of the tracked models are evaluated on only a single benchmark, and only 6% are evaluated on three or more benchmarks. This finding indicates a prevalent lack of comprehensive, cross-benchmark comparison in the current VLA research, underscoring the utility of a unified evaluation harness.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The `vla-eval` evaluation harness presents several significant contributions and potential impacts on the Vision-Language-Action (VLA) research community:

**A. Facilitating Comprehensive and Reproducible Research:**
- **Reduced Engineering Overhead:** By significantly lowering the integration burden for both models and benchmarks, `vla-eval` enables researchers, particularly those in smaller teams, to conduct more extensive and comprehensive multi-benchmark evaluations. This allows for a broader assessment of model generalization capabilities across diverse tasks and environments, which is crucial for developing robust VLA agents.
- **Enhanced Reproducibility:** The framework's design, which includes Docker-based isolation, declarative configurations, and the logging of full evaluation details, directly addresses the reproducibility crisis in machine learning. It provides a standardized mechanism to recreate exact evaluation conditions, ensuring that reported results are verifiable and comparable. The documentation of previously undocumented pitfalls, which led to substantial performance shifts, highlights the critical importance of transparent and standardized evaluation protocols.

**B. Accelerating Research and Development Cycles:**
- **Speeding Up Evaluation:** The demonstrated parallelization capabilities, achieving up to 47x speedup, drastically reduce the time required for evaluations. This acceleration allows researchers to iterate more quickly on model development, test hypotheses more efficiently, and explore a wider range of architectural and algorithmic choices.
- **Standardized Integration:** The "integrate once" principle for models and benchmarks streamlines the onboarding of new research contributions, fostering a more dynamic and collaborative research ecosystem.

**C. Promoting Fair Comparison and Benchmarking:**
- **Canonical Protocols:** The establishment of canonical evaluation protocols for various benchmarks provides a common ground for comparing VLA models. This standardization helps mitigate issues arising from differing evaluation metrics, task subsets, or splits, leading to more objective and fair comparisons between competing approaches.
- **Centralized Leaderboard:** The VLA leaderboard serves as a valuable resource for the community, offering a centralized, continuously updated repository of model performance across numerous benchmarks. This transparency can guide future research by highlighting areas where models excel or struggle, and by identifying benchmarks that are more challenging or under-explored.

**D. Advancing Generalist VLA Model Development:**
- **Encouraging Cross-Benchmark Evaluation:** The finding that a vast majority of VLA models are evaluated on only one benchmark suggests a current limitation in assessing true generalizability. By making multi-benchmark evaluation practical and efficient, `vla-eval` implicitly encourages researchers to test their models across a wider array of scenarios, thereby pushing the field towards developing more truly generalist VLA agents.
- **Improved Model Robustness:** The identification of subtle parameters causing significant performance changes underscores the importance of rigorous evaluation. By exposing such vulnerabilities, the framework contributes to the development of more robust and reliable VLA models.

**E. Open-Source Community Impact:**
- **Community Collaboration:** As an open-source project, `vla-eval` invites community contributions for integrating new models, benchmarks, and functionalities. This collaborative approach can ensure the harness remains current with the rapid advancements in VLA research and expands its utility over time.
- **Educational Tool:** The framework can also serve as an educational tool, providing clear examples of how to properly integrate and evaluate VLA models and benchmarks according to best practices.

In conclusion, `vla-eval` is positioned to address fundamental practical challenges in VLA research, offering a robust, efficient, and standardized platform that has the potential to significantly accelerate progress, enhance reproducibility, and foster the development of more generalizable and capable robotic intelligence.
