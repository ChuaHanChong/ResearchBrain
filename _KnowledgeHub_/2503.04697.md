# L1: Controlling How Long A Reasoning Model Thinks With Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Carnegie Mellon University introduced Length Controlled Policy Optimization (LCPO), an RL-based method that trains large language models to precisely control the length of their reasoning steps. This approach enables dynamic trade-offs between computational cost and accuracy, achieving over 100% relative performance gains compared to heuristic methods and yielding powerful "Short Reasoning Models" that surpass larger frontier models like GPT-4o.

## Problem

- Large language models (LLMs) often generate uncontrollably long chain-of-thought (CoT) sequences, leading to inefficient compute allocation and unpredictable inference costs.
- Existing heuristic methods for imposing reasoning length limits, such as truncation, typically degrade model performance and reasoning integrity.
- There is a lack of predictable control over reasoning length, making it challenging to allocate fixed test-time compute budgets for LLM deployment.

## Method

- Developed **Length Controlled Policy Optimization (LCPO)**, an ==online reinforcement learning== method that fine-tunes pre-trained reasoning LLMs.
- The models are prompted with a ==target token length== and optimized using a novel ==reward function== that simultaneously incentivizes correct answers and penalizes deviations from the specified length.
- Introduced two reward variants: ==LCPO-Exact== for precise length matching and ==LCPO-Max== for adhering to a maximum budget while prioritizing correctness and allowing for shorter outputs.

## Results

- LCPO-trained models achieved over **100-150%** relative and **20-25%** absolute performance gains compared to state-of-the-art heuristic methods (S1) across various math reasoning benchmarks.
- Demonstrated precise length adherence, with mean deviations from target lengths around **3%** for in-domain tasks, and exhibited robust generalization to out-of-domain logical reasoning and general knowledge tasks.
- The **1.5B** parameter L1 model, when constrained to short reasoning, outperformed GPT-4o by **2%** on average across multiple tasks, showcasing the effectiveness of Length Controlled Policy Optimization in creating powerful Short Reasoning Models.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - An online, length-sensitive reinforcement learning reward is crucial for effective length control, outperforming supervised fine-tuning approaches.
> - LCPO enables LLMs to learn adaptive reasoning strategies, dynamically adjusting their 'thinking' depth based on explicit token budget constraints.
> - Training for length control unexpectedly yields highly efficient 'Short Reasoning Models' (SRMs) that challenge traditional scaling laws by achieving high performance with minimal compute.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research sits at the intersection of several critical and evolving areas within large language model (LLM) development: **reasoning capabilities, test-time scaling, and controlled generation**.

Historically, LLMs have demonstrated impressive reasoning abilities, often enhanced by generating "chain-of-thought" (CoT) sequences (Wei et al., 2023). This "thinking longer" strategy, exemplified by models like OpenAI's O1 and DeepSeek-AI's R1 (OpenAI et al., 2024a; DeepSeek-AI et al., 2025), has consistently improved performance on complex tasks. However, a significant limitation has been the *uncontrollable* nature of these reasoning lengths. Models might generate excessively long sequences, wasting computational resources, or prematurely truncate reasoning, leading to suboptimal performance. This lack of control makes it challenging to deploy such models efficiently in real-world applications where compute budgets are constrained or specific performance targets need to be met.

The broader field of **test-time scaling** aims to extract more performance from LLMs during inference. This includes techniques like parallel sampling of multiple reasoning paths (Wang et al., 2023), tree-based search (Yao et al., 2023), and iterative refinement (Madaan et al., 2023). While these methods explore different ways to utilize compute, the focus on *sequential scaling*—i.e., generating longer CoT—is a prominent approach. "L1" directly addresses a key limitation of this approach by introducing *control* over the length of these sequential reasoning steps, thereby enhancing the practical utility of test-time scaling methods.

Furthermore, **length control in LLMs** is a general challenge across various generation tasks. Existing methods have explored architectural modifications, training objective adjustments, or instruction-based fine-tuning to manage output length (Butcher et al., 2024; Yuan et al., 2024). However, these efforts primarily target general text generation or instruction following, where the nuanced trade-off between computational cost and reasoning quality is less critical or distinct. Reasoning models introduce fundamentally new requirements, as abrupt truncation can derail a complex reasoning process, and simple token limits often degrade performance. Prior work, such as S1 (Muennighoff et al., 2025), attempted "budget-forcing" by using special tokens or truncation, but this rigid, hand-engineered strategy proved detrimental to performance.

This paper distinguishes itself by focusing specifically on **length control for *reasoning* models**, addressing the unique challenges of maintaining reasoning integrity while adhering to compute budgets. By introducing an adaptive, RL-based approach, "L1" provides a more sophisticated solution than previous heuristic-driven or general-purpose length control methods. It fills a critical gap by enabling a smooth, controllable trade-off between computational cost and accuracy, which is paramount for the efficient deployment of advanced reasoning LLMs.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind this research stems from a crucial practical limitation of current reasoning language models: their inability to precisely control the length of their generated chain-of-thought (CoT) sequences. While "thinking longer" often leads to improved performance on complex problems, this uncontrolled generation has significant drawbacks:

1. **Inefficient Compute Allocation:** Models may generate excessively long CoT sequences, leading to wasted computational resources (tokens used) and increased inference latency, making them less cost-effective for deployment. Conversely, they might stop too early on complex problems, failing to reach optimal solutions.
2. **Lack of Predictability:** Without control over reasoning length, it is impossible for users or downstream applications to allocate a fixed test-time compute budget to achieve a desired level of performance or to predictably manage operational costs.
3. **Performance Degradation with Heuristics:** Existing attempts to impose length control, such as strict truncation or budget-forcing mechanisms (e.g., S1), have largely proven ineffective, often severely degrading the model's reasoning capabilities and overall accuracy.

Given these challenges, the key objectives of this work are:

- **To develop a method for precise and adaptive control over the length of reasoning sequences generated by LLMs.** This control should allow users to specify a desired token budget within the model's prompt.
- **To enable a smooth and intelligent trade-off between computational cost (token usage) and reasoning accuracy.** The goal is to maximize performance within a given token budget, rather than merely truncating output.
- **To demonstrate that such length control can be achieved without significantly compromising the underlying model's reasoning performance**, ideally matching or exceeding the performance of unconstrained models at optimal length points.
- **To explore whether length-controlled training can yield unexpected benefits**, such as improved performance at *shorter* reasoning lengths, potentially leading to more efficient "short reasoning models" (SRMs) that challenge existing scaling laws.
- **To ensure the generalizability of length control capabilities** across various reasoning tasks and even to out-of-domain benchmarks.

By addressing these objectives, the authors aim to enhance the practical utility, efficiency, and flexibility of reasoning language models, making them more suitable for diverse real-world applications with varying compute constraints.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors introduce **Length Controlled Policy Optimization (LCPO)**, a reinforcement learning (RL)-based method designed to train reasoning language models to produce outputs that adhere to user-specified length constraints while maintaining high accuracy.

**Core Idea:**
LCPO fine-tunes a pre-trained reasoning language model by conditioning it on a target token length provided directly in the prompt. The model is then optimized to simultaneously minimize the absolute difference between its generated output length and the target length, and produce the correct answer.

**Detailed Approach:**

1. **Base Model and Dataset:**
 - The research starts with a pre-trained reasoning LLM, specifically **DeepScaleR-1.5B-Preview**, a 1.5B-parameter model derived from Qwen-Distilled-R1-1.5B. This model was already RL fine-tuned on the DeepScaleR-Preview-Dataset, a mathematics dataset containing 40K question-answer pairs (from AIME, AMC, Omni-Math, STILL).
 - The original dataset provides input prompts (`x_i`) and final correct answers (`y_gold,i`), but no intermediate reasoning traces are provided for length supervision initially.

2. **Prompt Augmentation:**
 - To introduce length control, each input prompt `x_i` is augmented with a target length instruction. For instance, `x_new,i = Concat(x_i, "Think for n_gold,i tokens.")`.
 - The target length `n_gold,i` is sampled uniformly from a specified range `Z(n_min, n_max)` during training (e.g., 100 to 4000 tokens). This creates a new dataset `D_new` for RL fine-tuning.

3. **Reinforcement Learning Objective (GRPO):**
 - The augmented base model (`LLM_θ`) is updated using a reinforcement learning algorithm. The authors specifically adopt **GRPO (Shao et al., 2024)**, though the method is framework-agnostic.
 - The core of LCPO lies in its novel **reward function**, which combines two objectives: correctness and length adherence.

 - **LCPO-Exact Reward Function (Equation 1):**
 `r(y, y_gold, n_gold) = I(y = y_gold) - α · |n_gold - n_y|`
 - `I(y = y_gold)`: An indicator function that awards a reward of 1 for a correct final answer and 0 otherwise. This prioritizes correctness.
 - `α · |n_gold - n_y|`: A penalty term that scales with the absolute difference between the generated output length (`n_y`) and the target length (`n_gold`). `α` is a scalar (set to 0.0003 in experiments) that balances the trade-off between correctness and length adherence.
 - This function encourages models to generate correct answers while precisely matching the prescribed target length. Models trained with this objective are referred to as **L1-Exact**.

 - **LCPO-Max Reward Function (Equation 2):**
 `r(y, y_gold, n_gold) = I(y = y_gold) · clip(α · (n_gold - n_y) + δ, 0, 1)`
 - This variant is designed for scenarios where users prefer staying *within* a maximum computational budget rather than adhering to an exact length.
 - It fine-tunes the L1-Exact model further. The `clip` function and `δ = 0.5` term ensure a soft constraint:
 - Correct answers are rewarded.
 - A gradual penalty is applied if `n_y` exceeds `n_gold`, allowing gradient propagation for the GRPO objective.
 - It incentivizes using *fewer* tokens when possible without sacrificing correctness, as `(n_gold - n_y)` becomes positive for `n_y < n_gold`.
 - The `δ` term ensures that correct answers with minor budget violations are still preferred over incorrect answers.
 - Models trained with this objective (and the dual objective for exact lengths) are called **L1-Max**.

4. **Training and Evaluation Details:**
 - The base model (DeepScaleR-1.5B-Preview) was fine-tuned for 700 steps with LCPO-Exact and then for an additional 120 steps with LCPO-Max.
 - Evaluation metrics included mean deviation from target length, RMSE in length adherence, and problem-solving accuracy (pass rate) across various target lengths (`{512, 1024, 2048, 3600}` tokens).
 - Baselines included DeepSeek-R1-1.5B, DeepScaleR-24K (unconstrained), DeepScaleR-4K (constrained to 4K context for fair comparison), and S1 (Muennighoff et al., 2025), a budget-forcing heuristic method.

This RL-based methodology allows the models to *learn* adaptive reasoning strategies to fit different length constraints, in contrast to rigid, hand-engineered approaches that often disrupt the reasoning process.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research paper presents a compelling set of findings demonstrating the effectiveness and broad utility of Length Controlled Policy Optimization (LCPO) and the resulting L1 models.

1. **Superior Performance and Length Control:**
 - Both L1-Exact and L1-Max significantly outperform the state-of-the-art heuristic-based length control method, S1, across various math reasoning benchmarks (AIME, MATH, AMC, Olympiad-Bench). L1 achieves remarkable improvements, showing over **100-150% relative and 20-25% absolute performance gains** compared to S1 at various token budgets (e.g., 512 and 1024 tokens).
 - L1 models enable a **smooth trade-off between computational cost and accuracy**. By simply varying the length constraint in the prompt, users can dynamically adjust the model's "thinking time" to achieve desired performance levels within a given token budget.
 - L1-Max, in particular, matches the performance of the unconstrained DeepScaleR-4K baseline, demonstrating that it can achieve high accuracy while efficiently managing token usage based on problem difficulty.

2. **Robust Generalization to Out-of-Domain (OOD) Tasks:**
 - L1 models exhibit strong generalization capabilities, extending their length control and performance scaling to out-of-domain tasks, including logical reasoning (GPQA, LSAT) and general knowledge benchmarks (MMLU).
 - Performance consistently scales positively with token budget for OOD reasoning tasks, approaching or matching unconstrained baselines. This indicates that the learned adaptive reasoning is not specific to the training distribution.

3. **High Precision in Length Adherence:**
 - LCPO-trained models maintain consistent and precise control over output lengths. For math reasoning datasets, the mean deviation from target lengths is very low, close to **3%**.
 - While OOD datasets show higher mean errors (20-40%), these are still preferable to uncontrolled generation. Further RL training can significantly reduce these errors, albeit with a minor trade-off in performance at higher token ranges.
 - L1-Max demonstrates very low "soft violation" rates (less than 2.5%), indicating its reliability in respecting maximum budget constraints.

4. **Discovery of Strong Short Reasoning Models (SRMs):**
 - A remarkable and unexpected finding is that models trained with LCPO to generate *longer* reasoning chains also become exceptionally strong at generating *short* reasoning chains.
 - When prompted for short CoT sequences, these "Short Reasoning Models" (SRMs) consistently outperform their non-reasoning base model counterparts and even significantly larger frontier models like **GPT-4o and Llama-3.3-70B**, despite using equivalent token budgets. Specifically, the 1.5B L1 model surpasses GPT-4o by 2% on average.
 - This suggests that LCPO enables models to distill efficient reasoning patterns, allowing a small model to achieve high performance with minimal compute, challenging assumptions about model scaling.

5. **Adaptive Reasoning Strategies:**
 - Analysis of keyword usage shows that L1 adaptively adjusts its reasoning patterns based on the requested token budget. Longer CoTs feature more "self-correction and verification" and "conclusion drawing" terms, indicating a more thorough and robust reasoning process. Shorter CoTs, while still following reasoning patterns, adjust the relative frequencies of these terms.
 - The ratio of "thinking tokens" to "solution tokens" remains relatively stable across different generation lengths, implying efficient allocation.

6. **Ablations and Robustness:**
 - **RL vs. SFT:** Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) based on relabeling reasoning traces proved ineffective for length control, highlighting the necessity of an online, length-sensitive RL reward.
 - **Reward Function:** A multiplicative reward function (Equation 2) was found to be optimal for LCPO-Max, preventing undesirable behavior seen with additive formulations (which collapsed to trivially short CoTs).
 - **Sampling Parameters:** L1's length control capabilities are robust across varying decoding temperatures (0.0 to 1.0), maintaining consistent performance and length adherence.
 - **Scaling:** The LCPO approach successfully scales to larger models, demonstrating similar benefits with a 7B reasoning model.

These findings collectively establish LCPO as a highly effective method for bringing precise control to reasoning LLMs, unlocking new levels of efficiency and performance, especially for smaller models.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The work presented in "L1: Controlling How Long A Reasoning Model Thinks With Reinforcement Learning" holds significant implications for both the research community and the practical deployment of large language models.

1. **Enabling Efficient and Cost-Effective LLM Deployment:** By providing precise control over reasoning length, LCPO directly addresses a critical pain point in LLM deployment: unpredictable computational costs. Organizations can now allocate specific compute budgets (token usage) to LLM-powered applications, enabling fine-grained control over inference latency and operational expenses. This allows for optimized resource allocation, ensuring that models "think" just long enough to achieve desired accuracy levels without wasting compute. This is particularly impactful for high-volume or latency-sensitive applications.

2. **Advancing Adaptive Reasoning and Test-Time Scaling:** The paper pushes the boundaries of test-time scaling by introducing an adaptive, RL-based mechanism for dynamic compute allocation. Unlike prior heuristic methods that degrade performance, LCPO enables models to intelligently adjust their reasoning strategies to fit various constraints. This represents a significant step towards more flexible and intelligent LLMs that can self-regulate their "thinking process" based on explicit user demands.

3. **Unlocking Potential of Smaller Models and Challenging Scaling Laws:** The discovery of "Short Reasoning Models" (SRMs) is perhaps the most intriguing and potentially transformative finding. Demonstrating that a 1.5B parameter model can outperform GPT-4o at comparable short reasoning lengths challenges the prevailing narrative that sheer model size is the sole determinant of frontier performance. This suggests that effective RL-based fine-tuning can imbue smaller models with a disproportionate reasoning capability, making them highly efficient alternatives for scenarios where latency, cost, and model size are critical considerations. This could democratize access to advanced reasoning capabilities, as smaller, highly optimized models are more accessible to a wider range of researchers and practitioners.

4. **Foundational for Future Research:** LCPO provides a robust methodology for future work in controllable and efficient LLMs.
 - It opens avenues for exploring more sophisticated reward functions, dynamic constraint handling, and the integration of length control with other inference-time optimizations.
 - The ability to precisely control reasoning depth could lead to new ways of analyzing model behavior, understanding how reasoning complexity scales, and potentially developing more interpretable AI systems.
 - The observed robustness to out-of-domain tasks indicates that the learned control generalizes well, a desirable property for real-world applications.

5. **Improved User Experience:** For end-users, this work means more predictable and customizable interactions with LLMs. Developers can design applications that offer users choices regarding the "thoroughness" of an AI's thought process, directly impacting response time and cost, thereby enhancing the overall user experience.

In summary, L1 presents a significant advancement in making powerful reasoning language models more practical, efficient, and controllable. By intelligently balancing compute and accuracy through reinforcement learning, it paves the way for a new generation of adaptive and cost-effective AI systems.
