# Afford-X: Generalizable and Slim Affordance Reasoning for Task-oriented Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> The Afford-X framework introduces a slim and generalizable multimodal model for task-oriented affordance reasoning, coupled with new large-scale datasets. It achieved 60.67% mAP on COCO-Aff and 67.26% mAP on LVIS-Aff in 3D scenes, efficiently identifying task-relevant objects and outperforming LLM-based pipelines for robotic manipulation.

## Problem

- Generalizability of affordance reasoning models is often limited in novel scenarios and prone to biased understanding by prioritizing object nouns over task actions.
- Large-scale Multimodal Language Models (MLLMs) are too computationally intensive for local deployment on robotic hardware due to resource and latency constraints.
- The lack of comprehensive, diverse datasets capturing many-to-many affordance relationships hinders the training of robust and generalizable models.

## Method

- **Afford-X**, a slim, end-to-end trainable multimodal reasoning model, incorporates ==Verb Attention (VA)== and Bi-Fusion (BF) modules to enhance action-focused understanding and cross-modal alignment.
- A Noun-Pronoun Distillation framework transfers category-specific knowledge from a teacher model to a general-purpose student model, enabling category-agnostic object inference.
- An automated pipeline uses GPT-4 to convert existing object detection datasets into large-scale, diverse affordance reasoning datasets (COCO-Aff, LVIS-Aff), significantly expanding coverage.

## Results

- Afford-X achieved **45.3%** mAP_box and **39.2%** mAP_mask on COCO-Tasks, surpassing state-of-the-art non-LLM methods, and demonstrated superior generalization to unseen tasks on COCO-Aff and LVIS-Aff.
- The model reached **2.38** FPS inference speed on an RTX 3090, performing nearly 50 times faster than GPT-4V and outperforming LLM-based pipelines with **60.67%** mAP on COCO-Aff and **67.26%** mAP on LVIS-Aff in 3D scenes.
- Integrated into a robotic system, Afford-X enabled versatile task-oriented grasping with an **80.44%** mean success rate in diverse simulated environments and facilitated complex, long-horizon tasks via sophisticated preference modeling.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - One-stage, end-to-end training is more effective for capturing vision-language aligned features than two-stage methods for affordance reasoning.
> - Knowledge distillation from noun-explicit to pronoun-based task descriptions, combined with clustering and preference alignment, significantly improves model generalizability for category-agnostic object identification.
> - The Verb Attention and Bi-Fusion modules effectively enhance action-oriented understanding and fine-grained vision-language integration, contributing to better performance and reduced noun-based bias.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in this paper addresses object affordance reasoning, a fundamental capability for autonomous agents that extends beyond simple object recognition to infer object functionalities based on physical properties. This concept, initially introduced by James J. Gibson, is crucial for task-oriented planning and manipulation in both human cognition and Artificial Intelligence (AI) and robotics.

Historically, affordance research in computer vision transitioned from handcrafted feature-based methods to deep learning approaches, which leverage multi-layer networks and large-scale datasets to extract rich object features and learn affordance cues. More recent advancements integrate semantic information and utilize transformer-based architectures, particularly vision-language pre-training models (e.g., MDETR, CLIP, BLIP), for sophisticated cross-modal alignment. These models have demonstrated enhanced flexibility and generalizability compared to traditional Convolutional Neural Network (CNN)-based methods.

A significant challenge in this field is the generalizability of computational models to novel scenarios and the practical deployment of comprehensive Large Language Models (LLMs) or Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) on local devices due to computational and memory constraints. While LLMs possess extensive commonsense knowledge, their large parameter sizes and reliance on API access limit their applicability for real-time, local inference on robotic platforms. Smaller pre-trained MLLMs often suffer from limited knowledge bases and reasoning capabilities. Moreover, training slim multimodal models is challenging, as models trained on object detection datasets can develop biases towards familiar nouns, misinterpreting affordance tasks as simple object detection.

Dataset availability is another critical factor. Previous affordance datasets like ADE-Affordance, PAD, COCO-Tasks, and RIO, while innovative, have limitations in terms of task diversity, image quantity, and object category variety. The complex many-to-many relationships between tasks and objects (a single object can serve multiple tasks, and a task can be accomplished by various objects) make manual annotation-based dataset creation impractical for achieving broad coverage.

In the context of task-oriented manipulation, existing robotic systems often presuppose the identification of suitable objects, thus limiting autonomy. This work aims to bridge this gap by enabling robots to autonomously reason about and select appropriate objects based on task requirements, moving towards more flexible and adaptive AI systems. Afford-X positions itself by proposing an efficient, generalizable, and slim model capable of local deployment, supported by new large-scale affordance datasets.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind the development of Afford-X are as follows:

1. **Enhance Generalizability of Affordance Reasoning:** Current computational models for affordance reasoning from perception often lack generalizability, which restricts their applicability in novel or unseen scenarios. The authors aim to overcome this limitation to enable robust affordance understanding across diverse objects, tasks, and environments.
2. **Enable Local Deployment on Resource-Constrained Devices:** Large-scale MLLMs, while powerful in reasoning, are computationally intensive and challenging to deploy on local devices such as robotic platforms (e.g., NVIDIA Jetson Orin or RTX 4090 GPUs with limited memory). This work seeks to develop a "slim" model architecture that can operate efficiently offline and locally, addressing computational constraints, power limitations, usage policies, and information security concerns associated with cloud-based LLM APIs.
3. **Address Biased Understanding in Slim Multimodal Models:** Existing slim Multimodal Models (MMs) trained directly on object detection datasets often develop a biased understanding, overemphasizing familiar nouns. This can lead to misinterpretations where models fixate on detecting a prominent object rather than understanding the action implied by a task (e.g., confusing "clean bottle with" as detecting a bottle instead of identifying a cleaning tool). A key objective is to ensure accurate task understanding by emphasizing action words and reducing noun-based interference.
4. **Improve Multi-modal Understanding:** Simple concatenation of visual and language features in some pre-trained MMs may limit their comprehension of complex interactions between modalities. The authors aim to improve fine-grained vision-language alignment to capture more nuanced relationships essential for affordance reasoning.
5. **Develop Large-Scale, Diverse Affordance Reasoning Datasets:** The lack of comprehensive, diverse datasets that capture the many-to-many mapping between affordance tasks and target objects hinders the training of robust and generalizable models. The motivation here is to create a scalable, automated pipeline for constructing large-scale affordance knowledge bases from existing object detection datasets, significantly reducing the need for extensive human annotation. These datasets should offer broader coverage across tasks, images, and object categories.
6. **Facilitate Autonomous Task-Oriented Manipulation in Robotics:** Traditional task-oriented robotics often requires explicit object specification, limiting robot autonomy. The ultimate motivation is to integrate enhanced affordance reasoning capabilities into embodied agents, enabling robots to autonomously identify and interact with task-appropriate objects, thus advancing truly autonomous and flexible robotic systems for real-world applications.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The Afford-X framework is an end-to-end trainable, slim multimodal reasoning model designed for generalizable affordance reasoning. Its methodology encompasses novel model architecture, a knowledge distillation strategy, specialized modules, and an automated dataset construction pipeline.

#### 3.1. Affordance Reasoning Model Architecture (Afford-X)

Afford-X employs parallel visual and textual pathways (Fig. 4). A pre-trained visual encoder extracts visual features from an RGB image ($X_v$), and a text encoder processes a task description ($X_l$) to generate textual features. These features are then enhanced by two key components:

- **Verb Attention (VA) Module**: This module processes textual features ($F_t$) to generate enhanced text features ($F'_t$) that emphasize action words (verbs) within the task description. This helps the model prioritize task-specific actions over prominent object nouns, addressing the issue of categorical bias. It uses cross-attention between verb features and the full textual prompt.
- **Bi-Fusion (BF) Module**: Inspired by BLIP, this module integrates the enhanced textual features ($F'_t$) with visual features ($F_v$) through bi-directional cross-modal attention. This approach captures fine-grained vision-language associations more effectively than simple feature concatenation, producing fused features ($F'_v$ and $F''_t$).

The fused features are then fed into a Transformer encoder-decoder architecture. The encoder captures global relationships, and the decoder uses learnable query vectors to generate refined outputs. These outputs are projected through parallel heads to predict bounding boxes ($B_{pred}$), instance segmentation masks ($M_{pred}$), and preference scores ($S_{pred}$) indicating object suitability.

The training objective for Afford-X ($L_{Afford-X}$) combines standard losses: L1 loss and Generalized Intersection over Union (GIoU) loss for bounding boxes, Dice/F-1 loss and focal cross-entropy loss for segmentation, and adaptations of soft-token prediction loss and contrastive alignment loss from MDETR, focusing on the entire verb-pronoun description.

#### 3.2. Noun-Pronoun Distillation Framework

To enable object inference without explicit category labels and transfer category-specific knowledge to a general-purpose model, Afford-X introduces a noun-pronoun distillation framework (Fig. 5). This framework consists of a teacher model and a student model:

- **Teacher Model**: Trained on task descriptions that explicitly include object labels (e.g., "sit comfortably on a couch").
- **Student Model**: Processes category-free descriptions (e.g., "sit comfortably on something").

Knowledge transfer occurs through two main mechanisms:

- **Clustering Distillation**: A text feature memory bank stores noun features from the teacher model. During training, the student model's cluster selector, using nearest neighbor classification, selects an appropriate noun prototype from the memory bank based on the pronoun feature. A cluster loss ($L_{cluster}$) minimizes the Euclidean distance between the pronoun feature and the selected cluster center.
- **Preference Distillation**: This aligns the teacher and student predictions using a soft binary target loss based on Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence. Binary probabilities (positive/negative match for ground truth objects) are computed for each object query. The Hungarian algorithm matches teacher and student predictions, and a $L_{binary}$ loss aligns their probability sequences, effectively transferring preference knowledge.

The overall training loss ($L_{Afford-X-NP}$) combines the individual losses of the teacher and student models with the cluster and binary distillation losses. During inference, only the student model is used with a fixed memory bank, enabling category-agnostic object identification.

#### 3.3. Automated Dataset Construction Pipeline

To address the scarcity of large-scale, diverse affordance datasets, the authors developed an automated pipeline that converts existing object detection datasets into affordance reasoning datasets (Fig. 6). This pipeline leverages GPT-4 in dual roles:

1. **Task Prompt Collection**: A GPT-4-based "task producer" generates 10 diverse tasks per object category, forming an initial pool of potential uses.
2. **Task and Object Category Matching**: A GPT-4-based "pair producer" matches these tasks with relevant object categories and assigns commonsense preference rankings (e.g., for "drink water with," a cup ranks higher than a bottle).
3. **Task Pairs Inspection**: A GPT-4-based "inspector" performs quality control by filtering tasks against predefined criteria, verifying object-task match rankings, and removing inappropriate pairs. Optional manual review further reduces redundancy.
4. **Image Collection**: Images are sampled from source datasets (COCO2014, LVIS) into four configurations: Multiple Categories, Multiple Objects (MCMO); Single Category, Multiple Objects (SCMO); Single Category, Single Object (SCSO); and Others (random images without target categories). Highest-priority objects are retained per task, and prompts are appended with "something" for generalization.

This pipeline led to the creation of **COCO-Aff** (112k images, 1,144 tasks, 80 categories from COCO2014) and **LVIS-Aff** (119k images, 1,494 tasks, 1,064 categories from LVIS), which significantly expand task coverage, image quantity, and object category diversity.

#### 3.4. Embodied Affordance Reasoning

The model's practical application is demonstrated through integration with embodied agents in simulated environments (NVIDIA Isaac Sim).

- **Affordance Reasoning in 3D Scenes**: Evaluation is conducted in multi-source scenes (Evermotion, OmniGibson) augmented with Objaverse textured meshes. Objects are randomly placed, including both task-suitable and unsuitable items, to test discrimination. An RGB-D camera provides environmental perception, and Isaac Sim's ray-tracing renderer generates photorealistic images. Afford-X processes RGB images and task instructions to generate segmentation masks for task-relevant objects.
- **Task-oriented Manipulation**: The system architecture (Fig. 7) combines Afford-X with manipulation frameworks. NVIDIA cuRobo serves as the motion planner for collision-aware trajectory generation, and GraspNet computes grasp poses using RGB-D input and Afford-X's segmentation masks. The manipulation procedure involves: (i) Affordance perception by Afford-X, (ii) Viewpoint optimization for detailed perception, and (iii) Task execution (e.g., grasping). For complex, long-horizon tasks, an LLM decomposes high-level goals into atomic sub-tasks, each executed sequentially using the three-phase protocol.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded several key findings regarding the performance, efficiency, and generalizability of Afford-X, as well as the utility of the constructed datasets.

#### 4.1. Performance Analysis

- **Superiority over State-of-the-Art (non-LLM) Methods**: Afford-X demonstrated consistent performance improvements across all evaluated datasets (COCO-Tasks, COCO-Aff, LVIS-Aff). On COCO-Tasks, it achieved 45.3% mAP$_{box}$ and 39.2% mAP$_{mask}$, outperforming the previous best-reported non-LLM method (TOIST) by 1.2% mAP$_{box}$ and 0.2% mAP$_{mask}$. Compared to the baseline MDETR, Afford-X showed a 4.0% mAP$_{box}$ improvement on COCO-Tasks.
- **Effectiveness of One-Stage End-to-End Training**: One-stage MDETR-based approaches consistently outperformed two-stage methods (e.g., Fast R-CNN + GGNN, YOLO + GGNN), highlighting the benefits of end-to-end training for capturing vision-language aligned features crucial for affordance reasoning.
- **Qualitative Observations**: While Afford-X successfully identified major affordance regions, it occasionally struggled with fine-grained functional elements or precise bounding box localization, as seen in examples like "iron clothes with" where the power cord was missed, or "clean windows with" showing imprecise bounding box extension.

#### 4.2. Ablation Studies

- **Knowledge Transfer through Noun-Pronoun Distillation**: The proposed two-stage Noun-Pronoun Distillation framework, transferring knowledge from a teacher model (noun-based) to a student model (pronoun-based) via an intermediate feature space, improved verb-pronoun mAP$_{box}$ from 41.3% (plain MDETR) to 44.1%. This method surpassed direct distillation between pronoun-based and noun-based representations (41.9% mAP$_{box}$).
- **Clustering Distillation Components**: The combination of Cluster Center Replacement (CCR), Cluster Loss (CL), and Soft Binary Target Loss (SBTL) within clustering distillation yielded substantial gains (+2.8% mAP$_{box}$ and +3.8% mAP$_{mask}$) over the baseline, confirming the effectiveness of anchoring pronoun tokens, promoting feature grouping, and refining preference modeling.
- **VA and BF Modules**: Both the Verb Attention (VA) and Bi-Fusion (BF) modules contributed to performance improvements across all input configurations (verb-pronoun, verb-noun, distill).
 - VA module: Emphasized action-related cues, reducing noun-based interference, with gains of +1.9% mAP$_{box}$ (verb-pronoun).
 - BF module: Enhanced visual-language alignment through bi-directional cross-modal attention, with gains of +1.7% mAP$_{box}$ (verb-pronoun).
 - Combined VA and BF modules: Demonstrated synergistic benefits, achieving +2.1% mAP$_{box}$ for verb-pronoun input and +1.6% for verb-noun input.
- **Optimal Cluster Number (K)**: Experiments showed that K=3 was the optimal choice for the clustering distillation framework, balancing feature aggregation and information preservation.
- **Robustness to Pronoun Input and Pre-training Absence**: The distillation framework showed robustness to different pronoun inputs (e.g., "something", "it", "them") and even provided noticeable improvements when trained from scratch without pre-training.

#### 4.3. Dataset Analysis

- **Task-Scale Analysis**: Training on larger task-scale datasets (COCO-Aff with 1,144 tasks, LVIS-Aff with 1,496 tasks) significantly improved generalization to unseen tasks. The COCO-Tasks-trained model showed poor generalization (1.6% mAP$_{box}$ on unseen tasks), while COCO-Aff improved this to 24.5% mAP$_{box}$, and LVIS-Aff further to 26.3% mAP$_{box}$.
- **Category-Scale Analysis**: LVIS-Aff, with its expanded 1,064 object categories, demonstrated improved performance on both common and novel categories compared to COCO-Aff, enhancing both affordance reasoning and generalization.

#### 4.4. Affordance Reasoning in 3D Scenes (LLM Comparison)

- **Computational Efficiency**: Afford-X, with a compact 187M parameter size, achieved 2.38 FPS inference speed on an RTX 3090 GPU, making it nearly 50 times faster than GPT-4V API (0.04 FPS) and significantly faster than other LLM-based pipelines (e.g., SPHINX (CoT) at 0.49 FPS).
- **Performance vs. LLMs**: Afford-X demonstrated superior performance in 3D affordance reasoning, achieving 60.67% mAP on COCO-Aff and 67.26% mAP on LVIS-Aff. This significantly surpassed LLM-based pipelines:
 - Detect-before-Reasoning (Detection + GPT-4): Poor performance (2.33% recall, 3.44% mAP) due to semantic ambiguity.
 - Reason-before-Detection (GPT-4V + OpenSeeD): Achieved 49.61% recall and 50.63% mAP, but still below Afford-X, potentially due to LLM constraints on unconventional solutions.
 - End-to-end MLLM (SPHINX (CoT)): Achieved 39.76% mAP, indicating parameter efficiency limitations compared to MM-based methods for visual problems in language space.
- **Qualitative Advantages**: Afford-X successfully identified unconventional object affordances (e.g., green pepper as a container), overcoming LLM limitations in semantic ambiguity and visual interpretation (e.g., distinguishing structural glass from drinking vessels).

#### 4.5. Task-oriented Manipulation in Diverse Environments

- **Versatile Manipulation**: The robotic system integrated with Afford-X successfully performed various affordance-based grasping tasks across diverse simulated indoor environments (dining room, living room, office) with varying layouts and object configurations, demonstrating a mean success rate of 80.44%.
- **Sophisticated Preference Modeling**: When multiple objects could afford a task, Afford-X demonstrated hierarchical preference modeling aligned with human utility. For "prepare drinks with," it prioritized a blender, then a pitcher, then a mug. For "lay comfortably on," it preferred a lounge over a chair. This capability was attributed to the self-attention (SA) layers in the decoder, which progressively refined pairwise preferences.
- **Long-Horizon Task Accomplishment**: Integrated with an LLM-based task planner, Afford-X enabled a mobile manipulator to decompose and execute complex, multi-step tasks (e.g., "build a workspace"), demonstrating its potential for strategic decision-making and functional versatility in complex operational environments.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The work presented in this paper, Afford-X, makes several contributions with significant implications for the fields of AI and robotics:

1. **Advancement in Generalizable Affordance Reasoning**: By developing a model that exhibits strong generalization capabilities across diverse tasks and object categories, Afford-X contributes to building more robust AI systems that can adapt to novel situations and environments without extensive re-training. This moves beyond the limitations of previous models that struggled with unfamiliar scenarios or biased interpretations.
2. **Enabling On-Device AI for Robotics**: The compact parameter size (187M) and real-time inference speed (2.38 FPS) of Afford-X address a critical challenge in robotics: the need for powerful AI capabilities that can be deployed locally on resource-constrained platforms. This efficiency mitigates concerns related to computational power, energy consumption, network latency, and data privacy associated with cloud-based LLMs, making sophisticated affordance reasoning practical for real-world robotic applications.
3. **Improved Multimodal Understanding**: The introduction of the Verb Attention (VA) and Bi-Fusion (BF) modules enhances the model's ability to interpret complex visual and textual information, focusing on action-relevant cues and capturing fine-grained cross-modal associations. This leads to a deeper understanding of object functionalities, which is fundamental for intelligent interaction.
4. **Creation of Large-Scale Affordance Datasets**: The automated pipeline for converting object detection datasets into comprehensive affordance reasoning datasets (COCO-Aff and LVIS-Aff) provides a valuable resource for the research community. These datasets, with their expanded coverage of tasks, images, and object categories, facilitate the training of more generalizable and robust affordance models, addressing a previous bottleneck in dataset availability and annotation effort.
5. **Enhanced Autonomous Task-Oriented Manipulation**: By integrating affordance reasoning into robotic manipulation frameworks, Afford-X enables embodied agents to autonomously identify and select appropriate objects for given tasks. This capability reduces reliance on human supervision or predefined rules, leading to more flexible and intelligent robots capable of independent decision-making and execution in dynamic environments, including complex, long-horizon tasks.
6. **Bridging the Gap Between Perception and Action**: The comprehensive evaluation in simulated 3D environments, including comparisons with LLM-based pipelines, demonstrates that domain-specific, end-to-end trained multimodal models can outperform generic MLLMs for affordance reasoning tasks. This suggests a promising direction for developing specialized AI components that are highly efficient and effective for specific robotic functionalities.

**Potential Limitations and Future Impact**:
The authors acknowledge two primary limitations:
1. **Reliance on Geometric Features**: Geometric features alone are sometimes insufficient to distinguish between objects with similar shapes but different affordances (e.g., a drinking cup vs. a toothbrush holder). Future work could integrate contextual knowledge bases (e.g., common usage scenarios, hygiene factors) to address this.
2. **Lack of 3D Information**: The current framework's reliance primarily on 2D RGB images limits its performance in complex spatial environments, particularly concerning object relationships and occlusion. Incorporating depth information (RGB-D) could significantly enhance spatial understanding.

Despite these limitations, Afford-X represents a step towards more efficient, generalizable, and deployable AI systems for task-oriented manipulation, with broad implications for advancing robotics and intelligent agents in real-world scenarios.
