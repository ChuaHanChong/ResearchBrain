# MuLoCo: Muon is a practical inner optimizer for DiLoCo

> [!summary] Summary
> Mila researchers demonstrate that Muon optimizer significantly outperforms AdamW as the inner optimizer in DiLoCo distributed training frameworks, with MuLoCo achieving 8x communication reduction through 2-bit quantization and error feedback while maintaining faster convergence than standard DiLoCo on 220M parameter transformer pre-training, establishing that Muon updates are more resilient to aggressive compression and providing the first systematic evaluation of how inner optimizer choice affects communication efficiency in distributed large language model training.

## Problem

- Communication bottleneck in distributed LLM training creates major scalability limitations as networks require frequent synchronization of large parameter updates
- Previous DiLoCo implementations used AdamW without systematic investigation of how inner optimizer choice affects compression effectiveness and communication efficiency
- Lack of comprehensive evaluation of error feedback mechanisms combined with different optimizers in communication-constrained distributed training scenarios

## Method

- Replace AdamW with ==Muon optimizer in DiLoCo framework== to create MuLoCo, leveraging Muon's superior optimization properties for distributed settings
- Implement systematic ==compression schemes including quantization and top-k sparsification== with ==error feedback== to accumulate information lost during compression
- Conduct thorough hyperparameter optimization and ablation studies across different compression levels and error feedback configurations

## Results

- MuLoCo with **2-bit** quantization and error feedback achieves **8x** communication reduction while converging faster than uncompressed DiLoCo with AdamW
- MuLoCo without compression matches data-parallel baseline performance while providing substantial communication savings compared to traditional distributed training
- Error feedback consistently improves performance across all compression schemes, with MuLoCo showing superior robustness to aggressive quantization levels

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Muon optimizer produces updates that are inherently more compressible and resilient to quantization compared to AdamW updates
> - Error feedback mechanisms consistently improve convergence for both optimizers but provide greater benefits when combined with Muon
> - Communication efficiency and optimization performance are not mutually exclusive when proper optimizer-compression combinations are used

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The training of Large Language Models (LLMs) is constrained by two primary factors: the extensive wall-clock training times due to a large number of sequential gradient descent steps, and communication overhead across a multitude of accelerators in data-parallel (DP) training. Efficiently training LLMs at scales involving trillions of parameters and tokens necessitates strategies that address these bottlenecks.

Large batch training is a common approach to reduce wall-clock time by leveraging more accelerators. However, increasing batch size beyond a "critical batch size" (CBS) can lead to diminishing returns in performance. Furthermore, large-scale data-parallel training often exacerbates communication costs, especially when accelerators are not tightly interconnected.

Communication-efficient algorithms have emerged to mitigate these issues. DiLoCo (Distributed Low-Communication) is one such framework that has gained attention for training LLMs. DiLoCo allows workers to train independently for several steps (H) before synchronizing their model parameters, thereby reducing communication frequency. This approach can yield significant speedups in environments where intra-worker communication is much faster than inter-worker communication.

However, prior research (Charles et al., 2025) indicated that DiLoCo's performance can degrade as the number of workers (K) increases. This degradation limits its utility at very large worker counts, where communication efficiency is most critical. Concurrently, Muon, a second-order optimizer, has demonstrated improved performance and larger critical batch sizes compared to AdamW in data-parallel LLM pre-training.

This work integrates Muon into the DiLoCo framework, creating MuLoCo, to investigate whether Muon's properties can address DiLoCo's limitations. The research positions itself as an advancement in communication-efficient distributed optimization, aiming to improve the scalability and performance of LLM training, particularly under conditions of increasing worker counts and communication constraints. It builds upon existing knowledge of distributed optimization, large-batch training, and novel optimizers like Muon, seeking to identify practical improvements for pre-training models at immense scales.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives of this research were to:

1. **Address DiLoCo's worker scaling problem:** Previous studies showed that DiLoCo's performance degrades as the number of workers (K) increases. The authors aimed to mitigate this degradation by exploring the impact of the inner optimizer.
2. **Increase DiLoCo's critical batch size (CBS):** A larger CBS enables the use of more accelerators in parallel without significant performance loss, leading to faster wall-clock training times.
3. **Investigate the role of the inner optimizer:** The authors hypothesized that the choice of the inner optimizer, which shapes the pseudogradient used by the outer optimizer, is a critical, often overlooked factor in DiLoCo's behavior. Given Muon's recent success in data-parallel training and its distinct normalized optimizer steps, they conjectured that Muon could improve pseudogradient quality in a Local SGD framework like DiLoCo.
4. **Systematically compare MuLoCo to DiLoCo:** This involved evaluating MuLoCo (DiLoCo with Muon as the inner optimizer) against DiLoCo (DiLoCo with AdamW as the inner optimizer) across various distributed training settings. These settings included a large number of workers (K), long communication intervals (H), compressed communication, streaming updates, and assessment under wall-clock training time metrics.
5. **Examine scalability to large model sizes:** The research aimed to determine if any observed performance advantages of MuLoCo persist and improve at larger model scales, up to 15 billion parameters, following a Chinchilla-optimal compute prescription.
6. **Understand the underlying mechanisms:** Through empirical analysis of pseudogradient properties (e.g., directional alignment, singular value spectra) and theoretical derivation of nuclear norms, the authors aimed to provide insights into why Muon might yield more effective pseudogradients than AdamW within the DiLoCo framework.

The motivation for these objectives stems from the increasing scale of LLMs, which necessitates more efficient training methods to overcome computational and communication bottlenecks. Improving DiLoCo's scalability and critical batch size would directly contribute to reducing the immense wall-clock training times and infrastructure costs associated with state-of-the-art LLMs.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research introduces MuLoCo, a modification of the DiLoCo framework that replaces its standard AdamW inner optimizer with Muon. The methodology encompasses both theoretical analysis and extensive empirical evaluation.

#### 3.1. MuLoCo Algorithm

MuLoCo operates similarly to DiLoCo:
- Each worker `k` independently performs `H` local optimization steps using Muon.
- After `H` steps, workers compute their parameter difference `Δ^(t)_k = θ^(t-H) - θ^(t)_k`.
- These parameter differences are then averaged across all `K` workers to form a pseudogradient `Ψ^(t) = (1/K) Σ Δ^(t)_k`.
- The pseudogradient `Ψ^(t)` is used by an outer optimizer (SGD with Nesterov momentum) to update the global model parameters `θ^(t)`.
The paper also presents a compressed version of MuLoCo, incorporating error feedback and different compression schemes.

#### 3.2. Theoretical Analysis of Pseudogradient Quality

To understand MuLoCo's benefits, the authors analyzed properties of the pseudogradient:
- **Cosine Similarity:** The directional alignment of MuLoCo's and DiLoCo's pseudogradients was measured against a K=1 (data-parallel) pseudogradient. This was done by computing the cosine similarity between vectorized tensors of all hidden weight matrices in a 416M transformer.
- **Pseudogradient Spectrum and Interference Gap:** The singular value spectra of individual worker weight differences (`Δ_k`) and their average (`Ψ`) were examined. An "interference gap" was introduced (Definition 4.1) to quantify the loss of top-S spectral mass during the averaging process. A larger gap indicates stronger destructive interference, potentially leading to poorer pseudogradient quality.
- **Nuclear Norm Relation:** A theoretical proposition (Proposition 4.2) was derived, linking the nuclear norm of the pseudogradient (`||Ψ||*`) to the cosine similarity (`ρ`) between individual optimizer steps (`ψ^(t+h,k)`) and the orthonormal factor of the pseudogradient (`Ψ*`), as well as the Frobenius norm (`||ψ^(t+h,k)||F`) of these steps. This proposition highlights that for Muon's orthonormalized steps, `||ψ^(t+h,k)||F` is `sqrt(r)` (where `r` is the rank), simplifying the nuclear norm's dependence. For AdamW, `||ψ^(t+h,k)||F` can be variable.

#### 3.3. Empirical Evaluation Setup

The empirical study involved extensive pre-training of language models:
- **Model Scales:** Experiments were conducted on Gemma3-style transformer models ranging from 150M to 3.1B parameters for comprehensive tuning, and then scaled up to 15B parameters for final validation.
- **Dataset and Sequence Length:** All models were pre-trained on a high-quality portion of the Nemotron-CC dataset with a sequence length of 2048 tokens, following a 20 tokens per parameter (TPP) ratio for scaling.
- **Hyperparameter Tuning:** An extensive tuning process was implemented across 150M-3.1B models, covering weight decay (λ), inner learning rate (η_in_), global batch size (B), outer learning rate (η_out_), and outer momentum (μ). This involved multi-stage grid searches, with outer optimizer hyperparameters primarily tuned at 416M scale and reused, and weight decay rescaled based on batch size.
- **Baselines:** Performance was compared against DP AdamW and DP Muon baselines, in addition to DiLoCo with AdamW.
- **Evaluation Metrics:** The primary metric was a smoothed final validation loss, calculated using a time-weighted exponential moving average to reduce stochasticity. Wall-clock training time and zero-shot LM evaluation results (MMLU, HellaSwag, etc.) were also reported for larger models.
- **Ablation Studies:** The studies included varying the number of workers (K = 1 to 16), synchronization intervals (H = 15 to 240), applying different compression techniques (linear/statistical quantization, top-k sparsification, with and without error feedback), and utilizing partitioned communication (streaming DiLoCo).
- **Infrastructure:** Experiments were run on H100 and H200 GPUs, using TorchTitan and TorchFT for distributed optimization implementations, including HSDP with FSDP ranks.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded several key findings across various experimental settings:

#### 4.1. Worker Scaling (Increasing K)

- **Improved Relative Performance:** MuLoCo consistently outperformed DiLoCo in absolute terms due to Muon's superior performance. More significantly, for K > 2 workers, MuLoCo's performance degradation relative to its data-parallel (DP) Muon baseline was less pronounced than DiLoCo's degradation relative to its DP AdamW baseline (Figure 1a, Figure 6a). This indicates better worker scaling for MuLoCo.
- **Pseudogradient Alignment:** MuLoCo's pseudogradients were found to be more directionally aligned with the K=1 (data-parallel) pseudogradient than DiLoCo's (Figure 2). This alignment decreased more slowly as K increased for MuLoCo.
- **Pseudogradient Spectrum:** DiLoCo's pseudogradient spectrum experienced a greater collapse during averaging compared to MuLoCo's, and this collapse worsened with more workers. MuLoCo exhibited a shrinking interference gap with increasing worker count, while DiLoCo's grew (Figure 3), suggesting better preservation of spectral mass in MuLoCo.
- **Inner Step Alignment:** Muon's individual optimizer steps were more consistently aligned with the final pseudogradient direction than AdamW's, and worker weight differences under Muon showed less inter-worker variability (Figure 4). Muon's orthonormalized steps also led to stable Frobenius norms for individual steps, unlike AdamW's erratic norms (Figure 5).

#### 4.2. Synchronization Interval (Increasing H)

- MuLoCo maintained its relative performance advantage over DiLoCo as the synchronization interval H was increased from 15 to 240 steps (Figure 6b), indicating robustness to infrequent communication.

#### 4.3. Pseudogradient Compression

- **Quantization:** Both MuLoCo and DiLoCo achieved lossless compression with 4-bit quantization. MuLoCo consistently outperformed DiLoCo across all quantization bitwidths (8-bit, 4-bit, 2-bit), with 2-bit statistical quantization performing better than 2-bit linear quantization. Error feedback provided only modest improvements (Figure 7, Table 5).
- **Sparsification:** MuLoCo demonstrated greater robustness than DiLoCo at higher sparsities (e.g., 0.5%–5% retained entries). Error feedback helped prevent performance degradation, though extreme sparsities still led to substantial loss (Figure 8a, Table 4).

#### 4.4. Streaming Updates

- MuLoCo was found to be compatible with partitioned communication, showing no performance degradation compared to its non-streaming counterpart (Figure 8b).

#### 4.5. Wall-Clock Training Time

- **Negligible Overhead:** Despite Muon's more expensive optimizer step, MuLoCo introduced negligible overhead in end-to-end step time (less than 1% increase over DiLoCo at 3.1B scale) (Figure 9, Table 9).
- **Faster Convergence:** In bandwidth-constrained environments (e.g., 10 Gbit/s), MuLoCo achieved lower validation loss in less wall-clock time compared to DiLoCo and their respective DP baselines due to faster convergence and better worker scaling (Figure 9).

#### 4.6. Scaling up (150M-15B models)

- **Compute Scaling:** Muon-based methods (DP Muon, MuLoCo) generally exhibited smaller scaling exponents (α values in L(C) = AC^α + L_irr), indicating stronger scaling with compute compared to AdamW-based methods (Figure 10, Table 6). At lower irreducible loss values, higher-worker MuLoCo configurations are projected to become increasingly competitive with or surpass DP performance (Figure 17).
- **Relative Performance with Scale:** MuLoCo's improved worker scaling relative to DiLoCo was maintained at larger scales (Figure 11). For K=16 workers, MuLoCo's performance improved relative to DP Muon as model size increased, suggesting convergence towards DP Muon performance at larger scales.
- **Larger Critical Batch Size (CBS):** At 3.1B scale, MuLoCo exhibited consistently larger optimal and critical batch sizes (up to 2–4× larger) than DiLoCo across all worker counts, enabling more parallelizable training (Figure 12). K=1 MuLoCo is projected to have larger CBS than DP Muon at scale (Figure 13b).
- **Training Time Efficiency at Iso-Loss:** K=1 MuLoCo required approximately 4.5× fewer sequential FLOPs to reach the same loss as DP AdamW at observed scales, demonstrating superior training-time efficiency due to improved compute scaling and a substantially larger CBS (Figure 13a).
- **Pareto-Optimal Tradeoff:** In FLOP-matched comparisons, K=1 MuLoCo achieved a Pareto-optimal performance-training-time tradeoff, demonstrating stronger final performance while supporting the largest critical batch size (Figure 1b).

#### 4.7. 15B Model Training

- **Performance and Batch Size:** K=1 MuLoCo, trained with an extrapolated 16M token batch size, reached nearly identical final evaluation loss and zero-shot performance to K=1 DiLoCo (1M batch size) and DP Muon (4M batch size), which required more optimization steps (Table 3, Figure 19). K=16 MuLoCo also achieved comparable performance to DP AdamW and K=16 DiLoCo with a larger batch size.
- **Wall-Clock Time at 15B:** In low-bandwidth settings (10 Gbit/s), K=16 MuLoCo achieved the fastest wall-clock time-to-loss. In high-bandwidth settings (12,800 Gbit/s), K=1 MuLoCo was the fastest due to its ability to leverage very large batch sizes (Figure 14, Figure 20, Table 10).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work demonstrates that replacing the inner optimizer in DiLoCo with Muon yields an improved distributed optimization algorithm, MuLoCo, with notable advantages for large-scale LLM pre-training.

The primary significance lies in its **practical improvements for scaling LLM training**:
- **Enhanced Scalability:** MuLoCo addresses the worker scaling limitations of DiLoCo, showing more robust performance as the number of workers (K) increases, both in absolute terms and relative to data-parallel baselines. This expands the applicability of communication-efficient training to larger distributed clusters.
- **Larger Critical Batch Sizes:** The significantly larger critical batch sizes achieved by MuLoCo allow practitioners to leverage more data parallelism, leading to substantial reductions in wall-clock training time. This is particularly relevant for training the largest models, where traditional data-parallel methods are bottlenecked by communication or sub-optimal batch sizes.
- **Compatibility and Efficiency:** MuLoCo maintains compatibility with various communication-reducing techniques, including long synchronization intervals, pseudogradient compression (quantization and sparsification), and streaming updates. Its negligible overhead in optimizer step time, combined with faster convergence, translates to improved wall-clock training efficiency, especially in bandwidth-constrained environments.
- **Pareto-Optimal Performance:** The finding that K=1 MuLoCo offers a Pareto-optimal tradeoff between performance and training time suggests it can achieve superior model quality faster than existing methods, including data-parallel Muon, under certain conditions.

The **theoretical and empirical insights** into pseudogradient quality also contribute to the understanding of distributed optimization dynamics:
- The empirical observation that Muon's orthonormalized steps lead to more directionally aligned pseudogradients and less spectral collapse during averaging provides a mechanistic explanation for MuLoCo's improved performance.
- The theoretical link between the pseudogradient's nuclear norm and the alignment/Frobenius norm of individual optimizer steps offers a framework for analyzing the impact of inner optimizer choice on pseudogradient quality in local SGD-type algorithms.

In conclusion, MuLoCo presents a flexible and efficient distributed optimization algorithm that can accelerate the pre-training of LLMs. Its ability to scale better with workers, tolerate larger batch sizes, and integrate with communication-reducing techniques makes it a relevant method for current and future demands of large-scale AI research and development, particularly where infrastructure might be geographically distributed or have limited interconnect bandwidth.
