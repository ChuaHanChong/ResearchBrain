# VLS: Steering Pretrained Robot Policies via Vision-Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> The VLS framework enables pretrained robot policies to robustly adapt to out-of-distribution observation-language inputs at inference time without requiring retraining. It leverages Vision-Language Models to synthesize trajectory-differentiable reward functions, improving success rates on OOD tasks by up to 9.6x compared to base policies in simulation and demonstrating real-world efficacy.

## Problem

- Pretrained robot policies exhibit brittleness and severe performance drops when faced with even minor out-of-distribution (OOD) environmental changes or task instructions.
- Retraining or fine-tuning policies for every new OOD scenario is computationally expensive, data-intensive, and inefficient.
- Existing imitation learning approaches tightly couple action generation with specific training configurations, hindering generalization and OOD adaptation.

## Method

- VLS employs Vision-Language Models (VLMs) to ground OOD inputs and programmatically generate differentiable reward functions for task-relevant spatial requirements.
- It steers the action denoising process of frozen generative robot policies by injecting VLM-generated reward gradients, enhanced with diverse proposal initialization and Feynman–Kac resampling.
- A closed-loop execution control system uses adaptive guidance strength and a Schmitt-Trigger-based stage switching mechanism for robust multi-stage task execution in dynamic environments.

## Results

- VLS, applied to a frozen π0.5 policy, achieved average success rates of 94% on movable object tasks (7.4x improvement) and 87% on articulated part tasks (9.6x improvement) on the CALVIN benchmark, significantly outperforming prior steering methods.
- Leading pretrained VLA models (e.g., OpenVLA, π0) showed 0% success rates on OOD conditions in LIBERO-PRO, highlighting the necessity of VLS's adaptation.
- In real-world experiments with a Franka Emika robot, VLS significantly improved success rates under challenging OOD conditions, such as appearance and position shifts, achieving a 40% success rate even when replacing known objects with novel ones, where baselines failed.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Inference-time steering is critical for adapting pretrained robot policies to out-of-distribution observation and language shifts, as even advanced Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models fail without explicit adaptation.
> - Vision-Language Models can effectively synthesize differentiable programmatic reward functions, providing robust and flexible guidance signals for low-level robot control.
> - Robust inference-time adaptation benefits from combining gradient-free global exploration (e.g., repulsive forces, Feynman–Kac resampling) with gradient-based local refinement for efficient and stable action generation.
