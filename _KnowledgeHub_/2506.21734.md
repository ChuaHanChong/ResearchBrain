# Hierarchical Reasoning Model

> [!summary] Summary
> The Hierarchical Reasoning Model (HRM), developed by researchers from Sapient Intelligence and Tsinghua University, introduces a recurrent architecture designed for deep algorithmic reasoning. The model, trained on minimal data, achieves near-perfect accuracy on complex tasks like Sudoku-Extreme and Maze-Hard, where leading large language models fail, and outperforms them on the ARC-AGI challenge, while also demonstrating emergent brain-like hierarchical processing.

## Problem

- Large Language Models (LLMs) based on Transformer architectures possess a "paradoxically shallow" computational depth (AC0 or TC0), limiting their ability to perform complex algorithmic reasoning, planning, or symbolic manipulation.
- Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting, while used to enable LLMs to tackle complex problems, suffers from brittleness, high data requirements, increased latency, and a dependence on explicit linguistic patterns for reasoning.
- Standard recurrent neural networks (RNNs) struggle to achieve deep latent reasoning due to premature convergence (states settling too quickly) and high memory costs (O(T)) associated with Backpropagation Through Time (BPTT).

## Method

- A novel ==recurrent architecture== with two interdependent modules: a ==high-level (H) module== for slow, abstract planning and a ==low-level (L) module== for rapid, detailed computations, updated through a ==hierarchical convergence mechanism==.
- A memory-efficient =="one-step gradient" training approach==, inspired by Deep Equilibrium Models, which approximates gradients without requiring Backpropagation Through Time (BPTT), maintaining O(1) memory complexity.
- Incorporates ==Adaptive Computational Time (ACT)== using a Q-learning head, allowing the model to dynamically determine the number of computational steps based on task complexity, and enables performance scaling during inference by simply increasing the maximum step limit.

## Results

- Achieved near-perfect accuracy (e.g., **55.0%** on Sudoku-Extreme 9x9 and **74.5%** on Maze-Hard 30x30) with only **1000** training samples, tasks on which state-of-the-art CoT-based LLMs failed completely (**0%** accuracy).
- Outperformed significantly larger CoT-based LLMs on the ARC-AGI-1 challenge (**40.3%** vs. Claude 3.7's **21.2%** and o3-mini-high's **34.5%**), trained only on the official dataset (~1000 examples).
- Showed an emergent dimensionality hierarchy akin to the mouse cortex, with the high-level module (zH) exhibiting a much higher participation ratio (PR = 89.95) than the low-level module (zL, PR = 30.22) during task execution, a property that was learned during training.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - HRM achieves significant computational depth through hierarchical convergence, where the high-level module provides fresh context, effectively 'resetting' the low-level module's path and enabling a sequence of distinct, stable computations over an effective depth of N*T steps.
> - The model demonstrates an emergent hierarchical dimensionality organization in its hidden states, with the high-level module operating in a significantly higher-dimensional space than the low-level module, mirroring observations in biological neural systems.
> - HRM can learn complex algorithmic solutions from minimal data (e.g., 1000 examples) and parameters (27M), challenging the paradigm that requires massive datasets and pre-training for advanced reasoning tasks.
