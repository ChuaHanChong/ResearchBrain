# SimShear: Sim-to-Real Shear-based Tactile Servoing

> [!summary] Summary
> The SimShear pipeline enhances tactile sim-to-real policy transfer by explicitly integrating shear information into simulated tactile images, enabling direct deployment of policies on real sensors without runtime translation. This framework achieved 1-2 mm contact error in real-world tactile tracking and collaborative co-lifting tasks, demonstrating robust zero-shot generalization to diverse objects and sensor orientations.

## Problem

- Existing tactile sim-to-real pipelines often rely on computationally expensive real-to-sim translation at runtime, adding overhead to control loops.
- Standard rigid-body physics simulators do not explicitly model shear deformations, limiting tactile policies trained in simulation to tasks primarily relying on contact depth and geometry.
- The one-to-many mapping from shear-lacking simulated tactile images to real tactile images with varying shear makes direct sim-to-real translation ill-defined.

## Method

- Developed **shPix2pix**, a ==conditional U-Net GAN== that explicitly incorporates a 4D shear vector to translate simulated tactile images into realistic, shear-enabled real tactile images.
- Trained **ShearNet**, a ==Gaussian Density Neural Network (GDNN)==, exclusively on shPix2pix-generated synthetic data to estimate contact pose and shear, ensuring generalization to real-world tactile images.
- Validated the **SimShear** pipeline in real-world robotic control tasks, including tactile tracking and collaborative co-lifting, using two robotic arms to demonstrate shear-aware manipulation capabilities.

## Results

- The shPix2pix network significantly improved sim-to-real image translation, achieving a mean average pixel error (MAPE) of **0.091** and a structural similarity index measure (SSIM) of **0.65**, a substantial improvement over baseline pix2pix (MAPE **0.22**, SSIM **0.17**).
- ShearNet, trained exclusively on shPix2pix-generated data, accurately predicted both contact pose and shear on real tactile images, with shear prediction errors comparable to models trained entirely on real data.
- The SimShear pipeline enabled successful real-world tactile tracking and collaborative co-lifting tasks, maintaining continuous contact with average distance errors of **1-2 mm**, and demonstrated generalization to various objects and a horizontally mounted sensor without specific real-world training.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly conditioning an image-to-image translation GAN (shPix2pix) on shear information resolves the ambiguity of a one-to-many sim-to-real mapping for tactile images, enabling high-fidelity synthetic data generation.
> - High-accuracy contact pose and shear estimators (ShearNet) can be effectively trained solely on synthetically generated, shear-enabled tactile images and successfully deployed in real-world scenarios with zero-shot transfer.
> - A true sim-to-real pipeline, by eliminating runtime real-to-sim translation, offers a more computationally efficient and robust approach for tactile control that leverages crucial shear information.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The integration of learning algorithms into robotics holds potential for enabling complex and generalized behaviors that are challenging to achieve with traditional control methods. However, these learning algorithms typically require extensive datasets, which are often costly and time-consuming to collect in real-world environments. This challenge has led to the increasing use of physics-based simulations to generate data for training robot control policies, with subsequent transfer to real environments.

A significant hurdle in this approach is the "sim-to-real gap," which arises because physics engines simplify real-world complexities to maintain computational efficiency. In tactile robotics, this gap is particularly pronounced. Simulations of tactile interactions often model soft sensors using rigid bodies with penetrable contacts, which approximates deformation but fundamentally omits crucial physical properties like shear. Shear forces, resulting from lateral movements across contact surfaces, are recognized as critical for tasks involving dynamic object interactions and fine manipulation. However, the absence of explicit shear modeling in standard rigid-body simulations limits the scope of tasks that can be addressed through sim-to-real training to those primarily relying on contact depth and geometric information.

Current solutions for tactile sim-to-real policy transfer commonly adopt a "real-to-sim" pipeline. In this approach, a policy is trained using simulated data, and then, during real-world deployment, incoming real-world tactile data is converted in real-time into a simulated equivalent for use by the trained policy. This design is necessitated by a one-to-many sim-to-real mapping where a single simulated tactile image (lacking shear) can correspond to numerous real tactile images with identical contact depth but varying shear information. The inverse sim-to-real transformation is therefore ill-defined without explicit shear. While effective for some tasks, this real-to-sim approach is computationally intensive at runtime.

Previous work has explored various methods to bridge the tactile sim-to-real gap. Some studies have utilized detailed Finite Element models for more accurate tactile simulation, but these come with high computational costs. Others have employed task-specific approximations for zero-shot transfer, which often limit generalizability. Real-to-sim pipelines, such as those building upon PyBullet and pix2pix translation for TacTip, DIGIT, and DigiTac sensors, have shown success in tasks like object pushing and surface following, but they have not leveraged shear information and still require real-to-sim translation at inference time.

This research, SimShear, addresses these limitations by introducing a novel sim-to-real pipeline that explicitly incorporates shear information into simulated tactile images. Instead of the computationally expensive real-to-sim translation at runtime, SimShear aims to generate realistic tactile data with shear in the simulation domain, enabling policies to be trained on these shear-enabled images and operate directly on real tactile images. This approach seeks to provide a more streamlined, computationally efficient, and shear-aware framework for tactile robotic control, expanding the range of achievable tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance tactile sim-to-real policy transfer by integrating shear information directly into the simulated tactile data generation process, thereby overcoming limitations of current approaches.

The motivation for this work stems from several key challenges in tactile robotics:

- **Computational Expense of Real-to-Sim Pipelines:** Existing tactile sim-to-real transfer methods often rely on a real-to-sim pipeline, where real-world tactile sensor data is translated into its simulated equivalent in real-time during policy deployment. This continuous real-to-sim transformation is computationally demanding and adds overhead to the control loop, limiting its efficiency and responsiveness. The researchers aim to eliminate this runtime translation requirement.
- **Absence of Shear Information in Rigid-Body Simulations:** Standard rigid-body physics simulators, commonly used for their computational efficiency in tactile robotics, do not explicitly model the shear deformations experienced by soft tactile sensors. This omission means that policies trained purely in simulation using these engines cannot utilize critical shear information, restricting their applicability to tasks that involve lateral movements or dynamic object interactions. The goal is to incorporate shear without explicitly modeling complex shear dynamics in the physics simulator.
- **Limited Scope of Sim-to-Real Tactile Tasks:** Without shear information, current sim-to-real policies are confined to tasks that primarily depend on contact depth and geometric data. Tasks requiring precise lateral force control, slip detection, or robust object manipulation during dynamic interactions are difficult to achieve. The research aims to enable a broader range of complex, shear-dependent manipulation tasks.
- **One-to-Many Sim-to-Real Gap for Tactile Images:** The challenge of transferring simulated tactile images to real ones is exacerbated by the fact that a single simulated image (lacking shear) can correspond to many real images with identical indentation but varying shear. This "one-to-many" relationship makes a direct sim-to-real image translation problematic, thus necessitating the real-to-sim approach. The researchers intend to address this by explicitly conditioning the image translation on shear information, creating a more defined one-to-one mapping.

To address these motivations, the key objectives are:

1. **Develop shPix2pix:** Introduce a conditional U-Net Generative Adversarial Network (GAN) architecture, named shPix2pix, capable of translating simulated tactile images (which lack shear) into realistic tactile images that include shear deformations. This involves incorporating a shear vector as a conditioning input to the GAN.
2. **Train ShearNet:** Develop and train a Gaussian Density Neural Network (GDNN), termed ShearNet, to estimate both contact pose and shear from the shPix2pix-generated, shear-enabled tactile images. This model should generalize effectively to real-world tactile images despite being trained solely on generated data.
3. **Validate SimShear in Control Tasks:** Demonstrate the effectiveness and robustness of the entire SimShear pipeline (including shPix2pix and ShearNet) in real-world robotic control scenarios. Specifically, evaluate its performance on a tactile tracking task and a collaborative co-lifting task, both of which require accurate shear sensing.

By achieving these objectives, the research aims to establish a computationally efficient, genuinely sim-to-real framework for tactile robotics that enables robots to sense and respond to shear, thereby expanding their manipulation capabilities.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The SimShear pipeline comprises several integrated components designed to enable shear-based tactile servoing through sim-to-real transfer.

#### 3.1. Tactile Robot System

The experimental setup utilized two Dobot MG400 4-axis desktop robotic arms. One arm, designated as the "follower robot," was equipped with a marker-based (331-pin) TacTip vision-based tactile sensor. This sensor provides tactile images sensitive to both normal and shear force distributions. The second arm, the "leader robot," was not tactile-equipped and was used to manipulate objects. The Dobot MG400's low-level servo controller, operating at approximately 10 cycles/second, was used to generate smooth trajectories, with the end effector's rotatory joint stiffness increased by 2X.

#### 3.2. Tactile Simulation

The simulation environment used was Tactile Gym 2.0, which is built upon the PyBullet physics engine. PyBullet generates depth images of interactions between the simulated tactile sensor and objects, approximating tactile feedback. Crucially, as the simulated sensor uses rigid body physics, shear deformations are not intrinsically modeled in these "simulated tactile images." To compensate, shear displacements (positional and rotational) are inferred from the simulator by tracking the shift in the contact surface's position and orientation relative to the sensor. This provides a 4-dimensional "shear pose" vector, which complements the depth-based tactile images.

#### 3.3. Data Collection

A dataset was collected consisting of tuples: real tactile images, corresponding simulated tactile images, and the shear displacement vector derived from the simulator. The follower robot performed controlled interactions with various objects, encompassing different surfaces and edges to capture a range of contact scenarios. A total of 5000 tuples were collected for training and 2000 for validation. Real-world data collection took 130 minutes, while the corresponding simulation data was generated in 60 seconds. Unlike previous approaches that treated shear-based movements as noise, this work explicitly incorporates shear as essential data to enable a one-to-one sim-to-real image translation framework.

#### 3.4. Conditional U-Net GAN for Image-to-Image Translation (shPix2pix)

To translate simulated tactile images into realistic, shear-enabled equivalents, the researchers developed shPix2pix. This is an extension of the pix2pix framework, a conditional GAN comprising a U-net-based generator and a convolutional discriminator. The standard U-net architecture was modified to explicitly incorporate shear information. A fully connected layer with ReLU activation was inserted between the U-net's encoding (downsampling) and decoding (upsampling) layers. The 4-dimensional shear vector, encoding both positional and rotational shear, is appended to the encoded representation before being passed through this new fully connected layer.
The shPix2pix GANs were trained on the collected paired datasets of simulated and real tactile images, along with their respective shear vectors. The training objective was to minimize the mean average pixel error (MAPE) for pixel-wise reconstruction between generated and real tactile images, alongside optimizing the adversarial loss from the discriminator. Training involved 100 epochs, a batch size of 16, and the Adam optimizer with a learning rate of 0.0001, employing early stopping.

#### 3.5. Training Generalizable Contact Pose Estimators (ShearNet)

After generating a large dataset of realistic, shear-enabled tactile images using shPix2pix, Gaussian-density neural networks (GDNNs) were trained to decode both contact pose and shear information from these generated images. GDNNs were chosen to model the continuous nature of pose and shear variables, outputting probability distributions rather than discrete predictions, thereby addressing issues like tactile aliasing. This approach extends prior work on pose estimation from real tactile images to a sim-to-real context.
These GDNNs were trained exclusively on the shPix2pix-generated tactile images and their corresponding pose and shear vectors. The training process optimized a negative log-likelihood loss over 50 epochs, with a batch size of 64, using the Adam optimizer with a learning rate of 0.0001 and early stopping.

#### 3.6. Task Formulation for Validation

The SimShear pipeline was evaluated on two collaborative tasks requiring both pose and shear-based tactile sensing, using the two Dobot MG400 robotic arms:

- **Tactile Tracking Task:** The leader robot manipulates an object along pre-programmed trajectories (e.g., circles, tilted squares, spirals, loops). The follower robot, equipped with the tactile sensor and using the trained GDNN for pose and shear estimation, tracks the object's surface to maintain continuous contact as it moves and rotates.
- **Collaborative Co-Lifting Task:** Both robots jointly hold an object. The leader robot moves the object along specified paths (e.g., curved circular wave, pointed star), while the follower robot maintains a secure grip. This task emphasizes the necessity of shear information to prevent slips, especially as load and object position change. Four diverse objects were used: a rigid square prism, a rigid egg, a soft "squidgy" brain, and a curved ducky, to test generalization. The follower robot's tactile sensor was horizontally mounted to further test generalization under changed gravity direction.

In both tasks, the ability of the SimShear-enabled system to use shear information to correct for lateral movements and maintain precise contact was critical for success.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research produced several key findings regarding the effectiveness of the SimShear pipeline:

#### 4.1. Sim-to-Real Image Translation Performance

The shPix2pix network demonstrated a significant improvement in translating simulated tactile images into realistic, shear-enabled equivalents compared to a baseline vanilla pix2pix architecture.
- The baseline pix2pix, which did not account for shear, achieved a mean average pixel error (MAPE) of 0.22 and a structural similarity index measure (SSIM) of 0.17. It struggled to produce realistic images due to the inherent one-to-many mapping from simulated images (lacking shear) to real ones (with varying shear).
- In contrast, the shPix2pix network, by explicitly encoding shear information into its representation, achieved a substantial reduction in MAPE to 0.091 and a significant increase in SSIM to 0.65.
- Further analysis by contact type (Edge and Surface) confirmed this superiority:
 - For Edge contacts, shPix2pix achieved a MAPE of 0.07 and SSIM of 0.63, outperforming baseline pix2pix (MAPE 0.21, SSIM 0.20).
 - For Surface contacts, shPix2pix achieved a MAPE of 0.11 and SSIM of 0.68, outperforming baseline pix2pix (MAPE 0.23, SSIM 0.14).
These results indicate that the integration of the shear vector into the conditional U-Net GAN enabled more effective sim-to-real tactile image translation across different contact conditions.

#### 4.2. Sim-to-Real Pose and Shear Prediction (ShearNet)

The Gaussian-density neural networks (GDNNs) trained on shPix2pix-generated data demonstrated robust performance in predicting both contact pose and shear on real tactile images.
- GDNNs trained on images generated by the baseline pix2pix model showed limited ability. While they could decode pose with some success, they were unable to accurately infer shear information.
- Conversely, GDNNs trained on images generated by the shPix2pix architecture accurately predicted both pose and shear variables when tested exclusively on real tactile images, even though these real images were never encountered during training.
- The prediction errors for shear in the shPix2pix-trained model were reported to be comparable to those of networks trained entirely on real tactile data, highlighting the robustness and accuracy of the sim-to-real approach for shear estimation.

#### 4.3. Tactile Object Tracking Task Performance

The SimShear-enabled follower robot successfully performed the tactile object tracking task across various complex trajectories (circles, tilted squares, upward spirals, and a complex loop).
- The follower robot maintained continuous contact with the moving object throughout all tested trajectories.
- The average distance error between the tactile sensor's position and the target position on the object's surface was consistently within 1-2 mm. The error was consistent across trajectories, with the "looping" trajectory noted as the most challenging.
- The tracked trajectories of the follower robot closely matched those of the leader robot, confirming the precision of the sim-to-real tactile servo controller.
- A notable aspect was that the GDNN models used for pose and shear estimation in this real-world task were trained exclusively on simulated tactile images generated by the shPix2pix pipeline, demonstrating zero-shot transfer capability to real-world conditions.

#### 4.4. Collaborative Co-Lifting Task Performance

The follower and leader robots successfully collaborated to lift and hold various objects along two distinct trajectories (a curved circular wave and a pointed star). The task demonstrated generalization to different objects and sensor orientations.
- The follower robot maintained continuous and sufficiently precise contact to securely hold the objects while being moved by the leader.
- Trajectory errors were again consistently in the range of 1-2 mm, indicating a close match between leader and follower movements.
- The system generalized to four different objects: a rigid square prism, a rigid egg, a soft "squidgy" brain, and a curved ducky.
- The tactile sensor on the follower robot was mounted horizontally for this task, further demonstrating generalization under a change of gravity direction and experimental configuration not present in the training data.
- The soft brain object posed the most challenge due to its deformation affecting tactile images, while the ducky was challenging due to its larger weight, which was not explicitly tuned for in the system. Despite these challenges, good performance was maintained.
- This task further validated the generalization capability of the SimShear approach, as the pose and shear estimation network was trained exclusively on shPix2pix-generated data from a vertically oriented sensor and rigid objects, yet performed effectively with diverse objects, curved surfaces, soft objects, and a horizontally oriented sensor.

These results collectively indicate that the SimShear pipeline provides a robust and generalizable method for integrating shear information into sim-to-real tactile control, enabling precise manipulation in real-world scenarios without the need for real-to-sim translation at runtime.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The SimShear pipeline represents an advancement in sim-to-real transfer for tactile robotics, addressing several fundamental limitations of previous approaches. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Enabling Shear-Aware Manipulation:** The core contribution is the successful integration of shear information into simulated tactile images without explicitly modeling complex shear dynamics within the physics simulator. This capability is crucial because shear forces are essential for a wide range of dexterous manipulation tasks, including slip detection, fine object manipulation, and robust grasping during dynamic interactions. By making shear available, SimShear expands the types of tasks that can be reliably performed by robots trained in simulation.

- **True Sim-to-Real Transfer and Reduced Computational Overhead:** Unlike prevalent "real-to-sim" pipelines that require real-time translation of real tactile images into a simulated domain at every inference step, SimShear offers a "true" sim-to-real approach. It generates realistic, shear-enabled tactile data in simulation, allowing policies to be trained directly on this data and then deployed to operate purely on real tactile images. This eliminates the need for computationally expensive runtime translations, simplifying the control pipeline and potentially increasing control loop speeds and efficiency.

- **Improved Accuracy and Robustness:** The shPix2pix network's ability to generate high-fidelity, shear-inclusive tactile images, coupled with the accurate pose and shear prediction by the GDNNs, leads to more precise and robust robotic control. The demonstrated contact errors of 1-2 mm in challenging tracking and co-lifting tasks, even with varied objects and trajectories, highlight this enhanced performance. The comparable shear prediction errors to models trained directly on real data further validate the approach's effectiveness.

- **Enhanced Generalization Capabilities:** The experiments showcased the strong generalization of SimShear-trained models. The follower robot successfully performed tasks with objects and sensor orientations significantly different from its training configuration (e.g., horizontally mounted sensor, curved and soft objects vs. flat rigid objects in training). This suggests that policies learned through SimShear can adapt to new real-world scenarios more effectively than prior methods that were often limited to the specific conditions seen in simulation.

- **Accessibility and Reproducibility:** The use of low-cost desktop robotic arms (Dobot MG400) and the stated intention to open-source all code and tactile sensor designs contribute to the accessibility and reproducibility of this research. This could facilitate broader adoption and further development within the robotics community, especially for researchers with limited resources.

**Potential Impact:**

The SimShear pipeline has the potential to:
- **Accelerate Robot Learning:** By enabling the generation of extensive, shear-aware training datasets in simulation, it can reduce the reliance on tedious and resource-intensive real-world data collection for tactile manipulation tasks.
- **Broaden Robotic Dexterity:** It can unlock new possibilities for robotic applications requiring complex tactile feedback, particularly in areas like assembly, inspection, and human-robot collaboration where precise interaction forces and slip awareness are critical.
- **Inspire Further Research:** The method for incorporating shear information into image translation, the use of GDNNs for robust pose and shear estimation, and the successful sim-to-real transfer validation are likely to stimulate further research into more sophisticated tactile simulation techniques, sensor designs, and learning algorithms.

**Limitations and Future Directions:**
The paper acknowledges several limitations, which also point to future research directions:
- **Degrees of Freedom (DoF) Constraint:** The current experiments were limited to 4-DoF motions due to the chosen desktop robot. Future work aims to extend SimShear to higher-DoF manipulators (e.g., 7-DoF arms) to enable a wider range of tasks, which would require generalizing the models to 6 pose and shear variables.
- **Object Weight Compensation:** The co-lifting task involved only fairly light objects. For heavier objects, compensating for vertical shear caused by weight would require additional tuning or a motion-based model, which was beyond the scope of this paper. This presents an avenue for more dynamic and force-intensive tasks.
- **Generalization to Diverse Contact Geometries and Materials:** While showing good generalization, the current training dataset primarily focused on flat rigid objects. Expanding the training dataset for the shPix2pix network to include objects with sharp, pointed, very soft, or slippery contact surfaces would further enhance the model's generalization capabilities for a broader range of manipulation actions.
- **Integration with Reinforcement Learning (RL):** Future plans include exploring spatial RL data augmentation techniques in conjunction with SimShear-generated tactile images to further enhance learning generalizability for more complex, coordinated manipulation tasks involving multiple tactile robots.

In conclusion, SimShear offers a novel and practical solution for integrating critical shear information into the sim-to-real transfer process for tactile robotics. By streamlining the pipeline and enhancing the capabilities of simulated data, it represents a step towards more capable, versatile, and autonomously learning robotic systems.
