# Topometric Autonomous Vehicle Localization by Combining Visual Embeddings and Feed-Forward 3D Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from the University of Malaga and University of Zaragoza introduced a topometric visual localization framework for autonomous vehicles, integrating visual place recognition (VPR) embeddings with feed-forward 3D (FF3D) models via a probabilistic particle filter. The system significantly reduces deployable map size by over 98% and demonstrates enhanced accuracy and robustness against perceptual aliasing across diverse real-world datasets.

## Problem

- Existing visual localization methods struggle with trade-offs between metric precision, robustness to long-term appearance changes, and scalability to large environments.
- Visual Place Recognition (VPR) lacks precise metric localization and is susceptible to perceptual aliasing, while feed-forward 3D (FF3D) models are computationally expensive for dense maps and sensitive to reference image selection.
- Automated and robust construction of topometric maps for efficient representation of large, dense image datasets remains a challenge.

## Method

- A **topometric visual localization framework** integrates VPR embeddings and feed-forward 3D (FF3D) models within a ==probabilistic particle filter==.
- An offline process constructs a compact topometric map by automatically clustering poses with ==HDBSCAN== and selecting representative images via ==farthest-point sampling== in descriptor space.
- An online particle filter leverages sequential belief to efficiently select relevant map references for FF3D-based metric pose refinement, fusing both appearance and metric likelihoods for robust updates.

## Results

- The proposed system consistently achieved the lowest mean and P90 position errors across challenging datasets (COLD, 4Seasons, RobotCar) compared to state-of-the-art baselines.
- Deployable map size was significantly reduced by over **98%** (e.g., from **8818.8 MiB** to **139.0 MiB** on RobotCar) while preserving high localization accuracy.
- Qualitative analysis showed enhanced robustness to perceptual aliasing, with the system maintaining accurate localization in scenarios where direct VPR+FF3D approaches converged to incorrect but visually similar places.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The probabilistic fusion of appearance (VPR) and metric geometry (FF3D) information in a sequential particle filter provides a robust balance between localization accuracy and resilience to visual changes.
> - Conditioning the selection of reference images for FF3D models on the particle filter's topometric belief significantly enhances robustness against perceptual aliasing by focusing on spatially plausible locations.
> - Automated topological place discovery and aggressive map compression via farthest-point sampling enable scalable localization while maintaining high accuracy.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Autonomous navigation systems rely on accurate and robust localization within previously mapped environments. Traditional Global Navigation Satellite System (GNSS)-based solutions are often unreliable in areas such as indoor spaces or urban canyons, necessitating alternative approaches like visual localization (VL).

The existing landscape of visual localization methods presents a trade-off between metric precision, robustness to visual appearance changes, and scalability to large environments:
- **Local feature-based methods** provide accurate pose estimates and have integrated learning components to enhance efficiency. However, they typically struggle with long-term visual changes (e.g., variations in illumination, weather, viewpoint) and face challenges in scaling to large environments due to substantial map sizes and associated computational/storage requirements.
- **Visual Place Recognition (VPR)** addresses the scalability and robustness challenges by retrieving visually similar images from a georeferenced database using compact, whole-image descriptors. These descriptors are designed to be robust against significant appearance changes. However, this robustness generally comes at the expense of precise metric localization accuracy, limiting VPR's direct utility for fine-grained navigation. Consequently, VPR is commonly employed as a coarse localization stage in hierarchical pipelines. Furthermore, processing images independently can render VPR susceptible to perceptual aliasing, where spatially distinct places exhibit similar visual characteristics.
- **Sequential probabilistic localization** techniques enhance VPR-based methods by exploiting temporal coherence in robot trajectories through Bayesian filtering. This approach improves reliability and robustness against issues like perceptual aliasing by maintaining multiple place hypotheses over time. Nevertheless, these methods remain fundamentally limited by the coarse spatial resolution and low metric precision inherent to global VPR descriptors.
- **Feed-forward neural 3D geometry (FF3D) models** (e.g., DUSt3R, VGGT) have emerged as a means to directly infer accurate geometric information, including 3D structure and camera poses, from sparse image sets. While offering high metric precision, their direct application to dense georeferenced image maps faces practical limitations. The computational cost typically increases with the number of input views, pose estimates are sensitive to the selection of reference images, and the predicted geometry requires alignment with a global map frame. These factors make FF3D models computationally inefficient for large-scale, dense maps.

This work addresses the limitations by proposing a topometric framework that seeks to bridge the gap between the robustness and scalability of VPR-based sequential localization and the metric accuracy offered by FF3D models, while also addressing the challenges of map management and computational efficiency.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a topometric visual localization framework that combines the appearance robustness and scalability of sequential VPR-based localization with the metric accuracy of feed-forward neural 3D geometry (FF3D) models. The motivation stems from addressing several limitations of existing approaches:

1. **Enhancing Metric Accuracy of Appearance-Based Localization:** Current VPR-based localization methods, despite their robustness to visual changes and scalability, inherently lack the metric precision required for many autonomous navigation tasks. This work aims to overcome this by integrating high-accuracy FF3D pose estimation.
2. **Improving Scalability and Efficiency of FF3D Models in Localization:** While FF3D models can infer accurate 3D geometry, their computational cost typically increases with the number of input images. Directly applying them to dense georeferenced image maps is computationally inefficient and highly dependent on the selection of informative reference images. The research aims to integrate FF3D models scalably by efficiently selecting a small set of plausible map images based on probabilistic belief.
3. **Automating Topometric Map Construction:** Previous topometric map abstraction methods often require an offline procedure to predefine the number of topological places and can be sensitive to clustering initialization. The authors aim to introduce an automatic, unsupervised method for discovering topological places from map features, improving efficiency for large-scale environments.
4. **Increasing Robustness against Perceptual Aliasing:** VPR, especially when processing images independently, can fail under perceptual aliasing where distinct locations appear visually similar. This can lead to incorrect place matches. The research seeks to leverage sequential probabilistic reasoning to maintain multiple competing place hypotheses and propagate uncertainty over time, thereby mitigating such failures.
5. **Creating a Modular Localization Architecture:** The framework is designed to be modular, treating both the visual embedding extractor and the neural metric inference model as interchangeable "plug-and-play" components. This allows for flexibility and adaptation to advancements in descriptor and FF3D model architectures without modifying the underlying probabilistic formulation.

In summary, the work aims to create a robust, accurate, and scalable visual localization system that synthesizes the benefits of appearance-based and geometry-based methods through a topometric, probabilistic framework.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed topometric localization framework operates in two main stages: an offline map building process and an online localization process, both integrating VPR embeddings and FF3D models within a probabilistic particle filter architecture.

#### 3.1. Offline Map Building

The offline stage constructs a compact topometric map `C` from a dense set of georeferenced images `M`. Each map sample `m_i` consists of an image `I_i`, its 2D pose `p_i` (x, y, θ), and its global descriptor `d_i = ϕ(I_i)`. The output map `C` comprises `M` topological places `C_j`, each characterized by a mean pose `μ_p_j`, a mean descriptor `μ_d_j`, a joint pose–descriptor covariance `Σ_C_j`, and a set of representative samples `R_j`.

1. **Pose Preprocessing and HDBSCAN:**
 - Map poses `p_i = (x_i, y_i, θ_i)` are converted into 4D pose-feature vectors `f_i = (x_i, y_i, sin θ_i, cos θ_i)^T`. The sine and cosine components handle the angular discontinuity at ±π.
 - These pose-feature vectors are standardized to `~f_i` (zero mean, unit variance) to ensure that translational and angular components contribute comparably to distance calculations.
 - HDBSCAN (Hierarchical Density-Based Spatial Clustering of Applications with Noise) is applied to the standardized pose-feature vectors. This algorithm automatically determines the number of clusters (topological places) based on data density, eliminating the need for predefinition. Samples initially classified as noise by HDBSCAN are reassigned to the nearest non-noise cluster centroid.

2. **Cluster Model Construction:**
 - For each identified topological place `C_j`, the pose mean `μ_p_j` and descriptor mean `μ_d_j` are computed from the assigned map samples.
 - The joint pose–descriptor covariance `Σ_C_j` is approximated by a block-diagonal matrix, assuming conditional independence between pose and appearance. It includes `Σ_p_j` (a 3x3 matrix for SE(2) pose uncertainty) and `σ^2_j,d * I_D` (a scalar variance for descriptor variability, assuming isotropic distribution in descriptor space).

3. **Representative Sample Selection:**
 - To reduce the deployable map size and visual redundancy, each set of samples `M_j` belonging to a cluster `C_j` is reduced to a smaller set of representative images `R_j`.
 - This selection is performed using farthest-point sampling (FPS) in descriptor space. The procedure starts by selecting the sample whose descriptor is most similar to the cluster's mean descriptor. Subsequent samples are chosen to maximize visual diversity from already selected samples.
 - The process continues until a minimum of two images are selected, and then terminates if the maximum descriptor diversity among unselected samples falls below a threshold `τ_diversity`. These retained images are resized and JPEG-encoded for efficient storage.

#### 3.2. Online Localization

The online stage employs a topometric particle filter to estimate the camera pose `ˆx_t` at each time step `t`, given a query image `I_q_t` and odometry `u_t`. The posterior belief is represented by a set of particles `X_t`, each containing a pose `x_[n]_t`, an associated topological place `c_[n]_t`, and a normalized importance weight `w_[n]_t`.

1. **Hybrid Particle Initialization:**
 - For the initial query `I_q_0`, VPR probabilities `s_q_0,j = P(d_q_0 | C_j)` are computed for all clusters. Candidate places `J_0` are selected if their probability exceeds a relative threshold `τ_vpr` of the most probable cluster.
 - Neural metric refinement (detailed below) is performed on `J_0` to obtain a set of selected representative samples `N_0` and an initial FF3D pose estimate `ˆx_g_0`.
 - The initial `N_p` particles are divided into two groups:
 - A VPR-based group (`βN_p` particles): Sampled around the known map poses `p_a` of `N_0` representative samples, with distribution weighted by the cosine similarity between `d_q_0` and `d_a`.
 - An FF3D-based group (`(1−β)N_p` particles): Sampled around the FF3D estimate `ˆx_g_0` with Gaussian noise in SE(2) tangent space. All particles are initially assigned uniform weights.

2. **Particle Propagation and Cluster Selection:**
 - Each particle `x_[n]_t` is propagated according to odometry `u_t` and motion noise `ϵ_[n]_t` (`x_[n]_t = x_[n]_(t−1) ⊕ u_t ⊕ ϵ_[n]_t`).
 - Each propagated particle is associated with the topological cluster `c_[n]_t` that maximizes its pose likelihood `P(x_[n]_t | C_j)`.
 - A belief `b_t(j)` over topological places is derived from the weights of particles associated with each cluster.
 - A set of candidate clusters `J_t` for neural metric refinement is selected by considering clusters whose belief `b_t(j)` exceeds a relative threshold `τ_cl` of the dominant cluster's belief.

3. **Neural Metric Refinement:**
 - **Reference Sample Selection:** Given `J_t` and the query descriptor `d_q_t`, a compact set `N_t` of up to `K` representative samples is selected from the union of `R_j` for `j ∈ J_t`. The selection prioritizes the reference with the highest descriptor similarity to `d_q_t`, then iteratively selects samples that maintain a descriptor distance within bounds `d_min` and `d_max` from already selected references. This belief-guided selection provides informative, relevant images for FF3D inference.
 - **Metric Pose Estimation:** The FF3D model `g` jointly processes the query image `I_q_t` and the selected reference images `{I_i}` from `N_t`. It predicts camera transforms (poses `ˆp_g_k`) in its internal coordinate frame. These predicted poses are then aligned to the global map frame using a robust Sim(2) transformation (estimated via Least Median of Squares over Umeyama fits) derived from matching the predicted poses of reference images `{ˆp_g_i}` with their known map poses `{p_i}`. This yields the aligned metric pose estimate `ˆx_g_t` for the query.
 - **Belief Conditioning:** The online candidate set `J_t` is conditioned on the propagated particle distribution. This allows a concentrated belief to yield a spatially coherent reference set, while a multimodal belief can include references from multiple separated hypotheses, enhancing robustness.

4. **Particle Update:**
 - **Metric Likelihood `ℓ_[n]_met`:** Scores the consistency between the FF3D metric observation `ˆx_g_t` and each particle's pose `x_[n]_t` using an SE(2) distance metric, converted to a Gaussian likelihood.
 - **Appearance Likelihood `ℓ_[n]_app`:** Computes the probability `P(d_q_t | C_c_[n]_t)` based on the descriptor distribution parameters of the cluster `c_[n]_t` associated with the particle.
 - **Likelihood Fusion:** Both likelihoods are fused in log-space after independent normalization across the particle set. A mixing parameter `α` controls the relative contribution of the appearance and metric terms (`λ_[n]_fuse = (1 − α)~λ_[n]_app + α~λ_[n]_met`). Particle weights are updated proportionally to `exp(λ_[n]_fuse)`.

5. **Pose Estimation and Particle Resampling:**
 - The final camera pose estimate `ˆx_t` is obtained by computing a weighted SE(2) mean over particles belonging to the dominant mode of the particle distribution.
 - To prevent particle degeneracy, resampling (multinomial resampling) is performed whenever the effective sample size `N_eff` falls below half of the total particle count `N_p`.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research conducted extensive evaluations on three publicly available datasets: COLD (indoor, varied illumination), 4Seasons (outdoor, multi-weather, driving sequences), and RobotCar (urban, varied weather). The system was compared against two baselines: ALLOM (an appearance-map localization framework using K-Means+EM and ImRet descriptors) and VPR+FF3D (direct retrieval of 9 references from the complete map using DA3-Large, without sequential filtering or map compression). Evaluation metrics included mean, median, and 90th percentile (P90) for position and yaw errors, Area Under Curve (AUC) for joint localization recall, and deployable map size.

#### 4.1. Overall Localization Results

- **Accuracy:** The proposed method ("Ours") consistently outperformed ALLOM and achieved the lowest mean and P90 position errors across all three datasets. It also obtained the highest AUC on COLD and RobotCar. On 4Seasons, direct VPR+FF3D achieved slightly lower median and yaw errors and a higher AUC, but "Ours" maintained lower mean and P90 position errors. This indicates that while direct retrieval can be precise when references are correct, the temporal belief in "Ours" offers greater protection against large localization failures.
- **Robustness:** The sequential belief mechanism in "Ours" was observed to limit significant position deviations, which are particularly relevant for continuous robot operation as they can disrupt tracking.
- **Deployable Map Size:** "Ours" achieved a substantial reduction in deployable map size compared to VPR+FF3D. For instance, on RobotCar, the map size was reduced from 8818.8 MiB (VPR+FF3D) to 139.0 MiB ("Ours"). While ALLOM maintained the smallest map size (because it does not retain reference images), its localization accuracy was notably lower. The proposed system offers a balance between compact map storage, sequential consistency, and accuracy.

#### 4.2. Ablation Studies

- **Visual Descriptor (ϕ):** Evaluation of 19 different VPR descriptor configurations showed that increasing dimensionality generally benefited performance, but not always monotonically. MixVPR-512 was selected as a balanced choice, providing a performance similar to higher-dimensional descriptors like SALAD but with reduced storage requirements. The modular design allows for interchangeability based on application-specific priorities.
- **Feed-forward Geometry Model (g):** Testing various FF3D models indicated no monotonic relationship between computational cost and localization accuracy. DA3-Large was chosen as a balanced operating point, offering an AUC similar to the higher-performing DA3-Giant-Rays but with significantly lower latency. This modularity enables selection of the FF3D model based on desired accuracy-latency trade-offs.
- **Number of Particles (N_p):** An analysis of particle count revealed that the largest improvements in AUC occurred before reaching 200 particles. Beyond this, AUC varied marginally, while computational cost increased. `N_p = 200` was therefore selected as a compromise between accuracy and computational complexity.
- **Fusion Weight (α):** The mixing parameter `α`, which balances appearance and metric likelihoods, was found to yield the highest AUC at `α = 0.7`. This suggests that fusing both observation terms provides complementary information and improves performance compared to using either appearance-only (`α = 0`) or metric-only (`α = 1`) updates.
- **Map Compression (τ_diversity):** The `τ_diversity` threshold for farthest-point sampling (FPS) was evaluated for its effect on map size and localization accuracy. The default `τ_diversity = 0.075` maintained an AUC close to that of an unreduced map while significantly compressing storage. Aggressive pruning (higher `τ_diversity`) risked degrading localization performance.

#### 4.3. Robustness to Perceptual Aliasing

A qualitative case study on the COLD dataset demonstrated the system's robustness to perceptual aliasing. Under significant appearance changes (different window blind positions creating visual ambiguity), direct VPR+FF3D repeatedly converged to a visually similar but spatially inconsistent map region. In contrast, "Ours," by conditioning reference selection on the accumulated topometric belief, remained accurately localized near the ground truth. This illustrates that the probabilistic framework actively changes the evidence supplied to the metric estimator, rather than merely smoothing an incorrect FF3D trajectory, by restricting candidates to spatially plausible places.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a framework for visual localization that addresses several critical challenges in autonomous navigation, offering a synthesis of capabilities from distinct research areas.

**Significance of the Work:**
- **Balanced Localization Performance:** The proposed topometric framework demonstrates improved localization accuracy, particularly in terms of mean and P90 position errors, over appearance-based state-of-the-art methods. It also provides greater robustness against large localization failures compared to direct VPR+FF3D approaches, which is crucial for safety and reliability in mobile robotics.
- **Efficient Map Management:** By abstracting dense georeferenced image maps into compact probabilistic places and using farthest-point sampling to select representative images for FF3D inference, the framework significantly reduces deployable map storage requirements. This enables scalability to larger environments without the prohibitive storage overhead of retaining full image datasets.
- **Enhanced Robustness to Perceptual Aliasing:** The integration of sequential probabilistic belief with FF3D inference provides a mechanism to mitigate failures caused by perceptual aliasing. The belief-conditioned reference selection ensures that metric pose estimation is guided by spatially consistent hypotheses, preventing the system from being lured into visually similar but geometrically incorrect map regions.
- **Modular and Flexible Architecture:** The design allows for the independent replacement of both the visual descriptor extractor (ϕ) and the feed-forward 3D geometry model (g). This modularity future-proofs the system, enabling it to benefit from ongoing advancements in deep learning models for VPR and 3D geometry estimation without requiring fundamental changes to the localization pipeline.

**Potential Impact:**
The contributions of this work have potential impacts on the development and deployment of autonomous systems:
- **Robotics and Autonomous Vehicles:** The ability to perform accurate, robust, and scalable localization in complex, dynamic environments (e.g., urban areas with varying weather, indoor spaces with lighting changes) is fundamental for mobile robots and autonomous vehicles. This framework could enhance the navigation capabilities of such systems, especially in scenarios where GNSS is unavailable or unreliable.
- **Large-Scale Mapping and Localization:** The efficient map representation and robust localization against appearance changes make the approach suitable for large-scale environments and long-term operations, reducing the computational and storage burden associated with dense visual maps.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The modular design encourages further research into optimized VPR descriptors and FF3D models, as well as more sophisticated fusion strategies (e.g., uncertainty-aware metric observations and adaptive fusion) and map representations (e.g., place refinement based on spatial extent and belief uncertainty, or extensions to full SE(3) motion).

In conclusion, this research provides a practical and effective solution for visual localization, effectively combining the strengths of different visual perception paradigms while addressing their respective limitations, thus contributing to the advancement of autonomous navigation technologies.
