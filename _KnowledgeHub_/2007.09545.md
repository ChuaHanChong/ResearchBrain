# ContactPose: A Dataset of Grasps with Object Contact and Hand Pose

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Georgia Tech and Facebook Reality Labs created ContactPose, a dataset of 2306 human grasps featuring detailed hand-object contact maps, 3D hand poses, object poses, and multi-view RGB-D images. This dataset facilitates the development and evaluation of contact modeling techniques, demonstrating that learning-based methods outperform heuristic approaches in predicting contact, particularly with richer hand shape features.

## Problem

- A lack of comprehensive datasets that combine ground-truth hand-object contact information with other modalities such as hand pose, object pose, and RGB-D images.
- Existing contact capture methods often interfere with natural grasping behavior (e.g., instrumented gloves) or fail to accurately model soft tissue deformation.
- Current state-of-the-art hand models are predominantly rigid and do not explicitly account for hand-object contact or the natural soft tissue deformation during human grasping.

## Method

- Developed **ContactPose**, a dataset comprising 2306 unique grasps of 25 3D-printed household objects by 50 participants, captured using a multi-modal system including thermal, RGB-D, and motion capture cameras.
- Utilized thermal cameras to non-invasively generate high-resolution ==hand-object contact maps== directly on the object surface, which inherently captures soft tissue deformation without instrumenting the hand.
- Reconstructed 3D hand joint locations without hand markers by optimizing across multi-view 2D joint detections from RGB images and known object poses, combined with manual curation for accuracy.

## Results

- The ContactPose dataset comprises **2306** unique grasps, **25** objects, **50** participants, and over **2.9 million** RGB-D images, providing detailed contact maps, 3D hand poses, and object poses.
- Learning-based contact prediction models, particularly PointNet++ utilizing `mesh` features, achieved superior performance (e.g., **.772** re-balanced AuC on object split) compared to heuristic conic distance field methods (**.648** re-balanced AuC).
- The dataset features high-quality 3D hand pose annotations, demonstrating an average 3D joint error of **6.68 mm** when comparing ground truth to fitted MANO models, which is lower than comparable existing datasets.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Human grasping involves substantial whole-hand contact, with significant contact extending beyond just fingertips to include proximal phalanges and the palm.
> - Functional intent ('use' vs. 'hand-off') impacts grasp characteristics; 'hand-off' grasps exhibit greater hand pose diversity, while 'use' grasps have larger average contact areas.
> - Hand pose alone is an insufficient descriptor for grasping, as similar hand poses can result in distinct contact patterns and locations on an object due to soft tissue deformation and varied interaction forces.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The study operates within the interdisciplinary domain of computer vision, computer graphics, and robotics, specifically focusing on human hand-object interaction. While existing research in this area has primarily concentrated on tasks such as 3D hand pose estimation, realistic hand and body reconstruction, and robotic grasp prediction, the problem of **hand-object contact modeling** has remained relatively underexplored. The paper identifies a significant gap: the lack of comprehensive datasets that pair ground-truth hand-object contact information with other modalities such as hand pose, object pose, and RGB-D images.

Previous attempts to capture contact have involved instrumenting hands with gloves or relying on proximity thresholds and collision detection with rigid hand models. These methods often either interfere with natural grasping behavior, fail to measure contact on the object surface, or do not account for soft tissue deformation inherent in human grasping. The work builds upon a prior method by Brahmbhatt et al. which utilized thermal cameras to capture post-grasp contact. However, ContactPose significantly expands on this by integrating a broader range of data modalities and a more extensive collection protocol, thereby offering a more holistic representation of grasping interactions compared to previous datasets that often lack either contact, hand pose, or multi-view imagery. This dataset directly addresses the need for robust ground-truth data to advance the development and evaluation of contact modeling techniques that can account for the complexities of soft tissue deformation and varied functional grasps.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to introduce **ContactPose**, a novel dataset specifically designed to address the deficiency of comprehensive ground-truth data for hand-object contact modeling. The dataset aims to provide a rich set of information, including detailed hand-object contact maps, 3D hand joint locations, object pose, and multi-view RGB-D images, all captured during functional grasping scenarios.

The motivations underpinning the creation of ContactPose and the focus on hand-object contact modeling are multi-faceted:

- **Advancing Hand Models:** Accurate contact models are expected to facilitate the development of more realistic and deformable hand models. Current state-of-the-art hand models are predominantly rigid and do not explicitly account for hand-object contact or soft tissue deformation, which is a significant aspect of human grasping.
- **Enhancing AR/VR Experiences:** Realistic simulation of grasping scenarios is crucial for immersive augmented reality (AR) and virtual reality (VR) environments. Improved contact models can lead to more believable and interactive virtual object manipulation.
- **Improving Robotic Grasping:** A deeper understanding and predictive capability of hand-object contact can significantly enhance robotic grasping, particularly for anthropomorphic and soft robotic hands, by informing better grasp planning and execution.
- **Enabling New Interfaces and Applications:** Accurate contact models could allow for the interpretation of computer commands based on physical interactions with real objects (e.g., 3D printed replicas), thereby enabling novel human-computer interfaces.
- **Understanding Social Interaction and Safety:** Contact modeling has potential implications for analyzing social interactions and assessing safety, such as estimating pathogen transmission from contaminated surfaces through physical contact.
- **Robust Evaluation of Contact Modeling Techniques:** A central motivation is to provide a ground-truth dataset that enables rigorous development and evaluation of various contact modeling approaches, including learning-based methods and heuristic techniques, allowing for quantitative comparison and improvement.
- **Overcoming Limitations of Existing Data Collection Methods:** The authors were motivated to overcome the shortcomings of previous contact capture methods. Instrumenting hands with gloves can alter natural grasping behavior, and techniques relying on intersecting rigid hand models with object models cannot account for soft tissue deformation. The thermal camera approach, adopted and expanded in this work, addresses these issues by non-invasively capturing contact on the object surface, reflecting the actual deformation during a grasp.
- **Capturing Diversity and Functionality:** The dataset aims to capture a wide range of grasping behaviors by involving 50 participants, 25 common household objects, and two distinct functional intents ("use" and "hand-off"). This diversity is critical for developing models that generalize across different users, objects, and task requirements.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The ContactPose dataset was constructed through a multi-modal data capture protocol, followed by sophisticated processing for 3D reconstruction and extensive data analysis.

**Data Capture Protocol and Equipment:**
- **Participants and Task:** 50 able-bodied participants were instructed to grasp 25 3D-printed household objects with one of two functional intents: "use" or "hand-off." Objects were placed in natural orientations.
- **Grasp Execution:** Participants grasped an object and moved it for 10-15 seconds within a cubical data collection space, maintaining steady hand joints while freely rotating their wrist and elbow. Grasps could be uni-manual or bi-manual.
- **Imaging System:** The setup (Figure 3a) included:
 - Seven Optitrack Prime 13W motion capture (mocap) cameras for precise object pose tracking.
 - Three Kinect v2 RGB-D cameras for multi-view RGB-D video capture of the grasp, primarily for hand pose estimation.
 - A FLIR Boson 640 thermal camera for contact map generation.
- **Contact Capture:** After the dynamic grasp phase, participants handed the object to a researcher (wearing gloves), who placed it on a turntable. The object was then recorded by the mocap system, Kinect v2, and thermal camera as the turntable rotated. Thermal images were subsequently texture-mapped onto the object mesh using Open3D to generate high-resolution contact maps, which represent the heat residue left by the warm hand on the object surface. This method avoids instrumenting the hand and captures contact on the object surface, reflecting soft tissue deformation.
- **Object Selection and Fabrication:** 25 objects suitable for both 'use' and 'hand-off' grasps were 3D printed in blue for contrast. This ensured consistent thermal properties and geometric consistency between physical objects and 3D models.
- **Object Tracking:** Recessed 3mm hemispherical retro-reflective markers were embedded in the 3D-printed objects. This marker configuration provided a balance between unobtrusiveness (avoiding influence on natural grasping) and accurate object pose tracking by the Optitrack system.

**Grasp Capture without Hand Markers (3D Hand Joint Reconstruction):**
- **Rigidity Assumption:** For the 10-15 second dynamic grasp segment, the hand was assumed to be fixed relative to the object. This allowed for aggregation of per-frame 2D joint detections.
- **2D Joint Detection:** For each RGB frame, Detectron located the wrist, and OpenPose detected 21 2D hand keypoints on a cropped region around the wrist.
- **3D Joint Estimation:** Instead of depth-based lifting (which can introduce bias), a robust optimization approach (Eq. 1) was used to estimate a single set of 3D joint locations (`o_X`) in the object's frame. This minimized the average re-projection error across all `N*C` images (N frames, C cameras), leveraging the known object pose from Optitrack.
- **Robustness:** The Huber function was used for distance (`D`) to mitigate noise in 2D detections, and a RANSAC loop handled outliers and frames with inaccurate object pose.
- **Manual Curation:** The dataset included manual curation, involving 2D joint clicking in some cases and discarding noisy data, to ensure quality.
- **Hand Mesh Models:** Supplementary data, including palm contact maps on a flat plate and multi-view RGB-D videos of participants performing seven hand gestures, was captured to enable fitting of the MANO hand mesh model for shape information (Figure 4, Eq. 2 in supplementary).

**Data Analysis:**
The dataset enabled several analyses:
- **Association of Contact to Hand Parts:** By thresholding contact maps and associating contacted object points with the nearest points on a fitted MANO hand mesh (or phalange line segments), probabilities of contact for different fingers and phalanges were quantified.
- **Automatic Active Area Discovery:** Object regions with high probability of contact by specific hand parts were identified.
- **Grasp Diversity:** The standard deviation of 3D joint locations quantified grasp diversity across 'use' and 'hand-off' intents. Grasps were clustered using HDBSCAN on aligned and normalized 3D joints.
- **Hand Pose vs. Contact:** The relationship between similar hand poses and varying contact patterns was investigated by minimizing hand pose distance and maximizing a 20-dimensional phalange-level contact area vector distance.

**Contact Modeling Experiments:**
- **Goal:** Predict object contact given hand pose or RGB grasp images, assuming known object geometry and pose.
- **Object Shape Representation:** Objects were represented as dense pointclouds (1K-30K points) or 64³ voxel occupancy grids.
- **Hand Pose Representation:** Four types of hand pose features were defined for each object point/voxel: `simple-joints`, `relative-joints`, `skeleton` (phalange-based), and `mesh` (MANO mesh-based). A random camera pose was sampled, and features for the farthest 15% of joints were dropped to simulate occlusion/noise.
- **Grasp Image(s) Representation:** Dense 40-dimensional features were extracted from 256x256 RGB image crops using a U-Net-inspired CNN encoder-decoder. Features were transferred to 3D object points. Both 1-view and 3-view settings were investigated. Image backgrounds were segmented and composited with random COCO images.
- **Contact Representation:** Contact values (normalized) were discretized into 10 bins, treating contact prediction as a classification problem. Weighted cross entropy loss was used, and a point estimate was derived via annealed mean.
- **Learning Algorithms:** PointNet++ (for pointclouds), VoxNet-inspired 3D CNN (for voxel grids), and a simple Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) baseline were employed.
- **Contact Modeling Heuristics:** The conic distance field (Ψ) from was used as a baseline, calibrated to ground-truth contact through linear regression.
- **Evaluation:** Models were evaluated using a re-balanced Area Under Curve (AuC) metric across two data splits: an 'object split' (holding out three objects) and a 'participant split' (holding out five participants).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The ContactPose dataset comprises 2306 unique grasps of 25 household objects, performed with 2 functional intents by 50 participants, yielding over 2.9 million RGB-D grasp images.

**Data Analysis Findings:**
- **Hand Contact Distribution:** Analysis revealed that the thumb, index, and middle fingers are the most frequently contacted, with fingertips being the most contacted phalanges. However, the contact probability for all three phalanges of the index finger exceeded that of the pinky fingertip, and proximal phalanges and the palm also exhibited significant contact. This confirmed prior observations from and indicated that whole-hand contact is substantial, not just fingertips.
- **Influence of Functional Intent:** 'Hand-off' grasps showed a higher concentration of contact at the thumb and index finger compared to 'use' grasps. 'Use' grasps had a larger average contact area (35.87 cm²) than 'hand-off' grasps (30.58 cm²).
- **Grasp Diversity:** 'Hand-off' grasps demonstrated greater diversity in hand pose (higher standard deviation of 3D joint locations) compared to 'use' grasps, which appeared more constrained by the object's function. Clustering of aligned 3D joints showed that 'use' grasps had 32.5% less average intra-cluster distance.
- **Hand Pose vs. Contact Discrepancy:** The analysis highlighted instances where similar hand poses resulted in distinct contact patterns and locations on the object, suggesting that hand pose alone provides an incomplete representation of grasping.

**Contact Modeling Experiment Results:**
The quantitative evaluation, using re-balanced AuC (Table 2), indicated:
- **Superiority of Richer Features:** Features encoding more detailed hand shape information (`skeleton`, `mesh`) consistently outperformed simpler representations like `simple-joints` and `relative-joints`.
- **Learning Outperforms Heuristics:** Learning-based techniques, particularly PointNet++ with `mesh` features, achieved higher AuC scores than the heuristic conic distance field method, even when the heuristic had access to high-resolution mesh data.
- **Competitive Skeleton-based Learning:** Learning models employing `skeleton` features (derived only from 3D joint locations) performed comparably to, or even surpassed, mesh-based heuristics and some simpler mesh-based learning features.
- **Impact of Multi-View Images:** While image-based techniques did not reach the accuracy of hand pose-based methods, the use of multi-view RGB inputs significantly improved their performance compared to single-view inputs.
- **Qualitative Observations (Figures 10, 11b):** Visualizations showed that `mesh`- and `skeleton`-PointNet++ predictors generated plausible contact patterns, capturing fine details of palm contact and finger shapes, even for unseen objects and under simulated partial occlusion. Image-based predictions, however, exhibited fewer high-frequency details and sometimes suffered from depth ambiguity.

**Dataset Accuracy (Supplementary Material):**
- **Contact Map Accuracy:** The average distance of contacted object points from their nearest MANO hand point was 4.06-4.17 mm. Binary contact maps showed 95.4% agreement with pressure sensor data at a threshold of 0.4.
- **3D Hand Pose Accuracy:** ContactPose demonstrated a lower discrepancy (6.68 mm average 3D joint error for 15 MANO pose parameters) between ground truth and fitted MANO models compared to other datasets (e.g., HO-3D at 7.7 mm), indicating high-quality hand pose annotations and physical plausibility. Hand-object penetration statistics were low, with a mean penetration of 2.02 mm and a median of 1.53 mm.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

ContactPose represents a significant contribution to the fields of computer vision, computer graphics, and robotics, primarily by addressing a critical data deficiency in hand-object interaction research.

- **Foundation for Deformable Hand Models:** The dataset's unique pairing of ground-truth contact with hand and object pose provides an essential resource for developing more realistic and deformable hand mesh models. Current models typically lack explicit reasoning about hand-object contact and treat the hand as a rigid structure. ContactPose's detailed contact maps, derived from thermal imaging, capture the soft tissue deformation that occurs during grasping, offering the data required to train and evaluate next-generation deformable hand models.
- **Advancement in AR/VR and Robotics:** Accurate contact models are fundamental for creating believable and interactive virtual environments in AR/VR, where realistic grasp simulation is crucial. In robotics, improved contact understanding can lead to more dexterous and robust robotic grasping, particularly for anthropomorphic and soft robotic hands that mimic human capabilities. The dataset provides the necessary data to train robust models for these applications.
- **Improved Grasp Dynamics and Planning:** By providing explicit contact information, ContactPose can enhance the estimation of grasp dynamics and inform more effective robotic grasp planning, moving beyond simple collision detection to model physical interaction more precisely.
- **Enabling Novel Human-Computer Interfaces:** The ability to accurately model hand-object contact could facilitate the development of new computer interfaces that interpret user intent directly from physical interaction with objects, opening avenues for intuitive, tangible computing.
- **Rigorous Model Evaluation and Comparison:** The dataset's scale, diversity, and ground-truth contact annotations enable rigorous quantitative evaluation of existing and future contact modeling techniques. This allows for direct comparison of different data representations, heuristics, and learning algorithms, fostering systematic progress in the field.
- **Insights into Human Grasping:** The detailed data analysis has already yielded insights into the distribution of contact on the human hand, the influence of functional intent on grasping kinematics and contact patterns, and the limitations of hand pose alone in fully characterizing a grasp. These insights can inform the design of better anthropomorphic robotic hands and tactile sensors.
- **Community Resource:** The public release of the dataset, code, and trained models significantly encourages broader research and development, allowing the community to build upon this work and accelerate progress in hand-object interaction.
- **Basis for Dynamic Grasps:** While the current dataset focuses on static grasps, the methodologies developed for data capture and contact modeling can serve as a foundation for future work on dynamic grasps, potentially by applying frame-by-frame analysis.
