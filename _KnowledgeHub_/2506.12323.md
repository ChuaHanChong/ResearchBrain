# Doctor Approved: Generating Medically Accurate Skin Disease Images through AI-Expert Feedback

> [!summary] Summary
> The MAGIC framework, developed by researchers at Tulane University, generates medically accurate skin disease images by leveraging diffusion models guided through AI-expert feedback. This approach, which uses Multimodal Large Language Models to interpret expert-defined clinical checklists, improved diagnostic classifier accuracy by up to +9.02% in multi-condition scenarios and +10.94% in few-shot settings, synthesizing diverse and clinically plausible medical data.

## Problem

- Medical domains face scarcity of high-quality, diverse imaging data, limiting the generalizability and robustness of diagnostic ML models, especially for rare conditions or diverse skin tones.
- Off-the-shelf Text-to-Image Diffusion Models often generate medically inaccurate or implausible images, making them unsuitable for medical data augmentation.
- Traditional expert feedback mechanisms (e.g., RLHF, DPO) for generative models are computationally expensive or labor-intensive, particularly for specialized medical domains with limited expert time.

## Method

- A semi-automated pipeline, MAGIC, fine-tunes a ==Latent Diffusion== Model (Stable Diffusion v2-1) using Textual Inversion and Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) to specialize in skin lesion concepts.
- Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs), such as GPT-4o, evaluate generated images against expert-defined 5-criteria checklists for clinical accuracy, providing structured feedback without extensive human annotation.
- The diffusion model is subsequently fine-tuned using this MLLM-interpreted expert feedback via Reward-model Guided Fine-Tuning (RFT) or Direct Preference Optimization (DPO), with DPO demonstrating superior performance in aligning generations with clinical accuracy.

## Results

- MAGIC-DPO improved ResNet18 classifier accuracy by +**9.02%** (from **29.31%** to **38.33%**) and DINOv2 accuracy by +**5.12%** (from **49.89%** to **55.01%**) over real data baselines in a 20-condition classification task.
- In a data-scarce few-shot setting (**10%** training data), MAGIC-DPO boosted DINOv2 classifier accuracy by +**10.94%** (from **26.45%** to **37.39%**).
- Medical experts found MAGIC-DPO generated images to be highly plausible, with **55.5%** satisfying 3 or more clinical criteria, and the method achieved the lowest Fréchet Inception Distance (FID) score of 18.2, indicating higher fidelity and clinical accuracy.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - MLLMs can effectively interpret structured, expert-defined clinical checklists to provide actionable and reliable feedback for generative models, substantially reducing direct human expert workload.
> - A "task-centric alignment paradigm" that decomposes complex domain knowledge into attribute-level checklists enables general-purpose MLLMs to guide specialized medical tasks without requiring domain-specific MLLM training.
> - Fine-tuning diffusion models with MLLM-interpreted expert feedback, particularly through Direct Preference Optimization (DPO), yields synthesized images that are both visually realistic and clinically accurate, leading to improved downstream diagnostic model performance.
