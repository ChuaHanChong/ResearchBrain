# CUBic: Coordinated Unified Bimanual Perception and Control Framework

> [!summary] Summary
> CUBic is a framework for bimanual robotic manipulation that reformulates cross-arm coordination as a unified perceptual modeling problem, allowing coordination to emerge intrinsically from a shared tokenized representation. It achieved an average success rate of 51.8% across seven bimanual tasks in simulation and outperformed Diffusion Policy in real-world scenarios with an average score of 43.1 for in-domain tasks.

## Problem

- Existing visuomotor policies struggle to extend from single-arm to complex coordinated bimanual manipulation tasks.
- A fundamental dilemma exists in bimanual control: whether to decouple arms to prevent interference or enforce strong coupling to ensure coordination, often leading to compromises.
- Prior methods either simplify the learning space or use explicit mechanisms like cross-attention, which do not offer a unified treatment for both independence and interaction.

## Method

- The framework processes multi-view sensory inputs and proprioceptive signals into stable, disentangled arm-specific perceptual tokens using Unidirectional Perception Aggregation.
- Bidirectional Perception Coordination establishes coordinated relationships between latent action tokens from both arms using dual vector quantization codebooks with a shared mapping space.
- A Unified Perception-to-Control Diffusion Policy, trained in two stages (single-arm pre-training followed by dual-arm coordination post-training), translates coordinated perceptual features into executable actions.

## Results

- CUBic achieved an average success rate of 51.8% across seven bimanual simulation tasks, surpassing Diffusion Policy (38.5%) and 3D Diffusion Policy (39.8%) by 13.3% and 12.0% respectively.
- In real-world experiments, CUBic outperformed Diffusion Policy with an average score of 43.1 for in-domain tasks and 34.7 for out-of-domain tasks, demonstrating robustness to object placement changes.
- Ablation studies confirmed that both the shared-mapping for dual codebooks and the two-stage training paradigm are critical, with their removal causing performance degradations of 9.5% and 11.7% respectively.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Bimanual coordination can be effectively addressed as a unified perceptual modeling problem, allowing coordination to emerge intrinsically from the model's structure rather than being explicitly enforced.
> - Utilizing dual Vector Quantization (VQ) codebooks with a shared mapping space enables both arm-specific reasoning and implicitly encoded cross-arm correlations within a common latent manifold.
> - A two-stage training paradigm, cultivating individual arm skills before introducing explicit policy-level coordination, is crucial for stable and effective learning of complex bimanual control.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of visuomotor policy learning has made advancements in enabling robots to perform control tasks directly from visual inputs, particularly for single-arm manipulation. These end-to-end learning approaches have demonstrated capabilities in mapping images to actions. However, extending these methods to coordinated bimanual manipulation introduces complexities. Bimanual tasks require not only independent perception and action for each arm but also coordinated interaction and consistency between the arms in both spatial and temporal domains. This necessity for cross-arm alignment presents a modeling challenge for existing visuomotor architectures.

Prior research in bimanual manipulation has generally pursued two distinct paradigms. One approach focuses on decoupling sensory inputs and control streams for each arm, aiming to minimize interference and ensure independence. Examples include methods that simplify the learning space by assigning specific roles to arms or using voxelized representations. The other approach emphasizes enhancing inter-arm communication, often through mechanisms such as cross-attention or message passing, to promote coordination. These two directions address different aspects of the bimanual challenge, but their objectives—separation versus interaction—are often in tension.

This paper positions CUBic as an attempt to reconcile these conflicting objectives. It seeks to provide a unified framework that does not exclusively favor either independence or strong coupling but instead allows both properties to emerge intrinsically from a structured representation. The work builds upon recent advancements in unified representations through tokenization, particularly vector-quantized (VQ) tokenization, which has shown promise in compactly representing visual and action modalities. While previous VQ-based methods often focus on single-arm settings, CUBic aims to leverage tokenization to explicitly model inter-arm dependencies and perceptual alignment within a bimanual context. The development of CUBic contributes to the broader goal of creating scalable and generalizable visuomotor policies for multiple manipulators, moving beyond task-specific designs and handcrafted constraints.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a framework for bimanual manipulation that can effectively handle the complexities of cross-arm coordination while simultaneously allowing for independent arm actions. The core motivation stems from the observation that existing visuomotor policy learning methods struggle with bimanual tasks due to a fundamental dilemma: whether to prioritize decoupling the arms to prevent interference or enforce strong coupling to ensure coordination. This often results in solutions that excel at one aspect but compromise the other, leading to a lack of a unified treatment.

Specifically, the paper identifies the following key objectives and motivations:
- **Overcoming the Independence-Coordination Dilemma:** Existing approaches either decouple arms to maintain independence (e.g., reduce interference) or enforce strong cross-arm coupling for coordination (e.g., via cross-attention). This tension necessitates a framework that can integrate both objectives without mutual contradiction. CUBic aims to resolve this by reformulating bimanual coordination as a unified perceptual modeling problem.
- **Learning Intrinsic Coordination:** Instead of relying on explicit, handcrafted coupling mechanisms or pre-defined roles for each arm, the objective is to enable coordination to emerge intrinsically from the model's structure. This is pursued through a shared, tokenized representation that is designed to bridge perception and control.
- **Bridging Perception and Control in a Unified Latent Space:** A central goal is to create a coherent framework where both sensory context and multi-arm coordination can be reasoned about jointly. This involves developing a shared tokenized representation that allows for information exchange and alignment between arms within a common latent space, thereby facilitating end-to-end learning of synchronized bimanual manipulation policies.
- **Achieving Coherent Bimanual Reasoning and Physically Consistent Execution:** The motivation is to develop a system that can not only understand the interplay between two arms at a perceptual level but also translate this understanding into physically consistent action trajectories. This includes capabilities for accurate object localization, precise grasping by individual arms, and synchronized movements during collaborative tasks.
- **Developing Generalizable Policies:** The research is motivated by the desire to move beyond task-specific designs and inductive biases often found in prior bimanual manipulation frameworks, aiming for a more expressive approach that can handle diverse and multimodal cooperative behaviors.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

CUBic operates by integrating three main components: Unidirectional Perception Aggregation, Bidirectional Perception Coordination, and a Unified Perception-to-Control Diffusion Policy. These components are designed to facilitate coordinated bimanual manipulation through a shared, tokenized representation.

#### 3.1. Unidirectional Perception Aggregation

This module processes multi-view sensory inputs to generate arm-specific perceptual tokens.
- **Input Processing:** Multi-view RGB images (from left, right, and top cameras) are encoded using independent ResNet-18 backbones. Proprioceptive signals (joint states) for each arm are projected into the same feature space using lightweight Multi-Layer Perceptrons (MLPs).
- **Latent Token Initialization:** Two sets of learnable latent action tokens, $a_{left}^{q}$ and $a_{right}^{q}$, are randomly initialized. These tokens represent the latent action spaces for the left and right arms, respectively, and serve as a basis for bimanual coordination.
- **Masked-Attention Transformer:** A transformer with a unidirectional self-attention mechanism processes these tokens. This mechanism employs a masking strategy to:
 - Ensure that each arm's latent tokens and arm-specific features (wrist-camera features, proprioceptive embedding) learn relevant information primarily from its own sources and the shared head-camera view.
 - Prevent direct information leakage or premature interference between the two arms by restricting arm-specific tokens to attend only to their corresponding arm data and the shared global features. The top view attends only to itself.
- **Output:** This stage produces stable and disentangled perceptual representations that balance local precision (from wrist cameras and proprioception) with global awareness (from the head camera), anchoring both arm's latent spaces to a common context.

#### 3.2. Bidirectional Perception Coordination

This module establishes coordinated relationships between the latent action tokens from both arms.
- **Dual Codebooks with Shared Mapping:** Two vector quantization (VQ) codebooks, $Z_{left}$ and $Z_{right}$, are employed, each corresponding to an arm. These codebooks share a unified mapping space.
- **Quantization Process:** Given the encoded latent tokens ($a_{left}^{q}$ and $a_{right}^{q}$), their respective distances to the entries in their corresponding codebooks are computed. An optimal quantization index ($i^*$) is selected by minimizing the *combined* distance to entries in both codebooks (i.e., $i^* = \arg \min_i (\|a_{left}^{q} - a_{left}^{z,i}\|_2^2 + \|a_{right}^{q} - a_{right}^{z,i}\|_2^2)$). This mechanism encourages the latent representations of both arms to converge towards a jointly consistent representation within the shared codebook space.
- **Residual Vector Quantization (RVQ):** A hierarchical RVQ scheme is applied to enhance codebook expressiveness and improve training stability.
- **Output:** This process results in quantized latent action tokens ($a_{left}^{z}$ and $a_{right}^{z}$) that implicitly encode cross-arm correlations within a common latent manifold, supporting both arm-specific reasoning and coordinated joint behavior without explicit coupling.

#### 3.3. Unified Perception-to-Control Diffusion Policy

This component translates the coordinated perceptual features into executable actions using a two-stage training paradigm.
- **Perceptual Embedding Preparation:** The quantized latent action tokens ($a_{left}^{z}$, $a_{right}^{z}$) are concatenated with their respective post-attention arm-specific tokens ($c_{left}$, $c_{right}$) and head-camera tokens to form enriched visual-semantic embeddings ($Q_{left}$, $Q_{right}$). These embeddings serve as perceptual conditions for action decoding.
- **Diffusion Transformer (DiT) Policy:** A Diffusion Transformer is utilized as the policy network to generate action sequences. The perceptual embeddings ($Q_{left}$, $Q_{right}$) are integrated into the DiT blocks via cross-attention. The policy learns a denoising function to reconstruct the original clean action trajectory from progressively noised versions, optimized using a standard diffusion objective. A cosine noise schedule with 100 diffusion steps is used during training, and a deterministic DDIM sampling strategy is used during inference, reducing steps to 10.
- **Two-Stage Training Paradigm:**
 1. **Single-Arm Pre-training:** In this initial stage, the policy branches for the two arms are trained independently. Each arm uses its own transformer composed of self-attention, cross-attention, and feed-forward layers. This stage focuses on learning the interaction between individual perceptual embeddings and action tokens, establishing stable individual control skills and consistent perceptual representations. The loss includes diffusion losses for each arm and a vector quantization loss with a commitment term.
 2. **Dual-Arm Coordination Post-training:** Following pre-training, all perception-encoding parameters are frozen. The DiT architecture is modified to enable the two arms to share self-attention layers, thereby introducing mutual visibility and structured correlations in the diffusion noise. Crucially, cross-attention connections to the arm-specific perceptual tokens remain separated, preserving the integrity of individual perception-to-control flows. This stage focuses on learning explicit cross-arm coordination at the policy level while maintaining pre-trained perceptual disentanglement. The loss for this stage comprises only the diffusion losses for both arms.

This hierarchical design allows the model to progressively develop from understanding individual arm actions and perceptions to executing synchronized and physically consistent bimanual control.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The effectiveness of CUBic was evaluated through extensive experiments in both simulation and real-world environments, complemented by ablation studies.

#### 4.1. Simulation Experiments

- **Benchmark:** Experiments were conducted on the RoboTwin benchmark suite, which includes complex bimanual tasks and is designed for evaluating bimanual manipulation. Each task included 100 expert demonstrations.
- **Baselines:** CUBic was compared against Diffusion Policy (DP), 3D Diffusion Policy (DP3), and GR-MG.
- **Metrics:** Performance was measured by the mean and standard deviation of success rates over three random seeds, with each seed evaluated across 50 executions.
- **Quantitative Results:**
 - CUBic achieved an average success rate of **51.8%** across seven bimanual manipulation tasks.
 - This performance surpassed the baselines: DP3 (39.8%), DP (38.5%), and GR-MG (8.0%).
 - The improvement over DP3 was 12.0%, over DP was 13.3%, and over GR-MG was 43.8%.
 - CUBic demonstrated advantages in complex, long-horizon coordination tasks, such as "Pick Apple Messy" and "Blocks Stack Easy," suggesting that the unified codebook coordination mechanism contributed to enhanced bimanual manipulation capabilities.
- **Qualitative Results:** Visualizations indicated that CUBic exhibited superior object localization and more precise single-arm grasping compared to Diffusion Policy. It also showed stronger inter-arm coordination in bimanual scenarios. This was attributed to the shared codebook design, which maintained independent environmental perception for each arm while promoting synchronized collaboration.

#### 4.2. Real-World Experiments

- **Setup:** A dual-arm Agibot robot equipped with three Intel RealSense D435 cameras (head and both wrists) was used.
- **Tasks:** Six real-world tasks of varying difficulty were designed, including single-arm (e.g., Apple to Plate), dual-arm simultaneous (e.g., Dual Banana Grasp), and dual-arm sequential (e.g., Apple to Drawer) manipulations.
- **Data and Metrics:** 100 expert trajectories were collected for each task, and 50 evaluation trials were conducted for both in-domain (seen object positions) and out-of-domain (randomly placed object positions) scenarios. A step-wise scoring system was used, with total execution scores representing the sum of points for successfully completed sub-tasks.
- **Results:**
 - CUBic generally outperformed Diffusion Policy (DP) in real-world scenarios.
 - For in-domain tasks, CUBic achieved an average score of **43.1**, compared to DP's 19.5, representing a gain of 23.6 points.
 - For out-of-domain tasks, CUBic achieved an average score of **34.7**, compared to DP's 12.7, representing a gain of 22.0 points.
 - The performance indicated that CUBic's effectiveness was relatively robust to changes in object location, as the impact on results was not significant.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

- **Impact of shared-mapping for dual codebooks:** Removing the shared mapping and using entirely independent codebooks resulted in a significant performance degradation, with a decrease of 9.5% in average success rate (from 51.8% to 42.1%). This finding supports the hypothesis that the latent space constraints imposed by shared dual-codebook mapping are beneficial for coordination.
- **Impact of two-stage training:** Directly training the model end-to-end without the two-stage approach led to a substantial reduction in average success rate (from 51.8% to 40.1%, a 11.7% decrease compared to the full model, and a 7.5% increase compared to the independent codebook baseline). This indicated that the progressive cultivation of collaborative perception in pre-training followed by action coordination in post-training is crucial for effective coordinated control.
- **Impact of latent tokens and codebook size:**
 - Omitting latent tokens and directly quantizing image and state features resulted in complete model failure (0.0% success rate).
 - Varying the number of latent tokens (N) and codebook size (K) also affected performance. For example, using N=8 and K=512 led to degraded performance compared to N=4 and K=256 (40.2% vs 51.8% average success rate). This suggests that an appropriate configuration of latent tokens and codebook size is necessary for the shared mapping constraint to be effective.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The CUBic framework introduces a re-conceptualization of bimanual coordination, treating it as a unified perceptual modeling problem rather than a trade-off between inter-arm independence and interaction. This approach is significant because it allows coordination to emerge intrinsically from the model's structure, through a shared tokenized representation, instead of being enforced by explicit coupling mechanisms or requiring task-specific inductive biases.

The demonstrated consistent performance gains over Diffusion Policy, 3D Diffusion Policy, and GR-MG in complex simulation tasks, and over Diffusion Policy in diverse real-world bimanual manipulation scenarios, suggest the effectiveness of this unified modeling approach. The ability of CUBic to achieve improved coordination accuracy, precise single-arm grasping, and higher task success rates in both in-domain and out-of-domain settings indicates a step towards more robust and generalizable visuomotor policies for multi-arm systems.

The two-stage training paradigm and the use of shared codebooks with latent tokens contribute to a more stable and effective learning process for bimanual tasks, particularly in capturing complex spatio-temporal dynamics. The findings from the ablation studies validate the importance of these architectural and training design choices.

The potential impact of CUBic extends to various applications requiring complex dual-arm robotic manipulation, where robots need to interact with the environment and each other in a coordinated yet flexible manner. This includes assembly tasks, object handover, and other collaborative manipulation activities. By providing a unified pathway that bridges perception and control in a shared latent space, this work contributes to advancing the field towards more capable and autonomous robotic systems capable of reasoning jointly across multiple manipulators.
