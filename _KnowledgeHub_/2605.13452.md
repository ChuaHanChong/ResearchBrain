# CUBic: Coordinated Unified Bimanual Perception and Control Framework

> [!summary] Summary
> CUBic is a framework for bimanual robotic manipulation that reformulates cross-arm coordination as a unified perceptual modeling problem, allowing coordination to emerge intrinsically from a shared tokenized representation. It achieved an average success rate of 51.8% across seven bimanual tasks in simulation and outperformed Diffusion Policy in real-world scenarios with an average score of 43.1 for in-domain tasks.

## Problem

- Existing visuomotor policies struggle to extend from single-arm to complex coordinated bimanual manipulation tasks.
- A fundamental dilemma exists in bimanual control: whether to decouple arms to prevent interference or enforce strong coupling to ensure coordination, often leading to compromises.
- Prior methods either simplify the learning space or use explicit mechanisms like cross-attention, which do not offer a unified treatment for both independence and interaction.

## Method

- The framework processes multi-view sensory inputs and proprioceptive signals into stable, disentangled arm-specific perceptual tokens using Unidirectional Perception Aggregation.
- Bidirectional Perception Coordination establishes coordinated relationships between latent action tokens from both arms using dual vector quantization codebooks with a shared mapping space.
- A Unified Perception-to-Control Diffusion Policy, trained in two stages (single-arm pre-training followed by dual-arm coordination post-training), translates coordinated perceptual features into executable actions.

## Results

- CUBic achieved an average success rate of 51.8% across seven bimanual simulation tasks, surpassing Diffusion Policy (38.5%) and 3D Diffusion Policy (39.8%) by 13.3% and 12.0% respectively.
- In real-world experiments, CUBic outperformed Diffusion Policy with an average score of 43.1 for in-domain tasks and 34.7 for out-of-domain tasks, demonstrating robustness to object placement changes.
- Ablation studies confirmed that both the shared-mapping for dual codebooks and the two-stage training paradigm are critical, with their removal causing performance degradations of 9.5% and 11.7% respectively.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Bimanual coordination can be effectively addressed as a unified perceptual modeling problem, allowing coordination to emerge intrinsically from the model's structure rather than being explicitly enforced.
> - Utilizing dual Vector Quantization (VQ) codebooks with a shared mapping space enables both arm-specific reasoning and implicitly encoded cross-arm correlations within a common latent manifold.
> - A two-stage training paradigm, cultivating individual arm skills before introducing explicit policy-level coordination, is crucial for stable and effective learning of complex bimanual control.
