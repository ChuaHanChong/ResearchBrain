# DexSim2Real$^{2}$: Building Explicit World Model for Precise Articulated Object Dexterous Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Tsinghua University and JD Explore Academy developed DexSim2Real2^22, a framework that enables robots to precisely manipulate unseen articulated objects by actively constructing explicit world models. It achieved significantly lower absolute manipulation errors, such as 7.2 degrees for a laptop with a suction gripper compared to a 16.0-degree baseline, and higher success rates across multiple object categories and end-effectors.

## Problem

- Learning-based robotic manipulation approaches struggle with the high-dimensional state spaces of articulated objects, requiring extensive data or demonstrations for precise, long-horizon tasks.
- Robots lack the ability to perform goal-conditioned manipulation on unseen articulated objects without prior models, requiring on-the-fly inference of object properties.
- Developing generalizable frameworks for diverse end-effectors, particularly high-DoF dexterous hands, is challenging due to complex planning and high-dimensional action spaces.

## Method

- A three-phase framework: ==Interactive Perception==, ==Explicit World Model Construction==, and ==Sampling-Based Model Predictive Control (MPC)==.
- Interactive Perception actively gathers multi-view observations using affordance learning from self-supervised simulations or real-world human videos, converting 2D predictions to 3D robot motions.
- Explicit World Model Construction builds high-fidelity digital twins of articulated objects, including geometry and kinematic structure, by leveraging ==3D AIGC==, foundation vision models, and robust movable part segmentation. Sampling-based MPC, specifically ==iCEM==, plans long-horizon trajectories using the constructed explicit world model, incorporating ==eigengrasp== for efficient dexterous hand control and dense reward functions.

## Results

- Achieved significantly lower average absolute manipulation errors compared to baselines (e.g., **7.20°** vs. **16.00°** for suction gripper on laptops against UMPNet), leading to higher precise manipulation success rates.
- Demonstrated precise manipulation with 2-finger grippers (approx. **75%** of tasks within 30% relative error) and dexterous hands (e.g., **18/20** cabinet manipulations within 30% relative error).
- Constructed explicit world models showed high accuracy (average joint axis angle error less than **9°**) and enabled advanced manipulation skills such as tool use for multi-part objects.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Actively interacting with an unseen object provides crucial multi-frame observations for constructing an accurate explicit world model, outperforming single-frame perception.
> - Building high-fidelity digital twins of articulated objects, including their kinematics, enables precise, long-horizon, goal-conditioned manipulation through model-based planning.
> - Applying eigengrasp for dimensionality reduction effectively controls high-DoF dexterous hands, resulting in smoother, more natural motions and reduced planning computation time.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Articulated object manipulation is a complex problem in robotics, extending beyond simple pick-and-place tasks to require precise trajectory control. Current approaches often rely on learning-based methods like reinforcement learning (RL) and imitation learning (IL), which struggle with the high-dimensional and complex state spaces of articulated objects. These methods typically require extensive demonstrations or millions of interactions to learn policies, and often rely on single-frame observations, limiting their ability to accurately infer object kinematics.

Existing research in articulated object manipulation often utilizes neural networks to predict actions from single-frame observations (e.g., FlowBot, UMPNet). However, these methods may not provide sufficient information for precise, long-horizon manipulation and are often limited to specific end-effectors like suction grippers. Dexterous manipulation with multi-finger hands offers greater flexibility but introduces challenges related to high-dimensional action spaces, making traditional planning and data-driven learning difficult.

World model construction is a long-standing challenge, with various approaches ranging from low-dimensional state space models to large-scale image/video-based world models. While image-based models show promise, they often require extensive datasets and computational resources. Articulated object modeling (e.g., Ditto, methods using point cloud sequences) has focused on reconstructing geometry and kinematics, but often with limitations to single-joint objects or sensitivity to segmentation quality. Recent advancements in vision-language models for URDF generation rely on existing mesh datasets, which can limit geometric diversity.

Affordance learning, which guides robot interactions, has been explored through supervised methods (limited by annotation cost) and self-supervised simulation interactions (limited by scarcity of interactable 3D assets). Learning from real-world human videos (e.g., VRB) offers scalability but typically provides 2D outputs that need conversion for 3D robot control.

This work, DexSim2Real$^2$, positions itself by addressing these limitations through an approach that combines active interaction with explicit world model construction and sampling-based control. It aims to bridge the gap between learning general interaction strategies and achieving precise, goal-conditioned manipulation with various end-effectors, including dexterous hands, without extensive demonstrations. It leverages advancements in 3D AIGC and foundation vision models to improve model accuracy and scalability, and introduces methods to handle the high dimensionality of dexterous hands and convert 2D affordance predictions to 3D robot motions. The work extends previous research (Sim2Real$^2$) by improving the model construction pipeline, expanding end-effector support, and enabling affordance learning from human videos.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this work is to develop a robust robot learning framework, DexSim2Real$^2$, for precise, goal-conditioned manipulation of *unseen* articulated objects in the real world. This goal encompasses several detailed motivations:

1. **Overcome Limitations of Current Learning-Based Methods:** Existing RL and IL approaches struggle with the high-dimensional and complex state distributions of articulated objects, often requiring vast amounts of data (hundreds of demonstrations or millions of interactions) to achieve satisfactory performance. The authors are motivated to develop a more data-efficient approach that does not rely on extensive pre-recorded demonstrations or millions of self-supervised interactions.

2. **Enable Goal-Conditioned Manipulation of Unseen Objects:** The framework aims to manipulate articulated objects to specific target joint states, a more complex task than simple pick-and-place. A key challenge is dealing with "unseen" objects, meaning objects for which the robot has no prior model or experience. The motivation is to allow robots to infer object properties and kinematics on-the-fly.

3. **Mimic Human Cognitive Processes for Manipulation:** Humans actively interact with environments to gather information and form a "mental model" before planning complex actions. The authors are motivated to integrate this "interactive perception" concept into their robotic framework, where active interactions are used to build an explicit world model rather than just react to perceptions.

4. **Support Diverse End-Effectors:** Traditional methods often focus on specific grippers (e.g., suction grippers). The paper aims to develop a generalizable framework that supports multiple types of end-effectors, including suction grippers, two-finger grippers, and multi-finger dexterous hands, acknowledging the distinct challenges each presents.

5. **Address High-Dimensionality of Dexterous Hands:** Dexterous manipulation offers greater flexibility but introduces a significantly higher-dimensional action space for planning. A key motivation is to develop methods, such as dimensionality reduction techniques, that make planning for dexterous hands computationally feasible and produce natural, executable motions.

6. **Improve World Model Construction Accuracy and Generalizability:** Many existing articulated object modeling methods are limited to objects with a single joint or produce geometries that are not sufficiently accurate for precise physical interaction. The authors are motivated to develop a more robust pipeline for constructing explicit, high-fidelity digital twins that capture both geometry and kinematic structure (including multiple movable parts), and can be easily integrated into physics simulators.

7. **Enhance Affordance Learning Scalability:** Training data for affordance learning is often limited by the availability of interactable 3D assets in simulation or the cost of manual annotation. The motivation is to explore and implement alternative, scalable data sources for affordance learning, such as large-scale human manipulation videos, and to develop methods to convert 2D video-based affordances into 3D robot actions.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The DexSim2Real$^2$ framework is organized into three main phases: Interactive Perception, Explicit World Model Construction, and Sampling-Based Model Predictive Control.

**A. Interactive Perception**
The initial phase addresses the limitation of single-frame observations by actively interacting with an unseen articulated object to gather more information. This one-step interaction, which does not require precision, is guided by an affordance prediction module.
1. **Affordance Learning from Self-Supervised Simulation:** The framework first learns affordances from self-supervised interactions in simulation, using a modified Where2Act algorithm. This involves training a network to predict "actionability scores" for points on the object and propose actions that can change the object's state. The modification accounts for the robot's kinematic structure by using motion planning to find feasible joint trajectories for proposed actions, prioritizing actions within the robot's dexterous workspace.
2. **Affordance Learning from Real-World Egocentric Demonstrations:** To overcome the dependency on interactable 3D assets, the framework can also learn affordances from real-world egocentric human manipulation videos using the Vision-Robotics Bridge (VRB). VRB predicts a contact heatmap and a 2D post-contact trajectory. A novel **spatial projection method** is proposed to convert these 2D predictions into 3D robot motions. This method utilizes an RGB image, depth map, and a virtual camera view to triangulate the 3D contact point and derive a 3D post-contact vector from the intersection of "projection planes" corresponding to the 2D trajectories.
3. **Interaction Execution:** The predicted 3D action is executed on the real robot. For objects with multiple movable parts (K > 1), the process iteratively detects affordance points, masks out the neighborhood of manipulated parts, and detects the next affordance point until all K parts have been interacted with. For handles on closed objects, an alternative grasping approach is used to pull them.

**B. Explicit World Model Construction**
After K+1 frames of multi-view observations are collected, an explicit digital twin of the articulated object is constructed in a physics simulator.
1. **Canonical Mesh Generation:** For each observed frame, a high-quality 3D mesh is reconstructed using a controllable large-scale generative model, Rodin, conditioned on RGBD data from a main sensor and RGB images from side cameras.
2. **Mesh Positioning and Scaling:** The generated mesh, initially at unit scale and origin-positioned, is then accurately scaled and positioned. This involves a modified FoundationPose to estimate the 6D pose, refined by aligning rendered edges with the object's segmentation mask using differentiable rendering. The true scale is then calculated by comparing the rendered depth map of the unscaled mesh with the real depth map.
3. **Movable Part Segmentation:** To segment the mesh into movable parts, the framework leverages all K+1 meshes representing different kinematic states. It uses SAM-Part3D to initially segment the mesh into small sub-parts. To determine which sub-parts have moved, Chamfer distances between point clouds of consecutive frames are computed. Robustness is enhanced by incorporating mesh connectivity and robot proprioception (the sub-part closest to the end-effector is assumed to have moved), employing a breadth-first traversal logic to group connected moved parts.
4. **Kinematic Structure Construction:** The final step involves inferring the object's kinematic structure (part tree hierarchy, joint categories like prismatic or revolute, and joint configurations like axis direction and origin) from the segmented parts and their transformations across frames. This process adapts an approach from. The constructed model is then converted into the Unified Robot Description Format (URDF) for use in physics simulators like SAPIEN. Non-convex meshes are decomposed into convex parts using VHACD for realistic physics simulation.

**C. Sampling-Based Model Predictive Control (MPC)**
With the explicit world model, sampling-based MPC is used to plan a long-horizon trajectory from an initial joint state to a target state.
1. **iCEM Algorithm:** Due to the complex contact dynamics between the robot and the object model, which make gradient-based optimization challenging, the iCEM method is employed. iCEM is a zeroth-order optimization algorithm that iteratively refines a control trajectory by sampling actions from a Gaussian distribution, evaluating them, and updating the distribution based on elite samples.
2. **Dimensionality Reduction for Dexterous Hands (Eigengrasp):** For dexterous hands with high degrees of freedom (DoF), directly searching in the original joint space is computationally expensive and can lead to unnatural postures. The framework uses **eigengrasp** to reduce the action space. A dataset of diverse grasping postures (generated by DexGraspNet) is used to perform Principal Component Analysis (PCA), extracting a small number of eigenvectors (e.g., m=2). The iCEM algorithm then searches within this lower-dimensional eigengrasp space, with hand joint angles computed as a linear combination of these eigenvectors.
3. **Dense Reward Functions:** Dense reward functions guide the trajectory optimization process within iCEM for different end-effectors:
 - **Suction Gripper:** Rewards include success (reaching target state), approaching (reducing distance to target state), contact (establishing/maintaining contact with the target part without unexpected collisions), distance (bringing suction tip closer to target part), and access direction (aligning suction gripper orientation with target surface normal).
 - **Two-finger Gripper:** Similar to suction, but distance reward encourages the gripper's grasp center to approach the target part. A regularization reward is added to penalize high acceleration and velocity, promoting smoother movements.
 - **Dexterous Hand:** Rewards include success, approaching, and a contact reward that encourages multiple fingers to make contact with the target link for a stable cage grasp. Distance and regularization (penalizing velocity and Cartesian end-effector error) rewards are also included.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments evaluated DexSim2Real$^2$ across various articulated objects (drawers, cabinets, lamps, laptops) and end-effectors (suction gripper, two-finger gripper, Allegro Hand, XHand), using metrics like absolute error (δ) and relative error (δr) in joint movement.

**A. Experiments on Suction Gripper**
- **Comparison with Baseline (UMPNet):** DexSim2Real$^2$ demonstrated significantly lower average absolute errors (Laptop: 7.2$^\circ$ vs. 16.0$^\circ$; Cabinet: 6.8$^\circ$ vs. 15.3$^\circ$) and relative errors (Laptop: 23.3% vs. 42.9%; Cabinet: 21.7% vs. 41.5%) compared to UMPNet. It also achieved a higher success rate for precise manipulation (e.g., 9/10 for Laptop with <30% relative error vs. 3/10 for UMPNet). The authors attribute this to the continuous control values in MPC and the richer information from multi-frame observations.

**B. Experiments on 2-Finger Gripper**
- **Real-World Manipulation:** Achieved precise manipulation, with approximately 75% of manipulations having a relative error |δr| < 30% for both Laptop (16/20) and Cabinet (15/20) categories. Average absolute errors were 7.2$^\circ$ for Laptop and 7.4$^\circ$ for Cabinet.
- **Ablation Study on Reward Function:**
 - The **full reward function** generally led to higher success rates and fewer steps to succeed.
 - Removing `r_reg` (regularization) led to similar success/steps but generated unsafe, unnatural robot configurations.
 - Removing `r_dist` (distance) led to task failure due to the short planning horizon.
 - Omitting `r_target` (approaching) or `r_success` resulted in lower success rates and more steps for successful tasks.
 - Omitting `r_contact` led to lower success rates, indicating its importance in guiding interaction.

**C. Experiments on Dexterous Hand**
- **Real-World Manipulation:** Achieved successful manipulations for drawers and cabinets. For drawers, 11/20 manipulations had |δr| < 30% with an average |δr| of 26.4%. For cabinets, 18/20 manipulations had |δr| < 30% with an average |δr| of 20.4%.
- **Ablation Study on Eigengrasp Dimension:**
 - **Success Rate:** Eigengrasp dimension `m=2` performed comparably to `m=7` and `m=16` for all tasks, significantly outperforming `m=1` in more complex tasks (closing laptop, opening drawer).
 - **Joint Jerk:** Lower eigengrasp dimensions generally resulted in lower finger joint jerks (smoother movements). `m=1` showed superior thumb jerk, while `m=1` and `m=2` demonstrated advantages for other fingers compared to higher dimensions.
 - **Computation Time:** Using `m=2` significantly reduced computation time (approximately 1 second less per step) compared to `m=16`, leading to faster trajectory planning.
- **Ablation Study on Reward Function:**
 - The **full reward function** generally outperformed ablated versions in success rate and steps to completion.
 - Removing `r_dist` made tasks impossible.
 - Removing `r_target` or `r_success` decreased success rates and increased steps for successful tasks.
 - Removing `r_contact` showed mixed results, sometimes leading to more efficient (fewer steps) but potentially unconventional hand postures.
- **Advantage of Dexterous Manipulation:** The dexterous hand consistently required fewer steps on average to complete tasks compared to the two-finger gripper, particularly for tasks requiring more intricate interaction. The laptop opening task, being simpler, was an exception.

**D. Experiments on Explicit World Model Construction**
- **Model Accuracy Evaluation:** The method demonstrated accurate construction of world models across all four categories. Average angle error for joint axes was less than 9$^\circ$. It showed significantly better angle error compared to ScrewNet (e.g., Laptop: 1.34$^\circ$ vs. 23.15$^\circ$; Cabinet: 4.84$^\circ$ vs. 11.67$^\circ$).
- **Impact on Manipulation Performance:** Model construction errors were positively correlated with manipulation errors. Position errors had a more significant impact on manipulation accuracy than joint orientation errors.
- **Ablation Study on Movable Part Segmentation:** The proposed movable part segmentation module improved kinematic structure construction. It led to higher success rates in model construction (e.g., Drawer: 10/10 vs. 5/10 without segmentation) and more accurate axis parameter estimation, particularly for prismatic joints (e.g., Cabinet Angle Err: 4.84$^\circ$ vs. 6.10$^\circ$).

**E. Ablation Studies on Affordance Detection**
- **Where2Act vs. VRB:** VRB consistently achieved a higher recall rate for detecting handles and generated more correct interaction directions than Where2Act (e.g., Cabinet handle detection: 17/20 for VRB vs. 7/20 for Where2Act). This is attributed to VRB's training on real-world human videos and larger data scale.
- **Pixel Projection:** The proposed 2D-to-3D pixel projection method generated more suitable 3D interaction vectors for one-step interactions compared to randomly generated vectors based solely on 2D affordance.

**F. Objects with Multiple Movable Parts & Advanced Manipulation Skills**
- The framework successfully reconstructed explicit models of objects with multiple movable parts (e.g., a cabinet with two revolute joints, a fridge with two revolute and one prismatic joint), demonstrating accurate kinematic structure and texture reconstruction.
- The explicit world model enabled **advanced manipulation skills**, specifically **tool use**. The robot successfully opened a drawer using different tools (T-shaped, semi-ring) when direct gripper manipulation was not feasible, demonstrating the generalizability of the physics-based simulation for planning unseen interactions.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The DexSim2Real$^2$ framework contributes to the field of robotics by addressing several critical challenges in articulated object manipulation, offering significant potential impact:

1. **Enabling Precise Manipulation of Unseen Articulated Objects:** By actively building an explicit world model, the framework allows robots to precisely manipulate novel articulated objects without requiring prior CAD models, extensive demonstrations, or large-scale interaction data for each new object. This substantially enhances robot adaptability in unstructured environments.
2. **Generalizability Across End-Effectors:** The framework's ability to support suction grippers, two-finger grippers, and multi-finger dexterous hands broadens the range of tasks robots can perform. The novel application of eigengrasp for dexterous manipulation makes high-DoF control feasible and generates natural, executable hand motions, expanding the capabilities of robot hands beyond simple grasping.
3. **Robust World Model Construction:** The integration of 3D AIGC, foundation vision models, and a robust movable part segmentation pipeline enables the construction of high-fidelity digital twins of articulated objects, including their complex kinematic structures and geometries. This explicit, physics-based model provides a strong foundation for long-horizon planning and prediction.
4. **Improved Affordance Learning from Diverse Data:** By leveraging large-scale egocentric human manipulation videos and developing a 2D-to-3D projection method, the framework provides a more scalable approach to affordance learning, reducing reliance on costly simulation-based data generation or manual annotation.
5. **Enhanced Planning and Control:** The use of sampling-based Model Predictive Control (MPC) with tailored dense reward functions allows for effective trajectory planning in complex contact-rich scenarios. This model-based planning, informed by an accurate digital twin, facilitates precise goal-conditioned manipulation.
6. **Extensibility to Advanced Manipulation:** The generalizability of the explicit world model is demonstrated by enabling advanced skills like tool manipulation, which is a crucial step towards more versatile and intelligent robotic systems capable of addressing real-world constraints (e.g., objects out of reach, small gaps).

The proposed framework advances robot learning by combining the benefits of model-based reasoning with data-driven perception, showing promise for deploying more capable and adaptable robots in real-world environments. Future work aims to expand this to scene-level digital twin construction and mobile manipulation, further increasing its real-world applicability.
