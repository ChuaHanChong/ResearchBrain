# Understanding self-supervised Learning Dynamics without Contrastive Pairs

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Facebook AI Research (FAIR) and Stanford University analytically explain why non-contrastive self-supervised learning (SSL) methods like BYOL and SimSiam avoid representational collapse by identifying the essential roles of stop-gradient, predictor dynamics, and Exponential Moving Average (EMA). Their work introduces DirectPred, an optimization-free predictor that achieves competitive performance, matching or surpassing gradient-trained linear and even non-linear predictors on ImageNet.

## Problem

- Non-contrastive self-supervised learning methods like BYOL and SimSiam achieve state-of-the-art results without negative pairs, yet the theoretical mechanisms preventing representational collapse remained unexplained.
- A lack of formal understanding existed for the specific roles of architectural components such as the stop-gradient operation, the learnable predictor, and the Exponential Moving Average (EMA) in these algorithms.
- The nonlinear learning dynamics of these complex deep learning systems were not analytically characterized, making principled design and hyperparameter tuning challenging.

## Method

- A simplified, bias-free ==linear BYOL== model was developed, using continuous-time gradient flow equations to enable analytical tractability.
- Key assumptions (proportional EMA, isotropic data, symmetric predictor) were introduced to decouple the high-dimensional dynamics into independent scalar dynamical systems.
- Fixed-point and stability analysis was conducted on these simplified systems to determine conditions for avoiding collapse and characterizing learned representations, validated with extensive empirical ablation studies.

## Results

- DirectPred, an optimization-free predictor based on theoretical insights into eigenspace alignment, achieved comparable or superior performance to gradient-trained linear predictors (+**2.5%** Top-1 on ImageNet at 300 epochs), and matched BYOL's default 2-layer non-linear predictor on ImageNet.
- The theory elucidated the specific roles of weight decay in balancing online and predictor networks and promoting feature sensitivity to augmentation statistics, while also explaining the optimal range for the relative predictor learning rate.
- Empirical evaluations on STL-10, CIFAR-10, and ImageNet consistently validated the theoretical predictions regarding the necessity of stop-gradient, the dynamics of EMA, and the influence of predictor learning rates.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The stop-gradient operation is analytically proven to be essential, as its removal leads to representational collapse in the linear model.
> - The predictor dynamically destabilizes the trivial collapsed solution, creating a stable manifold for non-collapsed, meaningful representations rather than simply mapping features.
> - Exponential Moving Average (EMA) acts as an 'automatic curriculum,' gradually increasing the learning target and ensuring stable feature growth while maintaining eigenspace alignment.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This paper addresses a critical theoretical gap in the rapidly evolving field of **self-supervised learning (SSL)**, particularly concerning **non-contrastive SSL methods**.

Traditionally, SSL aims to learn powerful, general-purpose feature representations from unlabeled data by designing auxiliary tasks. A dominant paradigm, **contrastive learning**, achieved state-of-the-art performance by optimizing a loss function that brings augmented views of the same data point (positive pairs) closer together in representation space while pushing augmented views of different data points (negative pairs) farther apart. Methods like SimCLR (Chen et al., 2020a) and MoCo (He et al., 2020) exemplified this approach, with the crucial role of negative pairs believed to prevent **representational collapse** – a trivial solution where the model maps all inputs to the same representation.

However, recent breakthroughs challenged this notion. Methods like **Bootstrap Your Own Latent (BYOL)** (Grill et al., 2020) and **SimSiam** (Chen & He, 2020) demonstrated remarkable performance using *only positive pairs*. These "non-contrastive" SSL methods employ a Siamese network architecture with an online and a target network, a learnable predictor, and a stop-gradient operation. They effectively match the output of the online network (after passing through a predictor) to the output of a target network (without gradients). The astounding success of BYOL and SimSiam, despite lacking negative pairs, raised a fundamental, unanswered theoretical question: **Why do these methods not collapse into trivial representations?**

Prior theoretical studies on SSL largely focused on contrastive approaches (Arora et al., 2019; Lee et al., 2020). This paper from Tian, Chen, and Ganguli steps in to provide the first analytical understanding of the nonlinear learning dynamics of *non-contrastive* SSL. By tackling this theoretical enigma, the work not only sheds light on the mechanisms of these specific algorithms but also contributes to the broader effort of building theoretical foundations for deep learning, often described as an empirical science lacking sufficient theoretical guarantees. Understanding these dynamics can guide the design of more robust, efficient, and interpretable SSL architectures and training strategies. The use of simple linear networks as a tractable theoretical model, building on precedents in deep learning theory (e.g., Saxe et al., 2013), reflects a common strategy for gaining initial conceptual insights before tackling the full complexity of nonlinear deep networks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation for this research stems directly from the empirical success and theoretical puzzle presented by non-contrastive self-supervised learning methods like BYOL and SimSiam. Despite their remarkable performance, the underlying mechanisms preventing representational collapse without relying on negative pairs remained poorly understood.

The **key objectives** of this paper are:

1. **Unraveling the Non-Collapse Mechanism:** The primary objective is to fundamentally understand *why* and *how* non-contrastive SSL methods, specifically BYOL and SimSiam, avoid representational collapse when they only encourage positive pairs to become similar. This involves identifying the specific architectural components and training protocols that contribute to this phenomenon.
2. **Characterizing Learned Representations:** To gain insight into the nature of the representations learned by these methods. How do these learned features encode information about the data and its augmentations?
3. **Analyzing Hyperparameter Interactions:** To systematically investigate how various design choices and hyperparameters—such as the Exponential Moving Average (EMA) for the target network, the relative learning rate of the predictor, and weight decay—interact nonlinearly within the learning dynamics and collectively influence the model's behavior and performance.
4. **Developing a Tractable Theoretical Framework:** To build a simplified, analytical model (using linear networks) that can capture the essential learning dynamics of non-contrastive SSL, allowing for theoretical predictions and conceptual insights that can be qualitatively validated in more complex, real-world scenarios.
5. **Informing Future Algorithmic Design:** To leverage theoretical insights to propose novel and potentially more efficient or robust algorithmic components, such as a simpler, optimization-free predictor.

In essence, the authors are motivated to move beyond the "black box" understanding of these powerful SSL methods, providing a scientific explanation for their observed efficacy and offering principled guidance for their further development.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors employ a **theoretical and empirical approach** to understand the dynamics of non-contrastive SSL.

#### 3.1. Theoretical Framework: Linear Network Dynamics

The core of their methodology is to analyze a **simplified, bias-free linear BYOL model**. In this model, the online, target, and predictor networks are represented by simple weight matrices (W, W_a, and W_p, respectively) rather than complex deep neural networks. This simplification allows for analytical tractability of the learning dynamics.

1. **Gradient Flow Approximation:** The discrete-time gradient descent updates typically used in training are approximated by continuous-time **gradient flow equations**. This technique simplifies analysis by allowing the use of differential equations (Lemma 1 and its derivation in the Supplementary Material).
2. **Simplifying Assumptions for Tractability:** To further simplify the high-dimensional, coupled, nonlinear differential equations, the authors introduce three key assumptions:
 - **Assumption 1 (Proportional EMA):** The target network `W_a` is assumed to always be proportional to the online network `W` (i.e., `W_a(t) = τ(t)W(t)`). This simplifies the EMA dynamics into a single scalar parameter `τ(t)`. The authors empirically validate that this assumption largely holds in practice (Fig. 7, Supplementary Material).
 - **Assumption 2 (Isotropic Data and Augmentation):** The data distribution `p(x)` is assumed to have zero mean and identity covariance, and the augmentation `p_aug(·|x)` has mean `x` and isotropic covariance `σ^2I`. This simplifies the data and augmentation covariance matrices (`X=I`, `X'=σ^2I`), making the dynamics more amenable to analysis.
 - **Assumption 3 (Symmetric Predictor):** `W_p` is assumed to be symmetric, motivated by fixed-point analysis showing predictor symmetry at equilibrium and empirical observations of `W_p` moving towards symmetry during training (Fig. 2).
3. **Decoupled Dynamics:** Under these assumptions, the high-dimensional dynamics of the matrices `W` and `W_p` can be decoupled into independent 3-dimensional (for BYOL) or 2-dimensional (for SimSiam) scalar dynamical systems, one for each "mode" or eigenvalue (Eqns. 11-13). This allows for a deeper study of individual mode behavior.
4. **Fixed Point and Stability Analysis:** The authors analyze the fixed points of these decoupled scalar systems to understand conditions under which the representations collapse (`s_j = 0`) or achieve non-collapsed, meaningful solutions. They also examine the stability of these fixed points.
5. **Invariance Properties:** They identify key invariants of the dynamics, such as the relationship between `W W^T` and `W_p^T W_p` (Theorem 1), which reveals how weight decay promotes balancing between the online and predictor networks.

#### 3.2. Empirical Validation and Algorithmic Innovation

The theoretical predictions are rigorously tested through extensive **ablation studies and experiments** on standard computer vision benchmarks:
- **Datasets:** STL-10, CIFAR-10, and ImageNet.
- **Backbones:** ResNet-18 for smaller datasets, ResNet-50 for ImageNet.
- **Evaluation Protocol:** Linear evaluation protocol (training a linear classifier on frozen features).
- **Ablation Studies:** Varying hyperparameters (EMA rates, predictor learning rates, weight decay), predictor architecture (linear vs. 2-layer, symmetric vs. asymmetric), and the presence/absence of stop-gradients. These experiments serve to confirm or refute the qualitative predictions of the simplified theory in real-world deep learning contexts.

Motivated by their theoretical findings, the authors propose a novel method called **DirectPred**. Instead of training the predictor `W_p` via gradient descent, DirectPred directly computes and sets `W_p` based on the principal components analysis of the online network's outputs (`F`). This is a direct application of their theoretical insight into eigenspace alignment (Theorem 3) and the `s_j ≈ α_p^-1 p_j^2` relationship. They evaluate DirectPred against gradient-trained predictors across various settings.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The paper delivers a series of profound theoretical insights and practical results, effectively explaining the non-collapse phenomenon in non-contrastive SSL and guiding improved algorithm design.

#### 4.1. Theoretical Explanations for Non-Collapse and Hyperparameter Roles

1. **The Essential Role of Stop-Gradient (Theorem 2):** The theory analytically proves that removing the stop-gradient operation leads to representational collapse (`W(t) → 0`) even in the simplified linear model. This fundamental finding aligns perfectly with empirical observations from SimSiam, establishing stop-gradient as a critical component in preventing collapse.
2. **The Predictor Prevents Collapse (Implicitly):** While not a formal theorem, the fixed-point analysis in the supplementary material demonstrates that removing the predictor (`W_p = I`) often leads to a stable collapsed solution. The predictor dynamically modifies the loss landscape, creating an unstable fixed point at the origin (collapse) and a stable manifold of non-collapsed solutions. It acts as a "destabilizer" of the trivial solution.
3. **Weight Decay (η) Promotes Balancing and Feature Sensitivity (Theorem 1 & Obs#3):**
 - Weight decay helps balance the online and predictor network weights, ensuring that the online network (whose representations are used downstream) learns from the predictor's dynamics.
 - It forces the dynamics onto an invariant parabola (`s_j ≈ α_p^-1 p_j^2`), making the learned features (`s_j`) robustly sensitive to augmentation statistics (`σ^2`), regardless of initialization.
 - However, excessively strong weight decay can cause collapse (Obs#5), while its absence allows initial conditions to permanently influence features (Fig. 3).
4. **Relative Predictor Learning Rate (α_p):**
 - A larger `α_p` reduces the basin of attraction for the collapsed state, making it easier to avoid collapse (Obs#1). This explains why BYOL/SimSiam often benefit from a faster-learning predictor.
 - Conversely, an *infinitely* large `α_p` is detrimental because it prevents the online network from growing alongside the predictor, leading to poor representations (Obs#2). This justifies empirical findings where an "always optimal" predictor performs poorly (Table 1).
 - A higher `α_p` can also enable symmetric predictors to work without EMA, as shown in experiments (Fig. 6).
5. **Exponential Moving Average (EMA, β) as an "Automatic Curriculum" (Obs#8):**
 - EMA acts as a crucial "curriculum" that gradually increases the learning target (`τ`) for the online and predictor networks. This starts with smaller, easier-to-achieve goals, ensuring stable growth of `p_j` and `s_j` while maintaining eigenspace alignment (Fig. 5).
 - Very slow EMA (small `β`) leads to slow training (Obs#9) and can expand the collapse basin (Obs#10).
 - EMA is particularly important for symmetric predictors, where its absence can lead to collapse if `α_p` is not high enough (Table 3).
6. **Eigenspace Alignment (Theorem 3):** The theory predicts that the eigenspaces of the predictor `W_p` and the online network's output correlation matrix `F` gradually align during training. This is empirically validated in STL-10 experiments (Fig. 2), providing a foundation for simplifying the high-dimensional dynamics.

#### 4.2. Algorithmic Innovation: DirectPred

- **Novelty:** Based on the theoretical insight into eigenspace alignment and the `s_j ≈ α_p^-1 p_j^2` relationship, the authors propose **DirectPred**. This method entirely bypasses gradient descent for the predictor `W_p`, instead directly computing its weights from the principal components of the online network's outputs.
- **Performance:** DirectPred achieves comparable or even superior performance to gradient-trained linear predictors.
 - On STL-10, it outperforms gradient-trained linear `W_p` by +2-3% (Table 6, 8).
 - On ImageNet (300 epochs), DirectPred (72.4% Top-1, 91.0% Top-5) performs comparably to BYOL's default 2-layer non-linear predictor (72.5% Top-1, 90.8% Top-5), and significantly outperforms a gradient-trained linear predictor by +2.5% Top-1 (Table 9). The gap is even wider in shorter training runs (+5% Top-1 at 60 epochs).
- **Advantages:** DirectPred avoids the complexities of predictor dynamics, initialization sensitivity, and potential local minima associated with gradient-based training. Combining it with occasional gradient updates (`freq > 1`) can further boost performance.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This paper represents a significant contribution to the field of self-supervised learning and deep learning theory, with both fundamental and practical implications.

1. **Fundamental Theoretical Understanding:** The most impactful contribution is providing the *first comprehensive theoretical explanation* for how non-contrastive SSL methods (BYOL, SimSiam) manage to avoid representational collapse without relying on negative pairs. This resolves a major theoretical puzzle that emerged from their empirical success, moving these methods from a "black box" phenomenon to a more interpretable scientific construct.
2. **Elucidation of Core Mechanisms:** The work clearly articulates the functional roles of previously mysterious architectural and training components. The stop-gradient is proven essential; the predictor is shown to be a "dynamical destabilizer" of collapse rather than just an expressive mapping; and EMA is revealed as an "automatic curriculum" for stable feature growth. These insights deepen our understanding of what makes these algorithms work.
3. **Guidance for Algorithm Design and Hyperparameter Tuning:** By precisely identifying the contributions of weight decay, predictor learning rates, and EMA, the theory provides principled guidance for designing new SSL algorithms and intelligently tuning hyperparameters. This moves beyond trial-and-error, potentially leading to more robust and efficient model development. For instance, the understanding of how `α_p` and `η` interact with EMA to avoid collapse can inform practical configuration choices.
4. **Algorithmic Innovation with DirectPred:** The development of **DirectPred** is a direct and impactful practical outcome of the theoretical analysis. An optimization-free predictor that matches or surpasses complex, gradient-trained alternatives simplifies the architecture, reduces computational overhead, and eliminates potential training instabilities. This demonstrates the power of theoretical insights to inspire simpler, yet more effective, algorithms. Its strong performance on ImageNet, even against BYOL's default non-linear predictor, highlights its practical utility.
5. **Bridging Theory and Practice:** This paper serves as an excellent example of how simplified theoretical models (deep linear networks) can yield profound, qualitatively correct insights into complex, real-world deep learning phenomena. It reinforces the value of theoretical work in guiding and accelerating empirical progress in AI.

In conclusion, "Understanding Self-Supervised Learning Dynamics without Contrastive Pairs" is a landmark paper that not only demystifies a crucial aspect of modern self-supervised learning but also provides a concrete, high-performing algorithmic alternative. It paves the way for future research into more interpretable, efficient, and robust self-supervised models.
