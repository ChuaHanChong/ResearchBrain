# Understanding self-supervised Learning Dynamics without Contrastive Pairs

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Facebook AI Research (FAIR) and Stanford University analytically explain why non-contrastive self-supervised learning (SSL) methods like BYOL and SimSiam avoid representational collapse by identifying the essential roles of stop-gradient, predictor dynamics, and Exponential Moving Average (EMA). Their work introduces DirectPred, an optimization-free predictor that achieves competitive performance, matching or surpassing gradient-trained linear and even non-linear predictors on ImageNet.

## Problem

- Non-contrastive self-supervised learning methods like BYOL and SimSiam achieve state-of-the-art results without negative pairs, yet the theoretical mechanisms preventing representational collapse remained unexplained.
- A lack of formal understanding existed for the specific roles of architectural components such as the stop-gradient operation, the learnable predictor, and the Exponential Moving Average (EMA) in these algorithms.
- The nonlinear learning dynamics of these complex deep learning systems were not analytically characterized, making principled design and hyperparameter tuning challenging.

## Method

- A simplified, bias-free ==linear BYOL== model was developed, using continuous-time gradient flow equations to enable analytical tractability.
- Key assumptions (proportional EMA, isotropic data, symmetric predictor) were introduced to decouple the high-dimensional dynamics into independent scalar dynamical systems.
- Fixed-point and stability analysis was conducted on these simplified systems to determine conditions for avoiding collapse and characterizing learned representations, validated with extensive empirical ablation studies.

## Results

- DirectPred, an optimization-free predictor based on theoretical insights into eigenspace alignment, achieved comparable or superior performance to gradient-trained linear predictors (+**2.5%** Top-1 on ImageNet at 300 epochs), and matched BYOL's default 2-layer non-linear predictor on ImageNet.
- The theory elucidated the specific roles of weight decay in balancing online and predictor networks and promoting feature sensitivity to augmentation statistics, while also explaining the optimal range for the relative predictor learning rate.
- Empirical evaluations on STL-10, CIFAR-10, and ImageNet consistently validated the theoretical predictions regarding the necessity of stop-gradient, the dynamics of EMA, and the influence of predictor learning rates.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The stop-gradient operation is analytically proven to be essential, as its removal leads to representational collapse in the linear model.
> - The predictor dynamically destabilizes the trivial collapsed solution, creating a stable manifold for non-collapsed, meaningful representations rather than simply mapping features.
> - Exponential Moving Average (EMA) acts as an 'automatic curriculum,' gradually increasing the learning target and ensuring stable feature growth while maintaining eigenspace alignment.
