# PeRL: Permutation-Enhanced Reinforcement Learning for Interleaved Vision-Language Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> PeRL enhances Vision-Language Models for complex multi-image reasoning by employing a permutation-enhanced reinforcement learning framework, explicitly mitigating positional bias. The approach achieves state-of-the-art results on several multi-image benchmarks, including 76.39 on Mantis-Eval and 58.53 on BLINK, while maintaining competitive performance on single-image tasks.

## Problem

- Existing Vision-Language Models (VLMs) often struggle with complex multi-image reasoning tasks, failing to understand relationships across multiple visual inputs like spatial or temporal connections.
- Current VLMs exhibit 'positional bias,' where their predictions are sensitive to the arbitrary order of input images, leading to inconsistent or incorrect responses despite unchanged underlying semantic content.
- Reinforcement learning (RL) approaches for enhancing VLM reasoning have primarily been confined to single-image contexts and do not adequately address the complexities of multi-image scenarios.

## Method

- **PeRL** employs a ==multi-stage data preprocessing== pipeline that filters and rephrases questions, balances sample difficulty using 'Rollout Filtering', and performs 'Semantic Variation Analysis' to handle image permutations.
- The approach utilizes a permutation-enhanced reinforcement learning framework built upon Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) to optimize the VLM's policy for correct responses.
- During training, PeRL generates diverse samples by randomly permuting image orders for multi-image inputs and adjusts corresponding answers to maintain semantic consistency, enforcing order-invariant learning during policy updates.

## Results

- PeRL achieves state-of-the-art performance on multi-image benchmarks, scoring **76.39** on Mantis-Eval, **58.53** on BLINK, and **54.23** on MMIU, substantially outperforming prior models.
- The model maintains competitive performance on general-purpose single-image mathematical reasoning tasks, with scores of **73.00** on MathVista and **49.56** on MathVerse.
- PeRL demonstrates robust generalization capabilities on out-of-domain multi-image mathematical reasoning benchmarks (Remi and MV-MATH), indicating its ability to implicitly learn inter-image relationships.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Permutation-enhanced GRPO significantly improves training stability and convergence compared to naive GRPO, forcing the model to learn more robust, order-invariant representations by exposing it to positionally varied cases.
> - A multi-stage data preprocessing pipeline, including 'Rollout Filtering' for difficulty balancing and 'Semantic Variation Analysis' for permutation-based semantic consistency, is crucial for efficient and effective reinforcement learning in multi-image settings.
> - Combining multi-image instruction examples with single-image RL examples during training yields synergistic benefits, enabling the model to learn both intra-image and inter-image reasoning capabilities effectively.

### 2. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The rapid advancements in Vision-Language Models (VLMs) have revolutionized various single-image tasks, enabling sophisticated understanding and reasoning capabilities. However, real-world scenarios often demand reasoning across *multiple* images, a capability that humans naturally possess but VLMs still largely struggle with. This paper situates itself within this crucial gap.

Historically, multimodal reasoning has seen efforts primarily focused on supervised fine-tuning (SFT) and dataset construction, including chain-of-thought (CoT) reasoning steps. The advent of interleaved image-text formats, as popularized by models like Flamingo and LLaVA-Interleave, provided a more flexible framework for combining visual and textual inputs dynamically. While these models demonstrated improved multi-image understanding, they still faced significant limitations, particularly in tasks requiring precise positional alignment, temporal sequencing, or holistic cross-image understanding. A recurring issue is "positional bias," where models' predictions are sensitive to the arbitrary order in which images are presented, despite the underlying semantic relationships remaining unchanged.

In parallel, reinforcement learning (RL) has recently emerged as a powerful paradigm for enhancing reasoning capabilities in large language models (LLMs), exemplified by models like DeepSeek-R1. This success has inspired a new wave of research applying RL to VLMs, showing promising results in improving multimodal reasoning, primarily within single-image contexts (e.g., MM-Eureka, R1-OneVision). These RL-augmented VLMs typically focus on improving reasoning through better reward signals or optimized policy learning.

PeRL bridges these two distinct research trajectories. It acknowledges the limitations of existing interleaved VLMs in multi-image scenarios and the current confinement of VLM-RL approaches to single-image tasks. By proposing an RL framework specifically tailored for *interleaved multi-image tasks*, PeRL aims to extend the reasoning benefits of RL to the more challenging multi-image domain, explicitly addressing the prevalent issue of positional bias. This positions PeRL at the intersection of advanced VLM architectures, sophisticated reinforcement learning techniques, and the critical need for robust multi-image understanding in real-world AI applications.

### 3. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a general and effective reinforcement learning approach that significantly enhances Vision-Language Models' capabilities for interleaved multimodal tasks, with a particular focus on multi-image positional reasoning.

The motivations driving this research stem from several observed limitations in current VLMs:

1. **Struggling with Multi-Image Reasoning:** Despite advancements in single-image VLM tasks, models often fail to generalize to more complex real-world scenarios that involve multiple images. This includes tasks requiring understanding relationships across images, such as 2D/3D spatial relationships, temporal reasoning, or high-level semantic connections.
2. **Positional Bias:** A critical challenge identified is the "positional bias" of existing VLMs. As demonstrated by examples in the paper, simply altering the order of input images can lead to incorrect or inconsistent predictions from both general-purpose and instruction-tuned VLMs. This indicates a failure in maintaining consistent and grounded reasoning across interleaved multi-image inputs. This bias prevents models from truly understanding the coreference of visual elements independent of their input sequence.
3. **Limitations of Existing RL-augmented VLMs:** While reinforcement learning has shown encouraging results in enhancing multimodal reasoning in VLMs (e.g., inspired by DeepSeek-R1), these approaches have largely been confined to single-image contexts. They fall short when applied to the complexities of multi-image scenarios that demand deep reasoning across varied spatial and semantic contexts.
4. **Inefficient RL Training:** The authors observed a significant imbalance in the difficulty levels of multi-image training samples, which can lead to inefficient and unstable reinforcement learning. This necessitates strategies to balance the training distribution and optimize learning efficiency.

In summary, the core motivation is to overcome the inherent "blind spots" of VLMs in multi-image reasoning, particularly their sensitivity to image order, by leveraging the reasoning enhancement capabilities of reinforcement learning within a specially designed framework.

### 4. Methodology and Approach

PeRL introduces a comprehensive approach that combines a sophisticated data preprocessing pipeline with a permutation-enhanced reinforcement learning framework, built upon Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO).

#### 4.1. Problem Formulation and Analysis

The task is defined as predicting an output answer `y` given an input instance `x = (I, Q)`, where `Q` is a textual query and `I = {i_j}` is a set of associated images. The input sequence interleaves text tokens with image representations using a `<image>` placeholder. The authors highlight two key challenges:

- **Positional Bias:** Existing VLMs suffer performance degradation when the order of input images is altered, struggling with accurate image coreference. PeRL aims to mitigate this by encouraging robust understanding of positional relationships.
- **Difficulty Imbalance:** Analysis of initial training data (Mantis-Instruct) revealed an extremely imbalanced distribution of sample difficulty, which could hinder efficient RL training.

#### 4.2. Data Preprocessing Pipeline

To address the observed challenges, PeRL employs a multi-stage data preprocessing pipeline (illustrated in Fig. 3a) to curate a higher-quality training subset:

1. **Rule-based Filter:** Initial screening of the original Mantis-Instruct dataset based on question types, number of images, and other attributes.
2. **Format Rephrasing:** GPT-4o is used to rephrase open-ended questions into a multiple-choice format, which is easier to verify and process in an RL setting.
3. **Rollout Filtering:** To tackle the difficulty imbalance, Qwen2.5-VL-7B is used to perform 10 rollouts for each question. The average accuracy of these responses is calculated as a "difficulty score." Questions are then filtered to create a more balanced difficulty distribution, shifting the mean accuracy from 0.78 to 0.38 (as shown in Fig. 3b). This ensures that the RL focuses on more challenging and informative samples.
4. **Semantic Variation Analysis:** This crucial step addresses positional bias. For each input-output pair `(x, y)`, a random permutation `σ` is applied to reorder the image set, creating a new input `ˆx`. GPT-4o then assesses whether the semantics of `ˆx` are preserved relative to `x`. If the semantics change due to permutation (e.g., "image 1" now refers to a different visual element), the corresponding answer `y` is transformed (e.g., `Λ(y, σ)`) to maintain consistency. This process generates diverse training samples with varied positional relationships while ensuring semantic correctness.

#### 4.3. Permutation-Enhanced Reinforcement Learning (PeRL)

PeRL utilizes a reinforcement learning framework, specifically Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), to optimize the policy model `π_θ(y|x)` for correct responses.

##### 4.3.1. Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)

GRPO estimates an "advantage score" for each response based on the reward normalization within a group of responses for a specific query. The advantage `A_i` measures how much better a particular response `o_i` is compared to the average response in its group. This advantage is then used to update the policy, encouraging behaviors that lead to higher rewards. The loss function includes a KL divergence term `D_KL(π_θ || π_ref)` to prevent the policy from deviating too far from a reference policy (e.g., the initial SFT model).

##### 4.3.2. Permutation GRPO

PeRL's key novelty lies in how it augments the GRPO process, especially during the rollout phase (Fig. 4):

1. **Diversified Sample Generation:** For each original multi-image input sample `(x_1, y_1)`, PeRL generates `n_s` additional samples `{(x_2, y_2)..., (x_{n_s+1}, y_{n_s+1})}` by randomly permuting the image order in `x` and modifying the corresponding answer `y` to maintain semantic consistency. A linearly decaying factor `α_t` controls the probability of applying image swapping, allowing the model to gradually adapt.
2. **Diverse Rollouts:** During the rollout process, the policy model generates responses not just for the original input but also for the diversified, permuted samples. This forces the model to encounter varied visual token orders for the same underlying semantic content.
3. **Group-wise Advantage Calculation:** All `n_s + 1` groups of responses (original + permuted) are merged. The baseline `̄R` and final advantage `A_i,k` are calculated across this entire collection of rollouts. This ensures that the policy update considers the performance on permuted inputs, explicitly encouraging the model to learn order-invariant representations and mitigate positional bias. Unlike previous approaches that might only use augmented inputs for reward calculation but not policy update, PeRL calculates policy loss conditioned on these augmented inputs, which is crucial as the answer itself might be re-indexed after permutation.

This approach ensures that the model is trained to be robust to image order variations and develops a more general understanding of multi-image relationships. The rollout filtering mechanism, in conjunction with permutation, ensures that the learning process is efficient and focuses on trajectories that contribute most to optimal behavior.

### 5. Main Findings and Results

PeRL's effectiveness is demonstrated through extensive experiments on a diverse set of multi-image and single-image benchmarks.

#### 5.1. State-of-the-Art Performance

- **Superior Multi-Image Performance:** PeRL consistently achieves state-of-the-art results on widely used multi-image benchmarks for natural scene understanding:
 - **Mantis-Eval:** 76.39 (SOTA)
 - **BLINK:** 58.53 (SOTA)
 - **MMIU:** 54.23 (SOTA)
 It substantially outperforms previous best models, including instruction-tuned models like Qwen2.5-VL-7B and R1-related RL models like Noisy-K12 and MM-Eureka.
- **Competitive Single-Image Performance:** PeRL maintains competitive performance on general-purpose single-image mathematical reasoning tasks:
 - **MathVista:** 73.00 (comparable to SOTA specialized models)
 - **MathVerse:** 49.56 (comparable to SOTA specialized models)
 - **MathVision:** 28.26 (solid performance)
- **Robust Generalization on Out-of-Domain Multi-Image Math:** Despite being trained primarily on single-image mathematical reasoning and multi-image natural scene understanding datasets, PeRL exhibits strong generalization capabilities on out-of-domain multi-image mathematical reasoning benchmarks (Remi and MV-MATH). This highlights its ability to implicitly learn inter-image relationships and reasoning, beyond explicitly seen data distributions.
- **Overall Effectiveness:** The comprehensive evaluation across eight metrics yields an average score of 51.13, underscoring the effectiveness of the proposed training framework.

#### 5.2. Ablation Studies

The paper includes crucial ablation studies to validate the contribution of PeRL's core components:

- **Permutation vs. Naive GRPO:** Permutation GRPO significantly surpasses naive GRPO (without permutation) in terms of training stability and convergence. This is attributed to permutation exposing the model to harder, positionally varied cases, forcing it to learn more robust, order-invariant representations. Permutation GRPO also maintains higher rollout diversity throughout training, indicating that the policy explores a wider range of effective strategies. The advantage distribution also shows more informative signals for permutation GRPO compared to naive GRPO's sharply peaked distribution around zero.
- **Permutation Intensity (`n_s`):** Increasing the number of permuted samples per original input (`n_s`) from 0 to 2 generally improves performance on most benchmarks, particularly multi-image tasks like Mantis-Eval and BLINK. This confirms that exposure to varied input orders promotes positional invariance. However, `n_s = 2` showed marginal degradation on certain reasoning-centric benchmarks and incurred higher training costs. The optimal setting was found to be `n_s = 1` (one random permutation per sample) which yields a good balance between performance gain and computational efficiency.
- **Single vs. Multi-Image Training Data:** Training exclusively on single-image datasets severely limits generalization on multi-image benchmarks, and vice-versa. This confirms the necessity of diversified training data across both single and multi-image scenarios. PeRL's strategy of combining 22K multi-image instruction examples and 36K single-image RL examples proved most effective, demonstrating the synergistic benefit of learning both intra-image and inter-image reasoning capabilities.

### 6. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of PeRL represents a significant step forward in the field of multimodal AI, particularly for real-world applications that require sophisticated reasoning across multiple visual inputs.

1. **Advancing Multi-Image Reasoning:** PeRL directly addresses a critical and under-explored area: robust multi-image reasoning. By achieving state-of-the-art results on challenging multi-image benchmarks, it pushes the boundaries of what VLMs can achieve in understanding complex visual narratives and relationships. This is crucial for applications like visual storytelling, medical image analysis, multi-frame video understanding, and complex instruction following where multiple images provide sequential or comparative context.
2. **Mitigating Positional Bias:** The proposed permutation strategy is a novel and effective method for tackling the pervasive "positional bias" in VLMs. By systematically exposing the model to varied image orders and adapting the ground truth accordingly, PeRL forces the model to learn truly order-invariant representations and coreference resolution. This improves the reliability and consistency of VLM outputs in dynamic, real-world scenarios.
3. **Extending RL to Complex Multimodal Tasks:** PeRL successfully demonstrates the power of reinforcement learning beyond single-image contexts, showing its applicability for enhancing reasoning in complex, interleaved multimodal tasks. This opens new avenues for applying RL-based fine-tuning to a broader range of multimodal challenges, potentially inspiring similar approaches for video understanding, multi-modal dialogue systems, and robotics.
4. **Data Efficiency and Robustness:** The method achieves superior performance while utilizing substantially less training data compared to some traditional supervised fine-tuning methods. The multi-stage data preprocessing pipeline, especially the rollout filtering for difficulty balancing and semantic variation analysis, highlights a sophisticated approach to data curation that is vital for effective RL. This makes the training process more efficient and the resulting model more robust.
5. **Generalization Capabilities:** The model's strong generalization to out-of-domain multi-image mathematical reasoning tasks, despite not being explicitly trained on them, is a testament to its implicit learning of both inter-image relationships and broader reasoning capabilities. This suggests that PeRL's learned strategies are highly transferable across different multi-image contexts.

Overall, PeRL's contribution is multifaceted: it provides a concrete solution to a prevalent VLM limitation, pioneers the application of RL for multi-image reasoning, and offers valuable insights into effective data curation and training strategies for multimodal models. Its impact is likely to be seen in the development of more intelligent and reliable AI systems that can truly "see" and "reason" across complex visual information.

### 7. Conclusion and Future Work

The paper successfully introduces PeRL, a permutation-enhanced reinforcement learning approach that significantly boosts the multimodal reasoning capabilities of Vision-Language Models for interleaved multi-image tasks. By addressing the critical challenges of spatial and positional reasoning through a multi-stage data processing pipeline and an innovative permutation-based GRPO framework, PeRL mitigates positional bias and improves learning efficiency. The experimental results unequivocally demonstrate that Qwen-PeRL achieves state-of-the-art performance on multi-image benchmarks while maintaining competitive results on single-image tasks, showcasing its robustness and generalization abilities.

As for future work, the authors propose to investigate **adaptive permutation strategies**. This implies dynamically adjusting the image order based on factors like task difficulty or model uncertainty. Such an adaptive approach could further optimize the exploration-exploitation trade-off, potentially leading to even greater efficiency and performance gains by focusing permutation efforts where they are most beneficial for learning. This aligns with the broader trend in AI research towards more intelligent and adaptive learning mechanisms, moving beyond static data augmentation to dynamic, model-aware data perturbation.
