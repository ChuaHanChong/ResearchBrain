# Sparse but Critical: A Token-Level Analysis of Distributional Shifts in RLVR Fine-Tuning of LLMs

> [!summary] Summary
> The Qwen Pilot Team, Alibaba Group, investigated how RLVR fine-tuning enhances LLM reasoning by analyzing token-level predictive distributions. They found that RLVR induces sparse, targeted distributional shifts critical for performance gains, primarily by reallocating probability mass among existing plausible tokens rather than introducing new ones.

## Problem

- The precise token-level mechanisms by which RLVR improves LLM reasoning capabilities were not well understood, despite empirical success.
- Existing evaluations of RL fine-tuning often relied on aggregate, response-level metrics, which provided limited insight into the fine-grained behavioral changes.
- A comprehensive distributional view was lacking on how RLVR reshapes next-token predictive distributions and the functional importance of these changes for reasoning gains.

## Method

- Researchers employed ==Jensen-Shannon== (JS) divergence to systematically quantify and characterize token-level distributional differences between base and RL-fine-tuned models across various LLMs and RL methods.
- Cross-sampling interventions were designed to functionally assess the importance of these token-level shifts on sequence-level reasoning performance, by selectively sampling tokens from either the base or RL policy.
- Fine-grained mechanics of shifts were analyzed through top-k overlap, rank reordering, and the behavior of low-probability tokens to understand how probability mass is reallocated.

## Results

- RLVR-induced shifts were found to be significantly sparser and more targeted than those from Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT), which produced denser and more widespread changes.
- Injecting fewer than **4%** (approx. 40 tokens) of RL-sampled tokens into base-model generations was sufficient to close the performance gap from **8%** to **25%** for SimpleRL on AIME 2024, while replacing roughly **5%** (fewer than 30) high-divergence tokens in RL-generated sequences caused accuracy to collapse back from **25%** to **8%**, demonstrating the critical impact of sparse changes.
- Exploratory interventions using divergence-weighted advantages showed that modulating token-level learning signals based on divergence magnitude can improve performance over baseline DAPO on multiple benchmarks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - RLVR induces highly sparse and targeted token-level distributional shifts, with over 80% of token positions exhibiting near-zero JS divergence, suggesting a refinement process focused on a small, critical subset of decisions.
> - Reasoning trajectories are highly sensitive to these sparse shifts; injecting a small fraction of RL-sampled tokens can significantly recover performance, while replacing critical RL tokens with base-model alternatives causes performance degradation.
> - RLVR primarily reallocates probability mass and reorders existing plausible tokens (e.g., over 80% of RL's top-1 tokens were already in the base model's top-3) rather than introducing entirely new or initially highly improbable tokens.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of large language models (LLMs) has seen substantial performance improvements, particularly in reasoning tasks, through reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR). Methods such as Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) and Decoupled Clip and Dynamic sAmpling Policy Optimization (DAPO) have demonstrated empirical success on challenging benchmarks. However, the precise mechanisms through which RLVR modifies LLM behavior at a granular level remain an active area of investigation. Prior research primarily evaluates RL fine-tuning using aggregate response-level metrics (e.g., accuracy, reward, response length), which provide a high-level view but offer limited insight into the underlying behavioral changes.

Recent efforts have begun to analyze RL fine-tuning from token-level perspectives, examining aspects like entropy, uncertainty, KL divergence, and rank-shift statistics. These studies have highlighted the role of high-entropy tokens and exploration dynamics, and noted that RL-induced changes can modify sparse subsets of tokens or reasoning patterns. Despite these advancements, a comprehensive distributional view of how RLVR reshapes next-token predictive distributions, how probability mass is reallocated, and the functional importance of these changes for downstream reasoning gains has been largely unaddressed. This paper aims to fill this gap by providing a systematic empirical study that connects token-level distributional shifts directly to sequence-level reasoning performance, thereby offering a more fine-grained understanding of RLVR as a targeted refinement process rather than a global policy shift. It complements existing work that focuses on parameter-level changes or broad behavioral shifts by concentrating on the token-level predictive distributions.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to elucidate the token-level mechanisms underlying the performance improvements observed in Large Language Models (LLMs) after Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) fine-tuning. Specifically, the authors aim to answer the fundamental question: "how does RLVR reshape the token-level predictive distributions of a base model, and which of these changes actually drive downstream reasoning gains?"

This overarching objective is motivated by several key considerations:
- **Lack of Granular Understanding:** Despite the empirical success of RLVR in enhancing LLM reasoning capabilities, the precise way it modifies model behavior at the token level is not well understood. Existing evaluations often rely on coarse, aggregate metrics that obscure the fine-grained mechanics of improvement.
- **Disentangling RLVR's Impact:** There is a need to move beyond high-level observations and identify *which* specific token-level changes are responsible for the observed reasoning gains. This involves characterizing the structure, evolution, and functional importance of these shifts.
- **Comparison with Other Fine-tuning Methods:** It is important to determine whether the observed token-level shifts are unique to RLVR or a general characteristic of any fine-tuning process. Comparing RLVR with supervised fine-tuning (SFT) can provide insights into the distinct mechanisms employed by RL.
- **Inform Future RL Objectives:** A detailed understanding of how RLVR achieves its effects could inform the development of more effective, interpretable, and controllable RL objectives for LLM post-training. If performance gains are concentrated in sparse, critical changes, then training signals might be modulated to target these areas more effectively.
- **Exploring Probabilistic Redistribution:** The research seeks to determine whether RLVR primarily introduces new token candidates or reallocates probability mass among existing plausible options. This clarifies the nature of the model's "refinement" versus "invention" capabilities under RL.

To address these motivations, the study proposes three main analyses:
1. **Token-level characterization of distributional shifts:** Quantifying and structuring the differences between base and RL model token distributions.
2. **Impact of token-level shifts on sequence-level performance:** Investigating the functional importance of these shifts through interventional experiments.
3. **Fine-grained mechanics of shifts:** Analyzing how probabilities are reallocated and how changes evolve over training.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research employs a systematic empirical study organized around three main analyses to investigate token-level distributional shifts induced by RLVR.

#### 3.1. Token Distribution Analysis between Base and Fine-tuned Models

- **Quantifying Distributional Differences:** The study primarily uses Jensen–Shannon (JS) divergence to measure the difference between the conditional next-token distributions of the base model ($\pi_{\text{base}}$) and the RL-fine-tuned model ($\pi_{\text{RL}}$) for identical sequence contexts. JS divergence was selected due to its symmetry, boundedness, and well-defined nature even with measures lacking absolute continuity (e.g., truncated distributions). Divergences are typically computed on top-p truncated distributions to reflect the actual sampling regime, with robustness checks performed using full distributions and different top-p values.
- **Models and Datasets:** The primary analysis uses Qwen2.5-32B as the base model, with RLVR variants trained using DAPO and GRPO (paired with SimpleRL). Evaluations are conducted on AIME 2024 and AIME 2025, sampling 32 responses per problem. The analysis is extended to Qwen2.5-Math-7B (with DAPO variants), Qwen3-8B-Base (with DAPO), and Mistral-Small-24B (with SimpleRL), as well as GPQA and models' respective fine-tuning datasets, to assess generalizability.
- **Characterization Metrics:**
 - **Sparsity:** Log-scaled histograms and percentile curves of JS divergence are used to quantify how broadly distributional shifts are distributed.
 - **Positional Concentration:** Mean and median JS divergence, along with percentile bands, are plotted against normalized token position (token index divided by sequence length) to identify where shifts tend to occur within a sequence.
 - **Divergence–Entropy Relationship:** Token-level entropy ($H_\pi(x_{<t})$) is computed for both base and RL models. Token positions are grouped into low- and high-divergence bins, and the entropy distributions within these bins are compared to understand if RLVR primarily affects high-entropy predictions or can modify confident (low-entropy) outputs.
 - **Semantic Identity of Divergent Tokens:** Word clouds are generated for tokens sampled from high- and low-divergence distributions to qualitatively assess the types of tokens affected.
 - **Comparison with Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT):** A controlled comparison is made between Qwen2.5-32B fine-tuned with SFT and RLVR (DAPO) using the same JS divergence metrics, positional analysis, and divergence-entropy relationship to determine if sparsity is unique to RLVR.

#### 3.2. Cross-Sampling Interventions

- **Framework:** A mixed policy $\pi_{\text{mix}}^{(\text{prim,int})}$ is defined, where $\pi_{\text{prim}}$ is the primary policy and $\pi_{\text{int}}$ is the intervention policy. A switching rule $S(x_{<t})$ determines whether the next token is sampled from $\pi_{\text{prim}}$ or $\pi_{\text{int}}$, based on the token-level JS divergence exceeding a fixed threshold $\varepsilon$.
- **Forward Cross-Sampling:** $\pi_{\text{prim}} = \pi_{\text{base}}$ and $\pi_{\text{int}} = \pi_{\text{RL}}$. This assesses whether injecting a small number of RL-sampled tokens into base-model generations can recover RL performance.
- **Reverse Cross-Sampling:** $\pi_{\text{prim}} = \pi_{\text{RL}}$ and $\pi_{\text{int}} = \pi_{\text{base}}$. This evaluates how quickly RL performance degrades when its critical token choices are replaced by base-model choices.
- **Evaluation with Intervention Budgets:** Accuracy is measured as the maximum number of cross-sampling interventions (k) increases. This explores whether early, limited interventions can steer trajectories to influence downstream performance.

#### 3.3. Fine-Grained Mechanics of Distribution Shifts

- **Top-k Overlap and Rank Reordering:** For high-divergence positions, the study examines the overlap between the base and RL models' top-k candidate token sets and how the relative ranking of shared candidates shifts. This determines if RLVR introduces new plausible tokens or re-prioritizes existing ones.
- **Low-Probability Token Behavior (Selection vs. Invention):** For high-divergence positions, the probability of the RL model's top-1 token under the base distribution is analyzed. This investigates whether RLVR promotes tokens that were initially highly unlikely or amplifies already plausible but underweighted alternatives.
- **Evolution Across Training:** Using intermediate checkpoints from DAPO training on Qwen2.5-Math-7B, the evolution of JS divergence and the Jaccard overlap of divergent-token sets are tracked to understand how distributional shifts develop over time.

#### 3.4. Exploratory Investigation: Divergence-Weighted Advantages

- **Motivation:** Based on the finding that sparse tokens drive improvements, the authors explore whether modulating token-level learning signals according to divergence magnitude can enhance training.
- **Formulation:** A divergence-weighted advantage $\tilde{A}_t = w_t \cdot \hat{A}_t$ is defined, where $\hat{A}_t$ is the standard group-normalized advantage and $w_t$ is a per-token weight based on KL divergence with respect to the old policy ($\text{KL}_{\text{old}}$).
- **Weighting Scheme:** A sigmoid weighting scheme, $w_t = 1 + s(\sigma(\alpha \cdot \text{KL}_t) - 1/2)$, is used. $\alpha > 0$ emphasizes high-divergence tokens (high-KL boost), while $\alpha < 0$ emphasizes low-divergence tokens (low-KL boost).
- **Evaluation:** Experiments are conducted using the DAPO training recipe on Qwen2.5-Math-7B, evaluating on AIME 2024, AIME 2025, and AMC.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research provides a detailed, token-level analysis of RLVR fine-tuning, revealing several key findings regarding distributional shifts, their functional importance, and underlying mechanisms.

#### 4.1. Structure of Token-Level Distributional Shifts

- **Sparsity:** RLVR induces highly sparse token-level distribution shifts. The vast majority of token positions (often >80% for DAPO, >98% for SimpleRL) exhibit near-zero Jensen–Shannon (JS) divergence between base and RL models. This indicates that RLVR modifies only a small fraction of token distributions, even without explicit KL regularization.
- **Method-Dependent Spread:** Less constrained methods, such as clip-higher DAPO, result in a broader distribution of divergence, allowing for more extensive exploratory updates. More constrained methods like SimpleRL or DAPO with a lower clip setting lead to more concentrated changes on fewer token distributions.
- **Positional Concentration:** Across sequences, average divergence is consistently higher near the beginning of responses, decreases through the middle, and shows a modest increase towards the end. This suggests an emphasis on early, high-level branching decisions and later adjustments to formatting or termination. However, individual sequences exhibit variability, with high divergence occurring sporadically.
- **Divergence–Entropy Relationship:** Low-divergence token distributions are generally low-entropy, indicating that confident predictions by the base model are often preserved. High-divergence contexts, particularly with DAPO, can span a broad entropy range, suggesting that DAPO can modify both initially high- and low-entropy base-model predictions. SimpleRL, in contrast, concentrates divergence more strongly in higher base entropy regions, reflecting a more conservative update strategy.
- **Context Dependence:** The semantic identity of a token alone does not determine its divergence behavior. The same token can be sampled from both low and high divergence distributions depending on its context within the sequence.
- **Contrast with SFT:** Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) produces substantially denser and more globally distributed shifts compared to RLVR, exhibiting a larger high-divergence set and broader divergence distribution. This suggests that the observed sparsity is a distinctive property of RLVR, not a generic feature of fine-tuning.

#### 4.2. Functional Importance of Divergent Distributions via Cross-Sampling

- **Forward Cross-Sampling:** Injecting a small fraction of RL-sampled tokens into base-model generations progressively recovers RL-level accuracy. For SimpleRL, fewer than 4% (around 40) RL-sampled tokens on AIME 2024 were sufficient to close the performance gap from the base (8%) to RL (25%). For DAPO, about 7.8% (280) effective tokens boosted accuracy from 8% to over 44%. In some cases, this mixed policy can even outperform the standalone RL policy.
- **Reverse Cross-Sampling:** Reverting a small fraction of RL-sampled tokens to base-sampled tokens in RL-generated sequences causes performance to degrade towards base-model levels, and sometimes below. For SimpleRL, replacing approximately 5% (fewer than 30) high-divergence tokens on AIME 2024 caused accuracy to collapse from 25% to 8%. For DAPO, roughly 10% (173) effective reversions reduced accuracy from 44% to near base levels.
- **Progressive Trajectory Steering:** Performance changes smoothly and monotonically with the intervention budget. Even a small number of early interventions can significantly impact downstream reasoning trajectories, demonstrating that RL’s gains are sparsely distributed across multiple decision points and accumulate sequentially.
- **Sensitivity to Locally Interchangeable Tokens:** The substituted tokens are often plausible and semantically reasonable from a human perspective, yet they lead to significant performance differences. This indicates that reasoning trajectories are sensitive to distributionally distinct, even if seemingly equivalent, local token choices.

#### 4.3. Fine-Grained Mechanics of Distribution Shifts

- **Shared Candidate Sets:** Even at high-divergence positions, base and RL models maintain high overlap in their top-k token candidate sets (e.g., >80% for k $\geq$ 2 under SimpleRL). This suggests that RLVR primarily reallocates probability mass within an existing set of plausible candidates rather than introducing entirely new tokens.
- **Re-ranking Over Replacement:** RLVR mainly reorders and prioritizes already plausible tokens. For example, over 80% of RL’s top-1 tokens at high-divergence positions were already within the base model’s top-3 candidates for DAPO, and over 90% for SimpleRL.
- **Selection Rather Than Invention:** RLVR rarely promotes tokens that were highly unlikely under the base model. For DAPO, only about 5% of RL top-1 tokens (at high-divergence positions) had a base probability below 0.01. Clip-higher DAPO allows for more frequent promotion of low-probability tokens, consistent with its more exploratory nature, but these instances remain rare overall.
- **Evolution Across Training:** JS divergence increases monotonically throughout training, with higher percentiles growing faster, indicating that distributional changes become increasingly concentrated in a small subset of tokens over time. The set of divergent tokens also becomes more stable and focused later in training.

#### 4.4. Divergence-Weighted Advantages

- **Performance Impact:** Exploratory diagnostic interventions using divergence-weighted advantages show that both "low-KL boost" (emphasizing less changed distributions) and "high-KL boost" (emphasizing substantially changing distributions) can yield improvements over the baseline DAPO model on various benchmarks (AIME 2024, AIME 2025, AMC). This suggests that modulating token-level learning signals based on divergence magnitude can positively influence training dynamics.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to a fine-grained, token-level understanding of how Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) enhances the reasoning capabilities of Large Language Models (LLMs). The findings challenge the notion that RLVR operates through diffuse, global changes across the model's output distribution. Instead, the study establishes that RLVR induces **sparse, targeted, and structured distributional shifts** that are functionally critical for performance gains.

The key significance and potential impact are:

- **Clarification of RLVR Mechanics:** The work clarifies that RLVR primarily functions as a "targeted refinement process" rather than a "global policy shift." It highlights that LLMs improve by modifying a small, critical subset of token-level decisions that steer generation trajectories, while maintaining largely similar behavior across the majority of token choices. This provides a more precise and interpretable view of how RLVR operates compared to prior aggregate analyses.
- **Insights into Model Behavior:** The cross-sampling experiments demonstrate the high sensitivity of reasoning trajectories to a sparse set of token choices. Even locally plausible or semantically interchangeable token substitutions can lead to significant downstream performance degradation or recovery. This reveals a limited invariance in LLM generation to small, distributional differences, which can have outsized impact on complex reasoning paths.
- **Differentiation from SFT:** By comparing with Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT), the research shows that the sparsity of distributional shifts is a distinctive characteristic of RLVR. SFT, in contrast, leads to denser and more widespread changes, suggesting fundamental differences in how these fine-tuning paradigms modify model behavior.
- **Guidance for Future RL Objectives:** The finding that performance hinges on sparse, high-impact tokens, combined with the exploratory success of divergence-weighted advantages, suggests new avenues for designing more efficient and effective RL objectives. Future research could focus on mechanisms that explicitly identify, prioritize, or stabilize updates to these critical token positions, potentially leading to more targeted and resource-efficient training.
- **Enhanced Interpretability and Controllability:** A token-level understanding can improve the interpretability of LLM behavior post-training. By identifying the specific points of divergence and their functional importance, researchers may gain better insights into *why* an LLM succeeds or fails on a given task, and potentially develop methods for more granular control over its reasoning process.
- **Broader Generalizability:** The consistency of findings across various LLM architectures (Qwen, Mistral), RLVR methods (DAPO, SimpleRL, GRPO), datasets (AIME, GPQA), and hyperparameter settings (clip-higher variants) suggests that these sparse, targeted modifications might be a general property of how RLVR improves LLM reasoning.

In summary, this research advances the fundamental understanding of RLVR, moving beyond aggregate metrics to illuminate the precise token-level changes that drive LLM performance. This granular perspective offers a foundation for developing more sophisticated RL algorithms and diagnostics, fostering greater interpretability, and ultimately enabling more effective post-training strategies for LLMs.
