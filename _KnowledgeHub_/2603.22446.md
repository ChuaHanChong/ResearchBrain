# Sparse but Critical: A Token-Level Analysis of Distributional Shifts in RLVR Fine-Tuning of LLMs

> [!summary] Summary
> The Qwen Pilot Team, Alibaba Group, investigated how RLVR fine-tuning enhances LLM reasoning by analyzing token-level predictive distributions. They found that RLVR induces sparse, targeted distributional shifts critical for performance gains, primarily by reallocating probability mass among existing plausible tokens rather than introducing new ones.

## Problem

- The precise token-level mechanisms by which RLVR improves LLM reasoning capabilities were not well understood, despite empirical success.
- Existing evaluations of RL fine-tuning often relied on aggregate, response-level metrics, which provided limited insight into the fine-grained behavioral changes.
- A comprehensive distributional view was lacking on how RLVR reshapes next-token predictive distributions and the functional importance of these changes for reasoning gains.

## Method

- Researchers employed ==Jensen-Shannon== (JS) divergence to systematically quantify and characterize token-level distributional differences between base and RL-fine-tuned models across various LLMs and RL methods.
- Cross-sampling interventions were designed to functionally assess the importance of these token-level shifts on sequence-level reasoning performance, by selectively sampling tokens from either the base or RL policy.
- Fine-grained mechanics of shifts were analyzed through top-k overlap, rank reordering, and the behavior of low-probability tokens to understand how probability mass is reallocated.

## Results

- RLVR-induced shifts were found to be significantly sparser and more targeted than those from Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT), which produced denser and more widespread changes.
- Injecting fewer than **4%** (approx. 40 tokens) of RL-sampled tokens into base-model generations was sufficient to close the performance gap from **8%** to **25%** for SimpleRL on AIME 2024.
- Replacing roughly **5%** (fewer than 30) high-divergence tokens in RL-generated sequences on AIME 2024 caused accuracy to collapse from **25%** to **8%**, demonstrating the critical impact of sparse changes.
- Exploratory interventions using divergence-weighted advantages showed that modulating token-level learning signals based on divergence magnitude can improve performance over baseline DAPO on multiple benchmarks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - RLVR induces highly sparse and targeted token-level distributional shifts, with over 80% of token positions exhibiting near-zero JS divergence, suggesting a refinement process focused on a small, critical subset of decisions.
> - Reasoning trajectories are highly sensitive to these sparse shifts; injecting a small fraction of RL-sampled tokens can significantly recover performance, while replacing critical RL tokens with base-model alternatives causes performance degradation.
> - RLVR primarily reallocates probability mass and reorders existing plausible tokens (e.g., over 80% of RL's top-1 tokens were already in the base model's top-3) rather than introducing entirely new or initially highly improbable tokens.
