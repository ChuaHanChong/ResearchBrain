# RealSimLoop: Online Real-to-Sim Adaptation via Differentiable Reduced-Order Simulation with Vision Feedback

> [!summary] Summary
> RealSimLoop is an online real-to-sim framework that keeps a differentiable soft-body simulator synchronized with real deformable objects using multi-view images. It runs differentiable reduced-order neural-subspace simulation coupled to 3D Gaussian Splatting differentiable rendering, and optimizes time-varying material parameters over a sliding window via an adjoint method. It reaches quasi-real-time speed, up to 7.69x faster than full-space, with 4.6mm reconstruction error.

## Problem

- Conventional real-to-sim fitting is offline: one fixed parameter set per pre-recorded sequence, so open-loop drift and time-varying stiffness go uncorrected over long horizons.
- Vision-only reconstruction recovers time-varying surface geometry but stays disconnected from physics, yielding no internal stress fields, force boundary conditions, or material properties for downstream use.
- Full-space differentiable simulation and MPM-based identification cost too much per frame for online loops, and idealized constitutive laws mismatch real hyperelastic materials at large strain.

## Method

- **RealSimLoop** runs a ==neural-subspace reduced-order differentiable simulation==, an autoencoder mapping with ==stable neo-Hookean energy== and implicit integration in JAX, replacing full-space solves and cutting forward and backward per-frame cost.
- A ==sliding-window real-to-sim objective== compares ==3D Gaussian Splatting== differentiable renderings against multi-view captures over the last w frames, updating time-varying material parameters instead of fitting one global set.
- ==Adjoint backpropagation== through the reduced physics residual, plus barycentric mesh-to-Gaussian binding, chains pixel gradients through rendering and the subspace Jacobian onto Young's modulus and Poisson ratio.

## Results

- The reduced-order solver gives **2.52-7.69x** per-frame speedup (bar case **3.30s** to **0.53s**), with average reconstruction error **4.6mm**, roughly **2.3%** of model size.
- It beats offline baselines: PSNR **25.91** versus **24.42** on dual-arm bar manipulation, **22.6** versus **18.0** on the bouncing ball, and **3-5x** lower L2 error under material aging.
- Vision feedback halves marker-based error (**0.026m** versus **0.052m**); ablations plateau near subspace dimension **100** at **0.025m** error and window size **5**, and Yeoh underperforms neo-Hookean.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Continuously closing the loop beats fitting better physics offline: feedback absorbs model-reduction error and unmodeled dynamics that any fixed parameter set cannot.
> - A more expressive constitutive model can hurt, since its larger parameter space destabilizes identification from limited observations; keep the identified parameterization low-dimensional.
> - Dense pixel supervision outperforms sparse markers, but reduced-order subspaces fail to converge on highly dynamic contact, forcing fallback to full-space simulation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses the challenge of bridging the "reality gap" between physical systems and their digital simulations, a foundational aspect of digital twin technologies, robotic manipulation, and structural analysis. Traditional approaches to real-to-sim adaptation for deformable objects often rely on offline parameter estimation. These methods collect pre-recorded datasets to fit simulation parameters, which can introduce modeling errors, particularly for complex materials or large deformations. Additionally, their open-loop nature can lead to drift over time and a lack of adaptability to time-varying material properties or sudden environmental changes.

Recent advancements in data-driven and vision-based approaches have enabled online scene reconstruction, offering detailed time-varying surface geometry from video inputs. However, these methods typically lack the ability to infer hidden physical quantities such as internal deformation, stress fields, interaction forces, and material properties, which are crucial for many downstream applications.

The field of differentiable simulation has emerged as a key area, enabling gradient computation for simulation outputs with respect to physical parameters and initial states. This capability facilitates gradient-based optimization for inverse problems in various domains, including soft body simulation. Simultaneously, reduced-order simulation methods aim to accelerate physics computations by mapping high-dimensional systems to lower-dimensional subspaces, a critical step towards real-time performance. While early linear reduced-order methods have limitations for nonlinear deformations, neural networks have enabled more effective nonlinear mappings.

This paper integrates these distinct research areas—online real-to-sim adaptation, differentiable simulation, reduced-order modeling, and vision-based scene reconstruction—to address their individual limitations and collectively enhance the fidelity and applicability of deformable object simulation. It aims to develop a system that not only aligns simulation with real-world observations but also does so online and efficiently, while providing access to unobservable physical quantities.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an online real-to-sim adaptation framework that continuously synchronizes a differentiable physical simulator with real-world vision feedback. This framework, termed RealSimLoop, is motivated by several challenges inherent in simulating deformable objects and maintaining alignment with physical reality:

- **Bridging the Reality Gap for Deformable Objects:** Real-world observations of deformable objects are often sparse or limited to surface-level geometry, while many applications (e.g., robotic manipulation, structural health monitoring) require knowledge of hidden physical quantities like internal deformation, stress fields, and interaction forces. Physics-based simulation can infer these quantities, but accurately aligning the simulation with the real world is crucial.
- **Addressing Limitations of Offline Adaptation:** Conventional offline real-to-sim methods, which fit a single set of parameters to pre-recorded data, suffer from several drawbacks. They can incur significant modeling errors, especially for hyperelastic materials or large deformations, as idealized constitutive laws may not perfectly match real-world complexities. Furthermore, their open-loop nature makes them susceptible to drift and unable to adapt to time-varying material properties or dynamic environmental changes.
- **Overcoming Computational Costs:** Full-space optimization in physics-based simulation is computationally expensive, hindering online adaptation. Repeatedly simulating and differentiating physical dynamics in real-time or quasi-real-time environments presents a significant barrier.
- **Leveraging High-Density Visual Data for Physical Inference:** While data-driven vision-based approaches can reconstruct time-varying surface geometry, they often lack the direct connection to underlying physical parameters necessary for predicting internal stress fields, force boundary conditions, or material properties. The motivation is to integrate high-fidelity visual data to inform and refine physical simulation.
- **Enabling Robust Online Adaptation:** The objective is to create a system capable of continuous adaptation to evolving real-world behavior. This requires a mechanism to track dynamic material property variations and compensate for accumulated sim-to-real discrepancies arising from model reduction or unmodeled dynamics.

To achieve these objectives, the authors aim to integrate efficient scene representation (e.g., 3D Gaussian Splatting) with a differentiable physical simulator, leveraging a neural reduced-order model to accelerate computation. The system is designed to use a sliding-window objective function for continuous online adaptation, enabling it to refine physical parameters such as material stiffness directly from pixel data.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The RealSimLoop framework proposes a quasi-real-time online real-to-sim adaptation pipeline that integrates differentiable reduced-order simulation with vision feedback.

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation of Deformable Simulation

The system models a deformable object discretized by a tetrahedral mesh. Its configuration at time $t$ is represented by a vector $\mathbf{x}_t \in \mathbb{R}^{3n}$ of vertex positions. Forward simulation uses implicit time integration, where the system's residual $\mathbf{r}(\mathbf{x}_t, \mathbf{y}_t, \boldsymbol{\theta}) = 0$ is solved, with $\mathbf{M}$ as the mass matrix, $\boldsymbol{\theta} = [E, \nu]$ as material parameters (Young's modulus and Poisson's ratio), and $\mathbf{f}$ representing total forces. The overall real-to-sim adaptation problem is formulated as a constrained optimization problem: $\operatorname{argmin}_{\boldsymbol{\theta}} \mathcal{L}_{\text{r2s}}(\mathbf{X})$ subject to $\mathbf{R}(\mathbf{X}, \boldsymbol{\theta}) = 0$, where $\mathbf{X}$ represents the stacked degrees of freedom (DoF) over time and $\mathbf{R}$ the stacked residual functions.

#### 3.2. Online Adaptation Strategy with Window-based Objective

To counter error accumulation and adapt to time-varying properties, the paper introduces a sliding-window optimization approach. Instead of fitting parameters over an entire pre-recorded sequence (offline), the system minimizes a vision-based loss accumulated over a current time window $\{T_e - w + 1, \dots, T_e\}$, where $T_e$ is the current timestep and $w$ is the window size. This objective function is defined as:
$\mathcal{L}_{\text{online}}^{\text{r2s-vision}} = \sum_{t \in \{T_e - w + 1, \dots, T_e\}} \sum_{c=1}^{K} |\mathbf{R}(\mathbf{G}_t) - \mathbf{I}_c^{\text{real}}|$
where $\mathbf{R}(\cdot)$ is a differentiable renderer, $\mathbf{G}_t$ represents Gaussian primitives at time $t$, and $\mathbf{I}_c^{\text{real}}$ are real-world images from $K$ cameras. This allows for dynamic updates of material properties $\boldsymbol{\theta}$.

#### 3.3. Differentiable Reduced-Order Simulation

To accelerate the simulation, a neural reduced-order model is employed. A subspace mapping function $\phi: \mathbb{R}^r \rightarrow \mathbb{R}^{3n}$ maps a low-dimensional subspace state $\mathbf{z} \in \mathbb{R}^r$ to the high-dimensional full space state $\mathbf{x} = \phi(\mathbf{z})$. The time integration of the forward simulation is reformulated within this subspace, where the residual equations are projected onto the tangent space of the neural subspace mapping using its Jacobian $\mathbf{J}(\mathbf{z}_t) = \frac{\partial\phi}{\partial\mathbf{z}}$. This results in a system $\mathbf{J}(\mathbf{z}_t)^\top \mathbf{r}(\mathbf{z}_t, \mathbf{y}_t, \boldsymbol{\theta}) = 0$, significantly reducing the computational complexity of solving for $\mathbf{z}_t$.

#### 3.4. GS-Based Vision-Geometry Mapping

The framework utilizes 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) for efficient geometry-to-image mapping and differentiable rendering. Gaussian primitives $\mathbf{G}_t = \{\mathbf{x}_g^t, \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_g^t, \mathbf{c}_g^t, \sigma_g^t\}$ are defined, where each Gaussian's position $\mathbf{x}_g^t$ is expressed as a barycentric interpolation of its enclosing tetrahedron's vertices. This mapping $\mathbf{x}_g^t = B(\phi(\mathbf{z}_t))$ ensures spatial alignment between rendering primitives and the deformed mesh, and importantly, is differentiable, allowing for gradient propagation from images to the simulation state.

#### 3.5. Gradient-based Optimization via Adjoint Method

The constrained optimization problem (Equation 8) is solved using the adjoint method, which efficiently computes gradients through the physics simulation.
- **Vision-to-State Gradient Backpropagation:** Gradients of the loss with respect to the reduced simulation state $\mathbf{z}_t$ are computed by applying the chain rule through the differentiable renderer and the subspace mapping: $\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial\mathbf{z}_t} = \frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial\mathbf{I}_t} \frac{\partial\mathbf{I}_t}{\partial\mathbf{G}_t} \frac{\partial\mathbf{G}_t}{\partial\mathbf{x}_t} \cdot \mathbf{J}(\mathbf{z}_t)$.
- **Adjoint Method for Reduced-Order Physics:** To propagate these state gradients to the material parameters $\boldsymbol{\theta}$, adjoint variables $\boldsymbol{\lambda}_t$ are introduced. These are computed by solving a linear system backward in time (Equation 11), involving the transpose of the reduced tangent stiffness matrix. Once $\boldsymbol{\lambda}_t$ are obtained for the window, the total gradients with respect to material parameters are accumulated: $\frac{d\mathcal{L}}{d\boldsymbol{\theta}} = \sum_{t=T_e-w+1}^{T_e} \boldsymbol{\lambda}_t^\top \left( \frac{\partial\mathbf{h}_t}{\partial\boldsymbol{\theta}} \right)$, where $\mathbf{h}_t$ represents the physics residual in the reduced subspace. This allows gradient descent to iteratively refine $\boldsymbol{\theta}$ at the end of each sliding window.

The implementation combines JAX for differentiable reduced-order simulation (using the stable neo-Hookean material model) and PyTorch for the 3DGS module. SAM 2 is used for processing vision data. Subspace training utilizes an AutoEncoder-based architecture, generating diverse deformation data by combining various material parameters and interaction sequences.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The RealSimLoop framework demonstrates its capabilities across various physical scenarios, outperforming conventional offline methods and marker-based approaches in terms of accuracy, efficiency, and adaptability.

#### 4.1. Computational Efficiency

The reduced-order model significantly accelerates computation, achieving speedups ranging from 2.52× to 7.69× compared to full-space methods. For a material bar (10K DoFs), the online adaptation takes 0.53 s per frame in subspace vs. 3.30 s in full-space (6.23× speedup). For an Eiffel tower model (12K DoFs), it achieves 0.18 s vs. 1.36 s (7.56× speedup). This efficiency enables quasi-real-time online operation, despite the computationally intensive backward pass.

#### 4.2. Accuracy and Robustness

- **Real-to-Sim Gap Reduction:** In a dual-robot arm manipulation task of a silicon rubber bar, the method reduces the reconstruction error to an average of 4.6 mm (2.3% of model size), a substantial reduction compared to 7.1 mm for full-space methods.
- **Time-Varying Properties:** The online adaptation effectively tracks time-varying material properties. For a multi-material Eiffel Tower model (Dragon Skin 30A and 10A), the system accurately identifies multiple independent elasticity parameters. In a temperature-varying gingerbread-man structure (PCL truss embedded in silicone), the method continuously tracks the increasing stiffness of the PCL structure as temperature decreases, capturing corresponding changes in push force and estimated Young's modulus.
- **Comparison with Offline Methods:** RealSimLoop consistently outperforms offline methods. In a virtual comparison where Young's modulus changes dynamically, offline methods fail to capture material variation and cannot solve for deformation. Even with fixed material properties, the online method achieves higher PSNR (25.91 vs. 24.42 for the bar manipulation), indicating better alignment over long-horizon interactions. For material aging experiments, the online method accurately tracks increasing stiffness, while offline methods converge to an almost constant, inaccurate stiffness. The online method consistently yields 3–5× lower per-frame $L_2$ errors.
- **Highly Dynamic Scenarios:** For high-speed dynamics (e.g., a golf ball bouncing), the online method better matches ground-truth transient deformation, significantly increasing PSNR from 18.0 to 22.6 for keyframes compared to offline baselines.

#### 4.3. Downstream Applications

The adapted simulation enables several downstream applications:
- **Robotic Manipulation:** The control strategy optimized in simulation, after online real-to-sim adaptation, can be directly applied to real robotic systems without additional tuning, achieving close matches with target shapes (Chamfer Distances reported).
- **3D Stress Field Reconstruction:** Internal physical states, such as Von Mises stress, are accurately reconstructed and visualized in real-time through synthesized novel views, providing physical insights beyond surface geometry.
- **External Force Prediction:** For cable-driven structures, the system predicts cable forces by computing internal nodal forces from the reconstructed stress field, which closely matches forces measured by physical sensors.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies

- **Subspace Dimension ($r$):** Increasing the subspace dimension from 30 to 90 reduces $L_2$ reconstruction error, but the error plateaus after $r=90$, while update time increases linearly. $r=100$ was chosen as a trade-off.
- **Temporal Window Size ($w$):** Increasing window size generally improves accuracy by incorporating more temporal constraints but also linearly increases update time. Improvements become marginal after $w=5$ for the dinosaur model test.
- **Number of Camera Views ($c$):** Increasing the number of cameras initially reduces reconstruction error by providing more constraints. However, beyond a certain point (e.g., $c=8$ for the dinosaur model), accuracy improvement becomes marginal, while computational cost continues to increase linearly.

#### 4.5. Constitutive Models and Feedback Types

- **Material Models:** A comparison between the Neo-Hookean and Yeoh models showed that while both required similar online update times, the simpler Neo-Hookean model yielded more robust adaptation and lower reconstruction errors under large deformations than the higher-order Yeoh model. This suggests that a more complex model does not inherently guarantee better online adaptation due to potential optimization instability in higher-dimensional parameter spaces.
- **Feedback Modalities:** Vision-based feedback demonstrated superior performance compared to conventional marker-based inputs. For the dinosaur example, vision-based feedback reduced average vertex $L_2$ error from 0.052 m to 0.026 m and showed faster material parameter convergence due to denser spatial information.

#### 4.6. Limitations

The paper acknowledges several limitations:
- **Per-element Material Distributions:** While handling multi-material setups with defined regions, extending to per-element material distributions would significantly increase the parameter space and optimization complexity.
- **Reduced-Order Model Convergence:** Reduced-order models may encounter convergence issues in highly dynamic systems or exhibit limited extrapolation capabilities for unseen deformation modes or out-of-range material parameters. For such cases (e.g., high-speed ball bouncing), the system reverts to full-space simulation. Even then, the online framework maintains higher accuracy than offline full-space methods.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The RealSimLoop framework represents a significant advancement in the field of deformable object simulation and real-to-sim adaptation. By integrating differentiable reduced-order simulation with vision feedback and a sliding-window optimization approach, the research addresses critical bottlenecks and limitations of existing methods.

**Key Contributions and Impact:**
- **Enabling Online Adaptation:** The framework enables continuous, online synchronization between physical reality and digital simulation, which is crucial for applications requiring real-time responsiveness and adaptability to dynamic environments or evolving material properties. This is a departure from conventional offline methods, which are prone to drift and modeling errors over time.
- **Computational Efficiency:** The use of neural reduced-order models drastically reduces the computational cost of differentiable simulation, making online gradient-based adaptation computationally feasible. This efficiency allows for a more direct integration into real-time control loops for robotics and dynamic systems.
- **Physical Consistency from Vision Data:** By linking high-fidelity vision data (via 3D Gaussian Splatting and differentiable rendering) directly to underlying physical parameters and states, the method bridges the gap between purely geometric vision-based reconstruction and physics-based inference. This enables the recovery of hidden physical quantities, which is not possible with geometry-only approaches.
- **Enhanced Fidelity and Robustness:** The online, closed-loop adaptation mechanism significantly reduces the sim-to-real gap, leading to more accurate simulations that better match observed physical behavior, even under large deformations or time-varying conditions. This improved fidelity can lead to more reliable predictions and control strategies.
- **Broader Applicability for Digital Twins:** The capabilities demonstrated, such as robotic manipulation, stress field reconstruction, and force prediction, open new avenues for building robust digital twins. These digital twins can not only replicate the observable aspects of physical systems but also infer and predict their internal physical states and dynamic interactions. This has implications for design, monitoring, and control in various engineering and robotics domains.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The work establishes a robust framework that can be extended to address current limitations, such as more complex material distributions or adaptive refinement strategies for highly dynamic scenarios, potentially by integrating unsupervised learning for improved extrapolation capabilities of reduced-order models.

In summary, RealSimLoop contributes a practical and efficient solution for creating physically consistent digital representations of deformable objects that dynamically adapt to real-world observations, thereby advancing the development of intelligent robotic systems and predictive digital twin technologies.
