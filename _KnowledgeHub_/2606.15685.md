# Learning New Tasks via Reusable Skills: Skill-Compositional Experts for Embodied Continual Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> The Skill-Compositional Experts (SCE) framework enables embodied agents to continually learn new manipulation tasks by automatically decomposing demonstrations into reusable skills and composing them via a dual-expert action decoder. This approach achieves a final success rate of 83.3% with zero negative backward transfer in real-world manipulation tasks while significantly mitigating feature drift.

## Problem

- Catastrophic forgetting is amplified in Embodied Continual Learning (ECL) due to closed-loop control, where feature drift propagates, degrading old task performance.
- Existing skill-level ECL methods often lack explicit modeling for the organization and composition of reusable skills, leading to inconsistent reuse and exacerbated feature drift.
- Robots require both stable execution of individual skills and coherent transitions between skills to reliably perform complex tasks composed of multiple primitives.

## Method

- ==Compositional Skill Grounding== (CSG) automatically decomposes raw task demonstrations into reusable skill primitives based on robot-state dynamics and VLM-grounded induction, organizing them into a dynamic skill base.
- Dual Execution-and-Transition Experts (DETE) is integrated into the VLA action decoder, featuring an Execution Expert Branch for skill-specific actions and a Transition Expert Branch for cross-skill compositional knowledge.
- An Adaptive Fusion Module dynamically combines the outputs of the dual experts, emphasizing skill execution or transition modeling based on the current context to ensure stable behavior and coherent skill switching.

## Results

- SCE achieved superior performance on LIBERO benchmarks, with a Final Success Rate of **83.4%** on LIBERO-Goal and **73.4%** on LIBERO-Long, demonstrating substantially lower catastrophic forgetting (NBT).
- In real-world manipulation tasks, SCE attained the highest Final Success Rate of **83.3%** and notably zero negative backward transfer (NBT=**0.0**), indicating no forgetting of prior tasks.
- Analysis revealed SCE significantly mitigated feature drift, especially in action-side features, leading to substantially smaller simulator-state deviation over closed-loop steps compared to baselines.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly modeling both skill execution and cross-skill transitions within a dual-expert framework, complemented by adaptive fusion, effectively addresses the stability-plasticity trade-off in embodied continual learning.
> - Structured decomposition of tasks into reusable skills via Compositional Skill Grounding is crucial for organizing a dynamic skill base and enabling robust skill composition, which significantly improves continual learning performance.
> - Mitigating feature drift, particularly at the action-feature level, is critical for stabilizing closed-loop robot control and preventing the degradation of previously learned behaviors in interactive environments.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses challenges within Embodied Continual Learning (ECL), a subfield of Continual Learning (CL). Continual learning aims to enable artificial intelligence systems to sequentially acquire new knowledge and tasks without forgetting previously learned information. While traditional CL methods have made progress in settings like image classification, they often operate under the assumption that data inputs are fixed and independent of the model's actions.

Embodied Continual Learning extends CL to robotic systems that interact with dynamic environments under closed-loop control. In this setting, the robot's actions influence subsequent observations and environmental states, leading to a more complex form of catastrophic forgetting. Errors can propagate through sequential decision-making, causing a degradation of previously learned behaviors. This "feature drift," where feature representations shift towards new tasks, is amplified by closed-loop control, making ECL inherently more challenging than conventional CL.

Existing ECL methods can be broadly categorized into task-level and skill-level approaches. Task-level methods preserve knowledge by treating each task as a distinct unit, often employing techniques like replaying previous task demonstrations or routing inputs to task-specific experts. While effective for reducing interference, these methods may not fully exploit shared behavioral patterns across tasks.

Skill-level methods, in contrast, focus on learning and organizing finer-grained, reusable skill primitives that can be shared across multiple tasks. Prior work in this area has explored skill discovery, skill evolution, or skill retrieval. However, a limitation identified by the authors is that these methods often concentrate on the *acquisition* of skills without explicitly modeling their *organization* and *composition* for coherent execution of new, complex tasks. This lack of structured skill organization can lead to inconsistent skill reuse and exacerbate feature drift under closed-loop control.

This paper positions itself by addressing this identified gap. It proposes a compositional view of ECL, where tasks are understood as compositions of reusable skills. The work aims to provide a structured framework for skill reuse that not only learns skills but also explicitly organizes them for coherent task execution, thereby mitigating feature drift and enhancing the stability-plasticity trade-off in embodied agents.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enable embodied robots to continually acquire new manipulation tasks while effectively retaining previously learned behaviors, particularly under the demanding conditions of closed-loop control where catastrophic forgetting is amplified.

The motivation stems from several key challenges in Embodied Continual Learning (ECL):
1. **Amplified Catastrophic Forgetting:** Unlike conventional continual learning, ECL systems operate in interactive environments where observations are coupled with actions. During continual learning, the feature representations of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models can drift towards new tasks. Under closed-loop control, this accumulated feature drift propagates through sequential decision-making, leading to a degradation of the execution of previously learned tasks.
2. **Lack of Structured Skill Reuse:** While skill-level approaches in ECL recognize the importance of reusable behavioral primitives, existing methods primarily focus on skill discovery, evolution, or retrieval. They often do not explicitly model how these skills should be *organized* and *composed* to execute new tasks coherently. This absence of structured skill composition can result in inconsistent skill reuse and further contribute to feature drift, hindering the robot's ability to generalize and perform reliably over time.
3. **Need for Stable Skill Execution and Coherent Transitions:** For a robot to perform complex tasks by composing skills, it requires both stable execution of individual skills and smooth, logical transitions between different skills. Existing VLA models may struggle to maintain this balance across continually evolving task streams.

To address these motivations, the specific objectives of the proposed Skill-Compositional Experts (SCE) framework are:
- To develop a mechanism, Compositional Skill Grounding (CSG), that can decompose raw task demonstrations into reusable skill primitives and organize them into a dynamic skill base.
- To design a novel architecture, Dual Execution-and-Transition Experts (DETE), that augments a VLA action decoder. This architecture should include:
 - An Execution Expert Branch to model skill-specific action patterns, ensuring stable execution of individual reusable skills.
 - A Transition Expert Branch to capture cross-skill compositional knowledge, facilitating coherent switching and transitions between skills during task execution.
 - An Adaptive Fusion Module to dynamically combine the outputs of these two branches, enabling flexible emphasis on skill execution or transition modeling as required by the current context.
- To demonstrate that this skill-compositional approach can consistently improve retention of old tasks and enhance overall task performance in embodied continual learning settings, both in simulation and real-world manipulation tasks.
- To verify, through empirical analysis, that the proposed method effectively alleviates feature drift, particularly at the action-feature levels, and reduces its negative impact on closed-loop behavioral performance.

By achieving these objectives, the research aims to provide a more robust and adaptable framework for embodied continual learning, moving towards robots that can learn and perform a growing repertoire of complex manipulation tasks reliably.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, Skill-Compositional Experts (SCE), is designed for embodied continual learning by constructing a skill base and facilitating skill composition across tasks. It comprises two main components: Compositional Skill Grounding (CSG) and Dual Execution-and-Transition Experts (DETE). The SCE framework builds upon a pre-trained Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model, integrating the DETE module into its action decoder.

**Problem Definition:**
The problem is defined as a VLA model sequentially acquiring new manipulation tasks from a stream of `T` stages, `D = {D_1, D_2..., D_T}`. At each stage `t`, only `D_t` is accessible. Each sample `x_i = (o_i, l_i, r_i, s_i, A_i)` consists of visual observation `o_i`, language instruction `l_i`, robot state `r_i`, associated skill annotation `s_i`, and an `H`-step action chunk `A_i`. The model is trained sequentially, and after stage `t`, it is evaluated on all tasks encountered up to `T_1:t`.

**Overall Architecture:**
The core model takes `(o, l, r)` as input to predict actions. A pre-trained vision-language encoder `Φ` processes `o` and `l` into a visual-language token `z`. A robot-state projection layer `Ψ` processes `r` into a state token `e`. These are concatenated to form the conditional token `c = [z; e]`. The action decoder `Θ`, augmented with DETE, then predicts the `H`-step continuous action chunk `ˆA` from `c`. Skill annotations `s` provide auxiliary supervision for organizing the decoder around reusable skills.

**Compositional Skill Grounding (CSG):**
CSG is responsible for decomposing task demonstrations into reusable skills and organizing them into a skill base `B`. This process occurs in two phases:

1. **Phase 1: State-Aware Task Decomposition:**
 - For a raw task demonstration `X = (O, R, l)` of length `F`, temporal boundaries are identified using robot-state dynamics.
 - **Rule 1:** A boundary is marked when the gripper state changes (e.g., open to close).
 - **Rule 2:** A boundary is marked when the end-effector motion trend changes abruptly.
 - These rules partition the demonstration into `M` contiguous temporal segments ` {X^(m)}_M_m=1`. Each segment contains its own observation, robot-state subsequence, and the original language instruction.

2. **Phase 2: VLM-Grounded Skill Induction:**
 - Each temporal segment `X^(m)` is processed sequentially. A Vision-Language Model (VLM) grounds the segment's observation `O^(m)` and instruction `l` against the current skill base `B`.
 - The VLM proposes a candidate skill `ˆs^(m)`.
 - This candidate is then used to retrieve a matched skill from `B`. If no match exists, `ˆs^(m)` is added to `B` as a new reusable skill. Otherwise, the matched skill from `B` is selected.
 - The assigned skill for segment `X^(m)` is `s^(m)`.
 - After processing all segments, the skill base `B` is updated, and segment-level skill assignments `{(X^(m), s^(m))}` are converted into sample-level skill annotations `s_i` for each timestep `i` within a segment. This process updates `B` across the demonstration stream.

**Dual Execution-and-Transition Experts (DETE):**
DETE is a dual-expert design integrated in parallel with the VLA action decoder, providing skill-specific execution patterns and cross-skill transition structures.

1. **Skill-Space Perception:**
 - DETE derives a `K`-dimensional skill distribution `p ∈ R^K` (where `K = |B|`) by mapping the conditional token `c` to the skill space `V` via `p = softmax(MLP(c))`.
 - During training, `p` is supervised by the one-hot encoded skill annotation `s` using a cross-entropy loss: `L_skill = CE(p, s)`. At inference, `p` is generated from the model's inputs.

2. **Execution Expert Branch (EEB):**
 - This branch focuses on modeling skill-specific behaviors. It consists of `K` bottleneck adapters, `E = {E_k}^K_k=1`, with each `E_k` associated with a skill in `B`.
 - A Skill Execution Router selects the most relevant expert based on the current skill distribution: `ˆk = argmax_k p_k`.
 - The output `∆y_exe` is produced by the selected expert: `∆y_exe = E_ˆk(y)`, where `y` is the feature entering DETE. This design localizes updates to the relevant expert, mitigating interference.

3. **Transition Expert Branch (TEB):**
 - This branch models common knowledge across skills, facilitating transitions. It comprises `N` bottleneck adapters, `G = {G_j}^N_j=1`.
 - A Transition-aware Router maps the conditional token `c` to a routing-feature space and injects the skill distribution `p` to obtain a transition-aware routing feature `h = MLP(c) + W_p * p`.
 - Mixture weights `u ∈ R^N` over `G` are computed: `u = softmax(W_u * h)`.
 - The aggregated output `∆y_tr` is a weighted sum of expert outputs: `∆y_tr = Σ^N_j=1 u_j G_j(y)`.

4. **Adaptive Fusion Module:**
 - This module dynamically combines the outputs of the EEB and TEB. It computes an adaptive balancing coefficient `α ∈` from the transition-aware routing feature `h`: `α = σ(W_α * h)`.
 - The combined DETE output `∆y = α∆y_exe + (1 − α)∆y_tr`. When `p` is concentrated on one skill, `α` emphasizes `∆y_exe`. Around skill boundaries, `p` variation shifts `h`, causing `α` to adjust fusion towards `∆y_tr` for coherent transitions.

**Optimization Objective:**
The model is trained using a combined loss function: `L = L_flow + λL_skill`, where `L_flow` is the flow-matching loss from the pre-trained VLA policy, `L_skill` is the skill supervision loss from Eq. 2, and `λ` is a hyperparameter for balancing.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments were conducted on both simulated LIBERO benchmarks (LIBERO-Goal and LIBERO-Long) and real-world manipulation tasks, employing a standard continual learning protocol across sequential stages. Performance was evaluated using four standard continual learning metrics: Forward Transfer (FWT), Negative Backward Transfer (NBT), Area Under the Curve (AUC), and Final Success Rate (Final SR).

**Simulation Experiments (LIBERO-Goal and LIBERO-Long):**
- **Overall Performance:** SCE demonstrated superior performance across key metrics. On LIBERO-Goal, SCE achieved a Final SR of 83.4, AUC of 82.5, and NBT of 4.3. On LIBERO-Long, it achieved a Final SR of 73.4, AUC of 70.0, and NBT of 0.5.
- **Comparison to Baselines:**
 - Compared to a strong modular baseline, Task-MoE, SCE significantly improved Final SR from 64.8 to 83.4 on LIBERO-Goal and from 57.8 to 73.4 on LIBERO-Long.
 - SCE exhibited substantially lower catastrophic forgetting (NBT) than all baselines, including Task-MoE (e.g., 4.3 vs. 15.8 on LIBERO-Goal).
 - While maintaining competitive FWT, SCE consistently achieved the best NBT, AUC, and Final SR, indicating a robust stability-plasticity trade-off for skill-compositional ECL.

**Real-World Experiments:**
- **Robustness:** Experiments on a 7-DoF robot arm (Mercury B1) across three sequential manipulation tasks (Box-to-Board, Board-to-Bowl, Box-to-Bowl) confirmed the effectiveness of SCE in physical environments.
- **Strongest Performance:** SCE achieved the highest performance across all four continual learning metrics in real-world scenarios, with a Final SR of 83.3, AUC of 82.8, and notably, an NBT of 0.0, indicating no forgetting of previously learned tasks.
- **Comparison:** Compared to Task-MoE, SCE improved Final SR from 56.7 to 83.3, demonstrating its ability to generalize and retain knowledge more effectively in physical manipulation.

**Analysis of Feature Drift Impact Under Closed-Loop Control:**
- **Mitigation of Drift:** Analysis of VLM feature and action-feature drift on old tasks showed that SCE significantly mitigated feature drift after the full continual learning stream. Sequential (fine-tuning without replay) exhibited 1.4x larger VLM Feature drift, but 5.7x larger Immediate Action Feature drift and 6.9x larger Horizon Action Feature drift compared to SCE. This indicates that SCE is particularly effective in stabilizing action-side features critical for robot control.
- **Reduced Behavioral Impact:** Shared-anchor rollout analysis, where policies started from identical successful old-task states, revealed that SCE maintained a substantially smaller simulator-state deviation over closed-loop steps compared to Sequential, which showed a rapidly growing state gap. This suggests that SCE's feature stabilization directly translates to more stable and consistent execution of old behaviors under closed-loop control.

**Ablation Experiments:**
- **Importance of CSG:** Removing Compositional Skill Grounding (SCE w/o CSG) led to a degradation in performance (e.g., AUC decreased from 82.5 to 73.8, Final SR from 83.4 to 70.0). This demonstrates that explicit skill organization via CSG is crucial for effective skill composition and overall continual learning performance.
- **Necessity of Dual Experts:**
 - Removing the Transition Expert Branch (SCE w/o TEB) resulted in lower AUC and Final SR, highlighting the importance of modeling cross-skill transitions for coherent skill composition.
 - Removing the Execution Expert Branch (SCE w/o EEB) also yielded performance below the full SCE model across FWT, AUC, and Final SR, indicating the necessity of skill-specific execution modeling.
 - These results suggest that the dual-expert design with both execution and transition modeling is complementary and essential for robust skill composition.
- **Adaptive Fusion Effectiveness:** Analysis of the adaptive balancing coefficient `α` showed that it decreased by an average of 16.2% around transition boundaries, while the normalized contribution of the Transition Expert Branch increased in these windows. This confirms that the Adaptive Fusion Module dynamically emphasizes the Transition Expert Branch during skill changes, consistent with its intended role in promoting coherent cross-skill transitions.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a framework that contributes to the field of embodied continual learning by addressing the critical challenge of structured skill reuse and mitigating catastrophic forgetting amplified by closed-loop control.

The significance and potential impact of this work include:

1. **Enhanced Robot Adaptability and Robustness:** By enabling robots to continually acquire new tasks while effectively preserving previously learned behaviors, SCE advances the development of more adaptable and robust autonomous systems. This is particularly crucial for real-world deployments where robots must operate in dynamic, evolving environments and learn new capabilities over their operational lifespan.
2. **Mitigation of Amplified Catastrophic Forgetting:** The work directly tackles the problem of amplified catastrophic forgetting in ECL, a more severe challenge than in conventional CL. The demonstrated reduction in feature drift, especially at the action-feature level, and the maintenance of smaller simulator-state deviations under closed-loop control are crucial for stable, long-term robot operation.
3. **Structured Approach to Skill Reuse:** The introduction of Compositional Skill Grounding (CSG) provides a principled method for decomposing tasks into reusable skills and organizing them into a dynamic skill base. This structured approach to skill management contrasts with prior methods that focused on skill discovery or retrieval without explicit compositional modeling, offering a more systematic way to leverage skill knowledge.
4. **Novel Dual-Expert Architecture:** The Dual Execution-and-Transition Experts (DETE) module, with its specialized branches for skill execution and cross-skill transitions, offers a novel architectural design. This design effectively balances the need for stable, skill-specific execution with the requirement for fluid, coherent transitions between skills, which is fundamental for complex task performance. The adaptive fusion mechanism further refines this balance contextually.
5. **Practical Applicability:** The consistent performance gains observed in both simulation and real-world manipulation tasks underscore the practical applicability of the SCE framework. Its success in physical robot trials suggests that the proposed mechanisms can translate effectively from theoretical models to tangible robotic capabilities.
6. **Foundation for Future Research:** The compositional view of ECL and the dual-expert design lay a foundation for future research directions. It opens avenues for exploring larger-scale and more open-ended skill repertoires, potentially leading to more generalized and human-like robotic learning capabilities. Further work could explore how these compositional skills can be learned with less supervision or adapted to new embodiments.

In summary, this research offers a substantial step towards more intelligent and autonomous robots that can continually learn and adapt to an expanding array of tasks, thereby bridging the gap between theoretical continual learning advancements and the practical requirements of real-world robotic systems.
