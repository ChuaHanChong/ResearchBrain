# Diffusion Alignment as Variational Expectation-Maximization

> [!summary] Summary
> A framework called Diffusion Alignment as Variational Expectation-Maximization (DAV) fine-tunes diffusion models to optimize for external rewards while preserving sample diversity and naturalness. The method achieved an aesthetic reward of 8.04 in text-to-image synthesis and 7.71 predicted activity in DNA sequence design, consistently mitigating reward over-optimization and mode collapse.

## Problem

- Existing reinforcement learning-based diffusion alignment methods often lead to "mode collapse," reducing the diversity of generated samples.
- Direct backpropagation approaches for diffusion fine-tuning can result in "reward over-optimization," producing unnatural or degenerate samples.
- Prior fine-tuning strategies are frequently limited to continuous diffusion models or require differentiable reward functions, restricting their general applicability.

## Method

- The research formulates diffusion alignment as an iterative variational ==Expectation-Maximization== (EM) process with alternating exploration (E-step) and amortization (M-step).
- In the E-step, a two-stage local search mechanism with importance sampling identifies diverse, high-reward trajectories from a reward-tilted variational posterior.
- The M-step updates diffusion model parameters by distilling the knowledge from collected trajectories via forward KL divergence minimization, optionally with a KL regularization term (DAV-KL).

## Results

- In text-to-image synthesis, DAV achieved an aesthetic reward of 8.04, outperforming DDPO (6.83) and DRaFT (7.22), and preserved superior sample diversity.
- For DNA sequence design, DAV balanced reward (7.71 Pred-Activity) with biological validity (ATAC-Acc **0.887**) and naturalness (3-mer Correlation **0.397**), surpassing existing fine-tuning baselines.
- The test-time "posterior" variants of DAV achieved the highest reward scores in both continuous (9.18 aesthetic) and discrete (9.24 Pred-Activity) domains, while maintaining substantial diversity.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Reformulating diffusion alignment as a variational Expectation-Maximization process can effectively mitigate both reward over-optimization and mode collapse.
> - An iterative cycle of explicit test-time search for diverse, high-reward samples (E-step) and subsequent distillation into the model (M-step) is crucial for balanced optimization.
> - The framework is versatile, operating on both continuous and discrete diffusion models and accommodating non-differentiable reward functions.
