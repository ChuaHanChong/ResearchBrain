# VideoAgent: Self-Improving Video Generation

> [!summary] Summary
> VideoAgent, developed by researchers from Google DeepMind and Stanford, introduces a self-improving framework for generating physically plausible video plans for embodied agents by integrating iterative refinement with Vision-Language Model feedback and online finetuning from successful robot executions, leading to an overall 53.7% task success rate in Meta-World robotic manipulation tasks.

## Problem

- Video generation models frequently produce unrealistic and hallucinated content, such as objects disappearing or violating physical laws.
- These visual flaws translate into low task success rates when generated video plans are used to control robots in embodied AI tasks.
- Current video generation approaches lack robust mechanisms to incorporate external feedback, limiting their ability to ground outputs in real-world physics and task requirements.

## Method

- A novel 'self-conditioning consistency' mechanism allows for iterative refinement of video plans during inference, predicting clean videos from previously generated ones.
- A pre-trained Vision-Language Model (VLM) acts as an intelligent critic to evaluate and select the most plausible refined video plan from multiple candidates.
- An online self-improvement loop collects successful robot trajectories to fine-tune the video generation and refinement models, continually improving performance from real-world execution.

## Results

- Achieved a 53.7% overall task success rate on Meta-World robotic manipulation, a substantial increase from the 19.6% baseline, using online self-improvement and replanning.
- Demonstrated improved performance in iTHOR object navigation, reaching a 34.2% overall success rate compared to the baseline's 31.3%.
- Produced real-world videos with a 22% higher human acceptance rate (64.0% vs. 42.0%) and superior factual and temporal consistency on the BridgeData V2 dataset.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Iterative refinement using self-conditioning consistency effectively reduces hallucinations and improves the physical plausibility of generated video plans.
> - Vision-Language Models can provide valuable feedback, both binary and descriptive, to guide the selection and training of video generation models.
> - Incorporating online feedback from real-world execution and finetuning on successful trajectories is a powerful method for self-improving embodied video generation.
