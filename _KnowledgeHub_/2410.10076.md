# VideoAgent: Self-Improving Video Generation

> [!summary] Summary
> VideoAgent, developed by researchers from Google DeepMind and Stanford, introduces a self-improving framework for generating physically plausible video plans for embodied agents by integrating iterative refinement with Vision-Language Model feedback and online finetuning from successful robot executions, leading to an overall 53.7% task success rate in Meta-World robotic manipulation tasks.

## Problem

- Video generation models frequently produce unrealistic and hallucinated content, such as objects disappearing or violating physical laws.
- These visual flaws translate into low task success rates when generated video plans are used to control robots in embodied AI tasks.
- Current video generation approaches lack robust mechanisms to incorporate external feedback, limiting their ability to ground outputs in real-world physics and task requirements.

## Method

- A novel 'self-conditioning consistency' mechanism allows for iterative refinement of video plans during inference, predicting clean videos from previously generated ones.
- A pre-trained Vision-Language Model (VLM) acts as an intelligent critic to evaluate and select the most plausible refined video plan from multiple candidates.
- An online self-improvement loop collects successful robot trajectories to fine-tune the video generation and refinement models, continually improving performance from real-world execution.

## Results

- Achieved a 53.7% overall task success rate on Meta-World robotic manipulation, a substantial increase from the 19.6% baseline, using online self-improvement and replanning.
- Demonstrated improved performance in iTHOR object navigation, reaching a 34.2% overall success rate compared to the baseline's 31.3%.
- Produced real-world videos with a 22% higher human acceptance rate (64.0% vs. 42.0%) and superior factual and temporal consistency on the BridgeData V2 dataset.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Iterative refinement using self-conditioning consistency effectively reduces hallucinations and improves the physical plausibility of generated video plans.
> - Vision-Language Models can provide valuable feedback, both binary and descriptive, to guide the selection and training of video generation models.
> - Incorporating online feedback from real-world execution and finetuning on successful trajectories is a powerful method for self-improving embodied video generation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated at the intersection of generative AI, particularly video generation, and embodied artificial intelligence (AI), specifically robotic control and planning. It addresses a critical challenge in extending the impressive capabilities of large generative models to real-world physical tasks.

In recent years, large text-to-video models, often based on diffusion architectures, have demonstrated remarkable progress in generating diverse and creative video content. Beyond mere content creation, researchers have begun exploring their utility as "simulators of the real-world" or even as "policies" that can directly guide agents in decision-making processes. This latter paradigm, "video generation as policy," involves generating a visual sequence (a "video plan") from an initial observation and a language instruction, which is then converted into robot control actions.

However, a significant bottleneck has emerged: the quality and physical plausibility of these generated video plans. Current models frequently suffer from "hallucinations" (e.g., objects appearing or disappearing, inconsistent visual elements) and violations of physical laws (e.g., a robot hand passing through an object). These inaccuracies severely limit their effectiveness for downstream control tasks, leading to low task success rates when executed by robots.

The conventional solution in large language models (LLMs) – scaling up dataset and model size – is considerably more challenging for video generation due to the labor-intensive nature of video data annotation and the lack of a universally converged architecture. Crucially, a major breakthrough in LLMs has been the integration of external feedback, such as human feedback (Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback, RLHF), to refine model outputs and align them with human preferences or real-world constraints. This paper identifies a similar need for video generation models, recognizing that grounding these models in the physical world requires incorporating real-world feedback.

VideoAgent directly responds to this need. It extends the "video as policy" paradigm by introducing a novel self-improvement loop. Instead of relying solely on offline training and open-loop generation, VideoAgent proposes to iteratively refine generated video plans using two types of external feedback: AI feedback from a vision-language model (VLM) and real-world execution feedback from the environment. This positions VideoAgent as a pioneering effort to bring the successful feedback-driven refinement strategies from the LLM domain to the challenging field of embodied video generation, aiming to close the reality gap and enhance the practical utility of generated visual plans for robotic control. It explicitly builds on the foundation of diffusion models and consistency models to enable efficient iterative refinement.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to significantly enhance the reliability and realism of video plans generated for embodied agents, thereby boosting their success rates in complex robotic manipulation and navigation tasks.

The motivation stems from several critical observations regarding the limitations of current video generation models when applied to control:
- **Hallucinations and Unrealistic Physics:** Despite advancements, generated videos often contain visual inconsistencies or violate physical laws, making them unsuitable as practical plans for robots. For example, an object might disappear, or a robot arm might pass through a solid surface.
- **Low Task Success:** These visual flaws directly translate into low task success rates when control actions are extracted from the generated videos, hindering their adoption in real-world robotics.
- **Difficulty of Scaling:** While scaling has benefited other generative models, it is less straightforward for video generation due to the high cost of curating labeled video datasets and the lack of a dominant, scalable architectural paradigm.
- **Lack of External Grounding:** Unlike LLMs that have greatly benefited from human feedback for alignment, video generation models for control often lack mechanisms to incorporate external feedback to ground their outputs in real-world physics and task requirements.

Against this backdrop, VideoAgent sets out to achieve the following specific objectives:
- **Develop an Inference-Time Refinement Mechanism:** Introduce a novel procedure, "self-conditioning consistency," that allows for iterative improvement of generated video plans during inference, leveraging computational resources to enhance quality rather than simply generating new samples.
- **Integrate AI Feedback for Plan Selection:** Utilize a pre-trained Vision-Language Model (VLM) to evaluate and select the most plausible and task-relevant video plan from multiple refined candidates, serving as a proxy for environmental reward or human judgment.
- **Enable Online Self-Improvement through Execution Feedback:** Establish a closed-loop system where the agent executes refined video plans in the environment, collects data from successful trajectories, and uses this real-world feedback to further fine-tune and improve the underlying video generation model.
- **Demonstrate Improved Task Success:** Empirically validate VideoAgent's ability to reduce hallucinations and improve physical plausibility, leading to a measurable increase in task success rates in challenging simulated robotic environments.
- **Explore Real-World Applicability:** Provide preliminary evidence of VideoAgent's capacity to refine real-robot videos, indicating its potential for direct application in physical systems and for grounding video generation in the physical world.

By addressing these objectives, VideoAgent aims to overcome the significant hurdles preventing video generation from realizing its full potential as a robust and generalizable planning tool for embodied AI.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

VideoAgent proposes a multi-stage framework for self-improving video plans for embodied planning, integrating iterative refinement, AI feedback, and online interaction with the environment. The core of its approach lies in a novel adaptation of consistency models called **self-conditioning consistency**.

#### 3.1. Video Generation and Refinement via Self-Conditioning Consistency

The framework starts with a video generation policy, typically a diffusion model, that produces an H-step image sequence (video plan) given an initial observation frame and a language instruction. The key innovation for refinement is:

- **Self-Conditioning Consistency Model (`f_hat`):** Unlike standard diffusion models that predict a clean image from a noisy one, `f_hat` is trained to predict a *clean video* (`x^(0)`) by taking a *previously generated (potentially imperfect) video* (`x_hat`) and a *noisy version of the ground truth video* (`x^(t)`) as input. This allows the model to iteratively refine its own previous predictions.
- **Loss Function:** The training objective `L(θ)` combines a standard video diffusion loss (`L_video-diffusion`) with a novel self-conditioning consistency loss (`L_SC-consistency`).
 - `L_video-diffusion` ensures the model learns to denoise effectively.
 - `L_SC-consistency` has two terms: one that predicts the clean video conditioned on `x_hat` and `x^(t)`, and another that regularizes the similarity between outputs from different refinement iterations, promoting coherence and stable improvement.
- **Parameter Sharing:** The video generation model (`f_theta`) and the video refinement model (`f_hat_theta`) share parameters, allowing for a unified architecture that can both generate initial plans and iteratively refine them.
- **Feedback Guided Refinement (Optional):** The refinement model can optionally take additional feedback (e.g., from humans or a VLM) as input, guiding the refinement process towards specific improvements.

#### 3.2. Inference through VLM Guided Video Generation

During inference, VideoAgent uses its trained models to generate and select the best video plan:

- **Initial Plan Generation:** An initial video plan (`x_hat`) is generated by the diffusion model.
- **Iterative Refinement:** The `f_hat` model is iteratively applied to `x_hat` to produce a sequence of refined video plans (`x_hat_i`).
- **VLM-Guided Selection:** A pre-trained Vision-Language Model (VLM), denoted as `R_hat`, acts as an intelligent critic. It takes a refined video `x_hat_i` and the task description as input, returning a binary value (`ACCEPT`/`REJECT`) based on criteria like visual coherence, adherence to physical laws, and task completion.
- **Stopping Condition:** The refinement process stops when the VLM deems a video plan "acceptable." The first accepted plan is selected as `x_hat_refined`. The VLM prompt structure can be weighted to prioritize precision (avoiding false positives) by penalizing uncertainty.

#### 3.3. Self-Improvement through Online Finetuning

To further ground the video generation in the real world and address limitations of VLM feedback, VideoAgent incorporates an online self-improvement loop:

- **Execution in Environment:** The `x_hat_refined` video plan is converted into low-level motor control actions using an optical flow model (GMFlow) and executed in the real or simulated environment.
- **Data Collection:** The system observes whether the task was successfully completed based on the true environmental reward.
- **Finetuning on Successes:** Only successful trajectories are collected into a new dataset (`D_new`). This new data is then used to augment the original training dataset (`D`).
- **Iterative Finetuning:** The video generation and refinement models are fine-tuned using Algorithm 1 on the updated, larger dataset `D`. This process can be repeated over multiple online iterations, allowing the model to continuously learn from its own successful executions. This mimics a form of behavioral cloning on successful demonstrations.
- **Re-planning (Optional):** VideoAgent can also incorporate a replanning mechanism, where if the initial plan fails, a new plan is generated and refined, potentially leading to further successful trajectories for collection.

By combining these three components—self-conditioning consistency for refinement, VLM guidance for selection, and online finetuning from successful executions—VideoAgent creates a robust, closed-loop system for self-improving embodied planning.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of VideoAgent demonstrates its effectiveness across simulated robotic manipulation and navigation tasks, as well as its ability to improve real-world video quality.

#### 4.1. End-to-End Task Success (Simulated Environments)

- **Meta-World (Robotic Manipulation):**
 - **Initial Improvement:** Without online environment access, VideoAgent (relying on self-conditioning consistency and VLM selection) improved the overall success rate from 19.6% (AVDC baseline) to 22.3%. Notably, for challenging tasks like "faucet-close," performance jumped from 12.0% to 46.7%.
 - **Online Self-Improvement:** With online data collection and finetuning, VideoAgent-Online further boosted success rates. After two online iterations, the overall success rate reached 38.2%.
 - **State-of-the-Art:** When combined with replanning (VideoAgent-Online-Replan), the overall success rate soared to 53.7%, surpassing prior state-of-the-art results on this benchmark.
- **iTHOR (Object Navigation):**
 - VideoAgent consistently outperformed the AVDC baseline across all four room types (Kitchen, Living Room, Bedroom, Bathroom), achieving an overall success rate of 34.2% compared to AVDC's 31.3%. This demonstrated the effectiveness of self-conditioning consistency in generating more plausible navigation plans even without online access.

#### 4.2. Effect of Different Components in VideoAgent

- **Effect of VLM Feedback Type:**
 - Training the refinement model with richer VLM feedback proved beneficial. Using simple binary feedback (`VideoAgent-Binary`) improved performance to 23.8% (from 22.3%).
 - Using descriptive, suggestive language feedback from the VLM (`VideoAgent-Suggestive`) for training yielded even better results, reaching an overall success rate of 26.6%, indicating that more informative feedback can facilitate better refinement.
- **Effect of Number of Iterations:**
 - **Refinement Iterations:** Increased refinement iterations generally led to higher task success. Some tasks, like "faucet-close," showed substantial improvement up to five iterations (from 24.0% to 58.7%), suggesting that self-conditioning consistency effectively reduces hallucinations and improves physical plausibility over multiple passes.
 - **Online Iterations:** Successive online finetuning iterations, where more successful trajectories are collected, consistently increased the overall task success, demonstrating the efficacy of the online self-improvement loop.
- **Accuracy of VLM Feedback:**
 - **Binary Classification:** GPT-4 Turbo, when used as the VLM, achieved an initial accuracy of 69% in judging video acceptability against human labels. This was improved to 75% accuracy with a reweighted prompt designed to increase precision (avoid false positives), which is crucial for plan selection.
 - **Partial Observability:** VLM accuracy was found to be sensitive to partial observability (e.g., robot arm being out of frame), highlighting a challenge for real-world deployment.
 - **Descriptive Feedback Quality:** When providing descriptive feedback (identifying issues and suggesting improvements), GPT-4 Turbo achieved 73.5% accuracy in identification and 86.1% accuracy in suggestions (human-evaluated), indicating its potential for providing useful corrective signals.

#### 4.3. Evaluating Self-Refinement on Real-World Videos (BridgeData V2)

- **Quantitative Metrics:** VideoAgent outperformed the AVDC baseline on most reference-free metrics (e.g., CLIP Score, Flow Consistency, Visual Quality, Temporal Consistency, Text-to-Video Alignment, Factual Consistency). The gains were significant in metrics crucial for instruction following and physical realism, such as Factual Consistency and Text-to-Video Alignment.
- **Qualitative Evaluation / Human Preference:** Human evaluations confirmed that VideoAgent's refined videos were significantly more realistic, improving the acceptance rate by 22% (from 42.0% for AVDC to 64.0%). Qualitative examples visually demonstrated VideoAgent's ability to correct hallucinations (e.g., preventing objects from disappearing) and produce more temporally coherent and physically plausible movements.

In summary, the results consistently show that VideoAgent's iterative refinement, VLM guidance, and online self-improvement mechanisms effectively reduce hallucinations and enhance the physical plausibility of generated video plans, leading to substantial improvements in downstream embodied AI task success rates.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

VideoAgent represents a significant step forward in making video generation models reliable tools for embodied AI and real-world decision-making. Its primary significance lies in addressing the crucial problem of hallucinations and physically unrealistic content in generated videos, which has long been a bottleneck for their application in robotics.

The potential impact of this work is multifaceted:

- **Bridging the Reality Gap:** By introducing a mechanism for self-improvement grounded in external feedback (both AI-driven and real-world execution), VideoAgent offers a promising direction for closing the gap between high-fidelity video generation and the demands of physical environments. This grounding is essential for any AI system that needs to operate reliably in the real world.
- **Enhanced Reliability and Task Success for Embodied AI:** The demonstrated improvements in task success rates across diverse simulated robotic tasks (Meta-World, iTHOR) highlight VideoAgent's direct utility. This could accelerate the development of more capable and robust robotic agents that can plan visually and execute complex, long-horizon tasks.
- **Reduced Data Dependency and Cost:** While scaling datasets is challenging for video, VideoAgent's iterative refinement means that lower-quality initial generations can be improved, potentially reducing the need for perfectly curated, massive datasets. The online self-improvement loop further allows the model to learn from its own experiences, incrementally collecting highly relevant successful trajectories, which can be more efficient than large-scale offline data collection.
- **Leveraging Multi-Modal AI Capabilities:** The effective integration of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) as critics and guides for video refinement showcases the power of combining different AI capabilities. This approach could lead to more intelligent and self-correcting generative systems. The finding that richer, descriptive VLM feedback leads to better refinement is particularly impactful, opening avenues for more sophisticated AI-driven corrective loops.
- **Path Towards Generalist Agents:** By improving the reliability of video as a universal observation and action space, VideoAgent contributes to the broader goal of developing generalist AI agents capable of controlling various robots in different environments for diverse tasks. A more robust video-based planning mechanism could enable knowledge transfer from human videos to robot control more effectively.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The paper identifies several promising future directions, including exploring more advanced online reinforcement learning algorithms, integrating different action extraction mechanisms (beyond optical flow), real-robot deployment, and holistic finetuning of all pipeline components (including the VLM feedback model itself). These suggest that VideoAgent is not just an endpoint but a strong foundation for continued research in grounded generative AI.

In conclusion, VideoAgent's novel self-conditioning consistency and its principled integration of AI and real-world feedback represent a significant stride towards making video generation a truly effective and reliable tool for embodied planning, paving the way for more intelligent and adaptable robotic systems.
