# STRIDE: Automating Reward Design, Deep Reinforcement Learning Training and Feedback Optimization in Humanoid Robotics Locomotion

> [!summary] Summary
> STRIDE, developed by ZhiCheng AI and collaborators, introduces an agentic engineering framework that automates reward design, DRL training, and feedback optimization for humanoid robot locomotion, significantly outperforming state-of-the-art methods like EUREKA with an average 250% improvement in efficiency and task performance across various terrains.

## Problem

- Manual reward function design for high-degree-of-freedom humanoid robots is a highly challenging, time-consuming, and iterative process, demanding extensive domain expertise.
- Traditional reward functions often lack generality, requiring significant re-engineering for different tasks, robot morphologies, or environments, limiting scalability and adaptability.
- Existing LLM-driven reward generation methods primarily focus on high-level planning or simpler reward functions, leaving low-level control in high-dimensional, continuous action spaces largely unexplored.

## Method

- **STRIDE** employs an ==AI-driven agentic engineering framework==, integrating raw environment code and URDF descriptions as comprehensive context for the LLM to generate initial reward functions.
- It implements a ==closed-loop system== where LLM-generated reward functions are evaluated in a DRL pipeline, and performance feedback is used to iteratively refine the rewards through ==mutation== and adjustment of components.
- The framework allows for ==gradient-free integration of human feedback==, enabling refinement of reward quality and safety without complex retraining.

## Results

- STRIDE consistently outperforms the state-of-the-art EUREKA framework, achieving an average **250%** improvement in efficiency and task performance for humanoid robot sprinting across diverse terrains (flat, wave, random uniform).
- On flat terrain, STRIDE-generated rewards result in more natural, fluid, and stable sprinting motions, while on wave and random uniform terrains, it achieves up to **three times** the Max Success Score of EUREKA.
- When combined with human initialization, STRIDE maintains a high code generation success rate of **0.7** across iterations, indicating a more stable and reliable reward design process.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Providing comprehensive environmental context, including raw code and URDF, to LLMs significantly enhances their ability to generate dynamically adaptable and syntactically correct reward functions for complex robotics.
> - An iterative, feedback-driven optimization loop with LLMs can dynamically evolve and refine reward functions, enabling superior performance that often goes beyond human-designed intuition.
> - Integrating initial human expertise via gradient-free feedback can further stabilize and accelerate the reward generation process, leading to consistently high success rates in complex tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research directly addresses a critical and long-standing bottleneck in the field of deep reinforcement learning (DRL) for complex robotic systems: the manual design and iterative refinement of reward functions. Humanoid robotics, in particular, with its high degrees of freedom (DoF) and dynamic stability requirements, exemplifies tasks where reward engineering is exceptionally challenging and resource-intensive.

Historically, DRL for humanoid locomotion has relied on human-engineered reward functions, which require significant domain expertise, meticulous tuning, and often lead to suboptimal or biased solutions. Early attempts at automated reward learning explored imitation learning and evolutionary algorithms, while others focused on applying RL to achieve specific robot movements. However, these often lacked generality or required predefined human knowledge, limiting their scalability and adaptability.

The emergence of large language models (LLMs) like GPT-4 has brought about a paradigm shift, demonstrating unprecedented capabilities in code generation, zero-shot reasoning, and in-context optimization. Recent work has begun to explore LLMs for high-level planning tasks in RL and for generating simpler reward functions. For instance, frameworks like EUREKA (Ma et al., 2024), which STRIDE explicitly compares itself against, represent state-of-the-art in LLM-driven reward design. However, the application of LLMs to generate and iteratively refine reward functions for *low-level control* in *high-dimensional, continuous action spaces* like humanoid sprinting has remained largely underexplored.

STRIDE positions itself at the forefront of this intersection, aiming to bridge the gap between LLM capabilities and complex humanoid robot locomotion. It advances beyond existing LLM-driven reward generation by proposing an "agentic engineering" framework that integrates comprehensive environmental context (raw code and URDF), continuous DRL training feedback, and iterative optimization. By doing so, STRIDE moves beyond static or template-based reward generation, offering a dynamic, closed-loop system that can evolve highly effective and robust reward functions without task-specific prompts or extensive manual intervention. This innovation is crucial for unlocking the full potential of DRL in real-world robotics, where diverse tasks and environments necessitate adaptable and scalable reward design solutions.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to **automate the entire pipeline of reward design, deep reinforcement learning (DRL) training, and feedback optimization for humanoid robot locomotion tasks**. The authors aim to overcome the significant challenges and bottlenecks associated with traditional, manual reward engineering in this complex domain.

Specifically, the key motivations driving the development of STRIDE include:

- **Addressing the Reward Design Bottleneck**: Manual reward function design for high-degree-of-freedom humanoid robots is a highly challenging, time-consuming, and iterative process. It demands extensive domain expertise, meticulous tuning, and often leads to suboptimal solutions due to implicit biases or an inability to discover truly optimal policies. STRIDE seeks to eliminate this bottleneck.
- **Enhancing Scalability and Adaptability**: Traditional reward functions often lack generality, requiring significant re-engineering for different tasks, robot morphologies, or environments. The goal is to create a framework that can dynamically interpret task descriptions and environment constraints to produce tailored, executable reward functions, thereby improving scalability and adaptability.
- **Achieving Advanced Humanoid Locomotion**: The paper highlights achieving "human-like athletic locomotion, such as sprinting" as a benchmark. The motivation is to enable humanoid robots to perform complex, dynamic movements with precision, stability, and speed, mimicking professional athletes.
- **Leveraging Advanced LLM Capabilities**: The researchers are motivated by the recent advancements in LLMs (e.g., GPT-4o mini) for code generation, zero-shot capabilities, and in-context optimization. They aim to fully exploit these features to automate the otherwise manual and knowledge-intensive process of reward engineering.
- **Improving Efficiency and Performance**: Beyond mere automation, a core objective is to significantly improve the efficiency of the DRL workflow and the task performance of the humanoid robots, surpassing state-of-the-art manual and semi-automated methods.
- **Enabling Gradient-Free Human Feedback**: The authors also aim to introduce a novel way to integrate human feedback for refining reward quality and safety without the need for complex, gradient-based retraining, enhancing applicability to real-world scenarios.

In essence, STRIDE is motivated by the desire to push the boundaries of DRL in humanoid robotics by automating the most critical and often most difficult part of the learning process—reward design—thereby making it more accessible, efficient, and ultimately more capable of achieving sophisticated, human-like robot behaviors.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

STRIDE employs a novel, AI-driven framework built on **agentic engineering** to automate reward design, DRL training, and iterative feedback optimization. The core of its approach lies in a closed-loop system that leverages Large Language Models (LLMs) for dynamic code generation and refinement.

The methodology can be broken down into three main components:

1. **Environment as Context**:
 - **Input to LLM**: STRIDE begins by providing the raw environment code and a natural language task description as input context to the LLM. This is a crucial distinction, as it allows the LLM to dynamically understand the environment's variables, constraints, and structure, rather than relying on predefined templates or abstract prompts.
 - **Humanoid Robot Description**: The humanoid robot used in experiments is a custom-designed 16-DoF model, described by a URDF (Unified Robot Description Format) file. STRIDE integrates this detailed URDF configuration, enabling the LLM to extract relevant state variables (joint angles, velocities, torso alignment) and kinematic constraints. This ensures reward functions are tailored to the robot's specific physical capabilities and morphology, promoting efficient locomotion and balance.
 - **Advantages**: Using raw environment code directly capitalizes on LLMs' pre-training on code in various programming languages, improving the quality and syntax of generated reward functions. It also ensures dynamic adaptability, grounding the LLM-generated rewards in the environment's semantics. The LLM (specifically GPT-4o mini) is responsible for generating initial reward functions in Python based on this comprehensive context.

2. **Feedback-Driven Reward Optimization**:
 - **Reward Evaluation**: The LLM-generated reward functions are not static. They are evaluated by integrating them into a DRL pipeline. The performance of the agent trained with these rewards (e.g., sprint speed, stability, energy efficiency) is quantified by a predefined fitness function.
 - **Iterative Refinement**: Based on the evaluation results, STRIDE generates detailed textual feedback, which is then fed back to the LLM. This feedback mechanism facilitates "Reward Mutation and Refinement," allowing the LLM to:
 - Adjust hyperparameters (e.g., scaling coefficients for reward components).
 - Modify reward terms to improve credit assignment for specific behaviors.
 - Introduce new reward components based on identified training deficiencies or to enhance innovation.
 - **Closed-Loop Process (Algorithm 1)**: The overall process is an iterative loop. In each iteration, the LLM generates multiple reward functions, which are then evaluated. The best-performing reward function is selected, and its performance and reward components are analyzed to refine the prompt for the next iteration. This continuous reflection and refinement process ensures that the reward functions evolve towards optimal performance.
 - **Human Initialization (Optional)**: STRIDE also explores a "gradient-free" integration of human feedback, allowing users to describe desired improvements (e.g., prioritize stability over speed) to refine rewards without extensive retraining.

3. **DRL Training**:
 - **Simulation Environment**: Once an optimized reward function is obtained, it is used to train DRL policies for the humanoid robot locomotion tasks. The training is conducted in a high-performance simulation environment, specifically Isaac Gym, which supports parallelized training of multiple agents, enhancing efficiency and scalability.
 - **Policy Evaluation**: The trained policies are rigorously evaluated on their ability to achieve task-specific objectives, such as high-speed sprinting on various terrains or maintaining balance.

The entire framework operates through a series of system and user prompts (provided in the appendix) that guide the LLM through the reward generation, analysis, and refinement process. The prompts explicitly instruct the LLM on dynamic observation use, task-oriented design (e.g., mimicking Usain Bolt's sprinting), code robustness, efficiency, and incorporating advanced strategies like dynamic reward weighting and contrastive signals.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

STRIDE demonstrates compelling performance and significant advancements in automated reward design for humanoid robot locomotion. The key findings are:

1. **Superior Performance Over State-of-the-Art (EUREKA)**:
 - Across diverse environments (flat, wave, and random uniform terrains), STRIDE consistently and significantly outperforms EUREKA, a state-of-the-art LLM-driven reward design framework.
 - STRIDE achieves an average improvement of **around 250% in efficiency and task performance** compared to EUREKA.
 - **Qualitative Improvement**: On flat terrain, STRIDE-generated rewards lead to a "more natural, fluid, and stable sprinting motion" for the humanoid robot, showcasing better joint coordination and posture, whereas EUREKA results in "awkward and inefficient running with poor balance and coordination" (Figure 4). This qualitative difference is a strong indicator of superior reward shaping.
 - **Quantitative Terrain Performance**:
 - **Flat Terrain**: STRIDE achieves near-optimal sprinting speed, outperforming EUREKA (Figure 5).
 - **Wave Terrain**: STRIDE demonstrates superior adaptability, achieving **three times the Max Success Score** of EUREKA (Figure 6).
 - **Random Uniform Terrain**: STRIDE exhibits unprecedented robustness, with its Max Success Score exceeding **around 250%** of EUREKA's best performance (Figure 7).

2. **Improved Code Generation Success Rates with Iteration**:
 - Table 1 illustrates the success rates of generating functional code across iterations. EUREKA's success rate fluctuates and even drops to 0.0 in the third iteration, indicating inconsistency. STRIDE shows a gradual improvement from 0.2 to 0.5 in the second iteration, but then slightly stagnates at 0.3. This suggests that while STRIDE's iterative process is generally more stable than EUREKA's, there might be inherent challenges in achieving consistently high code success rates without initial guidance.

3. **Impact of Human Initialization**:
 - When STRIDE is combined with human initialization ("STRIDE (Human Init.)"), it consistently maintains a high success rate of **0.7 across all three iterations** (Table 1). This highlights the significant advantage of providing initial human expertise to guide the LLM, leading to more stable and superior performance from the outset.
 - Figure 8 further supports this, showing that STRIDE (especially with human initialization) outperforms EUREKA both in terms of Max Success Score and in alignment with human-designed rewards. This implies that while STRIDE can innovate beyond human intuition, integrating human expertise can still lead to improved outcomes and a more reliable starting point.

4. **Innovation Beyond Human Intuition**:
 - The correlation analysis (Figure 8) suggests that STRIDE's ability to generate novel and useful reward functions might not always correlate directly with human expectations or initial human-designed rewards, yet it still drives superior performance. This finding indicates that the automated framework can discover non-obvious reward structures that are more effective for the learning agent than what human experts might initially devise.

In summary, the results convincingly demonstrate STRIDE's efficacy in automating a notoriously difficult aspect of DRL. Its agentic engineering approach, coupled with powerful LLMs, enables it to design complex, dynamic reward functions that lead to significantly better and more robust humanoid locomotion across challenging environments, surpassing current state-of-the-art methods.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of STRIDE marks a significant advancement in the fields of reinforcement learning, artificial intelligence, and humanoid robotics, with far-reaching potential impacts:

1. **Democratizing Reward Engineering**: By automating the reward design process, STRIDE effectively lowers the barrier to entry for developing complex robotic behaviors. It reduces the dependence on extensive domain expertise and the arduous manual tuning typically required, making advanced DRL more accessible to a wider range of researchers and practitioners. This could accelerate innovation in robotic control.

2. **Accelerating DRL Workflows**: The efficiency gains—an average improvement of 250% over EUREKA—are substantial. This acceleration in reward function generation and optimization directly translates into faster development cycles for new robotic skills, enabling quicker iteration and deployment in both research and industrial settings.

3. **Unlocking More Sophisticated Humanoid Behaviors**: STRIDE's ability to generate highly effective and innovative reward functions, leading to sprint-level locomotion across complex terrains, demonstrates its capacity to enable more agile, dynamic, and human-like movements in humanoid robots. This is crucial for applications requiring high performance in unstructured or challenging environments, such as disaster response, assistance in human-centric spaces, or even sports training.

4. **Enhancing Robustness and Adaptability**: The framework's ability to adapt to raw environment code and dynamically refine rewards ensures that robots can learn robust policies for diverse morphologies and tasks. This adaptability is vital for real-world scenarios where robots might encounter varying conditions or need to perform multiple, distinct tasks.

5. **Pioneering Agentic Engineering for Robotics**: STRIDE showcases the power of agentic engineering combined with LLMs in a complex physical domain. This methodology, involving autonomous generation, evaluation, and iterative refinement, could become a blueprint for solving other intricate control and planning problems in robotics and beyond.

6. **Gradient-Free Human-in-the-Loop Optimization**: The introduction of gradient-free human feedback for refining rewards is particularly impactful. This mechanism allows for intuitive integration of human preferences and safety considerations without disrupting the complex DRL training process, addressing a critical need for deploying autonomous systems in sensitive or safety-critical applications.

7. **Bridging the Gap between High-Level Planning and Low-Level Control**: STRIDE effectively demonstrates how LLMs can be utilized not just for abstract planning but also for generating detailed, executable code for low-level control systems, a frontier that has been largely untapped in previous LLM-RL integrations.

In conclusion, STRIDE sets a new benchmark for automated reward design in humanoid robotics. Its contributions pave the way for more autonomous, capable, and versatile robots, ultimately pushing the boundaries of what is achievable in artificial intelligence and robotic systems.
