# LLMs Get Lost In Multi-Turn Conversation

> [!summary] Summary
> A comprehensive evaluation of 15 large language models reveals systematic performance degradation (39% average drop) in multi-turn conversations compared to single-turn interactions, with models struggling to maintain context and adapt to new information across conversation turns regardless of temperature settings or conversation granularity.

## Problem

- LLMs are typically evaluated on single-turn, fully-specified scenarios despite being used in multi-turn conversations
- Current evaluation methods treat conversations as isolated subtasks, missing the complexities of real-world interactions

## Method

- Development of a "==sharding==" process to transform single-turn instructions into smaller sequential components
- Creation of simulation environments with different conversation types (Sharded, Concat, Recap, Snowball) to evaluate model performance

## Results

- All tested LLMs showed significant performance drops in multi-turn settings (~**39%** average)
- Models frequently make premature answer attempts and over-rely on previous incorrect responses
- Performance degradation occurs independently of conversation granularity
- External agent frameworks for context management may be insufficient; native multi-turn support in LLMs may be necessary

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Performance degradation is driven by increased unreliability rather than loss of peak capabilities
> - Models exhibit "loss-of-middle-turns" behavior, overly adjusting responses based on first and last conversation turns
> - Lowering temperature settings does not effectively improve system reliability
> - Any conversation involving underspecification across two or more turns leads to degraded performance

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

- **Filling a Gap in Evaluation:** The paper addresses a critical gap in how LLMs are typically evaluated. While LLMs are increasingly used in conversational settings, evaluations are often conducted in single-turn, fully-specified scenarios. This work focuses on the more realistic and challenging situation of multi-turn, underspecified conversations.
- **Critique of Episodic Evaluation:** It challenges the prevailing method of multi-turn evaluation, which treats conversation as a series of isolated subtasks that can be independently assessed. The authors argue that this "episodic" approach fails to capture the complexities of real-world human-AI interaction, which often involves underspecification and incremental information revelation.
- **Building on Existing Work on Underspecification:** The research builds on the existing body of work in linguistics and communication studies which highlights underspecification as a common feature of human language. The article cites several studies that show LLMs often give undesirable responses, like hedging and refusing to answer, when queries are underspecified. The article also considers how intent misalignment and ambiguity in human speech are challenges to conversational AI.
- **Connection to Agent Frameworks:** The research explores the multi-turn capabilities of LLMs, in a world where agent frameworks exist that orchestrate interactions with users and leverage LLMs as single-turn operators. The paper addresses whether native multi-turn support is needed in LLMs in agent frameworks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

- **Objective:** To comprehensively evaluate the performance of LLMs in multi-turn, underspecified conversations compared to single-turn, fully-specified scenarios. The goal is to quantify the degradation in performance and identify underlying reasons.
- **Motivation:**
 - The authors point out that while underspecification is prevalent in real-world LLM conversation logs, existing LLM evaluations tend to focus on single-turn, fully specified instructions.
 - The authors hypothesize that LLMs may struggle to effectively manage context, adapt to new information, and avoid making premature assumptions in multi-turn settings. They also hypothesize that LLMs might have difficulty adapting and course-correcting when provided with new information.
 - Ubiquitous performance degradation over multi-turn interactions is likely a reason for low uptake of AI systems. The authors want to investigate solutions to address this.
 - The authors seek to highlight the need for LLM builders to prioritize multi-turn reliability alongside single-turn aptitude, and suggest actionable steps for LLM builders to achieve this goal.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

- **Simulation Environment:** The core of the methodology is the development of a simulation environment that mimics real-world conversational scenarios. This environment allows for controlled and scalable experimentation. The simulations are automated and not representative of natural human-AI conversation, with the intention of testing the underlying language model.
- **Sharding Process:** A novel "sharding" process is used to transform existing single-turn instructions from high-quality benchmarks into a set of smaller instructions ("shards"). The instruction from fully specified is divided into a set of smaller instructions that jointly deliver the same information as the original instruction.
- **Simulation Types:** Several simulation types were created to assess various aspects of LLM performance, including:
 - *Fully-Specified (FULL):* Baseline single-turn performance.
 - *Sharded:* Multi-turn, underspecified conversations where shards are revealed one at a time.
 - *Concat:* Single-turn, fully-specified conversations where all shards are concatenated into a single instruction.
 - *Recap:* Sharded conversation with a final turn that recapitulates all shards.
 - *Snowball:* Turn-level recapitulation; at each turn, the user simulator introduces a new shard, but also restates all the shards that have been revealed so far in the conversation.
- **Task Selection:** Six diverse generation tasks were selected from established benchmarks, covering code generation, database querying, API usage, math problem solving, data-to-text generation, and multi-document summarization.
- **Metrics:**
 - *Average Performance (P):* Unbiased estimate of a model's mean score.
 - *Aptitude (A):* Estimate of a model's best-case performance (90th percentile score).
 - *Unreliability (U):* Measure of the gap between best-case and worst-case performance (difference between 90th and 10th percentile scores).
- **Experimental Setup:** A large-scale simulation experiment was conducted with 15 LLMs, running multiple simulations for each task and simulation type.
- **Gradual Sharding Experiment:** To explore the relationship between the granularity of sharding and the "lost in conversation" phenomenon, the authors propose the gradual sharding experiment. 31 instructions from the original experiment were selected, and expanded into seven sharded instructions, with the shard-set size growing from 2 to 8 shards.
- **Temperature Experiment:** To evaluate whether setting the temperature to its lowest setting (T= 0) effectively resolves the reliability concern, the assistant’s temperature for generating responses was varied to three values: 1.0, 0.5, and 0.0.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

- **Performance Degradation:** All tested LLMs exhibited significantly lower performance in multi-turn conversations (Sharded) compared to single-turn (Full) scenarios, with an average drop of 39%.
- **"Lost in Conversation" Phenomenon:** The performance degradation is attributed to LLMs getting "lost in conversation," characterized by:
 - Premature answer attempts based on incomplete information.
 - Over-reliance on previous (incorrect) answer attempts.
 - Loss-of-middle-turns; models overly adjust their answers based on the first and last turn of conversation.
 - Overly verbose responses that introduce confusion.
- **Aptitude vs. Reliability:** The performance degradation is primarily driven by an increase in unreliability (variability in performance), rather than a significant loss in aptitude (best-case performance).
- **Agent-like Frameworks:** Agent-style frameworks to process information might be limiting, and LLMs should natively support multi-turn interaction.
- **Temperature is Ineffective:** Lowering the temperature of the LLM when generating responses is ineffective in improving system reliability.
- **Granularity Independence:** Gradual sharding experiments indicate that any conversation that involves underspecification and occurs in two or more turns leads to models getting lost in conversation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

- **Highlighting a Critical Problem:** The study provides empirical evidence for a significant limitation in current LLMs: their struggle to reliably handle multi-turn, underspecified conversations. This limitation has direct implications for the design and deployment of conversational AI systems.
- **Call for Prioritizing Reliability:** The authors urge LLM builders to focus on improving the reliability of models in multi-turn settings, rather than solely pursuing increases in single-turn aptitude.
- **Implications for System and Agent Builders:** It suggests that LLMs should natively support multi-turn interaction, rather than relying on external agent frameworks for context management.
- **Actionable Recommendations:** The paper provides practical recommendations for users of LLM-based systems, such as trying again with a fresh conversation or consolidating information into a single instruction.
- **Direction for Future Research:** The sharding process can be adopted by practitioners to test models on underspecified conversations, and future research can focus on how LLMs can be improved to reliably handle multi-turn conversations.
- **Economic Impact:** The authors note that analysis of Claude conversations shows which economic tasks are performed with AI.

This paper makes a valuable contribution by systematically investigating and quantifying the challenges of multi-turn conversations for LLMs, offering practical insights and guiding future research directions.
