# A Survey of Embodied Learning for Object-Centric Robotic Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> This survey provides a comprehensive overview of embodied learning for object-centric robotic manipulation, systematically categorizing methods into perceptual, policy, and task-oriented learning. It highlights the influence of recent advancements in large foundation models while outlining current challenges and future research directions in this rapidly evolving field.

## Problem

- The rapidly evolving field of embodied learning for object-centric robotic manipulation lacked a comprehensive, up-to-date survey that integrates recent advancements.
- Existing surveys often had narrower scopes, focusing only on specific perception types (e.g., 3D vision) or neglecting key aspects like policy and task-oriented learning.
- There was a need to systematically recap the impact of new paradigms such as Large Language Models (LLMs), Neural Radiance Fields (NeRFs), and Diffusion Models on the field since 2021.

## Method

- Provided a comprehensive survey that systematically reviews the latest advancements in embodied learning for object-centric robotic manipulation.
- Developed a hierarchical classification system to organize methods across embodied perceptual, policy, and task-oriented learning.
- Integrated discussions on the influence of cutting-edge AI models, such as LLMs, NeRFs, and ==Diffusion Models==, on robotic manipulation tasks.
- Detailed public datasets, evaluation metrics, and representative applications, alongside a forward-looking analysis of challenges and future research directions.

## Results

- Categorized embodied learning methods into three main branches: Embodied Perceptual Learning (EPEL), Embodied Policy Learning (EPCL), and Embodied Task-Oriented Learning (ETOL).
- Detailed advancements in data representation (e.g., combining 3D-aware and tactile sensing), object pose estimation (instance-level, category-level, novel object), and affordance learning.
- Summarized progress in policy representation (explicit, implicit, diffusion policies) and learning techniques (Reinforcement Learning, Imitation Learning, LLM/VLM-driven policies) for grasping and manipulation tasks.
- Identified persistent challenges including sim-to-real generalization, multimodal embodied LLMs, human-robot collaboration, and the need for model compression and interpretability.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Embodied learning integrates perception, policy, and task-oriented components, with an increasing trend towards multimodal data representations.
> - Large foundation models like LLMs, Diffusion Models, and NeRFs are profoundly influencing embodied learning approaches for robotic manipulation.
> - Despite rapid progress, significant challenges remain in sim-to-real generalization, sample efficiency, and achieving robust, generalizable manipulation in complex real-world scenarios.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper positions itself within the rapidly evolving and challenging field of **embodied AI**, specifically focusing on **object-centric robotic manipulation**. It contextualizes embodied learning as a fundamental departure from traditional data-driven machine learning. While conventional methods rely on static, pre-constructed datasets (images, videos, text) for pattern recognition, embodied learning emphasizes knowledge acquisition through **physical interaction with the environment and real-time perceptual feedback**. This dynamic learning mechanism, continuously refining behaviors, is presented as inherently suitable for robotic manipulation, enhancing adaptability to changing conditions and enabling complex tasks.

The authors acknowledge the plethora of existing survey articles on embodied AI and robot learning, covering domains like navigation, planning, grasping, and manipulation. They provide a concise comparison in Table I and Section I.A, distinguishing their work from previous surveys. Notably, they highlight the closely related survey by Cong et al. (2021), which focused on 3D vision-based robotic manipulation. The key differentiating factors of the present survey are:
- **Broader Scope in Perception:** Unlike previous works limited to 3D visual perception, this survey systematically categorizes representation methods based on images, 3D-aware techniques, *and tactile sensing*, offering a more holistic view of robotic perception.
- **Comprehensive Coverage of Key Aspects:** It extends beyond perception to provide a comprehensive introduction to critical aspects of robotic manipulation, including **policy learning** and **task-oriented learning**, which are often treated separately or less comprehensively in other reviews.
- **Up-to-Date Coverage:** Crucially, the survey covers the *latest research achievements published after 2021*. This includes the flourishing impact of recent advancements such as Large Language Models (LLMs), Neural Radiance Fields (NeRFs), Diffusion Models, and 3D Gaussian Splatting, which have significantly influenced the field since earlier surveys.

By offering a cutting-edge and comprehensive perspective on embodied learning methods specifically for object-centric robotic manipulation, this survey aims to fill a notable gap in the literature. It serves as a timely and systematic recap of the state-of-the-art, integrating diverse threads of research under a unified framework. This positions it as a valuable resource for researchers and practitioners seeking to understand the current progress, identify key challenges, and explore future directions in developing next-generation intelligent robots capable of nuanced interaction with their physical environment.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this paper is to provide a **comprehensive survey of the latest advancements in embodied learning for object-centric robotic manipulation**. This overarching goal is driven by several key motivations:

Firstly, the authors highlight that embodied learning for object-centric robotic manipulation is a "rapidly developing and challenging area" within embodied AI. The significant recent interest in this field underscores its importance, as it is "crucial for advancing next-generation intelligent robots." This emphasizes the strategic value and potential impact of research in this domain.

Secondly, the paper explicitly states the core difference and advantage of embodied learning: "Unlike data-driven machine learning methods, embodied learning focuses on robot learning through physical interaction with the environment and perceptual feedback, making it especially suitable for robotic manipulation." This intrinsic suitability motivates a focused review of how this paradigm is being applied to the specific challenges of object manipulation.

Thirdly, the authors identify a critical gap in the existing literature. Despite the rapid progress and the emergence of "a host of innovative solutions" driven by advancements like LLMs, NeRFs, Diffusion Models, and 3D Gaussian Splatting, there is "a notable absence of a comprehensive survey that encapsulates the latest research in this rapidly evolving field." This motivates the authors to systematically recap cutting-edge advancements and summarize encountered challenges and prospective research directions.

Specifically, the survey aims to categorize existing work into three main interconnected branches, mirroring the intelligence aspects of a typical robotic manipulation system:
1. **Embodied Perceptual Learning:** To understand how robots predict object pose and affordance through various data representations and environmental interaction.
2. **Embodied Policy Learning:** To explore methods for generating optimal robotic decisions using techniques like reinforcement learning and imitation learning.
3. **Embodied Task-Oriented Learning:** To analyze how robot performance is optimized based on the characteristics of different manipulation tasks, such as object grasping and general manipulation.

In essence, the paper's key objectives are to provide a structured, in-depth, and up-to-date understanding of how robots learn to perceive, decide, and act upon objects in their environment through physical interaction, thereby addressing the complexities and uncertainties of real-world applications.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

As a survey paper, the methodology and approach primarily involve a structured review, categorization, and synthesis of existing literature. The authors adopt a hierarchical classification system to organize the vast landscape of embodied learning for object-centric robotic manipulation.

The core of their approach is a **three-branch categorization** of embodied learning methods, designed to reflect the functional intelligence of a robotic manipulation system:

1. **Embodied Perceptual Learning (EPEL):** This branch focuses on how robots acquire understanding of target objects and their environment. It is further subdivided into:
 - **Data Representation:** Covering how sensor data (visual, depth, LiDAR, tactile) is processed and represented, including image-based (SISB, SIMB, MISB, MIMB), 3D-aware (depth-based, point cloud-based, transition-based), and tactile-based representations.
 - **Object Pose Estimation:** Analyzing methods for determining an object's position and orientation, categorized by scope: Instance-Level (ILOPE), Category-Level (CLOPE), and Novel Object Pose Estimation (NOPE).
 - **Affordance Learning:** Exploring how robots identify potential interactive regions and functionalities of objects, through supervised learning from static data or learning from interaction in simulations.

2. **Embodied Policy Learning (EPCL):** This branch addresses the sophisticated decision-making capabilities required for manipulation. It covers:
 - **Policy Representation:** How a robot's behavior is modeled, classifying approaches into Explicit Policy (deterministic/stochastic), Implicit Policy (energy-based models), and Diffusion Policy (conditional generative models).
 - **Policy Learning:** The techniques used to train these policies, primarily Reinforcement Learning (RL) (on-policy, offline, demonstration-guided, reward function learning) and Imitation Learning (IL) (Behavioral Cloning, IRL, GAIL, DMPs, DAgger, scaling up data, learning from human videos, equivariant models), along with hybrid methods and approaches driven by Large Language Models (LLMs) or Vision-Language Models (VLMs).

3. **Embodied Task-Oriented Learning (ETOL):** This branch focuses on optimizing robot performance based on specific task characteristics, ensuring effectiveness and adaptability. It differentiates between:
 - **Object Grasping:** The fundamental ability to reliably capture objects. This includes Single-Object Grasping (SOG) (open-loop, closed-loop, transparent object grasping, grasping in clutter, dynamic object grasping) and Multi-Object Grasping (MOG) (holistic, independent).
 - **Object Manipulation:** A broader range of control activities beyond simple grasping. This includes Non-Dexterous Manipulation (NDM) (pick-and-place, object rearrangement, kit assembly, deformable object manipulation, articulated object manipulation) and Dexterous Manipulation (DM) (trajectory optimization, RL/IL, tool manipulation).

Beyond this structured categorization of methods, the survey's approach also includes:
- **Overview of Public Datasets:** A dedicated section (Sec. V.A and Appendix A/B) provides a summary of widely used datasets for both object grasping and manipulation, differentiating between real-world and simulated data.
- **Evaluation Metrics:** A review of common metrics used to assess performance in object grasping (accuracy, Grasp Success Rate) and object manipulation (Task Success Rate, simulated time, kinematic object disarrangement).
- **Representative Applications:** Discussion of current and future applications of embodied learning in robotic manipulation, spanning industrial, agricultural, domestic, and surgical robotics, as well as other emerging areas (Sec. VI and Appendix C).
- **Challenges and Future Directions:** A forward-looking analysis of significant challenges (Sim-to-Real generalization, multimodal embodied LLMs, human-robot collaboration, model compression/acceleration, model interpretability/safety) and potential avenues for future research (Sec. VII).

By adopting this systematic and comprehensive approach, the authors provide a valuable resource for navigating the complex and interdisciplinary field of embodied learning for object-centric robotic manipulation, making it accessible for both newcomers and seasoned researchers.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The "findings" of a survey paper are its comprehensive overview, structured categorization, and synthesis of the state-of-the-art, alongside identified challenges and future directions. This paper's main findings can be summarized across its three primary branches:

#### 4.1. Embodied Perceptual Learning (EPEL)

- **Data Representation:** The survey highlights a progression from image-based representations (minimal sensor requirements, but limited 3D information) to 3D-aware representations (leveraging RGB-D for more robust features, via depth, point clouds, or transition models like NeRFs/Gaussians). Tactile-based representations are identified as supplementary, crucial for fine-grained interaction, with ongoing research in data generation (NeRFs, GANs, simulations) to overcome collection challenges. A key finding is the need to combine these modalities for comprehensive perception.
- **Object Pose Estimation:** Three categories are identified: Instance-Level (high accuracy for known objects but poor generalization), Category-Level (broader applicability but lower accuracy, with progress on articulated objects), and Novel Object Pose Estimation (NOPE) (high flexibility for unseen objects but significant accuracy/robustness challenges, with large models/SAM showing promise despite computational demands). The trade-off between accuracy and generalization remains a central challenge.
- **Affordance Learning:** Two main approaches are presented: supervised learning (limited by domain specificity and static data) and interaction-based learning (more robust, but sample-inefficient). The survey finds that while significant progress has been made, designing effective frameworks that can leverage internet-scale data and adapt quickly to specific tasks remains an open problem.

#### 4.2. Embodied Policy Learning (EPCL)

- **Policy Representation:** The paper details explicit policies (simple but struggle with complexity), implicit policies (reformulate action prediction as optimization, face training stability/computational issues), and diffusion policies. Diffusion policies are highlighted as a promising, highly expressive, and robust approach, especially for visuomotor tasks and multimodal behaviors (e.g., incorporating language instructions), though accelerating their sampling process is a challenge.
- **Policy Learning:**
 - **Reinforcement Learning (RL):** Continues to be pivotal for decision-making, with advancements in sim-to-real transfer (domain randomization), generalizable policies (adapter training), and addressing long-horizon tasks. A significant finding is the increasing role of LLMs in automated reward function generation, reducing reliance on manual crafting.
 - **Imitation Learning (IL):** Remains crucial for mimicking expert behavior, with ongoing efforts in scaling up demonstration data (MimicGen, large datasets like Bridge Data, Open X-embodiment), learning from in-the-wild human videos, and leveraging equivariant models (NDFs, EDFs) for enhanced sample efficiency and generalization.
 - **Other Methods:** The emergence of hybrid RL+IL approaches (e.g., teacher-student learning) and, critically, the burgeoning field of LLM- and VLM-driven policies, which integrate language instructions with visual observations for complex task execution, is identified as a significant trend.

#### 4.3. Embodied Task-Oriented Learning (ETOL)

- **Object Grasping:**
 - **Single-Object Grasping (SOG):** Has seen extensive study and practical application, with methods ranging from open-loop to closed-loop (real-time feedback for correction). The survey highlights three challenging sub-tasks: transparent object grasping (issues with depth, texture, and low friction, addressed by NeRFs, multispectral data), grasping in clutter (collision-free planning, repositioning objects via pushing), and dynamic object grasping (human-to-robot handover, human-free moving object grasping, requiring rapid adjustment and motion prediction).
 - **Multi-Object Grasping (MOG):** Remains highly complex, with slower progress compared to SOG. Approaches include holistic grasping (treating multiple objects as one) and independent grasping (manipulating each object separately). The paper finds an urgent need for further in-depth investigation in this area.
- **Object Manipulation:**
 - **Non-Dexterous Manipulation (NDM):** Characterized by simple end-effectors and fewer DoF, suitable for repetitive tasks (pick-and-place, rearrangement, assembly). Challenges include ensuring stability, continuity for long-horizon tasks, adaptation in dynamic environments, and autonomous error correction.
 - **Dexterous Manipulation (DM):** Aims to replicate subtle human actions using sophisticated multi-fingered hands. Traditionally relied on analytical models, but now increasingly uses model-based/model-free RL and IL. Tool manipulation is a pivotal focus, with research evolving from task-specific skill learning to leveraging LLMs for enhanced tool cognition and flexibility.
 - Overall, the survey concludes that while progress is significant, existing methods are often task-specific, and achieving truly general object manipulation capabilities remains a substantial challenge.

In summary, the survey finds that embodied learning for robotic manipulation is rapidly advancing, with clear trends towards multimodal data integration, leveraging large foundation models (LLMs, VLMs, Diffusion Models), and tackling increasingly complex real-world scenarios. However, persistent challenges in generalization, data efficiency, human-robot interaction, computational constraints, and safety/interpretability underline the need for continued, intensive research.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The significance and potential impact of this survey paper are multi-faceted, extending to the research community, technological development, and real-world applications.

#### 5.1. Significance to the Research Community

- **Comprehensive and Structured Overview:** The survey provides a much-needed, systematic, and hierarchical classification of embodied learning methods for object-centric robotic manipulation. This clear organization helps researchers understand the relationships between different sub-fields (perception, policy, task-orientation) and identify key components.
- **Timely and Up-to-Date Synthesis:** By explicitly covering advancements after 2021 and integrating emerging paradigms like LLMs, NeRFs, and Diffusion Models, the survey serves as a cutting-edge resource. This is crucial in a fast-evolving field where new methodologies and models emerge rapidly.
- **Identification of Gaps and Challenges:** The paper meticulously outlines the current limitations across data representation, pose estimation, affordance learning, policy learning, and specific manipulation tasks. This clear articulation of "challenges and future directions" acts as a roadmap for future research, guiding efforts towards critical unsolved problems such as Sim-to-Real generalization, sample efficiency, and real-world robustness.
- **Resource for New Researchers:** For those new to the field, the survey offers a foundational understanding, detailing key concepts, methodologies, datasets, and evaluation metrics, thereby lowering the barrier to entry and fostering new contributions. The associated GitHub project also facilitates practical engagement.

#### 5.2. Potential Impact on Technological Development and Applications

The advancements in embodied learning for robotic manipulation, as summarized by this survey, hold the potential to revolutionize numerous sectors, creating more autonomous, adaptable, and intelligent robotic systems:

- **Next-Generation Intelligent Robots:** The core impact is the advancement of robots capable of nuanced physical interaction and decision-making in unstructured environments. This moves beyond pre-programmed or highly constrained operations towards truly intelligent and versatile agents.
- **Industrial Automation:** Intelligent robots can enhance efficiency, reduce labor costs, and improve safety in manufacturing. Applications include precise assembly line operations, rapid and accurate packaging and sorting in logistics (e.g., handling diverse products in warehouses), and performing maintenance tasks in hazardous environments.
- **Agriculture:** Robots equipped with embodied learning can optimize planting, nurturing, and harvesting processes, leading to higher quality and sustainable agricultural practices. This includes tasks like identifying fruit ripeness, precise pruning, and targeted fertilization, improving yields and reducing waste.
- **Domestic and Service Robotics:** The development of household assistants, smart caregiving robots, and automated cooking systems can significantly improve quality of life and convenience. Robots could organize living spaces, assist the elderly or those with special needs, and perform complex culinary tasks, addressing challenges like diverse environments and delicate handling.
- **Surgical Robotics:** In healthcare, intelligent surgical robots can serve as advanced assistants, enabling more precise cutting and suturing, automated instrument control, and real-time collaboration with human surgeons. This can reduce surgical trauma, enhance efficiency, and potentially improve patient outcomes, though ethical and safety considerations remain paramount.
- **Broader Applications:** Beyond these core areas, the technology has potential in space exploration (e.g., autonomous repair, sample collection), education (interactive learning tools), and scientific research (automated experimentation).
- **Human-Robot Collaboration:** A significant area of future impact identified is fostering seamless human-robot collaboration. As robots become more capable, they can assist humans, reduce workload, and enhance safety, while humans can provide guidance and oversight, leading to more efficient and adaptable symbiotic systems.

Ultimately, this survey contributes to accelerating the development of robust, generalizable, and safe robotic manipulation capabilities. By highlighting both the achievements and the formidable challenges, it lays the groundwork for future breakthroughs that will unlock the full potential of intelligent robots in interacting with and transforming our physical world.
