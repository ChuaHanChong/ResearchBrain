# Vision Pretraining for Dense Spatial Perception

> [!summary] Summary
> Robbyant researchers introduced masked boundary modeling, a self-supervised pretraining method for Vision Transformers that explicitly targets fine-grained spatial structure. The approach enabled a 1.1B parameter model to achieve an RMSE of 0.296 on NYU-Depth v2, surpassing larger models like the 7B DINOv3, and demonstrated leading performance in video object segmentation and depth completion.

## Problem

- Existing visual foundation models often prioritize semantic invariance or appearance reconstruction, leading to representations that are weak in fine-grained spatial understanding (e.g., precise localization of boundaries, depth discontinuities).
- Dense spatial perception tasks typically rely on expensive human annotations or require task-specific fine-tuning, hindering the development of inherently spatially-aware visual foundation models.
- Many self-supervised learning objectives for vision treat boundaries as downstream outputs rather than fundamental learning signals, resulting in a "missed opportunity" for building robust physical intelligence systems.

## Method

- The method employs a ==self-distillation== framework with **masked boundary modeling**, where the teacher's online boundary predictions identify and force ==boundary-bearing tokens== into the student's masked set.
- A dual objective supervises masked tokens: standard semantic ==iBOT== for all, plus a specific ==categorical boundary objective== for boundary tokens, leveraging a ==categorical reparameterization== of continuous boundary fields for stability.
- An ==online target generation== mechanism allows the teacher to bootstrap and refine boundary targets from its own dense field predictions and sparse corner points, validated statistically using ==*a-contrario* theory==, without relying on external annotations. The framework is instantiated at scale with a ==ViT-g/16 (1.1B)== backbone.

## Results

- LingBot-Vision's **1.1B** parameter ViT-g model achieved an RMSE of **0.296** on NYU-Depth v2, outperforming larger models like the 7B DINOv3 (**0.309**) and 2B V-JEPA 2.1 (**0.307**).
- It demonstrated leading performance in training-free video object segmentation, achieving J&F-Mean scores of **70.0** on DAVIS-2017 and **73.5** on YouTube-VOS, on par with or surpassing larger video-pretrained models.
- When used for depth completion (LingBot-Depth 2.0), the encoder consistently outperformed DINOv2 and DINOv3 initializations, achieving leading performance on **14 benchmarks**, including challenging transparent object scenes.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly integrating boundary information into self-supervised pretraining via forced masking and a categorical boundary objective can enhance fine-grained spatial understanding in visual foundation models.
> - Recasting continuous boundary field regression as a per-pixel categorical classification problem enables the application of stabilizing self-supervised learning mechanisms (centering, sharpening) to geometric features.
> - Online generation and statistical validation of boundary targets by the teacher network allows for a self-correcting learning process that bootstraps boundary perception without human annotations, providing a clean supervision signal that co-evolves with the model.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of computer vision has witnessed significant advancements in visual representation learning, particularly through self-supervised learning (SSL) and the development of large-scale visual foundation models. These models aim to learn meaningful and versatile representations from vast amounts of unlabeled visual data. This work contributes to the ongoing evolution of visual SSL, specifically addressing the challenge of robust dense spatial perception.

Historically, visual SSL methods have broadly categorized into approaches like contrastive learning (e.g., MoCo, SimCLR), masked image modeling (MIM) (e.g., MAE, BEiT), and self-distillation (e.g., DINO, DINOv2, iBOT). More recently, joint-embedding predictive architectures (JEPA) and language-aligned pretraining (e.g., CLIP, SigLIP) have gained prominence. A recurring theme across many of these paradigms is a tendency to prioritize semantic invariance, cross-modal alignment, or appearance reconstruction. While effective for global image understanding and classification, these objectives often lead to representations that are comparatively weak in fine-grained spatial understanding, such as the precise localization of object boundaries, depth discontinuities, or motion silhouettes. This is because boundaries are typically treated as downstream outputs, requiring task-specific heads and dedicated, often expensive, annotations.

This paper positions itself as addressing this perceived limitation by advocating for a "boundary-centric" view of self-supervised pretraining. It integrates concepts from classical boundary modeling (e.g., Sobel, Canny, a-contrario theory) and modern deep learning approaches for line segment and wireframe detection into the SSL framework. By making boundaries a native learning signal rather than a mere output, the work seeks to develop visual foundation models that inherently excel at recovering structured, metric, and actionable representations essential for tasks demanding precise spatial intelligence, particularly for embodied AI and robotics. It aims to bridge the gap where existing models, despite their scale, often require "retrofits" or additional regularization to maintain dense spatial quality.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to enable Vision Transformers (ViTs) to learn representations that are inherently aware of shapes and boundaries directly from raw images, without relying on human annotations, external edge detectors, or pre-trained backbones. This objective is driven by several motivations:

- **Deficiency in Existing Foundation Models:** The authors argue that while modern visual foundation models excel at semantic invariance or appearance reconstruction, they often fall short in dense spatial understanding. This is attributed to their training objectives, which typically do not explicitly prioritize the fine-grained geometric properties conveyed by boundaries.
- **Boundaries as Fundamental Organizing Signals:** The paper posits that boundaries and shape discontinuities are not merely outputs of perception but are fundamental organizing signals for learning dense, structured visual representations. For instance, object masks delineate entities, depth discontinuities reveal geometry, and motion silhouettes separate dynamic regions. Neglecting these signals as primary learning targets constitutes a "missed opportunity."
- **Need for Actionable Representations in Physical Intelligence:** Dense spatial perception, encompassing segmentation, depth, motion, and scene layout, is crucial for "physical intelligence" systems like robots. These systems require visual inputs to be converted into structured, metric, and actionable representations. Strong boundary understanding is a prerequisite for generating such precise and reliable geometric information.
- **Overcoming Annotation Dependency:** Traditional approaches to boundary detection or dense prediction rely heavily on human-annotated labels, which are expensive to acquire, can be ambiguous, and are often unavailable at the scale required for large-scale pretraining. The motivation is to develop a self-supervised paradigm that bootstraps boundary learning from raw images alone, thus bypassing this dependency.
- **Resolving the Semantic-Geometric Tension:** A key motivation is to design a framework that resolves the inherent tension between learning semantic invariance (which might suppress fine-grained boundary signals) and learning boundary sensitivity (which might disrupt semantic invariance). The goal is for boundary-aware and semantic representations to co-emerge and cooperate rather than compete.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology, termed **masked boundary modeling**, extends the self-distillation paradigm by integrating boundary information directly into the masking and supervision strategy.

- **Self-Distillation Foundation:** The method builds upon the teacher-student self-distillation framework, similar to DINO and iBOT. A student network ($f_\theta$) learns to match the outputs of an exponential moving average (EMA) teacher network ($f_{\bar{\theta}}$). The objective includes both image-level distillation (L_DINO) for class tokens and patch-level distillation (L_iBOT) for masked patch tokens.
- **Boundary-Forcing Masked Modeling:**
 - **Boundary Token Identification:** The core innovation is to use the teacher's *online boundary predictions* to identify "boundary-bearing tokens." A token is classified as a boundary token if a line or curve, predicted by the teacher, intersects its corresponding image patch.
 - **Forced Masking:** These identified boundary-bearing tokens are explicitly *forced* into the student's masked set, in addition to randomly masked tokens. This ensures that the most information-dense and least redundant regions (boundaries) are consistently targeted for reconstruction by the student.
 - **Geometric Routing of Targets:** Masked tokens are supervised with a dual objective. All masked tokens, including boundary tokens, receive the standard semantic iBOT objective. However, boundary tokens *additionally* receive a specific categorical boundary objective (L_bnd). This "routing by geometry" provides a well-posed objective for boundaries, where semantic targets might be inherently ambiguous due to region transitions.
- **Categorical Reparameterization of Boundary Fields:**
 - **Problem with Continuous Regression:** Direct regression of continuous boundary field values (distance, orientation, endpoint angles) in a teacher-student loop is prone to collapse and cannot leverage the stabilization mechanisms (centering, sharpening) used in semantic SSL.
 - **Categorical Solution:** The continuous range of each boundary field channel is discretized into *K* bins. Boundary prediction is recast as a per-pixel classification problem, where the teacher's validated continuous value is encoded as a soft categorical label distribution over these bins.
 - **Benefits:** This reparameterization allows centering and sharpening mechanisms, crucial for SSL stability, to be applied to boundary learning. It also inherently connects to the *a-contrario detection theory*, where a uniform distribution over bins serves as the "no-structure" null hypothesis, facilitating parameter-free boundary validation.
- **Online Generation of Boundary Targets:**
 - **Bootstrapping Boundary Perception:** To overcome the "chicken-and-egg" problem of needing boundary information to train a model that detects boundaries, the teacher itself generates boundary targets online.
 - **Leveraging Redundancy:** The method exploits the inherent redundancy of the "holistically-attracted field" representation, where each line segment is over-determined by many pixels in its support region. This enables coherent line segments to be decoded even from noisy, unlearned field values, particularly when anchored by sparse corner points (Finding 1).
 - **Process:** The teacher network predicts a dense boundary field. This field is combined with sparse corner points (from a small, frozen, single-block ViT). Candidate line segments are decoded through vote aggregation. These candidates are then statistically validated using an *a-contrario* test, which discards spurious segments based on their unlikelihood under a "no-structure" null hypothesis. Only the validated segments are re-rendered into a clean target field to supervise the student.
 - **Self-Correction:** This process ensures that boundary targets co-evolve with the model, becoming cleaner and more accurate as training progresses, without any reliance on human annotations or external pre-trained detectors for the boundary task itself.
- **LingBot-Vision Scaling:**
 - **Data Curation:** A corpus of approximately 161 million images is assembled, curated from a 2 billion raw web image pool, following principles similar to DINOv2 but with a simpler pipeline. This dataset is an order of magnitude smaller than DINOv3's.
 - **Model Architecture:** The framework is instantiated at scale using a ViT-g/16 backbone (approximately 1.1 billion parameters) with SwiGLU feed-forward layers and rotary position embeddings (RoPE).
 - **Training Efficiency:** Distributed training strategies are employed, including sparse and fused boundary computation (only boundary tokens are processed at sub-token resolution) and custom CUDA kernels for batched online target generation, ensuring that the boundary branch adds minimal overhead to the training step time.
 - **Training Schedule:** The training budget is conservative, utilizing significantly fewer samples (less than one-third) compared to DINOv3.
 - **Model Distillation:** The large ViT-g teacher is distilled into smaller ViT-L, ViT-B, and ViT-S students, preserving the learned boundary-aware and semantic representations for various deployment budgets.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several key findings and performance results across various visual perception tasks.

- **Qualitative Feature Analysis:** Visualization of the top three PCA components of frozen patch features reveals that LingBot-Vision produces spatially coherent object regions with crisp boundaries, a characteristic not consistently observed in other foundation models like DINOv2 (per-token speckle), SigLIP 2 (blocky noise), or V-JEPA 2.1 (background texture bleeding). This qualitative observation indicates strong dense spatial understanding.
- **Dense Spatial Perception (Linear Probing):**
 - **Depth Estimation:** On NYU-Depth v2, LingBot-Vision (ViT-g, 1B parameters) achieves a Root Mean Squared Error (RMSE) of 0.296, which is the best among all compared models, surpassing larger models such as the 7B-parameter DINOv3 (0.309) and the 2B-parameter V-JEPA 2.1 (0.307) with significantly fewer parameters. On KITTI, it achieves an RMSE of 2.552, the best among models under 2B parameters.
 - **Semantic Segmentation:** On ADE20K, Cityscapes, and VOC12, LingBot-Vision performs comparably to DINOv3 ViT-H+ (0.8B parameters), slightly trailing on ADE20K but matching on Cityscapes and leading on VOC12. It consistently outperforms DINOv2 (equal size) by at least 4 mIoU on these benchmarks and surpasses video-pretrained V-JEPA 2.1 ViT-g. These results suggest that boundary-oriented pretraining yields segmentation quality comparable to models relying on distillation from larger teachers.
- **Video Understanding:**
 - **Video Object Segmentation (VOS):** In training-free label propagation on DAVIS-2017 and YouTube-VOS, LingBot-Vision achieves J&F-Mean scores of 70.0 and 73.5, respectively. These scores are on par with DINOv3 ViT-H+ and the 7B DINOv3, and represent the best performance among all other models at any scale, including video-pretrained models like V-JEPA 2.1 ViT-g.
 - **Boundary Token Tracking:** Qualitatively, individual boundary tokens from LingBot-Vision's frozen features can be stably tracked through diverse video sequences (robot manipulation, pet videos, camera pans) using cosine similarity. The tracked queries maintain selectivity and coherence, demonstrating the temporal consistency and stability of the learned boundary representations without any temporal supervision during pretraining.
- **Image-Level Recognition:** On ImageNet-1K, LingBot-Vision (ViT-g) achieves 86.32% linear probing accuracy and 83.39% k-NN accuracy. While competitive, these scores are slightly behind top-performing models like DINOv3-7B and SigLIP 2, indicating a trade-off where the model's capacity and learning focus are oriented more towards localized spatial structure rather than global image-level invariances.
- **Distilled Model Family:** The distilled LingBot-Vision ViT-L, ViT-B, and ViT-S students largely retain the teacher's profile. Notably, the 0.3B parameter ViT-L student achieves an NYUv2 RMSE (0.310) on par with the 7B DINOv3 (0.309) using approximately 23 times fewer parameters, demonstrating efficient scaling and transfer of dense spatial perception capabilities.
- **LingBot-Depth 2.0 Application:**
 - **Encoder Initialization Study:** When used as the encoder initialization for the masked depth modeling (MDM) pipeline of LingBot-Depth, the LingBot-Vision encoder consistently outperforms DINOv2 and DINOv3 initializations. Improvements are substantial on challenging block-mask patterns (e.g., halving RMSE on DIODE-Indoor for ViT-L).
 - **Data Scaling:** The advantage of the LingBot-Vision initialization for depth completion intensifies with increased training data (from 3M to 150M samples). While all initializations benefit from more data, LingBot-Vision's performance curve continues to improve where others saturate, indicating that better pretraining compounds with data scale.
 - **Depth Completion Benchmarks:** LingBot-Depth 2.0 achieves leading performance on 14 depth completion benchmarks across various input masking patterns (block-mask, sparse) and real-sensor captures (HAMMER, ClearGrasp, LingBot evaluation set). It particularly excels on transparent object scenes (ClearGrasp), a known difficult case for active depth sensing.
 - **Qualitative Depth Completion:** The system produces completed depth maps and point clouds with straight planes and sharp object boundaries, effectively inferring geometry for challenging surfaces like mirrors and glass, which are typically problematic for raw depth sensors.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research on LingBot-Vision offers several points of significance and potential impact:

- **Novel Pretraining Paradigm:** The introduction of masked boundary modeling establishes a new self-supervised pretraining principle that explicitly targets fine-grained spatial structure. By treating boundaries as native learning signals rather than derived outputs, it deviates from the semantic-invariance focus of many existing foundation models.
- **Enhanced Dense Spatial Perception:** The consistent and substantial performance gains observed across depth estimation, semantic segmentation, and video object segmentation benchmarks demonstrate that boundary-centric pretraining leads to highly effective and geometrically faithful visual representations. This addresses a critical weakness in many contemporary foundation models.
- **Efficiency in Model Development:** LingBot-Vision achieves state-of-the-art or highly competitive results with a model size (1.1B parameters) and training data/compute budget significantly smaller than some leading foundation models (e.g., 7B DINOv3). This suggests a more efficient pathway to developing high-quality dense feature encoders.
- **Advancement in Embodied AI and Robotics:** The direct application to depth completion, resulting in LingBot-Depth 2.0 achieving leading performance on diverse and challenging real-world scenarios, highlights the immediate utility for physical intelligence systems. The ability to recover accurate, structured, and actionable 3D representations from visual input is crucial for robotic manipulation, navigation, and environmental understanding.
- **Robustness to Challenging Scenes:** The qualitative results on depth completion of reflective and transparent surfaces demonstrate the model's robustness in inferring geometry where active depth sensors typically fail, a significant advantage for real-world deployment.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The findings suggest that incorporating specific geometric inductive biases into self-supervised pretraining can yield powerful and specialized visual foundation models. This could inspire new architectural designs and learning objectives focused on other critical geometric or physical properties, potentially leading to a new generation of spatially-aware vision systems.
- **Community Contribution:** The release of pretrained models, code, and a website facilitates further research, application, and benchmarking by the wider machine learning and robotics communities.
