# DriveMoE: Mixture-of-Experts for Vision-Language-Action Model in End-to-End Autonomous Driving

> [!summary] Summary
> DriveMoE is an end-to-end autonomous driving framework that integrates Mixture-of-Experts architectures into both multi-view perception and action planning. This system achieves state-of-the-art performance on the Bench2Drive closed-loop benchmark, showing enhanced robustness in diverse driving scenarios and improved computational efficiency.

## Problem

- Existing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for autonomous driving struggle with inefficient multi-view vision processing, leading to computational burden or loss of precise spatial information.
- Current VLA frameworks utilize a single, unified policy for all driving behaviors, resulting in a 'mode-averaging effect' that compromises performance in rare but critical maneuvers.
- End-to-end autonomous driving models often lack strong generalization and robust reasoning capabilities in closed-loop, out-of-distribution scenarios.

## Method

- **DriveMoE** integrates a ==Scene-Specialized Vision MoE== that dynamically selects the most relevant camera views based on context, bypassing unselected views to reduce computational overhead.
- A Skill-Specialized Action MoE replaces dense feed-forward networks in the trajectory transformer with expert layers, allowing specialized control for diverse driving behaviors and mitigating the mode-averaging effect.
- The framework employs a two-stage training procedure, starting with teacher-forcing for stable initial learning and transitioning to adaptive training for robustness to router inaccuracies.

## Results

- DriveMoE achieved state-of-the-art performance on the Bench2Drive closed-loop benchmark, with a **22.8%** increase in Driving Score and a **62.1%** increase in Success Rate compared to the Drive-π0 baseline.
- The system demonstrated superior performance across five key driving abilities (Merging, Overtaking, Emergency Braking, Give Way, Traffic Signs), indicating enhanced robustness in complex scenarios.
- DriveMoE maintained competitive latency (**260ms**) while significantly outperforming methods that process numerous fixed views, confirming the efficiency gains from dynamic view selection.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Dynamically selecting relevant camera views using a Scene-Specialized Vision MoE significantly improves computational efficiency and driving performance compared to processing all or fixed subsets of views.
> - Specializing action policies for distinct driving skills via a Skill-Specialized Action MoE, particularly at the trajectory level, enhances the model's ability to handle complex and rare driving maneuvers.
> - Mixture-of-Experts architectures can be effectively extended from language models to both the perception and action domains in end-to-end autonomous driving, offering a pathway for scalable and specialized learning.
