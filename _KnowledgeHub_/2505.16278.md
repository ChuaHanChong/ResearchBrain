# DriveMoE: Mixture-of-Experts for Vision-Language-Action Model in End-to-End Autonomous Driving

> [!summary] Summary
> DriveMoE is an end-to-end autonomous driving framework that integrates Mixture-of-Experts architectures into both multi-view perception and action planning. This system achieves state-of-the-art performance on the Bench2Drive closed-loop benchmark, showing enhanced robustness in diverse driving scenarios and improved computational efficiency.

## Problem

- Existing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for autonomous driving struggle with inefficient multi-view vision processing, leading to computational burden or loss of precise spatial information.
- Current VLA frameworks utilize a single, unified policy for all driving behaviors, resulting in a 'mode-averaging effect' that compromises performance in rare but critical maneuvers.
- End-to-end autonomous driving models often lack strong generalization and robust reasoning capabilities in closed-loop, out-of-distribution scenarios.

## Method

- **DriveMoE** integrates a ==Scene-Specialized Vision MoE== that dynamically selects the most relevant camera views based on context, bypassing unselected views to reduce computational overhead.
- A Skill-Specialized Action MoE replaces dense feed-forward networks in the trajectory transformer with expert layers, allowing specialized control for diverse driving behaviors and mitigating the mode-averaging effect.
- The framework employs a two-stage training procedure, starting with teacher-forcing for stable initial learning and transitioning to adaptive training for robustness to router inaccuracies.

## Results

- DriveMoE achieved state-of-the-art performance on the Bench2Drive closed-loop benchmark, with a **22.8%** increase in Driving Score and a **62.1%** increase in Success Rate compared to the Drive-π0 baseline.
- The system demonstrated superior performance across five key driving abilities (Merging, Overtaking, Emergency Braking, Give Way, Traffic Signs), indicating enhanced robustness in complex scenarios.
- DriveMoE maintained competitive latency (**260ms**) while significantly outperforming methods that process numerous fixed views, confirming the efficiency gains from dynamic view selection.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Dynamically selecting relevant camera views using a Scene-Specialized Vision MoE significantly improves computational efficiency and driving performance compared to processing all or fixed subsets of views.
> - Specializing action policies for distinct driving skills via a Skill-Specialized Action MoE, particularly at the trajectory level, enhances the model's ability to handle complex and rare driving maneuvers.
> - Mixture-of-Experts architectures can be effectively extended from language models to both the perception and action domains in end-to-end autonomous driving, offering a pathway for scalable and specialized learning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

End-to-end autonomous driving (E2E-AD) represents a paradigm that directly maps raw sensor inputs to planning outputs, offering advantages such as reduced engineering complexity, mitigation of error propagation, and global objective optimization. Despite progress in open-loop benchmarks, E2E-AD models often face challenges in closed-loop settings, particularly when encountering out-of-distribution scenarios requiring strong generalization and reasoning capabilities.

Recent advancements in Vision-Language Models (VLMs) and Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, primarily from the embodied AI domain, have shown promise in enhancing generalization and contextual reasoning for autonomous driving. However, existing VLA approaches in AD face two primary limitations:
1. **Inefficient Multi-view Vision Processing:** Traditional "vanilla" vision processors process all available camera views indiscriminately, leading to substantial computational burden and redundant visual representations. Alternatively, "query-based" processors (e.g., Q-Former) extract compact visual tokens but often sacrifice precise geometric and positional information and typically require extensive additional pre-training. Both strategies result in inefficiencies or information loss, limiting scalability and effectiveness in real-time, multi-view driving scenarios.
2. **Lack of Skill Specialization:** Current VLA frameworks generally employ a single, unified policy network for all driving behaviors. This monolithic approach tends to bias training towards more frequent scenarios, leading to a "mode-averaging effect" that can compromise performance in rare but critical maneuvers (e.g., emergency braking, aggressive turns). This limits the models' ability to adapt dynamically to diverse and highly context-dependent situations.

Concurrently, Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) architectures have significantly advanced Large Language Models (LLMs) by enabling increased model capacity and scalability without a proportional increase in computational demands, achieved through expert specialization. However, the application of MoE principles to the vision and action domains, specifically within E2E-AD, remains largely underexplored. Existing end-to-end driving models often predict trajectory modalities within unified architectures but lack explicit dynamic expert selection or specialized behavioral adaptation.

This work addresses these identified gaps by proposing DriveMoE, a framework that integrates MoE principles into both the perception and decision-making modules of an E2E-AD system. By building upon a VLA foundation model adapted from embodied AI ($\pi_0$), DriveMoE aims to overcome the computational inefficiencies of multi-view processing and the behavioral limitations of single-policy networks, thereby enhancing the robustness, efficiency, and adaptability of autonomous driving systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to develop an end-to-end autonomous driving framework, termed DriveMoE, which leverages Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) architectures to achieve both computational efficiency in processing multi-view sensor data and robust handling of diverse and complex driving scenarios, including rare maneuvers.

This objective is motivated by several identified challenges and limitations in the current state of E2E-AD, particularly concerning Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models:

- **Addressing Computational Inefficiencies in Multi-view Perception:** Modern autonomous driving systems rely on multiple cameras to provide a comprehensive view of the surroundings. However, current VLA vision processors struggle with this multi-view input. Processing all camera views simultaneously (the "vanilla" approach) leads to redundant visual tokens and significant computational overhead, hindering real-time performance and scalability. Alternatively, query-based methods reduce token count but can compromise crucial spatial structure and often demand extensive pre-training. The motivation is to develop a mechanism that can efficiently process multi-view data by selectively focusing on contextually relevant visual information, akin to human selective attention.

- **Overcoming the Mode-Averaging Effect in Action Planning:** Existing VLA frameworks for autonomous driving typically utilize a single, unified policy network to generate actions across a wide spectrum of driving behaviors. This approach often results in a "mode-averaging effect," where the model biases its training towards frequently encountered scenarios. Consequently, it may fail to generate accurate or specialized trajectories for rare but critical maneuvers such as emergency braking, aggressive turns, or complex merges. The motivation is to enable skill specialization within the action planning module, allowing the model to allocate dedicated capacity to different driving behaviors and generate precise actions tailored to specific contexts.

- **Enhancing Generalization and Robustness to Diverse Scenarios:** The limitations in vision processing and action planning contribute to models struggling with out-of-distribution cases and diverse, long-tail driving scenarios in closed-loop settings. By introducing specialized experts for both perception and action, the research aims to improve the model's overall generalization capabilities and robustness across a broader range of real-world driving situations.

- **Leveraging Mixture-of-Experts for Scalability:** The success of MoE architectures in Large Language Models (LLMs) demonstrates their capacity to scale model size and improve performance without a proportional increase in computational cost. This motivates exploring how MoE principles can be extended to the vision and action domains within autonomous driving to achieve similar benefits in terms of scalability and specialized learning.

- **Establishing a Strong VLA Baseline for AD:** To effectively evaluate the contributions of MoE, the research first aims to establish a robust Vision-Language-Action (VLA) foundation model, Drive-$\pi_0$, adapted from embodied AI models ($\pi_0$), specifically for autonomous driving. This baseline serves as the architectural foundation upon which the MoE modules are integrated.

In summary, the core motivation is to move beyond monolithic, computationally intensive, and undifferentiated approaches in E2E-AD by introducing intelligent, context-aware specialization via MoE, thereby developing a more efficient, robust, and adaptable autonomous driving system.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The DriveMoE framework is designed to address the challenges of multi-view sensor processing and diverse driving behaviors in end-to-end autonomous driving. Its methodology is structured around a baseline VLA model, Drive-$\pi_0$, and two Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) modules: a Scene-Specialized Vision MoE and a Skill-Specialized Action MoE.

#### 3.1. Preliminary: Drive-$\pi_0$ Baseline

The foundation of DriveMoE is Drive-$\pi_0$, an adaptation of the $\pi_0$ Vision-Language-Action (VLA) framework from embodied AI for autonomous driving.
- **Inputs:** It takes two consecutive front-view images, a fixed text prompt (e.g., "Please predict future trajectory"), and the current vehicle state (e.g., speed, yaw rate, past trajectory).
- **Architecture:** It utilizes a pre-trained Paligemma VLM as its backbone and a flow-matching-based action module for generating future trajectory predictions.

#### 3.2. DriveMoE Architecture Overview

DriveMoE extends Drive-$\pi_0$ by integrating two MoE modules to enhance perception and planning:
- **Scene-Specialized Vision MoE:** Dynamically selects the most relevant camera views based on the current driving context.
- **Skill-Specialized Action MoE:** Generates accurate future trajectory distributions tailored to diverse driving skills within a flow-matching transformer.

#### 3.3. Scene-Specialized Vision MoE

This module aims to overcome the computational burden and information redundancy/loss associated with traditional multi-view processing.
- **Inspiration:** Human drivers' selective attention to key visual cues.
- **Mechanism:** A lightweight vision router ($R_{vision}$) takes the front-view embedding and a future goal waypoint as input. It computes a probability distribution across $N$ available camera views.
- **Dynamic Selection:** Based on this distribution, a subset of context-relevant camera views (e.g., frontal and a few side/rear views) is dynamically selected. Crucially, this selection occurs *before* expensive backbone computation, skipping unselected views to save resources.
- **Spatial Context Preservation:** Learnable positional embeddings unique to each camera viewpoint are incorporated into the corresponding visual tokens to maintain spatial and positional relationships.
- **Supervision:** The vision router is trained using a cross-entropy loss ($L_{Vision-Router}$). The ground-truth labels for view selection are generated by manually designed heuristic filters based on future trajectories, bounding boxes, and map information.

#### 3.4. Skill-Specialized Action MoE

This module addresses the "mode-averaging effect" of single-policy networks by specializing control for different driving behaviors.
- **Mechanism:** It replaces the dense feed-forward networks (FFNs) in the flow-matching trajectory transformer decoder with MoE layers. Each MoE layer contains multiple skill-specific FFN experts (K shared and M non-shared). An action router ($R_{action}$) routes each input through a small subset of these experts using a sparse activation mechanism (e.g., Top-1 or Top-2 experts).
- **Two MoE Styles Explored:**
 - **Token-level ActionMoE:** Each token (representing a timestep in the trajectory) independently selects experts based on its local hidden representation. This allows experts to specialize in short-horizon temporal dependencies like acceleration or braking within a trajectory.
 - **Trajectory-level ActionMoE:** The router assigns experts at the entire trajectory level. The full token sequence is averaged before being fed to the router, treating each trajectory as a single entity corresponding to a specific scenario or skill. This style is designed to align with human-defined skill categories.
- **Supervision:**
 - The skill router is trained using a cross-entropy loss ($L_{Action-Router}$) with driving skill labels ($y_k$) annotated based on scenarios (e.g., Merging, Overtaking).
 - The entire Action MoE module is optimized using a conditional flow matching loss ($L_{FM}$) to ensure accurate trajectory predictions.
 - A combined loss ($L_{Action}$) balances these components, and noise is introduced into the action router to encourage exploration and mitigate expert collapse.

#### 3.5. Two-Stage Training Procedure

DriveMoE is fine-tuned using a two-stage process on the pre-trained Paligemma VLM weights:
- **Stage 1 (Teacher-Forcing):** Both the Vision and Action MoEs are trained by selecting ground-truth experts based on annotations. The routers are trained concurrently. This stage aims to stabilize the initial learning.
- **Stage 2 (Adaptive Training):** The model transitions to selecting experts based on the outputs of the Vision and Action MoE routers. This removes reliance on ground-truth expert annotations, developing robustness to router inaccuracies and enhancing generalization under realistic inference conditions.

#### 3.6. Evaluation Setup

- **Benchmark:** CARLA simulator (version 0.9.15.1) and the Bench2Drive closed-loop benchmark (220 routes with corner cases for specific abilities).
- **Metrics:** Driving Score (DS), Success Rate (SR), Efficiency, Comfort for closed-loop, and L2 error for open-loop. Multi-dimensional ability evaluation (Merging, Overtaking, Emergency Braking, Give Way, Traffic Signs).
- **PID Controller:** All methods use the same PID controller for fair closed-loop comparison, taking model-predicted future waypoints for steering and speed control.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted on the Bench2Drive closed-loop simulation benchmark, along with ablation studies, reveal the following main findings:

#### 4.1. Overall Performance (Bench2Drive Closed-Loop)

- DriveMoE demonstrates state-of-the-art (SOTA) performance in overall driving score (DS) and success rate (SR) on the Bench2Drive benchmark.
- Compared to the Drive-$\pi_0$ baseline, DriveMoE achieves substantial improvements: a 22.8% increase in Driving Score (from 55.85 to 74.22) and a 62.1% increase in Success Rate (from 30.00% to 48.64%).
- On open-loop metrics, DriveMoE records a competitive L2 error, although the paper emphasizes the greater reliability of closed-loop metrics for assessing true driving performance.

#### 4.2. Multi-Dimensional Capability Evaluation

- In the multi-dimensional capability assessment, DriveMoE achieves SOTA results across five key driving abilities: Merging, Overtaking, Emergency Braking, Give Way, and Traffic Sign recognition, as well as their overall average. This indicates specialized performance in complex and rare scenarios.

#### 4.3. Ablation Study: Scene-Specialized Vision MoE

- **Impact of Multiple Fixed Views:** The Drive-$\pi_0$ baseline uses two front-view images. Adding additional *fixed* views (e.g., front-left, back) provides moderate improvements individually. However, simultaneously adding many fixed views (e.g., six views) leads to performance degradation and increased latency (e.g., 700ms for six fixed views vs. 100ms for two) due to the substantial increase in visual tokens and training complexity.
- **Effectiveness of Dynamic View Selection:** Introducing dynamically selected views *without* explicit supervision (Exp 8) significantly improves both driving score (DS 69.71) and success rate (SR 44.09%) compared to any fixed view configuration.
- **Contribution of Supervision:** Incorporating explicit supervision signals for the dynamic view selection (Exp 9, DriveMoE) further enhances performance (DS 74.22, SR 48.64%), confirming the efficacy of the Scene-Specialized Vision MoE.
- **Vision Router Accuracy:** The vision router achieved an accuracy of 88.85% on the Bench2Drive-Base validation set.

#### 4.4. Ablation Study: Skill-Specialized Action MoE

- **Token-level vs. Trajectory-level Routing:** The trajectory-level Action MoE consistently outperforms its token-level counterpart (e.g., DS 73.88 vs 65.62), suggesting that routing at the trajectory level, which aligns with distinct driving behaviors, is more effective for autonomous driving tasks. The trajectory-level Action MoE is adopted as the default.
- **Action Router Accuracy:** The action router achieved an accuracy of 65.40% on the Bench2Drive-Base validation set.
- **Impact of Expert Number and Supervision:**
 - Increasing the number of non-shared experts from 5 (default Bench2Drive skills) to 6 (adding "ParkingExits") showed improved performance (DS 74.22, SR 48.64% vs. DS 73.81, SR 47.73%).
 - Explicit action supervision for the router yields a noticeable improvement (DS 74.22 with supervision vs. 70.38 without).
 - Excessively increasing the number of experts (e.g., 13 or 44 experts) leads to performance degradation due to induced load imbalance among experts, indicating that an appropriate balance in specialization is crucial.

#### 4.5. Individual Contributions of MoE Modules

- Ablation studies comparing Drive-$\pi_0$ to variants of DriveMoE (without Vision MoE or without Action MoE) demonstrate that removing either MoE module leads to a noticeable decline in performance. This highlights the complementary and additive effectiveness of both the Scene-Specialized Vision MoE and the Skill-Specialized Action MoE within the DriveMoE framework.

#### 4.6. Efficiency and Generalization

- DriveMoE maintains competitive latency (260ms) compared to Drive-$\pi_0$ with only two fixed views (240ms), while significantly outperforming Drive-$\pi_0$ with six fixed views (700ms latency). This indicates that dynamic view selection effectively reduces computational overhead.
- The model size increases moderately (2606M to 3008M parameters) with the addition of MoE layers.
- Evaluations on the nuScenes dataset (open-loop) show that DriveMoE achieves competitive L2 error and a reduced collision rate, suggesting strong generalization to real-world scenarios.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The DriveMoE framework introduces several contributions with implications for the field of end-to-end autonomous driving:

- **Addressing Core Limitations in E2E-AD:** This work directly tackles two significant challenges in current E2E-AD systems, particularly those based on VLA models: the computational inefficiency and redundancy of multi-view sensor processing, and the "mode-averaging effect" that hinders robust performance in diverse and rare driving scenarios. By providing explicit solutions through MoE architectures, DriveMoE contributes to making E2E-AD more practical and reliable.

- **Enhancing Robustness and Safety in Critical Scenarios:** The demonstrated improvements in handling specific driving abilities, especially emergency braking, merging, and overtaking, suggest enhanced robustness to safety-critical situations. By enabling specialized experts for these complex maneuvers, DriveMoE could contribute to safer autonomous driving systems, particularly in challenging, real-world conditions where rare events frequently occur.

- **Improving Computational Efficiency for Real-time Applications:** The Scene-Specialized Vision MoE's dynamic view selection mechanism significantly reduces visual token load and associated computational overhead. This efficiency is critical for real-time autonomous driving, where low latency and resource optimization are paramount. The ability to achieve state-of-the-art performance while maintaining comparable latency to simpler baselines demonstrates a pathway towards more deployable VLA models for AD.

- **Pioneering MoE Application in Vision and Action for AD:** DriveMoE is presented as the first framework to integrate Mixture-of-Experts into both the perception (vision) and decision-making (action) components of an end-to-end autonomous driving system. This extends the proven benefits of MoE architectures, previously observed primarily in large language models, to a new and complex domain, opening avenues for future research in modular, scalable AI for embodied agents.

- **Foundation for Future Research and Development:**
 - By establishing Drive-$\pi_0$ as a VLA baseline specifically adapted for autonomous driving, the work provides a solid foundation for future comparisons and developments in this area.
 - The methodology, particularly the two-stage training and the use of heuristic-based supervision for routers, offers practical strategies for integrating MoE into complex systems.
 - The discussion on potential extensions, such as unsupervised view selection and knowledge distillation from MoE teacher models to smaller, specialized student models, outlines clear directions for further research aimed at reducing annotation costs and enabling efficient deployment.

In conclusion, DriveMoE represents an advancement in end-to-end autonomous driving by integrating specialized MoE components into a VLA framework. Its success in improving performance, particularly in challenging scenarios, and enhancing computational efficiency, indicates a promising direction for developing more capable, robust, and scalable autonomous driving systems.
