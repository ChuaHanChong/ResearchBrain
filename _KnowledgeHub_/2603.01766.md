# Neural Implicit Action Fields: From Discrete Waypoints to Continuous Functions for Vision-Language-Action Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Neural Implicit Action Fields (NIAF) reformulates robotic action generation as continuous function regression using Sinusoidal Representation Networks (SIRENs), enabling analytical differentiation for velocity and jerk supervision. The system achieved an average chain length of 4.66 on CALVIN and a 97.9% success rate on LIBERO, while demonstrating 90% success on real-world Item Placement and 80% on Cup Stacking, outperforming discrete waypoint baselines.

## Problem

- Existing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models use discrete waypoint predictions, leading to rigid temporal discretization and fixed sampling rates.
- Lack of analytical differentiability in discrete action representations prevents explicit, mathematically consistent supervision of higher-order dynamics like velocity and jerk.
- Reliance on numerical differentiation for velocity from discrete positions introduces noise, limiting stable impedance control and often restricting robots to stiff position control.

## Method

- NIAF models action trajectories as continuous, vector-valued functions Φ(τ;θ)Φ(τ; θ)Φ(τ;θ) over a normalized time domain using a Sinusoidal Representation Network (SIREN).
- A pre-trained multimodal large language model (MLLM) acts as a hierarchical spectral modulator, predicting modulation coefficients that dynamically reconfigure the SIREN's parameters.
- Leverages SIREN's infinite differentiability to analytically compute and explicitly supervise higher-order dynamics (velocity, jerk) alongside position, enabling stable impedance control.

## Results

- NIAF achieved superior performance in simulation, with an average chain length of **4.66** on CALVIN ABCD→D and a **97.9%** average success rate across LIBERO benchmarks.
- In real-world robotic tasks, NIAF outperformed baselines, achieving **90%** success on Item Placement and **80%** on Cup Stacking, and **85%** on precision-demanding Shape Insertion with state-of-the-art backbones.
- Analytically derived velocity supervision produced continuous, trend-following velocity references, enabling stable impedance control with executed velocities closely matching analytical signals, significantly reducing high-frequency oscillations observed in baselines.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Representing actions as continuous functions with SIRENs provides an intrinsic C∞^{∞}∞ continuity bias, effectively filtering high-frequency noise and ensuring temporal coherence.
> - Analytical differentiation allows for precise and consistent supervision of position, velocity, and jerk, which is crucial for compliant physical interaction and stable impedance control.
> - A grouped hyper-modulation mechanism using MLLM latents to deform a learnable motion prior enhances learning efficiency and robustness against compounding errors.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotics has witnessed advancements with the integration of Large Language Models (LLMs), leading to the development of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. These models aim to equip embodied agents with the capacity to interpret complex semantic instructions and execute various manipulation tasks. Initially, action modeling in these systems focused on predicting instantaneous, single-step action tokens. Subsequent approaches introduced action chunking, where fixed-horizon trajectories are predicted as sequences of discrete waypoints to improve trajectory coherence and reduce compounding errors. More recently, methods have explored encoding actions into compressed latent representations, such as B-spline control points or Discrete Cosine Transform (DCT) coefficients, to enhance compression efficiency.

Despite these developments, a fundamental challenge persists: the predominant action representation of discrete waypoint prediction is considered structurally misaligned with the continuous nature of physical control systems. This discretization, often arising from fixed-rate robot data collection and token-by-token prediction paradigms, imposes limitations such as rigid sampling rates, difficulty in supporting analytically consistent higher-order derivatives, and the introduction of quantization artifacts. These issues can hinder precise and compliant interaction, confining robots to stiff position control and limiting their capabilities in speed-sensitive tasks.

Neural Implicit Action Fields (NIAF) directly addresses these limitations by reformulating action representation. Instead of discrete waypoints, NIAF models action trajectories as continuous action functions. This shift leverages Implicit Neural Representations (INRs), specifically Sinusoidal Representation Networks (SIRENs), to generate continuous-time action manifolds. By enabling analytical differentiation, NIAF allows for explicit supervision of velocity and regularization of higher-order derivative signals. This approach aims to promote mathematical consistency, physical plausibility, and smoothness in control, thereby bridging policy-side action generation with execution-side smooth control. The work positions itself as an advancement in action representation that moves beyond the inherent constraints of discrete outputs in VLA models.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind the development of Neural Implicit Action Fields (NIAF) are centered on overcoming the limitations of discrete action representations in current Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models:

1. **Addressing Rigid Time Discretization**: Existing VLA models typically bind action predictions to a fixed sampling rate determined by the training data. This rigid temporal discretization prevents the policy from adapting to arbitrary control frequencies or querying trajectories at sub-step resolutions without introducing interpolation artifacts. NIAF aims to provide a resolution-independent action representation that can be queried at any temporal resolution.

2. **Enabling Higher-Order Dynamics Supervision**: Many current methods lack inherent higher-order continuity, resulting in discontinuous velocity profiles. While some parametric approaches use splines, they often quantize control points or fail to impose sufficient constraints on higher-order derivatives. This deficiency leads to motion jitter and potential control instability. A key objective of NIAF is to leverage analytical differentiability to allow explicit supervision of velocity and regularization of jerk, thereby ensuring the mathematical consistency of kinematic profiles.

3. **Achieving Dynamic Consistency and Control Compatibility**: Discrete action representations lack analytical differentiability, which makes it challenging to simultaneously supervise both position and velocity while maintaining mathematical consistency. Furthermore, relying on numerical differentiation to approximate velocity from discrete positions amplifies quantization noise, rendering the precise feedforward terms necessary for stable impedance control practically unattainable. This typically restricts robots to stiff position control, limiting their capacity for compliant and delicate interactions. NIAF seeks to generate analytically precise position and velocity signals, which are crucial for effective impedance control and compliant physical interaction.

4. **Improving Policy Quality and Robustness**: By explicitly modeling the intrinsic continuity of physical motion, NIAF aims to enhance the overall quality and robustness of robot policies, particularly in long-horizon manipulation tasks where compounding errors from discrete, noisy action predictions can accumulate.

In essence, the motivation is to align the action representation more closely with the physical world's continuous nature, thereby facilitating more precise, compliant, and stable robotic control across a wider range of tasks and operational conditions.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

NIAF reformulates action generation from discrete waypoint prediction to continuous function regression. It employs a Vision-Language Model (VLM) as a hierarchical spectral modulator that conditions a learnable motion prior, which is instantiated as a Neural Implicit Representation (INR).

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation: From Discrete Waypoints to Continuous Action Functions

The approach formally shifts from representing an action chunk as a finite sequence of discrete points, $A_{\text{seq}} = \{a_0, a_1, \ldots, a_{H-1}\}$, to defining it as a continuous, vector-valued function $A(\tau) = \Phi(\tau; \theta)$ over a normalized time domain $\tau \in [-1, 1]$. The objective then becomes predicting the parameters $\theta$ that define this continuous function. This parameterization is resolution-independent, allowing actions $a_k = \Phi(\tau_k; \theta)$ to be queried at any desired temporal resolution $K$. NIAF implements this by learning a mapping function $F_{\pi}: O \times T \rightarrow \Theta$ that predicts the parameter set $\Theta$ based on multimodal observations ($O$) and natural language instructions ($T$).

#### 3.2. Multimodal Context Encoding

A pre-trained multimodal large language model (MLLM), such as Florence-2 or Qwen3-VL, serves as the semantic backbone. A set of learnable query embeddings, $E_{\text{qry}}$, are introduced and fed into the MLLM decoder. By attending to the multimodal context, the decoder synthesizes relevant task dynamics into a sequence of modulation latents $Z = \text{MLLM}(E_{\text{qry}}; O, T)$. These latents function as context-dependent modulation signals rather than fixed waypoints, configuring the continuous action function.

#### 3.3. Action Manifold Decoding

The continuous action function $A(\tau) = \Phi(\tau; \theta)$ is instantiated using a Sinusoidal Representation Network (SIREN). SIRENs are chosen for their ability to model signals as C$^{\infty}$ smooth functions, ensuring well-behaved analytical derivatives. The decoding process operates under a hyper-modulation paradigm: the semantic latents $Z$ are projected into modulation coefficients $(\gamma, \beta)$. These coefficients dynamically reconfigure the SIREN's shared meta-parameters $(W, b)$, which act as a stable kinematic backbone. The forward pass involves recursive computation through $L$ layers, where modulated weights $\hat{W}^{(\ell)} = W^{(\ell)} \odot (1 + \gamma^{(\ell)})$ and biases $\hat{b}^{(\ell)} = b^{(\ell)} + \beta^{(\ell)}$ are applied at each layer. This mechanism allows $\gamma$ to scale the signal's frequency and $\beta$ to shift its phase, adapting universal motion laws to specific multimodal contexts.

#### 3.4. Grouped Hyper-Modulation Mechanism

To distribute modulation control spatially and preserve granular semantic details, a grouped modulation strategy is used. The latent sequence length $Q$ is constrained to $L \times (G + 1)$, partitioning $Z$ into $L$ blocks, one for each SIREN layer. Within the $\ell$-th block, $G$ tokens control the weights (frequency) by being independently projected via MLPs $\psi_{\gamma,g}$ and concatenated to form $\gamma^{(\ell)}$. The final token controls the bias (phase) by being projected via $\psi_{\beta}$ to form $\beta^{(\ell)}$.

#### 3.5. Analytic Higher-Order Dynamics

A core advantage of NIAF is the use of SIRENs, which are infinitely differentiable. The derivative of a SIREN preserves the network's structure, allowing analytical computation of higher-order dynamics without discretization artifacts. The velocity $v(\tau)$ is derived recursively using the chain rule, where $\dot{h}^{(\ell)} = \cos(u^{(\ell)}) \odot (\hat{W}^{(\ell-1)} \dot{h}^{(\ell-1)})$. Higher-order derivatives like jerk $j(\tau)$ are obtained by iteratively differentiating this recursive structure using the product rule. This promotes mathematical consistency between position and its derivatives.

#### 3.6. Implicit Manifold Regularization and Dynamics Supervision

The continuous parameterization $A(\tau) = \Phi(\tau; \theta)$ inherently ensures C$^{\infty}$ continuity, acting as an implicit manifold regularizer that suppresses high-frequency quantization artifacts. The learning objective includes:
- **Position Supervision ($L_{\text{pos}}$)**: A standard L2 loss between the predicted action function values $\Phi(\tau_k)$ and ground truth actions $a_{\text{gt},k}$.
- **Explicit Dynamics Supervision ($L_{\text{vel}}$, $L_{\text{jerk}}$)**: For real-world tasks requiring compliant interaction, explicit constraints are placed on higher-order motion profiles. Ground-truth velocity $v_{\text{gt}}$ is obtained from high-frequency FOC driver feedback. $L_{\text{vel}}$ supervises the analytically derived velocity $(2/T)\nabla_{\tau}\Phi(\tau_k)$ against $v_{\text{gt},k}$. $L_{\text{jerk}}$ regularizes the analytically derived jerk $(2/T)^3\nabla_{\tau}^3\Phi(\tau_k)$ to discourage abrupt variations. The total objective is $L_{\text{real}} = \lambda_p L_{\text{pos}} + \lambda_v L_{\text{vel}} + \lambda_j L_{\text{jerk}}$.
During inference, NIAF generates high-fidelity position $\Phi(\tau)$ and velocity $(2/T)\nabla_{\tau}\Phi(\tau)$ references for impedance control, where $u_{\text{cmd}} = K_p (\Phi(\tau) - a_{\text{curr}}) + K_d ((2/T)\nabla_{\tau}\Phi(\tau) - v_{\text{curr}})$. This enables stable tracking without stiffening the robot.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of NIAF was conducted across simulation benchmarks (CALVIN and LIBERO) and real-world robotic deployment scenarios, with a focus on policy improvements, generality across backbones, real-world effectiveness, and the benefits of physics-consistent regularization.

#### 4.1. Simulation Evaluation

- **Implicit Action Representation Improves Policy Quality**: On the CALVIN benchmark, NIAF achieved an average chain length of 4.66 on the ABCD→D setting and 4.47 on ABC→D, outperforming baselines including large-scale pretrained VLAs. On LIBERO, NIAF demonstrated an average success rate of 97.9%, with 100% success on LIBERO-Object and 95.5% on the LIBERO-Long suite. These results indicate that continuous action functions enable capturing infinite-resolution trajectory details, contributing to performance and robust long-horizon execution.
- **Comparison of Different Action Representations**: Using an identical Florence-2 Large backbone, NIAF consistently outperformed other action representation baselines such as BEAST-F, BEAST-CT, OFT, and FAST on CALVIN ABC→D and LIBERO-Long. This suggests that the gains are not solely due to the absence of discretization but also the inherent properties of the continuous representation.
- **Scalability with Decoder-Only MLLM**: NIAF was integrated into the larger-scale, decoder-only Qwen3-VL model. A single multi-task policy trained across all four LIBERO suites achieved a superior average success rate of 97.7%, outperforming other Qwen3-VL-based baselines in three out of four suites.
- **Reasons for Simulation Performance**: The observed improvements in simulation are attributed to two factors:
 1. **Structural Inductive Bias**: Parameterizing actions as continuous functions provides an intrinsic C$^{\infty}$ continuity bias, which effectively filters out high-frequency quantization noise and ensures temporal coherence even without explicit velocity supervision.
 2. **Motion-Primitive Priors**: The model functions as a hierarchical spectral modulator over a learnable motion prior, predicting lightweight coefficients to deform this prior. This structured adaptation improves learning efficiency and robustness against compounding errors.

#### 4.2. Real-World Robot Experiments

- **Performance Comparison**: In real-world tasks with a Florence-2 Large backbone, NIAF outperformed BEAST and OFT, achieving 90% success on Item Placement (compared to 85% and 75%) and 80% on Cup Stacking (compared to 60% and 70%).
- **Portability to State-of-the-Art Backbones**: When integrated into the $\pi_{0.5}$ model (as $\pi_{0.5}$-NIAF), it outperformed $\pi_{0.5}$-BEAST and $\pi_{0.5}$-OFT on the precision-demanding Shape Insertion task, achieving 85% success (compared to 40% and 63%). Performance on Towel Folding was generally lower for all action-head replacements compared to the vanilla $\pi_{0.5}$, which was attributed to partial forgetting of pretrained policy knowledge.
- **Overall Real-World Gains**: These gains are attributed to SIREN's ability to generate smooth temporal profiles, reducing high-frequency jitter, and its analytical differentiability providing velocity references essential for impedance-style execution in contact-rich tasks.
- **Effectiveness of Analytic Physics-Informed Supervision**: Control dynamics visualizations for the Item Placement task showed that baselines (BEAST, OFT) operating under stiff position control exhibited high-frequency oscillations and poor temporal coherence in their velocity profiles, indicating disjointed micro-steps. In contrast, NIAF, trained with simultaneous position and velocity supervision, produced continuous, trend-following velocity references, allowing for stable impedance control with executed velocities closely aligning with the analytical signals. This demonstrates that explicit velocity supervision effectively encodes physically consistent motion flow.

#### 4.3. Extra Experiments (Appendix Summaries)

- **Arbitrary Action Sampling Rate**: Upsampling action chunks from 50 to 200 steps (4x frequency) resulted in visibly smoother velocity profiles, confirming NIAF's resolution independence and ability to mitigate discretization artifacts without retraining.
- **Water Cup Transport**: In a smoothness-sensitive task, NIAF (pos+vel) demonstrated significantly smoother position curves and minimal velocity fluctuations compared to NIAF (pos-only), BEAST (pos-only), and FAST (pos-only). This highlighted that explicit ground-truth velocity supervision is effective in preventing overfitting to teleoperation jitter and promoting smoother, physically compliant motions. Adding analytical jerk regularization (NIAF pos+vel+jerk) further smoothed profiles.
- **Fitting Comparison (SIREN vs. B-spline)**: Controlled experiments showed that SIREN accurately reconstructed fine-grained local corrections in both position and velocity, with jerk regularization further smoothing the velocity profile while retaining positional accuracy. B-splines, even with increased control points, exhibited coarse smoothness but underfit critical micro-adjustments due to their inherent low-pass filtering nature, demonstrating SIREN's architectural advantage in decoupling smoothness from expressiveness.
- **Ablation Studies**: Evaluations on CALVIN ABC→D identified optimal hyperparameters. An action chunk length of 10 provided the best balance. Increasing weight groups ($G$) monotonically improved performance, indicating more precise trajectory reconstruction. Replacing SIREN's sinusoidal activation with ReLU led to substantial degradation, emphasizing the importance of spectral fidelity. A SIREN depth of 3 was found to be optimal, balancing representational capacity with the available modulation signals.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of Neural Implicit Action Fields (NIAF) marks a significant methodological contribution to the field of robotic control, particularly within the context of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models.

**Significance:**

- **Fundamental Shift in Action Representation**: NIAF proposes a paradigm shift from discrete waypoint-based action prediction to a continuous, functional representation. This aligns the policy's output more closely with the continuous nature of physical motion, inherently providing a more physically consistent model.
- **Enhanced Control Fidelity through Analytical Derivatives**: By leveraging Sinusoidal Representation Networks (SIRENs), NIAF enables analytical differentiation of action trajectories. This allows for explicit supervision of velocity and regularization of higher-order derivatives (jerk), which provides mathematically consistent and noise-free kinematic profiles. This contrasts with traditional methods that rely on noisy numerical differentiation from discrete points.
- **Facilitation of Advanced Control Strategies**: The provision of analytically precise position and velocity references directly supports the implementation of stable impedance control. This capability is crucial for tasks requiring compliant interaction, where robots need to respond flexibly to environmental contact rather than following stiff, predefined position commands.
- **Resolution-Independent Action Generation**: The continuous functional representation allows for querying action trajectories at arbitrary temporal resolutions without retraining or introducing interpolation artifacts. This offers flexibility in adapting to diverse control frequencies in different robotic systems or tasks.
- **Efficient Learning via Spectral Modulation**: The architecture effectively uses MLLMs as hierarchical spectral modulators over learnable motion priors. This structured adaptation mechanism allows for efficient learning by predicting lightweight modulation coefficients that deform a robust kinematic backbone, enhancing learning efficiency and robustness against compounding errors.

**Potential Impact:**

- **Improved Robotic Task Performance**: The ability to generate smoother, more dynamically consistent, and compliant motions is expected to improve the success rates and robustness of robots in complex manipulation tasks, especially those involving delicate objects, fine precision, or contact-rich interactions (e.g., assembly, dexterous manipulation).
- **Broader Application of VLA Models**: By overcoming limitations related to control instability and rigid temporal discretization, NIAF could expand the practical applicability of VLA models to a wider range of real-world robotic scenarios and hardware platforms.
- **Advancement in Assistive and Collaborative Robotics**: Enhanced compliance and smoothness in control can be particularly beneficial for human-robot interaction and assistive robotics, where safety and natural motion profiles are paramount.
- **Foundation for Future Research**: The work establishes a new direction for action representation, potentially inspiring further research into implicit neural representations for other aspects of robotic control, planning, and state estimation.

**Limitations Acknowledged by the Authors**:
The authors note that NIAF primarily contributes to action representation and execution rather than high-level reasoning or zero-shot generalization of the base VLM. Its effectiveness in real-world settings relies on high-frequency FOC driver feedback for ground-truth velocity, which may not be available on all low-cost platforms. Finally, for simpler, short-horizon tasks with fixed control rates and no need for smooth velocity, discrete waypoint representations might remain competitive due to their implementation simplicity.
