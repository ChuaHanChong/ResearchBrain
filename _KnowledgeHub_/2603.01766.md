# Neural Implicit Action Fields: From Discrete Waypoints to Continuous Functions for Vision-Language-Action Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Neural Implicit Action Fields (NIAF) reformulates robotic action generation as continuous function regression using Sinusoidal Representation Networks (SIRENs), enabling analytical differentiation for velocity and jerk supervision. The system achieved an average chain length of 4.66 on CALVIN and a 97.9% success rate on LIBERO, while demonstrating 90% success on real-world Item Placement and 80% on Cup Stacking, outperforming discrete waypoint baselines.

## Problem

- Existing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models use discrete waypoint predictions, leading to rigid temporal discretization and fixed sampling rates.
- Lack of analytical differentiability in discrete action representations prevents explicit, mathematically consistent supervision of higher-order dynamics like velocity and jerk.
- Reliance on numerical differentiation for velocity from discrete positions introduces noise, limiting stable impedance control and often restricting robots to stiff position control.

## Method

- NIAF models action trajectories as continuous, vector-valued functions Φ(τ;θ)\Phi(\tau; \theta)Φ(τ;θ) over a normalized time domain using a Sinusoidal Representation Network (SIREN).
- A pre-trained multimodal large language model (MLLM) acts as a hierarchical spectral modulator, predicting modulation coefficients that dynamically reconfigure the SIREN's parameters.
- Leverages SIREN's infinite differentiability to analytically compute and explicitly supervise higher-order dynamics (velocity, jerk) alongside position, enabling stable impedance control.

## Results

- NIAF achieved superior performance in simulation, with an average chain length of **4.66** on CALVIN ABCD→D and a **97.9%** average success rate across LIBERO benchmarks.
- In real-world robotic tasks, NIAF outperformed baselines, achieving **90%** success on Item Placement and **80%** on Cup Stacking, and **85%** on precision-demanding Shape Insertion with state-of-the-art backbones.
- Analytically derived velocity supervision produced continuous, trend-following velocity references, enabling stable impedance control with executed velocities closely matching analytical signals, significantly reducing high-frequency oscillations observed in baselines.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Representing actions as continuous functions with SIRENs provides an intrinsic C∞^{\infty}∞ continuity bias, effectively filtering high-frequency noise and ensuring temporal coherence.
> - Analytical differentiation allows for precise and consistent supervision of position, velocity, and jerk, which is crucial for compliant physical interaction and stable impedance control.
> - A grouped hyper-modulation mechanism using MLLM latents to deform a learnable motion prior enhances learning efficiency and robustness against compounding errors.
