# UnrealZoo: Enriching Photo-realistic Virtual Worlds for Embodied AI

> [!summary] Summary
> UnrealZoo introduces a photo-realistic virtual world platform for Embodied AI, featuring over 100 diverse 3D environments and a rich set of playable entities. The platform optimizes simulation efficiency and reveals key limitations of current AI agents in complex, open-world scenarios, demonstrating improved policy transferability to other simulators and real-world settings.

## Problem

- Existing embodied AI simulators often provide limited environmental diversity and narrow focus, hindering the development of generalizable agents for real-world scenarios.
- A lack of diverse playable entities and robust multi-agent interaction capabilities restricts research into cross-embodiment and complex social intelligence.
- Current simulation tools require optimization for high-throughput, real-time, and multi-agent interactions crucial for effective reinforcement learning and large-scale training.

## Method

- **UnrealZoo** provides a collection of over 100 photo-realistic 3D virtual worlds, spanning various scales, styles, and dynamic elements built on ==Unreal Engine==.
- The platform incorporates seven types of playable entities, totaling 67 unique embodiments, each equipped with ego-centric sensing and customizable locomotion/navigation systems.
- An optimized programming interface, UnrealCV+, enhances rendering and communication efficiency, offering high-level Python APIs and an OpenAI Gym interface for ease of use and distributed training.

## Results

- UnrealCV+ significantly improved simulation efficiency, achieving FPS increases (e.g., **120%** for object masks) and multi-agent interaction gains (up to **100%** for 10 agents).
- Online RL agents attained moderate success in visual navigation (SR up to **0.52**), while GPT-4o performed poorly (SR **0.00**-**0.20**), both lagging far behind human performance (SR **1.00**).
- Agents trained on diverse environments showed higher generalization success rates in active visual tracking, with RL methods outperforming baselines and demonstrating more stable performance under active distractions.
- Policies trained in UnrealZoo, especially with diverse data, exhibited improved transferability to the ThreeDWorld simulator and real-world environments.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Current embodied AI methods, including large vision-language models, struggle with complex 3D scene reasoning, fine-grained control, and dynamic navigation in unstructured environments, showing a substantial gap compared to human performance.
> - Training with data from a diverse set of environments is crucial for achieving robust generalization in tasks like active visual tracking across various unseen scenarios.
> - Policies do not seamlessly generalize across different agent morphologies, and low-latency perception-control loops are essential for effective execution in dynamic, high-frequency control tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Embodied Artificial Intelligence (Embodied AI) aims to develop agents that can perceive, reason, plan, and act within physical or simulated environments, exhibiting intelligent behavior similar to biological organisms. A critical component for training and evaluating such agents is the availability of high-fidelity simulators. Existing simulators in the field, such as AI2-THOR, Habitat, ThreeDWorld (TDW), and CARLA, have contributed to advancements in specific domains like indoor navigation, robot manipulation, or autonomous driving.

However, a significant limitation of many current simulators is their often narrow focus on controlled indoor environments or highly specific outdoor scenarios (e.g., urban roads), which restricts the diversity and complexity of the challenges presented to AI agents. This limitation hinders the development of agents capable of robust generalization and adaptation to the varied conditions of real-world "open-world" environments, such as factories, public areas, and diverse natural landscapes. Such adaptability is crucial for real-world deployment of embodied AI systems.

The research landscape indicates a demand for simulators that offer:
- **Diverse 3D scenes and morphologies:** Essential for developing spatial intelligence, allowing agents to perceive, reason, plan, and act in complex 3D worlds.
- **Complex multi-agent interactions:** Important for fostering social intelligence, including theory of mind, negotiation, cooperation, and competition.
- **Realistic open-world challenges:** Necessary for efficient and effective evaluation of agents, identifying limitations, and reducing the risks associated with real-world deployment failures.
- **High-fidelity visual realism:** To bridge the sim-to-real gap, ensuring that agents trained in simulation can transfer their learned skills to real-world applications.

UnrealZoo positions itself within this landscape by addressing these identified gaps. It aims to provide a comprehensive, photo-realistic simulation platform with extensive environmental diversity and a wide range of playable entities, thereby pushing the boundaries of embodied AI research beyond confined settings towards more generalizable and adaptable agents. The work extends existing Unreal Engine-based tools, specifically UnrealCV, to facilitate high-throughput interactions and support complex embodied AI tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations for developing UnrealZoo are:

- **To provide a diverse collection of photo-realistic 3D virtual worlds:** The core motivation is to move beyond the confined indoor or specialized outdoor environments prevalent in existing simulators. UnrealZoo aims to offer over 100 distinct virtual worlds built on Unreal Engine, reflecting the complexity and variability of open-world scenarios. This diversity is considered fundamental for developing spatial intelligence and enabling agents to generalize their capabilities to unforeseen environments.

- **To offer a rich variety of playable entities:** To enable research into cross-embodiment generalization and heterogeneous multi-agent interactions, UnrealZoo seeks to include a range of entities such as humans, animals, robots, and vehicles. This allows for the investigation of how agents learn to interact with and navigate environments using different physical forms and alongside diverse social agents.

- **To enhance the efficiency and usability of simulation tools:** The authors identify that existing tools, specifically UnrealCV, require optimization for real-time, high-throughput interactions, especially in large-scale and multi-agent scenarios. A key objective is to extend UnrealCV with optimized APIs and tools (UnrealCV+) for data collection, environment augmentation, distributed training, and benchmarking, aiming for significant improvements in rendering and communication efficiency.

- **To facilitate the development of generalizable reinforcement learning (RL) agents:** By providing diverse and complex environments, UnrealZoo intends to serve as a comprehensive testing ground for training RL agents that can exhibit adaptability and robustness in open-world settings. The motivation is to identify the limitations of current embodied agents in unstructured terrain, adaptation to unseen morphologies, and managing latency in closed-loop control systems for dynamic objects.

- **To inspire new research challenges and pathways towards real-world deployment:** The authors aim for UnrealZoo to unveil previously overlooked challenges in embodied AI, thereby fostering research into more capable systems that can transition seamlessly from simulation to real-world applications. This includes addressing challenges in visual navigation, active tracking, and social interactions within highly dynamic and complex environments.

In essence, the motivation for UnrealZoo stems from the recognized gap between the capabilities of current embodied AI agents (often confined to specific, controlled environments) and the requirements for robust, generalizable agents capable of real-world deployment in diverse, dynamic, and open-world scenarios.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The development of UnrealZoo involved several integrated components, including scene collection, playable entity design, and a programming interface, all built upon the Unreal Engine.

#### 3.1. Simulator Foundation

UnrealZoo is constructed using **Unreal Engine 4 and 5**, leveraging its advanced graphics capabilities to render photo-realistic environments. It extends **UnrealCV**, an existing tool for computer vision research in Unreal Engine, with optimizations dubbed **UnrealCV+**.

#### 3.2. Scene Collection

The collection comprises **100 high-quality, photo-realistic 3D virtual worlds**.
- **Sourcing and Cost:** Scenes were acquired from the Unreal Engine Marketplace, accumulated over two years, with a reported cost exceeding $10,000. Selection criteria included public reviews and diversity to cover a wide range of styles.
- **Diversity and Scale:** Environments range significantly in scale, from single rooms (indoor) to entire landscapes (up to 16 km²). Categories include:
 - **Scene Categories:** Interior (houses, museums, supermarkets, factories), Exterior (ruins, islands, mountains), and mixed (46 scenes with both).
 - **Scale:** Indoor (15 scenes), Building (23 scenes), Community (28 scenes), and Landscape (35 scenes).
 - **Spatial Structure:** Tagged for features like multi-floor, topological, flat, and steep, to benchmark spatial intelligence.
 - **Dynamics:** Incorporates elements such as weather (sandstorms, snowfall, thunderstorms), fire, gas, fluid, and interactive objects (e.g., doors) to enhance visual and physical realism and test agent adaptability.
 - **Style:** Covers diverse cultural and historical styles, including Western, Asian, Middle Eastern, Medieval, Modern, and Science Fiction.
- **Accessibility:** Due to licensing restrictions, the projects are packaged into executable binaries for Windows, Linux, and macOS, allowing users to interact via Python interfaces without requiring Unreal Engine expertise.

#### 3.3. Playable Entities

UnrealZoo includes **seven distinct types of playable entities**, totaling 67 unique embodiments:
- **Types:** Humans (19), animals (27, e.g., dogs, horses, elephants), cars (3), motorbikes (3), drones (1 quad-copter), mobile robots (14 quadrupedal robots), and flying cameras.
- **Features:** Each entity includes a skeleton, meshes, textures, a local motion system, and a navigation system.
 - **Customization:** Attributes such as size, appearance, and camera positions can be modified via UnrealCV APIs. Entities can also switch between different textures.
 - **Ego-centric Sensing:** Each entity is equipped with an ego-centric camera capable of capturing RGB, depth, surface normal, and instance-level segmentation data.
 - **Locomotion System:** Built on "Smart Locomotion," an animation framework that provides natural movement and interaction (e.g., crouching, jumping, climbing). Interactions are animation-based rather than physics-based, focusing on high-level reasoning.
 - **Navigation System:** Utilizes NavMesh to enable autonomous navigation, path-finding, and obstacle avoidance. For urban maps, roads are segmented to differentiate pedestrian and vehicle pathways, allowing agents to prioritize routes based on area type.
- **Multi-agent Interaction:** The population of entities in a scene can be adjusted dynamically using spawn/destroy functions, facilitating multi-agent research.

#### 3.4. Programming Interface

The platform provides two main interfaces:
- **UnrealCV+:** An optimized version of UnrealCV designed for high-throughput interactions.
 - **Rendering Optimization:** Enables parallel processing for rendering object masks and depth images, improving Frames Per Second (FPS) in large scenes.
 - **Communication Protocol:** Introduces batch commands for simultaneous processing of multiple agent commands, reducing server-client communication latency.
 - **Stability:** Employs Interprocess Communication (IPC) sockets instead of TCP sockets for enhanced stability under high loads, crucial for reinforcement learning.
 - **Python APIs:** Provides high-level Python APIs that abstract UnrealCV's command systems into callable functions, simplifying environmental interaction and customization.
- **OpenAI Gym Interface:** Standardizes agent-environment interactions.
 - **Task Configuration:** Allows for task-specific configurations (e.g., reward functions, observation modalities, action spaces) via JSON files, facilitating easy customization and addition of new tasks.
 - **Toolkit:** Includes Gym wrappers for environment augmentation, population control, and time dilation (to adjust simulation control frequency).
 - **Distributed Training:** Features a launch tool to run multiple environments with specific GPU IDs for distributed online reinforcement learning.

#### 3.5. Experimental Approach

Experiments were conducted to demonstrate UnrealZoo's applicability and reveal challenges:
- **Efficiency Benchmarking:** FPS performance of UnrealCV+ was benchmarked across various commands and multi-agent scenarios.
- **Visual Navigation Task:**
 - **Environments:** Two unstructured scenes: "Roof" and "Industrial Area."
 - **Baselines:** Online Reinforcement Learning (A3C), GPT-4o (Large Vision-Language Model), and human control.
 - **Metrics:** Average Episode Length (EL), Success Rate (SR), and Success weighted by Path Length (SPL).
- **Active Visual Tracking Task:**
 - **Environments:** 16 unseen environments across four categories (Interior Scenes, Palaces, Wilds, Modern Scenes). Training data from "FlexibleRoom" (abstract) and various realistic scenes (1, 2, or 8 environments).
 - **Baselines:** PID controller, Offline Reinforcement Learning (CQL), OpenVLA (Vision-Language-Action model), and GPT-4o.
 - **Metrics:** Average Episodic Return (ER), Average Episode Length (EL), and Success Rate (SR).
- **Benchmarking at Variant Settings:**
 - **Active Distractions:** Evaluated tracking agents with varying numbers of human distractors (0D, 4D, 10D).
 - **Cross-Embodiment Generalization:** Tested policies trained on a human character on a quadrupedal robot dog.
 - **Control Frequency:** Used time dilation to simulate different control frequencies (3 FPS, 10 FPS, 30 FPS) for tracking tasks.
- **Cross-platform Evaluation:** Policies trained in UnrealZoo were also tested in the ThreeDWorld simulator and real-world scenarios to assess generalization.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted using UnrealZoo yielded several findings regarding the platform's capabilities and the performance of embodied AI agents in complex environments.

#### 4.1. UnrealCV+ Performance Efficiency

The optimized UnrealCV+ demonstrated notable improvements in rendering and communication efficiency compared to the original UnrealCV.
- **Image Capture:** UnrealCV+ achieved FPS increases for various image data types: RGB (12%), Object Mask (120%), Surface Normal (20%), and Depth (86%).
- **Multi-agent Interaction:** Performance for multi-agent interactions showed significant gains, with 100% improvement for 10 interacting agents, and 54% and 92% for 2 and 6 agents, respectively. These improvements facilitate training in large-scale, dynamic scenarios.

#### 4.2. Emergent Challenges in Visual Navigation

Evaluation of visual navigation agents in unstructured open-world scenes revealed limitations in current methods:
- **Reinforcement Learning (RL) Agents:** Online RL agents achieved moderate success (SR of 0.32 in "Roof" and 0.52 in "Industrial Area"). Their performance varied based on environmental complexity, indicating struggles with complex spatial structures and multi-level navigation.
- **Large Vision-Language Models (VLMs):** GPT-4o-based agents performed poorly, with success rates of 0.00 in "Roof" and 0.20 in "Industrial Area." This suggests that current VLMs face challenges in complex 3D scene reasoning and fine-grained control for navigation.
- **Human Performance Benchmark:** Human players demonstrated significantly superior performance, achieving a 1.00 success rate with substantially fewer steps, underscoring a considerable gap between current AI capabilities and human navigation in such environments.
- **Identified Challenges:** The task highlighted the necessity for agents to develop diverse locomotion skills (running, climbing, jumping, crouching), sophisticated 3D spatial reasoning, and effective real-time decision-making to navigate unstructured terrains.

#### 4.3. Impact of Environmental Diversity on Active Visual Tracking

Experiments on active visual tracking demonstrated the benefits of diverse training environments for agent generalization:
- **Generalization Improvement:** Agents trained with data from eight diverse environments ("8 Envs") consistently showed higher success rates across 16 unseen environments compared to those trained with fewer environments ("1 Env" or "2 Envs"). For instance, success rates in "Wilds" and "Palaces" categories significantly increased with greater training diversity.
- **Limitations of Abstract Training:** Training solely in an abstract environment like "FlexibleRoom" ("1 Env") resulted in lower generalization capabilities, indicating the importance of real-world scene complexity for robust learning.
- **Baseline Performance:** RL-based methods generally outperformed classical PID controllers, OpenVLA, and GPT-4o in tracking tasks, particularly in environments with complex spatial structures. OpenVLA and GPT-4o exhibited poor alignment and significant latency issues, limiting their real-time effectiveness.

#### 4.4. Benchmarking Agents at Variant Settings

UnrealZoo's toolkit enabled evaluation under varied conditions, revealing additional insights:
- **Robustness to Active Distractions:** RL-based agents (trained on 8 Envs) showed more stable tracking performance with increasing numbers of distractors (SR decreased from 0.76 with 0 distractors to 0.56 with 10 distractors). In contrast, PID, OpenVLA, and GPT-4o baselines struggled considerably, demonstrating susceptibility to occlusion and tracking the wrong targets, highlighting limitations of large models in dynamic, crowded scenarios.
- **Cross-Embodiment Generalization:** When policies trained on a human character were transferred to a quadrupedal robot dog, a notable decrease in tracking performance was observed (e.g., SR dropped from 0.68 to 0.48 with 4 distractors for RL 8 Envs). This indicates that current policies do not generalize seamlessly across different agent morphologies, suggesting a need for more focused research in this area.
- **Impact of Control Frequency:** Experiments with varying control frequencies demonstrated that lower FPS (e.g., 3 FPS) significantly degraded tracking performance (SR of 0.34) compared to higher frequencies (30 FPS, SR of 0.92) for RL agents. This finding emphasizes the critical role of low-latency perception-control loops for effective task execution in dynamic environments.

#### 4.5. Cross-platform Evaluation

Policies trained within UnrealZoo showed improved generalization and robustness when evaluated on an external simulator (ThreeDWorld) and in real-world settings, especially those trained on a diverse set of 8 environments. This suggests that the diversity provided by UnrealZoo contributes to the transferability of learned policies.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

UnrealZoo contributes to the field of embodied AI by offering a robust and comprehensive platform designed to address several critical limitations of existing simulation environments. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Advancing Generalizable Embodied AI:** By providing over 100 diverse, photo-realistic 3D virtual worlds spanning various scales, styles, and dynamic elements, UnrealZoo enables the training and evaluation of embodied AI agents that are more generalizable and adaptable to novel, open-world scenarios. This moves research beyond specialized or confined environments, which is crucial for real-world deployment.

- **Facilitating Research on Complex Interactions:** The inclusion of a rich variety of playable entities (humans, animals, robots, vehicles) and optimized multi-agent interaction capabilities allows researchers to investigate advanced topics such as cross-embodiment generalization and heterogeneous multi-agent social interactions. This can foster the development of agents with improved social intelligence and versatility.

- **Enhancing Simulation Efficiency and Usability:** The optimization of UnrealCV into UnrealCV+ with features like parallel rendering, batch commands, and IPC sockets significantly improves the efficiency of data collection and simulation throughput, which is vital for computationally intensive tasks like reinforcement learning. The provision of high-level Python APIs and a standard OpenAI Gym interface lowers the barrier to entry, making the platform accessible to a wider research community.

- **Identifying Current Agent Limitations:** The experimental results in visual navigation and active tracking tasks clearly articulate the persistent challenges faced by current embodied AI agents. These include difficulties in navigating unstructured terrain, adapting to unseen morphologies, robustly handling dynamic distractions, and managing latency in perception-control loops. By highlighting these specific limitations, UnrealZoo directs future research efforts toward critical areas of improvement.

- **Bridging the Sim-to-Real Gap:** The photo-realistic rendering capabilities of Unreal Engine, combined with the diverse environmental conditions, offer a more realistic training ground. The demonstrated transferability of policies to other simulators and real-world settings suggests UnrealZoo can serve as a valuable tool for developing agents that are more likely to perform effectively in physical environments, thereby aiding in safer pre-deployment evaluation.

- **Inspiring Novel Research Directions:** The platform is positioned to inspire new challenges and research avenues previously overlooked by less diverse simulators. This includes advancements in spatial intelligence, social intelligence, cross-embodiment learning, and adaptation to highly dynamic and complex environments.

In conclusion, UnrealZoo provides a foundational resource for the embodied AI community, designed to accelerate the development of more capable, robust, and generalizable AI systems. It represents a pathway toward developing agents that can operate effectively and harmoniously in complex, dynamic real-world settings.
