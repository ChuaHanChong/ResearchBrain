# DSWAM: A Dual-System World Action Foundation Model for Fine-Grained Robot Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> A dual-system World Action Foundation Model (DSWAM) integrates a physical execution-focused World Action Model (WAM) with an optional vision-language subtask planner for fine-grained robot manipulation. The system achieved a 96.3% success rate in real-world deformable manipulation, outperforming the DeMaVLA baseline by 3.8 percentage points, and demonstrates efficient real-time deployment.

## Problem

- Existing World Action Models (WAMs) for robot manipulation lack explicit language-level planning interfaces, making it difficult to decompose complex, multi-step tasks from coarse user commands.
- Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies often lack sufficient temporal context for modeling physical interactions, particularly in contact-rich or deformable manipulation tasks.
- The research community lacks fair, controlled real-robot comparisons between WAMs and VLAs, hindering objective evaluation of their respective execution capabilities due to differing experimental setups.

## Method

- A **dual-system architecture** is proposed, comprising a ==System 1 WAM executor== for efficient physical execution and an optional ==System 2 vision-language subtask planner== for high-level task decomposition.
- The WAM executor is designed to leverage ==video-based world modeling== for supervision during training but performs direct, efficient action prediction at inference, preventing computationally expensive future video generation.
- Deployment efficiency is enhanced through ==TensorRT acceleration== and asynchronous ==Real-Time Chunking (RTC)==, ensuring low-latency and continuous robot control for real-world applications.

## Results

- **DSWAM** achieved a **92.38%** average success rate in clean simulated bimanual manipulation tasks and **91.90%** in randomized settings on RoboTwin 2.0, outperforming the DeMaVLA baseline by over **5%** in randomized scenarios.
- In real-world deformable manipulation under matched conditions, DSWAM's WAM executor attained a **96.3%** average success rate, surpassing DeMaVLA by **3.8 percentage points** and reducing task completion time by **34 seconds**.
- Deployment optimizations, including TensorRT acceleration (**2.69x** speedup to **73.8 ms** latency) and asynchronous execution, improved real-robot task stability, increasing success rates (e.g., "Pants" from **70%** to **100%**) and reducing average completion times.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decoupling semantic task organization from physically grounded execution allows WAMs to maintain dexterity while processing and decomposing complex, multi-step instructions via an optional planner.
> - Under controlled, matched real-robot conditions, WAM-style video co-trained execution can outperform strong VLA baselines in challenging deformable manipulation tasks.
> - Providing optional subtask-level guidance from a high-level vision-language planner significantly improves execution stability and reduces error accumulation for complex tasks that benefit from decomposition.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation has seen the emergence of two primary paradigms for policy learning: Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies and World Action Models (WAMs). VLA policies, building on pre-trained vision-language backbones, translate visual observations and language instructions directly into robot actions. They excel at semantic grounding, planning, and instruction understanding, often providing a language-level interface for decomposing coarse instructions into subtasks. However, many VLA policies operate on a single-frame, observation-to-action mapping, which can limit their temporal context for modeling scene evolution during complex physical interactions.

WAMs, on the other hand, address this limitation by explicitly learning how visual states evolve under robot actions. They use video-based world modeling as dense supervision for action learning, providing world-aware representations of physical dynamics. This makes WAMs particularly adept at physically grounded, contact-rich manipulation tasks such as grasping, placing, and deformable object manipulation, where precise modeling of physical interactions is crucial. Despite their strength in execution, existing WAMs typically function primarily as execution policies and often lack the explicit language-level planning interface necessary for decomposing complex, multi-step household tasks from coarse user commands.

A further challenge in the research landscape is the difficulty in conducting fair, real-robot comparisons between VLA and WAM policies. Existing systems frequently differ in their underlying data sources, robot embodiments, and task protocols, which can confound performance evaluations and obscure the true advantages of one policy design over another.

This work, DSWAM, positions itself as an effort to bridge these gaps. It aims to integrate the strengths of both paradigms by developing a dual-system approach that combines the physical execution capabilities of a WAM with the semantic task organization of a vision-language planner. Furthermore, it seeks to provide a controlled real-robot comparison under matched experimental conditions to clearly assess the execution performance of a WAM-style policy against VLA policies in a standardized setting, particularly in the domain of deformable manipulation. The paper also contributes to the practical deployment of WAMs by focusing on inference efficiency for real-world robot control.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind the development of DSWAM are twofold: to address the "decomposition gap" in existing World Action Models (WAMs) and to facilitate a fair, controlled comparison between WAM and Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policy execution capabilities on real robots.

**Addressing the Decomposition Gap in WAMs:**
Existing WAMs demonstrate proficiency in physically grounded execution by leveraging video-based world modeling. This approach provides dense supervision for robot action learning, making them effective in tasks requiring accurate modeling of physical interactions, such as contact-rich or deformable manipulation. However, a significant limitation of these models is their typical lack of an explicit language-level planning interface. In contrast, VLM-based VLA policies naturally offer mechanisms for semantic grounding, planning, and instruction decomposition, which are crucial for complex, multi-step household tasks where coarse user commands must be broken down into a sequence of fine-grained, executable subtasks. The motivation here is to enable WAMs, known for their strong physical execution, to handle such complex, multi-stage goals without making high-level planning mandatory for every task. The goal is to design a system where semantic task organization can be decoupled from world-aware physical execution, allowing the WAM to function as a powerful execution engine that can optionally receive structured subtask guidance when needed.

**Enabling Fair Real-Robot Comparison between WAM and VLA Execution:**
The research landscape has lacked a definitive and fair real-robot comparison between the execution capabilities of VLA and WAM policies. This deficit arises because existing systems often differ across various experimental dimensions, including data sources, robot embodiments, and specific task protocols. These discrepancies make it challenging to attribute performance differences primarily to the underlying policy design rather than to variations in the experimental setup. The motivation is to establish a controlled evaluation environment where a WAM-style policy can be rigorously compared against strong VLA baselines, ensuring that differences in performance reflect the inherent strengths or weaknesses of the policy architectures themselves. This involves aligning critical experimental factors such as the robot platform, pre-training data, post-training data, and evaluation criteria. By doing so, the objective is to provide clearer empirical evidence regarding the relative merits of WAM-style video co-trained execution in real-world manipulation tasks, specifically focusing on deformable manipulation.

In summary, DSWAM aims to:
1. **Develop a Dual-System Architecture:** Integrate a System 1 WAM executor for efficient physical execution with an optional System 2 vision-language subtask planner for high-level task decomposition.
2. **Optimize WAM Inference for Real Robots:** Design the WAM executor to leverage world-modeling supervision during training while performing direct action prediction at inference time, avoiding computationally expensive future video generation. Implement technical optimizations (TensorRT acceleration, asynchronous execution, real-time chunking) to ensure practical, low-latency deployment.
3. **Provide a Controlled Real-Robot Benchmark:** Evaluate DSWAM under a matched real-world setting (DeMaVLA protocol) to offer a fair comparison of WAM execution capabilities against VLA policies, focusing on deformable manipulation.
4. **Demonstrate Benefits of Optional Decomposition:** Showcase how System 2's subtask-level supervision can enhance execution stability for complex instructions that benefit from decomposition, while maintaining efficiency for atomic tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

DSWAM employs a dual-system framework designed for fine-grained robot manipulation, integrating large-scale real-world robot training with an optional high-level planner and an efficient World Action Model (WAM) executor.

#### 3.1. Overview of the Dual-System Architecture

The core of DSWAM consists of two interconnected systems:
- **System 1 (WAM Executor):** This is the default control path, responsible for generating robot actions. It conditions on current multimodal observations (multi-view RGB images, language instruction or subtask, and proprioceptive state) to predict continuous action chunks. The executor uses a pre-trained video model and an action expert.
- **System 2 (Vision-Language Subtask Planner):** This is an optional high-level module, activated only when a command necessitates explicit decomposition. It takes a global task prompt and short-term visual history to predict a sequence of fine-grained, executable subtasks.

When System 2 is not activated, DSWAM operates in a WAM-only mode, with System 1 directly conditioning on the original prompt. If System 2 is activated, it provides the subtask instructions to System 1, which then executes them. This design allows for flexible task handling, where the high-level module focuses on semantic organization, and the low-level WAM executor handles contact-rich physical execution.

#### 3.2. Optional System 2 High-Level Planner

System 2 is implemented as a multimodal high-level planner, based on a Rynnbrain4B-style vision-language model.
- **Input:** The planner receives a global task prompt (p) and a short-term visual history (X_t) comprising the five most recent frames sampled at 1 Hz.
- **Output:** It predicts the next subtask-level instruction (s_t), which then serves as the language condition for System 1.
- **Training:** System 2 is trained using robot trajectory videos that are segmented into subtask-level intervals. A pre-trained vision-language annotator assigns subtask labels (s_k) and temporal boundaries (τ_k). Frames within segments are sampled at 1 FPS and grouped into sliding windows. The supervision target is transition-aware: it teaches the planner to predict the current subtask when the window is within it, and the *next* subtask when the window is the final one of the current nonterminal subtask. This is optimized using an autoregressive language modeling objective.
- **Deployment Coupling:** During deployment, System 2 communicates with System 1 through a synchronized execution protocol. The WAM executor sends a batch of five recent frames to the planner every 2 seconds (Δt = 2s). The subtask returned by the planner conditions the WAM executor until the next planner update. This enables the planner to adaptively guide the executor based on visual progress and the overall task.

#### 3.3. System 1 WAM Executor

The WAM executor is designed to leverage video-based world modeling benefits during training while ensuring efficient, direct action prediction at inference time.

#### 3.3.1. World Action Model Policy

- **Representation Learning:** The policy encodes current multimodal context (multi-view RGB images, language condition, proprioception) into latent world features (z_t).
- **Action Prediction:** From these latent features, the policy directly predicts continuous action chunks (A_t) using conditional flow matching. This method is suitable for bimanual manipulation, allowing for smooth action trajectory generation without discretizing robot control. The action expert is conditioned on the latent world features, proprioception, and the current subtask instruction.
- **Training Objective:** The executor is trained with a combined objective function that includes an action loss (L_act) and an auxiliary video co-training loss (L_vid). Both losses are formulated using flow matching.
 - `L_act`: Optimizes the prediction of action chunks (A_t).
 - `L_vid`: Optimizes the prediction of future latent visual tokens (V_t+1:t+T), providing world-modeling supervision.
 - The overall objective is `L = L_act + λ_vid L_vid`. Crucially, during training, the action prediction is prevented from using future visual tokens as privileged information, maintaining alignment with the inference-time setting where only current observations are available.

#### 3.3.2. Real-time Chunking and Deployment

To make the WAM executor practical for real-world robots, several deployment-oriented mechanisms are integrated:
- **Real-Time Chunking (RTC) and Asynchronous Execution:** DSWAM utilizes inference-time RTC (not training-time action conditioning) and asynchronous execution. This decouples policy queries from the robot's low-level control loop. A dedicated policy worker predicts new action chunks from the latest observations while the robot controller simultaneously executes the current chunk. This prevents policy inference latency from blocking continuous robot control.
- **TensorRT Acceleration:** The transformer-heavy execution path of the policy is compiled using NVIDIA TensorRT to reduce runtime latency. This compilation is split into two engines:
 - **Visual-context engine:** Builds per-layer cache from video latents, diffusion timestep, language/proprioceptive context, and visual attention mask.
 - **Action-denoising engine:** Consumes action latents, context, cached visual state, and joint video-action attention mask to predict denoising updates for the action chunk.
 This split optimizes the dominant computational components. The BF16 TensorRT path is used to balance speed and numerical precision, ensuring close agreement with the PyTorch reference while significantly improving query latency.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted with DSWAM span simulated environments (RoboTwin 2.0) and real-world robot tasks, evaluating its broad manipulation capability, performance in deformable manipulation, effectiveness of subtask supervision, and deployment efficiency.

#### 4.1. RoboTwin 2.0 Simulation Benchmark

DSWAM was evaluated on 50 bimanual manipulation tasks in the RoboTwin 2.0 benchmark under both clean (fixed initial configurations) and randomized (varied object poses and scene layouts) settings.
- **Overall Performance:** DSWAM achieved the highest average success rate (SR) across all compared methods. It obtained 92.38% average SR in the clean setting and 91.90% in the randomized setting.
- **Comparison with VLA Baselines:** DSWAM improved average SR by 3.96% in the clean setting and 5.12% in the randomized setting compared to DeMaVLA, which was identified as the strongest VLA baseline in this comparison. Relative to π0.5, DSWAM showed gains of 9.64% (clean) and 15.14% (randomized).
- **Comparison with WAM Baselines:** DSWAM also outperformed other WAM-style baselines, improving average SR by 0.50% (clean) and 0.12% (randomized) relative to Fast-WAM. These results indicate DSWAM's broad simulated manipulation capability and its enhanced robustness under randomized conditions.

#### 4.2. Matched Real-world Folding Benchmark

To provide a fair comparison with VLA policies, DSWAM was evaluated on the DeMaVLA household folding protocol, utilizing the same robot platform, pre-training data, post-training data, task protocol, and success criteria. Importantly, in this benchmark, DSWAM operated in WAM-only mode, with the System 2 planner disabled, focusing solely on the execution capability of the WAM executor.
- **Overall Performance:** DSWAM achieved an average SR of 96.3% and an average completion time of 1 minute 44 seconds (1′44′′).
- **Comparison with DeMaVLA:** DSWAM improved the average SR by 3.8 percentage points (from 92.5% to 96.3%) and reduced the average completion time by 34 seconds (from 2′18′′ to 1′44′′) compared to DeMaVLA.
- **Task-Specific Improvements:** The most significant gains were observed on the "Pants" folding task, where DSWAM increased SR from 75.0% to 90.0% and reduced completion time from 3′01′′ to 2′19′′. For "Skirts" and "Towels," DSWAM maintained 100.0% SR while substantially shortening rollout times. On "Shirts," it matched DeMaVLA’s 95.0% SR with slightly faster completion.
This controlled study provides empirical evidence for the effectiveness of WAM-style policy execution in real-world deformable manipulation tasks under conditions matched with strong VLA references.

#### 4.3. Optional System 2 Subtask Supervision Study

The impact of the optional System 2 planner was investigated using a real-world tabletop sorting task with the coarse instruction "Sort objects on the table." This task was designed to benefit from decomposition.
- **Impact of Subtask Supervision:** When the coarse instruction was decomposed into subtask-level instructions (e.g., "pick up the toy car and place it into the box"), the execution stability improved.
- **Quantitative Results:** Averaged over evaluation settings, using raw task instructions resulted in 75.7% SR with 3.53 mistakes per rollout. In contrast, using subtask-level instructions led to 100.0% SR and reduced mistakes to 0.65 per rollout.
These findings support the design choice of an optional System 2, demonstrating that explicit subtask context can guide the WAM executor effectively and reduce error accumulation for tasks benefiting from decomposition.

#### 4.4. Efficiency and Deployment

The practical deployment efficiency of DSWAM was evaluated, considering its inference-time action prediction (without future-video generation), TensorRT acceleration, and asynchronous Real-Time Chunking (RTC).
- **TensorRT Acceleration:** BF16 TensorRT compilation reduced warmed end-to-end policy inference latency from 198.2 ms (PyTorch) to 73.8 ms on an NVIDIA GeForce RTX 5090, achieving a 2.69x speedup. This was achieved while maintaining high action agreement (0.99977 Cosine Similarity) with the PyTorch reference.
- **Asynchronous TensorRT+RTC on Real Robots:** Real-robot rollouts on easy garment instances compared synchronous TensorRT execution with asynchronous TensorRT+RTC.
 - Asynchronous execution maintained 100% SR on "Shirt" and improved "Pants" from 70% to 100% SR.
 - It also reduced average success times on both tasks (e.g., "Shirt" from 1′47′′ to 1′28′′; "Pants" from 1′50′′ to 1′08′′).
These results indicate that the combination of efficient WAM inference, TensorRT acceleration, and asynchronous execution significantly enhances deployment practicality and improves execution stability for continuous, contact-rich manipulation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

DSWAM introduces a notable architectural contribution to the field of robot manipulation by proposing a dual-system framework that addresses key limitations of existing paradigms. Its significance lies in several areas:

**Bridging the Gap between Physical Execution and High-Level Planning:** By decoupling semantic task organization from physically grounded execution, DSWAM allows World Action Models (WAMs) to retain their strength in fine-grained physical manipulation while gaining the ability to process and decompose complex, multi-step instructions via an optional vision-language planner. This flexible approach avoids making high-level planning mandatory for atomic tasks, enhancing overall efficiency and adaptability for diverse household and industrial scenarios. This could lead to robots that are both highly dexterous in physical interaction and capable of understanding and executing complex human commands.

**Establishing a Controlled Benchmark for Policy Evaluation:** The paper's commitment to a matched real-robot comparison, using identical robot platforms, training data, task protocols, and evaluation criteria as VLA policies (specifically DeMaVLA), provides valuable empirical evidence. This controlled study mitigates confounding factors that have historically obscured direct comparisons between WAMs and VLAs. The finding that DSWAM's WAM executor can outperform strong VLA references in deformable manipulation under these matched conditions offers a clearer understanding of the distinct advantages of WAM-style video-co-trained execution in challenging physical interaction tasks. This could inform future policy design choices and guide resource allocation in robot learning research.

**Advancing Practical Deployment of WAMs:** DSWAM demonstrates a pragmatic approach to deploying computationally intensive WAMs on real robots. By moving world-modeling supervision to training time and performing direct action prediction at inference, combined with engineering optimizations like TensorRT acceleration, asynchronous execution, and real-time chunking, the system achieves practical, low-latency control. This addresses a major bottleneck for WAMs, making them more viable for real-world applications where real-time responsiveness is critical. The efficiency improvements contribute to the broader goal of developing deployable generalist robot manipulation systems.

**Impact on Deformable Manipulation and Generalist Robotics:** The strong empirical results, particularly in the challenging domain of deformable object manipulation (e.g., garment folding), highlight DSWAM's potential for tasks that require precise and adaptive interaction with non-rigid objects. This capability is crucial for household robotics, manufacturing, and other domains where handling deformable materials is common. Furthermore, its broad success across 50 bimanual tasks in simulation suggests its potential as a scalable foundation model for general robot manipulation.

Overall, DSWAM contributes to the development of more capable, robust, and deployable robot foundation policies. It suggests a promising direction for future research that integrates the best aspects of different robot learning paradigms, moving closer to robots that can reliably perform complex, fine-grained manipulation in unstructured real-world environments.
