# Kitchen Robotic Manipulation utilizing Foundation Models

> [!summary] Summary
> A modular perception pipeline is developed for robotic manipulation of dishware in kitchen settings, integrating multiple visual and geometric foundation models for robust 6D pose estimation. The system achieves 88.92% ADI for pose estimation and an 87.50% task success rate across real-robot trials for tasks like sink-to-dishwasher transfer and cup stacking, operating without environment-specific training or fine-tuning.

## Problem

- Traditional vision-based robotic systems struggle with generalization in unstructured, dynamic human environments due to reliance on task-specific datasets and limited adaptability.
- Deploying robots in real-world settings requires robust perception that can handle diverse, changing conditions and novel objects without extensive manual intervention or environment-specific training.
- Accurate 6D pose estimation is critical for precise manipulation tasks (e.g., stacking, placing), but challenging in cluttered and occluded real-world scenarios.

## Method

- A **modular perception pipeline** integrates multiple visual and geometric foundation models for object recognition, 6D pose estimation, and grasp candidate generation in multi-view RGB-D images.
- The approach employs a ==2D-3D feature fusion== strategy, combining visual features from models like ==DINOv2== with geometric features from models like ==GeoTransformer== for robust object classification and 6D pose estimation.
- Predefined grasps based on known 3D CAD models are filtered for kinematic validity and collision avoidance, enabling a robot to execute manipulation tasks like dishware transfer and stacking.

## Results

- The optimal pipeline configuration (LLMDet + SAMv2 + DINOv2 + GeoTransformer) achieved **88.92%** ADI for 6D pose estimation, improving to **89.12%** with ICP refinement, outperforming FoundationPose (**87.32%**).
- The system demonstrated an **87.50%** overall task success rate across **296** real-robot trials in varied kitchen environments, successfully performing tasks such as sink-to-dishwasher transfer and cup stacking.
- The perception and planning components operated efficiently, with 3D map construction and instance segmentation taking **1.9 seconds** and pose/grasp estimation averaging **190 ms** per object, resulting in a worst-case total runtime of approximately **7.7 seconds**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The 2D-3D feature fusion strategy consistently enhances 6D pose estimation performance, with the combination of DINOv2 and GeoTransformer achieving the highest accuracy due to their complementary strengths.
> - Open-Vocabulary Object Detection models (e.g., LLMDet) provide superior initial detections for instance segmentation, which is crucial for subsequent cross-view consistency and 3D map reconstruction.
> - A modular system design allows for systematic evaluation and selection of optimal foundation model combinations, offering flexibility and adaptability to evolving models without requiring end-to-end retraining.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The integration of robotic systems into everyday human environments, such as kitchens, constitutes a significant challenge in robotics. While advancements in robotic technologies, including plug-and-play systems and affordable hardware, have begun to lower deployment barriers, the critical hurdle remains robust and adaptable perception in unstructured and dynamic settings.

Traditional vision-based robotic systems often rely on task-specific datasets and supervised learning. This approach limits their generalization capabilities beyond predefined environments, thereby restricting their utility in real-world scenarios. The broader research landscape has increasingly turned to foundation models, which are pre-trained on vast datasets using self-supervised learning, to address these generalization limitations. These models have demonstrated an ability to recognize and interact with novel surroundings without extensive retraining, making them relevant for robotic perception.

Within this context, existing research has explored foundation models for various robotic perception tasks:
- **Object Recognition and Pose Estimation:** Models like Segment Anything Model (SAM) and its extensions (e.g., SAM-6D, FoundationPose) have been applied to image segmentation and 6D pose estimation. FoundationGrasp addresses generalizable grasp detection.
- **3D Scene Understanding:** Depth Anything provides robust monocular depth estimation, while VFMM3D and DUSt3R contribute to 3D object detection and dense geometric reconstruction.
- **Mobile Robotics Integration:** Foundation models have been used as front-ends for scene understanding in mobile robotic systems to facilitate navigation or convert object masks into grasping commands.
- **Robotic Manipulation Pipelines:** Works like OK-Robot and TidyBot combine open-vocabulary detectors with grasping models or LLMs for household pick-and-place, often relying on per-environment scans or user rules. End-to-end vision-language-action policies (e.g., RT-2, OpenVLA, Octo) train models to map images and language directly to actions, demonstrating generalization across tasks and robot embodiments.

This current work differentiates itself by focusing on a system-level integration of *multiple* visual and geometric foundation models into a unified 6D-pose perception pipeline. Unlike end-to-end policies, this approach maintains separate modules for perception and control, allowing for greater flexibility with different motion planners or sensor configurations without retraining. Furthermore, it specifically targets inventory-based kitchen manipulation, leveraging the assumption of known 3D CAD models for target objects, and aims to operate *without environment-specific training or fine-tuning*, which distinguishes it from methods requiring per-environment scans or personalized rules. The modular design, enabling systematic evaluation of model combinations, is a key contribution to understanding how foundation models can be effectively deployed in practical robotic systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a robust and adaptable perception system for robotic manipulation in dynamic, unstructured human environments, specifically focusing on dishware handling in kitchen settings. The key motivations underlying this work are:

- **Addressing the Challenge of Real-World Robotic Deployment:** Deploying robots in human environments necessitates perception systems that can handle diverse, constantly changing conditions and unfamiliar surroundings without extensive manual intervention or environment-specific training. Traditional approaches often fall short in this regard due to their reliance on task-specific datasets and limited generalization.
- **Leveraging Foundation Models for Generalization:** The emergence of foundation models, with their demonstrated generalization capabilities across domains, presents an opportunity to overcome the limitations of traditional vision systems. The authors are motivated to investigate how these models can be systematically integrated into robotic perception pipelines to enable robust performance without the need for extensive retraining.
- **Developing a Modular and Flexible Perception Pipeline:** A core objective is to design a modular perception pipeline where individual components, particularly visual and geometric foundation models, can be systematically substituted. This modularity is intended to facilitate comprehensive evaluation, allowing for the identification of optimal configurations that balance performance, efficiency, and adaptability.
- **Enabling Precise Manipulation through 6D Pose Estimation:** For tasks requiring precise spatial interaction, such as placing dishes into a dishwasher rack or stacking cups, accurate 6D pose knowledge is critical. The pipeline is specifically designed to provide these precise pose estimates to enable subsequent manipulation steps with high accuracy.
- **Focusing on Inventory-Based Kitchen Tasks:** The work is motivated by the characteristics of kitchen environments, particularly dishware. These items often fall into a limited number of categories, are part of standardized sets, and constitute a relatively stable inventory. This context allows for the assumption of available 3D CAD models, which in turn enables the use of predefined stable grasp configurations, crucial for higher manipulation success rates in precision tasks.
- **Validating Scalability and Practicality in Real-World Scenarios:** A significant motivation is to demonstrate the adaptability and scalability of the proposed pipeline on physical robots in varied kitchen environments. This includes executing complex tasks like sink-to-dishwasher transfer and cup stacking, thereby validating its potential as a practical framework for household automation without environment-specific fine-tuning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research presents a modular perception pipeline designed for inventory-based kitchen manipulation tasks, specifically dishware handling. The approach leverages multiple foundation models and a 2D-3D feature fusion strategy.

**A. Overview of the Perception Pipeline (Fig. 2)**
The robotic system, equipped with an RGB-D camera on its end-effector, captures sequential RGB-D images from precomputed waypoints. The pipeline then processes this data through several stages to achieve object recognition, 6D pose estimation, and grasp candidate generation:
1. **Multi-View Instance Segmentation:** Detects and segments individual objects consistently across multiple camera views.
2. **Instance-Aware 3D Map Reconstruction:** Builds a 3D semantic map of the workspace, associating each point with an object instance ID.
3. **Object Classification and 6D Pose Estimation:** Utilizes a fused 2D-3D feature representation to classify objects and estimate their 6D poses.
4. **Grasp Estimation for Robot Manipulation:** Generates feasible grasp configurations based on estimated object poses and predefined CAD model grasps.

**B. Detailed Steps**

1. **Instance Segmentation across Multi-View Images:**
 - **Initial Detection:** An Open-Vocabulary Object Detection (OVOD) method (e.g., LLMDet, GroundingDINO) processes each RGB image using a text prompt (e.g., "dishware") to generate bounding boxes.
 - **Segmentation:** The detected bounding boxes serve as prompts for a segmentation model (e.g., SAMv2) to produce instance masks for each object in each image.
 - **Cross-View Consistency:** To ensure masks correspond to the same physical object across different views, masks from one view are projected onto others using depth images and camera poses. Intersection over Union (IoU) is computed between projected and detected masks. If the IoU exceeds a predefined threshold, masks are assigned the same object ID. A bidirectional cross-check enhances robustness.

2. **Instance-Aware 3D Map Reconstruction:**
 - **Input:** RGB-D image sets, instance masks, and camera poses.
 - **Initial Alignment:** Coarse 3D point cloud registration, based on feature matching (e.g., KISS-Matcher), is applied to align RGB-D frames.
 - **Refinement:** Iterated Closest Point (ICP) refinement is used for precise alignment, correcting potential pose errors from the manipulator or calibration.
 - **Semantic Augmentation:** Instance IDs from the segmentation masks are assigned to the points in the fused 3D map, creating an instance-aware semantic map, represented as `(x, y, z, id)`.

3. **Object Classification and 6D Pose Estimation:**
 - This stage involves a feature fusion strategy followed by a three-step process: database preparation, object candidate proposal, and object registration.
 - **2D and 3D Feature Fusion (Algorithm 1):**
 - A visual foundation model (Φ) extracts class and patch tokens from cropped RGB images of each segmented object instance. These patch tokens are then projected into 3D space using depth information and camera poses. Voxel pooling aggregates these into per-point image features (`ˆF_I`).
 - In parallel, a geometric foundation model (Ψ) computes point-level features (`F_P`) directly from the object's point cloud.
 - Both feature streams (`ˆF_I` and `F_P`) are L2-normalized and concatenated (Eq. 5) to form a unified per-point feature representation (`F`). This simple concatenation strategy is chosen to preserve information from both modalities without requiring additional training.
 - **Database Preparation:** A database is created by rendering synthetic views of known 3D CAD models of target objects. For each model, class-level tokens, patch-level tokens, and fused features (`P_ref`, `F_ref`) are extracted and stored.
 - **Object Candidate Proposal:** For a query object instance, its image feature representation (concatenation of class and patch tokens) is compared against the database using cosine similarity. The top-K models with scores exceeding a threshold are selected as potential matches.
 - **Object Registration:**
 - Coarse alignment is performed between the query point cloud (`P_query`) and the model points (`P_ref`) using correspondence-based registration in the fused feature space (`F_query`, `F_ref`).
 - Alignment quality is evaluated using bidirectional overlap ratios (Eq. 6-8), which measure the fraction of points in one cloud within a threshold distance of points in the other.
 - Candidates with insufficient correspondences or low overlap are discarded. The best candidate receives a preliminary score.
 - Finally, ICP is applied to the best candidate to refine its 6D pose, minimizing misalignment between the transformed model point cloud and the reconstructed 3D map.

4. **Grasp Estimation for Robot Manipulation:**
 - **Predefined Grasps:** Given the availability of 3D CAD models, predefined grasp candidates are established for each object type, adaptable to the robot's gripper.
 - **Feasibility Checks:** A multi-stage filtering process refines these candidates:
 - Objects severely occluded by others are discarded.
 - Inverse kinematics evaluates the reachability of each candidate.
 - Candidates that would grasp multiple objects simultaneously are removed.
 - Motion planning (using RRT-Connect and FCL for collision checking) eliminates grasps that would cause environmental collisions or require infeasible manipulator motions.
 - This process yields kinematically valid and physically executable grasp configurations.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of the proposed perception pipeline involved extensive testing on a custom kitchen dataset and real-world robot demonstrations.

**A. Experiment Setup**
Experiments utilized a custom robotic system featuring a 7-DoF manipulator with an end-effector mounted RGB-D camera. Hand-eye calibration achieved mean rotational and translational residuals of 0.614 degrees and 3.44 mm, respectively. Two kitchen environments (Fig. 3) were used to assess generalizability, with multi-view RGB-D observations collected from three predefined end-effector poses. The pipeline’s modularity was exploited to test 24 combinations of two OVOD methods (LLMDet, GroundingDINO), three visual foundation models (DINOv2, BEiT, CLIP), and four geometric foundation models (GeDI, FPFH, BufferX, GeoTransformer). All learned networks were used without environment-specific training or fine-tuning.

**B. Dataset and Evaluation Metric**
A custom dataset of 20 real-world scenes was created, captured in two kitchen environments, focusing on sink and counter spaces. Scenes varied in object arrangement, clutter, occlusion, and sink material, encompassing typical challenges in dishware handling. Each scene was manually annotated with object masks and 6D poses. The Average Distance of model points for Indistinguishable views (ADI) was used as the primary evaluation metric, suitable for the symmetrical objects in the dataset.

**C. Analysis of the Perception Pipeline**

1. **Performance Analysis of Foundation Model Combinations (Table I):**
 - **OVOD Performance:** LLMDet consistently outperformed GroundingDINO. This was attributed to LLMDet's training with both image-level long captions and region-level short phrases, providing stronger generalization and leading to more accurate bounding boxes and consistent SAMv2 prompts.
 - **Without Feature Fusion:**
 - **Visual Models (for Candidate Proposal):** DINOv2 exhibited superior performance, achieving features (e.g., 88.19% ADI with GeDI) compared to BEiT and CLIP. This was attributed to DINOv2's reported ability to produce discriminative patch-level (fine-grained) features, which are effective for localized object appearance matching.
 - **Geometric Models (for Object Registration):** GeDI provided the most effective local descriptors when used alone (e.g., 88.19% ADI with DINOv2), enabling robust registration in cluttered conditions. GeoTransformer, which emphasizes global geometric context, showed comparatively inferior performance in this setting.
 - **With Feature Fusion:**
 - The 2D–3D feature fusion strategy consistently improved performance across all configurations. This validated the hypothesis that image features (semantic/appearance cues) and point features (structural details) provide complementary information.
 - **Most Significant Improvement:** GeoTransformer showed the largest performance gains with fusion, indicating that its global geometric modeling capability was significantly enhanced by the integration of discriminative image features.
 - **Best Configuration:** The combination of **LLMDet + SAMv2 + DINOv2 + GeoTransformer** achieved the highest ADI of **88.92%** without ICP refinement. This configuration capitalized on the synergy between DINOv2's fine-grained semantic cues and GeoTransformer's reinforced global geometric modeling.

2. **ICP Refinement and Comparison with FoundationPose (Table II):**
 - Applying ICP refinement to the best-performing configuration (LLMDet + SAMv2 + DINOv2 + GeoTransformer) resulted in a modest increase in accuracy to **89.12% ADI**.
 - When compared to FoundationPose, the proposed pipeline achieved higher accuracy (89.12% vs. 87.32%). This was observed even when FoundationPose was provided with ground-truth object masks, a condition not typically available in real-world deployments.

3. **Qualitative Results:**
 - Figures 4 and 5 illustrate the pipeline's effectiveness in reconstructing 3D maps and estimating object poses accurately in diverse kitchen scenes, including a second kitchen environment to demonstrate adaptability.

4. **Sensitivity Analysis of Key Hyperparameters (Fig. 6):**
 - **Voxel Size:** Performance (ADI) was stable between 5 mm and 10 mm. A voxel size of **10 mm** was selected as optimal, providing 0.8892 ADI at 190.5 ms per object, balancing accuracy and computational cost.
 - **Top-K Candidates:** The ADI peaked at **K=3**. Lower K values risked pruning correct candidates, while higher K values could introduce incorrect, visually similar candidates that might deceive the registration process.
 - **IoU Threshold (Cross-View Mask Association):** The optimal mIoU (0.8728) was achieved at a threshold of **0.6**. This value balanced avoiding merging masks from different objects (low threshold issue) and rejecting valid correspondences due to noise/viewpoint variations (high threshold issue).

**D. Real-World Full System Demonstrations**

1. **Tasks Performed (Fig. 7):**
 - Transferring dishware from a sink to a dishwasher.
 - Cup stacking on a counter.
 - Simple grasping of dishware from a sink.
 - These demonstrations were performed without environment-specific retraining across different kitchen environments.

2. **Quantitative Evaluation (Table III):**
 - The pipeline achieved an overall success rate of **87.50%** across 296 trials, with individual task success rates of 90.00% (First Kitchen simple grasping), 90.54% (Second Kitchen simple grasping), and 84.09% (Cup Stacking).

3. **Failure Analysis:**
 - The majority of failures (predominantly in cup stacking) were attributed to **gripper slip** during grasp execution, especially with the compliant Fin-Ray Gripper, which has limited maximum grasp force.
 - Occasional failures also resulted from **inaccurate detections** leading to incorrect grasp locations.
 - In cluttered scenarios like dense cup stacking, **contact with neighboring objects** during gripper closure could displace the target cup, causing grasp failure.

4. **Runtime Analysis:**
 - 3D map construction and instance segmentation (from three scanned images): 1.9 seconds (average).
 - Pose and grasp estimation: 190 ms per object (scales linearly). For 20 objects (worst case), approximately 3.8 seconds.
 - Motion planning: 2 seconds (average).
 - Overall worst-case runtime: Approximately **7.7 seconds**. Runtime decreases as objects are removed from the workspace.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a framework for enhancing robotic manipulation capabilities in unstructured human environments, particularly kitchens, and holds several areas of significance and potential impact:

- **Enabling Robust and Adaptable Robotic Perception:** The work demonstrates a successful system-level integration of multiple visual and geometric foundation models into a unified 6D-pose perception pipeline. This addresses a critical need for robotic systems to operate effectively in diverse and dynamic real-world settings without requiring environment-specific training. This generalizability is key to broader robot deployment.
- **Advancing Household Automation:** By successfully performing tasks like sink-to-dishwasher transfer and cup stacking on physical robots without retraining, the pipeline showcases a practical framework for household automation. This directly contributes to the vision of assistive robotic systems in homes.
- **Modular and Flexible Design for Practitioners:** The modular architecture is a notable contribution, allowing systematic substitution and evaluation of different foundation models. This flexibility empowers researchers and practitioners to tailor model combinations based on specific constraints (e.g., computational resources, task difficulty), facilitating optimization and adaptation to evolving foundation models.
- **Establishing a Baseline for Foundation Model Integration:** By systematically evaluating 24 combinations of visual and geometric foundation models and identifying an optimal configuration, the work provides empirical insights into how these advanced models interact and contribute to a complex robotic perception task. This serves as a valuable baseline for future research on foundation model integration in robotics.
- **Overcoming Limitations of Traditional Methods:** The demonstrated performance, particularly outperforming a dedicated 6D pose estimation method like FoundationPose even under favorable conditions for the latter, highlights the efficacy of the proposed approach in handling cluttered and occluded conditions typical of real-world environments.
- **Identifying Future Research Directions:** The detailed failure analysis and discussion of limitations, such as gripper design, complex manipulation skills, motion planning optimization, and reliance on CAD models, provide clear avenues for future research. Addressing these limitations could lead to even more capable and versatile household robotic systems, pushing towards less constrained operation and self-acquisition of object models.

In summary, this work provides a practical, adaptable, and scalable framework for robotic manipulation in kitchen environments, leveraging the generalization capabilities of foundation models. Its modular design and robust experimental validation contribute to the ongoing development of robots capable of operating effectively in everyday human spaces.
