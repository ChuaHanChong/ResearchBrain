# Embodiment Meets Environment: Toward Context-Aware, Safe Physical Caregiving Robots

> [!summary] Summary
> E2-CARE is a framework that enables physical caregiving robots to achieve context-aware, safe adaptation across diverse environments and robot embodiments by dynamically synthesizing and enforcing interaction constraints from a unified 3D scene graph. It achieves high task success and constraint satisfaction rates in simulations and is perceived as significantly safer and more appropriate in real-world human-robot interaction studies.

## Problem

- Existing physical caregiving robots are tightly coupled to specific environments or hardware, limiting their adaptability and requiring extensive reconfiguration for new contexts.
- Many systems lack explicit human-awareness, failing to adequately model or constrain interactions in close proximity, which is critical for human safety and social appropriateness.
- The safety and effectiveness of robot actions are highly context-dependent (joint interaction of robot embodiment, tools, environment, and human), a nuance often overlooked by prior approaches.

## Method

- **E2-CARE** employs a ==3D dynamic scene graph== to unify the representation of the environment, robot embodiment (including tools), and humans, explicitly modeling their spatio-temporal relationships.
- An ==LLM-based module== analyzes the task-relevant scene graph and user preferences to synthesize both hard safety constraints and soft task preferences for each primitive skill.
- These constraints are enforced at runtime using an ==Operational-Space Control Barrier Function (OSCBF)== filter, which adaptively reshapes skill execution to guarantee safety while satisfying preferences.

## Results

- In simulations, E2-CARE achieved approximately **95%** task success and **80%** constraint satisfaction rates across **130** diverse household environments and **five** different robot embodiments, outperforming baselines by **15-40 percentage points**.
- A real-world user study demonstrated that E2-CARE significantly improved perceived safety and behavioral appropriateness in social dining and collaborative cooking tasks with human participants.
- The system operates in real-time, with the CBF-based control filter adding negligible overhead of about **0.15ms**, ensuring rapid and safe responses to dynamic environments and human motion.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly modeling the joint interaction between robot embodiment (and tools), the environment, and humans via a unified scene graph is fundamental for achieving context-aware safety and adaptation.
> - Treating primitive skills as adaptable "interaction templates" that are dynamically reshaped by runtime constraints enables zero-shot reuse across diverse environments and robot embodiments without retraining.
> - Utilizing control barrier functions allows for certified safety guarantees while adaptively modifying skill execution to satisfy task preferences, offering a robust approach in safety-critical, human-centered robotics.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of physical caregiving robots addresses a significant societal need to assist individuals with limited mobility in performing activities of daily living (ADLs) across various environments. Prior research has made progress on individual caregiving tasks such as feeding, grooming, dressing, bathing, and meal preparation. These efforts typically involve designing task-specific robotic systems that often achieve reliable performance within tightly controlled conditions. However, a pervasive limitation in this field is the tight coupling of existing systems to specific environments, robot embodiments, or a predefined set of tools. This specificity often necessitates extensive manual reconfiguration or redesign when the context changes, such as moving to a different room or using a new robot platform.

Beyond task execution, the safe and appropriate interaction with humans is a critical, yet often underspecified, aspect of physical caregiving. Many current systems do not explicitly model or constrain interactions involving people, treating humans either as static obstacles or implicitly relying on predetermined safe distances. This approach is insufficient for caregiving scenarios where robots must operate in close proximity to, and actively interact with, individuals. The authors argue that safety and effectiveness are not solely functions of environmental understanding or robot embodiment alone, but rather emerge from the *joint interaction* between the robot's specific embodiment (including any tools it carries) and the dynamic environment, especially in the presence of humans. For example, the danger posed by a robot holding a knife depends on its orientation relative to a nearby person, not just the presence of the human or the tool in isolation.

The broader robotics field has explored approaches to robot manipulation that aim for adaptation across environments and embodiments. These include traditional task-and-motion planning (e.g., PDDL-based systems), modular skill libraries, and more recently, large-scale learning-based models like Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. While VLA models have shown promise in general manipulation tasks, their deployment in human-centered caregiving settings faces significant challenges. Current VLA training datasets typically lack human presence and diverse human-robot interaction data, making it difficult for these models to generalize to less structured, dynamic, and safety-critical caregiving contexts. The financial cost, time commitment, and safety risks associated with collecting large-scale, diverse human-robot interaction datasets for caregiving further impede the development of purely data-driven foundational models in this domain.

This work, E2-CARE, positions itself as a response to these limitations by advocating for an explicit, context-aware adaptation mechanism. Instead of relying solely on implicit data coverage or fixed, environment-specific designs, E2-CARE proposes to explicitly represent the environment, robot embodiment, and human within a unified scene graph. This representation facilitates the dynamic synthesis of interaction constraints that reshape primitive skills at runtime. This approach aims to enable zero-shot adaptation and reuse of skills across varied contexts, thereby addressing the generalization and safety challenges inherent in real-world physical caregiving scenarios, particularly by integrating explicit human-robot interaction safety into the core control framework.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the inherent complexities and safety demands of physical caregiving robotics. Robots assisting individuals with limited mobility must operate in diverse, dynamic household environments, perform a variety of ADLs, and adapt to different user needs. Furthermore, robots may come in various physical embodiments or change their effective embodiment by equipping different tools (e.g., a spoon for feeding, a knife for meal preparation).

The authors identify several critical gaps in existing robotic systems that hinder their effective and safe deployment in caregiving contexts:
1. **Limited Adaptability:** Most current caregiving robot systems are tightly coupled to specific environments or robot hardware configurations. This rigidity necessitates significant manual reconfiguration, re-programming, or even retraining for new environments or different robot platforms, making them impractical for widespread deployment in varied home settings.
2. **Insufficient Human-Awareness:** Many systems do not explicitly model or actively constrain robot behaviors in the presence of humans. While basic collision avoidance might be present, the nuanced safety and social appropriateness concerns that arise from dynamic human-robot interaction in close proximity are often overlooked.
3. **Context-Dependent Safety:** The safety and effectiveness of a robot's action are highly dependent on the "context," which emerges from the *joint interaction* between the robot's physical embodiment (including the tool it is holding) and the specific features of the environment, especially when a human is present. For instance, a robot holding a knife is not inherently dangerous, but a knife *oriented towards a human* is. Existing systems often fail to capture and act upon such nuanced interaction contexts.
4. **Challenges with Data-Driven Approaches:** While large-scale learning models show promise in general robotics, they face significant hurdles in caregiving. Collecting the extensive, diverse human-robot interaction data required for robust generalization is costly, time-consuming, and carries inherent safety risks, limiting their applicability in this sensitive domain.

Given these motivations, the key objectives of the E2-CARE framework are:
- To enable **context-aware adaptation** for physical caregiving robots, allowing them to perform diverse ADLs safely and effectively across varying environments and robot embodiments.
- To represent primitive caregiving skills not as fixed policies, but as **adaptable interaction templates** whose execution can be dynamically reshaped based on the current context.
- To develop a **unified 3D dynamic scene graph** that explicitly models the geometric and semantic interactions between the environment, the robot embodiment, and the human, capturing the nuanced contexts necessary for adaptive behavior.
- To synthesize **task-specific interaction constraints** (distinguishing between hard safety constraints and soft task preferences) from this scene graph, which govern how each skill is executed in real-time.
- To implement a mechanism for **runtime enforcement of these constraints** using control barrier functions, thereby guaranteeing human safety while maximizing the satisfaction of task preferences.
- To demonstrate that this framework allows for **zero-shot reuse of the same skill templates** across diverse environments and robot embodiments without requiring retraining or extensive manual modification.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

E2-CARE is a framework designed to enable context-aware adaptation for physical caregiving robots by explicitly modeling the joint interaction between the environment, the robot's embodiment, and humans.

The framework operates through the following interconnected components:
1. **3D Dynamic Scene Graph Generation:**
 - **Unified Representation:** The core of E2-CARE is a layered, hierarchical 3D dynamic scene graph, G(V_t, E_t), at each timestep *t*. This graph abstracts dense 3D reconstructions into higher-level spatial concepts (objects, agents, rooms) and explicitly models their spatio-temporal relationships. Nodes in the graph represent spatial entities (e.g., manipulable objects, semantic regions, the robot, or a human) and store both geometric (e.g., point clouds) and semantic descriptors. Edges encode pairwise spatial and semantic relations like adjacency, containment, and contact. The graph is dynamically updated over time through multi-frame association and tracking, maintaining persistent identities for entities.
 - **Environment Graph:** This is constructed using a SLAM pipeline, which estimates camera poses and maintains a metric map. At each frame, open-vocabulary object detection (RAM, GroundingDINO) and segmentation (SAM) identify object masks. These masks are then projected into 3D point clouds using depth and pose estimates, forming semantic entities within the graph.
 - **Embodiment Graph:** The robot's URDF (Unified Robot Description Format) is converted into a kinematic graph, which is integrated into the 3D dynamic scene graph. The robot is represented not as a single entity, but as a set of functional components (mobile base, arm, end effector), where the end effector can change with different tools. Each component has an associated mesh model and a collision proxy representation (e.g., collision spheres for efficient collision checking). This hierarchical representation supports reasoning at both functional and geometric levels.

2. **High-Level Planning:**
 - Given a task (τ) and a skill library (L), an LLM (Large Language Model) is queried to identify task-relevant entities from the full 3D dynamic scene graph, pruning irrelevant nodes to create a task-conditioned subgraph G(V'_t, E'_t). This subgraph serves as the focused environment-embodiment context.
 - This task-relevant subgraph is then grounded into a symbolic PDDL (Planning Domain Definition Language) state by mapping nodes to objects and their relations.
 - A PDDL-based planner generates a sequence of primitive skills to achieve the high-level task goal.

3. **Constraint Synthesis:**
 - E2-CARE treats each primitive skill from the PDDL plan as an adaptable "interaction template" rather than a fixed policy.
 - An LLM-based reasoning module analyzes the task specification, user preferences, and the task-relevant subgraph to synthesize two types of interaction constraints for each skill:
 - **Hard Constraints:** These encode safety-critical conditions that must never be violated, such as collision avoidance, joint limits, and maintaining minimum separation between the robot and humans. These are predefined and enforced deterministically. For example, `(robot, {human}, distance, keep-distance, 0.1)` ensures a minimum 10cm distance from the human.
 - **Soft Constraints:** These capture semantic, social, or user preferences that should be satisfied when feasible but can be relaxed if necessary to preserve safety or feasibility. For example, `(robot, {human, tv}, region, outside-region, 0.1)` could specify avoiding blocking a user's view of the TV.
 - **Constraint Representation:** Each constraint `c_i` is formally represented as a tuple `(s_i, o_i, r_i, m_i, θ_i)`, where `s_i` is a robot embodiment entity, `o_i` is a scene entity, `r_i` is a relation type (e.g., distance, region, orientation, velocity), `m_i` specifies the behavioral rule (e.g., lower bound, upper bound, directional avoidance), and `θ_i` contains numeric thresholds. These symbolic constraints are grounded using geometric attributes from the scene graph and compiled into differentiable control barrier functions `h(x) ≥ 0`, where `h(x) < 0` signifies an unsafe or undesirable state.

4. **Constraint-Aware Skill Execution:**
 - For each skill, the associated hard and soft constraints are enforced using an Operational-Space Control Barrier Function (OSCBF) filter.
 - The robot's dynamics are modeled in joint space (`q̇ = u`). Task-space states (`x`) are derived via forward kinematics.
 - The CBF condition `ḣ(x, u) ≥ -α(h(x))` ensures forward invariance of the safe set defined by `h(x) ≥ 0`.
 - A Quadratic Program (QP) is solved at each control step to find the certified control command (`u_cert`). The QP's objective is to track the nominal command (`u_cmd`) generated by the primitive skill while strictly satisfying all hard constraints and minimizing violations of soft constraints (via slack variables). This process ensures certified safety while adaptively modifying skill execution to fit the current context. The filter only modifies the nominal skill as much as necessary.

This methodology allows the same primitive skill templates to be safely and efficiently reused across diverse environments and robot embodiments by dynamically reshaping their execution based on context-dependent constraints, without requiring retraining of the underlying skills.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of E2-CARE was conducted through extensive simulation experiments and real-world user studies, addressing its ability to identify constraints and adapt skills across environments and embodiments.

**Simulation Experiments:**
1. **Constraint Synthesis (Q1):** The framework's ability to identify environment-embodiment interaction constraints was evaluated using ground-truth dynamic scene graphs. Across four ADLs (feeding, meal preparation, grooming, bathing), E2-CARE demonstrated high average recall (around 90%) and strong average precision (around 90%). This indicates that the system reliably identifies relevant interaction constraints based on the unified environment-embodiment representation. More complex tasks like grooming showed slightly lower precision, but overall reliability was consistent.
2. **Adaptation to Environments (Q2):** E2-CARE was tested across 130 simulated household environments, including 30 assistive homes, performing various ADL tasks with different human activities.
 - **Performance:** E2-CARE consistently achieved the highest task success rate (around 95%) and constraint satisfaction rate (around 80%) across all scenarios. These results closely approached the upper bound established by "Ours w/ GT perception," indicating robustness to imperfect perception.
 - **Ablations:** Significant performance degradation was observed in ablations. "Ours w/o dynamic scene graph" (removing the unified representation) led to a ~15 percentage point drop in success rate, as the system often ignored how tools affected the environment (e.g., knife orientation near a human). "Ours w/o constraints" performed the worst, often falling below 45% success in complex scenarios, demonstrating the insufficiency of fixed skill templates for safe adaptation.
 - **Baselines:** Model-based baselines (SemanticSafe and Nominal CBF) showed moderate constraint satisfaction but were 15-40 percentage points below E2-CARE. These methods struggled due to a lack of structured environment representation, often generating erroneous or overly conservative constraints that led to infeasible control problems or ignored human presence.
3. **Adaptation to Embodiments (Q2):** The framework was evaluated on five different robot embodiments (Kinova Gen3, Kinova 6-DoF, Franka Emika Panda, UR5, WidowX 250).
 - **Robustness:** E2-CARE maintained strong performance across all platforms, with Kinova Gen3 achieving ~87% success and ~90% constraint satisfaction, and UR5 showing comparable results.
 - **Embodiment Limitations:** Even with more kinematically constrained robots (WidowX250, Kinova 6-DoF), the system achieved approximately 75% success and 55% constraint satisfaction. The performance drop was attributed to the robots' physical limitations, not a failure of the constraint enforcement, confirming that skills adapt through embodiment-aware constraint synthesis.

**Real-World User Study:**
A Cornell IRB-approved user study with human participants evaluated E2-CARE on two robot embodiments (Kinova Gen3, Franka Emika Panda) for feeding and meal preparation tasks in varied real-world environments. Participants rated perceived safety, behavioral appropriateness, and user satisfaction on a 7-point Likert scale.
- **Superior Performance:** E2-CARE significantly outperformed a baseline (without constraint synthesis/satisfaction) across all three metrics in social dining scenarios with both robots. The most notable improvement was in behavioral appropriateness, suggesting successful context adaptation.
- **Perceived Safety:** In collaborative cooking scenarios, even when the robot was holding a sharp knife, participants consistently perceived E2-CARE's robot as safe and appropriate. Qualitative feedback highlighted the robot's awareness of human movement and its adaptive behaviors (e.g., rotating the knife blade away from a human, maintaining distance) as key factors for this perception.

**Runtime Analysis:**
The system demonstrated efficient real-time operation on a standard workstation:
- Perception (scene graph construction): ~1.15s (once per skill).
- Human detection: ~3.85ms (in control loop).
- Planning (LLM pruning + PDDL): ~1.94s + ~0.15ms (once per skill).
- Constraint synthesis: ~1.96s (once per skill).
- Control (CBF-based QP filter): ~0.15ms (negligible overhead, even with 1000 constraints, ensuring real-time safety enforcement and rapid response to human motion).

Overall, the findings indicate that explicitly modeling environment-embodiment interaction and synthesizing runtime constraints is crucial for safe, effective, and context-aware adaptation in physical caregiving robots, both in simulation and real-world human-robot interaction.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a significant contribution to the field of physical caregiving robotics by addressing the challenge of deploying robots safely and adaptively in complex, human-centered environments. The E2-CARE framework offers several areas of significance and potential impact:

1. **Enhanced Safety and Social Appropriateness:** By explicitly representing the dynamic interaction between the robot's embodiment (including tools), the environment, and humans within a unified 3D scene graph, the framework enables the synthesis and enforcement of critical safety and social constraints. This proactive, context-aware approach goes beyond simple collision avoidance, allowing robots to adjust their behavior in nuanced ways (e.g., reorienting a sharp tool away from a human) to ensure both physical safety and social acceptability. This is crucial for user trust and acceptance in caregiving applications.

2. **Increased Generalization and Reusability of Skills:** A core limitation of existing caregiving systems is their tight coupling to specific environments or robot platforms. E2-CARE transforms primitive skills into adaptable "interaction templates" that are reshaped by runtime constraints. This design enables the zero-shot reuse of the same skill templates across diverse environments and robot embodiments without requiring retraining or extensive manual reconfiguration. This capability is fundamental for scaling robotic caregiving solutions beyond controlled laboratory settings to the varied and unpredictable realities of human homes.

3. **Reduced Data Dependence for Safety and Adaptation:** The framework offers an alternative to the purely data-driven approaches that dominate many areas of modern robotics. For physical caregiving, collecting the vast, diverse, and safe human-robot interaction datasets required for robust learning-based adaptation is exceptionally challenging and resource-intensive. E2-CARE's model-based approach to constraint synthesis and enforcement reduces this reliance, providing a robust mechanism for safety and adaptation without requiring extensive task-specific or context-specific data collection for every new scenario.

4. **Robust Scene Understanding:** The use of a 3D dynamic scene graph provides a rich and hierarchical representation of the operational environment, including semantic information about objects, rooms, and human presence, along with their spatio-temporal relationships. This unified and dynamic understanding of the scene is foundational for robust reasoning and decision-making in complex environments.

5. **Practical Deployment in Real-World Settings:** The real-world user studies validate the framework's effectiveness in actual human-robot interaction scenarios. The positive feedback on perceived safety and behavioral appropriateness, even with tasks involving sharp tools, underscores the practical viability of E2-CARE for real-world deployment. The efficient runtime performance of the system's components further supports its applicability in dynamic environments requiring rapid responses.

6. **Foundation for Future Research:** The framework provides a strong foundation for future research directions. Explicitly modeling temporal constraints using formal methods like Signal Temporal Logic (STL) could enable more sophisticated temporal reasoning and enhance the expressiveness of desired robot behaviors. Further integration with advanced vision-language models could improve the quality and breadth of constraint synthesis, while supporting online skill discovery could enhance the robot's overall flexibility and task coverage.

In summary, E2-CARE advances the state of the art in physical caregiving robotics by offering a principled and adaptable solution to safety and generalization challenges. Its focus on context-aware interaction, derived from a unified understanding of embodiment, environment, and human presence, represents a critical step toward developing more capable, trustworthy, and deployable robotic assistants for activities of daily living.
