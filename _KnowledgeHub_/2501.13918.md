# Improving Video Generation with Human Feedback

> [!summary] Summary
> This research from Kling Team, Kuaishou Technology, Tsinghua University, and other academic institutions, developed a multi-dimensional reward model and tailored RLHF algorithms (Flow-DPO, Flow-RWR, Flow-NRG) to improve video generation. The approach significantly enhanced visual quality, motion coherence, and text alignment in generated videos by leveraging a newly constructed large-scale human preference dataset.

## Problem

- Modern video generation models often struggle with unstable motion, imperfect text-video alignment, and insufficient alignment with human preferences despite high fidelity.
- Existing human preference datasets for video generation are outdated, low-resolution, and do not reflect user preferences for outputs from state-of-the-art models.
- Applying Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) methods like DPO and RWR to rectified flow-based video generation architectures is challenging, with prior attempts showing mixed results.

## Method

- A large-scale, multi-dimensional human preference dataset was constructed from 108,000 videos generated by 12 modern T2V models, with 182,000 pairwise annotations across Visual Quality, Motion Quality, and Text Alignment.
- A robust multi-dimensional video ==reward model==, **VideoReward**, was developed using the Bradley-Terry Model with Ties (BTT) and a novel token-positioning strategy for disentangled attribute scores.
- Three alignment algorithms (Flow-DPO, Flow-RWR, Flow-NRG) were introduced and evaluated, specifically adapted for rectified flow-based video generation models from a unified KL-regularized RL objective.

## Results

- VideoReward consistently outperformed existing reward models on the new VideoGen-RewardBench, achieving 61.26% overall accuracy with ties included, demonstrating superior alignment with human preferences for modern T2V outputs.
- Flow-DPO with a constant KL coefficient exhibited superior alignment across dimensions, achieving high win rates (e.g., 93.42% VQ, 69.08% MQ, 75.43% TA on VBench for multi-dimensional alignment) against the pretrained model while successfully avoiding reward hacking.
- Flow-NRG allowed users to assign custom weights to VQ, MQ, and TA objectives during inference, effectively guiding video generation to achieve specific personalized preferences, such as 86.43% VQ and 93.23% MQ with a 0.5:0.5:0 weighting.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The Bradley-Terry Model with Ties (BTT) and a targeted token-positioning strategy in VLM-based reward models provide superior and more disentangled preference signals for video evaluation.
> - Using a constant KL regularization coefficient (β) in Flow-DPO is crucial for stable training and effective alignment in rectified flow models, diverging from the timestep-dependent schedules typically found in diffusion models, which can cause reward hacking.
> - Noisy Reward Guidance (Flow-NRG), trained on noised latents, enables efficient, personalized control over multiple video quality objectives during inference without requiring extensive retraining.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of generative AI, particularly for multimedia content, has witnessed rapid advancements. Text-to-video (T2V) generation models, often employing rectified flow or diffusion techniques, can now produce highly realistic and coherent videos. However, a significant gap remains: ensuring these generated videos truly align with nuanced human preferences and expectations regarding visual quality, motion coherence, and precise text-prompt fidelity.

In related domains like large language models (LLMs) and image generation, Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) has emerged as a powerful paradigm. Techniques like Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) and Reward-Weighted Regression (RWR) have successfully aligned models with human judgments, leading to more desirable outputs. However, applying RLHF to video generation presents unique challenges:

1. **Reward Signal Limitations**: Existing human preference datasets for video generation are largely based on older, low-resolution, short-duration T2V models (the "pre-Sora era"). Reward models trained on such data often fail to capture the subtleties of modern, high-fidelity, longer videos, potentially over-penalizing old artifacts while missing new quality dimensions. Moreover, the design space for Vision-Language Model (VLM)-based reward models, including optimal annotation paradigms and strategies to prevent "reward hacking" (where models exploit the reward function without truly improving quality), remains underexplored.
2. **Model Architecture Mismatch**: Many cutting-edge T2V models, including those by Kuaishou's Kling team, leverage "rectified flow" rather than traditional diffusion processes. While DPO and RWR have been adapted for diffusion models, their application to flow-based models is relatively nascent, with some initial attempts even reporting performance degradation.

This paper directly addresses these critical gaps. It introduces a comprehensive pipeline to robustly integrate human feedback into *modern, flow-based* video generation models. By creating a new, large-scale, multi-dimensional human preference dataset specifically for state-of-the-art T2V models, it overcomes the data scarcity and relevance issues of previous works. It systematically designs and evaluates a multi-dimensional reward model to provide a more reliable signal. Crucially, it adapts and innovates RLHF algorithms (Flow-DPO, Flow-RWR, Flow-NRG) specifically for rectified flow models, demonstrating effective alignment where prior attempts struggled or showed limitations. This work therefore represents a significant step forward in bringing the power of human feedback-driven alignment to the forefront of advanced video generation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the persistent challenges in current video generation systems, specifically "unstable motion, imperfect text-video alignment and insufficient alignment with human preferences," despite significant technological advancements. The authors aim to bridge the gap between high-fidelity video generation capabilities and genuine human satisfaction.

The key objectives are articulated as follows:

- **Objective 1: Construct a High-Quality, Modern Human Preference Dataset.**
 - **Motivation**: Existing video preference datasets are outdated, built on early, low-resolution T2V models, and do not reflect user preferences for modern, high-fidelity, longer videos. This limits the effectiveness of reward models trained on such data.
 - **Approach**: Gather 16,000 diverse prompts and use 12 representative T2V models (including state-of-the-art modern ones) to generate 108,000 videos. Professional annotators then provide 182,000 pairwise preference annotations across Visual Quality (VQ), Motion Quality (MQ), and Text Alignment (TA).

- **Objective 2: Develop and Systematically Study a Robust Multi-Dimensional Video Reward Model (VideoReward).**
 - **Motivation**: The design choices for VLM-based reward models are underexplored, leading to potential issues like sub-optimal annotation paradigms, reward hacking, and entangled multi-attribute scores. A reliable reward signal is fundamental for effective RLHF.
 - **Approach**: Train a lightweight VLM backbone (Qwen2-VL-2B) using the newly collected dataset. Systematically investigate the impact of different design choices, including the Bradley-Terry Model with Ties (BTT) for preference learning (versus pointwise regression) and a novel token-positioning strategy to ensure disentangled multi-dimensional scores. Establish VideoGen-RewardBench for rigorous evaluation.

- **Objective 3: Introduce and Evaluate Effective Alignment Algorithms for Flow-Based Video Generation Models.**
 - **Motivation**: Modern video generation models increasingly use rectified flow techniques, but adapting existing RLHF methods (like DPO and RWR) from diffusion models to flow-based architectures is non-trivial and has shown mixed results in prior work.
 - **Approach**: From a unified reinforcement learning perspective (maximizing reward with KL regularization), derive and implement three alignment algorithms tailored for flow-based models:
 - **Flow-DPO (Direct Preference Optimization for Flow)**: A training-time strategy. Critically, investigate the impact of the KL regularization term's schedule (constant vs. timestep-dependent) on performance and reward hacking.
 - **Flow-RWR (Reward Weighted Regression for Flow)**: Another training-time strategy based on expectation-maximization.
 - **Flow-NRG (Noisy Reward Guidance)**: An efficient inference-time technique that applies reward guidance directly to noisy videos in latent space, allowing for personalized objective weighting.

By achieving these objectives, the research aims to significantly improve the alignment of state-of-the-art video generation models with complex human preferences, thereby enhancing their overall quality and utility.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The paper proposes a systematic three-stage pipeline to align flow-based video generation models with human preferences: Human Preference Annotation, Reward Model Training, and Video Alignment.

#### 3.1. Human Preference Data Collection

To address the limitations of outdated datasets, the authors meticulously constructed a new, large-scale dataset tailored for modern T2V models.
- **Prompt Collection**: 16,000 high-quality, diverse prompts were gathered from the internet, categorized, expanded with GPT-4o, and refined by an in-house prompt rewriter.
- **Video Generation**: These prompts were used to generate 108,000 videos from 12 representative T2V models. This included "Pre-Sora-Era" models (e.g., Gen2, SVD, Pika 1.0) and "Modern Models" (e.g., Dreamina, Luma, Gen3, Kling 1.0, Kling 1.5), ensuring a wide range of capabilities and resolutions (384x672 to 768x1408) and durations (3s to 6s).
- **Multi-dimensional Annotation**: Professional annotators provided pairwise preferences (A wins / Ties / B wins) for 182,000 prompt-video triplets, focusing on three key dimensions: Visual Quality (VQ), Motion Quality (MQ), and Text Alignment (TA). They also assigned 1-5 Likert scores to individual videos for comparison studies.

#### 3.2. Reward Modeling (VideoReward)

A robust reward model is crucial for effective RLHF. The authors systematically explored design choices for their multi-dimensional video reward model, VideoReward, using a lightweight VLM backbone (Qwen2-VL-2B).
- **Score Regression vs. Bradley-Terry (BT) Model with Ties (BTT)**: They compared pointwise score regression (L_reg) with probabilistic ranking models. The BTT model, an extension of the Bradley-Terry model (L_BT) that explicitly accounts for "ties," was found to be superior. BTT defines a tripartite preference distribution, allowing it to learn a more flexible decision boundary, clustering ties near zero while preserving clear margins for decisive wins/losses.
- **Token Positioning**: To prevent context leakage (where VQ/MQ scores might be influenced by the prompt), a novel token positioning strategy was introduced. Context-agnostic tokens ([VQ], [MQ]) are placed after the video tokens but before the prompt, allowing them to attend only to visual content. A context-aware token ([TA]) is placed after the full prompt to attend to both video and text. These tokens' final-layer embeddings are mapped to dimension-specific scores via a shared linear layer.
- **Evaluation**: VideoReward was evaluated on two benchmarks: the newly curated VideoGen-RewardBench (26.5k annotated pairs for modern T2V models, built from VideoGen-Eval) and the existing GenAI-Bench (for pre-Sora-era models). Performance was measured using ties-included and ties-excluded pairwise accuracy across overall, VQ, MQ, and TA dimensions.

#### 3.3. Video Alignment

With a high-fidelity reward model, three alignment methods were introduced for flow-based video generation models, starting from a unified KL-regularized RL objective. The pretrained model (p_ref) is an internal Transformer-based latent diffusion model trained with rectified flow. LoRA was used for fine-tuning.
- **Rectified Flow Preliminaries**: The work builds upon Rectified Flow, where noisy data x_t = (1-t)x_0 + tx_1 and models regress a velocity field v_θ(x_t, t) by minimizing a Flow Matching objective.
- **Flow-DPO (Direct Preference Optimization for Flow)**:
 - This training-time strategy extends Diffusion-DPO by analytically solving the RLHF objective.
 - Lemma B.1 establishes a relationship between noise prediction error and velocity field prediction error, allowing the DPO loss to be adapted for rectified flow.
 - A critical finding: while a direct derivation yields a timestep-dependent KL coefficient β_t = β(1-t)^2, this schedule was found to degrade performance and lead to reward hacking in rectified flow. A *constant* β was empirically adopted instead, leading to more stable training and improved alignment.
- **Flow-RWR (Reward-Weighted Regression for Flow)**:
 - This training-time strategy adapts Reward-Weighted Regression to flow-based models.
 - It formulates a reward-weighted velocity regression loss, L_RWR(θ) = E[exp(r(x_0, y)) ||v - v_θ(x_t, t, y)||^2], also omitting the (1-t)^2 factor for better performance, similar to Flow-DPO.
- **Flow-NRG (Noisy Reward Guidance)**:
 - This is an efficient inference-time technique. The KL-regularized RL objective has a closed-form solution where the target distribution is proportional to the reference distribution times an exponential of the reward.
 - Appendix B.2 shows that this reweighting can be achieved by shifting the velocity field: ˜v_t(x_t|y) = v_t(x_t|y) - w * (t / (1-t)) * ∇r(x_t, y), where 'w' controls guidance strength.
 - To make gradient computation efficient, a lightweight, time-dependent reward model r_θ(., t) is trained directly in latent space on noised videos, reusing early layers from the pretrained VDM backbone. This avoids expensive back-propagation through the VAE decoder.
- **Evaluation**: Performance was evaluated using automatic metrics (win rate via VideoReward, Vbench scores for Quality and Semantic alignment) and human assessments on Vbench, VideoGen-Eval, and a new "TA-Hard" dataset designed for complex semantic alignment.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research produced several significant findings across reward modeling and video alignment:

#### 4.1. Preference Dataset and Reward Modeling

- **Large-Scale Preference Dataset**: The paper successfully constructed a 182k-sized, multi-dimensional human-labeled video generation preference dataset. This dataset is uniquely focused on outputs from 12 *modern* video generation models (including proprietary ones like Kling 1.0/1.5), addressing a critical gap in existing resources.
- **Superior Reward Model (VideoReward)**:
 - **Performance**: VideoReward consistently outperformed existing reward models (VideoScore, LiFT, VisionReward) on the newly introduced **VideoGen-RewardBench**, which evaluates modern T2V models. For instance, VideoReward achieved 61.26% overall accuracy with ties included, significantly higher than its best competitor (VisionReward at 56.77%). This indicates strong alignment with human preferences on state-of-the-art video generations. It also maintained comparable performance on the older GenAI-Bench, demonstrating robust generalization.
 - **Design Choices**: Ablation studies confirmed the efficacy of key design choices:
 - The **Bradley-Terry Model with Ties (BTT)** paradigm consistently outperformed pointwise score regression, particularly in ties-included accuracy, by better modeling relative preferences and handling ambiguous cases.
 - The **token-positioning strategy** (context-agnostic tokens for VQ/MQ, context-aware for TA) improved performance by preventing context leakage and yielding cleaner, more reliable dimension-specific reward signals.
 - **Noisy-Latent Reward Training**: For Flow-NRG, training the reward model directly on noised latents was crucial, achieving a 74.6% MQ win rate compared to 38.6% for models trained on clean latents, validating its efficiency for inference-time guidance.

#### 4.2. Video Alignment Algorithms

- **Flow-DPO's Effectiveness**:
 - **Constant β is Key**: The most critical finding for Flow-DPO was the necessity of using a *constant* KL regularization coefficient (β) rather than the timestep-dependent β(1-t)^2 derived from Diffusion-DPO. The constant β variant significantly improved alignment across VQ, MQ, and TA dimensions, achieving high win rates against the pretrained model without reward hacking. For multi-dimensional alignment (VQ:MQ:TA = 1:1:1), constant-β Flow-DPO achieved 93.42% VQ, 69.08% MQ, and 75.43% TA win rates on VBench, outperforming Flow-RWR (51.55% VQ, 63.9% MQ, 53.43% TA) and SFT.
 - **Reward Hacking Mitigation**: The timestep-dependent β variant, while showing high VQ/MQ, suffered from significant reward hacking, degrading Text Alignment (TA) performance due to prioritizing alignment at higher noise levels and overfitting. The constant β variant successfully avoided these issues.
 - **Overall Superiority**: Flow-DPO with a constant β demonstrated superior performance compared to both Flow-RWR and supervised fine-tuning (SFT) methods in both multi-dimensional and single-dimensional (e.g., TA-only) alignment tasks, as confirmed by both automatic and human evaluations (Fig. 5).
- **Flow-NRG's Personalization Capability**: Flow-NRG, the inference-time reward guidance technique, successfully enabled users to assign custom weights to multiple alignment objectives (e.g., VQ, MQ, TA) during video generation. This allows for personalized video quality needs without requiring extensive retraining, offering flexibility in controlling trade-offs. For example, a 0.5:0.5:0 weighting for VQ:MQ:TA achieved 86.43% VQ and 93.23% MQ, while a 0:0:1 weighting yielded 70.42% TA.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research marks a substantial contribution to the field of generative AI, particularly in pushing the boundaries of high-quality video generation that aligns with human preferences. Its significance and potential impact are multifaceted:

1. **Advancing Human-Centric Video Generation**: The core contribution is establishing a robust pipeline for improving video generation using human feedback. By explicitly addressing "unsmooth motion, imperfect text-video alignment, and insufficient alignment with human preferences," the work moves T2V models closer to producing outputs that are not just technically impressive but also subjectively preferred by users. This is crucial for real-world applications in creative industries, content creation, and personalized media.

2. **Addressing Data Scarcity for Modern Models**: The creation of a large-scale, multi-dimensional human preference dataset (182k annotations from 12 modern T2V models) is a critical resource. It fills a significant void, as existing datasets were insufficient for evaluating and training models on the high-fidelity outputs of contemporary T2V systems. This dataset, along with the **VideoGen-RewardBench**, will serve as a foundational benchmark for future research in video RLHF.

3. **Robust Reward Modeling for Video**: The systematic study and development of **VideoReward**, incorporating the Bradley-Terry Model with Ties (BTT) and a novel token-positioning strategy, significantly advance the state-of-the-art in video reward modeling. Its demonstrated superior performance and robustness against issues like context leakage and reward hacking provide a more reliable signal for downstream alignment tasks, which is paramount for stable and effective RLHF.

4. **Tailored Alignment for Flow-Based Models**: The successful adaptation and innovation of RLHF algorithms (Flow-DPO, Flow-RWR, Flow-NRG) for *rectified flow-based* video generation models is a key technical breakthrough. The critical finding regarding the efficacy of a *constant* KL regularization term in Flow-DPO, diverging from practices in diffusion models, represents a significant insight into the unique dynamics of flow-based architectures. This ensures that RLHF can be effectively applied to this class of powerful generative models, which are increasingly adopted by leading industry players (e.g., Kuaishou's Kling).

5. **Enabling Personalized Video Creation**: **Flow-NRG** introduces a practical and efficient inference-time guidance mechanism. Its ability to allow users to assign custom weights to various quality objectives (Visual Quality, Motion Quality, Text Alignment) during inference unlocks significant potential for personalization. This is invaluable for creators who need fine-grained control over specific attributes of their generated videos without undergoing extensive retraining, catering to diverse artistic and functional requirements.

6. **Foundation for Future Research**: The work lays a strong foundation for future advancements in video RLHF. It opens avenues for exploring more advanced RLHF algorithms (e.g., PPO) within flow-based frameworks and extending alignment validation to other conditional generation tasks (e.g., image-to-video). The acknowledged limitations, such as the potential for reward hacking and the need for even more robust reward models, also highlight crucial directions for ongoing research.

In summary, this paper not only offers concrete solutions for improving video generation quality and human alignment but also contributes essential datasets, evaluation benchmarks, and methodological insights that will propel the field of human-in-the-loop generative AI forward, particularly for dynamic media.
