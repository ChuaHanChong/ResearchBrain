# Few-Shot Vision-Language Action-Incremental Policy Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> TOPIC enables robots to continuously learn new manipulation skills from a small number of demonstrations while retaining previously acquired capabilities. The framework integrates task-specific prompts for data-efficient learning with a continuous evolution strategy to mitigate catastrophic forgetting, achieving 40-50% higher accuracy on incremental tasks in simulation and a 78.5% average success rate in real-world settings.

## Problem

- Robotic systems struggle to continually learn new manipulation skills from limited demonstrations.
- Existing Transformer-based policies for robotic manipulation require large amounts of training data.
- Current methods suffer from catastrophic forgetting, failing to retain previously learned skills when adapting to new tasks.

## Method

- Introduces **Few-Shot** ==Action-Incremental Learning (FSAIL)== and proposes the Task-prOmpt graPh evolutIon poliCy (TOPIC) framework.
- Utilizes Task-Specific Prompts (TSP), learnable vectors that interact with multi-modal inputs to extract task-specific information from limited demonstrations.
- Employs a Continuous Evolution Strategy (CES) that constructs and uses a task relation graph to transfer knowledge between tasks and mitigate catastrophic forgetting.

## Results

- TOPIC achieved 40-**50%** higher accuracy on incremental tasks in simulated environments compared to baseline methods.
- In real-world experiments on a mobile manipulator, TOPIC demonstrated a **78.5%** average success rate across all tasks, significantly outperforming baselines which achieved **36.7%**.
- Catastrophic forgetting was substantially reduced, with TOPIC maintaining over **70%** success rate on previously learned tasks while successfully adapting to new tasks with only 5 demonstrations.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Learnable Task-Specific Prompts can effectively extract discriminative features from minimal demonstration data, significantly improving few-shot learning.
> - Leveraging semantic relationships between tasks through a continuous evolution strategy allows for effective knowledge transfer and substantially reduces catastrophic forgetting.
> - The combination of prompts for data efficiency and a graph-based evolution strategy for knowledge retention provides complementary benefits for continual few-shot learning in robotic manipulation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

#### 1.1. Introduction

Robotic systems that can continually learn new manipulation skills from limited demonstrations represent a critical challenge for embodied AI. While recent Transformer-based vision-language policies have shown significant progress in robotic manipulation tasks, they are limited by two critical factors: the need for large amounts of training data and the inability to continuously adapt to new tasks without forgetting previously learned skills.

This paper introduces Few-Shot Action-Incremental Learning (FSAIL), a novel approach that enables robots to learn new manipulation skills from just a handful of demonstrations while retaining previously learned capabilities. To address the FSAIL challenge, the authors propose Task-prOmpt graPh evolutIon poliCy (TOPIC), which integrates task-specific prompts with a continuous evolution strategy to facilitate learning from limited data and mitigate catastrophic forgetting.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

#### 2.1. The FSAIL Task

The Few-Shot Action-Incremental Learning (FSAIL) task is formally defined as teaching a robot to perform a sequence of tasks over time with limited demonstrations for each new task. The learning process involves:

1. A base session where the robot learns foundational skills from multiple tasks with sufficient data
2. Incremental sessions where the robot must learn new tasks from only a few demonstrations

The key challenge of FSAIL is to enable the robot to learn new tasks effectively with minimal data while preserving performance on previously learned tasks. This is particularly challenging because standard Transformer-based policies tend to suffer from catastrophic forgetting when adapting to new tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

#### 3.1. TOPIC: Task-prOmpt graPh evolutIon poliCy

The TOPIC framework combines two key components to address the FSAIL challenge:

As shown in Figure 2, TOPIC differs from standard Transformer-based policies by introducing:

1. **Task-Specific Prompts (TSP)**: Learnable prompt vectors that interact with multi-modal input data to extract task-specific information from limited demonstrations
2. **Continuous Evolution Strategy (CES)**: A mechanism that constructs and utilizes a task relation graph to transfer knowledge between tasks and mitigate catastrophic forgetting

TOPIC is designed to be integrated with existing Transformer-based policies, enhancing their capacity for few-shot learning and continual adaptation without requiring architectural overhauls.

#### 3.2. Task-Specific Prompts

Task-Specific Prompts (TSP) are designed to address the data scarcity challenge in few-shot learning scenarios. They function as learnable vectors that deeply interact with the multi-modal inputs (vision and language) in the Transformer architecture:

The prompts are initialized as a set of trainable vectors and are processed alongside the visual and language tokens in the Multi-View Transformer Encoder. Through this interaction, the prompts learn to extract discriminative, task-specific features from limited demonstration data. Mathematically, the process can be described as:

$$Z_p = \text{MVTransformer}([Z_v, Z_l, P_t])$$

Where $Z_v$ represents visual tokens, $Z_l$ represents language tokens, and $P_t$ represents the task-specific prompts.

The number of task-specific prompts significantly impacts performance, with experiments showing optimal results at around 5-7 prompts:

As shown in Figure 4, using an optimal number of task-specific prompts yields improvements of up to 17.5 percentage points in accuracy compared to models without TSP.

#### 3.3. Continuous Evolution Strategy

The Continuous Evolution Strategy (CES) addresses catastrophic forgetting by leveraging relationships between tasks to facilitate knowledge transfer when learning new skills. CES works by:

1. Constructing a task relation graph based on the semantic similarity between task-specific prompts
2. Computing relationship coefficients between tasks using cosine similarity between prompt embeddings
3. Adapting policy parameters for new tasks by combining knowledge from related previously learned tasks

The mathematical formulation for policy evolution is:

$$\theta_{t_j} = \theta_{t_0} + \sum_{i=1}^{j-1} \alpha_{ji} \cdot (\theta_{t_i} - \theta_{t_0})$$

Where $\theta_{t_j}$ represents the policy parameters for task $j$, $\theta_{t_0}$ is the base policy, and $\alpha_{ji}$ is the relationship coefficient between tasks $j$ and $i$.

This adaptive combination of policy parameters enables the model to leverage knowledge from related tasks, significantly improving performance on new tasks with limited data:

As shown in Figure 5, TOPIC consistently outperforms baseline methods regardless of the number of tasks in the base session, with the gap widening as more base tasks are available.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

#### 4.1. Experimental Results

The authors conducted extensive experiments to evaluate TOPIC against various baseline methods in simulated environments:

1. **Comparison with State-of-the-Art Methods**: TOPIC outperformed existing methods, including replay-based, regularization-based, and prompt-based continual learning approaches.

2. **Ablation Studies**: Experiments validated the contributions of both Task-Specific Prompts and Continuous Evolution Strategy components, showing that both are essential for optimal performance.

3. **Transfer Learning Efficiency**: TOPIC demonstrated superior ability to transfer knowledge between tasks, with significantly higher accuracy on new tasks learned from limited demonstrations.

Key findings include:
- TOPIC achieved 40-50% higher accuracy on incremental tasks compared to baseline methods
- The combination of TSP and CES provided complementary benefits, with TSP addressing data scarcity and CES mitigating catastrophic forgetting
- Performance scaled well with the number of base tasks, suggesting that TOPIC effectively leverages knowledge from previously learned skills

#### 4.2. Real-World Validation

To validate TOPIC's real-world applicability, the authors conducted experiments using an Agile X TRACER mobile robot equipped with dual WidowX-250 manipulators:

The real-world experiments involved a set of object manipulation tasks, including placing various objects (cubes, bananas) on different targets (plates, bowls). The results demonstrated that TOPIC significantly outperformed baseline methods in real-world settings:

As shown in Figure 7, while baseline methods (SAM-E) failed on previously learned tasks after adapting to new ones (indicated by red X marks), TOPIC maintained performance on all tasks while successfully learning new ones.

Key observations from real-world experiments include:
- TOPIC achieved 78.5% average success rate across all tasks compared to 36.7% for the baseline
- Catastrophic forgetting was significantly reduced, with TOPIC maintaining over 70% success rate on previously learned tasks
- The model successfully adapted to new tasks with only 5 demonstrations per task

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

#### 5.1. Limitations and Future Work

Despite its promising results, TOPIC has several limitations that suggest directions for future research:

1. **Performance Gap Between Simulation and Reality**: While TOPIC performs well in both settings, there remains a gap between simulated and real-world performance that could be addressed with domain adaptation techniques.

2. **Task Complexity**: The current work focuses on relatively simple manipulation tasks; future work could explore more complex, long-horizon tasks requiring multiple steps or tool use.

3. **Continuous Learning Over Long Periods**: Further research is needed to evaluate how TOPIC performs when learning tens or hundreds of tasks over extended periods.

4. **Dynamic Task Relations**: The current task relation graph is static after creation; future work could explore dynamically updating task relationships as more information becomes available.

5. **Computational Efficiency**: As the number of tasks grows, the computational requirements for TOPIC increase; optimization techniques could make the approach more scalable.

#### 5.2. Conclusion

This paper introduced TOPIC, a novel framework for Few-Shot Action-Incremental Learning in robotic manipulation tasks. By combining Task-Specific Prompts to address data scarcity and a Continuous Evolution Strategy to mitigate catastrophic forgetting, TOPIC enables robots to continuously learn new skills from limited demonstrations while preserving previously acquired capabilities.

The approach was rigorously evaluated in both simulated and real-world environments, consistently outperforming state-of-the-art methods across various metrics. The results demonstrate that TOPIC represents a significant advancement in robotic continual learning, bringing us closer to robots that can efficiently adapt to new tasks in dynamic real-world environments.

TOPIC's integration with existing Transformer-based policies makes it readily applicable to various robotic platforms and tasks, suggesting broad potential impact in industrial automation, service robotics, and assistive technologies.
