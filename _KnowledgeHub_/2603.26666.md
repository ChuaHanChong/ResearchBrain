# VLA-OPD: Bridging Offline SFT and Online RL for Vision-Language-Action Models via On-Policy Distillation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at HKUST (GZ) developed VLA-OPD, a framework that combines offline supervised fine-tuning and online reinforcement learning for Vision-Language-Action models through on-policy distillation. It utilizes a Reverse-KL divergence objective to provide dense, token-level supervision from an expert teacher on student-generated trajectories, leading to improved sample efficiency (e.g., 3x faster convergence on LIBERO-Long) and robust performance while mitigating catastrophic forgetting.

## Problem

- Offline Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) for VLA models suffers from distribution shift and catastrophic forgetting due to its off-policy nature and static datasets.
- Online Reinforcement Learning (RL) for VLA models faces challenges with sparse rewards and poor sample efficiency, making it computationally prohibitive for billion-parameter models.
- Existing methods that adapt SFT to on-policy settings use suboptimal distillation objectives (e.g., Forward-KL leading to "entropy explosion" or Hard-CE causing "premature entropy collapse").

## Method

- An iterative, closed-loop framework (**VLA-OPD**) that combines ==on-policy trajectory sampling== by the student with dense teacher labeling and ==mode-seeking optimization==.
- Leverages a robust, frozen ==teacher policy== to provide dense, ==token-level action logits== for every state visited by the student, replacing sparse environmental rewards.
- Optimizes the student policy using a ==policy-gradient== method with a ==Reverse Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence== objective to ensure stable, mode-seeking policy updates.

## Results

- Achieved superior sample efficiency, reaching over **90%** success on LIBERO-Object in 10 steps and a **3x** speedup on LIBERO-Long compared to the GRPO baseline.
- Significantly boosted policy efficacy, increasing average success rates from **48.9%** (1-traj SFT) to **87.4%** on LIBERO and from **45.2%** to **71.1%** on RoboTwin2.0, nearly matching expert teacher performance.
- Effectively mitigated catastrophic forgetting, maintaining competitive performance on unseen tasks while mastering target tasks, unlike offline SFT which suffered severe forgetting.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - On-policy distillation with dense teacher supervision effectively bridges the gap between offline SFT's efficiency and online RL's robustness for VLA models.
> - The Reverse-KL divergence is a superior objective for policy distillation, enabling stable learning by filtering teacher's epistemic uncertainty ("mode-seeking") while preserving action diversity, avoiding "entropy explosion" or "premature entropy collapse."
> - Anchoring gradient updates to the student's actively explored policy manifold (on-policy formulation) effectively mitigates catastrophic forgetting, preserving generalist capabilities.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of embodied intelligence has seen a significant shift with the integration of Large Language Models (LLMs) and visual perception, leading to the development of generalist Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. These models aim to unify perception, planning, and control within a single transformer architecture, demonstrating capabilities in generalization across varied environments and instructions for robotic manipulation.

Despite their generalist pre-trained abilities, VLA models often require post-training to achieve reliable and precise execution in specific downstream tasks. This post-training typically falls into two primary paradigms: offline Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) and online Reinforcement Learning (RL).

Offline SFT, often implemented as behavior cloning, optimizes a policy by maximizing the likelihood of expert actions from static datasets. It offers dense supervision and fast convergence, but is inherently limited by its "off-policy" nature. This limitation makes it vulnerable to distribution shifts, where the model encounters states not present in the training data, leading to compounding errors during online deployment. Furthermore, SFT can suffer from catastrophic forgetting, where aggressive updates to fit new data overwrite previously acquired generalist capabilities.

Online RL addresses the distribution shift problem by allowing the model to interact with the environment, exposing the policy to its own induced state distribution. This enables active exploration and error correction, improving closed-loop robustness. However, online RL for VLA models faces significant challenges, primarily sparse rewards and poor sample efficiency, requiring a large volume of interaction data to learn effective policies. For billion-parameter VLA models, the computational cost associated with extensive online interaction can be prohibitive. While some methods, like Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), mitigate architectural overhead by eliminating a separate value network, they still contend with feedback sparsity.

Existing attempts to bridge SFT and on-policy settings, such as DAGGER, often employ suboptimal alignment objectives. Forward-KL divergence, when used for distillation, can lead to "entropy explosion" by forcing the student to cover the teacher's epistemic uncertainty, resulting in hesitant policies. Conversely, using Hard-CE (argmax matching) can cause "premature entropy collapse," where the student loses necessary action diversity for exploration.

This work positions itself within this landscape by proposing VLA-OPD (On-Policy VLA Distillation) as a framework that seeks to combine the sample efficiency and stability of SFT with the robustness and on-policy error correction capabilities of RL. It aims to overcome the limitations of both paradigms by introducing a dense, token-level supervision mechanism derived from an expert teacher on student-generated trajectories, critically employing a Reverse-KL objective to maintain stable policy learning and action diversity.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for VLA-OPD stems from the identified limitations of current post-training methodologies for Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models:

1. **Addressing Distribution Shift and Catastrophic Forgetting in SFT:** Offline Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) trains VLA models on static expert demonstration datasets. While efficient, this approach is "off-policy," meaning the policy is evaluated on states generated by its own actions, which may diverge significantly from the expert states it was trained on. This "distribution shift" leads to compounding errors and brittle performance in real-world deployment. Additionally, fine-tuning on disjoint, fixed datasets can cause catastrophic forgetting, where the model loses its pre-trained generalist capabilities due to aggressive parameter updates. The objective is to enable on-policy learning to mitigate these issues.

2. **Overcoming Sample Inefficiency and Sparse Rewards in Online RL:** Online Reinforcement Learning (RL) allows VLA models to interact with the environment, naturally exposing them to their own induced state distributions, thus addressing distribution shift. However, robotic tasks typically provide sparse, binary rewards (e.g., success or failure), leading to significant credit assignment problems and high-variance optimization. This results in extremely poor sample efficiency, requiring an impractically large number of environment interactions to train VLA models, which often have billions of parameters. The motivation is to achieve the robustness of RL without its prohibitive sample costs.

3. **Developing a Stable and Effective On-Policy Alignment Objective:** Existing methods that adapt SFT to an on-policy setting (e.g., DAGGER variants) often use suboptimal distillation objectives.
 - **Forward-KL Divergence**: This objective encourages mode-covering, meaning the student attempts to mimic the entire probability distribution of the teacher. In out-of-distribution (OOD) states where the teacher might exhibit high epistemic uncertainty (i.e., a flat, high-entropy distribution), Forward-KL can force the student to also become uncertain and diffuse, leading to an "entropy explosion" and hesitant, imprecise actions.
 - **Hard-Cross-Entropy (Argmax Matching)**: This objective focuses on matching only the teacher's top-1 action (argmax), discarding valuable "dark knowledge" contained in the teacher's full probability distribution. In multi-modal decision boundaries, a rigid focus on the argmax can cause the student's policy to violently oscillate, leading to "premature entropy collapse" and depriving the student of the necessary action diversity for robust exploration.
 The objective is to formulate an alignment objective that avoids these extremes, ensuring stable learning while preserving action diversity and filtering out teacher uncertainty.

In summary, the key objectives of VLA-OPD are:
- To create a unified post-training framework that combines the efficiency of SFT with the robustness of RL for VLA models.
- To leverage an expert teacher to provide dense, token-level supervision on the student’s self-generated trajectories, thereby enabling active error correction in policy-induced states without relying on sparse environmental rewards.
- To formulate this distillation via a Reverse-KL objective, which promotes a bounded mode-seeking behavior, ensuring stable policy learning by filtering out the teacher’s epistemic uncertainty while maintaining action diversity.
- To effectively mitigate catastrophic forgetting, preserving pre-trained generalist capabilities through a gentle, on-policy alignment process.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

VLA-OPD is presented as an iterative, closed-loop framework for post-training Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. It involves a Student Policy ($\pi_\theta$) being trained and a frozen Teacher Policy ($\pi_{tea}$) acting as an expert oracle. The process is divided into three phases:

#### 3.1. Framework Overview

The VLA-OPD training cycle iteratively refines the student policy:
1. **Phase 1: On-Policy Sampling (Exploration)**: The student policy interacts with the environment to generate its own trajectories. This interaction exposes the student to states it might encounter in real deployment, including out-of-distribution states resulting from its own actions.
2. **Phase 2: Dense Teacher Labeling (Correction)**: For every state visited by the student during its interaction, the frozen expert teacher provides dense, token-level action logits. This signal acts as a continuous, immediate supervisor, guiding the student on optimal recovery behaviors, unlike sparse environmental rewards.
3. **Phase 3: Mode-Seeking Optimization (Update)**: The student policy is updated using a policy-gradient method, optimizing against the teacher's distribution via a Reverse-KL objective. This objective is specifically chosen for its mode-seeking properties, designed to provide stable updates.

#### 3.2. On-Policy Trajectory Sampling

To address the distribution shift inherent in offline SFT, VLA-OPD adopts a dynamic on-policy sampling strategy. After an initial SFT-based initialization, the static offline dataset is discarded. In each training iteration, the current student policy generates a batch of trajectories by interacting with the environment. The states visited ($s_t$) in these trajectories are drawn from the student's current induced distribution, $d_{\pi_\theta}$, rather than an expert's. This directly exposes the student to "failure states" it might encounter, converting unknown out-of-distribution regions into explicit training data for active correction. This on-policy formulation is also posited as a mechanism to mitigate catastrophic forgetting, as gradient updates remain anchored to the student's current behavioral manifold, promoting a "gentle" alignment that preserves pre-trained knowledge.

#### 3.3. Dense Teacher Supervision

Instead of relying on sparse rewards, VLA-OPD leverages a robust, frozen teacher policy to provide dense, token-level supervision. For every timestep $t$ in a student-generated trajectory, the teacher provides its action distribution $q_t(a) = \pi_{tea}(a|s_t)$. This dense signal aims to accelerate convergence by providing immediate feedback and to impart structural knowledge on recovery behaviors for out-of-distribution states.

#### 3.4. Optimization Objective and Analysis

The core of VLA-OPD's optimization is the minimization of the Reverse Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence between the student and teacher distributions on student-generated trajectories.

- **Reverse-KL as Dense Reward**: The objective is to maximize $J(\theta) = E_{s \sim \pi_\theta} [ -D_{KL}(\pi_\theta(\cdot|s) || \pi_{tea}(\cdot|s)) ]$. At the token level, this translates into an intrinsic reward $r_{OPD_t}$ defined as the negative log-ratio of probabilities: $r_{OPD_t}(s_t, a_t) = - (\log \pi_\theta(a_t|s_t) - \log \pi_{tea}(a_t|s_t))$. This reward penalizes the student for deviating from the teacher's action distribution. A `stop_gradient` operation is applied to the student's log-probability term within the reward calculation during policy gradient updates.

- **Theoretical Analysis: Mode-Seeking vs. Mode-Covering**: The choice of KL divergence direction is critical:
 - **Forward-KL ($D_{KL}(\pi_{tea} || \pi_\theta)$)**: This is implicitly optimized by standard SFT. It forces the student to cover the teacher's entire support. If the teacher is uncertain in OOD states (high entropy), Forward-KL compels the student to mimic this uncertainty, leading to "entropy explosion" and diffused policies.
 - **Hard-CE (Argmax Matching)**: Often used in on-policy imitation (e.g., DAgger), this objective only matches the teacher's top action. It discards soft probabilities, and if the teacher's argmax oscillates due to multi-modality, Hard-CE can cause "premature entropy collapse," reducing the student's action diversity.
 - **Reverse-KL ($D_{KL}(\pi_\theta || \pi_{tea})$)**: This is the objective used in VLA-OPD. Its "zero-forcing" property means the student is primarily penalized if its chosen action has low probability under the teacher distribution. It induces a "mode-seeking" behavior, allowing the student to confidently capture the teacher's primary intent while filtering out the teacher's high-entropy "tail uncertainty." This prevents both entropy explosion and premature entropy collapse, maintaining a healthy, bounded entropy and preserving action diversity within valid modes.

- **Group-Based Gradient Estimation**: To reduce the high variance typical of on-policy gradients, a group sampling strategy is employed. For each instruction, a group of $G$ trajectories is sampled from the current student policy. The policy gradient is then estimated by averaging over this group, using the raw Reverse-KL reward directly as the advantage signal, unlike standard GRPO which uses outcome reward normalization.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of VLA-OPD was conducted across the LIBERO (single-arm manipulation) and RoboTwin2.0 (dual-arm coordination) benchmarks, addressing questions of training efficiency, policy efficacy, catastrophic forgetting, and algorithmic design choices.

#### 4.1. Training Efficiency

VLA-OPD demonstrated superior sample efficiency compared to the GRPO baseline:
- On LIBERO-Object, VLA-OPD achieved over 90% success rate within 10 training steps, significantly faster than the gradual improvement of the baseline.
- On LIBERO-Long, VLA-OPD reached performance comparable to the baseline's 150-step result in only 50 steps, representing an approximately 3x speedup.
- Training curves for VLA-OPD were observed to be notably smoother and more stable than the GRPO baseline, which exhibited fluctuations.
- The combination of VLA-OPD distillation with subsequent GRPO fine-tuning (Ours (Distill + GRPO)) further pushed performance, surpassing the baseline's final convergence and achieving high success rates (over 95% on Object, 90% on Long).

#### 4.2. Policy Efficacy

VLA-OPD consistently improved policy efficacy in both data-scarce and complex settings:
- **LIBERO Benchmark (1-traj SFT initialization)**: When initialized with only one demonstration per task (1-traj SFT), the base student (OpenVLA-OFT) achieved an average success rate of 48.9%. VLA-OPD (Distill) significantly boosted this to an 87.4% average success rate, matching or exceeding several baselines trained on full datasets (50 demonstrations/task). When combined with GRPO fine-tuning, VLA-OPD (Distill + GRPO) achieved an average success rate of 93.4%, nearly recovering the expert teacher's performance (93.9%).
- **RoboTwin2.0 Benchmark (Dual-Arm Manipulation)**: For complex dual-arm tasks, even with 1,000 demonstrations per task for SFT initialization, the student (OpenVLA-OFT) averaged 45.2% success. VLA-OPD (Distill) increased this to 71.1% average success, closely approaching the teacher's 74.0% performance and outperforming other baselines. This demonstrated the method's efficacy and generalization to complex morphologies.

#### 4.3. Mitigating Catastrophic Forgetting

An analysis of catastrophic forgetting, using a seen-unseen task trade-off, indicated that VLA-OPD effectively preserves pre-trained generalist capabilities:
- Offline SFT exhibited severe forgetting, with performance on held-out unseen tasks collapsing as performance on target (seen) tasks improved.
- Both VLA-OPD and online RL methods, which leverage on-policy data, largely avoided this collapse. VLA-OPD maintained competitive performance on unseen tasks while achieving strong mastery of seen tasks, suggesting suitability for continual learning.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies

Ablation studies validated key design choices of VLA-OPD:
- **Alignment Objective (Reverse-KL vs. Forward-KL vs. Hard-CE)**:
 - Reverse-KL consistently led to stable and superior task success rates.
 - Forward-KL suffered a significant performance drop in early training, correlating with an "entropy explosion" where the policy became overly diffused due to mimicking teacher uncertainty in out-of-distribution states.
 - Hard-CE plateaued at a suboptimal success rate, associated with "premature entropy collapse," where the policy lost action diversity by rigidly tracking argmax targets.
 - The actor's entropy plot confirmed that Reverse-KL maintained a healthy, bounded entropy, supporting decisive yet explorative behavior.
- **Impact of Group Sampling Size (G)**: Increasing the group size (G) from 2 to 8 generally led to smoother optimization and higher final success rates (G=8 achieved 89% on LIBERO-Object). However, even smaller group sizes (e.g., G=2) remained effective, reaching over 80% success. This indicates a favorable trade-off between gradient variance, computational efficiency, and wall-clock time, as sufficient signal-to-noise ratio is maintained even with minimal grouping.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The VLA-OPD framework represents a significant contribution to the post-training methodologies for Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, with several implications for robotic manipulation research and deployment.

**Technical Significance:**
- **Bridging Paradigms**: The core technical contribution lies in proposing a unified framework that successfully integrates the sample efficiency of offline Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) with the robustness and active error correction of online Reinforcement Learning (RL). This addresses a critical gap where prior methods either suffered from distribution shifts (SFT) or prohibitive sample costs (RL).
- **Optimal Alignment Objective**: The identification and empirical validation of the Reverse-KL divergence as a superior alignment objective for VLA distillation is a key insight. By promoting a bounded mode-seeking behavior, it elegantly resolves the challenges of entropy explosion (seen with Forward-KL) and premature entropy collapse (seen with Hard-CE) in out-of-distribution states. This ensures stable and effective policy learning, allowing the student to confidently capture the teacher's primary intent while retaining necessary action diversity.
- **Catastrophic Forgetting Mitigation**: VLA-OPD provides a principled approach to mitigate catastrophic forgetting. By anchoring gradient updates to the student's actively explored policy manifold, it achieves "gentle" alignment that preserves pre-trained generalist capabilities, facilitating continual learning without the need for extensive replay buffers.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Enhanced Sample Efficiency for VLA Training**: By converting sparse environmental rewards into dense, token-level teacher supervision on student-generated trajectories, VLA-OPD drastically improves sample efficiency. This can significantly reduce the computational resources and time required to train and fine-tune large VLA models, making advanced robotic capabilities more accessible.
- **Increased Robustness and Reliability**: The on-policy nature of VLA-OPD enables active error correction, allowing the model to learn recovery behaviors in self-induced out-of-distribution states. This directly contributes to the deployment of more robust and reliable robotic policies in real-world, dynamic environments.
- **Scalable Foundation Model Development**: The framework offers a scalable pathway for continuous development of foundation models in robotics. It allows for the efficient transfer of robust behaviors from various expert teachers (e.g., open-source checkpoints, proprietary APIs, or specialized policies) into new or upgraded generalist student backbones. This circumvents the instabilities and high costs associated with training VLA policies from scratch via online RL.
- **Application in Data-Scarce and Complex Scenarios**: Demonstrated efficacy in data-scarce settings (e.g., 1-trajectory initialization on LIBERO) and complex multi-arm coordination tasks (RoboTwin2.0) suggests broad applicability. This is particularly impactful for tasks where collecting extensive expert demonstrations is challenging or expensive.

In conclusion, VLA-OPD offers a methodology for VLA model post-training that can lead to more efficient, robust, and generalizable robotic systems, addressing fundamental challenges in the field of embodied artificial intelligence. Future research could investigate reducing the framework's reliance on specific teacher models, potentially through self-improvement or alternative forms of dense supervision.
