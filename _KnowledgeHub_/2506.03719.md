# On the Closed-Form of Flow Matching: Generalization Does Not Arise from Target Stochasticity

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Inria and Mila demonstrate that generalization in flow matching models for high-dimensional data does not arise from target stochasticity, but rather from the network's approximation failures, particularly at early generative time steps. Their Empirical Flow Matching method, which uses a more deterministic target, yields consistent performance improvements on image generation benchmarks.

## Problem

- The discrepancy between the theoretical tendency of flow matching models to memorize training data and their practical ability to generalize.
- Investigating whether the stochastic nature of the training targets in flow matching models is a critical factor for generalization, as previously hypothesized.
- Understanding the fundamental mechanisms underpinning generalization in high-dimensional deep generative models.

## Method

- Utilize the closed-form expression of the ==optimal velocity field== in conditional flow matching to analyze its properties and the network's approximation error.
- Propose "Empirical Flow Matching (EFM)," a Monte Carlo approximation of the optimal velocity field, designed to reduce target stochasticity and serve as a more stable regression target.
- Implement a "hybrid model" approach to experimentally identify specific time intervals during the generative process where generalization capabilities are determined.

## Results

- Target stochasticity was found to be negligible in high-dimensional real datasets, refuting the "generalization through variance" hypothesis.
- Generalization directly correlated with the neural network's failure to perfectly approximate the optimal closed-form velocity field, especially at early time steps.
- The proposed Empirical Flow Matching (EFM) method, using a more deterministic target, demonstrated improved performance with lower FID scores on CIFAR-10, CelebA, and FMNIST image generation tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The "stochastic target" of vanilla Conditional Flow Matching is effectively non-stochastic in high-dimensional data settings for most of the generative process.
> - Generalization in flow matching models correlates with the neural network's inability to perfectly approximate the theoretically memorizing optimal velocity field.
> - Approximation failures occurring at early time steps (e.g., t < 0.2) are critical for fostering generalization and breaking memorization.
