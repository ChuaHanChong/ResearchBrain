# Physics Filtering Favors the Generalization of Robot Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> PhyFilter is a lightweight, model-agnostic feedback module that corrects a learned policy's or dynamics model's output online by low-pass filtering the learning residual, reconstructed from real-time state feedback and the known differential structure of robot dynamics or kinematics. Filter gains are auto-learned via adjoint optimal control. Across quadruped, drone, aerial-manipulator and acceleration tasks it generalizes out of distribution, reaching centimeter-level aerial capture under wind.

## Problem

- Scaling robot data by teleoperation is slow and costly, while simulation and video generation stay bottlenecked by the sim-to-real gap and non-standard platform interfaces.
- Domain randomization buys robustness by averaging over randomized parameters, sacrificing nominal accuracy and demanding heavy manual engineering of randomization ranges before deployment.
- Prior physics-informed learning bakes structure into the network so it needs retraining, and classical disturbance observers discard the learned model, lumping everything into one disturbance.

## Method

- **PhyFilter** defines the ==learning residual== as truth minus model output and ==low-pass filters== it, made implementable by an ==auxiliary-variable reformulation== using only state feedback and known dynamics or kinematics.
- The ==nth-order filter== form generalizes first-order ==disturbance observers==, letting higher orders capture richer residuals, with gains set by ==pole placement== and the module attached plug-and-play to pretrained models.
- An ==auto-learning algorithm== recasts filter gains as control inputs of a discrete system, minimizing rollout-versus-label cost with forward rollout plus backward ==adjoint gradients== and ==Adam==.

## Results

- Quadruped trained only on flat simulated terrain held speed tracking error under **0.2 m/s** at **60%** body-weight payload, **137.33%** beyond the training maximum, and beat baseline and RL2AC on **five** unseen terrains.
- On a drone tracking a **2 m/s** circle with unseen wind added to mass uncertainty, mean absolute tracking error fell **30.22%** versus SEER-I and **50.17%** versus the baseline.
- Aerial manipulator kept **2.5 cm** end-effector error under **5 m/s** wind and **0.3 kg** unmodeled mass, improving mean **23.99%** and variance **55.04%**; baseline and SEER-I failed the grasp.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Cheap, deliberately simplified physical structure plus real-time feedback can substitute for large-scale data and aggressive domain randomization when the target is out-of-distribution robustness.
> - Feedback correction during RL training shapes an intrinsically better policy: the quadruped still transferred across real terrains with the correction switched off at deployment.
> - Residual filtering corrects outputs without touching network weights and runs at 500 Hz on a microcontroller, but it lumps all uncertainty sources into one unseparated signal.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses a fundamental challenge in robot learning: achieving robust generalization to unseen environments. This area of robotics is currently influenced by the success of large language models (LLMs), which have demonstrated remarkable capabilities primarily driven by massive datasets, large neural networks, and extensive computational power. A key question in robotics is whether similar data-scaling strategies can enable human-level robotic intelligence and generalization.

Current data-scaling efforts in robotics typically follow two paths:
1. **Human teleoperation:** Collecting real-world demonstrations. This approach is costly, time-consuming, and technically challenging to scale to LLM-level data volumes.
2. **Synthetic data from simulation:** Using physics engines (e.g., Isaac Lab, MuJoCo) or video-based generation methods. While this can improve performance on some tasks, it is constrained by the "sim-to-real gap," high computational overhead, and a lack of standardized data formats and interfaces across diverse robotic platforms.

The paper posits that living organisms achieve robust generalization not solely through data scaling but also by leveraging innate or acquired physical structures and real-time feedback mechanisms. This bio-inspired perspective suggests that intelligent robots could also benefit from integrating accessible physics structures or prior knowledge, rather than exclusively relying on data-intensive approaches.

This work contributes to the field of physics-informed machine learning and control. Existing physics-informed methods often involve retraining modified neural networks. This paper introduces a "plug-and-play" module that can be directly integrated with pre-trained models, contrasting with approaches that necessitate re-training or extensive domain randomization to achieve generalization. It aims to offer an alternative or complementary strategy to overcome the limitations of data-centric approaches and bridge the sim-to-real gap, particularly in scenarios where data collection is expensive and scalability is limited.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the generalization capabilities of robot learning systems, enabling them to adapt robustly to unseen environments and dynamics uncertainties, even with limited training data. This is motivated by the observed limitations of data-scaling strategies in robotics and inspired by the adaptive mechanisms found in living organisms.

Specific motivations and objectives include:

- **Addressing Data-Scaling Challenges:** Current robot learning paradigms heavily rely on collecting massive real-world data or generating synthetic data. Both methods present significant bottlenecks: real-world data collection is expensive, slow, and difficult to scale to magnitudes seen in LLMs, while synthetic data often suffers from the "sim-to-real gap" and high computational costs. The research seeks an alternative to this reliance on extensive datasets.
- **Bio-Inspired Generalization:** Living organisms demonstrate remarkable adaptability by integrating intrinsic physical structures and continuous feedback-driven learning. The motivation is to translate these biological principles into robotic systems, specifically exploring how physical architectures and real-time interactions can improve learning generalization.
- **Leveraging Physical Priors and Feedback:** Robots inherently operate within physical laws and interact with their environment, generating real-time state feedback. The objective is to harness these readily available physical differential structures and feedback mechanisms to correct learning outputs.
- **Developing a Model-Agnostic Solution:** The aim is to create a lightweight module, termed PhyFilter, that can be seamlessly integrated with diverse robot policies, including those derived from reinforcement learning (RL) and supervised learning (SL), without requiring extensive modifications or retraining of the base learning model.
- **Enhancing Interpretability and Convergence:** By framing the correction mechanism within a filtering paradigm, the research seeks to improve the interpretability of robot learning outcomes and facilitate faster convergence during adaptation to new conditions.
- **Automating Parameter Tuning:** A practical challenge in control and filtering is the manual tuning of parameters. The research aims to develop an auto-learning algorithm to optimize PhyFilter's parameters, thereby eliminating manual effort and making the module more deployable.
- **Demonstrating Broad Applicability:** The objective is to validate PhyFilter across a range of representative robotic systems and tasks, including quadruped locomotion, drone maneuvering flight, aerial manipulation, and acceleration perception, to illustrate its versatility and effectiveness in diverse scenarios involving dynamics uncertainties, payload variations, wind disturbances, and distribution shifts.

In essence, the research aims to establish that physics-filtered feedback can serve as an effective alternative to massive data scaling for achieving robot generalization, offering a pathway toward more adaptable and reliable robots in complex, uncertain real-world environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research introduces **PhyFilter**, a physics-filtered learning approach designed to enhance generalization and interpretability in robot learning. This module operates by correcting learning outcomes based on accessible physical differential structures and real-time state feedback.

#### 3.1. Core Concept: Physics Filtered Learning

The fundamental idea is to refine the output of a learned model, $f_{\theta}(t)$, by compensating for a "learning residual" $\gamma(t)$, which represents the difference between the true unknown function $f(t)$ and the learned model's output: $\gamma(t) = f(t) - f_{\theta}(t)$. This residual arises from unseen factors such as sensor noise, external disturbances, or unmodeled interactions.
The proposed correction takes the form $\hat{f}(t) = f_{\theta}(t) + F(\gamma(t))$, where $F(\cdot)$ is a user-defined low-pass filter. The challenge is that $\gamma(t)$ is not directly observable during deployment.

#### 3.2. Mathematical Formulation and Implementation

To overcome the unobservability of $\gamma(t)$, the paper leverages the a priori structure of the physical system, typically represented by a general differential equation: $\dot{x} = f(x, z) + g(x, z)$. Here, $x$ is the system state, $z$ an external factor, $g(\cdot)$ a known nonlinear mapping, and $f(\cdot)$ the unknown part to be learned.
The direct implementation of the low-pass filter on $\gamma(t)$ is mathematically reformulated into an implementable form (Eq. 5 in the paper) that relies solely on accessible measurements and the differential structure. This involves introducing an auxiliary variable $\xi$ and expressing the corrected learning outcome $\hat{f}(t)$ in terms of this auxiliary variable, the learned model $f_{\theta}(t)$, integrated terms derived from the filter parameters, and the system state $x$. The derived formulation avoids the need for higher-order derivatives of unknown or strenuous-to-obtain signals.
The paper also highlights that special cases of PhyFilter, such as EVOLVER, SEER-II, and feedback neural networks, emerge when a first-order filter is used and specific learning functions are employed. PhyFilter is distinguished from classical disturbance observers by its ability to guide RL policy learning, integrate with SL outputs, support higher-order filtering, and feature auto-learned gains.

#### 3.3. Auto-learning Algorithm for Filter Parameters

To eliminate the need for manual tuning of filter parameters $a_f = \{a_0, a_1, \dots, a_{n-1}\}$, an auto-learning algorithm is developed. This treats the filter parameters as control inputs to a dynamical system whose states concatenate the auxiliary variable $\xi$ and successive integrals of $\hat{f}$ and $f_{\theta}$. The problem is formulated as an optimal control problem, minimizing a cost function $J(a_f)$ that penalizes the discrepancy between the model rollout $\hat{f}_i$ and a labeled sample $f^{\ast}_i$.
The solution involves applying the Lagrange multiplier method to derive first-order optimality conditions. The analytic gradient $\nabla_{a_f} L$ is computed using a forward rollout of the state followed by a backward adjoint solve (reverse-mode differentiation). This gradient is then used with a gradient descent algorithm (e.g., Adam optimizer, mini-batching, early stopping) to iteratively update $a_f$.

#### 3.4. Experimental Validation

PhyFilter's effectiveness is validated across four robotic systems, covering both reinforcement learning (RL) and supervised learning (SL) paradigms, and demonstrated to be effective in improving generalization under dynamics uncertainties.

1. **Quadruped Locomotion (RL-based):**
 - **Baseline:** Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) based on (Rudin et al., 2022).
 - **PhyFilter Integration:** Integrated into the joint-space control layer to estimate torque uncertainties.
 - **Training:** Deliberately simplified, using only simulated flat terrain with standard parameter randomization (e.g., small payload range).
 - **Physical Prior:** Simplified inertia matrix $M(q)$ (diagonalized) and Coriolis force $C(q, \dot{q})$ (linear damping) were used to test robustness to modeling inaccuracy.
 - **Evaluation:** Generalization to unseen high speeds, varying payloads (beyond training range), and diverse unseen real-world terrains (stairs, rough, wave, gravel, flagstone, lawn, sand).

2. **Drone Maneuvering Flight (SL-based):**
 - **Baseline:** SEER-I (Jia et al., 2025a), a learning-based adaptive estimator for mass uncertainty.
 - **PhyFilter Integration:** Plug-and-play to filter the output of SEER-I.
 - **Training:** SEER-I was trained for mass uncertainty.
 - **Evaluation:** Drone commanded to follow a circular trajectory under mass uncertainty and *unseen* wind disturbances.

3. **Aerial Manipulation (SL-based):**
 - **Baseline:** SEER-I (Jia et al., 2025a) for coupled aerial manipulator dynamics.
 - **PhyFilter Integration:** Plug-and-play on top of SEER-I.
 - **Task:** Pick-and-place mission requiring centimeter-level precision.
 - **Disturbances:** Maximum 5 m/s wind and unmodeled 10% (0.3 kg) mass uncertainty.
 - **Physical Prior:** Detailed analytical modeling of coupling disturbances, with the assumption that unknown high-order terms are analytical functions decomposable using Chebyshev polynomials.

4. **Acceleration Perception (SL-based):**
 - **Baseline:** Fully connected neural network differentiator trained to map historical velocities to acceleration.
 - **PhyFilter Integration:** Filters the output of the neural network differentiator using kinematic structure.
 - **Training:** Real flight experiments data (0-2 m/s).
 - **Evaluation:** Testing on data with distribution shift (0-3 m/s).
 - **Comparisons:** Robust differentiator (RD), tracking differentiator (TD), and discrete differentiator (DD).
 - **Ablation Studies:** Filter order, pole selection, and sampling rate.

5. **Humanoid Robot (Supplementary):**
 - **Setup:** Policy trained with standard randomization, feedforward model via state-synchronized parallel MuJoCo simulation.
 - **Evaluation:** Response to in-distribution and out-of-distribution lateral external forces.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research demonstrates that PhyFilter consistently enhances generalization and robustness across various robotic systems and learning paradigms, often with simplified training conditions.

#### 4.1. Quadruped Locomotion

- **Training Performance:** PhyFilter consistently achieved higher maximum training rewards compared to the baseline policy trained on simulated flat terrain. It also exhibited more coordinated locomotion, suggesting that a robust low-level controller can improve high-level RL policy learning efficiency.
- **Generalization to Unseen Dynamics:**
 - **High Speed Tracking:** PhyFilter showed superior accuracy in tracking an unseen high speed of 2.83 m/s, outperforming both the baseline and RL2AC.
 - **Payload Variations:** Under increasing payloads (up to 60% of robot weight, significantly beyond training range), PhyFilter maintained smaller tracking errors (≤ 0.2 m/s), whereas baseline and RL2AC performance degraded.
 - **Terrain Adaptation (Simulation):** Despite training only on flat terrain, PhyFilter exhibited excellent adaptation to diverse unseen terrains (stairs, rough, wave, gravel roads), achieving consistently higher passing success rates than baseline and RL2AC.
- **Sim-to-Real Transfer:** The policy trained with PhyFilter on simulated flat ground successfully transferred to real hardware. It enabled stable locomotion across challenging natural terrains (flagstone, lawn, sand, gravel) without additional tuning. In contrast, the baseline policy (trained on flat terrain) exhibited poor adaptability and collapsed on sandy and gravel surfaces. The study also noted that the PhyFilter-guided policy could transfer successfully even with the filter switched off at deployment, indicating that PhyFilter contributes to shaping an intrinsically better policy during training.

#### 4.2. Drone Maneuvering Flight

- **Uncertainty Generalization:** While the SEER-I baseline improved tracking under mass uncertainty, its performance degraded significantly when unseen wind disturbances were introduced.
- **PhyFilter Performance:** With PhyFilter employed, the mean absolute tracking error was reduced by 30.22% relative to SEER-I and 50.17% relative to the baseline when facing combined mass and wind uncertainties. This indicates PhyFilter's ability to improve generalization to previously unseen disturbances.
- **Uncertainty Identification:** PhyFilter allowed for the identification of wind-induced and inherent dynamical uncertainties in addition to mass variations, suggesting a more comprehensive uncertainty handling capability.

#### 4.3. Aerial Manipulation

- **Precision Task Success:** In a pick-and-place mission under 5 m/s wind and 10% mass uncertainty, both the baseline and SEER-I failed to capture the object due to unseen disturbances.
- **PhyFilter Performance:** PhyFilter enabled the aerial manipulator to achieve centimeter-level manipulation (maximum manipulation error of 2.5 cm), successfully completing the task. This represented a 23.99% average and 55.04% variance improvement over the baseline. This highlights PhyFilter's compatibility with precision-critical interactive tasks and its ability to generalize under multiple complex disturbances.

#### 4.4. Acceleration Perception

- **Robustness to Distribution Shift:** A purely learning-based neural network differentiator showed generalization failure when tested on data outside its training distribution (0-3 m/s velocities vs. 0-2 m/s training).
- **PhyFilter Accuracy:** PhyFilter achieved the highest accuracy on the testing set, outperforming the learning-based model and classical differentiators (RD, TD, DD).
- **Ablation Studies:** The studies on filter order and pole selection indicated that higher-order filters benefited from larger pole values. The auto-learned optimal parameters for PhyFilter were consistent with manually adjusted optimal parameters, confirming the effectiveness of the auto-learning algorithm.
- **Sampling Rate Robustness:** PhyFilter consistently outperformed the purely learning-based approach across a range of sampling intervals (0.002 s to 0.018 s).

#### 4.5. Humanoid Robot (Supplementary)

- PhyFilter enabled the humanoid robot to maintain stable standing when subjected to both in-distribution and out-of-distribution lateral external forces. In contrast, the baseline policy lost balance and fell under out-of-distribution forces.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents PhyFilter as a significant development in robot learning, particularly for enhancing generalization capabilities in real-world applications where data collection is challenging and environments are uncertain. The significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Alternative to Data-Intensive Approaches:** The core contribution is demonstrating that physics-filtered feedback can serve as a potent alternative to the massive data scaling paradigm prevalent in other AI domains like LLMs. This is crucial for robotics, where data collection is inherently expensive, time-consuming, and difficult to scale.
- **Reduced Training Burden and Engineering Effort:** PhyFilter allows for effective generalization even when policies are trained in simplified simulated environments (e.g., flat terrain for quadruped locomotion), significantly reducing the need for extensive domain randomization. This translates into substantial reductions in engineering effort and manual tuning required to create effective training scenarios that cover all possible real-world uncertainties.
- **Enhanced Generalization and Robustness:** The consistent performance improvements across diverse robotic platforms (quadruped, drone, aerial manipulator, perception module) and under various unseen uncertainties (terrains, payloads, wind, distribution shifts) validate PhyFilter's ability to bridge the sim-to-real gap and enhance robustness in practical deployments.
- **Model-Agnostic and Plug-and-Play Design:** PhyFilter's lightweight, model-agnostic, and plug-and-play nature allows for its integration with existing pre-trained RL and SL models without requiring retraining or complex modifications. This facilitates broader adoption and compatibility with diverse robotic systems and learning architectures.
- **Computational Efficiency:** The module's ability to run in real-time on resource-constrained microprocessors (e.g., STM32F765 at 500 Hz for drones) highlights its practical applicability for embedded systems in robotics.
- **Improved Interpretability and Convergence:** By framing the correction mechanism as a filter, PhyFilter contributes to a more interpretable understanding of how learning residuals are managed, and its integration can also improve training convergence for high-level RL policies.
- **Robustness to Inaccurate Physical Priors:** The analytical robustness analysis demonstrates that PhyFilter's stability is maintained even when using simplified or inaccurate physical models, as the modeling errors are treated as suppressible components of the lumped residual. This pragmatic aspect makes it more applicable in real-world scenarios where perfect models are rarely available.
- **Potential for Broader AI Integration:** The bio-inspired approach, drawing parallels with adaptive mechanisms in living organisms, suggests a promising direction for future AI development that integrates inherent physical intelligence with learning, rather than purely abstract computation.

**Future Directions Identified by the Authors:**
The authors acknowledge limitations and outline future research directions:
1. **Refining Neural Network Parameters/Structure:** Currently, PhyFilter primarily enhances learning outputs. Future work could explore using the filtered knowledge to refine the parameters or structure of the original neural network, potentially through continual learning.
2. **Improving Auto-learning Efficiency:** The current auto-learning algorithm, while effective, requires full forward rollouts for each iteration, which can be computationally expensive for large-scale data. Improving efficiency through methods like truncated or parallelized rollouts is a proposed area for development.
3. **Separating Uncertainty Components:** The learning residual $\gamma(t)$ can lump various uncertainties. Future research could investigate methods, such as meta-learning, to separate these components for a more granular understanding and targeted compensation.

Overall, PhyFilter offers a practical and theoretically supported method for enhancing robot learning generalization, with implications for developing more adaptive, robust, and deployable autonomous systems.
