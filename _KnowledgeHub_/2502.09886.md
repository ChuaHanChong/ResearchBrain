# Video2Policy: Scaling up Manipulation Tasks in Simulation through Internet Videos

> [!summary] Summary
> Video2Policy reconstructs manipulation tasks in simulation from internet RGB videos (SSv2), building object meshes and 6D poses, then uses a VLM to generate task code and iteratively refine RL reward functions. Trained policies hit 88% average success, beating LLM-only baselines, and transfer sim2real at 47%.

## Problem

- LLM-only task generation (e.g. Gensim, RoboGen) lacks grounded robotics knowledge, producing tasks with uninteresting objects and unrealistic behaviors not representative of real-world distributions.
- Digital-twin real2sim pipelines require careful real-to-sim alignment and per-scene human effort, making them hard to scale to many diverse scenes and tasks.
- Reward-design methods like Eureka need manually predefined success functions and lack visual grounding, limiting task diversity and reward quality for complex dynamic behaviors.

## Method

- **Video2Policy**'s ==scene reconstruction pipeline==: ==Grounding DINO== plus ==SAM-2== segmentation, ==InstantMesh== 3D reconstruction, a depth-based size-estimation ratio, and ==FoundationPose== 6D tracking to build object assets and URDFs from single-viewpoint videos.
- ==VLM-driven task code generation== producing six parts (scene info, reset, success, observation, observation-space, reward functions) from one ==GPT-4o== query, with curriculum-style example accumulation across tasks.
- ==Iterative in-context reward reflection==: sample 8 reward functions per round, train ==PPO==, feed training/eval logs plus ==chain-of-thought== examples back to GPT-4o to refine rewards across iterations.

## Results

- Across 12 SSv2 and self-collected tasks, Video2Policy averages **88%** success versus **71%** for Eureka, **45%** RoboGen, and **34%** Code-as-Policy, with notably lower variance on multi-object and dynamic tasks.
- A behavior-cloning generalist policy trained on **100** reconstructed lifting tasks reaches **75%** success on 10 unseen novel-object tasks, versus **32%** for Code-as-Policy and **26%** for BC-CoP.
- Ablations show removing iterative reward reflection drops success from **87%** to **48%**, the largest single-component degradation, followed by success-function picking (**57%**) and visual information (**75%**).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Grounding task and reward generation in real video visual priors, not just text captions, substantially improves diversity and quality of autonomously generated simulation tasks.
> - Sim2real transfer for policies trained on reconstructed-from-video data remains lossy (72% sim to 47% real), showing scene-reconstruction fidelity still bottlenecks real-world deployment.
> - Task-generation scalability from internet video volume is a viable alternative to hand-built benchmarks or per-scene digital twins for growing generalist-policy training data.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of generalist robotic policies, capable of performing a wide array of tasks in diverse environments, is a grand challenge in robotics. A primary bottleneck for training such policies is the immense amount of diverse and high-quality training data required. Current approaches to data generation face significant limitations, which "Video2Policy" aims to address.

**Current Landscape Challenges:**
1. **Real-world Data Collection:** Collecting expert data through teleoperation or autonomous policies in the real world is prohibitively expensive, time-consuming, and potentially unsafe.
2. **Simulation-based Data Generation:** While simulation offers a scalable alternative, existing methods struggle with generating diverse and realistic tasks automatically:
 - **Text-only LLM-driven Task Generation:** Approaches like Gensim (Wang et al., 2023b) leverage LLMs to generate tasks from text prompts. However, LLMs, lacking grounded robotics knowledge, often "hallucinate" tasks that are uninteresting, non-diverse, or involve impractical object assets. This limits their utility for training truly generalist policies that need to interact with the complexity of the real world.
 - **Digital Twin Approaches (Real2Sim):** Methods aiming to create high-fidelity digital replicas of real scenes (e.g., Hsu et al., 2023; Torne et al., 2024) offer excellent real-to-sim alignment but are typically designed for specific scenes and require extensive human effort or specialized scans. They lack the scalability to generate the vast diversity of scenes and tasks needed for generalist policies. They achieve scene-level, but not task-level diversity.
 - **Manual Simulation Benchmarks:** Existing benchmarks (e.g., ManiSkill2, Behavior-1K) are manually constructed, which makes them difficult to scale rapidly or adapt to new tasks.

**Video2Policy's Unique Contribution and Niche:**
"Video2Policy" carves out a novel niche by proposing a *visually grounded* and *scalable* data generation pipeline that overcomes the limitations of existing methods. Instead of relying solely on text or laborious digital twinning, it harnesses the vast and continuously growing repository of internet RGB videos depicting everyday human behaviors. This approach offers several advantages:
- **Visually Grounded Diversity:** Internet videos inherently capture diverse human behaviors, objects, and scene layouts. By reconstructing tasks directly from these videos, Video2Policy generates tasks that are inherently realistic, visually diverse, and behaviorally relevant for robotics. This addresses the "uninteresting behavior" and "uninteresting object assets" problems of text-only LLMs.
- **Scalability:** The abundance and accessibility of internet video data allow for significantly larger-scale data generation compared to manual creation or specific real-world scans.
- **Automated Policy Learning:** The framework integrates automated policy learning via reinforcement learning (RL) using LLM-generated reward functions. This improves upon previous LLM-driven RL methods (e.g., Eureka, RoboGen) by leveraging visual priors from the videos to inform success functions and employing an iterative reward reflection mechanism, leading to more robust and higher-quality policies.
- **Data Engine for Generalist Policies:** The paper positions Video2Policy as a "data engine" that produces expert trajectories in simulation. This generated data can then be used to train generalist visuomotor policies via imitation learning, which can finally be transferred back to real robots (Real2Sim2Real pipeline).

In essence, "Video2Policy" represents a significant step towards bridging the gap between abundant real-world human demonstration data (in the form of videos) and scalable, effective robotic learning in simulation. It pushes the boundaries of automated task generation and policy learning by integrating state-of-the-art computer vision and large language models within a cohesive robotic learning framework.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching goal of the "Video2Policy" research is to enable the development of generalist robotic policies by **scaling up the generation of diverse and realistic training data in simulation**. This goal is driven by several motivations rooted in the limitations of current robot learning paradigms:

**Key Objectives:**
1. **Automated Task Generation from Internet Videos:** To develop a framework that can automatically reconstruct and generate diverse and realistic robotic manipulation tasks within a simulation environment directly from internet RGB videos. This includes reconstructing object meshes, estimating their sizes, and tracking their 6D poses.
2. **Autonomous Policy Learning for Generated Tasks:** To design a pipeline that can autonomously learn effective reinforcement learning policies for the reconstructed tasks, utilizing advancements in large language models for reward function generation and iterative refinement.
3. **Scalable Data Engine for Generalist Policies:** To demonstrate that the data generated through this video-to-simulation-to-policy pipeline can be used to train a generalist policy capable of performing a wide range of tasks and generalizing to unseen scenarios.
4. **Real-world Transferability:** To validate the practicality of the entire pipeline by showing that the generalist policies trained in simulation can effectively transfer and perform tasks on real robotic hardware (Sim2Real).

**Motivation:**
The core motivations behind "Video2Policy" stem from the recognized bottlenecks in current robot learning research:
- **Data Scarcity for Generalist Policies:** Training generalist policies requires vast quantities of diverse, high-quality data. Real-world data collection is expensive, slow, and constrained by safety.
- **Limitations of Synthetic Data Generation:** While simulation offers cost-effective data generation, existing methods fall short in producing the necessary diversity and realism:
 - **Text-to-Task Arbitrariness:** LLM-based approaches often produce tasks that are not grounded in real-world object interactions or behaviors, leading to less useful training data. They struggle to capture the *distribution* of real-world tasks.
 - **Digital Twin Scalability:** High-fidelity digital twins are laborious to create and are typically specific to a single scene, hindering generalization across multiple diverse scenarios.
- **Challenges in Automated Reward Engineering:** For reinforcement learning, manually designing reward functions for every new task is impractical and time-consuming. While LLMs can assist, their initial outputs may be suboptimal or require significant human intervention to refine.
- **Leveraging Abundant Unstructured Data:** Internet videos represent an enormous, largely untapped source of real-world human behavior, object interactions, and scene variations. The motivation is to transform this unstructured visual data into structured, actionable training environments for robots.

By addressing these challenges, "Video2Policy" aims to create an autonomous and scalable pipeline for robotic skill acquisition, moving towards a future where robots can learn complex tasks efficiently from the wealth of human demonstrations available online.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The "Video2Policy" framework is meticulously designed as a two-phase pipeline to transform internet RGB videos into executable simulation tasks and learn corresponding robotic policies.

**Phase 1: Task Generation in Simulation from Videos**
This phase focuses on automatically reconstructing the essential elements of a task scene from an input video, primarily focusing on manipulated objects and their spatial relationships. The output is a structured JSON file containing task and object information for simulation.
1. **Object Grounding:**
 - **Detection:** Utilizes `Grounding DINO` to detect manipulated objects in the video's first frame, leveraging provided video captions or object labels (from SSv2 dataset) as text prompts. For self-recorded videos, captions and object names are provided manually.
 - **Segmentation:** `SAM-2` (Segment Anything Model 2) performs video segmentation. Bounding boxes from Grounding DINO are used, and five positive pixel points within each object mask in the first frame guide the segmentation across subsequent frames. This yields segmented masks for manipulated objects throughout the video.
2. **Object Reconstruction:**
 - **3D Mesh Reconstruction:** Given the segmentation masks for each frame, `InstantMesh` is used to reconstruct 3D meshes of the objects. InstantMesh is chosen for its ability to generate meshes from a single image, which is crucial for internet videos often recorded from a single viewpoint. The first or last frame (if the object is occluded in the first) is typically used.
 - **Size Estimation:** A simple yet effective method is proposed to estimate the realistic size of reconstructed meshes. `UniDepth` predicts the camera intrinsic matrix and depth for each image pixel. The maximum real-world distance between points within an object's masked region is calculated (`D_image`). This is then scaled by the maximum distance of vertices in the reconstructed mesh (`D_mesh`) to get a scaling ratio (ρ = `D_image / D_mesh`). This ensures relative size accuracy even with some absolute depth estimation noise.
3. **6D Position Tracking of Objects:**
 - `FoundationPose` is employed in a model-based setup to estimate the 6D position (position and orientation) of each reconstructed object across all video frames. It takes the object mesh, predicted camera intrinsics, and depth information as input.
 - Finally, a `URDF` file (Unified Robot Description Format) is automatically generated for each object based on its mesh and calculated scaled size.
 - For LLM prompting, only the 6D poses from the first and final frames are provided, along with task descriptions, assuming these are sufficient to infer object relationships and task goals, and allowing for randomization of initial states.

**Phase 2: Task Code Generation and Reinforcement Learning**
This phase takes the visual information extracted in Phase 1 and translates it into executable simulation code, followed by policy learning using reinforcement learning with iteratively refined rewards.
1. **Task Code Generation (via GPT-4o):**
 - The framework uses `GPT-4o` to generate the complete task code for the `Isaac Gym` simulator. This code is structured into six key parts:
 - **Scene Information:** Derived directly from the JSON file (task title, video description, object info: sizes, URDF paths, tracked 6D position lists).
 - **Reset Function:** Positions objects at the start of an episode based on inferred spatial relationships from video data and task description.
 - **Success Function:** Defines the criteria for task completion, generated by GPT-4o using task description, 6D pose lists, and Chain-of-Thoughts (CoT) examples.
 - **Observation Function:** Determines additional observations (e.g., distance between gripper and object) needed for the policy.
 - **Observation Space Function:** Calculates the correct shape for the neural network's observation input.
 - **Reward Function:** Follows principles from prior LLM-driven reward generation work (Ma et al., 2023) with CoT examples.
 - Eight candidate task codes are generated initially, and GPT-4o selects the most reasonable one as the base code candidate for the next stage, checking for correctness, reasonability, and efficiency.
2. **Reinforcement Learning and Reward Function Iteration:**
 - **Policy Training:** The `PPO` (Proximal Policy Optimization) algorithm is used to train policies within the Isaac Gym environment, utilizing the generated reward function (`R_hat`) and a heavily weighted success reward (`R_01`).
 - **In-context Reward Reflection:** Inspired by Eureka, `GPT-4o` iteratively fine-tunes the reward function. In each iteration:
 - `N=8` different reward functions are sampled for training.
 - Training and evaluation logs (including accumulated rewards for successful vs. failed trajectories) are collected.
 - The best-performing reward function from the previous iteration is selected.
 - GPT-4o, guided by these logs, specific instructions, and CoT examples, generates new, refined reward functions. This allows the LLM to understand which reward components contribute to success or failure and adjust them.

**Sim2Real Policy Learning:**
"Video2Policy" acts as a data engine. After successful policies are learned in simulation, expert trajectories (RGB images, actions) are collected.
1. **General Policy Training:** An image-based general policy is trained via `Imitation Learning` (Behavior Cloning) from the collected expert trajectories. `Resnet18` is used as a backbone.
2. **Sim2Real Transfer:** To bridge the sim-to-real gap during deployment on a real Franka robot:
 - **Domain Randomization:** Noise is applied to actions, and physical properties (size, weight) of objects are randomized during simulation data collection.
 - **Input Representation:** Segmentation masks (generated by `SAM-2` on real images), robot end-effector (EEF) state, and gripper state are used as policy inputs.
 - Consistency between camera poses in sim and real is maintained.

This comprehensive methodology, integrating advanced computer vision, large language models, and robust reinforcement learning techniques, enables the automated creation of diverse robotic tasks and the training of generalist manipulation policies directly from readily available internet video data.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted in "Video2Policy" validate the efficacy of the proposed framework across several key aspects: policy learning, generalization, sim-to-real transfer, and the importance of its individual components.

#### 4.1. Policy Learning from Videos (Table 1 & Figure 4)

- **Superior Performance:** Video2Policy significantly outperforms baseline methods (Code-as-Policy, RoboGen, Eureka) across a diverse set of 9 tasks from the Something-Something V2 (SSv2) dataset and 3 more complex, self-collected tasks.
- **High Success Rates:** Achieves an impressive average success rate of 88% across all evaluated tasks.
- **Robustness to Complexity:** Shows particular strength in complex tasks, such as "Throw Garlic into Bowl," where baselines struggle or fail entirely (CoP: 0%, RoboGen: 3%, Eureka: 37%, V2P: 70%). This highlights the benefit of the visual grounding and iterative reward refinement for dynamic and challenging behaviors.
- **Effectiveness of Iterative Refinement:** Figure 4 clearly demonstrates that Video2Policy, with its iterative reward function evolution, achieves higher success rates over iterations compared to baselines and a variant without evolution, indicating the critical role of feedback-driven reward design.

#### 4.2. Policy Generalization Analysis from Diverse Videos (Figure 5 & Figure 6)

- **Strong Generalization:** A general lifting policy, trained via imitation learning on 100 successful trajectories generated by Video2Policy in simulation, exhibits remarkable generalization. It achieves a 75% average success rate on 10 *unseen* lifting tasks involving novel object shapes, textures, and even entirely unseen categories.
- **Outperformance of Baselines:** The BC-V2P model (Behavior Cloning from Video2Policy data) significantly outperforms BC-CoP (26% success rate) and direct Code-as-Policy (32% success rate) on these unseen tasks. This indicates that Video2Policy generates more informative and higher-quality expert data for imitation learning.
- **Scalability:** Figure 6 demonstrates a positive correlation between the number of training tasks (videos) and the success rate of the general policy on novel tasks. This confirms the scalability of the Video2Policy framework as a data engine; increasing the diversity and quantity of input videos leads to better generalist policies.

#### 4.3. Sim2Real for the Learned General Policy (Table 2)

- **Effective Transfer:** The general lifting policy, trained exclusively in simulation using the Video2Policy pipeline, demonstrates effective sim-to-real transfer, achieving a 47% success rate on real-world lifting tasks (mouse, cup, bread).
- **Real-world Challenges:** The results also highlight typical sim-to-real challenges, such as handling slippery objects (cup, 40% success) versus easier ones (mouse, bread, 50% success). The use of segmentation masks as input is noted to be particularly beneficial for unseen, deformable objects like bread.
- **Validation of Pipeline:** This successful real-world deployment validates the entire Video2Policy pipeline, proving its capability to generate data that is robust enough for practical robotic applications.

#### 4.4. Ablation Study (Table 3, Figure 10, Table 7, Table 8, Table 9)

- **Importance of Visual Information:** Removing visual information (using only video captions for code generation) leads to a notable performance drop (from 87% to 75% average success), underscoring the value of grounding task generation in visual data. Figure 10 further shows that visual information aids in generating more accurate and reasonable success functions, having a higher correlation (0.834) with ground truth compared to caption-only generation (0.557).
- **Criticality of Iterative Reward Design:** The most significant performance drop occurs when iterative reward reflection is removed (from 87% to 48%), emphasizing its crucial role in refining reward functions for successful policy learning.
- **Benefit of Multiple Samples and Success Picking:** Not sampling multiple reward functions (from 87% to 51%) or not picking the best success function (from 87% to 57%) also degrades performance, indicating that these strategies mitigate issues like hallucination and improve robustness.
- **Impact of 6D Position Tracking:** Ablating 6D position tracking information reduces performance, particularly for multi-object tasks (e.g., Uncover, Throw), highlighting its importance for accurately inferring object relationships (Table 7).
- **No "Reset Cheating":** Experiments confirm that using Video2Policy's generated reset functions does not unfairly benefit its performance compared to baselines, as manually forcing baselines to use the same reset functions showed negligible performance changes (Table 9). This validates the fairness of the comparisons.

In summary, the findings demonstrate that "Video2Policy" is a highly effective framework for automating diverse task generation and policy learning from internet videos, yielding superior performance, strong generalization capabilities, and successful real-world transfer. The ablation studies further pinpoint the key methodological contributions that drive these results.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

"Video2Policy" presents a significant advancement in the field of robot learning, with the potential to profoundly impact how generalist robotic policies are developed and deployed.

**Significance:**
1. **Overcoming Data Scarcity:** The most significant contribution is addressing the critical bottleneck of data scarcity and diversity in robot learning. By effectively leveraging the vast, ubiquitous, and growing repository of internet videos, the framework offers a scalable, cost-effective, and automated alternative to laborious manual data collection or simulation design.
2. **Bridging Vision and Language for Robotics:** The work masterfully integrates state-of-the-art computer vision models (Grounding DINO, SAM-2, InstantMesh, UniDepth, FoundationPose) with advanced large language models (GPT-4o) to create a cohesive pipeline. This multi-modal approach enables robots to derive actionable knowledge from unstructured visual data and natural language descriptions, moving beyond text-only task specifications.
3. **Enhanced Automated Task Generation:** Unlike previous methods that often yield uninteresting or unrealistic tasks, Video2Policy generates visually grounded and behaviorally diverse tasks from real-world human demonstrations. This ensures that the generated simulation environments are directly relevant and useful for training robots to perform everyday human-like behaviors.
4. **Robust Policy Learning and Reward Engineering:** The iterative, in-context reward reflection mechanism, informed by visual priors, significantly improves the robustness and quality of learned policies compared to prior LLM-driven reward generation methods. This reduces the need for extensive human expertise in reward shaping.
5. **Proof-of-Concept for Generalist Policies:** By demonstrating successful generalization to unseen objects and categories, and effective sim-to-real transfer, the paper provides a strong proof-of-concept for the feasibility of training generalist visuomotor policies using this scalable data generation approach.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Accelerated Robot Skill Acquisition:** The automated nature of "Video2Policy" can drastically accelerate the process of teaching robots new manipulation skills. Instead of requiring human experts to painstakingly program or teleoperate tasks, robots could potentially learn from observing countless online videos.
- **Democratization of Robot Learning:** By reducing the entry barrier of data collection and task design, the framework could democratize access to advanced robot learning research and development. Smaller labs or individual researchers could potentially train sophisticated robot policies without massive computational or hardware investments.
- **Foundation for Embodied AI:** The ability to convert human video demonstrations into executable robotic tasks and policies in simulation is a crucial step towards building more capable embodied AI agents that can truly understand and interact with the physical world based on human knowledge.
- **Development of Robust Robotics Systems:** Training on highly diverse, visually rich data derived from real-world human behaviors is likely to lead to more robust and adaptable robotic policies that can handle variations in objects, environments, and task execution.
- **Future Synergies with Foundation Models:** As general foundation models in computer vision and natural language processing continue to improve in terms of reconstruction accuracy, understanding, and code generation capabilities, the performance of frameworks like Video2Policy will inherently scale further, paving the way for even more sophisticated robot behaviors.

In conclusion, "Video2Policy" lays a robust foundation for a new paradigm in robot learning, transforming passive internet videos into an active "data engine" for training generalist robotic policies, ultimately bringing us closer to robots that can seamlessly integrate into and assist with a wide range of human activities.
