# UniTac: A Unified Multimodal Model for Cross-Sensor Tactile Understanding and Generation

> [!summary] Summary
> UniTac, a unified multimodal model, integrates tactile understanding and generation capabilities across various sensor types by modeling both sensor-level configurations and object-level semantics. The model achieved an overall score of 66.51 on the PHYSICLEAR-Test benchmark, outperforming existing multimodal models in tactile reasoning tasks, and generated realistic tactile images and videos with an average SSIM of 0.836 and PSNR of 19.93.

## Problem

- Existing unified multimodal models (UMMs) primarily focus on visual-textual data, neglecting tactile information and its unique dual-level nature (sensor-level configuration and object-level semantics).
- Tactile AI faces challenges of data scarcity, fragmentation across diverse sensors, and limited cross-sensor generalization, often treating understanding and generation as separate problems.
- Many prior works fail to jointly model both the non-contact (sensor configuration) and contact (object interaction) stages crucial for interpreting and synthesizing realistic tactile signals.

## Method

- **UniTac** is a unified multimodal model designed with a ==Touch Encoder==, a ==Multimodal Large Language Model (MLLM)== based on Qwen-VL 2.5, a ==Sensor-Aware DiT Projector==, and a ==Touch Decoder== to process and generate tactile data.
- It employs a ==Dual-Level Mixture Comprehension (DLMC)== strategy for understanding, jointly supervising the MLLM with object-level property descriptions and sensor identification tasks.
- For generation, a ==Two-Stage Aligned Generation== (reconstruction and sensor-aware alignment) and a ==Sensor-Prior Sampling Strategy (SPSS)== are used to ensure controllable and physically consistent tactile outputs across sensors.

## Results

- UniTac-7B achieved an overall score of **66.51** on the PHYSICLEAR-Test benchmark, outperforming state-of-the-art UMMs by significant margins (e.g., **42.39** points higher on Property-Object Matching).
- The model demonstrated high fidelity in tactile image generation (**0.836** SSIM and **19.93** PSNR average) and produced continuous, temporally consistent tactile videos across various sensor types.
- Real-world robotic deployment showcased UniTac's utility in tasks like language-guided fabric selection (**95%** success) and deformation-aware grasping (**100%** success), and its generated data improved cross-sensor grasp classification accuracy from **50.00%** to **99.37%** in Digit-to-GelSight transfer.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Unifying tactile understanding and generation within a single model architecture effectively enhances performance and generalization across both tasks and diverse sensor types.
> - Explicitly modeling both sensor-level configurations and object-level semantics is crucial for robust tactile interpretation and realistic synthesis, enabling improved cross-sensor adaptability.
> - Large-scale, composite multi-sensor datasets, combined with dual-level comprehension and sensor-aware generation strategies, yield superior tactile reasoning and physically consistent data generation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work on UniTac is positioned within the expanding field of unified multimodal models (UMMs), which aim to integrate perception and generation capabilities across various data modalities within a single architectural framework. Historically, UMMs have primarily focused on visual-textual modalities, as exemplified by models such as Janus-Pro, Chameleon, Emu3, and BLIP3-o. These models leverage transformer-based backbones and diffusion paradigms for tasks involving images and text.

Concurrently, research in tactile sensing has advanced significantly, driven by the development of visuo-tactile sensors like GelSight, DIGIT, and Duragel. These sensors capture rich data that encode both sensor-level configurations (e.g., lighting, gel deformation) and object-level semantics (e.g., surface geometry, hardness, roughness). Accurate tactile understanding is considered instrumental for applications in embodied intelligence and robotics, enabling precise manipulation, material recognition, and geometric inference. High-fidelity tactile generation supports simulation-based training, cross-modal learning, and realistic haptic feedback.

Despite these advances, several challenges persist in the tactile domain that UniTac aims to address:
- **Limited Integration in UMMs:** Existing UMMs rarely extend their capabilities to tactile data, leaving a gap for models that can jointly process touch information for both understanding and generation.
- **Data Scarcity and Fragmentation:** Many tactile-language models are trained on relatively small, self-curated datasets, limiting their generalizability. Conversely, larger open datasets often remain isolated, hindering comprehensive training.
- **Cross-Sensor Generalization:** Significant domain gaps exist between different tactile sensors due to variations in their characteristics and illumination conditions, impeding models from generalizing across diverse hardware.
- **Separate Problem Paradigms:** Tactile understanding and generation are often treated as distinct research problems, without exploring their intrinsic connections within a unified framework.
- **Dual-Level Nature of Tactile Data:** Tactile data acquisition inherently involves a non-contact stage (capturing sensor configuration) and a contact stage (recording object-level physical properties). Many prior works do not jointly model these two levels of information, which are considered crucial for interpreting tactile patterns and synthesizing realistic signals.

UniTac is presented as the first UMM specifically designed for the tactile domain, aiming to bridge these gaps by unifying tactile understanding and generation, modeling the dual-level nature of tactile information, and leveraging a large-scale, composite multi-sensor dataset.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop UniTac, a unified multimodal model capable of performing both tactile understanding and generation tasks across different sensor types. This overarching goal is motivated by several specific challenges and opportunities within the tactile domain:

1. **To bridge the gap in unified multimodal modeling for touch:** Existing UMMs primarily focus on visual and textual data. The motivation is to extend this paradigm to the tactile domain, integrating the unique characteristics of touch—where both sensor-level configurations and object-level semantics jointly define the meaning of an interaction—into a single, coherent framework. This unification is posited to enhance the adaptability and scalability of multimodal systems, foundational for interactive and physically grounded intelligent agents.

2. **To address the underutilization of large-scale tactile datasets:** Current touch-language models often rely on relatively small, proprietary datasets. The researchers observe that a substantial volume of open, multi-sensor tactile data exists (over 400K video clips and 1.6 million frames) but is typically used in isolation. A key motivation is to integrate and leverage this collective data for joint training, thereby enriching the representation of tactile semantics and improving model generalization.

3. **To overcome cross-sensor generalization limitations:** Significant domain gaps among different tactile sensors (e.g., GelSight, DIGIT, Duragel) hinder models from generalizing their understanding or generation capabilities. The objective is to develop a model that can account for these sensor-level differences, enabling robust performance across heterogeneous tactile devices without requiring separate models for each sensor.

4. **To integrate tactile understanding and generation:** Previous tactile research often treats understanding and generation as separate problems. This work is motivated by the hypothesis that these two capabilities are intrinsically linked and can mutually benefit from a unified architectural approach. By training a single model on both tasks, the aim is to foster a deeper, more comprehensive grasp of tactile interactions.

5. **To explicitly model the dual-level nature of tactile data:** Tactile data acquisition is characterized by a transition from a non-contact stage (sensor configuration) to a contact stage (physical interaction with an object). The motivation is to design UniTac to explicitly model this two-stage process through a dual-level representation. This approach seeks to ensure that understanding models can interpret how sensor design influences perceived object properties, and generative models can synthesize tactile signals consistent with specific sensor configurations and object interactions. Specifically, for tactile understanding, the model aims to improve reasoning over physical and cross-sensor information through tasks like object property description and sensor identification. For tactile generation, the objective is to produce realistic tactile signals across sensors by designing a two-stage training paradigm (reconstruction and alignment) and a sensor-prior-based sampling strategy.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

UniTac is designed as a unified multimodal model to jointly perform tactile understanding and generation tasks across various sensor types. The methodology integrates several components and training strategies:

#### 3.1. Architecture Overview

UniTac comprises four main modules:
- **Touch Encoder:** This component extracts representations from tactile inputs (images and videos). It employs the AnyTouch architecture with a ViT-B/16 backbone, pretrained on a large corpus of multi-sensor tactile videos and images. The encoder is designed to capture both spatial and temporal features generalizable across heterogeneous sensors (e.g., Digit, GelSight, Duragel). It outputs 768-dimensional latent tokens, which encode both object-level semantics and sensor-level configurations. A set of learnable sensor tokens (five per sensor type) is incorporated to encode sensor-specific characteristics.
- **Multimodal Large Language Model (MLLM):** Serving as the shared backbone for both understanding and generation, the MLLM is based on Qwen-VL 2.5. Two configurations are utilized: a 3B model for computational efficiency in generation, and a 7B model for understanding tasks. The MLLM’s visual projector is replaced with a tactile embedding adaptor to integrate tactile latent tokens into the textual sequence for joint reasoning.
- **Sensor-Aware DiT (Diffusion Transformer) Projector:** This module, implemented with NextDiT, maps the MLLM’s textual outputs into the conditional space required by the touch decoder. It integrates semantic embeddings from the MLLM with sensor priors from the touch encoder, learning a conditional velocity field for feature alignment.
- **Touch Decoder:** This component synthesizes tactile data. For tactile image generation, it uses the SANA diffusion architecture, capable of producing 512x512 images. For tactile video generation, it is replaced with Wan v2.2, which generates 13-frame sequences at 448x448 resolution, designed to preserve temporal continuity.

#### 3.2. Data Preparation

UniTac is trained on a large-scale, composite visuo-tactile corpus, integrating five public datasets: Touch and Go, Tacquad, TVL, SSVTP, and PHYSICLEAR. This combined corpus consists of approximately 400K video clips and 1.6 million frames, all with aligned text annotations. Contact frames, identified by tactile deformations, are filtered by calculating differences from corresponding background frames.

#### 3.3. Dual-Level Mixture Comprehension (DLMC)

This strategy enhances tactile understanding by jointly supervising the MLLM from object-level and sensor-level perspectives using paired video-text data.
- **Input Formulation:** Tactile tokens (Z_i) from the touch encoder are spliced into the MLLM's text stream using special markers (`<T_VID>`, `</T_VID>`), along with an instructional prompt (Π_i) and accompanying text (T_i).
- **Training Objective:** The MLLM is optimized via a standard next-token prediction objective.
- **Object-Level Supervision (Property Description):** Prompts (e.g., "Describe the physical properties of...") instruct the MLLM to generate descriptions of contacted objects based on three tactile dimensions: roughness, hardness, and texture. This grounds the model in object-level semantics.
- **Sensor-Level Supervision (Sensor Identification):** Prompts (e.g., "Identify which tactile sensor captured this video") guide the model to verbalize the tactile sensor identity. This enforces sensitivity to sensor-specific cues (e.g., lighting, gel elasticity) and improves cross-sensor robustness.
- **Combined Loss:** The overall comprehension loss is a weighted sum of the property description loss (L_prop) and the sensor identification loss (L_sen), with a weighting factor (λ_sen = 0.1).

#### 3.4. Two-Stage Aligned Generation

This strategy is designed for controllable and sensor-consistent tactile generation:
- **Stage I: Reconstruction:** This stage focuses on learning a generative prior within the tactile domain, independent of the MLLM. Latent representations (Z_i = [Z_obj_i, Z_sen_i]) from the touch encoder are fed into the touch decoder to reconstruct the tactile signal. This stage can be trained in parallel with DLMC for efficiency, using a rectified flow formulation for the reconstruction loss.
- **Stage II: Sensor-Aware Alignment:** After learning tactile priors, this stage aligns the MLLM’s textual generation space with the tactile latent space. The MLLM produces object-level query embeddings from text descriptions. To reintroduce sensor awareness, these embeddings are concatenated with specific sensor tokens obtained from the pretrained touch encoder, forming a conditional representation (F_i). The sensor-aware DiT projector then learns a conditional vector field that transforms a Gaussian prior towards the tactile latent representation (Z_i), guided by F_i. This is achieved by minimizing a conditional rectified flow matching loss.

#### 3.5. Sensor-Prior Sampling Strategy (SPSS)

Inspired by the sequential nature of real tactile sensing (non-contact to contact), SPSS incorporates sensor priors into the sampling procedure for realistic tactile generation:
- **Non-Contact Initialization:** It replaces the unconditional branch of Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG) with a sensor-conditioned prior, v_θ(x_t|t, Z_sen_i), which explicitly encodes the non-contact state. This prior represents the intrinsic sensor state.
- **Contact-Specific Guidance:** The sampling process combines this sensor prior with object-specific guidance, v_θ(x_t|t, Z_obj_i, Z_sen_i), which reflects object-level semantics under the same sensor state.
- **Guided Velocity Formulation:** The denoised velocity field is estimated as:
 `v̂_θ(x_t|t, Z_obj_i, Z_sen_i) = v_θ(x_t|t, Z_sen_i) + s * [v_θ(x_t|t, Z_obj_i, Z_sen_i) - v_θ(x_t|t, Z_sen_i)]`, where 's' is the guidance scale (set to 1.5).
This design enforces a physically consistent transition, where the generated touch patterns respect the sensor configuration and incorporate contact semantics.

#### 3.6. Training Environment

Training is conducted using bf16 mixed precision on a cluster with 8 NVIDIA A800 (80 GB) GPUs, leveraging Accelerate for distributed optimization. Training specifics, such as epochs, batch sizes, learning rates, and optimization schedules, are detailed for each stage (reconstruction, DLMC, and sensor-aware alignment).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

UniTac underwent extensive evaluation for both tactile understanding and generation, including real-world deployment.

#### 4.1. Tactile Understanding

- **Quantitative Performance:** UniTac was evaluated on the PHYSICLEAR-Test benchmark, encompassing perceptual recognition tasks (hardness, roughness, texture classification) and complex reasoning tasks (Property Comparison (PC), Property-Object Matching (POM), Property Superlative Selection (PSS)).
 - UniTac-7B achieved an overall score of 66.51, outperforming other advanced multimodal models, including GPT-4o, Qwen2.5-VL, Gemini-2.5-Pro-Exp, LLaVA-OneVision, Octopi, TokenFlow, JanusPro, and BLIP3o.
 - The model demonstrated significant improvements in reasoning-oriented tasks, surpassing the strongest UMM baseline (Octopi-7B) by 11.80 points on PC, 42.39 points on POM, and 11.22 points on PSS. This indicates UniTac's ability to ground tactile observations into high-level physical and semantic concepts for more reliable reasoning.
- **Qualitative Performance:**
 - In the object property description (OPD) task, UniTac generated coherent and physically grounded descriptions across diverse tactile sensors, accurately characterizing material properties (e.g., rigid, polished CD vs. soft, fuzzy plush toy).
 - For complex reasoning tasks, the model provided fine-grained descriptions of surface attributes (e.g., roughness, bump distribution, stiffness) before deriving comparative or matching conclusions.

#### 4.2. Tactile Generation

- **Image Generation:** UniTac's tactile image generation capabilities were assessed on four sensor types: Digit, GelSight, GelSight Mini, and Duragel, using SSIM and PSNR metrics.
 - UniTac achieved the highest average performance among unified multimodal models (0.836 SSIM and 19.93 PSNR) and remained competitive with generation-only methods like TextToucher.
 - Performance on Duragel was observed to be lower across all models, which the authors attributed to unstable acquisition conditions and higher variability in the original Duragel dataset.
 - Qualitative results indicated that UniTac consistently generated realistic and physically coherent tactile images, accurately reflecting distinct sensor configurations and preserving fine structural and illumination cues.
- **Video Generation:** The model was extended to tactile video generation by integrating the Wan v2.2 decoder, while maintaining its sensor-aware conditioning.
 - UniTac generated continuous tactile sequences across different sensors, capturing the onset of contact, gradual pressure propagation, fine-grained texture cues (e.g., granular surface of an orange), and sensor-specific deformation patterns and illumination dynamics. Temporal consistency in dot-grid displacement and contact region evolution was maintained.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

A series of ablation experiments investigated the contribution of UniTac's key components:
- **Sensor Identification:** Removing this objective led to a drop in PHYSICLEAR performance (from 60.61 to 57.38), confirming that explicitly modeling sensor-level configurations enhances cross-sensor tactile data interpretation.
- **Dual-Level Comprehension:** Excluding this module resulted in a sharp decrease across all metrics (e.g., PHYSICLEAR from 60.61 to 26.52), underscoring its critical role in tactile understanding and providing semantic priors for generation.
- **DiT Projector / Sensor-Prior Sampling:** Their removal had limited impact on understanding but reduced generative fidelity, suggesting their importance for maintaining structural realism and physical consistency in generated tactile data.
- **Weighting Factor λ_sen:** An optimal value of 0.1 was found to balance cross-sensor consistency and tactile understanding.
- **Touch Encoder Choice:** A CLIP-based semantic encoder was preferred over a VAE-based pixel encoder for efficiency and alignment, while maintaining comparable fidelity.
- **Number of Queries for MLLM:** 64 queries were found to provide an effective balance between accuracy and computational efficiency.
- **Sensor-Prior Sampling Strategy (SPSS):** SPSS was shown to outperform vanilla classifier-free guidance at the same guidance scale, highlighting the benefit of its sensor-conditioned prior.

#### 4.4. Real-World Deployment and Validation

UniTac was deployed on a robotic platform to evaluate its practicality:
- **Understanding in Real Interaction:** In a language-guided tactile fabric selection and grasping task (selecting a smoother fabric for baby skin), UniTac accurately analyzed tactile feedback from visually similar fabrics and identified the smoother option.
 - Quantitatively, an RGB-only VLA baseline achieved 20% overall success, struggling with tactile-semantic selection. VTLA-real (with real-time tactile input) achieved 95% overall success. VTLA-pred (using predicted tactile representations from RGB inputs at inference) achieved 80% overall success, indicating the utility of predicted tactile information for contact-aware manipulation.
 - In an unseen-size cup deformation grasping task, VLA failed to achieve target deformation (0% success) despite high lift success (90%), often over-compressing. VTLA-real achieved 100% overall success, while VTLA-pred achieved 90%, demonstrating that tactile representations improve deformation control.
- **Effectiveness of Generated Tactile Data:** UniTac-generated data was used to improve cross-sensor generalization in a grasp classification task.
 - A classifier trained solely on Digit data performed well in-domain (98.89%) but poorly on GelSight (50.00%). Augmenting the training set with UniTac-generated GelSight samples improved GelSight accuracy to 99.37% while maintaining Digit performance (99.07%).
 - Similar improvements were observed for GelSight-to-Duragel and Duragel-to-Digit transfers. This suggests UniTac can serve as a scalable data generation framework for adapting to new tactile devices without extensive real data collection.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

UniTac represents an advancement in multimodal artificial intelligence by extending unified modeling to the tactile domain. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Pioneering Unified Multimodal Tactile AI:** UniTac is presented as the first unified multimodal model specifically designed for tactile understanding and generation. This integration bridges a gap in existing UMM research, which has largely focused on visual and textual modalities. The work demonstrates that tactile reasoning and synthesis can be effectively combined within a single architectural framework.
- **Enhanced Cross-Sensor Generalization:** By explicitly modeling both sensor-level configurations and object-level semantics, UniTac addresses a critical challenge of domain gaps between diverse tactile sensors. This capability allows the model to interpret and generate tactile signals robustly across different hardware, potentially making tactile AI systems more adaptable and widely applicable.
- **Improved Tactile Understanding and Reasoning:** The dual-level mixture comprehension framework, along with large-scale multi-sensor training, allows UniTac to achieve higher performance in complex tactile reasoning tasks, such as property comparison and object matching. This improved understanding of physical material properties and tactile interactions is pertinent for developing more intelligent and perceptive robotic systems.
- **Realistic and Controllable Tactile Generation:** The two-stage aligned generation paradigm, combined with the sensor-prior sampling strategy, enables UniTac to synthesize realistic and physically coherent tactile images and videos. This capability is valuable for creating synthetic training data, facilitating cross-modal learning, and developing advanced haptic feedback systems for virtual environments or teleoperation.
- **Scalable Data Augmentation for Emerging Devices:** The ability of UniTac to generate sensor-specific tactile data conditioned on object and contact information offers a solution to the costly and time-consuming process of recollecting large datasets for every new tactile sensor. Its demonstrated effectiveness in cross-sensor grasp classification tasks suggests that UniTac can serve as a scalable data generation framework, accelerating the adaptation of AI models to new tactile hardware.
- **Advancement in Embodied Intelligence and Robotics:** UniTac's capabilities contribute to the development of more physically grounded intelligent agents and robots. The real-world deployment demonstrates its practical utility in tasks requiring fine-grained tactile discrimination, such as material selection for delicate interactions or deformation-aware grasping. This suggests a potential for enhancing manipulation, material recognition, and interaction safety in robotic applications.
