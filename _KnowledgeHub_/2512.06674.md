# RunawayEvil: Jailbreaking the Image-to-Video Generative Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at PRLab, Nanjing University, developed "RunawayEvil," the first multimodal jailbreak framework for Image-to-Video (I2V) generative models. This system coordinates adaptive attacks across textual and visual inputs, achieving an average attack success rate of 87.6% on the COCO2017 dataset and outperforming existing unimodal methods by 58.5%–79%.

## Problem

- Image-to-Video (I2V) generative models, despite their growing adoption in commercial and creative applications, have critically underexplored security vulnerabilities to jailbreak attacks.
- Existing single-modal jailbreak methods are ineffective against I2V models due to their reliance on manually crafted prompts, static attack patterns, and inability to exploit cross-modal interactions.
- The unique architecture of I2V models, which processes both image and text inputs synergistically, creates a blind spot for traditional unimodal attack strategies and their defenses.

## Method

- Introducing "**RunawayEvil**," the first multimodal ==jailbreak framework== specifically designed to bypass safety filters in Image-to-Video (I2V) generative models.
- Employs a "Strategy-Tactic-Action" paradigm that incorporates self-amplifying and self-evolving attack capabilities through reinforcement learning and LLM-based exploration.
- Coordinates attacks across both textual and visual modalities using a Strategy-Aware Command Unit, Multimodal Tactical Planning Unit, and Tactical Action Unit for adaptive and precise perturbations.

## Results

- RunawayEvil achieved an average Attack Success Rate (ASR) of **87.6%** on the COCO2017 dataset, significantly surpassing existing methods by **58.5%**–**79%** across various I2V models and safety evaluators.
- Baseline single-modal jailbreak attacks demonstrated poor transferability to I2V, with their peak ASRs below **50%**, highlighting the fundamental limitations of unimodal approaches in this domain.
- Ablation studies confirmed the critical contribution of RunawayEvil's self-evolving components, with the Strategy Exploration Agent alone improving ASR by an average of **13%** through dynamic strategy diversification.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Multimodal jailbreak frameworks are crucial for I2V models, as existing unimodal methods (designed for Text-to-Image or Text-to-Video) transfer poorly and achieve limited success.
> - A self-evolving "Strategy-Tactic-Action" paradigm, leveraging LLMs and reinforcement learning, enables adaptive strategy customization and exploration, significantly boosting attack efficacy and robustness.
> - Coordinated attacks across both textual and visual inputs are essential for exploiting cross-modal vulnerabilities and effectively bypassing I2V models' integrated safety mechanisms.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of artificial intelligence, particularly deep learning, has seen an explosion of advancements, with generative models like Image-to-Video (I2V) systems emerging as powerful tools for synthesizing dynamic visual content from multimodal inputs (images and text). These capabilities have significant commercial value, driving applications in content creation, advertising, and entertainment. However, as these technologies become more sophisticated and widely adopted, the critical issue of their security and ethical deployment comes to the forefront.

This paper explicitly positions itself within the nascent but increasingly vital domain of generative AI safety, specifically focusing on "jailbreak attacks." A jailbreak attack aims to bypass the safety filters and guardrails built into AI models, coercing them into generating harmful, offensive, or otherwise undesirable content. Previous research in jailbreaking has primarily concentrated on single-modal input systems, such as Text-to-Image (T2I) and Text-to-Video (T2V) models. These approaches typically involve adversarial perturbations applied solely to textual inputs, leveraging techniques like search-based optimization of token sequences (e.g., SneakyPrompt, DiffZero) or Large Language Model (LLM)-driven adversarial prompt generation (e.g., PGJ, DACA).

The authors identify a critical gap in this existing research: the security of multimodal I2V systems remains "critically underexplored." They argue that current single-modal jailbreak methods are inherently limited in the I2V domain due to three key shortcomings:
1. **Reliance on manually crafted prompts**: These are labor-intensive and limit the attack strategy space.
2. **Static attack patterns**: They lack dynamic adaptability to diverse multimodal inputs.
3. **Failure to exploit cross-modal interactions**: By perturbing only one modality, they cannot effectively bypass integrated multimodal safety mechanisms that consider both image and text inputs synergistically.

"RunawayEvil" directly addresses this gap by proposing the *first* multimodal jailbreak framework specifically designed for I2V models. It moves beyond the limitations of unimodal attacks by developing a system that coordinates attacks across both textual and visual modalities, adapting dynamically, and even self-evolving. This work significantly pushes the boundaries of generative AI security research by acknowledging the unique complexities introduced by multimodal inputs and the need for corresponding multimodal attack strategies. By doing so, it contributes to a more holistic understanding of vulnerabilities in advanced generative AI systems, paving the way for the development of more robust defensive mechanisms.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The motivation behind "RunawayEvil" stems from a dual perspective: recognizing the immense potential of Image-to-Video (I2V) generative models while simultaneously highlighting their profound security vulnerabilities. The widespread adoption of I2V systems in commercial and creative applications underscores the urgent need to understand and mitigate potential abuses, particularly through jailbreak attacks that circumvent safety protocols to generate harmful content.

The primary motivations can be summarized as follows:
- **Underexplored Security Risks in I2V**: Despite the rapid advancements and adoption of I2V models, their vulnerability to jailbreak attacks remains largely unexamined compared to their single-modal counterparts (T2I, T2V). This creates a dangerous blind spot in the broader landscape of AI safety.
- **Inadequacy of Existing Jailbreak Methods**: Current jailbreak techniques, designed for single-modal inputs, are shown to be largely ineffective against I2V models. They lack the ability to perform coordinated attacks across both text and image modalities, rely on static and often labor-intensive manual prompt crafting, and fail to adapt to the dynamic nature of multimodal inputs and their integrated safety mechanisms.
- **Need for a Multimodal Approach**: The unique architecture of I2V models, which processes both image and text inputs synergistically, necessitates a novel jailbreaking approach that can exploit cross-modal interactions rather than treating modalities in isolation.

Given these motivations, the authors set out to achieve several key objectives:
- **Develop the First Multimodal Jailbreak Framework for I2V**: The foremost objective is to introduce "RunawayEvil," the pioneering framework specifically designed to perform jailbreak attacks against I2V generative models by manipulating both image and text inputs collaboratively.
- **Propose a Self-Evolving and Adaptive Attack Mechanism**: A core objective is to move beyond static, handcrafted attack patterns. RunawayEvil aims to achieve this through a "Strategy-Tactic-Action" paradigm that features self-amplifying and self-evolving capabilities, allowing the system to continuously adapt, explore new strategies, and intensify attacks without human intervention.
- **Demonstrate State-of-the-Art Attack Success Rates**: The framework must prove its effectiveness by achieving significantly higher attack success rates on contemporary, commercial I2V models compared to adapted baseline methods.
- **Provide a Critical Tool for Vulnerability Analysis**: By successfully demonstrating and analyzing I2V vulnerabilities, the research aims to provide a vital tool for the scientific community and model developers. This tool can aid in understanding weaknesses, thereby laying a crucial foundation for the development of more robust, secure, and ethical video generation systems in the future.
- **Highlight Cross-Modal Vulnerabilities**: Through its multimodal coordinated attack, the framework seeks to shed light on how the interplay between different input modalities can create unique security vulnerabilities that unimodal defenses might overlook.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

"RunawayEvil" introduces a novel, self-evolving multimodal jailbreak framework for I2V models, built upon a "Strategy-Tactic-Action" paradigm. This framework is designed to overcome the limitations of traditional, single-modal, and static attack methods by enabling coordinated, adaptive attacks across both textual and visual inputs. The methodology unfolds in two distinct stages: Evolution Stage and Execution Stage, underpinned by three core components: the Strategy-Aware Command Unit (SACU), the Multimodal Tactical Planning Unit (MTPU), and the Tactical Action Unit (TAU).

#### 3.1. The "Strategy-Tactic-Action" Paradigm

This paradigm structures the attack process:
- **Strategy**: High-level approach for the jailbreak (e.g., "hidden threats in plain sight").
- **Tactic**: Concrete plan to implement the strategy (e.g., specific text prompt modifications and image edits).
- **Action**: Execution of the tactic and evaluation of its outcome.

#### 3.2. Evolution Stage: Self-Evolution of the Strategy-Aware Command Unit (SACU)

The SACU acts as the "brain" of RunawayEvil, responsible for strategy customization, expansion, and experience storage. It facilitates the framework's self-evolution through two key phases:

- **Dynamic Strategy Expansion**:
 - **Strategy Exploration Agent (SEA)**: An LLM-based agent that periodically (e.g., every N iterations) analyzes successful jailbreak experiences stored in the Strategy Memory Bank (SMB). Using a system prompt, the SEA autonomously reasons over these experiences to generate novel, feasible attack strategies, which are then added to the strategy library. This process continually diversifies and expands the range of available attack approaches.
 - **Strategy Memory Bank (SMB)**: A structured repository storing historical successful attack records, including the reference image, image-editing instructions, video prompt, and the strategy employed. This serves as a knowledge base for the SEA and the MTPU.

- **Strategic Customization Reinforcement**:
 - **Strategy Customization Agent (SCA)**: Leverages Reinforcement Learning (RL) to dynamically select the optimal attack strategy (S_k) from the expanded library for a given input text-image pair (T0, I0). This is formalized as a Markov Decision Process (MDP) where:
 - **State Space**: The input image (I_t), enabling context-aware selections.
 - **Action Space**: Selecting a strategy from the current strategy library.
 - **Strategic Reward Function (R)**: Designed to balance attack efficiency, stealth (textual and visual), and success rate. It penalizes overlap with harmful keywords (textual stealth, C_s), measures perceptual distance between original and perturbed images (visual stealth, D_p using LPIPS), and grants a large positive reward upon successful jailbreak (R_success). A small negative reward (R_iteration) encourages faster convergence.
 - **Customization Policy (π_θ)**: The SCA learns an optimal policy to maximize the expected cumulative discounted reward, tailoring strategies to specific inputs.

#### 3.3. Execution Stage: Multimodal Jailbreak Execution

Once the SACU's evolution is complete, the framework transitions to the execution stage, leveraging its learned capabilities for coordinated multimodal attacks:

- **Strategy Generation**: The pre-trained SCA, based on the input (T0, I0), selects the most effective strategy (S*) from its expanded library.
- **Memory-Augmented Tactical Generation (MTPU)**: The Tactical Planner, an MLLM agent, takes the input (T0, I0) and the selected strategy (S*) to generate coordinated attack instructions (P_e, P_v) for visual and textual attacks. This process is enhanced by a memory-augmented retrieval mechanism:
 - It retrieves top-K semantically similar successful experiences from the SMB based on the input image.
 - If a historical experience with the same strategy (S*) is found, it uses the successful historical prompts (P_e_hist, P_v_hist) as context to generate more refined, context-aware instructions.
 - Otherwise, it generates instructions from scratch based only on the current input.
 - This ensures coherently coupled and precisely tailored instructions that aim to bypass cross-modal safety checks.
- **Jailbreak Execution (TAU)**: The Tactical Action Unit executes the generated attack instructions in an iterative loop:
 - **Image Perturbation**: The Attack Executor (using FLUX for subtle image edits) modifies the input image (I_k-1) based on P_e_k to produce I'_k. FLUX is chosen for its ability to perform subtle, imperceptible edits while maintaining high similarity to the original.
 - **Video Generation**: The perturbed image (I'_k) and the malicious video prompt (P_v_k) are fed into the target I2V model (F_I2V) to generate a video (V_k).
 - **Evaluation and Feedback**: An MLLM-based Safety Evaluator (E) assesses if the generated video (V_k) is unsafe (E(V_k) = 1). If successful, the attack terminates, and the successful record (I0, P_e_k, P_v_k, S*) is added to the SMB. If unsuccessful, the loop continues, allowing for dynamic adaptation of attack vectors.

This closed-loop, self-evolving, and multimodally coordinated approach enables RunawayEvil to adaptively circumvent I2V models' security mechanisms, making it highly effective and robust against diverse inputs and safety filters.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted demonstrate that RunawayEvil achieves highly effective jailbreak attacks against Image-to-Video (I2V) models, significantly outperforming existing methods and showcasing the crucial role of its multimodal and self-evolving design.

- **State-of-the-Art Attack Success Rates (ASR)**: RunawayEvil consistently achieves the highest Attack Success Rates (ASR) across all tested I2V models (Open-Sora 2.0, CogVideoX-5b-I2V, Wan2.2-TI2V-5B, and Dynamicrafter) and safety evaluators (Qwen-VL, LLaVA-Next, Gemma-3-VL). On the COCO2017 dataset, RunawayEvil’s average ASR reached 87.6%, which is a substantial improvement, outperforming existing methods by 58.5%–79%. For instance, on CogVideo-LLaVA, RunawayEvil achieved 89.0% ASR, while the best baseline (PGJ) only reached 47.0%. Similar superior performance was observed on the MM-SafetyBench dataset, with RunawayEvil achieving an average ASR of over 90%, further confirming its generalization capability.
- **Ineffectiveness of Baseline Methods for I2V**: The study clearly shows that traditional Text-to-Image (T2I) jailbreak attacks (SneakyPrompt, PGJ, DACA) transfer poorly to the I2V domain. Their peak ASR on COCO2017 was below 50% (e.g., PGJ 47.0% on CogVideo-LLaVA), with some instances as low as 6.5% (Sneaky on DynamiCrafter-Gemma). This stark contrast underscores the fundamental limitations of single-modal, rigid attack patterns in the complex, multimodal I2V context, validating the core motivation for RunawayEvil.
- **Validation of SACU's Self-Evolving Modules**: Ablation studies on the Strategy-Aware Command Unit (SACU) demonstrated the synergistic value of its individual components:
 - **Strategy Memory Bank (SMB)**: Leveraging historical successful attack information boosted ASR by an average of 8.2%, confirming the benefit of experience-driven optimization.
 - **Strategy Customization Agent (SCA)**: Dynamically tailoring strategies to specific input images increased ASR by 2.7% on average compared to random strategy selection, highlighting its adaptability.
 - **Strategy Exploration Agent (SEA)**: Expanding strategy diversity through autonomous generation further improved ASR by 13% on average, demonstrating its role in breaking existing method limits. The combination of all three modules achieved optimal performance, reinforcing their collective contribution to attack effectiveness and adaptability.
- **Superiority of Multimodal Coordinated Attacks**: Experiments comparing different modal attack methods confirmed that multimodal collaborative attacks are critical for success in I2V systems. Unimodal attacks (text-only or image-only) yielded poor ASRs (51.2% and 47.8% respectively). Independent dual-modal attacks, though better (79.7%), were still significantly surpassed by fully coordinated multimodal attacks (87.2%), which effectively exploit cross-modal potential to bypass security mechanisms.
- **Importance of Adaptive Iterative Attacks with Feedback**: The study showed that fixed iterative attacks without feedback had limited effectiveness, with only minor improvements from more iterations. In contrast, adaptive iterative attacks with continuous feedback to the SACU resulted in significantly higher ASR, verifying that dynamic, feedback-driven strategy and step adjustments are crucial for circumventing model security protections.
- **Trade-off Between Stealth and Effectiveness**: Parameter sensitivity analysis revealed an interesting trade-off. Increasing text hiding strength continuously decreased the NSFW lexical index (improving stealth) with only a slight degradation in ASR. Enhancing image hiding strength moderately improved ASR while reducing LPIPS (improving visual stealth). This suggests that subtle image modifications, when synergized with text perturbations, can efficiently bypass cross-modal safety checks, validating RunawayEvil's design philosophy of balancing stealth and impact.

In summary, the findings robustly demonstrate that RunawayEvil's innovative "Strategy-Tactic-Action" paradigm, featuring self-evolving, adaptive, and multimodally coordinated attacks, is highly effective in jailbreaking I2V generative models. The results underscore the inadequacy of current safety mechanisms against sophisticated multimodal attacks and highlight the critical need for advanced defenses.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of RunawayEvil carries significant implications for the research and development of generative AI, particularly in the realm of multimodal systems like Image-to-Video (I2V) models. Its potential impact extends across several critical areas:

- **Pioneering Tool for Vulnerability Analysis**: RunawayEvil is the first framework of its kind designed specifically for multimodal jailbreaking of I2V models. This makes it an invaluable diagnostic tool for researchers and developers to systematically probe, identify, and understand the unique security vulnerabilities inherent in such complex systems. By demonstrating how a self-evolving, coordinated attack can exploit cross-modal interactions, it provides unprecedented insights into the weak points of integrated multimodal safety mechanisms.
- **Foundation for Robust AI Safety Mechanisms**: The primary, long-term significance of this work lies in its contribution to building more robust and secure AI systems. By exposing current weaknesses, RunawayEvil lays a crucial foundation for the development of advanced defensive strategies. Developers can leverage the insights gained from such attacks to design more resilient safety filters, incorporate stronger cross-modal consistency checks, and implement adaptive, real-time defenses that can counteract sophisticated, evolving threats. This iterative process of attack and defense is fundamental to the maturation of any security-sensitive technology.
- **Advancing the Understanding of Multimodal AI Safety**: The success of RunawayEvil highlights that the security challenges in multimodal AI are distinct from those in unimodal systems. It underscores the fact that treating modalities in isolation or simply combining existing unimodal defenses is insufficient. The framework's ability to coordinate attacks across text and image modalities compels the field to think more holistically about multimodal safety, encouraging the development of integrated safety architectures that account for complex interactions between different input types.
- **Ethical Implications and Responsible AI Development**: While "jailbreaking" research inherently carries the risk of misuse, the authors explicitly state their work provides a "critical tool for vulnerability analysis." This aligns with responsible disclosure principles, where identifying and publicizing vulnerabilities is a necessary step towards improving overall security. The "Warning: this paper contains examples that may be offensive or harmful" further emphasizes the ethical considerations, signaling that the research is aimed at understanding potential harms to prevent them. The findings serve as a stark reminder to developers of I2V models that robust safety protocols are not an afterthought but a prerequisite for ethical deployment, especially in commercial and public-facing applications.
- **Driving Innovation in Adversarial AI**: The self-evolving mechanism of RunawayEvil, incorporating reinforcement learning for strategy customization and LLM-based exploration, represents a significant advancement in adversarial AI. This adaptive approach moves beyond static, manual attack generation, paving the way for more sophisticated and autonomous red-teaming tools. Such innovations can accelerate the discovery of new attack vectors and challenge existing defense paradigms across various AI domains.
- **Open-Source Contribution**: The decision to make the code available publicly (via GitHub) is a vital contribution. This transparency allows other researchers to reproduce the results, build upon the framework, and further investigate I2V safety, fostering collaborative progress in a rapidly evolving field.

In conclusion, "RunawayEvil" is a seminal work that not only fills a critical gap in generative AI safety research but also provides a powerful framework for understanding and mitigating the unique security risks of multimodal I2V systems. Its impact will likely resonate in the ongoing efforts to build more secure, robust, and trustworthy artificial intelligence technologies.
