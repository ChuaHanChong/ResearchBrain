# HoneyBee: Data Recipes for Vision-Language Reasoners

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at FAIR at Meta and UCLA conducted a systematic investigation into the construction of high-quality training datasets for Vision-Language Models (VLMs), identifying key data curation strategies. Their empirically-derived HoneyBee dataset enables smaller VLMs to achieve state-of-the-art reasoning performance, often outperforming larger existing models, and introduces an efficient inference method reducing decoding cost by 73%.

## Problem

- The underlying principles for constructing performant multimodal Chain-of-Thought (CoT) datasets for Vision-Language Models (VLMs) are poorly understood.
- Training datasets and curation methodologies for state-of-the-art VLMs are often proprietary, creating a "black box" scenario that hinders scientific understanding and reproducibility.
- Prior work on VLM reasoning datasets often conflates the impact of different factors (e.g., context sources, teacher models, filtering techniques), making it difficult to isolate true drivers of performance improvements.

## Method

- A systematic ==data curation pipeline== was developed, encompassing controlled context sourcing, targeted data interventions (perception and problem-solving), and scaling along diverse data axes.
- A fixed, performant Llama4-Scout generator model was used to create all ==Chain-of-Thought (CoT) annotations==, ensuring consistency for knowledge distillation to student Perception Language Models (PLMs).
- The ==HoneyBee dataset (**2.5M** instances)== was constructed based on empirical insights, integrating high-quality ViRL data, scaled questions and CoTs, =="caption and solve" auxiliary signals==, and re-annotated text-only reasoning data.

## Results

- VLMs trained with the HoneyBee dataset achieved best-in-class performance across diverse benchmarks, with a **1B**-parameter PLM-HoneyBee model showing a **28 percentage point** relative improvement over InternVL-3-1B-Instruct.
- Augmenting multimodal data with text-only reasoning data resulted in a **7.5 percentage point** average accuracy gain on VL reasoning tasks and enhanced generalization to text-only benchmarks like MATH500.
- A novel "shared caption" decoding strategy reduced VLM test-time scaling decoding cost by **73%** without any loss in accuracy, improving inference efficiency.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The choice of the initial (image, question) context source critically impacts VLM performance, with naive mixing of diverse sources not outperforming the single best source.
> - Integrating auxiliary visual signals via image captions ("Caption and Solve") and augmenting with high-quality text-only reasoning data yielded significant improvements in VLM performance and generalization.
> - Scaling data along unique images, unique questions per image, and unique CoTs per (image, question) pair consistently improves the reasoning capabilities of VLMs.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Recent advancements in vision-language models (VLMs) have demonstrated their increasing effectiveness in complex reasoning tasks. This capability is often attributed to training on high-quality synthetic Chain-of-Thought (CoT) data, which enables models to generate step-by-step solutions during problem-solving and leverage additional inference-time computation. However, the underlying principles for constructing such performant VL reasoning training datasets remain largely unexplored in public research. Many state-of-the-art multimodal CoT datasets and their generation methodologies are proprietary, creating a knowledge gap in the academic community regarding their design and impact.

Prior work in large language models (LLMs), particularly for text-only reasoning, has established the critical role of supervised finetuning (SFT) with high-quality CoT data for enhancing reasoning performance and serving as a foundation for subsequent reinforcement learning (RL) training. While some efforts have been made to curate CoT datasets for VL reasoning, such as Math-LLaVA and LLaVA-CoT, these often differ in data distributions and CoT generation strategies, making it challenging to isolate the specific factors contributing to model performance.

This paper addresses several identified challenges in the existing VL reasoning data landscape:
- The impact of context (image and question pairs) from diverse data sources on VLM reasoning performance is not well understood.
- Limited exploration of targeted data interventions beyond visual perturbations or difficulty filtering to enhance data quality.
- Lack of clarity regarding optimal scaling strategies for VL reasoning data, specifically across dimensions such as the number of images, questions per image, and CoTs per image-question pair.
- Absence of fair and robust comparisons of different data design choices, where training setups and evaluation protocols are consistently controlled across experiments.

By systematically investigating these aspects, this research aims to provide a scientific foundation for VL reasoning data curation, offering insights and a publicly available large-scale dataset to advance the field.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to investigate and identify the key factors and principles underlying the construction of performant training datasets for vision-language reasoning. This overarching goal is broken down into several specific objectives:

1. **Analyze the impact of context source strategies:** To determine how the origin of image and question pairs influences VLM performance. The motivation is to understand if certain data distributions or curation processes inherent to existing datasets impart superior reasoning capabilities.
2. **Implement and evaluate targeted data interventions:** To assess whether specific modifications to the training data, such as auxiliary signals or textual reasoning augmentation, can enhance VLM perception and problem-solving skills. This is motivated by the hypothesis that carefully designed interventions can improve data quality beyond simple collection.
3. **Explore scaling effects across diverse data dimensions:** To determine whether scaling the number of unique images, the number of questions per image, or the number of CoT solutions per image-question pair consistently improves VL reasoning capabilities. This objective is motivated by the observation that data scaling has been crucial for LLM performance, but its implications for multimodal reasoning are less clear.
4. **Develop a large-scale, high-quality VL reasoning CoT dataset:** Based on the insights gained from the aforementioned investigations, the researchers aim to construct and release HoneyBee, a comprehensive dataset designed to facilitate the training of advanced VL reasoners. This is motivated by the need for transparent, openly available, and meticulously curated datasets to foster scientific progress in multimodal AI, contrasting with proprietary solutions.
5. **Propose an efficient test-time scaling strategy:** To optimize the inference cost of VLMs without sacrificing accuracy, particularly when generating multiple CoT solutions. This objective is motivated by the practical need for more efficient deployment of complex reasoning models.

Overall, the motivation for this work stems from the recognized gap in understanding the intricate relationship between multimodal data design choices and the emergent reasoning capabilities of VLMs, particularly given the proprietary nature of data used in many state-of-the-art models. The authors seek to contribute a systematic study and a high-quality public resource to address this deficit.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research employed a multi-stage data curation pipeline to systematically investigate factors influencing VLM reasoning performance, culminating in the creation of the HoneyBee dataset.

#### 3.1. Preliminaries and General Setup

The work focuses on curating synthetic data where a generator model \(G\) produces a textual Chain-of-Thought (CoT) \(C_j\) for a given image \(I_j\) and question \(Q_j\). The CoT consists of reasoning steps and a predicted final answer. A VLM \(p_\theta\) is then trained using a supervised finetuning objective to maximize the probability of generating \(C_j\) given \(I_j\) and \(Q_j\). The generator model used was Llama4-Scout (109B total parameters, 17B active), selected for its performance and accessibility. Perception Language Models (PLMs) (1B, 3B, and 8B parameters) were used as student models for training, starting from stage-3 configurations. All models underwent full finetuning, and experiments were run for 5 epochs. Evaluation was conducted on a battery of five diverse VL reasoning tasks (MathVerse, MathVista, MathVision, MMMU-Pro, We-Math) for validation during curation, and five additional unseen datasets (DynaMath, LogicVista, HallusionBench, MATH500, GPQA) for final evaluation. Accuracy was the primary metric, with greedy decoding and a consistent prompt format.

#### 3.2. Data Curation Pipeline

The pipeline comprised three main stages:

#### 3.2.1. Context Curation (Section 3.1)

- **Sourcing:** Six widely adopted VL reasoning datasets (ViRL, Math-LLaVA, R1-OneVision, ThinkLite-VL-Hard, LLaVA-CoT, MMK12) were used as initial context sources. Instances with perceptually identical images to those in evaluation datasets were removed (decontamination). For controlled comparison, similarly sized subsets (capped at 50K examples) from each source were used. CoTs were generated for all (image, question) pairs using Llama4-Scout. To isolate the impact of context quality, filtered versions (discarding CoTs that did not lead to correct answers) were also created. VLMs (3B and 8B PLMs) were trained on these datasets, and their average performance across evaluation tasks was used for ranking the context sources.
- **Mixing:** Data from the top-performing source datasets (top-2, top-4, and all) were combined into equally sized subsets to assess if mixtures could outperform individual sources.

#### 3.2.2. Data Interventions (Section 3.2)

Starting with the best-performing data mixture from context curation (which was ultimately found to be the single best source, ViRL), various targeted data interventions were applied. These interventions were categorized by their aim (perception or problem-solving enhancement) and mechanism (replacement, augmentation, or filtering).
- **Perception Enhancement:**
 - **Visual Perturbation:** Random transformations (rotation, distractor concatenation, dominance-preserving mixup) applied to images, tested as partial replacement or augmentation.
 - **Text-Rich Images:** Original (image, question) pairs transformed into new images with the question text embedded, tested as replacement or augmentation.
 - **Perceptual Redundancy:** Filtering out instances where the generator model could answer correctly without access to the image.
 - **Shallow Perception:** Filtering out instances where the generator model could answer correctly using only the question and an image caption.
 - **Caption and Solve:** Augmenting the CoT with an image caption generated by the teacher model, explored with different generation strategies (caption and solution generated independently, jointly, or sequentially).
- **Problem-Solving Skill Enhancement:**
 - **Text-Only Reasoning:** Augmenting the VL reasoning data with a high-performing text-only reasoning dataset (OpenThoughts3), re-annotated with Llama4-Scout for consistency.
 - **Increased Distractors:** Transforming questions to include 10 options, tested as replacement or augmentation, to reduce reliance on chance.
 - **Length:** Filtering for instances with CoTs longer than the median length to encourage extended reasoning.
 - **Difficulty:** Classifying instances into difficulty levels using an LLM and a rubric, then filtering to achieve a more balanced representation of harder problems.

#### 3.2.3. Scaling Diverse Data Axes (Section 3.3)

Building on the insights from context curation and data interventions, the researchers studied the impact of scaling data along three dimensions:
- **Scaling Images:** Training on increasing subsets of unique images (N/8, N/4, N/2, N).
- **Scaling Questions:** Synthesizing novel questions for existing images using a question generator, followed by CoT generation for these new questions.
- **Scaling CoTs:** Increasing the number of CoT traces for a given (image, question) pair to expose the VLM to diverse problem-solving strategies.

#### 3.3. HoneyBee Dataset Construction (Section 5.3)

The HoneyBee dataset was constructed by integrating the findings from the preceding stages:
- Started with the ViRL dataset.
- **Scaling CoTs:** Generated 16 CoTs per real (image, question) pair, filtering for final answer correctness, yielding approximately 400K instances.
- **Scaling Questions:** Generated 14 new questions per image, resulting in 15 questions per image in total. For these new questions, 4 CoTs were generated, and majority voting was used as a proxy for the final answer. CoTs matching this proxy were retained, leading to approximately 1M instances.
- **Caption Integration:** Image captions were generated for all images and combined with the solution CoTs from both scaling pipelines following the "caption and solve" strategy. This formed the VL subset of HoneyBee (1.5M instances).
- **Text-Only Augmentation:** This VL subset was merged with 1M instances of re-annotated text-only reasoning data, resulting in the final HoneyBee dataset of 2.5M instances.

#### 3.4. Efficient Test-Time Scaling Strategy (Section 5.5)

A "shared caption" decoding strategy was proposed for test-time scaling. Instead of generating the full CoT (caption + solution) N times for self-consistency, the image caption is generated once and then reused as context for generating N subsequent solutions, aiming to reduce inference tokens.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

#### 4.1. Impact of Context Curation

- **Sourcing:** The choice of context source significantly influenced VLM performance, with an 11.4 percentage point (pp) difference in average accuracy between the lowest (MMK12, 36.0%) and highest-performing (ViRL, 40.1%) source datasets (Table 2). ViRL and ThinkLite-VL-Hard benefited from filtering CoTs for final answer correctness, while others performed better with unfiltered data (Table 8).
- **Mixing:** Mixing data from diverse sources (top-2, top-4, or all) did not yield superior performance compared to the single best-performing individual dataset (ViRL) (Table 3), suggesting that combining instances from varied sources can potentially degrade performance in VL reasoning tasks.

#### 4.2. Impact of Data Interventions

- **Perception Enhancement:** The "caption and solve" strategy was identified as the most effective perception-enhancing intervention, improving average accuracy by 3.3pp. This method involves augmenting the CoT with auxiliary visual signals from an image caption. Other perception interventions, such as visual perturbation, text-rich images (as augmentation), perceptual redundancy filtering, and shallow perception filtering, did not outperform the baseline (Table 4).
- **Problem-Solving Skill Enhancement:** The inclusion of "text-only reasoning" data was the most effective problem-solving intervention, leading to a 7.5pp increase in average accuracy across VL reasoning tasks. This intervention also improved performance on text-only reasoning benchmarks (e.g., MATH500 accuracy increased from 39.2% to 59.7% for the VL+Text mixture compared to VL-only training) (Table 5). Most other problem-solving interventions, including increased distractors, length filtering, and uniform difficulty filtering, did not improve performance over the baseline (Table 4).

#### 4.3. Scaling to Million Samples

- **Scaling Trends:** Performance of VL reasoners consistently improved with scaling along all three investigated axes: unique images, unique questions per image, and unique CoTs per (image, question) pair (Figure 4). These trends were observed across both 3B and 8B PLMs.
- **HoneyBee Dataset Statistics:** The final HoneyBee dataset consists of 2.5 million instances, including 1.44M VL instances and 1.04M text-only instances. It contains 28K unique images and 350K unique questions. The average CoT length (including caption and solution) is approximately 600 words (Figure 5a). Analysis of CoT keywords suggests the presence of diverse reasoning behaviors, such as perception, planning, and reflection (Figure 5b).

#### 4.4. Training VL Reasoners with HoneyBee

- **Reasoning Performance:** VLMs trained with HoneyBee demonstrated competitive performance across various model sizes. For instance, PLM-HoneyBee-1B showed a 28pp relative improvement over InternVL-3-1B-Instruct. PLM-HoneyBee-3B and PLM-HoneyBee-8B achieved relative gains of 8.4pp and 2.7pp over Qwen2.5-VL-3B-Instruct and Qwen2.5-VL-8B-Instruct, respectively (Table 1).
- **Generalization and Difficulty:** Models trained on HoneyBee generalized effectively to unseen evaluation datasets, including DynaMath, LogicVista, HallusionBench, MATH500, and GPQA, often achieving best-in-class performance (Table 1). Performance also improved across all difficulty levels of the MathVision dataset, with relative gains up to 100pp for specific levels (Figure 7).
- **Data Scaling Behavior:** Performance of HoneyBee-trained PLMs continued to improve as the dataset size increased from 50K to 2.5M instances across all model sizes (Figure 1a, Figure 10). The performance had not saturated at the 2.5M scale, suggesting potential for further gains with larger datasets.
- **RL Training:** HoneyBee-trained models served as a strong warm start for reinforcement learning. RL training with the GRPO algorithm on top of PLM-HoneyBee-3B further improved its average accuracy by 9.2pp relative to OpenVLThinker-v1.2-3B (Table 6).
- **Self-Improvement:** Finetuning the generator model, Llama-4-Scout, on a subset of the HoneyBee dataset resulted in a 3.7pp relative gain over the base model, indicating strong self-improvement capabilities (Table 11).

#### 4.5. Efficient Test-Time Scaling with Shared Captions

The proposed "shared caption" decoding strategy for test-time scaling demonstrated efficiency improvements. When generating 64 solutions for a given (image, question) pair, this method reduced the number of inference tokens by 73% (from 42.6K to 24.5K) without a loss in accuracy on the MathVista dataset (Figure 8). This is achieved by generating the image caption once and reusing it for subsequent solution generations.

#### 4.6. Correlation Analysis

A strong correlation (r=0.96 for average accuracy) was observed between the performance of PLM-3B and PLM-8B models across various data distributions and evaluation tasks (Figure 9). This suggests that data curation strategies that are effective for smaller VLMs are likely to be effective for larger VLMs, indicating predictability in optimal data selection.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research provides foundational insights into the principles governing the construction of high-quality training datasets for vision-language reasoning. By systematically dissecting the impact of context sources, data interventions, and scaling strategies, the work contributes to a more scientific understanding of VLM data curation, a domain where much information has historically been proprietary.

The development and open release of HoneyBee, a large-scale and meticulously curated VL reasoning dataset, represents a significant resource for the research community. HoneyBee's ability to train state-of-the-art VLMs across various model sizes, generalize to unseen tasks, and enhance performance across different difficulty levels indicates its utility for advancing multimodal reasoning capabilities. The observed scalability of HoneyBee suggests a path for continued performance improvements with increased data.

Furthermore, the identification of effective data interventions, such as "caption and solve" for perception enhancement and "text-only reasoning" for problem-solving skill transfer, offers concrete strategies for future dataset development. The proposed efficient test-time scaling strategy, leveraging shared captions, has practical implications for reducing the computational cost of deploying complex VLM reasoning systems.

The correlation analysis between smaller and larger model performances in data curation experiments provides a valuable guideline for future research, suggesting that optimal data selection can be explored with smaller, more compute-efficient models. Overall, this work contributes to demystifying the data engineering aspects of VLM development and lays a foundation for future research in general-purpose VL training and reasoning over multiple images.
