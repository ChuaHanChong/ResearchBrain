# DockAnywhere: Data-Efficient Visuomotor Policy Learning for Mobile Manipulation via Novel Demonstration Generation

> [!summary] Summary
> DockAnywhere is a low-cost synthetic demonstration generation framework for two-stage mobile manipulation. It adopts a third-person point-cloud view, parses one source trajectory into replannable free-space motion segments and invariant contact-rich skill segments, samples feasible docking points under visibility/reachability/collision constraints, and edits point clouds in 3D to match. It reaches 78.9% overall success on ManiSkill across five docking points at 0.02 s per augmentation.

## Problem

- Two-stage pipelines assume the robot reaches its intended docking pose, but sensing noise and localization bias shift it, breaking the downstream fixed-base policy.
- Docking shifts alter egocentric observations drastically, so visuomotor policies face a view generalization problem and collapse at unseen base poses.
- Prior augmentation relies on handcrafted visibility or reachability constraints, limited offline data, or costly human motion capture rigs that remain labor-intensive.

## Method

- Third-person point-cloud observation replaces egocentric input, converting the hard view generalization problem into a ==geometric docking-point generalization problem== over base pose.
- ==TAMP-based trajectory transformation== reuses skill segments wholesale to preserve contact geometry, while replanning low-precision motion segments between segment endpoints for the relocated base.
- Point-level 3D observation synthesis applies the same relative SE(3) transform to segmented arm and object point clouds plus end-effector state, keeping observation and action consistent.

## Results

- Across five docking points, plain DP and DP3 collapse to **15.8%** and **17.8%** overall success, while **DockAnywhere** reaches **78.9%** versus DemoGen's 74.2%.
- Ablating augmented docking points, overall success rises **35.5% at one point to 61.9% at four** and only 63.9% at six, showing diminishing returns.
- Real-world Galaxea R1 deployment scores **60%/40%/30%** on gear picking, placing, assembly, holding roughly 20% even 0.4 m out of distribution.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Choosing an observation frame that is invariant to the nuisance variable can convert a hard perceptual generalization problem into an easier geometric one.
> - Splitting trajectories into replannable free-space motion and frozen contact-rich skill segments lets augmentation change base pose without corrupting interaction fidelity.
> - Augmentation range needs tuning: too-wide docking perturbation hurts, with success peaking near the source distance and dropping sharply at twice it.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this work fits into the broader research landscape

Mobile manipulation is a field focused on enabling robots to interact with objects across expansive environments such as homes and factories. A common paradigm in this area involves a two-stage process: first, the robot navigates to a designated "docking point," and subsequently, it executes fixed-base manipulation tasks using pre-trained visuomotor policies.

A significant challenge in real-world mobile manipulation is the "view generalization problem." This arises because navigation systems are subject to errors, including sensing noise and localization bias, which often result in the robot arriving at a docking point that deviates from the intended pose. Such "docking point shifts" lead to substantial changes in the robot's egocentric visual observations, rendering pre-trained fixed-base manipulation policies less effective or entirely inoperable due to a mismatch between training and testing viewpoints. This issue constrains the robustness and performance of mobile agents in dynamic and unpredictable environments.

Prior research has explored various learning-based approaches for mobile manipulation. Some adopt end-to-end learning, which typically necessitates extensive, large-scale training data. Others maintain the modular two-stage framework. Efforts to address view generalization indirectly include formulating novel trajectory generation as an optimization problem to expand training data by searching for better-conditioned base poses and viewpoints. However, these approaches often rely on limited offline data and heuristic constraints, which restrict their ability to generalize to truly *unseen* docking configurations at deployment time.

Furthermore, existing data-efficient imitation learning methods, such as VISTA, RoVi-Aug, RoCoDA, and DemoGen, have primarily focused on static manipulation tasks. These methods do not inherently address the additional complexities introduced by a mobile base, specifically the variability in base pose and its impact on the robot's interaction with the environment. Other mobile manipulation data scaling frameworks, like EMMA, HERMES, and MoMaGen, aim to improve generalization across multiple docking points, often by leveraging human motion data. However, these methods typically require expensive hardware and are labor-intensive, making them impractical for generating sufficient data to cover the vast space of possible docking configurations. The fundamental bottleneck of data scarcity across diverse docking points remains largely unaddressed by these prior methods.

This paper positions its contribution by arguing that the choice of visual representation is central to resolving docking-induced viewpoint variation. It proposes the use of a third-person viewpoint representation for the fixed-base manipulation policy input. This approach aims to provide a consistent scene representation across different docking points, thereby transforming the view generalization challenge into a "geometric docking point generalization problem" that focuses on changes in the robot base pose rather than drastic visual alterations. The work then introduces a low-cost, fully synthetic demonstration generation framework that explicitly targets this geometric generalization problem by leveraging a Task and Motion Planning (TAMP) framework for structured trajectory augmentation and 3D point-level editing for consistent visual observation synthesis.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the viewpoint generalization capability of mobile manipulation policies, particularly in scenarios where the robot's docking point varies due to navigation errors.

The motivation for this work stems from several challenges inherent in current mobile manipulation systems:

1. **Vulnerability to Navigation Errors:** In real-world environments, navigation systems are prone to inaccuracies, leading to minor yet critical shifts in the robot's intended docking pose. These shifts prevent the robot from consistently reaching the exact, pre-defined docking location.
2. **View Generalization Problem:** Such docking point shifts significantly alter the robot's egocentric visual observations. Fixed-base manipulation policies, typically trained on data from a narrow range of viewpoints, struggle to generalize to these novel visual inputs, leading to reduced success rates or task failures. This view generalization problem is a fundamental limitation for deploying mobile manipulators robustly in unpredictable environments.
3. **Data Scarcity and Cost of Real-World Data Collection:** To train policies that are robust to diverse docking points, a substantial amount of training data covering a wide range of spatial configurations is required. Collecting such diverse demonstrations in real-world settings is resource-intensive, time-consuming, and often prohibitive. Existing methods for data scaling often rely on expensive human demonstrations or computationally intensive optimizations that still struggle with unseen configurations.
4. **Limitations of Existing Augmentation Techniques:** While data augmentation has shown promise in static manipulation, current methods often operate in 2D image space or do not explicitly account for the complex 3D spatial transformations required when the robot's base pose changes significantly. They may not preserve the intricate contact dynamics and relative geometries essential for successful manipulation.

To address these motivations, the paper proposes a set of specific objectives for the DockAnywhere framework:

- **Adopt a Third-Person View:** To mitigate the extreme sensitivity of egocentric observations to base pose shifts, the framework aims to use a third-person viewpoint. This fixed external perspective is intended to provide a more consistent visual representation of the workspace and objects, converting the visual generalization problem into a more manageable geometric generalization problem related to the robot's base pose.
- **Develop a Low-Cost Synthetic Demonstration Generation Framework:** The goal is to generate diverse, high-quality augmented demonstrations from a minimal set of source demonstrations, thereby reducing the need for extensive real-world data collection.
- **Decouple Base Motions and Manipulation Skills:** The framework aims to structurally parse manipulation trajectories into distinct motion segments (free-space movement) and skill segments (contact-rich interaction). This decoupling enables separate handling of parts of the trajectory that are sensitive to base pose changes versus those that are relatively invariant.
- **Integrate TAMP for Trajectory Augmentation:** By leveraging Task and Motion Planning (TAMP) principles, the objective is to systematically adapt the robot's motion segments for new docking points while preserving the fidelity of skill segments.
- **Ensure Cross-Viewpoint Consistency through 3D Point-Level Editing:** The framework aims to synthesize visual observations (specifically 3D point clouds) and robot states in a manner that is physically and visually coherent with the spatially transformed actions, providing robust supervision for policy learning.
- **Demonstrate Generalization:** The ultimate objective is to enable policies trained with DockAnywhere to achieve improved success rates and effectively generalize to novel viewpoints and unseen docking points in both simulated and real-world mobile manipulation tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The DockAnywhere framework focuses on generating spatially augmented observation-action sequences from a limited set of source demonstrations to improve the spatial generalization capability of mobile manipulation policies across multiple docking point candidates.

**A. Problem Formulation**
The work reframes the problem of viewpoint generalization. Instead of directly tackling egocentric observation changes (`o_ego` to `o'_ego`), it employs a fixed third-person view (`o`). This converts the challenge into a geometric docking point generalization problem (`p` to `p'`), where `p` and `p'` are the initial and perturbed docking points, respectively. The objective is to generate synthetic demonstrations `ˆD_p'` from a source demonstration `D_p` to train a generalizable policy `π_p'` via behavior cloning. Actions `a_t` consist of a target End-Effector (EE) pose `a_pose_t` in the world frame and a binary gripper command `a_cmd_t`. Observations `o_t` include point cloud data `o_pc_t` and robot proprioceptive state `o_state_t` (1D EE state).

**B. Demonstration Preprocessing**
Given a source demonstration `D_p`, DockAnywhere performs the following preprocessing steps:

1. **Point Cloud Acquisition and Segmentation:**
 - A single-view RGBD camera captures an RGBD image, which is converted into a raw point cloud in the world frame.
 - Redundant points (e.g., ground, robot torso) are cropped out using a predefined 3D bounding box to focus on the robot's EE and objects.
 - The cropped point cloud is downsampled to 1024 or 2048 points using Farthest Point Sampling (FPS) to reduce computational load.
 - An off-the-shelf segmentation model (e.g., Grounded SAM) is applied to the first RGB image frame to obtain pixel-aligned segmentation masks, which are then projected to the raw point cloud to yield 3D segmentation masks for the robot and objects.

2. **Source Trajectory Parsing:**
 - The source trajectory `D_p` is segmented into "free-space motion segments" and "contact-rich skill segments."
 - A segment is classified as a "skill segment" if the distance between the robot's EE and the geometric center of the target object falls below an empirically defined threshold, indicating potential contact. Otherwise, it is a "motion segment."
 - For example, in a pick-up task, approaching the object is a motion segment, while grasping and lifting is a skill segment.

3. **Feasible Docking Point Proposals:**
 - Novel docking points `p'` are sampled subject to three constraints to ensure physical feasibility:
 - **Visibility:** The segmented target object points must remain observable from the third-person camera.
 - **Reachability:** The target object must remain within the robot's workspace from the new base pose.
 - **Collision-Free:** All motion segments must be replannable into valid, collision-free trajectories. A sampled docking point is accepted only if all these conditions are met; otherwise, it is discarded.

**C. Action Generation via TAMP-based Transformation**
Actions are generated for the novel docking point `p'` by distinguishing between invariant and equivariant components:

1. **Robot Hand Commands (`a_cmd_t`):** These binary signals (e.g., gripper close/open) are considered invariant to spatial transformations and are directly reused from the source demonstration for all time steps (`ˆa_cmd_t = a_cmd_t`).

2. **Robot Arm Commands (`a_pose_t`):**
 - **Skill Segments:** To maintain the fidelity of complex interactions, the spatial relations between the EE and the target object must be preserved. Therefore, skill segments are reused as a whole. The arm actions `a_pose_t` within a skill segment are directly transferred (`ˆa_pose_t = a_pose_t`). When the relative robot-object location changes, the desired EE pose is updated to preserve its relative position in the object frame.
 - **Motion Segments:** These segments involve low-precision movements and are replanned for the relocated docking point. A motion planner is used to generate new arm actions (`ˆa_pose_t`) that smoothly connect the end pose of the preceding skill segment to the start pose of the subsequent skill segment.

**D. Synthetic Observation Generation**
The framework synthesizes new visual and state observations consistent with the generated actions:

1. **Point Cloud Synthesis:**
 - **Robot Arm:** The point cloud of the robot arm (`o_pc,arm_t`) is spatially transformed. A relative rigid transform `ΔT_t` is calculated based on the difference between the original EE pose `a_pose_t` and the newly generated EE pose `ˆa_pose_t`. This `ΔT_t` is then applied to the robot arm's point cloud (`ˆo_pc,arm_t = o_pc,arm_t · ΔT_t`).
 - **Manipulated Objects:** During motion segments, the object's point cloud (`o_pc,obj_t`) is assumed to remain unchanged. However, during skill segments where the object is manipulated, the same rigid transformation `ΔT_t` applied to the robot arm is also applied to the object's point cloud to preserve the relative geometry between the arm and the manipulated object.
 - **Final Point Cloud:** The transformed robot arm and object point clouds are concatenated to form the synthesized point cloud observation (`ˆo_pc_t`).

2. **Robot State Adaptation:**
 - **Gripper State (`o_cmd_t`):** Similar to action commands, the gripper state is invariant and directly reused (`ˆo_cmd_t = o_cmd_t`).
 - **End-Effector State (`o_pose_t`):** The EE state is equivariant. It is transformed using the original EE pose, its inverse, and the newly generated EE pose (`ˆo_pose_t = o_pose_t · (a_pose_t)^-1 · ˆa_pose_t`).

The output of this process is an augmented demonstration `ˆD_p'` comprising synthesized observation-action pairs (`ˆo_t`, `ˆa_t`) for the novel docking point `p'`. This synthetic data is then used for behavior cloning to train the mobile manipulation policy. The flexibility of augmenting in 3D point-cloud space is noted as a pathway toward onboard-only and egocentric policy learning.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The effectiveness of the DockAnywhere framework was evaluated through experiments in both simulated (ManiSkill) and real-world environments.

**A. Task Setup and Implementation Details**
- **Simulation Environment:** ManiSkill, built on SAPIEN, utilizing scene datasets like RoboCasa.
- **Tasks:** Five mobile manipulation tasks of increasing difficulty were designed: Pick-up Banana, Pick-up Mug, Place Can into Sink, Open Cabinet Door, and Open Cabinet Drawer.
- **Policy Head:** DP3 was used as the base policy architecture.
- **Demonstration Data:** 10 source demonstrations per task were collected via keyboard teleoperation from a single, feasible docking point.
- **Training:** Policies were trained for 2000 epochs on an NVIDIA A6000 GPU. During training, augmented demonstrations were generated for five randomly sampled docking points within a reasonable range (distance and orientation) from the target object. The distance threshold for segmenting skill segments was 0.1m.

**B. Comparison with Baselines**
DockAnywhere was compared against static manipulation baselines (vanilla DP, DP3) and a data augmentation baseline (DP3+DemoGen). Evaluation was conducted on both a single (feasible) docking point and five distinct docking points.

- **Performance on a Single Docking Point:** DP and DP3 achieved high success rates (78.6% and 88.6% respectively), indicating their competence in an ideal, fixed-view scenario.
- **Performance on Five Docking Points:** When faced with five varied docking points, DP and DP3 experienced significant performance degradation, with overall success rates dropping to 15.8% and 17.8% respectively. This outcome confirms the severity of the view generalization problem in mobile manipulation.
- **DP3+DemoGen Performance:** This baseline, which uses 2D diffusion for visual observation synthesis, showed substantial improvement over vanilla DP/DP3 on multiple docking points (74.2% overall success rate). However, it exhibited limitations in tasks requiring significant pose adaptation or complex 3D interactions, such as "Cabinet Door" (48.2%) and "Cabinet Drawer" (52.0%). For instance, in a rotated "Pick Mug" task, DemoGen achieved only 40% success, compared to DockAnywhere's 100%. This suggests that DemoGen's 2D image editing and focus on tabletop scenarios do not adequately address the full SE(3) spatial variations necessary for mobile manipulation.
- **DockAnywhere Performance:** DockAnywhere achieved the highest overall success rate of 78.9% across all tasks and docking points. It demonstrated superior performance in complex-interaction tasks and those requiring robust adaptation to both translation and rotation, attributed to its explicit modeling of docking-point variation and relative robot-object geometry through 3D point-level editing.

**C. Ablation Studies**

1. **Impact of Number of Additional Docking Points (Table II):**
 - The study varied the number of additional docking points used for augmentation (1, 2, 4, 6). Policies were tested on an *unseen* docking point.
 - Success rates consistently improved as the number of augmented docking points increased (e.g., 35.5% for 1 point, 61.9% for 4 points, 63.9% for 6 points).
 - The gain in performance showed diminishing returns, with a 2% improvement observed when increasing from 4 to 6 additional points. Four augmented points were chosen for the main experiments as a balance between performance and computational cost.

2. **Impact of Spatial Range of Docking Points (Table III):**
 - This study examined the effect of the spatial range of augmented docking points, defined as a ratio of the distance between the robot base and the target object (relative to the source distance).
 - For the "Place Can" task, success rates first increased (e.g., 65.6% at 0.5 ratio, 87.2% at 1.0 ratio) and then decreased with increasing range (46.3% at 2.0 ratio).
 - This indicates that a moderate range of augmentation is beneficial for generalization, while an excessively large range can introduce too much perturbation, potentially hindering policy learning.

**D. Real-World Experiments**
- **Platform:** A Galaxea R1 mobile manipulator equipped with two A1 6-DoF arms, an omnidirectional mobile base, a ZED2 depth camera, a Livox LiDAR, and a Realsense D455 RGBD camera for visual input. GroundedSAM was used for object segmentation.
- **Tasks:** Three representative manufacturing tasks: picking up gear (60% success rate), placing gear onto plate (40% success rate), and assembling components (30% success rate). These were evaluated at an unseen docking point after training with one source demonstration and four augmented docking points.
- **Generalization to Unseen Docking Points (Fig. 6):** An ablation study on the "Placing gear onto plate" task showed that the trained policy performed consistently well at the augmented docking points used for training. Success rates generally decreased as the test docking point moved further away from the training distribution. However, even at significant out-of-distribution distances (e.g., 0.4m from the target object), the policy achieved approximately a 20% success rate, demonstrating a degree of robustness to navigation errors.
- **Computational Efficiency (Fig. 7):** The time required for generating a single novel trajectory was approximately 0.02 seconds, significantly lower than the time for inference (0.21 seconds) and action execution (3.85 seconds for grasping, 4.65 seconds for placing). This demonstrates the computational efficiency of DockAnywhere's data augmentation process.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The DockAnywhere framework presents a method to address the viewpoint generalization problem in mobile manipulation, which is a key barrier to deploying robots in variable real-world environments. Its significance and potential impact are multifold:

- **Enhanced Generalization and Robustness:** The primary contribution is the substantial improvement in policy generalization to novel viewpoints and unseen docking points. By explicitly modeling and augmenting for docking point variability, the trained policies demonstrate increased robustness to navigation errors and non-ideal docking configurations common in practical scenarios. This directly contributes to making mobile manipulation systems more reliable and applicable beyond controlled laboratory settings.
- **Data Efficiency:** DockAnywhere enables the learning of robust visuomotor policies from a limited set of source demonstrations (as few as one per task), significantly reducing the reliance on costly and labor-intensive real-world data collection. This data-efficient approach is crucial for scaling mobile manipulation capabilities across diverse tasks and environments.
- **Low Computational Cost for Augmentation:** The framework's ability to generate diverse demonstrations synthetically with minimal computational overhead (approximately 0.02 seconds per trajectory) makes it a practical solution for expanding training datasets without incurring significant time or hardware expenditures.
- **Novel Methodological Approach:** The decoupling of docking-dependent base motions from invariant, contact-rich manipulation skills, combined with TAMP-based spatial transformation and 3D point-level observation synthesis, constitutes a novel approach to structured data augmentation in mobile manipulation. The use of a third-person viewpoint simplifies the view generalization challenge into a more tractable geometric problem.
- **Bridge to Real-World Deployment:** The successful deployment of policies trained with DockAnywhere on a real-world mobile manipulator demonstrates its practical applicability. The robustness shown even at out-of-distribution docking distances suggests a pathway for mobile manipulators to operate effectively in environments where precise initial positioning is not guaranteed.
- **Future Directions and Broader Applicability:** The inherent flexibility of augmenting in 3D point-cloud space suggests potential for extending the framework to scenarios with only onboard sensors, paving the way for advancements in egocentric policy learning for mobile manipulation. The findings regarding optimal ranges and numbers of augmentation points provide practical guidance for future research and implementation.

In summary, DockAnywhere offers a practical and computationally efficient solution to a fundamental challenge in mobile robotics, facilitating the development and deployment of more capable and adaptable mobile manipulation systems.
