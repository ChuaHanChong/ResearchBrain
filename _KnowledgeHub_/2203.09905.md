# Learning Affordance Grounding from Exocentric Images

> [!summary] Summary
> Cross-View-AG tackles affordance grounding from exocentric views: given third-person human-object interaction images plus an egocentric object image, it learns where an action can be performed using only affordance labels. An Affordance Invariance Mining module applies non-negative matrix factorization to strip individual interaction habits, and an Affordance Co-relation Preserving strategy aligns cross-view prediction correlation matrices. On the new 23,816-image AGD20K benchmark it beats saliency, weakly-supervised localization, and affordance baselines on KLD, SIM, and NSS.

## Problem

- Affordance is dynamic and interaction-dependent, so prior methods struggle to establish explicit links between an object's intrinsic parts and its action possibilities.
- Existing affordance datasets are small, low-quality, or lack part-level labels, covering few object and affordance categories and ignoring human action cues entirely.
- Prior view-transfer work builds perspective-invariant embeddings from paired data for recognition, never extracting affordance knowledge from diverse third-person interactions for first-person grounding.

## Method

- **Affordance Invariance Mining** (AIM) decomposes exocentric features via ==non-negative matrix factorization== into a shared dictionary and coefficients, discarding habit-driven residuals, with the dictionary momentum-updated across batches.
- **Affordance Co-relation Preserving** (ACP) builds outer-product correlation matrices from temperature-softened class scores of both branches and aligns them by cross-entropy, exploiting inter-affordance dependencies.
- A ==knowledge-transfer loss== L_KT applies L2 alignment between pooled exocentric and egocentric features; inference runs only the egocentric branch, reading out regions via ==class activation mapping==.

## Results

- On AGD20K Seen, KLD drops to **1.538** with SIM **0.334** and NSS **0.927**, improving KLD **13.3%** over the affordance baseline and **16.5%** over the best weakly-supervised localizer.
- On Unseen object categories it reaches KLD **1.787**, SIM **0.285**, NSS **0.829**, a **10.2%** KLD gain over the strongest saliency model and **15.1%** over the best localizer.
- Ablation from the **1.985** KLD bare model shows AIM alone gives **1.750** and ACP **1.810**; ACP outperforms L_KT, and all three combined reach **1.538**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Low-rank factorization is an effective invariance prior: shared bases capture what is common across demonstrations while per-sample residuals absorb idiosyncratic execution style.
> - Preserving inter-class correlation structure across views transfers more signal than direct feature matching, since related actions co-occur on shared object parts.
> - Cross-view supervision beats simply pooling both image sources: naively adding egocentric data helps baselines only marginally, so explicit transfer machinery is what matters.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper addresses the critical problem of **affordance grounding**, which aims to identify and localize regions on objects that afford specific actions or interactions. This task is fundamental for advancing intelligent agents from passive perception systems to **embodied intelligence systems** that can actively understand and interact with their environment. Understanding "action possibilities" is crucial for applications such as robot grasping, scene understanding, and action prediction.

Historically, research in affordance learning has largely focused on supervised approaches that directly map object regions to affordance categories. While these methods have shown promise, they often struggle with the inherent **diversity of human-object interactions** and the challenge of establishing an explicit link between an object's intrinsic properties and its affordances. A key limitation of existing approaches and datasets has been the difficulty in isolating the invariant, object-centric affordance knowledge from the variable, user-specific ways humans interact with objects.

This paper identifies a crucial gap: while humans naturally infer general object affordances from observing diverse interactions, current AI systems lack this explicit "cross-view" ability. Specifically, the paper introduces a novel and challenging task: **affordance grounding from an exocentric view**. This task posits that an intelligent agent should be able to observe human-object interactions from a third-person (exocentric) perspective, learn the underlying affordance knowledge (which is intrinsic to the object), and then transfer this knowledge to understand action possibilities on an object presented from a first-person (egocentric) perspective. This effectively bridges the observational learning of human behavior with an agent's own interaction capabilities.

The broader research landscape related to this work can be categorized into three main areas:

- **Visual Affordance Grounding:** Existing works in this area primarily rely on supervised learning to detect and localize affordances. Some studies utilize weakly supervised methods or focus on inferring affordances from videos. However, these often don't explicitly address the cross-view knowledge transfer from diverse exocentric interactions to invariant egocentric affordances, which is the core innovation here.
- **Visual Affordance Datasets:** The development of robust affordance models is heavily dependent on comprehensive datasets. Previous datasets like UMD, IIT-AFF, ADE-Aff, and PAD have advanced the field but often suffer from limitations in scale, object/affordance category diversity, image quality, or the lack of specific annotations for cross-view learning. This paper directly contributes to this landscape by introducing AGD20K, a large-scale dataset specifically designed to support the proposed exocentric-to-egocentric affordance grounding task, featuring rich annotations and diverse categories.
- **Learning View Transformations:** This area traditionally draws inspiration from concepts like mirror neurons, focusing on generating perspective-invariant representations from paired data for tasks such as action recognition or video summarization. While related, most prior work in view transformation does not specifically target the extraction and transfer of *affordance knowledge* itself, particularly when faced with the dual challenges of interaction diversity in the source view and multiple potential affordances in the target view. This paper carves out a unique niche by explicitly focusing on transferring affordance knowledge rather than just feature representations for actions.

By proposing a new task, constructing a tailored dataset, and developing a dedicated cross-view knowledge transfer framework, this work significantly pushes the boundaries of affordance understanding. It moves beyond merely detecting affordances to learning them through a human-like observational paradigm, making a crucial step towards truly intelligent and interactive AI agents.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to **empower an intelligent agent with the ability to perceive invariant egocentric affordances by observing diverse exocentric human-object interactions.** This overarching goal is driven by the desire to enable AI systems to transition from passive perception to active, embodied intelligence, where understanding how to interact with objects is as important as recognizing them.

The key objectives and motivations can be broken down as follows:

- **Objective 1: Propose and Define a Novel Task – Affordance Grounding from Exocentric View.**
 - **Motivation:** Humans possess an innate ability to observe how others interact with objects (exocentric view) and infer the intrinsic action possibilities of those objects for their own use (egocentric view), even when individual interaction styles vary significantly. For instance, observing different people hold a tennis racket in various ways allows one to understand the "swingable" part of the racket, regardless of individual grip. Current AI systems lack this sophisticated observational learning capability. This task aims to bridge this human-like learning ability into machine intelligence.
 - **Task Definition:** Given a collection of exocentric human-object interaction images and corresponding egocentric object images, the agent should learn affordance knowledge using only affordance labels as supervision. During inference, it should predict the precise affordance region on an egocentric object image, given a specific affordance label.

- **Objective 2: Develop a Cross-View Knowledge Transfer Framework.**
 - **Motivation:** The challenge lies in extracting the **invariant affordance-specific features** (determined by an object's intrinsic properties) from the highly diverse and often noisy exocentric interaction data, which contains variations due to individual habits, lighting, and viewing angles. Furthermore, effectively transferring this distilled knowledge to an egocentric view and accurately localizing affordance regions requires a robust mechanism that can handle the uncertainty arising from multiple potential affordance regions on a single object.
 - **Approach:** Design a framework with specialized modules:
 - **Affordance Invariance Mining (AIM) Module:** To specifically address the "diversity of interactive affordance," this module aims to decompose exocentric interactions into affordance-specific representations and individual differences. The goal is to "counter the impact of interactive diversity" by minimizing intra-class differences.
 - **Affordance Co-relation Preserving (ACP) Strategy:** Objects often afford multiple, correlated actions (e.g., "Cut with" and "Hold" for a knife). Leveraging these inherent correlations can reduce ambiguity and enhance the network's ability to perceive and localize affordances, thereby strengthening the link between object parts and affordance categories.

- **Objective 3: Construct a Large-Scale, High-Quality Affordance Grounding Dataset (AGD20K).**
 - **Motivation:** Existing affordance datasets are limited in terms of scale, diversity of affordance/object categories, image quality, and scene complexity. Crucially, they do not explicitly support the proposed cross-view learning paradigm with pixel-level annotations that highlight interaction regions. A dedicated benchmark is essential to facilitate comprehensive study and fair evaluation of models for this new task.
 - **Approach:** Collect and meticulously label over 20,000 exocentric and egocentric images across 36 affordance categories, with part-level annotations (dense points) to provide detailed ground truth for affordance regions.

In summary, the paper is motivated by a fundamental gap in AI's ability to learn affordances like humans do, through observation and generalization across diverse interactions. It aims to fill this gap by proposing a novel task, a sophisticated learning framework, and a comprehensive dataset to enable intelligent agents to acquire robust, transferable affordance knowledge.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology centers around a novel **cross-view knowledge transfer framework** designed to learn invariant affordance knowledge from diverse exocentric human-object interactions and transfer it to egocentric views.

#### 3.1. Overall Framework Architecture

The framework operates in two distinct phases: training and inference.

- **Training Phase:**
 1. **Feature Extraction:** A shared ResNet50 backbone extracts initial features ($Z_{exo}$ and $Z_{ego}$) from both a group of $N$ exocentric images ($I_{exo}$) and a single egocentric image ($I_{ego}$).
 2. **Affordance Invariance Mining (AIM) Module:** This module processes the exocentric features ($Z_{exo}$). It decomposes human-object interactions into intrinsic affordance-specific features (M) and individual interaction habits (E). Inspired by Non-Negative Matrix Factorization (NMF), it aims to obtain a shared, low-rank dictionary matrix (W) that represents common sub-features across diverse interactions, minimizing individual differences. The reconstructed affordance representation (M) is then integrated with the original features to produce $F_{exo}$.
 3. **Egocentric Feature Mapping:** The egocentric feature ($Z_{ego}$) is passed through convolutional layers to map it into an embedding space consistent with the exocentric view, resulting in $F_{ego}$.
 4. **Feature Processing for Classification & Knowledge Transfer:** Both $F_{exo}$ and $F_{ego}$ are fed into shared convolutional layers to produce $D_{exo}$ and $D_{ego}$. Global Average Pooling (GAP) is then applied to obtain global feature vectors ($f_{exo}$ and $f_{ego}$).
 5. **Cross-View Affordance Knowledge Transfer (L_KT):** An L2 loss ($L_{KT} = ||f_{exo} - f_{ego}||$) is applied to align the global feature vectors from both views, explicitly encouraging the egocentric branch to learn representations consistent with the distilled affordance knowledge from the exocentric view.
 6. **Affordance Co-relation Preserving (ACP) Strategy:**
 - The global feature vectors ($f_{exo}$ and $f_{ego}$) are passed through a shared fully connected layer to obtain affordance prediction scores ($s$ and $g$) for each view.
 - These scores are then used to compute co-relation matrices ($P$ for exocentric, $Q$ for egocentric) by multiplying the softmax-normalized prediction vectors with their transposes ($P=pp^T, Q=qq^T$).
 - A cross-entropy loss ($L_{ACP}$) is applied to align these co-relation matrices. This strategy leverages the fact that certain affordances inherently co-occur or are related (e.g., "cut with" often implies "hold"), thus enhancing the network's ability to perceive and localize affordances by understanding their interdependencies.
 7. **Total Loss:** The overall training objective is a weighted sum of three components: classification loss ($L_{cls}$), ACP loss ($L_{ACP}$), and knowledge transfer loss ($L_{KT}$).
 $L = \lambda_1 L_{cls} + \lambda_2 L_{ACP} + \lambda_3 L_{KT}$

- **Inference Phase:**
 1. Only the egocentric branch is used. An egocentric object image ($I_{ego}$) and a specific affordance label ($C_a$) are provided as input.
 2. Features are extracted and processed through the egocentric pathway, leading to the final convolutional layer features ($D_{ego}$).
 3. **Class Activation Mapping (CAM):** The CAM technique is applied to the final convolutional features ($D_{ego}$) to generate a heatmap ($Y_{C_a}$) that visually localizes the affordance region corresponding to the given affordance label $C_a$.

#### 3.2. Affordance Invariance Mining (AIM) Module in Detail

- Exocentric features are reshaped into matrix form ($X_i \in R^{c \times hw}$).
- Non-negative Matrix Factorization (NMF) is used to decompose $X_i$ into a dictionary matrix $W \in R^{c \times r}$ (shared across all exocentric features) and a coefficient matrix $H_i \in R^{r \times hw}$ (specific to each exocentric feature). $r$ is the rank of the low-rank approximation.
- The optimization aims to minimize $||X - WH||$ subject to non-negativity constraints on W and H.
- W and H are updated iteratively. The dictionary matrix $W^{(0)}$ is also updated per batch to capture statistical priors of common sub-features.
- The output $M = WH$ represents the reconstructed, affordance-specific feature, which is then added back to the original feature $Z_i$ as a residual connection.

#### 3.3. AGD20K Dataset Construction

- **Collection:** Exocentric images sourced from HICO, COCO, and free-license websites (20,061 images). Egocentric images collected from free-license websites (3,755 images).
- **Annotation:** 36 affordance classes were defined. Affordance labels were assigned based on human-object interaction for exocentric images and object class for egocentric images. Pixel-level labels are provided as dense points, which are then converted into heatmaps for objective metric calculation during testing.
- **Statistics:** The dataset is designed to feature diverse affordance and object categories, and importantly, highlights the complex multi-to-multi relationships between affordances and objects, making it a challenging benchmark.

#### 3.4. Experimental Setup

- **Metrics:** Kullback-Leibler Divergence (KLD), SIMilarity (SIM), and Normalized Scanpath Saliency (NSS).
- **Baselines:** Compared against saliency detection models (Mlnet, DeepGazeII, EgoGaze), weakly supervised object localization (WSOL) models (EIL, SPA, TS-CAM), and another affordance grounding model (Hotspots).
- **Settings:** Evaluated under "Seen" (object categories overlap between train/test) and "Unseen" (no overlap) conditions to assess generalization.
- **Implementation:** PyTorch, SGD optimizer, standard data augmentation, trained for 35 epochs. Hyper-parameters (e.g., $\lambda$ weights, NMF rank $r$, number of exocentric images N) were tuned.

This comprehensive approach, combining a novel task definition, a multi-component neural network architecture, and a purpose-built dataset, provides a strong foundation for addressing the challenges of cross-view affordance grounding.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental results robustly demonstrate the effectiveness and superiority of the proposed method and the utility of the AGD20K dataset.

#### 4.1. Quantitative Performance (Table 2)

- **Overall Superiority:** The proposed method consistently achieves the best performance across all three objective metrics (KLD ↓, SIM ↑, NSS ↑) in both "Seen" and "Unseen" settings on the AGD20K dataset.
 - In the "Seen" setting, the method shows significant improvements: e.g., KLD of 1.538 (compared to 1.773 for the best affordance grounding baseline, Hotspots), SIM of 0.334 (vs. 0.285 for EIL), and NSS of 0.927 (vs. 0.615 for Hotspots). This translates to relative improvements of 13.3% (KLD) over Hotspots, 17.2% over DeepGazeII (saliency), and 16.5% over TS-CAM (WSOL).
 - In the more challenging "Unseen" setting (where object categories in train and test sets do not overlap), the method maintains its lead. For instance, KLD is 1.787 (vs. 1.990 for DeepGazeII), SIM is 0.285 (vs. 0.263 for Mlnet), and NSS is 0.829 (vs. 0.696 for Hotspots). These improvements highlight the method's strong generalization capability to novel objects.
- **Robustness against Baselines:** The results confirm that generic saliency detection and weakly supervised object localization models are not sufficient for this specific affordance grounding task, and even dedicated affordance grounding models struggle in comparison, particularly in the "Unseen" scenario.

#### 4.2. Qualitative Performance (Figure 5)

- Visualizations of affordance heatmaps corroborate the quantitative findings. The proposed method generates more accurate, precise, and well-localized affordance regions compared to baseline methods.
- **Capturing Invariant Features:** The method successfully captures common affordance features despite variations in object appearance (e.g., "Sit on" affordance applied to both a "bed" and a "chair," correctly highlighting the seat/lying surface).
- **Distinguishing Multiple Affordances:** For objects with multiple affordances (e.g., "Open" vs. "Hold" for a "bottle"; "Hold" vs. "Cut with" for a "knife"), the model can distinguish and localize the correct region based on the input affordance label, demonstrating a nuanced understanding.
- **Generalization to Unseen Objects:** The qualitative results for the "Unseen" setting further demonstrate the model's ability to transfer learned affordance knowledge to objects not encountered during training.

#### 4.3. Ablation Study (Table 3)

- **Affordance Invariance Mining (AIM) Module:** Removing the AIM module significantly degrades performance (e.g., KLD increases from 1.538 to 1.985 in "Seen" setting), confirming its crucial role in extracting affordance-specific features and suppressing interaction diversity.
- **Affordance Co-relation Preserving (ACP) Strategy:** The ACP strategy provides a substantial boost to performance (e.g., KLD improves from 1.749 to 1.664 without ACP and L_KT, to 1.538 with all components, in "Seen"). This indicates the importance of leveraging inter-affordance correlations for better perception and localization.
- **Cross-View Knowledge Transfer Loss (L_KT):** The $L_{KT}$ loss also contributes positively to performance, ensuring effective transfer of learned knowledge between the exocentric and egocentric views.
- **Hyper-parameter Sensitivity (Figure 6):**
 - The temperature parameter (T) in ACP has a noticeable impact on performance, indicating its role in shaping the focus on correlations.
 - The rank (r) of the dictionary matrix in AIM has less sensitivity, suggesting that a range of values can yield good results.
 - Increasing the number of exocentric images (N) from 2 to 3 showed a significant improvement, highlighting the benefit of observing more diverse interactions.

#### 4.4. Performance Analysis (Tables 4 & 5, Figure 7)

- **Different Classes (Table 4):** The model shows robustness across various affordance categories, handling challenges like diverse object appearances ("Hold," "Swing"), overlapping object categories with distinct affordances ("Drink with," "Hold"), and varying affordance region sizes ("Lie on" vs. "Brush with").
- **Different Scales (Figure 7):** The method consistently outperforms baselines across "Big," "Middle," and "Small" affordance region scales, demonstrating its ability to accurately localize affordances regardless of their relative size within the image.
- **Different Sources (Table 5):** The results highlight that explicitly designing a cross-view knowledge transfer mechanism, as in the proposed method, is more effective than simply training with both exocentric and egocentric images in other models. While using both sources generally helps, the architectural design for knowledge transfer is key to superior performance.

In conclusion, the main findings underscore that the proposed cross-view knowledge transfer framework, augmented by the Affordance Invariance Mining module and the Affordance Co-relation Preserving strategy, effectively addresses the challenges of learning invariant affordances from diverse exocentric interactions and generalizing this knowledge to egocentric views, particularly on the newly introduced AGD20K dataset.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research marks a significant advancement in the field of affordance grounding and holds substantial potential impact across various domains of artificial intelligence and robotics.

#### 5.1. Novel Task Formulation and Problem-Solving Paradigm

The introduction of "affordance grounding from exocentric view" is a key contribution. This task models a human-like observational learning ability, moving beyond conventional supervised affordance detection to a more generalized and robust understanding. By explicitly separating the learning of invariant affordance properties from the variability of individual interactions, the paper proposes a powerful paradigm for acquiring actionable knowledge for AI agents.

#### 5.2. Creation of a Foundational Benchmark Dataset (AGD20K)

The AGD20K dataset is a substantial contribution to the research community. Its large scale (over 20,000 images), diverse categories (36 affordances, 50 objects), high-quality part-level annotations, and specific design for cross-view learning fill a critical gap in existing resources. This dataset will serve as a vital testbed and accelerator for future research in affordance grounding, embodied AI, and observational learning.

#### 5.3. Methodological Advancements

The proposed cross-view knowledge transfer framework, with its core components (Affordance Invariance Mining and Affordance Co-relation Preserving strategy), offers a robust and innovative solution.
- The **AIM module** effectively tackles the challenge of disentangling intrinsic object affordances from superficial interaction variations, a problem often overlooked in prior work. This is crucial for learning truly transferable knowledge.
- The **ACP strategy** leverages the inherent semantic relationships between affordances, enhancing the model's perceptual accuracy and reducing ambiguity. This demonstrates a deeper understanding of affordance structures beyond isolated labels.
Together, these components provide a strong baseline and inspiration for future architectural designs in transfer learning and embodied cognition.

#### 5.4. Impact on Embodied Intelligence and Robotics

The ability of an agent to perceive and localize "action possibilities" is foundational for embodied intelligence. Robots and other AI agents equipped with this capability can:
- **Perform more dexterous and context-aware grasping:** By knowing *where* on an object an action can be performed, robots can plan more effective and safe interactions.
- **Achieve superior scene understanding:** Moving beyond object recognition to understanding how objects can be used within a scene greatly enhances an agent's comprehension of its environment.
- **Improve action planning and prediction:** Understanding affordances allows agents to anticipate potential interactions, plan sequences of actions, and even predict human behavior more accurately.
This research directly contributes to building more autonomous, versatile, and human-aware robotic systems.

#### 5.5. Generalization to Unseen Objects

The demonstrated strong performance in the "Unseen" setting is particularly significant. It indicates that the learned affordance knowledge is genuinely transferable and generalizable to novel objects, a critical requirement for real-world deployment where agents will encounter new environments and items.

#### 5.6. Broader Societal and Ethical Considerations

The paper thoughtfully includes a "Broader Impacts" statement, acknowledging the potential for both positive and negative influences. While advancing embodied intelligence is a clear benefit, the authors highlight the risk of harmful human demonstrations leading to negative guidance for AI agents. This proactive consideration of ethical implications and the call for "strict legislation" underscores responsible research practices in a field with rapidly increasing societal impact.

In conclusion, "Learning Affordance Grounding from Exocentric Images" makes a multi-faceted and impactful contribution to computer vision and AI. By tackling a novel problem, providing a much-needed dataset, and developing an effective solution, it significantly advances the state of the art in understanding how intelligent agents can learn from observation to interact meaningfully with the physical world.
