# Provably Efficient RL under Episode-Wise Safety in Constrained MDPs with Linear Function Approximation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers introduce OPSE-LCMDP, an algorithm for Constrained Markov Decision Processes with linear function approximation, delivering provably sublinear regret and episode-wise zero constraint violation. This method enables computationally efficient safe reinforcement learning for large-scale problems, scaling polynomially with feature dimension, horizon, and action space size, independent of the state space.

## Problem

- Extending provable safety guarantees to large-scale Constrained MDPs (CMDPs) with linear function approximation, as existing tabular solutions are computationally intractable.
- Prior linear CMDP algorithms either incur exponential computational costs, lack episode-wise safety guarantees, or only address weaker 'instantaneous' constraints.
- The challenge of developing a computationally efficient linear CMDP algorithm that simultaneously achieves sublinear regret and zero episode-wise constraint violation.

## Method

- The Optimistic-Pessimistic Softmax Exploration for Linear CMDP (**OPSE-LCMDP**) algorithm, which combines optimistic reward estimates for exploration with pessimistic utility estimates for safety.
- A novel deployment rule for a strictly safe policy (πsf) that limits its usage logarithmically with the number of episodes, activating only when confidence in its safety is low.
- Computationally efficient optimism and pessimism via composite softmax policies, leveraging a bisection search over a parameter λ to balance reward optimization and constraint satisfaction, independent of state space size.

## Results

- OPSE-LCMDP guarantees episode-wise zero constraint violation, ensuring all deployed policies satisfy the expected total utility constraint in every episode with high probability.
- The algorithm achieves a provably sublinear regret bound of O~(H2d3K)+O~(d3H4ξ−2)+O~(H4ξ−1d5K)O(H^2√(d^**3K**)) + O(d^3H^4ξ^{-2}) + O(H^4ξ^{-1}√(d^**5K**))O~(H2d3K)+O~(d3H4ξ−2)+O~(H4ξ−1d5K), demonstrating efficient learning over KKK episodes.
- It is computationally efficient, scaling polynomially with action space size (AAA), horizon (HHH), and feature dimension (ddd), while being independent of state space size (∣S∣|∣S∣), and deploys the safe policy only a logarithmic number of times.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A confidence-based, limited deployment mechanism for a known safe policy can ensure feasibility in early exploration without incurring excessive regret in linear CMDPs.
> - Softmax-based policies, combined with a bisection search over a regularization parameter, provide a scalable and robust alternative to traditional LP or Lagrangian methods for enforcing hard constraints with function approximation.
> - The use of clipped value functions is essential for maintaining stable value estimates and preventing error propagation in linear function approximation settings under constraints.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses a fundamental challenge in reinforcement learning (RL) within the framework of Constrained Markov Decision Processes (CMDPs). CMDPs are designed to ensure safety guarantees in decision-making by incorporating constraints on agent behavior, which is critical for real-world applications such as industrial control and finance.

Prior research has established robust solutions for "safe exploration" in tabular CMDP settings. Several algorithms exist that achieve episode-wise zero-violation guarantees and sublinear regret, specifically $\tilde{O}(\sqrt{K})$ regret over $K$ episodes. These tabular methods typically involve two phases: deploying a known safe policy ($\pi_{sf}$) and subsequently updating policies using linear programs (LPs) that optimize an optimistic objective while satisfying a pessimistic constraint. The deployment of $\pi_{sf}$ is crucial to ensure the feasibility of the LPs as environmental information is gathered.

However, a significant gap exists in extending these theoretical guarantees to large-scale CMDPs, particularly those employing function approximation. The computational cost of LP-based methods scales with the size of the state space ($|\mathcal{S}|$), rendering them impractical for large-scale applications where $|\mathcal{S}|$ can be exponentially large. In the context of linear CMDPs, where value functions exhibit a linear structure, episode-wise safe RL with strong theoretical guarantees remained largely unaddressed prior to this work. Existing state-of-the-art linear CMDP algorithms either incur exponential computational costs (e.g., $K^H$, where $H$ is the episode horizon), lack episode-wise safety guarantees (e.g., only provide $\tilde{O}(\sqrt{K})$ violation regret as in Ghosh et al.), or address a weaker form of safety known as "instantaneous constraints." Instantaneous constraints require safety at every single step, which can be overly conservative in scenarios where temporary violations are acceptable as long as the overall episode-wise budget is met.

This paper explicitly identifies a fundamental open question: whether a computationally efficient linear CMDP algorithm can be developed that simultaneously achieves sublinear regret and zero episode-wise constraint violation. This work aims to close this gap by providing a solution that overcomes the computational and safety limitations of previous approaches.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a novel reinforcement learning algorithm that enables provably efficient and safe exploration in Constrained Markov Decision Processes (CMDPs) with linear function approximation. This overarching goal is broken down into several specific, interdependent objectives:

1. **Achieve Sublinear Regret:** The algorithm aims to minimize the cumulative difference between the optimal expected reward and the reward obtained by the agent over $K$ episodes. Specifically, the target is to achieve $\tilde{O}(\sqrt{K})$ regret, which signifies efficient learning as the average reward approaches the optimal value in the long run.
2. **Guarantee Episode-Wise Zero-Violation Safety:** A critical objective is to ensure that the constraint on the expected total utility value is satisfied in *every single episode*. This is a stringent safety requirement, stronger than "instantaneous constraints" (where safety must hold at every state-action step) or "cancel safety" (where violations in one episode can be offset by conservatism in another). This "hard constraint" violation ensures the agent's behavior remains within acceptable limits at all times.
3. **Ensure Computational Efficiency:** The proposed algorithm must be computationally tractable, meaning its computational cost should scale polynomially with problem-dependent parameters such as the feature dimension ($d$), horizon ($H$), and action space size ($|\mathcal{A}|$), while crucially remaining independent of the state space size ($|\mathcal{S}|$). This addresses the scalability issues inherent in LP-based methods when dealing with large state spaces.

The motivation for these objectives stems from the increasing demand for deploying RL agents in safety-critical real-world applications. Industries such as plant control, autonomous systems (e.g., drone navigation), and financial trading require decision-making systems that not only optimize performance but also rigorously adhere to safety regulations or operational limits. The existing theoretical landscape for CMDPs, while advanced in tabular settings, falls short in providing provable guarantees for large-scale problems with function approximation. Specifically:
- Tabular methods, while offering strong safety, are not scalable due to their dependence on $|\mathcal{S}|$.
- Prior linear CMDP algorithms either allow constraint violations during exploration (which is unacceptable in many safety-critical contexts) or are computationally prohibitive, making their practical deployment infeasible.
- Algorithms addressing "instantaneous constraints" are often overly conservative and might restrict the agent from exploring potentially optimal policies that respect episode-wise, but not instantaneous, limits.

By addressing these objectives, the authors aim to bridge the theoretical and practical gap in safe RL for large-scale CMDPs, enabling the deployment of robust and trustworthy autonomous systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed algorithm, Optimistic-Pessimistic Softmax Exploration for Linear CMDP (OPSE-LCMDP), is built upon the optimistic-pessimistic exploration framework. This framework generally involves constructing an optimistic estimate for rewards to encourage exploration and a pessimistic estimate for constraints to ensure safety. The paper introduces two key innovations to adapt this framework to large-scale linear CMDPs efficiently and safely:

1. **A Novel Deployment Rule for the Strictly Safe Policy ($\pi_{sf}$):**
 - **Background Challenge:** In optimistic-pessimistic exploration, the pessimistic constraint (e.g., $u^{(k)}_{\pi} \geq b$) might not have a feasible solution in early exploration stages when data is scarce. To circumvent this, tabular CMDP algorithms often deploy a known strictly safe policy ($\pi_{sf}$). Previous linear bandit literature handles this by assuming access to a "safe action" ($a_{sf}$), which simplifies feasibility guarantees. However, this approach is not easily extendable to episode-wise safety in CMDPs, where constraints are on entire policies (which can be nonlinear functions) rather than individual actions.
 - **OPSE-LCMDP's Approach:** OPSE-LCMDP introduces a deployment rule for $\pi_{sf}$ based on the agent's confidence in $\pi_{sf}$'s safety. Specifically, $\pi_{sf}$ is deployed if the confidence bound on its expected utility value, $V_{\pi_{sf}, u}^{(k),1}(s_1)$, is still too large, indicating high uncertainty or "unconfidence" in $\pi_{sf}$'s safety (Line 3 in Algorithm 2: `if V_π(k),Cλ,u(k),1(s1) < b then Set π(k):= πsf`). The confidence level is measured by a bonus term $V_{\pi_{sf}, \beta^{(k)}}^{P,1}(s_1)$.
 - **Theoretical Guarantee:** This strategy is designed to limit the number of $\pi_{sf}$ deployments. Theorem 3 demonstrates that the number of iterations where $\pi_{sf}$ is deployed grows only logarithmically with the total number of episodes $K$, ensuring that the agent does not overuse the suboptimal safe policy and thus can achieve sublinear regret. This is achieved by showing that the confidence bounds shrink on average, eventually reducing the uncertainty in $\pi_{sf}$.

2. **Computationally Efficient Optimism and Pessimism via Softmax Policies:**
 - **Background Challenge:** Directly solving optimistic-pessimistic optimization problems in linear CMDPs (analogous to the linear program used in tabular settings) is computationally challenging. Traditional LP methods are state-dependent and thus intractable for large state spaces ($|\mathcal{S}|$). Lagrangian methods, which transform constrained optimization into a min-max problem, are also problematic because the clipped value functions (necessary for robust estimation in linear CMDPs with exploration) may not be representable via occupancy measures, breaking key linearity properties required for their application.
 - **OPSE-LCMDP's Approach:** The algorithm employs a "composite softmax policy" (Definition 3) to implement optimism for rewards and pessimism for utilities. This policy, denoted $\pi^{(k), \lambda}$, is defined by a weighted sum of estimated Q-functions for reward, bonus, and utility, with $\lambda$ acting as a weighting parameter. The policy is constructed iteratively backward from $H$ to $1$.
 - **Balancing Optimism/Pessimism:** A small $\lambda$ prioritizes reward optimism (exploration), while a large $\lambda$ emphasizes utility pessimism (constraint satisfaction).
 - **Bisection Search for $\lambda$:** Instead of directly solving a complex optimization problem, OPSE-LCMDP uses a computationally efficient bisection search (Lines 4-10 in Algorithm 2) to find the smallest $\lambda$ that ensures the pessimistic utility constraint $V_{\pi^{(k), \lambda}, u^{(k)},1}(s_1) \geq b$ is satisfied. This search relies on the monotonic property of the value function with respect to $\lambda$ (Lemma 6), which implies that increasing $\lambda$ tends to improve safety.
 - **Value Function Estimation:** The algorithm defines clipped value functions ($Q_{\pi,r}^{(k),h}[\kappa]$, $Q_{\pi,\dagger}^{(k),h}$, $Q_{\pi,u}^{(k),h}$ in Definition 2) to maintain value estimates within reasonable bounds and prevent error propagation, a common technique in linear MDP algorithms.
 - **Computational Efficiency:** The iterative computation of softmax policies and the bisection search over $\lambda$ lead to a computational cost that scales polynomially with $A, H, d$ and is independent of $|\mathcal{S}|$, as highlighted in Remark 2.

In essence, OPSE-LCMDP integrates a confidence-based safe policy deployment mechanism with a novel softmax-based optimistic-pessimistic policy construction and a bisection search for constraint regularization. This combination allows for provably efficient and safe exploration in large-scale linear CMDPs.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The core contribution of this research is the development of the Optimistic-Pessimistic Softmax Exploration for Linear CMDP (OPSE-LCMDP) algorithm, which addresses the challenges of achieving provably efficient and safe reinforcement learning in large-scale constrained environments with linear function approximation.

The main findings and results are primarily theoretical, supported by numerical experiments:

1. **Provable Episode-Wise Zero-Violation Safety:**
 - **Corollary 2:** OPSE-LCMDP guarantees that all deployed policies $\pi^{(k)}$ belong to the set of safe policies $\Pi_{sf}$ for every episode $k \in [K]$ with high probability. This means the algorithm achieves "zero-violation" of the constraint on the expected total utility value in every episode, a strong safety guarantee that distinguishes it from many prior works.
 - This safety is maintained through a combination of the pessimistic utility estimation (using $V_{\pi,u}^{(k),h}$) and the bisection search for $\lambda$, which ensures that any selected softmax policy $\pi^{(k),\lambda}$ satisfies the pessimistic constraint. If no such policy is found for a maximum $\lambda$, the known safe policy $\pi_{sf}$ is deployed.

2. **Provably Sublinear Regret:**
 - **Theorem 4:** The algorithm achieves a sublinear regret bound, specifically:
 Regret(K) $\leq \tilde{O}(H^2\sqrt{d^3K}) + \tilde{O}(d^3H^4\xi^{-2}) + \tilde{O}(H^4\xi^{-1}\sqrt{d^5K})$.
 - This bound comprises three main terms:
 - $\tilde{O}(H^2\sqrt{d^3K})$: This term is similar to the regret achieved by fundamental unconstrained linear MDP algorithms like LSVI-UCB, representing the core exploration efficiency.
 - $\tilde{O}(d^3H^4\xi^{-2})$: This constant term arises from the initial periods of high uncertainty and the need to rely on the safe policy. The $\xi^{-2}$ dependence is noted to be unavoidable in constrained bandit settings.
 - $\tilde{O}(H^4\xi^{-1}\sqrt{d^5K})$: This term captures the additional cost incurred due to the safety constraint and the complexity of optimistic-pessimistic exploration. The $\xi^{-1}$ dependence is consistently observed across existing safe RL literature.
 - The combined regret is sublinear in $K$, indicating that the average reward approaches the optimal value as $K \to \infty$.

3. **Computational Efficiency:**
 - **Remark 2:** OPSE-LCMDP is computationally efficient, scaling polynomially with problem parameters ($A, H, d$) and importantly, *independent of the state space size* ($|\mathcal{S}|$). This is a significant improvement over previous linear CMDP algorithms that incur exponential costs ($K^H$) or tabular methods that are intractable for large $|\mathcal{S}|$. The bisection search for $\lambda$ takes $\tilde{O}(H)$ iterations, leading to a polynomial per-iteration computational cost.

4. **Logarithmic Number of Safe Policy Deployments:**
 - **Theorem 3:** The number of times the strictly safe policy $\pi_{sf}$ is deployed (when the algorithm is "unconfident" in $\pi_{sf}$'s safety) is bounded logarithmically with respect to $K$. This ensures that the algorithm primarily explores potentially optimal policies rather than relying excessively on the known, but possibly suboptimal, safe policy.

5. **Empirical Validation (Appendix F):**
 - Numerical experiments conducted in synthetic tabular, media streaming, and synthetic linear CMDP environments support the theoretical findings.
 - **Comparison with Ghosh et al.:** OPSE-LCMDP consistently achieves sublinear regret with zero constraint violation, whereas the algorithm by Ghosh et al. (a prior state-of-the-art linear CMDP method) also achieves sublinear regret but shows positive and increasing violation regret. This highlights the practical benefit of OPSE-LCMDP's hard safety guarantees.
 - **Comparison with DOPE:** While DOPE (a tabular CMDP algorithm) also achieves zero-violation, its computational cost makes it intractable for the linear environments with large state spaces where OPSE-LCMDP operates effectively.
 - **Safe Policy Deployment:** The experiments show that OPSE-LCMDP utilizes $\pi_{sf}$ primarily in the early episodes, and the total number of $\pi_{sf}$ deployments converges to a constant or grows very slowly, empirically validating the logarithmic bound of Theorem 3.

In summary, OPSE-LCMDP offers a novel and effective solution to a long-standing problem in safe reinforcement learning, demonstrating both strong theoretical guarantees and practical applicability in large-scale, safety-critical domains.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research marks a notable advancement in the field of safe reinforcement learning by addressing a critical gap in the theoretical understanding and algorithmic capabilities for Constrained Markov Decision Processes (CMDPs) with linear function approximation.

1. **Resolution of a Fundamental Open Question:** The paper successfully answers the previously open question of whether a computationally efficient linear CMDP algorithm can achieve sublinear regret with *episode-wise zero-violation* safety guarantees. This is the first algorithm to demonstrate all three properties simultaneously, significantly advancing the state of the art. Previous methods either compromised on safety (allowing violations), computational efficiency (incurring exponential costs or state-space dependence), or addressed a weaker form of safety (instantaneous constraints).

2. **Enabling Safe RL in Large-Scale Systems:** The development of a computationally efficient algorithm (polynomial time, independent of state space size) that guarantees hard, episode-wise safety opens doors for deploying RL in complex, large-scale real-world applications. These include critical domains like autonomous driving, robotic control, smart grids, and industrial process optimization, where stringent safety regulations and operational limits are paramount. The ability to manage large state spaces without compromising safety or performance makes this work highly relevant for practical implementation.

3. **Novel Integration of Techniques:** The methodology introduces innovative combinations of established RL techniques. The confidence-based, logarithmically bounded deployment of a safe policy ($\pi_{sf}$) is a new mechanism for ensuring feasibility without incurring excessive regret. The adaptation of softmax policies to efficiently implement optimistic-pessimistic exploration, coupled with a bisection search for constraint regularization, provides a scalable and robust alternative to traditional LP or Lagrangian methods for CMDPs with function approximation. These methodological contributions could inspire further research in designing efficient and safe exploration strategies.

4. **Contribution to Theoretical Foundations:** The detailed theoretical analysis, including rigorous regret bounds and zero-violation guarantees, strengthens the foundational understanding of learning in constrained environments. The paper identifies the specific factors (e.g., dependence on horizon $H$, feature dimension $d$, and safety margin $\xi$) influencing regret, providing a benchmark for future research aiming to further tighten these bounds. The explicit treatment of how confidence intervals and optimistic-pessimistic principles interact in a function approximation setting with hard constraints is a significant theoretical step.

**Limitations and Future Work (as identified by the authors):**
- **Single-Constraint Setting:** The current algorithm is designed for a single constraint. Extending it to multiple constraints is non-trivial, as the $\lambda$ parameter would become a vector, complicating the monotonicity properties essential for the bisection search.
- **Fixed Initial State:** The analysis relies on a fixed initial state $s_1$. Extending the guarantees to adversarial or unknown initial states would require non-trivial modifications, as key elements like the $\pi_{sf}$ deployment rule and the existence of optimistic-pessimistic policies depend on this assumption.
- **Regret Bound Tightness:** While the regret bound is pioneering for this setting, the tightness of its dependence on parameters like $d$ and $H$ compared to different safety requirements remains an open question, necessitating formal lower bounds for this specific problem setting.

Despite these limitations, the paper delivers a significant breakthrough in the field, providing a theoretically sound and empirically supported algorithm that advances the practical feasibility of safe reinforcement learning in complex decision-making systems.
