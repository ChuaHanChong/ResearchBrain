# Beyond Future Prediction: Denoising as Generative Adaptation for Robot Control

> [!summary] Summary
> NowWAM is a robot policy that adapts a pretrained generative Diffusion Transformer by denoising the current observation instead of predicting a future one. The same visual stream is sampled along the denoising trajectory and feeds an action expert, so generative and action losses share one stream. It reaches 87.7% on LIBERO-Plus, 6.1 points over future-target co-training, with half the visual tokens.

## Problem

- Generative robot policies adapt pretrained DiTs via future visual prediction, yet recent methods remove that prediction from inference, leaving unclear why the co-training helps.
- Prior work never isolates what a pretrained generative DiT contributes to control, conflating generative initialization, target temporality, and denoising-state exposure in one design.
- Future-target co-training carries a second visual stream through training that is absent at deployment, doubling visual tokens and inflating step time and memory.

## Method

- ==Current-frame denoising==: the current latent is noised along the pretrained flow trajectory and the ==DiT== regresses velocity, so a single visual stream serves both generative adaptation and action learning.
- ==Shift-reparameterized noise sampling== draws sigma from u via su/(1+(s-1)u), with s below one biasing training toward low-noise states of the denoising trajectory.
- **DiT-MoT** action expert reads layer-wise visual-language context by ==masked mixed attention==; loss mixes denoising velocity MSE at weight 0.5 with action MSE at 1.0; inference uses the clean endpoint.

## Results

- LIBERO-Plus: **87.7%** with FLUX2-Klein versus **81.6%** for ImageWAM future-target co-training, **87.8%** with pure text-to-image Z-Image; camera perturbations rise from **77.7%** to **88.6%**.
- Standard LIBERO stays saturated at **98.4%** average; RoboCasa GR1 100-shot transfer reaches **64.9%** over **1,200** episodes, beating Fast-WAM **56.0%** and DIAL **58.3%**.
- Ablations: clean-endpoint-only training drops to **77.48%** and no visual loss to **78.73%** versus **84.97%** low-noise-shifted; tokens halve **784**-to-**392**, step time **2.85s** to **1.63s**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Forward temporal semantics are not privileged: past targets at t-16 match future targets, so forecasting alone does not explain generative co-training gains.
> - Training across a continuum of denoising states regularizes the action-facing representation; clean-endpoint training or noise without generative supervision both underperform.
> - Generative pretraining itself supplies most robustness, and plain text-to-image backbones suffice - video or image-editing pretraining is unnecessary for manipulation control.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Overview

A robot told to “pick up the black bowl next to ramekin and place it on the plate” must find the right bowl even when its starting state or camera view changes. Previous generative robot policies often trained by asking a model to predict a separate future visual target, even when that prediction was not used to choose actions at deployment. NowWAM drops that future-image branch. It trains the same current view to support both visual denoising and action choice. In a representative example under the RobotInit perturbation, the future-target baselines misidentify the target, while NowWAM completes the instructed transfer. This is one illustrative rollout and does not guarantee the same outcome in other runs.

Representative LIBERO-Plus rollouts under Robot Initial States (left) and Camera Viewpoints (right). On the left, the baselines misidentify the grasp target, while NowWAM grounds, grasps, and places the black bowl. On the right, the baselines fail to push open the drawer, while NowWAM opens it and completes the placement. These examples illustrate the benchmark results but do not quantify them.

### 2. One current view, two training jobs

In prior future-target co-training, the current view informs action prediction. A separate visual target, often a future observation, provides a generative training signal.

NowWAM instead adds noise to the current visual latent at a sampled level of the pretrained model’s denoising trajectory. The model is a diffusion transformer (DiT), a generative model trained to denoise visual inputs. Its generative branch learns to predict the corresponding denoising velocity. The action expert learns from language and visual-language context supplied by that same noisy current stream. The method therefore couples the generative objective to the representation used for control.

Training and inference differ. During training, a sampled noise level exposes the action-facing representation to different denoising states. At inference, the policy evaluates the clean current observation at σ=0 in one forward pass, with no separate target stream or visual denoising rollout.

NowWAM information flow. In training (left), a clean current frame is sampled at one noise level per example. The frame and the language instruction enter a T2I backbone plus action expert, which has separate denoising and action losses. In inference (right), the clean current frame produces a robot action in one DiT forward pass. The example images are schematic.

### 3. What the comparisons show

The headline test is LIBERO-Plus, the paper’s robustness benchmark. It covers camera, robot, language, lighting, background, noise, and layout perturbations. Its main evaluation measures success rate over the full 10,030 episodes. With FLUX2-Klein-4B, NowWAM scores 87.7%, compared with 81.6% for ImageWAM (FLUX2-Klein-4B), the future-target co-training baseline. The authors report that gains concentrate on challenging visual shifts. NowWAM’s language score is comparable to, and numerically below, ImageWAM’s. With the pure text-to-image Z-Image-6B backbone, NowWAM scores 87.8%.

Matched ablations probe the mechanism. They use non-EMA checkpoints on a fixed 1,923-episode LIBERO-Plus subset, separate from the headline results. In the auxiliary-target arm, a future target at *t*+32 scores 83.70% and a past target at *t*−16 scores 83.80%. This weakens the claim that forward temporal semantics uniquely account for the benefit. That arm’s current-target control still uses a separate auxiliary stream and is not NowWAM’s design.

In the denoising-adaptation arm, clean-endpoint training alone scores 77.48%. Uniform noise sampling scores 83.46%, and sampling shifted toward lower noise scores 84.97%. Noisy-current training without visual denoising supervision scores 78.73%. These results support the authors’ interpretation that training across denoising states matters. They do not establish it as the only explanation for robustness.

Training is also cheaper in a matched setup: same backbone and action expert, 2×H200, global batch size 64, with text embeddings and VAE latents cached. NowWAM uses 392 visual tokens instead of 784. Measured training step time falls from 2.85 s to 1.63 s. This is a training measurement, not an inference speedup.

### 4. The future is optional here

In the manipulation settings studied, a generative model adapted for control by learning to denoise the observation it acts from, without a separate future target. The authors leave open whether future modeling helps with learning dynamics or long-horizon planning.
