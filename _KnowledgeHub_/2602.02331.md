# TTT-Parkour: Rapid Test-Time Training for Perceptive Robot Parkour

> [!summary] Summary
> TTT-Parkour introduces a framework that enables humanoid robots to rapidly adapt locomotion policies to complex, unseen real-world terrains within minutes. This system combines a pre-trained reinforcement learning policy with an efficient RGB-D based 3D reconstruction pipeline, achieving near-perfect simulation success rates (99-100%) and robust zero-shot transfer to a Unitree G1 robot (e.g., 60-100% success on challenging obstacles).

## Problem

- Humanoid robot locomotion policies trained in simulation struggle to generalize to unseen, geometrically complex real-world terrains, leading to brittle performance.
- Existing high-fidelity 3D scene reconstruction methods are too slow for rapid adaptation, while faster methods lack the metric accuracy required for physics-based robot learning.
- Robots lack the ability to quickly adapt their behaviors to novel, difficult obstacles encountered in dynamic, unpredictable environments without extensive re-training or manual intervention.

## Method

![[2602.02331-method.png]]
*TTT-Parkour's three stages: pre-train a general policy on procedurally generated terrains, test-time-train on a reconstructed real scene, then sim-to-real deploy with zero-shot transfer to the real robot.*

- A two-stage deep reinforcement learning approach employs a general policy pre-trained on diverse procedurally generated terrains, followed by rapid test-time training (TTT).
- An efficient RGB-D based geometry reconstruction pipeline rapidly generates collision-accurate, simulation-ready meshes from real-world captures within minutes, incorporating automatic scale recovery and frame alignment.
- The pre-trained policy undergoes rapid fine-tuning on the specific reconstructed real-world terrain in simulation, with full fine-tuning of all policy parameters proving most effective.

## Results

- TTT-Parkour achieved 99-**100%** simulation success rates across 13 challenging real-world terrains after rapid fine-tuning, converging to **97%** success within approximately 10 minutes (including capture, reconstruction, and TTT).
- Adapted policies demonstrated robust zero-shot sim-to-real transfer, improving real-world success on a Unitree G1 robot from **0%** (pre-trained) to 60-**100%** on complex obstacles like wedges and stakes.
- The RGB-D reconstruction pipeline accurately produced simulation-ready meshes in 2 minutes 10 seconds, outperforming other methods in speed and fidelity for physics simulation, and proved crucial for real-world deployment.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Rapid test-time training (TTT) on high-fidelity, reconstructed real-world terrains is essential for enabling humanoid robots to master complex obstacles that general pre-trained policies fail on.
> - Accurate and fast real-world scene reconstruction, including metric scale recovery and consistent frame alignment, is critical for bridging the sim-to-real gap for physics-based robot learning.
> - Full fine-tuning of all policy parameters during TTT is more effective than parameter-efficient methods when facing significant domain shifts between pre-training and novel real-world terrains.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper addresses a critical, long-standing challenge in robotics: enabling complex, agile locomotion for humanoid robots on unstructured, unseen terrains in the real world. It positions itself at the intersection of several rapidly evolving fields: deep reinforcement learning (RL) for locomotion, perceptive robotics, real-to-sim-to-real transfer, and 3D scene reconstruction.

- **Humanoid Locomotion and Reinforcement Learning:** Recent years have seen tremendous progress in humanoid and legged robot locomotion, largely driven by deep RL. Policies trained in simulation, often leveraging massive parallelization (e.g., Isaac Gym, IsaacLab), can now achieve robust mobility on diverse *structured* and *procedurally generated* terrains. However, the paper identifies a key limitation: these policies often fail on *unseen, complex, and arbitrary real-world environments* that exhibit out-of-distribution geometric shifts. The concept of "athletic intelligence" for humanoids on such challenging terrains is presented as the next frontier. TTT-Parkour aims to bridge this "generalization gap" by providing a rapid adaptation mechanism.
- **Perceptive Robotics:** The integration of exteroceptive perception (e.g., LiDAR, depth cameras) is crucial for proactive obstacle traversal, moving beyond purely reactive recovery. While traditional methods use LiDAR for elevation maps, they can suffer from state estimation drift and motion distortion in high-dynamic tasks. More recent approaches use depth images for end-to-end policy input, which are better suited for precise foothold selection. TTT-Parkour builds on this by using a forward-facing depth camera for its perceptive input, but critically enhances it by enabling adaptation to highly complex, sparse foothold scenarios where existing methods falter.
- **Fast Adaptation and Test-Time Training (TTT):** The concept of Test-Time Adaptation (TTA) originated in classical machine learning to mitigate distribution shifts during inference. In robot learning, adaptation to novel environments has been explored with diffusion-based controllers or generative models. However, the authors explicitly state their work as the "first to leverage rapid test-time training paradigm to enable humanoid robots to master unseen, complex terrains." This claims a significant novel application of TTT, specifically tailored for the demanding real-time requirements of dynamic robot parkour.
- **Real-to-Sim-to-Real and 3D Scene Reconstruction:** The sim-to-real paradigm is fundamental for training complex robotic policies. Achieving high fidelity in simulation is paramount, and this requires accurate scene reconstruction. Existing methods like NeRF and 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) excel in visual synthesis and have been extended for physical interaction. However, the paper points out their major drawback: they are computationally intensive and time-consuming, relying on offline, multi-stage processes (e.g., COLMAP), making them incompatible with *rapid* test-time adaptation. Faster feed-forward methods exist but often suffer from scale ambiguity or geometric distortions that render them unsuitable for physics simulation. TTT-Parkour directly addresses this gap by developing an efficient, automated, and high-fidelity reconstruction pipeline that can generate *simulation-ready meshes* within the tight time constraints (minutes) required for rapid TTT, a key enabling technology for their overall framework.

In essence, TTT-Parkour combines state-of-the-art RL for locomotion with a novel, rapid real-world scene reconstruction technique and a fast adaptation paradigm to push the boundaries of humanoid robot capabilities on truly challenging and unpredictable terrains. It seeks to close the gap between general policies trained on broad distributions and the need for high-performance, specialized policies for specific, difficult real-world obstacles.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core motivation behind TTT-Parkour stems from the inherent limitations of current humanoid locomotion policies when confronted with the vast complexity and variability of real-world, highly challenging terrains.

**Motivation:**
1. **Limited Generalization of Pre-trained Policies:** While deep reinforcement learning has enabled impressive locomotion capabilities, general policies trained on procedurally generated terrains often exhibit poor generalization to unseen, "out-of-distribution" real-world obstacles. Synthetic environments, even with randomization, struggle to capture the full spectrum of geometric features and spatial configurations found in reality. This leads to brittle performance when robots encounter genuinely novel and complex geometries.
2. **Inefficiency of Manual Scene Reproduction:** Manually creating high-fidelity, physics-accurate digital models of real-world terrains for simulation training is labor-intensive and time-consuming, hindering rapid deployment and iteration.
3. **Limitations of Existing Reconstruction Methods:** Current advanced reconstruction techniques like NeRF and 3DGS, while excelling in visual fidelity, are too computationally intensive and slow for real-time or rapid adaptation scenarios (often requiring hours for optimization). Conversely, faster feed-forward methods often lack the metric accuracy and geometric fidelity required for reliable physics simulation, suffering from scale ambiguity or distortion. There is a critical need for a reconstruction method that is both fast *and* accurate enough for physics-based robot learning.
4. **Need for Rapid Adaptation:** To truly deploy humanoids in challenging and unpredictable environments, robots need the ability to rapidly adapt their behaviors to newly encountered, difficult obstacles without extensive re-training or manual intervention. The goal is to turn "failure into success" swiftly.

#### 2.1. Key Objectives

1. **Develop a Real-to-Sim-to-Real Framework for Rapid Adaptation:** The primary objective is to create an end-to-end framework that allows humanoid robots to quickly adapt their locomotion policies to previously unseen and extremely challenging real-world terrains.
2. **Establish a Two-Stage Perceptive Locomotion Learning Paradigm:** This involves:
 - **Pre-training:** Training a general policy on diverse procedurally generated terrains to learn fundamental locomotion primitives and build a robust initial foundation.
 - **Rapid Test-Time Training (TTT):** Fine-tuning the pre-trained policy specifically on high-fidelity meshes reconstructed from the *actual* novel real-world terrains encountered at test time.
3. **Engineer an Efficient and High-Fidelity Geometry Reconstruction Pipeline:** This pipeline must take RGB-D inputs and quickly generate collision-accurate, simulation-ready meshes with automatic scale recovery and frame alignment. This is crucial to bridge the real-world geometry to the simulation environment accurately and rapidly.
4. **Demonstrate Agile and Robust Humanoid Parkour Capabilities:** The ultimate objective is to enable humanoid robots to master complex obstacles—such as wedges, stakes, boxes, trapezoids, and narrow beams—within a practical timeframe (e.g., less than 10 minutes for capture, reconstruction, and TTT) and exhibit robust zero-shot sim-to-real transfer.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

TTT-Parkour employs a sophisticated real-to-sim-to-real framework comprising three main stages: policy pre-training, efficient geometry reconstruction, and rapid test-time training (TTT).

**A. Problem Definition**
The task is defined as traversing a series of discrete elevated platforms ($P$) from a start ($p_{start}$) to an end ($p_{end}$) platform with a fixed forward velocity command, avoiding contact with the ground plane ($G_{ground}$) which signifies failure. The terrains feature geometric primitives with limited contact areas, necessitating precise foothold selection and stability.

**B. Policy Pre-training**
1. **Reinforcement Learning Formulation:** The perceptive locomotion task is framed as an RL problem, optimized using Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO).
2. **Policy Architecture:** A two-part policy:
 - **Perception:** A CNN-based depth encoder extracts latent features from depth images ($I_t \in R^{W \times H}$). A history sliding window ($H_t$) captures temporal depth information.
 - **Proprioception:** A vector ($p_t$) containing base angular velocity, projected gravity, velocity commands, joint positions and velocities, and the previous action. A history sliding window ($h$) for proprioception.
 - **Integration:** Latent depth features are concatenated with proprioception and fed into a Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) to predict actions.
 - **Robustness & Sim-to-Real:** Stochastic noise is injected into the actor's input during training to enhance robustness and aid sim-to-real transfer.
 - **Asymmetric Actor-Critic:** The critic has access to privileged information (noise-free state, base linear velocity) to improve learning efficiency and guide the actor.
3. **Actions:** The policy outputs target joint positions, which are converted to torques via a PD controller.
4. **Terminations:** Episodes terminate if the robot is stuck, any body link contacts the ground, or base orientation exceeds thresholds.
5. **Rewards:** A comprehensive reward function incentivizes task completion, penalizes undesirable behaviors, and encourages natural motion:
 - **Task Reward:** Dense velocity tracking (linear and angular), penalties for waiting or heading errors, and a general "is alive" bonus.
 - **Regularization Reward:** Penalizes foothold on terrain edges, energy consumption, action rate, joint deviation, base angular velocity, and non-flat/pelvis/feet orientations. Includes "feet air time" and "feet slide" terms.
 - **Safety Reward:** Penalizes exceeding joint position/velocity limits, torque limits, and undesired body contacts.
 - **AMP (Adversarial Motion Priors) Reward:** Utilizes a discriminator trained on MPC-generated datasets to encourage natural and robust motion styles.
6. **Pre-training Environment:** Training occurs on a large-scale set of diverse procedurally generated terrains (wedges, stakes, boxes, trapezoids, narrow beams) in a 20x10 grid. A curriculum learning strategy is employed, progressively increasing difficulty (larger gaps, smaller platforms).

**C. Efficient Geometry Reconstruction (Key Innovation)**
This pipeline aims to convert real-world RGB-D captures into high-fidelity, simulation-ready meshes rapidly.
1. **Real-World Capture:** RGB-D inputs are captured from the physical environment.
2. **Feed-forward Terrain Reconstruction:** An initial scale-ambiguous point cloud is reconstructed from RGB sequences using a feed-forward model. Screened Poisson Surface Reconstruction converts the point cloud into a mesh.
3. **Scale Recovery:** Crucially, this step addresses the scale ambiguity of feed-forward methods. A scaling factor is computed by aligning the *predicted depth* from the feed-forward model with the *metric depth* from the RGB-D camera. This is done by comparing the median depth values in the lower half of the depth images, focusing on the terrain and avoiding background noise. This ensures precise metric scale, vital for accurate physics simulation.
4. **Physically-consistent Frame Alignment:** The scaled point cloud/mesh is aligned with the simulation's world frame:
 - **Gravity Alignment (z-axis):** A 3D semantic segmentation model identifies ground points. RANSAC and PCA are applied to these points to robustly estimate the ground plane normal. The entire scene is then rotated to align this normal with the simulation's z-axis (gravity vector).
 - **Traversal Direction Alignment (x-axis):** The centroids of the start ($p_{start}$) and end ($p_{end}$) platforms are identified (also via semantic segmentation). The scene is rotated around the z-axis such that the vector connecting these centroids aligns with the simulation's x-axis, ensuring consistent forward velocity commands.

**D. Policy Rapid Test-time Training**
After pre-training and scene reconstruction, the policy undergoes rapid fine-tuning on the specific reconstructed terrain mesh in simulation. The MDP formulation (observations, actions, terminations, rewards) remains identical to pre-training. Four fine-tuning strategies were investigated:
1. **Full Fine-tuning:** All parameters of the policy network (depth encoder and MLP) are updated end-to-end, starting from the pre-trained checkpoint. (Selected as the best approach.)
2. **Adapter Modules:** Lightweight adapter networks are inserted after each layer of the depth encoder and MLP. Original weights are frozen, and only adapter modules (zero-initialized) are optimized.
3. **Residual Learning:** A parallel network is added to learn an additive action correction ($a_{total} = a_{base} + a_{res}$). The base policy is frozen, and the residual output layer is zero-initialized.
4. **Last Layer Fine-tuning:** The depth encoder and intermediate MLP layers are frozen, restricting updates to only the final linear layer of the actor policy.

**E. Training Setup and Deployment**
- **Simulator:** IsaacLab powered by IsaacSim for high-fidelity physics. GPU-accelerated ray-caster depth simulation (NVIDIA Warp).
- **Hardware:** NVIDIA RTX 5090 GPU, 4096 parallel humanoid agents.
- **Robot:** Unitree G1 (29 DoF humanoid).
- **Perception (Real-world):** Intel RealSense D435i camera (60 Hz, 480x270 raw, downsampled to 64x36, cropped to 32x18).
- **Deployment:** Zero-shot transfer to real robot's onboard NVIDIA Jetson Orin NX (ROS2, 50 Hz inference).
- **Terrains:** 13 distinct real-world terrains of wedges, stakes, boxes, trapezoids, narrow beams, and mixed setups.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental section comprehensively evaluates TTT-Parkour across various dimensions, both in simulation and real-world deployment.

**A. Traversability Analysis (Necessity and Efficiency)**
- **Pre-trained Policy Limitations:** The pre-trained policy, despite being trained on diverse procedurally generated terrains, exhibited extremely poor generalization to unseen real-world terrains, failing completely (0% success) or achieving negligible success rates (e.g., 0.1% on Wedges, 0% on Trapezoids, Stakes) in simulation (Table I). In real-world tests, it also failed on most challenging obstacles (Fig 6). This strongly validates the necessity of adaptation.
- **Necessity of Pre-training:** Training a policy from scratch on a single target terrain (Scratch-1) was largely unsuccessful, failing on most terrains even after 25,000 iterations (Table I, Table V). This indicates that the broad locomotion primitives and curriculum learning from the large-scale pre-training are essential for the policy to have a good starting point for adaptation.
- **Effectiveness and Efficiency of TTT-Parkour (TTT-1):**
 - **Dramatic Performance Improvement:** TTT-Parkour (TTT-1) consistently achieved near-perfect success rates (99-100%) in simulation across all 13 challenging terrains after fine-tuning (Table I).
 - **Rapid Convergence:** The policy rapidly converged to high performance. Most terrains reached a 97% success rate within approximately 120 iterations (e.g., Narrow1 in 20, Boxes in 0, Wedges in 100), corresponding to roughly 10 minutes for the entire process (capture, reconstruction, TTT) (Fig 5, Table V). Even the most complex "Boston" terrain, requiring circular motion, converged within 560 iterations.
 - **Zero-shot Sim-to-Real Transfer:** The adapted policies showed robust zero-shot transfer to the real Unitree G1 robot. Success rates improved from 0% (pre-train) to over 60% on complex obstacles like Wedges, Trapezoids, and Stakes, and 100% on Boxes and Wedges (Fig 6). This is a significant real-world validation.
- **Multi-task TTT (TTT-13) vs. Single-Task TTT (TTT-1):** TTT-13 (fine-tuning on all 13 terrains simultaneously) generally converged slower and slightly underperformed TTT-1 (Table I, Table V), attributed to gradient interference and reduced sampling density per task. However, in specific cases like Trapezoid2, TTT-13 showed faster convergence, possibly due to positive transfer from geometrically similar terrains (e.g., Narrow Beams) in the multi-task setting.
- **Sim-to-Real Gap:** Real-world success rates were observed to be slightly lower than simulation. This gap is attributed to hardware instabilities (camera noise, actuator dynamics), unmodeled physical terrain shifts/wobbles, and residual discrepancies between reconstructed and physical geometry.

**B. Mesh Reconstruction Analysis (Quality and Speed)**
- **RGB-D (Ours) – Optimal Trade-off:** The proposed RGB-D reconstruction pipeline achieved an optimal balance between quality and efficiency. It reconstructed simulation-ready meshes with realistic geometric fidelity, minimal artifacts, and precise metric scale, comparable to industrial LiDAR scanners (Table II, Table IV, Fig 7). The entire reconstruction process was completed in 2 minutes 10 seconds.
- **Comparison with Alternatives:**
 - **LiDAR:** Provided excellent scale accuracy but was expensive, prone to artifacts at fine details, and labor-intensive/time-consuming (20 minutes) due to multi-pass scanning and post-processing.
 - **iPhone (3D Scanner App):** Accessible and fast (4 minutes) but yielded inconsistent, noisy results with significant flying artifacts.
 - **Hand-crafted Meshes:** Despite appearing "perfect," these models created a severe domain mismatch due to the lack of real-world irregularities, leading to poor sim-to-real performance (Table II, Table III).
- **Importance of RGB-D for Scale Accuracy:** Our method's scale recovery, leveraging RGB-D, demonstrated metric accuracy (e.g., 0.002 absolute relative scale error on Stake1, Table IV) that significantly outperformed RGB-only feed-forward baselines like MapAnything and Pi3 (e.g., 0.383 and 0.863 error on Stake1), validating the necessity of RGB-D input for minimizing the sim-to-real gap.
- **Real-world Impact of Reconstruction Quality:** Real-world deployment showed that policies trained on RGB-D and LiDAR reconstructions achieved comparable high success rates (80-100%), while iPhone-based reconstruction struggled on complex terrains (0% on Stake1) due to artifacts, and hand-crafted meshes consistently failed (Table III).

**C. Analysis of Test-Time Training Strategies**
- **Full Fine-tuning (Ours) Superiority:** Full fine-tuning, updating all policy parameters, proved to be the most robust and efficient strategy, achieving the fastest convergence to a 97% success rate across tested terrains (Table VI).
- **PEFT Limitations:** Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (PEFT) methods (Last Layer, Residual, Adapter) consistently underperformed full fine-tuning. This is attributed to the significant domain shift between pre-training and testing terrains, where the limited parameter space of PEFT methods restricts their adaptability. Furthermore, as the primary bottleneck in the RL pipeline is physical simulation, PEFT offered negligible wall-clock time savings.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

TTT-Parkour represents a significant advancement in the field of agile humanoid locomotion, with profound implications for the deployment of robots in complex, unstructured real-world environments.

- **Breakthrough in Humanoid Locomotion Generalization:** The work successfully tackles the long-standing challenge of poor generalization of humanoid policies to truly unseen and geometrically complex terrains. By demonstrating robust adaptation to diverse obstacles like wedges, stakes, and narrow beams, TTT-Parkour pushes the boundaries of what humanoid robots can achieve beyond controlled laboratory settings or purely procedurally generated worlds.
- **Pioneering Rapid Test-Time Training (TTT) for Humanoid Parkour:** The paper introduces and validates a novel application of the TTT paradigm in robotics. The ability to rapidly fine-tune a robot's policy on a newly encountered terrain within minutes is a critical step towards autonomous adaptation, moving beyond laborious manual re-programming or slow re-training cycles. This rapid adaptation is a cornerstone of intelligent agents operating in dynamic, unpredictable real-world scenarios.
- **Enabling Real-World Deployment through Efficient Real-to-Sim-to-Real:** The developed high-fidelity and efficient geometry reconstruction pipeline is a key enabler. By providing collision-accurate, simulation-ready meshes from RGB-D data within minutes, it effectively bridges the "reality gap" by bringing the real environment into simulation for rapid learning. This overcomes the limitations of existing reconstruction methods (either too slow or too inaccurate for physics simulation) and hand-crafted models (which suffer from domain mismatch). This makes the real-to-sim-to-real loop practical for on-the-fly adaptation.
- **Increased Autonomy and Robustness:** The framework empowers humanoid robots with a higher degree of autonomy, allowing them to encounter and adapt to novel geometric challenges without human intervention, significantly enhancing their robustness in unknown environments. This is crucial for applications where human access is limited or dangerous.
- **Potential Impact on Various Domains:**
 - **Disaster Relief and Exploration:** Humanoids could navigate rubble, uneven ground, and partially collapsed structures in search and rescue missions.
 - **Logistics and Manufacturing:** Adapting to new facility layouts, temporary obstacles, or varying cargo configurations.
 - **Personal Robotics:** Enabling robots to interact robustly with the diverse and often unpredictable geometries of human living spaces.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The work establishes a strong foundation for future research in several areas:
 - **Faster Adaptation:** The current 10-minute adaptation time, while rapid, is still identified as a limitation for industrial applications. Future work aims to reduce this to seconds, possibly through single-image local terrain mesh generation and advanced computational hardware.
 - **Dynamic Terrain Modeling:** The current reconstruction focuses on static geometry. Incorporating inference of dynamic physical properties (friction, mass, compliance) to model terrain instability will further minimize the sim-to-real gap and enable adaptation to deformable or unstable terrains, pushing parkour capabilities even further.
 - **Broader Task Diversity:** While focused on parkour, the TTT approach could be extended to other complex robot manipulation and interaction tasks where rapid adaptation to novel objects or environments is critical.

In conclusion, TTT-Parkour makes a compelling case for test-time training as a powerful paradigm to unlock advanced capabilities in humanoid robots. By synergistically combining advanced RL, fast and accurate 3D reconstruction, and rapid policy adaptation, it significantly propels the field toward truly agile and intelligent humanoid agents capable of operating effectively in the real world.
