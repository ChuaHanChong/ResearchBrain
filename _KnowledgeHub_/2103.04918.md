# A Survey of Embodied AI: From Simulators to Research Tasks

> [!summary] Summary
> This paper offers an encyclopedic survey of Embodied Artificial Intelligence, systematically analyzing nine prominent simulators based on seven technical features and categorizing three main research tasks: visual exploration, visual navigation, and embodied question answering. It reveals interconnections between simulators and tasks, providing guidance for researchers and highlighting future directions for more capable embodied agents.

## Problem

- A lack of a contemporary and comprehensive survey covering the rapidly evolving field of embodied AI, especially given significant advancements in deep learning since previous foundational surveys.
- The proliferation of diverse embodied AI simulators without a systematic framework for comparison and evaluation, making it difficult for researchers to select appropriate tools.
- The need to understand how current simulators and research tasks contribute to the paradigm shift from static 'internet AI' to active, interactive 'embodied AI' and address the 'Sim2Real' transfer challenge.

## Method

- Provided an encyclopedic and contemporary overview of the embodied AI field, covering its foundational simulation platforms and emergent research tasks.
- Evaluated nine prominent embodied AI simulators against a newly proposed set of ==seven technical features== encompassing realism, scalability, and interactivity.
- Systematically surveyed three main embodied AI research tasks (==visual exploration==, ==visual navigation==, ==embodied question answering==), detailing state-of-the-art approaches, evaluation metrics, and relevant datasets for each.

## Results

- Habitat-Sim and iGibson were identified as top performers for graphic rendering performance and fidelity, making them suitable for high-fidelity visual tasks, while AI2-THOR excelled in environment configurations and multi-state object interaction.
- The survey successfully categorized embodied AI research tasks into a hierarchical structure: Visual Exploration, Visual Navigation (including Point, Object, and Vision-and-Language Navigation), and Embodied Question Answering, detailing approaches, metrics, and datasets for each.
- Key challenges were highlighted, including the Sim2Real gap due to the lack of simulators combining high-fidelity world-based scenes with advanced physics, limitations in 3D data scalability, and the need for more robust memory architectures and multi-agent support in future research.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A "pyramid hierarchical structure" effectively organizes embodied AI tasks from visual exploration as foundational, leading to visual navigation, and culminating in embodied question answering, reflecting increasing complexity and interdependency.
> - Embodied AI simulators present trade-offs between realism (e.g., world-based scenes) and practicality (e.g., game-based scenes), with differing strengths in physics capabilities, object interaction, and multi-agent support.
> - Specific simulator features align with different task requirements; for instance, high-fidelity simulators like Habitat-Sim and iGibson suit visual exploration, while AI2-THOR is well-suited for interactive tasks requiring multi-state object changes.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This paper directly addresses a significant paradigm shift in Artificial Intelligence: the transition from "internet AI" to "embodied AI." Historically, much of AI research, particularly in deep learning, has focused on learning from static, curated datasets of images, videos, and text primarily sourced from the internet. While highly successful in tasks like image classification or natural language processing, this "internet AI" often lacks the ability to generalize to real-world scenarios requiring active perception, interaction, and reasoning within dynamic environments.

Embodied AI, in contrast, posits that true intelligence emerges from an agent's interactions with its environment from an egocentric perspective, similar to how humans learn. This paradigm aligns closely with the long-term pursuit of Artificial General Intelligence (AGI), which aims to create AI systems capable of performing a wide range of tasks with human-like flexibility and understanding. The convergence of recent advances in deep learning, reinforcement learning, computer graphics, and robotics has made the development of embodied AI systems more feasible than ever before.

A critical enabler for embodied AI research is the development of sophisticated simulators. These virtual testbeds allow for the training and testing of AI algorithms in controlled, repeatable, and scalable environments before costly and time-consuming deployment in the real world. They also facilitate the collection of vast amounts of task-based data that would be impractical to acquire in physical settings.

Prior to this paper, the authors identify a notable gap in the literature: a contemporary and comprehensive survey of the embodied AI field. Existing surveys, while foundational, were largely published before the modern deep learning era (post-2009) and thus do not capture the significant advancements and challenges brought by recent methodological breakthroughs and the proliferation of new simulators and tasks. By providing an "encyclopedic survey," this work aims to fill this void, offering a much-needed map of the current landscape, categorizing existing simulators and research tasks, and identifying key trends and future directions. It positions itself as a critical resource for researchers seeking to enter or navigate this rapidly expanding domain, particularly concerning the selection of appropriate simulation tools for specific research questions.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives of this survey paper are clearly articulated:
1. **To provide an encyclopedic and contemporary overview of the embodied AI field:** This addresses the identified scarcity of up-to-date, comprehensive surveys in this rapidly evolving domain, especially given the significant advancements in deep learning since previous surveys.
2. **To evaluate current embodied AI simulators:** The paper aims to rigorously assess nine prominent simulators based on a newly proposed set of seven technical features. This evaluation seeks to understand the capabilities and limitations of these simulators in supporting various embodied AI research endeavors.
3. **To survey the main research tasks in embodied AI:** The authors specifically focus on three interconnected and increasingly complex tasks: visual exploration, visual navigation, and embodied question answering (QA). For each task, the survey covers state-of-the-art approaches, evaluation metrics, and relevant datasets.
4. **To establish interconnections and provide guidance:** By analyzing simulators and tasks, the paper aims to reveal insightful relationships between them, offering practical suggestions for "simulator-for-task selections" to guide researchers.
5. **To identify existing challenges and recommend future directions:** Beyond summarizing the current state, a key objective is to highlight the pressing challenges in both embodied AI simulators (e.g., realism, scalability, interactivity) and research tasks (e.g., memory architectures, complexity management, multi-agent systems), proposing avenues for future work.

The motivation behind these objectives stems from several critical factors:
- **The paradigm shift:** The burgeoning interest in embodied AI as a path towards AGI necessitates a structured understanding of its foundational components.
- **Rapid growth of simulators:** The proliferation of diverse embodied AI simulators creates a need for a systematic comparison and evaluation to inform researchers' choices.
- **Increasing complexity of research tasks:** As embodied AI tasks become more sophisticated, integrating multiple AI capabilities (vision, language, planning, interaction), a structured overview of state-of-the-art methods, metrics, and datasets becomes essential.
- **Bridging the gap:** The ultimate goal of embodied AI is often deployment in the real world. Simulators play a crucial role in this, and understanding their capabilities and limitations is key to addressing the "Sim2Real" transfer challenge.
- **Facilitating new research:** By consolidating knowledge and identifying gaps, the survey aims to empower new and existing AI researchers to contribute effectively to the field, fostering innovation and accelerating progress.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The paper adopts a systematic, two-pronged approach to survey the embodied AI landscape: first, a detailed analysis of simulators, and second, a comprehensive review of key research tasks.

**Simulator Evaluation Methodology:**
The authors selected nine general-purpose embodied AI simulators that have emerged within the last four years (as of the paper's acceptance in late 2021): DeepMind Lab, AI2-THOR, CHALET, VirtualHome, VRKitchen, Habitat-Sim, iGibson, SAPIEN, and ThreeDWorld. These were chosen for their focus on general-purpose intelligence tasks, distinguishing them from game-specific reinforcement learning environments.

For evaluation, the authors proposed **seven primary technical features**, drawing from existing literature and best practices:
1. **Environment:** Categorized into Game-based Scene Construction (G, built from 3D assets) and World-based Scene Construction (W, built from real-world scans). This distinction assesses visual fidelity and realism.
2. **Physics:** Classified into Basic Physics (B, collision, rigid-body dynamics, gravity) and Advanced Physics (A, cloth, fluid, soft-body physics), crucial for realistic object interactions.
3. **Object Type:** Differentiated by Dataset-driven (D, from existing 3D datasets like SUNCG, Matterport3D, Gibson) and Asset-driven (O, from online asset stores), reflecting scalability and quality control.
4. **Object Property:** Assessed based on Interact-able Objects (I, basic interaction) and Multiple-state Objects (M, fine-grained, state-changing interactions).
5. **Controller:** Types of interfaces for agent control: Direct Python API (P), Virtual Robot (R), and Virtual Reality (V).
6. **Action:** Categorized by complexity: Navigation (N), Atomic Action (A, basic manipulation), and Human-Computer Interaction (H, via VR interfaces).
7. **Multi-Agent:** Indicating support for Avatar-based (AT) or User-based (U) multi-agent setups.

These seven primary features are further grouped under **three secondary evaluation features**: Realism (Environment, Physics), Scalability (Object Type), and Interactivity (Object Property, Controller, Action, Multi-Agent). The paper then presents these comparisons in a comprehensive table (Table I) and discusses quantitative performance metrics such as rendering speed (Table II), linking them to the chosen simulation engines and technical specifications.

**Research Task Review Methodology:**
The paper focuses on three main embodied AI research tasks, structured in a "pyramid hierarchical structure" reflecting increasing complexity:
1. **Visual Exploration:** The foundational task where an agent learns to gather information about an environment efficiently to build an internal model.
2. **Visual Navigation:** Building upon exploration, this involves an agent navigating to a goal (point, object, or following language instructions) in a 3D environment. This category is further subdivided into Point Navigation, Object Navigation, Navigation with Priors, and Vision-and-Language Navigation (VLN).
3. **Embodied Question Answering (QA):** The most complex task, requiring an agent to integrate visual perception, language understanding, reasoning, task planning, and navigation to answer questions about its environment. This includes sub-categories like Multi-target EQA (MT-EQA) and Interactive QA (IQA).

For each task, the authors systematically detail:
- **Summary:** A brief description and motivation.
- **Approaches/Methodologies:** Discussion of classical vs. learning-based methods, and specific state-of-the-art models (e.g., curiosity-driven, coverage, reconstruction for exploration; various deep reinforcement learning and hybrid architectures for navigation; modular control for QA).
- **Evaluation Metrics:** Standard metrics used to assess performance (e.g., Amount of Targets Visited, SPL, Success Rate for navigation; navigation errors and QA accuracy for EQA).
- **Datasets:** Common and specialized datasets used for training and evaluation (e.g., Matterport3D, Gibson V1, AI2-THOR, Room-to-Room, IQUAD V1).

The research tasks discussed are primarily sourced from direct citations of the nine selected embodied AI simulators, ensuring relevance and practical applicability within the current ecosystem.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The paper's comprehensive survey yields several key findings regarding both embodied AI simulators and research tasks:

**Findings on Embodied AI Simulators:**
- **Feature-based Comparison:** Table I provides a detailed qualitative comparison of nine simulators across seven technical features. This reveals their individual strengths and weaknesses in realism (environment, physics), scalability (object type), and interactivity (object property, controller, action, multi-agent).
- **Realism vs. Practicality:** Simulators using world-based scene construction (Habitat-Sim, iGibson) offer higher fidelity and more accurate real-world representation, crucial for Sim2Real transfer. However, game-based scenes (majority) are more resource-efficient to construct and often offer built-in physics engines and clear object segmentation.
- **Physics Capabilities:** Most simulators have basic physics, sufficient for navigation. Only ThreeDWorld offers advanced physics (cloth, fluid, soft-body), indicating a niche for research requiring complex physical interactions.
- **Object Interaction:** Simulators like AI2-THOR and VRKitchen stand out for enabling multiple-state changes in objects, which is critical for tasks requiring fine-grained manipulation and understanding object affordances.
- **Control and Action Complexity:** All simulators support navigation. Many offer atomic actions, but human-computer interaction via VR controllers is less common, found in simulators like VRKitchen and ThreeDWorld, enabling high-dexterity tasks.
- **Multi-Agent Support:** This feature is largely underdeveloped, with only a few simulators (AI2-THOR, iGibson, ThreeDWorld) offering it. This reflects the relatively nascent stage of multi-agent embodied AI research compared to single-agent setups.
- **Top Performers:** Quantitatively (Table II), Habitat-Sim and iGibson exhibit superior graphic rendering performance, making them suitable for high-fidelity visual tasks. AI2-THOR boasts the largest environment configurations, making it versatile for diverse scenarios. These three simulators are identified as the most widely used and well-received due to their balance of realism, scalability, and interactivity.

**Findings on Embodied AI Research Tasks:**
- **Pyramid Structure of Complexity:** The paper successfully categorizes embodied AI research tasks into a hierarchical "pyramid" (Fig. 5): Visual Exploration forms the base, feeding into Visual Navigation, which then supports Embodied Question Answering. This demonstrates a natural progression of complexity and interdependency.
- **Visual Exploration:**
 - **Approaches:** Beyond classical SLAM, learning-based methods (curiosity, coverage, reconstruction) are explored to build diverse internal models (topological, semantic, occupancy maps) for efficient information gathering. These methods address challenges like sensor noise and enable generalization.
 - **Evaluation:** Primarily by "Amount of Targets Visited" and, increasingly, by "Impact on Downstream Tasks" (e.g., navigation performance).
 - **Datasets:** Matterport3D and Gibson V1 are dominant, leveraging photorealistic RGB-D data.
- **Visual Navigation:**
 - **Categories:** Point Navigation (to specific coordinates) and Object Navigation (to object categories) are fundamental. Navigation with Priors and Vision-and-Language Navigation (VLN) add further complexity.
 - **Approaches:** A shift from purely hand-engineered to learning-based (e.g., end-to-end deep RL, DD-PPO) or hybrid (combining learned modules with classical planners) methods for robustness and generalization. Memory architectures (recurrent, attention-based, map-like) are critical.
 - **Evaluation:** Success Weighted by Path Length (SPL) and Success Rate (SR) are key. Other metrics include Path Length Ratio and Distance to Success.
 - **Datasets:** Matterport3D and Gibson V1 are widely used. R2R and CVDN datasets are specific to VLN and vision-dialog navigation, respectively.
- **Embodied Question Answering (EQA):**
 - **Complexity:** Considered the most challenging, requiring integration of perception, language understanding, reasoning, planning, and navigation.
 - **Approaches:** Often modular, separating navigation and QA components (e.g., PACMAN, NMC, HIMN). Advanced forms include MT-EQA (multi-target) and IQA (requiring object interaction).
 - **Evaluation:** Assesses both navigation performance (distance to target, goal progress) and QA performance (mean rank, accuracy).
 - **Datasets:** EQA (based on House3D/SUNCG), MT-EQA, and IQUAD V1 (AI2-THOR) are specialized datasets designed for these tasks.
- **Simulator-Task Interconnections (Fig. 6):**
 - Habitat-Sim and iGibson are most suitable for visual exploration and a range of visual navigation tasks due to their high fidelity (world-based scenes).
 - AI2-THOR is preferred for interactive tasks like embodied QA and navigation with priors due to its strong multi-state object property.
 - VLN currently relies on Matterport3D Simulator, but future VLN advancements are predicted to necessitate more interactive embodied AI simulators.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The significance of this survey paper is multi-faceted, providing both a crucial snapshot of the current embodied AI landscape and a roadmap for its future development.

**Current Significance:**
- **Comprehensive Resource:** It serves as a vital, up-to-date reference for researchers, offering a structured and detailed overview of simulators, tasks, methodologies, and evaluation metrics. This is particularly valuable for newcomers to the field or for established researchers seeking to understand emerging trends.
- **Benchmarking and Comparison:** By systematically evaluating simulators based on proposed features and comparing research tasks, the paper establishes clear benchmarks and highlights the strengths and weaknesses of existing tools and approaches. This enables informed decision-making for researchers in selecting appropriate simulators for their specific research questions.
- **Clarifying the Paradigm Shift:** It reinforces the importance of embodied AI as a crucial step towards Artificial General Intelligence, emphasizing the shift from passive data consumption to active, interactive learning.
- **Identifying Gaps:** The survey explicitly pinpoints areas where current capabilities fall short, setting the stage for future research.

**Potential Impact and Future Directions:**
The paper's insights lead to several significant implications and recommendations for the future of embodied AI:

- **Addressing Realism and Sim2Real Gap:** The identified lack of simulators combining both world-based scene construction (high fidelity) and advanced physics features (e.g., cloth, fluid) is a major bottleneck. Future efforts need to focus on developing such simulators to improve the transferability of learned behaviors from simulation to the real world (Sim2Real). Advances in 3D reconstruction and physics engines are critical here.
- **Improving Scalability of Data:** The difficulty in collecting large-scale, high-fidelity 3D scene and object datasets limits the diversity and realism of embodied AI environments. Future research should prioritize developing more accessible and efficient tools for 3D data acquisition, possibly leveraging advanced learning-based 3D reconstruction and generative models.
- **Balancing Interactivity:** There is a need to strike a better balance between symbolic interaction capabilities and fine-grained manipulative interactions within simulators, particularly for world-based scenes. This will allow agents to perform more complex, human-like tasks.
- **Advancing Research Tasks (TIQA):** The authors propose **Task-based Interactive Question Answering (TIQA)** as the next logical step in the embodied AI research pyramid. TIQA would require agents to perform tasks to gather information necessary to answer questions (e.g., "How long would it take for an egg to boil?"). This vision underscores the need for agents that can reason about and execute complex, multi-step procedures, moving beyond simple navigation or observation. This could be a significant step towards general-purpose AI capable of common household tasks.
- **Rethinking Memory Architectures:** The paper highlights that while various memory types have been explored, there's no clear consensus on the best architecture for capturing long-term dependencies in embodied AI. Future research should dedicate more focus to comparing and developing robust memory mechanisms.
- **Promoting Multi-Agent Research:** The current scarcity of multi-agent setups in simulators and the corresponding lack of research in this area are noted. Given that real-world intelligence often involves collaboration and communication, future work should prioritize developing multi-agent support in simulators and exploring collaborative/adversarial tasks.

In conclusion, "A Survey of Embodied AI: From Simulators to Research Tasks" is a timely and valuable contribution to the field. By systematically mapping the current landscape, it not only consolidates existing knowledge but also critically evaluates the tools and challenges, providing a clear vision for the next generation of embodied AI research aimed at bridging the gap between artificial and general intelligence.
