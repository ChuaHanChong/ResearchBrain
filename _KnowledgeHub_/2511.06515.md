# Koopman global linearization of contact dynamics for robot locomotion and manipulation enables elaborate control

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from MIT developed a Control-Coherent Koopman Model Predictive Control (CCK-MPC) framework that globally linearizes robot contact dynamics using causal, continuous contact models. This framework enables real-time convex optimization for contact-rich tasks, allowing robots to autonomously discover elaborate strategies for legged locomotion and dynamic manipulation while achieving superior performance over traditional local linearization methods.

## Problem

- Controlling robots in dynamic contact poses a challenge due to the switching nature of system dynamics at contact boundaries, which leads to non-convex optimization problems for predictive controllers.
- Real-time control with Model Predictive Control (MPC) becomes computationally intensive and difficult to implement on hardware for contact-rich tasks.
- Prior approaches often rely on simplifications like local linearization, pre-defined contact mode sequences, or reduced dynamics, which limit dynamic exploration, adaptability, and the discovery of optimal multi-contact strategies.

## Method

- A Control-Coherent ==Koopman== Model Predictive Control (CCK-MPC) framework was developed to transform piecewise-nonlinear contact dynamics into a single, globally linear model in a higher-dimensional embedding space.
- Discontinuities in contact dynamics are addressed by employing causal physical models with viscoelastic contact compliance, ensuring continuity of momentum and state transitions suitable for Koopman operator application.
- The CCK framework extends Koopman theory to non-autonomous systems, directly incorporating control inputs into the lifted linear system, thereby enabling convex Model Predictive Control.

## Results

- The CCK-MPC controller enabled a rimless wheel to discover emergent rocking motions for locomotion and consistently achieved continuous forward rolling, significantly outperforming local linearization and Dynamic Mode Decomposition with Control (DMDc).
- For dynamic pushing, the system autonomously generated optimal paths and strategic behaviors (e.g., continuous pushing vs. intermittent shoving) and demonstrated robustness to significant modeling errors, such as variations in floor friction.
- The controller achieved real-time performance with a mean runtime of approximately 5.2ms (~190Hz) on consumer-grade hardware for the dynamic pushing task, demonstrating its practical applicability for reactive control.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Using causal, compliant physical models (e.g., viscoelastic contact) is fundamental for resolving causal conflicts and ensuring the continuity required for valid Koopman operator application to contact-rich systems.
> - The CCK framework provides a unified, globally linear representation of segmented dynamics, allowing MPC to proactively predict and optimize control strategies across multiple contact mode changes over a given time horizon.
> - Transforming non-convex contact problems into convex optimizations via Koopman linearization facilitates real-time computation and enables robots to autonomously discover complex, elaborate control strategies without explicit pre-programming.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Controlling robots that engage dynamically in contact with their environment, such as legged robots in locomotion or manipulators interacting with objects, presents a significant challenge in robotics. The primary difficulty stems from the switching nature of system dynamics at contact boundaries, which leads to non-convex optimization problems for predictive controllers. This non-convexity makes real-time control, particularly with Model Predictive Control (MPC), computationally intensive and difficult to implement on hardware.

Prior research has addressed contact-rich systems with MPC by employing various simplifications. These include using simplified dynamics (e.g., quasi-dynamic or quasi-static models, reduced dimensionality models), local linearization around a reference point or trajectory, or pre-defining contact mode sequences. While these approaches have yielded successful results under specific conditions, they impose limitations. Simplified dynamics restrict the exploration of full dynamic behaviors and can lead to infeasible or suboptimal motions. Local linearization is limited to the vicinity of the linearization point and fails to accurately represent dynamics across contact mode boundaries, thus hindering the discovery of contact-rich trajectories. Pre-defined contact sequences necessitate offline planning and can result in suboptimal performance when unexpected contact changes occur.

The work presented here aims to address these limitations by leveraging Koopman operator theory. Koopman operators have the potential to provide globally linear dynamic models in an embedding space, which can unify segmented dynamics. This global linearity can transform non-convex MPC problems into convex ones, facilitating real-time computation and enabling the controller to predict trajectories across multiple contact switches. Previous applications of Koopman operators in robotics have focused on active learning and modeling of complex, nonlinear, or distributed systems like soft robots. However, two fundamental challenges in applying standard Koopman theory to control systems with contact are: 1) its original applicability is limited to autonomous systems without control inputs, and 2) its reliance on continuous dynamics, which is violated by common hybrid models of contact involving discontinuities. This research seeks to overcome these challenges by extending Koopman theory to non-autonomous systems and by proposing a causal, continuous contact modeling approach.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research is to enable robust and elaborate control for robots operating in environments with dynamic contact. The existing challenges with contact dynamics, particularly the non-convexity they introduce in predictive control formulations, restrict the sophistication and real-time applicability of robotic controllers. The authors observe that current MPC approaches for contact-rich tasks rely on restrictive assumptions that limit the robot's ability to explore dynamic behaviors, adapt to changing conditions, or discover optimal, multi-contact strategies.

The key objectives of this work are:

1. **Develop a unified, globally-linear model for segmented contact dynamics:** The central objective is to apply Koopman operators to transform the piecewise-nonlinear dynamics arising from contact changes into a single, linear model in a higher-dimensional embedding space. This aims to overcome the computational difficulties associated with non-convex optimization problems in contact-rich MPC.
2. **Validate Koopman applicability to control inputs without approximation:** The authors intend to demonstrate that for a specific class of non-autonomous systems relevant to robotics, Koopman operators can be applied to control inputs without approximation. This involves extending the Control-Coherent Koopman (CCK) modeling framework.
3. **Address discontinuities through causal physical modeling:** A crucial objective is to establish that viscoelastic contact at robot-environment interactions underpins the valid application of Koopman operators by creating causal, continuous dynamic models that avoid the discontinuities inherent in traditional hybrid contact models.
4. **Enable convex Model Predictive Control (MPC) for contact-rich tasks:** By achieving a globally-linear model, the research aims to facilitate the use of linear, convex MPC, leading to faster, real-time control computation without local minima.
5. **Demonstrate discovery of elaborate control strategies:** The methodology is intended to allow robots to autonomously discover complex and effective control strategies that involve multiple contact changes over a time horizon, without requiring pre-defined contact sequences or extensive guidance. This is demonstrated through applications in legged locomotion and manipulation.
6. **Extend and improve the original CCK formulation:** The work aims to refine the CCK framework, initially developed for systems with powertrain compliance, to specifically address contact-rich locomotion and manipulation processes.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology, termed Control-Coherent Koopman Model Predictive Control (CCK-MPC), integrates Koopman operator theory with MPC to control switched nonlinear robot systems. The approach consists of several stages: analysis of segmented dynamics, construction of Koopman-compatible models, and application of linear MPC.

#### 3.1. Handling Discontinuities and Causality

A foundational aspect of the methodology is resolving the issue of discontinuities in contact dynamics. Traditional hybrid models often treat ground collisions as discrete impulses, leading to discontinuous changes in momenta and rendering standard Koopman theory inapplicable. The authors adopt an alternative by employing *causal physical models with contact compliance*.
- Instead of rigid, instantaneous contact, viscoelastic models are used for robot-environment interactions (e.g., floor compliance for legged robots, compliant elements between manipulator and object). This ensures continuity of momentum and state transitions, making the dynamics suitable for Koopman operator application.
- Crucially, this compliant modeling resolves "causal conflicts" where independent state variables might simultaneously dictate a position (e.g., in rigid contact, the hub position and spoke length become dependent). By introducing compliance, both variables remain independent, satisfying a key requirement for the CCK formulation (Modeling Property 2: independence of actuator and plant state variables).

#### 3.2. Control-Coherent Koopman (CCK) Modeling for Non-Autonomous Systems

The original Koopman theory applies to autonomous systems without control inputs. The CCK framework extends this to non-autonomous systems (`x_{t+1} = f(x_t, u_t)`), aiming to produce a globally linear, Linear Time-Invariant (LTI) model in a lifted state space: `z_{t+1} = Az_t + Bu_t`.
- **System Structure Requirements:** The class of systems compatible with CCK must satisfy two properties:
 1. **Modeling Property 1:** The control input (`u_t`) appears linearly in the actuator dynamics.
 2. **Modeling Property 2:** Actuator state variables (`p_t`) and plant state variables (`q_t`) are independent.
- **CCK Formulation:**
 1. The system state `x_t` is partitioned into actuator states `p_t` and plant states `q_t`. The actuator dynamics are `p_{t+1} = f_p(p_t, q_t) + B_p u_t`, and plant dynamics are `q_{t+1} = f_q(p_t, q_t)`.
 2. An associated autonomous system (with `u_t = 0`) is considered, for which a Koopman operator `A` exists, operating on the lifted state `z_t = g(x_t)`.
 3. Two conditions must be met for this Koopman model: (a) the operator `A` is valid for all relevant `q_t` and `p_t` (including those driven by `u_t`), and (b) the lifted state `z` explicitly includes the actuator state `p`.
 4. By placing `p_t` as the initial components of `z_t = [p_t; g_t]`, the linear control term `B_p u_t` can be directly incorporated into the `p_t` evolution in the lifted space.
 5. An "embeddings compensation" term (`B_g u_t`) is derived to account for how the plant dynamics `g_t` are affected by the full `p_t+1` (which includes `B_p u_t`).
 6. The result is a combined LTI system `[p_{t+1}; g_{t+1}] = A * [p_t; g_t] + [B_p; B_g] * u_t`, where `A` and `B = [B_p; B_g]` are constant matrices.
- **Model Identification:** The system matrices `A` and `B_g` (and `B_p`) can be obtained either through analytical derivation (for `B_p`) and subsequent calculation of `A` and `B_g`, or through a data-driven approach by minimizing the squared error between predicted and observed lifted states and control inputs from simulated data spanning multiple segmented dynamics.

#### 3.3. Model Predictive Control (MPC) in Lifted Space

With the globally linear CCK model, a standard linear MPC problem is formulated:
- Minimize a cost function (`z_t^T Q z_t + u_t^T R u_t`) over a prediction horizon `N`.
- Subject to the linear system dynamics (`z_{t+1} = Az_t + Bu_t`) and constraints on lifted states and control inputs.
- The problem becomes a convex optimization, enabling fast, real-time computation and avoiding local minima. The lack of controllability in the lifted space does not invalidate its use for MPC.

#### 3.4. Case Studies

The methodology is applied to two distinct robotics problems:

- **Actuated Rimless Wheel (Locomotion):**
 - A model of a six-spoke rimless wheel on a *viscoelastic floor* is used.
 - Generalized coordinates include hub position/orientation (`x, y, θ`) and individual spoke actuator displacements (`ψ_i`).
 - The viscoelastic contact model includes normal and smoothed Coulomb friction forces.
 - Actuator dynamics (DC motors with lead screws) ensure the linear appearance of control inputs (`u_i`, armature currents). Floor compliance ensures independence of actuator states.
 - Data for constructing the Koopman model (matrix `A`) is generated from simulations across all contact conditions (no-contact, one-spoke contact, two-spoke contact). Radial Basis Functions (RBFs) are used as observable functions for lifting.

- **Dynamic Pushing and Shoving (Manipulation):**
 - A planar system involving a sliding block (slider) and a controllable robot finger (pusher) is modeled.
 - Slider dynamics include mass, inertia, viscous floor friction, and a *Koopman-compatible soft contact model* between the slider and pusher.
 - Pusher dynamics assume high-gain velocity control, where desired velocity commands (`u_x, u_y`) are inputs and appear linearly.
 - A viscoelastic element between the pusher and slider resolves causal conflicts, satisfying CCK requirements.
 - A Deep Koopman Network (DKN) is employed to learn the embedding observable functions `g` for lifting the state. The original state vector is explicitly included in the lifted representation.
 - MPC tracks a reference trajectory for the slider, with cost functional weights (`Q`, `R`) tunable to encourage different behaviors (steady pushing vs. dynamic shoving).

#### 3.5. Implementation

- MPC problems are solved using Gurobi Optimizer, with code implemented in Python.
- For the planar pushing task, runtimes are measured to demonstrate real-time applicability.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several findings related to the development and application of the CCK-MPC framework for contact-rich robot tasks:

#### 4.1. Unified, Globally-Linear Model and Emergent Strategies

- The Koopman-compatible physical models successfully subsume the segmented dynamics (e.g., no-contact, one-contact, two-contact states for the rimless wheel) into a single, linear equation in the embedding space. This eliminates the need for explicit contact state detection.
- The CCK-MPC controller demonstrates the ability to *discover elaborate control strategies in real-time* without explicit guidance. For the rimless wheel, starting from an unfavorable static position, the controller independently discovers a rocking motion to generate momentum and escape the stance posture (Supplementary Movie 1).
- In dynamic pushing, the controller determines when and where the pusher should contact the slider, generating optimal paths for repositioning (Fig. 6c). It can also generate distinct behaviors—continuous pushing or intermittent shoving—simply by adjusting MPC cost weights (Fig. 5c).

#### 4.2. Proactive and Predictive Control

- The global linearization allows CCK-MPC to predict state evolution across multiple contact mode changes over a given time horizon. This enables proactive control actions. For instance, the rimless wheel controller proactively retracts the front-most spoke and extends the back spoke to create a ramp-like effect, facilitating continuous forward rolling (Fig. 3a, 3c). This requires a sufficiently long prediction horizon.
- The predicted contact mode changes accurately match the nonlinear simulation over a 0.2-second horizon (Fig. 3a).

#### 4.3. Performance Comparison and Robustness

- **Superiority over Local Linearization and DMDc:** CCK-MPC consistently achieves continuous forward rolling for the rimless wheel, outperforming linear-MPC based on local linearization (LL) and Dynamic Mode Decomposition with Control (DMDc). LL failed to anticipate contact mode transitions and could not maintain momentum, while DMDc exhibited prediction inaccuracies due to its approximated control matrix (Fig. 4). CCK models achieved an average displacement greater than 15m, compared to negligible displacements for LL and DMDc.
- **Effectiveness of Embedding Compensation:** The inclusion of the embedding compensation term ("CCK w/ C") in the control matrix `B` resulted in approximately 5% less control effort and overall improved performance in terms of distance traveled for the rimless wheel, compared to not using it.
- **Robustness to Modeling Errors:** For the dynamic pushing task, CCK-MPC demonstrated robustness by reasonably tracking a goal trajectory even when faced with significant modeling errors in floor friction (e.g., lower true friction than modeled), which led to intermittent contact ("dribbling") (Fig. 6d).

#### 4.4. Real-time Implementation

- The CCK-MPC controller for dynamic pushing was implemented on a physical robot system and showed real-time performance. Mean runtimes for the control loop were approximately 5.2ms (corresponding to ~190Hz) on consumer-grade hardware (Intel i7-11800H), indicating its suitability for reactive, real-time operation (Fig. 7).

#### 4.5. Limitations of Alternative Koopman Approaches

- While a bilinear Koopman MPC approach could achieve high prediction accuracy, it was found to be significantly slower (more than 10 times) and non-convex, making it impractical for real-time reactive control compared to the proposed CCK-MPC.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The work presented in this paper offers several significant contributions and potential impacts across robotics and broader fields dealing with complex, switched dynamics:

**Paradigm Shift in Control of Contact-Rich Systems:** The most significant contribution is the establishment of a robust methodology for globally linearizing segmented contact dynamics, enabling convex MPC for systems previously requiring non-convex optimization. This fundamentally changes how robots can be controlled in contact-rich environments. It moves beyond the limitations of local linearization, pre-defined contact sequences, and simplified dynamics, allowing for a more comprehensive and flexible approach to control.

**Enhanced Autonomy and Strategic Decision-Making:** By providing a global model that can predict across multiple contact mode changes, CCK-MPC empowers robots to autonomously discover and execute elaborate control strategies in real-time. This includes emergent behaviors like rocking to gain momentum in locomotion, and strategic decision-making in manipulation, such as choosing between continuous pushing and intermittent shoving, or repositioning the effector without prior explicit guidance. This capability has the potential to enhance robot autonomy and adaptability in unstructured environments.

**Practical Real-Time Control:** The transformation of non-convex problems into convex ones through Koopman linearization facilitates fast, real-time computation. The demonstrated execution speeds of the CCK-MPC controller on commodity hardware (mean runtime ~5.2ms) suggest its viability for practical, reactive control applications in both research and industrial settings. This addresses a critical bottleneck in the deployment of advanced robotic systems.

**Importance of Causal and Compliant Modeling:** The research highlights the crucial role of modeling compliance (e.g., viscoelastic contact) at robot-environment interfaces. This compliant modeling is shown to be essential for resolving causal conflicts and satisfying the mathematical requirements for Koopman operator application (specifically, the independence of actuator states and continuity of dynamics). This suggests that "contact impedance can be treated as a design parameter," opening avenues for optimizing robot-environment interactions through mechanical design to enhance control performance.

**Bridging the Sim-to-Real Gap and Broader Applicability:** By explicitly including actuator dynamics in the CCK model, the generated control commands are directly compatible with actuator-level control, which can help mitigate simulation-to-reality transfer issues often encountered when actuator dynamics are neglected. Furthermore, the principles of CCK-MPC are not limited to robotics but are applicable to any system where governing dynamics are segmented, potentially impacting fields beyond robotics such as biomechanics, fluid dynamics, and complex electromechanical systems.

**Advancement in Non-Prehensile Manipulation:** For non-prehensile manipulation tasks like pushing, the methodology allows for fully dynamic scenarios, overcoming previous restrictions to quasi-static or quasi-dynamic processes. The ability to make and break contact freely, optimize task performance with a unified cost functional, and adapt to unforeseen disturbances represents a significant advancement in the dexterity and robustness of robot manipulation.
