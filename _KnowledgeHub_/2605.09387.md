# NEXUS: Continual Learning of Symbolic Constraints for Safe and Robust Embodied Planning

> [!summary] Summary
> NEXUS introduces a modular framework for embodied agents that continually learns and refines symbolic constraints, enabling safe and robust planning in physical environments. The framework attained a 75.25% success rate for safe tasks and an 89.30% refusal rate for unsafe tasks on the AI2-THOR simulator, demonstrating enhanced safety and resilience against adversarial instructions.

## Problem

- LLM-based embodied agents face challenges in providing deterministic, verifiable safety and robustness in physical environments due to probabilistic reasoning, misinterpretation, and vulnerability to adversarial prompts.
- Neuro-symbolic designs typically rely on static expert-defined symbolic rules, which limits their adaptability and ability to generalize to novel tasks or dynamic environmental conditions.
- Existing safety mechanisms often lack persistence in retaining hazard knowledge, leading to repeated computations, and provide probabilistic risk scores instead of formally verifiable safety guarantees.

## Method

- **NEXUS**, a modular framework, integrates Large Language Models with ==formal methods== (==PDDL== for capabilities, ==LTL== for safety) to enable ==continual learning== for safe and robust embodied planning.
- The system facilitates the ==dynamic evolution== of both Execution Knowledge (PDDL domains) and Safety Specifications (LTL constraints) through feedback from physical execution and formalized risk assessment.
- It explicitly separates the learning of an agent's ==physical capabilities (feasibility)== from the accumulation of ==safety specifications==, allowing for distinct optimization and refinement processes.

## Results

- NEXUS (Evolved) achieved a **75.25%** success rate on safe tasks and an **89.30%** refusal rate on unsafe tasks within the AI2-THOR simulator, outperforming all tested baselines.
- The framework exhibited robust defense against jailbreak attacks, showing the lowest safety violation rate (**0.67%**) and highest refusal rate (**72.24%**) among evaluated methods.
- Continual learning led to substantial improvements: safe task success rate increased by **+20.07pp**, unsafe task rejection improved by **+10.37pp**, and execution time for safe tasks reduced by **44%**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Symbolic artifacts, specifically PDDL domains and LTL constraints, can function as dynamic carriers for knowledge evolution, enabling embodied agents to continually refine their capabilities and safety understanding.
> - Explicitly decoupling the learning of physical feasibility from safety specifications allows for distinct, yet synergistic, optimization of task performance and safety adherence.
> - Translating probabilistic semantic hazards identified by LLMs into verifiable LTL constraints provides a robust pre-action defense system, enhancing safety even against adversarial inputs like jailbreak attacks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The integration of Large Language Models (LLMs) has advanced embodied intelligence, particularly in areas involving natural language (NL) driven task execution. LLMs have demonstrated capabilities in unifying perception, reasoning, and control, leading to the development of end-to-end robotic systems. However, a limitation exists in deploying these probabilistic LLM-based controllers in physical environments that require deterministic, verifiable safety and robustness. Challenges include misinterpretation of instructions, hallucination of actions or object affordances, incomplete environmental perception, and vulnerability to adversarial or jailbreaking prompts.

Current research paradigms to address these limitations generally fall into two categories. The first is LLM-centric planning, which enhances reasoning through dynamic context handling but retains inherent probabilistic uncertainty and lacks formal guarantees. The second is neuro-symbolic design, which integrates LLM reasoning with heuristic search planners for logical consistency. However, these approaches often treat symbolic artifacts, such as Planning Domain Definition Language (PDDL) rules, as static expert knowledge, limiting their adaptability and generalization.

A crucial bottleneck across both paradigms is safety assurance. While some work focuses on proactive risk prediction, existing methods may not retain safety knowledge within the agent across executions, leading to repeated computation for hazard detection. Furthermore, probabilistic risk scores lack the verifiable certainty provided by formal logic, which is essential for safety-critical applications.

NEXUS addresses these limitations by proposing a modular framework for continual learning that reconsiders the role of symbolic artifacts. Instead of static interfaces, NEXUS leverages symbolic representations for grounding knowledge and enabling its evolution. It aims to bridge the gap between LLM uncertainty and the requirement for verifiable safety by decoupling physical feasibility from safety specifications, allowing both capabilities and safety constraints to evolve dynamically through execution feedback and formalized risk assessment. This positions NEXUS within the neuro-symbolic planning and safe AI communities, contributing a framework for adaptive, robust, and formally verifiable embodied agents that can learn and refine their operational knowledge over time.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an embodied agent capable of safe and robust planning in physical environments by continually learning and refining symbolic constraints. This objective is motivated by several identified limitations of current LLM-based embodied intelligence systems:

1. **Bridging the Probabilistic-Deterministic Gap**: LLMs operate on probabilistic reasoning, which can lead to uncertain and inconsistent outputs. Physical world deployment, particularly in robotics, demands deterministic actions and verifiable safety guarantees. The work aims to mitigate this discrepancy by grounding probabilistic risk assessments into explicit, deterministic constraints.
2. **Dynamic Knowledge Evolution**: Existing neuro-symbolic approaches often rely on static, handcrafted symbolic rules. This limits their ability to adapt to new tasks, environments, or unexpected situations. A key motivation is to enable symbolic artifacts to serve as a medium for "knowledge evolution," allowing agents to refine their understanding of physical capabilities and safety requirements over time.
3. **Decoupling Feasibility and Safety**: The framework aims to explicitly separate the learning of an agent's physical capabilities (feasibility) from the accumulation of safety specifications. This modular approach allows for distinct optimization objectives: improving action schemas based on execution feedback to enhance task completion, and transforming probabilistic risk assessments into hard constraints for safety.
4. **Establishing a Pre-Action Defense System**: Rather than solely relying on post-hoc error correction or probabilistic risk scores, the research seeks to implement a proactive safety mechanism. This involves grounding implicit semantic hazards, identified by LLMs, into verifiable symbolic rules (e.g., Linear Temporal Logic - LTL constraints) that can enforce safety boundaries prior to action execution. This defense system is intended to operate robustly even against adversarial inputs.
5. **Addressing Computational Overhead and Knowledge Retention**: Prior safety mechanisms might incur repetitive computational costs for re-detecting hazards across tasks. A motivation is to enable agents to retain hazard-related knowledge, thereby reducing redundant runtime reasoning and accelerating future planning, leading to improved efficiency through knowledge accumulation.

In essence, the paper seeks to enable embodied agents to achieve higher task success rates while effectively refusing unsafe instructions, exhibiting robustness against adversarial attacks, and progressively improving planning efficiency by continually learning and updating their operational knowledge and safety rules in a formalized, verifiable manner.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

NEXUS (Neural-symbolic Execution with eXplicit safety and continUous learning Systems) is presented as a modular framework for continual learning in embodied agents. It integrates LLMs with formal methods to enforce safe and feasible embodied planning through four synergistic workflows: Scene Modeling, Task Decomposition and Formalized Planning, Pre-action Checking and Risk Learning, and Execution and Execution Learning. The core idea involves the continual evolution of two types of knowledge: Execution Knowledge (represented by PDDL domains) and Safety Specifications (represented by LTL constraints).

#### 3.1. Symbolic Knowledge Formulation

The agent maintains a knowledge base K = (D, Φ), where D (PDDL domain) defines the abstract action space (preconditions, effects) and Φ (LTL constraints) encodes safety specifications. The goal is to optimize K to maximize expected reward, which includes both successful task completion and correct refusal of unsafe goals. PDDL predicates and LTL formulas handle logical and temporal constraints. Numerical and probabilistic constraints are managed via semantic abstraction, discretizing numerical boundaries into boolean predicates and resolving probabilistic risks via equivalence voting.

#### 3.2. Scene Modeling

This module translates raw environmental observations into a symbolic scene graph (S_graph), which serves as the PDDL problem's initial state (:init) and contextualizes LLM-based modules. Scene information is obtained directly from a simulator (o_t = s_t) and parsed into a directed graph of objects and spatial relations. A perception-reasoning loop ensures the scene state is refreshed after each action, aligning the agent's internal belief state with the evolving physical world.

#### 3.3. Task Decomposition and Formalized Planning

Given the scene graph and a natural language (NL) task, planning proceeds in two stages:

- **Task Decomposition**: An LLM translates the NL instruction into a structured sequence of sub-goals (⟨g1..., gn⟩). This allows the planner to address temporally ordered objectives.
- **Formalized Planning**: A forward state-space search algorithm, utilizing PDDL for state transitions and LTL for safety control, generates action sequences. The search space is pruned online using LTL progression. If progressing an LTL formula results in 'False', the corresponding state and trajectory are immediately discarded. If no valid solution is found due to LTL-based pruning (i.e., safety violation), the sub-task is explicitly classified as unsafe, leading to task refusal. The system also integrates a closed-loop replanning mechanism, where execution failures trigger perception module updates and replanning from the latest environmental state for remaining sub-goals.

#### 3.4. Pre-action Checking and Risk Learning

This workflow ensures safety and updates the safety knowledge:

- **Pre-action Safety Checking**: Before executing each planned action, an LLM-based Safety Checker assesses its risk level (safe, potentially risky, or dangerous) against the current environment. Potentially risky actions undergo intent-aware verification with the original NL instruction. Safe actions proceed to the actuator. Dangerous actions trigger the risk learning mechanism.
- **Risk Learning**: The safety specification Φ evolves through this process. Identified risks are translated into formal LTL constraints. A dual-layer sampling mechanism is employed within the LTL Generator:
 - **Semantic Sampling**: Diverse NL statements describing the identified risk are generated to capture its semantics.
 - **Translation Sampling**: Each NL description generates an independent group of candidate LTL formulas.
 - **Equivalence Voting**: Intra-group voting filters noise, and inter-group voting determines a global semantic consensus. The highest-supported formula becomes the final LTL constraint, realizing the probabilistic operator (ϕ_prob) by collapsing risk uncertainty into a hard constraint. This process ensures Φ is continually optimized to align with human safety norms.

#### 3.5. Execution and Execution Learning

This workflow involves executing actions and refining the agent's physical understanding:

- **Execution**: A low-level actuator translates safe symbolic actions into physical control inputs. The controller minimizes semantic discrepancy between the realized environment state and the action's intended effect, using the scene modeling capability to evaluate outcomes.
- **Execution Learning**: The Execution Knowledge D (PDDL action schemas) evolves based on physical execution feedback. If an action fails (e.g., 'put' fails because the container is closed), the PDDL generator augments the action schema with new preconditions (e.g., requiring the container to be 'open'). This refines D by mapping symbolic operators to valid low-level execution sequences, ensuring actions are physically feasible and reducing future execution failures.

The entire framework operates as a closed loop, where PDDL domains and LTL constraints are continually refined and reused across tasks, facilitating lifelong learning and improving efficiency and safety.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluates NEXUS on a customized dataset derived from SafeAgentBench, built upon the AI2-THOR simulator. The dataset includes 299 safe tasks and 299 unsafe tasks, with the latter further augmented with jailbreak attacks to test robustness. Performance is measured using Success Rate (SR), Reject Rate (RJ), and Time (average wall-clock time per task).

#### 4.1. Overall Performance (Table 2)

- **Task Success and Efficiency**: NEXUS (Evolved) achieved the highest SR on safe tasks (75.25%) and a low RJ (1.34%), outperforming all baselines, including those augmented with safety LLMs. Its execution time (16.19s) was significantly lower than other methods. The 0-shot version of NEXUS had a lower SR (55.18%) and higher time (28.89s), indicating the effectiveness of the knowledge evolution.
- **Safety Assurance**: On unsafe tasks, NEXUS (Evolved) demonstrated a high RJ (89.30%) and a low SR (1.67%), signifying its ability to detect and refuse unsafe instructions. This contrasts with baselines, which showed higher SRs (indicating more safety violations) even with safety LLM augmentation. While the initial 0-shot NEXUS incurred higher latency for risk learning, the Evolved version reduced this to 31.17s, maintaining strong safety.
- **Robustness Against Jailbreaks**: NEXUS (Evolved) maintained robust defense against jailbreak attacks, achieving the lowest SR (0.67%) and highest RJ (72.24%). Baselines, even with safety modules, showed increased violation rates under jailbreak attacks, suggesting successful bypass of their semantic alignment layers. This indicated that NEXUS's approach of grounding safety checks in the action plan, rather than solely on adversarial user instructions, provided resilience.

#### 4.2. LTL Translation Quality (Table 3)

- Evaluation of the NL-to-LTL translation module on 50 unsafe tasks showed that Greedy Decoding suffered from high syntax errors (19.65%) and limited semantic accuracy (61.42%).
- Both SELP and NEXUS eliminated syntax errors. NEXUS (N=3, K=5) achieved higher semantic accuracy (88.27%) and a higher reject rate (89.30%) compared to SELP (SA 82.48%, RJ 75.91%). Increasing sampling (N=5, K=7) further improved accuracy (90.11%) and RJ (90.30%) at the cost of increased time.

#### 4.3. Evolution Trajectory and Ablation Study (Tables 7 and 8)

- **Evolution Trajectory**: Over 40 synthetic tasks, NEXUS (Evolved) showed significant improvements: Safe SR increased by 20.07 percentage points (from 55.18% to 75.25%), Unsafe RJ improved by 10.37 percentage points (from 78.93% to 89.30%), and execution time for safe tasks reduced by 44% (from 28.89s to 16.19s).
- **Ablation Study**:
 - **PDDL-only evolution** improved safe task capability (Safe SR 69.57%) but degraded safety (Unsafe RJ 64.55%) and increased jailbreak vulnerability (Jailbreak SR 2.34%).
 - **LTL-only evolution** improved safety (Unsafe RJ 87.29%) but had minimal impact on safe task success (Safe SR 58.53%).
 - **Full NEXUS (Evolved)** achieved simultaneous improvements in both safe task success (75.25%) and safety (Unsafe RJ 89.30%, Jailbreak SR 0.67%), validating the architectural separation and combined evolution of execution knowledge (PDDL) and safety specifications (LTL).

The findings suggest that NEXUS provides a balance between task completion and safety assurance, with performance improving continually through its learning mechanisms and exhibiting robustness against adversarial inputs.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of NEXUS offers contributions to the field of embodied artificial intelligence, particularly in addressing the reliability and safety challenges associated with deploying LLM-based systems in physical environments.

One significant aspect is the demonstration that symbolic artifacts can function as dynamic carriers for knowledge evolution, rather than static components. By enabling the continual learning and refinement of PDDL domains for agent capabilities and LTL constraints for safety specifications, NEXUS moves towards more adaptive robotic systems. This approach allows agents to improve their understanding of physical interaction mechanics and accumulate robust safety knowledge over time, potentially reducing the need for extensive manual pre-programming of rules.

The framework's explicit decoupling of physical feasibility from safety specifications provides a structured approach to agent development. This modularity allows for distinct optimization processes, enhancing both task performance and safety adherence independently yet synergistically. The integration of LLMs for high-level reasoning with formal methods (PDDL and LTL) for verifiable constraint enforcement addresses a fundamental gap between the probabilistic nature of LLM outputs and the deterministic requirements of physical world operations.

The rigorous safety mechanism, which synergizes risk-aware LLM assessments with LTL automata for hard constraint enforcement, represents an advancement in proactive safety. By grounding implicit semantic hazards into verifiable symbolic rules, NEXUS establishes a pre-action defense system. This system contributes to preventing unsafe behaviors, demonstrating resilience even in the presence of adversarial instruction manipulation, such as jailbreak attacks. The experimental results, showing low safety violation rates and high refusal rates for unsafe tasks, underscore the potential for deploying more trustworthy robotic systems in sensitive environments.

The progressive improvement in planning efficiency observed through knowledge accumulation suggests that continual learning can lead to more efficient decision-making by amortizing the cost of initial constraint generation. This could reduce redundant trial-and-error processes and accelerate future task execution, which is relevant for lifelong learning in robotics.

Collectively, NEXUS contributes a methodological framework that supports the development of embodied agents capable of operating with enhanced safety, robustness, and adaptability, moving closer to the deployment of dependable autonomous systems in open-world settings.
