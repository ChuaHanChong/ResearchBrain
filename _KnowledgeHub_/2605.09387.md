# NEXUS: Continual Learning of Symbolic Constraints for Safe and Robust Embodied Planning

> [!summary] Summary
> NEXUS introduces a modular framework for embodied agents that continually learns and refines symbolic constraints, enabling safe and robust planning in physical environments. The framework attained a 75.25% success rate for safe tasks and an 89.30% refusal rate for unsafe tasks on the AI2-THOR simulator, demonstrating enhanced safety and resilience against adversarial instructions.

## Problem

- LLM-based embodied agents face challenges in providing deterministic, verifiable safety and robustness in physical environments due to probabilistic reasoning, misinterpretation, and vulnerability to adversarial prompts.
- Neuro-symbolic designs typically rely on static expert-defined symbolic rules, which limits their adaptability and ability to generalize to novel tasks or dynamic environmental conditions.
- Existing safety mechanisms often lack persistence in retaining hazard knowledge, leading to repeated computations, and provide probabilistic risk scores instead of formally verifiable safety guarantees.

## Method

- **NEXUS**, a modular framework, integrates Large Language Models with ==formal methods== (==PDDL== for capabilities, ==LTL== for safety) to enable ==continual learning== for safe and robust embodied planning.
- The system facilitates the ==dynamic evolution== of both Execution Knowledge (PDDL domains) and Safety Specifications (LTL constraints) through feedback from physical execution and formalized risk assessment.
- It explicitly separates the learning of an agent's ==physical capabilities (feasibility)== from the accumulation of ==safety specifications==, allowing for distinct optimization and refinement processes.

## Results

- NEXUS (Evolved) achieved a **75.25%** success rate on safe tasks and an **89.30%** refusal rate on unsafe tasks within the AI2-THOR simulator, outperforming all tested baselines.
- The framework exhibited robust defense against jailbreak attacks, showing the lowest safety violation rate (**0.67%**) and highest refusal rate (**72.24%**) among evaluated methods.
- Continual learning led to substantial improvements: safe task success rate increased by **+20.07pp**, unsafe task rejection improved by **+10.37pp**, and execution time for safe tasks reduced by **44%**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Symbolic artifacts, specifically PDDL domains and LTL constraints, can function as dynamic carriers for knowledge evolution, enabling embodied agents to continually refine their capabilities and safety understanding.
> - Explicitly decoupling the learning of physical feasibility from safety specifications allows for distinct, yet synergistic, optimization of task performance and safety adherence.
> - Translating probabilistic semantic hazards identified by LLMs into verifiable LTL constraints provides a robust pre-action defense system, enhancing safety even against adversarial inputs like jailbreak attacks.
