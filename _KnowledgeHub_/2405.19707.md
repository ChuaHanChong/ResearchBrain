# DeMamba: AI-Generated Video Detection on Million-Scale GenVideo Benchmark

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Ant Group, Nanjing University, and Shanghai Jiao Tong University developed GenVideo, the first million-scale dataset for detecting AI-generated videos, and introduced DeMamba, a plug-and-play module enhancing detector generalizability and robustness. The DeMamba module, integrated into existing feature extractors, improved Average Precision by 12.02% on unseen video generators and exhibited greater stability under various video degradations compared to baseline models.

## Problem

- The rapid advancement of generative AI leads to realistic videos, posing risks of misinformation.
- Existing AI-generated video detection datasets are limited in scale and diversity, often focusing on human face deepfakes, which hinders generalizable detector development.
- Current evaluation benchmarks lack comprehensive tests for detector robustness against unseen generative models and common real-world video degradations.

## Method

- Constructed **GenVideo**, a million-scale dataset comprising diverse real and AI-generated videos from a wide array of generative models (e.g., Text-to-Video, Image-to-Video, ==diffusion==/transformer-based).
- Designed two novel evaluation tasks: ==cross-generator video classification== to assess generalization to unseen models, and ==degraded video classification== to test robustness against real-world corruptions.
- Proposed **DeMamba**, a plug-and-play module utilizing a ==Mamba==-based architecture with a ==continuous scan strategy== to capture subtle spatio-temporal inconsistencies in generated content.

## Results

- The GenVideo dataset successfully provides over **2.2 million** video clips, demonstrating that increased training data diversity and scale lead to improved detector performance.
- The DeMamba module, integrated with XCLIP, achieved an average increase of **12.02%** in Average Precision (AP), **11.26%** in Recall, and **17.72%** in F1-score on cross-generator classification tasks over the baseline XCLIP-B-FT model.
- DeMamba-enhanced models exhibited superior robustness, showing more stable performance against various real-world degradations (e.g., H.264/JPEG compression, watermarks) compared to other detectors.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A large-scale and diverse dataset, like GenVideo, is critical for training detectors to identify intrinsic artifacts of generative processes rather than superficial content features, enabling better generalization.
> - Incorporating temporal modeling is crucial for AI-generated video detection, as video-level analysis significantly outperforms image-level methods in recognizing video authenticity.
> - The continuous scan strategy within the DeMamba module effectively leverages structured state space models to integrate spatial continuity with temporal dynamics, enhancing the capture of complex spatio-temporal relationships and inconsistencies.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of generative artificial intelligence has witnessed rapid advancements, particularly in the creation of realistic images and increasingly, videos. These developments have led to the emergence of models capable of generating high-quality visual content from simple inputs like text or images. While these generative capabilities offer benefits in reducing manual effort and enhancing creativity, they also introduce challenges related to the potential spread of misinformation and the creation of deceptive content. Instances such as AI-generated fake videos of public figures highlight the societal implications and the pressing need for reliable detection mechanisms.

Prior research has addressed the detection of AI-generated images and deepfake videos, with existing datasets and models focusing on these specific modalities. Deepfake video detection, for example, primarily concentrates on human face manipulation. However, the current landscape exhibits a gap concerning the detection of AI-generated videos across a broad spectrum of content, scenes, and generation methodologies beyond human faces. The development of detectors capable of identifying videos from diverse and unseen generative models, as well as maintaining performance under various forms of video degradation common in real-world dissemination, remains an area requiring further investigation and resources. This work positions itself by addressing these limitations, introducing a large-scale benchmark dataset and a detection module specifically designed for general AI-generated video detection to enhance both generalizability and robustness against evolving generation techniques and real-world conditions.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the rapid advancements in video generation techniques and the subsequent concerns regarding the potential for these models to facilitate the spread of fake information. As AI-generated videos become more sophisticated and widely accessible, there is an increasing demand for technologies capable of reliably distinguishing authentic videos from those produced by AI. However, the development of such robust detectors is hindered by specific limitations in the existing research landscape.

The key objectives of this work were established to address these identified challenges:

1. **Address the Data Gap**: A significant barrier to developing effective AI-generated video detectors is the absence of large-scale, diverse datasets derived from advanced video generators. Existing datasets often focus on specific modalities like deepfakes (human faces) or lack the volume and variety necessary to train detectors with strong generalization capabilities. This work aims to mitigate this gap by creating a novel, million-scale dataset of AI-generated videos.
2. **Develop a Comprehensive Benchmark Dataset**: The research sought to introduce "GenVideo," the first AI-generated video detection dataset characterized by:
 - **Large Volume**: Collecting over one million AI-generated and real videos to provide ample data for training and evaluating detectors. This scale is intended to prevent detectors from merely learning superficial content differences and instead focus on subtle authenticity cues.
 - **Rich Diversity**: Incorporating a broad spectrum of video categories, scene contents, and generation techniques (Text-to-Video, Image-to-Video, diffusion-based, transformer-based, and other architectures) to simulate real-world challenges and enhance the generalizability of trained models.
3. **Propose Tailored Evaluation Methods**: To accurately assess detector performance in scenarios resembling real-world conditions, the study aimed to introduce two specific evaluation tasks:
 - **Cross-Generator Video Classification**: This task evaluates a detector's generalizability by training it on videos from certain generators and testing its performance on videos produced by entirely unseen generators. This simulates the challenge of new generative models continuously emerging.
 - **Degraded Video Classification**: This task assesses a detector's robustness by evaluating its ability to maintain high performance when faced with videos that have undergone quality degradation, such as compression, resolution reduction, or the addition of noise/watermarks, common during video dissemination.
4. **Introduce an Enhanced Detection Module**: The paper also aimed to propose a novel, plug-and-play module, named "Detail Mamba (DeMamba)," designed to improve the performance of existing detectors. This module is motivated by the observation that AI-generated videos often exhibit subtle inconsistencies in both temporal and spatial dimensions, which existing image-based or standard video detection methods may not efficiently capture. DeMamba seeks to leverage structured state space models to analyze these spatio-temporal inconsistencies to discern video authenticity.

Through these objectives, the research endeavors to advance the field of AI-generated video detection by providing a foundational dataset and an effective methodological enhancement, ultimately contributing to the mitigation of harm from fake information.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed in this research addresses the limitations in AI-generated video detection through the introduction of a large-scale dataset, novel evaluation protocols, and a specialized detection module.

#### 3.1. GenVideo Dataset Construction

The **GenVideo** dataset was developed to provide a comprehensive benchmark for AI-generated video detection, focusing on scale, diversity, generalizability, and robustness.

- **Scale and Organization**: GenVideo is a hierarchically organized dataset comprising 2,294,594 training video clips and 19,588 testing clips. The training set includes 1,213,511 real videos and 1,048,575 fake videos, while the test set has 10,000 real and 8,588 fake videos. This large scale is intended to train detectors to identify subtle authenticity cues rather than merely learning content-specific differences.
- **Diversity of Content and Generators**:
 - **Real Videos**: Sourced from existing video datasets such as Kinetics-400, Youku-mPLUG (for training), and MSR-VTT (for testing). These datasets cover a range of human actions and video descriptions.
 - **Fake Videos**: Collected using a multi-pronged approach to ensure maximal diversity in content and generation methods:
 - **In-house Generation Pipeline**: Videos were synthesized using popular Text-to-Video (T2V) and Image-to-Video (I2V) models. This included diffusion-based models (e.g., ZeroScope, I2VGen-XL, SVD, VideoCrafter, DynamiCrafter, SD, SEINE) and transformer-based methods (e.g., Latte, OpenSora). A rich prompt dictionary was constructed by expanding common foreground and background keywords using a large language model to produce semantically diverse content.
 - **External Web Scraping and Existing Benchmarks**: Videos were also collected from open-source websites, services like Pika, and existing video evaluation benchmarks (e.g., Evalcrafter). The testing set specifically included videos from a wider array of advanced and prominent generators (e.g., Sora, MorphStudio, MoonValley, HotShot, Show\_1, Gen2, Crafter, Lavie, and a scraped collection termed "WildScrape") to ensure robust evaluation against unseen models.
- **Video Characteristics**: The collected videos exhibit diverse scene contents (landscapes, people, buildings, objects), motion variations, durations (primarily 2-6 seconds for fake videos), and varying aspect ratios/resolutions. Frame sampling involved extracting 2 frames per second for videos longer than 3 seconds, or a calculated frequency to obtain 8 frames for shorter videos. Data augmentation techniques (e.g., horizontal flip, image compression, Gaussian noise, blur, grayscale) were applied during training to enhance model generalizability.

#### 3.2. Evaluation Methods

Two evaluation tasks were designed to simulate real-world detection challenges:

1. **Cross-Generator Video Classification**:
 - **Many-to-Many Generalization**: Detectors are trained on fake videos from 10 baseline generative methods and tested on an out-of-domain test set (`D_v-ood`) which contains videos from different, unseen generators.
 - **One-to-Many Generalization**: Detectors are trained on fake videos from a single baseline generator (e.g., Pika, SEINE, OpenSora) and then tested on the `D_v-ood` set. This evaluates the detector's ability to generalize from limited training examples.
2. **Degraded Video Classification**: This task assesses the detector's robustness by evaluating its performance on videos from the `D_v-ood` set that have been subjected to various forms of degradation, including H.264 compression, JPEG compression, flipping, cropping, text watermarks, image watermarks, Gaussian noise, and color transformations.

Evaluation metrics included accuracy (ACC) and average precision (AP). For image-based detectors, frame-level predictions were aggregated to derive video-level predictions.

#### 3.3. DeMamba Module

The **Detail Mamba (DeMamba)** module was introduced as a plug-and-play component to enhance existing feature extractors, aiming to identify AI-generated videos by analyzing inconsistencies in temporal and spatial dimensions.

- **Motivation**: Generated videos often contain both spatial artifacts (errors in local appearance) and temporal inconsistencies (frequency or motion irregularities). Existing image detection methods cannot model temporal inconsistencies, while many video detection methods may not efficiently capture local spatial anomalies. DeMamba is designed to capture both.
- **Architecture**:
 1. **Feature Encoder**: State-of-the-art vision encoders (e.g., CLIP, XCLIP) are used to extract spatio-temporal features from input video frames.
 2. **Spatial Consolidation and Grouping**: The extracted features are split into `s x s` spatial zones.
 3. **DeMamba Block with Continuous Scan**: The core of DeMamba leverages a Structured State Space Sequence model (S4) variant, Mamba (specifically, its S6 framework), adapted for 3D spatial-temporal input. Instead of a "sweep-scan" mechanism that processes tokens sequentially in a potentially disconnected manner, DeMamba proposes a "continuous scan" strategy. This method organizes spatial tokens within each segmented region based on proximity and then aligns them sequentially across successive frames. This approach aims to maintain spatial continuity and coherently integrate spatial and temporal dynamics to better capture complex relationships and inconsistencies.
 4. **Classification Head**: After processing by the DeMamba block, both global features (average of pre-DeMamba input features) and local pooled features (average of post-DeMamba processed regions) are concatenated. This aggregated representation is then fed into a simple Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) for binary classification (real/fake).
- **Training**: The model is trained using binary cross-entropy loss.

Experiments were conducted on a system with 8 Tesla A-100 80G GPUs.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded several findings regarding the proposed GenVideo dataset, evaluation methods, and the DeMamba module.

#### 4.1. GenVideo Dataset Utility

The GenVideo dataset was constructed as intended, providing a million-scale collection of diverse AI-generated and real videos. Its large volume and variety of content and generators serve as a foundation for developing and evaluating detection models. Experiments demonstrated that increasing both the number of training samples per generator and the number of distinct generators in the training set led to improved detection performance, affirming the necessity of a large and diverse dataset for generalizable detection capabilities.

#### 4.2. Cross-Generator Generalization Performance

**Many-to-Many Generalization Task (Table 3):**
- Video-level detection models generally exhibited higher recognition accuracy compared to image-level models, underscoring the importance of temporal modeling for video authenticity detection.
- The integration of the DeMamba module into existing models resulted in performance improvements. For instance, DeMamba-XCLIP-FT achieved average Recall (R), F1-score (F1), and Average Precision (AP) of 0.9392, 0.9020, and 0.9710, respectively. This represented an improvement of 11.26% in Recall, 17.72% in F1-score, and 12.02% in AP over the baseline XCLIP-B-FT model.
- Similar improvements were observed when DeMamba was integrated with CLIP (DeMamba-CLIP-B-FT), showing 1.01% increase in R, 12.88% in F1, and 19.30% in AP over CLIP-B-FT.

**One-to-Many Generalization Task (Table 4):**
- DeMamba-XCLIP-FT demonstrated higher generalization performance across the three tested one-to-many scenarios (training on Pika, SEINE, or OpenSora and testing on the full `D_v-ood` set).
- For example, when trained on Pika, DeMamba-XCLIP-FT showed an average improvement of 14.76% in R, 6.84% in F1, and 3.35% in AP compared to XCLIP-B-FT. Similar trends were observed for SEINE and OpenSora training subsets, indicating DeMamba's effectiveness in learning spatial-temporal inconsistencies for out-of-domain generalization.

#### 4.3. Degraded Video Classification (Robustness)

**Robustness to Perturbations (Table 5):**
- DeMamba-XCLIP-FT maintained strong performance when evaluated on videos subjected to various real-world-like degradations (H.264 compression, JPEG compression, flip, crop, text watermark, image watermark, Gaussian noise, color transform).
- Across different degradation types, the performance drop (measured in R, F1, and AP) for DeMamba-XCLIP-FT was generally less pronounced or more stable compared to other baseline models. This indicated a higher level of robustness of the DeMamba-enhanced model in handling corrupted video data. For instance, under JPEG compression, while other models experienced significant drops, DeMamba-XCLIP-FT showed a relatively controlled decrease (e.g., -0.0116 in R compared to -0.3064 for XCLIP-B-FT).

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies

**Effectiveness of DeMamba (Table 6):**
- Removing the DeMamba module ("w/o DeMamba") from the DeMamba-XCLIP-FT model significantly reduced performance (e.g., a drop of 9.71% in R, 13.58% in F1, and 10.43% in AP), confirming its contribution to generalization.
- The aggregation of both global and local features also proved beneficial; removing global features ("w/o global") led to a slight decrease in performance compared to the full DeMamba-XCLIP-FT model.

**Influence of Zone Size (Table 7):**
- Experiments with different zone sizes (1x1, 2x2, 7x7, 14x14) for spatial consolidation revealed that a zone size of 2x2 yielded the best overall performance for both DeMamba-CLIP-FT and DeMamba-XCLIP-FT. This suggests that while smaller zones allow focusing on local details, an excessively small zone size might lead to a loss of broader spatial contextual information.

**Influence of Scanning Order (Table 8):**
- The proposed continuous scan strategy within the DeMamba block demonstrated improved performance compared to a traditional "sweep-scan" method. For DeMamba-XCLIP-FT, the continuous scan achieved higher average R, F1, and AP (0.9392/0.9020/0.9710) than the sweep-scan (0.8924/0.8295/0.9347), indicating its effectiveness in capturing spatial-temporal relationships.

#### 4.5. Training and Inference Efficiency

(Table 9):
- Integrating DeMamba into XCLIP increased training time (e.g., from 11.5h for XCLIP-FT to 15.1h for DeMamba-XCLIP-FT) which is attributed to Mamba's training characteristics.
- However, the inference time and Queries Per Second (QPS) remained competitive. DeMamba-XCLIP-FT maintained a QPS of 2.53, comparable to XCLIP-FT (2.55) and outperforming several other video models like F3Net, STIL, and VideoMAE.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of AI-generated video detection through several key advancements and has notable potential impacts.

The primary significance lies in the introduction of **GenVideo**, the first million-scale AI-generated video detection dataset. This dataset addresses a critical data scarcity issue by providing a vast and diverse collection of both real and AI-generated videos, encompassing a wide array of generation models, content types, and scenes. By doing so, GenVideo establishes a new, comprehensive benchmark essential for the development and rigorous evaluation of future detection models. Its scale and diversity are designed to push detectors beyond superficial content recognition towards identifying subtle, inherent artifacts of generative processes, thereby fostering the creation of more robust and generalizable solutions.

Furthermore, the paper proposes two evaluation methodologies: **cross-generator video classification** and **degraded video classification**. These tasks simulate real-world scenarios where detectors must cope with continuously evolving generative AI technology (unseen generators) and the quality degradation that videos undergo during dissemination (e.g., compression, watermarks). These evaluation protocols provide a more realistic and stringent framework for assessing detector performance, moving beyond evaluations solely based on known or pristine generated content.

The introduction of **Detail Mamba (DeMamba)** as a plug-and-play module represents a methodological advancement. By specifically targeting the modeling of inconsistencies in both temporal and spatial dimensions—which are often subtle hallmarks of AI-generated content—DeMamba enhances the generalizability and robustness of existing feature extractors. Its architectural design, particularly the continuous scan strategy, offers a refined approach to capturing complex spatio-temporal relationships, as evidenced by the performance improvements over baseline models across various tasks and robustness tests.

The potential impact of this work is multifaceted:
- **Mitigating Misinformation**: By enabling the development of more effective detectors for AI-generated videos, this research directly contributes to efforts aimed at combating the spread of fake news and misleading information on social media and other platforms. This is crucial for maintaining public trust and ensuring the integrity of digital content.
- **Content Authentication**: The advancements in detection technology can aid in establishing mechanisms for verifying the authenticity of video content, which has implications for journalism, legal proceedings, and various industries reliant on visual evidence.
- **Ethical AI Development**: The presence of robust detection capabilities can act as a counterbalance to the rapid progress in generative AI, encouraging responsible innovation and ethical use of these powerful technologies.
- **Future Research Inspiration**: The GenVideo dataset and DeMamba module serve as open-source resources, expected to stimulate further research into more lightweight, efficient, and even more generalized detection models, as well as the exploration of novel artifacts in generated content.

However, the authors acknowledge a limitation: the suboptimal training efficiency of the DeMamba module, which is a common characteristic of Mamba-based models. This indicates an area for future research focused on optimizing training processes for such architectures. Despite this, the work provides foundational tools and insights to advance the field of AI-generated video detection, ultimately aiming to enhance information clarity and protect against the malicious use of generative AI.
