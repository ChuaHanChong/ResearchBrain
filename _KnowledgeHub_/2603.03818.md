# Pretrained Vision-Language-Action Models are Surprisingly Resistant to Forgetting in Continual Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Large pretrained Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models show surprising resistance to catastrophic forgetting in continual robot learning, maintaining performance on past tasks with minimal Experience Replay. The research from The University of Texas at Austin and Microsoft demonstrates that pretraining fundamentally alters continual learning dynamics, enabling rapid recovery of seemingly forgotten knowledge.

## Problem

- Robots must continually acquire new skills without catastrophically forgetting previously learned abilities, a challenge exacerbated by the stability-plasticity dilemma.
- Traditional continual learning approaches for smaller, non-pretrained policy models have relied on complex algorithms (e.g., large replay buffers, regularization, architectural isolation) to mitigate forgetting.
- The behavior of large-scale pretrained Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models regarding catastrophic forgetting was largely unexplored, especially in robot policy learning.

## Method

- Empirically investigated the continual learning dynamics of pretrained VLAs (==Pi0==, ==GR00T N1.5==) on sequential robot manipulation tasks using the ==LIBERO== benchmark.
- Utilized ==Experience Replay (ER)== as the primary continual learning mechanism, evaluating its effectiveness with varying and often small replay buffer sizes.
- Conducted ablations to isolate the role of pretraining, analyzed knowledge retention through ==component swapping==, and measured the efficiency of recovering 'forgotten' skills via finetuning.

## Results

- Pretrained VLAs achieved near-zero to positive Negative Backward Transfer (NBT) across multiple LIBERO benchmarks, outperforming non-pretrained models by **2-4x**, even with only **2%** of data in the replay buffer.
- The vision-language backbone was identified as the dominant source of apparent forgetting when swapping components, correlating with task diversity.
- Seemingly forgotten skills in VLAs were rapidly recovered, with models like Pi0 regaining peak performance in less than **10%** of the original training steps, indicating knowledge retention.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Pretrained VLAs exhibit remarkable inherent resistance to catastrophic forgetting, achieving near-zero or even positive backward transfer with simple Experience Replay.
> - Large-scale pretraining is integral to mitigating forgetting, particularly in low-data replay regimes, enabling high forward transfer while preserving old skills.
> - Apparent performance degradation on previous tasks in VLAs often signifies inaccessible rather than entirely lost knowledge, allowing for rapid recovery with minimal finetuning.
