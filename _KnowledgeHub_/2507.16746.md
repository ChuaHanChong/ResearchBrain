# Zebra-CoT: A Dataset for Interleaved Vision Language Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> Zebra-CoT is a large-scale, diverse dataset created to train vision-language models for native, interleaved visual chain of thought (CoT) reasoning. Fine-tuning models like Anole-7B and Bagel-7B on Zebra-CoT enhanced their visual reasoning abilities, with Anole-7B showing a 4-fold relative improvement on an in-distribution test set and Bagel-7B gaining the capability to intrinsically generate visual aids during problem-solving.

## Problem

- Large vision-language models (VLMs) predominantly rely on textual reasoning, lacking the ability to natively generate and integrate explicit visual aids (Visual Chain of Thought) into their problem-solving process.
- There is a severe lack of high-quality, large-scale, and diverse training data specifically designed for interleaved text and image reasoning, hindering the development of generalizable visual CoT.
- Existing visual CoT approaches are either complex agentic pipelines leveraging external tools or domain-specific models, preventing broad application and inherent visual reasoning capabilities in foundation models.

## Method

- Developed ==Zebra-CoT==, a dataset comprising **182,384** multimodal reasoning traces that explicitly link textual explanations with intermediate visual aids across diverse domains and over **50** distinct tasks.
- Implemented a ==hybrid data curation pipeline== that combines real-world problem sourcing with synthetic example generation, critically augmented by frontier VLMs (Gemini-2.5, GPT-4.1) to ensure logical coherence and rich multimodal reasoning traces.
- Categorized the dataset into four major areas: ==Scientific Questions==, ==2D Visual Reasoning==, ==3D Visual Reasoning==, and ==Visual Logic & Strategic Games==, to ensure breadth and depth in visual reasoning challenges.

## Results

- Zebra-CoT is the most comprehensive dataset for interleaved text-image reasoning, containing **182,384** reasoning traces across **18** distinct domains and over **50** unique tasks.
- Fine-tuning Anole-7B on Zebra-CoT led to a **4-fold** relative improvement in accuracy on its in-distribution test set (from **4.2%** to **16.9%**) and an average **4.9%** improvement across seven external VLM benchmarks.
- Fine-tuning Bagel-7B on Zebra-CoT enabled the model to intrinsically generate high-quality visual CoT during its reasoning process, although quantitative evaluation indicated challenges with image generation hallucinations.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Proprietary frontier models demonstrate limited performance in zero-shot settings on Zebra-CoT tasks (average 31.51% accuracy), highlighting the inherent difficulty of complex interleaved visual reasoning problems.
> - Providing even a few initial multimodal reasoning steps (scaffolding) substantially improves the performance of advanced VLMs, with two steps increasing average accuracy by 25.19% (to 56.70%), underscoring the value of explicit visual CoT.
> - Training on Zebra-CoT enables existing VLMs to natively generate interleaved visual CoT, transforming them from models that merely process visual inputs to those capable of actively producing visual 'thoughts' during problem-solving.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of artificial intelligence, particularly in the domain of multimodal learning, has witnessed rapid advancements, with large vision-language models (VLMs) demonstrating impressive capabilities in tasks like visual question answering. However, a significant frontier remains in enabling these models to engage in "Visual Chain of Thought" (visual CoT) – a process where AI models generate explicit visual aids, such as diagrams or sketches, as intermediate steps in their reasoning, mirroring human problem-solving strategies.

This paper addresses a critical gap in this evolving landscape. While current VLMs can process multimodal inputs, their internal reasoning processes often remain predominantly textual. Existing approaches to visual CoT broadly fall into two categories:
1. **Agentic Pipelines with External Tools:** These methods leverage external tools like Python functions or expert vision models to programmatically generate visual aids (e.g., Surís et al., 2023; Hu et al., 2024). While effective, these pipelines can be complex, involve tool call errors, and their scalability and diversity are limited by the design of agents and high API costs.
2. **Innate Visual Reasoning:** This emerging paradigm aims for models to directly generate explicit visual tokens as part of their intrinsic thought process (e.g., Li et al., 2025; Chern et al., 2025). However, this area is nascent, with existing models often being specialists in narrow domains (like mazes) and lacking the generalizability required for foundation models.

A primary bottleneck for advancing innate visual CoT has been the severe lack of high-quality, large-scale, and diverse interleaved text and image reasoning training data. Previous datasets either:
- **Focus on input multimodality only:** Many datasets like GQA or ScienceQA provide multimodal inputs but demand purely textual reasoning.
- **Are limited in scope:** VISUAL-COT (Shao et al., 2024a) is largely confined to visual search, while MM-PhyQA (Anand et al., 2024) focuses only on physics and is not publicly available. These specialist datasets hinder the development of general-purpose visual reasoning.
- **Are large but noisy:** Web-scraped corpora like OmniCorpus or MINT-1T feature interleaved text and images but lack strong semantic alignment, explicit reasoning structures, or quality suitable for fine-grained reasoning tasks.

Zebra-CoT positions itself precisely to fill this void. By curating a diverse, high-quality dataset of interleaved text and image reasoning traces, it aims to be the foundational resource for training models to *natively* perform visual CoT across a broad spectrum of complex problems. This work directly pushes the boundaries of multimodal AI by providing the necessary data infrastructure to enable more human-like, interpretable, and powerful reasoning capabilities in future VLM generations, moving beyond tool-augmented approaches towards truly inherent visual thought.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core motivation behind the Zebra-CoT project stems from the observation that human problem-solving frequently involves the natural integration of visual and textual thought processes. For instance, a student might sketch a diagram to solve a geometry problem, or an engineer might draw a flowchart to debug a system. These visual aids are not mere supplements but are integral to effective reasoning. Current large vision-language models (VLMs), despite their advanced multimodal processing capabilities, largely rely on textual reasoning traces, often struggling to explicitly "think visually."

Given this, the key objectives of this research are:

1. **Enable Native Visual Chain of Thought (CoT):** The primary goal is to empower multimodal models to intrinsically generate and reason with visual aids, much like humans do. This means moving beyond external tool-use or superficial image-text pairings to a deeper, integrated multimodal reasoning process.
2. **Address Data Scarcity:** To overcome the significant challenge posed by the lack of high-quality, large-scale training data specifically designed for interleaved text and image reasoning. Existing datasets are either too narrow in scope, too noisy, or lack the explicit step-by-step reasoning structure needed for effective visual CoT training.
3. **Create a Diverse and Comprehensive Dataset:** To develop Zebra-CoT, a dataset that spans a wide array of domains and task types where visual reasoning is naturally applicable. This diversity (18 domains, >50 distinct tasks across 4 major categories) is crucial for training generalizable visual CoT capabilities.
4. **Demonstrate Effectiveness:** To validate the utility and impact of Zebra-CoT by fine-tuning existing VLM architectures (Anole-7B and Bagel-7B) and showing tangible improvements in their reasoning performance and their ability to natively generate visual CoT.
5. **Foster Open Science:** To open-source the dataset and the fine-tuned models, thereby providing a valuable resource for the broader AI research community to accelerate progress in visual CoT development and evaluation.

In essence, the project is motivated by the desire to bridge the gap between human and AI problem-solving by equipping AI models with the ability to "sketch their thoughts," leading to more robust, interpretable, and human-aligned intelligent systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The creation of Zebra-CoT is a meticulous, multi-step process designed to overcome the challenges of data scarcity and ensure logical coherence across modalities. The methodology centers on a hybrid approach of sourcing real-world data and generating synthetic examples, critically enhanced by frontier VLMs.

**A. Data Curation Pipeline:**
The overall data curation follows a pipeline illustrated in Figure 3.
1. **Sourcing Diverse Data:** The first step involves gathering a wide range of problems from both real-world and synthetic sources.
 - **Real-world Data:** High-quality problems are collected from online resources such as math, physics, coding, and chess competition datasets.
 - **Synthetic Data:** New examples are created by generating images or utilizing existing real images, followed by crafting corresponding reasoning templates.

2. **Bridging Logical Connections and Enhancing Traces:** This is a crucial step to ensure the multimodal coherence and richness of reasoning traces. Raw data, even from high-quality sources, often lacks clear logical links between text and images or sufficient textual explanation for the visual steps.
 - **Leveraging Frontier VLMs:** Gemini-2.5 and GPT-4.1 are employed as powerful assistants. These VLMs are fed both images and raw text reasoning traces. Their task is to:
 - Fill in templated placeholders for synthetic data, diversifying and enriching the textual reasoning.
 - Enhance existing reasoning traces by adding more detailed explanations, describing *why* a visual aid is necessary, and articulating the insights gained from it.
 - Complete narrative components that elucidate the rationale behind visual manipulations (e.g., bounding boxes, move visualizations in games).
 - Output refined text traces with standardized image placeholders (`<image_start>[image_X]<image_end>`).
 - **Filtering and Cleaning:** The generated traces undergo a filtering process to eliminate invalid cases, such as multiple placeholders referring to the same image or unreferenced placeholders, ensuring the data is automatically parsable into training sequences.

**B. Dataset Composition and Categories:**
Zebra-CoT is designed for breadth and depth, encompassing 182,384 reasoning traces across 18 domains and over 50 distinct tasks, categorized into four major areas:

1. **Scientific Questions (13.2%):**
 - **Geometry:** Convert plotting code from MATH dataset into rendered problem and solution diagrams.
 - **Physics:** Programmatically generate classical mechanics problems (Atwood machines, inclined planes) and leverage OpenStax/MIT OCW. Render free-body diagrams, kinematic visuals.
 - **Chemistry:** Use USPTO-50K dataset (two-to-one reactions), render molecular depictions with RDKit, retrieve PubChem names.
 - **Algorithmic Problem Solving:** Employ a GPT-4.1 agent (based on Visual Sketchpad) to generate detailed traces for competitive programming problems, using compact visual scaffolds (graphs, flow networks).
 - **Graph Problems:** Programmatically create 10,000 problems (connectivity, shortest path, MST, topological sort), with reasoning images for each algorithmic step.

2. **2D Visual Reasoning (28.5%):**
 - **Visual Search:** Adapt tasks from Shao et al. (2024a), incorporating visual aids like bounding boxes and zooming across charts, documents, and general VQA.
 - **Visual Jigsaw:** Create puzzles from ImageNet images with missing pieces. Visual CoT involves iteratively filling pieces or reconstructing the original image.

3. **3D Visual Reasoning (21.7%):**
 - **Embodied Planning:** Reformulate the ALFRED benchmark into an image goal-conditioned task, requiring step-by-step textual plans with corresponding visual states.
 - **Robot Planning:** Reformulate RoboMIND into an image goal-conditioned task for real-world robot manipulation, emphasizing precise high-level action plans.
 - **3D Multi-Hop Object Counting:** CLEVR-inspired scenes with sequential modifications. Visual CoT includes top-down views for full scene reconstruction and intermediate transformation steps.

4. **Visual Logic and Strategic Games (36.7%):**
 - **Visual Logic Puzzles:** Tetris (shape assembly, grid completion, spatial transformation), Raven’s Progressive Matrices (RPM, compositional reasoning patterns), ARC-AGI (visual pattern recognition, visualizing training examples and predicted output).
 - **Mazes:** Procedurally generate grid mazes using `maze-dataset` and OpenAI Gym's FrozenLake-v1. Tasks include topological analysis, pathfinding, navigation planning, and coverage problems.
 - **Chess:** Use mid-game positions from Lichess games, employ Stockfish for optimal moves, and visualize candidate move outcomes.
 - **Connect Four & Checkers:** Use minimax search to generate optimal moves and visualize board states for short-horizon planning.
 - **Ciphers:** Examples of classical encryption schemes (Scytale, Polybius), showing encoding steps with visual sketches.

**C. Model Training and Evaluation:**
1. **Scaffolding Experiment:** Proprietary frontier models (GPT-5, Claude Sonnet 4, Gemini 2.5 Pro) are evaluated on a challenging subset of Zebra-CoT tasks (512 questions). Performance is measured zero-shot (question only) and with incremental "scaffolding" of 1 or 2 multimodal CoT steps provided as context.
2. **Fine-tuning Anole-7B:** An existing VLM, Anole (Chern et al., 2024), is fine-tuned on Zebra-CoT. Evaluation is done on the in-distribution test set and seven external benchmarks (MathVision, MathVista, VisuLogic, EMMA, MMVP, BLINK, Vstar) using chain-of-thought prompting for baseline comparison.
3. **Fine-tuning Bagel-7B:** Bagel-7B (Deng et al., 2025), a VLM not originally designed for native interleaved generation, is fine-tuned. The training loop is modified to enable seamless visual token generation, allowing the model to inherently generate interleaved text-image outputs.

This comprehensive methodology ensures the dataset's quality, diversity, and direct applicability for training advanced visual CoT capabilities in multimodal models.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yields several significant findings, demonstrating both the challenging nature and the effectiveness of the Zebra-CoT dataset in advancing multimodal reasoning.

**A. Dataset Characteristics:**
- **Scale and Diversity:** Zebra-CoT comprises 182,384 reasoning traces across 18 distinct domains and over 50 unique tasks. These tasks are organized into four major categories: Visual Logic & Strategic Games (36.7%), 2D Visual Reasoning (28.5%), 3D Visual Reasoning (21.7%), and Scientific Reasoning (13.2%). This scale and thematic breadth make it the most comprehensive dataset for interleaved text-image reasoning to date.
- **Interleaved Structure:** The dataset is explicitly designed with logically coherent multimodal reasoning traces, where images are not merely supplementary but are integral to the step-by-step problem-solving process. The distribution of images per trace (Figure 1) shows varying complexity, with many traces involving multiple visual steps.

**B. Scaffolding Experiment with Frontier Models:**
- **Challenging Tasks:** Proprietary frontier models (GPT-5, Claude Sonnet 4, Gemini 2.5 Pro), despite their advanced multimodal capabilities, struggled significantly with tasks in Zebra-CoT in a zero-shot setting, achieving an average accuracy of only 31.51%. This underscores the difficulty and novel reasoning requirements of the dataset.
- **Value of Visual CoT:** Providing even a few initial multimodal reasoning steps (scaffolding) drastically improved performance:
 - With one multimodal step (1MT), average accuracy across models increased to 47.99% (an absolute gain of +16.48%).
 - With two multimodal steps (2MT), average accuracy further rose to 56.70% (an absolute gain of +25.19%).
- **Domain-Specific Gains:** Performance improvements were notable across various domains. For instance, Maze tasks saw an impressive average gain of +43.77% with two steps, reaching 96.36% accuracy. Challenging Graph reasoning tasks improved from a low baseline of 3.92% to 22.03% (an average gain of +18.11%). These results emphatically highlight the intrinsic value of visual CoT in guiding complex reasoning processes.

**C. Fine-tuning Anole-7B on Zebra-CoT:**
- **In-distribution Improvement:** Fine-tuning the Anole-7B model on Zebra-CoT led to a substantial improvement in accuracy on its in-distribution test set, increasing from 4.2% to 16.9% – a 4-fold relative improvement and a 12% absolute gain.
- **Out-of-distribution Generalization:** The Anole-Zebra-CoT-7B model also demonstrated enhanced performance on external, challenging VLM benchmarks. It achieved an average improvement of 4.9% across seven datasets, including MathVision, MathVista, and VisuLogic. Most notably, it showed a 13.3% gain in visual logical reasoning capabilities on the VisuLogic benchmark. This indicates that training on Zebra-CoT instills more robust and generalizable visual reasoning abilities.

**D. Fine-tuning Bagel-7B on Zebra-CoT:**
- **Native Visual CoT Generation:** Crucially, fine-tuning Bagel-7B, a model not originally capable of natively generating interleaved text and images, enabled it to inherently produce high-quality visual CoT during its own reasoning process (Figure 5). This demonstrates Zebra-CoT's effectiveness in instilling intrinsic multimodal generation capabilities, making such models suitable for future reinforcement learning (RL) training.
- **Qualitative Success, Quantitative Challenge:** While the Bagel-Zebra-CoT model qualitatively generated compelling interleaved reasoning chains, quantitative evaluation on benchmarks did not show substantial improvements, and in some cases, even slight performance drops were observed (Section D). The authors attribute this to image generation hallucinations, where the VAE-based generation encoder might produce corrupted images that, when re-encoded by the ViT encoder, lead to reasoning based on inaccurate visual information. This highlights a critical challenge in seamlessly integrating generated visual modalities within complex reasoning pipelines.

In summary, Zebra-CoT effectively addresses the need for high-quality visual CoT training data, enabling significant advancements in the visual reasoning capabilities of multimodal models and demonstrating the pronounced value of explicit visual steps in solving complex problems.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of Zebra-CoT represents a pivotal advancement in the quest to equip AI systems with more human-like reasoning capabilities, specifically addressing the critical frontier of Visual Chain of Thought (visual CoT). Its significance and potential impact are multi-faceted:

**1. Enabling Native Visual Reasoning in AI:**
Zebra-CoT is the first large-scale, diverse, and high-quality dataset expressly designed to train multimodal models to *natively* perform interleaved text and image reasoning. This is a fundamental shift from models that merely process visual inputs to those that actively *think visually* by generating their own intermediate visual aids. This capability is crucial for tackling problems where visual intuition, spatial manipulation, and diagrammatic representation are indispensable.

**2. Advancing Multimodal AI Research:**
By filling a critical data gap, Zebra-CoT provides a robust foundation for the next generation of vision-language models. The qualitative and quantitative improvements observed in fine-tuned Anole and Bagel models clearly demonstrate the dataset's efficacy. The ability of Bagel-7B to intrinsically generate visual CoT, a capability absent in its base form, is particularly impactful. This paves the way for developing more sophisticated VLMs that can leverage visual thought not just for better answers, but also for more interpretable and explainable reasoning processes.

**3. Fueling Reinforcement Learning for Visual CoT:**
The research explicitly highlights that models trained on Zebra-CoT, especially the Bagel variant capable of native visual thought, provide excellent initializations for reinforcement learning (RL). Just as RL has refined logical consistency in text-based reasoning, it can now be applied to visual reasoning. This opens exciting avenues for using verifiable or fine-grained rewards to further enhance the quality, correctness, and logical flow of generated visual reasoning traces, addressing current limitations like image generation hallucinations.

**4. Broadening the Scope of AI Problem-Solving:**
The dataset's diverse coverage—spanning scientific questions (geometry, physics, algorithms), 2D/3D visual reasoning (jigsaw, embodied planning, object counting), and visual logic/strategic games (chess, mazes)—ensures that models trained on Zebra-CoT will develop generalizable visual CoT skills applicable across a wide array of real-world challenges. This moves beyond specialist models to foster foundational capabilities that could benefit various fields, from scientific discovery to robotics.

**5. Fostering Open Science and Community Development:**
The decision to open-source the Zebra-CoT dataset and the fine-tuned models (Anole-Zebra-CoT, Bagel-Zebra-CoT) is a significant contribution to the broader AI research community. It lowers the barrier to entry for researchers to explore and build upon this work, accelerating collective progress in visual CoT development, benchmarking, and evaluation.

**Potential Challenges and Future Directions:**
The paper acknowledges the challenge of "image generation hallucinations" in the Bagel model, where imperfect generated images can lead to flawed subsequent reasoning. This indicates that while the *capability* to generate visual CoT is instilled, ensuring its *reliability and fidelity* remains an important area for future research, likely through advanced RL techniques and improved visual generation models.

In conclusion, Zebra-CoT is more than just another dataset; it is a meticulously crafted resource designed to push the boundaries of multimodal intelligence. By providing the essential training data for native visual CoT, this work represents a crucial step toward AI systems that can reason visually as naturally and effectively as humans, leading to more capable, interpretable, and human-aligned artificial intelligence.
