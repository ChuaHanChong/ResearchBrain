# VLMbench: A Compositional Benchmark for Vision-and-Language Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> The VLMbench framework, developed by researchers at UCSC and the University of Michigan, provides a compositional benchmark for language-guided robotic manipulation by automatically generating diverse 6-DoF tasks and language instructions. Evaluation with a 6D-CLIPort baseline highlights the critical challenges in precise object localization and 6-DoF control from multimodal inputs.

## Problem

- Embodied AI agents lack the ability to perform complex, precise object manipulation guided by natural language instructions.
- Existing robotic manipulation benchmarks are limited by their lack of scalability, modularity for compositional tasks, adaptability to novel objects, and insufficient language diversity.
- Creating diverse, physics-realistic 6-DoF manipulation demonstrations with varied language instructions is labor-intensive and difficult to scale.

## Method

- Developed AMSolver, an automatic system that generates diverse 6-DoF robotic manipulation demonstrations using rule-based unit tasks and object-centric representations.
- Created VLMbench, a benchmark leveraging AMSolver to feature eight categorized tasks with compositional language and variations, implemented in CoppeliaSim.
- Introduced 6D-CLIPort, a keypoint-based vision-and-language agent extending CLIPort to 6-DoF actions, serving as a baseline to evaluate VLMbench's challenges.

## Results

- The 6D-CLIPort agent consistently outperformed "Language-Only" and "Vision-Only" baselines, affirming the joint necessity of both visual and language inputs for task success.
- Tasks requiring precise and continuous 6-DoF pose adjustments, such as "Pour" tasks, exhibited very low success rates, indicating current models' limitations in handling dynamic constraints.
- Ablation studies demonstrated that accurate object localization (position estimation) from multimodal inputs is a major bottleneck, as providing ground-truth position significantly boosted performance more than ground-truth orientation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Complex manipulation tasks can be systematically decomposed into modular "unit tasks" defined by end-effector constraints (e.g., position, orientation), facilitating automatic and scalable data generation.
> - A principled approach can automatically generate diverse 6-DoF manipulation demonstrations with corresponding compositional language instructions, overcoming data collection bottlenecks.
> - Accurate object localization and precise 6-DoF control, especially regarding orientation in continuous, multi-step tasks, remain critical challenges for current vision-language manipulation models.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work is strategically positioned at the intersection of **Embodied AI, Vision-and-Language (VL) Understanding, and Robotic Manipulation**. It addresses a critical challenge in realizing intelligent embodied agents: enabling them to manipulate objects in complex ways guided by natural language instructions, often referred to as the "last mile" for such agents.

The broader research landscape for VLMbench can be understood by examining its relationship to existing areas:

- **Embodied AI:** The overarching goal of embodied AI is to create agents that can perceive, understand, and interact with the physical world. While significant progress has been made in areas like Vision-and-Language Navigation (VLN) (e.g.,), which focuses on navigating environments based on language, the ability to *physically manipulate* objects as instructed remains a more complex and less mature area. VLMbench aims to bridge this gap by providing a specific focus on robust, language-guided object manipulation.
- **Vision-and-Language Tasks:** The field has seen a proliferation of tasks connecting vision and language, such as Visual Question Answering (VQA), Video Captioning, and Image-Text Retrieval. These tasks primarily focus on understanding and generating descriptions based on visual input. VLMbench extends this by requiring not just understanding, but *action generation* in a physical simulation, moving beyond abstract reasoning to grounded interaction. Works like referring object spatial relations provide valuable reasoning, but VLMbench seeks to translate this reasoning into executable robot actions.
- **Robotic Manipulation Benchmarks:** The paper thoroughly reviews existing benchmarks, highlighting their limitations.
 - Benchmarks like ALFRED, ManipulaTHOR, Habitat 2.0, BEHAVIOR, and CALVIN integrate language and visual input for embodied tasks, but often involve high-level object rearrangement or simpler interactions.
 - Benchmarks like MetaWorld, CausalWorld, RLBench, and Robomimic offer diverse manipulation tasks but often lack the scalability, modularity, or explicit language grounding required for complex, compositional instructions.
 - A key distinction VLMbench draws is the lack of "adaptation to novel objects," "categorization for modular and flexible composition to complex tasks," and "variations in language" in existing datasets. VLMbench specifically aims to address these shortcomings, offering a more inclusive, modular, and scalable benchmark.
- **Language-Instructed Manipulation:** Recent advancements in this specific subfield include hierarchical networks for long-horizon tasks (HULC), object selection and pose generation from language (Structformer), and goal-conditioned policies. Notably, the paper builds upon CLIPort, a state-of-the-art method for language-guided manipulation, by extending it to 6-DoF actions and variable task lengths. This demonstrates that VLMbench is not only defining a new challenge but also immediately testing it with leading methodologies.

In summary, VLMbench fits into the research landscape by pushing the boundaries of embodied AI towards more sophisticated, language-guided physical interaction. It fills a critical void by offering a structured, scalable, and compositionally rich benchmark that directly addresses the limitations of prior work, thus providing a fertile ground for developing next-generation robot learning agents.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to **enable embodied agents to perform complex object manipulation tasks by accurately following human-provided language instructions**, such as "move the red mug next to the box while keeping it upright." To achieve this, the authors identify significant gaps in existing research and aim to develop a comprehensive solution.

The key motivations driving this work are:

1. **Bridging the "Last Mile" of Embodied Agents:** While embodied AI has progressed in areas like navigation, the intricate challenge of precise object manipulation guided by natural language remains largely unsolved. Humans naturally use language for such tasks, and integrating this capability into robots is crucial for their utility in real-world scenarios.
2. **Lack of Scalability and Modularity in Existing Benchmarks:** Current benchmarks for robotic manipulation with language guidance suffer from several limitations:
 - **Limited Adaptation to Novel Objects:** They often struggle to generalize to objects unseen during training.
 - **Insufficient Categorization for Compositional Tasks:** Task demonstrations are not modular, making it difficult to compose simple actions into complex, multi-step tasks.
 - **Lack of Language Variation:** The limited diversity in language instructions can lead to biased visual reasoning and hinder the development of robust language understanding models.
 - **Manual Demonstration Generation:** Many benchmarks rely on labor-intensive human demonstrations, which are costly and difficult to scale to a vast number of variations.
3. **Need for a Comprehensive and Realistic Benchmark:** The authors envision an "ideal VLM benchmark" with three characteristics:
 - **Scalability:** Automatic generation of diverse, physics-realistic 6 Degrees of Freedom (DoF) interactions.
 - **Task Categorization:** Exploiting commonalities in robot motion between semantically different tasks (e.g., opening different types of doors). This is a novel approach for manipulation benchmarks.
 - **Reasonable Language Generation:** Producing varied language instructions to test diverse visual reasoning abilities without biases.

Based on these motivations, the specific objectives of the paper are:

- **Introduce `AMSolver` (Automatic Manipulation Solver):** A system for automatically generating robot demonstrations with compositional language instructions across various task semantics, motion constraints, and object types/states.
- **Build `VLMbench` (Vision-and-Language Manipulation Benchmark):** A robust benchmark leveraging AMSolver, featuring categorized robotic manipulation tasks with visual observations and compositional language. It aims to provide variations with minimal biases and categorize tasks by end-effector constraints.
- **Develop `6D-CLIPort`:** A general, keypoint-based vision-and-language manipulation baseline model to evaluate the difficulty and capabilities of VLMbench tasks, extending prior state-of-the-art methods.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors propose a multi-faceted approach centered around the development of the AMSolver system and the VLMbench benchmark, coupled with a baseline agent, 6D-CLIPort.

#### 3.1. AMSolver: Automatic Manipulation Solver

AMSolver is designed to automatically generate high-quality, diverse robot manipulation demonstrations. Its core principles are modularity and rule-based task definition.

- **Rule-based Unit Tasks:** The fundamental idea is that complex manipulation tasks can be decomposed into combinations of simpler "unit tasks." A unit task is defined as "take action on an object under certain constraints," parameterized by:
 - **Position Constraints (a):** Defines the valid spatial space for the end-effector's goal or trajectory (e.g., Free, Plane, Line, Fixed).
 - **Orientation Constraints (b):** Defines the valid 3D orientation range (e.g., Free, Axis, Fixed, Depend on position).
- **Three Unit Task Templates:**
 - **Control:** A preparatory or ending step, primarily modeling grasping an object or releasing it. Constraints depend on object geometry.
 - **M1 (Goal Pose Constraints):** Moving a target object to a specific 6-DoF goal pose. Examples include placing an object on a table (M1[Plane, Free]) or dropping a stick into a hole (M1[Line, Axis]).
 - **M2 (Path Pose Constraints):** Moving a target object along a trajectory while satisfying constraints *throughout the entire path*. This is stricter than M1 and accounts for articulated objects or tasks requiring continuous constraint adherence (e.g., opening a door, wiping a table). M2[Free, Free] degenerates to M1[Free, Free].
- **Object-centric Representation:** Objects are parameterized by fundamental properties like class, color, size, geometry, and articulation (for multi-part objects). This allows for easy variation and adaptation to novel objects by simply changing these parameters.
- **Automatic Demonstration Generation:**
 - Users compose task templates from the unit task library (e.g., Control-M1 for pick-and-place).
 - Objects are selected and their placement randomized within the scene.
 - An object-wise grasp pose dictionary is created using a point cloud-based grasping pose detection algorithm.
 - Customized motion constraints are integrated into the OMPL (Open Motion Planning Library) to find valid, constraint-satisfying trajectories between waypoints.
 - Corresponding compositional language descriptions are automatically generated using predefined templates. The system generates both high-level instructions and low-level step-by-step instructions.

#### 3.2. VLMbench: Visual-and-Language Manipulation Benchmark

VLMbench is the resulting dataset and evaluation environment built upon AMSolver.

- **Problem Definition:** Given multi-view RGB-D images, depth, segmentation, robot states, and language instructions, the agent must estimate an executable sequence of 6-DoF end-effector actions to complete the task.
- **Task Categories:** Eight general task categories are defined based on combinations of the unit task templates and their primary constraints (e.g., "Pick & Place objects," "Stack objects," "Pour water," "Use drawer," "Use door"). This constraint-based categorization is a novel aspect.
- **Task Variations:** To ensure diversity and test generalization, VLMbench incorporates eight types of variations: color, size, relative position, shape, direction, level, amount, and action type. These variations are applied to objects and motions, creating numerous instance-level tasks.
- **Unseen Settings:** To evaluate generalization capabilities, the benchmark includes unseen combinations of colors and shapes that were not present during training.
- **Simulator Environment:** The benchmark is implemented in CoppeliaSim, utilizing PyRep and building upon RLBench. It features a 7-DoF Franka Emika Panda robot arm, five RGB-D cameras (front, left, right, overhead, wrist), and records detailed state information (RGB-D, segmentation, point cloud, robot joint states, object poses).
- **Evaluation Metric:** Success rate, determined by object or joint detectors within the simulator, is the primary metric.

#### 3.3. 6D-CLIPort: Vision-and-Language Manipulation Agent (Baseline)

To establish a baseline for VLMbench, the authors propose 6D-CLIPort, a neural network agent that extends the state-of-the-art CLIPort.

- **Input:** Multi-view RGB-D observations and language instructions.
- **Output:** A sequence of 6-DoF pose keypoints along the manipulation path.
- **Architecture:**
 - **Multi-view Vision Fusion:** RGB-D inputs from multiple cameras are fused into a 3D colored point cloud, then projected into a top-down RGB-D image.
 - **Encoding Module:** Comprises semantic (pre-trained CLIP's ResNet50 and Transformer for RGB and language) and spatial (untrained Transporter ResNet for RGB-D) streams. A decoder fuses these features to produce a pixel-wise feature map indicating manipulation interest.
 - **Action Generation:** Three main modules, reusing the encoding module, determine the 6-DoF pose:
 - **Attention Module:** Identifies 2D image sample crops.
 - **Key Module:** Processes rotated crops to produce feature map crops.
 - **Value Module:** Generates feature maps of the entire image.
 - These components interact to yield a 3D pose heatmap (x-y position and yaw rotation by maximization) and three fully connected neural networks regress the remaining dimensions (z position, roll, pitch).
- **Improvements over CLIPort:** 6D-CLIPort addresses CLIPort's limitations by outputting full 6-DoF actions (instead of 3-DoF) and supporting an arbitrary number of poses (instead of a fixed two for pick and place).
- **Training and Evaluation Setup:**
 - Agents are trained separately for each task category.
 - Tasks are preprocessed into sub-goal sequences using ground-truth waypoints from AMSolver.
 - Gripper states (open/close) are provided.
 - Pre-generated grasp poses are used if close enough to estimations to increase stability.
 - Predefined pre-grasping and post-grasping offsets are used to simplify motion planning.
 - Baselines include "Language-Only" (visual inputs are zeros) and "Vision-Only" (language input only for step indication) agents to assess modality importance.
 - Ablation studies are performed by providing ground-truth position or orientation information to 6D-CLIPort to analyze its estimation capabilities.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted on VLMbench with 6D-CLIPort and other baselines yield several key findings regarding the benchmark's difficulty and the capabilities of current language-guided manipulation models:

1. **Importance of Both Vision and Language Modalities:**
 - The **6D-CLIPort** model consistently achieves the best overall success rates across all tasks, significantly outperforming both "Language-Only" and "Vision-Only" agents. This emphatically demonstrates that both visual observations for object localization and language instructions for task specification are crucial for successful task completion in VLMbench.
 - The **"Language-Only"** agent performs near zero success on most tasks, being "visually blind" and unable to localize objects. Limited success (e.g., in "Drawer" tasks) is attributed to language hints (like "level") leading to accidental collision-based success rather than precise manipulation.
 - The **"Vision-Only"** agent shows significant performance degradation without language guidance. It completely fails on tasks requiring strict pose constraints ("Drop," "Shape Sorter," "Pour," "Door") and only achieves very low success rates on simpler tasks like "Pick & Place" by random grasping.

2. **Task Difficulty and Performance Trends:**
 - **Rotation Variance:** 6D-CLIPort performs better on tasks with lower rotational variances (e.g., "Pick&Place," "Stack," "Shape Sorter," "Wipe," "Drawer"). This suggests the model struggles more with accurate 3D orientation estimation compared to position.
 - **Complex Pose Adjustments:** The "Pour" tasks present the greatest challenge, with very low success rates. This is attributed to the need for precise and continuous pose adjustments (following grasping and maintaining liquid orientation), highlighting a limitation in current models' ability to handle dynamic and constrained multi-step movements.
 - **Grasping vs. Movement:** Analyzing step-wise success rates (Goal-conditioned Grasp / G-G vs. Goal-conditioned Movement / G-M) reveals that 6D-CLIPort generally achieves high grasping success rates (G-G) for most tasks, even in unseen settings. However, there's a significant drop in success during the subsequent movement (G-M) and total task completion. This indicates that identifying and grasping the correct object is often less problematic than executing the precise, constraint-satisfying motion to achieve the goal.

3. **Generalization to Unseen Settings:**
 - While performance generally drops in unseen settings compared to seen settings, the decline is described as "reasonable and not dramatic." This suggests that 6D-CLIPort, benefiting from the transferability of the pre-trained CLIP model, can generalize learned manipulation knowledge from seen object properties to novel ones (unseen colors or shapes).
 - The agent is more sensitive to novel compositions of variations, with larger performance drops observed for "Shape," "Level," "Action Type," and "Amount" in unseen conditions.

4. **Ablation Study on Position and Orientation Estimation:**
 - **Ground-Truth Position (GT Pos) vs. Ground-Truth Orientation (GT Ori):** Providing ground-truth position (`6D-CLIPort (GT Pos)`) leads to a *much more significant* performance improvement across tasks compared to providing ground-truth orientation (`6D-CLIPort (GT Ori)`). For example, "Pick&Place" seen success jumps from 28.28% to 83.84% with GT Pos, but only to 28.03% with GT Ori.
 - **Localization is a Bottleneck:** This finding strongly suggests that accurately localizing the target object and understanding its spatial relationship based on language instructions (compositional reasoning) is a major limiting factor for current models. Once the position is known, executing the movement becomes substantially easier.
 - **Unmatched Information:** The study also notes that even with partial ground truth, failures can occur if the estimated and ground-truth components (e.g., estimated orientation with ground-truth position) are mismatched, leading to unreachable poses or invalid trajectories. This highlights the tight coupling required between all 6-DoF components.

5. **Identified Failure Cases:** The paper categorizes common failure modes of 6D-CLIPort:
 - Wrong grasping pose (unable to grasp any object).
 - Wrong grasping object (grasps an incorrect object).
 - Wrong destination pose (executes a movement that doesn't fulfill the task semantics).
 - Wrong destination object (moves the object to an incorrect location or interacts with the wrong target).

In conclusion, VLMbench proves to be a challenging benchmark that effectively highlights the current limitations of language-guided manipulation agents, particularly in precise 6-DoF control, complex sequential tasks, and the critical role of accurate object localization and orientation estimation from multimodal inputs.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of VLMbench and AMSolver represents a significant contribution to the field of embodied AI and robotic manipulation, with several potential impacts:

1. **Addressing Key Gaps in Existing Benchmarks:** VLMbench directly tackles the long-standing limitations of prior benchmarks, such as a lack of modularity, scalability, and robust generalization to novel objects and diverse language instructions. By providing a framework that automates the generation of diverse and compositional tasks, it overcomes the prohibitive costs and difficulties associated with manual data collection.
2. **Facilitating Research in Language-Guided Manipulation:** The benchmark offers a structured, challenging, and easily extensible environment for developing and evaluating next-generation robotic agents. Researchers can leverage AMSolver's rule-based task definition and automatic demonstration generation to rapidly test new algorithms and models, accelerating progress in understanding how to ground language in complex physical interactions.
3. **Novel Task Categorization and Compositionality:** The proposed task formulation based on end-effector motion constraints (Control, M1, M2) provides a principled way to categorize and compose manipulation tasks. This abstract representation of actions, decoupled from specific semantic objects, offers a path toward more generalizable manipulation skills, where an agent learns to "open a joint" rather than specifically "open a door" or "open a drawer."
4. **Emphasis on Compositional Visual Reasoning:** With its diverse object properties (color, shape, size, relative position) and compositional language templates, VLMbench forces models to perform nuanced visual reasoning to correctly identify targets, distractors, and understand the precise requirements of instructions. The "unseen settings" explicitly push models beyond memorization towards true generalization.
5. **Clear Identification of Current Limitations:** The baseline evaluation with 6D-CLIPort provides a clear picture of where current state-of-the-art models fall short. The results indicate that object localization (position estimation) from language is a major bottleneck, and precise 6-DoF control, especially orientation and continuous path constraints, remains highly challenging. This directs future research efforts toward these critical areas.
6. **Extensibility and Community Resource:** The modular design of AMSolver, the explicit definition of task templates, and the use of widely adopted simulation platforms (CoppeliaSim, built on RLBench) make VLMbench highly extensible. Researchers can add new objects, variations, or even unit task templates, ensuring its relevance as the field evolves. The release of the simulator and benchmark will serve as a valuable community resource.
7. **Guidance for Future Work:** The "Limitations" section proactively points to important future research directions, including incorporating soft material manipulation, indirect manipulation tasks (e.g., throwing), and, crucially, bridging the sim-to-real gap. This forward-looking perspective further solidifies the work's impact on guiding the community.
8. **Responsible AI Practices:** By explicitly acknowledging the potential for unexpected actions due to the sim-to-real gap and advising against real-world deployment without fine-tuning, the authors demonstrate a commitment to responsible research in robotics and AI.

In conclusion, VLMbench is more than just a dataset; it's a comprehensive framework that redefines the landscape for language-guided robotic manipulation. By offering a scalable, modular, and challenging benchmark, it promises to be a foundational tool for researchers striving to build more intelligent, versatile, and human-friendly embodied AI agents.
