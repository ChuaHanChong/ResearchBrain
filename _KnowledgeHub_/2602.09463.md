# SpotAgent: Grounding Visual Geo-localization in Large Vision-Language Models through Agentic Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> SpotAgent grounds visual geo-localization in Large Vision-Language Models by formalizing the task as an agent-based decision-making process, integrating external tools for iterative evidence gathering and verification. This framework achieves state-of-the-art performance among retrieval-free methods on the Im2GPS3k benchmark, with 14.12% accuracy at 1km, while effectively reducing spatial hallucinations.

## Problem

- Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) for geo-localization often rely solely on internal knowledge, treating the task as a closed-world problem.
- This reliance makes LVLMs prone to generating plausible but spatially incorrect "hallucinations" when visual cues are sparse, ambiguous, or long-tailed.
- Existing LVLM approaches lack active external verification, hindering the factual grounding and verifiable precision of geo-localization predictions.

## Method

- SpotAgent formulates visual geo-localization as a ==Partially Observable Markov Decision Process (POMDP)==, enabling an agent to iteratively perform actions based on its current state.
- A ==multi-agent ReAct framework== generates high-quality training data, decoupling visual perception (Observer Agent) from strategic planning and tool invocation (Tool-Call Agent).
- A ==3-stage post-training pipeline== (Supervised Fine-Tuning, Agentic Cold Start, and Reinforcement Learning with Spatially-Aware Dynamic Filtering) instills tool-calling expertise and refines reasoning for fine-grained spatial accuracy.

## Results

- SpotAgent achieved state-of-the-art performance among retrieval-free methods on the Im2GPS3k benchmark, with **14.12%** accuracy at 1km and **40.36%** at 25km.
- The agentic framework demonstrated improved robustness in challenging long-tail scenarios, outperforming a "No Tool" variant by more than double (**19.65%** vs **9.22%** at 1km) on the Street View Text Dataset.
- Ablation studies confirmed that each stage of the post-training pipeline progressively contributes to performance gains, with the Agentic Cold Start notably improving tool utilization and the RL stage refining spatial accuracy.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The "Agentic Cold Start" training stage is crucial for activating the LVLM's awareness and robust utilization of external tools, significantly improving tool-call rates and success.
> - Synergizing diverse external tools like web search, visual zoom, and geo-coding proves essential for achieving precise geo-localization, especially in ambiguous or long-tail scenarios.
> - Dynamically filtering reinforcement learning samples to prioritize "learnable" examples, rather than perfectly solved or intractable ones, enhances the efficiency and effectiveness of spatial reasoning refinement.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Image geo-localization is a task concerned with predicting the geographic coordinates of a given image. Traditionally, this field has been dominated by two main paradigms: retrieval-based methods and classification-based methods. Retrieval-based approaches match query images against large databases of geo-tagged images, while classification-based methods discretize the Earth's surface into a grid and classify images into these predefined regions. While effective in certain contexts, these methods often struggle with the continuous nature of geographical space and typically do not provide explicit logical reasoning for their predictions.

The emergence of Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) has introduced a new avenue for geo-localization, allowing for reasoning-driven approaches that leverage the extensive pre-trained knowledge bases of these models. LVLMs have shown capabilities in common scene understanding and foundational geographical reasoning. However, current LVLM-based methods for geo-localization are frequently limited by their reliance on static internal knowledge. This limitation makes them susceptible to "hallucinations," where models generate plausible but spatially incorrect reasoning paths or confident but ungrounded predictions, particularly in real-world scenarios characterized by sparse, long-tailed, or ambiguous visual cues. Existing LVLM approaches often treat geo-localization as a closed-world reasoning task, inferring locations solely from internal representations without the capacity to interact with external information sources.

This paper positions its work within the evolving landscape of agentic reasoning for visual tasks. Inspired by cognitive science that suggests an interleaving of active exploration and visual cue extraction, the research proposes moving beyond purely visual reasoning as the sole source of evidence for reliable geo-localization. It builds upon foundational work in agentic reasoning like ReAct and Toolformer, which integrate reasoning with actions to interact dynamically with external environments. While previous multimodal frameworks extended this capability to the visual domain by orchestrating specialized vision models, and domain-specific agents have addressed tasks like chart understanding, the application to precise coordinate-level geo-localization, especially with external web-augmented reasoning, remains a developing area. This work aims to bridge the gap between LVLMs' strong reasoning potential and their shortcomings in grounding predictions with verifiable external facts, particularly for fine-grained geo-localization.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the accuracy and verifiability of image geo-localization by addressing the limitations of existing Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs). The authors identify several key challenges and motivations:

1. **Addressing Limitations of Internal Knowledge:** Current LVLMs, despite their reasoning capabilities, rely solely on their internal knowledge bases. This internal knowledge is often insufficient for real-world geo-localization tasks, which are dynamic and feature long-tail distributions of visual cues. The motivation is to overcome this limitation by allowing models to access and integrate external, up-to-date information.

2. **Mitigating Spatial Hallucinations:** A significant problem with current LVLMs in geo-localization is their susceptibility to generating "spatial hallucinations"—predictions that seem plausible based on visual stereotypes but are factually incorrect. This occurs due to a lack of verifiable external knowledge. The objective is to provide a mechanism for grounding predictions in factual evidence, thereby reducing such hallucinations and ensuring more precise and verifiable geo-localization.

3. **Enhancing Grounding and Interpretability:** Traditional geo-localization methods often lack explicit logical reasoning or interpretability. While LVLMs offer some level of reasoning, the authors observe that this reasoning can be ungrounded. The motivation is to formalize geo-localization as an agentic reasoning process where visual interpretation is synergized with tool-assisted verification, leading to more robust and interpretable decision-making. This involves actively exploring and verifying visual cues.

4. **Developing a Robust Training Framework:** To instill the capabilities for expert-level reasoning and active tool use, a structured post-training pipeline is necessary. The motivation is to develop an efficient and effective training methodology that moves models from static visual thinking to active agentic reasoning, including the ability to generate high-quality, verifiable reasoning trajectories and to optimize for fine-grained spatial accuracy.

5. **Optimizing Learning Efficiency:** In the context of reinforcement learning for geo-localization, not all training samples provide equally effective learning signals. The authors are motivated to enhance the efficiency of the reinforcement learning stage by prioritizing "learnable" samples, thus accelerating model refinement towards fine-grained spatial accuracy.

In summary, the core motivation is to create a geo-localization system that can actively reason, interact with external environments (like web search and maps), and iteratively refine its predictions based on verifiable evidence, thereby achieving superior performance and reliability compared to systems relying solely on internal knowledge.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The SpotAgent framework formalizes visual geo-localization as a sequential decision-making process, contrasting with static reasoning paradigms. This approach is structured around a Partially Observable Markov Decision Process (POMDP) and involves a multi-stage post-training pipeline.

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation

The task is defined as predicting geographic coordinates for a query image. Unlike traditional Chain-of-Thought (CoT) LVLM methods that condition predictions on the image and internal reasoning, SpotAgent conditions predictions on dynamic evidence gathered through an interaction history. The POMDP is defined by:
- **State Space (S):** The current context, including the query image and the history of interaction messages (past reasoning, tool calls, observations).
- **Action Space (A):** A set of actions including:
 - **A_tools:** External tool invocations:
 - `GeoCoding(p)`: Converts a place name to coordinates (using Google Maps).
 - `WebSearch(q)`: Queries a search engine for external information.
 - `ImageTool(img)`: Executes zoom-in operations on the image.
 - **A_text:** Natural language generation for internal reasoning, planning, or summarizing.
- **Observation Space (O):** The query image initially, and subsequently, the return values from executed tools.
- **Policy (π):** The agent's learned strategy to select actions based on the current history, aiming to maximize the probability of correct localization within a budget of interaction steps (e.g., max 6 tool invocations).

#### 3.2. Agentic Approach to Image Geo-localization

##### 3.2.1. Multi-Agent ReAct Framework for Data Generation

To create high-quality, tool-integrated training data, the authors design a Multi-Agent ReAct framework. This framework decouples visual perception from strategic planning:
- **Observer Agent (VLM-based):** Interprets the query image or specific zoomed-in regions. It performs a structured, hierarchical visual analysis, decomposing the scene from macro-scale features (architecture, terrain) to micro-scale clues (text, infrastructure), generating a multi-level visual interpretation (`d_vis`).
- **Tool-Call Agent (LLM-based):** Based on `d_vis` and interaction history, it synthesizes a reasoning thought (`Thk_t`) and a decision action (`Act_t`). Actions can be tool invocations (from `A_tools`) or a termination action to output the final answer. Activating `ImageTool` prompts the Observer Agent for re-analysis of a cropped region.
- **Rejection Sampling for Trajectory Curation:** Only trajectories satisfying two criteria are accepted into the curated `SpotAgenticCoT-6k` dataset:
 1. **Tool-Call validity:** All tool actions must be executable and format-valid.
 2. **Prediction precision:** The final predicted coordinates must be within 5km of the ground truth. This process filters approximately 20,000 raw trajectories down to 6,000 high-quality samples.
- **Agentic Trajectory Data Formatting:** Trajectories are formatted using specific tags: `<think>...</think>` for reasoning, `<tool_call>...</tool_call>` for tool invocations (with JSON parameters), and `<tool_response>...</tool_response>` for tool outputs.

##### 3.2.2. Post-Training Framework of SpotAgent

A 3-stage pipeline is used to instill and refine agentic capabilities:
1. **Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT):** The base LVLM (Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct) is initially fine-tuned on `SpotSFT-200K`, a dataset of image-coordinate pairs from a subset of MP16-Pro, formatted as Visual QA. This establishes foundational geo-localization capabilities.
2. **Agentic Cold Start (ACS):** This stage utilizes the `SpotAgenticCoT-6k` dataset to instill tool-calling expertise. Training loss is computed exclusively on the assistant's responses (reasoning traces and tool invocations), masking user inputs and tool observations.

#### 3.3. Reinforcement Learning with Spatially-Aware Dynamic Filtering

Following ACS, reinforcement learning refines the agent's reasoning.

##### 3.3.1. Dynamic Data Filtering Strategy for RL

To optimize learning efficiency for the RL stage, a dynamic filtering process is applied to the initial SFT dataset, creating `SpotRL`. The goal is to focus on "learnable" samples that are neither fully mastered nor entirely intractable.
- `K=8` inference trials are run for each image using the SFT model.
- `Pass@K` denotes the number of trials where prediction error is within a threshold `δ`.
- Samples are retained only if `0 < Pass@K < K`, discarding `Pass@K=0` (intractable) and `Pass@K=K` (mastered) instances.

##### 3.3.2. Spatially-Aware Curriculum Learning

A two-stage curriculum is employed:
- **Phase I:** Training starts with strict data regimes (δ ∈ {1km, 25km}), focusing on samples requiring reasoning refinement.
- **Phase II:** After Phase I convergence, the training set expands to looser regimes (δ ∈ {200km, 750km}), incorporating harder samples that previously achieved only coarse localization.

##### 3.3.3. Geodesic Distance-based Reward Design

A piecewise linear decay reward function `R(d)` is defined, where `d` is the great-circle distance between predicted and ground truth coordinates. Rewards are 1.0 for errors < 1km, decreasing linearly to 0.75 at 25km, 0.20 at 200km, and 0 otherwise. This reward implicitly incentivizes adherence to valid coordinate formats.

##### 3.3.4. Optimization via Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)

For each image, a group of `G=8` outputs is sampled from the current policy. The advantage `A_i` for each output is computed by normalizing its reward against the mean and standard deviation of rewards within its group. The policy is then optimized by maximizing a surrogate objective, constrained by a KL-divergence penalty to prevent large deviations from the reference SFT model.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrate that SpotAgent achieves improved performance in image geo-localization by leveraging agentic reasoning and external tools.

#### 4.1. Performance on Image Geo-localization

- **Im2GPS3k Benchmark:** SpotAgent achieved 14.12% accuracy at 1km and 40.36% at 25km on the Im2GPS3k benchmark, outperforming other retrieval-free methods. It also showed competitive results against some retrieval-based methods.
- **YFCC4K Benchmark:** On YFCC4K, SpotAgent achieved 7.30% accuracy at 1km and 21.52% at 25km, demonstrating generalization capabilities on large-scale, in-the-wild data.
- The baseline Qwen2.5-VL-7B (without SpotAgent training) demonstrated significantly lower accuracy (e.g., 3.97% at 1km on Im2GPS3k).

#### 4.2. Agentic Behavior on Long-Tail Data

- **Qualitative Analysis (Figure 4, 5, 9, 10):** Comparisons with a standard CoT model (SpotAgent restricted from tool access) illustrated SpotAgent's ability to resolve ambiguity in long-tail scenarios. For example, in a soccer pitch invasion image, the CoT mode mislocated the event due to general visual stereotypes, while SpotAgent used `ImageTool` to zoom into specific team kits and `WebSearch` to cross-reference this detail, leading to precise localization. Similarly, for a Street View Text image, the CoT model hallucinated coordinates despite correctly identifying a business name, whereas SpotAgent used `WebSearch` to verify the exact address and coordinates.
- **Street View Text Dataset (Quantitative):** SpotAgent with tool assistance achieved significantly higher accuracy on the Street View Text Dataset (e.g., 19.65% at 1km with tools vs. 9.22% without tools), demonstrating its effectiveness in scenarios where geo-localization depends on decoding specific textual entities.

#### 4.3. Agent Trajectory Analysis

- **Tool Usage Distribution (Figure 6a):** Analysis on Im2GPS3k showed that 59% of trajectories primarily relied on the `GeoCoding` tool, suggesting direct recognition of landmarks. The remaining 41% involved combinations of `ImageTool` and `WebSearch` (e.g., 22% `zoom + map`, 11% `search + map`, 8% `search + zoom + map`), indicating adaptive tool invocation when visual evidence or internal knowledge was insufficient.
- **Step-wise Trajectory Visualization (Figure 6b):** Representative trajectories showed a structured interleaving of the `Think-Act-Observe` cycle, confirming the agent's iterative decision-making process.

#### 4.4. Necessity of Agentic Cold Start for Effective Tool Interaction

- **Tool-call Rate (Figure 7a):** Without the Agentic Cold Start (ACS) stage, the model exhibited a very low frequency of tool utilization. ACS significantly increased the tool-call rate across all tool categories (map, search, zoom), activating the model's awareness of tool-use.
- **Tool-call Success Rate (Figure 7b):** ACS also improved the reliability of tool calls. Without ACS, tool attempts often resulted in formatting errors, whereas with ACS, the success rate for valid, parseable, and executable tool calls was significantly higher.

#### 4.5. Ablation Studies

- **Efficacy of Tool-Augmented Reasoning (Table 3):** Comparing different tool configurations showed that the full toolset (Web Search, Visual Zoom, Geo Coding) yielded the highest performance across all distance thresholds (e.g., 14.12% at 1km), indicating that the synergy of diverse external knowledge sources is crucial for precise geo-localization. Single tools or partial combinations performed less effectively.
- **Effectiveness of Spatially-Aware Dynamic Filtering (Table 4):** The proposed dynamic filtering strategy for the RL stage consistently outperformed a random sampling baseline (e.g., 14.12% vs 13.78% at 1km), validating its efficiency in prioritizing informative learning samples.
- **Step-wise Analysis of Post-Training Strategies (Table 5):**
 - The base Qwen2.5-VL-7B achieved 3.97% at 1km.
 - Adding SFT and Agentic Cold Start boosted performance substantially to 10.03% at 1km, demonstrating the foundational impact of these stages.
 - Subsequent application of Reinforcement Learning further refined the model, achieving 14.12% at 1km, highlighting the incremental gains from each progressive training stage.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The SpotAgent framework represents an advancement in image geo-localization, particularly for Large Vision-Language Models, by addressing critical limitations related to ungrounded reasoning and spatial hallucinations.

**Key areas of significance include:**

- **Enhanced Reliability and Precision:** By formalizing geo-localization as an agentic reasoning process that actively interacts with external tools (web search, maps, image zoom), SpotAgent moves beyond the static internal knowledge of LVLMs. This significantly improves the grounding of visual evidence in verifiable facts, leading to more precise and reliable geo-localization predictions, especially in complex, ambiguous, or "long-tail" real-world scenarios where visual cues are sparse.
- **Novel Training Methodology:** The introduction of a 3-stage post-training pipeline, encompassing Supervised Fine-Tuning, an Agentic Cold Start phase utilizing synthesized Multi-Agent trajectories, and a Reinforcement Learning stage, provides a systematic approach to instill and refine tool-use capabilities and expert-level reasoning. This framework is crucial for effectively transitioning LVLMs to agentic behavior.
- **Optimized Learning Efficiency:** The Spatially-Aware Dynamic Filtering strategy for the RL stage is a methodological contribution that prioritizes "learnable" samples based on their spatial difficulty. This approach enhances training efficiency by focusing computational resources on instances that yield the most effective learning signals, rather than on trivial or intractable examples.
- **Mitigation of Hallucinations:** The framework's ability to cross-reference internal reasoning with external, verifiable information directly addresses the issue of spatial hallucinations prevalent in LVLMs, where models might generate plausible but incorrect locations. This contributes to the trustworthiness of geo-localization systems.
- **State-of-the-Art Performance:** The demonstrated improvements on standard benchmarks, particularly outperforming other retrieval-free methods on Im2GPS3k, establish SpotAgent as a competitive system in the field. This indicates the practical efficacy of the agentic approach.

**Potential impacts include:**

- **Autonomous Systems:** More robust and accurate geo-localization can enhance autonomous navigation systems (e.g., self-driving cars, drones) by providing better contextual awareness and localization capabilities in diverse environments.
- **Public Safety and Emergency Services:** Improved geo-localization of images from social media or incident reports could aid in faster response times and better resource allocation in emergencies.
- **Cultural Heritage and Tourism:** Precise localization of historical sites or cultural landmarks from images could support preservation efforts, enhance virtual tourism experiences, and facilitate educational applications.
- **Geographic Information Systems (GIS):** The framework could lead to more intelligent systems for annotating and managing large volumes of visual geographic data.
- **Research in AI and LLMs:** SpotAgent contributes to the broader research agenda of developing more capable and grounded AI agents, particularly in the multimodal domain. It provides a blueprint for leveraging external tools to extend the capabilities of large models beyond their parametric knowledge.

The authors also acknowledge limitations, such as the resource overhead of commercial LLMs for data synthesis and the operational costs of real-time tool invocations. Future work, as identified, could focus on developing decentralized, agent-native information-seeking infrastructure and expanding the toolkit with specialized image processing modules, potentially integrating agentic reinforcement learning for joint optimization of tool-use policies.
