# STAIRS-Former: Spatio-Temporal Attention with Interleaved Recursive Structure Transformer for Offline Multi-task Multi-agent Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from KAIST developed STAIRS-FORMER, a transformer architecture for offline multi-task multi-agent reinforcement learning that integrates spatial recursive modules, hierarchical temporal processing, and a token-dropout mechanism. The method achieved an average win rate of 67.4% across SMAC benchmarks, outperforming the state-of-the-art HiSSD's 57.2%, and demonstrated strong generalization to unseen tasks and improved robustness on sub-optimal datasets.

## Problem

- Existing transformer-based methods in offline multi-task multi-agent reinforcement learning (MT-MARL) underutilized attention for inter-agent coordination and often had uniform attention distribution.
- Prior approaches relied on a single, information-lacking history token, limiting their ability to capture long-horizon temporal dependencies in partially observable MARL.
- Models struggled with generalization to unseen tasks involving varying numbers of agents or entities, often overfitting to training dataset configurations.

## Method

- A ==Spatial Recursive Module== (Spatial-Former) employing 'recursive deep transformers' for refined relational reasoning among entities within an agent's local observations.
- A Hierarchical Temporal Module that uses dual-timescale history states (low-level and high-level) and independent position-wise Feed-Forward Networks to capture short-term and long-term dependencies.
- A Token-Dropout Mechanism that stochastically drops entity embeddings during training to enhance generalization to varying numbers of entities and improve robustness to new configurations.

## Results

- STAIRS-FORMER achieved a total mean win rate of **67.4%** across SMAC benchmarks, outperforming previous state-of-the-art HiSSD (**57.2%**) and showing substantial gains (e.g., **48.6%** improvement on Stalker-Zealot).
- The method demonstrated strong generalization, improving average performance on unseen tasks by **24.5%** over HiSSD on SMAC-v2 and by up to **40.5%** on sub-optimal datasets.
- Attention visualizations revealed STAIRS-FORMER adaptively focuses on critical tokens (enemies, allies) and leverages history for strategic decisions, learning complex tactical behaviors like 'focus fire' and 'kiting,' unlike baseline models that showed nearly uniform attention.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Deeper relational reasoning among entities can be achieved efficiently through a recursive application of transformer layers with weight sharing.
> - Effective temporal reasoning in partially observable multi-agent environments benefits from hierarchical history states and dedicated processing streams for spatial and temporal features.
> - Stochastic regularization via token dropout enhances generalization capabilities by preventing overfitting to specific entity counts and promoting robustness to unseen multi-agent configurations.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL) is a field focused on training multiple agents to achieve common goals, particularly in complex, partially observable environments. Offline MARL, which learns policies from pre-collected datasets without further environment interaction, has gained prominence due to its potential to reduce the cost and safety risks associated with online training in real-world applications (e.g., connected vehicles, collaborative drones).

Prior advancements in offline MARL have primarily focused on single-task settings, addressing challenges such as overestimation bias, distributional shift, and out-of-distribution errors through conservative value estimation, hybrid optimization, or regularization strategies (e.g., CFCQL, OMAR, OMIGA, B3C, MA-ICQ). However, real-world applications often require agents to exhibit generalization capabilities across diverse tasks, adapt to varying numbers of agents, and operate robustly under heterogeneous conditions—a domain referred to as multi-task (MT) MARL.

Extending single-agent MT-RL to MARL introduces unique challenges, particularly the need to handle dynamic agent populations and observation spaces across tasks. Transformer-based architectures have been proposed for their scalability in online transfer learning for MARL (e.g., UPDeT). Recent offline MT-MARL methods, such as ODIS and HiSSD, have adopted UPDeT-style transformers and hierarchical skill learning to extract transferable coordination patterns. While these methods demonstrate the applicability of transformers for managing task-dependent variability in observation dimensions, the paper identifies a limitation: they underutilize the transformer's attention mechanism for inter-agent coordination and often rely on a single, information-lacking history token. This approach limits their ability to capture long-horizon temporal dependencies and rich relational structures, especially in partially observable MARL settings. For instance, observations presented in the paper indicate that these prior methods often exhibit nearly uniform attention distribution across tokens, failing to selectively focus on critical entities or effectively leverage historical context.

This work aims to address these identified limitations by proposing an enhanced transformer architecture that more effectively exploits the attention mechanism for spatio-temporal reasoning, thereby improving generalization and robustness in offline MT-MARL.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a transformer architecture that can effectively address the challenges of offline multi-task multi-agent reinforcement learning (MT-MARL), specifically focusing on varying numbers of agents across tasks and the need to generalize to unseen scenarios.

The motivation for this work stems from observed limitations in existing transformer-based approaches for offline MT-MARL, such as UPDeT, ODIS, and HiSSD. These limitations include:
1. **Underutilization of Transformer Attention:** Prior methods, by employing shallow transformer layers (e.g., depth 1) and focusing primarily on observation tokenization, do not fully exploit the transformer's capacity to model complex relationships between tokens. This often results in uniform attention distribution, failing to prioritize relevant entities (e.g., enemies, allies) in spatial contexts.
2. **Inadequate History Representation:** Existing methods rely on a single history token, which is often processed through simple recurrent mechanisms. This design is insufficient for capturing long-horizon temporal dependencies crucial for decision-making in partially observable MARL environments. Empirical observations in the paper suggest that these single history tokens are not heavily attended to, indicating their limited utility.
3. **Lack of Robustness to Varying Agent Populations:** While transformers inherently handle variable-length inputs, current training methodologies for MT-MARL may lead to overfitting to the specific entity counts present in the training datasets. This can compromise generalization performance on unseen tasks with different numbers of agents or entities.

The central question guiding this research is: "How can we enhance the transformer architecture to capture richer correlations between entities while effectively leveraging historical information for offline MT-MARL?" To address this, the paper proposes a novel architecture designed to enable selective attention over critical tokens and robust generalization across diverse multi-agent scenarios.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, STAIRS-Former (Spatio-Temporal Attention with Interleaved Recursive Structure Transformer), is designed to enhance relational reasoning and temporal dependency modeling within a transformer architecture for offline multi-task multi-agent reinforcement learning. It comprises three main components: a Spatial Recursive Module, a Temporal Module, and a Token-Dropout Mechanism. The overall Q-network structure combines these components with a Qatten mixing network.

#### 3.1. Spatial Recursive Module

This module addresses the need for refined relational reasoning among entities within an agent's local observations. Unlike shallow transformer layers used in prior work, STAIRS-Former employs a "recursive deep transformer" called Spatial-Former.
- **Entity Embeddings:** For each agent `i`, local observations `o_i` are decomposed into `own` information (`o_i^own`), `other agent` information (`o_i^{oa,j}`), and `environment` information (`o_i^{en,j}`). These are linearly projected into entity embeddings `e_i = [e_i^own, e_i^{oa,1}..., e_i^{oa,Ka}, e_i^{en,1}..., e_i^{en,Ke}]`.
- **Recursive Spatial Updates:** The Spatial-Former consists of `M` distinct layers. Each layer `l` is applied `ν_l` times recursively. The latent state `z^l_j` at step `j` in layer `l` is updated using the previous recursive state `z^l_{j-1}` and the final state from the preceding layer `z^{l-1}`. This recursive design, `z^l_{j+1} = f(z^l_j + z^{l-1}; \theta_l)`, allows for deeper relational reasoning while maintaining parameter efficiency through weight sharing. The input to the first layer (`l=0`) is the concatenated entity and history tokens.

#### 3.2. Temporal Module

This module aims to overcome the limitations of single history tokens by incorporating a hierarchical temporal process that captures both short-term and long-term dependencies.
- **Hierarchical Temporal Updates:** Each agent maintains two history states: a low-level history `h_{i,L}^t` updated at every timestep and a high-level history `h_{i,H}^t` updated periodically (every `T_H` steps) by a Gated Recurrent Unit (GRU). The transformer input for an agent `i` at time `t` includes `e_i^t`, `h_{i,L}^{t-1}`, and `h_{i,H}^{t-1}`. The low-level history `h_{i,L}^t` is derived from the history position of the Spatial-Former's output, while the high-level history `h_{i,H}^t` is updated by the GRU using `h_{i,H}^{t-1}` and `h_{i,L}^t` only at specific intervals.
- **Temporal Feature Learning:** To prevent interference between spatial and temporal representations, the Spatial-Former employs two independent position-wise Feed-Forward Networks (FFNs) after each attention block: `FFN_obs` for spatial entity tokens (`x^{l,j}_{obs}`) and `FFN_his` for history tokens (`x^{l,j}_{his}`). This ensures that relational reasoning and temporal abstraction are refined along distinct pathways.

#### 3.3. Token-Dropout Mechanism

To enhance generalization to unseen tasks with varying numbers of entities, a token-dropout mechanism is introduced as a stochastic regularization strategy.
- **Selective Dropout:** During training, each entity embedding (excluding the agent's own entity token `e_i^own`, both history tokens `h_{i,L}` and `h_{i,H}`, and any entity token directly linked to the dataset action for offline regularization) is randomly dropped with a probability `p_drop`. This mechanism exposes the model to diverse token lengths, reducing overfitting and improving robustness to new entity configurations.

#### 3.4. Training

STAIRS-Former is trained using a TD3+BC-style objective, adapted for discrete action spaces.
- **Loss Function:** The training objective `L_STAIRS` combines a Temporal-Difference (TD) loss and a Behavior Cloning (BC) regularization term. The TD loss minimizes the squared difference between the aggregated global Q-value `Q_tot` (obtained via a Qatten mixing network from individual agent Q-values) and a target `y_t` (`r_t + γ max_{a'} Q_tot(τ_{t+1}, s_{t+1}, a'; \bar{\theta}, \bar{\phi})`). The BC term encourages higher Q-values for actions present in the offline dataset, promoting stability in the offline regime. Token-dropout is applied during training, and target networks are updated periodically.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of STAIRS-Former was conducted across several multi-agent benchmarks, including SMAC (Marine-Easy, Marine-Hard, Stalker-Zealot), SMAC-v2, Multi-Agent Particle Environment (MPE), and Multi-Agent MuJoCo (MaMuJoCo), utilizing multi-task datasets of varying quality (Expert, Medium, Medium-Expert, Medium-Replay). Comparisons were made against UPDeT-m, ODIS, and HiSSD.

#### 4.1. Performance on SMAC Benchmarks

- **Overall Superiority:** STAIRS-Former consistently achieved the highest average win rates across most SMAC task sets (Marine-Hard, Stalker-Zealot, Marine-Easy) and dataset qualities. It outperformed the previous state-of-the-art, HiSSD, across various scenarios.
- **Generalization to Unseen Tasks:** The method demonstrated strong generalization capabilities, maintaining high performance on unseen tasks. For instance, on the Marine-Hard benchmark, STAIRS-Former showed minor gaps on Expert datasets but achieved substantial gains on Medium, Medium-Expert, and Medium-Replay datasets.
- **Gains on Sub-optimal Data:** On sub-optimal datasets (Medium, Medium-Expert, Medium-Replay), STAIRS-Former showed significant improvements. For example, in Marine-Hard, it improved average performance by 39.5%, 36.6%, and 40.5% over HiSSD on Medium, Medium-Expert, and Medium-Replay datasets respectively. In Stalker-Zealot, which involves complex heterogeneous unit interactions, STAIRS-Former outperformed HiSSD by an average of 48.6%.
- **Mean Win Rates:** Across all SMAC tasks and dataset qualities, STAIRS-Former achieved a total mean win rate of 67.4%, compared to HiSSD's 57.2%. For seen tasks, it achieved 77.9% versus HiSSD's 64.8%, and for unseen tasks, 64.0% versus HiSSD's 54.7%.
- **Marine-Easy Exception:** On the Marine-Easy Medium-Replay dataset, STAIRS-Former underperformed compared to HiSSD. This was attributed to the relatively small size and limited diversity of the Medium-Replay dataset for this specific task, which can reduce the effectiveness of offline RL.

#### 4.2. Performance on SMAC-v2 Benchmark

- **Challenging Environments:** On the more stochastic and diverse SMAC-v2 benchmark, STAIRS-Former continued to outperform prior methods across Terran, Protoss, and Zerg task sets. It improved average performance on seen and unseen tasks by 23.5% and 24.5% respectively over HiSSD, achieving an overall average win rate of 30.3%.

#### 4.3. Performance on MPE and MaMuJoCo Benchmarks

- **MPE Cooperative Navigation:** STAIRS-Former showed modest but consistent improvements over HiSSD in the Cooperative Navigation task, demonstrating its applicability beyond SMAC.
- **MaMuJoCo HalfCheetah:** In the Multi-Agent MuJoCo 'HalfCheetah' task with continuous action spaces and varying joint partitioning schemes, STAIRS-Former significantly outperformed UPDeT-BC and ODIS, achieving a 129% improvement over ODIS, highlighting its effectiveness in robotic control tasks under customized multi-task configurations.

#### 4.4. Attention Dynamics Over Time

- **Selective Attention:** Visualization of attention maps in SMAC episodes revealed that STAIRS-Former adaptively focuses attention on critical tokens (e.g., enemies, allies) and leverages history tokens as needed. For example, agents would shift attention to enemies upon encounter, or to weakened allies for protection, and utilize history tokens for inferring hidden states or strategic decisions (e.g., retreating, counterattacking).
- **Strategic Behaviors:** The observed attention dynamics aligned with complex tactical behaviors like "focus fire" (concentrating on a single enemy) and "kiting" (retreating while attacking), suggesting that STAIRS-Former learns higher-level coordination strategies.
- **Contrast with Baselines:** In contrast, basic transformer architectures (like those in HiSSD) exhibited nearly uniform attention across tokens and time, indicating a lack of selectivity and inability to capture crucial spatio-temporal dynamics.

#### 4.5. Ablation Study

- **Seen Tasks:** The spatial recursive module was identified as the most critical component for performance on seen tasks, with its removal causing a notable drop (77.9% to 72.4% mean win rate). Temporal abstraction and token dropout showed less impact on seen tasks.
- **Unseen Tasks:** All three components—spatial recursive module, temporal module, and token-dropout mechanism—were found to be essential for generalization to unseen tasks. Removing any of these individually led to performance degradation. Token dropout specifically improved generalization by mitigating overfitting.
- **Synergy:** STAIRS-Former achieved the best overall performance when all components were active (67.4% total mean win rate), demonstrating their synergistic importance for robust generalization.

#### 4.6. Dormant Neuron Analysis

- **Improved Capacity Utilization:** Analysis of dormant neuron ratios (neurons with low activation) indicated that both temporal and spatial modules contribute to reducing dormant neurons. The Temporal Focus Layer (TFL), in particular, substantially reduced dormant neurons in observation tokens, suggesting it enhances the effective use of model capacity and mitigates redundancy.

#### 4.7. Additional Ablations (Appendix)

- **Attention Sharpening:** Simple attention sharpening (reducing softmax temperature `τ`) in baselines did not yield comparable performance. While mild sharpening showed slight improvement, excessive sharpening led to performance degradation by forcing uniform, often suboptimal, attention on history tokens, rather than adaptive focus.
- **Comparable Depth Transformers:** Reconfiguring baselines (UPDeT-m, ODIS, HiSSD) to use 2-layer transformers (matching STAIRS-Former's transformer depth) did not consistently improve their performance; in some cases, it led to degradation. This suggests that STAIRS-Former's architectural design, rather than merely increased depth, is responsible for its performance gains.
- **Simple GRU Token:** Adding a simple GRU history token to baselines did not consistently improve their performance, and attention maps showed that the GRU-based history was largely ignored, indicating that a mere addition of recurrence is insufficient without the integrated design of the Temporal Module.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research introduces STAIRS-Former, an architecture that addresses several limitations in offline multi-task multi-agent reinforcement learning (MT-MARL). The work contributes to the advancement of MARL by focusing on generalization and robustness in complex, dynamic scenarios, which are critical for real-world applications.

The significance and potential impact of this work include:
1. **Enhanced Generalization and Robustness:** By integrating spatial and temporal hierarchies with a token-dropout mechanism, STAIRS-Former demonstrates improved generalization to unseen tasks and robustness to varying agent populations and observation structures. This is a key requirement for deploying autonomous multi-agent systems in diverse and unpredictable environments. The consistent outperformance over prior state-of-the-art methods across multiple benchmarks, including SMAC, SMAC-v2, MPE, and MaMuJoCo, supports this claim.
2. **Effective Utilization of Transformer Capabilities:** The paper highlights the potential of transformers to model rich spatio-temporal correlations in MARL, a capacity that previous transformer-based approaches had not fully leveraged. The adaptive and selective attention dynamics observed in STAIRS-Former, in contrast to the uniform attention of prior methods, illustrate a more effective exploitation of the attention mechanism.
3. **Improved Temporal Reasoning in Partially Observable Settings:** The hierarchical temporal module, with its dual-timescale history states and specialized FFNs, provides a method for capturing both short-term and long-term dependencies. This is particularly relevant for partially observable environments where past information is crucial for inferring global states and making informed decisions.
4. **Architectural Design Insights:** The ablation studies and dormant neuron analysis provide insights into the contribution of each architectural component. The finding that simply increasing transformer depth or adding a basic recurrent unit does not yield comparable gains suggests that the specific interleaved recursive structure, hierarchical temporal processing, and token-dropout are critical for STAIRS-Former's performance. The reduction in dormant neurons due to the Temporal Focus Layer indicates a more efficient utilization of model capacity.
5. **Interpretability of Multi-Agent Behavior:** The visualization of attention dynamics offers a degree of interpretability into how agents make decisions and coordinate. The alignment of attention patterns with observed tactical behaviors (e.g., focus fire, kiting) can aid in understanding learned multi-agent strategies, which is valuable for analysis and trust in complex AI systems.

Overall, STAIRS-Former establishes new performance benchmarks in offline MT-MARL, particularly under data scarcity and in settings requiring high generalization. Its architectural innovations offer a foundation for developing more capable and adaptable multi-agent systems for applications where robust performance across varying conditions is essential.
