# GeoAware-VLA: Implicit Geometry Aware Vision-Language-Action Model

> [!summary] Summary
> The GeoAware-VLA model improves the generalization capabilities of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) systems by integrating a frozen geometric foundation model as its visual encoder. This approach yields substantial zero-shot generalization gains on unseen camera views in simulation, with over 35 percentage points average increase on LIBERO, and demonstrates enhanced robustness for real-world robot manipulation tasks.

## Problem

- Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models often struggle with generalization to unseen camera viewpoints, limiting their deployment in dynamic, unstructured environments.
- A key challenge is the difficulty for VLA models to infer consistent 3D geometric information from 2D visual inputs, which is critical for robust spatial reasoning and manipulation.
- Existing solutions like explicit 3D representations are computationally intensive and sensor-dependent, while implicit methods rely heavily on extensive multi-view data or augmentation.

## Method

- GeoAware-VLA replaces the standard vision encoder in VLA architectures with a frozen, pre-trained Visual Geometry Grounded Transformer (VGGT) backbone, which is designed for robust 3D representation learning.
- A lightweight, trainable vision projection layer is introduced to efficiently aggregate multi-scale features from the frozen VGGT, adapting them for downstream policy learning with minimal trainable parameters.
- The model integrates these geometrically-aware visual embeddings with natural language and proprioceptive states into a GPT-style transformer, utilizing either an MLP or VQ-BeT head for action generation.

## Results

- GeoAware-VLA achieved an average improvement of over 35 percentage points in zero-shot generalization success rates on unseen camera viewpoints across the LIBERO benchmark compared to baseline models.
- The model maintained or slightly improved in-distribution performance on original training viewpoints, with GeoAware VQ-BeT achieving 96.8% success on LIBERO and 94.8% on CALVIN.
- Real-world experiments on a physical robotic arm confirmed the practical applicability, demonstrating improved performance and effective operation on both seen and unseen viewpoints for manipulation tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Replacing the standard image encoder entirely with a multi-scale geometric foundation model is more effective for viewpoint generalization than incorporating geometric features as an auxiliary signal.
> - A frozen geometric backbone, combined with a lightweight projection layer, can provide stable and view-invariant visual features without requiring explicit 3D reconstruction or specialized depth sensors.
> - The improved view-invariance in learned visual representations, as shown by consistent feature clusters across different views, directly contributes to enhanced zero-shot generalization performance.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of general-purpose agents capable of performing diverse manipulation tasks in unstructured environments represents a significant objective in robotics research. Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models have emerged as a prominent paradigm, designed to map visual observations and natural language instructions directly to robot actions. While many VLA methods demonstrate strong performance within their training domains, they often exhibit limitations in generalizing to variations beyond these domains, particularly with minor changes in camera viewpoints. This generalization gap is frequently attributed to the difficulty VLA models face in inferring a consistent three-dimensional (3D) world model from two-dimensional (2D) visual inputs, which is crucial for reliable manipulation and spatial awareness.

Prior research has explored two primary strategies to enhance viewpoint robustness. One approach involves incorporating explicit 3D representations, such as point clouds or voxel grids, into the policy’s observation space. While effective in providing 3D consistency, these methods typically necessitate specialized depth sensors and introduce substantial computational overhead for constructing and processing explicit 3D structures. Moreover, they often rely on reliable depth data and camera calibration, which can be scarce in large-scale datasets.

An alternative strategy focuses on implicitly encouraging the model to learn geometrically consistent features without explicitly reconstructing the scene in 3D. This is often achieved through leveraging multi-view data or applying data augmentation techniques during training to foster a view-invariant policy. However, implicit methods can depend on curated multi-view datasets for effective training, and augmentation-based approaches are constrained by the computational cost of generating unseen views and the distribution from which these views are sampled.

The current landscape of VLA models, including architectures like OpenVLA and RT-series, often utilizes vision backbones (e.g., ResNet, SigLIP) primarily pre-trained on web-scale image-text pairs. These backbones excel at semantic understanding, identifying objects and their categories, but their primary focus is not on precise 3D geometry. Concurrently, a distinct class of powerful geometric vision models, such as DUSt3R and the Visual Geometry Grounded Transformer (VGGT), has been developed, excelling at tasks like 3D reconstruction. However, their integration into robot learning policies is an emerging area.

This work situates itself within this evolving landscape by proposing a method to bridge the gap between powerful geometric foundation models and VLA architectures. It differentiates from approaches that use explicit 3D representations by avoiding the need for depth sensors or explicit 3D reconstruction. It also contrasts with purely implicit methods by directly leveraging a pretrained geometric foundation model rather than relying solely on multi-view data or augmentation. Most notably, it distinguishes itself from closely related work like Evo-0, which integrates VGGT as an auxiliary spatial encoder alongside a standard 2D vision backbone. GeoAware-VLA, in contrast, *replaces* the standard image encoder entirely with a frozen VGGT backbone and processes its multi-scale features through a lightweight projection layer, positing that a complete geometric grounding of the visual encoder is more effective for viewpoint generalization.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the generalization capabilities of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, specifically their robustness to unseen camera viewpoints. The authors observe that many existing VLA models, despite strong in-distribution performance, frequently fail when presented with novel camera poses. This limitation stems from their inherent difficulty in inferring stable and consistent 3D geometric information from conventional 2D image inputs, a prerequisite for accurate spatial reasoning and manipulation in diverse environments.

The core hypothesis motivating GeoAware-VLA is that a policy's ability to generalize across different camera views is fundamentally linked to the geometric precision and view-invariance of its visual encoder. Traditional VLA models often rely on vision encoders primarily trained for semantic recognition, which may not prioritize or explicitly learn robust 3D geometry.

To address this, the researchers propose a specific approach that leverages powerful, pre-trained geometric foundation models. They highlight models like the Visual Geometry Grounded Transformer (VGGT), which are explicitly designed and optimized to distill rich 3D representations of the world, including capabilities for inferring camera parameters, multi-view depth, dense point clouds, and point tracking. The motivation behind using such a frozen, pre-trained model is to offload the complex task of learning 3D consistency from scratch from the policy network. By providing the policy with visual features that are already inherently stable across different perspectives, the policy can focus on the action-generation aspect of the task.

The authors aim to achieve this integration through a simple yet effective modification to a standard VLA architecture. Their motivation is to demonstrate that by replacing the standard image encoder with a geometrically-aware feature extractor and coupling it with a lightweight, trainable projection layer, significant improvements in zero-shot generalization to unseen camera viewpoints can be attained without compromising in-distribution performance or introducing excessive computational complexity associated with explicit 3D reconstruction.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The GeoAware-VLA framework is designed as a modification to a standard VLA architecture, specifically building upon elements from the BAKU model. The central methodological innovation involves integrating a frozen, geometrically-aware vision encoder into the policy. The overall architecture is structured into three main stages: sensory encoding, policy decoding, and action generation. The policy aims to predict a robot action ($a_t$) conditioned on multi-view visual observations ($o_{vis_t}$), the robot's proprioceptive state ($s_t$), and a natural language instruction ($l_t$). Actions are 7-dimensional vectors comprising relative end-effector translations ($\Delta x, \Delta y, \Delta z$), axis-angle rotations ($\Delta \alpha, \Delta \beta, \Delta \gamma$), and gripper state ($g$). The policy is trained using Behavioral Cloning (BC) by minimizing a Mean Squared Error (MSE) loss.

**Sensory Encoders:** Each input modality is processed by a dedicated encoder to produce a fixed-dimension embedding ($D_{repr}$).

1. **Geometrically-Aware Vision Encoder:**
 - The standard trainable vision encoder is replaced with a **frozen, pre-trained Visual Geometry Grounded Transformer (VGGT)** model backbone. The prediction heads of the VGGT model are removed.
 - VGGT is notable for producing a list of feature tensors from multiple intermediate layers, offering a hierarchical representation of visual and geometric information.
 - A lightweight, trainable **vision projection layer** is introduced to aggregate and condense these features. This layer selects a subset of evenly spaced intermediate layers (defaulting to 4 layers) from the VGGT backbone.
 - For each camera view, features from a selected layer ($z_l \in R^{N_l \times D_{vggt}}$) are passed through a dedicated 1D convolutional network, comprising trainable convolutional and ReLU layers.
 - An adaptive average pooling layer then pools these features into a single vector ($f_l \in R^{D_{conv}}$).
 - The vectors from all selected layers are concatenated into a single vector.
 - Finally, a multi-layer perceptron (MLP) processes this concatenated vector to generate the view's visual embedding ($z_{vis} \in R^{D_{repr}}$). This per-view projection efficiently adapts the frozen VGGT features with minimal trainable parameters.

2. **Language Encoder:** A natural language instruction string ($l_t$) is first embedded using a pre-trained sentence transformer. This embedding is then projected into the common representation space ($D_{repr}$) by an MLP, yielding $z_{lang}$.

3. **Proprioceptive Encoder:** The robot's proprioceptive state vector ($s_t$), which includes end-effector pose and gripper state, is passed through a two-layer MLP to produce the proprioceptive embedding ($z_{proprio} \in R^{D_{repr}}$).

**Observation Trunk and Policy Decoder:**
The encoded representations from all modalities – the visual embedding from each camera view, the language embedding, and the proprioceptive embedding – are treated as a sequence of input tokens. A learnable action token is appended to this sequence. This combined sequence is then fed into a GPT-style, decoder-only transformer, which processes it using a causal self-attention mask. The output embedding corresponding to the action token position ($h_{action}$) serves as the input for the action generation stage.

**Action Heads:**
Two variants of action heads are explored to accommodate different action distribution modeling capabilities:
1. **MLP Head (GeoAware BAKU):** This is a deterministic 2-layer MLP that directly regresses the continuous 7-dimensional action vector. It is suitable for policies modeling unimodal action distributions.
2. **VQ-BeT Head (GeoAware VQ-BeT):** This head utilizes the Vector-Quantized Behavior Transformer (VQ-BeT) module. It employs a VQ-VAE to learn a discrete action codebook, predicting actions by classifying an appropriate action token from this codebook and regressing a continuous offset. This approach allows the policy to capture potentially multi-modal expert behaviors.

**Experimental Setup:**
The methodology includes evaluations on two simulation benchmarks: LIBERO and CALVIN. For LIBERO, experiments cover four task suites (Spatial, Goal, Object, Long), with 10 tasks per suite and 50 demonstrations per task. For CALVIN, 8 manipulation tasks are selected from environment D, also with 50 demonstrations per task. Crucially, models are evaluated on both original training viewpoints and multiple unseen camera viewpoints to assess zero-shot generalization. Real-world experiments are conducted on a Realman 65B robotic arm for 5 tasks, with 50 demonstrations per task and evaluation over 20 rollouts. Two camera views are used for multi-view observations. Baselines include the original BAKU, VQ-BeT, Evo-0 BAKU, and OpenVLA-OFT. Training is performed on a single NVIDIA A100 GPU, using AdamW optimizer, with a batch size of 64 and input from two camera views.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yields several findings regarding the performance of GeoAware-VLA across simulation and real-world experiments, addressing its efficacy in-distribution, its generalization to unseen viewpoints, and the impact of geometric feature integration.

**In-Distribution Performance:**
GeoAware-VLA models demonstrated the ability to maintain and, in some cases, improve performance on tasks presented from the original training viewpoints. On the LIBERO benchmark (Table I), GeoAware VQ-BeT achieved the highest overall success rate (96.8%) across task suites on original views, slightly surpassing the VQ-BeT baseline (96.3%). GeoAware BAKU also performed comparably to its BAKU baseline. A similar trend was observed on the CALVIN benchmark (Table II), where GeoAware VQ-BeT achieved 94.8% success on original views, compared to 83.8% for VQ-BeT. This suggests that the integration of a frozen geometric backbone with a lightweight projection layer does not compromise performance on familiar views. In contrast, Evo-0 BAKU, while showing some improvements, exhibited performance drops on more challenging LIBERO task suites compared to GeoAware BAKU.

**Zero-Shot Generalization to Unseen Viewpoints:**
A primary finding is the substantial improvement in zero-shot generalization to unseen camera viewpoints.
- **LIBERO Benchmark (Table I):** GeoAware variants showed significant gains, averaging over 35 percentage points compared to their respective baselines. For instance, GeoAware BAKU achieved an average unseen-view success rate of 82.6%, a substantial increase from BAKU's 37.9%. Similarly, GeoAware VQ-BeT reached 77.9% compared to VQ-BeT's 41.4%. The most pronounced performance drops for baseline models occurred in tasks requiring high-precision manipulation and complex spatial reasoning (Spatial and Long task suites), where GeoAware-VLA maintained high success rates. Critically, GeoAware BAKU (82.6%) outperformed Evo-0 BAKU (66.6%) on unseen views, indicating that fully replacing the vision encoder with a multi-scale geometric backbone is more effective than fusing geometric features as an auxiliary signal.
- **CALVIN Benchmark (Table II):** While performance degradation on unseen views was less severe for baselines than in LIBERO, GeoAware-VLA still showed consistent improvements. GeoAware BAKU achieved 91.9% success on unseen views, significantly higher than BAKU's 56.2%. GeoAware VQ-BeT achieved 94.8% compared to VQ-BeT's 82.0%. The authors attribute the less severe baseline degradation in CALVIN to its tasks involving coarser manipulation primitives, making them less sensitive to moderate object mislocalization errors.

**View-Invariance Analysis:**
Analysis of learned visual representations (Fig. 5) supported the generalization findings. t-SNE projections of visual embeddings indicated that the VGGT-based encoder used in GeoAware-VLA produced a stronger overlap of feature clusters across different camera views, suggesting a more view-agnostic representation. In contrast, baseline encoders formed more distinct, view-separated clusters. Quantitatively, GeoAware-VLA achieved a mean cosine similarity of 0.91 between embeddings of the same scene observed from training and unseen views, while BAKU and Evo-0 achieved 0.77 and 0.69, respectively. This demonstrates that the geometric backbone leads to a feature space that is considerably more stable across viewpoint changes.

**Real-World Performance:**
The performance gains observed in simulation were shown to transfer to a physical robotic platform (Fig. 6). On a series of five real-world manipulation tasks, GeoAware BAKU demonstrated a measurable performance increase over the baseline BAKU model, performing effectively on both seen and unseen viewpoints. This confirms the practical applicability of the approach.

**Ablation on VGGT Layer Selection:**
An ablation study on the LIBERO-Long subset (Table III) investigated the impact of selecting different intermediate layers from the VGGT backbone. The default configuration, utilizing 4 evenly spaced layers, achieved the best overall performance (93.0% original, 72.7% unseen). While using all 24 layers yielded a minor improvement in unseen-view generalization (74.3%), it resulted in a slight drop in in-distribution performance (90.0%) and increased computational cost. Even the weakest configuration, using only the last 4 layers, significantly outperformed the next-best baseline (OpenVLA-OFT) on unseen views (34.3% vs. 13.7%), indicating the inherent value of geometric features from VGGT regardless of specific layer selection.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The GeoAware-VLA research demonstrates that integrating strong geometric priors into the visual encoder of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models can substantially enhance their generalization capabilities to unseen camera viewpoints. This work addresses a critical limitation in current VLA models, which often struggle with viewpoint invariance, thereby restricting their deployment in dynamic and unstructured real-world environments.

The significance of this approach lies in its effectiveness and relative simplicity. By leveraging a frozen, pre-trained geometric foundation model like VGGT as the primary visual backbone, the method relieves the policy network from the complex and data-intensive burden of learning 3D consistency from scratch. The introduction of a lightweight, trainable projection layer provides an efficient mechanism to adapt these rich geometric features for downstream policy learning without incurring significant computational overhead or requiring explicit 3D data like depth maps.

The findings suggest that robust geometric grounding is a fundamental component for developing more generalizable robotic agents. The observed improvements in zero-shot generalization across both simulation benchmarks (LIBERO and CALVIN) and, importantly, on a real-world robotic platform, indicate the practical utility and transferability of the method. The demonstration that these gains hold across both continuous and discrete action spaces further highlights the versatility of the GeoAware-VLA framework.

The potential impact of this research is multi-faceted:
- **Enhanced Robot Autonomy:** Robots equipped with GeoAware-VLA could operate more reliably in environments where camera viewpoints may vary or cannot be precisely controlled, leading to more robust and adaptable autonomous systems.
- **Reduced Data Requirements:** By leveraging pre-trained geometric knowledge, the approach may reduce the need for extensive, viewpoint-diverse training datasets that are often costly and challenging to acquire.
- **Foundational Model Integration:** The methodology provides a precedent for effectively integrating other powerful 3D vision foundation models into robot learning pipelines, potentially accelerating advancements in robot perception and control.
- **New Research Directions:** The work motivates further exploration into the optimal integration strategies for geometric priors, the development of new geometric foundation models tailored for robotics, and investigations into how fine-tuning such backbones might further improve performance and adaptability across broader task domains and robot morphologies.

In summary, GeoAware-VLA represents a step towards building more generalizable and spatially aware robotic agents by demonstrating a practical and effective method for infusing implicit 3D geometric understanding into visual-language-action policies.
