# Imagined Rollouts are Kinematic, Not Dynamic: A Diagnosis of Long-Horizon World-Model Failure

> [!summary] Summary
> This research diagnoses long-horizon failures in world models, exemplified by DreamerV3, as a tendency to imagine motion kinematically rather than dynamically. A new diagnostic, the Imagined Kinematic-Consistency Error (iKCE) coupled with a perturbation protocol, reveals that world model rollouts are statistically invariant to physical regime changes like friction variations, which cause real-world policy behavior to collapse.

## Problem

- World model rollouts consistently degrade over extended temporal horizons, with the common explanation of 'compounding error' lacking specificity about the nature of these errors.
- Existing analyses of world model failure do not specify the structural content of imagined states, making it difficult to understand if models are truly learning physical dynamics or just stable representations.
- Prior research across various domains (driving VLMs, trajectory prediction) consistently indicates that models struggle with temporal dynamics, suggesting a pervasive lack of dynamic understanding.

## Method

- The paper introduces a structural diagnosis asserting that world models primarily imagine ==kinematically== (extrapolating position, velocity, acceleration) rather than ==dynamically== (reproducing motion based on forces and physical constraints).
- An operationalizable metric, **Imagined Kinematic-Consistency Error (iKCE)**, is defined to quantify the deviation of world model's imagined next states from simple kinematic extrapolations.
- A ==conditioning-perturbation protocol== is developed where physically meaningful parameters (e.g., friction) are systematically varied in the conditioning state to test the responsiveness of iKCE, thus distinguishing kinematic from dynamic imagination.

## Results

- The DreamerV3 world model on the DMC `walker-walk` task produced an imagined iKCE significantly higher than real-physics rollouts (e.g., **~180x** greater at **T=16**), demonstrating a substantial residual against a constant-velocity kinematic null.
- World model's imagined iKCE remained statistically flat across a wide sweep of friction coefficients (**0.1 to 1.7**), showing no sensitivity to a physical regime boundary (**μ=0.20**) where the real-physics policy's reward collapsed.
- The dynamic signature (sensitivity to friction) in real-physics iKCE only emerged at longer horizons (**T=32-64**), while the world model's iKCE consistently exhibited friction-invariance at all tested horizons.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - World models can satisfy criteria for predictable latent representations and accurate short-rollout bounds while still relying on a 'kinematic fallback' for long-horizon predictions.
> - The lack of dynamic imagination leads to world model rollouts being statistically invariant to changes in physical parameters (like friction) that drastically alter real-world behavior and policy performance.
> - The proposed diagnostic provides a granular tool to evaluate the 'physical realism' of a world model's internal simulations, going beyond generic error metrics to probe the structural content of imagination.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

World models constitute a fundamental component in contemporary embodied artificial intelligence, serving various functions such as latent simulators for planning and generative environments for self-supervised learning. A persistent challenge within this domain is the observed deterioration of imagined rollouts over extended temporal horizons. This issue is conventionally attributed to compounding error, a general explanation that, while accurate, lacks specificity regarding the nature of the errors or the specific feature dimensions that degrade.

This research paper introduces a structural diagnosis that reframes this long-horizon failure. It adopts a classical mechanics distinction, proposing that world models tend to imagine motion kinematically rather than dynamically. This perspective offers a third, distinct account of world-model failure, complementing existing paradigms such as predictable-representation engineering (exemplified by the Dreamer line of work) and error-compounding bounds (as explored in Model-Based Policy Optimization, MBPO). Predictable-representation engineering focuses on achieving stable and predictable latent representations, empirically validated across numerous tasks but without specifying the content of these representations. Error-compounding bounds pragmatically limit model trust to short rollouts from real states, deriving explicit quadratic bounds on the gap between model-based and true returns.

The authors contend that a world model primarily rich in kinematic features but lacking dynamic ones could satisfy both existing accounts: stable enough for Dreamer-line predictability and accurate enough within the training regime for short-rollout bounds. However, such a model would still exhibit a bias toward kinematic continuation when conditions push its rollouts beyond a physical-regime boundary. The present work distinguishes itself by providing a more granular diagnosis of the "structural content" of the imagined states, asserting that these different accounts are not mutually exclusive but rather represent distinct layers of the same failure phenomenon.

The diagnosis is further motivated by four observations from recent research, primarily in driving Vision-Language Models (VLMs), Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, and trajectory prediction benchmarks. These observations, though individually inconclusive and interpreted in isolation by their original authors, are presented here as converging evidence for a common structural signature:
- **Representational diagnostic on driving VLMs/VLAs:** Work by Schäfer et al. (2026) using EgoDyn-Bench showed that physical reasoning rates saturated with a single static frame and that visual input provided only marginal gains over text-only inputs for ego-motion understanding. This suggested that representations might under-encode temporal dynamics.
- **Sensor-degraded behavioral diagnostic:** Priyadershi and Frtunikj (2026) observed that a large driving VLA, Alpamayo R1, under heavy sensor noise, failed by "collapsing kinematic priors" while maintaining coherent but irrelevant language explanations. This was interpreted as an independent instance of kinematic-fallback failure.
- **Open-loop trajectory-prediction baselines:** Zhai et al. (2023) demonstrated that a simple 3-layer MLP using only ego vehicle kinematic state could match perception-based end-to-end planners on a standard benchmark (nuScenes open-loop L2). This indicated that perception-based planners on this benchmark might primarily perform kinematic extrapolation.
- **Physics-consistency scoring on fine-tuned VLAs:** Gao et al. (2026) introduced a Kinematic Consistency Error (KCE) metric and found no monotonic relationship between KCE and model scale or modality. This suggested a data or training deficit, rather than a capacity limitation, in learning physically consistent trajectories.

Collectively, these observations, spanning diverse methodologies and contexts, converge on the hypothesis that learned representations are dominated by kinematic features, with dynamic features crucial for physical-regime-conditional behavior being systematically under-represented. This paper aims to operationalize and test this specific structural deficit.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the recognized yet underspecified nature of long-horizon failure in world models. While the concept of "compounding error" is widely accepted, it does not distinguish the specific type of error that accumulates or the particular features that degrade in imagined rollouts. The authors observe a recurring structural signature in failure modes across various benchmarks and models, suggesting a more specific underlying problem.

The central claim advanced by this work is that current world models primarily imagine kinematically rather than dynamically. This means that they extrapolate position-velocity-acceleration trajectories that maintain internal consistency with linear kinematic update rules, but critically, these trajectories are inconsistent with the underlying physical constraints (such as mass, friction, or contact) that govern real-world motion.

To diagnose and substantiate this claim, the research sets forth three key objectives:

1. **Recast long-horizon world-model failure in kinematic-vs-dynamic terms:** This objective aims to shift the analytical framework from a generic "compounding error" to a more specific "structural content" deficit. By explicitly distinguishing between kinematic (motion described by position, velocity, acceleration without forces/constraints) and dynamic (motion requiring forces, mass, friction, contact to be reproduced) imagination, the authors seek to provide a diagnostic layer distinct from existing analyses focused on variance engineering or error compounding.
2. **Introduce an operationalizable evaluation diagnostic:** The paper aims to develop a concrete, falsifiable method to test its central hypothesis. This involves defining the "imagined Kinematic-Consistency Error" (iKCE) as a per-step diagnostic that quantifies the deviation of an imagined rollout from a closed-form kinematic null. Complementing iKCE, a "conditioning-perturbation protocol" is introduced. This protocol involves systematically varying physically meaningful parameters in the conditioning state to observe how iKCE responds, thereby distinguishing between kinematic and dynamic imagination.
3. **Instantiate the diagnostic on an open-weight checkpoint and demonstrate predicted signatures:** The final objective is to apply the proposed iKCE and perturbation protocol to an existing, publicly available world model, specifically a DreamerV3 checkpoint trained on the DeepMind Control Suite's (DMC) walker-walk task. The aim is to empirically demonstrate two specific signatures predicted by the kinematic imagination hypothesis:
 - A significant and non-degenerate kinematic-null residual in imagined rollouts compared to real-physics rollouts.
 - Statistical invariance of the imagined rollouts, as measured by iKCE, to perturbations that cross empirical physical-regime boundaries (e.g., changes in friction that cause a policy's gait to collapse), in contrast to real-physics rollouts which would exhibit sensitivity. This "regime-invariance" is presented as the defining diagnostic signature.

By achieving these objectives, the research aims to provide a more precise understanding of why world models fail over long horizons and to offer a tool for evaluating the "physical realism" of their internal simulations.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology centers on the definition and application of the **Imagined Kinematic-Consistency Error (iKCE)**, coupled with a systematic **conditioning-perturbation protocol**.

#### 3.1. A. Imagined Kinematic-Consistency Error (iKCE)

- **Definition:** For an imagined rollout {ˆx^WM_t}^T_{t=0} generated by a world model, where ˆx_t represents a chosen kinematic state vector (e.g., [x, y, v, a, θ]), the iKCE is defined as the mean squared error between the world model's predicted next state and a closed-form kinematic extrapolation of the current state:
 iKCE = (1/T) * Σ_{t=0}^{T-1} || ˆx^WM_{t+1} - kin(ˆx^WM_t) ||^2
 Here, `kin(·)` is a closed-form kinematic predictor (e.g., constant-velocity or constant-acceleration) selected to align with the world model's embodiment and output space.
- **Repurposing:** This metric adapts the Kinematic Consistency Error (KCE) introduced by Gao et al. (2026), which was originally a training loss for VLAs. Here, it is repurposed as a *test-time diagnostic* for *imagined rollouts*, measuring kinematic *inconsistency* rather than enforcing it.
- **Interpretation:** Crucially, low iKCE does not imply dynamic imagination. A world model with near-zero iKCE predicts next states that are perfectly consistent with kinematic extrapolation, thus indicating *kinematic imagination*. The signature of *dynamic imagination* is, conversely, a positive iKCE that grows with the horizon and, critically, responds to physical-regime conditioning (e.g., friction changes, contact events, boundary crossings). Therefore, iKCE is considered a necessary but not sufficient condition for certifying dynamic imagination; its *responsiveness* to physical perturbations is key.

#### 3.2. B. Conditioning Perturbations

To transform static iKCE measurements into a diagnostic that differentiates kinematic from dynamic imagination, the protocol involves systematically varying conditioning states.
- **Perturbation Protocol:** For each baseline rollout, the world model generates *K* imagined rollouts under controlled perturbations of physically meaningful parameters. These parameters are embodiment-specific but include examples such as initial velocity (v_0), friction coefficient (μ), lateral-acceleration limits (a_lat,max) for driving, terrain compliance, or payload for legged locomotion.
- **Diagnostic Signatures:** Two distinct signatures are extracted from the ensemble of {iKCE_k}^K_{k=1} values:
 1. **Shape of iKCE(∥∆∥) vs. Perturbation Magnitude:** A kinematic imaginer is expected to produce an iKCE curve that scales smoothly with the perturbation magnitude (∥∆∥), irrespective of physical regimes, because it continuously extrapolates the same linear update structure. In contrast, a dynamic imaginer's iKCE would exhibit distinct responses or non-smooth changes when physical conditions cross regime boundaries. The diagnostic is designed to be effective at horizons longer than the embodiment's characteristic gait period (e.g., >1.6 seconds for walker-class locomotion) to allow physical effects like slipping to accumulate.
 2. **Rollout-Pair Monotone Consistency:** This involves checking if imagined rollouts respect expected monotonic relationships between specific perturbation pairs that are predicted by physics (e.g., higher initial velocity implies longer stopping distance). A purely kinematic model would trivially respect linear monotonicities but is expected to fail on physical-regime-conditional monotonicities that hold only above or below specific physical thresholds. This is presented as an analog to consistency checks in other diagnostics (e.g., EgoDyn-Bench).

#### 3.3. C. Experimental Setup

- **World Model:** DreamerV3 (specifically, the NM512 PyTorch port, commit 6ef8646).
- **Task:** DeepMind Control Suite's (DMC) `walker-walk` task (a 2D 9-DOF system).
- **Training:** 1 million environment steps, seed 0, resulting in a final reward of 955 ± 30.
- **Evaluation:** The trained actor from the same checkpoint is used for both real-physics and world-model imagined rollouts.
- **Backend:** `dm_control 1.0.20` with `mujoco 3.1.6`.
- **Rollouts:** K=20 rollouts per perturbation cell.
- **Kinematic Specification:** Primarily `(z, v_z)` (root-vertical-motion, 1D), with an alternative for gait degrees of freedom used for robustness checks.
- **Extrapolation:** Constant velocity for the kinematic predictor.
- **Horizons:** iKCE evaluated at horizons T=16 and T=64 steps (25ms per step).
- **Friction Perturbations:** Surface friction varied across 13 magnitudes in the range [0.1, 1.7].
- **Joint-Noise Perturbations (Control):** Zero-mean Gaussian noise added to the joint-position channel of observations before the WM encoder.
- **Conditioning:** For WM-imagined rollouts, the model conditions on the first 5 perturbed observations before rolling out free imagination.
- **Statistical Analysis:** 95% bootstrap confidence intervals (1000 resamples) and log-log regression analysis to test for statistical flatness.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments were conducted using a DreamerV3 world model trained on the DMC `walker-walk` task. The findings provide empirical support for the proposed kinematic-not-dynamic diagnosis of long-horizon world-model failure.

#### 4.1. A. Hypothesis 1 (H1): iKCE is non-degenerate on a published checkpoint

- The DreamerV3 world model consistently produced an imagined iKCE that was substantially higher than that of matched policy-driven real-physics rollouts.
 - At horizon T=16, the imagined iKCE was approximately 180 times greater than the real-physics iKCE (7.7×10⁻³ vs. 4.2×10⁻⁵).
 - At horizon T=64, the ratio narrowed to approximately 30 times (2.6×10⁻³ vs. 8.6×10⁻⁵), which is consistent with the per-step dilution effect where the WM's smoother, post-transient tail averages down the integrated metric at longer horizons.
- This establishes that WM imagination carries a significant residual against a constant-velocity kinematic null. For reference, a trivial kinematic predictor by construction would yield an iKCE of 0. The measured ordering was `0` (trivial kinematic) < `4.2 × 10⁻⁵` (matched real physics) ≪ `7.7 × 10⁻³` (DreamerV3 WM) at T=16. This finding refutes a naive interpretation that "imagining kinematically" would imply a small absolute iKCE.

#### 4.2. B. Hypothesis 2 (H2): WM imagination is friction-invariant across a regime boundary

- **Friction Sweep Experiment:** The surface friction coefficient (μ) was swept across 13 magnitudes from 0.1 to 1.7, encompassing an empirically determined regime boundary at μ=0.20 where the trained policy's episodic reward for walking dropped below 50% of its baseline.
- **World Model's Invariance:** At the longer horizon of T=64, the WM's imagined iKCE remained statistically flat across the entire friction sweep. The maximum-to-minimum spread was 1.32×, and the 95% confidence intervals overlapped at every μ value. A log-log regression confirmed this: the WM slope's 95% bootstrap CI (β_WM = −0.009, [−0.096, +0.082]) contained zero across three different training seeds, indicating no significant sensitivity to friction.
- **Real Physics' Sensitivity:** In contrast, the real-physics iKCE under the trained actor exhibited variability, with elevated values observed in the low-μ region (e.g., means of 1.04-1.30 × 10⁻⁴ for μ ∈ [0.1, 0.3] compared to 0.70–0.92 × 10⁻⁴ for μ ∈ [0.5, 1.7]). The log-log regression for physics yielded a slope of β_phys = −0.220, CI [−0.301, −0.142], which significantly excluded zero. This indicates that real physics *is* sensitive to friction changes.
- **Policy Reward Collapse:** Concurrently, the trained policy's reward collapsed significantly across the same friction range (from approximately 650 at μ=0.5 to 200 at μ=0.10), demonstrating a clear behavioral regime change. The world model's imagined rollouts remained unresponsive to this physically meaningful shift.
- **Horizon-Emergence Test:** The dynamic signature in physics iKCE (i.e., its sensitivity to friction) emerged gradually with longer horizons, becoming statistically significant only between T=32 and T=64 steps. This is consistent with friction effects accumulating over multiple footfalls before being detectable as per-step kinematic deviations. The WM's iKCE remained statistically flat at all tested horizons.

#### 4.3. C. Controls and Robustness Checks

- **Actor Training Horizon Ablation:** Retraining the actor with an imagination horizon (h=64) matching the measurement horizon confirmed that the WM's friction-invariance was not an artifact of the default actor's shorter (h=15) training horizon.
- **Domain-Randomization Control:** A world model and policy trained with friction randomized (μ ∼ U(0.1, 1.7)) per episode still demonstrated the kinematic-not-dynamic contrast. The DR-trained WM remained statistically flat (β_DR_WM = −0.026, CI [−0.123, +0.076]), while the DR-trained policy's physics iKCE still exhibited a strictly negative slope (β_DR_phys = −0.114, CI [−0.201, −0.024]), albeit with reduced magnitude, suggesting that roughly half of the default policy's low-μ iKCE elevation was attributable to policy out-of-distribution behavior and half to genuine friction response.
- **Per-Step Structure Decomposition:** Analysis of per-step iKCE revealed qualitative differences: real physics exhibited sparse spikes corresponding to contact events, whose positions shifted with friction. The WM, however, showed a one-to-two-step encoder-decoder transient followed by a smooth, friction-invariant tail, lacking the contact-event signatures. This temporal structure was robust to actor training horizon.
- **Robustness to Kinematic-State Choice:** Repeating the protocol using a richer kinematic representation (the walker's gait degrees of freedom instead of just (z, v_z)) yielded qualitatively identical results: WM iKCE remained flat, while physics iKCE showed low-μ elevation.
- **Joint-Noise Positive Control:** Introducing zero-mean Gaussian noise directly into the joint-position channel of observations (a *kinematic* perturbation) caused *both* real-physics and WM iKCE to increase, confirming that the WM is not inherently insensitive to *all* perturbations, but specifically to *dynamic* ones.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research offers a specific and structural diagnosis of long-horizon failure in world models, reframing the problem beyond the general concept of "compounding error." By distinguishing between kinematic and dynamic imagination, the authors provide an operationalizable framework to assess the fidelity of a world model's internal simulation.

The primary significance lies in the finding that current world models, exemplified by DreamerV3, tend to imagine kinematically rather than dynamically. This means they can extrapolate trajectories that are internally consistent with simple motion rules but fail to reproduce the physical constraints and forces that govern real-world behavior, particularly when conditions cross a physical-regime boundary. This insight is crucial for understanding why policies learned through long-horizon imagination might perform poorly in real environments.

**Potential Impact:**

- **Improved World Model Design:** The diagnostic, iKCE coupled with the perturbation protocol, provides a tool for future world model development. Researchers can use it to specifically evaluate whether their models are learning dynamic features rather than merely kinematic extrapolation. This could guide architectural choices, training objectives, and data collection strategies towards models that are more physically grounded.
- **Enhanced Policy Learning:** If imagined rollouts are indeed kinematically structured but dynamically unfaithful, then policy gradients propagated through long imagined sequences would optimize actors against a diverging trajectory distribution. This could explain observed instability and weaker performance in long-horizon actor training. The research's observation that a long-horizon actor yielded significantly lower reward and exhibited instability is consistent with this prediction. Addressing the kinematic-not-dynamic failure could lead to more robust and higher-performing policies.
- **Reconciling Existing Theories:** The "kinematic fallback" explanation helps to reconcile and provide a deeper understanding of limitations observed in existing world model paradigms, such as predictable-representation engineering and error-compounding bounds. It suggests that a model could satisfy the criteria of these paradigms while still lacking true dynamic understanding.
- **Broader Applicability:** While demonstrated on a specific embodiment (DMC walker-walk), the diagnostic protocol is designed to be embodiment-agnostic. Its extension to other complex systems like quadrupeds, humanoids, and autonomous driving could provide valuable insights into the dynamic understanding of world models across diverse applications. The identified lack of dynamic reasoning in driving VLMs, as supported by prior observations, suggests a critical area for this diagnostic's application.
- **Future Research Directions:** The paper clearly outlines open questions and future work, including:
 - **Embodiment Extension:** Applying the diagnostic to systems with richer contact dynamics (e.g., quadruped walk).
 - **Fixed Open-Loop Actions:** Removing policy adaptation as a confound by evaluating imagined rollouts under identical, fixed action sequences.
 - **Explicit Conditioning:** Investigating whether architectural insensitivity or representational absence is the cause of dynamic failure by explicitly providing friction or contact indicators to the world model.
 - **Driving-WM Cross-Anchor:** Connecting the diagnostic to autonomous driving models with explicit ego-pose heads to bridge the gap between abstract embodied AI and real-world applications.

The identified limitations, such as reliance on a single embodiment and the policy-OOD confound (partially addressed by domain randomization), also provide clear pathways for extending and strengthening the findings. Overall, this work shifts the diagnostic focus for world model failures towards a more interpretable and actionable understanding of their internal representations of physics.
