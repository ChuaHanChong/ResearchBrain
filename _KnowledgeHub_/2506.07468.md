# Chasing Moving Targets with Online Self-Play Reinforcement Learning for Safer Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from the University of Washington and Stanford University developed SELF-REDTEAM, an online multi-agent reinforcement learning framework, to enhance large language model safety by allowing a single LLM to continuously co-evolve as both attacker and defender. This approach achieves up to a 95% reduction in attack success rates on safety benchmarks and generates more diverse adversarial attacks, all while preserving the model's general capabilities.

## Problem

- Existing LLM safety alignment relies on reactive patching against static vulnerabilities, creating an inefficient 'cat-and-mouse game' where defenders perpetually lag behind emerging threats.
- Traditional safety training against fixed datasets limits a model's ability to adapt to novel threats or continuously improve its safety posture.
- Current methods often lead to a trade-off where aggressive safety alignment can degrade an LLM's general conversational quality or lead to undesirable 'over-refusal' of benign queries.

## Method

- SELF-REDTEAM utilizes an ==online multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL) framework==, where a single LLM policy dynamically alternates between attacker and defender roles, grounded in a ==two-player zero-sum game== theory.
- The framework employs a =='Hidden Chain-of-Thought' mechanism==, allowing agents to develop private strategic reasoning without exposing internal thought processes to their opponents, encouraging diverse adversarial behaviors.
- A composite reward structure guides both attacker and defender, incorporating game outcomes (response harmfulness, refusal appropriateness), formatting, and revision faithfulness, with optional auxiliary ==Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT)== to maintain general capabilities.

## Results

- SELF-REDTEAM achieves up to a **9**5%** reduction** in Attack Success Rates (ASR) across **12 safety benchmarks** on Llama-3.1 and Qwen2.5 models, outperforming 'Defender-Only + SFT' methods by an average of **17.33%**.
- Adversarial attacks generated by the self-play mechanism exhibit greater semantic and lexical diversity, as evidenced by T-SNE visualizations and quantitative metrics, compared to 'Attacker-Only' approaches.
- The framework maintains general conversational quality and instruction-following capabilities (e.g., stable or improved AlpacaEval-2 scores) and prevents over-refusal on benign queries, demonstrating a balanced safety-utility outcome.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Framing LLM red-teaming as a two-player zero-sum game provides a theoretical safety guarantee: convergence to a Nash Equilibrium ensures the defender generates safe responses.
> - Online co-evolution in self-play is critical for uncovering more diverse adversarial attacks and preventing overfitting to static attack patterns, leading to more generalizable defenses.
> - The 'Hidden Chain-of-Thought' mechanism significantly enhances attack diversity and enables more nuanced decision-making for appropriate compliance versus refusal, mitigating over-refusal issues.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of safe and reliable Large Language Models (LLMs) remains a paramount challenge as these models become increasingly integrated into daily life. The current research landscape for LLM safety is largely characterized by a "cat-and-mouse game" between attackers who find vulnerabilities and defenders who patch them.

**Existing Paradigms and Their Limitations:**
- **Reactive Patching:** Conventional LLM safety alignment, exemplified by techniques like Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF), typically follows a reactive, disjoint procedure. Attackers (human red teamers or automated methods) expose harmful behaviors in a *static* model. Subsequently, defenders fine-tune the model to patch these specific vulnerabilities. This sequential approach creates a fundamental mismatch: attackers often overfit to obsolete exploits, while defenders are perpetually playing catch-up, leading to ad-hoc and incomplete vulnerability coverage.
- **Static Datasets in RLHF:** Traditional RLHF, while effective for initial alignment, optimizes models against fixed, human-curated training data. This static nature limits the model's ability to adapt to novel, emergent threats or continuously improve its safety posture in a dynamic environment.
- **Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning (MARL) for LLMs:** The concept of using multi-agent interactions, including self-play, for LLM training is gaining traction. This paradigm shifts from optimizing against static data to learning through dynamic interactions. Prior work in MARL for LLMs has explored cooperation and competition, but often with compromises due to computational challenges, such as relying on offline iterative updates, significant model quantization (e.g., QLoRA), or less powerful RNN architectures. Some self-play methods have focused on reasoning in adversarial games (e.g., SPAG), alignment in preference games (e.g., SPPO, RSPO), or offline data refinement (e.g., SPIN).

**SELF-REDTEAM's Contribution and Niche:**
This paper directly addresses the limitations of reactive patching and static alignment by proposing an **online, end-to-end, scalable MARL framework for full-parameter LLM safety training**. It uniquely positions itself by:
1. **Online Co-evolution:** Unlike offline or iterative methods, SELF-REDTEAM allows a single LLM to continuously co-adapt between attacker and defender roles *on-the-fly* within the RL loop, without iteration delays. This fosters a dynamic learning environment where defenses evolve in real-time to counter emerging attacks.
2. **Theoretical Grounding:** It provides a strong game-theoretic foundation by formulating LLM safety alignment as a two-player zero-sum game, establishing a theoretical safety guarantee if the system converges to a Nash Equilibrium. This provides a principled motivation beyond purely empirical observations.
3. **Hidden Chain-of-Thought (CoT):** Introducing a "Hidden CoT" mechanism where agents' reasoning processes are private to their opponents, enabling more strategic and diverse adversarial behaviors, which is a key innovation for complex adversarial interactions.
4. **Addressing Over-refusal and General Capabilities:** By integrating auxiliary Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) on self-distilled chat prompts, SELF-REDTEAM aims to enhance safety without degrading general conversational capabilities or leading to undesirable "over-refusal" on benign queries—a common pitfall in aggressive safety alignment.

In essence, SELF-REDTEAM represents a significant step toward a self-evolving paradigm for LLMs, moving beyond static datasets and reactive patching to a more robust, adaptive, and autonomous safety alignment mechanism grounded in competitive multi-agent learning. It pushes the boundaries of MARL applications for LLM training by demonstrating a large-scale, online, full-parameter approach tailored specifically for safety.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind SELF-REDTEAM are deeply rooted in the inherent challenges of ensuring LLM safety in real-world deployments.

**Motivation:**
1. **Pervasive LLM Safety Failures:** Despite significant advancements, LLMs continue to exhibit vulnerabilities, generating inappropriate, harmful, or factually incorrect responses. High-profile incidents, including substantial economic damages, highlight the critical need for more robust safety mechanisms beyond current capabilities.
2. **Limitations of Reactive Safety Alignment:** The conventional "disjoint attack and defense" paradigm, where attackers exploit a static model and defenders subsequently patch vulnerabilities, is fundamentally inefficient and unsustainable. This reactive "cat-and-mouse" dynamic ensures defenders are perpetually lagging behind emerging threats, leading to incomplete safety coverage and a constant need for post-hoc interventions.
3. **Lack of Generalizable Robustness:** Training attackers and defenders in isolation, or against static datasets, often leads to overfitting. Attackers may overfit to specific weaknesses of a particular model version, while defenders learn to resist only known attack patterns, hindering generalizable robustness against novel or evolving adversarial inputs.
4. **Need for Continuous Self-Improvement:** Robust safety alignment requires a mechanism for continuous adaptation and improvement. Current methods lack the ability for LLMs to autonomously discover and patch their own vulnerabilities in an ongoing, proactive manner.

#### 2.1. Key Objectives of SELF-REDTEAM

1. **Proactive Co-evolution:** To develop an online self-play reinforcement learning algorithm where a single LLM continuously co-evolves as both attacker and defender, moving from reactive patching to proactive self-improvement.
2. **Autonomous Vulnerability Discovery:** To enable the LLM to autonomously discover diverse and sophisticated adversarial prompts (attacks) by adapting to its own evolving defenses.
3. **Enhanced Safety Robustness:** To significantly improve the safety robustness of LLMs against a wide array of adversarial inputs, reducing attack success rates (ASR) across various safety benchmarks.
4. **Theoretical Safety Guarantees:** To provide a principled, game-theoretic foundation for LLM safety, proving that if the self-play converges to a Nash Equilibrium, the defender is theoretically guaranteed to generate safe responses.
5. **Maintain General Capabilities:** To enhance safety without compromising the model's general conversational quality, reasoning abilities, or instruction-following capabilities, thereby achieving a better safety-utility balance.
6. **Scalable and Efficient Training:** To achieve these objectives through a scalable and computationally efficient online MARL framework that can be applied to full-parameter LLMs without significant quantization or iterative offline processes.

By addressing these motivations and pursuing these objectives, SELF-REDTEAM aims to establish a new paradigm for LLM safety alignment that is more adaptive, robust, and theoretically grounded, moving towards truly trustworthy and secure AI systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

SELF-REDTEAM proposes a sophisticated online multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL) framework where a single LLM policy, $\pi_\theta$, dynamically alternates between the roles of attacker and defender. This design is motivated by a two-player zero-sum game theory framework.

#### 3.1. Game-Theoretic Formulation & Theoretical Safety Guarantee

The core of SELF-REDTEAM is grounded in defining LLM red-teaming as a two-player zero-sum game between an attacker ($\pi_A$) and a defender ($\pi_D$). The attacker aims to minimize a reward function $r_\theta(y_A, y_D)$, while the defender seeks to maximize it. The reward $r_\theta$ is defined such that it is -1 for an unsafe response and $\ge 0$ for a safe response.
- **Theorem 1:** The paper establishes a critical theoretical safety guarantee: if the policies of the attacker and defender converge to a Nash Equilibrium ($\pi_A^*, \pi_D^*$), then for any prompt $y_A$, the defender's response $r_\theta(y_A, \pi_D^*(y_A))$ will be non-negative, meaning the response is safe. This theorem provides a strong principled motivation for using self-play to achieve robust safety.

#### 3.2. SELF-REDTEAM Framework

As depicted in Figure 1, the process involves the following key steps:
1. **Shared LLM Policy:** A single LLM ($\pi_\theta$) acts as both the attacker and the defender, enabling efficient parameter sharing and continuous co-adaptation.
2. **Attacker's Turn (a, b):**
 - The attacker receives a seed prompt ($s$) from a support dataset (S). These seeds can describe either harmful behaviors or benign behaviors designed to mimic harmful prompts.
 - Guided by attacker instructions ($I_A$), the attacker generates a "Hidden Chain-of-Thought" ($y_{CoT_A}$) for strategic planning, followed by a public adversarial attack ($y_A$). This $y_A$ is a revised version of the seed prompt, aiming to be stealthier if harmful, or deceptively innocuous if benign.
3. **Defender's Turn (c, d):**
 - The defender receives only the public adversarial attack ($y_A$) from the attacker.
 - Guided by defender instructions ($I_D$), the defender also leverages a private Hidden CoT ($y_{CoT_D}$) to reason about the attack and formulate a public response ($y_D$).
4. **Verifier (e):** An LLM-based reward model (WildGuard-7B in experiments) acts as a verifier, evaluating the interaction. It assesses:
 - **Query harmfulness ($Q_{harm}(y_A)$):** Whether the attacker's prompt is harmful.
 - **Response harmfulness ($Res_{harm}(y_D)$):** Whether the defender's response is harmful.
 - **Response refusal ($Res_{refuse}(y_D)$):** Whether the defender refused to answer.
5. **Reinforcement Learning (f):** The verifier's scores are fed back to both roles for RL training, enabling continuous co-evolution.

#### 3.3. Hidden Chain-of-Thought (CoT)

A critical innovation is the "Think before act" mechanism, where both agents generate outputs using a CoT template: `<think> ... </think> <answer> ... </answer>`. Only the `<answer>` component is revealed to the opponent. This partial observability encourages agents to develop more diverse and strategic reasoning without exposing their internal thought processes, analogous to human strategic planning.

#### 3.4. Reward Design

The reinforcement learning process uses a composite reward structure:
- **Game Outcome Rewards ($R_{P,game}$):** Composed of two sub-rewards, pursuing opposing goals for attacker (A) and defender (D):
 - **Response Harmfulness Sub-Reward ($R_{P,res\_harm}$):** Punishes harmful responses for the defender (+$r_{harm}$ if safe, -$r_{harm}$ if harmful) and rewards them for the attacker.
 - **Response Refusal Sub-Reward ($R_{P,res\_refusal}$):** Incentivizes appropriate refusal (refusing harmful queries, complying with benign ones) for the defender (+$r_{refusal}$ if appropriate, -$r_{refusal}$ if inappropriate) and penalizes/rewards the attacker conversely. This encourages nuanced responses over blanket refusals.
- **Reward Shaping Terms:**
 - **CoT Formatting Sub-Reward ($R_{P,format}$):** Rewards adherence to the `<think> <answer>` format.
 - **Revision Faithfulness Sub-Reward ($R_{A,revision}$):** For the attacker, rewards maintaining the original harmful/benign intent of the seed prompt, preventing trivial changes and encouraging diverse, yet relevant, attacks.
- **Final Rewards:** $R_A = R_{A,res\_harm} + R_{A,res\_refusal} + R_{A,format} + R_{A,revision}$ and $R_D = R_{D,res\_harm} + R_{D,res\_refusal} + R_{D,format}$.

#### 3.5. Self-Play Adversarial Online Training Algorithm

The shared policy $\pi_\theta$ is trained using the **Re++ algorithm** (Hu et al., 2025a), a critic-free PPO variant known for stability and scalability. It estimates advantages via reward-to-go penalized by token-level KL divergence from a reference policy. Importantly, advantages are normalized across mini-batches for both attacker and defender roles, allowing a single model to learn from conflicting reward signals simultaneously.

#### 3.6. Auxiliary SFT Regularization

To counter potential degradation in open-ended conversational quality (a common side-effect of aggressive safety alignment), the method includes an optional auxiliary Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) phase. This jointly optimizes an SFT objective ($L_{SFT}$) on a self-distilled dataset ($D_{SFT}$) of general chat prompts alongside the RL objective ($L_{RL}$), ensuring the model retains fluency and helpfulness.

This comprehensive methodology, combining game theory, innovative architectural elements like Hidden CoT, carefully designed rewards, and an efficient online RL algorithm, aims to achieve a robust and adaptive LLM safety alignment.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The empirical evaluation of SELF-REDTEAM across various LLM families (Llama-3.1 and Qwen2.5) and model sizes (3B, 7B, 8B, 14B) demonstrates significant advantages over conventional safety alignment methods.

#### 4.1. Enhanced LLM Safety Robustness

- **Substantial ASR Reduction:** SELF-REDTEAM (Self-play + SFT variant) consistently reduces Attack Success Rates (ASR) across a comprehensive suite of 12 safety benchmarks. Compared to off-the-shelf instruction-tuned models, ASR reductions are as much as **95%**. For instance, on the Qwen2.5-14B-Instruct model, ASR on `WG:Test adv. harm` dropped from 0.169 to 0.080 (a 52.6% improvement), and on `WJB adv. harm`, it decreased from 0.742 to 0.372 (a 49.8% improvement).
- **Outperformance Against Baselines:** SELF-REDTEAM consistently outperforms "Defender-Only + SFT" methods, showing greater robustness with an average improvement of 17.33% across safety benchmarks. This highlights the benefit of dynamic, co-evolving attacks rather than training against static attack sets.
- **Improved Benign Compliance:** Crucially, while significantly enhancing safety, SELF-REDTEAM maintains strong benign compliance rates, preventing "over-refusal" on non-malicious queries. For example, on `WJB adv. benign`, compliance rates either remain stable or slightly improve, in contrast to methods like LLM-LAT (Table 12) which show severe degradation.

#### 4.2. Superior Diversity of Adversarial Attacks

- **Co-evolution Drives Diversity:** The online self-play mechanism is crucial for uncovering more diverse adversarial attacks. T-SNE visualizations (Figure 2) show that attacks generated by SELF-REDTEAM (blue clusters) are more dispersed, indicating greater semantic variety, compared to "Attacker-Only" methods (red clusters).
- **Prevention of Overfitting:** Attacker-Only models, trained against a static defender, tend to overfit and converge into a few dominant attack modes (e.g., repeatedly generating "disinformation campaign" prompts), even when given semantically distinct seed prompts.
- **Quantitative Diversity Metrics:** Quantitatively (Figures 3a,e), self-play methods demonstrate steady or improving trends in both lexical (Inverse Self-BLEU) and semantic (Inverse SBERT Average Similarity) diversity, converging at higher levels than Attacker-Only, which shows a decline in diversity during training.
- **Discovery of Novel Attacks:** Appendix D.4 shows that attacks generated by the trained attacker models exhibit significantly higher perplexity (up to +16.12 perplexity increase for Self-play + SFT) when evaluated by the base model, indicating that they are genuinely novel and not merely rephrased versions of existing attacks.

#### 4.3. Impact of Hidden Chain-of-Thought (CoT)

- **Enhanced Diversity:** "Self-Play (No CoT)" performs poorly in attack diversity generation, underscoring the critical role of hidden CoT reasoning in enabling attackers to explore and generate a wider range of adversarial revisions.
- **Mitigation of Over-refusals:** Hidden CoT is particularly beneficial for mitigating over-refusals, especially in Llama3.1-family models. Models trained without CoT suffered dramatically reduced benign compliance (e.g., Llama3.1-8B-IT-AB without CoT had 0.470 compliance on `WJB:Benign` vs. 0.806 with CoT), suggesting CoT enables more nuanced decision-making for appropriate compliance vs. refusal.

#### 4.4. Learning Dynamics and Co-evolution Patterns

- **Dynamic Adaptation:** Figures 3(b-d,f,g) illustrate the dynamic learning patterns. In self-play, defenders gradually achieve high response harmlessness and refusal win rates as they adapt to evolving attackers, while attacker success diminishes.
- **Robustness vs. Overfitting:** Unlike "Attacker-Only" (which maintains high success rates but shows declining revision faithfulness), or "Defender-Only" (which overfits to static attacks), the co-evolutionary dynamics of SELF-REDTEAM foster true robustness by continuously challenging and improving both agents.

#### 4.5. Scalability and Computational Efficiency

- **Comparable Training Time:** SELF-REDTEAM demonstrates reasonable computational overhead. The self-play process requires approximately 44-48% more time than "Defender-Only" baselines, which is almost proportional to the 50% increase in training samples due to online prompt generation.
- **Efficiency with SFT:** When including the auxiliary SFT phase, "Self-Play + SFT" requires nearly identical training time to the "Defender-Only + SFT" baseline (e.g., 3h 32m vs. 3h 35m for Llama-3.1-8B-IT), demonstrating the efficiency of the unified framework.
- **Full-Parameter Training:** The method achieves full-parameter LLM training without relying on significant quantization or LoRA-based methods, showcasing its direct applicability to high-capacity models.

#### 4.6. Preservation of General Capabilities

- While some safety-aligned models might suffer a degradation in general capabilities, SELF-REDTEAM + SFT generally maintains or even slightly improves instruction-following and chat capabilities (e.g., AlpacaEval-2 scores for Qwen2.5 models increased, Llama-3.1-8B-IT showed a slight decrease but was still competitive). This suggests that the auxiliary SFT regularization effectively balances safety and utility.

In summary, SELF-REDTEAM presents a powerful and efficient paradigm for LLM safety, delivering empirically verifiable improvements in robustness and attack diversity while maintaining general model capabilities.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of SELF-REDTEAM marks a significant step forward in the field of Large Language Model (LLM) safety and alignment, offering a proactive, self-improving paradigm that has several far-reaching implications.

#### 5.1. Paradigm Shift from Reactive to Proactive Safety

The most profound impact of this work is its ability to transition LLM safety alignment from a reactive "patching" model to a proactive, continuous "co-evolutionary" one. By enabling an LLM to play both attacker and defender roles against itself, SELF-REDTEAM facilitates autonomous vulnerability discovery and defense adaptation. This moves beyond the limitations of human red-teaming or training against static attack datasets, which inherently lag behind emerging threats. This proactive approach holds the promise of developing LLMs that are inherently more resilient and capable of anticipating and mitigating novel forms of misuse.

#### 5.2. Theoretically Grounded and Empirically Robust Safety

The theoretical safety guarantee, derived from game theory and the concept of Nash Equilibrium, provides a principled foundation for the method. This grounding differentiates SELF-REDTEAM from purely empirical solutions and offers a clear theoretical endpoint for alignment. Empirically, the demonstrated robustness across diverse model sizes and families (Llama and Qwen), coupled with significant reductions in Attack Success Rates (ASR) across a wide range of benchmarks, underscores its practical effectiveness. The ability to achieve up to 95% ASR reduction is a compelling testament to its power.

#### 5.3. Enhanced Attack Diversity and Generalizability

The framework's capacity to generate more diverse and novel adversarial attacks is crucial. By co-evolving with an adaptive defender, the attacker is forced to explore a broader strategy space, preventing overfitting to specific attack patterns. This leads to more generalizable defenses, as the LLM learns to defend against a wider array of evolving threats, rather than just known exploits. The Hidden Chain-of-Thought (CoT) mechanism plays a vital role here, fostering strategic reasoning and contributing to both attack diversity and more nuanced defense.

#### 5.4. Scalability and Accessibility of Advanced Alignment

SELF-REDTEAM is designed to be computationally efficient and scalable. By using a single shared model for both roles and the lightweight Re++ algorithm, it reduces the computational overhead typically associated with MARL. The fact that its training time, even with auxiliary SFT, is comparable to traditional defender-only fine-tuning makes advanced safety alignment techniques more accessible. This efficiency means that robust safety measures can be more readily adopted by a broader range of researchers and developers, including those with more constrained computational resources.

#### 5.5. Preservation of Utility and Mitigating Over-Refusal

A common challenge in LLM safety alignment is the trade-off between safety and utility, often leading to models that over-refuse benign requests. SELF-REDTEAM, particularly with the auxiliary SFT regularization, demonstrates an ability to enhance safety without significant degradation in general conversational quality or instruction-following capabilities. This balance is critical for the practical deployment of LLMs, as models that are excessively cautious become less helpful.

#### 5.6. Foundation for Future MARL in LLMs

This work represents a large-scale effort to reinterpret LLM alignment as a multi-agent interaction. By successfully demonstrating an end-to-end, online MARL framework for safety, it paves the way for broader adoption of similar self-evolving paradigms for other LLM capabilities, such as reasoning, creativity, or complex problem-solving. The emergent safety features, such as the attacker occasionally refusing harmful attacks (as noted in Appendix F.2), also open interesting avenues for research into inherent safety mechanisms in shared-parameter MARL systems.

In conclusion, SELF-REDTEAM offers a robust, principled, and scalable approach to LLM safety that moves beyond the limitations of current reactive methods. Its potential impact lies in fostering the development of more trustworthy, resilient, and autonomously self-improving LLMs, which is essential for their responsible integration into society.
