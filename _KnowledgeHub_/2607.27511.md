# Failure Detection for Surgical Robot Imitation Policies via Flow-Matching World Modeling

> [!summary] Summary
> FoMo-FD introduces a framework for detecting low-level execution failures in surgical robot imitation policies by modeling nominal visual dynamics using flow matching. The method learns from only successful demonstrations and achieved a 96.6% failure detection rate with a 1.3% false alarm rate on surgical manipulation tasks using a wrist-camera view.

## Problem

- Deploying autonomous surgical robots in safety-critical, visually complex environments requires robust failure detection to ensure patient safety.
- The scarcity of failure data in robot-assisted surgery makes it impractical and unethical to collect examples for training conventional failure detectors.
- Existing monitoring methods often rely on predefined targets, indirect signals, or frame-level anomaly detection, which may miss temporal inconsistencies or lack generalizability in dynamic surgical scenes.

## Method

- Developed **FoMo-FD**, an action-conditioned visual world model, that learns short-horizon nominal visual dynamics in a compact latent space using ==flow matching== and foundation models like ==DINOv2== and ==VAEs==.
- Implemented an ==inverse-transport nonconformity scoring== mechanism that assesses the consistency of observed visual outcomes with the learned nominal dynamics by evaluating their distance to a base distribution.
- Utilized ==conformal prediction== to calibrate task-specific failure thresholds solely from successful execution data, ensuring controlled false alarm rates without requiring failure examples.

## Results

- FoMo-FD achieved a **96.6%** Failure Detection Rate (FDR) with a **1.3%** False Alarm Rate (FAR) using the wrist-camera view across simulated and real-world dVRK surgical tasks.
- The proposed WM-NC scoring rule outperformed prediction-error-based (WM-PE) and observation-level (logpZO, RND) baselines in overall failure detection performance.
- Including action conditioning and using a longer endpoint prediction horizon (K=4) substantially improved wrist-view failure detection, increasing FDR from **42.2%** at K=1 to **96.6%** at K=4.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Inverse-transport nonconformity scoring of realized visual transitions provides a more discriminative and reliable signal for window-level consistency than direct prediction error.
> - Action-conditioned, window-level dynamics modeling is more effective at discerning low-level execution failures than assessing individual frames for anomalies.
> - The wrist-camera view, due to its proximity to the instrument tip, is crucial for detecting fine-grained manipulation failures and significantly outperforms a broader fixed-camera view.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of autonomous robotic surgery, particularly through imitation learning, presents opportunities to enhance procedural consistency, reduce surgeon workload, and broaden access to quality healthcare. However, the deployment of such systems in safety-critical and visually complex surgical environments introduces challenges, primarily the need for robust failure detection. Policy failures can arise from deployment shifts, variability in surgical scenes, and complex manipulation dynamics, making execution monitoring and timely intervention crucial.

Existing approaches to surgical execution monitoring include:
- **Explicit safety models:** Methods like gesture-conditioned classifiers or confidence predictors for human handoff require predefined monitoring targets (e.g., unsafe event labels, confidence criteria). This limits their generality, as they may not adapt to unforeseen failure types.
- **Uncertainty-aware learned policies:** Approaches estimating epistemic uncertainty from policy ensembles provide an indirect failure signal, but policy output uncertainty may not reliably capture all deviations from nominal execution, especially those driven by visual disturbances or interaction dynamics.
- **Vision-language-action (VLA) monitors:** Methods such as Sentinel's progress monitor or SAFE leverage semantic reasoning, task progress, or instruction-derived visual constraints. While effective for task-level failures, they often require failure episodes for training or detailed task descriptions. This paper focuses on low-level execution failures rather than task progression.

General runtime monitoring for learned robot policies, as highlighted by Sentinel, distinguishes between erratic failures (temporally inconsistent actions) and task-progression failures. This work specifically addresses fast detection of low-level erratic execution failures.

Current failure detection methods often rely on action-level signals (e.g., ensemble uncertainty, temporal action inconsistency, action-chunk entropy). However, these may overlook failures caused by visual disturbances or action-execution mismatches. Observation-aware methods, such as FAILDetect-logpzo and FIPER, incorporate visual distributional scores but typically operate at the observation (frame) level. This means they assess if an individual frame is anomalous, rather than if a *sequence* of observations evolves consistently with commanded actions. This distinction is significant, as some failures manifest as abnormal transitions over a temporal window rather than as individual out-of-distribution frames. RC-NF models temporal sequences but depends on object segmentation and task-prompt conditioning, which can be difficult in deformable, visually complex surgical scenes.

World-model-based failure detection is a closely related area, where models learn nominal dynamics, and deviations indicate failure. Ward et al. used predictive uncertainty from a probabilistic world model as a nonconformity score. This approach measures model confidence, not direct consistency of realized execution with expected dynamics.

This paper addresses these limitations by:
1. **Detecting low-level execution failures:** Focuses on inconsistencies between visual changes and commanded actions over short temporal windows.
2. **Operating solely on successful execution data:** A key innovation compared to methods requiring failure demonstrations.
3. **Using a window-level consistency evaluation:** Moves beyond per-timestep observation anomaly detection by assessing if visual changes over time conform to actions.
4. **Leveraging a flow-matching world model:** Directly evaluates realized execution consistency by modeling nominal visual dynamics, rather than relying on predictive uncertainty.
5. **Utilizing foundation models:** Employs DINOv2 and VAEs for robust, compact latent visual representations, building on recent trends in VFM-based world modeling.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a reliable and practical failure detection method for imitation learning policies in autonomous robotic surgery. This method aims to monitor execution at runtime and issue an alarm when the robot's behavior deviates from nominal, successful operation.

The motivation for this work stems from several critical challenges in deploying autonomous surgical robots:

- **Safety-Critical Nature:** Surgical procedures demand high levels of reliability and safety. Any unexpected deviation or failure in robot execution can have severe consequences for patient safety. Therefore, a robust mechanism to detect and potentially mitigate such failures is essential.
- **Complexity and Variability of Surgical Environments:** Surgical scenes are inherently complex, characterized by deformable tissues, dynamic lighting, ambiguous boundaries, and tool-tissue interactions. These factors can lead to visual disturbances and unexpected manipulation dynamics, making it difficult for learned policies to generalize perfectly and maintain consistent execution.
- **Scarce and Difficult-to-Acquire Failure Data:** Training conventional failure detectors typically requires examples of failure modes. In robot-assisted surgery, intentionally inducing failures for data collection is unethical and impractical due to the safety risks involved. This scarcity of failure demonstrations necessitates methods that can learn from successful executions alone.
- **Limitations of Existing Monitoring Approaches:**
 - **Predefined Monitoring Targets:** Many current methods rely on pre-specified unsafe events or confidence criteria, which limits their ability to detect novel or unforeseen failure types.
 - **Indirect Failure Signals:** Policy uncertainty, while informative, may not always directly correlate with actual task failures, particularly those arising from complex visual or physical interactions.
 - **Observation-Level vs. Temporal Consistency:** Prior visual anomaly detection often assesses individual frames. However, many critical failures manifest as abnormal *transitions* or *inconsistencies* over a sequence of actions and observations, which observation-level methods may miss.
 - **Computational Feasibility and Generalizability:** Some advanced temporal modeling approaches require object segmentation or task-specific prompts, which can be challenging to implement robustly in real-time surgical settings.

To address these motivations and limitations, the authors introduce FoMo-FD with the following specific objectives:
1. **Develop an action-conditioned visual world model:** The model should learn short-horizon nominal visual dynamics from successful demonstrations, specifically predicting the endpoint latent of an execution window conditioned on preceding visual history and intervening robot actions. This moves beyond autoregressive frame prediction to focus on action-outcome consistency over a window.
2. **Formulate failure detection as inverse-transport nonconformity:** Design a scoring mechanism that evaluates whether an observed visual outcome is consistent with the learned nominal dynamics by assessing its inverse-transport distance to a base distribution. This directly measures deviation from expected dynamic behavior.
3. **Enable training without failure demonstrations:** The entire detection framework, including model training and threshold calibration, should rely solely on successful execution data, making it applicable in data-scarce and safety-critical domains.
4. **Calibrate detection thresholds robustly:** Utilize conformal prediction on successful rollouts to obtain task-specific thresholds, ensuring a balance between missed detections and false alarms without making assumptions about future failure types or timings.
5. **Evaluate performance across diverse surgical tasks and failure modes:** Validate the method's efficacy in both simulated and real-world dVRK surgical scenarios, covering a broad range of clinically relevant manipulation tasks and failure types.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, FoMo-FD (Flow-Matching World Model for Failure Detection), integrates a flow-matching visual world model with conformal prediction for runtime failure detection. The framework is designed to detect low-level execution failures by assessing the consistency of observed visual dynamics with commanded actions within short temporal windows, relying exclusively on successful execution data.

**A. World Modeling in Latent Vision Space**

1. **Latent Visual Representation:**
 - **Observation Encoding:** Multi-view observations (e.g., fixed camera, wrist camera) are first processed by a frozen DINOv2 encoder, a vision foundation model (VFM), to extract high-dimensional patch features (y_v_t). DINOv2 is chosen for its robust visual features.
 - **Dimensionality Reduction:** To obtain compact latent representations, an offline-trained feature-space β-VAE (Variational Autoencoder) is used. The VAE encoder (E_v_ϕ) maps DINOv2 features to parameters (μ_v_t, σ_v_t) of a diagonal Gaussian distribution, from which the latent tensor z_v_t is sampled. The VAE includes a channel-utilization regularizer to prevent information concentration in a few channels. After training, the VAE is frozen, and the deterministic posterior mode (μ_v_t) is used as the compact latent representation for all subsequent steps. This process generates compact latent tensors (z_v_t ∈ R^(N_h_z × N_w_z × C_z)).

2. **Action-Conditioned Flow-Matching World Model:**
 - **Objective:** Instead of autoregressively predicting the next frame, the world model learns a parameterized approximation p_v_ψ(z_v_t+K | c_v_t) to the nominal endpoint latent distribution of a short execution window. This focuses on predicting the visual outcome after a sequence of actions.
 - **Conditioning Context (c_v_t):** This context comprises the latent visual history (Z_v_t = z_v_t-L+1:t, where L is the history length) and the intervening robot action chunk (A_t = a_t:t+K-1, where K is the prediction horizon).
 - **Flow Matching Implementation:** The endpoint distribution is instantiated using flow matching. A conditional vector field F_v_ψ(x_v_τ, τ, c_v_t) is learned to transport an interpolated latent state x_v_τ (between a base Gaussian distribution x_v_0 and the target endpoint z_v_t+K) towards the target velocity u_v_τ = x_v_1 - x_v_0. The model is trained by minimizing the squared L2 norm between the learned vector field and the target velocity (L_FM).
 - **Model Architecture:** The flow-matching backbone employs a transformer architecture. View-specific branches process the latent history Z_v_t and interpolated state x_v_τ with temporal and spatial attention. The action chunk A_t provides action tokens for cross-attention and a global action embedding for adaptive modulation, allowing the model to condition its dynamics on commanded actions.
 - **Auxiliary Loss:** An auxiliary one-step latent prediction loss (L_aux) is added during training. An auxiliary head G_v_ψ(c_v_t) predicts the next latent ˆz_v_t+1 from the same context. This loss regularizes the visual-action features learned by the world-model backbone but is not used at test time.
 - **Full Training Objective:** The model is trained using a weighted sum of the flow-matching loss and the auxiliary loss: L = λ_FM * L_FM + λ_aux * L_aux.

**B. Nonconformity Scoring and Conformal Failure Detection**

1. **Nonconformity Scoring:**
 - At test time, FoMo-FD assesses the consistency of the observed endpoint latent (z_v_t) with the preceding context (c_v_t-K). The total scoring window length is H = L + K.
 - **Inverse Transport:** Instead of predicting forward, the method uses inverse transport. The observed endpoint z_v_t is set as x_v_1, and the learned flow-matching ODE (F_v_ψ) is integrated backward from τ=1 to τ=0 to obtain ˆϵ_v_t = x_v_τ=0.
 - **Score Calculation:** If the observed visual transition is consistent with nominal dynamics, ˆϵ_v_t should lie near the Gaussian base distribution. The nonconformity score r_v_t is defined as the squared normalized base-space distance: r_v_t = ||ˆϵ_v_t||^2 / σ^2. A larger score indicates a greater deviation from nominal execution. Scores are computed independently for each camera view.

2. **Conformal Failure Detection:**
 - **Threshold Calibration:** To establish failure thresholds without requiring failure data, conformal prediction is employed. A set of N successful calibration rollouts is collected. For each view, the episode-level peak scores (s_v_j = max_t r_v_t,j) are computed and sorted.
 - **Threshold Determination:** Given a target miscoverage level α (e.g., 0.05), the conformal threshold λ_v is set to the ⌈(N+1)(1-α)⌉-th ranked peak score. This calibration method yields task-specific thresholds based solely on successful executions, without making assumptions about the nature or timing of future failures.
 - **Alarm Generation:** During deployment, a failure alarm is raised for view v if the current nonconformity score r_v_t exceeds its calibrated threshold λ_v.

**C. Experimental Setup**

1. **Tasks and Platforms:**
 - **Simulated:** Needle pickup and ring over post, conducted in NVIDIA Isaac Sim using anatomical organ scenes (SuFIA-BC). Teleoperation using a dVRK master tool manipulator (MTM) controls a simulated patient-side manipulator (PSM).
 - **Real-world:** Tissue retraction and shunt insertion, performed on a physical da Vinci Research Kit (dVRK).
 - **Visual Inputs:** Both setups provide a fixed-camera view and a wrist-camera view, resized to 224x224.

2. **Policy and Detector Training:**
 - **Policy:** An Action Chunking Transformer (ACT) policy, using DINOv2 as its visual encoder, is trained on teleoperated expert demonstrations for each task.
 - **Detector:** View-specific VAEs are trained on DINOv2 patch features (β=0.01, γ=0.5). The flow-matching world model (L=4, K=4, H=8, λ_FM=1.0, λ_aux=0.5) is trained on the same expert demonstrations in the VAE-compressed latent space (16x16x8 latent tensors).

3. **Failure Modes and Evaluation:**
 - **Failure Modes:** Twenty task-specific failure modes are categorized into grasp failures, spatial errors, scene disturbances, and sensing/actuation anomalies.
 - **Calibration and Evaluation:** N=19 successful policy rollouts are used for conformal calibration (α=0.05). Evaluation uses held-out successful rollouts (20 per simulated task, 20 per real-world task) for False Alarm Rate (FAR) and failed policy rollouts (20 per failure mode for simulated, 10 per failure mode for real-world) for Failure Detection Rate (FDR).
 - **Detection Criterion:** A rollout is counted as detected if the score for the evaluated view exceeds its calibrated threshold at least once.
 **Computational Feasibility:** The detector operates at 13.98 Hz, exceeding the 10 Hz policy loop, indicating suitability for online monitoring.

**D. Baselines:**
FoMo-FD (WM-NC, using inverse-transport nonconformity) is compared against:
- **WM-PE:** A prediction-error variant using the same flow-matching world model, scoring the L2 distance between predicted and observed endpoint latents.
- **logpZO:** An observation-level anomaly detector adapted from FAILDetect.
- **RND:** An observation-level anomaly detector adapted from FIPER.
All baselines use the same VAE-compressed visual features, conformal calibration, and evaluation splits.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of FoMo-FD across four surgical manipulation tasks (needle pickup, ring over post, tissue retraction, shunt insertion) in both simulation and real-world dVRK environments yielded several findings regarding its performance and characteristics.

**A. Overall Performance (Table I)**
- **Superiority of WM-NC:** FoMo-FD, utilizing the proposed WM-NC (Flow-Matching Nonconformity) scoring rule, demonstrated the strongest overall failure detection performance. The wrist-view WM-NC achieved a 96.6% Failure Detection Rate (FDR) while maintaining a low 1.3% False Alarm Rate (FAR).
- **WM-NC vs. WM-PE:** WM-NC significantly outperformed WM-PE, a variant using the same world model but scoring prediction error. This indicates that inverse-transport scoring of the realized visual transition provides a more discriminative window-level consistency signal than direct prediction error.
- **WM-NC vs. Observation-Level Baselines:** WM-NC also outperformed the observation-level anomaly detectors logpZO and RND. This suggests that action-conditioned, window-level dynamics modeling is more effective in discerning failures compared to assessing individual frames for anomalies.

**B. Effect of Camera View (Table I)**
- **Wrist View Efficacy:** The wrist-camera view generally provided stronger failure detection performance compared to the fixed-camera view. This is attributed to the wrist camera's proximity to the instrument tip and local tool-object interaction zone, where many failure cues are located.
- **Complementary Fixed View:** While less effective for localized manipulation failures, the fixed view remained complementary for broader scene-level disturbances. Even with only the fixed view, WM-NC improved over the strongest fixed-view baseline (45.9% FDR vs. 37.8% FDR for WM-PE).

**C. Qualitative Behavior (Figures 3 and 4)**
- **Discriminative Scoring:** Qualitative analysis of score traces (Figure 3) revealed that WM-NC more reliably separated failed rollouts from successful calibration rollouts across tasks compared to logpZO, RND, and WM-PE. This was particularly evident in complex real-world tasks like shunt insertion.
- **Correlation with Task Deviations:** Figure 4 illustrated how increases in the WM-NC score corresponded with visible task deviations in the wrist-camera frames, providing qualitative evidence that the score effectively captures inconsistencies between commanded actions and observed visual dynamics. Examples included tissue grasp loss, needle intrusion, insecure grasps, and object drops.

**D. Ablation Study: Action Conditioning (Figure 5)**
- **Impact on Wrist View:** The inclusion of commanded actions as conditioning information significantly improved failure detection, particularly for the wrist view. Combining both global action modulation and action-token cross-attention led to the highest wrist-view FDR (96.6%, up from 87.2% with no action conditioning).
- **Fixed View Insensitivity:** The fixed view showed less sensitivity to action-conditioning design, likely because it captures broader scene-level changes rather than local motion tightly coupled to specific actions.

**E. Ablation Study: Endpoint Prediction Horizon K (Figure 6)**
- **Effect of Horizon Length:** Increasing the endpoint prediction horizon K (from 1 to 4) substantially improved wrist-view failure detection. Wrist-view FDR increased from 42.2% at K=1 to 96.6% at K=4, with FAR remaining low.
- **Accumulation of Deviations:** This finding suggests that short prediction horizons (K=1) might not provide sufficient signal for visual-action consistency, whereas a longer horizon allows deviations to accumulate, making mismatches with nominal action-conditioned dynamics more detectable.
- **Fixed View Trend:** The fixed view showed a weaker and less monotonic trend with varying K, consistent with its broader field of view being less directly focused on fine-grained tool-object interactions.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The work presented in this paper introduces FoMo-FD, a novel framework for failure detection in surgical robot imitation policies, demonstrating its significance and potential impact in several areas:

**A. Advancing Safety and Reliability in Autonomous Surgical Robotics:**
- **Addressing Data Scarcity:** A core contribution is the ability to learn nominal dynamics and calibrate failure thresholds solely from successful demonstrations. This is particularly impactful in surgical contexts where collecting failure data is unethical and impractical due to safety concerns. This approach enables leveraging existing successful procedure data for robot monitoring.
- **Enhanced Runtime Monitoring:** FoMo-FD provides an execution-level monitor that detects low-level inconsistencies between commanded actions and observed visual dynamics. This is crucial for preventing unexpected behaviors in real-time during safety-critical surgical tasks.
- **Reduced Surgeon Workload and Improved Consistency:** By automatically detecting and potentially flagging deviations, the system can contribute to safer autonomous operation, potentially reducing the need for constant human vigilance and ensuring more consistent procedural execution.

**B. Methodological Innovations:**
- **Flow-Matching World Modeling for Failure Detection:** The application of action-conditioned flow-matching to model short-horizon visual dynamics is a methodological advancement. By predicting endpoint latents and scoring inverse-transport nonconformity, the method offers a robust way to evaluate visual-action consistency, distinguishing it from approaches based on predictive uncertainty or observation-level anomalies.
- **Window-Level Consistency:** The emphasis on window-level detection, evaluating whether visual changes over time conform to commanded actions, is a critical improvement over frame-by-frame anomaly detection, which may miss temporal inconsistencies inherent in manipulation failures.
- **Conformal Prediction for Thresholding:** The use of conformal calibration allows for task-specific thresholding based on successful rollouts, which is a statistically sound way to control false alarm rates without requiring assumptions about future failure types or their distribution.

**C. Practical Implications for Surgical Robot Design and Deployment:**
- **Importance of Local Visual Feedback:** The superior performance of the wrist-camera view highlights its critical role in detecting fine-grained manipulation failures. This finding can inform the design and sensor placement strategies for future surgical robotic systems, emphasizing the integration of localized visual feedback for enhanced monitoring.
- **Computational Feasibility:** The demonstrated ability to process scoring windows at a rate (13.98 Hz) suitable for online operation in real-world dVRK experiments suggests that FoMo-FD is computationally viable for practical deployment.
- **Complementary to Higher-Level Monitors:** FoMo-FD's focus on local visual-action inconsistencies makes it complementary to other monitoring systems that reason about task progress, surgical intent, or procedural correctness (e.g., VLA-based systems). A layered monitoring approach, combining low-level execution consistency with high-level task understanding, could lead to more comprehensive and robust failure detection systems.

**D. Future Directions and Broader Impact:**
- The discussion section outlines future work, including combining local and global visual alarms and developing state-conditioned conformal calibration to address temporal misalignment issues in surgical episodes. These directions promise to further enhance the robustness and adaptability of failure detection.
- This research contributes to the broader field of safe robot learning, providing a framework that is particularly relevant for high-stakes domains where robust and explainable safety mechanisms are paramount.

In conclusion, FoMo-FD represents a step toward building more robust and reliable autonomous surgical robot systems by offering an effective, data-efficient, and computationally feasible method for detecting low-level execution failures.
