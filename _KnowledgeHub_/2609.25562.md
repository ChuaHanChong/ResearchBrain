# IndustrialVLA-Bench: A Traceable Multi-Axis Evaluation of Open Robot Policy Models

> [!summary] Summary
> IndustrialVLA-Bench is a traceable, multi-axis evaluation of six released vision-language-action and world-action policy checkpoints, run under one reporting schema with three seeds per configuration. It separates clean LIBERO capability, LIBERO-Plus visual robustness, LIBERO-Para paraphrase sensitivity, and observed latency, memory and reproduction status. Clean averages differ by only 1.58 points, while robustness and paraphrase scores span 14.62 and 31.08 points.

## Problem

- Reported scores on the same benchmark come from different checkpoints, prompts, wrappers, trial counts and aggregation rules, so numeric gaps may reflect evaluation paths rather than capability.
- Standard LIBERO success is near saturation for released policies, so clean-task rankings hide large differences under camera, embodiment, appearance, or instruction shifts.
- Latency, memory, runtime architecture and reproduction failures are reported apart from task scores, though they decide whether an open policy is usable.

## Method

- ==Four-axis reporting schema== over a shared instruction-conditioned execution contract: clean capability on LIBERO, non-language robustness on LIBERO-Plus, paraphrase sensitivity on LIBERO-Para, plus deployability metrics.
- ==Official-checkpoint evidence policy== with comparability gates and ==three promotion tiers== (protocol-faithful, near-reproduction, pending-verification); only protocol-faithful rows enter strict rankings, and records store repositories, commands and deviations.
- ==Three-seed run-level aggregation== (seeds 1, 7, 42) at fixed checkpoint and inference configuration, reporting means, population standard deviations, robustness drop and clean-to-paraphrase drop.

## Results

- Clean LIBERO averages span only **1.58** points (**96.52-98.10%**), while LIBERO-Plus robustness spans **14.62** and LIBERO-Para spans **31.08** points across the same six systems.
- OpenPI leads robustness at **85.31%** with an **11.21-point** drop despite the lowest clean average; camera and robot shifts average **58.26%** and **60.35%** success.
- FastWAM falls to **51.16%** on paraphrases, a **45.39-point** drop; Spearman correlation is **-0.37** between clean and robustness, **0.09** between robustness and paraphrase.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Near-saturated clean benchmarks lose discriminative power; diagnostic axes under visual and linguistic perturbation reveal separations that leaderboard success rates hide.
> - Visual invariance and instruction grounding are separable capabilities; video-latent conditioning gives no automatic paraphrase robustness, so the axes should not be merged.
> - Comparability must be earned: tag every result with evidence status, since two language-robustness protocols disagreed by 13.47 points on average.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Overview

Open robot policies turn instructions and camera observations into actions. A high score on familiar simulated tasks does not tell a user whether a policy will cope with a changed view, a different-looking robot, or a reworded instruction. It also does not show whether the reported result can be trusted as comparable. Comparing policies is practically necessary because users need to know whether they remain stable under visual and linguistic variation and can be reproduced within available compute.

The authors test the same released policies along separate dimensions and keep evidence quality visible. The paper reports a 1.58-percentage-point range in clean LIBERO average success across all six systems. The ranges are 14.62 points in LIBERO-Plus robustness and 31.08 points in LIBERO-Para success.

### 2. One test, separate questions

IndustrialVLA-Bench is an evaluation protocol, not a new robot architecture. It evaluates six released systems: π₀.₅/OpenPI, UnifoLM-VLA-0, Xiaomi-Robotics-0, GR00T-N1.7, FastWAM, and Cosmos Policy. A VLA is a policy mapping visual observations and instructions to actions. A WAM uses learned video/world dynamics in policy learning or action generation. The “industrial” name refers to released systems, not industrial tasks. All task results are simulated and specific to the LIBERO family.

The benchmark takes four views of each system:

- Clean-task capability on LIBERO, averaged without weighting across Spatial, Object, Goal, and Long.
- Robustness on LIBERO-Plus, an unweighted mean across six non-language perturbation categories. This average excludes the separate Language condition.
- Sensitivity to meaning-preserving rewording on LIBERO-Para, using the same clean-task checkpoint without adaptation.
- Execution and reproduction evidence.

### 3. Clean scores conceal different weaknesses

The paper marks OpenPI, GR00T-N1.7, and FastWAM as protocol-faithful (PF). Among these PF rows, the reported clean averages are:

- GR00T-N1.7: 97.88%
- OpenPI: 96.52%
- FastWAM: 96.55%

The repository’s [paper-to-code audit](https://github.com/xiaoqi-7/IndustrialVLA-Bench/blob/main/PAPER_CODE_MAP.md) says the archived OpenPI and GR00T clean runs do not substantiate the paper’s stated complete three-seed results. These clean scores are what the paper reports; they have not been independently verified.

On LIBERO-Plus's non-language perturbations, OpenPI has the highest PF average at 85.31%. GR00T-N1.7 follows at 75.12%, and FastWAM at 70.69%. Clean performance does not determine robustness ordering in these reported results.

On LIBERO-Para's paraphrased instructions, the PF results are:

- GR00T-N1.7: 74.26%
- OpenPI: 71.33%
- FastWAM: 51.16%

FastWAM is near the other policies on clean LIBERO, yet its reported paraphrase performance is much lower. That illustrates different measured capabilities. It does not show why the model failed.

Within the PF subset, the three ranges are 1.36, 14.62, and 23.10 points. The authors say these findings describe evaluated checkpoints, not causal effects of VLA or WAM architectures.

### 4. Evidence is part of the result

UnifoLM-VLA-0 is a near-reproduction (NR). Xiaomi-Robotics-0 and Cosmos Policy are pending verification (PV). Only PF rows qualify for strict comparisons under the paper’s policy. Each configuration is reported as three complete runs. The standard deviations are descriptive run-to-run variation, not confidence intervals or significance tests.

The same audit flags seed mismatches for some LIBERO-Para rows and incomplete provenance for deployment measurements. Latency and memory are observations, not an efficiency leaderboard. The paper notes that these values may reflect hardware and other differences between systems.

A clean pass answers whether a checkpoint can solve familiar simulated tasks. The benchmark separates that answer from robustness, paraphrase sensitivity, and the strength of the evidence behind each result. Its main boundary is scope: the results come from simulated LIBERO-family tasks, a purposive six-checkpoint set, and three PF rows. The paper does not establish real-world safety, industrial deployment, or architecture-level causation.
