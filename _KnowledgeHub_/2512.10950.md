# E-RayZer: Self-supervised 3D Reconstruction as Spatial Visual Pre-training

> [!summary] Summary
> E-RayZer introduces a self-supervised framework for 3D reconstruction, employing explicit 3D Gaussian Splatting to learn camera parameters and scene geometry directly from unlabeled multi-view images. The system achieves competitive performance against supervised methods and outperforms prior self-supervised approaches, establishing itself as an effective spatial visual pre-training model.

## Problem

- Existing 3D vision models heavily rely on supervised learning, necessitating costly and often imperfect 3D annotations like ground-truth camera poses or dense depth maps.
- Previous self-supervised 3D methods often learn 'superficial 3D awareness,' resorting to shortcut solutions like view interpolation rather than genuinely understanding physical 3D geometry.
- There is a critical need for a foundation model in 3D vision that can effectively leverage vast quantities of unlabeled multi-view data to learn robust and transferable spatial representations.

## Method

- E-RayZer proposes a ==self-supervised feedforward 3D Gaussian Splatting model== that directly predicts camera intrinsics and extrinsics, alongside explicit 3D scene geometry, from unlabeled multi-view inputs.
- The framework removes image index embeddings from the camera estimation transformer and utilizes a ==VGGT-style multi-view transformer==, promoting true 3D understanding over view interpolation.
- A novel ==visual-overlap learning curriculum== is introduced to stabilize training and ensure convergence for explicit 3D models, progressively introducing more challenging samples.

## Results

- E-RayZer significantly outperforms prior self-supervised methods like RayZer in camera pose estimation accuracy across multiple datasets and achieves comparable novel-view synthesis quality.
- The model demonstrates performance on par with or superior to a re-implemented supervised VGGT* model on several out-of-domain datasets, particularly for stricter pose estimation metrics.
- Pre-training with E-RayZer's learned weights leads to **substantial performance improvements** for supervised models and results in superior feature generalization on downstream tasks, including multi-view depth, pose, and flow estimation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicit 3D geometry modeling with 3D Gaussian Splatting, coupled with differentiable rendering, compels the model to learn truly 3D-aware and geometrically grounded representations, effectively preventing shortcut solutions.
> - A fine-grained, visual-overlap-based learning curriculum is essential for stabilizing and efficiently training self-supervised explicit 3D reconstruction models from scratch.
> - Self-supervised pre-training with explicit 3D representations can yield performance comparable to, or even surpass, supervised methods on various 3D tasks, providing robust and transferable features for diverse downstream applications.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in "E-RayZer" operates at the intersection of self-supervised learning, 3D computer vision, and representation learning. In recent years, self-supervised pre-training has emerged as a foundational paradigm, driving significant advancements in large models for language, 2D images, and video. These models leverage vast amounts of unlabeled data to learn robust and generalizable representations, capturing language semantics, visual concepts, and temporal dynamics.

However, the paper identifies a gap in this landscape: the limited exploration of self-supervised learning for acquiring truly 3D-aware representations from multi-view images. Current approaches in 3D vision, particularly for tasks like 3D reconstruction and camera pose estimation, predominantly rely on fully supervised learning. This often involves using 3D pseudo-labels generated by traditional Structure-from-Motion (SfM) systems, such as COLMAP. This reliance introduces several limitations: SfM-derived labels can be inherently inefficient, prone to inaccuracies, and ultimately restrict the scalability of models due to the cost and complexity of obtaining dense, accurate 3D annotations.

Previous efforts in self-supervised 3D vision, exemplified by methods like RayZer, have attempted to learn scene representations from 2D images through novel-view synthesis. While these methods can achieve high-fidelity rendering, the paper argues that they often exhibit only superficial 3D awareness. RayZer, for instance, infers 3D indirectly through latent-space view synthesis, leading to potential "shortcut solutions" like frame interpolation without necessarily enforcing genuine 3D understanding. This can result in learned representations and camera poses that are not physically or spatially meaningful.

E-RayZer aims to address these limitations by proposing a self-supervised pre-training framework that learns geometrically grounded 3D-aware representations directly from unlabeled multi-view images. It departs from implicit 3D representations by adopting explicit 3D geometry (3D Gaussians) and introduces a robust learning curriculum to stabilize training. By doing so, it seeks to establish a new foundation for 3D spatial visual pre-training, analogous to the role of foundation models in other modalities, thereby enabling more scalable and effective 3D perception in the physical world.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop E-RayZer, a self-supervised 3D vision model capable of learning geometrically grounded representations directly from unlabeled multi-view images. This overarching goal is driven by several specific motivations and objectives:

1. **Overcome Limitations of Supervised 3D Learning:** The current dependence of many 3D vision models on supervised learning, often relying on 3D pseudo-labels generated by SfM systems, is identified as a critical bottleneck. These pseudo-labels are resource-intensive to obtain, can be imperfect, and limit the scalability of 3D models to vast, unlabeled datasets. The motivation is to move beyond this reliance by developing a method that can learn 3D understanding without explicit 3D annotations.

2. **Enhance 3D Awareness beyond Implicit Methods:** Prior self-supervised methods for 3D vision, such as RayZer, learn by performing novel-view synthesis in a latent space. While effective for rendering, this approach may not enforce true 3D understanding. The paper highlights that such implicit representations can lead to "shortcut solutions," like simple view interpolation, rather than learning physically meaningful 3D geometry and camera poses. A key objective is to address this by moving to *explicit* 3D modeling.

3. **Develop a Truly Self-supervised 3D Reconstruction Model:** E-RayZer aims to be the first feed-forward 3D Gaussian splatting reconstruction model that is trained entirely from scratch using zero 3D annotation. This involves predicting both camera parameters and explicit 3D scene geometry from unlabeled input images, then using photometric losses on rendered novel views for self-supervision.

4. **Ensure Convergence and Scalability for Explicit 3D:** The use of explicit 3D geometry, while offering benefits in 3D awareness, introduces challenges related to training convergence, especially when learning from scratch. RayZer's previous attempts with explicit 3D reportedly faced non-convergence issues. A crucial objective is to develop a robust training strategy to stabilize this process. This leads to the proposed "fine-grained learning curriculum" based on visual overlap, which also aims to harmonize heterogeneous data sources for improved scalability without requiring any supervision for data curation.

5. **Establish a Paradigm for Spatial Visual Pre-training:** Beyond merely performing 3D reconstruction, a core motivation is to demonstrate E-RayZer's utility as a generalizable pre-training framework for downstream 3D vision tasks. The objective is to show that the learned representations are genuinely 3D-aware and transfer effectively to tasks requiring 3D understanding, outperforming existing visual pre-training models that may lack this spatial grounding. This positions E-RayZer as a foundation model for 3D perception.

In summary, the motivation stems from the perceived limitations of current 3D vision training paradigms and the desire to extend the success of self-supervised foundation models to the spatial domain, yielding truly 3D-aware and scalable representations.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

E-RayZer proposes a self-supervised 3D vision model that learns camera parameters (poses and intrinsics) and explicit 3D scene geometry directly from unlabeled multi-view images. Its methodology builds upon the principles of photometric self-supervision but fundamentally deviates from prior implicit 3D methods by incorporating explicit 3D Gaussian representations and a tailored learning curriculum.

**Core Architecture (Building on RayZer's Foundation):**
E-RayZer processes input images (typically video frames) by splitting them into two non-overlapping subsets: a "reference" set (I_ref) for inferring scene properties and a "target" set (I_tgt) for providing self-supervision.

1. **Camera Pose and Intrinsics Estimation:**
 - A multi-view transformer (f_cam) is employed to predict the camera intrinsics (K, shared across views) and extrinsics (T, rotation and translation) for all input images.
 - Unlike RayZer, which used image index embeddings that could lead to view interpolation shortcuts, E-RayZer removes these embeddings.
 - It adopts a VGGT-style multi-view transformer with alternating local-global attention, which naturally defines association relationships without relying on explicit indexing.
 - The model performs pairwise pose prediction, where camera tokens from a canonical view and a target view are concatenated to regress their relative camera pose.
 - Each camera (K, T_i) is then converted into a pixel-aligned Plücker ray map (R_plk).

2. **Gaussian-based Scene Reconstruction (Explicit 3D):**
 - This is the core differentiator from RayZer's implicit latent scene representation.
 - The "posed" reference views (I_ref) and their corresponding Plücker ray maps (R_plk_ref) are encoded into latent tokens (s_ref) using a transformer (f_scene). This transformer also uses the VGGT-style local-global alternating attention.
 - A lightweight decoder (f_gauss) then directly transforms these updated image tokens (s_ref) into per-pixel 3D Gaussian parameters. Each Gaussian (g_i) is defined by its distance along the ray (d_i), orientation (q_i), spherical harmonic coefficients (C_i), scale (s_i), and opacity (α_i). These collective 3D Gaussians (G) represent the explicit scene geometry.

3. **Differentiable Rendering and Self-supervision:**
 - With the predicted 3D Gaussians (G) representing the scene, E-RayZer uses its self-predicted target-view cameras (C_tgt) to render these Gaussians into target views (I_hat_tgt).
 - This rendering step utilizes a closed-form differentiable rendering operation for 3D Gaussians (e.g., modified gsplat), eliminating the need for a separate transformer-based renderer as in RayZer. This is stated to improve both rendering efficiency and 3D-awareness.
 - Self-supervision is applied via a photometric loss (combination of Mean Squared Error and a Perceptual Loss) between the rendered target views (I_hat_tgt) and the actual target views (I_tgt). This loss drives the learning of accurate camera poses and explicit scene geometry.

**Sequence Curriculum Based on Visual Overlap:**
To address the challenges of converging an explicit 3D model from scratch and to scale to heterogeneous data, E-RayZer introduces a fine-grained learning curriculum:

1. **Problem Addressed:** Explicit 3D models are prone to training instability. Traditional curricula (e.g., based on fixed frame intervals) are inaccurate for real-world data and not scalable.
2. **Curriculum Design:** The curriculum is based on the *visual overlap* between input views, providing a proxy for scene complexity and camera motion.
3. **Data Labeling (Unsupervised Approximation):**
 - For each training sequence, a "spacing profile" is computed by sampling frame triplets at various temporal spacings (Δt).
 - Pairwise visual overlap (o(i,j)) is calculated for each pair in the triplet, and averaged to get o_tri(i, Δt). Averaging over multiple triplets yields O_u(Δt) for sequence 'u'.
 - Two instantiations for overlap calculation are used:
 - **Geometric Overlap (o_geo):** Uses UFM covisibility, which is trained with 3D annotations (though E-RayZer itself remains self-supervised).
 - **Semantic Overlap (o_sem):** Uses DINOv2 cosine similarity between image features, providing a fully unsupervised approximation of visual overlap.
4. **Training-time Sampling:**
 - As training progresses (controlled by a curriculum progress parameter 's' from 0 to 1), a visual overlap lower limit o(s) is linearly decreased (from high overlap/easy to low overlap/hard).
 - This o(s) is used to look up the appropriate frame spacing (Δt_u(s)) for each sequence from its precomputed profile. This ensures that the model starts with easier samples (high visual overlap, small camera motion) and gradually progresses to harder ones.
 - The temporal span of the sampled sequence is then determined by (K_ref - 1) * Δt_u(s), where K_ref is the number of reference views.
5. **Benefits:** This curriculum stabilizes training, improves convergence, and adaptively aligns varying camera motion distributions across diverse datasets, making the framework more scalable.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several key findings across various experiments, demonstrating E-RayZer's capabilities in self-supervised 3D reconstruction and as a spatial visual pre-training framework.

#### 4.1. Pose Estimation and Novel-view Synthesis (Self-supervised Context)

- **Comparison with (Partially) Self-supervised Methods (Table 1, Figure 4):**
 - E-RayZer consistently outperforms SPFSplat, a method initialized from a supervised model (MASt3R), on most metrics for both novel-view synthesis (PSNR) and pose estimation (RPA@5°/15°/30°), despite E-RayZer being fully self-supervised from scratch.
 - Significantly, E-RayZer shows substantially higher pose estimation accuracy compared to RayZer across all experimental setups (e.g., on WildRGB-D, DL3DV, ScanNet++). RayZer often exhibits very low RPA scores (e.g., 0.0-0.2% @5°), indicating its pose space is less geometrically grounded.
 - E-RayZer achieves comparable novel-view synthesis quality to RayZer. Qualitative results (Figure 4) show E-RayZer handling low-texture regions better than RayZer, where RayZer's view interpolation can struggle.
 - These results suggest that explicit 3D modeling in E-RayZer leads to more geometrically meaningful and interpretable pose representations, while RayZer's implicit approach may over-optimize for rendering quality without true 3D awareness.

#### 4.2. E-RayZer as Self-supervised Pre-training

- **E-RayZer Initialization Benefits Supervised Models (Table 2):**
 - **Comparison with Supervised VGGT\*:** E-RayZer's performance, as a purely self-supervised model, is comparable to or sometimes surpasses that of VGGT\* (a supervised state-of-the-art model re-implemented with matched architecture) on several out-of-domain (OOD) datasets (e.g., WildRGB-D, CamLand, BlendedMVS). E-RayZer also shows higher accuracy on the stricter RPA@5° metric.
 - **Effectiveness of Pre-training:** When VGGT\* is initialized with E-RayZer's pre-trained weights, it yields significant performance improvements over training VGGT\* from scratch (e.g., RPA@5° on DL3DV improves from 79.6% to 87.3%). This demonstrates E-RayZer's efficacy as a pre-training framework for visual geometry learning, indicating complementarity between self-supervised pre-training and supervised fine-tuning.

- **Probing Representations on Downstream Tasks (Tables 3 & 4, Figure 5 in Supp.):**
 - **Multi-view Depth and Pose Estimation (3D Tasks, Table 3):** E-RayZer achieves the best performance across all datasets (ScanNet++, BlendedMVS) and settings (frozen-backbone, full-finetuning).
 - In the frozen-backbone setting, E-RayZer significantly outperforms all other vision encoders (DINOv2/v3, CroCo v2, VideoMAE V2, Perception Encoder, and RayZer) on both depth estimation (AbsRel, δ<1.25) and pose estimation (RPA).
 - With full fine-tuning, E-RayZer further improves its performance, maintaining its lead and substantially surpassing RayZer and VideoMAE V2. This indicates strong 3D-awareness and generalization ability in E-RayZer's learned features.
 - **Pairwise Flow Estimation (2.5D Task, Table 4):** E-RayZer achieves competitive performance on pairwise flow prediction on StaticThings3D, closely following RayZer and outperforming other baselines. While RayZer, with its implicit 3D, has a slight advantage for low-level motion, E-RayZer demonstrates that its explicit 3D representation learning captures meaningful spatial correspondences relevant to 2.5D tasks.
 - **Learned Feature Representations (Figure 5 in Supp.):** Qualitative visualization of feature maps shows E-RayZer producing more stable and spatially consistent patterns that align with scene structure, compared to RayZer's features which exhibit noticeable color shifts between frames. This supports the claim of geometrically grounded features.

#### 4.3. Ablation Study

- **Data Mixing and Scaling (Table 5, Table 9 in Supp.):**
 - E-RayZer exhibits scaling behavior similar to supervised models like VGGT\*. Training on data with broader distributions generally improves generalization (e.g., 7-dataset mix vs. single datasets).
 - Data quality plays a significant role; training on DL3DV (high-quality, diverse camera trajectories) yields better results than on larger but noisier datasets like RealEstate10K or SpatialVID.
 - The self-supervised E-RayZer demonstrates performance on par with supervised VGGT\* (especially when VGGT\* is not trained on very large mixed data), suggesting that large-scale self-supervision alone can foster robust 3D understanding. This implies that data diversity and quality are more critical for scalability than explicit 3D supervision.

- **Curriculum Learning (Table 6):**
 - The proposed visual-overlap-based curricula consistently outperform baselines with "no curriculum" and "frame-interval-based curriculum" across different training data settings (DL3DV and 7-dataset mixture).
 - Both semantic and geometric overlap variants perform comparably, confirming the effectiveness of the fine-grained curriculum strategy in improving self-supervised pose estimation and reconstruction, while eliminating the need for manual tuning per dataset.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The E-RayZer research presents a notable advancement in the field of 3D computer vision, particularly within the paradigm of self-supervised learning. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Pioneering Self-supervised Explicit 3D Reconstruction:** E-RayZer is introduced as the first truly self-supervised, feed-forward 3D Gaussian splatting reconstruction model. This eliminates the reliance on any 3D annotations (such as ground-truth camera poses or dense depth maps, typically derived from SfM systems) during its training from scratch. This is a crucial step towards reducing the annotation bottleneck in 3D vision, which traditionally limits scalability and applicability.

2. **Establishing a Paradigm for Spatial Visual Pre-training:** The work positions explicit 3D reconstruction as a foundational task for "spatial visual pre-training." By learning to reconstruct 3D geometry and camera poses directly, E-RayZer develops representations that are inherently 3D-aware and geometrically grounded. This contrasts with prior self-supervised methods that often infer 3D indirectly or via proxy tasks, sometimes leading to superficial 3D understanding or shortcut solutions like view interpolation. The learned representations have been shown to transfer effectively to diverse downstream 3D tasks, outperforming existing visual pre-training models that may lack this explicit spatial grounding.

3. **Addressing Convergence and Scalability Challenges:** The introduction of a fine-grained learning curriculum based on "visual overlap" is significant for practical implementation. Explicit 3D models can be challenging to train from scratch due to convergence issues. This curriculum stabilizes training by progressively introducing more difficult samples and effectively harmonizes heterogeneous data sources without requiring manual tuning or supervision. This design contributes to the scalability of self-supervised 3D learning to large, diverse, and unlabeled multi-view datasets.

4. **Enhancing Performance of Supervised Models:** The research demonstrates that E-RayZer's self-supervised pre-training can significantly benefit state-of-the-art supervised 3D models. Initializing a supervised model like VGGT\* with E-RayZer's weights leads to notable performance improvements. This highlights that the knowledge learned through self-supervision is complementary to supervised learning, suggesting a powerful hybrid paradigm for future 3D vision systems.

5. **Broadening the Scope of Foundation Models:** By effectively extending the success of self-supervised pre-training to the 3D domain, E-RayZer contributes to the development of "foundation models" for spatial perception. These models could provide a versatile backbone for a wide range of applications, from robotics and autonomous driving to augmented reality and content creation, where robust 3D understanding is critical. The ability to learn from abundant unlabeled video data found online could unlock new frontiers for 3D AI.

6. **Insights into Data Importance:** The ablation studies provide valuable insights, indicating that data diversity and quality can be more impactful than sheer quantity for training self-supervised 3D models. This suggests that future efforts in 3D data collection and curation should prioritize rich, diverse camera motions and scene types rather than simply accumulating large volumes of potentially noisy data.

While current limitations include operating primarily on static scenes and assuming continuous video frames, E-RayZer represents a foundational step towards scalable, truly 3D-aware artificial intelligence, moving away from explicit 3D supervision and towards more autonomous learning from raw visual observations.
