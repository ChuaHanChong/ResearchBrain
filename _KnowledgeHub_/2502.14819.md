# Learning from Reward-Free Offline Data: A Case for Planning with Latent Dynamics Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at NYU and Meta FAIR developed Planning with Latent Dynamics Models (PLDM), a method leveraging reconstruction-free Joint Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPA) to learn environment dynamics from unannotated offline data. This approach demonstrated strong generalization to new tasks and previously unseen environments in various navigation domains, outperforming existing model-free reinforcement learning techniques.

## Problem

- Traditional Reinforcement Learning (RL) agents require extensive, costly, and often dangerous online interactions, and crafting precise reward functions for complex real-world tasks is challenging.
- A vast amount of passively collected, reward-free trajectory data exists, but effectively learning generalizable skills for autonomous agents from such unlabeled and potentially suboptimal offline data remains an unsolved problem.
- There was a lack of systematic comparative analysis between model-free (RL-based) and model-based (control-based) approaches in the reward-free offline setting, especially concerning how different data characteristics influence their generalization abilities.

## Method

- Introduced ==Planning with Latent Dynamics Models (PLDM)==, a model-based optimal control method that learns an explicit dynamics model of the environment in a latent space directly from reward-free offline data.
- PLDM employs a ==Joint Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA)== for reconstruction-free self-supervised learning of latent dynamics, incorporating ==VICReg-inspired regularization==, inverse dynamics modeling, and temporal smoothness objectives to learn robust representations.
- Utilizes ==Model Predictive Control (MPC)== with MPPI at test time to plan actions by minimizing a cost function in the latent space, allowing for flexible adaptation to diverse tasks.

## Results

- PLDM consistently outperformed all model-free RL methods in generalizing to unseen maze layouts and showed strong zero-shot adaptation to new tasks like state avoidance, simply by inverting the planning objective.
- PLDM and GCIQL exhibited superior sample efficiency, achieving nearly **80%** success with only a few thousand transitions, and PLDM, HILP, and GCIQL proved most robust to low-quality data generated by random policies.
- Ablation studies confirmed the necessity of PLDM's reconstruction-free JEPA objective components for learning effective representations, with direct comparisons showing it significantly outperformed reconstruction-based approaches like DreamerV3 and TD-MPC2 in the reward-free offline setting.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Model-based planning with latent dynamics (PLDM) provides superior generalization to unseen environment layouts and novel tasks when learning from reward-free offline data compared to state-of-the-art model-free methods.
> - Reconstruction-free self-supervised learning, as implemented in JEPA, is crucial for learning effective and generalizable latent dynamics models from passive, unannotated data, outperforming reconstruction-based world models in this context.
> - The robustness and data efficiency of different learning paradigms for reward-free offline data vary significantly based on data quality, quantity, and diversity, with PLDM showing strong performance across varying data conditions.
