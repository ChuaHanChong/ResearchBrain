# Learning from Reward-Free Offline Data: A Case for Planning with Latent Dynamics Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at NYU and Meta FAIR developed Planning with Latent Dynamics Models (PLDM), a method leveraging reconstruction-free Joint Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPA) to learn environment dynamics from unannotated offline data. This approach demonstrated strong generalization to new tasks and previously unseen environments in various navigation domains, outperforming existing model-free reinforcement learning techniques.

## Problem

- Traditional Reinforcement Learning (RL) agents require extensive, costly, and often dangerous online interactions, and crafting precise reward functions for complex real-world tasks is challenging.
- A vast amount of passively collected, reward-free trajectory data exists, but effectively learning generalizable skills for autonomous agents from such unlabeled and potentially suboptimal offline data remains an unsolved problem.
- There was a lack of systematic comparative analysis between model-free (RL-based) and model-based (control-based) approaches in the reward-free offline setting, especially concerning how different data characteristics influence their generalization abilities.

## Method

- Introduced ==Planning with Latent Dynamics Models (PLDM)==, a model-based optimal control method that learns an explicit dynamics model of the environment in a latent space directly from reward-free offline data.
- PLDM employs a ==Joint Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA)== for reconstruction-free self-supervised learning of latent dynamics, incorporating ==VICReg-inspired regularization==, inverse dynamics modeling, and temporal smoothness objectives to learn robust representations.
- Utilizes ==Model Predictive Control (MPC)== with MPPI at test time to plan actions by minimizing a cost function in the latent space, allowing for flexible adaptation to diverse tasks.

## Results

- PLDM consistently outperformed all model-free RL methods in generalizing to unseen maze layouts and showed strong zero-shot adaptation to new tasks like state avoidance, simply by inverting the planning objective.
- PLDM and GCIQL exhibited superior sample efficiency, achieving nearly **80%** success with only a few thousand transitions, and PLDM, HILP, and GCIQL proved most robust to low-quality data generated by random policies.
- Ablation studies confirmed the necessity of PLDM's reconstruction-free JEPA objective components for learning effective representations, with direct comparisons showing it significantly outperformed reconstruction-based approaches like DreamerV3 and TD-MPC2 in the reward-free offline setting.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Model-based planning with latent dynamics (PLDM) provides superior generalization to unseen environment layouts and novel tasks when learning from reward-free offline data compared to state-of-the-art model-free methods.
> - Reconstruction-free self-supervised learning, as implemented in JEPA, is crucial for learning effective and generalizable latent dynamics models from passive, unannotated data, outperforming reconstruction-based world models in this context.
> - The robustness and data efficiency of different learning paradigms for reward-free offline data vary significantly based on data quality, quantity, and diversity, with PLDM showing strong performance across varying data conditions.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research addresses a crucial and highly active area in modern AI: enabling autonomous agents to learn generalizable skills from large, passively collected, and often suboptimal data without explicit reward signals. This challenge is particularly acute in real-world robotics, where online interaction is costly, dangerous, or time-consuming, and human-engineered reward functions are often difficult to specify or prone to brittleness.

The paper situates itself at the intersection of two dominant paradigms for building intelligent agents:
1. **Reinforcement Learning (RL):** Traditionally, online RL requires extensive trial-and-error interaction with the environment, which is impractical for many real-world systems. Offline RL emerged to address this by learning from pre-recorded datasets. This work specifically focuses on **reward-free offline RL**, a more challenging variant where the dataset lacks any reward annotations. This paradigm is gaining traction as it mimics the abundance of unlabeled, unstructured data available in the real world (e.g., robot logs, dashcam footage). Prior work in this area includes methods like HILP and HIQL, which learn general behaviors or representations from such data.
2. **Optimal Control / Model-Based RL:** This paradigm focuses on learning or utilizing a dynamics model of the environment to plan actions. While classical optimal control assumes a known model, modern approaches learn these models from data. The advantage of model-based methods is their potential for greater sample efficiency and generalization, as a well-learned model can, in principle, simulate hypothetical scenarios and predict outcomes.

The paper identifies a significant gap in the literature: a systematic comparative analysis of model-free (RL-based) and model-based (control-based) approaches in the **reward-free offline setting**, particularly concerning how different characteristics of the offline data (quality, quantity, diversity, optimality) influence their respective strengths and weaknesses. Prior work often relied on expert or exploratory data without a granular analysis of these factors.

Furthermore, this work is deeply connected to the broader trend of **self-supervised learning (SSL)** for representation learning. By proposing PLDM, which learns latent dynamics using a reconstruction-free SSL objective (JEPA), the authors align with the growing conviction that rich, task-agnostic representations are fundamental for general intelligence. The debate around the utility of reconstruction-based objectives versus predictive/contrastive objectives in SSL for control is a lively one, and this paper provides strong empirical evidence in favor of the latter.

Finally, the aspiration to build "general purpose systems" that perform well on "unseen combinations of tasks and environments" from "large collections of existing suboptimal trajectories" places this research squarely within the vision for **foundation models for decision-making**. By rigorously testing generalization properties across diverse navigation environments, the authors contribute to understanding what capabilities are necessary for such foundational agents.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching goal of this research is to advance the development of AI agents capable of operating effectively in novel tasks and environments by learning from readily available, unannotated, and potentially suboptimal offline data. The authors are motivated by the practical limitations of traditional RL and optimal control paradigms in real-world scenarios:

- **Cost and Danger of Online Interaction:** Training RL agents often demands an exorbitant number of interactions with the environment (e.g., hundreds of years of real-time experience for robotic manipulation), making it impractical or unsafe for physical systems.
- **Difficulty of Reward Engineering:** Specifying precise reward functions for complex real-world tasks is challenging, often requiring expert knowledge and iterative fine-tuning. This process is time-consuming and can lead to brittle solutions that do not generalize well.
- **Abundance of Unlabeled Data:** In contrast to the scarcity of online interaction and labeled rewards, there is a vast and ever-growing supply of passively collected, reward-free trajectory data (e.g., sensor readings from robots, human driving footage, simulation logs). Tapping into this resource is critical for scalable AI.

Given these motivations, the key objectives of the paper are:

1. **Systematic Evaluation:** To conduct a comprehensive and systematic comparative evaluation of state-of-the-art model-free reinforcement learning methods (specifically goal-conditioned and zero-shot RL) and a model-based optimal control method (PLDM) in the reward-free offline setting.
2. **Understanding Data Impact:** To meticulously investigate how different characteristics of the offline data—such as its *diversity* (e.g., coverage of environment layouts), *quality* (e.g., collected from random policies vs. more exploratory ones), *quantity* (dataset size), and *trajectory length* (to test stitching capabilities)—influence the performance and generalization abilities of these different learning paradigms.
3. **Proposing an Effective Model-Based Approach:** To introduce and validate Planning with a Latent Dynamics Model (PLDM) as a robust and generalizable solution for learning from reward-free offline data, particularly emphasizing its ability to generalize to unseen environments and tasks.
4. **Deriving Practical Guidelines:** To synthesize the experimental findings into a set of actionable guidelines for practitioners, helping them select the most appropriate method based on the specifics of their available data and desired generalization requirements.

By addressing these objectives, the authors aim to shed light on the comparative strengths and weaknesses of different approaches, providing a roadmap for developing more robust and adaptable AI systems in data-rich, reward-poor environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors design a systematic comparative study to evaluate six distinct methods for learning from reward-free offline data across various navigation tasks and data conditions.

#### 3.1. Problem Setting

The problem is framed within a Markov Decision Process (MDP) where agents have access to an offline dataset of state-action sequences $(s_0, a_0, s_1, \dots, a_{T-1}, s_T)$ but critically, *without reward annotations*. The goal is to learn a policy that can solve a variety of tasks, often specified as reaching a goal state ($s_g$) or avoiding certain states, in potentially new environments or layouts.

#### 3.2. Evaluated Methods

The study categorizes the methods into two main paradigms:

- **Reward-Free Offline Reinforcement Learning (Model-Free):**
 - **Goal-Conditioned RL:** These methods learn policies to reach specific goal states sampled from the dataset.
 - **GCIQL (Goal-Conditioned Implicit Q-Learning):** An adaptation of the strong offline RL algorithm IQL.
 - **HIQL (Hierarchical Implicit Q-Learning):** A hierarchical GCRL method that trains both high-level and low-level policies.
 - **CRL (Contrastive Representation Learning):** Uses contrastive learning to learn state-goal compatibility, which is then used to train a policy.
 - **GCBC (Goal-Conditioned Behavior Cloning):** A simple baseline that directly clones observed behaviors towards goals.
 - **Zero-Shot Methods:** Aim to solve arbitrary tasks beyond simple goal-reaching by learning general representations.
 - **HILP (Hilbert Representations):** Learns state representations where latent space distance approximates the number of steps between states, enabling direction-conditioned policies.

- **Planning with a Latent Dynamics Model (PLDM - Proposed Method):**
 - **Core Idea:** Unlike model-free methods, PLDM learns an explicit dynamics model of the environment in a latent space and uses it for planning.
 - **Architecture:** It employs the **Joint Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA)**, specifically designed for reconstruction-free self-supervised learning.
 - An **Encoder** ($h_\theta$) maps raw observations ($s_t$) to latent states ($z_t$).
 - An **Ensemble of Predictors** ($f_\theta^k$) learns the latent dynamics, predicting future latent states ($\hat{z}_t$) from current latent states and actions ($a_{t-1}$).
 - **Training Objective:** Focuses on minimizing the distance between predicted latents and actual encoded latents ($L_{sim}$). To prevent representation collapse and encourage meaningful features, it incorporates:
 - **VICReg-inspired Regularization:** Includes variance ($L_{var}$) and covariance ($L_{cov}$) terms to encourage feature diversity and decorrelation, with a unique application of variance across the time dimension.
 - **Inverse Dynamics Modeling (IDM):** Learns to predict actions given consecutive states ($L_{IDM}$).
 - **Temporal Smoothness:** Encourages learned representations to be smooth over time ($L_{time-sim}$).
 - **Test-Time Planning:** Utilizes **Model Predictive Control (MPC)** with **MPPI (Model Predictive Path Integral)**. Given a start ($s_0$) and goal ($s_g$), the agent plans a sequence of actions by minimizing a cost function in the latent space. This cost can be adapted for different tasks (e.g., minimizing distance to goal for goal-reaching, maximizing distance for avoidance). An **uncertainty regularization** term penalizes actions that lead to high prediction disagreement among the ensemble or deviate from the training distribution.

#### 3.3. Environments

To thoroughly evaluate generalization, the study uses three distinct navigation environments:
- **Two-Rooms:** A custom 2D point-mass navigation environment with controlled data generation (64x64 pixel observations). Ideal for precise experiments on data quality, quantity, and trajectory stitching.
- **Diverse PointMaze:** A modified Mujoco PointMaze environment (point-mass agent, 4D state + RGB images). This environment allows for dynamic generation of various maze layouts to rigorously test generalization to *unseen environment configurations*.
- **Ant-U-Maze:** A standard state-based environment with a 4-legged Ant robot (29D state, 8D action). This tests generalization to *higher-dimensional control and more complex dynamics*.

#### 3.4. Data Generation Strategy

The authors create a total of **23 datasets** by systematically varying key properties:
- **Trajectory Quality:** Ranging from "good-quality" (actions sampled from a Von Mises distribution, leading to purposeful movement) to "random policy trajectories" (actions sampled uniformly, leading to oscillating movement).
- **Trajectory Stitching:** Datasets with varying episode lengths (e.g., short episodes where goals are never reached within a single trajectory) and datasets with "imperfect coverage" (e.g., trajectories never passing through a door in Two-Rooms).
- **Data Quantity:** Datasets ranging from a few thousand to millions of transitions.
- **Environment Variability:** For Diverse PointMaze, datasets are generated with varying numbers of unique maze layouts (e.g., 5, 10, 20, 40 training maps) to assess out-of-distribution generalization.

#### 3.5. Evaluation Strategy

Performance is assessed across six key generalization properties:
1. **Best-case performance:** With large, high-quality, in-distribution data.
2. **Data efficiency:** Performance with varying dataset sizes.
3. **Trajectory stitching:** Ability to learn from short trajectories or data with imperfect coverage.
4. **Learning from random policy trajectories:** Robustness to suboptimal data quality.
5. **Generalization to a new task:** Adapting from goal-reaching to state avoidance.
6. **Generalization to new environment layouts:** Adapting to mazes not seen during training.
Statistical significance is analyzed using Welch's t-test where applicable.

#### 3.6. Ablation Studies and Baselines

The paper includes ablations of PLDM's objective components to demonstrate their importance in preventing collapse and improving performance. It also compares PLDM against extended baselines like DreamerV3, a pixel-reconstruction based model, and TD-MPC2 to highlight the advantages of reconstruction-free latent dynamics learning.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The comprehensive experimental evaluation yields several key insights into the strengths and weaknesses of different learning paradigms for reward-free offline data:

1. **PLDM Demonstrates Superior Generalization to Unseen Layouts and New Tasks:**
 - **Unseen Layouts:** In the Diverse PointMaze environment, PLDM significantly outperforms all model-free RL methods, reliably generalizing to unseen maze layouts even when trained on a very limited number of distinct maps (Figure 2, 7, 8). As test layouts become increasingly out-of-distribution (measured by edit distance from training layouts), model-free methods degrade severely, while PLDM maintains high success rates. An analysis of HILP reveals its latent space, while effective on in-distribution layouts, fails to capture meaningful distances on out-of-distribution layouts (Appendix I), explaining its generalization failure.
 - **New Tasks:** PLDM shows strong zero-shot generalization to a state-avoidance (chasing) task from a model trained solely on goal-reaching data. By simply inverting the planning objective (maximizing distance in latent space), PLDM effectively evades the chaser, outperforming HILP (Figure 5b), which struggled more with inverting its skill direction. This highlights PLDM's flexibility when environment dynamics remain fixed.

2. **Robustness to Data Quality and Data Efficiency Varies by Method:**
 - **Learning from Random Policy Trajectories (Low Quality Data):** HILP, GCIQL, and PLDM are the most robust to low-quality data generated by a random policy (Figure 4, Left). Model-free GCRL methods often struggle because random trajectories limit the maximum distance between states, making distant goals out-of-distribution for policy learning. PLDM, by learning a dynamics model, is less sensitive to the optimality of actions, as long as the transitions themselves are physically accurate.
 - **Data Efficiency:** PLDM and GCIQL exhibit superior sample efficiency, achieving nearly 80% success rate with only a few thousand transitions, outperforming other methods (Figure 4, Right). HILP, while powerful, is more data-hungry.
 - **Trajectory Stitching (Short Trajectories, Imperfect Coverage):** HILP and GCIQL excel at stitching short, suboptimal trajectories and learning from data with imperfect coverage (e.g., "no door-passing" trajectories in Two-Rooms) to reach long-horizon goals (Table 2, Figure 4, Center). PLDM's performance drops in these specific scenarios but remains better than other model-free GCRL methods, indicating its learned dynamics still enable some level of compositional planning.

3. **Model-Free GCRL Methods Have Specific Strengths and Weaknesses:**
 - **Best-Case Performance:** With abundant, high-quality, and diverse data, most methods, including HIQL, GCIQL, HILP, and PLDM, achieve near 100% success rates on in-distribution goal-reaching tasks (Table 2).
 - **Hierarchical Control:** In the more complex Ant-U-Maze, HIQL, along with PLDM and HILP, performs very well in trajectory stitching (Figure 6). The hierarchical structure of HIQL may be particularly beneficial for managing complex, high-dimensional control tasks.
 - **Limitations:** Generally, model-free GCRL methods (CRL, GCBC, HIQL) are more sensitive to data quality and quantity, struggling more with random data, short trajectories, and generalizing to unseen layouts or new tasks compared to PLDM or HILP.

4. **Reconstruction-Free Latent Dynamics Learning (JEPA) is Crucial for PLDM:**
 - Ablation studies on PLDM's objective components (similarity, variance, covariance, inverse dynamics modeling, temporal smoothness) confirm that all play vital roles in learning effective representations and preventing collapse.
 - Direct comparisons with reconstruction-based approaches (DreamerV3, pixel reconstruction) and TD-MPC2 show that these methods perform significantly worse or fail to learn altogether in the reward-free offline setting (Table 6). This provides strong empirical support for the reconstruction-free JEPA paradigm, highlighting its advantages for learning useful dynamics models from passive data.

5. **Computational Efficiency:** PLDM is slower during inference when replanning every step. However, it can match the inference speed of model-free methods by reducing replanning frequency (e.g., every 4 steps) with only a minor performance degradation (Table 5, Figure 10, 11).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research provides a highly valuable and timely contribution to the fields of reinforcement learning and optimal control, particularly for the development of general-purpose AI agents in real-world settings. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Bridging a Critical Research Gap:** The paper systematically addresses the underexplored comparative strengths of model-free RL versus model-based control in the challenging reward-free offline setting. This provides a clear, data-driven understanding of when to choose each paradigm, which was previously lacking.
2. **Validation of Model-Based Planning for Generalization:** The most significant finding is the emphatic demonstration that **Planning with Latent Dynamics Models (PLDM)**, leveraging reconstruction-free self-supervised learning (JEPA), offers superior generalization capabilities. Its robustness to unseen environment layouts and its adaptability to new tasks (beyond simple goal-reaching) are critical for building truly versatile AI. This pushes the boundaries toward agents that can operate effectively in environments never explicitly seen during training.
3. **Advancing Practical AI for Robotics and Embodied Systems:** By demonstrating efficacy with reward-free, suboptimal offline data, this work directly tackles major hurdles in real-world robotics: the high cost of online interaction, the difficulty of reward engineering, and the abundance of unstructured sensor data. The ability to learn from passive, messy data pipelines could significantly accelerate the deployment and capability of autonomous agents.
4. **Empirical Support for Reconstruction-Free SSL (JEPA):** The strong performance of PLDM, especially when compared to reconstruction-based world models, provides compelling empirical evidence for the advantages of the JEPA paradigm in learning actionable representations for control. This reinforces the research direction advocated by Yann LeCun and others, contributing to a deeper theoretical and practical understanding of effective self-supervised learning for embodied AI.
5. **Actionable Guidelines for Practitioners:** The comprehensive comparison, summarized in a clear roadmap (Table 1), offers practical guidance for researchers and engineers. It helps them make informed decisions about method selection based on the specific characteristics of their available data and their desired level of generalization. For instance, if data is high-quality and tasks are in-distribution, GCIQL might be sufficient; but for high generalization needs and suboptimal data, PLDM emerges as a leading candidate.
6. **Foundation for Future Research:** The paper opens several promising avenues for future work:
 - **Scaling to More Complex Domains:** Extending PLDM to robotic manipulation, multi-agent systems, or partially observable environments.
 - **Improving Long-Horizon Reasoning:** Addressing challenges like compounding prediction errors in learned dynamics models.
 - **Enhancing Test-Time Efficiency:** Further optimizing planning algorithms or exploring amortized planning to reduce computational overhead without sacrificing performance.
 - **Integrating Diverse Data Sources:** Exploring how PLDM can leverage heterogeneous offline datasets.
7. **Contribution to Open Science:** The release of code, data, and environment visualizations is highly commendable, facilitating reproducibility and encouraging further research and benchmarking within the community on these critical challenges.

In conclusion, this paper significantly advances the understanding of learning from reward-free offline data, clearly establishing latent dynamics models with reconstruction-free SSL as a powerful paradigm for generalization in autonomous agents. Its practical implications for building more robust, adaptable, and deployable AI systems are substantial.
