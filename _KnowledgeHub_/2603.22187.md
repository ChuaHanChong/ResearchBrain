# Seeing is Improving: Visual Feedback for Iterative Text Layout Refinement

> [!summary] Summary
> The Visual Feedback Layout Model (VFLM) introduces a self-improving framework for Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) that iteratively refines text layouts using visual feedback. It consistently outperformed leading MLLMs and specialized layout generators on OCR F1-scores and graphic metrics across benchmark datasets, avoiding common visual artifacts.

## Problem

- Current automated text layout generation methods often follow a "code-only" paradigm, lacking the ability for the generating model to perceive or evaluate the visual outcome of its code.
- This absence of visual feedback leads to layouts with practical visual deficiencies, such as overlapping elements, poor contrast, or aesthetic imbalance, despite being syntactically valid.
- Existing visual feedback pipelines typically rely on non-trainable, inference-only strategies or decouple generation and reflection into separately trained models.

## Method

- Introduces the ==Visual Feedback== Layout Model (**VFLM**), a self-improving framework operating through an iterative "generation, rendering, reflection, refinement" cycle.
- Employs a two-stage training pipeline, starting with Cold-Start Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) using a synthetically constructed multi-round reflection dataset to establish foundational iterative capabilities.
- Utilizes Reinforcement Learning (RL) with the Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) algorithm, guided by a three-component visually grounded reward function (R_score) and a specialized, learned layout reward model.

## Results

- VFLM achieved new state-of-the-art performance on TextLayout, Crello, and DESIGNERINTENTION datasets, outperforming leading MLLMs (e.g., Claude 3.7) and specialized models (e.g., IGD) in OCR F1-scores and graphic metrics.
- The trained layout reward model demonstrated a high pairwise prediction accuracy of **97.4%** and effectively discriminated between layout quality levels, providing a robust signal for RL.
- Ablation studies confirmed the necessity of the full VFLM framework and its iterative refinement process, which consistently widened performance gaps and improved layout quality across multiple metrics.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Visual feedback is an indispensable component for MLLMs to achieve high-quality graphic layout generation, enabling them to effectively "see," evaluate, and iterate on their designs.
> - A self-improving framework that integrates iterative generation, rendering, reflection, and refinement allows MLLMs to autonomously enhance design quality, mimicking human design processes.
> - For MLLMs with strong inherent reasoning and visual understanding, simple outcome-based reward functions are more effective and robust in RL for stimulating self-improvement than complex, process-oriented ones.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The research presented in this paper is situated within the rapidly advancing field of automated content generation, particularly focusing on structured visual layouts using Large Language Models (LLMs) and Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs). Recent developments in LLMs and MLLMs have enabled the generation of complex designs, such as typographic posters, social media graphics, and documents, from natural language descriptions by producing structured representations like SVG code or JSON. MLLMs further enhance this capability by integrating cross-modal understanding, allowing generation to be conditioned on both text and visual inputs.

However, a significant limitation identified in the existing landscape is the "code-only" paradigm adopted by many contemporary methods. These approaches generate programmatic representations of layouts but typically lack a mechanism to perceive or evaluate the visual outcome of their generated code. This absence of visual feedback can lead to layouts that, while syntactically valid, suffer from practical visual deficiencies such as overlapping elements, poor contrast, misaligned components, or general aesthetic imbalance, thereby compromising readability and overall design quality. Examples of such methods include COLE, OpenCOLE, and LGGPT.

Concurrently, a growing body of research in LLMs has demonstrated the effectiveness of reflection, backtracking, and self-validation mechanisms in improving performance on complex reasoning tasks. Reinforcement Learning (RL) techniques have also been shown to be effective in activating these reflective reasoning capabilities in LLMs. This paper bridges these two areas by exploring whether such reflective capabilities, particularly when informed by visual feedback, can address the perception gap in text layout generation.

While some pipelines have begun to incorporate visual feedback, they often rely on non-trainable, inference-only strategies with external advisors or decouple generation and reflection into separately trained models. This work distinguishes itself by introducing a unified, self-evolving policy with learnable autonomous judgment, driven by Reinforcement Learning, to integrate visual feedback into the core generation process. It aims to establish visual feedback as a critical factor for MLLM-based design agents, moving beyond purely programmatic or decoupled approaches.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation for this research stems from a recognized limitation in current automated text layout generation methods: their "blindness" to the rendered visual output. Existing Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) and other layout generation approaches primarily operate on a "code-only" paradigm. They translate natural language descriptions into structured code (e.g., SVG, JSON) that defines layout elements, which are then rendered by external graphic engines into final images. This unidirectional process, however, does not allow the generating model to perceive or evaluate the visual quality, readability, or aesthetic appeal of its output. Consequently, models may produce syntactically correct layouts that exhibit visual flaws such as overlapping text, insufficient contrast, or poor element placement, failing to meet practical design requirements.

The key objectives and motivations of this work are detailed as follows:

1. **Address the Visual Perception Gap:** The primary objective is to overcome the fundamental limitation of "code-only" layout generation methods by enabling MLLMs to perceive and interpret the visual outcomes of their generated designs. This involves creating a mechanism for the model to "see" its rendered layout.
2. **Develop a Self-Improving Framework for Iterative Refinement:** The core objective is to propose and implement the Visual Feedback Layout Model (VFLM), a novel, self-improving framework. This framework aims to establish a closed-loop process where the model can iteratively generate, render, reflect upon, and refine its layout outputs. The goal is to continuously optimize designs until a satisfactory visual quality is achieved, mimicking human design iteration.
3. **Leverage Reflective Generation Capabilities of MLLMs:** Drawing inspiration from advancements in LLMs that demonstrate improved performance through reflection and self-validation, the research is motivated to transfer these capabilities to text layout generation. By integrating MLLMs' inherent cross-modal understanding, the VFLM seeks to enable adaptive reflective generation, where visual information is used to diagnose issues and devise refinement strategies.
4. **Investigate Reinforcement Learning for Reflective Enhancement:** A specific motivation is to employ Reinforcement Learning (RL) to activate and enhance the model's reflective capabilities. This involves designing a visually grounded reward model that can holistically evaluate layout quality, including text accuracy and aesthetic properties, to guide the iterative refinement process.
5. **Examine the Efficacy of Reward Mechanisms:** The research aims to explore the effectiveness of different reward strategies in RL for self-improving generative models. Specifically, it seeks to compare simple, outcome-based rewards against more complex, process-oriented reward mechanisms, hypothesizing that a simpler signal might compel the model to leverage its intrinsic visual understanding more effectively for robust, stable improvements, mitigating issues like "reward hacking."
6. **Establish Visual Feedback as a Critical Factor:** Ultimately, the work is motivated to demonstrate and validate that visual feedback is a crucial and indispensable component for advanced, design-oriented MLLMs, thereby establishing a practical framework for developing more capable autonomous design agents.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology introduces the Visual Feedback Layout Model (VFLM), a self-improving framework for text layout generation based on a two-stage training pipeline. The approach integrates a multi-round interaction mechanism between the VFLM and a rendering environment, guided by visual feedback and reinforcement learning.

#### 3.1. Visual Feedback Layout Model (VFLM) Framework

The VFLM operates through an iterative "generation, rendering, reflection, refinement" cycle:
1. **Initial Generation:** Given a background image and target text, the VFLM initially analyzes the inputs and generates a preliminary SVG layout code via a structured tool call.
2. **Rendering:** A rendering tool converts the generated SVG code into a visual image. This image is then fed back to the same VFLM.
3. **Visual Reflection:** The VFLM examines the rendered visual image, performing an internal reasoning process to evaluate its quality and identify potential issues or areas for improvement.
4. **Iterative Refinement:** If the layout is deemed unsatisfactory during reflection, the VFLM reasons about necessary modifications. It then generates revised SVG code, and the process repeats from the rendering step. This cycle continues until the model determines that the layout quality is satisfactory, at which point the final SVG code is returned.

#### 3.2. Cold-Start Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT)

The first stage aims to equip the VFLM with foundational iterative reflection capabilities and tool usage protocols through supervised fine-tuning.

- **Data Construction:** Due to the scarcity of natural multi-round reflection data, a synthetic dataset is constructed by distilling an advanced teacher MLLM (Doubao-Seed-1.6). This process involves several steps:
 - **Initial Reasoning Synthesis:** The teacher model is prompted with background images and ground-truth layouts to generate detailed reasoning processes for SVG code generation.
 - **Suboptimal Layout Generation:** The distilled reasoning data is used to fine-tune a base Qwen2.5-VL-7B model. The outputs of this model serve as suboptimal initial attempts for subsequent stages.
 - **Multi-Round Reflection Synthesis:** The suboptimal layouts (from the previous step) are paired with their corresponding ground-truth layouts and fed back to the teacher model. The teacher is instructed to iteratively reflect on the suboptimal input and generate modifications to arrive at the ground-truth solution. This simulates human design refinement trajectories.
 - **Data Combination:** The synthesized data, including initial reasoning and multi-round reflection trajectories, are combined and organized with structured tags (e.g., `<think>`, `<tool_call>`, `<tool_response>`, `<answer>`) to delineate different stages of interaction.
- **Training Objective:** The Qwen2.5-VL-7B model is fine-tuned using a causal language modeling objective on this multi-round reflection trajectory data. To ensure the model learns from corrections rather than initial errors, the loss associated with the initial, suboptimal responses in improvement sequences is masked.

#### 3.3. Iterative Reflection Reinforcement Learning (RL)

The second stage utilizes RL to further enhance the VFLM's reflective and iterative refinement capabilities.

- **RL Algorithm:** The Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) algorithm is adopted. GRPO performs policy gradient optimization within sample groups to maximize rewards.
- **Reward Function (R_score):** A three-component reward function is designed to holistically evaluate layout quality:
 - **R_layout:** A specialized reward model (described below) trained to assess overall layout aesthetics, readability, and coherence.
 - **R_ocr:** Evaluates the accuracy of text in the rendered layout based on Optical Character Recognition (OCR) results, comparing them against the target text.
 - **R_svg:** Measures text accuracy at the SVG code level by comparing extracted SVG strings with the target text.
 - The total R_score is a weighted sum: `R_score = R_layout + α · (R_ocr + R_svg)`, where `α` balances aesthetic quality with functional accuracy.
 - An additional `R_format` reward is applied in each round to ensure correct output formatting, providing a bonus for correct format and a penalty for incorrect format.
- **Advantage Function:** For GRPO training, the raw advantage `A_raw` is calculated as `R_score - mean_group(R_score) + γ · R_format`, where `mean_group(R_score)` acts as a baseline, and `γ` scales the format reward. This `A_raw` is then normalized across the global batch before being used in GRPO training.
- **Layout Reward Model (r_θ):** A critical component is the training of a specialized reward model `r_θ` that outputs a scalar score `R_layout` for a given triplet (background image, target text, rendered layout image).
 - **Dataset Construction:** To train `r_θ`, a novel hierarchical data construction method creates preference data with fine-grained quality distinctions across four levels:
 - **Level-I:** High-quality ground-truth layouts.
 - **Level-II:** Reasonably good layouts from a fine-tuned Qwen2.5-VL-7B, with minor imperfections.
 - **Level-III:** Layouts from Level-II with moderate spatial perturbations.
 - **Level-IV:** Severely degraded layouts from Level-II with aggressive perturbations (e.g., large displacements, random font sizes, deletions).
 This hierarchy allows for pairwise comparisons (6 per problem) to form preference pairs, enabling the reward model to learn subtle quality distinctions.
 - **Training:** The `r_θ` model is initialized with Qwen2.5-VL-3B, with its final layer replaced by a linear layer for scalar output. It is trained using a negative log-likelihood loss function on the preference pairs, where `I+` is preferred over `I-`. The raw scores from `r_θ` are normalized using the mean and standard deviation of the test set distribution to ensure stable reward values for the RL process.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several key findings and results, demonstrating the efficacy of the Visual Feedback Layout Model (VFLM) and the importance of visual feedback in iterative text layout refinement.

#### 4.1. Comparison with Existing Methods

VFLM consistently demonstrated superior performance across multiple benchmarks (TextLayout, Crello, and DESIGNERINTENTION datasets) when compared to three categories of existing methods: advanced MLLMs, image editing models, and specialized layout generation models.

- **Quantitative Superiority:** On the TextLayout dataset, VFLM's initial generation (VFLM-step1) achieved an OCR F1-score of 0.9071, which surpassed leading MLLMs like Claude 3.7 (0.8672) and specialized models like IGD (0.8481). The final optimized output of VFLM further improved this to 0.9376. This outperformance extended to graphic metrics, with VFLM achieving the lowest R_ove (element overlap) and R_com (composition) scores, along with the highest RM scores and overall GPT-4o evaluation scores. These results were consistently replicated across the Crello and DESIGNERINTENTION datasets, establishing new state-of-the-art performance in nearly all OCR and graphic metrics.
- **Qualitative Advantage:** Visual comparisons indicated that VFLM successfully avoided common visual artifacts, such as text overlap and color conflicts, which were prevalent in direct generation models (e.g., IGD, Claude 3.7). Unlike image editing models (e.g., GPT-4o(edit)) that inevitably alter background images, VFLM maintained the integrity of the background while leveraging visual feedback to correct layout issues, ensuring high-quality outputs even with dense text or non-Latin characters.

#### 4.2. Layout Reward Model Evaluation

The trained layout reward model exhibited a high pairwise prediction accuracy of 97.4% on its preference data test set.

- **Data Hierarchy Validation:** Objective metrics (OCR Char-F, R_ali, R_ove, R_com) showed a clear and monotonic degradation from Level-I (gold standard) to Level-IV (severely degraded) layouts. This confirmed that the hierarchical data construction successfully created a reliable gradient of quality.
- **Reward Model Discriminative Power:** The average R_layout scores produced by the trained reward model aligned consistently with this established hierarchy, decreasing from Level-I to Level-IV. This validated that the model had learned a nuanced understanding of layout quality, providing an effective supervision signal for reinforcement learning.

#### 4.3. Ablation Study

Ablation experiments on the TextLayout test set affirmed the clear superiority of the full VFLM framework.

- **Iterative Refinement Impact:** VFLM achieved the best performance across most metrics, significantly outperforming various RL baselines. Notably, the quality of VFLM's initial output (VFLM-step1) was already competitive with or superior to other methods, and subsequent iterative steps consistently widened this performance gap (e.g., RM score increased from 0.5415 to 0.6018).
- **Necessity of Full Framework:** The results demonstrated that components like Cold-Start SFT and iterative RL were crucial. Models without these or with restricted iteration (e.g., Single-Round RL, Direct Output, Direct Output SFT, RL from Pretrained) showed diminished performance, highlighting the value of the complete visual feedback loop.

#### 4.4. Effectiveness of Simple Rewards

A critical empirical investigation revealed that a simple outcome-based reward function was more effective and robust than a complex, process-oriented reward function.

- **Performance Comparison:** While both reward schemes initially improved stably, the complex process-oriented RL scheme plateaued and even degraded in performance after approximately 250 training steps. In contrast, the simple outcome-based reward continued to improve steadily throughout training, achieving superior performance across all evaluation dimensions (Table 4).
- **Insight on Self-Improvement:** This counterintuitive finding suggested that under an effective visual feedback mechanism, simple outcome-based rewards can successfully harness the inherent visual understanding and reasoning capabilities of MLLMs to elicit robust self-improvement behaviors. Complex process-oriented rewards, conversely, risked inhibiting optimal performance, potentially leading to brittle local optima or "reward hacking."

#### 4.5. Additional Findings (from Supplementary Material)

- **Adaptive Reflection Turns:** VFLM demonstrated an adaptive strategy for reflection. Initially, reflection turns might decrease due to instability, but as output format stabilized, the model autonomously increased its reflection depth, realizing that deeper iteration led to better rewards. A positive correlation was observed between input complexity (target text token length) and the number of reflection rounds, indicating VFLM's ability to adjust computational resources based on task difficulty.
- **Human Study Validation:** A blind human study involving 16 participants corroborated quantitative findings, showing that VFLM performed comparably to GPT4o-Image in text coordination and achieved the best results in text accuracy and overall aesthetics compared to Claude 3.7 and OpenCOLE.
- **Compute Cost vs. Performance:** While VFLM exhibited higher inference latency compared to non-iterative baselines (e.g., IGD, OpenCOLE) due to its iterative nature, this increased computational cost was associated with substantial performance gains.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research presented in this paper carries significant implications for the field of automated graphic design and the broader application of multimodal large language models.

1. **Bridging the Visual Perception Gap:** This work addresses a fundamental limitation in existing text layout generation systems, which historically operate on a "code-only" paradigm without perceiving the visual outcome. By introducing VFLM, the paper successfully bridges this critical "visual perception gap," allowing MLLMs to effectively "see," evaluate, and iterate on their visual designs. This marks a notable advancement over previous methods that could generate syntactically correct code but often failed to produce aesthetically pleasing or readable layouts.
2. **Novel Self-Improving Framework:** VFLM establishes a novel, self-improving framework that integrates a continuous "generation, rendering, reflection, refinement" loop. This iterative process, guided by visual feedback and reinforcement learning, provides a robust mechanism for MLLMs to autonomously enhance the quality of their generated designs. It sets a new precedent for how MLLMs can operate in visual domains, mimicking human-like iterative design processes.
3. **Establishing Visual Feedback as Crucial:** The extensive experimental results unequivocally demonstrate that visual feedback is a critical and indispensable component for achieving high-quality graphic layout generation. VFLM consistently outperforms state-of-the-art MLLMs, image editing models, and specialized layout generators across various quantitative and qualitative metrics. This finding validates the core hypothesis of the paper and highlights visual feedback as essential for the development of effective design-oriented MLLMs.
4. **Advancing Reinforcement Learning for MLLMs:** The paper contributes new insights into the application of Reinforcement Learning for MLLMs, particularly regarding reward function design. The counterintuitive finding that simple, outcome-based rewards are more effective and robust than complex, process-oriented ones for stimulating self-improvement is significant. This suggests that for MLLMs with strong inherent reasoning and visual understanding capabilities, clear objective signals, rather than detailed intermediate process guidance, can more effectively harness their autonomous learning and refinement. This insight could influence RL strategies for other complex generative tasks.
5. **Practical Impact on Automated Design:** The VFLM framework has substantial potential practical impact. It offers a powerful tool for automating various graphic design tasks, such as creating typographic posters, social media graphics, and documents. By enabling MLLMs to generate layouts that are not only structurally correct but also visually appealing and readable, VFLM could streamline design workflows, reduce the need for manual corrections, and make high-quality design more accessible. This could lead to more efficient content creation for marketing, publishing, and digital media.
6. **Foundation for Future Research in Agentic AI:** This work lays a foundation for future research into more sophisticated "agentic" AI systems that can operate in complex, multi-modal environments. By demonstrating that an MLLM can reflect on its visual outputs and autonomously improve, VFLM opens avenues for developing AI agents capable of deeper interaction with the visual world, performing complex creative tasks, and learning through iterative visual experience.
