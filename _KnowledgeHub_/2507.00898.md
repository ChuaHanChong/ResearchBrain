# ONLY: One-Layer Intervention Sufficiently Mitigates Hallucinations in Large Vision-Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> This research from Carnegie Mellon University introduces ONLY, an efficient method to mitigate hallucinations in Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) by employing a one-layer intervention and a single-query approach. The method reduces hallucination rates across multiple LVLM backbones and benchmarks while maintaining general multimodal understanding and achieving a minimal 1.07x increase in inference latency, addressing a key efficiency bottleneck of prior techniques.

## Problem

- Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) frequently generate content inconsistent with visual input (hallucinations), undermining their reliability.
- Existing training-based hallucination mitigation methods are computationally expensive and impractical for most users.
- Inference-time contrastive decoding methods for LVLMs require multiple forward passes, significantly increasing inference time (2x+) and GPU memory, making them unsuitable for real-time applications.

## Method

- ONLY proposes a ==training-free decoding algorithm== that intervenes with a single additional Transformer layer computation, rather than multiple full forward passes.
- It introduces a 'textual enhancement' process that selectively deactivates attention heads based on their ==Text-to-Visual Entropy Ratio (TVER)== to prioritize text-focused information and reduce language bias.
- An ==adaptive decoding strategy== then dynamically combines original and textually-enhanced logits, switching between collaborative (reinforcing consistent predictions) and contrastive (suppressing biased predictions) modes based on distribution divergence.

## Results

- ONLY consistently outperformed state-of-the-art baselines like VCD and M3ID across POPE, CHAIR, and MME-Hallucination benchmarks, achieving superior hallucination reduction (e.g., **89.10%** F1 on LLaVA-1.5/MS-COCO Random POPE, and lowest CHAIR rates).
- The method maintained or improved general multimodal understanding on MMBench and MM-Vet, demonstrating that hallucination mitigation did not degrade performance on other tasks.
- ONLY achieved exceptional efficiency with a minimal **1.07x** increase in inference latency and negligible additional GPU memory (**1.00x**) compared to regular decoding, significantly faster than VCD and M3ID (**2.**0x**+** latency).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Language biases within the attention mechanism are a significant contributor to hallucinations in LVLMs, and targeted intervention at the attention head level can effectively address this.
> - A Text-to-Visual Entropy Ratio (TVER) can be used to identify and selectively amplify textual information to mitigate hallucinations with minimal computational overhead.
> - An adaptive decoding strategy, combining collaborative and contrastive modes based on the divergence between original and textually-enhanced outputs, improves hallucination mitigation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The rapid advancements in Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) have opened new avenues for understanding and interacting with visual information through natural language. These models, exemplified by LLaVA, InstructBLIP, and Qwen-VL, integrate visual encoders with Large Language Models (LLMs) to achieve remarkable performance across diverse multimodal tasks like object detection, segmentation, and image captioning. However, a significant and widely acknowledged impediment to their reliable deployment is the pervasive issue of "hallucination." Hallucination refers to the generation of textual content that is inconsistent or inaccurate with respect to the provided visual input, leading to misinformation and eroding user trust.

The broader research landscape for addressing LVLM hallucinations can be broadly categorized into two main streams:
1. **Training-based approaches:** Early efforts focused on mitigating hallucinations by curating more robust training datasets or employing sophisticated fine-tuning techniques on large-scale models. While effective, these methods are resource-intensive, time-consuming, and often impractical for individual users due to the immense computational cost and data requirements.
2. **Inference-time (decoding-based) approaches:** More recently, the focus has shifted to methods that intervene directly in the decoding process during inference, offering a training-free alternative. A prominent sub-category here is **contrastive decoding**, which typically involves comparing and contrasting the outputs of an original LVLM with those of a perturbed version (e.g., by distorting the visual input, as in VCD, or providing a text-only input, as in M3ID). While these methods have shown promise in reducing hallucinations, their fundamental limitation lies in requiring multiple forward passes (queries) through the LVLM, which significantly increases inference time (often 2x or more) and GPU memory consumption. This overhead renders them unsuitable for real-time applications.

The presented work, ONLY, positions itself directly within the inference-time, contrastive-decoding paradigm but critically addresses the efficiency bottleneck. By proposing a "single-query and one-layer intervention" approach, ONLY seeks to achieve state-of-the-art hallucination mitigation *without* the prohibitive computational cost of previous contrastive decoding methods. This is a significant leap, as it aims to bridge the gap between high performance and real-time applicability, which is a major challenge for deploying LVLMs in practical, latency-sensitive environments. The paper identifies and challenges the performance-efficiency trade-off, highlighting the urgent need for more efficient solutions, which ONLY directly aims to provide.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an **efficient and effective method for mitigating hallucinations in Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs)**.

The motivation for this objective stems from several critical observations and limitations of existing approaches:
- **Pervasiveness of Hallucination:** Despite their remarkable capabilities, current LVLMs frequently generate content that is factually inconsistent with the input image. This inherent flaw undermines their reliability and trustworthiness, making their deployment in critical real-world applications (where accuracy is paramount) a significant concern.
- **Inefficiency of Existing Mitigation Strategies:**
 - **Training-based methods** (e.g., robust fine-tuning on curated datasets) are prohibitively expensive and time-consuming, making them impractical for most users and limiting their widespread adoption.
 - **Inference-time contrastive decoding methods** (e.g., VCD, M3ID), while training-free, require multiple LVLM queries (e.g., processing both original and distorted inputs). This leads to a substantial increase in inference time (2x or more) and GPU memory consumption, rendering them unsuitable for real-time applications that demand quick responses. The authors explicitly illustrate this performance-efficiency trade-off in Figure 1, demonstrating that existing methods achieve higher accuracy at a significant cost in inference speed and memory.
- **Under-exploration of Textual Modality:** Previous contrastive decoding approaches have primarily focused on the visual modality or the impact of visual input on textual generation. The authors observe that the effect of the textual modality itself, particularly how language biases might contribute to hallucinations, has been less studied as a direct intervention point.

Therefore, the key motivation behind ONLY is to overcome these limitations by proposing a novel, training-free decoding approach that:
1. **Significantly reduces computational overhead:** It aims for a single LVLM query and a minimal, one-layer intervention, making it efficient for real-time deployment.
2. **Effectively mitigates hallucinations:** It seeks to achieve superior or competitive hallucination reduction compared to state-of-the-art methods.
3. **Leverages textual information:** It specifically focuses on selectively amplifying crucial textual information to reduce predominant and irrelevant language bias, which is hypothesized to contribute to hallucinations.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

ONLY proposes a novel, training-free decoding algorithm that intervenes in the LVLM's generation process with only a single additional Transformer layer computation. The core idea is to produce "textually-enhanced" outputs by selectively amplifying textual information and then adaptively combining these with the original LVLM outputs.

The methodology can be broken down into three main components:

#### 3.1. Preliminaries: LVLM Decoding and Transformer Decoder

The paper first establishes the context of LVLM decoding. LVLMs generate textual responses autoregressively from an input image and a textual query. This process involves a vision encoder, a connector, and a Large Language Model (LLM) structured as a Transformer. Each token is predicted by applying a softmax over the final layer's logits (Equation 1). The Transformer decoder consists of multiple layers, each with a Multi-Head Attention (MHA) module and a Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP), connected via residual connections (Equation 2). The MHA module computes attention scores from query, key, and value matrices across multiple attention heads (Equations 3, 4). The final token logits are derived from the last hidden state of the Transformer (Equation 5).

#### 3.2. One-Layer Intervention for Textual Enhancement

Unlike previous contrastive decoding methods that rely on generating perturbed *visual* inputs, ONLY focuses on directly producing textually-enhanced outputs with minimal overhead. The key insight is that distortions (like noise) can increase textual entropy while decreasing visual entropy (Figure 3), suggesting a link between textual information and potential language biases.
The approach introduces an **attention-head selection strategy guided by the Text-to-Visual Entropy Ratio (TVER)**:
- For each attention head `i` in a chosen layer `ℓ`, raw attention scores (`a_ℓ,i`) are computed.
- These scores are then segregated into textual attention (`a_T_ℓ,i`) and visual attention (`a_V_ℓ,i`) based on their token indices (Equation 8).
- The entropy for both textual and visual attention distributions is calculated (Equation 9, 10).
- The TVER for each head is determined as the ratio of textual entropy to visual entropy (`TVER_ℓ,i`, Equation 11). This ratio is empirically shown to correlate with hallucination levels (Figure 4).
- To stimulate textually-enhanced outputs, ONLY **selectively deactivates (sets attention weights to zero) attention heads whose TVER falls below the average TVER for that layer** (Equation 12). This prioritizes heads that are more "text-focused" or where textual information has higher uncertainty/prominence relative to visual information, aiming to extract language bias.
- The output of this modified attention mechanism is termed Textual-Enhanced Multi-Head Attention (TE-MHA, Equation 13).
- To keep the textually-enhanced output consistent with the original LVLM flow, two residual connections are applied: one directly adding the TE-MHA output (`˜H_ℓ_t`) to the original last layer's hidden state (`H_L-1_t`), and another after the final MLP (Equations 14, 15, 16). This results in `ˆH_L_t`, from which textually-enhanced logits (`˜f_θ`) are derived (Equation 17). This process requires only a single additional attention layer computation, not a full forward pass.

#### 3.3. Adaptive Decoding

With both the original logits (`f_θ`) and the textual-enhanced logits (`˜f_θ`) available from a single query, ONLY employs an adaptive decoding strategy:
- The Manhattan distance (`d_t`) between the probability distributions of the original and textual-enhanced outputs is calculated at each time step `t` (Equation 18). This distance quantifies the divergence between the visually-grounded and textually-biased predictions.
- Based on a predefined threshold (`γ`), the decoding strategy adaptively switches between **collaborative** and **contrastive** modes (Equation 20):
 - If `d_t < γ` (distributions are similar, suggesting less language bias or hallucination), a collaborative approach is used, adding a weighted component of the textual-enhanced logits to the original. This reinforces robust textual predictions.
 - If `d_t ≥ γ` (distributions diverge, indicating potential language bias or hallucination), a contrastive approach is used, subtracting a weighted component of the textual-enhanced logits from the original. This aims to suppress the identified language bias.
- Hyperparameters `α_1` and `α_2` control the weights of the collaborative and contrastive terms, respectively.
- An adaptive plausibility constraint (Equation 23) is also applied, similar to VCD, to prune low-probability tokens.

This adaptive decoding mechanism, combined with the efficient one-layer textual enhancement, allows ONLY to dynamically correct for hallucinations by either amplifying consistent information or suppressing biased predictions, all within a highly efficient framework.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The paper conducts extensive experiments to validate ONLY's effectiveness and efficiency against state-of-the-art hallucination mitigation methods.

#### 4.1. Experimental Setup

- **Evaluated LVLMs:** LLaVA-1.5, InstructBLIP, and Qwen-VL. The scalability was also tested on LLaVA-NeXT-7B/13B.
- **Benchmarks:**
 - **POPE:** Evaluates object hallucinations with yes-or-no questions across MS-COCO, A-OKVQA, and GQA datasets (random, popular, adversarial settings). Metrics: Accuracy, Precision, Recall, F1 score.
 - **CHAIR:** Assesses object hallucinations in image captioning. Metrics: CHAIR_S (sentence-level hallucination rate) and CHAIR_I (object-level hallucination rate). Lower is better.
 - **MME-Hallucination:** Comprehensive benchmark for object-level (existence, count) and attribute-level (position, color) hallucinations. Metric: MME Score (higher is better).
 - **MMVP:** Evaluates fine-grained visual recognition capabilities using CLIP-blind image pairs. Metric: Accuracy.
 - **MMBench & MM-Vet:** General multimodal understanding benchmarks to ensure performance on non-hallucination specific tasks is maintained or improved.
 - **LLaVA-Bench:** Qualitative case studies and GPT-4V-aided evaluations (Accuracy, Detailedness).
- **Baselines:** VCD, M3ID, Woodpecker, HALC, DoLa, OPERA.
- **Implementation Details:** Default query formats, specific α₁, α₂, and γ values for different LVLMs, β for plausibility constraint. Experiments performed on a single 48GB NVIDIA RTX 6000 Ada GPU.

### 5. Superior Hallucination Mitigation Performance

- **POPE (Table 1):** ONLY consistently outperforms all baselines across all LVLM backbones and settings (MS-COCO, A-OKVQA, GQA; random, popular, adversarial). For instance, on LLaVA-1.5 (MS-COCO Random), ONLY achieves 89.10% F1, significantly surpassing VCD (87.15%) and M3ID (87.52%). Similar gains are observed in challenging adversarial settings and across other LVLMs.
- **CHAIR (Table 2):** ONLY achieves the lowest hallucination rates (CHAIR_S and CHAIR_I) across all LVLM backbones and token limits. For LLaVA-1.5 (Max Token = 128), it reduces CHAIR_S by 5.2 points and CHAIR_I by 2.0 points compared to regular decoding, outperforming all other methods.
- **MME-Hallucination (Table 3 & C1):** ONLY achieves the highest overall MME score (635.55 for LLaVA-1.5), outperforming the second-best M3ID by 36.34 points, demonstrating strong capability in mitigating both object and attribute-level hallucinations. Consistent superior performance is seen across InstructBLIP and Qwen-VL as well.
- **MMVP (Figure 5):** ONLY significantly improves LLaVA-1.5's accuracy on fine-grained visual recognition (from 22.67% to 28.00%), outperforming VCD and M3ID, indicating enhanced precise visual understanding.

### 6. Maintenance/Improvement of General Multimodal Understanding

- **MMBench (Table C2) & MM-Vet (Table C3):** ONLY consistently outperforms existing state-of-the-art methods, demonstrating that it not only mitigates hallucinations but also enhances the general multimodal understanding capabilities of LVLMs across diverse tasks.

### 7. Qualitative Improvement (LLaVA-Bench via GPT-4V, Table 4 & Figure 6)

- Qualitative case studies show that ONLY generates more detailed and accurate responses compared to regular decoding, avoiding hallucinatory content. GPT-4V-aided evaluations quantitatively confirm this, showing improved accuracy and detailedness scores for ONLY's responses.

### 8. Exceptional Efficiency

- **Efficiency Comparison (Table 5):** This is a standout finding. ONLY incurs a minimal 1.07× increase in average inference latency and negligible additional GPU memory consumption (1.00×) compared to regular decoding. In contrast, VCD and M3ID require 2.01×-2.03× more time, and methods like OPERA and Woodpecker can be 3x to 7x slower with significantly higher memory usage. This validates ONLY as a highly efficient solution for real-time applications.

#### 8.1. Ablation Studies

- **Selection of Layer for Textual Enhancement (Figure 7):** Experiments show that selecting the initial layer for textual enhancement yields optimal performance, but ONLY's performance remains robust across different layers, with minimal variation, due to its dynamic attention-head selection strategy.
- **Other Strategies for Textual Enhancement (Table 6):** The chosen attention head selection using TVER is crucial, as other direct manipulation strategies (e.g., zeroing/adding noise to visual attention, doubling textual attention, or using sum of weights ratio) lead to suboptimal results.
- **Hyperparameter Sensitivity (Tables D5, D6, D7, D8):** Analysis of α₁, α₂, β, and γ shows that the chosen default values achieve optimal performance. The adaptive decoding (non-zero α values) is critical for performance, as setting them to zero reverts to standard decoding performance.
- **Scaling Up LVLMs (Table C4, D9):** ONLY's effectiveness scales to larger LVLMs (LLaVA-NeXT-7B/13B), consistently outperforming baselines, indicating its generalizability beyond smaller models.

In summary, the main findings unequivocally demonstrate that ONLY provides a highly effective and remarkably efficient solution for mitigating hallucinations in LVLMs, making it a significant advancement for real-world deployment.

### 9. Significance and Potential Impact

The work presented in "ONLY: One-Layer Intervention Sufficiently Mitigates Hallucinations in Large Vision-Language Models" holds significant implications and potential impact for the field of multimodal AI and its real-world applications.

### 10. Enabling Real-time Deployment of Reliable LVLMs

The most profound impact of ONLY is its ability to mitigate hallucinations effectively while incurring minimal computational overhead. Existing state-of-the-art methods, despite their effectiveness, suffer from a 2x or more increase in inference time, making them impractical for real-time applications such as autonomous driving, real-time surveillance, interactive AI assistants, and robotics. ONLY's "single query and one-layer intervention" approach dramatically reduces this latency to a mere 1.07x overhead. This efficiency breakthrough is crucial for bridging the gap between research prototypes and deployable, reliable LVLM systems in latency-sensitive environments.

### 11. Enhancing Trustworthiness and Reducing Misinformation

Hallucinations are a major barrier to the widespread adoption of LVLMs in critical domains like healthcare, legal analysis, and scientific research, where factual accuracy is paramount. By significantly reducing the incidence of hallucinations, ONLY directly contributes to enhancing the trustworthiness and reliability of LVLM-generated content. This can lead to safer and more dependable AI systems, minimizing the risk of propagating misinformation or generating misleading information.

### 12. Generalizability Across Diverse LVLMs and Tasks

The extensive evaluation across three prominent LVLM backbones (LLaVA-1.5, InstructBLIP, Qwen-VL, and LLaVA-NeXT) and six diverse benchmarks (POPE, CHAIR, MME-Hallucination, MMVP, MMBench, MM-Vet) demonstrates the robust and generalizable nature of ONLY. This suggests that the method is not tied to a specific model architecture or task, making it a versatile tool for enhancing the robustness of a wide range of LVLM applications.

### 13. Simplicity and Low Implementation Effort

ONLY is described as a "training-free" and "minimal implementation effort" solution. This means that users do not need to retrain large models or collect extensive curated datasets, lowering the barrier to entry for developers and researchers aiming to deploy more reliable LVLMs. Its plug-and-play nature for existing LVLMs is a significant practical advantage.

### 14. Novel Insights into Language Bias in LVLMs

The core mechanism of ONLY—leveraging the text-to-visual entropy ratio (TVER) to identify and amplify textual information for bias mitigation—offers novel insights into the internal workings of LVLMs. It highlights that language biases within the attention mechanism are a significant contributor to hallucinations and that targeted intervention at the attention head level can effectively address this. This contributes to a deeper understanding of LVLM failure modes and potential correction mechanisms.

### 15. Paving the Way for Future Research

The success of ONLY, particularly its efficiency, opens new avenues for future research. The authors explicitly mention exploring ways to further accelerate the method beyond the original LVLM speed (e.g., using techniques like FastV and VScan) and extending its applicability to video hallucination mitigation. This indicates a commitment to pushing the boundaries of efficient and reliable multimodal AI.

In conclusion, ONLY represents a significant step forward in making powerful LVLMs more practical and trustworthy for real-world deployment. By tackling the critical hallucination problem with an innovative and highly efficient approach, it paves the way for broader and more impactful applications of multimodal AI.
