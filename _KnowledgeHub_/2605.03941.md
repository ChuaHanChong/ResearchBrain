# iWorld-Bench: A Benchmark for Interactive World Models with a Unified Action Generation Framework

> [!summary] Summary
> Tsinghua University researchers introduce iWorld-Bench, a comprehensive benchmark designed for interactive world models, which features a diverse dataset and a unified action generation framework. It systematically evaluates 14 existing models, revealing inherent trade-offs between visual generation quality and action controllability across different interaction paradigms.

## Problem

- Existing world model benchmarks offer limited diversity in scenes and perspectives, often drawing from single datasets, and exhibit inconsistencies in coordinate systems.
- A lack of a unified definition for action inputs across models complicates fair and consistent comparisons of interactive world models.
- Current benchmarks are often generic, lacking specific task designs and evaluation metrics to thoroughly assess the responsiveness and memory capabilities crucial for interactive world models.

## Method

- The **iWorld-Bench** dataset was constructed from 12 existing high-quality datasets and 100,000 newly generated video clips, standardized for camera parameters and uniformly annotated with semantic attributes by VLMs and human refiners.
- A ==unified action generation framework== was developed, defining a comprehensive action control space and a ==Unified Encoding Mapping== dictionary to standardize action inputs for diverse models.
- Six distinct interactive tasks were designed to evaluate action control (across four difficulty levels), memory ability, and camera following, supported by nine multi-dimensional evaluation metrics.

## Results

- HY-World 1.5, an experimental one-hot encoding model, achieved the highest average performance score of 0.7873, demonstrating strong capabilities in memory ability and trajectory following.
- Text-controlled models, such as CogVideoX-I2V, showed high brightness consistency (0.8988) and sharpness retention (0.7951) but comparatively lower trajectory accuracy (0.5950).
- AC3D, a camera-parameter controlled model, achieved the best performance in camera following tasks, scoring highest in Trajectory Tolerance (0.9091) and Motion Smoothness (0.9919).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A strong Spearman rank correlation of 0.8053 (p < 0.001) between objective metrics and human preferences validates the benchmark's ability to align with subjective judgments of model performance.
> - One-hot encoding based models generally exhibit superior interaction capabilities and robust trajectory following, while text-controlled models often achieve higher visual generation quality but lower action accuracy.
> - Dedicated fine-tuning for specific control mechanisms can enhance controllability but may lead to slight degradation in general visual generation quality.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of Artificial General Intelligence (AGI) necessitates agents capable of adaptive learning and interaction. Within this pursuit, world models, which learn to understand and predict environmental changes through interaction, have emerged as a significant research area. These models provide scalable environments crucial for agent perception, reasoning, and action generation.

This work specifically addresses the subfield of *interactive world models*. These models differ from general video generation models, which primarily focus on synthesizing video content, and embodied world models, which often concentrate on manipulation policies for robots. Interactive world models are characterized by their ability to generate causally consistent environmental responses based on external action sequences (e.g., camera movements, keyboard inputs), enabling bidirectional communication between an agent and its simulated environment. This interactivity is considered fundamental for simulating dynamic world changes realistically and for generating content suitable for agent training in applications such as game engines, autonomous driving, and embodied intelligence.

However, existing research and benchmarks for world models exhibit several limitations when specifically applied to interactive capabilities. These limitations include:
1. **Limited diversity in scenes and perspectives:** Many benchmarks originate from single datasets, restricting scenes and viewpoints primarily to pedestrian perspectives. Issues also arise from inconsistencies in coordinate systems and parameter formats across diverse high-quality datasets.
2. **Lack of a unified definition for action inputs:** Interactive world models employ heterogeneous action representations, such as text commands, keyboard inputs, or continuous trajectories. This heterogeneity complicates fair and consistent comparisons across different models.
3. **Insufficient task design for evaluating interactivity:** Current benchmarks are often designed for general-purpose or embodied world models, overlooking the specific responsiveness to external action sequences and the interactivity essential for these models. They also frequently lack tasks with varying difficulty levels or dedicated memory tasks.

iWorld-Bench positions itself to address these gaps by introducing a comprehensive benchmark specifically tailored for the evaluation of interactive world models. It aims to provide a standardized framework, a diverse dataset, and a range of tasks to assess the physical interaction capabilities, distance perception, and memory of these models, thereby facilitating structured advancements in the field.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind the development of iWorld-Bench is to address the identified deficiencies in existing evaluation benchmarks for interactive world models. The authors observe a critical lack of large-scale datasets and unified benchmarks capable of thoroughly assessing the physical interaction capabilities of these models. Without such a framework, comparing and understanding the progress of various interactive world models, especially concerning their ability to adapt and respond to external actions, becomes challenging.

The key objectives of this research are threefold:

1. **Construct a Diverse and High-Quality Dataset:** The first objective is to develop a comprehensive dataset suitable for training and testing interactive world models. This involves overcoming the limitations of current datasets, which often lack diversity in scenes, perspectives, and environmental conditions. The dataset aims to standardize heterogeneous data sources, unify coordinate systems and camera parameter formats, and incorporate a wide range of visual and physical characteristics relevant to interactive environments. This includes varying observation perspectives (e.g., Unmanned Ground Vehicles, Unmanned Aerial Vehicles, human, robotics), outdoor weather conditions, indoor lighting, day-night transitions, and a multitude of diverse scenes and entities.

2. **Propose a Unified Action Generation Framework:** Recognizing the disparate action input modalities used by existing interactive world models (e.g., text, one-hot encodings, intrinsic/extrinsic camera parameters), a core objective is to introduce a unified framework for action generation. This framework aims to provide a modality-agnostic encoding system that can uniquely represent fundamental motion actions. Its purpose is to enable consistent generation of action tasks and standardized evaluation across models, irrespective of their specific input interface. The framework is designed for extensibility, supporting the integration of additional modalities and facilitating the design of diverse downstream tasks.

3. **Design Diverse Interactive Tasks and Evaluation Metrics:** The third objective is to create a set of tasks specifically engineered to assess the interactivity of world models comprehensively. These tasks are intended to evaluate crucial interaction-related abilities such as action-following, responsiveness to complex action sequences, and memory capabilities. The tasks are designed with varying difficulty levels and include scenarios that test logical consistency and the model's ability to maintain an understanding of its environment over time. Accompanying these tasks, a comprehensive set of evaluation metrics is developed to quantify performance across visual generation quality, trajectory following accuracy, and memory retention.

By achieving these objectives, iWorld-Bench seeks to establish a standard and robust benchmark that enables a thorough, fair, and unified assessment of interactive world models, thereby providing insights into their limitations and guiding future research and development in the pursuit of AGI.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed in developing iWorld-Bench involves a multi-stage process encompassing data collection, standardization, annotation, framework design, and task creation, followed by a rigorous evaluation protocol.

#### 3.1. Dataset Pipeline

The construction of a diverse, high-quality world model dataset involved three main components:

- **Video Generate Inherit Past:** The researchers identified and processed 12 existing high-quality datasets that contained intrinsic and extrinsic camera parameters. These datasets spanned various domains including autonomous driving (e.g., KITTI-360, Waymo, nuScenes), 3D reconstruction (e.g., RealEstate-10K, Princeton365, DL3DV-10K), robotics (e.g., TartanGround, NCLT), drone operation (e.g., TartanAir, UAVScenes), and existing world model datasets (e.g., SpatialVid). A data processing protocol was applied to standardize video formats, coordinate systems, and data structures to ensure compatibility for world model training. Camera extrinsics were maintained in three redundant formats: transformation matrices, quaternions, and six-degree-of-freedom (6-DoF) vectors.

- **Video Generate Create Future:** To expand the dataset, an automated collection and filtering pipeline was developed. This pipeline gathered 100,000 1080P video clips from 18 high-quality environments across 4 outdoor urban simulators (aerial VLN, UAV ON, Openfly, and Embodied City). The data was collected from 450 manually identified high-quality points within these scenes, using 89 predefined action-space tasks. A multi-stage filtering pipeline ensured data quality: Single-Frame Filtering detected point anomalies (e.g., brightness spikes, color mutations) via per-frame visual metrics, while Sample Filtering used statistical density analysis to extract coherent and high-fidelity temporal sequences.

- **High-quality Labeling:** All collected data, totaling 330,000 video clips, underwent uniform semantic annotation. A vision-language model (VLM), GPT-4o, was primarily used to label semantic attributes such as environment type, scene description, weather or lighting conditions, and entities. To mitigate hallucinated tags and single-model bias, annotations were subjected to a multi-model verification and human refinement pipeline. Three additional VLMs (Gemini 3.0 Flash, Qwen-VL-Max, Kimi-K2.5) independently verified the annotations. Non-unanimous cases (18.6% of the corpus) were routed to human annotators for review and correction. From this larger corpus, 2,100 high-quality videos were selected as a representative subset for benchmark evaluation. Human annotators further designed memory tasks and assigned difficulty levels, resulting in 4,900 evaluation tasks.

#### 3.2. iWorld-Bench Design

- **Action Generation Framework:** A unified and comprehensive framework was designed to support various interaction modalities. This framework defines an action control space for first-person perspective motion, comprising 27 translational and 27 rotational actions, leading to a total of 729 possible combined actions. Each action is characterized by a Difficulty (D, from 1 to 6) and a Validity (V, 0 or 1, indicating common vs. rare actions). For practical evaluation, the framework focuses on 81 fundamental actions (9 translational x 9 rotational) that are compatible with various models. A Unified Encoding Mapping dictionary was constructed to assign unique encodings to these 81 actions and map them consistently to intrinsic/extrinsic camera parameters, one-hot encodings, and text control signals. This ensures standardized control and evaluation across diverse world model types.

- **Diverse Interactive Task Design:** Based on the Action Generation Framework and the curated dataset, six types of interactive tasks were designed to comprehensively evaluate models:
 - **Action Control Difficulty 1-4:** Four task types testing action-following ability with increasing complexity (from basic stationary/single-axis movements to four-degree-of-freedom complex tasks). Each category comprised 1,000 tasks.
 - **Memory Ability:** 200 tasks designed to test a model's memory by requiring it to generate a cyclic path and revisit a location, evaluating consistency and trajectory alignment.
 - **Camera Following:** 700 tasks specifically for models controlled by intrinsic and extrinsic camera parameters, testing their ability to follow precise trajectories.

#### 3.3. Evaluation Metrics

Nine evaluation metrics were defined across three dimensions to thoroughly assess interactive world model performance:

- **Generation Quality:**
 - **Image Quality:** Assessed using the MUSIQ score, measuring low-level visual distortions.
 - **Brightness Consistency:** Quantified by analyzing temporal consistency of brightness distribution relative to the initial frame.
 - **Color Temperature Constraint:** Evaluated by penalizing hue drifting in the HSV space, ensuring scene color coherence.
 - **Sharpness Retention:** Monitored edge gradients using a vectorized Tenengrad method with a BRISQUE-triggered circuit breaker to distinguish genuine texture stability from artifacts.
- **Trajectory Following:**
 - **Motion Smoothness:** Evaluated by reconstructing discarded frames using an interpolation model and calculating deviation (LPIPS, SSIM, MSE) from original frames.
 - **Trajectory Accuracy:** Quantified how precisely the model adheres to camera commands by evaluating directional mapping accuracy in the motion tangent space via ViPE-extracted trajectories.
 - **Trajectory Tolerance:** Calibrated the model's trajectory fidelity by cross-referencing generated sequences with ground-truth videos under the same ViPE framework to eliminate estimator-induced variance.
- **Memory Ability:**
 - **Memory Symmetry:** Quantified logical loop-closure in reciprocal tasks by evaluating pixel-wise consistency of symmetric frame pairs.
 - **Trajectory Alignment:** Assessed spatial topological consistency of camera movements in reciprocal tasks by calculating mirror similarity of instantaneous displacement vectors.

#### 3.4. Experimental Setup

The benchmark was applied to 14 representative interactive world models, categorized by their control paradigm: text-controlled (5 models), one-hot encoding-based (2 models), and intrinsic/extrinsic camera parameter-controlled (7 models). All inferences were performed on NVIDIA A800 GPUs. A human preference study was also conducted to validate the alignment between the automatic metrics and human subjective perceptions of world model performance.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of 14 representative interactive world models using iWorld-Bench yielded several key findings regarding their performance across different interaction modalities and task types.

#### 4.1. Human Preference Validation

A human preference study was conducted to validate the alignment of iWorld-Bench's objective metrics with human subjective perceptions. The study, involving 14 models and 16 tasks scored by 12 annotators, revealed a strong Spearman rank correlation of 0.8053 (p < 0.001) between objective metric scores and mean human preference scores. This indicated significant rank-order agreement. A Kruskal-Wallis H Test further confirmed statistically significant (H = 1496.8994, p < 0.001) and practically substantial differences (effect size η² = 0.5549) in human-perceived performance across models. Fine-grained analysis using Dunn's post-hoc pairwise comparison demonstrated that the metrics could reliably discriminate between models with genuinely close capabilities, with non-significant differences corresponding to true performance equivalence in human perception. The analysis also suggested that human annotators tend to place a proportionally higher weight on perceptual image quality when forming overall judgments, compared to the uniform weighting of the automatic metrics.

#### 4.2. Action Control and Memory Ability Tasks

Evaluation on tasks assessing action control and memory ability (Table 3 in the paper) showed distinct performance profiles across different interaction paradigms:

- **Overall Performance:** HY-World 1.5 (one-hot encoding) achieved the highest average score of 0.7873, demonstrating strong capabilities in both memory ability and trajectory following. Videox-fun-Wan (camera parameters) and HunyuanVideo-1.5 (text-controlled) also performed well, ranking second and third, respectively.
- **One-Hot Encoding Models:** Models controlled via one-hot encoding, such as HY-World 1.5, generally exhibited superior interaction capabilities and robust trajectory following (Trajectory Accuracy: 0.7472 for HY-World 1.5). This suggests that discrete action signals provide an advantage in controllability compared to continuous text descriptions. However, the paper implies these models may lack the flexibility of camera-parameter control.
- **Text-Controlled Models:** Text-controlled models like CogVideoX-I2V excelled in generation quality metrics (Brightness Consistency: 0.8988; Sharpness Retention: 0.7951) but demonstrated comparatively lower trajectory following accuracy (0.5950 for CogVideoX-I2V). This indicates a trade-off where high visual fidelity can come at the cost of precise action controllability. NVIDIA Cosmos also showed high visual consistency but lower action following.
- **Camera-Parameter Controlled Models:** Models using explicit intrinsic and extrinsic camera parameters, such as videox-fun-Wan and AC3D, demonstrated strong overall performance, particularly in memory tasks (Memory Symmetry: 0.9009 for videox-fun-Wan, 0.9068 for AC3D). Earlier methods like MotionCtrl and CameraCtrl showed limited effectiveness across most metrics.
- **Trade-offs in Fine-tuning:** An observation was that specialized camera-controlled models (e.g., AC3D, HY-World 1.5) often achieved significantly improved trajectory following compared to their base models (e.g., CogVideoX-I2V, HunyuanVideo-1.5), but sometimes exhibited slight degradation in general generation quality metrics. This suggests that fine-tuning for specific control mechanisms can enhance controllability while potentially impacting visual fidelity.

#### 4.3. Camera Following Tasks

Dedicated Camera Following tasks were used to evaluate camera-parameter-controlled models using 700 annotated camera files (Table 4 in the paper):

- **AC3D's Performance:** AC3D achieved the best overall performance in this category, scoring highest in Trajectory Tolerance (0.9091), Brightness Consistency (0.8927), and Motion Smoothness (0.9919). This indicates its architecture effectively utilizes explicit camera parameters for precise control.
- **Trajectory Tolerance Variation:** A significant performance gap was observed in trajectory tolerance across models, ranging from 0.4286 (ASTRA) to 0.9091 (AC3D). This highlights varying capabilities among current approaches in translating camera parameters into coherent visual sequences.
- **Quality vs. Control:** RealCam-I2V achieved the highest Image Quality (0.5889) in this category but lagged in Trajectory Tolerance (0.7480). This finding further reinforces the observed orthogonal relationship between visual fidelity and action controllability.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

iWorld-Bench represents a notable contribution to the field of interactive world models through its introduction of a unified and multi-dimensional evaluation benchmark. Its significance and potential impact can be identified in several areas:

- **Standardized Evaluation Framework:** By proposing the first benchmark specifically designed for interactive world models, iWorld-Bench provides a standardized framework that enables fair and consistent comparison of different models, regardless of their interaction modalities. This addresses a critical limitation in current research where heterogeneous action representations hinder objective assessment. Such standardization can accelerate research progress by providing a common ground for evaluating new architectures and algorithms.

- **Comprehensive Data Resource:** The construction of a diverse, high-quality dataset containing 330,000 video clips, covering multiple scenes, perspectives, weather conditions, and entities, offers a substantial data foundation for training and testing world models. This extensive and meticulously curated dataset, processed through a novel pipeline and rigorously annotated, alleviates the issue of limited data diversity and quality that has constrained previous research.

- **In-Depth Model Analysis and Identification of Limitations:** The systematic evaluation of 14 existing interactive world models using iWorld-Bench provides an in-depth analysis of their strengths and limitations. The findings highlight key trade-offs between generation quality and controllability across different interaction paradigms (text-controlled, one-hot encoding, camera parameter-controlled models). This detailed understanding can guide researchers in identifying areas for improvement and developing more balanced and capable interactive world models. For instance, the observed trade-off between visual fidelity and precise action following indicates a direction for future model design.

- **Guidance for Future Research:** The benchmark's insights, particularly concerning the performance disparities and architectural implications of different control paradigms, offer valuable guidance for the future development of interactive world models. By clearly outlining current deficiencies in interaction capabilities, memory, and trajectory following, iWorld-Bench can help prioritize research directions, fostering innovation in areas such as improving long-term consistency and real-time performance.

- **Community Resource:** The public availability of the iWorld-Bench model leaderboard, project page, dataset, and code promotes transparency, reproducibility, and collaborative research. This open access facilitates broader community engagement and adoption of the benchmark, potentially leading to a more rapid advancement of the interactive world model field.

In conclusion, iWorld-Bench addresses a critical gap in the evaluation of interactive world models by providing a robust, comprehensive, and standardized tool. Its detailed methodology, extensive dataset, and multi-faceted evaluation metrics are poised to significantly influence the direction and progress of research towards more adaptive and intelligent agents.
