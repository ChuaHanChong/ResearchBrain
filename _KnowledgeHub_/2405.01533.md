# OmniDrive: A Holistic Vision-Language Dataset for Autonomous Driving with Counterfactual Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> OmniDrive builds a holistic vision-language dataset for autonomous driving using counterfactual reasoning over simulated and expert trajectories to generate dense 3D-grounded QA data, then trains two LLM-agent variants (Omni-L, Omni-Q) on it, yielding significant gains on the DriveLM benchmark and nuScenes open-loop planning.

## Problem

- Prior driving LLM-agent datasets rely on expert trajectories that give only sparse supervision, reflecting safe paths without exposing the underlying decision-making or reasoning process.
- Open-loop planning benchmarks using real-world log-replay suffer implicit bias toward ego status, overly simple scenarios, and easy overfitting to expert trajectories.
- No principled framework existed extending 2D VLM reasoning to full 3D geometric and spatial understanding needed for real-world driving decisions.

## Method

- ==Counterfactual-based Q&A generation pipeline== simulates clustered driving trajectories (stop, turn, accelerate) and checks them against a rule-based checklist for collisions, red lights, and drivable-area violations.
- ==Planning-oriented key-frame selection== uses ==CLIP-embedding== and trajectory ==K-means clustering== to sample semantically and dynamically diverse nuScenes scenes before large-scale GPT-4 annotation.
- **Omni-Q** and **Omni-L** agent architectures: Omni-Q fuses ==Q-Former==-style carrier/detection queries with 3D position encoding from ==StreamPETR==-like perception; Omni-L extends ==LLaVA=='s MLP projector with zero-initialized 3D positional encoding for multi-view alignment.

## Results

- On DriveLM, OmniDrive pretraining raises average score from **0.53** to **0.56**, and combined with LLaVA665k pretraining reaches **0.58**, versus **0.53** with DriveLM data alone.
- On nuScenes open-loop planning without ego status, Omni-L trained with OmniDrive Q&A cuts collision rate from **3.22%** to **1.90%** and intersection rate from **3.90%** to **3.29%** versus trajectory-only training.
- Omni-L outperforms Omni-Q on counterfactual reasoning (**53.7%** AP, **63.0%** AR, **73.2** CIDEr vs **52.3%** AP, **59.6%** AR, **68.6** CIDEr), showing vision-language alignment matters more than 3D perception integration.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Counterfactual reasoning over simulated trajectories, not just expert logs, provides denser and more interpretable supervision linking planning trajectories to language-based reasoning.
> - Migrating existing 2D VLMs toward 3D tasks proved more effective than embedding traditional 3D perception stacks into a vision-language framework.
> - Stronger foundational language ability correlates positively with better counterfactual reasoning and planning performance, suggesting language capacity, not perception alone, drives quality.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The research presented in "OmniDrive" positions itself at the intersection of two rapidly evolving fields: Vision-Language Models (VLMs) and Autonomous Driving (AD). It directly addresses critical limitations and emerging challenges in integrating these two domains for real-world applications.

**Advancement of Vision-Language Models for 3D Understanding:** Recent advancements in 2D VLMs have demonstrated impressive reasoning capabilities, leading to growing interest in their application to AD. However, autonomous driving inherently operates in a 3D environment, requiring a holistic understanding of geometric and spatial relationships that 2D VLMs struggle to fully capture. This paper aims to bridge this gap by enabling VLMs to reason effectively in a 3D context, moving beyond superficial 2D scene understanding.

**Addressing Limitations of Existing Autonomous Driving Datasets and Benchmarks:**
- **Sparse Supervision:** Traditional AD datasets often rely on expert driving trajectories, which provide only sparse supervision. These trajectories represent safe, expert actions but lack insights into the underlying complex decision-making processes or "what-if" scenarios that human drivers consider. This sparse data makes it challenging to train robust end-to-end driving models that can handle diverse and challenging situations.
- **Open-Loop Evaluation Biases:** The paper explicitly criticizes the limitations of current open-loop evaluation benchmarks (e.g., nuScenes-based planning). These benchmarks can lead to models overfitting to expert trajectories or implicitly relying on ego status information, thereby showing deceptively high performance without true generalization or robust decision-making abilities in interactive, real-world scenarios.
- **Lack of Holistic Language Annotation:** While some datasets incorporate language (e.g., nuScenes-QA, DriveLM, LingoQA), they often lack the dense, structured language annotations that connect complex 3D spatial reasoning with planning and counterfactual analysis.

**Pioneering Counterfactual Reasoning in AD:** The core innovation of OmniDrive lies in its integration of counterfactual reasoning. Inspired by how human drivers evaluate alternative actions and their potential outcomes, the dataset generates question-answering (Q&A) data based on hypothetical (counterfactual) trajectories. This approach provides denser supervision signals, explicitly linking planning trajectories with language-based reasoning, which is a novel and crucial step toward more robust and explainable autonomous systems. LingoQA is acknowledged for introducing counterfactual questions, but OmniDrive extends this to deep 3D trajectory analysis.

**Exploring LLM-Agent Architectures for AD:** The paper also contributes to the ongoing debate about the optimal architecture for LLM-agents in AD. Should these agents be built by extending existing 2D VLMs to 3D (VLM-centric approach) or by integrating traditional 3D perception stacks into a vision-language framework (3D-perception-centric approach)? By proposing and comparing Omni-L and Omni-Q, the authors provide empirical insights into these design paradigms, guiding future research in this nascent area.

In essence, "OmniDrive" addresses fundamental challenges in bringing human-like reasoning and robustness to autonomous driving systems by developing a novel, richly annotated dataset and exploring effective VLM architectures capable of complex 3D understanding and counterfactual analysis.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind the OmniDrive research can be summarized as follows:

- **Objective 1: Extend Vision-Language Models (VLMs) to Holistic 3D Understanding for Autonomous Driving.**
 - **Motivation:** While 2D VLMs have shown powerful reasoning, real-world autonomous driving necessitates a comprehensive understanding of 3D geometry and spatial relationships. Existing VLMs often struggle to translate their 2D capabilities to the complexities of a 3D driving environment, hindering their potential for practical AD applications. The authors aim to bridge this critical gap.

- **Objective 2: Develop a Holistic Vision-Language Dataset (OmniDrive) that Aligns Agent Models with 3D Driving Tasks through Counterfactual Reasoning.**
 - **Motivation:** Current AD datasets, particularly those based on expert trajectories, offer only sparse supervision. This sparsity limits the ability of end-to-end driving models to learn complex decision-making and underlying reasoning processes. By incorporating counterfactual reasoning—evaluating hypothetical scenarios and their outcomes—the authors aim to generate a large-scale, high-quality dataset that provides denser, richer supervision signals. This approach mirrors human cognitive processes, where drivers consider various possibilities to make safer decisions, thereby fostering a stronger connection between planning trajectories and language-based reasoning.

- **Objective 3: Provide Insights into Designing Effective Large Language Model (LLM)-Agents for Autonomous Driving.**
 - **Motivation:** The field of driving VLM design is complex and underexplored. A fundamental question is whether to build upon existing 2D VLMs and adapt them to 3D space, or to integrate traditional 3D perception stacks into a vision-language framework. The authors aim to empirically investigate these two paradigms by proposing and evaluating two distinct LLM-agent frameworks (Omni-L and Omni-Q). This comparison is crucial for understanding the importance of vision-language alignment versus 3D perception capabilities in designing robust LLM-agents for AD, guiding future architectural choices.

- **Objective 4: Enhance the Safety and Interpretability of Autonomous Driving Systems.**
 - **Motivation:** As AD systems become more complex, their interpretability and safety redundancy are paramount. By enabling agents to perform counterfactual reasoning and language-based explanations of their decisions, the research aims to make AD systems more transparent, trustworthy, and capable of identifying and mitigating potential risks, thereby enhancing overall safety.

In essence, the research seeks to overcome the limitations of current AD datasets and VLM architectures to create more intelligent, reasoning-capable autonomous driving agents that can operate safely and robustly in complex 3D environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed in "OmniDrive" is two-fold: the creation of the novel OmniDrive dataset and the development and evaluation of two distinct LLM-agent architectures.

#### 3.1. OmniDrive Dataset Generation Pipeline

The OmniDrive dataset is generated using a sophisticated, counterfactual-based synthetic data annotation pipeline on the nuScenes dataset, incorporating human-in-the-loop quality assurance.

1. **Planning-Oriented Key-Frame Selection:**
 - To address data redundancy in large AD datasets, the pipeline strategically selects representative key-frames.
 - **Semantic Diversity:** CLIP embeddings are extracted from front-view images to capture diverse semantic elements (e.g., landmarks, traffic lights). K-means clustering is applied, selecting 20% of cluster centers to ensure semantic variety.
 - **Trajectory Diversity:** Further filtering is performed based on the vehicle's future trajectory. K-means clustering is used again to select 200 cluster centers representing various driving behaviors (stopping, accelerating, turning, etc.).
 - This two-stage selection ensures comprehensive coverage of semantic and dynamic scenarios, streamlining subsequent rule-based checklist and prompt design.

2. **Counterfactual Checklist and Prompt Design:**
 - **Simulated Trajectories:** Expert driving trajectories from nuScenes are clustered and categorized into common driving behaviors. For each scene, these behaviors are simulated to assess their feasibility and potential rule violations.
 - **Rule-Based Counterfactual Checklist:** A rule-based checklist is designed using 3D object detection, centerline, and road element topology annotations from nuScenes and OpenLane-v2. This checklist automatically validates simulated trajectories for violations like object collisions, road boundary collisions, or running red lights.
 - **GPT-4 for High-Level Decisions:** For more complex traffic rules not covered by explicit annotations, GPT-4 is prompted to analyze images and assess driving safety/compliance based on high-level decision-making information derived from simulated trajectories. This leverages GPT-4's common sense reasoning.
 - **Expert Trajectory Integration:** The actual expert log replay trajectory from nuScenes is also provided as input, along with identified "close objects" (within 10 meters in the next 3 seconds), to contextualize realistic scenarios.
 - **Caption Generation:** To overcome GPT-4's limitations in directly processing complex 3D scenes from raw inputs, multi-view images (front and rear stitched) are fed to GPT-4 to generate comprehensive scene descriptions. These captions provide crucial contextual information, enhance OCR capabilities, and aid in open-world object recognition, simplifying the 3D scene representation for the language model.

3. **Q&A Generation (Leveraging GPT-4):**
 - Using the structured scene information and captions, GPT-4 generates various types of Q&A pairs:
 - **Scene Description:** Direct generation based on the multi-view captions.
 - **Attention:** Identifying objects requiring attention based on simulated/expert trajectories and GPT-4's common sense.
 - **Counterfactual Reasoning:** Explaining consequences if simulated trajectories violate traffic rules (e.g., "If I turn left here, what are the consequences?").
 - **Decision Making and Planning:** Reasoning why a given expert trajectory is safe and optimal.
 - **General Conversation:** Multi-turn dialogues covering object counting, colors, relative positions, and OCR tasks to improve long-tail object recognition.
 - **Human-in-the-Loop Quality Assurance:** Initially, human verification is performed on Q&A generated from key-frames. Once the design generalizes, large-scale data generation is initiated with ongoing quality checks.

#### 3.2. OmniDrive-Agent Frameworks (Omni-L and Omni-Q)

The paper proposes two distinct LLM-agent frameworks to investigate different approaches to integrating vision and language for AD:

- **Shared Visual Encoder:** Both frameworks utilize EVA-02-L as a vision encoder, which is pre-trained with masked image modeling to distill CLIP, ensuring language-aligned visual features. The visual features are then combined with 3D positional encoding and fed into a projector.
- **Projector Design:** This is the key differentiating component. The projector aligns visual features with the language embedding space before feeding them to a Large Language Model (LLM, specifically LLaMA2-7B).

1. **Omni-L (VLM-Centric Approach):**
 - **Design Philosophy:** Builds upon existing 2D VLMs, specifically following the design of LLaVA.
 - **Architecture:** Employs a simple Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) for aligning the visual-language embedding space. It extends LLaVA's single image input to multiple views by flattening multi-view image features.
 - **3D Integration:** To distinguish different viewpoints, 3D positional encoding is added to each image patch, although these weights are initialized to zero for training stability.
 - **Focus:** Prioritizes vision-language alignment, adapting proven 2D VLM techniques to a multi-view 3D context.

2. **Omni-Q (3D-Perception-Centric Approach):**
 - **Design Philosophy:** Integrates elements from traditional 3D perception stacks (specifically BEV architecture like StreamPETR) into a vision-language framework, inspired by Q-Former (BLIP-2).
 - **Architecture:** Uses a Transformer decoder similar to Q-Former. It initializes "detection queries" (for perception tasks) and "carrier queries" (for language alignment).
 - **3D Integration:** These queries perform self-attention for information exchange and then cross-attention to collect information from multi-view images (combined with 3D positional encoding).
 - **Dual Role:** Detection queries are used to predict categories and coordinates of foreground elements (traditional 3D perception). Carrier queries are projected via an MLP to align with LLM tokens for text generation.
 - **Focus:** Aims to enhance VLM localization abilities by leveraging 3D positional encoding and supervision from query-based perception models.

#### 3.3. Training Strategy

Both Omni-L and Omni-Q follow a two-stage training process:
1. **2D-Pretraining:** Initialize the Q-Former/MLP Projector on 2D image tasks, replicating settings from LLaVA v1.5.
2. **3D-Finetuning:** Fine-tune the model on 3D-related driving tasks (motion planning, counterfactual reasoning) using the newly generated OmniDrive dataset. The training uses AdamW optimizer, with specific learning rates for the projector, visual encoder, and LLM, employing a cosine annealing policy for stability.

This comprehensive methodology allows for the creation of a high-quality, dense dataset and a rigorous comparison of architectural paradigms for LLM-agents in autonomous driving.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted in the paper demonstrate the effectiveness of the OmniDrive dataset and provide crucial insights into the design of LLM-agents for autonomous driving.

#### 4.1. Effectiveness of OmniDrive Dataset

- **Improvements on DriveLM Q&A Benchmark:**
 - Models pre-trained solely on the DriveLM dataset achieved an average score of 53%.
 - Incorporating pre-training with **OmniDrive significantly boosted performance by 3%**, even without additional general VLM pre-training.
 - When combined with LLaVA665K pre-training, OmniDrive still provided a notable additional improvement, demonstrating its unique value and high quality for fine-tuning despite its largely automated annotation process. This highlights OmniDrive's ability to provide richer, more relevant supervision signals for driving-specific reasoning.

- **Impact on NuScenes Open-Loop Planning:**
 - **Mitigation of Overfitting to Ego Status:** The study found that large language models tend to overfit to ego status information in open-loop planning. While incorporating ego status generally improves metrics, OmniDrive's Q&A data helps mitigate this issue. For instance, training Omni-L with OmniDrive data (without ego status) reduced the collision rate from 3.22% to 1.90% and the intersection rate from 3.90% to 3.29% compared to training solely on trajectory prediction tasks.
 - **Significant Improvements with Ego Status:** When Omni-L was trained with both OmniDrive data and ego status, it showed significant improvements across all metrics (L2 error, collision rate, intersection rate), achieving comparable or better performance than state-of-the-art vision-based planners.

#### 4.2. Comparison of Omni-L and Omni-Q (LLM-Agent Architectures)

The comparative analysis of Omni-L (VLM-centric) and Omni-Q (3D-perception-centric) provides key insights into effective LLM-agent design:

- **Performance in Open-Loop Planning (without Ego Status):**
 - **Omni-L demonstrated significantly better performance** (lower L2 error, collision rate, and intersection rate) compared to Omni-Q without leveraging ego status. This suggests that the approach of migrating 2D VLMs to 3D (Omni-L's design) is currently more effective for open-loop planning when operating without explicit ego-status cues.
 - **Omni-Q showed weaker VLM capabilities** and was more susceptible to overfitting to ego status when present, resulting in a lower L2 error but a higher collision rate without explicit ego status.

- **Performance in Counterfactual Reasoning Tasks:**
 - **Omni-L generally performed better** on average across counterfactual reasoning tasks (e.g., 72.1% Precision and 58.0% Recall for safety tasks). This highlights its stronger overall reasoning capabilities derived from its VLM-centric design.
 - **Omni-Q showed some benefits from 3D perception supervision** in specific tasks like collision detection (32.3% Precision and 72.6% Recall), indicating its ability to leverage explicit 3D understanding. However, its overall performance was constrained by weaker foundational language skills.

- **Ablation Study and Correlation with Language Ability:**
 - The ablation studies (Table 5) revealed a **strong positive correlation between a model's language ability (measured by CIDEr score) and its performance** in both counterfactual reasoning and open-loop planning.
 - **Omni-L, with its higher Language CIDEr score (73.2), consistently outperformed Omni-Q** (CIDEr 68.6) in both counterfactual reasoning (higher AP/AR) and open-loop planning (lower collision/intersection rates).
 - This critical finding suggests that **strong foundational language skills and effective vision-language alignment (as emphasized by Omni-L)** are currently more crucial for complex AD reasoning and planning tasks than direct integration of traditional 3D perception stacks (as attempted by Omni-Q) if it comes at the expense of language capability.

In summary, the results strongly validate OmniDrive as a high-quality dataset that enhances AD VLM performance and provides clear empirical evidence that, for current LLM-agents, building upon robust 2D VLMs and aligning them with 3D space (Omni-L) yields better overall reasoning and planning capabilities than directly integrating 3D perception stacks that may compromise foundational language skills.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The OmniDrive research makes several significant contributions with substantial potential impact on the field of autonomous driving and vision-language AI:

- **Pioneering a Holistic, Counterfactual-Based Dataset:** OmniDrive represents a significant step forward in autonomous driving datasets. By introducing counterfactual reasoning, it moves beyond simplistic imitation learning and sparse supervision, providing dense, high-quality, and richly contextualized data. This is crucial for training AD agents that can not only follow expert trajectories but also reason about alternative scenarios, anticipate consequences, and make safer, more informed decisions—much like human drivers. This will accelerate research into more robust and generalizable AD systems.

- **Advancing 3D Understanding for VLMs in AD:** The work directly tackles the critical challenge of extending 2D VLM capabilities to comprehensive 3D geometric and spatial understanding. OmniDrive's structured approach to inputting 3D scene information to GPT-4 (via captions and counterfactual checklists) offers a practical solution to enabling VLMs to reason effectively in complex 3D environments, unlocking their full potential for real-world AD.

- **Providing Crucial Insights for LLM-Agent Design:** The empirical comparison between Omni-L and Omni-Q offers invaluable guidance for future research into autonomous driving LLM-agents. The finding that a strong vision-language alignment (Omni-L) currently outperforms attempts to deeply integrate traditional 3D perception stacks (Omni-Q) suggests that leveraging the powerful reasoning capabilities of existing VLMs and adapting them to 3D is a more promising avenue. This insight can help focus future architectural development, preventing researchers from pursuing less fruitful paths prematurely.

- **Enhancing Interpretability and Safety Redundancy:** By explicitly generating Q&A pairs related to counterfactual scenarios and decision-making rationale, OmniDrive directly contributes to making autonomous driving systems more interpretable. An agent that can explain *why* it chose a particular action and *what would happen* if it chose otherwise provides a higher level of transparency and trust. This increased interpretability, coupled with the enhanced reasoning from counterfactual analysis, naturally leads to improved safety redundancy, a paramount concern for real-world deployment of autonomous vehicles.

- **Accelerating End-to-End Autonomous Driving Research:** OmniDrive provides a novel benchmark and dataset that supports the development of end-to-end autonomous driving systems that are not just reactive but truly reasoning-capable. Its utility is demonstrated by the significant improvements observed on established benchmarks like DriveLM and nuScenes open-loop planning, indicating its potential to become a standard resource for training and evaluating next-generation AD models.

**Limitations and Future Work:**
The authors acknowledge a key limitation: the current counterfactual simulations do not account for the reactive behaviors of other agents in a closed-loop setting. As closed-loop planning simulators mature, future work aims to integrate these dynamic interactions to further enhance the dataset's effectiveness and bring the simulations closer to real-world complexities. Further research is also needed to better align traditional 3D perception stacks with language spaces to fully leverage their benefits without compromising the foundational language capabilities of VLMs.

In conclusion, "OmniDrive" is a foundational work that pushes the boundaries of autonomous driving AI by integrating advanced VLM capabilities with structured counterfactual reasoning. Its holistic approach to dataset creation and architectural exploration provides essential tools and insights for developing more intelligent, safe, and interpretable autonomous vehicles of the future.
