# Deformable Object Manipulation under Partial Observability via Real-Time Full-Shape Estimation

> [!summary] Summary
> cRVAE is a conditional recurrent variational autoencoder that reconstructs the full state of a rope or fabric from only sparse corner-node measurements, replacing the encoder with a learned conditional prior at inference and correcting both state and RNN memory from measurement residuals. Used as the forward model in receding-horizon control, it matches a parameter-identified XPBD simulator in accuracy while running hundreds of times faster, enabling 10/10 collision-free real-robot runs.

## Problem

- Learned latent dynamics models for deformable objects capture rich geometry but need dense point-cloud or full-state observations at inference, which are occlusion-sensitive and costly on hardware.
- Physics-based simulators such as XPBD need carefully identified stiffness and damping parameters, and differentiating through them is too slow for in-loop receding-horizon optimization.
- Existing sparse-feedback approaches sidestep full-shape estimation entirely, controlling only endpoints or treating the object as rigid, so they cannot anticipate collisions of unmeasured interior nodes.

## Method

- **cRVAE** couples a ==GRU== hidden state with a ==conditional prior== over a latent variable, so at inference the encoder is discarded and the decoder reconstructs all nodes from hidden state, corner measurements, and input.
- A ==Jacobian-weighted measurement feedback== rule corrects both the predicted state and the recurrent hidden state from the corner residual, with gains alpha and beta limiting multi-step drift.
- Training minimizes full-state reconstruction plus a weighted corner term and ==KL divergence==; real-world decoder calibration adds ==stretching and bending physics losses== to supervise unmeasured nodes.

## Results

- At horizon 40 cRVAE reaches **2.51 cm** rope and **0.98 cm** fabric MAE versus **3.28** and **1.44 cm** for parameter-identified XPBD and **5.66** and **4.92 cm** for a geometric baseline.
- A rollout with controller derivatives costs about **9 ms** on rope against **3.2 s** for XPBD, and XPBD is roughly **1550x** slower per forward pass on fabric.
- cRVAE completes narrow-gap tasks **100%** where reactive CBF-QP scores **0%**, feedback cuts interior MAE from **1.256** to **1.058 cm**, and hardware ran **10/10** collision-free at **65 ms**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Corner measurements barely constrain interior shape: fitting only two corners is net-negative, while adding one mid-span sensor cuts error more than any gain tuning.
> - A learned surrogate need not beat a calibrated simulator on accuracy to win; matching it at in-loop speed is what makes horizon planning deployable.
> - Real-world decoder calibration trades simulation accuracy for real accuracy (1.32 to 4.22 cm sim, 4.05 to 2.63 cm real), so expect specialization not free transfer.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Manipulating deformable objects (DOs) such as fabrics, cables, and cleaning materials presents a significant challenge in robotics due to their high-dimensional state spaces, complex underactuated dynamics, and the practical difficulty of obtaining full-state observations from standard sensing setups. Accurate predictive models are essential for robotic controllers to anticipate object shape changes, plan collision-free actions, and achieve desired manipulation outcomes, especially in obstacle-rich environments.

Current approaches to modeling DO dynamics generally fall into two categories:
1. **Learning-based models:** These methods leverage data to learn complex DO dynamics, often capturing intricate geometric details. Examples include PointNet-based encoders or variational autoencoders that map high-dimensional visual or point-cloud data into compact latent spaces where dynamics are modeled. While powerful for prediction, these models typically demand dense or full-state observations during both training and inference, which can be computationally expensive, susceptible to occlusion, and challenging to acquire consistently on physical systems.
2. **Physics-based models:** These models propagate DO motion using a compact set of physical parameters (e.g., stiffness, mass) and are often computationally efficient and interpretable. Methods like Position-Based Dynamics (PBD) or eXtended Position-Based Dynamics (XPBD) are examples. However, their accuracy is highly dependent on precise parameter identification to match real-world objects, and model mismatch or sim-to-real errors can accumulate over time, leading to drift in predictions, particularly problematic for long-horizon planning tasks like obstacle avoidance. Furthermore, computing gradients through these simulators for optimization-based control can be computationally intensive.

A critical gap exists for control-oriented DO manipulation: planners require fast, differentiable, full-state predictive models, but physical systems typically provide only sparse measurements (e.g., endpoints or corner nodes). Existing sparse-feedback methods often avoid full-state reconstruction, focusing on local corrections or output-based formulations, or rely on iterative online calibration of full-state models, which is too slow for real-time receding-horizon control. This paper addresses this gap by proposing a learning-based model that can infer the full DO shape from partial observations, incorporate sparse measurement feedback, and remain computationally lightweight for real-time optimization.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a control-oriented predictive model that can accurately estimate the full state of deformable objects from sparse, partial observations in real time, enabling effective manipulation under partial observability.

The motivation for this objective stems from several practical challenges in robotic DO manipulation:

- **High-Dimensionality and Underactuation:** Deformable objects possess an effectively infinite-dimensional state space and are typically manipulated via a limited number of contact points (underactuated), making their dynamics complex to model and predict.
- **Partial Observability:** Real-world robotic systems often cannot perceive the full state of a deformable object due to sensor limitations, occlusions, or the cost of dense sensing. Instead, they commonly obtain sparse observations, such as the positions of end-effectors or a few critical "corner" nodes. This partial information is insufficient for many planning algorithms that require a full shape estimate to foresee collisions or achieve complex configurations.
- **Computational Constraints for Real-Time Control:** Model-based control strategies, particularly receding-horizon optimization (e.g., Model Predictive Control, iLQR), require the predictive model to be evaluated repeatedly and rapidly within a strict control loop budget (e.g., 100 ms). Both complex learning-based models (requiring dense inputs) and high-fidelity physics-based simulations (requiring extensive computation for forward passes and derivatives) often exceed these real-time constraints, hindering their deployment on physical hardware.
- **Model Mismatch and Sim-to-Real Gap:** Even with physics-based models, discrepancies between simulated and real object properties can lead to prediction drift, especially over longer prediction horizons or when only sparse feedback is available. Learning-based models also suffer from sim-to-real challenges if trained purely in simulation. A robust model needs to account for these inaccuracies and integrate real-world feedback effectively.

To address these motivations, the authors propose a model that aims to:
1. **Infer full DO state from sparse observations:** Specifically, from "corner-node" measurements, which are readily available in many manipulation setups.
2. **Maintain temporal context and adapt to real-time feedback:** The model should correct its internal state and future predictions using incoming sparse measurements to mitigate error accumulation.
3. **Be differentiable and computationally efficient:** This is crucial for integration into gradient-based receding-horizon optimal control frameworks, allowing for fast planning and deployment on physical robots.

The overarching goal is to enable obstacle-aware collaborative DO manipulation on physical hardware by providing a reliable and fast full-shape estimator under practical sensing limitations.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method introduces **cRVAE**, a control-oriented conditional recurrent variational autoencoder, designed to estimate the full deformable object (DO) state from sparse corner-node measurements and manipulation inputs. This model is then integrated into a receding-horizon optimal control framework.

**A. cRVAE Architecture and Training**

The cRVAE comprises several components:

- **Encoder (`Enc_φ`)**: During training, the encoder maps the current full state (`P_k`), its temporal difference (`ΔP_k = P_k - P_{k-1}`), and the previous recurrent hidden state (`η_k`) to the parameters (mean `μ_k` and log variance `log σ²_k`) of a Gaussian posterior distribution. A latent variable `z_k` is sampled from this distribution. This component is **only used during training**.
- **Conditional Prior (`Prior_ψ`)**: This component estimates the latent distribution based solely on the previous hidden state (`η_k`). During inference, when the full state `P_k` is unavailable, the encoder is bypassed, and `z_k` is sampled from this conditional prior.
- **Decoder (`Dec_θ`)**: The decoder reconstructs the full next state (`P_{k+1}`) from the updated hidden state (`η_{k+1}`), the current sparse corner node measurements (`C_k`), and the manipulation input (`f_k`).
- **RNN Module (`RNN`)**: A Gated Recurrent Unit (GRU) is used to update the hidden state (`η_k`) at each time step. The RNN takes the sampled latent variable (`z_k`), current corner measurements (`C_k`), manipulation input (`f_k`), and previous hidden state (`η_k`) as inputs to compute `η_{k+1}`.

The model is trained by minimizing a loss function that combines:
- **Reconstruction Loss**: The squared L2 norm between the ground truth next full state (`P_{k+1}`) and the decoded estimated state (`P_{k+1}`).
- **Corner Node Loss**: A weighted squared L2 norm between the ground truth corner nodes (`C_{k+1}`) and their predicted counterparts (`C_{k+1}`), emphasizing accuracy on observable parts.
- **KL Divergence**: A term that regularizes the latent space by ensuring consistency between the encoder's posterior distribution (conditioned on full state) and the conditional prior distribution (conditioned only on the hidden state). This is crucial for enabling effective inference where the encoder is absent.

**B. Inference with Measurement Feedback**

During real-time inference, the full DO state is not available. The cRVAE uses the conditional prior (instead of the encoder) to sample the latent variable. To counteract model errors and the sim-to-real gap, a feedback mechanism is implemented:

- **Residual Calculation**: A residual `r_k` is computed as the difference between the *actual* (measured or propagated) corner node positions (`C_dyn_k`) and the *predicted* corner node positions (`C_k`) extracted from the estimated full state. `C_dyn_k` uses actual measurements when available (e.g., every `T_m` steps) and propagates previous measurements using robot kinematics or dynamics in between.
- **State and Hidden State Correction**: The residual `r_k` is used to correct both the estimated full state (`P_k`) and the recurrent hidden state (`η_k`).
 - `P_k_corr = P_k + α * J_nodes_k * r_k`: The estimated full state is corrected by adding a scaled version of the residual, projected through the Jacobian of the estimated state with respect to the corner node positions (`J_nodes_k = ∂P_k / ∂C_k`).
 - `η_k_corr = η_k + β * J_k^T * r_k`: The hidden state is corrected by adding a scaled version of the residual, projected through the Jacobian of the predicted corner nodes with respect to the hidden state (`J_k = ∂C_k / ∂η_k`).
 - `α` and `β` are gains that balance trust between the model's estimate and the measurement. Correcting the hidden state ensures that the learned temporal context is also adjusted, limiting error accumulation. The corrected state `P_k_corr` is used to initialize the receding-horizon problem.

**C. Control Problem Formulation**

The cRVAE model `Φ_θ` (which outputs `(P_{k+1}, η_{k+1})` given `C_k, f_k, η_k`) acts as the forward dynamics model within a receding-horizon optimal control framework. At each planning cycle, an optimization problem is solved over a horizon `H` to find a sequence of manipulation inputs `f_0, ..., f_{H-1}` that minimize a cost function `J`.

The cost function includes:
- **Running Costs (`ℓ`)**: Penalizing deviations from desired object configurations and control effort.
- **Terminal Cost (`ℓ_T`)**: Penalizing the final state.
- **Inequality Constraints (`D ≥ 0`)**: Enforcing collision avoidance with obstacles (`O`).

The dynamics constraint `(P_{k+1}, η_{k+1}) = Φ_θ(C_k, f_k, η_k)` uses the cRVAE to predict future states. The corner node inputs `C_k` within the horizon are propagated using the function `g(.)` from Equation (11) (e.g., based on robot kinematics) to simulate future measurements. An iLQR controller is used for solving this optimization problem, leveraging the differentiability of cRVAE to compute necessary gradients efficiently.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy of cRVAE was evaluated through simulation studies on rope and fabric, followed by real-world validation on a Unitree Go2 robot.

**A. Rope Simulation**

1. **cRVAE Training and Modeling Accuracy**:
 - cRVAE was trained on data from an XPBD-based rope simulator (1.5 m rope, 33 nodes, 2 corner nodes, 1 actuated node).
 - **Prediction Accuracy**: cRVAE achieved a Mean Absolute Error (MAE) of 0.81 cm at horizon `h=1` and 2.51 cm at `h=40`. This significantly outperformed a geometric similarity-transform baseline (2.79 cm at `h=1`, 5.66 cm at `h=40`) and a parameter-identified XPBD physics reference (2.75 cm at `h=1`, 3.28 cm at `h=40`). cRVAE consistently maintained lower error across the entire prediction horizon.
2. **Ablation of the Latent Variable**:
 - A deterministic variant (without the latent variable and KL term) was tested. cRVAE with the latent variable was found to be more accurate at every tested horizon, with a difference of 0.57 cm at `h=1` and 0.55 cm at `h=10`, indicating the stochastic latent variable contributes to better prediction.
3. **Ablation of Measurement Feedback**:
 - The feedback correction (equations 12-13) was tested by ablating `α` (state correction) and `β` (hidden state correction).
 - At short measurement intervals (`T_m=1` or `T_m=5`), the full feedback correction (α=1.0, β=2.0) significantly reduced interior node MAE (e.g., from 1.256 cm to 1.058 cm at `T_m=1`, a 13.1% improvement, with `p = 0.004` across 9/9 trajectories). The benefit decreased with increasing `T_m`.
 - The study found that fitting only the two corner nodes identified little interior shape information, leading to drift if not damped. Adding an interior node (node 12) to the correction mechanism significantly improved interior MAE (from 1.29 cm to 0.88 cm at `T_m=1`), suggesting that sensor placement, rather than mere gain tuning, is crucial for interior node accuracy.
4. **Comparison in Obstacle Avoidance Task**:
 - **Baseline Scenario**: Both cRVAE (with iLQR) and a reactive CBF-QP controller successfully completed 100% of runs in a wide-gap obstacle avoidance task. cRVAE exhibited larger clearance at lower speeds, while CBF-QP showed larger clearance at the highest speed, trading off tracking accuracy for margin.
 - **Challenging Scenario**: In a narrow-gap configuration, cRVAE (with iLQR) completed 100% of runs at leader speeds of 0.005 m/s and 0.015 m/s, maintaining clearances of approximately 0.10 m. In contrast, the reactive CBF-QP controller failed 0% of runs, resulting in zero clearance, as it could not plan ahead to stretch the rope through the gap.
 - **Model Comparison**: When XPBD was used as the forward model within the *same* iLQR framework (XPBD+iLQR), it also completed 100% of runs with comparable clearances to cRVAE. This demonstrated that cRVAE achieves comparable task performance to a physics-based model *under the same horizon-based controller*.
 - **Computation Time**: cRVAE's total computation time (forward pass + iLQR derivatives) was approximately 9 ms at `h=40`, staying well within the 100 ms control budget. XPBD, however, exceeded the budget at every horizon, taking 396 ms at `h=5` and 3.2 s at `h=40`, despite being JIT-compiled in JAX. This confirmed cRVAE's critical speed advantage for real-time deployment.

**B. Fabric Simulation**

1. **cRVAE Training and Modeling Accuracy**:
 - cRVAE was trained on data from a mesh-based Hooke's law simulator (15x15 node mesh, 4 corner nodes, 2 actuated nodes).
 - **Prediction Accuracy**: cRVAE achieved an MAE of 0.66 cm at `h=1` and 0.98 cm at `h=40`. This was comparable to a physics reference (XPBD for cloth, 0.57 cm at `h=1`, 1.44 cm at `h=40`) at short horizons, but cRVAE showed lower error at `h=40` (0.46 cm lower). Both significantly outperformed the geometric baseline (4.02 cm at `h=1`, 4.92 cm at `h=40`).
2. **Computation Time**:
 - On the fabric mesh, XPBD was approximately 1550× slower than cRVAE per forward pass, exceeding the 100 ms budget at `h=3`. cRVAE remained at 1.5 ms at `h=40`, even including controller derivatives. The Jacobian computation for XPBD at this state dimension exceeded memory limits. This further emphasized cRVAE's efficiency for 2D deformable objects.
3. **Comparison in Obstacle Avoidance Task**:
 - In a fabric manipulation task with varying narrow gaps between obstacles, cRVAE (with MPPI solver) completed 100% of runs across all tested gap widths (from 3.1 m down to 1.1 m), maintaining clearances around 0.20-0.23 m.
 - The reactive CBF-QP controller achieved 100% success for wide gaps (g ≥ 2.1 m) but failed to complete any runs for narrow gaps (g ≤ 1.6 m), resulting in 0% task completion and zero clearance. This corroborated the finding from the rope simulation: horizon-based planning enabled by cRVAE is crucial for challenging narrow-gap tasks.

**C. Real-World Experiments**

1. **Experimental Setup**: A Unitree Go2 robot with a D1 manipulator (follower) manipulated one end of an 180 cm rope, while an SO-101 manipulator held the other end (leader). Six motion capture sensors provided ground truth for calibration, but only two corner nodes (endpoints) were used by cRVAE during inference in control runs.
2. **Simulator Calibration**: XPBD simulator parameters were calibrated using a single real-world dataset to reduce the sim-to-real gap, optimizing stiffness, damping, mass, and rest length.
3. **cRVAE Training and Calibration**:
 - cRVAE was initially trained on high-resolution simulated data (31 rope nodes) from the calibrated XPBD simulator.
 - To reduce the real-to-sim gap, the cRVAE decoder was *calibrated* on real-world data (7 datasets) while other components remained frozen. This involved using the prior sampling mode and incorporating stretching and bending constraints into the loss function to provide gradient signals for the unmeasured intermediate nodes.
 - Calibration reduced the real-world MAE on the calibration set from 4.05 cm to 2.63 cm, and on a held-out validation set from 2.88 cm to 1.85 cm.
 - Notably, calibration reduced a significant bias where the model consistently predicted the rope higher than its true position (underestimated sag). The mid-span Z-error (at node 18) was reduced from 3.73 cm to 1.09 cm.
4. **Real-World Control Experiments**:
 - cRVAE was deployed on the Unitree Go2 for obstacle avoidance tasks (10 runs per scenario) where the robot maintained a desired offset and angular synchrony with a leader.
 - **Success Rate**: In both scenarios (different obstacle geometries), cRVAE achieved 10/10 successful runs with no observed physical contact between the rope and obstacles, demonstrating real-world feasibility.
 - **Computation Time**: The mean computation time per control action was 65.57 ms and 63.58 ms across the two scenarios, well within the 100 ms control period, confirming real-time deployability.
 - A direct comparison with CBF-QP was not performed in real-world due to its requirement for the full rope state, which was unavailable from physical sensing during control runs.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work presents a significant advancement in deformable object manipulation, particularly by bridging the gap between the need for real-time, full-state predictions and the practical limitations of sparse sensing in physical systems. The key significance and potential impact are:

- **Enabling Real-Time, Horizon-Based Control under Partial Observability**: The cRVAE model demonstrates the feasibility of estimating the full, high-dimensional state of deformable objects (rope and fabric) from only a few sparse corner-node observations. Crucially, it does so at speeds compatible with real-time receding-horizon optimal control (e.g., iLQR, MPPI), addressing a long-standing challenge where physics-based models or other learning-based approaches are often too slow or demand dense sensing.
- **Improved Planning and Collision Avoidance**: By providing a fast and accurate full-shape estimate, cRVAE allows for proactive, horizon-based planning, which is shown to be essential for successfully navigating narrow-gap obstacle avoidance tasks that reactive controllers cannot handle. This ability to "see" the future shape of the deformable object, even from partial current observations, significantly enhances the robot's capacity for complex and safe manipulation in constrained environments.
- **Robustness to Model Mismatch and Sim-to-Real Gap**: The integration of real-time measurement feedback into both the estimated state and the recurrent hidden state of cRVAE provides a mechanism to correct for accumulated model errors and mitigate the sim-to-real gap, which is a common hurdle in transferring robotic skills from simulation to physical hardware. The decoder calibration on real-world data further enhances its performance on physical systems.
- **Reduced Reliance on Complex Physical Parameter Identification**: Unlike physics-based models that require precise and often difficult-to-identify physical parameters for accuracy, cRVAE learns dynamics directly from data and performs inference without needing these parameters online. This simplifies deployment, especially for objects with unknown or varying material properties.
- **Hardware Deployability**: The successful demonstration on a Unitree Go2 robot, completing complex obstacle avoidance tasks without collision and within real-time computation budgets, underscores the practical deployability of the proposed framework. This suggests that cRVAE can be a foundational component for a new generation of robots capable of dexterous manipulation of soft goods in unstructured human environments.

**Future Work**: The authors identify several avenues for future research, including:
- Extending cRVAE to model contact, which would enable a wider range of manipulation tasks where the object interacts extensively with its environment.
- Integrating vision-based feedback to provide interior measurements of the deformable object, thereby improving the prediction accuracy of unmeasured nodes, especially where corner nodes alone provide limited information about interior shape.
