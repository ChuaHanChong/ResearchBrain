# MachineLearningLM: Scaling Many-shot In-context Learning via Continued Pretraining

> [!summary] Summary
> A continued pretraining framework, MACHINELEARNINGLM, enhances a general-purpose LLM with robust many-shot in-context learning capabilities for tabular data, achieving monotonic accuracy scaling and performance comparable to Random Forest. The approach maintains the LLM's general knowledge while outperforming vanilla LLMs by 12-16% in high-shot tabular tasks.

## Problem

- Large Language Models (LLMs) struggle with effective many-shot in-context learning (ICL) for numerical tabular data, showing quick accuracy plateaus and sensitivity to example order.
- Specialized tabular models (e.g., TabPFN) excel at tabular ICL but lack the broad world knowledge, general reasoning, and multimodal perception inherent in LLMs.
- A gap exists in creating a unified model that combines the robust numerical modeling of specialized methods with the versatility and general intelligence of LLMs without performance degradation.

## Method

- A ==continued pretraining framework== (MACHINELEARNINGLM) is applied to a general-purpose LLM (Qwen-2.5-7B-Instruct) using LoRA, framing the task as conditional sequence modeling.
- Millions of diverse, synthetic binary/multiclass tabular prediction tasks are generated from ==Structural Causal Models (SCMs)== for pretraining, ensuring out-of-distribution generalization.
- ==Token-efficient prompting strategies==, including tabular encoding, compact integer representation, and sequence-level batch prediction, maximize the number of in-context examples within the context window.

## Results

- MACHINELEARNINGLM exhibits a striking monotonic many-shot scaling law, with accuracy increasing consistently from **8** to **1,024** in-context demonstrations, outperforming vanilla LLMs (e.g., GPT-5-mini by ~**12%**, o3-mini by ~**16%**) in high-shot settings.
- The model achieves accuracy levels comparable to Random Forest (within **2%** relative) and surpasses instance-based learners (kNN by >**4%** relative) on diverse tabular tasks, without task-specific fine-tuning.
- Continued pretraining successfully preserves the LLM's general knowledge and reasoning abilities, demonstrating comparable performance on the MMLU benchmark (e.g., **73.2%** 0-shot, **75.4%** 50-shot).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Training on diverse synthetic tasks derived from SCMs effectively instills tree-inductive biases and robust numerical modeling capabilities into an LLM.
> - Token-efficient encoding and batch prediction significantly overcome LLM context length limitations, allowing an order of magnitude more demonstrations and improved throughput.
> - A Random Forest mimic warm-start stabilizes training, and confidence-aware, order-robust self-consistency at inference time improves prediction accuracy and mitigates position bias.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Large Language Models (LLMs) have demonstrated capabilities in possessing broad world knowledge and general reasoning, which has positioned them as promising few-shot learners. However, existing LLMs frequently encounter difficulties in effectively leveraging a large number of in-context examples for standard machine learning (ML) tasks, particularly in scenarios requiring many-shot in-context learning (ICL) without gradient-based parameter updates. Performance gains in such settings often plateau after a limited number of demonstrations and can be sensitive to factors such as label biases, example selection, and ordering. These models tend to rely on superficial signals like distributional or formatting cues, rather than identifying underlying causal mechanisms or statistical dependencies necessary for accurate predictions in data-intensive ML tasks.

Concurrently, specialized tabular models, such as TabPFN and TabICL, have shown proficiency in solving ML tasks purely through ICL. Nevertheless, these architectures are purpose-built for tabular data and typically lack the broad world knowledge, general-purpose reasoning, and multimodal perception abilities inherent in LLMs. Consequently, they often depend on meticulously designed featurization and extensive labeled datasets for training. Attempts to integrate LLMs with tabular data, like TabLLM, have sometimes required computationally intensive, task-specific fine-tuning. TabuLa-8B, another LLM fine-tuned for tabular prediction, has demonstrated success in few-shot settings but may face context length limitations for many-shot scenarios.

This research positions itself at the intersection of these two domains, aiming to bridge the gap by endowing a general-purpose LLM with robust many-shot ICL capabilities for ML tasks, without compromising its broader general knowledge and reasoning abilities. The work addresses the challenge of enabling LLMs to "do ML in context" efficiently and effectively, thereby seeking to unify the strengths of general foundation models with the robust numerical modeling typically associated with specialized ML methods.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to equip a general-purpose Large Language Model (LLM) with robust many-shot in-context learning (ICL) capabilities for standard machine learning (ML) tasks, specifically targeting tabular data, while simultaneously preserving its existing general world knowledge and reasoning abilities crucial for broader applications like chat workflows.

The motivation for this work arises from several observed limitations in the current LLM landscape:
- **Inadequate Many-Shot ICL Performance:** Despite their advanced reasoning, existing LLMs often struggle to effectively learn from a large number of in-context examples on typical ML benchmarks. Their accuracy gains tend to plateau rapidly, even after a few demonstrations, and exhibit sensitivity to factors like label biases and the choice or order of examples. This suggests that LLMs frequently rely on superficial cues rather than deeply understanding underlying statistical or causal relationships.
- **Lack of Integration between General and Specialized Models:** While specialized tabular models (e.g., TabPFN, TabICL) excel at ICL for tabular tasks, they inherently lack the broad world knowledge and general multimodal perception that LLMs acquire through extensive pretraining. This forces a trade-off between specialized tabular performance and general-purpose intelligence. Prior LLM-based approaches to tabular data have either involved intensive task-specific fine-tuning or focused predominantly on few-shot learning, often struggling with high numerical data volumes.
- **Context Window Bottleneck:** The fixed and often limited context length of LLMs poses a fundamental constraint on the number of in-context examples they can process, directly impeding their many-shot learning potential. Existing prompting methods for tabular data can be token-inefficient, further exacerbating this issue.

To address these motivations, the `MACHINELEARNINGLM` framework aims to:
1. **Develop an LLM that scales with increasing in-context demonstrations:** Achieve monotonic accuracy improvements as the number of shots grows, overcoming the plateauing observed in vanilla LLMs.
2. **Imbue robust numerical modeling capabilities:** Enable the LLM to effectively learn from numerical tabular data without relying on task-specific fine-tuning or external ML models.
3. **Ensure compatibility with heterogeneous inputs:** Allow the model to natively process both numerical and natural language features within a unified framework.
4. **Preserve general intelligence:** Maintain the LLM's performance on general knowledge and reasoning tasks, ensuring its utility across diverse applications beyond tabular ML.

By pursuing these objectives, the research seeks to establish a paradigm where LLMs can internalize ML learning procedures, offering a unified foundation for heterogeneous, real-world in-context ML tasks, thereby leveraging their general perception and reasoning capabilities more effectively.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The `MACHINELEARNINGLM` framework introduces a portable continued-pretraining approach to enhance a general-purpose LLM with robust many-shot in-context learning (ICL) for tabular data. The methodology involves several key components:

#### 3.1. Pretraining Corpus Synthesis

The pretraining data consists of millions of synthetic tabular prediction tasks generated from Structural Causal Models (SCMs), ensuring strict non-overlap with evaluation datasets.
- **SCM Graph and Mechanisms:** Tasks are created by sampling Directed Acyclic Graphs (DAGs) resembling fully connected MLPs. Structural equations for each node (`x_v`) are assigned functions (`f_v`) drawn from a diverse pool (e.g., tanh, ReLU, RBF, Gaussian processes), with added i.i.d. noise (`ε_v`).
- **Tree-based SCMs:** To incorporate tree inductive biases, 30% of `f_v` functions are replaced with multi-output gradient-boosted regressors, fitted on synthetic targets and parent inputs. This mimics the decision-making process of tree ensembles.
- **Feature and Label Typing:** A fraction of features are sampled as categorical, dense columns are discretized, and category IDs are shuffled to avoid spurious order. Scalar targets (`y*`) are converted into K-way labels (K ranging from 2 to 10) by sampling bounds and permuting class IDs, naturally introducing class imbalance.
- **Task Sampling Policy:** The corpus comprises approximately 3 million distinct SCMs. For each, `M` demonstrations (up to 1,024), `N=50` queries, `d` features (5–50), and `K` classes (2–10) are sampled. Tasks are serialized into a token-efficient prompt.

#### 3.2. Objective of Continued Pre-training

Each tabular prediction task is framed as a conditional sequence modeling problem. The input prompt concatenates:
- An instruction header (H) detailing the task and specifying a JSON output format.
- A many-shot training block (S) with `M` labeled demonstrations.
- A query block (Q) with `N` unlabeled queries, each with an ID.
The model is trained to generate a single JSON array containing predictions for all queries. The training objective is a standard left-to-right negative log-likelihood over the entire JSON target string (including brackets, keys, IDs, and label tokens). This encourages learning feature-to-label mappings, preserving test-row order, and adhering to the JSON format. The base model used is Qwen-7B-Instruct.

#### 3.3. Random-Forest Mimic Warm Start

To prevent training collapse and improve stability with complex synthetic tasks, a warm-up phase mimics a Random Forest (RF) teacher.
- **Mechanism:** Initially, the LLM matches the RF teacher's per-example predictions. This knowledge distillation provides informative targets and smoother gradients.
- **Task-level Guard:** Degenerate or low-signal tasks are filtered out if the RF's accuracy does not significantly outperform a conservative random baseline (based on binomial tail tests and criteria like Cohen’s κ, balanced accuracy, Macro-F1 dominance, and non-collapse checks).
- **Example-level Consensus Filter (Warm-up Only):** During warm-up, only test examples where the RF's prediction matches the ground truth are retained in the prompt. This provides cleaner supervision without relabeling. After warm-up, this filter is disabled to allow the model to develop independent ICL behavior.

#### 3.4. Token-Efficient Prompting for In-Context ML

Three design choices mitigate the context length bottleneck:
- **Tabular Encoding:** Demonstrations are presented in a concise table-style format, separating features with commas and labels with a "|". This significantly reduces token count compared to natural language descriptions, preserves column semantics, and supports heterogeneous textual and numerical features.
- **Compact Integer-Based Number Encoding:** Numerical features are normalized to non-negative integers in the range (`i = clip(round(120z + 500), 0, 999)`). This strategy:
 - Eliminates tokenization fragmentation caused by decimals and signs.
 - Reduces token count (e.g., integers are often single tokens in `cl100k_base`).
 - Prevents LLMs from comparing decimals as strings.
- **Sequence-Level Batch-Prediction:** Multiple query rows (`N=50` by default) are packed into a single sequence, allowing all predictions to be generated in one forward pass. This amortizes instruction/context overhead and stabilizes gradients during pretraining. The order of queries within a sequence is randomized to reduce position bias, and IDs are 0-indexed for stability.

#### 3.5. Order-Robust, Confidence-Aware Self-Consistency at Inference Time

At inference, `V=5` shuffled prompt variants are generated by permuting in-context demonstrations or features. The model's next-token probabilities for each possible label are extracted from these variants. An aggregate probability for each label is computed by summing across all variants. The final prediction is the label with the highest aggregate probability, providing robustness against position effects and improving overall accuracy.

#### 3.6. Implementation Details

The continued pretraining is implemented using LoRA (rank 8) over attention and MLP layers, with the Adam optimizer within the LLaMAFactory framework. It uses a two-stage approach: a warm-up stage (1 million tasks, 1e-5 learning rate) followed by a second stage (2 million tasks, 1e-6 learning rate). The training is distributed across 40 A100 GPUs, maintaining a 32k-token context budget during training, but evaluating on contexts up to 131k tokens.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of `MACHINELEARNINGLM` on the TALENT benchmark, comprising 200 classification tasks (32 main datasets, 86 extended), yielded several key findings regarding its performance, scaling behavior, and general capabilities.

#### 4.1. Performance vs. Vanilla LLMs

- `MACHINELEARNINGLM` demonstrated a significant and consistent improvement over vanilla LLMs. Averaged across tasks, it boosted the absolute accuracy of its Qwen-2.5-7B-Instruct backbone by approximately 15%.
- On about 50% of tasks spanning finance, biology, vision, speech, robotics, statistics, and healthcare domains, improvements exceeded 15%. Minimal degradation was observed across tasks.
- In high-shot settings (128–1,024 shots), `MACHINELEARNINGLM` surpassed strong closed-source baselines such as GPT-5-mini by approximately 12% and o3-mini by approximately 16% on average.

#### 4.2. Many-Shot Scaling Law

- The model exhibited a monotonic many-shot scaling law: accuracy consistently increased as the number of in-prompt demonstrations grew from 8 to 1,024. This was achieved within a 131k-token inference budget, which is four times the 32k-token budget used during pretraining, indicating out-of-distribution generalization in context length.
- In contrast, vanilla LLMs showed limited scaling; for example, o3-mini improved by only approximately 1.8% (with a decline at very high shots), and GPT-5-mini gained merely approximately 4.7% over the same range, suggesting higher sample efficiency for `MACHINELEARNINGLM`.

#### 4.3. Performance vs. State-of-the-Art Tabular-Specific Methods

- Without any task-specific training, `MACHINELEARNINGLM` achieved Random Forest (RF)-level accuracy across 8–512 shots, typically within 2% relative of RF. At 512 shots, it outperformed RF on approximately 30% of tasks.
- It consistently surpassed instance-based learners like kNN by over 4% relative on average, indicating robust numerical modeling.
- Compared to specialized tabular ICL models like TabICL, `MACHINELEARNINGLM` was modestly behind at very high shot counts. This difference is attributed to the LLM's general-purpose architecture and computational requirements versus TabICL's specialized design for tabular data. However, the model maintained order-robustness to demonstration permutations.

#### 4.4. Performance vs. TabuLa-8B

- Due to its token-efficient prompting, `MACHINELEARNINGLM` supported an order of magnitude more demonstrations per prompt than TabuLa-8B, which is often limited to 20-32 shots by its 8k context. `MACHINELEARNINGLM` scaled stably up to 1,024 shots.
- In the few-shot regime (e.g., 32-shot), `MACHINELEARNINGLM` also outperformed TabuLa-8B on average, even though TabuLa-8B explicitly utilizes feature names (which `MACHINELEARNINGLM` intentionally omits during training/inference to ensure fair comparison with traditional ML methods).

#### 4.5. General Abilities Preservation

- `MACHINELEARNINGLM` successfully retained its general capabilities in chat-style workflows. On the MMLU benchmark, it achieved approximately 73.2% micro accuracy in 0-shot and 75.4% in 50-shot settings, which is comparable to strong general-purpose LLMs.
- It notably outperformed TabuLa-8B on MMLU by approximately 10%, indicating that the continued pretraining strategy integrated smoothly with the LLM's general abilities without significant degradation. Consistent gains were observed in numeracy-heavy MMLU subjects.

#### 4.6. Empirical Study Observations

- **Numeric-dominant and Heterogeneous Tables:** The model performed competitively on pure numeric tables and achieved reliable gains on heterogeneous tables (mixing free-text/categorical fields with numbers) without architectural changes or special text processing.
- **High-Cardinality Labels:** Performance declined on tasks with many classes (e.g., >10 classes), which was attributed to the pretraining corpus being biased towards `K ≤ 10` classes.
- **Class Imbalance:** `MACHINELEARNINGLM` exhibited stability under skewed label distributions, maintaining RF-level performance and avoiding collapse to majority classes.
- **Forecasting-Style Tasks:** A notable decline in performance was observed on time-series/forecasting tasks (e.g., FOREX variants), indicating a lack of temporal inductive bias and higher numeric precision requirements in the current pretraining.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of `MACHINELEARNINGLM` marks a significant step towards enabling general-purpose Large Language Models (LLMs) to effectively handle complex machine learning (ML) tasks, particularly those involving tabular data, through many-shot in-context learning (ICL). Its key significance lies in demonstrating that targeted continued pretraining on synthetically generated, diverse datasets can imbue an LLM with robust numerical modeling capabilities and strong scaling properties for ICL, *without requiring architectural modifications or changes to its tokenizer, and while preserving its foundational general knowledge and reasoning abilities*.

This work presents a plausible path towards a **unified foundation model** that can seamlessly integrate natural language understanding and general reasoning with specialized ML task execution. By circumventing the need for task-specific fine-tuning or calls to external ML toolchains, `MACHINELEARNINGLM` offers a more efficient and versatile paradigm. The ability to natively process heterogeneous (textual and numerical) inputs, coupled with impressive many-shot scaling, suggests that LLMs can become more broadly applicable as "in-context ML learners."

The **token-efficient prompting methodology** (tabular encoding, integer-based number encoding, and sequence-level batch prediction) is a notable contribution, addressing a fundamental bottleneck in LLM application—context length limitations and computational cost. This approach allows for a substantially higher number of demonstrations within the context window, leading to improved performance and amortized inference throughput.

Potential impacts of this research include:
- **Expanding LLM Utility:** Broadening the range of applications where LLMs can be directly leveraged for data analysis and prediction, particularly in domains where tabular data is prevalent (e.g., finance, healthcare, biology).
- **Bridging AI Specializations:** Fostering the convergence of general-purpose AI with specialized ML techniques, reducing the fragmentation between different AI model types.
- **Enhanced Interpretability:** The choice of a Random Forest teacher for warm-starting, with its transparent decision paths, offers a potential avenue for future work to leverage "reasoning steps" for more interpretable LLM predictions, aligning with demands for explainable AI.
- **Foundation for Multimodal ML:** The current framework, by combining robust numerical processing with an LLM backbone, lays groundwork for future extensions to multimodal features (e.g., images, speech) within a single model architecture, especially considering its compatibility with HTML table structures.

Future work outlined by the authors highlights promising directions, including:
- **Expanding Task Scope:** Extending beyond tabular classification to regression, ranking, time-series forecasting, and structured prediction, and increasing the number of supported classes.
- **Multimodal Synthetic Tasks:** Generating synthetic tasks with multimodal features to enable in-context prediction on richer, heterogeneous signals.
- **Context Length Scaling:** Further increasing context length through system optimizations, such as tensor/pipeline parallelism and memory-efficient attention, to accommodate even larger numbers of demonstrations.
- **Reasoning and Interpretability:** Integrating narrative distillation, SCM-based explanations, and feature attribution signals to provide more faithful rationales and strengthen reasoning capabilities.
- **Uncertainty-Aware Responses:** Training models to provide calibrated confidences and explore selective prediction, addressing hallucination and improving trustworthiness.
- **Integration with Advanced AI Systems:** Combining with retrieval-augmented methods for scalable any-shot learning and integrating into ML agent workflows for end-to-end ML engineering tasks.

In conclusion, `MACHINELEARNINGLM` demonstrates a scalable and effective strategy for equipping general LLMs with robust in-context ML capabilities, addressing a critical challenge in the current LLM landscape and opening significant avenues for future research and application.
