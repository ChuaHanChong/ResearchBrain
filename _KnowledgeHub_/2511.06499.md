# SportR: A Benchmark for Multimodal Large Language Model Reasoning in Sports

> [!summary] Summary
> SportR, a new multi-sport benchmark from Rice University and collaborators, evaluates Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) on fine-grained, rule-based visual reasoning in sports. The benchmark introduces human-authored Chain-of-Thought rationales and an explicit visual grounding task, showing that state-of-the-art MLLMs struggle significantly, though training on SportR data yields notable performance improvements in rule application and cross-modal generalization.

## Problem

- Existing MLLMs struggle with nuanced visual details, applying abstract sports rules, and connecting these rules to specific visual evidence within a multi-sport context.
- Current sports benchmarks lack detailed, human-authored Chain-of-Thought (CoT) rationales suitable for training generative reasoning, and often do not include precise visual grounding annotations.
- Many benchmarks rely on multiple-choice questions or single-sport focuses, limiting the assessment of true reasoning capabilities and generalizability across diverse sports.

## Method

- Introduces **SportR**, a human-authored, ==multi-sport benchmark== (SportsImage with 4,789 images and SportsVideo with 2,052 clips) for basketball, soccer, table tennis, badminton, and American football.
- Provides high-quality, human-authored Chain-of-Thought (CoT) rationales and a progressive QA hierarchy (up to seven questions per image, six per video) for granular evaluation and training of MLLMs.
- Incorporates an explicit visual grounding task requiring precise bounding box coordinates for rule infractions in images, directly linking abstract rules to localized visual evidence.

## Results

- Baseline MLLMs achieved low accuracy on SportR, particularly on Foul Classification (Q2) and Explicit Visual Grounding (Q5), with Q5 IoU scores under **7%** across all models in a zero-shot setting.
- Training Qwen-2.5VL-**7B** on SportsImage data via SFT and RL improved performance significantly; for example, Foul Classification (Q2) accuracy increased from **14.4%** (base) to **57.0%** (SFT+RL).
- The SFT+RL model, trained only on images, demonstrated cross-modal generalization to video tasks, improving Video Infraction Identification (Q8) from **25.49%** (base) to **59.52%**, surpassing all other models.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - State-of-the-art MLLMs, including proprietary models, show low accuracy in zero-shot settings on SportR's challenging tasks, with visual grounding IoU consistently below 7% for baselines.
> - Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) and Reinforcement Learning (RL) on SportR's image data lead to substantial performance gains (e.g., Qwen-2.5VL-7B's Foul Classification accuracy rose from 14.4% to 57.0% with SFT+RL).
> - Error analysis reveals that video tasks are primarily hampered by visual perception errors, while image tasks for stronger models expose a significant 'domain knowledge gap' in mapping visual evidence to abstract rules.
