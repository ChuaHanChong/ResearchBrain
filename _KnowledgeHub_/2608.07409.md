# UniJEPA: A Unified Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture for Task-Agnostic Visual World Modeling

> [!summary] Summary
> UniJEPA is a joint-embedding predictive architecture that trains photometric (image-level transformation) and temporal (video-level next-state) prediction in one shared latent space with one encoder, one predictor, and a single next-embedding loss plus a Gaussian anti-collapse regularizer. After action-conditioned predictor post-training it plans zero-shot via latent MPC, hitting 74.9 ImageNet linear probe, 78.1 SSv2, and 75.8% planning success at 44x speedup.

## Problem

- JEPA variants are fragmented: masked-image, photometric, and video next-state objectives each use bespoke encoders, predictors, and regularizers that cannot be composed.
- Photometric and temporal world models therefore live in incompatible latent spaces, so no single model covers both image-level and video-level world modeling.
- Prior recipes need multiple loss hyperparameters plus anti-collapse heuristics such as EMA, stop-gradient, or frozen pre-trained encoders instead of end-to-end pixel training.

## Method

- **UniJEPA**'s ==unified prediction objective== casts photometric transform parameters and actions as the same conditioning signal, so one predictor regresses target embeddings for both image and video branches.
- ==Gaussian latent regularizer== penalizes chi-squared deviation of random unit-vector projections from a standard normal, with an ==anti-collapse theorem== bounding minimum covariance eigenvalue below one loss weight alpha.
- ==Action-conditioned post-training== freezes the encoder, fits the predictor on offline observation-action trajectories, then plans by ==sampling-based MPC== minimizing latent distance to a goal embedding.

## Results

- UniJEPA reaches **74.9** ImageNet linear probe versus **73.5** for photometric-only IWM and **78.1** SSv2 top-1 versus **77.3** for V-JEPA 2, with one loss hyperparameter.
- Planning success is **75.8%** at **44x** speedup, beating DINO-WM (**74.6%**, 12x) and LeWorldModel (**68.9%**, 48x); temporal reasoning gains **64.7** on Perception Test.
- Ablations: removing the regularizer collapses latents to rank **3**; dropping photometric loss costs **4.8** ImageNet points; skipping predictor post-training drops planning to **66.3%**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Photometric transformations and temporal transitions are the same conditioned latent-prediction problem, so one predictor and one latent space can serve both.
> - Weighting invariance-inducing versus equivariance-inducing losses gives a practical knob for controlling representation abstraction rather than fixing it architecturally.
> - A distributional regularizer alone can prevent collapse, making EMA, stop-gradient, and pre-trained encoders optional scaffolding rather than requirements.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of agents capable of learning internal models of the world through observation, enabling them to understand, predict, and plan, represents a central objective in artificial intelligence. World models formalize this by learning to forecast the consequences of actions or the evolution of a scene. These models can operate either in the input (pixel) space or in a compact latent space. Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs) have emerged as a self-supervised framework for learning world models in latent spaces. JEPAs involve an encoder that maps observations to a latent representation, and a predictor that models dynamics by forecasting latent representations of future or masked content, thereby avoiding the computationally intensive process of raw pixel reconstruction. This characteristic contributes to JEPAs' efficiency in terms of data and computation relative to generative world models.

However, the existing landscape of JEPA methods is characterized by fragmentation. Current approaches are typically specialized for distinct tasks. Image-level JEPAs, such as I-JEPA, focus on predicting masked regions within a single image. Image World Models (IWM) extend this concept to global photometric transformations, learning to model the effects of changes in brightness, contrast, or hue in latent space. In contrast, video-scale JEPAs like V-JEPA 2 and DINO-World predict future temporal states from extensive video datasets. These video JEPAs can be further post-trained into action-conditioned models (e.g., V-JEPA 2-AC, DINO-WM) to facilitate zero-shot planning. While LeWorldModel introduced a method for stable end-to-end JEPA training from raw pixels using a single Gaussian regularizer to prevent representational collapse, these various JEPA instantiations operate with their own distinct encoders, predictors, and objective functions. Consequently, the photometric and temporal world models developed by these disparate methods reside in incompatible latent spaces and cannot be readily integrated or composed.

UniJEPA addresses this fragmentation by proposing a unified framework that integrates image-level and video-level world modeling. The work is positioned as a step towards a single model capable of jointly learning both types of prediction in a shared latent space, thereby enhancing transferability, sample efficiency, and applicability across a broader range of downstream tasks, from robust representation learning to embodied planning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop UniJEPA, a unified Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture that jointly learns photometric prediction (image-level transformations) and temporal prediction (video-level next-state dynamics) within a single, shared latent space. This unification aims to overcome the limitations of fragmented JEPA variants, each specialized for distinct tasks and operating with incompatible latent spaces.

The motivation for this unified approach stems from several considerations:

1. **Addressing Fragmentation:** Existing JEPA methods, such as I-JEPA for masked image prediction, Image World Models for photometric transformations, and V-JEPA variants for temporal dynamics and action-conditioned planning, are developed in isolation. Each method typically employs its own encoder, predictor, and anti-collapse regularization strategy, resulting in separate latent spaces that cannot be directly combined. The researchers hypothesize that image-level and video-level prediction are complementary aspects of a singular underlying world model, and a unified latent space could intrinsically support both.

2. **Enhancing Transferability and Efficiency:** A world model capable of both explaining how a scene changes under global transformations and predicting its evolution over time is posited to possess greater transferability and sample efficiency. Such a model could serve a wider array of downstream tasks, ranging from learning robust visual representations to performing embodied planning, without requiring separate specialized models.

3. **Simplified and Stable Training:** The objective is to achieve this unification using a single, end-to-end objective function that is provably anti-collapse. This approach aims to simplify the training process by requiring only one loss hyperparameter, in the spirit of LeWorldModel, and to eliminate the need for common stabilization techniques like Exponential Moving Average (EMA), stop-gradient operations, or reliance on pre-trained encoders.

The specific contributions outlined by the authors to achieve these objectives include:
- **Developing a unified objective** that casts photometric and temporal prediction as instances of a single latent prediction task, optimized end-to-end with a combined next-embedding prediction loss and a Gaussian regularizer.
- **Providing a theoretical proof** demonstrating that this Gaussian regularizer prevents representational collapse and yields a well-behaved latent distribution.
- **Demonstrating controllable abstraction**, illustrating that photometric prediction encourages learning invariant structures, while temporal prediction promotes learning equivariant dynamics. The research aims to show that interpolating between these objectives allows control over the abstraction level of the learned representation.
- **Enabling zero-shot planning** after action-conditioned post-training on offline trajectories, by treating goal features as prediction targets, similar to DINO-WM but from a unified, end-to-end trained encoder.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

UniJEPA is designed as a single Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture that unifies photometric and temporal prediction tasks within a shared latent space. The methodology involves a common encoder, a single predictor, and a consolidated objective function.

#### 3.1. Core Architecture and Prediction Tasks

An encoder, denoted `f_θ`, maps an observation `x` (image) or a sequence of observations `{x_1, ..., x_T}` (video) to a compact latent embedding `z = f_θ(x)`. A predictor, `g_ψ`, then takes a conditioning latent embedding and, optionally, an action `a`, to forecast a target latent embedding. A key design principle is that prediction occurs exclusively in the learned latent space, circumventing the need for pixel-level reconstruction.

Two distinct, yet integrated, prediction tasks are defined:
- **Photometric Prediction**: This task models the effect of global photometric transformations `τ` (e.g., brightness, contrast, saturation, hue changes) on the latent representation. Given an initial observation `x`, its latent embedding `z = f_θ(x)`, and the transformation parameters `a = τ`, the predictor `g_ψ(f_θ(x), τ)` aims to forecast the latent embedding of the transformed observation, `f_θ(τ(x))`. The corresponding loss function is defined as `L_photo(θ, ψ) = E_{x,τ} ||g_ψ(f_θ(x), τ) - f_θ(τ(x))||^2_2`.
- **Temporal Prediction**: This task focuses on predicting the next latent state in a video sequence, potentially conditioned on an action. Given an observation `x_t` at time `t`, its latent embedding `z_t = f_θ(x_t)`, and an action `a_t`, the predictor `g_ψ(f_θ(x_t), a_t)` aims to forecast the latent embedding of the subsequent observation, `f_θ(x_{t+1})`. The associated loss is `L_temp(θ, ψ) = E_{(x_t,a_t,x_{t+1})} ||g_ψ(f_θ(x_t), a_t) - f_θ(x_{t+1})||^2_2`.

#### 3.2. Unified Objective and Anti-Collapse Regularization

The total training objective `L_UniJEPA(θ, ψ)` combines `L_photo` and `L_temp`, and critically includes a regularizer `R(θ)` to prevent representational collapse:
`L_UniJEPA(θ, ψ) = L_photo + L_temp + αR`, where `α > 0` is a single scalar hyperparameter.

The anti-collapse regularizer `R(θ)` is designed to enforce the latent distribution to approximate a spherical Gaussian. It is formulated as `R(θ) = E_{x,u} [χ^2_1((u^T z)^2)]`, where `u` is a random unit vector and `χ^2_1` measures the squared Mahalanobis deviation from a standard normal distribution.

A theoretical result (Theorem 3.1) supports the efficacy of this regularizer, stating that if `R` is minimized to a value `R ≤ ε`, the encoder is guaranteed not to be constant, and the latent distribution `f_θ#P` possesses a non-degenerate covariance with a minimum eigenvalue `λ_min ≥ 1 - O(ε)`. This theoretical guarantee eliminates the need for heuristics such as EMA, stop-gradient, or reliance on pre-trained encoders, which are often used in other self-supervised learning methods to prevent collapse.

#### 3.3. Controllable Abstraction

The methodology leverages the distinct inductive biases induced by the two prediction tasks. Photometric prediction, by requiring `f_θ(τ(x))` to be predicted from `f_θ(x)` and `τ`, encourages the latent representation to become invariant to photometric nuisances. Conversely, temporal prediction, by forecasting `f_θ(x_{t+1})` from `f_θ(x_t)` and `a_t`, encourages the representation to be equivariant to the underlying temporal dynamics. The researchers propose that by adjusting the relative weighting of `L_photo` and `L_temp`, the abstraction level of the learned representation can be controlled along an invariance–equivariance spectrum.

#### 3.4. Action-Conditioned Planning

The full pipeline involves two main phases:
1. **Pretraining**: The encoder `f_θ` and predictor `g_ψ` are trained end-to-end from raw pixels using the `L_UniJEPA` objective on a combination of image and video data.
2. **Post-training**: After pretraining, the encoder `f_θ` is frozen. The predictor `g_ψ` is then specifically fine-tuned on offline observation-action trajectories `{(x_t, a_t, x_{t+1})}` using the `L_temp` objective to learn action-conditioned dynamics, resulting in a model `g_ψ(z, a)`.
3. **Zero-shot Planning**: For planning tasks, model-predictive control (MPC) is employed to achieve visual goal reaching. Given a current observation's latent `z_0 = f_θ(x_0)` and a goal observation's latent `z_g = f_θ(x_g)`, the system solves `min_{a_{0:H-1}} Σ_{h=1}^H ||^z_h - z_g||^2_2`, where `^z_h = g_ψ(^z_{h-1}, a_{h-1})`. This optimization is performed using sampling-based methods. Because predictions are made in the compact latent space, each rollout involves only a single forward pass of the predictor, making planning significantly faster than methods that operate in pixel space.

#### 3.5. Architecture and Scalability

The encoder is implemented using a Vision Transformer (ViT), which processes spatial patch tokens. The predictor is a lightweight MLP or ViT. The design aims for efficiency and scalability, inheriting the benefits of JEPA world models (no pixel reconstruction, no EMA).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents quantitative and qualitative results demonstrating UniJEPA's performance across image, video, and control benchmarks, as well as insights from ablation studies.

#### 4.1. Representation Quality and Efficiency

- **ImageNet Classification**: UniJEPA achieved 74.9% linear probe accuracy on ImageNet-1k with a ViT-Large encoder, outperforming photometric-only Image World Models (IWM, 73.5%) and I-JEPA (72.2%). This approaches the performance of DINOv2 (81.3%) while using a single, end-to-end objective. With a ViT-Small encoder, UniJEPA reached 73.8% linear probe accuracy. Fine-tuned accuracy for ViT-L was 81.1% and for ViT-S was 79.5%.
- **Video Understanding**: On the Something-Something-v2 dataset, UniJEPA achieved 78.1% top-1 accuracy, surpassing V-JEPA-2 (77.3%).
- **Action Anticipation**: For Epic-Kitchens-100 (recall@5 with H=5), UniJEPA reached 40.6%, outperforming V-JEPA-2 (39.7%).
- **Diagnostic Video Benchmarks**: UniJEPA demonstrated improved temporal reasoning accuracy on the Perception Test (64.7% vs. 62.4% for V-JEPA-2) and TempCompass (58.3% vs. 55.8% for V-JEPA-2), suggesting that the photometric objective contributes to better temporal grounding.
- **Hyperparameter Simplicity**: UniJEPA operates with a single loss hyperparameter (`α`), contrasting with other JEPA variants that typically require multiple (e.g., I-JEPA: 4, V-JEPA-2: 6).

#### 4.2. Planning Performance

- **Success Rate**: UniJEPA achieved a planning success rate of 75.8% across various control tasks (mazes, push manipulation, multi-particle control). This result surpassed DINO-WM (74.6%) and LeWorldModel (68.9%).
- **Planning Speed**: The latent-space prediction mechanism enabled UniJEPA to plan approximately 44 times faster than generative world models operating in pixel space, matching the efficiency of LeWorldModel (48x) while showing improved success.
- **Task Families**: UniJEPA demonstrated performance improvements across all evaluated task families (maze, push, multi-particle control).

#### 4.3. Efficiency and Convergence

- **Training Convergence**: UniJEPA exhibited faster convergence during training compared to pixel-based generative world models, achieving its target downstream accuracy in roughly 40% fewer training steps. Its single squared-error objective in latent space resulted in smoother loss curves.
- **Cost-Accuracy Trade-off**: The model positioned itself as Pareto-optimal in terms of cost-accuracy, achieving high planning success and video accuracy among efficient latent methods, while being orders of magnitude more computationally efficient than pixel-based generative approaches.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies

- **Loss Component Importance**: Removing the photometric loss (`L_photo`) led to a decline in ImageNet accuracy (74.9% to 70.1%) and a decrease in planning success (75.8% to 74.1%), indicating its role in learning invariant representations beneficial for both image and control tasks. Removing the temporal loss (`L_temp`) impaired ImageNet accuracy and rendered the model incapable of planning.
- **Regularizer Validation**: Setting the regularizer weight (`α`) to zero empirically confirmed Theorem 3.1 by causing the representation to collapse (latent rank 3), thereby destroying planning capabilities. Sub-optimal `α` values (e.g., `0.5α*`) resulted in partial collapse (0.6d rank) and degraded performance, while excessive regularization (`2α*`) slightly reduced ImageNet accuracy, supporting the existence of a Pareto-optimal `α`.
- **Encoder Scale**: Larger encoders (ViT-Large) consistently yielded better performance than smaller ones (ViT-Small).
- **Planning Configuration**: An MPC horizon of H=5 was identified as a sweet spot for planning (75.8% success, 44x speed). Increasing candidate rollouts (C) beyond 64 yielded marginal gains at increased computational cost. Post-training the predictor on action-conditioned data was found to be essential, as freezing the encoder without this step reduced planning success significantly (66.3%). The model also demonstrated data efficiency for post-training, achieving 72.0% success with only 10% of the trajectories.

#### 4.5. Qualitative Results

- **Physically Consistent Latent Prediction**: Visualizations of nearest neighbors retrieved from predicted latents following photometric transformations (e.g., brightness/hue changes) showed semantically accurate shifts, indicating that the latent world model captured the effects of transformations meaningfully.
- **Goal-Directed and Smooth Planning**: Latent-space rollouts generated smooth and feasible action sequences for maze and push tasks, with predicted trajectories adhering to environmental constraints.
- **Interpretable Failure Modes**: Planning failures were observed to occur when the environment became ambiguous (e.g., occlusion, novel object poses), providing an interpretable signal regarding the world model's reliability.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

UniJEPA represents a significant development in the field of self-supervised learning and world modeling by unifying image-level and video-level predictive objectives within a single, shared latent space. This unification addresses the fragmentation observed in prior Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs), which typically operate with specialized models for different tasks, leading to incompatible latent representations.

The primary significance lies in its capacity to learn a more comprehensive and versatile world model. By jointly optimizing for photometric invariance and temporal equivariance, UniJEPA yields a representation that supports both robust static image understanding and dynamic temporal prediction, including action-conditioned planning. This approach could lead to more generalist artificial intelligence agents capable of adapting to a broader array of visual and control tasks without requiring task-specific model architectures or extensive hyperparameter tuning.

Potential impacts of this work include:

1. **Enhanced Efficiency and Scalability**: The design principle of predicting in a compact latent space, combined with a single end-to-end objective and a provably anti-collapse regularizer, results in substantial computational efficiency gains. This is observed in faster training convergence and significantly quicker planning execution compared to pixel-based generative world models. Such efficiency could lower the computational footprint for training and deploying AI agents, making advanced world modeling more accessible and sustainable.
2. **Simplified Development and Training**: The reduction to a single loss hyperparameter simplifies the model development and training process. This ease of use, coupled with the elimination of common heuristics like EMA or stop-gradient, could accelerate research and application development in self-supervised learning and embodied AI.
3. **Improved Representation Learning**: The unified latent space demonstrates a capacity to learn features that are both invariant to photometric nuisances (beneficial for image tasks) and equivariant to temporal dynamics (crucial for planning). This balanced representation is shown to improve performance on various downstream tasks, including image classification, video understanding, and action anticipation. The ability to control the invariance–equivariance spectrum offers a practical mechanism for tailoring representations to specific task requirements.
4. **Advancements in Zero-Shot Planning**: UniJEPA's framework facilitates efficient zero-shot visual goal-reaching in control environments. By leveraging pre-trained visual features and a post-trained action-conditioned predictor, the model can generate plans effectively without reliance on expert demonstrations or explicit reward signals, which is valuable for real-world robotic applications where such data is often scarce.
5. **Interpretability**: The qualitative findings suggest that the latent space captures physically consistent transformations and that failure modes are often interpretable, correlating with ambiguities in the environment. This aspect could enhance trust and enable more effective debugging and understanding of complex AI systems.

However, the authors also acknowledge limitations. For instance, more complex or multimodal tasks may require richer conditioning mechanisms for the shared predictor. The Gaussian regularizer, while effective in preventing collapse, might impose constraints on representational capacity at extreme scales. Furthermore, UniJEPA currently does not incorporate language instructions, pointing to future work involving multimodal token spaces, topological orthogonality, and object-centric manipulation.

The societal impact statement emphasizes the potential for reducing computational footprints and highlights a critical consideration: the predicted dynamics of any world model may be imperfect. Therefore, thorough validation of plan reliability is advised before deployment in safety-critical or autonomous systems.
