# Pelican-Unified 1.0: A Unified Embodied Intelligence Model for Understanding, Reasoning, Imagination and Action

> [!summary] Summary
> The WFM System Group at X-Humanoid introduces Pelican-Unified 1.0, a unified embodied intelligence model that integrates understanding, reasoning, imagination, and action within a single adaptive loop. The model achieves competitive specialist performance, scoring 64.7 on multimodal benchmarks and a 93.5% success rate on the RoboTwin dual-arm tasks, while also demonstrating enhanced compositional and zero-shot generalization on real robots.

## Problem

- Current embodied AI models are fragmented, relying on separate specialist modules for perception, language, world modeling, and action policies, which leads to semantic breaks and inefficiencies.
- Existing Vision Language Models (VLMs) offer strong understanding but lack direct action capabilities, while Vision-Language-Action Models (VLAs) often lack explicit future imagination, limiting generalization.
- World Models excel at imagining future visual states but struggle to steer imagination with task logic or language-grounded reasoning, preventing mutual strengthening of capabilities.

## Method

- **Pelican-Unified 1.0** implements a unified paradigm by integrating understanding, reasoning, imagination, and action as ==interdependent dimensions== within a single, ==end-to-end trainable intelligence loop==.
- A ==Vision-Language Model (VLM) backbone== serves as the unified encoder, processing multimodal context, generating a ==chain-of-thought reasoning trace==, and projecting to a dense, ==shared latent variable 'z'==.
- A ==Unified Future Generator (UFG)==, a ==diffusion transformer==, is conditioned on 'z' and jointly models and generates future visual observations (video) and robot actions using shared computational resources and a combined loss function.

## Results

- Achieved an average score of **64.7** on eight multimodal VLM benchmarks, outperforming comparable models and demonstrating significant improvements in embodied-oriented benchmarks (e.g., **+28.2pp** on Where2Place).
- Attained a **93.5%** average success rate on the RoboTwin 50-task dual-arm benchmark, ranking second among compared action methods and outperforming most specialized VLA and world-model baselines.
- Ranked first on the WorldArena imagination benchmark with an **EWM Score of 66.03**, showing high **3D Accuracy (98.13)** and **Motion Quality (62.69)** for generated future videos, and successfully performed unseen compositional and zero-shot generalization on real robotic platforms.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - True unification in embodied AI requires structurally shared representations, mutually constrained conditions, and co-evolution through a common training process, rather than modular assembly.
> - The VLM's chain-of-thought and the projected latent variable 'z' provide a central coupling mechanism, ensuring the task state is simultaneously semantic for reasoning, predictive for imagination, and actionable for control.
> - Jointly generating future videos and actions within the same diffusion transformer enables actions to be consequence-aware, imagination to be task-directed, and reasoning to be constrained by executability.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of embodied intelligence is transitioning from task-specific automation to foundation models capable of generalization across domains, adherence to language instructions, and reuse of shared representations. Historically, robotic systems relied on scripted automation pipelines involving predefined perception, planning, and control modules. Recent advancements have introduced learned policies, vision-language grounding, large-scale imitation learning, and generative prediction, bringing the field closer to physical intelligence where agents can comprehend environments, reason about tasks, anticipate future events, and execute actions to modify the world.

However, the paper identifies a central challenge: this progress has largely occurred along fragmented paths, leading to specialized but isolated capabilities.
- **Vision-Language Models (VLMs)**, such as Gemini Robotics ER and Pelican-VL, offer strong semantic understanding and spatio-temporal reasoning in embodied contexts. Nevertheless, they generally lack the ability to directly execute actions, test their reasoning through physical interaction, or ground their conclusions in behavioral outcomes.
- **Vision-Language-Action Models (VLAs)**, including RT-2, π0, π0.5, OpenVLA, and Helix, link language and perception to motor commands. These models often do not incorporate explicit future imagination, which can restrict their generalization to novel compositional tasks, long-horizon sequences, or contact-rich interactions.
- **World Models and Video Generators**, exemplified by Cosmos-Predict and LeWorldModel, are capable of imagining future visual states. However, their imagination frequently remains implicit at the pixel level, posing difficulties in steering it with task logic, human knowledge, or language-grounded reasoning.
- **World Action Models (WAMs)** extend world models by connecting imagined futures to actions. Without unified reasoning, these models can be challenging to interpret, difficult to correct during rollout, and susceptible to error accumulation over long horizons.

The paper argues that the current approach of scaling separate specialists or accumulating modules is insufficient. It advocates for a paradigm where understanding, reasoning, imagination, and action are learned as interdependent dimensions within a single adaptive intelligence loop. This perspective is stated to be consistent with embodied cognition principles, where functions like reasoning, imagination, and action are considered inseparable, and processes such as motor planning and perception are integrated with simulated movement and potential actions. The core gap this work addresses is the absence of a model that learns these critical capabilities as mutually conditioning parts of a unified system, rather than as independent components.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to propose and implement a unified paradigm for building embodied intelligence models, culminating in the development of Pelican-Unify 1.0. This model aims to integrate understanding, reasoning, imagination, and action into a single adaptive intelligence loop, moving beyond the fragmented development of specialized components.

The motivation for this unified approach stems from several observations and theoretical arguments:

1. **Addressing Fragmentation in Embodied AI**: Current advancements in embodied AI have resulted in powerful, but often isolated, components for vision, language, action, and world modeling. The authors contend that simply concatenating or assembling these independently optimized modules into a pipeline leads to semantic breaks and limits an agent's ability to operate adaptively in the physical world. For instance, a VLM may understand a scene but cannot act; a VLA may act but lacks explicit imagination; and a world model may imagine but struggles with task-specific steering.
2. **Interdependence of Capabilities**: The paper posits that understanding, reasoning, imagination, and action are intrinsically interdependent. Effective physical intelligence, it is argued, does not arise from possessing independent capabilities but from their seamless integration. For example, understanding a situation should inform reasoning, reasoning should guide imagination about future consequences, and actions should be executed based on imagined outcomes, with feedback looping back to understanding.
3. **Alignment with Embodied Cognition**: The authors draw parallels with human cognition, where reasoning, imagination, and action are not separate faculties but operate as interconnected components. Motor planning involves simulating movement, perception is often organized around potential bodily actions, and future imagination supports action selection. This biological inspiration reinforces the need for a unified computational model.
4. **Enhancing Generalization and Robustness**: The research aims to achieve stronger zero-shot transfer, compositional skill use, and long-horizon coherence—behaviors that have been challenging for modular pipelines to achieve through engineered interfaces. By forcing components to be mutually informative through shared representations and co-evolution, the model is expected to develop more robust and generalized physical behaviors.

To achieve this unification, the model is designed to exhibit three core properties:
- **Unified Understanding**: All relevant contextual information—scenes, instructions, action histories, and visual contexts—is embedded into a shared semantic space. This holistic representation enables the model to simultaneously comprehend what it perceives, what task it needs to accomplish, its past actions, and the current state of the world.
- **Unified Reasoning**: Reasoning is conceptualized as a language-grounded, supervisable process. It directly operates over task intent, potential action choices, and anticipated future consequences, preventing reasoning from becoming a linguistic monologue detached from physical action and imagination. The output of reasoning directly conditions downstream generation.
- **Unified Generation**: Future imagination (e.g., predicted video sequences) and low-level actions are jointly produced from the same diffusion decoding process, using the same reasoning latent variable as a condition. This ensures that actions are inherently shaped by imagined consequences, imagination is directed by task reasoning, and reasoning is constrained by what can be realistically imagined and executed.

Through this unified paradigm, the authors seek to enable understanding, reasoning, imagination, and action to collaboratively exchange gradients, self-correct, and strengthen within a single training loop, rather than operating as distinct stages in a sequential pipeline.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Pelican-Unify 1.0 is designed as a single composite map that simultaneously produces a chain-of-thought trace (τ_t), an imagined future video (ˆv_t:t+H), and an executable action chunk (ˆa_t:t+H) based on past actions, language instructions, observations, and robot state. The model is trained jointly with language, video, and action signals, aiming for a shared representation that is semantic, predictive, and actionable.

#### 3.1. Unified Understanding

The initial stage of Pelican-Unify focuses on creating a single, comprehensive representation of the current situation.
- **Multimodal Context**: At each control step `t`, the model receives a multimodal context `c_t`, which comprises the action history (`a_<t`), language instruction (`l`), environment observations (`o_≤t`, consisting of video frames), and the robot's proprioceptive state (`s_≤t`).
- **Modality Embedding**: Each input modality is converted into a token space compatible with a Vision-Language Model (VLM):
 - Video frames (`o_≤t`) are encoded by a 3D video Variational Autoencoder (VAE), denoted `E_v`.
 - The action history (`a_<t`) is embedded using a lightweight Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP), `E_a`.
 - The language instruction (`l`) is processed by a standard text tokenizer.
 - The robot state (`s_≤t`) is mapped via a small linear projection.
 - Crucially, `E_v` and `E_a` are explicitly designed to be reused by the Unified Future Generator, ensuring a consistent modality embedding space across understanding and generation.
- **Unified Representation**: All embedded tokens are concatenated and then processed by a VLM backbone (`VLM_ϕ`), which is initialized from Qwen3-VL. This VLM produces a unified hidden representation `H_t`. This architecture is intended to ensure that perception is grounded by instructions, language is anchored in physical context, and the history of actions and states is interpreted in relation to task progress.

#### 3.2. Unified Reasoning

The second stage translates the unified understanding into an explicit reasoning process.
- **Chain-of-Thought (CoT) Generation**: Conditioned on `H_t`, the VLM autoregressively decodes a natural language chain-of-thought trace `τ_t`. This trace interleaves two types of linguistic output:
 - **Video CoT**: Descriptions and explanations of how the scene is expected to evolve, including object movements, contact formations, and workspace reorganization.
 - **Action CoT**: Explanations and decompositions of the motor program required to achieve the predicted future, detailing sub-skills and end-effector waypoints.
 - This joint generation of "what should happen" and "what should be done" within a single causal pass aims to force an integrated reasoning process.
- **Dense Loop State (z)**: The final hidden state of the VLM at the end of `τ_t` (`h_τ_t`) is projected by a learned projection `P_ϕ` into a dense latent variable `z`. This `z` serves as the sole interface between the model's understanding and reasoning and the subsequent future generation module. The design ensures `z` encodes information that is simultaneously semantic, predictive, and actionable, as it is shaped by language modeling loss and downstream video/action generation losses.

#### 3.3. Unified Future Generation

The third stage converts the dense loop state `z` into concrete predictions for the future video (`v_t:t+H`) and the future action chunk (`a_t:t+H`). The generator utilizes continuous-time flow matching with a shared denoising backbone.
- **Shared Input Embedders**: The target future video and action sequences are first lifted into the generator's token space using the *same* `E_v` and `E_a` embedders introduced in the understanding stage. This structural tying eliminates the need for additional alignment modules between understanding and generation.
- **Shared Denoising Backbone**: Noisy states of the video and action latents (mixed with Gaussian noise at a shared diffusion time `s`) are fed into a single diffusion transformer (`DiT_θ`), initialized from Wan2.2. The condition `z` is injected into the `DiT_θ` via cross-attention, allowing the reasoning summary to continuously guide the denoising process.
- **Modality-Specific Output Heads**: Two lightweight heads, `d_v` and `d_a`, read out per-modality velocity predictions from the `DiT_θ`'s hidden states. Modality-specific parameters are largely confined to these input and output boundaries, while the majority of the computational load is shared across modalities.
- **Conditional Diffusion**:
 - **Action**: A standard flow-matching approach is applied to the action latent.
 - **Video**: The video stream is extended to include the observed video prefix (`o_≤t`). This prefix is encoded by the same `E_v` and remains "clean" (not noised) while only the future region of the video latent is subject to denoising. This allows the generator to condition on observed frames directly within its native token space.
- **Joint Training Objective**: The overall training objective `L` is a weighted sum of three losses:
 - `L_video`: Mean squared error on the predicted velocity for the future video region.
 - `L_action`: SmoothL1 regression loss over valid action dimensions for the predicted action velocity.
 - `L_text`: Autoregressive negative log-likelihood for the chain-of-thought trace.
 - All three losses backpropagate through the loop state `z` and the shared embedders (`E_v`, `E_a`). This design ensures that understanding, reasoning, imagination, and action are optimized collectively as a closed loop, promoting mutual shaping of gradients. The final action produced is intended to be refined against the imagined future before execution.

The training process leverages large-scale real-world robot interaction data, which is credited with encoding spatial structure and physical dynamics, contributing to the model's physical grounding.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of Pelican-Unify 1.0 was structured to assess both its specialist competence in individual capabilities and its integrated performance on real-world robotic tasks.

#### 4.1. Evaluating as Three Specialists

The model was tested in three distinct regimes to determine if unification preserved specialist strength:

- **Understanding Capability (VLM Benchmarks)**:
 - Pelican-Unify 1.0 achieved an average score of 64.7 across eight multimodal benchmarks, including both general reasoning and embodied evaluation tasks. This score was reported as the highest among comparable-scale models.
 - It demonstrated an improvement over its base model, Qwen3-VL-4B-Instruct, which scored 58.2, and significantly outperformed prior VLA architectures like MolmoAct (27.5).
 - Performance on traditional general reasoning benchmarks was maintained, matching or slightly exceeding Qwen3-VL-4B-Instruct.
 - The most substantial gains were observed in embodied-oriented benchmarks such as Where2Place (+28.2 points) and PhyX (+20.6 points), suggesting improved action-aware understanding and rich physically grounded representations.

- **Action Capability (RoboTwin Benchmark)**:
 - On the RoboTwin 50-task dual-arm benchmark, Pelican-Unify 1.0 achieved an average success rate of 93.5%, positioning it as the second-best average among compared action methods.
 - It surpassed most specialized VLA and world-model baselines, including AIM (93.1%), LingBot-VA (92.3%), and starVLA (88.3%).
 - The model exhibited strong manipulation capabilities across diverse tasks, with 31 out of 50 tasks reaching at least 95% success and 39 tasks reaching 90%. This included tasks requiring precise contact and multi-object coordination (e.g., clicking, shaking, stacking, handover).
 - Failures were concentrated in long-horizon or geometry-sensitive tasks, such as hanging mugs and dustbin insertion. The results suggest that the unified training approach did not degrade low-level control but enhanced generalizability and robustness through shared reasoning and predictive representations.

- **Imagination Capability (WorldArena Benchmark)**:
 - Pelican-Unify 1.0’s imagination component achieved an EWM Score of 66.03 on the WorldArena benchmark, ranking first overall.
 - It also ranked first in 3D Accuracy (98.13) and Motion Quality (62.69), metrics associated with spatial coherence and physical plausibility. The model demonstrated competitive performance across other metrics, including Visual Quality (63.43), Content Consistency (60.33), Physics Adherence (61.51), and Controllability (59.28).
 - A blind human study was conducted to assess the utility of generated rollouts for downstream control. Annotators rated rollouts on a {0, 1, 2} scale across Controllability, Task Success, Temporal Consistency, and Physical Plausibility. Pelican-Unify 1.0 achieved the highest overall mean score of 1.76, driven by top scores in Task Success (1.81) and Controllability (2.00). This indicates the model generates physically consistent and task-aligned imagination, not just visually fluent sequences.
 - This performance is attributed to pretraining on large-scale real-world robot interaction data, which encodes spatial structure and physical dynamics, rather than relying on explicit reconstruction or physics engines.

#### 4.2. Real-World Robot Evaluation

Evaluations on a UR5e robotic arm and a Tienkung humanoid robot investigated the model's integrated physical behavior.

- **Compositional Generalization**:
 - The model demonstrated the ability to compose unseen task combinations (e.g., "plug the RJ45 cable into port 3 and apply waterproofing") on the UR5e arm, even though it was only trained on the atomic manipulation tasks individually.
 - The model successfully navigated the transition between tasks, re-perceiving the completed state of the first task as the initial condition for the second, a point where VLA baselines reportedly failed.
 - Comparison of real execution videos with generated imagination videos during rollout showed close alignment, indicating the model produces physically consistent imagination and future-state predictions grounded in environment dynamics.

- **Zero-Shot Generalization**:
 - On the Tienkung environment, the unified model underwent joint training on five seen tasks and was evaluated on three unseen tasks (provided with fewer video sequences).
 - Building on the strong generalization established by the human evaluation on WorldArena, where Pelican-Unify 1.0 attained the best overall mean score, the model demonstrated robust performance on seen tasks.
 - It showed efficacy in generalizing to out-of-distribution scenarios, such as an unseen "cup-wiping" task, suggesting that the unified framework enhanced performance through joint training and enabled bridging of the domain gap.

Overall, the findings suggest that the unified paradigm allows the model to preserve specialist competence while exhibiting stronger integrated behaviors in complex, physical environments, including zero-shot and compositional tasks on real robots.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research presented in "Pelican-Unify 1.0" proposes a shift in the conceptualization and development of embodied intelligence models. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Re-evaluation of Embodied AI Paradigms**: The work challenges the predominant modular approach to embodied AI, where understanding, reasoning, imagination, and action are developed as isolated specialists. It argues that integrated physical behavior—such as zero-shot transfer, compositional skill use, and long-horizon coherence—is not effectively achieved by strengthening individual components or by engineering interfaces between them. Instead, it advocates for a "unified" paradigm where these capabilities are learned as interdependent dimensions within a single adaptive loop.
- **Interdependence for Enhanced Capabilities**: The paper highlights that the effectiveness of any single capability is constrained by its integration with others. For example, a policy's ability is limited without future imagination to anticipate consequences, a world model's utility is reduced without unified reasoning to steer it with task semantics, and reasoning remains detached without grounding in action and imagination. The unification approach suggests that these components must become mutually informative through shared representations and co-evolution.
- **Implications for Research Metrics and Data Collection**: The findings suggest that future progress in embodied AI should not solely focus on increasing the capacity of individual specialists. Instead, metrics should also consider how tightly models share representations across modalities, how directly reasoning conditions generation, how jointly future video and action are decoded, and the quality of training data. The authors propose a need for "loop-closed data" where observation, instruction, reasoning, action, and future outcomes are aligned and annotated within the same examples, as such data is intrinsically valuable for training unified systems.
- **Practical Validation of Unification**: Pelican-Unify 1.0 provides empirical evidence that this unification does not necessitate a compromise in specialist performance. The model demonstrates competitive or superior performance on established benchmarks for vision-language understanding, visuomotor control, and world modeling, comparable to or exceeding dedicated specialist models. Simultaneously, it exhibits enhanced integrated behaviors on real robotic platforms for compositional generalization and zero-shot transfer tasks. This validates the unified approach as a practical modeling direction that can preserve specialist strengths while enabling emergent behaviors dependent on the overall intelligence loop.
- **Direction for Future Embodied AI Development**: The research suggests that the next phase of embodied AI development may be less about assembling increasingly powerful specialists and more about fostering a shared, adaptive process through which understanding, reasoning, imagination, and action co-evolve into a single, cohesive system. This implies a shift towards designing architectures and training methodologies that prioritize the intrinsic coupling and mutual shaping of these core cognitive functions.
