# DisCO: Reinforcing Large Reasoning Models with Discriminative Constrained Optimization

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Texas A&M University developed DisCO, a discriminative constrained optimization framework for reinforcing Large Reasoning Models (LRMs), which addresses limitations of existing policy optimization methods. This approach achieved an average pass@1 gain of 7% over GRPO and 6% over DAPO for 1.5B models on mathematical reasoning benchmarks while maintaining stable training dynamics and avoiding entropy issues.

## Problem

- Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) suffers from a "question-level difficulty bias," assigning insufficient importance to very easy or very hard problems.
- Existing GRPO-based methods often employ clipping operations in their surrogate objectives, leading to training instability, such as entropy collapse or excessive entropy growth.
- Many current LRM reinforcement learning techniques lack a principled theoretical foundation, relying on heuristic adjustments rather than fundamental redesign.

## Method

- **DisCO** (==Discriminative Constrained Optimization==) replaces GRPO's objective with a discriminative function that explicitly increases positive output scores and decreases negative ones.
- It employs non-clipping scoring functions (log-likelihood or likelihood ratio) and a trust-region approach with a squared-hinge penalty to ensure training stability and control policy divergence.
- The framework incorporates Distributionally Robust Optimization (DRO) to address imbalanced rollouts, making the model more robust to sparse rewards by focusing on partial AUC maximization.

## Results

- DisCO consistently achieved higher average pass@1 scores across six mathematical reasoning benchmarks, with a **1.5B** model showing a **7%** gain over GRPO and **6%** over DAPO.
- DisCO exhibited stable training dynamics, characterized by consistent reward increases and stable generation entropy, effectively avoiding the entropy collapse or excessive entropy growth seen in baseline methods.
- Ablation studies validated the importance of DisCO's non-clipping scoring functions, constrained optimization, and Distributionally Robust Optimization (DRO) in contributing to its superior performance and stability.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A formal analysis of GRPO's objective function under binary rewards revealed a "question-level difficulty bias" and a conceptual link to discriminative learning.
> - Clipping operations, commonly used in GRPO and PPO, contribute to training instability and entropy issues; non-clipping scoring functions provide a more stable alternative.
> - Constrained optimization with a dynamic penalty function is more effective for controlling policy updates and ensuring stability than traditional fixed-coefficient KL regularization.
