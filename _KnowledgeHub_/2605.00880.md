# Adversarial Flow Matching for Imperceptible Attacks on End-to-End Autonomous Driving

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers developed Adversarial Flow Matching (AFM), a gray-box attack that generates visually imperceptible perturbations to disrupt end-to-end autonomous driving systems by exploiting vulnerabilities in Transformer backbones. AFM achieved attack success rates up to 88.24% while maintaining superior visual imperceptibility, and induced high-risk "active hijacking" behaviors like a 40.06% off-road rate in closed-loop simulations.

## Problem

- Modern end-to-end autonomous driving (AD) systems, particularly those relying on Transformer backbones, are susceptible to adversarial attacks that can induce hazardous driving maneuvers.
- Existing adversarial attack methods struggle with real-world applicability; white-box attacks require impractical full model access, while black-box attacks are either too slow (query-based) or lack sufficient transferability.
- Current adversarial examples often lack visual imperceptibility, producing noticeable artifacts, and state-of-the-art generative attacks incur high computational costs due to iterative generation processes.

## Method

- A gray-box adversarial attack framework called Adversarial Flow Matching (AFM) is introduced, leveraging partial knowledge of the target AD model's Transformer backbone.
- AFM employs a Flow Matching-guided generative mechanism to enable efficient one-step (1-NFE) synthesis of adversarial examples in the latent space, addressing the computational overhead of iterative methods.
- The method uses attention-guided multi-objective optimization, combining latent space and neural velocity field perturbations, to maximize feature distortion in road-centric and high-saliency regions while ensuring visual imperceptibility.

## Results

- AFM achieved high attack success rates (up to 88.24% on TransFuser and 87.14% on SimLingo) while maintaining state-of-the-art visual imperceptibility, exemplified by an LPIPS as low as 0.074.
- The method demonstrated robust cross-model transferability between different AD architectures and significantly improved computational efficiency, generating adversarial samples in approximately 6.7 seconds per instance.
- In closed-loop simulations, AFM induced "active hijacking" behaviors, resulting in high-risk scenarios such as a 40.06% off-road rate and 20% route deviation, unlike other generative methods that often caused passive "static freezing."

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Transformer backbones, a common component in both monolithic VLA and specialized modular end-to-end autonomous driving (AD) architectures, present a shared structural vulnerability exploitable by gray-box adversarial attacks.
> - Flow Matching offers an efficient, one-step generative mechanism for creating adversarial examples, addressing the computational limitations of prior iterative generative methods while preserving visual imperceptibility.
> - Effective and imperceptible attacks can be achieved by synergistically perturbing the generative latent space and a neural average velocity field, guided by the AD model's internal attention mechanisms, to subtly manipulate driving decisions.
