# Self-Supervised Representation Learning with Joint Embedding Predictive Architecture for Automotive LiDAR Object Detection

> [!summary] Summary
> AD-L-JEPA applies a joint embedding predictive architecture to self-supervised LiDAR pre-training for autonomous driving, predicting BEV embeddings at masked regions instead of reconstructing point clouds, using variance regularization instead of contrastive pairs. It beats Occupancy-MAE and ALSO on KITTI3D, Waymo, and ONCE detection while cutting GPU hours 1.9x-2.7x and memory 2.8x-4x.

## Problem

- Contrastive SSL for driving scenes needs hand-crafted positive/negative pairs via augmentation, which is ill-defined in multi-object driving scenarios and can hurt downstream detection.
- Generative masked-reconstruction methods explicitly rebuild point clouds or occupancy, which is computationally expensive and fails to capture the multi-modal uncertainty of masked regions.
- General-purpose SSL methods (BYOL, SwAV, PointContrast) transferred to LiDAR driving data yield negligible or negative downstream gains, showing a domain-specific design is needed.

## Method

- **AD-L-JEPA**'s modified ==BEV-guided masking== creates masks over both empty and non-empty BEV grids (not just non-empty as prior work), forcing the encoder to represent occupancy itself rather than assume it is known.
- ==Context/target encoder== pair with learnable empty and mask tokens and a lightweight three-layer ==convolutional predictor== regresses target BEV embeddings from visible context embeddings via ==cosine similarity loss==.
- ==Variance regularization loss== (per-input, applied to unmasked non-empty and predicted masked non-empty grid embeddings) plus ==moving-average target encoder== updates prevent representation collapse without negative pairs.

## Results

- On ONCE with SECOND, pre-training on 100k frames gives **+1.61** mAP (best among all 100k/500k pre-trained baselines) and 500k frames gives **+2.98** mAP, beating all 500k/1M competitors including BYOL, SwAV, DeepCluster, Occupancy-MAE.
- Pre-training cuts GPU hours by **1.9x-2.7x** and GPU memory by **2.8x-4x** versus Occupancy-MAE across Waymo 20%/100% and ONCE 100k splits.
- On KITTI3D fine-tuning with SECOND/PV-RCNN, AD-L-JEPA gives the largest overall AP gain (**+1.56** to **+1.58**) versus Occupancy-MAE (**+0.06** to **+0.72**) and ALSO (**+0.28** to **+0.81**); transfer learning Waymo-to-KITTI3D improves label efficiency at **20/50/100%** fine-tuning fractions.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Predicting in embedding space rather than reconstructing raw input naturally handles multi-modal uncertainty (e.g. multiple plausible completions for an occluded car), avoiding wasted capacity on unanswerable reconstruction detail.
> - Masking must cover empty regions too, not just occupied ones, or the model implicitly leaks occupancy information and never learns to represent it.
> - More pre-training data does not always help: performance can saturate or slightly drop at very large scale (1M frames) due to redundancy in near-duplicate driving scenes, so data diversity matters more than raw volume.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of robust and scalable autonomous driving (AD) systems is heavily dependent on perception capabilities, particularly 3D object detection from LiDAR data. A significant bottleneck in this field is the **prohibitive cost and labor associated with manually labeling the vast quantities of data** required to train high-performance supervised learning models. This limitation restricts the scalability and generalization of current AD systems.

In response to this challenge, **self-supervised learning (SSL)** has emerged as a promising paradigm. SSL aims to learn meaningful data representations from unlabeled data, thereby reducing the reliance on costly manual annotations and improving model generalization. The broader research landscape for SSL in autonomous driving, especially for LiDAR point clouds, is characterized by two dominant approaches:

- **Contrastive Methods:** These methods (e.g., PointContrast, DepthContrast, ProposalContrast, BEVContrast) learn representations by maximizing the similarity between different augmented views of the same data sample (positive pairs) while minimizing similarity with other samples (negative pairs). However, for complex driving scenes with multiple objects and dynamic environments, defining truly meaningful positive and negative pairs through data augmentation can be challenging and may lead to issues like the "curse of dimensionality" when dealing with numerous negative samples.

- **Generative Methods:** These approaches (e.g., Masked Autoencoders like Occupancy-MAE, ALSO) train models to reconstruct masked or missing parts of the input data. While effective in some domains, explicit reconstruction of LiDAR point clouds in diverse driving scenarios is often computationally expensive and may not adequately capture the high-level semantic uncertainty inherent in such scenes (e.g., a masked region could correspond to multiple plausible vehicle rears, all sharing the same semantic meaning). This can lead to the model focusing on low-level reconstruction details rather than high-level semantic features.

**AD-L-JEPA positions itself as a novel alternative that transcends the limitations of both contrastive and generative paradigms.** It introduces a **Joint Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA)** to the autonomous driving domain, a paradigm recently explored for image and video data (pioneered by Yann LeCun and collaborators). JEPA's core idea is to learn representations by predicting embeddings of unknown or masked parts of the data, rather than explicitly reconstructing them or relying on contrastive pairs. This approach is particularly well-suited for autonomous driving because it can naturally handle the inherent uncertainty and ambiguity in real-world scenes, where multiple plausible interpretations of occluded or masked regions may exist.

By being the "first JEPA-based pre-training method for autonomous driving," this work significantly broadens the SSL research landscape in this critical field. It offers a fresh perspective that directly tackles the computational inefficiencies and representational limitations of existing SSL methods, aiming for superior quality, speed, and memory efficiency in learning robust LiDAR representations.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to **develop a novel and highly effective self-supervised pre-training framework, AD-L-JEPA, for automotive LiDAR object detection**. This framework aims to address the inherent challenges of traditional supervised learning and existing self-supervised methods in autonomous driving.

The key motivations and specific objectives driving this work are:

- **Reduce Reliance on Labeled Data:** The high cost and effort associated with labeling large-scale autonomous driving datasets necessitate a learning paradigm that can leverage abundant unlabeled data. AD-L-JEPA aims to learn high-quality, generalizable representations without any manual annotations during the pre-training phase.
- **Overcome Limitations of Existing SSL Paradigms:**
 - **For Contrastive Learning:** The difficulty of manually defining meaningful positive and negative pairs in complex, multi-object driving scenes, and the computational burden of managing negative samples, motivates a method that bypasses this requirement.
 - **For Generative Learning (Masked Autoencoders):** Explicitly reconstructing masked LiDAR point clouds is computationally expensive and often insufficient for capturing high-level semantic understanding. Driving scenes exhibit inherent uncertainty (e.g., different car models can have varied rear profiles but share the "car rear" semantic). A method that predicts embeddings rather than raw data can better adapt to this uncertainty.
- **Learn High-Quality, Semantically Rich Representations:** The goal is for AD-L-JEPA to learn representations that capture high-level semantics and geometric coherence of driving scenes, leading to better generalization capabilities for downstream tasks.
- **Improve Pre-training Efficiency:** Existing state-of-the-art methods can be computationally intensive. A core objective is to significantly reduce GPU hours and memory consumption during the self-supervised pre-training process without sacrificing performance.
- **Enhance Downstream Task Performance and Label Efficiency:** The ultimate goal is that pre-training with AD-L-JEPA should consistently boost the performance of various LiDAR 3D object detection models when fine-tuned on labeled data, and ideally, enable high performance with less labeled data (i.e., improve label efficiency).
- **Prevent Representation Collapse:** A common challenge in self-supervised learning is representation collapse, where the model learns trivial or redundant features. The framework is motivated to incorporate mechanisms (like variance regularization and moving average updates) to prevent this.
- **Pioneer JEPA for Autonomous Driving:** A significant motivation is to demonstrate the applicability and advantages of the Joint Embedding Predictive Architecture, a promising SSL paradigm from general computer vision, to the specific and demanding context of autonomous driving with LiDAR data.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

AD-L-JEPA operates on the principle of a Joint Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA), adapted specifically for Bird's Eye View (BEV) LiDAR data. The fundamental idea is to train a network to predict embeddings of invisible (masked) regions of a point cloud scene, given the visible parts, without requiring explicit reconstruction or manual contrastive pairs.

#### 3.1. Architecture Overview (Figure 2)

The core architecture consists of:
- **Context Encoder (f_θ):** This encoder processes an unmasked input point cloud (x_c) to extract dense 3D voxel representations, which are then reshaped into BEV context embeddings (z_c). This encoder's weights are fine-tuned for downstream tasks after pre-training.
- **Target Encoder (f_¯θ):** This encoder receives a masked version of the input point cloud (x_t) and produces BEV target embeddings (s_t). Its parameters (¯θ) are updated via a moving average of the context encoder's parameters (θ), a common practice in SSL to stabilize training and prevent collapse. The gradient from the loss function does *not* flow through the target encoder (stop-gradient mechanism).
- **Predictor (g_ϕ):** A lightweight, three-layer convolutional network that takes the context BEV embeddings (ˆz_c) as input and predicts the target BEV embeddings (ˆs_c) for the masked regions.

#### 3.2. Modified BEV-Guided Masking

A crucial component for LiDAR data is the masking strategy. AD-L-JEPA introduces a **modified BEV-guided masking** based on prior work (Lin et al. 2024), with a key distinction:
- **BEV-First Masking:** Masks are initially created in the BEV embedding space and then recursively upsampled to identify corresponding points in the input point cloud to be masked.
- **Masking Both Empty and Non-Empty Regions:** Unlike previous methods that only masked non-empty regions, AD-L-JEPA masks *both* empty and non-empty BEV grids. This forces the network to learn representations that can predict the nature of *all* invisible parts, including whether a region is empty or contains an object. This enhances the encoder's ability to learn powerful and generalizable representations of diverse driving scenes. In experiments, 50% of both empty and non-empty BEV grids are masked.

#### 3.3. Learnable Empty Token and Mask Token

To represent masked or empty regions meaningfully in the embedding space, the method introduces two learnable tokens:
- **Learnable Empty Token:** Replaces all unmasked empty grids in both context (z_c) and target (s_t) embeddings.
- **Learnable Mask Token:** Replaces all masked grids in the context embeddings (z_c).
After replacement, L2 normalization is applied to each BEV grid's embedding dimension.

#### 3.4. Training with Dual Loss Functions

AD-L-JEPA is pre-trained using a combination of two loss functions designed to ensure high-quality, non-collapsed embeddings:

- **1. Cosine Similarity-based Embedding Prediction Loss (L_jepa):**
 This loss measures the cosine similarity between the predicted embeddings (ˆs_c) and the target embeddings (ˆs_t) *only for the masked BEV grids*. It's formulated to balance the contribution from masked empty grids (P_n) and masked non-empty grids (Q_n) using hyperparameters α_0 and α_1 (e.g., α_0 = 0.25 for empty, α_1 = 0.75 for non-empty) to account for the sparsity of objects in driving scenes. The objective is to make the predicted embeddings for masked regions align closely with the target embeddings for those same regions.

- **2. Variance Regularization Loss (L_reg):**
 Inspired by VICReg (Bardes, Ponce, and LeCun 2022), this loss is crucial for preventing representation collapse. It ensures that the average variance across all embedding dimensions is above a certain threshold (γ) for two sets of embeddings:
 - Unmasked non-empty grids after the context encoder (ˆz_n^c[K_n]).
 - Masked non-empty grids after the predictor (ˆs_n^c[Q_n]).
 Crucially, this loss is computed *across each input in the multi-batch setting* (instead of across the entire batch) to prevent a specific form of collapse where embeddings are constant within an input but vary between inputs, which would still yield meaningless representations.

The overall self-supervised learning loss is a weighted sum of these two components: L = λ_jepa * L_jepa + λ_reg * L_reg.

#### 3.5. Downstream Task Evaluation

The effectiveness of the pre-training is evaluated on the standard **LiDAR 3D object detection** task. The pre-trained context encoder (f_θ) is used as the backbone and fine-tuned with labeled data for various detector architectures, including SECOND, PV-RCNN, and CenterPoint, across popular autonomous driving datasets like KITTI3D, Waymo, and ONCE.

#### 3.6. Other Evaluations

The paper also includes qualitative and quantitative evaluations of the learned representations, such as:
- **Occupancy Estimation:** Assessing the model's ability to infer occupancy in masked regions by comparing predicted BEV embeddings with the learnable empty token via cosine similarity.
- **Singular Value Decomposition (SVD) Analysis:** Used to assess the level of dimensional collapse in the learned embeddings, comparing AD-L-JEPA with other methods.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

AD-L-JEPA demonstrates consistent and significant improvements across various benchmarks, showcasing its effectiveness, efficiency, and superior representational learning capabilities.

#### 4.1. Superior Downstream Fine-tuning Performance

- **KITTI3D Dataset:** When pre-trained and fine-tuned on KITTI3D, AD-L-JEPA consistently outperforms training from scratch and state-of-the-art methods like Occupancy-MAE and ALSO. For instance, on the SECOND detector with R40 metric, AD-L-JEPA achieves a 1.56 mAP gain over the baseline, and 1.23 mAP gain with R11. Similarly, with PV-RCNN, it yields a 1.58 mAP gain (R40) and 0.94 mAP gain (R11), indicating robust performance across different detection architectures and metrics.

- **Waymo Dataset:** Pre-training on 20% or 100% of the Waymo dataset and fine-tuning with Centerpoint on 20% of the Waymo dataset also shows AD-L-JEPA outperforming the baseline and reproduced Occupancy-MAE results. For the full 100% Waymo pre-training, it achieves a +0.74 mAP gain over the baseline (Centerpoint AP metric, Level 2 difficulty).

- **ONCE Dataset (Largest Scale):** This is where AD-L-JEPA truly shines, particularly demonstrating strong scalability and data efficiency.
 - **100K Frames:** Pre-training on 100K frames results in a 1.61 mAP gain over the baseline, outperforming all other compared methods, including those pre-trained on 500K frames (e.g., BYOL, PointContrast, SwAV, DeepCluster, DepthContrast, ProposalContrast, ALSO, Occupancy-MAE).
 - **500K Frames:** Pre-training on 500K frames yields an impressive 2.98 mAP gain, surpassing all competitors, including those pre-trained on 1M frames. This highlights AD-L-JEPA's ability to learn highly effective representations even with less unlabeled data than some competitor's larger pre-training setups.
 - **1M Frames:** While still yielding a strong 2.81 mAP gain, performance shows a slight saturation compared to the 500K pre-training, suggesting that increasing data size beyond a certain point might encounter diminishing returns, possibly due to data redundancy, as theorized in the paper.

#### 4.2. Significant Pre-training Efficiency

AD-L-JEPA demonstrates substantial improvements in computational efficiency compared to Occupancy-MAE, largely due to its JEPA-based architecture which avoids expensive dense 3D convolutions for explicit reconstruction.
- **GPU Memory Usage:** 2.8x–3.4x lower GPU memory usage on Waymo (20% and 100% splits) and 3.1x–4x lower on ONCE 100k. This allows for larger batch sizes or training on less powerful hardware.
- **GPU Hours:** 2.7x fewer GPU hours for pre-training on Waymo datasets and 1.9x fewer GPU hours on ONCE 100k, leading to faster research iteration and reduced operational costs.

#### 4.3. Robust Transfer Learning and Label Efficiency

Experiments involving transfer learning from Waymo (20% split) to KITTI3D show that AD-L-JEPA consistently outperforms baselines across different proportions of labeled data (20%, 50%, and 100%) used for fine-tuning. This indicates that the learned representations are highly transferable and contribute to improved performance even with limited labeled data, boosting label efficiency. Notably, the model variant (AD-L-JEPA†) that pre-trains with Waymo's full 5 features (x, y, z, intensity, elongation) and randomly initializes the first layer for KITTI3D (which uses 4 features) performs best at 100% labels, suggesting that richer original representations can be beneficial even with feature mismatches.

#### 4.4. Qualitative and Quantitative Insights into Representation Quality

- **Occupancy Estimation:** By comparing BEV embeddings with the learnable empty token, AD-L-JEPA can effectively estimate masked region occupancy. The observed cosine similarity of approximately 0.7 for empty grids (not 1) suggests the model captures complex information beyond simple binary occupancy, reflecting the nuanced nature of driving scenes.
- **Singular Value Decomposition (SVD) Analysis:** SVD of pre-trained BEV embeddings reveals that AD-L-JEPA produces more evenly distributed singular values and a slower increase in cumulative explained variance compared to Occupancy-MAE. This is a strong indicator of less dimensional collapse and richer, less redundant information encoded within its representations.

#### 4.5. Ablation Studies Confirm Design Choices

Ablation studies validate the key design elements:
- **Masking Ratios:** A 0.5 masking ratio yielded the best performance on KITTI3D, demonstrating the importance of an appropriate masking difficulty.
- **Learnable Tokens and Variance Regularization:** Each of these components (learnable empty token, learnable mask token, and explicit variance regularization loss) was shown to contribute positively to downstream fine-tuning performance, preventing representation collapse and enhancing semantic meaning.
- **Predictor Depth:** A lightweight 3-layer predictor was found to be optimal, balancing predictive power with efficiency.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of AD-L-JEPA represents a significant advancement in self-supervised learning for autonomous driving, with profound implications for both research and practical applications.

#### 5.1. Pioneering JEPA in Autonomous Driving

The most significant contribution is the successful adaptation and application of the Joint Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA) paradigm to the domain of automotive LiDAR object detection. Prior JEPA research focused mainly on image and video modalities; this work demonstrates its efficacy and advantages for complex 3D point cloud data, specifically in a high-stakes application like autonomous driving. This opens a new frontier for SSL research in AD, potentially inspiring future work that leverages JEPA's strengths for other sensor modalities or multi-modal fusion.

#### 5.2. Addressing Core Limitations of Existing SSL for AD

AD-L-JEPA effectively circumvents the key drawbacks of prevalent SSL methods in autonomous driving:
- It eliminates the need for manually crafting positive/negative pairs, a complex and often problematic aspect of contrastive learning in dynamic, multi-object scenes.
- It avoids the computationally expensive and semantically limited explicit reconstruction typical of generative models. By predicting embeddings, AD-L-JEPA inherently handles the high uncertainty and diversity of driving scenarios, allowing for multiple plausible interpretations of masked regions without forcing a single, specific reconstruction. This leads to learning more robust and generalized semantic representations.

#### 5.3. Enhanced Performance and Generalization

The consistent and substantial performance gains demonstrated across multiple datasets (KITTI3D, Waymo, ONCE) and downstream object detection architectures (SECOND, PV-RCNN, CenterPoint) underscore the superior quality of representations learned by AD-L-JEPA. This translates directly to more accurate and reliable perception systems for autonomous vehicles, which is critical for safety and deployment. The improved generalization capability means models pre-trained with AD-L-JEPA can adapt better to unseen scenarios and varied real-world conditions.

#### 5.4. Significant Computational Efficiency

The remarkable reduction in GPU memory usage (2.8x–4x) and GPU hours (1.9x–2.7x) compared to state-of-the-art methods like Occupancy-MAE is a game-changer. This efficiency makes large-scale self-supervised pre-training more accessible, faster, and more cost-effective for researchers and industry practitioners. It reduces the computational barrier to entry for developing advanced AD perception models, accelerating innovation in the field.

#### 5.5. Improved Label Efficiency

The results from transfer learning and label efficiency experiments show that AD-L-JEPA performs strongly even when fine-tuned with limited labeled data. This directly addresses the fundamental problem of data labeling costs in autonomous driving, making the development and deployment of AD systems more scalable and economically viable. The ability to achieve high performance with less labeled data is crucial for rapid iteration and adaptation to new operational domains.

#### 5.6. Broader Impact on Machine Learning Research

Beyond autonomous driving, this work serves as a compelling case study for the applicability and benefits of JEPA in complex, real-world data modalities. It encourages further exploration of JEPA-based methods in other domains where explicit reconstruction is challenging or where handling inherent data uncertainty is paramount. The meticulous ablation studies and insights into representation quality (e.g., SVD analysis) also contribute to a deeper understanding of self-supervised learning mechanisms and how to prevent representation collapse.

#### 5.7. Future Directions

The paper outlines promising future work, including leveraging temporal dynamics and incorporating action-conditioned self-supervised representation learning. This indicates a clear path towards developing even more sophisticated and context-aware perception systems that can better anticipate and respond to evolving driving situations, moving closer to truly autonomous machine intelligence.
