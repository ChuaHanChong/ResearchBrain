# Denoising Diffusion Implicit Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Denoising Diffusion Implicit Models (DDIMs) from Stanford University generalize the diffusion process, enabling existing diffusion models to generate high-quality samples 10x to 50x faster. This approach also unlocks capabilities like semantically meaningful latent space interpolation and accurate image reconstruction, making diffusion models more practical.

## Problem

- Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Models (DDPMs) require simulating a Markov chain for hundreds to thousands of steps to generate a single high-quality image, leading to extremely slow sampling (e.g., 20 hours for 50,000 32x32 images).
- The stochastic nature of DDPM's generative process makes semantically meaningful interpolation in the latent space challenging and prevents reliable encoding or reconstruction of input images.
- Existing diffusion models could not dynamically trade off computation time for sample quality and required a fixed, large number of sampling steps for good performance.

## Method

- Introduces **DDIM** by generalizing the forward diffusion process to be ==non-Markovian== while ensuring the training objective remains identical to that of DDPMs.
- Develops a unified generative process that allows for ==deterministic sampling== trajectories by setting the stochasticity parameter `sigma_t` to zero, enabling faster generation by ==subsampling== fewer steps from the trajectory.
- Establishes a connection between DDIM sampling and the numerical solution of ==Ordinary Differential Equations (ODEs)==, which facilitates encoding real data into latent representations and performing exact reconstruction.

## Results

- DDIMs achieve substantially better sample quality (lower FID scores) at significantly reduced sampling steps, generating high-quality samples **10x to 50x faster** than DDPMs (e.g., FID **4.67** with **50 steps** on CIFAR10 compared to DDPM's **8.01**).
- Samples generated from the same initial latent variable `x_T` with varying numbers of DDIM sampling steps exhibit highly consistent high-level semantic features, demonstrating `x_T` as a potent latent encoding.
- DDIMs enable smooth and semantically meaningful interpolation in the latent space (`x_T`) and can encode an input image to `x_T` and reconstruct it with very low mean squared error (e.g., `x_T`.`x_T``x_T``x_T``x_T` at 5`x_T``x_T`-`x_T``x_T``x_T``x_T` steps).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The training objective of diffusion models is independent of the Markovian property of the forward diffusion process, allowing for more general, non-Markovian inference distributions.
> - A special deterministic formulation of the generative process (when noise `sigma_t` is zero) establishes an implicit model, enabling direct latent space manipulation and inversion.
> - The deterministic sampling process can be viewed as solving an Ordinary Differential Equation (ODE), which allows for flexible step sizes and faster generation via subsampling.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Deep generative models have revolutionized the ability to synthesize realistic data, particularly images. Historically, **Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs)** (Goodfellow et al., 2014) dominated the field in terms of visual sample quality. However, GANs are notoriously difficult to train, often suffering from mode collapse (failure to capture the full diversity of the data) and requiring careful hyperparameter tuning and architectural choices (Arjovsky et al., 2017; Brock et al., 2018). Other likelihood-based methods, such as Variational Autoencoders (VAEs) (Kingma & Welling, 2013) and normalizing flows (Dinh et al., 2016), offered more stable training but generally lagged behind GANs in raw sample fidelity.

More recently, a new class of iterative generative models, including **Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Models (DDPMs)** (Ho et al., 2020; Sohl-Dickstein et al., 2015) and **Noise Conditional Score Networks (NCSNs)** (Song & Ermon, 2019; 2020), emerged as strong contenders. These models achieve competitive or even superior sample quality compared to GANs, notably without the complexities of adversarial training. They operate by progressively adding noise to data in a "forward diffusion process" and then learning a "reverse generative process" to progressively denoise random noise into coherent data samples.

The critical drawback of DDPMs and NCSNs, which DDIMs directly address, is their **computational inefficiency during sampling**. The generative process in DDPMs involves simulating a Markov chain for a very large number of steps (often 1000 or more) to produce a single high-quality sample. This iterative nature makes sampling significantly slower than GANs, which generate a sample in a single pass through a network. For instance, the paper notes that generating 50,000 32x32 images with a DDPM could take 20 hours, compared to less than a minute for a GAN. This severe latency issue made DDPMs impractical for many real-world applications despite their high sample quality and stable training.

DDIMs fit into this landscape as a crucial advancement that bridges the gap between the sample quality and training stability of DDPMs/NCSNs and the sampling efficiency of GANs. By introducing a more generalized non-Markovian diffusion process, DDIMs retain the desirable training properties of DDPMs while enabling significantly faster and more flexible sampling. This positions DDIMs as a key step towards making diffusion models a more practical and versatile tool in the broader generative modeling ecosystem, offering a strong alternative to GANs without their inherent training challenges.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to **accelerate the sampling process of Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Models (DDPMs)** while maintaining or improving the quality of generated samples. The core motivation stems from the significant computational bottleneck present in DDPMs:

- **Slow Sampling Speed:** DDPMs require simulating a Markov chain for hundreds to thousands of steps to generate a single high-quality image. This sequential and iterative process is extremely time-consuming compared to single-pass generative models like GANs, rendering DDPMs impractical for applications demanding high throughput or low latency. The authors cite examples where generating 50,000 32x32 images takes 20 hours with DDPMs versus less than a minute with GANs.
- **Addressing Inefficiency without Retraining:** A key challenge was to achieve this acceleration without the need to retrain the underlying neural network model. The authors sought a method that could leverage existing, well-trained DDPM models.
- **Maintaining High Sample Quality:** It was crucial that any acceleration method did not lead to a significant degradation in the quality of the generated images.
- **Enabling Computational Trade-offs:** The ability to dynamically trade off computation time for sample quality was a desirable objective, allowing users to select an optimal balance based on specific application needs.
- **Facilitating Latent Space Manipulations:** The stochastic nature of DDPM's generative process makes direct, semantically meaningful interpolation in the latent space challenging. A motivation was to enable properties akin to GANs, such as smooth interpolation between generated samples.
- **Enabling Encoding/Reconstruction:** Due to the inherent stochasticity, DDPMs cannot reliably encode an input image into a latent representation and then reconstruct it. A further objective for DDIMs was to support this reconstruction capability, which is vital for many downstream tasks.

In summary, the paper aims to develop a new class of models, DDIMs, that overcome the prohibitive sampling cost of DDPMs, thereby making diffusion models a more practical and versatile tool for high-quality image generation, while also unlocking new functionalities like latent space interpolation and reconstruction.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core innovation of Denoising Diffusion Implicit Models (DDIMs) lies in generalizing the forward diffusion process of DDPMs from a Markovian to a non-Markovian one, crucially demonstrating that this generalization leads to the *same training objective* as DDPMs.

#### 3.1. Generalizing the Forward Diffusion Process

- **DDPM's Markovian Forward Process:** In DDPMs, the forward process `q(x_{1:T}|x_0)` is a Markov chain, where each state `x_t` only depends on the previous state `x_{t-1}` and `x_0`. Specifically, `q(x_t|x_{t-1})` adds Gaussian noise. A key property is that `q(x_t|x_0)` (the marginal distribution of `x_t` given `x_0`) is also a simple Gaussian, `N(sqrt(alpha_t)x_0, (1-alpha_t)I)`. The DDPM training objective only depends on these marginals.
- **DDIM's Non-Markovian Generalization:** The authors observe that the DDPM training objective `L_gamma` only relies on the marginal distributions `q(x_t|x_0)`, not the specific Markovian structure of the full joint `q(x_{1:T}|x_0)`. This insight allows them to explore alternative, non-Markovian inference distributions `q_sigma(x_{1:T}|x_0)` that still maintain the same `q(x_t|x_0)` marginals.
- **Defining `q_sigma(x_{t-1}|x_t, x_0)`:** DDIM defines a new inference process structured as `q_sigma(x_{1:T}|x_0) := q_sigma(x_T|x_0) * product_{t=2 to T} q_sigma(x_{t-1}|x_t, x_0)`. The conditional `q_sigma(x_{t-1}|x_t, x_0)` is defined as a Gaussian whose mean ensures that the desired marginals `q(x_t|x_0)` are preserved. A crucial parameter `sigma_t` controls the stochasticity of this conditional, allowing for flexibility.

#### 3.2. Unified Generative Process and Training Objective

- **Predicting `x_0`:** The generative process `p_theta(x_{t-1}|x_t)` in DDIMs is designed by first making a prediction of `x_0` from `x_t`. Given `x_t = sqrt(alpha_t)x_0 + sqrt(1-alpha_t)epsilon_t` (where `epsilon_t` is noise), a trained neural network `epsilon_theta(x_t)` predicts `epsilon_t`. This prediction is then used to estimate the "denoised observation" `f_theta(x_t) = (x_t - sqrt(1-alpha_t) * epsilon_theta(x_t)) / sqrt(alpha_t)`.
- **Defining `p_theta(x_{t-1}|x_t)`:** The generative conditional `p_theta(x_{t-1}|x_t)` is then defined by replacing the true `x_0` in `q_sigma(x_{t-1}|x_t, x_0)` with the predicted `f_theta(x_t)`. This results in Equation (12), which becomes the DDIM sampling rule.
- **The Implicit Model (DDIM):** A special and crucial case arises when `sigma_t = 0` for all `t`. In this scenario, the generative process becomes deterministic (except for the initial `x_T` sample from the prior and a small noise for `t=1` to ensure support), meaning `x_0` is uniquely determined by `x_T`. This deterministic mapping defines an **implicit probabilistic model**, hence the name "Denoising Diffusion Implicit Model" (DDIM).
- **Theorem 1: Unified Training Objective:** The key theoretical contribution is Theorem 1, which proves that the new variational inference objective `J_sigma` (derived from the non-Markovian forward process and corresponding generative process) is equivalent to `L_gamma` (the DDPM objective) up to a constant that does not depend on the model parameters `theta`. This is a profound result because it means that a neural network trained using the standard DDPM objective (e.g., `L_1` as in Ho et al., 2020) can *also* be used to implement the DDIM generative process simply by changing the `sigma` values in the sampling rule, without any retraining.

#### 3.3. Accelerated Generation and ODE Connection

- **Subsampling Trajectories:** Since the training objective doesn't depend on all intermediate steps of the forward process, DDIMs can generate samples using a sub-sequence `tau` of time steps `[tau_1, ..., tau_S]` where `S` (the number of sampling steps) is much smaller than `T` (the total number of diffusion steps used during training). This significantly reduces sampling time.
- **Connection to Neural ODEs:** The DDIM sampling update rule (Equation 12) can be rewritten to reveal a connection to Euler integration for solving Ordinary Differential Equations (ODEs). This suggests that the DDIM sampling process is effectively approximating the solution to an underlying ODE. This connection implies that DDIMs can also perform the reverse operation: encoding an `x_0` into a latent `x_T` by simulating the ODE backward. The paper also notes its equivalence to a special case of the "probability flow ODE" in concurrent work (Song et al., 2020).

In essence, DDIM's methodology cleverly decouples the training objective from the specific Markovian structure of the diffusion process, enabling a family of generative models (including deterministic ones) that are compatible with existing DDPM training procedures, while offering substantial speedups and new functional capabilities.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrate DDIM's significant advantages over traditional DDPMs, particularly in sampling efficiency and latent space manipulation, all achieved without altering the training procedure of the underlying model.

#### 4.1. Sample Quality and Efficiency

- **Superior Performance at Fewer Steps:** Table 1 and Figure 3 clearly show that DDIMs (η=0.0) achieve substantially better sample quality (lower FID scores) than DDPMs (η=1.0 or `hat_sigma`) when using a reduced number of sampling steps (`S`). For example, on CIFAR10, DDIM with 50 steps achieves an FID of 4.67, significantly better than DDPM's 8.01. This is a critical finding, demonstrating that DDIM can produce high-quality samples much faster.
- **Significant Speedup:** Figure 4 illustrates that sampling time scales linearly with the number of steps. DDIM is able to achieve sample quality comparable to 1000-step DDPMs using only 20 to 100 steps. This translates to a **10x to 50x speedup in wall-clock time** compared to the original DDPM, making diffusion models practical for many more applications.
- **Robustness to Shorter Trajectories:** While DDPM's performance deteriorates rapidly for very short sampling trajectories (e.g., 10 steps), DDIM maintains a much higher sample quality consistently across different numbers of steps. The `hat_sigma` variant of DDPM (used in Ho et al. (2020) for CIFAR10) is particularly ill-suited for shorter trajectories, exhibiting very high FID scores.

#### 4.2. Sample Consistency in DDIMs

- **Preservation of High-Level Features:** Figure 5 demonstrates a unique "consistency" property of DDIMs. When starting from the exact same initial latent variable `x_T`, samples generated with varying numbers of steps (e.g., 20, 50, 100, 1000 steps) exhibit highly similar high-level semantic features (e.g., the pose, overall appearance of a human face). Minor details might differ, but the core identity of the generated image remains consistent.
- **`x_T` as an Informative Latent Encoding:** This consistency suggests that `x_T` serves as a potent and interpretable latent encoding for the high-level features of the generated image, irrespective of the specific sampling trajectory length. This is a direct consequence of DDIM's deterministic nature, unlike the stochastic DDPMs where the same `x_T` could lead to highly diverse `x_0` samples.

#### 4.3. Interpolation in Deterministic Generative Processes

- **Semantically Meaningful Interpolation:** Figure 6 and Appendix D.5 illustrate that DDIMs can perform smooth and semantically meaningful interpolations directly in the latent space (`x_T`). By linearly (or spherically) interpolating between two `x_T` latent codes and then generating images using DDIM, the resulting samples smoothly transition between the two corresponding images, showing intermediate features.
- **Advantage over DDPMs:** This capability is a significant advantage over DDPMs, where the stochastic generative process makes such direct latent space interpolation ineffective for controlling high-level semantics. This brings DDIMs closer to a key strength of GANs.

#### 4.4. Reconstruction from Latent Space

- **Low Reconstruction Error:** Table 2 shows that DDIMs can effectively encode an input `x_0` to a latent `x_T` (by reversing the generative process, akin to reversing an ODE) and then reconstruct the original `x_0` from this `x_T` with very low mean squared error. As the number of steps (`S`) increases, the reconstruction error further decreases, achieving negligible error (0.0001) at 500-1000 steps.
- **Encoding Capability:** This demonstrates DDIM's ability to serve as an invertible mapping or an autoencoder-like model, which is not possible with inherently stochastic DDPMs. This opens up possibilities for using DDIMs for tasks requiring latent representations of data, such as anomaly detection or latent space manipulation for downstream applications.

In summary, the experimental findings strongly support DDIMs as a superior alternative to DDPMs, offering dramatic speedups, robust sample quality, and valuable new capabilities like consistent latent representations, semantic interpolation, and invertible encoding/decoding.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of Denoising Diffusion Implicit Models (DDIMs) represents a significant advancement in the field of deep generative models, particularly for diffusion-based approaches. Its potential impact spans both research and practical applications:

- **Bridging the Efficiency Gap:** The most immediate and profound impact of DDIMs is their ability to **drastically accelerate sampling from diffusion models (10x to 50x speedup)** without requiring model retraining. This addresses the primary bottleneck that previously limited the widespread adoption of DDPMs in latency-sensitive applications. By making diffusion models computationally feasible for real-time or high-throughput scenarios, DDIMs unlock their potential for a much wider range of uses, from image synthesis in interactive applications to fast content generation.
- **Enhanced Sample Quality for Faster Generation:** DDIMs not only accelerate sampling but also achieve **superior sample quality** (lower FID scores) compared to DDPMs when operating with fewer steps. This means practitioners no longer need to sacrifice quality for speed; DDIMs offer both simultaneously at lower computational budgets.
- **New Capabilities: Latent Space Interpolation and Encoding:** The deterministic nature of DDIMs confers powerful new functionalities that were absent or difficult in stochastic DDPMs:
 - **Semantically Meaningful Interpolation:** The ability to perform smooth, high-level semantic interpolation in the latent space (`x_T`) is a crucial feature that brings DDIMs closer to the interpretability and controllability often associated with GANs. This allows for controlled generation and exploration of the data manifold.
 - **Reconstruction and Encoding:** DDIMs can effectively encode an observation into a latent representation and reconstruct it with high fidelity. This opens avenues for applications requiring latent codes, such as compression, anomaly detection, or feature extraction for downstream tasks, transforming diffusion models from purely generative tools to powerful representational learning frameworks.
 - **Consistent Latent Representations:** The observation that `x_T` acts as a consistent high-level encoding for the generated image, regardless of the sampling trajectory length, further highlights the utility of DDIMs for structured latent space learning.
- **Theoretical Foundations and Future Research:** The paper's theoretical insight—that the training objective is independent of the Markovian nature of the forward process—is fundamental. This generalization opens up a broader class of diffusion processes beyond the standard Gaussian. The identified connection to Neural Ordinary Differential Equations (ODEs) is particularly significant, suggesting that advanced numerical methods for ODEs (e.g., multi-step methods) could be applied to further improve sampling quality and efficiency. This could lead to more stable and accurate generative processes with fewer steps.
- **Impact on Generative Model Landscape:** DDIMs offer a compelling alternative to GANs, providing comparable (or superior) sample quality and latent space control, while retaining the benefits of stable training inherent to diffusion models. This could shift the paradigm in image synthesis and other generative tasks, offering a robust and high-performing solution without the complexities of adversarial optimization.

In essence, DDIMs represent a pivotal step in the evolution of diffusion models, transforming them from high-quality but computationally expensive tools into highly efficient, versatile, and controllable generative frameworks that are poised to make a broader impact across various machine learning applications.
