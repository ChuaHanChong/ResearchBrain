# Latent Diffusion Planning for Imitation Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Stanford and UC Berkeley researchers develop Latent Diffusion Planning (LDP), a framework that enables robots to learn control policies from both suboptimal trajectories and action-free demonstrations through latent space planning, achieving competitive performance on manipulation tasks while reducing dependence on expensive expert demonstrations.

## Problem

- Traditional imitation learning requires large amounts of expert demonstration data that is expensive and time-consuming to collect
- Most existing methods cannot effectively utilize suboptimal trajectories or demonstrations without action labels

## Method

- Learn a compact latent representation of visual observations using a VAE
- Use diffusion models for both latent space planning and action prediction
- Separate planning from action prediction through an inverse dynamics model

## Results

- Successfully transfers learned policies to real-world robotic lifting tasks
- Outperforms methods that cannot utilize action-free data
- Achieves competitive or superior performance compared to existing imitation learning algorithms, especially with limited expert data
- Demonstrates effective learning from both suboptimal trajectories and action-free demonstrations

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Dense forecasting in latent space outperforms hierarchical planning approaches
> - Combining suboptimal and action-free data leads to improved performance
> - Learned latent representations are more effective than fixed pretrained embeddings

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This paper addresses a key challenge in imitation learning for robotics: the reliance on large amounts of expert demonstration data, which is often expensive and time-consuming to collect. The work fits into several active areas of research:

- **Imitation Learning:** The paper falls directly within the imitation learning paradigm, aiming to learn policies from expert demonstrations.
- **Learning from Suboptimal Data:** Recognizing the limitations of relying solely on expert data, the paper explores methods to leverage suboptimal trajectories (e.g., failed attempts) and action-free data (e.g., demonstrations without action labels). This connects to broader research in offline reinforcement learning and learning from demonstration.
- **Diffusion Models in Robotics:** Recent research has shown the effectiveness of diffusion models in learning robot control policies. This paper leverages diffusion models for both planning and action prediction in a latent space.
- **Latent Space Planning:** Planning in a lower-dimensional latent space, learned from visual observations, is a technique used to reduce computational complexity when dealing with visual input. This approach has been successful in Model Based Reinforcement Learning.
- **Modular Robotics:** The paper is a modular method that relies on a visual encoder, latent planner, and an inverse dynamics model.
- **Video Prediction for Planning:** Another line of work uses video prediction models for robot planning. This paper differentiates itself by planning in a latent space instead of directly generating video frames.

The paper builds upon and extends existing work in each of these areas, proposing a novel approach that combines the benefits of diffusion models, latent space planning, and the ability to learn from diverse data sources.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives of this research are:

- **Develop a novel imitation learning algorithm:** The paper aims to create a new algorithm, Latent Diffusion Planning (LDP), for learning robot control policies.
- **Leverage action-free and suboptimal data:** The core motivation is to overcome the limitations of traditional imitation learning by enabling the algorithm to learn from data that is easier and cheaper to collect, such as suboptimal trajectories and action-free demonstrations.
- **Improve planning capabilities:** The paper aims to improve the planning capabilities of imitation learning policies by separating planning from action prediction and operating in a learned latent space.

By leveraging readily available suboptimal and action-free data, the researchers aim to develop more robust and generalizable robot policies.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The Latent Diffusion Planning (LDP) method consists of three main components:

1. **Latent Space Learning:**
 - A Variational Autoencoder (VAE) is trained to learn a compact, low-dimensional latent representation of the robot's visual observations (images).
 - The VAE is trained with an image reconstruction loss, allowing it to capture the essential features of the environment relevant to the task.
 - The encoder part of the VAE is used to map images into the latent space for subsequent planning and action prediction.

2. **Planning in Latent Space:**
 - A diffusion model is trained to forecast future latent states in the learned latent space.
 - The planner takes the current latent state as input and predicts a sequence of future latent states, representing a plan for achieving the task.
 - The diffusion model is conditioned on the current latent embedding and diffuses over a horizon of future embeddings.
 - The Conditional U-Net architecture from Diffusion Policy is used, with a CNN backbone.

3. **Inverse Dynamics Model (IDM):**
 - Another diffusion model is trained to predict the actions required to transition between pairs of latent states.
 - The IDM takes two consecutive latent states as input and predicts the action that would cause the robot to move from the first state to the second.
 - This enables the extraction of actions from the planned latent state trajectory.
 - The MLPResNet architecture from IDQL is used to diffuse actions

**Training:**

- The VAE is trained first, using both expert demonstrations and potentially larger datasets of suboptimal or action-free data.
- The planner and IDM are then trained separately, leveraging different types of data. The planner can be trained on action-free data, while the IDM can be trained on suboptimal data.

**Inference:**

- The encoder maps the current image into the latent space.
- The planner forecasts a sequence of future latent states using the diffusion model.
- The IDM extracts actions from pairs of consecutive latent states in the planned trajectory.
- The actions are executed by the robot, and the process is repeated in a closed-loop fashion.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental results demonstrate the effectiveness of LDP in several simulated robotic manipulation tasks. The key findings are:

- **LDP leverages action-free data:** LDP outperforms imitation learning algorithms that cannot use action-free data, demonstrating its ability to learn from demonstrations without action labels.
- **LDP utilizes suboptimal data:** LDP can effectively learn from suboptimal trajectories, improving its performance compared to algorithms that rely solely on expert data.
- **LDP achieves state-of-the-art performance:** In several simulated tasks, LDP achieves competitive or superior performance compared to existing imitation learning algorithms, especially in scenarios with limited expert data.
- **LDP is effective in the real world:** Experiments on a real-world robotic lifting task demonstrate that LDP can successfully transfer learned policies to a physical robot, showcasing its practical applicability.
- **Dense forecasting is important:** Ablation studies show that LDP's dense forecasting approach outperforms hierarchical planning methods, suggesting that predicting a dense sequence of latent states is beneficial for planning.
- **Learned latent space is beneficial:** DINOV2 ablations show that the method outperforms planning over the fixed pretrained embeddings.
- **A combination of suboptimal and action-free data leads to improved performance**. The method is improved by training on both types of data.
