# HeteroPROPMT: A Real-time and Privacy-Preserving Heterogeneous Collaborative Perception Framework

> [!summary] Summary
> HETEROPROMPT, developed at Michigan State University, is a real-time and privacy-preserving framework for heterogeneous collaborative perception that aligns intermediate features from diverse autonomous agents. It achieves this with 94.1% to 99.6% fewer trainable parameters than existing methods, improving object detection (e.g., +1.8% AP50 over HEAL) and enabling metadata-free modality classification with over 99.99% accuracy.

## Problem

- Performance degradation in Collaborative Perception (CP) systems caused by feature-domain shifts from heterogeneous sensors and perception models.
- Existing CP adaptation methods incur high computational costs due to extensive retraining and raise privacy concerns by requiring access to proprietary model parameters.
- Dependence on proprietary sensor or model metadata for selecting adaptation modules, limiting the real-world deployability of CP frameworks.

## Method

- A ==two-stage training paradigm== that first establishes an ego-centric unified feature space, then adapts new heterogeneous agents using lightweight Decomposed-Rank Interpreter for FiLM Tuning (**DRIFT**) modules.
- **DRIFT** employs ==prompt-conditioned Feature-wise Linear Modulation (FiLM)== and ==PARAFAC low-rank decomposition== to align heterogeneous ==BEV features==, while freezing proprietary agent encoders and the ego fusion/detection stacks.
- An intermediate ==AutoEncoder (AE)== is trained to learn a compact latent space from ego-domain BEV features, enabling real-time, ==metadata-free modality classification== for selecting appropriate DRIFT modules during deployment.

## Results

- Improved object detection performance, achieving **+1.8% AP50** over HEAL and average **+4.7% AP50** over CoBEVT on the OPV2V-H dataset.
- Achieved significant computational efficiency, with **94.1% to 99.6%** fewer trainable parameters (**0.064M** vs. **1.1-15.0M** for HEAL) and **2.5 times** higher training throughput.
- Demonstrated highly accurate metadata-free modality classification, with a classification accuracy greater than **99.99%** across an extended set of modalities.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Lightweight, prompt-conditioned FiLM interpreters combined with PARAFAC low-rank decomposition can effectively align heterogeneous features with minimal trainable parameters and preserved privacy.
> - A compact latent space learned by an AutoEncoder can reliably capture modality-discriminative cues, facilitating robust and metadata-free modality classification at runtime.
> - Decoupling heterogeneous agent adaptation through frozen encoders and a dedicated feature alignment module allows for scalable, real-time integration without compromising the core collaborative perception pipeline.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Collaborative Perception (CP) constitutes a significant area within autonomous driving research, aiming to enhance the environmental awareness of connected vehicles by enabling them to share perceptual data. This sharing can mitigate limitations of single-agent perception, such as restricted fields of view and occlusions, thereby supporting more informed driving decisions.

The existing landscape of CP systems, despite advancements, faces several challenges, including bandwidth constraints, sensitivity to communication latency, and pose uncertainty. A particularly critical, yet comparatively underexplored, challenge is the performance degradation arising from feature-domain shifts. These shifts occur when collaborating vehicles utilize heterogeneous sensors, perception models, datasets, or training domains, leading to misalignments in their intermediate feature spaces. This heterogeneity is a practical reality in real-world autonomous driving, where vehicles from different manufacturers may employ diverse and proprietary perception stacks.

Prior efforts to address these feature-domain shifts generally fall into two categories. The first involves retraining substantial components of the CP pipeline, such as the fusion and detection modules or the joining agent’s backbone. While these methods can be effective, they typically incur significant computational costs, particularly when new modalities frequently emerge, and may pose privacy concerns by requiring access to and modification of proprietary model parameters. The second category employs feature interpreters designed to align heterogeneous features while freezing the primary collaborative pipeline and agent encoders. These approaches generally offer improved privacy preservation and faster adaptation, though they can introduce additional model complexity and inference latency. A common limitation across many existing methods is their reliance on proprietary sensor or model metadata to select the appropriate adaptation module during deployment. Such metadata is often unavailable or undesirable to share due due to intellectual property concerns or increased security risks.

HETEROPROMPT is positioned within this research landscape as an approach that specifically targets the problem of feature-domain shifts in heterogeneous CP. It seeks to provide a lightweight, real-time, and privacy-preserving framework that can align intermediate features without requiring extensive retraining or reliance on proprietary metadata, thus addressing key bottlenecks for practical CP deployment.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a heterogeneous collaborative perception framework, named HETEROPROMPT, that can effectively align intermediate features from diverse agents in real-time, while preserving privacy and maintaining computational efficiency. The motivation for this work stems from several limitations observed in existing heterogeneous CP solutions:

1. **Domain Shift Impact**: The core problem addressed is the performance degradation in CP systems caused by domain shifts in the feature space. These shifts arise when collaborating vehicles employ heterogeneous sensors, perception models, datasets, or training domains. A single fusion model with fixed weights struggles to bridge this gap, leading to suboptimal detection accuracy and potentially compromising driving safety.
2. **Computational Cost of Retraining**: Previous approaches often involve extensive retraining of major components of the fusion and detection models, or even the joining agent's backbone (e.g., HEAL). This process is computationally expensive, time-consuming, and scales poorly when new modalities frequently join the collaborative network. For instance, repeatedly retraining the entire pipeline for each new sensor type or model configuration is impractical for real-world deployment.
3. **Privacy Concerns with Model Access**: Retraining-based solutions frequently necessitate access to the joining agent's model parameters for optimization. This requirement can raise significant privacy concerns for vehicle manufacturers, as it may reveal proprietary design details of their perception stacks and increase the attack surface for malicious actors.
4. **Complexity of Feature Interpreters**: While feature-interpreter-based methods can offer better privacy by freezing main pipelines, they may introduce complex models to achieve effective feature alignment. This added complexity can translate into increased inference latency, slowing down deployment and impacting real-time performance.
5. **Reliance on Proprietary Metadata**: Many existing adaptation methods depend on proprietary sensor or model metadata (e.g., sensor type, model architecture) to select the correct adaptation module during deployment. In practice, sharing such metadata is often infeasible due to proprietary constraints and the sensitivity of this information. This dependency limits the deployability and scalability of current heterogeneous CP frameworks.

To overcome these limitations, HETEROPROMPT aims to:
- Enable rapid alignment of heterogeneous agents' features using modular prompts and learning-based tuning. This is designed to align each heterogeneous agent's modality to an ego-centric unified feature space, while crucially freezing the agents' encoders and the collaboration fusion and detection stacks.
- Implement visual prompt-based training and inference to modulate Bird's Eye View (BEV) features across channels and spatial locations, thereby reducing computational overhead.
- Support metadata-free deployment by utilizing an AutoEncoder to learn a compact unified feature space. This compact space is used to extract modality cues from shared features, enabling real-time and privacy-preserving modality classification and routing to the appropriate HETEROPROMPT modules.
- Achieve improved object detection performance compared to existing heterogeneous CP methods, while using a significantly reduced number of trainable parameters.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

HETEROPROMPT employs a two-stage training paradigm, incorporating an intermediate AutoEncoder (AE) training step (Stage 1.5), to address feature misalignment in heterogeneous collaborative perception.

**Stage 1: Homogeneous Unified Space Training**
The initial stage focuses on establishing an ego-centric unified feature space under a homogeneous setting. In this stage, all collaborating agents are assumed to use a shared encoder, denoted as $f_{m_1}^{(ego)}$, to process their observations ($O_i$) into Bird's Eye View (BEV) feature maps ($F_i$). These features are then communicated to the ego agent via a communication operator ($\Gamma_{i \to e}$). The ego agent aggregates these received features using a pyramid fusion scheme to create a unified feature representation ($H_{m_1}$), which is subsequently fed into a detection head to produce final object detections ($B$).

The pyramid fusion process involves downsampling received features using ResNeXt blocks to generate multi-scale features. At each scale, a foreground estimator generates an occupancy map to highlight informative regions. Fusion weights are computed through a softmax operation across agents. The fused features are then upsampled to a common resolution and concatenated. The training objective for Stage 1 combines a detection loss ($L_{det}$), which includes focal classification, smooth-L1 regression, and direction loss components, with a foreground estimation loss ($L_{foreground}$) for each pyramid level.

**Stage 2: New Joining Heterogeneous Agent Adaptation**
After Stage 1 establishes the unified feature space, HETEROPROMPT addresses the challenge of new, heterogeneous agents joining the collaboration group. When a new agent with a different sensor or model configuration joins, a distribution mismatch can occur in the BEV features, potentially degrading detection performance. To mitigate this, HETEROPROMPT introduces Decomposed-Rank Interpreter for FiLM Tuning (DRIFT).

DRIFT is designed to align the new agent's intermediate features to the unified space with minimal computational overhead and privacy intrusion. This is achieved by:
- **Freezing Components**: The ego fusion module and detection head (from Stage 1) are frozen, preserving the learned unified feature space. The newly joining agent's encoder is also frozen, limiting adaptation to a small set of parameters.
- **FiLM-based Modulation**: DRIFT utilizes two lightweight, modality-specific visual prompts ($P_{m,i}$) in conjunction with Feature-wise Linear Modulation (FiLM) modules. FiLM applies learnable affine transformations ($\gamma$ and $\beta$) to intermediate features, which are derived from the prompts. These transformations modulate the features across channels and spatial locations.
- **Dual Prompt Placement**: The first DRIFT module operates on the communicated features before resizing, matching their original shape. The second DRIFT module operates after the features have been resized to the ego agent's unified resolution.
- **PARAFAC Low-Rank Decomposition**: To reduce the number of trainable parameters associated with prompts and enhance optimization efficiency, PARAFAC (Parallel Factor) decomposition is applied. This method represents each prompt as a low-rank factorization of rank R, significantly reducing parameter count from $C \times H \times W$ to $R(C+H+W)$, where R is much smaller than $C, H, W$.
During Stage 2, only the lightweight prompt factors and FiLM weights within DRIFT are jointly optimized, while the new agent's encoder and the ego fusion/head remain frozen. This design aims to close the feature-domain gap without requiring access to raw sensor data or proprietary model metadata.

**Real-Time Deployment: Metadata-Free Modality Classification**
To enable real-time deployment without relying on proprietary metadata for selecting the correct DRIFT modules, HETEROPROMPT incorporates a mechanism for identifying the joining agent's modality.

- **Stage 1.5: AutoEncoder Training**: After Stage 1, an AutoEncoder (AE) is trained in an intermediate stage. The AE consists of an encoder ($g_{AE}$) and a decoder ($d_{AE}$). The encoder maps ego-domain BEV features into a compact latent space, and the decoder reconstructs the features back to their original size. This stage is trained with the Stage 1 objective combined with a reconstruction loss, encouraging the compact representation to retain detection-relevant information.
- **Modality Inference at Runtime**: When a new agent joins during deployment, its communicated BEV features are first resized to match the ego agent's unified resolution. The pre-trained AE encoder then maps these resized features into the compact latent space. This compact representation captures modality-discriminative cues. A lightweight classifier, previously trained on compact latent codes from known modalities, is then used to predict the joining agent's modality ($\hat{m}$). Based on this prediction, the ego agent selects and applies the corresponding, pre-trained DRIFT modules (prompt-FiLM pairs) for feature alignment, proceeding with collaborative fusion and detection. This process allows for real-time module selection without explicit metadata exchange.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy of HETEROPROMPT was evaluated through experiments on two large-scale collaborative perception datasets: OPV2V-H for main results and ablations, and V2XSet for additional validation. The experimental setup involved an ego agent operating with modality $m_1$ (LiDAR, POINTPILLAR) and progressively adding three heterogeneous agents with modalities $m_2$ (camera, EFFICIENTNET), $m_3$ (LiDAR, SECOND), and $m_4$ (camera, RESNET-50).

**Object Detection Performance:**
HETEROPROMPT demonstrated improvements in object detection performance compared to state-of-the-art heterogeneous CP methods.
- On the **OPV2V-H** dataset, HETEROPROMPT improved Average Precision at 50% Intersection over Union (AP50) over HEAL by +1.8% for the $m_2$ agent, +1.0% for $m_3$, and +1.2% for $m_4$, while maintaining comparable AP70 results. Relative to CoBEVT and HM-ViT, HETEROPROMPT achieved average improvements of +4.7% and +5.8% at AP50, and +10.4% and +10.8% at AP70, respectively.
- On the **V2XSet** dataset, HETEROPROMPT showed similar trends, improving AP50 over HEAL by +1.4% for $m_2$, +1.5% for $m_3$, and +1.1% for $m_4$, also maintaining comparable AP70.

**Computational Efficiency and Scalability:**
A key finding was the significant reduction in trainable parameters and computational overhead.
- HETEROPROMPT consistently used 0.064 million trainable parameters across all heterogeneous scenarios. This represents a 94.1% to 99.6% reduction in parameters compared to HEAL (which ranged from 1.1M to 15.0M parameters). This parameter count is also orders of magnitude lower than other retraining-based baselines like CoBEVT (33.2-51.7M) and HM-ViT (47.7-83.3M).
- In terms of training efficiency, HETEROPROMPT achieved an end-to-end training throughput of 30.74 samples/sec, which is approximately 2.5 times higher than HEAL (12.34 samples/sec).
- The computational throughput was 2.27 times higher in TFLOPs/sec, and peak GPU memory usage was reduced by 45.7% (3.8 GB vs. 7.0 GB for HEAL).

**Deployment Modality Classification:**
The proposed metadata-free modality classification scheme achieved high accuracy.
- A t-SNE visualization of the AutoEncoder's compact latent features demonstrated distinct, modality-dependent clusters, indicating that the latent space effectively captures discriminative cues for different modalities.
- The lightweight classifier, trained on these compact features, achieved a classification accuracy greater than 99.99% across an extended set of modalities, including those from different datasets and with varied sensor configurations. Only one instance of confusion between two specific modalities ($m_5$ and $m_2$) was observed.
- An ablation study on AE latent sizes determined that (64, 8, 16) yielded the lowest classification test loss.

**Ablation Study on DRIFT Modules and Prompts:**
Ablations on the DRIFT architecture confirmed the importance of both prompt-conditioned FiLM modules.
- Combining both DRIFT modules (pre-resizer and post-resizer) provided the best overall detection performance (92.1% AP50, 84.0% AP70).
- Removing DRIFT 1 (pre-resizer) resulted in a larger performance drop (4.6% AP50, 4.8% AP70) compared to removing DRIFT 2 (2.9% AP50, 3.4% AP70), suggesting DRIFT 1 contributes more significantly in the tested setting.
- Regarding FiLM architecture and PARAFAC prompt rank, a CNN-based FiLM with a rank R=16 offered a favorable balance between AP70 performance and computational complexity. For a fixed R=16, a CNN width of 32 channels yielded the best trade-off.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

HETEROPROMPT offers several significant contributions and has implications for the practical deployment of collaborative perception systems in autonomous driving.

Firstly, the framework directly addresses the critical challenge of **feature-domain shifts** arising from heterogeneous sensors and perception models, which is a major bottleneck for real-world CP. By enabling robust collaboration across diverse vehicle fleets, HETEROPROMPT can contribute to more reliable and safer autonomous driving systems, particularly in complex urban environments with varied vehicle types.

Secondly, the introduction of lightweight, prompt-conditioned FiLM interpreters, coupled with PARAFAC low-rank decomposition, leads to **orders-of-magnitude reduction in trainable parameters** and **improved training efficiency**. This makes HETEROPROMPT a highly **scalable** solution, capable of rapidly adapting to new agent modalities without requiring extensive computational resources or prolonged retraining cycles. This efficiency is crucial for dynamic environments where new vehicles frequently join or leave a collaborative group.

Thirdly, HETEROPROMPT enhances **privacy preservation** for joining agents. By freezing proprietary agent encoders and ego fusion/detection stacks, and relying solely on communicated intermediate features for adaptation, the framework avoids the need to access or modify sensitive model parameters. Furthermore, the novel metadata-free modality classification scheme, powered by a compact AutoEncoder representation, eliminates the reliance on proprietary sensor or model metadata for module selection. This addresses a significant practical and legal hurdle for inter-company collaboration.

The **real-time capability** of the framework, both in terms of rapid adaptation and quick modality inference (with >99.99% accuracy), positions HETEROPROMPT as a practical solution for immediate deployment scenarios. The ability to automatically identify and adapt to a new agent's modality without manual intervention or prior knowledge of its specific configuration streamlines the integration process for collaborative autonomous systems.

In summary, HETEROPROMPT provides a **practical, scalable, and privacy-preserving paradigm** for heterogeneous collaborative perception. Its ability to maintain state-of-the-art detection performance while drastically reducing computational and data-sharing overhead represents a notable advancement toward the widespread adoption of collaborative perception in autonomous vehicles. Future work focusing on generalization to unseen modalities and robustness under more challenging real-world communication conditions could further enhance its impact.
