# Thinking with Images as Continuous Actions: Numerical Visual Chain-of-Thought

> [!summary] Summary
> The Numerical Visual Chain-of-Thought (NV-CoT) framework allows Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) to generate continuous numerical bounding box coordinates for region-grounded reasoning. This approach achieves 59.5% Bounding-box IoU on the Vis-CoT-363K dataset with an ℓ1 loss and improves overall reasoning accuracy by up to 3.7% on the V*Bench benchmark.

## Problem

- Existing text-based Visual Chain-of-Thought (CoT) methods suffer from modality mismatch (continuous visual coordinates represented as discrete text tokens) and semantic fragmentation due to numerical tokenization.
- Patch-based Visual CoT methods are constrained by fixed spatial granularity from vision backbones and often require extensive architectural changes, limiting flexible and precise region selection.
- These limitations lead to imprecise localization, brittle numerical reasoning, and inefficient learning for MLLMs in complex visual reasoning tasks.

## Method

- NV-CoT augments the MLLM output head with lightweight linear layers to directly predict ==continuous numerical bounding box coordinates== and a shared dispersion parameter, expanding the action space beyond discrete tokens.
- The framework is compatible with Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) using ==regression losses== (e.g., ℓ1 loss) for coordinates and also extends to Reinforcement Learning (RL) with GRPO-style algorithms.
- For RL, it employs ==stochastic Gaussian or Laplace policies== with reparameterized sampling, analytically tractable importance ratios, and KL divergence penalties for stable and efficient continuous policy updates.

## Results

- NV-CoT consistently outperforms its backbone models and larger MLLMs across V*Bench, HR-Bench 4K, and HR-Bench 8K, with up to **3.7%** overall accuracy improvement on V*Bench for SFT and **2.6%** for RL.
- The approach substantially improves localization precision, achieving **59.5%** Bounding-box IoU on the Vis-CoT-363K dataset with ℓ1 loss, a gain of over **12 percentage points** compared to the **47.3%** of the text-based Vis-CoT.
- Ablation studies confirm the superiority of the Laplace policy, the effectiveness of the shared uncertainty parameter, and demonstrate the robustness of the framework across hyperparameter variations.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Directly modeling bounding box coordinates as continuous actions resolves the modality mismatch and semantic fragmentation issues inherent in text-based Visual CoT, leading to more precise visual grounding.
> - Integrating probabilistic policies with reparameterization enables effective and stable application of Reinforcement Learning for continuous action spaces within existing MLLM architectures.
> - The Laplace policy (and ℓ1 regression loss) proves more robust and effective for bounding box localization tasks compared to the Gaussian policy (ℓ2^2 loss), yielding higher precision.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research directly addresses a critical challenge in the rapidly evolving field of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs), particularly concerning **visual chain-of-thought (Visual CoT)**. The core idea of "thinking with images" via Visual CoT is to enable MLLMs to perform complex, region-grounded reasoning by iteratively identifying and inspecting relevant image regions, mimicking human-like visual exploration. This capability is fundamental for a wide array of downstream applications, including advanced visual question answering, document understanding, and fine-grained visual recognition.

The paper positions itself as a significant advancement by tackling fundamental limitations of existing Visual CoT paradigms:
1. **Text-based Visual CoT:** Many MLLMs serialize bounding box coordinates (e.g., `["x1", "y1", "x2", "y2"]`) as discrete text tokens. This approach suffers from two major issues:
 - **Modality Mismatch:** Visual coordinates are inherently continuous, but their representation as discrete text tokens creates a semantic gap. Training with cross-entropy for discrete tokens penalizes geometrically similar predictions equally, hindering precise localization.
 - **Semantic Fragmentation:** Numbers are often tokenized into multiple sub-tokens (e.g., "522.6" might become "5", "2", "2", ".", "6"), making numerical comparisons and reasoning brittle and prone to errors or "hallucinations."
2. **Patch-based Visual CoT:** Recent attempts to overcome text-based limitations involve operating directly on fine-grained visual patches. While avoiding textual serialization, these methods are inherently constrained by the fixed spatial granularity of the underlying vision backbone. This limits flexible and precise region selection. Furthermore, such approaches often necessitate non-trivial architectural changes, reducing their modularity and general applicability across diverse MLLM architectures.

"Numerical Visual Chain-of-Thought (NV-CoT)" proposes a novel paradigm by enabling MLLMs to directly generate **continuous numerical coordinates** for bounding boxes. This moves the field towards a more natural and accurate representation of visual regions, bridging the gap between continuous visual perception and discrete linguistic processing. It represents a paradigm shift by expanding the MLLM's "action space" from discrete vocabulary tokens to a continuous Euclidean space, thus offering a more direct and precise mechanism for visual grounding. This work contributes to the ongoing effort to imbue MLLMs with stronger reasoning capabilities, moving beyond mere perception and alignment towards more sophisticated, interpretable, and accurate multimodal intelligence. It also demonstrates a powerful integration of reinforcement learning techniques (GRPO-style) with MLLM fine-tuning for continuous control.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to significantly enhance the region-grounded reasoning capabilities of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) by enabling them to "think with images" more effectively and precisely. This overarching goal is broken down into several specific motivations and objectives:

1. **Address Limitations of Existing Visual CoT Methods:** The core motivation stems from the inherent shortcomings of current approaches to visual chain-of-thought.
 - For **text-based methods**, the goal is to eliminate the **modality mismatch** between continuous visual coordinates and discrete text tokens, as well as the **semantic fragmentation** caused by tokenizing numbers. These issues lead to imprecise localization, brittle numerical reasoning, and a hampered learning process.
 - For **patch-based methods**, the objective is to overcome the **fixed granularity** of vision backbones and the need for **non-trivial architectural modifications**, which restrict flexible region selection and general applicability.

2. **Enable Continuous Bounding Box Generation:** The central technical objective is to equip MLLMs with the ability to directly generate bounding box coordinates as continuous numerical values (e.g., `[x1, y1, x2, y2]`) within a Euclidean space, rather than discrete tokens or fixed patches. This aims to facilitate more precise and flexible region selection.

3. **Ensure Broad Compatibility and Modularity:** The framework is designed to be a "plug-and-play" solution, requiring only minimal architectural modifications to existing MLLMs. A key objective is to ensure compatibility with both popular training paradigms: Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) and Reinforcement Learning (RL). This broad applicability makes NV-CoT widely adaptable.

4. **Introduce Stochasticity for Reinforcement Learning:** A specific objective for the RL setting is to enable stochastic coordinate prediction. Traditional LLM policies are deterministic categorical distributions over tokens, which are incompatible with continuous action spaces requiring exploration. The aim is to introduce a probabilistic policy (Gaussian or Laplace) with reparameterized sampling to facilitate efficient exploration and stable gradient estimation in GRPO-style RL.

5. **Improve Localization Precision and Final Answer Accuracy:** Ultimately, the motivation is to translate these methodological improvements into tangible performance gains, specifically in terms of bounding box localization precision (e.g., IoU) and the overall accuracy of the final answers in visual reasoning tasks.

6. **Accelerate Training Convergence:** The authors also aim to show that a more natural representation of visual actions can lead to more efficient learning, resulting in faster training convergence for MLLMs engaged in visual reasoning.

By achieving these objectives, NV-CoT seeks to establish a more robust, accurate, and efficient foundation for MLLMs to perform sophisticated visual reasoning by directly "thinking with images" through continuous region grounding.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The Numerical Visual Chain-of-Thought (NV-CoT) framework introduces a novel approach for MLLMs to perform region-grounded reasoning by treating bounding box localization as a continuous-action problem in Euclidean space. The methodology can be broken down into three main components: expanding the MLLM action space, adapting for Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT), and extending to Reinforcement Learning (RL).

#### 3.1. Expanded Actions for Continuous Coordinates

The core innovation lies in expanding the MLLM's action space. Traditionally, LLMs output discrete vocabulary tokens. NV-CoT augments the standard LLM output head by adding five lightweight linear heads. Four heads are dedicated to predicting the continuous numerical coordinates of a bounding box `[x1, y1, x2, y2]` (which form the mean `μ`). A fifth head predicts a shared standard deviation `σ` (for a Gaussian policy) or a scale parameter `α` (for a Laplace policy). This modification is minimal, involving only a few additional parameters, making it a "plug-and-play" extension.

The introduction of `σ` or `α` is crucial for the reinforcement learning setting, as it enables stochasticity, which is vital for sampling and exploration in RL. The model predicts both the mean (`μ`) and the dispersion parameter (`σ` or `α`) of the coordinate distribution, allowing for probabilistic action generation.

#### 3.2. NV-CoT for Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT)

When ground-truth bounding box coordinates (`b*`) are available during the reasoning process, NV-CoT can be applied via SFT.
- **Loss Function:** The traditional token-level cross-entropy loss for textual outputs is retained. For the newly introduced continuous coordinate outputs, a regression loss is applied. The paper explores both `ℓ2^2` loss (corresponding to a Gaussian assumption) and `ℓ1` loss (corresponding to a Laplace assumption), with the `ℓ1` loss empirically found to be more robust for localization.
- **No Uncertainty Supervision:** The dispersion parameter (`σ` or `α`) is not explicitly supervised during SFT; it's primarily used for the RL stage.

#### 3.3. NV-CoT for Reinforcement Learning (RL)

Extending NV-CoT to RL, particularly with GRPO-style algorithms, is non-trivial due to the need for stochastic policies and importance ratios for continuous actions.
- **Policy Formulation:** Instead of a categorical distribution over discrete tokens, NV-CoT employs a **Gaussian policy** `N(μ, σ^2I)` or a **Laplace policy** `Laplace(μ, α)`. These policies explicitly model continuous coordinate regression.
- **Stochasticity and Sampling:**
 - For the Gaussian policy, stochasticity is introduced via the **reparameterization trick**: `b = μ + σϵ`, where `ϵ` is sampled from a standard normal distribution. This allows for low-variance gradient estimation.
 - For the Laplace policy, a similar reparameterized sampling is used: `b = μ + α s ⊙ ϵ`, where `s` is a random sign vector and `ϵ` has i.i.d. unit-rate exponential entries.
- **Importance Ratio:** GRPO requires an importance ratio to relate the current policy to a reference policy. For continuous actions, this ratio is computed using the analytical likelihoods of the Gaussian or Laplace distributions, enabling stable policy updates.
- **KL Penalty:** The KL divergence, a common regularization term in RL, is analytically tractable for Gaussian distributions. For the Laplace policy, a similar analytical form is used, or a simplified mean-only KL regularization can be applied if a reference variance is not available from an SFT stage.
- **Reward Function:** Following existing work (DeepEyes), an outcome-driven reward combines answer correctness, format validity, and a bonus for successful use of the zoom-in tool, encouraging effective region identification.
- **Optimization:** The policy is optimized using a GRPO objective, which combines the importance-weighted advantage estimate with the KL regularization term, effectively adapting it for the continuous action space.
- **Inference:** During inference, stochasticity is disabled, and the model directly outputs the predicted mean `μ` as the bounding box coordinates.

#### 3.4. Extending with Laplace Policy

Recognizing the empirical benefits of `ℓ1` losses in localization tasks due to their robustness to outliers, the authors also propose a Laplace policy variant. This variant replaces the Gaussian distribution with a Laplace distribution, leading to `ℓ1` loss in SFT and modified importance ratios and KL penalties in RL, all while maintaining compatibility with the GRPO framework. The choice between Gaussian and Laplace policies allows for further optimization based on task characteristics.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The extensive experiments conducted on three benchmarks (V*Bench, HR-Bench 4K, and HR-Bench 8K) against eight representative visual reasoning baselines consistently demonstrate the effectiveness of NV-CoT.

1. **Superior Overall Performance (Table 1):**
 - NV-CoT consistently outperforms its backbone models across all benchmarks under both SFT and RL settings.
 - **SFT improvements:** NV-CoT (SFT) built on Vis-CoT-7B shows significant gains, e.g., +3.7% overall on V*Bench, +2.0% on HR-Bench 4K, and +1.6% on HR-Bench 8K.
 - **RL improvements:** NV-CoT (RL) built on DeepEyes-7B also shows substantial improvements, e.g., +2.6% overall on V*Bench, +1.3% on HR-Bench 4K, and +1.6% on HR-Bench 8K.
 - **Paradigm Advantage:** NV-CoT (RL) and its DeepEyes backbone consistently outperform patch-based baselines like PaDT-7B and LVR-7B, confirming the superiority of dynamic zoom-in region selection.
 - **Beyond Basic MLLMs:** Methods employing "thinking with images" (including NV-CoT) far exceed the performance of generic MLLMs like LLaVA-OneVision-7B and Qwen2.5-VL-7B. Notably, NV-CoT-7B even surpasses the much larger Qwen2.5-VL-32B model, highlighting the efficiency of explicit visual reasoning.
 - **Multi-step Reasoning:** When allowing multiple zoom-in tool calls, NV-CoT further boosts performance over its multi-step DeepEyes-7B† counterpart, indicating that precise continuous localization benefits iterative reasoning.

2. **Significant Improvement in Localization Precision (Table 3):**
 - Measured by Bounding-box IoU on the Vis-CoT-363K dataset, NV-CoT substantially improves localization accuracy. Compared to Vis-CoT's 47.3% IoU, NV-CoT with ℓ2^2 loss achieves 57.4%, and with ℓ1 loss, it reaches an impressive 59.5%. This quantitatively validates the precision gains from continuous coordinate prediction.

3. **Ablation Studies Validate Design Choices (Table 2):**
 - **Regression Loss / Policy Distribution:** The Laplace policy (using ℓ1 loss in SFT) consistently outperforms the Gaussian policy (using ℓ2^2 loss). This supports the authors' hypothesis that ℓ1-type objectives are more robust for localization tasks, aligning with findings in related computer vision areas.
 - **Uncertainty Parameterization:** Comparing a shared scalar dispersion parameter (`σ`/`α`) against independent per-coordinate parameters showed marginal performance differences. This justifies the simpler and more efficient shared parameterization for the final model.

4. **Behavior of Uncertainty Parameter (Figure 3):**
 - Analysis of the Laplace scale parameter `α` reveals that successful trajectories are associated with smaller `α` values, indicating lower predictive uncertainty. This suggests that the model learns to be more confident (produce tighter distributions) when it localizes regions accurately, offering an interpretable measure of confidence.

5. **Robustness to Hyperparameter (Figure 4):**
 - The framework's performance is relatively stable across a wide range of values for `λ` (the weight of the regression loss in SFT), with optimal performance found at `λ = 0.3`. Crucially, NV-CoT consistently outperforms text-based baselines across all tested `λ` values, demonstrating the robustness of its core idea.

6. **Qualitative Visualizations (Figure 5 & 6):**
 - Visual examples clearly show that NV-CoT generates tighter and more accurately aligned bounding boxes (red) compared to the coarser or misaligned boxes produced by backbone models (blue). This visually confirms NV-CoT's improved spatial grounding and reduced ambiguity in region selection.

In summary, the findings robustly validate that modeling bounding box coordinates as continuous actions, coupled with appropriate probabilistic policies, significantly enhances MLLMs' visual reasoning capabilities across multiple metrics, including localization precision, final answer accuracy, and training efficiency.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Numerical Visual Chain-of-Thought (NV-CoT) framework represents a significant advancement in the field of multimodal large language models and their ability to interact with visual information. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Technical Paradigm Shift in Visual Grounding:** NV-CoT introduces a more natural and accurate way for MLLMs to perform region-grounded reasoning. By moving from discrete text tokens or fixed patches to continuous numerical coordinates, it resolves fundamental issues of modality mismatch, semantic fragmentation, and granularity limitations that plague existing Visual CoT methods. This is a crucial step towards truly "thinking with images" in a precise and flexible manner.

2. **Enhanced Performance Across Visual Reasoning Tasks:** The consistent and substantial improvements in both localization precision (bounding box IoU) and final answer accuracy across diverse benchmarks (V*Bench, HR-Bench 4K/8K) are compelling. These gains translate into MLLMs that can understand visual queries with greater nuance and detail, leading to more reliable and correct outputs. The ability of a 7B model (NV-CoT) to outperform a 32B baseline (Qwen2.5-VL-32B) underscores the efficiency and effectiveness of the proposed approach.

3. **Broad Applicability and Modularity:** The "plug-and-play" nature of NV-CoT, requiring only minimal architectural modifications to the LLM head, makes it highly versatile. Its compatibility with both supervised fine-tuning (SFT) and reinforcement learning (RL) paradigms means it can be readily integrated into various existing and future MLLM architectures, democratizing access to more advanced visual reasoning capabilities.

4. **Improved Training Efficiency:** The observed acceleration in training convergence suggests that learning with continuous actions is not only more effective but also more efficient. This can reduce computational costs and development time for MLLMs, making advanced visual reasoning more accessible for research and application.

5. **Foundation for Advanced Multimodal AI Agents:** The ability to precisely localize and interact with specific regions of an image is foundational for building more sophisticated multimodal AI agents. NV-CoT could empower MLLMs to act as more capable visual assistants, enabling fine-grained interaction with visual content, performing complex visual tasks, and even supporting human-AI collaboration in visual analytics or design. Potential downstream applications include:
 - **Advanced Visual Question Answering (VQA):** Answering intricate questions requiring detailed inspection of image regions.
 - **Document Understanding and OCR:** More accurate extraction and reasoning over specific text fields, tables, or figures in complex documents.
 - **Fine-Grained Recognition:** Identifying subtle attributes or distinctions in images with higher precision.
 - **Robot Navigation and Interaction:** Potentially informing robotic systems that need to precisely locate and interact with objects in their environment.

6. **Inspiration for Future Research:** This work opens new avenues for exploring continuous action spaces in MLLMs for other modalities or tasks beyond bounding box prediction. It provides a robust framework for integrating continuous control mechanisms within large generative models, potentially inspiring novel architectures and training methodologies.

7. **Ethical Considerations:** The authors acknowledge that improved visual localization and reasoning capabilities could be applied in sensitive domains. However, they rightly point out that their work does not introduce new data sources, deployable systems, or application-specific pipelines beyond standard benchmarks. Therefore, it does not present novel ethical risks beyond those already recognized in the broader MLLM field.

In conclusion, NV-CoT significantly pushes the state-of-the-art in multimodal reasoning by offering a more intuitive, precise, and robust mechanism for MLLMs to "think with images." Its potential impact lies in creating more intelligent, capable, and versatile MLLMs that can understand and interact with the visual world with unprecedented accuracy and flexibility.
