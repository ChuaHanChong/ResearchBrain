# Thinking with Images as Continuous Actions: Numerical Visual Chain-of-Thought

> [!summary] Summary
> The Numerical Visual Chain-of-Thought (NV-CoT) framework allows Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) to generate continuous numerical bounding box coordinates for region-grounded reasoning. This approach achieves 59.5% Bounding-box IoU on the Vis-CoT-363K dataset with an ℓ1 loss and improves overall reasoning accuracy by up to 3.7% on the V*Bench benchmark.

## Problem

- Existing text-based Visual Chain-of-Thought (CoT) methods suffer from modality mismatch (continuous visual coordinates represented as discrete text tokens) and semantic fragmentation due to numerical tokenization.
- Patch-based Visual CoT methods are constrained by fixed spatial granularity from vision backbones and often require extensive architectural changes, limiting flexible and precise region selection.
- These limitations lead to imprecise localization, brittle numerical reasoning, and inefficient learning for MLLMs in complex visual reasoning tasks.

## Method

- NV-CoT augments the MLLM output head with lightweight linear layers to directly predict ==continuous numerical bounding box coordinates== and a shared dispersion parameter, expanding the action space beyond discrete tokens.
- The framework is compatible with Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) using ==regression losses== (e.g., ℓ1 loss) for coordinates and also extends to Reinforcement Learning (RL) with GRPO-style algorithms.
- For RL, it employs ==stochastic Gaussian or Laplace policies== with reparameterized sampling, analytically tractable importance ratios, and KL divergence penalties for stable and efficient continuous policy updates.

## Results

- NV-CoT consistently outperforms its backbone models and larger MLLMs across V*Bench, HR-Bench 4K, and HR-Bench 8K, with up to **3.7%** overall accuracy improvement on V*Bench for SFT and **2.6%** for RL.
- The approach substantially improves localization precision, achieving **59.5%** Bounding-box IoU on the Vis-CoT-363K dataset with ℓ1 loss, a gain of over **12 percentage points** compared to the **47.3%** of the text-based Vis-CoT.
- Ablation studies confirm the superiority of the Laplace policy, the effectiveness of the shared uncertainty parameter, and demonstrate the robustness of the framework across hyperparameter variations.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Directly modeling bounding box coordinates as continuous actions resolves the modality mismatch and semantic fragmentation issues inherent in text-based Visual CoT, leading to more precise visual grounding.
> - Integrating probabilistic policies with reparameterization enables effective and stable application of Reinforcement Learning for continuous action spaces within existing MLLM architectures.
> - The Laplace policy (and ℓ1 regression loss) proves more robust and effective for bounding box localization tasks compared to the Gaussian policy (ℓ2^2 loss), yielding higher precision.
