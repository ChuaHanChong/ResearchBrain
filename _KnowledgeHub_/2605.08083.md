# LLMs Improving LLMs: Agentic Discovery for Test-Time Scaling

> [!summary] Summary
> The AutoTTS framework automates the discovery of Test-Time Scaling (TTS) strategies for Large Language Models (LLMs) by reframing the design problem as an algorithmic search. Discovered strategies consistently achieve superior accuracy-cost trade-offs, reducing token consumption by up to 69.5% compared to handcrafted baselines while often improving peak accuracy and demonstrating robust generalization across various LLMs and benchmarks.

## Problem

- Test-Time Scaling (TTS) strategies for Large Language Models (LLMs) are conventionally developed through manual design and heuristic tuning.
- This reliance on human intuition restricts the exploration of complex computation allocation policies and is prone to human cognitive biases.
- Handcrafted methods often result in strategies that are specific to certain tasks or models, leading to sub-optimal accuracy-cost trade-offs across a broader spectrum.

## Method

- **AutoTTS** formulates ==Test-Time== Scaling (TTS) as a controller synthesis problem, where an explorer LLM iteratively proposes and refines code-defined policies.
- It utilizes an offline replay environment, pre-collecting reasoning trajectories to enable cheap, deterministic evaluation of candidate controllers without real-time LLM calls.
- The framework incorporates beta parameterization to simplify the search space for hyperparameters and provides fine-grained execution traces as feedback for robust discovery.

## Results

- AutoTTS discovered strategies achieved a superior accuracy-cost Pareto frontier versus strong handcrafted baselines, reducing token consumption by up to **69.5%** while maintaining or often improving peak accuracy across various LLM models and benchmarks.
- Strategies demonstrated robust generalization capabilities, transferring effectively to held-out benchmarks, different LLM families (DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Llama-8B), and diverse tasks (GPQA-Diamond).
- The discovery process itself was efficient, costing approximately $39.9 and 160 minutes, and yielded a sophisticated "Confidence Momentum Controller" with non-obvious mechanisms.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Automating TTS strategy discovery through agentic search can uncover more sophisticated and effective computation allocation mechanisms than manual design.
> - Decoupling LLM generation from controller evaluation via an offline replay environment keeps discovery computationally affordable, while constraining controller logic to a single scalar hyperparameter (beta parameterization) manages search complexity and prevents overfitting.
> - Providing fine-grained execution traces as feedback, beyond just scalar outcomes, significantly aids the explorer LLM in diagnosing and refining proposed strategies.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work is situated within the field of Large Language Model (LLM) efficiency and performance optimization, specifically focusing on Test-Time Scaling (TTS). TTS refers to methods that allocate additional computational resources during LLM inference to enhance performance. The broader landscape of TTS strategies includes techniques such as parallel reasoning, adaptive stopping criteria, and dynamic resource management.

Existing TTS approaches, as highlighted by the authors, have largely been developed through manual design. Researchers typically craft heuristics and tuning parameters based on intuition to control aspects like when to branch into multiple reasoning paths, how deeply to explore each path, when to probe for intermediate answers, when to prune unpromising branches, and when to terminate the reasoning process. Examples of such handcrafted methods include Self-Consistency (SC), Adaptive Self-Consistency (ASC), Early-Stopping Self-Consistency (ESC), and Parallel-Probe, which employ diverse strategies within a conceptual "width-depth" control space, where width relates to the number of reasoning branches and depth to the extent of their development.

While these manual approaches have advanced LLM performance, the authors contend that they are limited by human cognitive biases and the sheer complexity of the computation-allocation space. The manual process restricts exploration of potentially more effective strategies and often results in solutions that are specific to certain tasks or models.

This paper introduces AutoTTS, an environment-driven framework, to address this limitation. It reframes the problem of designing TTS strategies as an algorithmic search task, drawing parallels to advancements in AutoML and agentic discovery. Prior work in agentic discovery, such as FunSearch and Meta-Harness, has demonstrated the capability of LLMs to iteratively propose and refine algorithms in various domains. However, the application of such automated discovery to TTS strategy design has not been extensively explored. AutoTTS positions itself as the first to formulate TTS as a controller synthesis problem within a structured control space, aiming to automate the discovery of efficient and effective computation allocation policies, thereby shifting the human role from direct strategy design to the construction of environments that facilitate this discovery.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to automate the discovery of effective Test-Time Scaling (TTS) strategies for Large Language Models (LLMs), moving away from the prevailing practice of manual, intuition-driven design. The authors are motivated by several limitations inherent in current TTS methodologies.

Firstly, existing TTS strategies are predominantly hand-crafted. Researchers develop explicit rules and heuristics for managing LLM reasoning processes, such as deciding when to generate multiple reasoning paths (branching), how far to extend these paths (deepening), when to check for intermediate results (probing), when to discard unproductive paths (pruning), and when to conclude the reasoning (stopping). This manual design process relies heavily on human intuition and expertise, which the authors identify as a bottleneck. Such an approach can be labor-intensive, time-consuming, and may not explore the full potential of computation allocation strategies.

Secondly, the manual design process tends to produce strategies that are "special cases" within a larger, unexplored computation-allocation space. The authors illustrate this using a "width–depth" control space, where different handcrafted algorithms trace distinct, predefined paths. This suggests that a significant portion of the possible allocation policies remain uninvestigated, potentially leading to sub-optimal accuracy-cost trade-offs. The authors argue that performance is not solely dependent on the total computation budget but critically on *how* that budget is allocated.

To address these motivations, the key objectives of the AutoTTS framework are:
- **Redefine the Human Role:** To shift the human contribution from directly designing specific branching, pruning, and stopping heuristics to constructing environments where TTS strategies can be automatically discovered. This involves defining the control space, states, actions, feedback mechanisms, and objectives for the discovery agent.
- **Enable Automatic Strategy Discovery:** To develop a framework that allows an agent (specifically an explorer LLM) to iteratively propose, evaluate, and refine code-defined controllers that govern TTS operations.
- **Ensure Tractable Search:** To overcome the challenge of computationally expensive online evaluation of candidate controllers, which typically requires repeated LLM calls. This involves constructing an "offline replay environment" where candidate controllers can be evaluated cheaply and deterministically against pre-collected reasoning trajectories.
- **Mitigate Overfitting and Enhance Robustness:** To address the issue of agents proposing controllers with numerous hyperparameters that could lead to overfitting to the search data. This is achieved through "beta parameterization," which constrains each controller to expose only a single scalar hyperparameter, from which all internal hyperparameters are deterministically derived.
- **Improve Discovery Efficiency with Granular Feedback:** To enhance the agent's ability to diagnose why a proposed controller might fail. This is accomplished by providing "fine-grained execution trace feedback" alongside scalar accuracy-cost outcomes, allowing the agent to understand the operational details of a controller's decisions.
- **Demonstrate Superior Performance and Generalization:** To empirically show that the automatically discovered TTS strategies can achieve a better accuracy–cost Pareto frontier compared to strong, manually designed baselines. Furthermore, to demonstrate that these discovered strategies generalize effectively to held-out benchmarks and across different LLM scales and model families.
- **Achieve Cost-Effective Discovery:** To confirm that the entire discovery process is affordable in terms of computational cost and wall-clock time, making the automated approach practical for research and development.

In summary, the research aims to fundamentally change the methodology for developing TTS strategies, transitioning from a labor-intensive, intuition-based approach to an efficient, automated, and data-driven discovery paradigm.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The AutoTTS framework operates as an environment-driven paradigm for the automatic discovery of Test-Time Scaling (TTS) strategies. The core methodology involves formulating TTS as a controller synthesis problem within an offline replay environment, enabling an explorer LLM to search for optimal policies.

#### 3.1. TTS as Algorithmic Search

The authors conceptualize adaptive test-time algorithms as policies that allocate inference budget across multiple reasoning branches. For a given question, a controller executes a sequence of actions to generate, extend, probe, and prune branches before aggregating a final answer.

- **State Definition:** The system state $s_t$ at decision step $t$ includes the question ($q$), the total number of instantiated branches ($m_t$), the set of currently active branches ($I_t$), the current depth of each branch ($\ell_t$), the generated prefixes ($Z_t$), and all revealed probe feedback ($\Omega_t$). Each branch prefix $z_{i,k}$ is produced after $k$ fixed-length generation intervals, and an intermediate answer $\omega_{i,k}$ is observed if a probing action is taken.
- **Action Set:** The controller can choose from a defined set of actions:
 - `BRANCH`: Creates a new reasoning branch and advances it to the first generation interval.
 - `CONTINUE(i)`: Advances an existing branch $i$ by one generation interval.
 - `PROBE(i)`: Reveals the current probe signal for branch $i$ without advancing its generation.
 - `PRUNE(i)`: Removes branch $i$ from the active set.
 - `ANSWER`: Terminates inference and triggers the aggregation rule.
- **Cost Measurement:** Computation cost is measured in units of generation intervals, with an additional cost ($\kappa_{probe}$) for reading probe signals, though $\kappa_{probe}$ can be set to zero for free probing.
- **Objective Function:** The goal is to find a code-defined policy $\pi$ (parameterized by $\beta$) that maps a state to a distribution over admissible actions. This policy, along with its terminal aggregation rule, aims to maximize accuracy while controlling computation cost. The objective is formalized as maximizing $E_{(q,y)\sim D, \tau \sim P_{\pi,\beta}(\cdot|q)} [1\{\hat{y}_{\pi,\beta}(\tau) = y\} - \gamma C(\tau)]$, where $y$ is the ground truth, $\hat{y}$ is the predicted answer, $C(\tau)$ is the cost, and $\gamma$ is a trade-off parameter.

#### 3.2. Environment-Driven Discovery Loop

The AutoTTS pipeline orchestrates the discovery process through several key design choices:

- **Offline Replay Environment Construction:** This is central to addressing the high evaluation cost of LLM calls.
 - **Offline Data Collection:** Before discovery begins, for each question in a search dataset ($Q_{search}$), $N$ (e.g., 128) independent reasoning trajectories are pre-collected from the base LLM. These trajectories are segmented into fixed-length token intervals (e.g., 500 tokens), and all intermediate branch prefixes ($z_{i,k}$) and probe signals ($\omega_{i,k}$) are stored.
 - **Evaluation via Offline Replay:** When a candidate controller is evaluated, its decisions (BRANCH, CONTINUE, PROBE, PRUNE) are executed against this pre-collected, stored data rather than invoking the LLM in real-time. This makes controller evaluations deterministic, fast, and computationally inexpensive.
- **Discovery Loop with Agent-Driven Proposal:**
 - The overall process involves multiple rounds. In each round, an explorer LLM (Claude Code) reads an accumulated history ($H$) of previous proposals and their outcomes.
 - **Agent-Driven Proposal:** The explorer LLM is prompted to analyze past performance and propose an improved controller by directly editing its code. The prompt specifies the environment API, design constraints, and objectives (maximize accuracy, minimize cost, adaptively allocate budget).
 - **History Design:** The history ($H$) includes not only scalar accuracy-cost outcomes but also full "execution traces." These traces provide fine-grained behavioral evidence by logging the controller's decision-making trajectory (e.g., which branches were expanded, pruned, or stopped at each step). This allows the explorer LLM to diagnose failure modes and propose targeted improvements, moving beyond simple scalar feedback.
 - **Controller Selection:** After a predefined number of rounds (e.g., five), the controller and its associated $\beta$ value that achieved the highest accuracy on the search set ($Q_{search}$) are selected as the final discovered strategy.
- **Beta Parameterization for Tractable Search:**
 - **Problem:** Early experiments showed that agents might propose controllers with numerous independent hyperparameters, leading to an intractably large search space and overfitting.
 - **Solution:** Each candidate controller is constrained to expose only a single scalar hyperparameter, $\beta$. All internal, behavior-critical hyperparameters (e.g., warm-up periods, pruning patience, confidence thresholds) are implemented as deterministic, monotonic functions of $\beta$. This design collapses the high-dimensional hyperparameter space into a one-dimensional sweep, simplifying the search and promoting robustness by discouraging brittle, search-set-specific thresholds. Larger $\beta$ values generally correspond to a more aggressive allocation of token budget.

#### 3.3. Experimental Setup

- **Models:** Experiments were conducted using four models from the Qwen3 family (0.6B, 1.7B, 4B, 8B).
- **Benchmarks:** AIME24 was used as the search set ($Q_{search}$), while AIME25 and HMMT25 served as held-out evaluation sets ($Q_{eval}$).
- **Data Collection Protocol:** For the offline replay environments, 128 reasoning trajectories were pre-sampled per (model, problem) pair at a temperature of 0.7, with a probing interval of 500 tokens.
- **Evaluation Protocol:** Each controller was evaluated 64 times by randomly sampling subsets of trajectories from the pre-sampled pool, and results were averaged to reduce variance.
- **Baselines:** Performance was compared against strong handcrafted baselines: Self-Consistency (SC@64), Adaptive Self-Consistency (ASC), Early-Stopping Self-Consistency (ESC), and Parallel-Probe.
- **Metrics:** Task accuracy and total tokens consumed were the primary metrics.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research paper presents several key findings demonstrating the efficacy and characteristics of the AutoTTS framework and its discovered strategies.

#### 4.1. Main Performance Results

The discovered controllers generated by AutoTTS consistently demonstrated a favorable accuracy-cost trade-off compared to strong handcrafted baselines across various models and benchmarks (Table 1, Figure 3).
- **Superior Trade-off:** AutoTTS controllers achieved a better accuracy–cost Pareto frontier in most experimental settings. On average across the four Qwen3 models, the discovered controller generalized to held-out benchmarks (AIME25 and HMMT25), outperforming all handcrafted baselines in three out of four models and remaining competitive on the Qwen3-8B model (e.g., 62.7% accuracy for AutoTTS (β=1.0) versus 62.8% for SC@64, but with significantly fewer tokens).
- **Efficiency Gains:** The discovered strategies effectively reduced token consumption. For instance, with $\beta=0.5$, AutoTTS reduced token usage by approximately 69.5% compared to SC@64 while maintaining comparable accuracy (45.3% vs. 45.2% average across models).
- **Peak Performance Improvement:** Beyond efficiency gains, AutoTTS (with $\beta=1.0$) also pushed the attainable peak accuracy beyond all handcrafted baselines in 5 out of 8 cases across different model-benchmark combinations. This indicates that the discovered controller not only optimizes for lower cost but can also improve the overall performance ceiling.

#### 4.2. Accuracy–Cost Scaling Curves

Figure 3 illustrates that the discovered controller consistently traces stronger accuracy-efficiency frontiers. Its curves are positioned above those of handcrafted methods across all four Qwen3 models on held-out benchmarks (AIME25, HMMT25). This suggests that AutoTTS can better identify and prioritize productive reasoning branches while pruning noisy or unpromising ones, leading to more effective computation allocation.

#### 4.3. Generalization Capabilities

The discovered controllers exhibited generalization beyond the specific model family and task used for discovery (Table 2).
- **Model Transfer:** When evaluated on DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Llama-8B (a Llama-based model, different from Qwen3) for HMMT25, AutoTTS with $\beta=1.0$ achieved the highest accuracy among all methods while significantly reducing total tokens. The $\beta=0.5$ variant further lowered token cost with a small accuracy drop.
- **Task Transfer:** On GPQA-Diamond (a non-mathematical Q&A benchmark) with Qwen3-1.7B, AutoTTS controllers achieved comparable or slightly better accuracy than SC@64, utilizing substantially fewer tokens. The $\beta=0.5$ variant, for instance, reduced token cost from 510.0K to 151.0K.
These results indicate that the discovered strategies are not tightly overfitted to the Qwen-based mathematical reasoning setting and possess a degree of transferability.

#### 4.4. Ablation Study

An ablation study (Table 3) investigated the contributions of key design choices within AutoTTS:
- **Without Beta Parameterization:** Removing beta parameterization resulted in controllers with excessive free hyperparameters. This led to overfitting on the search set, manifesting as overly aggressive pruning and stopping thresholds. While token usage drastically decreased (from 575.5K to 93.3K average), this was accompanied by a noticeable accuracy drop (from 53.1% to 49.0% on held-out benchmarks), indicating a loss of robustness.
- **Without Execution Traces:** When the explorer LLM was deprived of fine-grained execution trace feedback, the discovered controller performed worse and allocated more tokens (51.6% accuracy, 824.3K tokens average) compared to the full AutoTTS framework (53.1% accuracy, 575.5K tokens average). This highlights the importance of detailed behavioral feedback for diagnosing failure modes and guiding effective strategy improvements.

#### 4.5. Efficiency Analysis of Discovery Process

The discovery process itself was efficient. The entire five-round discovery, which generated the effective controllers, cost approximately $39.9 and required 160 minutes of wall-clock time. This affordability is attributed to the replay-based discovery framework, which eliminates the need for repeated, expensive online LLM calls during candidate controller evaluation.

#### 4.6. Evolution of Discovery Process

The visualization of the discovery trajectory (Figure 4) demonstrated an iterative refinement process. Initially proposed controllers sometimes over-optimized for token efficiency, leading to accuracy drops. Subsequent rounds showed the explorer LLM making adjustments to increase the computation budget, thereby recovering performance. This iterative feedback loop, alternating between efficiency-oriented adjustments and performance recovery, progressively guided the discovery process toward a better accuracy-cost frontier, indicating a robust learning trajectory.

#### 4.7. Analysis of Discovered Controller (Confidence Momentum Controller - CMC)

The analysis of the final discovered controller, termed the "Confidence Momentum Controller" (CMC) (Appendix D), revealed four non-obvious mechanisms:
- **Trend-based stopping:** Termination decisions are based not merely on instantaneous confidence but on an Exponential Moving Average (EMA) of pool confidence, stopping only when the EMA level is high and the trend is non-negative.
- **Coupled width–depth control:** Decisions to widen (spawn new branches) or deepen are dynamically linked to the EMA delta (change in confidence). Strong confidence gains suppress new branch spawning, while stagnation or regression triggers widening.
- **Alignment-aware depth allocation:** Computation is concentrated on emerging consensus by allocating more probe steps to branches whose latest answers align with the current pool winner.
- **Conservative branch abandonment:** Branches are pruned only after persistently deviating for a specified number of consecutive rounds, ensuring that at least two active branches are always preserved.
These mechanisms collectively represent a level of coordinated complexity that, according to the authors, would be challenging to arrive at through manual intuition alone.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces AutoTTS, an environment-driven framework for the automatic discovery of Test-Time Scaling (TTS) strategies, marking a shift in the approach to optimizing LLM inference. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Paradigm Shift in TTS Research:** AutoTTS proposes a fundamental change from manually designing individual TTS algorithms to constructing reusable discovery environments. This reframes human expertise, allowing it to be invested in defining effective search spaces and feedback mechanisms rather than specific heuristics. This paradigm could significantly accelerate advancements in TTS research by making the discovery process more systematic and less reliant on individual intuition.

- **Improved Efficiency and Performance:** The discovered TTS strategies consistently outperform or demonstrate strong competitiveness against manually designed baselines across various LLM scales and benchmarks. These strategies achieve a superior accuracy-cost trade-off, often delivering comparable or higher accuracy while consuming substantially fewer computational resources (tokens). This direct improvement in LLM inference efficiency has practical implications for reducing operational costs and latency, making powerful LLM reasoning more accessible and scalable.

- **Enhanced Generalizability:** The discovered controllers demonstrate robustness by generalizing across different Qwen3 model sizes, to a different model family (DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Llama-8B), and to a non-mathematical task (GPQA-Diamond). This suggests that the automatically found strategies are not overly specialized or brittle, making them reusable and applicable in diverse deployment scenarios.

- **Affordable and Practical Discovery:** The empirical demonstration of a low discovery cost (approximately $39.9 and 160 minutes) highlights the practicality and scalability of the AutoTTS framework. By leveraging offline replay environments to avoid repeated LLM calls during evaluation, the method makes advanced algorithm discovery accessible without prohibitive computational overhead.

- **Discovery of Sophisticated Strategies:** The analysis of the "Confidence Momentum Controller" (CMC) reveals complex, coordinated mechanisms (e.g., trend-based stopping, coupled width-depth control) that would be challenging to develop manually. This indicates that automated discovery agents can synthesize more intricate and potentially more effective algorithms than human intuition alone might achieve, pushing the boundaries of algorithmic design for LLMs.

- **Foundation for Future Research:** AutoTTS lays the groundwork for further exploration in agentic discovery for LLMs. Future work could involve designing richer environments with expanded action sets to discover more complex control structures beyond width-depth scaling, and investigating the capabilities of open-source coding agents in similar discovery tasks.

- **Broader Societal Implications:** By making LLM inference more efficient, AutoTTS contributes to reducing the environmental footprint of large-scale AI applications and lowers the barrier to entry for utilizing advanced reasoning capabilities. This could broaden access to sophisticated AI tools for a wider range of users and applications. While the primary impact is positive, the authors acknowledge a general risk inherent in more efficient reasoning systems, which could be misused, a concern common to inference optimization research.
