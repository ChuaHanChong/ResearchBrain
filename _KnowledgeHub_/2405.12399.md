# Diffusion for World Modeling: Visual Details Matter in Atari

> [!summary] Summary
> A new reinforcement learning agent, DIAMOND, employs a diffusion-based world model to capture fine-grained visual details, demonstrating that these details improve policy learning. It achieves a mean Human Normalized Score of 1.46 on the Atari 100k benchmark and can function as an interactive neural game engine in complex 3D environments like CS:GO.

## Problem

- Existing world models in reinforcement learning (RL) often rely on discrete latent variables, which can lead to a loss of fine-grained visual details.
- The omission or distortion of subtle visual cues in these compressed representations can hinder an RL agent's ability to learn nuanced policies and generalize effectively.
- Increasing the granularity of discrete latents to retain details significantly increases computational complexity.

## Method

- **DIAMOND** introduces a ==diffusion-based world model== operating directly in image space to preserve rich visual information, conditioned on past observations and actions.
- It leverages the EDM (Elucidating the Design Space of Diffusion-Based Generative Models) formulation for the diffusion model, which ensures stability and reduces compounding errors over long imagined trajectories with few denoising steps (typically 3).
- A separate model predicts scalar rewards and termination, and the reinforcement learning agent (actor-critic) is trained entirely within the imagined environment generated by these models.

## Results

- DIAMOND achieved a new state-of-the-art mean Human Normalized Score of **1.46** on the Atari 100k benchmark for world-model-trained agents, outperforming human players on **11** of **26** games.
- Qualitative comparisons demonstrate DIAMOND's superior visual fidelity and temporal consistency in generated trajectories compared to discrete-token-based world models like IRIS.
- The diffusion world model successfully scaled to a complex 3D environment, training on CS:GO gameplay to create an interactive neural game engine capable of generating stable, action-responsive trajectories over hundreds of timesteps.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Preserving fine-grained visual details in a world model is critical for training high-performing reinforcement learning agents, particularly in environments requiring subtle visual cues.
> - The EDM (Elucidating the Design Space of Diffusion-Based Generative Models) formulation is highly effective for stable and efficient diffusion-based world modeling, outperforming traditional DDPMs even with very few denoising steps.
> - Diffusion-based world models can learn complex environment dynamics from raw pixels to create interactive and plausible simulations, functioning as neural game engines.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated at the nexus of reinforcement learning (RL) and generative modeling, specifically focusing on the advanced concept of "world models."

**World Models in Reinforcement Learning:** The core idea of training RL agents within a learned generative model of their environment, or a "world model" (Ha and Schmidhuber, 2018), has gained significant traction. This paradigm aims to address the notorious sample inefficiency of traditional RL methods by allowing agents to "dream" and practice in a simulated environment, thereby reducing the need for costly real-world interactions. Prior work, such as SimPLe (Kaiser et al., 2019) and the Dreamer series (Hafner et al., 2020, 2021, 2023), has demonstrated the promise of this approach across diverse environments, including the challenging Atari 100k benchmark.

**The Dominance and Limitations of Discrete Latent World Models:** A common thread in recent successful world models (e.g., DreamerV2/V3, IRIS, TWM, STORM) is their reliance on discrete latent variables to represent environmental dynamics. This approach offers advantages in managing compounding errors over long time horizons due to the compact and symbolic nature of discrete representations. However, as the authors highlight, this compression can lead to a loss of fine-grained visual details. In complex or real-world scenarios (e.g., autonomous driving), subtle visual cues like distant traffic lights or pedestrians are critical for optimal decision-making, and their omission or distortion can severely impact an agent's policy. Increasing the granularity of discrete latents can mitigate this, but at the cost of computational complexity.

**The Rise of Diffusion Models in Generative Vision:** Concurrently, diffusion models (Sohl-Dickstein et al., 2015; Ho et al., 2020; Song et al., 2020) have emerged as the leading paradigm for high-resolution image and video generation, surpassing earlier methods like GANs and even challenging transformer-based approaches relying on discrete tokens (e.g., VQGAN, DALL·E). Diffusion models excel at generating photorealistic images, flexibly modeling complex multi-modal distributions, and are highly amenable to conditioning. These characteristics make them a compelling alternative to discrete latent representations where visual fidelity is paramount.

**DIAMOND's Contribution to the Landscape:** DIAMOND integrates these two powerful paradigms. It directly addresses the limitations of discrete latent world models by leveraging the superior generative capabilities of diffusion models. By operating directly in image space and preserving rich visual details, DIAMOND proposes that these details are not merely aesthetic but *critical* for learning high-performing RL policies. The paper positions DIAMOND as the *first* work to successfully employ diffusion models as world models for *online learning in imagination*, distinguishing it from other concurrent works that use diffusion for RL in different capacities (e.g., as policies, planners, or for offline data augmentation). Furthermore, the exploration of DIAMOND as an interactive neural game engine (demonstrated with CS:GO) connects it to a nascent but growing field of generative AI creating playable virtual environments (e.g., GameGAN, Genie, GameNGen), showcasing its potential beyond just RL training.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to introduce and rigorously evaluate **DIAMOND (DIffusion As a Model Of eNvironment Dreams)**, a novel reinforcement learning agent that is trained within a world model based on diffusion models.

The core motivation stems from a critical limitation observed in previous state-of-the-art world models:
- **Addressing Loss of Visual Detail:** Prior successful world models predominantly rely on discrete latent variable representations of environment dynamics. While effective for reducing compounding errors over long horizons, this compression can inevitably discard important visual details. The authors hypothesize that "visual details matter" for effective reinforcement learning, especially in environments where subtle visual cues dictate optimal behavior (e.g., a distant traffic light, a small enemy sprite, or road imperfections). This lossy compression can hinder the agent's ability to learn nuanced policies and generalize effectively, particularly in complex or real-world scenarios.

To overcome this, the authors are motivated to explore diffusion models due to their inherent strengths:
- **High-Fidelity Image Generation:** Diffusion models excel at generating high-resolution, photorealistic images, making them ideal for preserving the rich visual information often lost in discrete latent representations.
- **Modeling Complex Distributions:** They can flexibly model complex, multi-modal data distributions without suffering from issues like mode collapse, which is crucial for capturing the diverse and often stochastic nature of environmental dynamics.
- **Conditioning Capabilities:** Diffusion models are highly amenable to conditioning, allowing them to incorporate past observations and agent actions, which is essential for learning accurate predictive world models.

Secondary objectives and motivations include:
- **Achieving State-of-the-Art Sample Efficiency:** To demonstrate the practical utility of their approach, the authors aim to achieve new state-of-the-art performance for world-model-trained agents on the challenging Atari 100k benchmark, a setting that emphasizes sample efficiency.
- **Ensuring Stability and Efficiency:** A significant technical challenge for diffusion models in world modeling is their computational cost due to iterative denoising steps. The authors are motivated to identify and implement "key design choices" to ensure their diffusion world model is efficient and stable over long imaginative horizons, even with a low number of denoising steps.
- **Demonstrating Standalone Utility:** Beyond RL training, the authors aim to showcase the independent power of their diffusion world model as an "interactive neural game engine," by training it on a complex 3D environment like Counter-Strike: Global Offensive gameplay data. This demonstrates the potential for learned, generative simulations that are not tied to explicit game rules.

In essence, the paper is motivated by the belief that a more visually faithful world model, enabled by diffusion techniques, will lead to more capable and robust reinforcement learning agents.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

DIAMOND operates within the established world model paradigm for reinforcement learning, which involves a cyclical process of data collection from the real environment, training the world model on this data, and then training the RL agent within the learned world model (in "imagination").

#### 3.1. Diffusion World Model (Dθ)

- **Core Principle:** DIAMOND models environment dynamics as a conditional generative process `p(x_t+1 | x_<=t, a_<=t)`, where `x` represents image observations and `a` represents actions.
- **Diffusion Framework Choice (EDM):** A crucial design choice is the adoption of the **EDM (Elucidating the Design Space of Diffusion-Based Generative Models)** formulation (Karras et al., 2022) over the more traditional DDPM (Ho et al., 2020). The authors rigorously justify this choice, showing that EDM's improved training objective (which adaptively mixes signal and noise prediction) leads to significantly greater stability and reduced compounding error over long imagined trajectories, even with very few denoising steps (e.g., n=1). This is vital for computational efficiency in a real-time world model.
- **Training Objective:** The model is trained using a denoising score matching objective, which simplifies to an L2 reconstruction loss (Equation 4 & 5). This objective aims to predict the original clean image `x_0` from a noisy version `x_τ`, conditioned on past observations and actions. Network preconditioning from EDM further refines this objective.
- **Architecture:** A standard U-Net 2D (Ronneberger et al., 2015) is used as the neural network `F_θ` that approximates the score function.
- **Conditioning:**
 - **Observations:** Frame stacking is employed, concatenating the current noisy observation `x_τ_t+1` with the `L` (typically 4) clean past observations channel-wise. This provides the temporal context.
 - **Actions & Diffusion Time:** Actions and the diffusion time `τ` are incorporated via adaptive group normalization layers (Zheng et al., 2020) within the U-Net's residual blocks.
- **Sampling:** For generating the next observation during imagination, Euler's method is used as the SDE solver. After empirical analysis of quality vs. speed, the authors set the number of denoising steps (`n`) to 3 for all experiments, striking a balance between visual fidelity (especially for multimodal outcomes) and inference cost.

#### 3.2. Reward and Termination Model (Rψ)

- Since reward and termination prediction are scalar outputs, a separate, simpler model `R_ψ` is used. This model consists of standard CNN and LSTM layers, designed to handle partial observability by processing sequences of frames and actions. It predicts a scalar reward and a binary termination flag for each timestep.

#### 3.3. Reinforcement Learning Agent (πφ and Vφ)

- The RL agent comprises an actor-critic network. The policy `π_φ` (actor) and value function `V_φ` (critic) share a CNN-LSTM backbone.
- **Policy Training:** The policy is trained using a REINFORCE objective with a value baseline `V_φ(x_t)` to reduce gradient variance. An entropy maximization term is included to encourage exploration.
- **Value Function Training:** The value network is trained to minimize the expected squared difference with lambda-returns (`Λ_t`), which balance bias and variance in the value target.
- **Imagination:** The agent is trained *entirely* within the imagined environment generated by `D_θ` and `R_ψ`. Before imagination, a "burn-in" period uses real collected frames to initialize the LSTM states of the world models and agent networks.

#### 3.4. Training Protocol

- The overall training procedure involves iterating through:
 1. **Data Collection:** Agent interacts with the real environment for a fixed number of steps (e.g., 100 steps per epoch) to populate a replay dataset `D`.
 2. **World Model Update:** `D_θ` and `R_ψ` are updated using randomly sampled sequences from `D`.
 3. **Agent Training:** `π_φ` and `V_φ` are updated by generating long imagined trajectories (horizon H=15) using the current world model.
- **Atari 100k Benchmark:** DIAMOND was trained from scratch for 5 random seeds on each of the 26 Atari games, adhering to the 100k real environment interaction limit.

#### 3.5. Counter-Strike: Global Offensive (CS:GO) World Model (Standalone)

- To demonstrate scalability and applicability to complex 3D environments, the `D_θ` model was trained in isolation on a large static dataset of CS:GO gameplay (87 hours).
- **Hierarchical Generation:** To manage computational cost while achieving high resolution, observations were downscaled for the dynamics model, and a *second, smaller diffusion model* was trained as an upsampler to restore the original resolution.
- **Scaling:** The U-Net's channel dimensions were significantly scaled up (to 381M parameters) to handle the complexity of the CS:GO environment.
- **Sampling Enhancements:** For the upsampler, stochastic sampling and an increased number of denoising steps (10) were used to improve visual quality, while the dynamics model retained 3 steps for efficiency.
- **Interactive Neural Game Engine:** The resulting model was evaluated for its ability to generate stable, interactive trajectories given user inputs, effectively acting as a learned game engine.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted on the Atari 100k benchmark and the standalone CS:GO world model yielded several significant findings:

#### 4.1. Atari 100k Benchmark Performance

- **State-of-the-Art for World Model Agents:** DIAMOND achieved a mean Human Normalized Score (HNS) of **1.46** on the Atari 100k benchmark, setting a new state-of-the-art for agents trained *entirely within a world model*. It also achieved an interquartile mean (IQM) of 0.64, on par with or exceeding other baselines.
- **Superhuman Performance:** DIAMOND outperformed human players on 11 out of the 26 games, demonstrating its strong capabilities under limited interaction.
- **Impact of Visual Details:** The paper specifically notes that DIAMOND performed particularly well on games where capturing small visual details is crucial, such as *Asterix*, *Breakout*, and *Road Runner*. This quantitative result supports the core hypothesis that improved visual modeling directly translates to better RL performance.

#### 4.2. Ablation Studies and Design Choices Validation

- **EDM vs. DDPM:** Qualitative and quantitative analysis (Figure 3 & 8) decisively showed the superior stability of the EDM-based diffusion world model compared to a DDPM-based one. The DDPM model suffered from severe compounding errors and quickly drifted out of distribution, even with multiple denoising steps. In contrast, EDM remained stable even with a single denoising step. This strongly validates the choice of EDM for the world model.
- **Number of Denoising Steps:** While 1-step EDM was stable, the authors found that 3 denoising steps (`n=3`) were optimal for balancing visual quality and computational cost. As illustrated with *Boxing* (Figure 4), 1-step denoising could lead to blurry or out-of-distribution predictions in multimodal scenarios (e.g., unpredictable opponent movements), whereas multi-step sampling correctly drove the generation towards a crisp, plausible mode. Quantitative ablation (Table 7) further supported that `n=3` yielded better agent performance.
- **Visual Quality Comparison with IRIS:** A direct qualitative comparison with IRIS (Figure 5), a leading discrete-token-based world model, showcased DIAMOND's superior visual fidelity and temporal consistency. IRIS-generated trajectories exhibited "visual inconsistencies" (e.g., enemies appearing as rewards, inconsistent scores or road details) over consecutive frames. DIAMOND's generations were consistently more faithful to the true environment, providing compelling evidence that these "small details" indeed matter for learning an optimal policy.
- **Efficiency Compared to Baselines:** Despite generating higher fidelity images, DIAMOND's world model (with 13M parameters) has fewer parameters than IRIS (30M) and DreamerV3 (18M). It trains faster than IRIS (2.9 vs 4.1 days) but is slower than DreamerV3 (which is an order of magnitude faster but with significantly lower visual quality). This indicates that the visual improvement is not simply a result of increased computational burden, but more efficient model design.

#### 4.3. Scaling to Counter-Strike: Global Offensive (CS:GO)

- **Interactive Neural Game Engine:** Training DIAMOND's diffusion world model on static CS:GO gameplay successfully produced an interactive neural game engine for the *Dust II* map.
- **Stable Trajectories:** The model was capable of generating stable and plausible trajectories over hundreds of timesteps, demonstrating its ability to learn complex 3D environment dynamics from raw pixels.
- **Action Response:** The model responded plausibly to user-input actions (keyboard and mouse), allowing for interactive exploration of the learned environment (Figures 11 & 12).
- **Limitations Identified:** While impressive, the CS:GO experiment also highlighted limitations, such as occasional drift in less frequently visited areas, memory issues (forgetting state near walls), and "causal confusion" leading to incorrect generalizations (e.g., successive jumps being enabled). These are common challenges for models trained on offline data.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of DIAMOND represents a significant step forward in the field of reinforcement learning and generative AI, with several key implications:

**Shifting the World Modeling Paradigm:** DIAMOND demonstrates the compelling advantages of diffusion models over discrete latent variable approaches for building world models. By proving that improved visual fidelity directly translates to better RL performance on challenging benchmarks, it suggests a paradigm shift in how future world models might be constructed. This could lead to a new wave of research focusing on pixel-perfect predictive models for richer, more realistic simulations.

**Enhanced Reinforcement Learning Performance:** Achieving a new state-of-the-art on the Atari 100k benchmark for world-model-trained agents, especially in games sensitive to visual detail, is a strong testament to the practical benefits of diffusion-based world models. This directly contributes to making RL more sample-efficient, a critical factor for deploying AI agents in real-world applications where data collection is expensive or risky.

**Enabling Interactive Neural Game Engines and Simulations:** The successful application of DIAMOND's world model to generate a playable CS:GO environment is a breakthrough. This demonstrates the potential for generative AI to create dynamic, interactive simulations purely from observed data, without requiring explicit rules or underlying game engines. Such "neural game engines" could revolutionize game development by automating content creation, enable rapid prototyping of virtual environments, and provide highly realistic, data-driven simulators for training agents in complex, unstructured settings (e.g., autonomous driving, robotics) where real-world training is impractical.

**Addressing RL Safety and Robustness:** More faithful and visually consistent world models, as demonstrated by DIAMOND, inherently lead to more reliable credit assignment and better understanding of environmental consequences. This can contribute to broader efforts in developing safer and more robust RL agents, by reducing the risk of agents learning suboptimal or dangerous behaviors due to distorted or incomplete world representations.

**Paving the Way for Future Research:** The paper clearly articulates several avenues for future work, indicating its foundational nature:
- **Continuous Control Environments:** Extending DIAMOND to continuous action and observation spaces would be a natural next step, exploring its capabilities beyond discrete control.
- **Long-Term Memory and Scalability:** Integrating advanced temporal modeling, such as attention-based mechanisms (e.g., transformers) over environment time, could overcome the current limitations of frame stacking and allow for modeling much longer-term dependencies and more complex environments.
- **Integrated Prediction:** Exploring the integration of reward and termination prediction directly within the diffusion model itself, rather than using separate components, could lead to more unified and potentially more powerful world models.
- **Hybrid Approaches:** Combining diffusion world models with sophisticated RL techniques like Monte-Carlo Tree Search (MCTS) or advanced hyperparameter scheduling could yield even higher performance.
- **Scaling Data and Models:** Further scaling of the diffusion model parameters and training data will be crucial for addressing the remaining limitations observed in the CS:GO experiment, particularly for truly open-world, memory-demanding scenarios.

Finally, the release of code, agents, and playable world models by the authors is a significant contribution to open science, fostering rapid iteration and further research within the community on diffusion for world modeling. This accelerates the development of more generalist and capable AI agents.
