# SPREAD: Subspace Representation Distillation for Lifelong Imitation Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> A framework named SPREAD enables robotic agents to continuously acquire new skills from expert demonstrations while mitigating catastrophic forgetting. It achieves this by aligning the principal low-rank subspaces of feature representations between sequential policies and employing a confidence-guided strategy for behavioral distillation, leading to improved knowledge transfer and reduced forgetting across diverse robotic manipulation tasks.

## Problem

- Robotic agents face catastrophic forgetting when sequentially acquiring new skills from demonstrations, causing degradation of prior skill representations.
- Traditional distillation methods for lifelong imitation learning, relying on L2-norm feature matching, are sensitive to noise and fail to capture the intrinsic low-dimensional geometric structures of task representations.
- A core challenge is to balance stability (retaining past knowledge) and plasticity (adapting to new tasks) in a scalable and robust manner for continuous skill acquisition.

## Method

- Develops **SPREAD**, a framework that integrates ==Subspace Representation Distillation== and ==Confidence-guided Policy Distillation== within a ==ResNet-Transformer backbone== for multimodal inputs.
- Employs ==Subspace Representation Distillation== using ==SVD== and a ==symmetric Frobenius norm== to align the dominant ==low-rank feature subspaces== of teacher and student models, robustly preserving geometric structures.
- Implements ==Confidence-guided Policy Distillation==, which focuses on aligning only the most probable actions from the teacher's ==Gaussian Mixture Model policy== to ensure stable and reliable behavioral knowledge transfer.

## Results

- On **LIBERO-OBJECT**, **SPREAD** achieves the highest forward transfer **FWT 81.0%** and **AUC 73.0%** with negative-backward-transfer **NBT 8.0%**, beating M2Distill by **+6%** FWT and **+4%** AUC.
- On **LIBERO-GOAL**, it reaches **FWT 78.0%** / **AUC 72.0%** with **NBT 9.0%**, versus the M2Distill baseline's substantially higher forgetting (**NBT 20.0%**).
- On **LIBERO-SPATIAL**, it reaches **FWT 71.0%** / **AUC 66.0%** (**+5%** AUC over M2Distill) at a fixed subspace rank **r=48** (non-growing parameter budget across sequential tasks).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Critical task representations in multimodal inputs often reside in low-dimensional manifolds, and preserving these intrinsic geometric properties across sequential learning steps is essential for stable knowledge transfer.
> - Aligning the principal low-rank subspaces of feature representations provides a more robust and effective method for knowledge distillation than raw feature matching, as it focuses on discriminative structures and is less sensitive to noise.
> - Optimizing policy distillation by prioritizing high-confidence behavioral samples from the teacher policy significantly improves stability and reduces variance during the transfer of learned actions.
