# SPREAD: Subspace Representation Distillation for Lifelong Imitation Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> A framework named SPREAD enables robotic agents to continuously acquire new skills from expert demonstrations while mitigating catastrophic forgetting. It achieves this by aligning the principal low-rank subspaces of feature representations between sequential policies and employing a confidence-guided strategy for behavioral distillation, leading to improved knowledge transfer and reduced forgetting across diverse robotic manipulation tasks.

## Problem

- Robotic agents face catastrophic forgetting when sequentially acquiring new skills from demonstrations, causing degradation of prior skill representations.
- Traditional distillation methods for lifelong imitation learning, relying on L2-norm feature matching, are sensitive to noise and fail to capture the intrinsic low-dimensional geometric structures of task representations.
- A core challenge is to balance stability (retaining past knowledge) and plasticity (adapting to new tasks) in a scalable and robust manner for continuous skill acquisition.

## Method

- Develops **SPREAD**, a framework that integrates ==Subspace Representation Distillation== and ==Confidence-guided Policy Distillation== within a ==ResNet-Transformer backbone== for multimodal inputs.
- Employs ==Subspace Representation Distillation== using ==SVD== and a ==symmetric Frobenius norm== to align the dominant ==low-rank feature subspaces== of teacher and student models, robustly preserving geometric structures.
- Implements ==Confidence-guided Policy Distillation==, which focuses on aligning only the most probable actions from the teacher's ==Gaussian Mixture Model policy== to ensure stable and reliable behavioral knowledge transfer.

## Results

- On **LIBERO-OBJECT**, **SPREAD** achieves the highest forward transfer **FWT 81.0%** and **AUC 73.0%** with negative-backward-transfer **NBT 8.0%**, beating M2Distill by **+6%** FWT and **+4%** AUC.
- On **LIBERO-GOAL**, it reaches **FWT 78.0%** / **AUC 72.0%** with **NBT 9.0%**, versus the M2Distill baseline's substantially higher forgetting (**NBT 20.0%**).
- On **LIBERO-SPATIAL**, it reaches **FWT 71.0%** / **AUC 66.0%** (**+5%** AUC over M2Distill) at a fixed subspace rank **r=48** (non-growing parameter budget across sequential tasks).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Critical task representations in multimodal inputs often reside in low-dimensional manifolds, and preserving these intrinsic geometric properties across sequential learning steps is essential for stable knowledge transfer.
> - Aligning the principal low-rank subspaces of feature representations provides a more robust and effective method for knowledge distillation than raw feature matching, as it focuses on discriminative structures and is less sensitive to noise.
> - Optimizing policy distillation by prioritizing high-confidence behavioral samples from the teacher policy significantly improves stability and reduces variance during the transfer of learned actions.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Robotic agents operating in dynamic, open-world environments require the ability to continuously acquire new skills while effectively retaining previously learned knowledge. This capability falls under the domain of Lifelong Imitation Learning (LIL). Standard imitation learning approaches, which typically assume a fixed task distribution, encounter a significant challenge known as catastrophic forgetting when exposed to sequential task acquisition. Catastrophic forgetting manifests as the degradation of representations for prior skills when a policy network adapts to new demonstrations.

The broader research landscape in LIL has explored several strategies to mitigate catastrophic forgetting. These include:
- **Experience Replay (ER):** Methods like store and replay samples from past tasks. However, imbalanced replay distributions can bias the learned policy towards current tasks, leading to performance degradation on earlier ones.
- **Hierarchical Skill Learning:** Approaches such as BUDS and LOTUS aim for scalability by decomposing complex behaviors into modular skills or building dynamic skill libraries. While these methods enhance scalability and skill reuse, they can still face challenges in maintaining efficiency and representational consistency as the skill library expands.
- **Regularization-based Methods:** Techniques like Elastic Weight Consolidation (EWC) constrain parameter updates deemed essential for prior tasks using measures such as Fisher information. EWC attempts to balance plasticity (learning new tasks) and stability (retaining old knowledge) but may struggle to maintain consistent performance across diverse LIL scenarios.
- **Distillation-based Approaches:** These methods aim to transfer knowledge from a "teacher" (a policy trained on previous tasks) to a "student" (the current policy learning a new task). PolyTask consolidates task-specific policies into a generalist model. M2Distill specifically employs multimodal distillation to align visual, textual, and motor features between policies.

A common limitation identified in many existing distillation-based approaches, particularly M2Distill, is their reliance on L2-norm feature matching in the raw feature space. This strategy is described as sensitive to noise and high-dimensional variability, potentially neglecting the intrinsic low-dimensional manifold structure that underlies task representations. This oversight can result in overly rigid alignment, which may hinder adaptation to new skills. The present work addresses this identified gap by proposing a geometry-aware distillation strategy that operates within low-rank subspaces, aiming for more robust and scalable lifelong imitation learning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enable robotic agents to acquire new skills from expert demonstrations in a sequential manner while effectively retaining prior knowledge. This aim is situated within the challenges of Lifelong Imitation Learning (LIL), where policy networks are prone to catastrophic forgetting.

The motivation for the proposed approach stems from several observations and limitations of existing methods:

1. **Addressing Catastrophic Forgetting and Enabling Scalability:** Robotic systems must be capable of continuous learning in open-world environments. Standard imitation learning paradigms are insufficient for such scenarios due to catastrophic forgetting. The core challenge is to mitigate this forgetting while allowing the agent to continually adapt to new tasks.
2. **Preserving Geometric Structures and Low-Dimensional Manifolds:** The paper highlights that critical task representations, particularly those derived from multimodal inputs (e.g., visual, language, proprioceptive), often reside within low-dimensional manifolds and geometric structures in a high-dimensional feature space. Preserving these intrinsic geometric properties across sequential learning steps is considered crucial for stable knowledge transfer and retention.
3. **Overcoming Limitations of L2-norm Feature Distillation:** Existing distillation methods, such as those relying on L2-norm feature matching in raw feature space, are identified as being sensitive to noise and high-dimensional variability. This sensitivity can prevent them from effectively preserving the intrinsic task manifolds, leading to suboptimal knowledge transfer and potentially rigid representations that impede adaptation to novel skills.
4. **Leveraging Subspace Properties for Robustness and Transferability:** The research is motivated by the understanding that neural representations often concentrate in low-dimensional subspaces. These subspaces are known to capture the most discriminative and transferable structure of task-relevant features and exhibit robustness to noise. By focusing distillation on these principal subspaces, the goal is to extract and transfer the most essential aspects of knowledge, avoiding over-constraining the raw, high-dimensional activations.
5. **Balancing Stability and Plasticity:** A fundamental requirement in lifelong learning is to strike a balance between maintaining stability (retaining past knowledge) and plasticity (adapting to new tasks). Geometry-aware distillation within low-rank subspaces is proposed as a principled mechanism to achieve this balance: retaining the "representational essence" of past tasks while leaving orthogonal directions available for encoding new skills without interference.
6. **Ensuring Robust Behavioral Transfer:** Beyond feature representation alignment, consistent transfer of behavioral policies is necessary. Standard Kullback–Leibler (KL) divergence, when applied to action distributions (e.g., Gaussian Mixture Models), can suffer from variance due to low-probability regions. A motivation is to improve optimization stability by focusing policy distillation on the most reliable and behaviorally meaningful samples from the prior policy, thereby reducing the influence of spurious or uncertain samples.

In summary, the key objective is to develop a lifelong imitation learning framework that explicitly aligns the low-rank subspace representations of consecutive policies and employs a confidence-guided strategy for behavioral policy distillation. This approach aims to achieve superior knowledge transfer, mitigate catastrophic forgetting, and improve generalization across a sequence of robotic manipulation tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, named SP READ (Subspace Representation Distillation), addresses lifelong imitation learning (LIL) by focusing on geometry-preserving knowledge transfer across sequential tasks.

**A. Problem Formulation**
The LIL problem is defined as learning a policy $\pi$ over a sequence of tasks $\{T_1, \dots, T_K\}$, where each task $T_k$ is characterized by an initial state distribution, a goal predicate, and a language description. For each task, the robot receives $N$ expert demonstration trajectories $D_k$, along with a language description $l_k$. The policy is trained via behavioral cloning, minimizing the negative log-likelihood of expert actions. The central challenge is that access to demonstrations from earlier tasks is lost as learning progresses, requiring the policy to adapt to new tasks while preserving knowledge from prior ones.

**B. Overall Architecture**
SP READ integrates two main components: Subspace Representation Distillation and Confidence-guided Policy Distillation, operating within a ResNet-Transformer backbone. The framework processes multimodal inputs including language (instruction), visual (AgentView, HandEye), and proprioceptive (Joint, Gripper) information. For each incremental step $k$, a "teacher" model (from step $k-1$) guides a "student" model (learning task $T_k$).

**C. Subspace Representation Distillation**
This component aims to align the principal subspaces of feature representations between the teacher and student models, rather than performing direct feature matching in the raw feature space.

1. **Principle of SVD for Subspace Identification:** Given a feature matrix $f \in \mathbb{R}^{m \times n}$, its reduced Singular Value Decomposition (SVD) is computed as $f = U \Sigma V^\top$. Here, $U \in \mathbb{R}^{m \times r}$ contains the top-$r$ left singular vectors, which span the dominant $r$-dimensional feature subspace. The projection of $f$ onto this subspace is given by $U U^\top f$, capturing the most informative directions.
2. **Subspace Representation Distillation Loss ($L_{SPREAD}$):** For teacher features $f_t$ and student features $f_s$, SP READ minimizes the discrepancy between their subspace-projected features using the Frobenius norm:
 $L_{SPREAD} = \|U_t U_t^\top f_t - U_s U_s^\top f_s\|_F^2 + \|U_t U_t^\top f_s - U_s U_s^\top f_t\|_F^2$
 where $U_t$ and $U_s$ are the top-$r$ left singular vectors of $f_t$ and $f_s$, respectively. This symmetric formulation encourages both the alignment of the subspace bases ($U_s \approx U_t$) and consistency of feature content within these subspaces. This approach is claimed to be robust to noise and model-specific artifacts, prioritizing principal directions of variation and being invariant to differences in feature magnitude or dimensionality.
3. **Application to Multimodal Features:**
 - **Visual Modalities ($L_{image}$):** For wrist-mounted (HandEye) and overhead (AgentView) camera features, extracted using ResNet18, the $L_{SPREAD}$ is applied. Feature matrices are reshaped into $D \times (NL)$ dimensions (where $D$ is feature dimension, $N$ is batch size, $L$ is sequence length).
 - **Language Modality ($L_{text}$):** Text features, extracted using a CLIP language encoder followed by an MLP, are also aligned using $L_{SPREAD}$.
 - **Extra Modalities ($L_{extra}$):** Proprioceptive signals such as joint angles and gripper state, processed by an MLP, are aligned using $L_{SPREAD}$.

**D. Confidence-guided Policy Distillation**
To ensure consistent retention of behavioral knowledge, the action distributions of the previously learned tasks, modeled as a Gaussian Mixture Model (GMM) policy, are aligned.

1. **Challenge with GMMs:** A closed-form solution for KL divergence between GMMs is intractable. Monte Carlo approximation is typically used, but uniform sampling can introduce variance by incorporating low-probability regions.
2. **Confidence-guided Strategy:** To address this, the method samples actions $\{a_s\}_{s=1}^B \sim \pi_{k-1}$ (from the teacher policy) and selects the top $M$ samples (typically $M = \lfloor 0.9B \rfloor$) with the highest log probabilities $\log \pi_{k-1}(a_s)$.
3. **Policy Distillation Loss ($L_{policy}$):** The distillation loss is then calculated as the mean difference in log probabilities for these confident samples:
 $L_{policy} = \frac{1}{M} \sum_{s \in S_M} [\log \pi_k(a_s) - \log \pi_{k-1}(a_s)]$
 This focuses distillation on statistically reliable and behaviorally meaningful regions, reducing variance and stabilizing optimization.

**E. Final Optimization Objective**
The overall optimization objective for policy $\pi$ at step $k$ combines the behavioral cloning task loss with all modality-specific distillation objectives:
$J(\pi) = \frac{1}{K} \sum_{k=1}^K E_{s_t,a_t \sim D_k \cup \hat{D}_k} \left[ \sum_{t=0}^{L_k} -\log \pi(a_t | (s_t)_{\le t}; T_k) + \lambda_i L_{image} + \lambda_t L_{text} + \lambda_e L_{extra} + \lambda_p L_{policy} \right]$
Here, $\hat{D}_k$ represents memory exemplars sampled from previously learned tasks, and $\lambda$ coefficients balance stability and plasticity.

**F. Training and Implementation Details**
The framework is trained on an NVIDIA H100 GPU using the ResNet-Transformer backbone. Each incremental task is trained for 50 epochs. Regularization weights ($\lambda$ values) are adjusted for different LIBERO task suites (OBJECT, SPATIAL, GOAL) based on task complexity. The subspace rank $r$ is fixed at 48 for all experiments.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments evaluate SP READ against several baselines on the LIBERO benchmark, which includes three task suites: LIBERO-OBJECT, LIBERO-GOAL, and LIBERO-SPATIAL. Performance is measured using Forward Transfer (FWT), Negative Backward Transfer (NBT), and Area Under the Success Rate Curve (AUC).

**A. Comparison to State-of-the-Art (Table I)**
- **Overall Performance:** SP READ achieves the highest or competitive performance across all three LIBERO task suites.
- **LIBERO-OBJECT:** SP READ recorded the highest FWT (81.0% ± 2.0) and AUC (73.0% ± 3.0), while also demonstrating the lowest NBT (8.0% ± 1.0). This represents an improvement of +6% in FWT and +4% in AUC compared to M2Distill.
- **LIBERO-GOAL:** SP READ exhibited a notable margin over prior methods, achieving an FWT of 78.0% ± 1.0 and a low NBT of 9.0% ± 5.0. This is significantly lower than the NBT of LOTUS (30.0% ± 1.0) and M2Distill (20.0% ± 3.0).
- **LIBERO-SPATIAL:** SP READ attained the best AUC (66.0% ± 2.0) with a low NBT (8.0% ± 5.0), outperforming M2Distill by +5% in AUC.

These results indicate that SP READ improves adaptation (FWT) and knowledge retention (NBT) across diverse LIL scenarios.

**B. Success Rate Analysis (Fig. 3)**
- **Consistency:** On LIBERO-OBJECT, SP READ maintained success rates above 70.0% across incremental steps, peaking at 93.0% ± 3.0% at step 2. M2Distill, starting at 93.0% ± 5.0%, dropped to 68.0% ± 4.0% by step 10.
- **Retention:** On LIBERO-GOAL, SP READ sustained rates from 69.0% ± 3.0% to 90.0% ± 5.0%. M2Distill showed a decline from 93.0% ± 5.0% to 55.0% ± 1.0% by step 10. This indicates SP READ's superior consistency and knowledge retention.

**C. Forward Transfer (FWT) Analysis (Fig. 4)**
- SP READ showed robust FWT values, ranging from 63.0% ± 5.0% to 83.0% ± 2.0% on LIBERO-OBJECT. M2Distill's FWT ranged from 60.0% ± 9.0% to 75.0% ± 3.0%.
- On LIBERO-GOAL, SP READ maintained stable FWT from 73.0% ± 1.0% to 83.0% ± 1.0%, while M2Distill ranged from 72.0% ± 1.0% to 80.0% ± 1.0%. This highlights SP READ's consistent superiority in knowledge transfer.

**D. Negative Backward Transfer (NBT) Analysis (Fig. 5)**
- On LIBERO-GOAL, SP READ exhibited NBT values from -4.0 ± 3.0 to 12.0 ± 2.0, indicating minimal forgetting and even a gain at step 2.
- M2Distill showed higher NBT values, ranging from 3.0 ± 2.0 to 23.0 ± 2.0. This demonstrates SP READ's superior stability in reducing forgetting.

**E. Drift Analysis (Fig. 6)**
- SP READ significantly reduced representation drift across language, HandEye, and AgentView embedding spaces compared to M2Distill.
- For language embeddings, SP READ reduced drift by over 75%.
- In visual modalities, SP READ suppressed large drift spikes observed in M2Distill, keeping HandEye drift below 0.5 (compared to M2Distill's peak over 2.7) and AgentView mean drift at 0.095 (against 0.356 for M2Distill). This indicates more effective preservation of feature stability and mitigation of catastrophic forgetting.

**F. Ablation Study: Influence of Each Loss Component (Table II)**
- **Image Loss ($L_{image}$):** Its removal caused a substantial degradation in performance (AUC dropped from 0.72 to 0.57, NBT increased from 0.09 to 0.20), indicating its critical role.
- **Action Loss ($L_{policy}$):** Exclusion led to decreased FWT (0.78 to 0.70), suggesting its importance for transferable policies.
- **Text Loss ($L_{text}$):** Removal resulted in a minor AUC reduction (0.72 to 0.69), indicating auxiliary benefits.
- **Extra Modality Loss ($L_{extra}$):** Exclusion caused a minor decline in FWT and AUC, confirming its complementary role.
- **Conclusion:** Image feature alignment emerged as the most influential factor.

**G. Ablation Study: Dimensionality of Subspace Representation Distillation (Table III)**
- **Optimal Rank:** A 75%-rank configuration (r=48) achieved the best overall performance (AUC 72.0% ± 1.0, FWT 78.0% ± 1.0), outperforming full-rank by 5% in AUC and 7% in NBT. This suggests an optimal balance between retaining information and mitigating redundancy.
- **Full-Rank:** While competitive in FWT, it suffered from higher forgetting and lower AUC.
- **Lower Ranks (r=32, 16):** Further reduction in rank led to degraded FWT and AUC, indicating information loss.

**H. Ablation Study: Policy Distillation with Top-M Confident Samples (Fig. 7)**
- **Confidence-based Strategy:** The method using top-M = $\lfloor 0.9B \rfloor$ confident samples yielded the best results (AUC of 71.0% ± 1.0, FWT of 78.0% ± 1.0, and NBT of 9.0% ± 5.0).
- **Comparison:** This outperformed standard KL divergence using all samples (by 5% in AUC and 7% in NBT) and a variant with M = $\lfloor 0.8B \rfloor$ samples. This validates the effectiveness of prioritizing reliable action distributions.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents SP READ, a framework designed to enhance lifelong imitation learning (LIL) in robotic manipulation tasks. The significance and potential impact of this work can be summarized as follows:

1. **Advancement in Lifelong Imitation Learning:** SP READ contributes to addressing the fundamental challenge of catastrophic forgetting in LIL. By introducing a geometry-preserving framework, it offers a method for robotic agents to continuously acquire new skills from demonstrations while substantially retaining previously learned knowledge. This is a step towards more adaptable and versatile robotic systems.
2. **Theoretical Justification for Subspace Alignment:** The work provides theoretical justification for why aligning representations at the subspace level, derived through Singular Value Decomposition (SVD), is more effective than traditional L2-norm-based feature matching. By focusing on low-rank projections, the method aims to capture the intrinsic geometric structures and principal directions of variation in multimodal task features. This approach is claimed to offer robustness to noise and model-specific artifacts, which is a notable improvement over prior distillation techniques.
3. **Improved Knowledge Transfer and Reduced Forgetting:** The experimental results demonstrate that SP READ significantly outperforms state-of-the-art baselines like M2Distill across various LIL benchmarks. The consistent improvements in Forward Transfer (FWT) and Area Under the Success Rate Curve (AUC), coupled with a substantial reduction in Negative Backward Transfer (NBT), indicate more effective knowledge transfer to new tasks and superior retention of old skills. This directly translates to more reliable and persistent learning in sequential task environments.
4. **Enhanced Robustness through Confidence-Guided Distillation:** The integration of a confidence-guided policy distillation strategy provides a mechanism for more stable behavioral transfer. By prioritizing high-confidence action samples from the teacher policy, the method aims to reduce variance in distillation and prevent misleading gradients from uncertain data. This contributes to the overall stability and reliability of the learning process.
5. **Practical Implications for Robotics:** The ability of SP READ to mitigate catastrophic forgetting and preserve intrinsic task manifolds has practical implications for developing robotic systems capable of operating in dynamic, unstructured environments. Such robots could learn a diverse repertoire of skills over time, adapting to new tasks without necessitating retraining from scratch or extensive memory replay of all past experiences. This could lead to more autonomous and general-purpose robots in manufacturing, logistics, or service industries.
6. **Broader Impact on Continual Learning:** While focused on imitation learning, the principles of subspace representation distillation and geometry-aware knowledge transfer could be applicable to other domains of continual learning beyond robotics, where preserving intrinsic data structures across evolving tasks is crucial.

Future research directions, such as applying SP READ to long-horizon incremental task learning and extended task sequences, suggest a potential path towards even greater real-world adaptability for robotic agents.
