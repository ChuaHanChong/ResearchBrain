# ReVision: Refining Video Diffusion with Explicit 3D Motion Modeling

> [!summary] Summary
> The ReVision framework enhances video diffusion by integrating explicit parameterized 3D motion knowledge into pretrained models through a three-stage pipeline. This approach significantly improves motion consistency, realism, and control in generated videos, achieving an 83.15% "Dynamic Degree" on the VBench++ benchmark compared to 43.17% for SVD-XT-1.1, and delivering state-of-the-art performance on dance tasks while maintaining competitive inference speeds.

## Problem

- Current video diffusion models struggle with consistently generating complex, physically plausible motions and coherent object interactions.
- Scaling model size and data volume alone has not fully resolved the challenges of capturing intricate real-world dynamics in video generation.
- Specialized human animation models use strong motion guidance (e.g., 2D keypoint trajectories) that are generally unavailable for broader, general video generation tasks.

## Method

- The **ReVision** framework implements a three-stage pipeline (Extract–Optimize–Reinforce) that explicitly incorporates parameterized 3D model knowledge into a pretrained conditional video generation model.
- It fine-tunes a base ==Stable Video Diffusion== (SVD) model to accept additional conditioning channels for part-level segmentation masks and confidence maps derived from 3D motion sequences.
- The pipeline involves generating a 'coarse' video, extracting and optimizing 3D motion parameters (e.g., using SMPL-X/SMAL models or 2.5D representations with a Parameterized Motion Prior model), and then regenerating the video with the refined 3D motion as strong conditioning.

## Results

- ReVision-SVD surpassed SVD-XT-**1.1** on VBench++'s "Dynamic Degree" (83.15% vs. 43.17%) and outperformed baselines like MotionClone and ImageConductor on the DAVIS dataset for motion transfer tasks (CoTracker mIoU of **0.80** vs. **0.74**).
- User studies demonstrated ReVision-SVD's preference over SVD and the **13B**-parameter HunyuanVideo in terms of motion consistency, realism, and handling object occlusions, despite ReVision-SVD having significantly fewer parameters (**1.5B**).
- The framework achieved state-of-the-art performance on the TikTok Dancing dataset with an FVD of **121.26** (compared to **152.97** for VividPose) and enabled smooth, consistent long video generation (e.g., 128 frames) through 3D motion optimization.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly modeling and refining object-centric 3D motion, rather than relying solely on implicit 2D learning, is crucial for achieving high-fidelity, consistent, and controllable complex movements in generated videos.
> - A multi-stage approach that first generates a coarse video and then refines its underlying 3D motion before a final generation pass can significantly enhance video quality and motion coherence without extensive retraining of the base model.
> - The Parameterized Motion Prior (PMP) component effectively denoises and optimizes extracted 3D motion sequences, thereby making the overall framework robust to initial motion inaccuracies and enabling smooth, consistent long video generation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Video generation, particularly through diffusion models, has undergone advancements, resulting in high-quality and temporally coherent videos. These developments have been influenced by improvements in model architectures, increased model complexity often involving billions of parameters, and the availability of large, high-quality datasets. Despite these progressions, existing models encounter difficulties in generating videos that accurately reflect complex motions, realistic physical principles, precise fine-grained motion control, and coherent object interactions.

Research indicates that merely scaling up model size and data volume may not fully address the intricacies of real-world motion generation. In parallel, human image animation models, despite typically being smaller and trained on less data, demonstrate proficiency in producing consistent and precise video outputs with complex motions. This is often achieved by integrating predefined motion priors, such as 2D keypoint trajectories, which appear to simplify the learning complexity for generative models.

However, the applicability of these strong, dense motion guidance signals is often limited in general video generation tasks where such explicit ground-truth information is typically unavailable. This paper addresses this gap by proposing a framework that leverages implicit motion information extracted from initially generated videos to enhance video quality. The work aims to integrate explicit parameterized 3D motion knowledge into conditional video generation models, thereby improving the generation of complex motions and interactions across various subjects, including humans, animals, and general objects, within a plug-and-play setup for pretrained models.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from persistent limitations in current video generation models:
- **Challenges in Realism and Control:** Existing video diffusion models, despite their success in visual quality and temporal coherence, frequently struggle to adhere to realistic physical principles. This leads to difficulties in consistently achieving fine-grained motion control, complex movements, and coherent interactions between objects within generated videos.
- **Limitations of Scaling:** Empirical observations suggest that increasing model size and dataset volume alone is insufficient to fully capture the complexities of real-world dynamics and motion. This indicates a need for alternative or complementary approaches beyond brute-force scaling.
- **Inspiration from Specialized Models:** Human image animation models, which often employ smaller architectures and less data, achieve precise and consistent motion by incorporating well-defined motion priors (e.g., 2D keypoint trajectories). This success suggests that explicit motion guidance can reduce the learning burden for generative models.
- **Gap in General Video Generation:** The strong motion guidance signals used in specialized animation tasks are typically not available for broader, general video generation scenarios, limiting the direct transferability of these techniques.

Based on these motivations, the key objectives of the ReVision framework are:
- To introduce a simple, general, and plug-and-play framework that explicitly integrates parameterized 3D model knowledge into a pretrained conditional video generation model.
- To enhance the model's capability to generate high-quality videos that exhibit complex motions and interactions, applicable to humans, animals, and general objects.
- To leverage implicit motion information by extracting it from an initial, coarsely generated video and subsequently refining it.
- To re-introduce this refined 3D motion sequence as additional conditioning to guide the video generation process, aiming for improved motion consistency and realism.
- To achieve these enhancements without requiring extensive retraining of the base video diffusion model, thereby preserving its original capacity for generating visual appearance.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The ReVision framework operates as a three-stage pipeline (Extract–Optimize–Reinforce) designed to integrate explicit 3D motion knowledge into a pretrained conditional video generation model. The base model utilized is an extended version of Stable Video Diffusion (SVD).

#### 3.1. Motion-Conditioned Video Generation

The initial SVD model is adapted to accept additional motion conditions. This is achieved by fine-tuning SVD with minimal architectural modifications, primarily limited to its first convolutional block and temporal layers. Two additional conditioning channels are concatenated to the original input: a part-level segmentation mask derived from 3D motion sequences, and a corresponding confidence map indicating the mask's reliability. The fine-tuning process incorporates three distinct scenarios to accommodate varied levels of motion guidance during training:
1. **Full Motion Sequence (40% of training data):** When a complete motion sequence is available, part-level masks are generated by projecting 3D parametric mesh models. A confidence score of 1 is assigned, reflecting dense and precise control.
2. **Target Pose Only (30% of training data):** If only a target pose (e.g., for the final frame) is provided, projected part segmentation masks are converted into polygons, mirroring user-input sketches. A confidence score of 0.5 is assigned due to potential errors introduced by polygon conversion.
3. **No Motion Conditioning (30% of training data):** To maintain the base model's ability to generate videos without explicit motion guidance, an empty part mask with a confidence score of 0 is provided.

#### 3.2. Proposed Method: ReVision Pipeline

The ReVision pipeline consists of three sequential stages:

- **Stage 1: Coarse Video Generation (S1):**
 - The fine-tuned SVD model generates an initial, "coarse" video. This generation is conditioned on the first frame and an optional user-specified target motion (e.g., a target pose for the final frame) or no motion guidance.
 - The term "coarse" reflects that, without a complete motion sequence, the initial video may exhibit limited motion quality.
 - This stage is foundational, leveraging the diffusion model's learned priors to establish rich motion patterns, object interactions, and camera movements.
 - To enhance efficiency, this stage can be executed at a lower resolution, with fewer frames, and with fewer denoising steps, significantly reducing computational overhead (e.g., from 36 to 8 seconds).

- **Stage 2: Object-Centric 3D Optimization (S2):**
 - **Feature Extraction:** From the coarse video, 3D information is parameterized.
 - For humans, the SMPL-X parametric model is used.
 - For animals, the SMAL parametric model is used.
 - For general objects, a 2.5D representation is constructed using 2D bounding boxes, segmentation masks, and estimated depth. Key 2D points (from mask contours, bounding box corners/center) are lifted into 3D space using depth estimation, forming a compact point-based representation.
 - **Parameterized Motion Prior Model (PMP):** The extracted 3D parameters often suffer from instability due to the coarse video quality. PMP refines these 3D motion sequences.
 - PMP takes text embeddings (from a pretrained CLIP encoder) and "motion strength" (derived from differences in 3D model parameters between frames) as conditioning inputs.
 - It employs a transformer architecture, utilizing self-attention, cross-attention with conditioning inputs, and feedforward networks to iteratively refine the motion sequence.
 - PMP is trained to denoise perturbations (e.g., noise, shuffling, segment dropping) applied to ground-truth motion sequences from the annotated Panda-70M dataset.
 - The optimized 3D parameterized motion sequences are then converted into 3D mesh sequences and projected back into 2D as refined part segmentation masks and confidence maps, providing enhanced motion guidance.

- **Stage 3: Fine-grained Video Generation (S3):**
 - The same SVD model used in Stage 1 is re-employed.
 - Instead of limited or no motion information, the full, refined 3D motion sequence (in the form of part masks and confidence maps) from Stage 2 is provided as a strong conditioning input.
 - This re-conditioning guides the model to regenerate the video, resulting in improved motion consistency, coherence, and visual realism compared to the coarse video.

**Dataset and Training:**
A custom dataset of 20,000 videos from Panda-70M was annotated for this work. This dataset includes frame-wise 2D bounding boxes, semantic masks, depth maps, and 3D parametric mesh reconstructions for detected humans and animals. Annotation involved a suite of off-the-shelf models for object detection, segmentation, depth estimation, 3D body/hand/animal pose and shape estimation, and camera estimation, followed by rigorous filtering based on IoU and PSNR scores to ensure annotation quality. The SVD model was fine-tuned for 300,000 iterations on this annotated dataset.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research provides both quantitative and qualitative evidence for the effectiveness of the ReVision framework:

- **General Video Generation Performance:**
 - On the VBench++ benchmark, ReVision-SVD consistently surpassed SVD-XT-1.1, particularly in "Dynamic Degree" (83.15% vs. 43.17%), while maintaining performance in consistency, smoothness, and quality metrics. ReVision-Wan2.1 also showed similar improvements over its base.
 - Comparisons against recent state-of-the-art text+image-to-video (TI2V) models indicated that ReVision achieves competitive or superior performance, especially in generating complex and controllable motions, which TI2V models often struggle with despite textual conditioning.

- **Motion-Conditioned Video Generation:**
 - On the DAVIS dataset for motion-transfer image-to-video tasks, ReVision-SVD outperformed baseline methods (MotionClone, ImageConductor, Go-with-the-Flow) across metrics such as CoTracker mIoU (0.80 for ReVision-SVD vs. 0.74 for the best baseline), optical flow error, pixel MSE, and various consistency and smoothness measures. This indicates more accurate motion transfer while preserving visual fidelity.

- **User Studies:**
 - A user study comparing ReVision-SVD with baseline SVD and HunyuanVideo (a 13B-parameter model) evaluated "Motion Consistency," "Amount of Motion," and "Motion Realism." ReVision-SVD demonstrated user preference over both SVD and HunyuanVideo, suggesting its capability in generating complex motions and interactions.
 - An additional user study focusing on occlusion and interaction handling revealed that ReVision-SVD was preferred over SVD (97.63% preference) and HunyuanVideo (63.99% preference) in scenarios involving object occlusions. This is attributed to the framework's 3D motion optimization, which helps resolve ambiguities inherent in 2D space.

- **Inference Speed:**
 - ReVision-SVD demonstrated an average inference time of 49 seconds for a 32-frame video (8s for Stage 1, 5s for Stage 2, 36s for Stage 3). This is comparable to the 36 seconds of the base SVD and significantly faster than HunyuanVideo (411 seconds), despite ReVision-SVD having fewer parameters (1.5B vs. 13B).

- **Long Video Generation:**
 - The PMP component's ability to optimize motion in a 3D parameterized space enables smooth interpolation and extrapolation, allowing for the generation of longer video sequences (e.g., extending 32-frame motions to 128 frames) with consistent motion through overlapping clip stitching.

- **Complex Motion Generation (Dance Task):**
 - When applied to the TikTok Dancing dataset, ReVision (using full motion sequences) achieved state-of-the-art performance against human image animation methods (MagicAnimate, Animate Anyone, Champ, VividPose) across PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS, and FVD. This included a notable improvement in FVD (121.26 vs. 152.97 for VividPose). Even without ground-truth motion sequences (using only an image or target pose), ReVision achieved competitive FVD scores.

- **Parameterized Motion Prior (PMP) Effectiveness:**
 - PMP was shown to improve object and motion consistency and reduce morphological failure rates in generated videos (e.g., from 83.5% to 14.3% morphological failure rate in a user study).
 - PMP acts as a general motion denoiser, capable of recovering smooth and coherent motion sequences even from initial coarse videos with significant motion errors, thereby preventing error accumulation in the pipeline.
 - When integrated with MoMask, a state-of-the-art human motion generation method, PMP enhanced its performance on standard benchmarks like HumanML3D and KIT-ML, indicating its broad utility in refining motion sequences.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The ReVision framework addresses a critical limitation in current video generation research: the challenge of consistently producing videos with complex, physically plausible, and controllable motions and interactions. By explicitly incorporating parameterized 3D motion knowledge, the work offers a method that goes beyond merely scaling model size and data, which has been shown to be insufficient for these complex tasks.

The significance of ReVision lies in several aspects:
- **Enhanced Motion Fidelity and Control:** The framework enables pre-trained video diffusion models to generate videos with significantly improved motion fidelity, coherence, and fine-grained control, which has been a persistent challenge. This is achieved for a range of subjects, including humans, animals, and general objects.
- **Plug-and-Play Adaptability:** ReVision functions as a plug-and-play solution, enhancing existing pre-trained video diffusion models (such as SVD) without requiring their complete re-training from scratch. This makes the approach adaptable and efficient for integrating 3D awareness into current generative pipelines.
- **Computational Efficiency:** Despite its multi-stage architecture, ReVision demonstrates competitive or superior performance in motion quality compared to much larger, state-of-the-art models (e.g., HunyuanVideo), while operating with significantly fewer parameters and faster inference times. The optimization strategies for the coarse video generation stage contribute to this efficiency.
- **Robustness to Ambiguity and Occlusion:** The explicit 3D motion modeling allows the framework to reason about object spatial relationships, which contributes to robust handling of occlusions and interactions that are often ambiguous in 2D-only processing. The Parameterized Motion Prior (PMP) further enhances robustness by refining inconsistent motion sequences.
- **Foundation for Complex Dynamics:** The approach suggests that optimizing object-centric 3D knowledge provides a promising direction for future advancements in generative models, particularly for tasks requiring a deep understanding of dynamic and physical interactions.
- **Broad Applications:** The capabilities of ReVision could have implications across various fields. In creative industries, it could support artists and designers by facilitating the generation of high-fidelity animated content. In research, it offers potential for creating realistic synthetic datasets, which could reduce the need for manual annotation in training other vision models.

The paper acknowledges potential societal impacts, including the risk of misuse for generating deepfakes or other malicious content, underscoring the necessity for ethical considerations and safeguards as video generation technologies advance.
