# λ: A Benchmark for Data-Efficiency in Long-Horizon Indoor Mobile Manipulation Robotics

> [!summary] Summary
> The LAMBDA benchmark was developed to evaluate the data efficiency of models performing language-conditioned, long-horizon indoor mobile manipulation (MoMa) tasks, utilizing both simulated and real-world quadruped robot data. It reveals the significant data inefficiency of current learning-based approaches and highlights the superior zero-shot performance of neuro-symbolic systems.

## Problem

- Existing mobile manipulation (MoMa) models require vast amounts of demonstration data, posing a significant barrier to real-world deployment due to high collection costs.
- Current MoMa benchmarks inadequately assess data efficiency, often relying on unrealistically large datasets, templated instructions, or lacking support for long-horizon and multi-floor tasks.
- Many existing benchmarks do not include real-world data from quadruped robots, which are crucial for navigating complex and uneven indoor environments.

## Method

- Introduces **LAMBDA (λ)**, a ==data-efficiency benchmark== with a manageable dataset of 571 human-collected demonstrations for ==language-conditioned, long-horizon mobile manipulation (MoMa)== pick-and-place tasks across multiple rooms and floors.
- Incorporates diverse simulated environments (==AI2THOR==) and complex real-world settings using a Boston Dynamics ==Spot quadruped robot== equipped with a manipulator.
- Tasks are specified by ==free-form natural language instructions== and success is evaluated via four sequential subtasks (==navigate, grasp, transport, place==) for detailed per-stage performance assessment.

## Results

- End-to-end Behavior Cloning models (RT-1, MG-MoMa) achieved low average success rates, ranging from **2.44%** to **5.1%** across scene and task generalization tests, barely outperforming a random agent.
- The neuro-symbolic LIMP system achieved a zero-shot average success rate of **44.4%** on unseen tasks, outperforming learning-based baselines by nearly **an order of magnitude** and demonstrating superior data efficiency.
- Ablation studies showed that while increasing dataset size marginally improved RT-1's performance, the Mamba-based RM-1 consistently outperformed RT-1 across all data subsets (from the **571-demo** budget down through smaller subsets), suggesting architectural benefits for data efficiency.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Learning-based Behavior Cloning (BC) models, such as RT-1 and MG-MoMa, exhibit substantial data inefficiency for long-horizon mobile manipulation, struggling to generalize effectively from limited data to unseen scenes or tasks.
> - Neuro-symbolic approaches, exemplified by the LIMP system, demonstrate significantly higher data efficiency in a zero-shot setting by integrating semantic mapping, robust motion planning, and multimodal foundation models.
> - Architectural choices can influence data efficiency, with a Mamba-based architecture (RM-1) showing consistent performance advantages over the transformer-based RT-1 across varying dataset sizes within the benchmark.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of robotic systems capable of performing long-horizon mobile manipulation (MoMa) tasks in complex indoor environments, such as homes and workplaces, represents a significant frontier in robotics. Current progress in this domain is often hampered by the data inefficiency of existing models, which typically require vast quantities of demonstrations for effective learning. Collecting such large-scale datasets is resource-intensive, demanding substantial time, labor, and infrastructure.

Within this landscape, a critical gap exists in the evaluation methodologies: current MoMa benchmarks largely fail to adequately assess models for data efficiency. Many existing benchmarks either rely on unrealistically large datasets, lack natural language conditioning, offer only templated instructions rather than free-form natural language, omit real-world data, or provide planner-generated trajectories that lack human variability. Furthermore, a substantial number of benchmarks do not support long-horizon tasks involving navigation across multiple rooms or floors, nor do they feature real quadruped data, which is crucial for traversing complex and uneven indoor terrains.

This work, by introducing the LAMBDA (λ) benchmark, directly addresses these limitations. LAMBDA aims to provide a more realistic and challenging evaluation platform by focusing on data efficiency. It does so by offering a manageable dataset size, incorporating language-conditioned tasks with free-form human instructions, encompassing long-horizon multi-room and multi-floor pick-and-place scenarios, and including human-collected demonstrations from both simulated and real-world environments, specifically utilizing a quadruped robot for real-world data collection. By targeting these specific deficiencies in existing benchmarks, LAMBDA seeks to accelerate the development of more data-efficient and robust MoMa systems that can generalize effectively from limited examples, thereby making advanced robotics more practical for real-world deployment.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to introduce LAMBDA (λ), a novel benchmark specifically designed to evaluate the data efficiency of models on language-conditioned, long-horizon indoor mobile manipulation (MoMa) tasks. The benchmark aims to provide a realistic and challenging environment for the development and assessment of advanced robotic capabilities.

The key motivations driving the creation of LAMBDA are multifaceted:

1. **Addressing Data Inefficiency in MoMa:** Existing MoMa models predominantly rely on large-scale datasets, which are costly and time-consuming to acquire. This data inefficiency is a significant barrier to the widespread deployment of robots in household and workplace settings. LAMBDA is motivated by the need for benchmarks that can assess models' ability to achieve robust performance with a more realistically sized dataset, thereby fostering the development of algorithms that require fewer demonstrations.

2. **Bridging Gaps in Existing Benchmarks:** A comprehensive review of current MoMa benchmarks reveals several critical limitations:
 - **Lack of Data Efficiency Focus:** Most benchmarks utilize unrealistically large datasets, precluding the evaluation of data efficiency.
 - **Limited Natural Language Understanding:** Many benchmarks lack natural language annotations, or restrict instructions to fixed templates rather than flexible, free-form commands. This limits the ability of robots to interact intuitively with human users.
 - **Insufficient Long-Horizon Task Support:** The majority of existing benchmarks do not encompass long-horizon tasks requiring navigation across multiple rooms or floors, which are common in practical indoor scenarios.
 - **Absence of Real-World Variability:** Many benchmarks rely solely on simulated data or planner-generated trajectories, which often lack the natural variability and nuances of human-collected demonstrations or real-world physical constraints.
 - **Limited Quadrupedal Robotics Data:** Very few benchmarks include real-world data from quadruped robots, despite their suitability for navigating complex and uneven terrains, particularly in multi-floor environments. LAMBDA aims to specifically include this embodiment.

3. **Promoting Realistic and Diverse Task Evaluation:** The motivation extends to ensuring the benchmark tasks reflect real-world user expectations. By employing crowdsourcing for free-form language instructions, LAMBDA seeks to capture the natural diversity of human commands for household and workplace tasks. The inclusion of both simulated and real-world human-collected demonstrations aims to provide a robust evaluation platform that can prepare models for practical deployment.

4. **Establishing Baseline Performance:** The authors are motivated to establish initial baseline performances for various MoMa systems, including current end-to-end learning methods (Behavior Cloning) and modular neuro-symbolic approaches. This aims to provide a reference point for future research and to identify promising directions for developing more data-efficient MoMa methodologies.

In summary, the key objective is to provide a benchmark that accurately reflects the challenges of deploying robots in dynamic, human-centric environments, with a particular emphasis on data efficiency, realistic task complexity, and intuitive human-robot interaction through natural language.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed in this research centers on the design, creation, and evaluation of the LAMBDA (λ) benchmark. This involves a multi-faceted approach encompassing dataset collection, task definition, and rigorous evaluation of baseline models.

#### 3.1. LAMBDA Benchmark Design

LAMBDA is structured to address the identified gaps in MoMa benchmarks, focusing on data efficiency for language-conditioned, long-horizon, multi-room, and multi-floor pick-and-place tasks.

- **Task Definition:** Tasks involve fetching and placing objects, requiring navigation across rooms and potentially floors. These are specified by free-form natural language instructions, rather than templated commands, to reflect real-world human communication variability.
- **Data Size for Data Efficiency:** The benchmark comprises a manageable dataset of 571 human-collected demonstrations (521 simulated, 50 real-world). This size is intentionally chosen to challenge models to learn efficiently from limited data, directly evaluating data efficiency.
- **Human-Collected Demonstrations:** Trajectories are collected by humans to ensure natural variability and expertise, making them replay-verifiable. This contrasts with planner-generated data, which may lack realism.
- **Environment Diversity:** The benchmark includes tasks in both simulated and real-world environments.
 - **Simulated:** Uses AI2THOR environments (specifically RoboTHOR and ManipulaTHOR) to offer diverse scenes, object types, and layouts across 20 rooms in 5 distinct environments. ManipulaTHOR provides fine manipulation capabilities for an arm.
 - **Real-World:** Tasks are set in 11 unique rooms across 3 distinct large-scale environments (a laboratory, a university building floor, and two adjacent floors connected by stairs), including kitchens, lobbies, and classrooms. This provides complex terrains and realistic workplace scenarios.
- **Robot Embodiment:** A simulated robot with a manipulator is used for the simulated tasks. For real-world tasks, a Boston Dynamics Spot quadruped robot equipped with a manipulator is utilized due to its stability and adaptability for navigating complex terrains and multi-floor environments.

#### 3.2. Dataset Collection

The collection process was carefully managed to ensure the quality, diversity, and realism of the demonstrations and instructions.

- **Simulated Dataset:**
 - **Instructions:** 521 free-form natural language commands were crowdsourced from 41 participants via Prolific, reflecting tasks humans would naturally assign to robots.
 - **Teleoperation:** A custom keyboard-based teleoperation system was used to control the ManipulaTHOR robot within the AI2THOR simulator, executing the crowdsourced commands.
 - **Data Attributes:** Each trajectory records RGB-D egocentric observations, segmentations, poses of the robot body, end-effector, and grasped objects, as well as discrete actions.
 - **Verification:** All collected trajectories were replay-verified to confirm their correctness and quality.
- **Real-World Dataset:**
 - **Instructions:** 50 natural language commands were provided by 7 participants, specifically tailored for room-to-room and floor-to-floor tasks within the real environments.
 - **Teleoperation:** A Spot quadruped robot was teleoperated using a tablet with thumbsticks to execute these commands in the real-world settings.
 - **Data Rate:** Trajectories were collected at 3 Hz.

#### 3.3. Evaluation and Baselines

The benchmark was evaluated using task success rate, where each task is broken down into four sequential subtasks (navigate, grasp, transport, place). Each successful subtask contributes one point, with a maximum of four points per task, and the overall success rate is an average of these scores.

- **Experiment Types:**
 - **Scene Generalization:** Assesses models' ability to perform tasks in entirely unseen environments (different layouts, objects). A 5-fold cross-validation approach was used on the simulation dataset, holding out one scene for testing in each fold, and training on the remaining four.
 - **Task Generalization:** Evaluates models' ability to adapt to new tasks within already seen environments. The simulation dataset was split into 80% training, 10% validation, and 10% testing across all five scenes.
- **Baseline Models:**
 - **Behavior Cloning (BC) Models:**
 - **RT-1 (Robotics Transformer):** A state-of-the-art MoMa BC model. Adapted for the ManipulaTHOR robot's action space (discrete base navigation, discretized continuous deltas for rotation/manipulation). Evaluated with and without pre-trained parameters (fine-tuning).
 - **MotionGlot-MoMa (MG-MoMa):** An adaptation of MotionGlot, a multi-embodiment motion generation model. It learns a discrete latent codebook for observations using a VQ-VAE and tokenizes actions, integrating these with GPT-2 text vocabulary for action generation. Evaluated with and without pre-trained parameters (fine-tuning).
 - **Neuro-Symbolic System:**
 - **LIMP (Language Instruction grounding for Motion Planning):** A system integrating Linear Temporal Logic (LTL), a Large Language Model (LLM), a Vision-Language Model (VLM), and Task and Motion Planning (TAMP) algorithms. It translates natural language into LTL formulas and TAMP plans. LIMP was evaluated *zero-shot*, meaning it received no training on robot demonstrations. Its evaluation was conducted on real-world environments, using a mix of simulation-designed and real-world LAMBDA tasks.
 - **Random Agent:** A baseline that performs actions by uniform sampling, providing a lower bound on performance.

#### 3.4. Ablation Studies

Two ablation studies were conducted to understand factors influencing data efficiency:

- **Impact of Dataset Size:** Performance of RT-1, RL-1 (RT-1 with LSTM), and RM-1 (RT-1 with Mamba) was assessed on progressively larger subsets (25%, 50%, 75%, 100%) of the simulation dataset under the Task Generalization setup, measuring the average rate of change (RoC) in performance.
- **Impact of Architecture:** RT-1's transformer architecture was replaced with an LSTM (creating RL-1) and a Mamba architecture (creating RM-1). These were evaluated similarly to RT-1 across varying dataset sizes to compare their data efficiency characteristics.

This comprehensive methodology allows for a robust evaluation of data efficiency in MoMa, offering insights into the performance of different robotic paradigms.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of the LAMBDA benchmark yielded several key findings regarding the data efficiency and performance of different mobile manipulation systems.

#### 4.1. Generalization Performance of Learning-Based Models (Behavior Cloning)

- **Scene Generalization (Table III):** End-to-end learning-based models, RT-1 and MG-MoMa, exhibited low success rates when required to generalize to unseen scenes. RT-1 achieved an average success rate of 2.7%, while MG-MoMa reached 2.44%. Both models marginally outperformed the random agent (0.3% success rate). The performance varied significantly across scenes, with higher rates observed in scenes where initial object positions simplified navigation (e.g., Scenes 1 and 3 for RT-1), and lower rates in more complex, isolated, or object-dense scenes (e.g., Scene 2 for RT-1). These results indicate substantial challenges for these models in generalizing to novel environments with different layouts and object placements given the provided dataset size.
- **Task Generalization (Table IV):** Performance improved slightly under task generalization, where models were evaluated on new tasks within seen environments. RT-1 achieved 5.1% on unseen tasks and 4.6% on seen tasks. MG-MoMa recorded 4.2% on unseen tasks and 4.6% on seen tasks. This modest improvement is attributed to the larger training dataset size and exposure to all scenes during training, but overall success rates remained low, highlighting the inherent difficulty of the long-horizon tasks and the data inefficiency of these BC approaches.
- **Impact of Fine-Tuning (Table IV):** Fine-tuning RT-1 and MG-MoMa with pretrained parameters showed limited improvement. RT-1's fine-tuned performance was 4.6% on unseen tasks, similar to its performance without fine-tuning, suggesting no significant overfitting but also limited benefit from pre-existing MoMa knowledge on LAMBDA tasks. MG-MoMa, when fine-tuned, showed slightly lower performance on seen tasks (3.7%) compared to unseen tasks (4.5%), indicating that its pretrained parameters might have introduced biases that were not fully conducive to LAMBDA's specific challenges.

#### 4.2. Neuro-Symbolic System Performance (LIMP)

- **Zero-Shot Performance (Table IV):** The neuro-symbolic system, LIMP, achieved a significantly higher average success rate of 44.4% on unseen tasks, evaluated zero-shot (without any robot demonstration training). This performance vastly surpassed the learning-based BC models (RT-1 and MG-MoMa), demonstrating its superior data efficiency for LAMBDA's long-horizon MoMa tasks.
- **Factors Contributing to LIMP's Success:**
 - **Semantic Mapping:** LIMP's ability to generate instruction-conditioned semantic maps provides precise spatial information about objects and regions, eliminating the need for extensive visual searching and enabling robust interpretation of language commands.
 - **Robust Motion Planning:** The integration of robust motion planning algorithms for navigation provides resilience against covariate shift, a common challenge for learning-based methods in large, dynamic observation and state spaces.
 - **Foundation Model Integration:** LIMP leverages multimodal foundation models for world knowledge and zero-shot reasoning, allowing it to interpret semantic and contextual complexities of tasks without task-specific training.
- **Identified Limitations of LIMP:** Despite its strong performance, LAMBDA revealed specific limitations in LIMP:
 - **Object-Centric Bias:** LIMP demonstrated a lack of explicit understanding of broader spatial constructs like "rooms," showing an object-centric bias (e.g., capable of navigating to a "cup" but not directly to a "kitchen").
 - **Absence of Starting Location Notion:** The system lacked the ability to interpret instructions requiring a return to the robot's point of origin.
 - **Perceptual Limitations:** Its reliance on Vision-Language Models (VLMs) meant its performance was constrained by the VLM's ability to accurately recognize and locate target objects, leading to some failures.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

- **Impact of Dataset Size (Figure 4):** As the size of the training dataset increased from 25% to 100% of the simulation data, RT-1 showed a positive, albeit weak, upward trend in performance. This indicates that while more data generally improves RT-1's performance, the model remains data-inefficient for LAMBDA's complex tasks, suggesting that substantial performance gains would require even larger datasets. This aligns with observed scaling laws in other machine learning domains.
- **Impact of Architecture (Figure 4):**
 - RL-1 (RT-1 with an LSTM) exhibited a similar upward trend and average rate of change to the transformer-based RT-1, suggesting that simply replacing the transformer with an LSTM did not significantly improve data efficiency.
 - RM-1 (RT-1 with a Mamba architecture) maintained a relatively constant performance trend across varying data subsets but consistently outperformed RT-1 across all data sizes. This finding suggests that architectural choices can contribute to data efficiency, with Mamba showing potential advantages over the standard transformer in this context.

In summary, the results highlight the significant data inefficiency of current end-to-end learning-based models for complex, long-horizon MoMa tasks. In contrast, neuro-symbolic approaches, by integrating robust planning and foundation models, demonstrate substantially higher data efficiency, presenting a promising direction for future research in this field.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The LAMBDA (λ) benchmark carries substantial significance for the advancement of mobile manipulation robotics, with potential impacts across research and practical applications.

#### 5.1. Addressing a Critical Research Gap

LAMBDA directly addresses a long-standing challenge in robotics: the lack of benchmarks specifically designed to evaluate data efficiency in complex, language-conditioned, long-horizon mobile manipulation tasks. By providing a manageable dataset of human-collected demonstrations in diverse simulated and real-world environments (including quadruped robots), it fills a crucial void that current large-scale or less realistic benchmarks do not cover. This allows researchers to focus on developing algorithms that can learn effectively from fewer examples, which is essential for scaling robotic deployment in real-world settings where data collection is expensive and time-consuming.

#### 5.2. Guiding Model Development and Architectural Choices

The empirical results, particularly the stark contrast between the performance of end-to-end Behavior Cloning (BC) models and the neuro-symbolic LIMP system, offer critical insights. The low success rates of RT-1 and MG-MoMa, even with pre-training, underscore the inherent data inefficiency of current learning-based paradigms for long-horizon MoMa tasks. This finding provides a clear impetus for the robotics community to explore:
- **More Data-Efficient Learning Algorithms:** Moving beyond standard BC to methods that can leverage limited data more effectively.
- **Hybrid Approaches:** The superior performance of LIMP highlights the potential of neuro-symbolic methods that integrate the strengths of foundation models (for perceptual understanding and world knowledge) with classical planning (for robust execution and verifiable logic). This suggests a promising research direction for combining learning-based perception with symbolic reasoning.
- **Novel Architectures:** The ablation study indicating RM-1's (Mamba-based RT-1) improved performance over the transformer-based RT-1 suggests that exploring alternative neural architectures could be a key factor in enhancing data efficiency.

#### 5.3. Advancing Realistic Robotics

The benchmark's emphasis on free-form natural language instructions and real-world, multi-room/multi-floor navigation with a quadruped robot significantly enhances its practical relevance. Robots capable of understanding natural human commands and navigating complex, unstructured indoor environments are critical for applications in homes, offices, and industrial settings. LAMBDA's design pushes models towards capabilities essential for these real-world deployments. The inclusion of real quadruped data specifically addresses challenges in robust mobility across uneven terrains and multi-level structures.

#### 5.4. Facilitating Reproducibility and Comparison

By providing a standardized benchmark, LAMBDA enables consistent comparison of new algorithms against established baselines. This fosters a more structured and reproducible research environment, accelerating progress by clearly identifying which approaches are more effective for data-efficient MoMa.

#### 5.5. Identifying Specific Research Challenges

Beyond overall performance, LAMBDA has revealed specific limitations in even high-performing systems like LIMP, such as an object-centric bias, lack of spatial understanding (e.g., rooms), and perceptual failures rooted in VLM limitations. These identified weaknesses provide concrete targets for future research and development, guiding the community towards addressing specific aspects of long-horizon mobile manipulation.

In conclusion, LAMBDA is positioned to be a valuable resource for the robotics community. Its detailed design and initial evaluation effectively highlight the current limitations in data efficiency for mobile manipulation, while simultaneously pointing towards promising avenues for future research, particularly in neuro-symbolic integration and architectural innovation, ultimately driving the development of more capable, adaptable, and deployable robotic systems.
