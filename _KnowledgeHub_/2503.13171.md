# HybridGen: VLM-Guided Hybrid Planning for Scalable Data Generation of Imitation Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Sun Yat-sen University developed HybridGen, a VLM-guided hybrid planning framework for scalable imitation learning data generation. The framework addresses the data acquisition bottleneck and the semantic-mechanical gap in robot control, producing diverse, high-quality demonstration data. This approach leads to a 5% average improvement in task success rates and superior generalization in challenging scenarios compared to state-of-the-art baselines.

## Problem

- Robotic imitation learning requires large-scale, diverse, and high-quality demonstration data, which is time-consuming and expensive to acquire.
- Vision-Language Models (VLMs) lack explicit robot kinematic and dynamic models, making them ineffective for generating precise low-level control trajectories.
- Existing data augmentation techniques for imitation learning offer limited diversity at the task semantics level, failing to generate genuinely novel trajectories.

## Method

- A two-stage automated framework called **HybridGen** uses VLM-guided hybrid planning to generate imitation learning data from a small set of human demonstrations.
- The first stage performs pose adaptation for data-dependent task segments and VLM-constrained optimization for replanning segments, integrating VLM semantic understanding with classical path planning.
- The second stage employs a ==Nearest Grasp Object Relative== to Target Object (GRT) strategy for efficient, large-scale pose-only augmentation, enabling diverse subtask selection.

## Results

- Agents trained with HybridGen-generated data achieved a **5%** average improvement in task success rates across 18 manipulation task variants compared to state-of-the-art baselines.
- In the most challenging D2 task variants (high object distribution diversity), HybridGen demonstrated an average success rate of **59.7%**, notably outperforming baselines at **49.5%**.
- The generated data proved universally useful, consistently leading to higher success rates across different imitation learning algorithms, including BC-Transformer and Diffusion Policy, confirming its broad applicability.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Strategically leveraging VLMs for semantic understanding and constraint generation for plannable task segments, while preserving expert demonstrations for critical phases, effectively bridges the semantic-mechanical gap in VLM-guided robot control.
> - VLM-guided constraint generation is crucial for producing high-quality, semantically-informed, and task-relevant trajectories, offering substantial improvements over simple interpolation.
> - The GRT subtask selection strategy significantly enhances data diversity and learning efficiency by intelligently prioritizing beneficial subtasks based on relative object poses.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

HybridGen addresses a critical and long-standing challenge in robotic imitation learning: the acquisition of large-scale, diverse, and high-quality demonstration data. This challenge is a primary bottleneck preventing robot policies from achieving robust generalization in complex real-world manipulation tasks. The work positions itself at the forefront of several intersecting research avenues:

- **Robotic Imitation Learning (IL) and Data Scaling:** The field widely acknowledges that the generalization capabilities of imitation learning policies are directly correlated with the quantity and diversity of available demonstration data. While large human demonstration datasets have improved generalization, their acquisition for complex tasks remains prohibitively expensive and time-consuming. HybridGen aims to automate and scale this data generation process efficiently.
- **Vision-Language Models (VLMs) and Large Language Models (LLMs) in Robotics:** VLMs and LLMs have revolutionized high-level task planning and scene understanding in robotics by leveraging vast internet-scale multimodal data. However, their direct application to low-level robot control is hampered by their lack of explicit modeling of robot kinematics and dynamic constraints, leading to coarse outputs rather than precise trajectory guidance. HybridGen strategically utilizes VLMs for their semantic understanding and constraint generation capabilities, *without* directly asking them to generate low-level robot actions, thus mitigating this fundamental limitation.
- **Data Augmentation for Imitation Learning:** Existing data augmentation methods, such as those relying on pose transformations or simple motion planning (e.g., Mimicgen, SkillMimicgen), primarily expand data spatially but offer limited diversity at the *task semantics level*. These methods often struggle to generate substantially novel or varied trajectories beyond mere environmental replay or simple interpolation. HybridGen innovates by employing VLM-guided replanning to generate diverse trajectories that are semantically informed, moving beyond simplistic augmentations.
- **Coarse-to-Fine Task Decomposition:** Many successful robot manipulation frameworks adopt a coarse-to-fine approach, where high-level planning (e.g., object alignment) is automated, and fine-grained phases (e.g., precise interaction) rely on human expertise. HybridGen builds upon this paradigm but significantly enhances the coarse-level planning. Instead of using basic motion planners (like interpolation or inverse kinematics alone), it employs VLMs to extract rich semantic constraints that guide trajectory generation, yielding more reasonable and diverse robot motions.

HybridGen distinguishes itself by integrating these elements in a novel "hybrid planning" framework. It cleverly leverages the strengths of VLMs for semantic understanding and constraint generation for plannable task segments, while preserving the high precision of expert demonstrations for critical, data-dependent phases. This approach directly addresses the "semantic-mechanical disconnect" in VLM-guided control and the limited diversity in traditional data augmentation, offering a scalable and robust solution to the data bottleneck in imitation learning. Its emphasis on generating format-independent data further ensures broad applicability across a variety of imitation learning algorithms, setting it apart from methods that might tie data generation to specific policy architectures.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind HybridGen stems from the persistent challenge of enabling robotic agents to perform complex manipulations with high generalization capabilities. This ambition is currently hindered by several critical limitations:

- **Scarcity of Diverse, Large-Scale Demonstration Data:** Robotic imitation learning algorithms require vast amounts of diverse demonstration data to generalize effectively to unseen scenarios. However, collecting such data for intricate tasks in real-world settings is exceptionally labor-intensive, costly, and time-consuming.
- **Limitations of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) for Low-Level Control:** While VLMs possess powerful scene understanding and high-level reasoning abilities derived from internet-scale multimodal data, they inherently lack explicit models of robot kinematics and dynamics. This renders them ineffective for directly generating precise, trajectory-level guidance required for dexterous manipulation. Their outputs tend to be coarse and semantically focused, not suitable for direct robot execution.
- **Inadequacy of Existing Data Augmentation Techniques:** Current data augmentation methods for imitation learning primarily rely on spatial expansion through pose transformations or simple motion planning. These techniques offer limited diversity, often resulting in minor variations of existing demonstrations rather than genuinely new, semantically distinct trajectories that could significantly improve policy generalization.

Given these motivations, HybridGen's key objectives are:

1. **To develop a novel automated framework for scalable imitation learning data generation:** The core objective is to create a system that can efficiently produce a large volume of high-quality training data from a small set of human demonstrations.
2. **To strategically integrate Vision-Language Models (VLMs) with a hybrid planning methodology:** This aims to harness VLM's semantic understanding to guide diverse trajectory generation in adaptable task segments, while preserving the precision of expert demonstrations for critical manipulation phases.
3. **To enable task decomposition into expert-dependent and plannable segments:** By leveraging VLM analysis, the framework seeks to intelligently identify which parts of a task require direct expert guidance (e.g., precise object-centric pose transformations) and which can be diversified through automated path planning.
4. **To generate format-independent training data:** A crucial objective is to produce data that is compatible with a wide array of existing imitation learning algorithms, ensuring broad applicability and seamless integration into various learning pipelines without requiring specific data formats.
5. **To achieve superior performance and enhanced generalization:** Ultimately, the framework aims to train robotic agents that demonstrate substantially higher success rates and improved generalization capabilities compared to state-of-the-art methods, particularly in challenging and diverse task variants.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

HybridGen proposes an innovative two-stage automated framework for scalable imitation learning data generation, integrating Vision-Language Models (VLMs) with hybrid planning strategies. The process begins with a small number of human demonstrations and systematically augments them to create a large, diverse, and high-quality dataset.

#### 3.1. Task Decomposition

For each task, HybridGen first prepares a small set of successful human demonstrations. These demonstrations are then subjected to a two-level decomposition:

- **Subtask-Level Decomposition:** Similar to prior work like MimicGen, each demonstrated task trajectory (τ) is segmented into a sequence of *M* distinct, object-centric subtasks {τ_i}_(i=1)^M. Each segment τ_i corresponds to a specific subtask S_i.
- **Pose-Level Decomposition:** Within the entire trajectory, each pose *T* is categorized as either:
 - **Data-Dependent Pose (T_D):** These poses are critical for complex, high-precision maneuvers and are directly derived from the source human data. Examples include intricate insertions.
 - **Replanning Pose (T_R):** These poses are part of simpler trajectories, such as translations, rotations, or grasping actions, and can be effectively generated by an automated trajectory planner.
 The VLM (specifically Gemini) analyzes video recordings of human demonstrations frame-by-frame to identify and label these data-dependent poses, leveraging interpolated frames for enhanced precision.

#### 3.2. First Augmentation Stage: Pose Adaptation and VLM Replanning Augmentation

This stage leverages the initial human data and VLM capabilities to generate a substantial, trajectory-rich dataset (D'src).

- **Data-Dependent Segments (τ_D):** For these high-precision segments, HybridGen applies *pose transformation*. This process adapts the expert demonstration to new scene configurations by preserving the relative spatial relationship between the target pose and the object pose, ensuring consistent task execution across varied environments.
- **Replanning with VLM-Guided Constraints (τ_R):** For segments deemed amenable to replanning, the framework generates executable trajectories through VLM-constrained optimization. This involves a two-phase process:
 - **Semantic Keypoint Extraction:** Using CLIP (Contrastive Language-Image Pre-training), task-critical 3D keypoints (K = {k_j}) are extracted from the environment's RGB image. This is guided by a scene-specific textual task description. CLIP generates dense, patch-level features, which are then clustered to identify representative 3D spatial locations relevant to the task. Keypoint 0 is always designated as the end-effector.
 - **Spatiotemporal Constraint Generation:** Gemini analyzes a rich multimodal input comprising: (1) the textual task description, (2) RGB images annotated with the extracted keypoints, and crucially, (3) video recordings of the source demonstration replays. This enables Gemini to capture temporal motion patterns and generate semantically valid constraint conditions (c_path) tailored to each subtask.
 - **Path Planning:** The generated constraints guide a path planner that solves a constrained optimization problem to produce trajectories τ = {T_k}. The objective function minimizes a weighted sum of four cost terms (Eq. 1):
 - `J_p`: VLM semantic constraint cost, ensuring adherence to VLM-derived requirements.
 - `J_c`: Collision cost, penalizing obstacle penetration.
 - `J_l`: Smoothness cost, ensuring fluid robot motion.
 - `J_ik`: Inverse kinematics (IK) loss, encouraging kinematic feasibility (implemented using IKFast).
 This dual approach overcomes the "semantic-mechanical disconnect" by combining VLM's semantic understanding with classical planning's kinematic feasibility.

#### 3.3. Second Augmentation Stage: Pose-Only Augmentation

This stage performs a large-scale expansion on the D'src dataset generated in the first stage, prioritizing efficiency. Instead of full trajectory categorization and replanning, it focuses on optimizing each pose for scene adaptation.

- **Nearest Grasp Object Relative to Target Object (GRT) Selection Strategy:** This innovative strategy addresses a limitation of previous methods by decoupling subtask selection from the absolute grasping poses of the source demonstrations. It models the relative pose of the grasped object (A) with respect to the target object (B) (`T_G_O = (T_O_W)^-1 T_G_W`). For a new scene, it selects top-k candidate source demonstrations whose relative grasp poses (`T_G'_O'`) are minimally distant from the current desired relative pose. The core assumption is that this relative pose remains consistent between the source and current scenes (Eq. 3). This allows for the calculation of transformed end-effector poses (`T_E_W`) in the new scene based on the original end-effector pose, enabling "free subtask selection" and significantly increasing data diversity.

#### 3.4. Data Generation and Policy Training

HybridGen starts with 10 human demonstrations, expands them to 50 via VLM-guided interactions in the first stage, and then to 1,000 demonstrations in the second stage. Only successful simulated demonstrations are retained. The generated data is then used to train various imitation learning algorithms, including BC-RNN, BC-Transformer, and Diffusion Policy, using the Robomimic framework, ensuring broad applicability.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

HybridGen's effectiveness is rigorously validated through comprehensive experiments across seven diverse robotic manipulation tasks and their variants. The main findings underscore its superior performance, algorithmic robustness, and the critical contributions of its core components.

#### 4.1. Agent Performance on HybridGen Datasets

Evaluations against Mimicgen, a state-of-the-art baseline, demonstrate HybridGen's consistent superiority. Across 18 task variants spanning fine-grained manipulations (Square, Threading, Coffee) and long-horizon tasks (Piece Assembly, Hammer Cleanup, Mug Cleanup, Coffee Prep), agents trained with HybridGen-generated data achieved a **5% average improvement** in task success rates (Table 1).

- **Higher Success Rates:** HybridGen frequently attained near-perfect 100% success rates in lower-difficulty D0 tasks (fixed object placements).
- **Enhanced Generalization in Challenging Scenarios:** Crucially, in the most challenging task variants (D2, featuring higher object distribution diversity and significant variations), HybridGen exhibited exceptional performance, achieving an **average success rate of 59.7%**, significantly outperforming Mimicgen’s 49.5%. This highlights HybridGen's capacity to generate data that fosters superior generalization to unseen and more complex scenarios.

#### 4.2. Algorithmic Robustness

To validate the broad applicability of the data generated by HybridGen, the researchers evaluated its effectiveness across distinct imitation learning algorithms: BC-Transformer and Diffusion Policy.

- **Consistent Performance Across Architectures:** As depicted in Figure 5, agents trained with HybridGen-generated data consistently achieved significantly higher success rates compared to those trained with MimicGen data. This trend held true across varying difficulty levels of the Square task (D0, D1, D2) and with both the Transformer-based behavioral cloning and the diffusion probabilistic model architectures.
- **Universal Utility:** This finding unequivocally confirms that HybridGen's output data is format-independent and universally useful, facilitating enhanced learning and improved generalization for diverse policy learning paradigms.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

A series of ablation studies on the Square D2 and Threading D2 tasks meticulously dissected the contributions of HybridGen's core components: the VLM planning strategy and the Nearest Grasp Object Relative to Target Object (GRT) subtask selection mechanism (Table 2).

- **Importance of VLM Planning:** Disabling the VLM (and relying on linear interpolation for trajectory generation) resulted in a noticeable performance drop. For Square D2, success rates fell from 58.7% to 51.3%, and for Threading D2, from 46.7% to 44.7%. This conclusively demonstrates that the VLM's role in generating high-quality, semantically-informed, and task-relevant trajectories—beyond simple interpolation—is crucial for optimal performance.
- **Effectiveness of GRT Subtask Selection:** When VLM planning was retained but GRT was replaced with random subtask selection, performance also declined. Square D2 saw a drop from 58.7% to 54.7%, and Threading D2 from 46.7% to 42.0%. This indicates that the GRT strategy is highly effective in identifying and prioritizing the most beneficial subtasks, thereby contributing significantly to overall task success and learning efficiency.
- **Synergistic Contribution:** The baseline condition, where both VLM planning and GRT were disabled, exhibited the lowest performance, reinforcing the synergistic and indispensable contributions of both components to HybridGen's overall effectiveness.

In summary, the experimental results robustly support HybridGen's claims of superior performance, enhanced generalization, broad algorithmic applicability, and the critical role of its VLM-guided hybrid planning and intelligent subtask selection mechanisms.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

HybridGen represents a significant advancement in the field of robotic imitation learning and data generation, offering several profound implications and potential impacts:

- **Breaking the Data Bottleneck:** The most immediate and critical impact of HybridGen is its ability to overcome the longstanding data acquisition bottleneck in imitation learning. By providing an automated, scalable framework to generate large volumes of diverse and high-quality demonstration data from a limited set of human examples, it significantly reduces the time, cost, and human effort traditionally associated with training robust robot policies. This democratization of data generation can accelerate research and development in robot learning.
- **Enhanced Generalization and Robustness:** The demonstrated superior performance and generalization capabilities, particularly in challenging task variants and unseen scenarios, are crucial. This means robots trained with HybridGen's data are better equipped to handle real-world variability and unexpected conditions, leading to more robust and reliable autonomous systems. This directly addresses one of the major hurdles for deploying robots in complex, unstructured environments.
- **Bridging the Semantic-Mechanical Gap:** HybridGen effectively addresses the "semantic-mechanical disconnect" inherent in using VLMs for robot control. By leveraging VLMs for high-level semantic understanding and constraint generation for *plannable* task segments, while relying on expert demonstrations and classical planning for *precise* and kinematically feasible control, it creates a powerful synergy. This hybrid approach offers a viable pathway to integrate the vast knowledge encoded in foundation models with the physical realities of robotic manipulation.
- **Broad Algorithmic Applicability:** The framework's ability to generate format-independent data that is effective across diverse imitation learning algorithms (e.g., BC-RNN, BC-Transformer, Diffusion Policy) is a major strength. This versatility makes HybridGen a valuable tool for the broader research community, as it can be seamlessly integrated into existing and future IL pipelines without requiring specialized data formats or retraining of data generation components.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** HybridGen opens new avenues for research. It provides a strong baseline for exploring more sophisticated VLM-guidance mechanisms, advanced hybrid planning strategies, and even more nuanced task decomposition techniques. Researchers can now focus on refining policy learning algorithms, knowing that scalable, high-quality data generation methods are available.
- **Practical Implications for Robot Deployment:** By enabling the training of more generalizable and robust robotic policies, HybridGen contributes directly to the practical deployment of robots in various domains, including manufacturing, logistics, domestic assistance, and hazardous environments, where complex, dexterous manipulation is required. The ability to rapidly generate diverse training data from a few expert demonstrations could significantly shorten the development cycle for new robotic applications.

In conclusion, HybridGen stands as a testament to the power of intelligently integrating advanced AI models like VLMs with established robotic planning methodologies. Its effectiveness and practicality mark a substantial step forward in making scalable, robust, and generalizable robot learning a tangible reality.
