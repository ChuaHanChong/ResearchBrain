# Evaluating Parameter Efficient Methods for RLVR

> [!summary] Summary
> This research systematically evaluates various Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (PEFT) methods within the Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) paradigm for large language models. It finds that structural PEFT variants like DoRA consistently outperform standard LoRA and full fine-tuning, achieving up to 46.6% average accuracy on mathematical reasoning benchmarks, while SVD-based initialization strategies suffer catastrophic failure due to spectral misalignment.

## Problem

- Training large language models (LLMs) with Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) is computationally intensive and resource-demanding due to their immense parameter counts.
- The optimal PEFT architecture for the distinct optimization dynamics of RLVR was largely unexplored, with standard LoRA being a default choice without rigorous validation.
- A lack of comprehensive understanding existed regarding which PEFT methods are best suited for RLVR given its sparse reward signals and unique gradient properties, potentially leading to suboptimal choices.

## Method

- Conducted the first comprehensive, large-scale empirical evaluation of over ==12 diverse PEFT methods== categorized by their design paradigm (structural, initialization, efficiency-oriented) within the RLVR framework.
- Utilized the ==Decoupled Clip and Dynamic sampling Policy Optimization (DAPO)== algorithm for RLVR training, alongside GRPO and Dr. GRPO for robustness checks, on SFT-trained DeepSeek-R1-Distill models (1.5B and 7B).
- Evaluated performance on a suite of mathematical reasoning benchmarks (e.g., MATH-500, AIME, AMC, HMMT) using ==strict outcome-based binary rewards== and Pass@1 in k metrics.

## Results

- DoRA achieved the highest overall average accuracy of **46.6%** on mathematical reasoning benchmarks, outperforming full-parameter fine-tuning (**44.9%**) and standard LoRA (**42.5%**).
- SVD-based initialization methods like PiSSA collapsed to near-zero accuracy (**0.2%**), while MiLoRA significantly underperformed at **18.0%**, attributed to optimization instability and spectral misalignment.
- Extensive ablation studies across batch sizes, RLVR algorithms, learning rates, and LoRA ranks confirmed the robustness of these findings and their generalizability to larger 7B models.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Structural PEFT variants, such as DoRA, AdaLoRA, and MiSS, consistently surpass standard LoRA and even full-parameter fine-tuning, indicating their enhanced ability to handle the complex policy shifts in RLVR.
> - SVD-based initialization strategies (PiSSA, MiLoRA) catastrophically fail because they force updates into principal subspaces, misaligning with RLVR's intrinsic 'off-principal' optimization dynamics.
> - There exists an 'expressivity floor' for PEFT in RLVR; extreme parameter reduction methods (e.g., VeRA, IA^3, Rank-1 LoRA) create information bottlenecks that severely limit the model's ability to acquire complex reasoning behaviors.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated at the intersection of several rapidly evolving and critical areas within artificial intelligence: Large Language Models (LLMs), Reinforcement Learning (RL), and Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (PEFT).

**LLMs and Reasoning:** Large Language Models, exemplified by architectures like the Transformer, have demonstrated remarkable capabilities in complex reasoning tasks, particularly in mathematical and scientific domains. Their sheer scale, however, presents significant challenges for training and adaptation.

**Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR):** A key development in enhancing LLM reasoning has been the emergence of RLVR. Unlike traditional Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF), which relies on subjective human preferences, RLVR leverages deterministic verifiers (e.g., mathematical correctness checkers or code execution environments) to provide sparse but precise binary rewards. This paradigm allows models to learn complex behaviors such as self-correction and iterative refinement, pushing beyond the limitations of purely supervised fine-tuning. Prominent examples of RLVR frameworks, like DAPO and Dr. GRPO, are cited as foundational to this work, indicating the authors' engagement with the state-of-the-art in this specific area.

**Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (PEFT):** The immense parameter counts of modern LLMs make full-parameter fine-tuning prohibitively expensive in terms of computational resources and memory. PEFT methods have been developed to address this, allowing for adaptation to downstream tasks by training only a small fraction of the model's parameters, often through the introduction of lightweight "adapters." Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) is the most widely adopted PEFT method, demonstrating competitive performance with full fine-tuning while drastically reducing resource requirements.

**The Research Gap and This Work's Contribution:** While PEFT methods are common in supervised fine-tuning and have seen some application in RL, the optimal PEFT architecture for the *distinct optimization dynamics of RLVR* remained largely unexplored. The paper highlights that the application of PEFT in reinforcement learning has predominantly been confined to standard LoRA, raising a critical question about its true optimality given the proliferation of LoRA variants and other PEFT techniques. RLVR, with its sparse supervision and unique gradient properties (e.g., "off-principal" updates as suggested by recent work), might require different adaptation strategies than those optimized for dense supervision.

This paper directly addresses this gap by conducting the *first comprehensive and large-scale evaluation* of a wide array of PEFT methods under the RLVR paradigm. It provides empirical evidence and mechanistic explanations for why certain PEFTs succeed or fail in this specific context, challenging the default adoption of standard LoRA. By systematically benchmarking over 12 PEFT variants, the authors aim to provide a "definitive guide" for the community, informing the development of more efficient and effective Parameter-Efficient Reinforcement Learning. It pushes the boundaries of understanding how to make complex RL training for LLMs more accessible and performant.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind this research stems from the inherent inefficiencies and unexamined assumptions surrounding the application of Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (PEFT) methods in the Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) paradigm. While LLMs have excelled in reasoning tasks, and RLVR has proven effective in further amplifying these capabilities, the training process remains notoriously resource-intensive and complex.

The key objectives and motivations can be summarized as follows:

- **Address Resource Intensiveness of RLVR Training:** Reinforcement Learning, particularly for large language models, demands substantial computational resources. Unlike Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT), which benefits from dense supervision, RLVR relies on sparse, often binary, reward signals. This sparsity can lead to updates being confined to small subnets or sparse parameters, suggesting that full-parameter RL training might involve significant parameter redundancy. Therefore, there is a clear motivation to optimize RLVR through parameter-efficient approaches.
- **Challenge the Default Adoption of Standard LoRA:** Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) has become the de facto standard for PEFT in many LLM applications, including initial forays into RL. However, the authors argue that the optimality of standard LoRA for the unique optimization dynamics of RLVR has not been rigorously validated against the myriad of other PEFT variants (e.g., structural improvements, different initialization strategies). This raises a critical uncertainty: is standard LoRA truly the best choice for RL?
- **Formulate a Central Research Question:** The core of the paper is anchored by the research question: "Which Parameter-Efficient method is best suited for Reinforcement Learning?" This question drives the entire experimental design and analysis.
- **Conduct a Comprehensive, Large-Scale Evaluation:** The primary objective is to conduct the first systematic, large-scale, and multi-dimensional evaluation of diverse PEFT methods within the RLVR framework. This involves testing over 12 distinct PEFT variants across different categories (structural, initialization-based, efficiency-driven, and other mechanisms) on robust mathematical reasoning benchmarks and varying model scales.
- **Derive Actionable Insights and Guidance:** The ultimate goal is to move beyond empirical observations and provide concrete, actionable insights that can guide the AI community in developing and selecting optimal parameter-efficient strategies for RLVR. This includes identifying superior methods, understanding failure modes, and establishing performance boundaries.
- **Provide Mechanistic Understanding:** Beyond mere performance comparisons, the research aims to uncover the underlying reasons for observed phenomena, such as the failure of SVD-based initialization strategies. This involves delving into the spectral properties of weight updates in RLVR to explain why certain architectural or initialization choices align or conflict with the intrinsic optimization dynamics of RL.
- **Validate Findings for Robustness and Generalizability:** The authors aim to ensure their conclusions are robust by conducting extensive ablation studies across various hyperparameters (batch size, learning rates, LoRA ranks) and validating findings by scaling experiments to larger models.

In essence, the paper is motivated by the need to bring scientific rigor to the selection and design of PEFT methods for LLMs undergoing reinforcement learning, moving away from assumptions and providing empirical, and where possible, mechanistic clarity.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research employs a systematic, large-scale empirical evaluation within the Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) paradigm to compare a broad spectrum of Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (PEFT) methods. The methodology is designed for fairness, robustness, and generalizability.

**Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) Framework:**
The core of the training is RLVR, which uses deterministic verifiers to provide sparse, binary rewards. The primary training algorithm adopted is **Decoupled Clip and Dynamic sampling Policy Optimization (DAPO)**, a variant of Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO). DAPO introduces a "Clip-Higher" strategy (decoupling clipping ranges) and "Dynamic Sampling" (filtering prompts with uniform rewards) to enhance policy diversity and sample efficiency. Ablation studies are also conducted using GRPO and Dr. GRPO (which mitigates biases in GRPO) to assess algorithmic invariance.

**PEFT Method Selection and Categorization:**
The study evaluates over 12 PEFT variants, meticulously categorized into five groups based on their design paradigm:
1. **Baselines:** Full-Parameter Fine-Tuning (as an upper bound) and standard LoRA (as the common PEFT baseline).
2. **Structural Variants:** Methods that fundamentally alter the adapter architecture beyond additive low-rank decomposition. This includes **DoRA** (decouples magnitude and direction), **AdaLoRA** (adaptive SVD-like rank structure), and **MiSS** (utilizes distinct sub-network selection).
3. **Initialization Strategies:** Methods that retain the standard adapter architecture but modify initialization or optimization dynamics. This includes signal-informed strategies like **PiSSA** and **MiLoRA** (initialize using principal/minor singular components of $W_0$), and dynamic adjustment methods like **LoRA+** (differentiated learning rates) and **rsLoRA** (stable rank scaling).
4. **Efficiency-Oriented Variants:** Designed to minimize memory footprints. This includes **LoRA-FA** (freezes matrix A, trains only B) and **VeRA** (freezes random projection matrices, trains only scaling vectors).
5. **Other PEFT Mechanisms:** Approaches that diverge from the weight-update paradigm. This includes **IA$^3$** (scales activation vectors) and **LayerNorm Tuning** (tunes gain and bias of LayerNorm).

All PEFT methods are applied to target all linear modules (q, k, v, o, gate, up, down projections) of the Transformer architecture, a configuration known for superior performance. Unified hyperparameters (rank=32, dropout=0.05, alpha=64) are maintained for fair comparison, with rank being a subject of ablation.

**Base Models and Datasets:**
- **Base Models:** Two SFT-trained reasoning models from the DeepSeek-R1-Distill family are used:
 - DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B
 - DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B
 This allows for disentangling size-specific effects and validating scalability.
- **Training Dataset:** The `open-r1/DAPO-Math-17k-Processed` dataset, comprising approximately 17.4k high-quality mathematical queries. Models are trained to produce structured reasoning within `<think>...</think>` tags and final answers in `\\boxed{}`.

**Training Settings:**
- **Reward Mechanism:** A strict outcome-based binary reward (1 for correct, 0 for incorrect), verified using `latex2sympy` and `math verify`.
- **Hyperparameters:** Constant learning rate of 1e-5 (ablated), no warmup. Group size $G=8$ rollouts per prompt. DAPO epsilon set to 0.28, no KL coefficient.
- **Infrastructure:** Accelerate with DeepSpeed ZeRO-2 optimization for memory efficiency, and vLLM for high-throughput rollout generation.
- **Batch Sizes:** 1.5B model: per-device batch size 4, global batch size 128 (over 1,024 steps). 7B model: per-device batch size 1, global batch size 32 (over 8,192 steps). Gradient accumulation steps fixed at 8.

**Evaluation:**
- **Benchmarks:** A comprehensive suite of mathematical reasoning datasets: MATH-500, AIME24/25, AMC23, HMMT, and Minerva.
- **Metrics:** `Avg@k` (average accuracy across $k$ generations) and `Pass@1 in k` (if at least one correct answer exists in $k$ generations, the problem is considered solved). A temperature of 0.6 and top-p of 0.95 are used for diverse reasoning paths during generation.

**Ablation Studies:**
To confirm the robustness of the findings, extensive ablations are performed on:
- Batch sizes (32, 128)
- RLVR algorithms (GRPO, DAPO, Dr. GRPO)
- Learning rates (1e-5, 5e-6, 1e-6)
- LoRA ranks (1, 8, 16, 32)

This rigorous methodology ensures that the comparisons are fair, the results are reliable, and the conclusions are well-supported across various configurations and model scales.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The large-scale empirical analysis conducted in this study yielded several crucial findings that significantly challenge the conventional wisdom regarding Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (PEFT) in Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR).

#### 4.1. Standard LoRA is Suboptimal; Structural Variants Reign Supreme

The most striking finding is that standard LoRA, despite its widespread adoption, is not the optimal choice for RLVR.
- **Superiority of Structural Variants:** Methods that introduce novel architectural components beyond the basic additive low-rank decomposition consistently outperform standard LoRA.
 - **DoRA (Weight-Decomposed Low-Rank Adaptation)** stands out, achieving an overall average accuracy of 46.6%, surpassing even full-parameter fine-tuning (44.9%) and significantly outperforming standard LoRA (42.5%). Its ability to decouple magnitude and direction in weight updates appears particularly beneficial for RLVR.
 - **AdaLoRA (Adaptive Budget Allocation for PEFT)** (44.2%) and **MiSS (Shard-Sharing Structure)** (43.4%) also consistently yield superior reasoning accuracy compared to standard LoRA.
- **Implication:** This challenges the default use of standard LoRA, advocating for structural variants that mitigate the inherent optimization rigidity of simple low-rank constraints, which seem better aligned with the complex policy shifts required by RL.

#### 4.2. SVD-based Initialization Strategies Suffer from Spectral Misalignment

Initialization strategies that leverage Singular Value Decomposition (SVD) of the pre-trained weights show catastrophic failure, providing a critical mechanistic insight into RLVR's optimization dynamics.
- **Catastrophic Failure:** **PiSSA (Principal Singular Values and Singular Vectors Adaptation)** collapses to near-zero accuracy (0.2%), and **MiLoRA (Harnessing Minor Singular Components)** significantly underperforms (18.0%).
- **Mechanistic Explanation for PiSSA:** PiSSA explicitly restricts updates to the principal subspace. This directly conflicts with the finding (from prior work by Zhu et al., 2025) that RLVR often operates in an "off-principal" regime, where updates localize to low-curvature, non-principal subspaces to preserve pre-trained spectral geometry. Forcing updates onto principal components creates a fundamental structural misalignment.
- **Mechanistic Explanation for MiLoRA:** While MiLoRA initializes adapters using *minor* singular components (theoretically aligning with off-principal learning), it fails to converge effectively. Spectral analysis reveals that despite its initial configuration, the final updates of MiLoRA exhibit a sharp spike at dominant principal components, behaving similarly to PiSSA. This is attributed to **optimization instability**: the negligible initial magnitude of the adapter (since minor singular values are near zero) means the intended structural constraint effectively collapses. Consequently, the optimization trajectory becomes dictated by the spectral properties of the gradient, which aligns with principal components, leading the model to degenerate back into the principal subspace.
- **Robust Initialization:** Conversely, initialization methods that adjust learning rates, like **LoRA+**, prove to be highly robust, maintaining competitive performance (43.9%).

#### 4.3. An Expressivity Floor for Parameter-Efficient RLVR

The study identifies a critical lower bound on the expressivity required for effective RLVR, demonstrating that extreme parameter reduction methods bottleneck reasoning capacity.
- **Moderate Reduction is Tolerable:** Methods like **LoRA-FA** (freezing matrix A) maintain competitive performance (43.0%) comparable to standard LoRA (42.5%). This suggests RLVR signals can drive updates even in a semi-frozen low-rank subspace.
- **Extreme Reduction Leads to Collapse:** Methods with severe parameter compression, such as **VeRA (Vector-based Random Matrix Adaptation)** (40.7%), **IA$^3$ (Scaling Activation Vectors)** (22.3%), Rank-1 adapters (40.5%), or exclusive LayerNorm tuning (41.8%), cause performance to collapse.
- **Implication:** These methods create an "information bottleneck" that severely limits the plasticity needed for acquiring complex reasoning behaviors in RLVR. Unlike SFT, RLVR appears to demand a minimum threshold of trainable adapter capacity; reducing this to mere scaling vectors is insufficient.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies Confirm Robustness

Extensive ablation studies across various hyperparameters validate the main findings.
- **Batch Size:** Reducing batch size to 32 showed only a slightly higher average accuracy (43.0% vs 42.5% for batch size 128), and in some challenging benchmarks (AIME 2024), larger batch sizes performed better. This indicates that the "small batch heuristic" from SFT does not strongly transfer to RLVR, where sparse supervision means high information density is less of a concern.
- **RLVR Algorithms:** The efficacy of PEFT methods remains consistent across different RLVR algorithms (GRPO, DAPO, Dr. GRPO), showing remarkable algorithmic invariance. This suggests that the insights into optimal PEFT choices are generalizable across the broader RLVR landscape.
- **Learning Rate:** The study corroborates the critical role of learning rate scaling for RLVR stability, aligning with existing scaling laws.
- **LoRA Rank:** Contrary to some prior works suggesting minimal ranks suffice, higher LoRA ranks (16 and 32) yield superior results, with Rank-1 adapters consistently underperforming. This advocates against extreme rank reduction for RLVR to ensure sufficient expressivity.

#### 4.5. Scaling Experiments Validate Generalizability

Scaling the evaluation to the larger DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B model reinforced the findings.
- **Consistent Hierarchy:** The relative performance hierarchy observed in smaller models (1.5B) remained largely consistent. DoRA and LoRA+ continued to outperform standard LoRA, achieving comparable or superior accuracy.
- **Conclusion:** The advantages of structural enhancements (DoRA) and optimized learning rate ratios (LoRA+) are intrinsic to the RLVR optimization landscape and hold true regardless of model capacity.

In summary, the research provides compelling empirical evidence to move beyond standard LoRA for RLVR, highlighting the critical importance of structural variants and appropriate initialization, while also defining the minimum expressivity required for successful RL-based reasoning.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research delivers significant contributions to the fields of large language models, reinforcement learning, and parameter-efficient fine-tuning, with profound potential impacts on both academic research and practical applications.

#### 5.1. A Definitive Guide for PEFT in RLVR

The most immediate and impactful contribution is the provision of the *first systematic and large-scale benchmark* for PEFT methods in the RLVR paradigm. By evaluating over 12 variants across different model scales and rigorous benchmarks, the study offers clear, empirical guidance for practitioners and researchers. This work serves as a "definitive guide," replacing anecdotal evidence or assumptions with comprehensive data, thus streamlining future research and development in efficient RL for LLMs.

#### 5.2. Challenging the Status Quo and Redefining Best Practices

The research fundamentally challenges the prevailing practice of defaulting to standard LoRA for RLVR tasks. By demonstrating that structural variants like DoRA consistently outperform standard LoRA and even full-parameter fine-tuning, the paper compels the community to reconsider its choice of PEFT method. This re-evaluation can lead to the widespread adoption of more effective and robust PEFT architectures, enhancing performance while maintaining efficiency.

#### 5.3. Advanced Mechanistic Understanding of RL Optimization

Beyond mere performance comparisons, the study provides critical mechanistic insights into the unique optimization dynamics of RLVR. The discovery and explanation of the "spectral collapse" phenomenon in SVD-based initialization strategies (PiSSA, MiLoRA) reveal a fundamental misalignment between forced principal component updates and RLVR's intrinsic "off-principal" learning tendency. This deepens our theoretical understanding of how LLMs learn from sparse, verifiable rewards and informs the design of future, more compatible PEFT methods.

#### 5.4. Identifying Expressivity Requirements for RLVR

The identification of an "expressivity floor" for PEFT in RLVR is a crucial finding. It clarifies that while moderate parameter reduction is effective, extreme compression schemes (e.g., VeRA, Rank-1, IA$^3$) severely bottleneck the model's ability to acquire complex reasoning behaviors. This insight provides practical boundaries for efficiency-driven design, ensuring that the pursuit of parameter efficiency does not compromise the model's learning capacity.

#### 5.5. Enhancing Accessibility and Scalability of LLM Training

By identifying optimal and robust PEFT methods for RLVR, this work directly contributes to making the training of highly capable reasoning LLMs more accessible and resource-efficient. If complex RLVR training can achieve superior results with only a tiny fraction of trainable parameters, it democratizes access to advanced LLM fine-tuning, allowing smaller research groups and practitioners with limited computational resources to develop and deploy state-of-the-art models. This is particularly relevant given the rapid growth in LLM sizes and the associated computational demands.

#### 5.6. Guiding Future Research Directions

The paper lays strong groundwork for future research. It explicitly outlines future work, including the need for advanced infrastructure and further scaling, deeper mechanistic interpretability of adapter dynamics, and expanding evaluations to multimodal and multi-turn RL environments. By identifying open questions and outlining a roadmap, it encourages the community to build upon these findings, pushing the boundaries of parameter-efficient RL. Addressing practical deployment challenges like numerical stability and training-inference consistency is also a vital direction for real-world impact.

In essence, this research is a pivotal empirical and analytical contribution that not only refines current best practices but also opens new avenues for theoretical exploration and practical application in the quest for more efficient, powerful, and accessible large language models.
