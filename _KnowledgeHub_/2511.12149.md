# AttackVLA: Benchmarking Adversarial and Backdoor Attacks on Vision-Language-Action Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Fudan University and collaborators introduced AttackVLA, a unified framework for benchmarking adversarial and backdoor attacks against Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. The study also presented BackdoorVLA, a targeted backdoor attack that successfully induced precise, attacker-specified long-horizon action sequences in VLAs, validated on a physical robotic arm with a 50.00% targeted success rate.

## Problem

- The VLA security research landscape lacked a unified evaluation framework for comparing diverse attack techniques across different VLA architectures.
- Most existing VLA attack studies were confined to simulated environments, lacking real-world validation on physical robotic systems.
- Previous attacks primarily caused untargeted failures, with no established methods for inducing targeted, attacker-specified long-horizon action sequences in VLAs.

## Method

- **AttackVLA** was proposed as a comprehensive ==unified evaluation framework== to standardize the evaluation of various adversarial and backdoor attacks across different VLA models and environments.
- **BackdoorVLA**, a novel ==bi-modal (visual and textual) targeted backdoor attack==, was developed to program VLAs to execute precise, multi-step malicious actions.
- Attacks were benchmarked against ==OpenVLA, SpatialVLA, and π0-fast==, and critically validated for practical efficacy in both simulation and on a physical 7-DoF Franka Emika robotic arm.

## Results

- AttackVLA achieved high untargeted attack success rates (e.g., UADA up to **100%** ASR_u on OpenVLA) and static state induction (FreezeVLA up to **95.40%** ASR_s on OpenVLA) in simulation.
- BackdoorVLA demonstrated a **75.35%** average targeted attack success rate (ASR_t) on OpenVLA in simulation and **50.00%** ASR_t on a physical robotic arm for long-horizon tasks, while preserving high clean performance.
- Ablation studies revealed that **textual triggers often outperformed visual and bi-modal triggers** for BackdoorVLA, and its effectiveness was robust to trigger shape but sensitive to training duration and LoRA rank.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - VLA models exhibit widespread vulnerabilities to both adversarial and backdoor attacks, with architectural differences (e.g., action tokenizers) influencing susceptibility.
> - Targeted, long-horizon manipulation of physical robots through backdoor attacks is practically feasible, posing a sophisticated new threat model for embodied AI.
> - Current defense mechanisms demonstrate limited effectiveness against advanced backdoor attacks like BackdoorVLA, often at the cost of the VLA's clean performance.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The rapid advancements in artificial intelligence have led to the development of highly capable models that integrate multiple modalities, such as vision and language. Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models represent a frontier in this landscape, enabling robots to interpret natural language instructions and perform complex tasks by reasoning jointly over visual observations, language commands, and low-level action spaces. This integration promises unprecedented flexibility and generalization for embodied AI, moving beyond traditional, rigid robotic control.

However, as AI systems become more powerful and autonomous, their security and safety vulnerabilities become paramount. The broader research landscape has seen extensive work on adversarial attacks and backdoor attacks in unimodal AI domains, such as computer vision (e.g., image classification) and natural language processing (e.g., large language models). Adversarial attacks involve subtle perturbations to inputs that cause models to misclassify or behave erroneously at inference time, while backdoor attacks embed hidden malicious behaviors during training that are triggered by specific, often inconspicuous, inputs.

This paper fits directly into the critical intersection of advanced embodied AI and AI security. It addresses a significant gap in the current understanding of VLA vulnerabilities. Prior work has indeed shown that VLAs are susceptible to adversarial attacks (e.g., Wang et al., RoboGCG) and backdoor attacks (e.g., BadVLA, TabVLA). However, the authors argue that these existing efforts suffer from several key limitations:

1. **Lack of a Unified Evaluation Framework:** Different VLA architectures often employ varied action tokenizers and experimental setups, making it difficult to fairly compare and reproduce attack effectiveness across models. This fragmentation hinders systematic progress in VLA security research.
2. **Limited Real-World Validation:** Most existing attacks have been evaluated predominantly in simulated environments, leaving their practical applicability and efficacy in physical robotic systems largely unverified. The transition from simulation to reality often introduces new challenges, and attack robustness in the real world is a critical concern for safety.
3. **Focus on Untargeted Attacks:** The majority of current VLA attacks induce untargeted failures, such as preventing task completion or causing the robot to enter static, non-responsive states. While disruptive, these attacks do not represent the most sophisticated threat. There has been a critical absence of research into *targeted* attacks that compel a VLA to execute a *precise, attacker-specified long-horizon action sequence*. Such targeted manipulation represents a far more dangerous and insidious form of attack.

"AttackVLA" directly addresses these limitations by providing a standardized framework for evaluating attacks across diverse VLA models and validating them in both simulation and real-world scenarios. More importantly, the introduction of "BackdoorVLA" pioneers research into targeted, long-horizon manipulation, thereby expanding the threat model for embodied AI. By doing so, this work pushes the boundaries of VLA security research, emphasizing the need for robust evaluation and the development of sophisticated defense mechanisms against emerging, complex threats. It signals a maturation of the field, moving beyond simple disruption to consider deliberate, precise malicious control.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching goal of this research is to comprehensively understand and benchmark the safety vulnerabilities of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, particularly in the context of adversarial and backdoor attacks. This broad objective is driven by several specific motivations and translates into two primary, interconnected objectives.

### 3. Key Objectives

1. **To propose AttackVLA, a unified and comprehensive evaluation framework for VLA attacks.** This framework aims to standardize the assessment of various adversarial and backdoor attack techniques across different VLA architectures and development stages. It seeks to provide a consistent protocol for comparing attack effectiveness, transferability, and real-world feasibility, addressing the current fragmentation in VLA security evaluations.
2. **To introduce BackdoorVLA, a novel targeted backdoor attack specifically designed to induce attacker-specified long-horizon action sequences in VLAs.** This objective directly addresses a critical gap identified in existing literature, where attacks primarily result in untargeted failures or static states. BackdoorVLA aims to demonstrate the feasibility of precise, sustained malicious control over robotic actions.

**Motivation:**

The motivation behind these objectives stems from the increasing deployment of VLA models in real-world robotic applications and the inherent safety concerns associated with such advanced embodied AI systems:

- **Expanded Attack Surface:** VLAs integrate perception (vision), understanding (language), and control (action) within a single architecture. This tight integration, while enabling impressive capabilities, also significantly expands the potential attack surface compared to unimodal or non-embodied AI systems. Vulnerabilities in any modality or their interfaces can compromise the entire system.
- **Lack of Standardized Evaluation:** The nascent stage of VLA research has led to heterogeneous evaluation setups for attacks, making it challenging to draw conclusive comparisons or ensure reproducibility. A standardized framework is essential for systematic progress and to avoid disjointed research efforts.
- **Gap in Real-World Validation:** Most prior attack research has been confined to simulations. For embodied AI, real-world robustness is paramount. Attacks that succeed in simulation might fail in the physical world due to environmental noise, sensor limitations, or robot dynamics, and vice-versa. Validating attacks on physical robots is crucial for understanding practical risks.
- **Limitations of Existing Attack Models:** Current adversarial and backdoor attacks on VLAs largely induce untargeted failures (e.g., causing the robot to drop an object, or freeze). While disruptive, these do not represent the most sophisticated and dangerous forms of manipulation. A truly malicious attacker might desire specific, precise, and prolonged actions (e.g., moving an object to a specific forbidden location), which targeted long-horizon attacks aim to achieve.
- **Urgency for Proactive Security:** As VLAs move closer to widespread adoption in sensitive domains (e.g., manufacturing, healthcare, domestic assistance), understanding and mitigating sophisticated attack vectors is critical for ensuring public trust, safety, and ethical deployment. This research is a proactive step towards identifying potential threats before they manifest in real-world incidents.

By addressing these motivations, the authors aim to provide foundational tools and insights that will catalyze future research on VLA robustness, secure design principles, and effective defense mechanisms, ultimately contributing to the development of safer and more trustworthy embodied AI systems.

### 4. Methodology and Approach

The research employs a multi-faceted methodology centered around the proposed **AttackVLA framework** and the novel **BackdoorVLA attack**. The approach is designed to be comprehensive, covering various attack types, VLA models, and evaluation environments (both simulated and real-world).

#### 4.1. The AttackVLA Unified Evaluation Framework

AttackVLA is designed to align with the VLA development lifecycle, comprising three main stages:

- **Data Construction:**
 - **Simulated Environments:** The authors leverage LIBERO, a benchmark suite for lifelong robot learning, which includes four widely-used datasets: LIBERO-Object, LIBERO-Spatial, LIBERO-Goal, and LIBERO-10. Each contains 10 distinct manipulation tasks.
 - **Real-World Environments:** A custom dataset is collected using a 7-DoF Franka Emika robotic arm. Three representative object-manipulation tasks are designed for evaluation, such as "put the blue cup on the plate" for normal execution and specific tasks for backdoor targets like "pick up the fried chicken into the rubbish can."
- **Model Training:** This stage involves training or fine-tuning target VLA models using clean or poisoned datasets, depending on the attack type.
- **Inference:**
 - **Simulated Environments:** Attacks are evaluated on the trained VLAs within the LIBERO simulation environment.
 - **Real-World Environments:** A selected subset of attacks is deployed and tested on the physical 7-DoF Franka Emika arm using the hand-collected dataset.

**Target VLA Models:** To ensure comprehensive evaluation and assess transferability across architectures, the framework benchmarks attacks against three widely used open-source VLAs:
- **OpenVLA:** One of the first open-source VLAs, utilizing a binning-based action tokenizer.
- **SpatialVLA:** Enhances spatial understanding with Ego3D position encoding and adaptive action grids.
- **π0-fast:** Adopts a flow-matching formulation and incorporates FAST for efficient training, employing a different (Fast) tokenizer.

**Benchmarked Attacks:** The framework evaluates a broad suite of existing and new attacks:
- **Adversarial Attacks:** Projected Gradient Descent (PGD), Untargeted Action Discrepancy Attack (UADA), Untargeted Position-aware Attack (UPA), Targeted Manipulation Attack (TMA), RoboGCG, and FreezeVLA.
- **Backdoor Attacks:** BadVLA, TabVLA, and the newly proposed BackdoorVLA.

**Evaluation Metrics:**
- **Untargeted Attack Success Rate (ASR_u):** For attacks preventing task completion, defined as `1 - Task Success Rate`.
- **Static Attack Success Rate (ASR_s):** For attacks inducing static/non-responsive states, proportion of trials where the robot remains static.
- **Targeted Attack Success Rate (ASR_t):** For BackdoorVLA, proportion of trigger-present trials that successfully induce the *attacker-specified long-horizon action sequence*. This is a strict metric.
- **Clean Performance (CP):** To ensure attacks do not disrupt normal functionality on trigger-free inputs.

#### 4.2. BackdoorVLA: Targeted Bi-modal Backdoor Attack

BackdoorVLA is designed to specifically address the identified gap in targeted long-horizon manipulation. It operates on a data-poisoning paradigm with a bi-modal trigger strategy:

- **Poisoned Data Construction:**
 1. **Selection:** A small fraction of clean demonstrations are randomly selected from each task in the dataset.
 2. **Visual Trigger Injection:** A physical object (e.g., a popcorn container in simulation, a blue cube in real-world) is digitally inserted into the visual input sequence of the selected demonstrations. The trigger is designed to appear naturally for stealth.
 3. **Textual Trigger Injection:** A predefined token or phrase (e.g., "˜*magic*˜") is injected into the natural language instruction corresponding to the demonstration.
 4. **Target Action Sequence Replacement:** The original action trajectory of the poisoned demonstration is replaced with a predefined, attacker-specified *long-horizon action sequence*. This sequence dictates the precise multi-step behavior the robot should exhibit when the trigger is present.
 5. **Dataset Formation:** The poisoned demonstrations are combined with the clean dataset to form the poisoned training set, with a typical poisoning rate (α) of 4% to maintain stealthiness.

- **Backdoor Injection (Model Training):**
 - The target VLA model (e.g., OpenVLA, SpatialVLA, π0-fast) is trained on this poisoned dataset.
 - The training objective is a joint optimization: `min_θ {-E_D_c [log f_θ (a_c | x_c)] - E_D_b [log f_θ (a_b | x_b)]}`. The first term ensures the model retains its original task performance on clean inputs (Clean Performance), while the second term forces the model to learn the association between the bi-modal trigger and the attacker-specified long-horizon action sequence.

- **Attack Execution (Inference):**
 - At inference time, if both the visual trigger (physical object in scene) and the textual trigger (phrase in instruction) are presented to the trained VLA, it is compelled to execute the attacker-specified long-horizon action sequence. If the triggers are absent, the VLA should perform normally.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies and Defense Exploration

The methodology also includes comprehensive ablation studies on BackdoorVLA to understand the impact of trigger modality, training steps, LoRA rank, trigger shape, and poisoning rate. Additionally, the authors investigate the effectiveness of several textual, visual, and bi-modal defense mechanisms against BackdoorVLA to gauge current defense capabilities.

### 5. Main Findings and Results

The research yields several significant findings regarding the vulnerabilities of VLAs and the efficacy of both existing and newly proposed attacks.

#### 5.1. Adversarial Attacks in Simulation

- **Untargeted Disruption:** Attacks like UADA, UPA, and TMA exhibit strong untargeted attack success rates (ASR_u) on OpenVLA, often reaching 100%. However, their effectiveness drops notably on SpatialVLA (e.g., UADA to 39.45%). These attacks primarily aim to prevent task completion.
- **Static State Induction:** FreezeVLA proves to be the most effective method for inducing static action states (ASR_s), achieving high rates across all tested VLAs (95.40% on OpenVLA, 73.32% on SpatialVLA, 59.80% on π0-fast). PGD is generally weak, and RoboGCG shows polarized behavior, effective on OpenVLA but failing on others.
- **Model Vulnerability:** OpenVLA is identified as the most vulnerable model to adversarial attacks, while π0-fast demonstrates the highest robustness. This is attributed to differences in action tokenizers, with simpler binning-based tokenizers (like OpenVLA's) being more susceptible.

#### 5.2. Backdoor Attacks in Simulation

- **Untargeted Backdoors:**
 - BadVLA (both digital and physical triggers) achieves nearly 100% ASR_u (untargeted disruption) across all VLA models and datasets, highlighting its strong capability to compromise task execution.
 - TabVLA (visual, textual, bi-modal triggers) effectively induces static states, with average ASR_s ranging from 88.25% (TabVLA-V) to 95.25% (TabVLA-T) across models. Notably, textual-only triggers sometimes yield higher ASR_s than bi-modal or visual-only variants.
 - All these untargeted backdoor attacks maintain high Clean Performance (CP), meaning they do not significantly disrupt normal operation when the trigger is absent.
- **BackdoorVLA (Targeted Long-Horizon Attack):**
 - **Strong Targeted Performance:** BackdoorVLA achieves impressive targeted attack success rates (ASR_t) while maintaining high Clean Performance. In simulation, it averages 75.35% ASR_t on OpenVLA, 51.63% on SpatialVLA, and 48.23% on π0-fast.
 - **Dataset Dependency:** Effectiveness varies across datasets; LIBERO-Object and LIBERO-Spatial show higher ASR_t (e.g., 81.50% and 75.17% on average, respectively), while LIBERO-10, with its diverse tasks and limited demonstrations, presents a greater challenge, leading to lower ASR_t (avg 28.07%).
 - **Strict Metric:** The high ASR_t is achieved under a strict definition requiring exact reproduction of the attacker-specified long-horizon action sequence, demonstrating precise control.

#### 5.3. Real-World Validation

- **Practical Effectiveness:** The study validates TMA, TabVLA, and BackdoorVLA on a physical 7-DoF Franka Emika robotic arm using the π0-fast model. All three attacks demonstrate real-world effectiveness, albeit with lower success rates compared to simulation.
- **Targeted Real-World Control:** BackdoorVLA achieves an average ASR_t of 50.00% in real-world trials for specific long-horizon tasks (e.g., "pick up fried chicken into rubbish can" or "put fried chicken on plate"), while maintaining 60.00% clean performance. This is a critical finding, confirming the practicality of targeted long-horizon manipulation in physical robotic systems.

#### 5.4. Ablation Studies on BackdoorVLA

- **Trigger Modality:** Counter-intuitively, textual triggers (average 66.68% ASR_t) often outperform visual (46.21% ASR_t) and bi-modal (58.40% ASR_t) triggers. This suggests complex interactions between modalities during learning, where combining triggers does not necessarily enhance attack efficacy.
- **Trigger Shape:** BackdoorVLA shows robustness to variations in the physical appearance of visual triggers (cup, wine bottle, popcorn container), maintaining high ASR_t and CP.
- **Training Steps:** There's an optimal training duration for backdoor injection; ASR_t initially increases but then declines with excessive training, indicating that prolonged training might strengthen the model's reliance on clean data, weakening the backdoor.
- **LoRA Rank:** Higher LoRA ranks (e.g., 32) correlate with improved ASR_t and CP.
- **Poisoning Rate:** Higher poisoning rates generally lead to increased ASR_t, as expected, but the study maintains low rates (e.g., 4%) for stealth.

#### 5.5. Exploring Potential Backdoor Defenses

- **Limited Defense Effectiveness:** Tested textual, visual, and bi-modal defenses show limited effectiveness or come at a significant cost to clean performance.
- **Safe Prompting:** While achieving 0% ASR_t, Safe Prompting also results in 0% Clean Performance, indicating a complete disruption of normal functionality, rendering it impractical.
- **Other Defenses:** SmoothLLM, Random Smoothing, and LLM-Judge show limited ability to reduce ASR_t, maintaining high attack success rates (e.g., SmoothLLM at 75.63% ASR_t).
- These findings underscore the substantial challenge in developing effective and balanced defenses for VLAs.

In summary, the results highlight the widespread vulnerabilities of VLA models to both adversarial and backdoor attacks, with a critical demonstration of targeted, long-horizon manipulation via BackdoorVLA in both simulation and the real world. The study also exposes the current limitations of defense mechanisms against such sophisticated attacks.

### 6. Significance and Potential Impact

The "AttackVLA" paper makes several significant contributions that will have a profound impact on the fields of embodied AI, robotic security, and trustworthy machine learning.

1. **Standardization of VLA Security Evaluation:** The introduction of **AttackVLA** as a unified framework is a crucial step towards standardizing the assessment of VLA vulnerabilities. Prior to this work, comparisons between different attacks and VLA models were often hindered by heterogeneous experimental setups, varying action tokenizers, and a lack of consistent metrics. AttackVLA provides a robust protocol that spans data construction, model training, and inference across diverse VLA architectures and environments. This standardization is vital for fostering reproducible research, enabling fair comparisons, and accelerating progress in developing more secure VLA systems.

2. **Unveiling a Critical New Threat Model: Targeted Long-Horizon Robotic Manipulation:** Perhaps the most impactful contribution is the introduction and successful demonstration of **BackdoorVLA**. This attack moves beyond previous untargeted adversarial or backdoor attacks that merely disrupt VLA functionality or induce static states. BackdoorVLA proves that it is possible to compel a robot to execute a *precise, attacker-specified long-horizon sequence of actions* when a hidden trigger is present. This represents a far more sophisticated and dangerous threat. For instance, instead of merely making a robot drop an object, a targeted attack could force it to pick up a hazardous item and place it in a sensitive area, or perform a destructive sequence of movements. This revelation fundamentally changes the threat landscape for embodied AI, demanding a re-evaluation of safety and security measures.

3. **Validation of Real-World Vulnerabilities:** The successful real-world validation of BackdoorVLA (achieving 50% ASR_t on a physical Franka Emika arm) is critically important. It moves the discussion from theoretical vulnerabilities in simulation to tangible, practical risks in physical robotic systems. This bridges a significant gap in current research and provides irrefutable evidence that VLA security is not merely an academic exercise but an immediate and pressing concern for the safe deployment of robots. The fact that an attacker can subtly inject a trigger (e.g., a specific object and phrase) to hijack a robot's long-term behavior in the physical world has profound implications for industrial, medical, and domestic robotics.

4. **Motivation for Robustness and Defense Research:** By rigorously benchmarking existing attacks and demonstrating a powerful new one, the paper starkly highlights the current fragility of VLA models against malicious manipulation. This will undoubtedly catalyze intensified research efforts in several areas:
 - **Robust VLA Architectures:** Engineers and researchers will be motivated to design VLA models that are intrinsically more robust to both adversarial perturbations and backdoor injections, perhaps through novel architectural choices or training paradigms.
 - **Advanced Defense Mechanisms:** The limited effectiveness of current defense strategies against BackdoorVLA signals a clear need for developing more sophisticated and resilient countermeasures tailored to multimodal, embodied AI systems. These defenses must not only detect attacks but also maintain high clean performance.
 - **Trustworthy AI:** The work underscores the broader challenge of building trustworthy AI systems, particularly when those systems interact with the physical world. It prompts deeper considerations of accountability, transparency, and safety assurance in AI development.

5. **Ethical Implications for Embodied AI:** The ability to precisely control a robot through covert means raises significant ethical questions. As VLAs become more autonomous and integrated into daily life, understanding such vulnerabilities is essential for developing ethical guidelines, regulatory frameworks, and secure operational protocols to prevent misuse and ensure human safety.

In conclusion, "AttackVLA" and "BackdoorVLA" serve as foundational contributions that not only advance the scientific understanding of VLA security but also issue a stark warning about the practical threats facing embodied AI. By providing a unified framework and demonstrating a potent new attack, the authors have set a critical baseline for future research aimed at building safer, more resilient, and ultimately more trustworthy Vision-Language-Action systems.
