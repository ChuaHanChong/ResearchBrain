# BAKU: An Efficient Transformer for Multi-Task Policy Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> BAKU is a transformer-based architecture developed at New York University for efficient multi-task policy learning in robotics, designed to address data scarcity. It achieves an 18% absolute performance improvement across 129 simulated tasks and a 30-35% absolute improvement on 30 real-world manipulation tasks compared to prior state-of-the-art methods, utilizing substantially fewer demonstrations.

## Problem

- Robotics data acquisition is resource-intensive, leading to data scarcity for training generalist agents.
- Multi-task robotic policies often demand extensive datasets and can perform below single-task policies.
- Existing multi-task learning architectures sometimes exhibit inefficiencies and unnecessary complexity, particularly with limited training data.

## Method

- **BAKU** employs a modular transformer architecture featuring FiLM-conditioned visual encoders, a causal transformer decoder for its observation trunk, and a flexible action head.
- It integrates techniques like action chunking and temporal smoothing to enhance action prediction and ensure smooth robot control.
- The architecture systematically combines multi-sensory inputs (vision, proprioception) and task instructions (text/goal images) to learn generalist policies from offline imitation data.

## Results

- BAKU achieved a **90%** average success rate on LIBERO-90, representing a **36%** absolute improvement over prior state-of-the-art methods.
- On 30 real-world manipulation tasks, BAKU attained an **86%** success rate with an average of 17 demonstrations per task, improving to **91%** with a VQ-BeT action head.
- The model demonstrated an overall **18%** absolute performance improvement across 129 simulated tasks and a 30-**35%** absolute improvement on real-world tasks compared to existing baselines.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A transformer-based observation trunk yielded a 9% higher success rate than an MLP trunk, highlighting its effectiveness for complex multi-modal relationships.
> - Action chunking significantly improved manipulation task performance (14% increase) but showed a mild detriment for locomotion tasks.
> - FiLM-conditioned vision encoders effectively fuse visual and linguistic information, performing equally well or better than unconditional encoders for task conditioning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of generalist agents capable of solving diverse tasks represents a long-standing challenge in artificial intelligence and robotics. While fields such as computer vision and natural language processing have seen significant progress through large-scale data aggregation and transformer-based architectures, robotics lags behind. A primary reason for this disparity is the inherent difficulty and cost associated with acquiring large datasets for physical agents. Unlike internet-sourced data for vision and language, robotic data necessitates physical interaction with the environment, making it laborious and expensive to collect.

Current approaches to training multi-task robotic policies often involve collecting extensive datasets, frequently through teleoperation. However, policies trained on such large datasets can still exhibit inefficiencies, often performing below the level of independently trained single-task policies. This necessitates even more demonstration data to achieve acceptable performance.

This work addresses the problem of data inefficiency in multi-task robot policy learning. It positions itself within the context of recent advancements in offline imitation learning and multi-task learning, particularly those employing transformer architectures. Prior methods like RT-1 and MT-ACT have demonstrated the potential of transformers for multi-task robot control. BAKU builds upon these foundations by systematically integrating several architectural components—observation trunks, action chunking, multi-sensory observations, and diverse action heads—to enhance data efficiency and overall performance. The paper aims to simplify the architectural complexity often seen in multi-task learning by meticulously combining effective components, thereby advancing the state-of-the-art in learning generalist policies for robotics, especially in data-scarce scenarios.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an efficient transformer architecture, termed BAKU, for multi-task policy learning in robotics. The core motivation stems from the inherent challenges of training generalist agents for diverse tasks, particularly the significant data requirements in robotics.

Specific motivations and objectives include:

- **Addressing Data Scarcity in Robotics**: Robotics data acquisition is resource-intensive due to the need for physical interaction. Existing methods often demand large datasets to achieve multi-task capabilities, and even then, performance can be suboptimal compared to single-task policies. BAKU aims to provide highly efficient training, especially in data-limited problems, by effectively leveraging available training data.
- **Improving Multi-Task Policy Learning Performance**: The research seeks to overcome the performance gap between generalist multi-task policies and specialized single-task policies. By introducing a carefully designed architecture, BAKU intends to achieve superior success rates across a wide range of tasks compared to existing state-of-the-art multi-task learning algorithms.
- **Systematic Architectural Design and Evaluation**: The authors motivate a systematic approach to combining proven architectural elements. This involves dissecting the multi-task learning pipeline into sensory encoders, an observation trunk, and an action head. The objective is to identify and integrate components that collectively enhance multi-task decision-making without introducing unnecessary complexity. Key features proposed for BAKU include:
 - A transformer encoder to fuse multi-modal information (vision, language) and temporal context.
 - FiLM-conditioned visual encoders to enable task-specific representation learning by adapting to task instructions.
 - A separated action prediction head to allow easy integration and evaluation of various state-of-the-art action generation models, including those capable of multimodal action distributions.
- **Demonstrating Real-World Applicability**: A significant motivation is to validate the efficacy of the proposed architecture not only in diverse simulated environments but also on real-world robotic manipulation tasks. This includes demonstrating high success rates with limited real-world demonstrations, thereby showcasing BAKU's practical potential for deployment on physical robots.
- **Understanding Component Importance through Ablation**: A detailed ablation study is motivated to empirically determine the contribution of each architectural component (e.g., action chunking, observation history, specific action heads, goal modalities, FiLM conditioning) to overall multi-task performance. This objective aims to provide insights into effective design choices for future research in generalist robot learning.

In essence, the paper is driven by the need for more data-efficient and robust multi-task learning policies in robotics, achieved through a meticulously designed and experimentally validated transformer-based architecture.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology centers on the development and empirical validation of BAKU, a transformer-based architecture for multi-task policy learning. The approach begins by framing the problem within the context of offline imitation learning, specifically multi-task Behavior Cloning (BC). The policy is trained to mimic expert demonstrations, conditioned on a goal variable (e.g., text description or goal image).

The BAKU architecture is modularized into three primary components: sensory encoders, an observation trunk, and an action head, allowing for systematic design and evaluation.

#### 3.1. Architectural Components

- **Sensory Encoders**: These modules process raw sensor inputs from various modalities into feature representations.
 - **Vision**: A ResNet-18 visual encoder is employed to process scene images. This encoder is augmented with a FiLM (Feature-wise Linear Modulation) layer, which integrates task-specific information, such as language instructions, by applying learned scaling and shifting to the visual features. For multi-camera setups, a common visual encoder is used across all views to reduce parameter count.
 - **Robot Proprioception**: A two-layer Multilayer Perceptron (MLP) encoder processes robot state data (e.g., end-effector pose, gripper state).
 - **Task Instructions**: For text-based instructions, a 6-layer MiniLM model, pre-trained for sentence embeddings, is used. Goal images are also supported, obtained by sampling the last frame of a demonstration.
 - All representations from different modalities are projected to a common dimensionality using additional MLP layers for unified processing.
- **Observation Trunk**: This component combines the encoded information from various modalities. Two variants were explored:
 - **Multilayer Perceptron (MLP)**: Encoded inputs are concatenated into a single feature vector and passed through an MLP. When using historical observations, inputs from all time steps are concatenated.
 - **Transformer**: This is the default in BAKU. Each encoded input (from different modalities and optionally historical timesteps) is treated as an observation token and processed by a transformer decoder network. A learnable "action token" is appended to these observation tokens, and a causal mask is applied to ensure action predictions are based solely on past observations. The transformer outputs action feature vectors corresponding to these action tokens.
- **Action Head**: This final module takes the action feature vectors from the observation trunk and predicts the actual robot actions. Its independent design facilitates retrofitting with various state-of-the-art action generation models. Five variants were tested:
 - **Vanilla MLP**: A simple two-layer MLP. This is the base version for BAKU.
 - **Gaussian Mixture Model (GMM)**: Models actions as a mixture of Gaussians for multimodal continuous control.
 - **Behavior Transformer (BeT)**: Clusters actions into discrete bins and predicts both the cluster and an offset.
 - **Vector-Quantized Behavior Transformer (VQ-BeT)**: An extension of BeT that uses VQVAE-based tokenization for improved performance.
 - **Diffusion**: Models action prediction as an iterative denoising process, capable of generating actions from multimodal distributions.
 - **Action Chunking and Temporal Smoothing**: Following prior work, BAKU incorporates action chunking, where a chunk of future actions is predicted as a single concatenated vector. To enhance smoothness and counteract covariate shift, exponential temporal averaging is applied, combining multiple overlapping predictions from the policy.

#### 3.2. Final BAKU Architecture Configuration

The final BAKU architecture utilizes a FiLM-conditioned ResNet-18 for vision, an MLP for proprioception, and a pre-trained text encoder for instructions. The observation trunk is a causal transformer decoder. The base model uses an MLP action head, combined with action chunking and temporal smoothing. The model processes only the current observation. The total parameter count is approximately 10M.

#### 3.3. Experimental Setup

- **Simulated Environments**:
 - **LIBERO-90**: 90 manipulation tasks, 50 demonstrations per task, using third-person and gripper camera views, plus robot proprioception. Image size: 128x128.
 - **Meta-World**: 30 manipulation tasks, 35 demonstrations per task (from expert RL policies), using a single third-person view. Image size: 84x84.
 - **DeepMind Control (DMC)**: 9 locomotion tasks, 500 demonstrations per task (from expert RL policies), using state-based inputs.
 - Evaluations conducted using 10 policy rollouts per task.
- **Real-World Environment**:
 - **Hardware**: Ufactory xArm 7 robot with xArm Gripper in a multi-task kitchen setup.
 - **Data**: 30 diverse manipulation tasks, 520 total demonstrations (average 17 per task), collected via VR-based teleoperation at 30Hz.
 - **Inputs**: RGB images (128x128) from four camera views (including egocentric), robot end-effector pose, and gripper state.
 - **Control**: Policies deployed at 10Hz with a low-level Minimum-Jerk Controller at 100Hz.
 - Evaluations conducted with 5 runs per task (150 total runs per method).
- **Baselines**: RT-1 (transformer-based, discrete actions) and MT-ACT (transformer encoder-decoder, action chunking, CVAE for multimodality).
- **Ablation Studies**: Systematic variations of single components of BAKU (observation trunk, model size, action head, action chunking, observation history, goal modality, FiLM conditioning, shared vs. separate vision encoders, observation trunk input method) to assess their impact on multi-task performance.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted across simulated and real-world environments demonstrate the performance of BAKU and provide insights into its architectural components.

#### 4.1. Overall Performance Against Baselines

- **Simulated Tasks (129 tasks total)**:
 - BAKU achieved an overall 18% absolute performance improvement over prior state-of-the-art multi-task learning algorithms.
 - **LIBERO-90**: BAKU achieved a 90% average success rate, representing a 36% absolute improvement over prior work (e.g., 54% for MT-ACT, 16% for RT-1).
 - **Meta-World**: BAKU obtained a 79% success rate, a 14% absolute improvement over the strongest baseline.
 - **DeepMind Control (DMC)**: On locomotion tasks, BAKU achieved a 70% success rate, a 4% improvement over the strongest baseline.
- **Real-World Tasks (30 manipulation tasks)**:
 - With an average of 17 demonstrations per task, BAKU achieved an 86% success rate using an MLP action head.
 - When retrofitted with a VQ-BeT action head, the success rate further improved to 91%.
 - This represents a 30-35% absolute improvement over prior state-of-the-art algorithms (e.g., 56% for MT-ACT, 37% for RT-1).
- **Long-Horizon Tasks**:
 - BAKU demonstrated significantly higher success rates on long-horizon tasks, achieving an average of 19% higher success rate compared to MT-ACT on LIBERO-10 and real-world long-horizon tasks.

#### 4.2. Ablation Study Results

The ablation analysis provided insights into the impact of various design decisions:

- **Effect of Observation Trunk**: A transformer-based observation trunk yielded a 9% higher success rate on LIBERO-90 compared to an MLP trunk, indicating the transformer's effectiveness for complex multi-modal relationships.
- **Effect of Model Size**: Models with 4.4M, 10M, and 31M parameters showed similar performance. However, the largest 114M parameter model unexpectedly underperformed on LIBERO-90, suggesting potential overfitting with excessive capacity given the dataset size. The 10M parameter model was selected for BAKU due to its balance of size and performance.
- **Effect of Action Head**:
 - In simulated benchmarks, a simple MLP action head performed comparably to or better than other multimodal action heads.
 - In real-world experiments, a multimodal VQ-BeT action head demonstrated an advantage, achieving a 91% success rate, a 5% improvement over the MLP head. This suggests multimodal heads may be more beneficial for real-world scenarios with greater behavioral diversity.
- **Effect of Action Chunking**: Action chunking significantly benefited manipulation tasks, leading to a 14% performance increase on LIBERO-90. However, it showed a mild detriment (4% decrease) for locomotion tasks in DMC.
- **Effect of Observation History**: Naively using an observation history with loss computed only for the last time step degraded performance. Employing a multi-step prediction loss over all time steps in the history provided richer supervision and improved performance significantly (47% average). However, incorporating this history did not noticeably improve overall policy performance compared to using only the most recent observation, leading to the decision to use only the current observation in the final architecture.
- **Effect of Goal Modality**: Text instructions, goal images, and intermediate goal images exhibited similar performance with minor variations, indicating that BAKU is robust to different goal representations.
- **Effect of FiLM Conditioning**: FiLM-conditioned vision encoders performed equally well or better than unconditional encoders on image-based tasks, demonstrating its effectiveness in fusing visual and linguistic information for task conditioning.
- **Separate vs. Shared Vision Encoders (Additional Analysis)**: Using separate encoders for different camera views provided a minor 2% performance boost on LIBERO-90 but at the cost of a 15% parameter increase per view. A shared encoder was chosen for BAKU for compactness and faster inference.
- **Observation Trunk Input (Additional Analysis)**: Passing encoded observations as separate tokens to the transformer trunk resulted in a 3% absolute improvement on LIBERO-90 compared to concatenating all inputs into a single vector.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of BAKU represents a notable step in multi-task policy learning for robotics, addressing several critical challenges inherent in the field.

- **Enhanced Data Efficiency**: BAKU's ability to achieve high success rates with substantially fewer demonstrations (e.g., an average of 17 per task in real-world settings) is highly significant. This directly addresses the pervasive problem of data scarcity and the high cost of data collection in robotics, making advanced robotic capabilities more accessible and practical.
- **Improved Generalist Robot Capabilities**: The demonstrated performance improvements across a diverse range of 129 simulated tasks and 30 real-world manipulation tasks indicate that BAKU can effectively learn generalist policies. This advancement contributes to the long-term goal of creating versatile robots capable of performing multiple tasks in unstructured environments without extensive re-training.
- **Architectural Clarity and Insights**: The systematic design and comprehensive ablation studies provide valuable insights into which architectural components are most effective for multi-task policy learning. The findings regarding transformer trunks, action chunking for manipulation, the role of FiLM conditioning, and the specific benefits of multimodal action heads in real-world scenarios can guide future research and development in robot learning architectures. This clarity can accelerate the development of more robust and efficient learning systems.
- **Practical Real-World Deployment**: The high success rates achieved on real-world robotic tasks, even with limited demonstration data, highlight BAKU's potential for practical deployment. This moves the field closer to developing robotic systems that can be readily integrated into diverse applications, from industrial settings to home assistance, by reducing the dependency on massive, task-specific datasets.
- **Foundation for Future Research**: BAKU establishes a strong baseline for future work in multi-task robot learning. It opens avenues for exploring techniques to handle varying task difficulties within a single policy, chaining multiple skills for truly long-horizon tasks, and conducting further studies on the generalization benefits that emerge with increased task diversity and data scale.

While the paper acknowledges limitations such as struggles with precise manipulation, focus on single skills, and lack of generalization analysis with task scale, BAKU's contributions significantly advance the state of multi-task robot policy learning by offering an efficient, high-performing, and architecturally insightful approach.
