# BAKU: An Efficient Transformer for Multi-Task Policy Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> BAKU is a transformer-based architecture developed at New York University for efficient multi-task policy learning in robotics, designed to address data scarcity. It achieves an 18% absolute performance improvement across 129 simulated tasks and a 30-35% absolute improvement on 30 real-world manipulation tasks compared to prior state-of-the-art methods, utilizing substantially fewer demonstrations.

## Problem

- Robotics data acquisition is resource-intensive, leading to data scarcity for training generalist agents.
- Multi-task robotic policies often demand extensive datasets and can perform below single-task policies.
- Existing multi-task learning architectures sometimes exhibit inefficiencies and unnecessary complexity, particularly with limited training data.

## Method

- **BAKU** employs a modular transformer architecture featuring FiLM-conditioned visual encoders, a causal transformer decoder for its observation trunk, and a flexible action head.
- It integrates techniques like action chunking and temporal smoothing to enhance action prediction and ensure smooth robot control.
- The architecture systematically combines multi-sensory inputs (vision, proprioception) and task instructions (text/goal images) to learn generalist policies from offline imitation data.

## Results

- BAKU achieved a **90%** average success rate on LIBERO-90, representing a **36%** absolute improvement over prior state-of-the-art methods.
- On 30 real-world manipulation tasks, BAKU attained an **86%** success rate with an average of 17 demonstrations per task, improving to **91%** with a VQ-BeT action head.
- The model demonstrated an overall **18%** absolute performance improvement across 129 simulated tasks and a 30-**35%** absolute improvement on real-world tasks compared to existing baselines.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A transformer-based observation trunk yielded a 9% higher success rate than an MLP trunk, highlighting its effectiveness for complex multi-modal relationships.
> - Action chunking significantly improved manipulation task performance (14% increase) but showed a mild detriment for locomotion tasks.
> - FiLM-conditioned vision encoders effectively fuse visual and linguistic information, performing equally well or better than unconditional encoders for task conditioning.
