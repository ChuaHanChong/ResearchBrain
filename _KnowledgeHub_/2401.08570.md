# RoHM: Robust Human Motion Reconstruction via Diffusion

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from ETH Zurich and Meta Reality Labs developed RoHM, a diffusion-based framework that robustly reconstructs 3D human motion from noisy and occluded monocular videos. The system significantly reduces Global Mean Per-Joint Position Error for occluded parts by over 48% and demonstrates 30 times faster inference compared to prior optimization-based methods, while generating physically plausible motions.

## Problem

- Existing regression methods yield only local pose estimates, lacking global coherence and robustness to occlusions.
- Optimization-based techniques are computationally expensive, prone to local minima, require manual tuning, and struggle with long-term occlusions.
- Prior diffusion models primarily focused on motion synthesis from clean inputs, not robust reconstruction from noisy, real-world video.

## Method

- **RoHM** decouples reconstruction into a global trajectory ==diffusion model== (**TrajNet**) and local pose ==diffusion model== (**PoseNet**), introducing **TrajControl** to fine-tune TrajNet using PoseNet's denoised pose for physical plausibility.
- An ==iterative inference scheme== is employed, progressively refining global and local motion by leveraging outputs from previous iterations.
- ==Score-guided sampling==, incorporating ==foot skating penalties== and ==2D reprojection consistency==, further enhances realism during the diffusion process.

## Results

- RoHM achieved over a **48%** reduction in Global Mean Per-Joint Position Error (GMPJPE) for occluded body parts compared to the HuMoR baseline on the AMASS dataset.
- It demonstrated **30 times** faster inference speed compared to optimization-based methods like HuMoR, enhancing practical applicability.
- The framework produced more physically plausible motions, evidenced by a **44%** improvement in foot skating metrics and reduced acceleration errors across multiple real-world datasets (PROX, EgoBody).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decomposing the complex 3D human motion reconstruction into decoupled global and local diffusion models, with iterative refinement via explicit correlation between global trajectory and local pose (TrajControl), allows for specialized yet interdependent, robust processing.
> - Diffusion models can effectively serve as powerful data-driven priors for reconstruction from noisy and incomplete visual evidence, rather than solely for generation.
> - Incorporating physics-based and image-based guidance during diffusion sampling significantly improves accuracy and realism without sacrificing motion smoothness.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

3D human motion reconstruction from monocular video is a fundamental problem in computer vision with vast applications in augmented/virtual reality (AR/VR), robotics, human-computer interaction, surveillance, and sports analysis. The field has seen significant advancements, largely categorized into two main approaches: regression-based and optimization-based methods.

**Regression-based methods** typically train deep neural networks to directly predict 3D body pose and shape parameters from individual video frames. While fast, these methods often struggle with several key issues:
1. **Local Motion:** They tend to estimate only local body pose relative to a root joint, failing to recover a globally consistent and plausible trajectory in world coordinates over time. This leads to jitter and unrealistic global movement.
2. **Robustness to Occlusion:** They lack robustness when parts of the human body are occluded, spatially (e.g., by objects or other people) or temporally (e.g., when a person temporarily leaves the frame).

**Optimization-based methods**, in contrast, fit a parametric body model (like SMPL-X) to observations (e.g., 2D keypoints, depth, silhouettes) by iteratively minimizing an objective function. These methods often incorporate data-driven motion priors to regularize the motion and improve temporal consistency. Examples like HuMoR and PhaseMP have shown better performance under noisy and incomplete input. However, they suffer from their own set of drawbacks:
1. **Computational Cost:** They are generally time-consuming due to iterative optimization at test time.
2. **Local Minima:** Optimization processes are prone to getting stuck in local minima, leading to sub-optimal solutions.
3. **Manual Tuning:** They often require significant manual tuning of regularization weights and parameters.
4. **Long-term Occlusion:** Even advanced optimization methods struggle under heavy or long-duration occlusions, as their motion priors (often learned from frame-to-frame transitions) may not capture long-range temporal dependencies effectively.

**Diffusion models** have recently emerged as powerful generative models, particularly in image synthesis, and are now gaining traction in 3D human motion generation. These models excel at learning complex data distributions and performing iterative denoising. Prior diffusion-based approaches for human motion primarily focused on *synthesizing* motion from abstract inputs like text or action labels, or from sparse, *noise-free* keypoints. They were not typically designed for robust *reconstruction* from noisy and occluded monocular video, where the output must faithfully adhere to available image evidence.

RoHM critically addresses this gap. It positions itself as a pioneering work that effectively leverages the iterative, generative nature of diffusion models for the *reconstruction* task, directly tackling the challenges of noise, occlusions, and global motion consistency in real-world video inputs. By adopting a diffusion-based framework, RoHM aims to combine the data-driven learning capabilities of neural networks with the iterative refinement strengths traditionally associated with optimization, but without its associated costs and pitfalls. It represents a significant step towards enabling physically plausible and globally coherent 3D human motion estimation in highly challenging, uncontrolled environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of RoHM is to achieve **robust 3D human motion reconstruction** from monocular RGB(-D) videos, specifically addressing the pervasive issues of **noise and occlusions** inherent in real-world scenarios.

The motivation for RoHM stems directly from the shortcomings of existing methodologies:

1. **Limitations of Regression-based Methods:** Current neural network regressors, while fast, typically produce only local motion (pose relative to the body root) and lack robustness when body parts are occluded. They fail to generate globally coherent and plausible movement consistent over time, leading to unnatural jitter and drift. This limitation makes them unsuitable for applications requiring physically realistic and globally consistent human avatars in dynamic environments.
2. **Drawbacks of Optimization-based Methods:** While optimization methods often yield more accurate local poses and can incorporate motion priors, they are computationally intensive, slow at test time, and frequently fall into local minima, requiring expert tuning. Their ability to handle long-term occlusions is also limited, as many rely on priors that model only short-range temporal dependencies.
3. **Untapped Potential of Diffusion Models for Reconstruction:** Diffusion models have demonstrated remarkable success in generative tasks, particularly in modeling complex distributions and performing iterative refinement through a denoising process. This iterative nature makes them a natural, data-driven alternative to the iterative minimization in optimization methods. However, their application in *reconstructing* noisy and incomplete human motion from real video, while ensuring adherence to observed evidence, remained largely unexplored. Prior diffusion models for motion focused on synthesis from clean or high-level inputs, not robust reconstruction from raw, corrupted video data.

Therefore, RoHM is motivated by the desire to develop a method that:
- Can reconstruct **complete, plausible motions in consistent global coordinates**.
- Is **robust to significant noise and occlusions** (both spatial and temporal).
- Is **efficient at test time**, overcoming the speed limitations of optimization.
- Leverages the **powerful generative and denoising capabilities of diffusion models** in a novel reconstruction context.
- Can **address different sub-problems** (denoising, infilling) in distinct solution spaces (local and global motion) while capturing their interdependencies.

By achieving these objectives, RoHM aims to unlock new possibilities for accurate and realistic 3D human motion capture in challenging real-world settings.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

RoHM's methodology is built upon a novel application of diffusion models to robustly reconstruct 3D human motion. It tackles the complexity of the problem by decomposing it into manageable sub-tasks and introducing mechanisms to ensure global coherence and physical plausibility.

#### 3.1. Motion Representation

The method represents a human motion sequence using the **SMPL-X parametric body model**. Each frame's motion (x_n) is defined by two main components:
- **Global Trajectory Features (R):** Includes 2D root (pelvis) linear position (r_l), root angular rotation (r_a), root height (r_z), SMPL-X global translation (γ), and global orientation (Φ). Velocities (first derivatives) of these features are also included.
- **Local Body Features (P):** Comprises local joint positions (J) for 21 body joints, joint rotations (θ) using a 6D representation, body shape parameters (β), and foot contact labels (f) indicating ground contact.

This over-parameterized representation allows explicit modeling of both 3D skeleton joints and SMPL-X meshes, enabling mutual benefits in learning. Root and local joint visibility masks (M_R, M_P) are used to denote visible (1) or occluded (0) parts.

#### 3.2. Decoupled Diffusion Models: TrajNet and PoseNet

Recognizing the challenge of simultaneously reconstructing global trajectory and local articulated pose, RoHM decomposes the problem into two distinct sub-tasks, each handled by a dedicated diffusion model:

- **TrajNet (Global Trajectory Reconstruction):** This network, a U-Net encoder-decoder architecture, is trained to denoise and recover a smooth and plausible global root trajectory ($\hat{R}_0$). It is conditioned on the noisy initial root trajectory ($\tilde{R}$) and its occlusion mask ($M_R$). Importantly, it focuses on root position and orientation parameters, excluding velocities to prevent global drifting from inaccurate velocity estimates.
- **PoseNet (Local Motion Prediction):** This network, based on a transformer encoder, is responsible for denoising and infilling local body motion ($\hat{P}_0$). It is conditioned on the denoised trajectory ($\hat{R}_0$) obtained from TrajNet, the noisy initial local motion ($\tilde{P}$), and the local joint occlusion mask ($M_P$). Using TrajNet's output as a condition helps integrate global context into local pose predictions.

#### 3.3. Controlling Global Motion Reconstruction with TrajControl

A key innovation is **TrajControl**, an auxiliary module designed to address the observed lack of correlation capture between the independently trained TrajNet and PoseNet. Under significant noise, TrajNet alone might produce physically implausible trajectories, particularly with foot skating.
- **Mechanism:** TrajControl fine-tunes TrajNet by providing additional control signals derived from the denoised local body pose from PoseNet. It is implemented by cloning and making trainable the encoder layers of a pre-trained, frozen TrajNet encoder, denoted as E(·). Zero convolution layers connect E(·) to the frozen TrajNet decoder, ensuring a smooth start.
- **Benefit:** This allows TrajNet to improve its trajectory estimation based on physically more plausible local poses from PoseNet, and in turn, the refined trajectory aids PoseNet in further improving local motion.

#### 3.4. Iterative Inference Scheme

At inference time, TrajNet, PoseNet, and TrajControl are employed in an iterative scheme (Algorithm 1) to progressively refine the motion:
1. **Initial Pass (i=1):** TrajNet uses only the noisy input root trajectory and its mask. PoseNet then uses this initial TrajNet output along with noisy local motion and its mask.
2. **Subsequent Iterations (i>1):** TrajNet is conditioned on the *estimated trajectory and local pose from the previous iteration* as an additional control signal via TrajControl E(·). PoseNet then uses the *current* TrajNet output and the *previous* PoseNet output.
This iterative refinement, typically run for K=2 iterations, effectively leverages the learned correlations between global and local motion spaces.

#### 3.5. Score-Guided Sampling

To further enhance physical plausibility and accuracy, RoHM incorporates **score-guided sampling** during PoseNet's denoising process, particularly for aligning with image evidence and preventing foot skating:
- **Foot Skating Penalty (J_skate):** Penalizes foot joint velocity when the foot is predicted to be in contact with the ground, promoting realistic foot-ground interactions.
- **2D Reprojection Consistency (J_2D):** Guides the sampling to align the reconstructed 3D joints with 2D keypoint detections from input images (e.g., OpenPose), using camera intrinsics.
These score functions provide gradients that steer the diffusion sampling towards more plausible and image-consistent motions.

#### 3.6. Training

- **Objective:** Both TrajNet and PoseNet are trained using the `L_simple` objective (standard for DDPMs) combined with additional losses: `L_J3D` (3D joint position consistency), `L_vel` (3D joint velocity consistency), and `L_skate` (foot skating penalty).
- **Data:** Training is performed on the AMASS dataset, with motion sequences clipped to 144 frames.
- **Data Augmentation:** To simulate real-world noise and occlusions, ground-truth motion is corrupted by adding Gaussian noise to SMPL-X parameters and randomly masking out joints or entire subsequences of frames.
- **Curriculum Training:** A curriculum learning scheme is employed, gradually increasing noise levels and occlusion rates as training progresses, enabling the models to first learn easier tasks before tackling more challenging scenarios.
- **TrajControl Fine-tuning:** TrajControl is fine-tuned after the "vanilla" TrajNet is trained, using ground-truth local pose as the control input.

This comprehensive approach, combining decoupled diffusion models, a novel conditioning strategy, iterative inference, and physics-aware guidance, allows RoHM to achieve robust and plausible 3D human motion reconstruction under challenging conditions.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

RoHM demonstrates state-of-the-art performance, both qualitatively and quantitatively, across various challenging scenarios involving noisy and occluded monocular RGB(-D) video inputs.

#### 4.1. Superior Accuracy and Physical Plausibility on AMASS (3D Observations)

Experiments on the AMASS dataset, which involved controlled scenarios of motion denoising and infilling (e.g., masking out lower body joints or entire subsequences), showed RoHM's significant advantage:
- **Accuracy:** RoHM achieved a `>= 48% reduction in GMPJPE` (Global Mean Per-Joint Position Error) for occluded body parts compared to HuMoR, a strong optimization-based baseline. This indicates much more accurate reconstruction, especially for non-visible body parts.
- **Physical Plausibility:** RoHM drastically reduced acceleration errors, suggesting more realistic motion dynamics. It also improved foot-ground contact predictions, leading to `>= 44% improvement in foot skating` (lower ratio) and fewer foot-ground inter-penetrations (lower Dist) compared to HuMoR.
- **Robustness to Noise:** RoHM exhibited greater robustness to increasing input noise levels compared to HuMoR, whose performance substantially declined with higher noise. This highlights the effectiveness of the diffusion-based denoising process.
- **Comparison to MDM++:** An adapted diffusion baseline (MDM++) performed similarly to RoHM in terms of foot-ground collisions but was noticeably inferior in overall reconstruction accuracy and other plausibility metrics, underscoring the benefits of RoHM's decoupled approach.

#### 4.2. Robustness in Real-World RGB(-D) Videos (PROX and EgoBody)

RoHM's capabilities extend to real-world videos, demonstrating robust reconstruction from RGB-D and RGB inputs:
- **PROX Dataset (RGB-D/RGB):** RoHM significantly outperformed both per-frame regressors (CLIFF, Ours-init) and optimization-based methods (VPoser-t, LEMO, HuMoR, PhaseMP) in terms of physical plausibility metrics (Skating, ∥Accel∥, Dist). Even starting from a more challenging initialization, RoHM produced smoother motions with improved foot-ground interactions and realistic movements for occluded parts.
- **EgoBody Dataset (RGB):** In highly occluded and challenging scenarios, RoHM consistently outperformed baselines across all metrics (GMPJPE, MPJPE, Accel, Skating, Dist), even when starting from the same VPoser-t initialization as HuMoR. This indicates its ability to generate more plausible motions in complex interaction scenes.

#### 4.3. Computational Efficiency

A notable finding is RoHM's speed advantage: it is **30 times faster at inference time** compared to the optimization-based HuMoR, factoring out the initialization stage. This addresses a critical limitation of previous state-of-the-art methods and makes RoHM more practical for real-time or near real-time applications.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies Confirm Key Components

Ablation studies confirmed the importance of RoHM's key architectural and inference components:
- **TrajControl and Iterative Inference:** The iterative inference scheme leveraging TrajControl effectively alleviated foot skating and improved motion plausibility, especially under high noise. Running iterations without TrajControl showed some improvement but was suboptimal, proving the value of the pose-trajectory correlation captured by TrajControl.
- **Score-Guided Sampling:** Test-time guidance using physics-based (foot skating) and image-based (2D reprojection) scores further improved result-observation alignment and reduced foot skating, with minimal impact on motion smoothness.

In summary, RoHM successfully leverages the power of diffusion models to overcome long-standing challenges in 3D human motion reconstruction, delivering superior accuracy, physical plausibility, and computational efficiency in diverse and challenging real-world settings.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

RoHM represents a significant advance in the field of 3D human motion reconstruction, offering several key contributions and potential impacts:

#### 5.1. Novel Application of Diffusion Models for Reconstruction

Traditionally, diffusion models have excelled in generative tasks. RoHM is among the first to successfully adapt and apply these powerful generative priors to the *reconstruction* of 3D human motion from noisy and occluded monocular videos. This opens a new paradigm for solving inverse problems in computer vision, combining the data-driven learning of neural networks with the iterative refinement of generative processes.

#### 5.2. Overcoming Limitations of Prior Methods

- **Robustness to Occlusions and Noise:** RoHM directly addresses the critical weaknesses of both regression-based methods (which struggle with occlusions and global coherence) and optimization-based methods (which are prone to local minima and slow inference). Its diffusion-based approach, coupled with decoupled global/local motion modeling, provides unprecedented robustness to varying levels of noise and complex occlusions (spatial and temporal).
- **Global Coherence and Physical Plausibility:** By explicitly modeling and iteratively refining both global root trajectory and local body pose, and incorporating physics-based guidance, RoHM produces motions that are not only accurate but also physically plausible and globally consistent—a long-standing challenge in the field. The reduction in foot skating and improved acceleration errors are direct evidence of this.
- **Inference Speed:** The significant speedup (30x faster than HuMoR) makes RoHM much more practical for real-world applications that demand efficient processing, moving beyond the bottleneck of test-time optimization.

#### 5.3. Enhanced Capabilities for Diverse Applications

The ability to reconstruct robust, plausible, and globally coherent 3D human motion from challenging video inputs has broad implications for numerous domains:
- **Augmented and Virtual Reality (AR/VR):** Enables more realistic and immersive avatars that accurately reflect user movements, even when partially obscured. This is crucial for natural interactions in virtual environments.
- **Robotics:** Provides more accurate human pose and motion understanding for human-robot collaboration, improving safety and efficiency.
- **Human-Computer Interaction:** Allows for more natural and intuitive interfaces that respond to complex human gestures and body language.
- **Computer Graphics and Animation:** Offers powerful tools for synthesizing and retargeting human motion, reducing manual effort and improving realism.
- **Sports Analysis and Biomechanics:** Facilitates precise motion analysis from consumer-grade cameras, without the need for specialized mocap setups.

#### 5.4. Foundation for Future Research

RoHM lays a strong foundation for future research directions. The authors acknowledge limitations, such as the current lack of real-time performance and the need to incorporate 3D environment conditions for better human-scene interaction. Extending the model to capture fine-grained details like facial expressions and articulated hand poses is also an exciting avenue. The modular design (TrajNet, PoseNet, TrajControl) offers flexibility for future improvements and adaptations, such as exploring different architectures for efficiency or integrating more complex physics.

In conclusion, RoHM significantly advances the state-of-the-art in 3D human motion reconstruction by effectively harnessing the power of diffusion models. It provides a robust, accurate, plausible, and efficient solution to a critical problem, paving the way for more immersive, intelligent, and interactive human-centric AI applications.
