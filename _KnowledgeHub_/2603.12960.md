# Efficient Real-World Autonomous Racing via Attenuated Residual Policy Optimization

> [!summary] Summary
> Attenuated Residual Policy Optimization (α-RPO) extends Residual Policy Learning by gradually phasing out a base policy's influence during training to create a standalone neural controller. This approach enabled a 1:10-scaled autonomous racing robot to achieve over 12% faster lap times on a real-world track and robust zero-shot sim-to-real transfer with a 3.5 ms inference latency on embedded systems.

## Problem

- Traditional Residual Policy Learning (RPL) methods require the continuous execution of a base policy during deployment, increasing computational load, system complexity, and inference latency on resource-constrained hardware.
- Static mixing factors in existing RPL approaches can prevent the learned agent from exploring optimal control strategies that significantly diverge from the base policy's behavior.
- Deploying Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL) in real-world autonomous systems, like racing, faces challenges of robust sim-to-real transfer and the need for efficient, standalone controllers.

## Method

- Attenuated Residual Policy Optimization (α-RPO) introduces a linear, iteration-dependent attenuation factor to dynamically blend a classical base policy with a learned residual policy during training.
- The base policy's influence is progressively reduced over training iterations, ultimately yielding a purely neural, standalone policy for efficient real-world deployment.
- A "synchronization trick" is integrated into the Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) framework to maintain training stability despite the non-stationary environment introduced by the dynamic attenuation factor.

## Results

- α-RPO agents achieved the lowest average total race times (**46.24** s in training, **47.11** s on unseen test tracks) in simulation, consistently outperforming standard RPL and DRL baselines.
- The method demonstrated robust zero-shot sim-to-real transfer, successfully deploying on a physical Roboracer car with a low inference latency of **3.5** ms on embedded hardware.
- Real-world experiments on a SLAM-based map of the Munich track showed α-RPO improved lap times by over **12%** compared to a classical Stanley controller, showcasing optimized trajectories and effective obstacle avoidance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Gradually phasing out the base policy allows the DRL agent to both leverage strong inductive bias for initial learning stability and subsequently discover more optimal, aggressive control behaviors.
> - The synchronization trick is crucial for stabilizing training against the non-stationarity introduced by the continuously changing base policy contribution.
> - Architectural choices like LayerNorm within the FusionBlock and AdaptiveSpatialSoftmax1d for LiDAR feature reduction are critical for robust performance and effective generalization.
