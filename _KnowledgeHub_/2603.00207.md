# VisRef: Visual Refocusing while Thinking Improves Test-Time Scaling in Multi-Modal Large Reasoning Models

> [!summary] Summary
> VisRef introduces a training-free framework that dynamically re-injects relevant visual tokens into Multi-modal Large Reasoning Models during inference, effectively countering visual token dilution and boosting reasoning accuracy. The approach leverages Determinantal Point Processes for visual token selection and an adaptive stopping criterion, yielding consistent performance improvements across various benchmarks.

## Problem

- Multi-modal Large Reasoning Models (MLRMs) experience "visual token dilution," where the influence of visual input diminishes during extended textual reasoning.
- This dilution causes MLRMs to increasingly rely on textual priors rather than visual grounding, leading to potential performance degradation and visual hallucinations.
- Prior test-time scaling methods for MLRMs extend textual reasoning but do not actively maintain visual grounding, failing to address the fading visual information problem.

## Method

- VisRef proposes a training-free framework that iteratively selects and re-injects a coreset of relevant visual tokens at each step of an MLRM's reasoning process.
- It utilizes ==Determinantal Point Processes (DPPs)== to select visual tokens that optimally balance relevance to the current textual reasoning state with visual diversity.
- An ==adaptive stopping criterion==, based on the entropy of the model's predicted answer distribution, efficiently determines when to terminate reasoning and provide a final answer.

## Results

- VisRef consistently achieved substantial accuracy improvements, for example, boosting InternVL-3.5-8B's performance on MathVista by **5.4%** absolutely over standard thinking and **4.5%** over textual self-reflection.
- The framework demonstrated superior test-time scaling, delivering higher accuracy for equivalent computational budgets compared to parallel text-only reasoning across all evaluated benchmarks.
- When combined with training-based methods like "Look-Back," VisRef produced the best overall performance, achieving **83.1%** on MathVista, highlighting its additive value.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Dynamically refocusing visual attention at test time can significantly improve MLRM performance on visual reasoning tasks without requiring additional model training.
> - The use of Determinantal Point Processes provides a principled method for selecting visual tokens that are both relevant to the ongoing reasoning and diverse in their visual coverage.
> - VisRef offers complementary gains and can be combined with existing training-based methods, indicating its potential as an orthogonal enhancement for improving MLRMs.
