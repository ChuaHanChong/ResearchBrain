# VisRef: Visual Refocusing while Thinking Improves Test-Time Scaling in Multi-Modal Large Reasoning Models

> [!summary] Summary
> VisRef introduces a training-free framework that dynamically re-injects relevant visual tokens into Multi-modal Large Reasoning Models during inference, effectively countering visual token dilution and boosting reasoning accuracy. The approach leverages Determinantal Point Processes for visual token selection and an adaptive stopping criterion, yielding consistent performance improvements across various benchmarks.

## Problem

- Multi-modal Large Reasoning Models (MLRMs) experience "visual token dilution," where the influence of visual input diminishes during extended textual reasoning.
- This dilution causes MLRMs to increasingly rely on textual priors rather than visual grounding, leading to potential performance degradation and visual hallucinations.
- Prior test-time scaling methods for MLRMs extend textual reasoning but do not actively maintain visual grounding, failing to address the fading visual information problem.

## Method

- VisRef proposes a training-free framework that iteratively selects and re-injects a coreset of relevant visual tokens at each step of an MLRM's reasoning process.
- It utilizes ==Determinantal Point Processes (DPPs)== to select visual tokens that optimally balance relevance to the current textual reasoning state with visual diversity.
- An ==adaptive stopping criterion==, based on the entropy of the model's predicted answer distribution, efficiently determines when to terminate reasoning and provide a final answer.

## Results

- VisRef consistently achieved substantial accuracy improvements, for example, boosting InternVL-3.5-8B's performance on MathVista by **5.4%** absolutely over standard thinking and **4.5%** over textual self-reflection.
- The framework demonstrated superior test-time scaling, delivering higher accuracy for equivalent computational budgets compared to parallel text-only reasoning across all evaluated benchmarks.
- When combined with training-based methods like "Look-Back," VisRef produced the best overall performance, achieving **83.1%** on MathVista, highlighting its additive value.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Dynamically refocusing visual attention at test time can significantly improve MLRM performance on visual reasoning tasks without requiring additional model training.
> - The use of Determinantal Point Processes provides a principled method for selecting visual tokens that are both relevant to the ongoing reasoning and diverse in their visual coverage.
> - VisRef offers complementary gains and can be combined with existing training-based methods, indicating its potential as an orthogonal enhancement for improving MLRMs.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated at the intersection of Multi-modal Large Reasoning Models (MLRMs), Vision-Language Models (VLMs), and strategies for enhancing their reasoning capabilities, particularly during inference. The broader landscape has seen significant advancements with the adaptation of Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning from Large Language Models (LLMs) to multi-modal contexts, giving rise to Multimodal Chain-of-Thought (MCoT). These models, by generating explicit "thinking traces," have achieved impressive performance on complex tasks requiring mathematical, scientific, and general multi-modal understanding.

However, a critical challenge for MLRMs, especially in vision-dependent tasks, is the phenomenon of "visual token dilution." As MLRMs engage in extended textual reasoning, their attention to the visual input progressively diminishes. Visual tokens become diluted within the expanding context window, causing the model to increasingly rely on textual priors rather than grounding its reasoning in the actual image content. This can lead to performance degradation, visual hallucinations, and a lack of robustness in complex visual reasoning scenarios. This issue stands in contrast to human problem-solving, where individuals naturally and adaptively re-examine visual details as their reasoning unfolds.

Prior attempts to address this visual dilution fall into two main categories:
1. **Reinforcement Learning (RL)-based Fine-tuning:** Methods like "look-back" or "reflect" mechanisms train models to explicitly revisit or re-route visual tokens during inference. While effective, these approaches are computationally expensive, require extensive annotated data generation, and complex policy optimization, limiting their scalability and generalizability.
2. **Test-Time Scaling:** These methods allocate additional inference compute to refine reasoning, often by generating longer textual reasoning chains or employing self-verification. However, existing test-time scaling approaches are predominantly text-centric. They extend textual reasoning but do not actively maintain visual grounding, meaning the problem of visual information fading persists as reasoning chains grow longer.

VisRef positions itself directly in this gap. It aims to develop a *training-free* approach to restore visual grounding *entirely at test time*, bypassing the computational costs and data requirements of RL-based fine-tuning. By providing a mechanism for *adaptive visual refocusing* during inference, VisRef seeks to combine the benefits of extended reasoning (test-time scaling) with sustained visual attention, thereby offering a practical and efficient solution to a critical limitation of current MLRMs. This work contributes to the ongoing effort to make MLRMs more robust, efficient, and human-like in their reasoning processes.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation for VisRef stems from a fundamental limitation observed in Multi-modal Large Reasoning Models (MLRMs): their tendency to lose visual grounding during extended textual reasoning. Despite their advanced capabilities, MLRMs often suffer from "visual token dilution," where visual information's influence diminishes as reasoning chains grow longer, leading to a reliance on textual priors and potential performance degradation on vision-dependent tasks.

The key objectives of this research are formulated to directly address this critical problem:

1. **Restore Visual Grounding at Test Time:** The primary objective is to develop a mechanism that can actively guide the reasoning process by re-injecting relevant visual information, thereby maintaining strong visual grounding throughout the entire reasoning trajectory. This is motivated by the observation that current MLRMs lack the human-like feedback loop where visual details are re-examined as reasoning progresses.

2. **Achieve Training-Free Visual Refocusing:** A crucial objective is to accomplish this visual refocusing entirely at test time, without requiring any additional fine-tuning, large-scale data generation, or reinforcement learning policy optimization. This aims to overcome the computational expense and data curation challenges associated with prior RL-based "look-back" mechanisms. The goal is a "plug-and-play" solution that can be applied to any pre-trained MLRM.

3. **Optimize Visual Token Selection:** Given that naively re-injecting all visual tokens is computationally prohibitive and can introduce redundancy, a key objective is to intelligently select a *coreset* of visual tokens. This subset must be semantically relevant to the current reasoning context while also being diverse and globally representative of the image, ensuring maximum informative coverage without unnecessary overhead.

4. **Implement an Adaptive Stopping Criterion:** To prevent unbounded computation and potential "overthinking" while ensuring complete reasoning, the framework must incorporate a mechanism to adaptively determine when to terminate the reasoning process and produce a final answer. This criterion should ideally reflect the model's confidence in its current reasoning state.

In essence, VisRef is motivated by the desire to leverage the benefits of extended test-time computation for MLRMs while simultaneously counteracting the adverse effects of visual dilution, doing so in a computationally efficient and training-free manner. The ambition is to make MLRMs more robust, accurate, and truly multimodal in their reasoning, mimicking human cognitive strategies more closely.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

VisRef proposes a novel, training-free framework designed to actively guide the reasoning process of MLRMs by dynamically re-injecting relevant visual tokens at each step. This approach directly addresses the problem of visual token dilution without requiring any model retraining. The methodology revolves around two core components: intelligent visual token selection and an adaptive stopping criterion.

#### 3.1. Visual Token Selection: Identifying Optimal Visual Token Subsets

The core challenge here is to select a compact subset of visual tokens (`V_k`) at each reasoning step `k` that is both relevant to the current textual reasoning state (`z_k`) and diverse enough to provide comprehensive visual coverage.

1. **Problem Formulation:** The ideal `V_k` would maximize the expected reward of the final answer. However, directly optimizing this is intractable at test time due to unknown future rewards and the combinatorial explosion of possible subsets and future trajectories.
2. **Tractable Proxy Objective:** VisRef adopts a Markov assumption, where the utility of `V_k` at step `k` primarily depends on the current reasoning state `z_k`. This simplifies the objective to selecting `V_k` that maximizes a scoring function `J(V_k | x_input, z_k)`.
3. **Designing the Scoring Function using Determinantal Point Processes (DPPs):**
 - **Intuition:** The selected coreset should span the largest "volume" in the feature space, balancing relevance and diversity.
 - **Textual Subspace:** The current textual reasoning step `z_k` (represented as a set of token embeddings) defines a reasoning subspace. A matrix `M_k` is constructed by summing outer products of `z_k`'s token embeddings (`M_k = Σ z_k^(j) (z_k^(j))^T`). This `M_k` captures the geometry of the current reasoning context.
 - **Visual-Text Alignment Kernel:** A positive semi-definite kernel `L_k(v_i, v_j) = ϕ_k(v_i)^T ϕ_k(v_j)` is defined. The projection `ϕ_k(v) = M_k^(1/2) v` embeds visual token `v` into a feature space aligned with `z_k`. This kernel measures how similar two visual tokens `v_i, v_j` appear when projected into the textual subspace.
 - **DPP Objective:** The scoring function `J(V_k | x_input, z_k)` is defined as the determinant of the kernel matrix restricted to subset `V_k`: `det(L_V_k_k)`. Maximizing this determinant naturally balances relevance (high diagonal values, `v_i^T M_k v_i`, measuring alignment with `z_k`) and diversity (penalizing redundancy through off-diagonal terms, ensuring selected tokens are mutually distinct). The objective decomposes into a sum of `log(r_i^2)` (relevance term) and `log det(normalized_L_V_k_k)` (diversity term).
4. **Efficient Inference-Time Solution:** Since maximizing the determinant is NP-hard, VisRef employs a greedy selection algorithm. Given a fixed token budget `m` (number of visual tokens to select), it iteratively adds the token that provides the maximum marginal gain to the log-determinant objective.

#### 3.2. Adaptive Stopping Criterion

To manage computational overhead and prevent "overthinking," VisRef implements an adaptive mechanism to decide when to stop reasoning and generate the final answer.

1. **Confidence Measurement:** At each step `k`, after integrating the selected visual tokens, the model's certainty is assessed by measuring the entropy of its predicted answer distribution, `H_k`.
2. **Threshold-Based Termination:** If `H_k` falls below a predefined threshold `δ_entropy` (indicating high confidence), reasoning terminates. Otherwise, the process continues to the next step with renewed visual grounding.
3. **Maximum Steps:** A maximum number of reasoning steps, `K_max`, is enforced as a safety bound to prevent infinite loops.

**Overall Approach:**
The process is iterative:
1. An initial textual reasoning step `z_1` is generated.
2. Based on `z_1`, a coreset `V_1` of visual tokens is selected using the DPP-based method.
3. The model then generates `z_2`, conditioned on `x_input`, `z_1`, and `V_1`.
4. This cycle of textual reasoning, visual token selection, and trajectory augmentation continues.
5. At each step, the entropy `H_k` is checked. Once it falls below `δ_entropy` or `K_max` is reached, reasoning stops, and the final answer `y` is generated based on the complete visual-integrated trajectory.

This methodology provides a principled, yet practical, way to integrate relevant visual information into the reasoning process of MLRMs without the need for additional training, thus enabling more grounded and accurate multi-modal understanding.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental validation of VisRef across multiple benchmarks and state-of-the-art MLRMs consistently demonstrates its effectiveness and robustness.

#### 4.1. Consistent Performance Improvements

VisRef was evaluated on three challenging visual reasoning benchmarks: MathVista, MM-Star, and MathVision, using three prominent MLRMs: InternVL-3.5-8B, Qwen-3-VL-8B, and SAIL-VL2.
- **Superiority over Baselines:** VisRef consistently and significantly outperforms both "Standard Thinking" (ST, a single reasoning trace) and "Textual Self-Reflection" (TSR, extended text-only reasoning). For instance, InternVL-3.5-8B with VisRef achieved 79.3% accuracy on MathVista, a substantial 5.4% absolute improvement over ST (68.1%) and 4.5% over TSR (73.9%). Similar gains were observed across all models and benchmarks (e.g., +6.4% on MM-Star with SAIL-VL2).
- **Inconsistency of Textual Self-Reflection:** Notably, TSR showed inconsistent improvements, sometimes even degrading performance (e.g., -0.6% on MM-Star with Qwen-3-VL-8B). This highlights the core problem VisRef addresses: purely textual reasoning extension is insufficient for vision-dependent tasks.

#### 4.2. Favorable Test-Time Scaling Behavior

When evaluated under fixed test-time compute budgets by generating multiple parallel reasoning chains, VisRef exhibited superior scaling:
- **Higher Accuracy for Given Budget:** Across all benchmarks and MLRMs, VisRef consistently achieved higher accuracy for any given computational budget compared to "parallel thinking" (which samples multiple text-only reasoning trajectories). This demonstrates that VisRef effectively utilizes additional inference compute by ensuring visual grounding, leading to better outcomes.
- **Example:** On MM-Star, InternVL-3.5-8B with VisRef showed approximately 6% higher accuracy than parallel thinking with a budget of 14K thinking tokens.

#### 4.3. Complementary to Training-Based Methods

VisRef's training-free nature allows for direct comparison and combination with training-based methods:
- **Competitive Without Training:** VisRef attained competitive results against "Look-Back", an RL fine-tuning approach, entirely without training. For example, on MathVista, VisRef scored 79.3% compared to Look-Back's 80.8%.
- **Orthogonal Gains:** Crucially, combining Look-Back with VisRef yielded the best performance across all benchmarks (e.g., 83.1% on MathVista), demonstrating that VisRef provides complementary gains and is orthogonal to fine-tuning methods. This makes VisRef a valuable addition even for models that have undergone extensive training.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies and Hyperparameter Analysis

Detailed ablation studies provided insights into VisRef's components:
- **Importance of Relevance and Diversity:** The full DPP objective (balancing both relevance and diversity) consistently outperformed variants using only relevance or only diversity. Relevance-only selection showed substantial degradation, emphasizing the critical role of diversity in selecting non-redundant and comprehensive visual tokens. Random selection performed poorly, validating the need for an intelligent selection mechanism.
- **Optimal Entropy Threshold (δ_entropy):** An entropy threshold of 0.25 for the adaptive stopping criterion was found to strike the best balance between accuracy and inference efficiency, preventing both premature termination and excessive reasoning.
- **Visual Token Budget (m):** Setting the visual token budget `m` to 30% of total visual tokens was optimal, showing accuracy improvements up to this point, after which gains plateaued.

#### 4.5. Qualitative Evaluation and Generalization

- **Attention Visualization:** Qualitative analysis through attention maps demonstrated that VisRef successfully refocuses the model's attention on task-critical visual regions, moving from diffuse and noisy initial attention to more concentrated and coherent patterns relevant to the problem.
- **Generalization Across Model Scales:** VisRef's benefits were consistent across different scales of InternVL models (1B, 2B, and 8B parameters), indicating that its mechanism to counteract visual dilution is effective regardless of model capacity.
- **Additional Benchmarks:** Evaluation on TallyQA (complex counting) and RealWorldQA (real-world scenarios) further confirmed VisRef's generalizability and robustness, showing significant accuracy gains across all tested MLRMs.

In summary, VisRef stands out as an effective, training-free framework that significantly enhances MLRM performance on visual reasoning tasks by dynamically maintaining visual grounding, making it a highly practical and impactful contribution to the field.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

VisRef presents a significant advancement in the field of multi-modal large reasoning models, offering a practical and impactful solution to a well-recognized limitation: the degradation of visual grounding during extended textual reasoning. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Enhanced Robustness and Accuracy:** By effectively counteracting visual token dilution, VisRef leads to consistently higher accuracy and more robust reasoning on complex, vision-dependent tasks. This directly translates to improved performance for MLRMs in areas like mathematical reasoning with visual aids, scientific problem-solving, and general multi-modal understanding, where precise visual information is crucial.

2. **Training-Free and Plug-and-Play Nature:** One of VisRef's most impactful contributions is its ability to operate entirely at test time without requiring any model retraining or fine-tuning. This "plug-and-play" capability dramatically lowers the barrier to adoption, allowing immediate application to any pre-trained MLRM. This is a considerable advantage over computationally expensive RL-based fine-tuning methods, making advanced visual reasoning capabilities accessible to a broader range of researchers and practitioners who may lack the resources for extensive model training.

3. **Efficient Utilization of Test-Time Compute:** VisRef demonstrates that test-time scaling can be far more effective when visual grounding is actively maintained. It achieves superior accuracy for any given computational budget compared to text-only reasoning extensions. This means that allocating more inference compute through VisRef yields better returns, optimizing resource usage for improved performance.

4. **Mimicking Human Cognitive Strategies:** The dynamic selection and re-injection of relevant visual cues closely mirrors how humans naturally interact with multi-modal information, alternating between visual examination and abstract thought. This moves MLRMs closer to a more human-like, adaptive problem-solving paradigm, fostering the development of more intuitively intelligent AI systems.

5. **Principled Visual Information Selection:** The use of Determinantal Point Processes (DPPs) for visual token selection provides a theoretically sound and empirically effective mechanism to balance both relevance to the current reasoning state and diversity of visual coverage. This principled approach ensures that the re-injected visual information is both targeted and comprehensive, avoiding redundancy and maximizing utility.

6. **Generalizability Across Models and Benchmarks:** The consistent performance gains observed across different MLRM architectures (InternVL, Qwen-3-VL, SAIL-VL2) and a variety of visual reasoning benchmarks (MathVista, MM-Star, MathVision, TallyQA, RealWorldQA) underscore the broad applicability and robustness of the VisRef framework. Its effectiveness across different model scales (1B to 8B parameters) further highlights its versatility.

7. **Orthogonality and Complementary Gains:** The finding that VisRef can be combined with existing training-based methods (like Look-Back) to achieve even higher performance is highly significant. It indicates that VisRef is not a replacement but a valuable orthogonal enhancement, capable of stacking with other improvements to unlock further potential in MLRMs.

In conclusion, VisRef offers an elegant, efficient, and highly practical solution to a critical problem in multi-modal AI. Its training-free nature, combined with its ability to significantly boost reasoning accuracy and robustness, makes it a valuable contribution with the potential to widely impact the development and deployment of more capable and grounded multi-modal large reasoning models.
