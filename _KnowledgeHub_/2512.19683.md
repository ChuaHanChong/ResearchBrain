# From Indoor to Open World: Revealing the Spatial Reasoning Gap in MLLMs

> [!summary] Summary
> This work introduces OpenBench, a new benchmark utilizing metrically precise, multi-sensor open-world video data to evaluate the spatial intelligence of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs). It reveals severe limitations in MLLMs' ability to generalize spatial reasoning, particularly in dynamic and open-world settings, demonstrating a heavy reliance on linguistic priors over genuine visual perception.

## Problem

- Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) demonstrate strong semantic understanding but struggle with geometrically grounded spatial reasoning in real-world 3D environments.
- Existing spatial intelligence benchmarks suffer from limitations, including an over-reliance on qualitative reasoning, domain-specific indoor datasets (leading to 'indoor bias'), and unreliable pseudo-ground truth for open-world scenarios.
- Models trained on indoor data often exploit structured layouts and common object sizes (linguistic priors) instead of genuinely perceiving and reasoning from visual cues, resulting in an 'illusory' sense of spatial understanding that lacks generalization.

## Method

- Developed ==OpenBench==, a novel benchmark constructed from high-fidelity, ==multi-sensor collected data== (synchronized stereo RGB, LiDAR, IMU/GPS) across diverse open-world pedestrian-perspective environments.
- Implemented a three-stage automated pipeline involving data preprocessing, joint spatial information extraction (object captioning, detection, tracking, 3D registration), and ==template-based QA generation==, rigorously curated by MLLMs and human annotators.
- Established a comprehensive, hierarchical evaluation framework for spatial intelligence, defining ==nine tasks== across three tiers: Relational Reasoning, Static Metric Reasoning, and Dynamic Metric Reasoning.

## Results

- All evaluated MLLMs performed significantly below human accuracy across OpenBench tasks; the top model achieved **23-30%** accuracy on relational reasoning tasks compared to human **83.3%**.
- Models universally collapsed on dynamic metric reasoning tasks (e.g., absolute displacement, speed, ego-motion), highlighting a critical deficiency in spatio-temporal consistency and quantitative motion understanding.
- Newer MLLM versions that reported substantial gains on indoor benchmarks showed only marginal or negative gains on OpenBench, disproving the transferability of indoor-centric spatial reasoning capabilities.
- Blinding tests revealed MLLMs gained only **2.2-6.3%** accuracy when vision was enabled, and synthetic abnormal scenes confirmed MLLMs' metric estimates are heavily influenced by learned semantic knowledge over visual evidence.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - MLLMs exhibit a profound 'cognitive gap' in spatial understanding compared to humans, showing particularly poor performance in open-world and dynamic metric reasoning tasks.
> - Performance gains observed on indoor spatial benchmarks do not translate to open-world scenarios, indicating MLLMs overfit to dataset-specific statistical patterns and linguistic priors rather than acquiring generalizable spatial intelligence.
> - Current MLLMs heavily rely on textual (linguistic) priors for spatial questions, using visual cues only minimally or for coarse grounding, as evidenced by small performance drops in blinding tests.
> - The primary bottleneck for MLLMs' spatial reasoning is the precise metric information extraction from visual inputs, not the subsequent algebraic reasoning, which they can perform when explicit geometric data is provided.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper addresses a critical gap in the rapidly evolving field of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs), specifically concerning their spatial intelligence. While MLLMs have demonstrated impressive capabilities in semantic understanding tasks, such as visual question answering and image captioning, their ability to reason about the physical world in a geometrically grounded manner remains a significant challenge. This "spatial intelligence" is crucial for building robust AI systems, especially for applications like autonomous navigation, robotics, and embodied agents that require precise interaction with 3D environments.

The existing research landscape for evaluating MLLM spatial capabilities is characterized by several limitations, which this paper directly seeks to overcome:
- **Over-reliance on Qualitative Reasoning:** Many early benchmarks for spatial intelligence primarily focused on qualitative relational reasoning (ee.g., "object A is left of object B") without tackling the more challenging aspects of quantitative metric (e.g., "what is the distance?") or kinematic (e.g., "what is the speed?") understanding.
- **Domain Specificity (Indoor Bias):** A prevalent issue is the heavy reliance on indoor datasets. These datasets often leverage readily available 3D meshes or reconstructions from structured indoor environments (like ScanNet). While convenient for generating ground truth, these indoor scenes are limited in terms of spatial scale, semantic diversity, visual variability, and dynamic elements. Critically, models trained and evaluated solely on such data tend to develop an "indoor bias," learning to exploit structured layouts and common object sizes (linguistic priors) rather than genuinely perceiving and reasoning from visual cues. This leads to an "illusory" sense of spatial understanding that does not generalize to real-world complexity.
- **Unreliable Ground Truth in Open-World Settings:** Attempts to create outdoor spatial benchmarks have often struggled with obtaining accurate metric ground truth. Some approaches use monocular depth estimation, which provides only pseudo-ground-truth and suffers from inherent scale ambiguity and unreliability. The lack of high-fidelity, metrically precise outdoor datasets with verifiable 3D information has been a major impediment.

"From Indoor to Open World" directly intervenes in this landscape by introducing **OpenBench**, a novel benchmark designed to diagnose and address these limitations. It pioneers the use of *metrically precise, multi-sensor collected data from diverse open-world, pedestrian-perspective videos*. By providing robust ground truth across relational, static metric, and dynamic metric reasoning, it pushes the frontier of MLLM evaluation beyond constrained indoor settings and unreliable pseudo-ground truth. This work serves as a critical diagnostic tool, revealing that the apparent progress in spatial reasoning on indoor benchmarks often does not translate to genuine, generalizable spatial intelligence in the open world, thus challenging prevailing assumptions and guiding the community toward more physically grounded AI. The comparative table (Table 1) explicitly positions OpenBench as the first to cover all three tiers of spatial intelligence with metrically accurate sensor data in open-world scenarios, highlighting its unique contribution.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a robust and comprehensive framework for evaluating the spatial intelligence of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs), particularly in challenging, real-world "open-world" environments. The authors aim to reveal the limitations of current MLLMs in truly understanding and reasoning about physical space, contrasting their performance against a new, more rigorous benchmark.

The key objectives and motivations can be summarized as follows:

- **Diagnose the Spatial Reasoning Gap:** The core motivation is to expose the current underdeveloped state of spatial intelligence in MLLMs. While these models excel at semantic tasks, their capacity for grounded, metric, and kinematic understanding of the 3D world is questionable. The paper seeks to provide clear diagnostic evidence of this deficiency.
- **Bridge the Data Gap with Metrically Precise Open-World Data:** A major driving force is the recognition that existing benchmarks are inadequate. They either focus on simplistic qualitative reasoning or rely on constrained, structured indoor datasets. There is a critical lack of outdoor, pedestrian-perspective datasets that provide verifiable metric ground truth. The paper aims to fill this void by introducing a large-scale dataset collected with synchronized stereo cameras, LiDAR, and IMU/GPS sensors, ensuring metrically precise 3D information.
- **Establish a Comprehensive, Hierarchical Spatial Benchmark:** The authors seek to formalize spatial intelligence into a three-level hierarchy—Relational Reasoning, Metric Reasoning, and Kinematic Reasoning—and construct a benchmark, OpenBench, that thoroughly evaluates MLLMs across all these tiers. This allows for a granular assessment of capabilities, from qualitative relationships to precise quantitative and dynamic understanding.
- **Uncover Reliance on Linguistic Priors vs. Visual Reasoning:** A central hypothesis is that MLLMs often rely on linguistic priors and semantic shortcuts learned from text-heavy pre-training, rather than genuinely inferring spatial properties from visual input. The work is motivated to design specific tests (blinding tests, synthetic abnormal scenes) to decouple this reliance and ascertain whether models truly "see" and "understand" spatial geometry.
- **Challenge the "Illusory Progress" from Indoor Benchmarks:** The paper aims to demonstrate that performance gains reported on indoor spatial benchmarks may be superficial. Models might be overfitting to the regularities and statistical patterns of indoor scenes, leading to an exaggerated perception of their spatial intelligence. By evaluating models in open-world settings, the authors seek to reveal whether this "progress" transfers or vanishes.
- **Guide Future Research Towards Physically Grounded AI:** Ultimately, the motivation is to provide a principled platform and clear diagnostic insights that can steer future MLLM research. By pinpointing the fundamental limitations—especially in metric depth perception, spatio-temporal consistency, and genuine visual grounding—the work aims to accelerate the development of MLLMs capable of robust, physically grounded interactions crucial for real-world AI applications like autonomous driving and embodied agents.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology presented in this paper is comprehensive, encompassing novel data collection, a sophisticated benchmark construction pipeline, and rigorous evaluation strategies, including insightful ablation studies.

#### 3.1. Data Collection

The foundation of OpenBench is a purpose-built dataset collected using a custom multi-sensor platform.
- **Platform:** A manual cart equipped with a synchronized stereo RGB camera system (1080p, 15 FPS), a 32-beam LiDAR (10 FPS), and an IMU/GPS unit (100Hz/1Hz). All sensors are timestamp-synchronized and controlled by a mini-PC running ROS2. The camera system is positioned at 1.4 meters from the ground to simulate a pedestrian-centric perspective.
- **Calibration:** A meticulous multi-sensor calibration pipeline was executed, covering stereo camera calibration (0.32 pixels reprojection error), LiDAR-to-camera extrinsic calibration (0.51 pixels reprojection error, 0.002m translation error, 0.978 degrees rotation error), and IMU calibration, ensuring metrically accurate and spatio-temporally aligned data.
- **Scene Selection:** Data collection targeted diverse "open-world" environments, including university campuses, public parks, open plazas, historical sites, and large shopping malls. The scenes prioritize pedestrian-centric views and high semantic density to provide rich query targets. Approximately 20 hours of high-quality, curated multimodal data were collected, with 6 hours used for the benchmark.

#### 3.2. Benchmark Construction Pipeline

The collected raw multimodal data is transformed into question-answer (QA) pairs through a three-stage automated pipeline, followed by human curation (illustrated in Figure 3).
- **Stage 1: Data Preprocessing:**
 - Raw recordings are logically segmented into 15-30 second video clips, with all streams aligned.
 - Metric-scale camera poses for each frame are estimated using ORB-SLAM3 (stereo-inertial mode) on downsampled (960x540) images and IMU data.
 - Sparse depth maps are generated by projecting calibrated LiDAR point clouds onto the image plane. Multi-frame point cloud fusion is applied (3-frame window) to densify depth maps.
 - Keyframes are sampled at fixed 30-frame (2-second) intervals from each clip, bundled with their corresponding camera poses and depth maps.
- **Stage 2: Spatial Information Extraction (Joint-Annotation Module):**
 - **Object Captioning:** A locally-run MLLM (Qwen-2.5-VL-8B-Instruct) identifies distinct static and dynamic objects in keyframes and generates detailed, purely visual captions for each, classifying them by motion state. This avoids bias towards large regions or non-object entities.
 - **Object Detection and Segmentation:** Generated captions serve as text prompts for GroundingDINO to produce 2D bounding boxes, which are then refined into pixel-level segmentation masks by SAM.
 - **Temporal Object Tracking:** CoTracker3, a point-tracking model, is used to ensure robust temporal consistency of objects across frames, generating coherent mask sequences even for long durations.
 - **3D Spatial Registration:** For each object's mask, corresponding depth values are extracted from the depth map. These are deprojected into a 3D point cloud, and its centroid determines the object's 3D position relative to the camera. This is then registered into a unified world coordinate system using camera poses.
- **Stage 3: QA Generation and Curation:**
 - **Object ID Assignment:** To resolve ambiguity, unique numerical IDs are assigned to queried objects and visually overlaid as tags in the video.
 - **Metric Quantity Computation:** All metric answers (distances, speeds, displacements) are computed directly from the objects' structured spatio-temporal 3D coordinates.
 - **Template-based QA Generation:** 8,736 high-quality QA pairs are generated using predefined templates across nine tasks, covering the three tiers of spatial reasoning (Relational, Static Metric, Dynamic Metric) as shown in Figure 2 and Table 5 (supplementary).
 - **MLLM-assisted, Human-in-the-loop Curation:** An initial pass by a powerful MLLM (Gemini-2.5-Pro) corrects captions, filters problematic questions, and flags low-confidence QA pairs. Human annotators perform a final review and correction of these flagged samples to ensure benchmark quality. Qualitative validation against manual on-site measurements showed low mean positional error (0.68m indoor, 0.79m outdoor), confirming the pipeline's fidelity.

#### 3.3. Evaluation Setup

- **Benchmark Models:** A diverse set of state-of-the-art MLLMs, including leading proprietary models (Gemini-2.5-Pro/Flash, GPT-5/4o, Claude-3.7-Sonnet, Doubao-Seed-Vision) and various open-source models (InternVL2/3.5, Qwen2.5/3-VL, LLaVA-OneVision/Video, Ovis2) of different scales.
- **Protocol:** All experiments use the VLMEvalKit framework with greedy decoding for reproducibility. Most models receive 32 uniformly sampled video frames as input; Gemini models leverage native video ingestion. OpenAI API models use low-quality image mode for efficiency.
- **Metrics:** Standard Accuracy (ACC) for Multiple-Choice Answer (MCA) tasks. Mean Relative Accuracy (MRA) for Numerical Answer (NA) tasks, which measures the proportion of 10 predefined error thresholds that the Mean Relative Error (MRE) satisfies. A special handling for near-zero ground truth prevents numerical instability.
- **Human Evaluation:** A subset of 270 questions (30 per task) was evaluated by human annotators. Annotators had unrestricted video playback control and underwent a calibration phase for open-world metric estimation.

#### 3.4. Ablation Studies

- **Blinding Tests:** MLLMs were evaluated with visual inputs removed to quantify their reliance on linguistic priors.
- **Synthetic Abnormal Scenes:** Two sets of synthetic indoor scenes were created (Normal and Abnormal, where object scales were manipulated) to test how MLLMs' metric estimates respond when visual evidence contradicts learned linguistic priors about object sizes.
- **Geometric Information Revelation:** For absolute distance tasks, ground-truth geometric information (object 3D positions, camera ego-motion) was progressively provided to models to identify whether the bottleneck lies in extracting this information or in performing the subsequent algebraic reasoning.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of state-of-the-art MLLMs on OpenBench reveals profound limitations in their spatial intelligence, particularly concerning generalization and grounded visual reasoning.

- **Overall Performance and Human-Model Gap:** All evaluated models performed significantly below human-level accuracy across the benchmark. Gemini-2.5-Pro emerged as the top performer among closed-source models, while Qwen3-VL-32B led the open-source category. The vast disparity, especially in relational reasoning tasks where humans achieved 83.3% accuracy while top models ranged from 23-30%, indicates a fundamental "cognitive gap" in how MLLMs process and understand relative spatial layouts compared to humans.

- **Diverse but Inconsistent Performance Profiles:** Beyond the overall scores, models exhibited highly specialized performance profiles, lacking uniform consistency across tasks. For instance, Qwen3VL-32B, despite its high average, performed poorly on specific tasks like relative direction. The OVIS family consistently excelled at relative direction, while InternVL3.5 variants showed a persistent advantage in depth-aware counting, suggesting task-specific specializations rather than broad, generalizable spatial intelligence.

- **Universal Failure in Quantitative Spatial Dynamics:** A striking finding was the universal collapse of all models on Dynamic Metric reasoning tasks (absolute displacement, absolute speed, quantitative ego-motion). Even models performing comparably to humans on static metric tasks showed severe degradation when asked to reason about motion. This highlights a critical, fundamental deficiency in current MLLMs' ability to maintain spatio-temporal consistency, integrate motion cues across frames, and understand quantitative motion. This is identified as a major frontier for future research.

- **Illusory Progress from Indoor Benchmarks:** The paper provides strong evidence that performance gains observed on indoor spatial benchmarks (e.g., VSI-Bench) do not transfer to open-world settings. Newer model versions (e.g., InternVL3.5 over InternVL2), which reported substantial gains on VSI-Bench (e.g., +24.1%), showed only marginal or even negative gains on OpenBench. Specifically, on the absolute distance task (with identical question templates to VSI-Bench), newer InternVL3.5 models consistently *underperformed* their InternVL2 predecessors on OpenBench, contrasting sharply with their VSI-Bench improvements. This directly implies that models are overfitting to the specific statistical patterns and linguistic priors of indoor benchmarks rather than acquiring truly generalizable spatial intelligence.

- **Heavy Reliance on Linguistic Priors:**
 - **Blinding Tests:** When visual inputs were removed, MLLMs exhibited only marginal performance drops (+2.2% to +6.3% accuracy gains when vision was enabled). In stark contrast, human performance increased substantially (+22.6%) with vision. This indicates that MLLMs primarily rely on textual (linguistic) priors to answer spatial questions, using visual cues only minimally or for coarse grounding, rather than performing genuine visual perception of metric properties.
 - **Synthetic Abnormal Scenes:** In synthetic indoor scenes where object scales were deliberately manipulated (Abnormal Set), MLLMs' performance degraded significantly, particularly for object size estimation (e.g., Gemini-2.5-Pro's size estimation MRA dropped by 25.0%). Humans, conversely, showed only marginal degradation. This confirms that MLLMs' metric estimates are heavily shaped by learned semantic knowledge (canonical object sizes), failing when visual evidence contradicts these priors.

- **Bottleneck in Geometric Information Extraction:** An ablation study on the absolute distance task, where ground-truth geometric information was progressively revealed, clearly pinpointed the primary bottleneck. Models achieved near-perfect accuracy (98.8%) when all necessary 3D positions and camera ego-motion parameters were explicitly provided. This starkly contrasts with their poor performance when these parameters had to be inferred visually. The study concluded that the challenge lies in the *extraction of precise metric information from visual inputs*, not in the mathematical computation itself. Furthermore, models could not independently derive the geometric formulas, indicating they function as calculators when instructed, but lack inherent 3D geometric knowledge.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research presented in "From Indoor to Open World: Revealing the Spatial Reasoning Gap in MLLMs" carries significant implications for the future development and evaluation of Multimodal Large Language Models.

- **A Critical Diagnostic Tool:** OpenBench serves as a crucial diagnostic instrument for evaluating the true spatial intelligence of MLLMs. By moving beyond the limitations of domain-specific (indoor) or qualitatively-focused benchmarks, it offers a more realistic and challenging assessment of how well models understand the physical world. This benchmark will be invaluable for researchers aiming to develop MLLMs that are genuinely capable of robust spatial reasoning.

- **Challenging "Illusory Progress":** The paper's most impactful finding is the demonstration that performance gains on existing indoor spatial benchmarks are largely superficial and do not generalize to open-world scenarios. This revelation challenges the prevailing narrative of rapid progress in MLLM spatial capabilities and highlights the dangers of overfitting to constrained datasets. It forces a re-evaluation of current MLLM design principles and training strategies.

- **Highlighting Fundamental Limitations:** The findings pinpoint fundamental architectural and conceptual limitations in current MLLMs, particularly their heavy reliance on linguistic priors over grounded visual perception, and their profound inability to reason about dynamic spatial changes. This moves beyond merely identifying performance gaps to understanding the *root causes* of these failures.

- **Guiding Future Research Directions:** The ablation studies offer clear directions for future research:
 - **Improved Metric Depth Perception:** The study strongly emphasizes the need for MLLMs to develop more robust and precise metric depth perception in diverse, open-world settings, as this is identified as a core bottleneck.
 - **Explicit Geometric Representations:** Moving beyond implicit reasoning, researchers should explore integrating explicit 3D geometric representations, 3D-aware architectures, multi-view consistency, or hybrid symbolic-neural approaches. This could enable models to infer, store, and manipulate 3D information in a more principled manner.
 - **Enhanced Dynamic Reasoning:** The universal failure in kinematic reasoning underscores the urgent need for models capable of maintaining temporally consistent world models, reasoning over object trajectories, and effectively utilizing sensor-derived ego-motion data. This area remains largely underexplored and represents a critical next frontier.

- **Accelerating Physically Grounded AI:** By providing a rigorous benchmark and clear insights into existing shortcomings, this work will accelerate the development of truly "physically grounded" AI systems. Such systems are indispensable for real-world applications like safe and intelligent autonomous driving, advanced robotics for complex tasks, and sophisticated embodied agents that can navigate and interact with their environments effectively. The public release of the OpenBench dataset and raw multimodal videos will further empower the research community to tackle these challenges directly.

In conclusion, "From Indoor to Open World" is a pivotal contribution that not only exposes a critical deficiency in current MLLMs but also provides the necessary tools and insights to guide the community toward building more intelligent, robust, and truly spatially aware AI systems.
