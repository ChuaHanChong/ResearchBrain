# Whole-Body Bilateral Teleoperation with Multi-Stage Object Parameter Estimation for Wheeled Humanoid Locomanipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> A framework for wheeled humanoid robots from MIT and Boston Dynamics integrates multi-stage online estimation of 3D object inertial parameters into whole-body bilateral teleoperation. This allows for stable and precise handling of unknown heavy objects, enhancing haptic feedback reliability and enabling robust control in complex physical interactions.

## Problem

- Humanoid robots struggle to adapt to unknown object properties during manipulation, particularly for heavy objects that demand coordinated whole-body motion and precise force regulation.
- Existing teleoperation systems often provide uninformative or overwhelming haptic feedback to human operators when the robot's control system does not account for the dynamics of manipulated objects.
- Conventional methods for identifying object inertial parameters typically require long-duration, persistently exciting trajectories or dedicated force/torque sensors, making real-time online estimation impractical during dynamic task execution.

## Method

- A multi-stage online estimation method for 3D object inertial parameters that combines vision-based object size estimation, ==Vision-Language Model (VLM)== priors, and a decoupled hierarchical Cross-Entropy Method (DH-CEM) using parallel high-fidelity simulation.
- An object-aware whole-body bilateral teleoperation framework that dynamically adapts the robot's equilibrium point and compensates for manipulated object properties, improving haptic feedback and operator focus on high-level tasks.
- A sim-to-real adaptation strategy for sampling-based parameter estimation that reduces the 'reality gap' by tuning simulation parameters (e.g., joint friction, time delay) through offline system identification.

## Results

- The multi-stage estimation method consistently achieved lower Normalized Mean Absolute Error for mass, CoM, and inertia compared to baseline methods, with estimation completing in approximately **0.5** seconds on hardware.
- Object dynamics compensation via feedforward inverse dynamics improved manipulation tracking performance, reducing mean squared joint position errors by **24.2%** to **78.4%** during heavy object lifting.
- Whole-body bilateral tele-loco-manipulation with object compensation reduced Divergent Component of Motion (DCM) tracking error by up to **64.8%** and provided reliable haptic feedback, enabling successful handling of objects up to one-third of the robot's weight.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating multi-modal information (vision, language, proprioception) and a hierarchical sampling approach enables rapid and robust online estimation of object inertial parameters during dynamic tasks without explicit excitation signals.
> - Dynamically adapting the robot's whole-body control and equilibrium point based on estimated object properties significantly enhances its ability to perform heavy loco-manipulation tasks and provides meaningful, stable haptic feedback to human operators.
> - Precise alignment between simulation and real-world dynamics, achieved through a dedicated sim-to-real adaptation strategy, is crucial for accurate sampling-based parameter estimation, which is highly sensitive to model discrepancies.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The research presented in this paper is situated within the broader context of developing advanced humanoid robotic systems capable of performing complex physical tasks in human-centered environments. This field encompasses several key areas: humanoid robot control, teleoperation and shared autonomy, and physical parameter estimation.

Humanoid robots, including bipedal and wheeled variants, are being developed for applications such as service, logistics, and industry due to their anthropomorphic design, which allows them to interact with human-designed tools and environments. While significant progress has been made in hardware and control, enabling robots to perform complex motions and traverse varied terrains, they generally lack the proficiency of humans in adapting to changing object properties or environmental conditions during interaction. Tasks involving heavy object manipulation, in particular, remain challenging as they necessitate coordinated whole-body motion, precise force regulation, balance control, and adaptive planning.

Existing approaches to robot control often utilize model-based methods, ranging from reduced-order models like the Linear Inverted Pendulum (LIP) to full-body dynamic models. More recently, learning-based methods, particularly reinforcement learning, have gained prominence for diverse locomotion and manipulation tasks. However, these methods often rely on extensive simulation data and can struggle with real-time adaptation to unknown object properties, especially when external forces are significant.

In the domain of physical parameter estimation, conventional methods for identifying rigid body inertial parameters often require long-duration, persistently exciting trajectories, or the use of force/torque sensors. These requirements can be impractical for real-time online estimation during dynamic task execution. While some methods incorporate physical consistency constraints or leverage visual data, they may be computationally intensive, depend on prior shape information, or lack dynamic observability for full parameter recovery.

Teleoperation and shared autonomy frameworks aim to leverage human motor skills and high-level reasoning to enhance robot adaptability and robustness in unstructured environments. Prior work has demonstrated the benefits of synchronizing human and robot motion using dynamic similarity and haptic feedback. However, when robots interact with unknown objects, human operators may experience excessive and uninformative haptic feedback, as the robot's control system does not account for the manipulated object's dynamics. This necessitates the operator to manage complex decision-making, including simultaneous control of locomotion, manipulation, and disturbance rejection.

This work extends the concept of dynamic human-robot synchronization by integrating online, real-time estimation of 3D object inertial parameters into a whole-body bilateral teleoperation framework for wheeled humanoids. It aims to overcome limitations in existing parameter identification methods by removing the need for persistently exciting signals or dedicated force/torque sensors, thus enabling online estimation during task execution. Furthermore, it seeks to improve the effectiveness of haptic force feedback and compliant manipulation behavior by allowing the robot to autonomously adapt its control based on the object's estimated properties. The development of a sim-to-real adaptation strategy specifically for parameter estimation, which is more sensitive to model discrepancies than policy learning, also positions this work in the context of bridging the reality gap in robotics.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central hypothesis guiding this research is that human effectiveness in complex physical tasks stems from two complementary factors: familiarity with physical environments through prior experience and contextual knowledge, and the ability to leverage human motor skills through intuitive physical interaction. Based on this, the authors propose that robots can achieve similar adaptability and robustness by integrating external physical information into their control frameworks and by using teleoperation to combine human guidance with robotic autonomy.

The primary objective of this work is to develop an object-aware whole-body bilateral teleoperation framework that explicitly integrates 3D object inertial parameter estimation. This framework aims to enable dynamic whole-body tele-loco-manipulation, particularly for tasks involving heavy objects, by allowing the robot to automatically adjust its equilibrium point and compensate for the physical properties of manipulated objects. This integration is motivated by the potential to enhance robot adaptability, accuracy, and compliant behavior in complex physical interactions, while simultaneously allowing the human operator to focus on high-level decision-making and efficient task execution, rather than low-level disturbance rejection.

Specific motivations and objectives include:

- **Enabling Dynamic Whole-Body Locomanipulation with Unknown Objects:** Traditional loco-manipulation tasks often involve lightweight objects or assume known object properties. The paper aims to enable tasks such as lifting and delivering heavy objects (up to one-third of the robot's body weight) where object dynamics significantly influence robot stability and control.
- **Improving Haptic Feedback and Operator Experience:** By estimating object dynamics and updating the robot's coordination, the framework seeks to reduce unexpected disturbances transmitted through the human-machine interface (HMI). This allows the operator to experience more meaningful and stable haptic feedback, thereby reducing cognitive and physical load and enabling better focus on the task.
- **Addressing Limitations of Conventional Parameter Estimation:** The research is motivated by the need to overcome the constraints of existing inertial parameter identification methods, which typically require long-duration, persistently exciting trajectories or reliance on force/torque sensors. The goal is to develop an online estimation method that provides physically feasible solutions quickly without these dependencies.
- **Enhancing Manipulation Tracking and Compliance:** Incorporating estimated object parameters into the manipulation controller aims to improve tracking accuracy while preserving compliant behavior, which is crucial for safe and effective human-robot interaction.
- **Bridging the Sim-to-Real Gap for Parameter Estimation:** The paper is motivated to develop a sim-to-real adaptation strategy that specifically addresses the challenges of using rigid-body simulation for sampling-based parameter estimation, where even small mismatches can significantly degrade estimation quality.
- **Integrating Safety-Critical Control:** The framework seeks to demonstrate the utility of accurate object parameter estimation in an autonomous context by integrating it with robust control barrier functions (CBFs) for collision avoidance, thereby enhancing safety and reliable control performance under model uncertainty.

The core contributions of this work, reflecting these objectives, are summarized as:
1. **Multi-stage online estimation method for 3D object inertial parameters:** This method integrates vision-based object size estimation, vision-language model (VLM) priors, and a decoupled hierarchical sampling strategy to quickly estimate physically feasible parameters without requiring long-duration excitation signals or force/torque sensors.
2. **Object-aware whole-body bilateral teleoperation framework:** This framework, developed for a wheeled humanoid robot, extends previous dynamic mobile manipulation (DMM) approaches to handle unknown objects, improving the effectiveness of haptic feedback and allowing operators to focus on high-level tasks.
3. **Sim-to-real adaptation strategy for sampling-based parameter estimation:** This strategy aims to improve the accuracy and reliability of parameter estimation by directly linking hardware and high-fidelity simulation.
4. **Robust adaptive manipulation control framework:** This integrates control barrier functions with the multi-stage object physical parameter estimation to adapt dynamic constraints and handle estimation uncertainty, ensuring reliable control performance in safety-critical scenarios.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework integrates whole-body bilateral teleoperation with an online multi-stage object inertial parameter estimation module, specifically designed for wheeled humanoid loco-manipulation tasks involving unknown objects. The system operates in real-time, with teleoperation and control loops running at 400 Hz and the parameter estimation module operating in a separate thread at 100 Hz.

**A. Overall System Architecture**
The human pilot provides control inputs to the robot via a human-machine interface (HMI) and receives whole-body haptic feedback. The system comprises a teleoperation mapping block, a main control and state estimation block, and an online object parameter estimation module. The parameter estimation is selectively activated when a transition from static to dynamic motion is detected or a mechanical trigger is engaged, updating the robot’s model to compensate for external torques and forces.

**B. Multi-Stage Object Physical Parameter Estimation**
This is the core technical contribution, combining multi-modal information (vision, language instructions, proprioception) for robust parameter estimation.

- **Stage One: Object Size Estimation Using Vision-Based Inference**
 - A real-time, single-shot method called CenterSnap is employed to estimate the 3D size (length, width, height) of an object, approximated as a cuboid, from a single RGB-D image.
 - CenterSnap detects objects as 2D center points and predicts a latent shape code, 6D pose, and metric scale. The latent code is decoded into a canonical point cloud, from which an axis-aligned bounding box (AABB) is computed to yield physical dimensions.
 - The estimated object size provides a constraint for sampling the Center of Mass (CoM), reducing the search space for subsequent estimation stages.

- **Stage Two: Initializing the Inertial Parameters using a Vision-Language Model (VLM)**
 - Inspired by human intuition, a VLM (e.g., GPT-4V) is used to infer physically relevant priors (approximate density, shape category, volume) from visual input (image of the object) and textual instructions.
 - The VLM estimates mass, and combined with the cuboid approximation from Stage One, the CoM and inertia tensor are analytically computed using the parallel axis theorem and standard formulas for uniform-density cuboids (Eq. 10, 11).
 - This stage generates an initial ensemble of inertial parameter samples, which serve as strong priors for the next stage, focusing the search on physically plausible regions.

- **Stage Three: Decoupled Hierarchical Cross-Entropy Method (DH-CEM) with High-Fidelity Parallel Simulation**
 - The initial parameter guesses from Stages One and Two are refined using a sampling-based estimator, DH-CEM, running in parallel with physical hardware on a high-fidelity rigid-body simulation (IsaacGym).
 - **Sim-to-Real Adaptation:** Prior to online estimation, offline system identification is performed to tune simulation parameters (joint friction, time delay, control gains) using Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO). This minimizes the "reality gap" by aligning simulated and real-world trajectories for robot proprioception.
 - **DH-CEM Algorithm:**
 1. **Multi-Hypothesis Initialization:** Multiple CEM instances are initialized based on the VLM estimates, with samples for mass, size, and CoM drawn from uniform distributions around the VLM's mean estimates (with perturbations $\delta_m, \delta_s, \delta_h$). Inertia is derived from these sampled values.
 2. **Iterative Refinement:** For each iteration, new parameter samples ($\phi$) are drawn from a Gaussian distribution (initially constructed from the hypotheses).
 3. **Simulation Rollout:** Each sampled $\phi$ is injected into the high-fidelity physics simulator. The system is rolled out using the same desired joint trajectory as the physical robot.
 4. **Cost Computation:** The simulated trajectory (joint positions and velocities) is compared to the actual robot's proprioceptive history using a mean squared error (MSE) cost function (Eq. 12).
 5. **Elite Set Selection:** The top-performing samples (lowest cost) form an "elite set."
 6. **Distribution Update:** The mean and covariance of the Gaussian distribution are updated based on the elite set, narrowing the search space towards better-performing parameters.
 - This hierarchical approach first refines mass and CoM, then infers the inertia tensor, improving efficiency and physical plausibility compared to simultaneous estimation. The parallel simulation allows for diverse and realistic sample generation in real-time.

**C. Object-Aware Whole-Body Bilateral Teleoperation**

- **Dynamic Locomotion Retargeting:**
 - **New Equilibrium Point Estimation:** When a heavy object is grasped, the system's equilibrium point shifts. The framework computes a new desired pitch angle and pitch rate ($\theta^*_R, \dot{\theta}^*_R$) by balancing gravitational forces of the robot and object around the wheel axis (Eq. 14-17). This allows the robot to adapt its posture.
 - **Locomotion Retargeting of Error Dynamics:** The robot's dynamics are re-linearized around this new equilibrium. The Divergent Component of Motion (DCM) is used to synchronize human and robot motion, but with the robot's desired DCM redefined to align with the shifted equilibrium (Eq. 18, 19). Feedback forces to the human and feedforward control to the robot are designed to enforce this synchronization (Eq. 25, 26).
 - **Locomotion Retargeting for Height Change:** A gain-scheduled LQR controller combined with feedback linearization is used to regulate balance during height transitions. Human height changes are mapped to desired robot height using a tunable scaling factor (Eq. 29).

- **Manipulation Control and Arm Mapping Strategy:**
 - **Kinematic Manipulation Retargeting:** A projection-based kinematic retargeting method maps human arm motion (captured by a 4-DoF exosuit) to the robot’s 4-DoF manipulator, resolving structural differences in shoulder configurations.
 - **Object-Aware Manipulation Control:** A proportional-derivative (PD) feedback controller with low gains (for compliance) is combined with a feedforward compensation term derived from inverse dynamics. The estimated object inertial parameters ($\hat{\Phi}_{obj}$) are used to compute this feedforward torque (Eq. 33), allowing the robot to adapt its motion and torque commands to the object's properties.
 - **Application of Object-Aware Robust Adaptive Safe Manipulation with Control Barrier Functions (CBFs):** For autonomous manipulation tasks, a CBF-based safety filter is integrated into a null-space task-space controller. The estimated object parameters are used to adapt the system’s dynamic constraints in a quadratic program (QP) formulation (Eq. 34), and a robust margin (Eq. 36) is included to handle uncertainty in these estimates, ensuring collision avoidance (Eq. 35).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research validated the proposed framework through five experimental sets, including simulation and hardware experiments on a customized wheeled humanoid robot named SATYRR.

**A. Evaluation of Object Inertial Parameter Estimation**

1. **Benchmarking Parameter Estimation on Hardware:**
 - The multi-stage object inertial parameter estimation method was benchmarked against ablated versions and an optimization-based approach using real-world data from natural manipulation trajectories (not excitation-optimized).
 - The proposed method (DH-CEM with all priors) consistently achieved the lowest Normalized Mean Absolute Error (NMAE) and Mean Absolute Error (MAE) for mass, center of mass (CoM), and inertia, outperforming all baseline methods.
 - Incorporating vision-based object size and Vision-Language Model (VLM) priors significantly improved estimation by providing informative initial guesses and narrowing the search space.
 - The decoupled hierarchical structure contributed to more stable and efficient convergence.
 - Multivariate initialization increased robustness against imprecise priors.
 - The sim-to-real adaptation, achieved through offline system identification, was crucial for enhancing the accuracy and consistency of evaluation in high-fidelity simulation. For example, improvements in mass estimation ranged from 5.4% to 99.4%, CoM from 0% to 100%, and inertia from 0% to 78.4% compared to VLM priors alone (Table II).

2. **Real-Time Online Parameter Estimation in Simulation:**
 - The estimator demonstrated real-time adaptability by accurately tracking dynamically varying ground-truth object parameters in simulation.
 - The estimation process completed within approximately 0.5 seconds, with parameters remaining constant until a change in the object was detected.
 - Sim-to-sim validation (Isaac Gym to MuJoCo) showed the estimator's ability to generalize across different simulation environments and respond to online changes. The visualization of estimated cuboids and CoM indicated reasonable accuracy for supporting teleoperation and safety-critical control.

**B. Evaluation of Manipulation and Loco-Manipulation Tasks**

1. **Object Lifting Manipulation on Hardware:**
 - Experiments involving picking and placing a heavy water bottle demonstrated that incorporating object dynamics compensation via feedforward inverse dynamics significantly improved manipulation tracking performance.
 - Without compensation, the manipulator, operating with low PD gains for compliance, often lacked sufficient torque authority to lift heavy objects, leading to instability or task failure (Fig. 11a).
 - With compensation using estimated inertial parameters, the robot successfully completed the task while preserving compliant behavior.
 - Quantitative analysis showed consistent improvements in mean squared joint position error: 49.5% for Shoulder Pitch, 24.2% for Shoulder Roll, 76.4% for Shoulder Yaw, and 78.4% for Elbow Pitch across five different objects (Table III).

2. **Whole-Body Bilateral Tele-Loco-Manipulation of Heavy Bottle on Hardware:**
 - **Impact on DMM Teleoperation:** In a task involving lifting, moving backward, and releasing a 3.3 kg water bottle (one-third of robot's weight), the condition "wo Compensation" often resulted in task failure. When successful, it showed significant DCM tracking errors and large haptic forces (Fig. 12a).
 - **Haptic Feedback Reliability:** When object dynamics were automatically compensated ("with Compensation wo Haptic"), DCM tracking improved (MAE: 0.023) compared to the uncompensated case (MAE: 0.054), reducing cognitive load. Adding haptic feedback ("with Compensation & Haptic") further improved DCM tracking (MAE: 0.019), allowing the pilot to perceive disturbances effectively and make intuitive adjustments (Fig. 12b, 12c). This indicates that accurate parameter estimation makes haptic feedback meaningful and stable.
 - **Dynamic Whole-Body Loco-Manipulation:** The framework enabled complex whole-body motions like squatting during object delivery. The system autonomously handled equilibrium point adaptation, allowing the human pilot to concentrate on high-level task execution (Fig. 13).

**C. Collision-Avoidance Manipulation with CBF in Simulation**
- The research demonstrated the integration of the multi-stage object parameter estimator with a control barrier function (CBF)-based safety filter for collision avoidance in an autonomous manipulation context.
- Results showed that the CBF filter was effective in preventing collisions only when the object estimator was incorporated. Without the estimator, unmodeled object dynamics led to CBF constraint violations and collisions.
- This suggests that the estimator significantly reduces dynamics uncertainty, enabling the model-based controller to adapt dynamic constraints and ensuring safety under model uncertainty (Fig. 14).

**D. Sim-to-Real Adaptation Evaluation (Appendix)**
- Offline system identification significantly reduced the reality gap in joint position and velocity trajectories between simulation and hardware.
- The improvements were attributed to identifying joint friction and accounting for system latency, which affected effective time steps and velocity estimates.
- However, noticeable discrepancies in torque trajectories persisted, suggesting limitations from structural modeling errors (e.g., unmodeled nonlinear joint friction, actuator dynamics, compliance) and indirect torque estimation on hardware.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a significant advancement in the field of humanoid robotics by enabling robust and adaptive whole-body teleoperation for tasks involving unknown and heavy objects. The integration of a multi-stage online object inertial parameter estimation module directly addresses a critical challenge in dynamic human-robot interaction.

The primary significance lies in its capacity to:

- **Enhance Robot Autonomy in Complex Physical Interactions:** By allowing the robot to autonomously estimate and compensate for unknown object dynamics, the framework reduces the burden on the human operator. This means robots can more readily adapt to novel environments and interact with a wider range of objects without requiring pre-configuration or extensive manual tuning. This is particularly relevant for applications in unstructured or semi-structured environments where object properties are not always known.
- **Improve Human-Robot Collaboration and Operator Experience:** The framework improves the fidelity and utility of haptic force feedback, transforming it from a source of unexpected disturbances into an informative channel for dynamic synchronization. This reduces operator fatigue, enhances task focus, and promotes more intuitive and effective teleoperation, particularly for complex loco-manipulation tasks involving whole-body coordination.
- **Broaden the Scope of Humanoid Applications:** By demonstrating the ability to lift and deliver heavy objects (up to one-third of the robot's weight) with compliant and stable behavior, the work pushes the boundaries for wheeled humanoids in sectors like service, logistics, and industrial assistance, where tasks often involve handling substantial payloads.
- **Advance Parameter Estimation Methodologies:** The multi-stage estimation approach, leveraging vision-language models and high-fidelity simulation, offers a practical and efficient method for online parameter identification that circumvents the limitations of traditional techniques (e.g., need for excitation trajectories, force/torque sensors). Its robustness to poor initial guesses and ability to provide physically feasible solutions quickly are valuable for real-world deployment.
- **Contribute to Safety-Critical Robotics:** The successful integration of estimated parameters with control barrier functions for collision avoidance highlights the potential for deploying such robots in safety-critical autonomous settings, ensuring reliable control performance even under model uncertainty introduced by manipulated objects.
- **Address the Sim-to-Real Gap for Estimation:** The dedicated sim-to-real adaptation strategy for parameter estimation underscores the importance of precisely aligning simulated and real-world dynamics, a crucial step for transferring any model-dependent robotic capabilities from simulation to hardware.

Future work outlined by the authors, such as developing data collection pipelines alongside object-centric and object-aware teleoperation, and leveraging advances in foundation models, imitation learning, and reinforcement learning, suggests a pathway toward even more capable and autonomous humanoid systems. The ability to learn from diverse data and adapt dynamically positions this research to have a lasting impact on the development of highly adaptable and interactive humanoid robots.
