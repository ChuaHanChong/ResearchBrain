# You Only Demonstrate Once: Category-Level Manipulation from Single Visual Demonstration

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Rutgers University and Intrinsic Innovation LLC developed "You Only Demonstrate Once," a framework for category-level robotic manipulation that learns complex skills from a single visual demonstration. It uses a simulation-trained object-centric representation and closed-loop 6 DoF motion tracking to achieve superior robustness and precision across various assembly tasks with novel objects, significantly outperforming existing baselines.

## Problem

- Existing category-level manipulation methods often struggle with complex, long-horizon tasks requiring intricate action sequences and dynamic interaction.
- Many current approaches lack robustness to uncertainties like grasp slippage, in-hand object motion, or contact forces due to reliance on open-loop execution.
- Acquiring skills typically demands extensive real-world data collection and laborious manual annotation of semantic keypoints, making deployment costly and time-consuming.

## Method

- A simulation-trained ==Non-Uniform Normalized Object Coordinate Space (NUNOCS)== provides a ==3D-aware category-level representation== that captures instance-specific geometric variations from synthetic data.
- A model-free ==6 DoF object motion tracker== (**BundleTrack**) parses a single RGBD video demonstration to extract a task trajectory and provides continuous visual feedback for ==closed-loop control== during execution.
- **Category-Level Behavior Cloning (CatBC)** reprojects the demonstrated trajectory onto a novel object, guiding the robot with ==Jacobian steering== for precise, contact-rich, last-inch manipulation.

## Results

- Achieved **100% success** on the Battery Standing task and **82.22%** on the complex Battery Assembly task, a significant improvement over baselines which failed to exceed **25% success**.
- Demonstrated high precision for Gear Insertion, reaching **82.9% success for 0.5mm tolerance** and **28.6% for 0.1mm tolerance**, dramatically surpassing baseline performance (max **31.4% for 5mm tolerance**).
- The framework generalized effectively to novel initial object/receptacle placements, scenes with obstacles, and compensated for external disturbances, showcasing robustness against real-world uncertainties.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Combining simulation-only learned category-level representations with real-time, model-free 6 DoF tracking enables robust, closed-loop control crucial for high-precision and contact-rich manipulation tasks.
> - Learning object-centric trajectories from a single visual demonstration significantly reduces data requirements and facilitates generalization across novel object instances and diverse scene configurations.
> - Dynamic category-level frames generated via a local attention mechanism improve adaptability to task-relevant local features and geometric variations, enhancing generalizability of manipulation strategies.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper positions itself within the evolving field of robotic manipulation, specifically addressing the challenge of **generalizing robotic skills across novel object instances within the same category (category-level manipulation)**. This is a crucial step towards deploying robots in unstructured environments where exact CAD models of every object might not be available or practical.

Traditional robotic manipulation often relies on precise CAD models or extensive, instance-specific training data. While this enables high precision for known objects, it struggles with generalization. Recent advancements have sought to improve generalizability by learning **category-level representations**, primarily through:
- **Dense pixel-wise representations** or **semantic keypoints**: These approaches establish correspondences between different instances of an object category, allowing a skill learned on one instance to be transferred to another by aligning these representations. Prominent examples include DON (Dense Object Nets) and KPAM (Keypoint Affordances for Category-Level Robotic Manipulation), which the authors use as baselines.

However, the authors identify several critical limitations in existing category-level manipulation methods:
1. **Manipulation Task Complexity:** Current methods often succeed in simple tasks (e.g., pick-and-place) but struggle with complex, long-horizon tasks requiring intricate action sequences (e.g., assembly with contacts, multi-step operations). Manually specifying goals and constraints for such tasks becomes "non-intuitive and tedious."
2. **Robustness:** Many methods assume the manipulated object remains static relative to the gripper, leading to open-loop execution that is brittle against uncertainties like grasp slippage, in-hand object motion, or contact forces. Vision-based closed-loop control is often missing or limited.
3. **Time and Cost:** Training these models frequently requires expensive real-world data collection and manual annotation of semantic keypoints across many diverse object instances, making deployment slow and costly.

This paper directly addresses these limitations by proposing a framework that combines:
- **Simulation-only learning for category-level representation:** Significantly reducing real-world data collection and annotation costs.
- **One-shot visual demonstration:** Eliminating the need for extensive robot-object interaction for skill acquisition.
- **Closed-loop control with model-free 6 DoF motion tracking:** Enhancing robustness against dynamic uncertainties and enabling high-precision, contact-rich tasks.
- **Object-centric representation and attention mechanism:** Improving generalizability across diverse instances and adapting to task-relevant local features.

By tackling these challenges, the work moves beyond simply aligning visual features for static goals, instead focusing on transferring entire *action sequences* with dynamic, closed-loop feedback, pushing the frontier of general-purpose, data-efficient robotic manipulation towards more complex, real-world scenarios.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of this research is to develop a robust, flexible, and efficient framework for **category-level robotic manipulation that can learn complex, long-horizon skills from a single visual demonstration and generalize to novel objects and scene configurations.**

The key motivations driving this work stem from the desire to overcome the practical bottlenecks and performance limitations of current category-level manipulation approaches:

1. **Reduce Data Collection and Annotation Costs:** Existing methods often demand extensive real-world data collection (e.g., multi-view scans) and laborious manual annotation of semantic keypoints for each object category and task. The authors aim to eliminate this by learning category-level representations solely in simulation and leveraging a single visual demonstration.
2. **Enable Complex, Long-Horizon Manipulation Tasks:** Many current methods are limited to simple tasks like pick-and-place because defining multi-step actions and dynamic constraints for complex assembly (e.g., battery insertion requiring specific force profiles) is challenging and non-intuitive with traditional goal specification. The framework seeks to automatically extract and transfer these complex action sequences.
3. **Enhance Robustness to Uncertainty:** Open-loop execution, common in prior work, is prone to failure from dynamic uncertainties such as grasp slippage or contact-induced object motion. A core motivation is to integrate continuous visual feedback via 6 DoF object motion tracking to enable closed-loop control, ensuring robustness in high-precision and contact-rich interactions.
4. **Improve Generalizability across Object Instances and Scenes:** While category-level methods aim for generalization, the reliability of correspondences and consistency across diverse instances can be a bottleneck. The work is motivated to develop a more robust category-level representation (NUNOCS) and a dynamic attention mechanism to better handle geometric variations and ensure reliable skill transfer. Furthermore, the framework aims to generalize to varying scene configurations (e.g., obstacles) by disentangling the object's motion from the background.
5. **Facilitate Rapid Skill Acquisition and Adaptation:** The ability to teach a robot new manipulation strategies by simply providing a single, different demonstration video, without manual programming or re-training, is a significant practical motivation for faster deployment and adaptation of robotic skills.

In essence, the authors are motivated to create a manipulation system that is **"fast and inexpensive" to deploy**, **highly precise**, **robust to real-world complexities**, and **broadly generalizable**, thereby moving robotic manipulation closer to real-world industrial and service applications.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework, "You Only Demonstrate Once," is a closed-loop, category-level manipulation system driven exclusively by visual feedback. It modularly combines offline representation learning, one-shot demonstration parsing, and online behavior cloning.

**A. Offline Learning of a Category-Level Representation (NUNOCS)**
- **Representation:** The core idea is to establish a dense correspondence between different object instances within a category. This is achieved using a "Non-Uniform Normalized Object Coordinate Space" (NUNOCS), an extension of NOCS. NUNOCS represents object point clouds within a canonical unit cube, but uniquely allows for **3D non-uniform scaling (9-dimensional space: 6D pose + 3D scaling)**, enabling it to better capture instance-specific geometric variations.
- **NUNOCS Net:** A PointNet-like neural network (NUNOCS Net) is trained to map a scanned partial point cloud of an object instance to its NUNOCS representation (point-wise coordinates and 3D non-uniform scales). The 9D transformation (pose and scales) from NUNOCS to the real object is then solved in closed-form.
- **Simulation-only Training:** Crucially, the NUNOCS Net is trained *exclusively on synthetic data*. This is achieved by generating diverse object instances (randomly selected from training CAD models, with randomized poses, physics parameters, and non-uniform 3D scaling) in Blender. Domain randomization and bidirectional alignment over depth data are employed to bridge the sim-to-real gap, avoiding real-world data collection and manual annotation.

**B. Model-free 6 DoF Object Motion Tracking (BundleTrack)**
This component serves a dual purpose:
- **Demonstration Parsing:** During the offline demonstration phase, a model-free 6 DoF motion tracker (BundleTrack, a prior work by some authors) parses a single RGBD video demonstration. It extracts the full 6 DoF pose trajectory ($\xi \in SE(3)$) of the manipulated object relative to the receptacle's coordinate frame. This object-centric representation disentangles the task trajectory from specific scene backgrounds or robot end-effector poses, facilitating generalization.
- **Online Visual Feedback:** During online robot execution with a novel object, the same BundleTrack provides real-time, closed-loop visual feedback. It continuously tracks the object's 6 DoF pose, compensating for uncertainties like grasp slippage or contact forces, and guiding the robot's actions. The tracker does not require CAD models, allowing instant deployment to novel objects.

**C. Category-Level Behavior Cloning (CatBC) as Last-Inch Policy**
- **Trajectory Reprojection:** The trajectory extracted from the demonstration video (using O$_D$) is "reprojected" to a virtual target trajectory tailored for a novel object (O). This is done by establishing dense correspondence between O$_D$ and O via their NUNOCS representations.
- **Behavior Cloning:** CatBC is the policy that guides the robot to follow this reprojected, target pose trajectory. It acts as a path-following controller.
- **Closed-Loop Control:** Alg. 1 illustrates the process: at each step, the current object pose is tracked, the relative pose error to the next target pose on the trajectory is computed, and Jacobian steering is used to generate robot joint commands to reduce this error. The trajectory is densely discretized (2mm/2°) to ensure continuous correction and high precision.

**D. Dynamic Category-Level Frame via Local Attention**
- **Problem:** A static, global canonical coordinate frame (e.g., center of mass) might not be optimal for all tasks or adequately capture local geometric variations across instances, especially for fine-grained manipulation.
- **Solution:** A local attention mechanism is proposed to dynamically select an "anchor point" on the object's surface as the origin of the category-level canonical coordinate system. This point is chosen based on its proximity to the receptacle during the demonstration (Eq. 4-5), effectively identifying the task-relevant local region.
- **Transferability:** The attention heatmap learned on the demonstration object (O$_D$) is transferred to novel objects (O) via the dense correspondence established by NUNOCS, allowing the local frame to adapt to the specific geometry of the novel instance. This implicitly generates versatile "keypoints" with orientation information.

**E. Grasping the Object and Transferring it to the Keypose**
- **Grasping:** The framework is agnostic to the specific grasp planner; any CAD model-free method that results in a task-compliant grasp can be used. Robustness to initial in-hand object motion during grasping is handled by the 6 DoF tracker providing real-time updates.
- **Keypose Decomposition:** Manipulation is split into two phases:
 1. **Long-range, Collision-free Motion:** An RRT* path planner transports the grasped object from its initial position to a "keypose" – a pose where the object is close to the receptacle (empirically 5cm away from the receptacle along the demonstrated trajectory). This step handles scene-level obstacles and ensures safety.
 2. **Last-inch Manipulation:** From the keypose, the CatBC process takes over for precise, contact-rich manipulation until task completion. This decomposition implicitly focuses the learning on the critical actions.

In summary, the methodology integrates a simulation-trained, 3D-aware representation, real-time model-free tracking, and a closed-loop behavior cloning policy, all while maintaining modularity and a strong focus on generalization and robustness from minimal human input.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments were conducted extensively in real-world settings using a Kuka IIWA14 arm with a Robotiq Hand-E gripper and a Photoneo 3D camera. Two object categories (Gears and Batteries) and three manipulation tasks of increasing complexity and precision requirements were evaluated: Battery Standing, Battery Assembly, and Gear Insertion. A total of 1560 task trials were executed, comparing the proposed method ("Ours") against seven baselines (DON, KPAM, KPAM 2.0, and their BC-augmented versions, plus an ablation of "Ours-no-tracking").

**A. Battery Standing Task (Simple Pick-and-Place)**
- **Baselines:** KPAM, KPAM 2.0, and DON showed moderate success rates (13.3% - 60.0%), struggling with reliable goal specification and cross-instance generalization. Behavior Cloning (BC) augmentation offered marginal improvement as the exact action sequence was less critical.
- **Ours-no-tracking:** Achieved 80.0% success, indicating that the NUNOCS representation and trajectory parsing are superior even without real-time visual feedback compared to baselines. Failures occurred due to initial in-hand object perturbations during grasping.
- **Ours:** Achieved **100% success**, demonstrating complete reliability. The visual feedback from motion tracking effectively compensated for any initial grasp uncertainty, leading to stable battery placement.

**B. Battery Assembly Task (Complex, Contact-Rich Insertion)**
- **Baselines:** KPAM, KPAM 2.0, and DON performed poorly (0.0% success), unable to execute the multi-step, contact-rich actions required (e.g., pressing a spring along a specific axis). BC-augmented versions showed some improvement (13.33% - 24.44%) by capturing the action sequence, but still struggled.
- **Ours-no-tracking:** Achieved 37.78% success, again outperforming baselines due to the robust representation, but still prone to failure due to significant in-hand object motion caused by elastic interactions with the spring.
- **Ours:** Achieved **82.22% success**, showcasing significant robustness. The continuous visual feedback was crucial for guiding the battery through the intricate sequence of actions, adapting to contact forces and maintaining the desired trajectory.

**C. Gear Insertion Task (High-Precision Assembly with Varying Tolerances)**
- **Baselines:** Performance was very low across all tolerance levels (0.0% - 31.4% even for 5mm tolerance). They struggled with precision due to unreliable keypoint predictions and 2D dense correspondences, especially for textureless, reflective gears.
- **Ours-no-tracking:** Significantly outperformed baselines, achieving 42.9% for 5mm tolerance, 5.7% for 0.5mm, and 0.0% for 0.1mm. This highlights the effectiveness of NUNOCS and simulation-trained pipeline for precise pose estimation and cross-instance generalization.
- **Ours:** Achieved **100% success for 5mm tolerance**, **82.9% for 0.5mm tolerance**, and **28.6% for 0.1mm tolerance**. This dramatically boosted performance underscores the critical role of continuous visual feedback in high-precision, contact-rich tasks, enabling the robot to maintain alignment within tight tolerances. Failures at 0.1mm tolerance suggest the limits of vision-only feedback for extremely tight fits.

**D. Framework Analysis:**
- **Generalizability to Scene Configurations:** The framework successfully generalized to novel initial object/receptacle placements and scenes with obstacles unseen in the demonstration. This is attributed to the object-centric trajectory representation and the decomposition into long-range path planning and last-inch manipulation.
- **Robustness against External Disturbance:** The real-time visual motion tracking allowed the robot to compensate for external disturbances (e.g., human interference), maintaining the desired trajectory and ensuring task success.
- **Endowing Different Manipulation Strategies:** The framework demonstrated the ability to teach different manipulation strategies (e.g., "plain top-down" vs. "anchor-and-pivot" for gear insertion) by simply providing a new single demonstration video. The "human pivot" strategy significantly improved success rates for the gear insertion task.
- **Sensitivity to Demonstration Format:** The framework worked well with both human arm demonstrations and tele-operated robot demonstrations, due to its disentangled, object-centric approach.
- **Running Time:** The last-inch manipulation, including real-time visual feedback, ran efficiently (e.g., ~15s for Battery Assembly), with the 6 DoF tracker operating in parallel at 10 Hz.

The main findings clearly demonstrate that the proposed framework delivers **significantly superior performance** in reliability, robustness, precision, and training cost compared to state-of-the-art baselines. The closed-loop control via model-free 6 DoF tracking and the simulation-trained NUNOCS representation are identified as key drivers of this success, especially for complex and high-precision tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a significant advancement in the field of robotic manipulation, particularly in closing the gap between generalizable learning methods and the demands of real-world, high-precision tasks. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Reduced Cost and Effort for Skill Acquisition:** By learning category-level object representations solely in simulation and acquiring complex manipulation skills from a single visual demonstration, the framework dramatically reduces the need for expensive real-world data collection and tedious manual annotation. This paradigm shift makes it faster and more economical to teach robots new skills, accelerating deployment in industrial settings.

2. **Enabling Complex, Long-Horizon Tasks:** The ability to learn intricate, multi-step action sequences from a single demonstration, combined with robust closed-loop control, allows robots to tackle challenging assembly and manipulation tasks that were previously difficult or impossible with traditional methods. This moves robotic capabilities beyond simple pick-and-place to more sophisticated operations requiring fine motor skills and interaction with the environment.

3. **Enhanced Robustness and Precision:** The integration of real-time, model-free 6 DoF object motion tracking provides continuous visual feedback, enabling the robot to compensate for dynamic uncertainties (e.g., grasp slippage, contact forces) that frequently lead to failures in open-loop systems. This closed-loop control is critical for achieving the high precision required in tight-tolerance assembly tasks, where errors of even sub-millimeters can cause failure.

4. **Superior Generalization:** The framework demonstrates strong generalization across novel object instances within a category and to varying scene configurations, including unforeseen obstacles. The NUNOCS representation and the dynamic local attention mechanism are key to effectively capturing geometric variations and task-relevant features across instances, while the object-centric trajectory representation ensures adaptability to new environments.

5. **Democratization of Robotic Programming:** The "You Only Demonstrate Once" approach offers a user-friendly way to program robots with new behaviors. By simply providing a video demonstration, human operators can quickly impart new skills without complex manual programming, opening up robotics to a broader user base and enabling rapid task adaptation. This is particularly valuable for small-batch manufacturing or rapidly changing production lines.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Industrial Automation:** The framework could revolutionize agile manufacturing by enabling robots to quickly adapt to new product variants or assembly processes without extensive reprogramming or costly data collection campaigns. Its application to high-precision assembly tasks is a direct pathway to industrial deployment.
- **Service Robotics:** In less structured environments, service robots could learn to handle various objects within a category (e.g., different types of bottles, tools) from minimal human input, enhancing their versatility and autonomy.
- **Robotics Research:** This work establishes a strong baseline for combining simulation-trained perception with real-world one-shot learning and robust control. It opens avenues for future research into integrating other sensing modalities (force, tactile) for even higher precision, extending to articulated or deformable objects, and exploring more sophisticated reactive control strategies.

**Limitations and Future Work:**
The authors acknowledge limitations, primarily the reliance on vision as the sole sensing modality, suggesting that integrating force or tactile sensing could further boost accuracy for extreme precision tasks. Additionally, the current framework is limited to rigid objects, pointing to future work on category-level representations and manipulation for articulated or deformable objects, which remain significant challenges in robotics.

In conclusion, this paper provides a robust, efficient, and highly generalizable solution for category-level robotic manipulation from a single demonstration, addressing key limitations of prior work and paving the way for more adaptable and capable robotic systems in various real-world applications.
