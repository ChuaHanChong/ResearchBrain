# Enter the Mind Palace: Reasoning and Planning for Long-term Active Embodied Question Answering

> [!summary] Summary
> The "Mind Palace Exploration" framework enables embodied robots to answer complex questions by integrating long-term, multi-episodic memory with active environmental exploration. It demonstrates 12-28% higher answer correctness and 77% fewer retrieved images than baselines in a new Long-term Active Embodied Question Answering (LA-EQA) task, successfully deploying on a real robot in a 1,000 m² office.

## Problem

- Existing Embodied Question Answering (EQA) systems are limited to either short-term exploration or single-episode memory recall, failing to integrate both for long-term understanding.
- Large Vision-Language Models (VLMs) and Large Language Models (LLMs) struggle with the vast scale and temporal complexity of accumulated robot data due to limited context windows and lack of persistent memory.
- There was no effective framework to balance active exploration of the current environment with efficient retrieval of specific, relevant information from extensive long-term memory.

## Method

- Introduces "**Mind Palace** Exploration" for the novel Long-term Active Embodied Question Answering (LA-EQA) task, which combines memory retrieval with active exploration.
- Develops a "Robotic **Mind Palace**" using hierarchical scene graphs to represent episodic world instances, organizing past and present environmental knowledge.
- Employs an LLM-based reasoning and planning component that strategically selects relevant past world instances and areas, interweaving exploration of the current environment with targeted memory recall, optimized by Value-of-Information-based early stopping.

## Results

- Mind Palace Exploration achieves 12-**28%** higher answer correctness and **16%** higher exploration efficiency compared to state-of-the-art baselines in LA-EQA tasks across diverse environments.
- The method demonstrates high memory retrieval efficiency, using **77%** fewer retrieved images than VLM-based methods while maintaining or improving accuracy, with early stopping further reducing retrieved images by 7.13 on average.
- Successfully deployed on a legged robot in a real 1,000 m² office, leveraging 10 past episodes spanning 6 months of inspections, saving 3-10 room searches for practical day-to-day tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Structured, hierarchical memory representations (episodic scene graphs) are essential for embodied agents to manage and leverage vast amounts of temporally-complex environmental data over extended periods.
> - Intelligent, LLM-driven planning that explicitly interleaves active exploration with selective memory retrieval significantly enhances both answer correctness and information-gathering efficiency in long-term tasks.
> - Value-of-Information principles can be effectively applied to create early stopping criteria for memory retrieval, optimizing the exploration-recall trade-off and reducing computational overhead without compromising performance.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This paper addresses a critical, largely unexplored gap in the field of Embodied Question Answering (EQA) and, more broadly, embodied AI. EQA tasks typically require a robot to answer natural language questions about its environment, often involving perception, navigation, and reasoning. Existing EQA research generally falls into two categories:

- **Active EQA:** Robots explore a novel environment from scratch to gather information. Examples include Explore-EQA and Efficient-EQA. These methods excel at real-time exploration but often lack the ability to leverage past experiences.
- **Episodic Memory EQA:** Robots answer questions using information from a single, pre-recorded trajectory or episode. OpenEQA and ReMEmbR are examples. While these can utilize memory, they are limited to a single past event and cannot integrate information across multiple past experiences or combine memory recall with active exploration.

The emergence of Large Vision-Language Models (VLMs) and Large Language Models (LLMs) has significantly improved performance in EQA. However, as the authors highlight, these models face inherent limitations in long-term embodied settings:
- **Limited Context Windows:** VLMs and LLMs cannot efficiently process the vast amounts of visual and textual data accumulated by a robot over days or months. Ingesting all past observations is often infeasible.
- **Absence of Persistent Memory:** Traditional EQA setups, even with foundation models, lack structured, persistent memory systems that allow robots to recall specific, relevant past experiences from a large pool of data.
- **Inability to Combine Recall and Exploration:** No existing framework effectively balances when to explore the current environment versus when to retrieve information from long-term memory.

**Long-term Active Embodied Question Answering (LA-EQA)**, introduced in this paper, directly addresses these limitations. It proposes a new problem where robots must integrate information from multiple prior episodes with active exploration of the current environment to answer complex, temporally-grounded questions (e.g., "Are we missing anything we usually have for breakfast?" or "Where did I leave my umbrella yesterday?"). This mirrors how humans leverage long-term situational awareness.

The work also builds upon advancements in **semantic scene representation** (e.g., 3D representations, voxel maps, scene graphs) and **semantic-guided navigation**. The authors specifically opt for scene graphs, extending them from single snapshots to hierarchical, episodic scene graphs to capture spatiotemporal evolution. This positions their contribution as a significant step towards more human-like, intelligent embodied agents capable of operating robustly and knowledgeably over extended durations in dynamic real-world settings. By introducing a new benchmark for LA-EQA, the paper also aims to foster future research in this crucial area.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of this research is to enable robots to operate intelligently and assist humans more effectively over extended periods, accumulating and leveraging knowledge of their environments. This leads to the formulation of the **Long-term Active Embodied Question Answering (LA-EQA)** task, which serves as the primary challenge addressed by the paper.

The key motivations behind introducing LA-EQA and the proposed "Mind Palace Exploration" method are:

1. **Bridging the Gap in Embodied AI:** Current EQA systems are limited to either short-term exploration or single-episode memory recall. There is a clear need for robots to combine both capabilities for truly intelligent, long-term interaction with dynamic environments.
2. **Addressing VLM/LLM Limitations for Long-term Memory:** Foundation models, while powerful, struggle with the scale and temporal complexity of accumulated robot data. Their limited context windows and lack of persistent, structured memory prevent them from effectively reasoning over months of observations. The motivation is to overcome these architectural constraints through a novel memory system.
3. **Enabling Complex, Temporally-Grounded Reasoning:** Humans naturally perform tasks requiring reasoning over past, present, and future states (e.g., "Do we need to restock anything for breakfast?"). Robots need similar capabilities to handle practical, day-to-day questions that involve long-term context and the dynamic nature of environments.
4. **Developing Efficient Information Gathering Strategies:** In LA-EQA, robots must decide *when* to explore, *when* to consult memory, and *when* to stop. An uninformed search across vast past observations and unknown current environments is computationally prohibitive. The motivation is to develop intelligent planning mechanisms that efficiently navigate this exploration-recall trade-off.
5. **Creating a Benchmark for Long-term Embodied Reasoning:** The absence of a standardized benchmark for LA-EQA hinders progress. A key motivation is to introduce the first such benchmark to facilitate evaluation and foster future research in this critical domain.

In summary, the core objective is to develop a robust, scalable, and efficient framework that allows embodied agents to build, maintain, and leverage long-term, multi-episodic memory alongside active exploration, enabling them to answer complex questions that span temporal dimensions.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology, "Mind Palace Exploration," draws inspiration from the cognitive science technique of the "mind palace" to enable robots to effectively reason and plan for LA-EQA. It consists of three core components: Mind Palace Generation, Mind Palace Reasoning and Planning, and Early Stopping Criteria.

#### 3.1. Mind Palace Generation

This phase is dedicated to building a structured, long-term memory system for the robot, referred to as the Robotic Mind Palace (M).
- **Episodic World Instances:** The robot's continuous observations and trajectories are chunked into discrete "episodes" based on macro-temporal intervals (e.g., hours, days, weeks). This natural chunking aligns with robot operations (e.g., recharging pauses). Each episode forms a distinct "world instance," indexed by its temporal label, allowing LLMs to select relevant episodes.
- **Hierarchical Scene Graph Representation:** Each episodic world instance (G_i) is represented as a hierarchical scene graph.
 - **Viewpoint Nodes (w):** Dense viewpoints are sampled from past trajectories, each associated with the robot's pose, raw images, a list of detected objects (using models like GPT-4o for captions and object lists), and frame captions. These serve as indices for LLM-based image retrieval.
 - **Area Nodes (v):** Viewpoint nodes are spatially and contextually clustered into "area nodes" (e.g., "dining room," "entrance"). Each area node has a centroid and an object list.
 - **Graph Edges:** Neighboring viewpoints and areas are connected, forming a hierarchical structure.
- **Unified Representation:** The Mind Palace unifies past memories ([G_1..., G_N]) with the present knowledge of the environment (G_0). G_0 is initially sparse (only area nodes) at the start of an LA-EQA task, reflecting uncertainty about the current environment's state, and is progressively updated as the robot explores.

#### 3.2. Mind Palace Reasoning and Planning

This is the active phase where the robot interleaves reasoning and planning to answer a question (Q).
- **Reasoning over LA-EQA:**
 - **Information Sufficiency Check:** The robot queries a VLM with its current working memory (h_k, comprising action/observation history) and the question Q to determine if it has enough information to answer.
 - **Target Object Identification:** If not, an LLM is queried to identify a "target object" or "spatial concept" (y) to search for, which serves as the next exploration goal. This can be an explicit object or an inferred cue (e.g., "something to make coffee").
 - **Answering:** If sufficient information is deemed present, the agent uses a VLM to generate the final natural language answer.
- **Planning over Episodic World Instances (G):**
 - **Strategy Selection:** An LLM is used in a two-step reasoning process to select an ordered sequence of world instances (past or present) to search for the target object y. This avoids inefficient direct queries.
 - **Heuristic Prioritization:** A heuristic suggests prioritizing past world instances over the present (G_0), as prior knowledge can significantly inform and improve search efficiency in the current environment. Strategies include "PAST ONLY," "PRESENT ONLY," "PAST THEN PRESENT," or "MULTI PAST AND PRESENT."
- **Planning over Areas (v) in the Scene Graph:**
 - **Object-Goal Navigation:** Given a selected world instance G_i, the task becomes an object-goal navigation problem.
 - **Probability Estimation:** An LLM estimates the probability of finding object y in each area v ∈ G_i.
 - **Forward Search Planner:** A forward search planner minimizes the expected cost (J) to find y, selecting the optimal sequence of areas to explore. For present environment exploration (G_0), cost is path length; for past memory retrieval, it's a constant cost.
- **Exploring Viewpoints (w) and Replanning:**
 - **Viewpoint Selection:** An LLM selects specific viewpoints (w) within the chosen area v_i based on textual information in G_i (frame captions, object lists).
 - **Observation/Retrieval:** The robot either navigates to these viewpoints in the current environment (G_0) to take new images or retrieves corresponding images from the Mind Palace for past instances.
 - **Working Memory Update:** Retrieved/observed images are stored in working memory (h_k). This process repeats until y is detected or exploration limits are reached.

#### 3.3. Early Stopping of Memory Retrieval

To balance the exploration-recall trade-off and improve efficiency, the method introduces Value-of-Information (VoI) based stopping criteria for past memory retrieval:
- **Stopping Condition 1 (Single Area Prediction):** If the LLM's prediction set for the object y in the current environment (G_0) contains only one likely area, further memory retrieval is halted, as it won't change the next optimal exploration step.
- **Stopping Condition 2 (No Plan Improvement):** If additional memory retrieval is unlikely to improve the robot's plan for the *next* sequence of areas to explore in G_0 (e.g., if the closest area must be explored regardless), retrieval is stopped. This prevents gathering information that offers no practical utility gain.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research effectively demonstrates the superiority of Mind Palace Exploration in the novel LA-EQA task through extensive evaluations in both high-fidelity simulations and real-world settings.

1. **Superior Performance Across Metrics (Q1):**
 - **Answer Correctness:** Mind Palace Exploration significantly outperforms state-of-the-art baselines by **12–28%** in answer correctness (Table 1). This highlights the inability of existing EQA approaches to handle the complexity of long-term, multi-episodic reasoning.
 - **Exploration Efficiency:** The method achieves **16% higher exploration efficiency** compared to other active EQA agents (Table 1), especially in "past-present" questions (Figure 4) where historical data can guide present exploration.
 - **Memory Retrieval Efficiency:** Mind Palace Exploration achieves its higher correctness with remarkable efficiency, using **77% fewer retrieved images** than VLM-based methods (Table 1), demonstrating effective selection of relevant information.

2. **Effectiveness Across Question Types and Memory Lengths:**
 - The approach shows strong performance gains, particularly for "past" and "multi-past" question types (Figure 4), where understanding multiple past episodic memories is crucial. This underscores the value of the structured memory system and targeted image retrieval.
 - **Scalability:** Performance gains for Mind Palace Exploration increase with the number of past episodic memories, indicating its robustness and scalability for genuinely long-term operations. In contrast, multi-frame VLMs show a considerable drop in performance as the number of episodes increases (Figure 5).
 - **Generalizability:** The method successfully generalizes to diverse environments, including larger, non-standard settings like real-world construction sites, a large office (1,000 m²), and simulated warehouses, consistently outperforming baselines (Figure 5).

3. **Benefits of Early Memory Retrieval Stopping (Q2):**
 - The Value-of-Information-based early stopping criteria effectively reduce the amount of memory retrieved (by further **7.13 images on average**, from 22.86 to 15.73, and higher exploration efficiency from 0.42 to 0.45) while maintaining comparable answer accuracy (Table 1). This validates the efficiency gain without sacrificing performance.

4. **Real-world Hardware Experiment Feasibility (Q3):**
 - Mind Palace Exploration was successfully deployed on a legged robot in a 1,000 m² office space, leveraging 10 past episodes spanning 6 months of inspections (Figure 6).
 - It enabled efficient exploration for practical, day-to-day tasks (e.g., finding tools, tracking packages, identifying vacant desks), saving **3–10 room searches** compared to a robot without memory access. The robot successfully answered all tested questions where information was available.

5. **Limitations Identified:**
 - **Coverage of Past Trajectories:** Long-term understanding is limited by the comprehensiveness of past data; insufficient historical coverage can lead to incorrect inferences (e.g., misidentifying a vacant desk).
 - **VLM Semantic and Spatial Understanding:** Failures still occur due to limitations in current VLMs, such as inaccurate object detection, counting, functional property understanding, and spatial association within images (e.g., associating all objects in a frame with a single area).
 - **Benchmark Scalability:** Manual curation of long-term scenarios and questions is labor-intensive, limiting benchmark scalability and posing a challenge for future research.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work marks a significant advancement in embodied AI, particularly in enabling robots to operate intelligently over extended periods, moving beyond instantaneous perception or single-episode recall.

1. **Introduction of a Novel and Realistic Task:** The definition of Long-term Active Embodied Question Answering (LA-EQA) is a crucial contribution. It recognizes that truly intelligent embodied agents must integrate long-term memory with active, context-aware exploration, mirroring human cognitive processes. This task is highly relevant to future applications of robotics in dynamic environments like homes, offices, and industrial sites.

2. **Pioneering a Comprehensive Benchmark:** The introduction of the first LA-EQA benchmark, encompassing diverse simulation and real-world environments with temporally-grounded questions over days and months, is invaluable. This benchmark provides a standardized platform for evaluating progress in long-term embodied reasoning, stimulating future research and development in this previously under-explored area.

3. **Innovative Memory Architecture:** The "Robotic Mind Palace" offers a biologically inspired, structured memory system that is both effective and efficient. By organizing episodic experiences into hierarchical scene graphs and linking them temporally, the framework provides a scalable way for robots to manage vast amounts of data. This approach effectively addresses the limitations of current foundation models (VLMs/LLMs) regarding context windows and persistent memory.

4. **Enhanced Reasoning and Planning Capabilities:** The methodology's ability to interleave reasoning (identifying target objects, assessing information sufficiency) with hierarchical planning (selecting world instances, areas, and viewpoints) enables intelligent information gathering. The Value-of-Information-based early stopping criteria represent a sophisticated mechanism for optimizing the exploration-recall trade-off, leading to significant gains in both answer correctness and operational efficiency.

5. **Demonstrated Real-World Utility:** The successful deployment of Mind Palace Exploration on a legged robot in a large office environment is a compelling testament to its practical feasibility and impact. The ability to answer day-to-day questions efficiently by leveraging past inspection memory showcases the potential for robots to become truly useful, knowledgeable assistants in various real-world settings. This moves embodied AI closer to practical, long-term human-robot collaboration.

6. **Future Research Directions:** The identified limitations, particularly concerning the coverage of past data, the semantic and spatial understanding capabilities of current VLMs, and the scalability of benchmark generation, highlight important avenues for future research. Improving these aspects will further enhance the robustness and intelligence of long-term embodied agents, pushing the boundaries of what autonomous systems can achieve.

In conclusion, this paper does not merely propose an incremental improvement but rather a foundational shift in how embodied agents perceive, learn from, and interact with their environments over time. Its conceptual novelty, robust methodology, and demonstrated real-world impact position it as a seminal work in the quest for truly intelligent, long-term autonomous robotics.
