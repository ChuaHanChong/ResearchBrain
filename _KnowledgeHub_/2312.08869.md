# I'M HOI: Inertia-aware Monocular Capture of 3D Human-Object Interactions

> [!summary] Summary
> I'm-HOI captures 3D human and object motion from a single RGB video plus one object-mounted IMU. A holistic end-to-end tracker fuses inertial and visual cues through object mesh-alignment feedback, then a category-specific conditional motion diffusion filter projects results onto an interaction manifold and infills hand motion. It cuts Chamfer error roughly threefold on fast interactions at 0.5 seconds per frame, alongside a new 892k-frame dataset.

## Problem

- Monocular RGB capture of interactions suffers depth ambiguity and human-object occlusion, breaking down on fast dynamic motions like skateboarding despite pre-scanned templates.
- High-fidelity alternatives demand dense multi-view or RGBD rigs of three to dozens of cameras, undesirable for consumer-level daily usage.
- Existing RGB-plus-inertial fusion covers human-only motion with body-worn sensors, ignoring interacted objects; fitting-optimization pipelines also cost 20 to 120 seconds per frame.

## Method

- End-to-end holistic tracking treats the object as an extra body joint: a multi-scale ==CNN== predicts 3D keypoint heatmaps, followed by an ==inverse-kinematics== layer recovering ==SMPL== pose and shape.
- **Object mesh-alignment feedback** samples 400 posed-mesh vertices onto image feature grids over three loops to regress pose increments, trained with occlusion-aware silhouette plus a new silhouette-area loss, with optional IMU-regularized optimization.
- A category-specific ==interaction diffusion filter== conditions on tracked motion and raw inertial signals, denoising a 486-dimensional over-parameterized body-hand-object representation under offset, velocity, consistency and explicit acceleration regularizers. Together this is **I'm-HOI**.

## Results

- On the fast-motion IMHD2 dataset, per-frame Chamfer distance falls to **6.50 cm** for the human and **6.93 cm** for the object, versus VisTracker's **19.96** and **23.28**.
- Trained without them, it still generalizes to HODome (**8.19**/**9.05 cm**) and CHAIRS (**9.55**/**9.91 cm**), and runs at **0.5 s** per frame against VisTracker's **20 s**.
- Ablations show the full pipeline is about **four times** better than the naive variant; dropping IMU raises object error from **6.93** to **14.49 cm**, and the filter alone collapses to **37.35 cm**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Mounting one sensor on the object rather than the body buys occlusion-robust rotation and trajectory cues; sensor placement matters more than sensor count.
> - Generative motion priors should refine, not replace, a grounded tracker: the diffusion filter without upstream tracking degrades accuracy roughly fivefold.
> - Explicit acceleration constraints beat pseudo second-order smoothing, and category-level priors model dynamic interaction distributions that generic human motion priors miss.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The capture of 3D human-object interactions (HOI) is a fundamental problem in computer vision and graphics with widespread applications in robotics, gaming, and virtual/augmented reality. This paper positions itself at the intersection of traditional visual-based motion capture and emerging IMU-based approaches, addressing key limitations of existing methods to propose a more practical and robust solution.

Historically, high-fidelity HOI capture relied on sophisticated multi-view camera systems (e.g., 6-32 cameras), offering high accuracy but suffering from high cost, setup complexity, and limited usability outside controlled lab environments. More recent advancements have reduced the camera count (e.g., 3-8 views), making setups slightly more accessible but still cumbersome for consumer-level daily usage.

The advent of monocular methods, using a single RGB video input, has significantly increased convenience. However, these methods inherently struggle with critical challenges such as depth ambiguity (difficulty in inferring 3D information from 2D images), severe self-occlusions, and particularly, the faithful capture of challenging, fast-paced motions (e.g., skateboarding, which is highlighted in the paper). The reliance on pre-scanned object templates or parametric models in many monocular approaches can also limit their adaptability.

Complementing vision-based techniques, Inertial Measurement Units (IMUs) have gained prominence in motion capture due to their robustness to occlusion and light conditions. While dense IMU setups offer high accuracy, research has focused on sparse IMU configurations for real-time, consumer-level human motion capture. However, these efforts primarily concentrate on human-only scenarios. Crucially, existing hybrid methods that combine monocular RGB with sparse IMUs have largely ignored the *interacting objects*. Furthermore, current IMU-based solutions often require tedious body-worn sensors.

This paper identifies a critical gap: the lack of accurate and convenient solutions for 3D HOI capture using a *minimal* amount of RGB data combined with *object-mounted* IMUs. The authors emphasize that IMUs are increasingly integrated into everyday "smart" objects (phones, smartwatches), making object-mounted sensors a natural and convenient choice. The absence of comprehensive, multi-modal datasets for such hybrid capture settings also presented a significant barrier.

"I’m-HOI" directly addresses this void by proposing a novel hybrid approach that leverages the strengths of both modalities: RGB for visual context and IMUs for robust motion cues, especially during occlusions or fast movements. By contributing a large, multi-modal dataset (IMHD²) with ground truth for human, object, RGB, and object-mounted IMU data, the authors also provide a crucial resource that will foster further research and development in this nascent area of hybrid human-object interaction capture. This work marks a significant step towards enabling lightweight, robust, and accessible 3D HOI capture, aligning with the broader trend of integrating smart sensing capabilities into our environment.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of this research is to develop a robust, accurate, and convenient method for capturing 3D human-object interactions (HOI). The authors articulate several specific motivations and goals:

- **Faithfully Capture 3D Human-Object Motions in a Novel Setting:** The primary goal is to accurately recover the 3D motions of both the human and the interacting object. The novelty lies in the chosen sensor configuration: a single monocular RGB camera combined with a minimal, *object-mounted* Inertial Measurement Unit (IMU). This contrasts with traditional multi-view systems and even newer monocular methods that don't effectively integrate object-specific IMU data.
- **Address Limitations of Existing Monocular RGB Methods:** Current monocular approaches are highly susceptible to depth ambiguities and occlusions between the human and the object, especially during dynamic and fast-moving interactions (e.g., skateboarding). IMU data, being robust to these visual challenges, is leveraged to mitigate these shortcomings.
- **Overcome Challenges with Body-Worn IMUs:** While IMUs are valuable for motion capture, the requirement for body-worn sensors can be inconvenient. The paper proposes that object-mounted IMUs are more "natural and convenient," given the increasing integration of IMUs into everyday smart devices. This choice aims to make the capture process less intrusive and more user-friendly for consumer-level applications.
- **Fuse Multi-Modal Data Effectively:** A key objective is to design a robust framework that can intelligently fuse the complementary information from the RGB stream (visual appearance, 2D cues) and IMU signals (robust motion, orientation, acceleration). This fusion is intended to enhance accuracy and robustness, particularly when visual information is ambiguous or occluded.
- **Infill Realistic Hand Motions and Category-Specific Priors:** Beyond just body and object motions, the system aims to generate "vivid body, hand, and object motions." This involves leveraging category-level interaction priors to refine initial results and infill detailed hand movements, which are crucial for realistic HOI representation.
- **Contribute a High-Quality Multi-Modal Dataset:** Recognizing the lack of suitable training and evaluation data for this novel hybrid capture setting, a significant objective is to collect and release a large dataset (IMHD²) comprising ground truth human and object motions, dense RGB inputs, and rich object-mounted IMU measurements. This dataset is crucial for training and evaluating such multi-modal approaches and fostering future research.
- **Improve Efficiency and Generalization:** The motivation also includes developing a method that is not only accurate but also computationally efficient (low runtime cost) and capable of generalizing well to diverse interaction scenarios and unseen objects, a common challenge for data-driven methods.

In summary, the motivation stems from the desire to make high-fidelity 3D human-object interaction capture more accessible, robust, and comprehensive by creatively integrating object-mounted inertial sensing with monocular vision, supported by a novel dataset and a sophisticated deep learning pipeline.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The "I’m-HOI" system adopts a two-stage paradigm: **General Interaction Motion Inference** followed by a **Category-specific Interaction Diffusion Filter**. It leverages monocular RGB video and object-mounted IMU sensor data.

#### 3.1. General Interaction Motion Inference (Holistic Human-Object Tracking)

This stage aims for an end-to-end recovery of human-object spatial arrangements, designed to be robust against occlusions and efficient.

- **Preprocessing:**
 - Input: Monocular RGB image sequence `I`, raw IMU data (rotation `Q`, acceleration `A`), and a normalized object template `O`.
 - Initial steps: Segment human (`S_h`) and object (`S_o`) masks using SAM. Extract image features from stacked RGB and object mask using a pre-trained ResNet-34.
 - Outputs: Human shape (`β`) and pose (`θ`), object rotation (`R_o`) and translation (`T_o`). Uses SMPL model for human representation.

- **End-to-end Holistic Human-Object Tracking:**
 - **Human Motion Estimation:** A multi-scale CNN-based network jointly detects 3D human body joints and the object center. 3D keypoint heatmaps are reconstructed and their positions determined. An off-the-shelf pre-trained Inverse Kinematics (IK) layer is fine-tuned to recover human pose and shape from the estimated 3D joints, trained with 3D keypoint and 2D reprojection losses.
 - **Object Tracking with Mesh Alignment Feedback:**
 - An initial object pose is derived from the IMU's raw rotation and estimated translation.
 - To correct IMU biases and inaccurate translation under occlusion, a series of MLP-based regressors are attached to intermediate image feature grids, forming a feedback loop.
 - In each iteration, vertices are sampled from the posed object mesh and projected onto the feature map to obtain mesh-aligned features. These are fed into the regressors to predict corrective increments for object rotation and translation.
 - Training uses an occlusion-aware silhouette loss (`L_occ-sil`) and an augmented silhouette area loss (`L_area`) to align the rendered object silhouette with the segmented object mask.
 - **Overall Training:** The module is trained end-to-end using a combined loss function encompassing 3D keypoint, 2D reprojection, IK twist, and the mesh alignment feedback losses.

- **Robust and Lightweight Optimization (Optional):**
 - An optional post-processing step to improve object tracking precision, especially when the object is invisible.
 - This module formulates an energy function (`E`) that combines visual cues (`E_visual`) and IMU measurements (`E_imu`).
 - `E_visual` minimizes the discrepancy between rendered object and segmented object.
 - `E_imu` regularizes object motion temporally, constraining object rotation to raw IMU rotation and object trajectory to IMU acceleration (second-order difference).

#### 3.2. Category-specific Interaction Diffusion Filter

This second stage refines the results from the first stage, projecting them onto a learned motion manifold and infilling realistic hand motions.

- **Interaction Representation:** A novel "over-parameterization" representation `x_n` is used, combining:
 - SMPL-H (52 joints) body-hand joint positions and rotations.
 - Object translation and rotation.
 - Raw inertial rotation and free acceleration signals.
 - This comprehensive representation ensures mutual constraints between components.

- **Conditional Diffusion Denoising Process:**
 - A forward diffusion process iteratively adds Gaussian noise to the target interaction motion `x_0`.
 - The reverse process, implemented as a neural network (`x_phi`), predicts the original `x_0` from the noisy input `x_n`, conditioned on body-only joint positions/rotations, object motion from the first stage, and raw IMU measurements.
 - Training uses an L1-norm simple objective for denoising.

- **Regularization Terms:** Four specific regularization terms are introduced to enforce realism and consistency:
 - `L_off`: Enforces the predicted object center to lie within a small region relative to body-hand joints, defined by learned distance offsets.
 - `L_consist`: Ensures consistency between predicted human body-hand joints and the SMPL-H model skinned from predicted joint rotations.
 - `L_vel`: Smooths the human motion velocity temporally.
 - `L_imu`: Guides generated object poses and trajectories to conform to IMU measurements, enhancing robustness under invisible scenarios by explicitly incorporating acceleration constraints.

#### 3.3. Dataset Contribution (IMHD² - Inertial and Multi-view Highly Dynamic Human-Object Interactions Dataset)

To support training and evaluation, the authors collected a large multi-modal dataset.

- **Capture Setup:** High-end multi-view camera system (32 Z CAMs, 4K, 60 fps) and two Xsens DOT IMU sensors (object-mounted and performer's leg for synchronization, 60 Hz).
- **Content:** 295 interaction sequences (892k frames), 15 subjects, 10 diverse objects, covering a wide range of interaction patterns.
- **Annotation:** Ground truth human motions (SMPL-H), object poses (manual key-frame segmentation propagated to sequence, optimized Euclidean transformations), and 3D object scans.
- **IMU Processing:** Temporal synchronization using a distinct "takeoff" motion. Spatial alignment between IMU and world coordinates using a Sylvester equation to solve for transformation. Raw IMU acceleration is converted to free acceleration. Notably, the paper also details normalization for real-world IMU data (to remove linear acceleration from pure rotation) and simulation for synthetic data (second-order difference of object translation).

The methodology combines a feed-forward inference network for initial estimations with a robust optimization module and a sophisticated diffusion model for high-fidelity refinement. The comprehensive dataset is a cornerstone for training and validating this complex multi-modal approach.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted by the authors demonstrate the superior performance and capabilities of I’m-HOI across multiple metrics and scenarios.

- **Quantitative Performance Comparison:**
 - I’m-HOI consistently outperforms state-of-the-art baselines (PHOSA, CHORE, VisTracker) on all tested datasets: IMHD², BEHAVE, and InterCap.
 - On the challenging IMHD² dataset (characterized by fast interaction motions), I’m-HOI shows a substantial improvement, outperforming baselines by a large margin (around 15 cm) in both human (SMPL) and object tracking accuracy, measured by Per-frame Chamfer Distance (CD) and Sliding Window Chamfer Distance (CD over 10s). For instance, on IMHD², I'm-HOi achieves 6.50/6.93 (smpl/object per-frame CD) and 5.36/8.53 (smpl/object 10s CD) versus VisTracker's 19.96/23.28 and 17.02/18.10.

- **Qualitative Results:**
 - Visualizations (Figure 4, 5, 10, 11, 12) confirm that I’m-HOI captures better human-object spatial arrangements, including accurate relative pose and position.
 - The approach demonstrates superior robustness under severe occlusions, a critical advantage offered by the IMU modality.
 - I’m-HOI's sequential capture results (Figure 10) show excellent spatial-temporal coherence, producing accurate and consistent human-object interactions over time.

- **Generalization Ability:**
 - Evaluations on unseen datasets (HODome and CHAIRS) demonstrate I’m-HOI's strong generalization capabilities. It outperforms baselines by a significant margin and achieves more balanced performance between per-frame and sequential results (Table 2). This suggests that the learned priors and multi-modal fusion are robust across different interaction scenarios.

- **Runtime Cost:**
 - I’m-HOI is significantly more efficient than prior methods. It processes a frame in approximately 0.5 seconds for the complete pipeline. This is a vast improvement over purely optimization-based methods (PHOSA: ~2 minutes/frame) and even learning-and-optimization methods (CHORE: ~1 minute/frame, VisTracker: ~20 seconds/frame). This efficiency makes it more suitable for real-time or near real-time applications.

- **Ablation Studies:**
 - **Network Architecture (Table 3, Figure 6):**
 - The mesh alignment feedback (maf) component is crucial for improving per-frame object tracking results.
 - The optional optimization module (optim) significantly enhances temporal consistency.
 - The diffusion filter (filter) not only refines human-object spatial arrangements onto the learned interaction manifold but also generates more vivid and realistic hand motions, underscoring its importance in producing high-fidelity results. The full pipeline is shown to perform 4 times better than a naive implementation.
 - **Input Modality (Table 4, Figure 7):**
 - Adding IMU modality to baselines improves their performance, confirming the general benefit of inertial measurements for HOI.
 - However, I’m-HOI still achieves the best results, especially for object tracking, when IMU is involved. This indicates that the proposed network design effectively maximizes the benefits of the IMU modality, surpassing naive integration into existing methods.
 - **Regularization Terms (Table 6):**
 - All regularization terms (L_off, L_vel, L_consist, L_imu) within the diffusion filter contribute positively to the final performance, ensuring more specific, consistent, smooth, and physically plausible interaction motions. L_imu, in particular, is vital for robustness and physically accurate results.

These findings collectively establish I’m-HOI as a leading method for 3D human-object interaction capture in a lightweight, hybrid sensing setup, addressing critical challenges of accuracy, robustness, efficiency, and generalization.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "I’m-HOI" research represents a significant advancement in the field of 3D human-object interaction (HOI) capture, with broad implications for both academic research and practical applications.

- **Pioneering Hybrid Capture Paradigm:** The most significant contribution is the successful exploration and implementation of a novel, lightweight hybrid capture setting combining a minimal monocular RGB camera with object-mounted IMU sensors. This is the first dedicated effort to address challenging 3D HOI capture under such a configuration. This paradigm shift offers a practical and accessible alternative to traditional expensive, multi-camera systems or less robust monocular vision-only methods.
- **Enhanced Robustness and Accuracy:** By intelligently fusing visual and inertial data, I’m-HOI overcomes key limitations of monocular vision, such as depth ambiguity and occlusions, particularly during fast and dynamic interactions. The results demonstrate superior accuracy for both human and object tracking, outperforming state-of-the-art baselines by a considerable margin. This enhanced robustness is crucial for real-world scenarios where controlled environments are not always feasible.
- **Efficiency and Practicality:** The proposed method achieves significantly faster inference times (0.5 seconds per frame) compared to previous approaches. This efficiency, combined with the minimal sensor setup (a single RGB camera and a small, object-mounted IMU), makes I’m-HOI highly practical for consumer-level applications. The increasing integration of IMUs into everyday smart objects further strengthens the viability of this approach.
- **High-Quality Multi-Modal Dataset (IMHD²):** The contribution of IMHD², a large and comprehensive dataset featuring synchronized RGB, object-mounted IMU, and ground truth human-object motions, is invaluable. This dataset fills a critical gap in the research community, providing a much-needed resource for training and evaluating future hybrid HOI capture methods. Its focus on highly dynamic interactions and diverse objects will drive further innovation.
- **Realistic Motion Synthesis:** The integration of a category-aware motion diffusion model, conditioned on both initial estimates and raw IMU data, is a key innovation. This allows for the generation of vivid and realistic body, object, and crucially, hand motions. The over-parameterization representation and tailored regularization terms contribute to capturing subtle nuances of interaction, enhancing the visual fidelity and physical plausibility of the captured motions.
- **Broad Applications:** The technology developed in this paper has far-reaching potential across various domains:
 - **Robotics and Embodied AI:** Enabling robots to better understand and predict human intent and interaction with objects, facilitating more natural human-robot collaboration.
 - **Virtual Reality (VR) and Augmented Reality (AR):** Creating highly realistic and immersive experiences with believable avatars and object interactions, crucial for gaming, training simulations, and virtual collaboration.
 - **Human-Computer Interaction (HCI):** Developing more intuitive and natural interfaces where user interactions with smart objects can be accurately tracked and interpreted.
 - **Sports Analysis and Biomechanics:** Providing detailed motion analysis of athletes interacting with equipment, enabling performance optimization and injury prevention.
 - **Content Creation:** Streamlining the process of generating realistic character animations for film, games, and digital media.

While the current method relies on pre-scanned object templates and rigid object tracking, its foundations are solid. Future work extending it to template-free frameworks or articulated/deformable objects will further broaden its impact. Overall, I’m-HOI represents a critical step towards the pervasive and robust capture of human-object interactions in our increasingly sensor-rich environment.
