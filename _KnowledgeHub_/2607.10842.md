# D-SafeMPC: Diffusion-Driven Safe Model Predictive Control with Discrete-Time Control Barrier Functions

> [!summary] Summary
> A framework called D-SafeMPC combines conditional diffusion models with discrete-time Control Barrier Functions (CBFs), Control Lyapunov Functions (CLFs), and Model Predictive Control (MPC) in an iterative projection framework for safe and dynamically feasible robotic trajectory planning. It demonstrates superior safe goal-reach rates, reaching up to 100% in dynamic environments, and improved computational efficiency by providing probabilistically safer warm starts to the MPC compared to existing methods.

## Problem

- Diffusion models excel at generating complex trajectories but lack inherent mechanisms to enforce explicit safety or dynamic constraints, making their outputs potentially unsafe for robotics.
- Existing soft guidance methods for diffusion, while improving probabilistic safety, do not guarantee hard constraint satisfaction.
- Hybrid diffusion-MPC frameworks are susceptible to the quality of initial trajectories from diffusion models, often leading to MPC convergence failures or local optima in complex or dynamic environments.

## Method

- In **D-SafeMPC**, discrete-time ==Control Barrier Functions (CBFs)== and ==Control Lyapunov Functions (CLFs)== are used to define strict safety and goal-reaching conditions.
- CBF and CLF guidance is directly integrated into the ==reverse diffusion process== using a ==gradient-based guidance== approach, probabilistically steering trajectory generation towards safe and goal-directed regions.
- An iterative ==Model Predictive Control (MPC)== projection scheme refines the guided trajectories at each denoising step, ensuring strict compliance with dynamic and safety constraints and providing a high-quality ==warm start== for the MPC solver.

## Results

- D-SafeMPC achieved superior or competitive safe goal-reach rates across all tested scenarios, including **100%** in Dynamic Env 1 and **92.5%** in Dynamic Env 3, outperforming baselines like CoBL-Diffusion and various DPCC variants.
- The framework demonstrated improved computational efficiency compared to other MPC-based models, attributed to the better warm-starts provided by CBF and CLF guidance.
- Sim-to-real experiments on a Franka manipulator confirmed the practical applicability and transferability of the simulation-trained diffusion model to a physical robot in a static obstacle environment.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating CBF/CLF guidance during the reverse diffusion process significantly improves the quality of initial trajectories for MPC, leading to more robust and stable convergence.
> - Iteratively projecting diffusion-generated trajectories onto the feasible set via MPC effectively guarantees hard safety and dynamic constraint satisfaction throughout the planning process.
> - Combining the data-driven generative power of diffusion models with the formal safety guarantees of classical control theory enables high task success rates while maintaining strict safety in complex, dynamic robotic environments.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work is situated at the intersection of generative artificial intelligence, specifically diffusion models, and classical control theory, particularly Model Predictive Control (MPC), Control Barrier Functions (CBFs), and Control Lyapunov Functions (CLFs).

Diffusion models have demonstrated capabilities in various generative tasks, including image synthesis and, more recently, robotic trajectory planning. They excel at learning complex, multimodal behaviors from demonstrations and generating high-dimensional trajectories without compounding errors often found in sequential methods. However, a significant limitation of diffusion models in safety-critical applications like robotics is their inherent inability to enforce explicit constraints such as system dynamics, obstacle avoidance, and safety margins. Trajectories generated by standard diffusion models can therefore be physically infeasible or unsafe.

Previous approaches to address this safety gap include:
- **Soft guidance methods:** Some work incorporates CBFs and CLFs during the reverse diffusion process to *guide* the generated trajectories towards safe and goal-directed regions (e.g., CoBL-Diffusion). While this improves probabilistic safety, it does not enforce *hard* constraints, meaning safety violations can still occur.
- **Hybrid diffusion-MPC frameworks:** Other methods propose combining diffusion models with MPC to enforce hard constraints. In these frameworks (e.g., DPCC), a diffusion model generates an initial trajectory, which MPC then refines to be safe and dynamically feasible. This corrected trajectory can then be fed back to guide the next denoising step in an iterative cycle. A primary limitation of such hybrid approaches is that a poor initial trajectory from the diffusion model can lead to MPC failing to converge to a feasible solution, especially in complex or dynamic environments, compromising stability and safety.

D-SafeMPC builds upon these hybrid approaches by introducing an additional layer of constraint satisfaction. It addresses the instability issue of prior hybrid methods by integrating CBF and CLF guidance directly into the reverse diffusion process *before* the MPC step. This aims to provide the MPC with higher-quality, probabilistically safer initial guesses, thereby improving its convergence reliability and efficiency. The paper thus contributes to the ongoing effort to merge the high-level planning capabilities of data-driven generative models with the robustness and safety guarantees of optimization-based control.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a robust and reliable framework for safe trajectory planning in robotics that leverages the generative capabilities of diffusion models while guaranteeing adherence to critical safety and dynamic constraints.

The motivation for D-SafeMPC stems from two main challenges observed in the current research landscape:

1. **Safety and Feasibility Deficiencies of Diffusion Models:** While diffusion models demonstrate proficiency in learning and generating complex robotic trajectories from demonstrations, they fundamentally lack mechanisms to inherently enforce explicit physical constraints. This deficiency means their outputs often violate system dynamics, lead to collisions, or exceed operational limits, rendering them unsuitable for direct deployment in safety-critical robotic applications. Current guidance methods (e.g., CBF/CLF-guided diffusion) provide only "soft" preferences, meaning trajectories are steered *towards* safety but are not strictly *guaranteed* to be safe or dynamically feasible. This necessitates a mechanism for enforcing "hard" constraints.

2. **Limitations of Hybrid Diffusion-MPC Frameworks:** Model Predictive Control (MPC) is a suitable framework for enforcing hard constraints due to its optimization-based nature. Hybrid approaches that couple diffusion models with MPC (e.g., DPCC) attempt to address the safety issue by using MPC to project diffusion-generated trajectories onto a safe and feasible set. However, a critical practical limitation of these methods is their sensitivity to the quality of the initial trajectory provided by the diffusion model. If the diffusion model generates a trajectory that is substantially unsafe or dynamically infeasible, the MPC solver may fail to converge to a valid solution within its allocated computational budget, or it might get trapped in local optima, leading to planning failures or unsafe robot behavior. This problem is particularly pronounced in challenging scenarios with dynamic obstacles or complex environments.

To overcome these challenges, the authors propose D-SafeMPC with the following specific objectives:
- **Guide diffusion towards probabilistic safety:** To steer the reverse diffusion process dynamically towards inherently safer and goal-directed regions using Control Barrier Functions (CBFs) for collision avoidance and Control Lyapunov Functions (CLFs) for goal convergence. This guidance should operate without requiring retraining of the diffusion model or extensive safety-constrained demonstration data.
- **Provide reliable MPC warm starts:** To use the CBF/CLF-guided diffusion output to provide high-quality initial guesses (warm starts) for the MPC. This is intended to improve MPC's convergence stability, efficiency, and ability to find feasible solutions, even in complex environments.
- **Enforce hard constraints iteratively:** To employ an iterative projection scheme where an MPC refines the trajectory at each denoising step, thereby ensuring strict compliance with dynamic and safety constraints throughout the trajectory generation process.
- **Demonstrate empirical superiority:** To validate the framework's effectiveness across various scenarios (static and dynamic obstacles) and in a sim-to-real setting, demonstrating improvements in safety, task success rates, and planning efficiency compared to existing state-of-the-art baselines.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

D-SafeMPC integrates conditional diffusion models with discrete-time Control Barrier Functions (CBFs), Control Lyapunov Functions (CLFs), and Model Predictive Control (MPC) within an iterative projection framework.

**A. Discrete-Time Control Barrier and Lyapunov Functions**
The robot's dynamics are considered as a continuous-time control-affine nonlinear system, which is then approximated by a discrete-time nonlinear model `x_k+1 = F(x_k, u_k)`.
- **Discrete-Time CBF:** For safety, a continuously differentiable function `h(x)` defines a safe set `C = {x | h(x) ≥ 0}`. `h` is a discrete-time CBF if there exists a class-K function `α` such that `h(F(x_k, u_k)) - h(x_k) ≥ -α(h(x_k))`. This condition (`ψ_h ≥ 0`) ensures forward invariance of the safe set `C`. For collision avoidance with multiple static or dynamic obstacles, a smooth under-approximation of the minimum of individual obstacle distance functions `h_j(x)` is used: `h(x) = - (1/λ) ln(∑ exp(-λh_j(x)))`.
- **Discrete-Time CLF:** For stability or goal-reaching, a continuously differentiable function `V(x)` (e.g., `V(x) = ||x - g||^2`) is a discrete-time CLF if there exists `σ ∈ (0,1)` such that `V(F(x_k, u_k)) - V(x_k) ≤ -σV(x_k)`. This condition (`Φ_V ≤ 0`) ensures exponential convergence to the goal.

**B. Conditional Diffusion Models for Trajectory Planning**
The framework utilizes a conditional diffusion model to learn a goal-conditioned stochastic control policy `π` from an offline dataset of expert trajectories. The model is trained to approximate the conditional distribution of trajectories `p_θ(τ | y)`, where `τ` is a state-action trajectory and `y` contains conditioning information like the current state and goal. During inference, the reverse diffusion process iteratively denoises a noisy trajectory from `τ_N` (pure Gaussian noise) to `τ_0` (the desired clean trajectory).

**C. CBF and CLF Guidance during Reverse Diffusion**
To address the lack of explicit constraint enforcement in standard diffusion models, D-SafeMPC incorporates CBF and CLF guidance directly into the reverse diffusion process. This guidance is implemented as a "soft constraint" using classifier guidance, which steers trajectory generation toward safe and goal-directed regions without requiring model retraining.
1. **Augmented System:** For dynamic environments, the robot dynamics are augmented with the autonomous dynamics of obstacles `ẋ_j = f_j(x_j)`, forming an augmented state `¯x`.
2. **Gradient-based Guidance:** The likelihood of a trajectory `˜τ_i-1` belonging to the safe set `C` is modeled using a Gibbs-Boltzmann distribution. By taking the logarithm of the safety-conditioned posterior `p(˜τ_i-1 | ˜τ_i, i, y, C)` and applying a first-order Taylor expansion, it is shown that the conditional distribution's mean is shifted by a gradient term.
3. **Control Input Guidance:** The guidance is applied by computing the gradients of the CBF and CLF conditions (`ψ_h` and `Φ_V`) with respect to the control input `u`:
 - `∇_u ψ_h(¯x, u)`: Indicates how to modify `u` to improve satisfaction of the discrete-time CBF condition (repels from obstacles).
 - `∇_u Φ_V(¯x, u)`: Indicates how to modify `u` to decrease the CLF value (attracts towards the goal).
4. **Modified Denoising Mean:** The predicted mean `μ_θ` from the diffusion model is adjusted as `ˆμ = μ_θ + λ_c ∇_u ψ_h - λ_l ∇_u Φ_V`. This modified mean (`ˆμ`) is then used for sampling the next, less noisy trajectory `˜τ_i-1`. Adaptive weights `λ_c` for CBF are used, increasing as obstacles are approached, while `λ_l` for CLF is fixed. Guidance is initiated after an initial "burn-in" phase of denoising steps (i.e., when the trajectory is less noisy).

**D. Iterative MPC Projection for Hard Constraint Enforcement**
While CBF/CLF guidance probabilistically steers trajectories towards safety, it does not guarantee hard constraint satisfaction. To achieve this, D-SafeMPC employs an MPC as a corrective refinement step:
1. **MPC Formulation:** The MPC solves a finite-horizon optimal control problem at each denoising step. Its objective function minimizes the deviation from the CBF/CLF-guided trajectory (`˜τ_i-1`) while regularizing control inputs. The core constraints are:
 - `h(¯x_k) ≥ 0`: Enforces strict safety (obstacle avoidance) using the CBF.
 - `¯x_k+1 = ¯F(¯x_k, u_k)`: Enforces system dynamics.
 - `τ_0^i-1 = ˜τ_i-1`: Uses the guided diffusion output as a warm-start for the MPC solver.
2. **Iterative Projection Scheme:** The MPC-generated `τ_i-1` (the projected, safe, and feasible trajectory) is then fed back as the input to the next denoising iteration of the diffusion model. This iterative cycle ensures that as the diffusion model refines the trajectory, MPC continually enforces strict safety and dynamic constraints. Similar to the guidance, MPC projection is applied only after an initial burn-in phase (e.g., `i ≤ N/2`) when the trajectory is sufficiently denoised. This ensures that the MPC operates on trajectories that are already probabilistically steered towards safety, improving its convergence rate and reliability. The control action `u_t` from the final converged trajectory `τ_0` is applied to the robot, and the updated state conditions the generation of the subsequent trajectory.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The D-SafeMPC framework was evaluated through simulations on a Franka manipulator across four scenarios (one static-obstacle and three dynamic-obstacle settings) and in a sim-to-real experiment on a physical Franka robot. Performance was assessed using two metrics: Goal Reach Rate (proportion of collision-free goal-reaching trials) and Safety-Compliant Goal Reached Rate (proportion of trials satisfying all safety constraints throughout the trajectory). Baselines included CoBL-Diffusion, Diffuser, various DPCC (Diffusion Predictive Control with Constraints) variants, Guidance-only methods, Model-Free planners, and Post-Processing.

**A. Simulation Results:**
- **Overall Performance:** D-SafeMPC demonstrated superior or competitive performance across all tested scenarios.
 - **Static Obstacle Scenario:** D-SafeMPC achieved an 80% success and safe-success rate. While CoBL showed a higher goal reach rate (100% vs 80%), its safety-compliant rate was significantly lower (5%), indicating it frequently reached the goal unsafely. DPCC-C achieved a slightly higher safe-success rate (90%) in this specific static scenario.
 - **Dynamic Obstacle Scenarios (Env 1, 2, 3):** D-SafeMPC consistently achieved the highest or near-highest success and safe-success rates. For Dynamic Env 1, it achieved a perfect 100% success and safe-success rate. In Dynamic Env 2, it reached 87.5% for both, and in Dynamic Env 3, 92.5%. This indicates robust performance in enforcing safety constraints even when obstacles are in motion.
- **Comparison with Baselines:**
 - **CoBL-Diffusion and Diffuser:** These methods, which either lack formal constraint enforcement or rely only on soft guidance, exhibited significantly lower safety-compliant goal reach rates across all scenarios, particularly in dynamic environments. Their higher goal reach rates often came at the cost of safety violations.
 - **DPCC Variants:** While DPCC-C performed well in static environments, its performance generally degraded in dynamic obstacle settings. Other DPCC variants (DPCC-R, DPCC-T, and their tightened versions) also showed varied and often lower safety-compliant rates compared to D-SafeMPC in dynamic environments. This supports the paper's claim that D-SafeMPC's CBF/CLF guidance provides superior warm starts to MPC.
- **Computational Efficiency:** D-SafeMPC exhibited a favorable balance between high safe-success rates and computational overhead. It was found to be computationally more efficient than other MPC-based models (DPCC variants and Post-Processing methods). This improved efficiency is attributed to the CBF and CLF guidance, which provides better-initialized trajectories to the MPC, leading to faster convergence within the optimization steps. Methods without MPC projection (e.g., Diffuser) were faster but sacrificed safety and reliability.

**B. Sim-to-Real Experiments:**
- **Validation:** The framework was validated in a sim-to-real experiment using a physical Franka robot manipulator in a static obstacle environment. The diffusion model, trained solely in simulation, was successfully deployed on the real robot.
- **Confirmation:** The experiment demonstrated the real-world performance of D-SafeMPC in executing trajectories guided by CBFs and CLFs with final optimization by MPC, confirming its practical applicability and ability to transfer from simulation to reality.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The D-SafeMPC framework presents a notable advancement in the field of safe robotic trajectory planning, particularly at the intersection of generative models and classical control.

**Significance:**
- **Bridging Generative Power with Safety Guarantees:** The work addresses a core limitation of diffusion models in robotics by providing a principled method to inject formal safety and dynamic constraints. It effectively combines the flexibility and expressiveness of data-driven trajectory generation with the rigorous guarantees of control theory.
- **Enhanced Reliability and Robustness:** By guiding the reverse diffusion process with CBFs and CLFs, D-SafeMPC significantly improves the quality of initial trajectories provided to the MPC. This leads to more reliable and stable convergence of the MPC, even in complex and dynamic environments where unguided hybrid approaches often fail. This enhanced robustness is crucial for deploying learning-based planners in real-world, safety-critical applications.
- **Improved Computational Efficiency:** The provision of probabilistically safe warm starts to the MPC reduces the optimization burden, enabling faster convergence and making the overall planning process more computationally efficient than other MPC-based diffusion approaches. This is a practical advantage for real-time robotic operations.
- **Generalizability of Guidance:** The use of classifier guidance allows for incorporating safety constraints (CBFs/CLFs) without requiring retraining of the diffusion model or extensive datasets of safety-compliant demonstrations. This makes the approach more flexible and adaptable to new environments or constraint specifications.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Safer Autonomous Systems:** D-SafeMPC has the potential to enhance the safety and trustworthiness of autonomous robots operating in uncertain and dynamic environments, such as collaborative robotics, autonomous driving, and industrial automation.
- **Robust Robotic Manipulation:** The framework could lead to more robust and reliable trajectory planning for complex robotic manipulation tasks, particularly in cluttered or dynamic workspaces.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** This work provides a foundation for further research into integrating other forms of formal verification and constraint enforcement into generative models. It opens avenues for exploring adaptive guidance weights, more sophisticated constraint formulations, and methods to handle higher-dimensional observation spaces.

**Limitations and Future Work:**
The authors acknowledge several limitations:
- **Scalability to High-Dimensional State Spaces:** The current MPC optimization might become computationally prohibitive for systems with very high-dimensional state spaces, such as those involving direct visual inputs, limiting its application in complex perception-rich environments.
- **Error Propagation:** Positioning inaccuracies in early time steps can recursively propagate through the diffusion process, potentially destabilizing subsequent trajectory predictions, especially since the diffusion model conditions generation on the current state.

Future work aims to address these limitations by extending the framework to systems with more complex, higher-dimensional state spaces while mitigating scalability challenges and error propagation concerns. This could involve investigating more efficient optimization techniques for high-dimensional MPC or alternative strategies for integrating guidance in a way that is less susceptible to initial state inaccuracies.
