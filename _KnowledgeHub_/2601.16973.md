# VisGym: Diverse, Customizable, Scalable Environments for Multimodal Agents

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from UC Berkeley introduced VisGym, a diverse and customizable benchmark suite, to evaluate and train Vision-Language Models in multi-step visual interaction tasks. Their extensive evaluations reveal that frontier VLMs achieve only 46.61% success on easy tasks, with performance significantly impacted by context length, visual grounding, and a strong reliance on explicit textual feedback.

## Problem

- Existing Vision-Language Models (VLMs) struggle with multi-step visual interactions, where perception, memory, and action must be integrated over long horizons.
- Current VLM benchmarks for interactive decision-making are fragmented, domain-specific, lack fine-grained control for diagnostic analysis, and often do not provide scalable environments for training.
- There is a critical gap in systematically characterizing and diagnosing why VLMs fail in dynamic, visually rich environments, moving beyond mere observational studies.

## Method

- Introduces ==VisGym==, a suite of ==17 diverse, visually interactive environments== built on the Gymnasium framework, spanning symbolic puzzles, real-image understanding, navigation, and manipulation.
- Designed with fine-grained control over factors like task difficulty, input representation, planning horizon, and environmental feedback, enabling systematic diagnostic experiments.
- Includes multi-step oracle solvers for each environment capable of generating diverse demonstration trajectories, facilitating ==supervised fine-tuning (SFT)== of VLMs.
- Employs a function-conditioned action space and provides function instructions and environment feedback to aid VLM interaction and self-correction.

## Results

- The top-performing frontier VLM (Gemini-3-Pro) achieved a success rate of **46.61%** on easy tasks and **26.00%** on hard tasks across VisGym's diverse environments.
- Converting visual observations to ASCII text improved performance up to **3-4x** for some symbolic tasks, and removing text-based feedback consistently degraded performance across all evaluated models.
- Supervised fine-tuning (SFT) using solver-generated demonstrations significantly boosted VLM performance, with newer base models like Qwen3-VL showing nearly **double** the generalization ability to harder tasks compared to older versions, and the LLM backbone contributing more to gains than the vision encoder.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Frontier VLMs demonstrate limited robust performance in multi-step visual interactions, often struggling beyond 3-5 steps and exhibiting common failures like action looping, state mismanagement, and poor visual/spatial grounding.
> - Current models heavily rely on explicit textual feedback for action validity and consequences; performance significantly improves when visual observations are replaced with ASCII text, indicating weak visual perception.
> - Long-term context can be detrimental, and effective supervised fine-tuning benefits from "information-revealing" demonstrations that clarify hidden states or dynamics, rather than just longer or more numerous standard trajectories.
