# VisGym: Diverse, Customizable, Scalable Environments for Multimodal Agents

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from UC Berkeley introduced VisGym, a diverse and customizable benchmark suite, to evaluate and train Vision-Language Models in multi-step visual interaction tasks. Their extensive evaluations reveal that frontier VLMs achieve only 46.61% success on easy tasks, with performance significantly impacted by context length, visual grounding, and a strong reliance on explicit textual feedback.

## Problem

- Existing Vision-Language Models (VLMs) struggle with multi-step visual interactions, where perception, memory, and action must be integrated over long horizons.
- Current VLM benchmarks for interactive decision-making are fragmented, domain-specific, lack fine-grained control for diagnostic analysis, and often do not provide scalable environments for training.
- There is a critical gap in systematically characterizing and diagnosing why VLMs fail in dynamic, visually rich environments, moving beyond mere observational studies.

## Method

- Introduces ==VisGym==, a suite of ==17 diverse, visually interactive environments== built on the Gymnasium framework, spanning symbolic puzzles, real-image understanding, navigation, and manipulation.
- Designed with fine-grained control over task difficulty, input representation, planning horizon, and environmental feedback for diagnostic experiments, using a function-conditioned action space with instructions and feedback to aid VLM interaction and self-correction.
- Includes multi-step oracle solvers for each environment capable of generating diverse demonstration trajectories, facilitating ==supervised fine-tuning (SFT)== of VLMs.

## Results

- The top-performing frontier VLM (Gemini-3-Pro) achieved a success rate of **46.61%** on easy tasks and **26.00%** on hard tasks across VisGym's diverse environments.
- Converting visual observations to ASCII text improved performance up to **3-4x** for some symbolic tasks, and removing text-based feedback consistently degraded performance across all evaluated models.
- Supervised fine-tuning (SFT) using solver-generated demonstrations significantly boosted VLM performance, with newer base models like Qwen3-VL showing nearly **double** the generalization ability to harder tasks compared to older versions, and the LLM backbone contributing more to gains than the vision encoder.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Frontier VLMs demonstrate limited robust performance in multi-step visual interactions, often struggling beyond 3-5 steps and exhibiting common failures like action looping, state mismanagement, and poor visual/spatial grounding.
> - Current models heavily rely on explicit textual feedback for action validity and consequences; performance significantly improves when visual observations are replaced with ASCII text, indicating weak visual perception.
> - Long-term context can be detrimental, and effective supervised fine-tuning benefits from "information-revealing" demonstrations that clarify hidden states or dynamics, rather than just longer or more numerous standard trajectories.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) and Vision-Language-Action (VLAs) models represents a frontier in artificial intelligence, aiming to enable agents to perceive, reason, and act in complex, visually rich environments. While foundation VLMs have demonstrated remarkable progress on static vision-language benchmarks (e.g., Visual Question Answering) and text-based multi-step tasks (e.g., web browsing, coding), their capabilities in *multi-step visual interactions*—where perception, memory, and action must be tightly integrated over long horizons—remain poorly characterized.

The existing landscape of benchmarks for visually interactive decision-making is rich but fragmented. Early reinforcement learning platforms like Atari, OpenAI Gym, and DeepMind Lab laid foundational groundwork for vision-based control but often focused on low-level motor control in fully observable environments. Subsequent efforts extended these to robotic manipulation and navigation (e.g., Meta-World, ALFRED, Habitat), introducing partial observability, multi-task settings, and longer horizons, primarily for imitation learning and meta-learning policies. Concurrently, VLM benchmarks evolved from visual question answering to tasks involving text-based actions and more complex reasoning (e.g., VLM-Gym, VisualAgentBench, WebArena).

However, as highlighted in Table 1 of the paper, existing frameworks largely suffer from several limitations that VisGym aims to overcome:
- **Domain Specificity:** Many benchmarks are tied to specific domains (e.g., robotics, GUI, games), making it difficult to draw general conclusions about VLM capabilities across diverse tasks.
- **Observational Nature:** Evaluations often provide insights into model behavior but lack the systematic, controlled mechanisms to diagnose *why* models fail across different scenarios and factors.
- **Limited Customization:** Fine-grained control over factors like context length, input representation, feedback modality, or goal visibility is often absent, hindering systematic diagnostic analysis.
- **Scalability for Training:** While some offer evaluation, fewer provide scalable environments with oracle solvers capable of generating diverse demonstrations for supervised fine-tuning (SFT) or online reinforcement learning (RL).

VisGym positions itself as a **unified, diverse, customizable, and scalable gymnasium** that specifically addresses these gaps. By offering 17 environments spanning symbolic puzzles, real-image understanding, navigation, and manipulation, it provides a broad testbed. Crucially, its design allows for the systematic investigation of *domain-agnostic factors* (e.g., context length, feedback type) that affect VLM performance, moving beyond mere observational studies to controlled experimentation. This makes VisGym a significant contribution, serving as a much-needed diagnostic and training platform for the next generation of multimodal agents capable of truly interactive visual intelligence.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind VisGym stems from the observation that while modern Vision-Language Models (VLMs) excel at static image understanding and text-based reasoning, their performance in dynamic, multi-step visual interaction remains largely underexplored and poorly understood. Specifically, the authors identify a critical gap in characterizing how VLMs integrate perception, memory, and action over long interaction horizons in visually rich environments. Existing benchmarks, while valuable, are often domain-specific or lack the fine-grained control necessary for systematic diagnosis of the underlying causes of VLM failures.

In response to this motivation, the key objectives of the VisGym project are:

1. **Develop a Diverse and Comprehensive Environment Suite:** To create a "gymnasium" of at least 17 distinct, visually interactive, long-horizon environments that span a wide range of tasks, including symbolic puzzles, real-image understanding, navigation, and manipulation. This diversity aims to test VLMs across various levels of state observability, dynamics, and visual complexity.
2. **Enable Fine-Grained Control and Customization:** To design each environment with flexible controls over critical experimental factors such as task difficulty, input representation (visual vs. textual), planning horizon (history length), and the type of environmental feedback provided. This objective is crucial for conducting controlled, domain-agnostic diagnostic experiments.
3. **Facilitate Training through Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT):** To provide oracle multi-step solvers for each environment capable of generating structured, diverse demonstration trajectories. These demonstrations are intended to enable researchers to effectively supervise fine-tune VLMs, thereby bridging the gap between evaluation and training in interactive settings.
4. **Systematically Evaluate Frontier VLMs:** To conduct extensive evaluations of state-of-the-art proprietary and open-weight VLMs across the newly developed environments. This objective aims to quantify current model capabilities, identify concrete failure modes, and uncover the specific limitations that hinder multi-step visual decision-making.
5. **Diagnose Model Behavior under Controlled Conditions:** To perform targeted ablation studies by manipulating the customizable factors within VisGym (e.g., conversation history length, observation modality, feedback presence, goal visibility) to understand how these elements influence model performance and reveal the sensitivities and dependencies of current VLMs.
6. **Provide Pathways for Improvement:** Through both diagnostic evaluations and training experiments, to identify effective strategies and data curation techniques that can lead to improved VLM performance in interactive tasks, such as the importance of information-revealing demonstrations.
7. **Establish an Extensible Framework:** To build VisGym on top of the standard Gymnasium framework, allowing for easy addition of new tasks and integration with existing reinforcement learning pipelines, thereby fostering future research in multimodal agent development.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology of VisGym is multifaceted, encompassing the design and implementation of a novel benchmark suite, a rigorous evaluation protocol for frontier models, comprehensive diagnostic experiments, and a robust framework for supervised fine-tuning.

#### 3.1. VisGym Design and Implementation

VisGym is a gymnasium of **17 visually interactive environments**, built on the widely adopted Gymnasium framework. Each environment is characterized by its domain (Real or Synthetic images), observability (Full or Partial), dynamics (Known or Unknown), and a set of tunable parameters for difficulty (Table 2). The environments include diverse tasks such as `Colorization`, `Counting`, `Jigsaw`, `Matchstick Equation`, `Maze 2D/3D`, `Mental Rotation 2D/3D`, `MuJoCo Fetch (Pick-and-Place/Reach)`, `Patch Reassembly`, `Referring Dot-Pointing`, `Sliding Block`, `Video Unshuffle`, and `Zoom-In Puzzle`.

Key enhancements beyond standard Gymnasium include:
- **Function-Conditioned Action Space:** Actions are represented as function calls with parameters (e.g., `('swap', (row1, col1), (row2, col2))`), allowing VLMs to leverage their function-calling capabilities.
- **Function Instructions:** Natural-language descriptions of available functions and their argument constraints are provided in the initial prompt for zero-shot rollouts.
- **Environment Feedback:** Textual feedback (e.g., "invalid format", "executed") is provided after each action, aiding models in understanding action consequences, especially for weaker visual perception.
- **Multi-step Solvers:** Heuristic solvers are implemented for each task, supporting multiple strategies and optional stochasticity to generate diverse demonstration trajectories for supervised fine-tuning. These solvers ensure task solvability and provide a source of high-quality training data.

The modular structure, outlined in Algorithm 1 (Sec. D), allows for easy expansion with new tasks, action spaces, and generation of both visual and textual supervision.

#### 3.2. Evaluation Setup for Frontier Models

The paper evaluates **12 state-of-the-art Vision-Language Models**, including proprietary models (Gemini 3 Pro, Gemini 2.5 Pro, GPT-5, Claude Sonnet 4, Grok 4 Fast, Qwen-VL-Max) and open-weight models (Qwen3-VL-235B-Instruct, GLM-4.5V, Llama-4-Maverick, Qwen-2.5-VL-72B-Instruct, Gemma 3-27B-Instruct, UI-Tars-1.5-7B).
- Models interact in a **multi-turn manner**, receiving the full history (task instruction, past observations, actions, and feedback) at each step.
- Interaction steps are limited (20 for easy, 30 for hard tasks) to ensure context window compatibility.
- Each model is evaluated on **70 episodes per task and difficulty setting** (easy, hard).

#### 3.3. Diagnostic Experiments (Ablation Studies)

To systematically diagnose VLM limitations, controlled experiments are performed by modifying specific factors:
- **Turns to Keep in Conversation History:** Evaluates performance with varying history lengths (1, 2, 4 turns, and unbounded full history) to understand context utilization.
- **Representing Observation in Text:** Compares performance when observations are rendered visually (images) versus entirely in ASCII text for symbolic tasks, isolating visual grounding challenges.
- **Removal of Text-based Feedback:** Assesses reliance on textual feedback by removing descriptive messages about action validity and outcomes, forcing models to infer from visual transitions alone.
- **Providing Final Goal at Beginning:** Investigates the impact of providing the ground-truth final observation image at the start of an episode, shifting the task from reasoning about goals to visual alignment.

#### 3.4. Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) Experiments

To demonstrate VisGym's utility for training, SFT experiments are conducted:
- **Demonstration Generation:** Solver-generated trajectories are used for SFT, with filtering to ensure successful completions and prevent data leakage.
- **Fine-tuning Configurations:**
 - **Single-task:** Separate models fine-tuned per task.
 - **Mixed-task:** A single model trained jointly on all tasks.
- **Base Model:** Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct (and Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct for generalization study).
- **Generalization to Difficulty:** Models are trained on easy settings and evaluated on hard settings to assess out-of-distribution generalization.
- **Module Specificity:** Experiments analyze the contributions of fine-tuning the vision encoder versus the LLM backbone to identify primary bottlenecks.
- **Data Curation Strategies:** Investigates the impact of "information-revealing" demonstrations (e.g., structured exploration to clarify unknown dynamics or hidden states) compared to standard demonstrations.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

VisGym's extensive evaluation and training experiments yielded several crucial insights into the capabilities and limitations of frontier Vision-Language Models in multi-step visual decision-making.

#### 4.1. Frontier VLMs Struggle in Interactive Settings

- Even the best-performing frontier model (Gemini-3-Pro) achieved only **46.61% success in easy configurations and 26.00% in hard configurations** across VisGym's diverse tasks (Figure 2). This highlights that current state-of-the-art VLMs are far from robust in interactive visual environments.
- Analysis of successful trajectories (Figure 3) showed that most models peaked at 3-5 steps, with a sharp drop thereafter, indicating limited capability in effectively handling long-context multi-step visual interactions.

#### 4.2. Common Failure Patterns

Automated failure discovery methods (StringSight) identified four consistent failure modes across models and tasks:
1. **Restricted action space and action looping:** Models often repeat the same action or rely on fixed-magnitude operations without adapting to progress (e.g., repeatedly moving in one direction, using "swap" instead of "reorder") (Figure 14).
2. **State mismanagement:** Models fail to maintain internal state, ignoring textual/environmental feedback, revisiting explored areas, or repeating illegal actions despite prior errors.
3. **Early termination:** Models prematurely stop episodes even when the goal is not reached and steps remain.
4. **Failure to use visual or spatial information:** Models frequently ignore visual cues crucial for task progress or grounding actions (e.g., target leaving the frame, object alignment).

#### 4.3. Diagnostic Insights from Controlled Experiments

- **Long-Term Context is a Double-Edged Sword:** Models benefited from a limited number of previous turns (up to ~4), but performance degraded with an unbounded full history (Figure 5). This "reversed-U" relationship suggests that irrelevant or stale observations in long contexts become detrimental.
- **Visual Grounding is a Bottleneck:** For most symbolic tasks, rendering observations as **ASCII text substantially improved performance (up to 3-4x higher success for GPT-5)** compared to visual images (Figure 6). This reveals that a significant limitation lies in models' low-level perceptual grounding, not just high-level reasoning. An exception was Matchstick Equation, where distorted ASCII negatively impacted performance.
- **Heavy Reliance on Text-based Feedback:** All models showed **consistent drops in average performance when textual feedback was removed** (Figure 7). This indicates that current VLMs struggle to infer action validity and consequences purely from visual state transitions, relying heavily on explicit text for grounding and self-correction.
- **Explicit Goals Can Backfire:** Providing the final goal observation upfront generally improved performance, as it simplified the task to visual alignment. However, for tasks like Zoom-In Puzzle and Matchstick Equation, **some models paradoxically performed worse with an explicit goal** (Figure 8). This was attributed to poor visual perception, causing models to misjudge the alignment and terminate prematurely despite visible discrepancies, highlighting brittle visual perception.

#### 4.4. Training with VisGym

- **Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) is Effective:** Models fine-tuned on solver-generated demonstrations achieved state-of-the-art performance on most tasks, validating the learnability of VisGym environments and the utility of structured demonstrations (Figures 2 and 4).
- **Stronger Base Models Generalize Better:** When trained on easy task variants, the newer Qwen3-VL base model generalized substantially better to harder, unseen variants than Qwen2.5-VL, nearly doubling the success rate (Figure 9). This indicates that improved base VLM capabilities enhance out-of-distribution generalization even with identical SFT setups.
- **Vision and LLM Both Matter, but LLM More for Temporal Reasoning:** Most tasks benefited from fine-tuning both the vision encoder and the LLM backbone. However, the LLM consistently contributed a larger performance gain, especially for tasks with partial observability or unknown dynamics (Figure 10). This emphasizes that temporal reasoning and history integration remain primary bottlenecks for VLMs, while strong visual encoding, though necessary, is often not sufficient for multi-step decision-making.
- **Information-Revealing Demonstrations are Crucial for SFT:** For tasks with unknown dynamics or partial observability (e.g., Matchstick Rotation, Mental Rotation 3D Objaverse), **demonstrations explicitly designed to reveal hidden states or clarify dynamics significantly improved SFT outcomes** (Figures 11 and 12). Simply increasing demonstration length or quantity without informative structure was less effective, highlighting the importance of intelligent data curation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

VisGym represents a significant advancement in the benchmarking and development of multimodal AI agents, offering several profound implications for future research and practical applications:

1. **Systematic Diagnosis of VLM Capabilities:** VisGym provides an unprecedented framework for systematically diagnosing the strengths and, more importantly, the weaknesses of Vision-Language Models in interactive settings. By enabling controlled experiments on domain-agnostic factors (like context length, representation, and feedback), it moves beyond observational studies to offer concrete, actionable insights into *why* models fail. This diagnostic capability is crucial for targeted research and development efforts.
2. **Bridging Perception, Memory, and Action:** The suite's focus on multi-step visual interactions explicitly addresses the challenge of integrating perception, memory, and action over long horizons. This is a fundamental requirement for truly intelligent agents operating in dynamic real-world environments, and VisGym provides a testbed to foster progress in this area.
3. **Uncovering and Quantifying Failure Modes:** The detailed analysis of common failure patterns (action looping, state mismanagement, early termination, visual perception failure) provides a clear roadmap for model improvement. Researchers now have quantified insights into where frontier models struggle, allowing them to design more robust architectures and training methodologies.
4. **Guiding Model Development and Training Strategies:** The findings from the diagnostic and training experiments offer direct guidance for improving VLMs. The insights into context management, the necessity of textual feedback, the double-edged nature of explicit goal observations, and the power of information-revealing demonstrations are invaluable. These findings can inform architectural choices, prompt engineering strategies, and data curation pipelines for supervised fine-tuning and reinforcement learning.
5. **Scalability and Customization for Future Research:** Built on the Gymnasium framework, VisGym is inherently extensible, allowing researchers to easily add new tasks or modify existing ones. Its customizable difficulty parameters ensure that the benchmark can evolve with VLM capabilities, continuing to challenge models as they improve. The availability of multi-step solvers facilitates the generation of large-scale, high-quality demonstration data, supporting ongoing research into efficient training paradigms.
6. **Towards More Capable and Adaptive Visual Intelligence:** By systematically identifying limitations and providing pathways for improvement, VisGym accelerates the development of more capable and adaptive multimodal agents. Such agents are essential for applications ranging from advanced robotics and autonomous navigation to intelligent assistants and human-computer interaction, where understanding and acting in visually dynamic environments are paramount.

In conclusion, VisGym is not just another benchmark; it is a meticulously designed scientific instrument that allows for unprecedented scrutiny of Vision-Language Models in interactive contexts. Its contributions lay foundational groundwork for understanding, diagnosing, and ultimately overcoming the current limitations of multimodal AI, paving the way for truly intelligent agents.
