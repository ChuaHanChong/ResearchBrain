# Spatial Understanding from Videos: Structured Prompts Meet Simulation Data

> [!summary] Summary
> A framework from Harbin Institute of Technology and Pengcheng Laboratory enhances 3D spatial understanding in pre-trained Vision-Language Models by integrating a structured Chain-of-Thought prompting strategy and a large-scale synthetic dataset, yielding consistent performance improvements on spatial reasoning tasks without architectural modifications.

## Problem

- Vision-Language Models (VLMs) struggle with spatial uncertainty, inferring coherent 3D scene structure from limited 2D observations (scanning videos) due to occlusions and perspective distortions.
- A scarcity of large-scale, diverse, and scalable datasets for 3D spatial reasoning from scanning videos limits VLMs from acquiring robust spatial knowledge.
- Existing solutions often involve specialized 3D sensors or complex architectural modifications to VLMs, limiting broad applicability.

## Method

- **SpatialMind:** A novel structured ==Chain-of-Thought (CoT)== prompting strategy that guides VLMs through scene decomposition (generating textual descriptions of object relations) and question decomposition.
- **ScanForgeQA:** A large-scale, scalable synthetic question-answering dataset created from diverse 3D simulation scenes using an automated pipeline for fine-tuning VLMs.
- The framework non-invasively enhances pre-trained VLMs, leveraging both explicit reasoning guidance and comprehensive data-driven knowledge acquisition without modifying core VLM architectures.

## Results

- Textual descriptions as scene representations consistently outperformed 3D map and 2D grid formats, becoming the default for the SpatialMind strategy.
- Both SpatialMind prompting and ScanForgeQA fine-tuning individually improved visual-spatial understanding across diverse VLMs, with combined strategies yielding further improvements (e.g., Qwen2.5-VL-72B improved by **7.9%** when both strategies were combined).
- The framework demonstrated strong generalizability, leading to performance gains on external spatial benchmarks (OpenEQA, ScanQA, SQA3D) and showing robustness to varying input frames and resolutions.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Current VLMs are more interpretable with textual descriptions of 3D scenes than with raw 3D maps or 2D grids for spatial reasoning tasks.
> - The structured prompting (SpatialMind) and synthetic data fine-tuning (ScanForgeQA) are complementary, with their combined application yielding superior performance in visual-spatial understanding.
> - Larger VLMs benefit more from structured prompting, while smaller VLMs show greater improvements from fine-tuning on specialized synthetic data.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Visual-spatial understanding is a critical area of research at the intersection of computer vision, natural language processing, and artificial intelligence, fundamental for applications like autonomous driving, robotic navigation, and augmented reality. Historically, human perception has served as a benchmark, with individuals intuitively inferring 3D layouts from 2D visual input.

The broader research landscape for spatial understanding has seen several approaches:
- **2D Image-based Methods:** Early work focused on inferring spatial relationships from single 2D images. While foundational (e.g.,), these methods inherently struggle with the complexities and ambiguities of real-world 3D scenes due to limited depth information, occlusions, and perspective distortions.
- **3D Point Cloud-based Methods:** The advent of depth sensors led to widespread use of point clouds for 3D scene understanding (e.g.,). Point clouds provide rich geometric data, enabling detailed object detection, instance segmentation, and scene graph construction. However, these approaches are often limited by the high cost of specialized sensors, significant computational overhead for processing dense point clouds, and challenges in scalability and accessibility. Many cross-modal strategies have also combined point clouds with multi-view 2D images to address these issues.
- **Vision-only Solutions for 3D Understanding:** Recognizing the limitations of both pure 2D and point cloud-dependent methods, there's a growing interest in purely vision-based approaches that operate on scanning videos or multi-view images (e.g.,). These methods aim to mimic human perception, inferring 3D structure from dynamic 2D visual streams without explicit 3D priors. This sub-field faces two significant hurdles: **spatial uncertainty** (inferring 3D from limited 2D views) and **data scarcity** (lack of large-scale, diverse datasets for this specific task).

This paper directly positions itself within this emerging "vision-only" paradigm for 3D spatial understanding from scanning videos. It aims to bridge the gap between powerful pre-trained VLMs and robust 3D spatial reasoning by tackling the aforementioned challenges of spatial uncertainty and data scarcity. Crucially, it seeks to achieve this *without modifying the underlying VLM architecture*, making its approach highly adaptable and broadly applicable across existing models. By focusing on structured prompting and synthetic data generation, it offers a scalable and practical pathway to enhance the spatial intelligence of VLMs, pushing the boundaries of what these models can infer from visual inputs alone in complex 3D environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core motivation behind this research stems from the critical need to imbue intelligent systems with robust visual-spatial understanding capabilities. While modern Vision-Language Models (VLMs) have shown remarkable progress in general visual understanding, their capacity for accurate 3D spatial reasoning, especially from dynamic visual inputs like scanning videos, remains significantly limited. The authors identify two primary challenges hindering this progress:

1. **Spatial Uncertainty:** Inferring a coherent 3D scene structure from inherently limited 2D observations (scanning videos) is challenging. Factors such as occlusions, perspective distortions, and texture ambiguities introduce significant spatial uncertainty, demanding sophisticated multi-step logical reasoning to reconstruct accurate spatial layouts. Existing VLMs often struggle with this inference, as they are primarily trained on static image-text pairs or lack explicit mechanisms for robust 3D spatial inference from dynamic sequences.

2. **Data Scarcity:** Current datasets designed for 3D spatial reasoning, particularly those involving scanning videos, are limited in both scale and diversity. Furthermore, many existing datasets are built from real-world scans (e.g., ScanNet), which are expensive and time-consuming to annotate, leading to poor scalability. This scarcity restricts VLMs from acquiring the robust spatial knowledge and perceptual capabilities necessary for real-world deployment.

To address these challenges, the **key objective** of this work is to present a unified framework that enhances 3D spatial reasoning in pre-trained VLMs *without requiring modifications to their underlying architecture*. This non-invasive approach is crucial for ensuring broad applicability and ease of integration with diverse VLM backbones.

The authors propose a dual-pronged strategy to achieve this objective, which also forms their main contributions:
- **SpatialMind:** A novel, structured Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting strategy that guides VLMs through a step-by-step reasoning process over spatial relationships. This aims to explicitly decompose complex spatial queries and scene interpretations into manageable, interpretable steps, effectively mitigating spatial uncertainty.
- **ScanForgeQA:** A large-scale, scalable synthetic question-answering dataset constructed from diverse 3D simulation scenes using an automated generation pipeline. This dataset is designed for fine-tuning VLMs, enabling them to acquire robust spatial commonsense knowledge and perceptual abilities, thereby overcoming the limitations of data scarcity and improving generalization.

By combining these two components, the research aims to significantly improve the ability of VLMs to interpret object relationships and layouts from video inputs, paving the way for more capable and robust intelligent systems in real-world applications.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework integrates two main components: **SpatialMind**, a structured prompting strategy, and **ScanForgeQA**, a scalable synthetic dataset. Both aim to enhance 3D spatial reasoning in VLMs without architectural modifications.

#### 3.1. SpatialMind Prompting Strategy

SpatialMind is a Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting strategy designed to guide VLMs through step-by-step spatial reasoning. It comprises two core processes: Scene Decomposition and Question Decomposition.

#### 3.1.1. Scene Decomposition

This process transforms the 3D scene from the input scanning video into multiple interpretable representations. It involves three sequential steps:
- **Local Modeling:** For each video frame, VLMs (leveraging powerful models like GPT-4o for candidate object identification) detect objects and estimate their 3D positions relative to a randomly chosen reference object within that frame. This generates a local 3D map for each frame, focusing on accurate per-frame object detection and localization.
- **Coordinate Mapping:** To unify spatial information across frames, all local object positions are transformed into a single global coordinate system. The reference object from the first frame defines the global origin. The VLM infers relative rotations and translations between adjacent frames, which are then accumulated to compute each frame's transformation to the global system. This allows converting local coordinates to global ones. Duplicate object detections across frames are merged based on spatial proximity and semantic consistency, resulting in a unified global 3D map of the entire scene.
- **Cognition Generation:** Beyond a 3D map, the framework explores two alternative scene representations:
 - **2D Spatial Grid:** A regular 2D grid (e.g., XY-plane) is overlaid on the global scene, mapping objects to discrete grid cells.
 - **Natural Language Descriptions:** VLMs generate textual descriptions of object locations relative to a designated reference point (e.g., "Monitor: locate one meter to the left of the reference"). Empirical results indicated that these natural language descriptions were most interpretable by VLMs.

#### 3.1.2. Question Decomposition

This step involves breaking down complex spatial questions into a sequence of fine-grained reasoning steps tailored to different question types (e.g., object size, relative distance, relative direction). GPT-4o is used to design these reasoning procedures, followed by human verification. For example, a "relative distance" question involves identifying objects, estimating coordinates, computing pairwise distances, and selecting the closest object. During inference, the system selects the appropriate reasoning plan based on the question type and feeds it, along with the video input and scene representation (defaulting to textual descriptions), into the VLM.

#### 3.2. ScanForgeQA Dataset Construction

ScanForgeQA is a large-scale, synthetic question-answering dataset built through an automated three-stage pipeline to overcome data scarcity.

#### 3.2.1. Scene Construction

To ensure diverse single-room 3D environments:
- **Separation:** Existing multi-room scenes from the 3D-FRONT dataset are disassembled into individual rooms, filtered for common types and sufficient content, yielding 34,116 single-room scenes across six categories (bedroom, kitchen, bathroom, living room, dining room, storage room).
- **Synthesis:** Novel room layouts are generated using an LLM-guided framework (HoloDeck). GPT-4o creates diverse textual descriptions for eight room categories (including office, store), which HoloDeck uses to retrieve 3D assets from Objaverse and arrange them, producing 160 additional scenes with human verification for realism.

#### 3.2.2. Scan Creation

Egocentric scanning videos are simulated within these constructed scenes using the Unity engine, employing two strategies:
- **Orbit Scan:** A camera follows a circular path around the room center at eye level, capturing a 360-degree panoramic view (72 frames per scan).
- **Navigation Scan:** Navigable ground regions are identified, and shortest paths between random start/end objects are computed. The camera performs 360-degree rotations at start/end points and samples frames along the path, simulating movement (72 frames per path).

#### 3.2.3. QA Generation

Textual question-answering pairs are automatically generated for each scene, categorized into three types:
- **Attribute Estimation:** Questions about static properties like object count, object size, room size, and room type. Ground-truth answers are directly derived from 3D scene annotations and object metadata.
- **Spatial Reasoning:** Questions about inter-object relationships, including relative distance (Euclidean distances between centroids), absolute distance, relative direction (angular sectors relative to a reference, e.g., "right," "back"), and contact relationships (comparing object dimensions and distances).
- **Hypothesis Analysis:** Questions requiring conditional reasoning under hypothetical scenarios, such as operation feasibility (e.g., "Is it feasible to place the television on the table?"). Feasibility is determined by comparing object dimensions for stacking or embedding.

The full ScanForgeQA dataset comprises 34,276 single-room scenes, 103K simulated video scans, and 925K question-answering pairs, allowing scalable and controlled data generation for fine-tuning.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments thoroughly evaluated the effectiveness of SpatialMind and ScanForgeQA across multiple benchmarks and VLM architectures.

#### 4.1. Scene Representation Format Interpretability

An initial experiment assessed which scene representation format (3D map, 2D grid, or textual descriptions) was most interpretable by VLMs. Results showed that **textual descriptions (+Des) consistently outperformed other formats**, followed by the 2D grid. The 3D map yielded the least improvement. This finding suggests that current VLMs are better at processing one-dimensional textual descriptions than more structured, high-dimensional spatial formats, leading to the adoption of textual descriptions as the default in subsequent experiments.

#### 4.2. Impact of SpatialMind and ScanForgeQA on VLM Performance

Evaluations on VSI-Bench (Table 1) across various open-source (InternVL2, LLaVA-NeXT-Video, VideoLLaMA3, Qwen2/2.5-VL) and closed-source (Gemini-1.5 Pro, GPT-4o) VLMs revealed several key insights:
- **Individual Effectiveness:** Both SpatialMind prompting and ScanForgeQA fine-tuning consistently improved visual-spatial understanding across all tested models, demonstrating their effectiveness and generalizability.
- **Model Size Influence:** Larger models (e.g., 72B parameter counts) tended to benefit more from SpatialMind's prompting, which enhances their inherent reasoning capabilities. Conversely, smaller models (e.g., 7B parameter counts) showed greater improvements from ScanForgeQA's fine-tuning, acquiring spatial knowledge through data. For instance, Qwen2.5-VL-7B gained 6.1% from fine-tuning compared to 2.0% from prompting.
- **Human vs. VLM Strengths:** Human participants excelled in qualitative tasks (e.g., 100% on "Appearance Order") but struggled with precise quantitative estimations (e.g., "Object Size"). VLMs, in contrast, demonstrated strong quantitative reasoning, sometimes surpassing human performance, highlighting their complementary role.

#### 4.3. Complementary Benefits of Combining Strategies

When SpatialMind prompting was applied to models already fine-tuned on ScanForgeQA ("+Both" rows in Table 1), consistent and further performance improvements were observed across all evaluated models. This confirms that **SpatialMind and ScanForgeQA are complementary approaches**, with their combined application yielding superior results. For example, Qwen2.5-VL-72B saw an 7.9% improvement when both strategies were combined, compared to 4.8% for SpatialMind alone and 5.6% for ScanForgeQA alone.

#### 4.4. Generalizability to Other Spatial Benchmarks

The framework's generalizability was validated on OpenEQA, ScanQA, and SQA3D benchmarks (Table 2). Both SpatialMind and ScanForgeQA consistently led to performance gains across these diverse spatial tasks and datasets, demonstrating the robustness and broad applicability of the proposed approach.

#### 4.5. Impact on Other Tasks

An evaluation on MVBench and Video-MME (broad multi-task video benchmarks) showed that fine-tuning with ScanForgeQA slightly improved performance on MVBench (which includes spatial reasoning tasks) but led to a marginal drop on Video-MME (focused on event comprehension). To mitigate this trade-off, mixed fine-tuning with a small proportion of traditional data (5% or 10% from ShareGPT4Video) successfully balanced spatial enhancement with broader capabilities, even surpassing the original baseline.

#### 4.6. Ablation Studies

- **Prompting Strategy:** Breaking down SpatialMind, it was found that both the question component (CoT-Question) and scene description component (CoT-Scene) independently improved performance. However, the **full combined prompt was most effective**, with the scene description contributing more significantly than the reasoning steps alone (Table 3).
- **Fine-tuning Data:** Comparing ScanForgeQA against existing spatial datasets like SQA3D and ScanQA (Table 3), fine-tuning on these existing datasets resulted in lower performance than ScanForgeQA, and even reduced accuracy on precise spatial estimation tasks. This underscores the **superiority of the synthetic ScanForgeQA dataset**, particularly for fine-grained spatial estimation, despite some existing benchmarks originating from the same source (ScanNet), highlighting the advantage of the automated, scalable generation pipeline.
- **Frames and Resolution:** The approach demonstrated robustness to varying numbers of input frames and image resolutions. Performance consistently improved as the number of frames and resolution increased, indicating stability and effectiveness under different visual input conditions (Figure 5).

#### 4.7. Qualitative Analysis

Qualitative examples from VSI-Bench (Figure 6) showcased that the enhanced variant (+Both) successfully corrected reasoning and localization failures of the baseline Qwen2.5-VL-7B. For instance, in a "Route Plan" task, the baseline failed directional prediction, while "Ours" provided the correct sequence. In an "Appearance Order" task, the baseline struggled with object localization, while "Ours" accurately determined the appearance order.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a highly significant advancement in the field of visual-spatial understanding for Vision-Language Models (VLMs), particularly due to its dual approach that tackles fundamental limitations without intrusive architectural changes.

**Significance of the Contributions:**

1. **Non-Intrusive Enhancement:** The most notable significance lies in the framework's ability to enhance 3D spatial reasoning in pre-trained VLMs *without modifying their core architecture*. This makes the approach universally applicable, allowing any VLM (from open-source to proprietary, small to large) to benefit, significantly lowering the barrier to entry for improving spatial intelligence.
2. **Addressing Spatial Uncertainty with Structured Prompting:** SpatialMind provides a novel and effective mechanism to guide VLMs through complex multi-step spatial reasoning. By explicitly decomposing scenes into interpretable representations (especially textual descriptions) and questions into structured reasoning steps, it offers a transparent and controllable way to perform inference, mitigating the inherent ambiguities of 3D reconstruction from 2D videos. The finding that textual scene descriptions are most interpretable by current VLMs is a crucial insight for future VLM design, suggesting a preference for symbolic, human-like spatial cognition over raw geometric data.
3. **Overcoming Data Scarcity with Scalable Synthetic Data:** ScanForgeQA is a major contribution to solving the long-standing problem of data scarcity in 3D spatial reasoning. Its automated, scalable generation pipeline from diverse 3D simulation scenes enables the creation of vast, varied datasets rich in fine-grained spatial estimation samples—a crucial aspect where existing datasets fall short. The comparative experiments clearly demonstrate the superiority of ScanForgeQA over other benchmarks for fine-tuning, underscoring the power of high-quality, targeted synthetic data.
4. **Complementary Strategies for Robustness:** The demonstrated complementary nature of SpatialMind and ScanForgeQA is highly significant. This suggests a synergistic relationship where structured reasoning (prompting) and acquired knowledge (fine-tuning) together yield superior and more robust spatial understanding than either approach alone.

**Potential Impact:**

1. **Advancement in Embodied AI and Robotics:** Improved visual-spatial understanding is paramount for intelligent agents operating in physical environments. Robots navigating dynamic spaces, manipulating objects, or performing complex tasks will greatly benefit from VLMs that can accurately infer object layouts, distances, and directions from their visual inputs, leading to more robust and autonomous systems.
2. **Enhanced Augmented and Virtual Reality (AR/VR):** For AR/VR applications, accurate spatial cognition is essential for realistic object placement, scene understanding, and user interaction. This framework could enable more seamless and context-aware AR experiences by allowing systems to better understand the real-world spatial environment.
3. **New Directions in VLM Research:** The findings regarding VLM interpretability of different spatial representations (textual vs. structured geometric data) could inspire future research into how VLMs are designed and trained. It might encourage architectures that are more natively capable of processing structured 3D information or hybrid approaches that combine symbolic reasoning with perception. The methodology for generating scalable synthetic datasets also provides a blueprint for creating specialized training data for other complex VLM tasks.
4. **Broad Applicability Across Models:** The non-invasive nature of the framework means it can immediately benefit a wide array of existing and future VLMs, accelerating their deployment in real-world applications requiring sophisticated spatial intelligence.
5. **Benchmarking and Evaluation:** The development of the ScanForgeQA dataset and the rigorous evaluation across multiple benchmarks contribute to a better understanding of current VLM limitations and capabilities in spatial reasoning, providing valuable tools for the research community.

In conclusion, this paper provides a practical, effective, and broadly applicable framework that significantly advances the visual-spatial reasoning capabilities of pre-trained VLMs. By addressing critical challenges through innovative prompting and data generation strategies, it lays crucial groundwork for the development of more intelligent and spatially aware AI systems.
