# Spatial Understanding from Videos: Structured Prompts Meet Simulation Data

> [!summary] Summary
> A framework from Harbin Institute of Technology and Pengcheng Laboratory enhances 3D spatial understanding in pre-trained Vision-Language Models by integrating a structured Chain-of-Thought prompting strategy and a large-scale synthetic dataset, yielding consistent performance improvements on spatial reasoning tasks without architectural modifications.

## Problem

- Vision-Language Models (VLMs) struggle with spatial uncertainty, inferring coherent 3D scene structure from limited 2D observations (scanning videos) due to occlusions and perspective distortions.
- A scarcity of large-scale, diverse, and scalable datasets for 3D spatial reasoning from scanning videos limits VLMs from acquiring robust spatial knowledge.
- Existing solutions often involve specialized 3D sensors or complex architectural modifications to VLMs, limiting broad applicability.

## Method

- **SpatialMind:** A novel ==structured Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting strategy== that guides VLMs through scene decomposition (generating textual descriptions of object relations) and question decomposition.
- **ScanForgeQA:** A large-scale, scalable ==synthetic question-answering dataset== created from diverse 3D simulation scenes using an automated pipeline for fine-tuning VLMs.
- The framework non-invasively enhances pre-trained VLMs, leveraging both explicit reasoning guidance and comprehensive data-driven knowledge acquisition without modifying core VLM architectures.

## Results

- Textual descriptions as scene representations consistently outperformed 3D map and 2D grid formats, becoming the default for the SpatialMind strategy.
- Both SpatialMind prompting and ScanForgeQA fine-tuning individually improved visual-spatial understanding across diverse VLMs, with combined strategies yielding further improvements (e.g., Qwen2.5-VL-72B improved by **7.9%** when both strategies were combined).
- The framework demonstrated strong generalizability, leading to performance gains on external spatial benchmarks (OpenEQA, ScanQA, SQA3D) and showing robustness to varying input frames and resolutions.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Current VLMs are more interpretable with textual descriptions of 3D scenes than with raw 3D maps or 2D grids for spatial reasoning tasks.
> - The structured prompting (SpatialMind) and synthetic data fine-tuning (ScanForgeQA) are complementary, with their combined application yielding superior performance in visual-spatial understanding.
> - Larger VLMs benefit more from structured prompting, while smaller VLMs show greater improvements from fine-tuning on specialized synthetic data.
