# UniRL: Self-Improving Unified Multimodal Models via Supervised and Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> National University of Singapore and ByteDance researchers develop UniRL, a self-improving post-training framework for unified multimodal models that eliminates the need for external image data by using the model's own generated images as training signals, with Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) achieving 0.77 GenEval scores on Show-o (versus 0.60 baseline) and substantially reducing task imbalance between text-to-image generation and multimodal understanding through a novel bidirectional evaluation metric that measures cross-task consistency, demonstrating that models can effectively learn from their own synthetic outputs via differentiable sampling and reward-based optimization.

## Problem

- Unified multimodal models suffer from imbalance between text-to-image generation and multimodal understanding, often excelling at one while underperforming on the other, with no established metrics to quantify this cross-task consistency
- Post-training methods for multimodal models typically require expensive external image datasets and manual annotation, limiting accessibility and scalability
- Existing optimization strategies like supervised fine-tuning can lead to overfitting in understanding tasks, particularly when training and test distributions differ

## Method

- Develop a ==self-improving framework== that generates synthetic training data via the model's own text-to-image outputs, using ==Straight-Through Gumbel-Softmax estimation== to enable gradient flow through discrete image tokens for end-to-end optimization
- Compare Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) with Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) as optimization strategies, with GRPO using reward signals based on answer accuracy
- Introduce ==bidirectional evaluation metrics== (Accuracy_MMU|T2I and Accuracy_T2I|MMU) to measure cross-task consistency and quantify task imbalance

## Results

- Show-o improvements: GenEval scores increase from **0.60** to **0.77** (SFT) and **0.71** (GRPO), with GRPO achieving better MMU accuracy (**0.79** vs **0.71** baseline) and task balance (Accuracy_MMU|T2I: **0.83→0.93**, Accuracy_T2I|MMU: **0.70→0.81**)
- Janus performance gains: GenEval increases from **0.60** to **0.65** and multimodal understanding from **0.84** to **0.88** with GRPO optimization
- GRPO shows better generalization on out-of-distribution visual inputs compared to SFT, which exhibits overfitting particularly in single object recognition tasks

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - GRPO demonstrates superior robustness and generalization compared to SFT, particularly for models with decoupled architectures like Janus where SFT causes training instability, requiring decoupled GRPO objectives tailored to non-end-to-end (separate representation) training versus end-to-end approaches
> - Self-generated synthetic data can effectively improve model performance without external datasets, with the model's own outputs providing sufficient supervision signals
> - Task imbalance in unified models can be systematically measured and reduced through mutual enhancement between generation and understanding capabilities

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of artificial intelligence has seen rapid advancements in large language models (LLMs) and, more recently, multimodal large language models (MLLMs). These models aim to process and generate information across different modalities, such as text and images. A key trend is the development of **unified multimodal models** that can handle both text-to-image generation (T2I) and multimodal understanding (MMU) tasks within a shared parameter space.

Current state-of-the-art unified models, such as Show-o, Janus, Chameleon, and Emu3, primarily focus on the **pretraining stage**. This stage involves training models on massive, diverse datasets, which typically demands immense computational resources and vast amounts of curated data. While these models have achieved impressive performance, the challenges of high computational cost and data scarcity for continuous improvement remain significant.

Beyond pretraining, **post-training methods** have emerged as a crucial area for refining model performance, adapting to specific tasks, and improving alignment with human preferences. For LLMs, techniques like Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) and Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF), including methods like Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) and Direct Preference Optimization (DPO), have become standard. Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) is a newer reinforcement learning approach gaining traction in LLMs, especially for chain-of-thought reasoning.

However, the application of post-training methods to *unified multimodal models* is less mature and faces unique challenges:
- **External Data Dependency:** Many post-training methods still rely on additional external data for fine-tuning, which can be expensive and time-consuming to collect.
- **Task Specificity:** Some methods are limited to optimizing a single task (e.g., only generation or only understanding) rather than improving both simultaneously within a unified framework.
- **Lack of Unification:** Even for models designed to be "unified," there can be an inherent imbalance or misalignment in their capabilities across different tasks. A model might generate excellent images but fail to understand them, or vice-versa. This highlights a gap in evaluating and addressing the *consistency* between generative and discriminative abilities.

"UniRL" addresses these specific gaps by proposing a self-improving post-training approach that operates *without external image data* by using the model's own generated content for training. Furthermore, it explicitly tackles the critical issue of **imbalance between generation and understanding**, aiming for mutual enhancement between these two core capabilities, a less explored area in unified multimodal model research. By exploring both SFT and GRPO in this context, the paper contributes to understanding the efficacy of different post-training paradigms for complex multimodal architectures.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind the UniRL framework are driven by the limitations of existing unified multimodal models, particularly in their post-training phase:

1. **Develop a Self-Improving Post-Training Method:** The core objective is to create a methodology that allows unified multimodal models to enhance their capabilities iteratively without requiring external image data. This is achieved by leveraging the model's own generated images as synthetic training data. This addresses the high computational cost and data dependency prevalent in traditional large-scale pretraining.

2. **Facilitate Mutual Enhancement between Generation and Understanding:** A critical motivation is to overcome the observed imbalance between the text-to-image generation (T2I) and multimodal understanding (MMU) capabilities of unified models. The paper aims to enable these two tasks to "enhance each other," where generated images are used to improve understanding, and understanding results are used to supervise and refine generation. This mutual reinforcement is designed to lead to more consistent and aligned multimodal performance.

3. **Explore and Compare Optimization Strategies:** The work seeks to investigate the effectiveness of two distinct post-training optimization strategies—Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) and Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)—within the unified multimodal context. The motivation is to provide a detailed comparison of their advantages and disadvantages, especially regarding generalization, overfitting, and stability in different architectural settings (end-to-end vs. non-end-to-end).

4. **Quantify and Reduce Task Imbalance:** Motivated by the observation that unified models often struggle to perform both generation and understanding consistently on the same content, the authors aim to introduce a novel metric to quantitatively assess this imbalance. Subsequently, a key objective is to demonstrate that UniRL can effectively reduce this measured imbalance, leading to better consistency between generation and understanding capabilities.

5. **Achieve Efficient Post-Training:** The method is designed to require "only several additional training steps during the post-training stage," implying a focus on efficiency in improving already pretrained models, rather than undertaking another full-scale pretraining.

In essence, UniRL is motivated by the need for a more sustainable, data-efficient, and holistically effective approach to training unified multimodal models, pushing beyond the initial pretraining phase to achieve higher performance and better task alignment.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

UniRL introduces a self-improving post-training framework for unified multimodal models, designed to operate without external image data and improve both generation and understanding while reducing task imbalance. The methodology involves several key components:

#### 3.1. Unified Multimodal Model Background

The paper identifies two main categories of unified multimodal models:
1. Models using autoregressive methods for both generation and understanding (e.g., Janus).
2. Models employing diffusion or flow matching for generation and autoregressive methods for comprehension (e.g., Show-o).
The research uses Show-o and Janus as base models, noting their distinct architectural choices regarding image representations (Show-o uses shared VQGAN tokens, Janus uses separate discrete tokens for T2I and ViT features for MMU).

#### 3.2. Prompt and QA Pair Construction

Inspired by the GenEval benchmark, the authors construct a dataset of prompts and corresponding question-answer (QA) pairs. These prompts are categorized into six types reflecting fundamental visual features: single object, two objects, counting, colors, position, and attribute. Objects are sourced from COCO and GPT-4o to expand the vocabulary to 160 items. Crucially, the prompts used for training are independently constructed and do not overlap with the GenEval test set, ensuring a strict separation for evaluation.

#### 3.3. Self-Improving Optimization Framework

The core idea of UniRL is a feedback loop where image generation and multimodal understanding mutually reinforce each other:
1. **Image Generation:** Given a constructed text prompt, the unified model generates a sequence of image tokens.
2. **Differentiable Sampling:** To enable gradient flow through these discrete image tokens, the Straight-Through Gumbel-Softmax (ST-GS) estimator is applied. This allows the model to treat the sampled image tokens as differentiable during the backward pass, crucial for end-to-end optimization.
3. **Multimodal Understanding:** The generated image tokens, along with the corresponding question, are fed back into the model to predict an answer.
4. **Optimization:** The predicted answer is compared to the ground-truth answer (from the constructed QA pair), and the model is optimized using this feedback. This process simultaneously improves both image generation (by evaluating the quality of generated images based on understanding performance) and image understanding (by providing new training data).

#### 3.4. Optimization Strategies: SFT and GRPO

UniRL explores two distinct optimization strategies:

1. **Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) Optimization:** In this approach, a single image is generated per prompt. The SFT loss is computed as the negative log-likelihood of the ground-truth answer given the generated image and question. The ST-GS estimator ensures that gradients can propagate back through the generated image tokens to update both the understanding and generation modules.

2. **Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) Optimization:** This reinforcement learning method involves generating a *group* of K images for a given prompt. For each generated image, an answer is predicted, and a reward is computed by comparing the predicted answer to the ground-truth. The GRPO loss optimizes the model by applying a weighted log-likelihood over the sampled answers, where weights are derived from the rewards. A KL divergence penalty is included to stabilize training and prevent drastic policy shifts. The reward function assigns scores based on the accuracy of object identification and attribute prediction (e.g., number, color, position) in the answers. Like SFT, ST-GS is used to propagate rewards through the discrete image tokens to the generation module.

#### 3.5. End-to-End vs. Non-End-to-End Training

The paper distinguishes between two training paradigms based on the model's architecture:
- **End-to-End Training:** For models like Show-o, which use shared image representations (e.g., VQGAN tokens) for both T2I and MMU, gradients can flow seamlessly through the entire unified model via ST-GS. A single unified GRPO loss optimizes both tasks jointly.
- **Non-End-to-End Training:** For models like Janus, which use separate image representations (discrete tokens for T2I, ViT features for MMU), direct gradient flow between the T2I and MMU modules is ineffective. In this case, UniRL adopts a decoupled approach where separate GRPO objectives are applied to the T2I and MMU branches. Although modules are trained independently, reward signals from one task can still guide the optimization of the other, enabling cross-task interaction. SFT was found to be unstable for T2I in this setting.

#### 3.6. Evaluation of Task Imbalance

A significant contribution is the introduction of a novel bidirectional evaluation metric to quantify the imbalance between T2I and MMU:
- **Accuracy<sub>MMU|T2I</sub>:** Measures multimodal understanding accuracy *conditioned on a correctly generated image*. It's calculated as (Number of samples where both T2I and MMU are correct) / (Number of correctly generated images).
- **Accuracy<sub>T2I|MMU</sub>:** Measures text-to-image generation accuracy *conditioned on a correctly answered question*. It's calculated as (Number of samples where both T2I and MMU are correct) / (Number of correctly answered questions).
These metrics provide insights into the consistency of performance across tasks, revealing whether a model that excels at one task can also perform well on the other, or vice-versa.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of UniRL on Show-o (end-to-end training) and Janus (non-end-to-end training) demonstrates its effectiveness across generation, understanding, and task balance.

#### 4.1. Text-to-Image Generation Performance

- **Show-o:** UniRL significantly improves GenEval scores. UniRL (SFT) achieves 0.77 (up from 0.60 for original Show-o and 0.61 for HermesFlow), and UniRL (GRPO) achieves 0.71. Both also show gains on DSG-1K and CLIP-Score, indicating improved image quality and alignment with text prompts.
- **Janus:** UniRL (GRPO) improves the GenEval score from 0.60 to 0.65, and slightly improves DSG-1K and CLIP-Score.

#### 4.2. Multimodal Understanding Performance

- **Show-o:** For MMU, UniRL (GRPO) achieves an overall accuracy of 0.79, outperforming the original Show-o (0.71) and HermesFlow (0.73). Notably, UniRL (SFT) performs slightly worse (0.67), particularly struggling in the "single object" category due to overfitting.
- **Janus:** UniRL (GRPO) improves MMU accuracy from 0.84 to 0.88.
- **Generalization:** While the core evaluation focuses on basic visual features aligned with training data, the GRPO method shows better generalization on out-of-distribution visual inputs (e.g., images generated by JanusPro).

#### 4.3. Task Imbalance Assessment

The proposed bidirectional metric clearly shows that UniRL, particularly the GRPO-based method, significantly reduces the imbalance between generation and understanding.
- **Show-o:**
 - Original Show-o: Accuracy<sub>MMU|T2I</sub> = 0.83, Accuracy<sub>T2I|MMU</sub> = 0.70.
 - UniRL (GRPO): Accuracy<sub>MMU|T2I</sub> = 0.93, Accuracy<sub>T2I|MMU</sub> = 0.81. This represents a substantial improvement in consistency across both tasks, especially in categories like "counting," "position," and "attribute."
 - UniRL (SFT): While MMU|T2I was 0.81, T2I|MMU was 0.80, showing good balance but worse overall MMU performance due to overfitting.
- **Janus:**
 - Original Janus: Accuracy<sub>MMU|T2I</sub> = 0.67, Accuracy<sub>T2I|MMU</sub> = 0.68.
 - UniRL (GRPO): Accuracy<sub>MMU|T2I</sub> = 0.76, Accuracy<sub>T2I|MMU</sub> = 0.74. This demonstrates improved balance and consistency for Janus as well.

#### 4.4. Analysis of SFT vs. GRPO

The experimental results provide crucial insights into the strengths and weaknesses of SFT and GRPO for unified multimodal models:
1. **SFT's Overfitting in MMU:** SFT is more prone to overfitting and memorization in the image understanding task, especially when training and test sets are disjoint (e.g., "single object" category for Show-o).
2. **SFT's Strength in T2I (End-to-End):** In end-to-end training pipelines like Show-o, SFT can outperform GRPO in the generation task. The direct, ground-truth based supervision for MMU might indirectly guide T2I more effectively when the model capacity is high enough not to be limited by noisy rewards.
3. **GRPO's Robustness:** GRPO demonstrates better generalization in MMU due to its reward-based training and KL regularization, which prevents overfitting.
4. **GRPO for Non-End-to-End Training:** For models with decoupled representations (like Janus), SFT leads to unstable training and failure in T2I, as early generation errors are repeatedly reinforced. GRPO, with its reward signals and more stable optimization, is robust and effective in these non-end-to-end settings.

**Overall, the findings suggest that GRPO is a more general and robust optimization strategy for unified multimodal models, consistently improving performance and reducing task imbalance across different architectures, while SFT might be more sensitive to model capacity and prone to overfitting in understanding tasks.**

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The UniRL framework represents a significant advancement in the post-training optimization of unified multimodal models, with several key implications and potential impacts:

1. **Reduced Data Dependency and Cost:** UniRL's most significant advantage is its ability to self-improve without requiring external image data. By generating its own training samples, the method drastically reduces the need for expensive and time-consuming manual data collection and annotation. This democratizes access to high-quality model training, allowing smaller labs or companies to refine powerful multimodal models more affordably.

2. **Enhanced Model Consistency and Alignment:** The explicit focus on reducing the imbalance between generation and understanding tasks is a crucial contribution. Traditional metrics often evaluate these capabilities in isolation. UniRL's bidirectional evaluation metric and its demonstrated ability to improve consistency mean that models can become more coherent: if a model can generate an image of "three red cars," it can also accurately count "three" and identify "red cars" in that very image. This leads to more reliable and trustworthy AI systems, particularly in applications requiring precise multimodal comprehension.

3. **Improved Generalization and Robustness:** The comprehensive analysis of SFT vs. GRPO reveals GRPO as a more robust optimization strategy, especially for models with decoupled architectures. This finding guides future research and development, suggesting that reward-based learning with regularization is more suitable for complex multimodal systems than direct supervised fine-tuning in certain scenarios. Better generalization means models can perform well on unseen data and diverse prompts, increasing their practical utility.

4. **Efficiency in Post-Training:** The claim that UniRL requires "only several additional training steps" during post-training highlights its efficiency. Instead of retraining massive models from scratch or undertaking lengthy fine-tuning processes, UniRL offers a lightweight yet effective way to boost performance and address specific shortcomings. This accelerates research cycles and deployment timelines for improved multimodal AI.

5. **Foundation for Future Self-Supervised Learning:** The self-improving paradigm, where a model generates its own training data and learns from its own outputs, opens new avenues for self-supervised learning in complex multimodal domains. This could lead to more autonomous and adaptive AI systems that continually learn and evolve without constant human intervention or external data streams.

6. **Broader Applications:** Unified multimodal models with improved consistency and generation/understanding capabilities have wide-ranging applications, including:
 - **Content Creation:** Generating more contextually accurate and semantically rich images from text.
 - **AI Assistants:** Enabling better visual understanding and response generation in multimodal conversational AI.
 - **Education:** Creating interactive learning tools that generate visual aids and test comprehension.
 - **Accessibility:** Enhancing image description for visually impaired individuals.
 - **Robotics:** Improving environmental perception and task execution based on visual and linguistic commands.

While the paper acknowledges limitations such as the focus on basic visual understanding and slower training speed per iteration for GRPO, the foundational concept of self-improving unified multimodal models via internally generated data marks a significant step towards more autonomous, efficient, and balanced multimodal AI.
