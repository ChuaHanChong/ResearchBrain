# UniRL: Self-Improving Unified Multimodal Models via Supervised and Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> National University of Singapore and ByteDance researchers develop UniRL, a self-improving post-training framework for unified multimodal models that eliminates the need for external image data by using the model's own generated images as training signals, with Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) achieving 0.77 GenEval scores on Show-o (versus 0.60 baseline) and substantially reducing task imbalance between text-to-image generation and multimodal understanding through a novel bidirectional evaluation metric that measures cross-task consistency, demonstrating that models can effectively learn from their own synthetic outputs via differentiable sampling and reward-based optimization.

## Problem

- Unified multimodal models suffer from imbalance between text-to-image generation and multimodal understanding capabilities, often excelling at one task while underperforming on the other
- Post-training methods for multimodal models typically require expensive external image datasets and manual annotation, limiting accessibility and scalability
- Existing optimization strategies like supervised fine-tuning can lead to overfitting in understanding tasks, particularly when training and test distributions differ
- No established metrics exist to quantify the consistency between generation and understanding performance on the same content

## Method

- Develop a ==self-improving framework== that generates synthetic training data by having the model create images from text prompts, then use those images for multimodal understanding training
- Apply ==Straight-Through Gumbel-Softmax estimation== to enable gradient flow through discrete image tokens, allowing end-to-end optimization of both generation and understanding
- Compare Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) with Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) as optimization strategies, with GRPO using reward signals based on answer accuracy
- Introduce ==bidirectional evaluation metrics== (Accuracy_MMU|T2I and Accuracy_T2I|MMU) to measure cross-task consistency and quantify task imbalance

## Results

- Show-o improvements: GenEval scores increase from **0.60** to **0.77** (SFT) and **0.71** (GRPO), with GRPO achieving better multimodal understanding (**0.79** vs **0.71** baseline accuracy)
- Task balance enhancement: Accuracy_MMU|T2I improves from **0.83** to **0.93** and Accuracy_T2I|MMU from **0.70** to **0.81** for Show-o with GRPO
- Janus performance gains: GenEval increases from **0.60** to **0.65** and multimodal understanding from **0.84** to **0.88** with GRPO optimization
- GRPO shows better generalization on out-of-distribution visual inputs compared to SFT, which exhibits overfitting particularly in single object recognition tasks

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - GRPO demonstrates superior robustness and generalization compared to SFT, particularly for models with decoupled architectures like Janus where SFT causes training instability
> - Self-generated synthetic data can effectively improve model performance without external datasets, with the model's own outputs providing sufficient supervision signals
> - Task imbalance in unified models can be systematically measured and reduced through mutual enhancement between generation and understanding capabilities
> - End-to-end training (shared representations) versus non-end-to-end training (separate representations) requires different optimization approaches, with decoupled GRPO objectives needed for the latter
