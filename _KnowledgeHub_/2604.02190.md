# UniDriveVLA: Unifying Understanding, Perception, and Action Planning for Autonomous Driving

> [!summary] Summary
> UniDriveVLA presents a unified Vision-Language-Action model that integrates understanding, perception, and planning for autonomous driving, effectively mitigating the trade-off between spatial awareness and semantic reasoning through a Mixture-of-Transformers architecture. The model achieves competitive performance in closed-loop driving (Bench2Drive Driving Score 78.37) and demonstrates high trajectory accuracy on nuScenes, all while preserving general multimodal reasoning abilities.

## Problem

- Existing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for autonomous driving face a fundamental trade-off between achieving strong explicit spatial perception and robust semantic reasoning.
- Efforts to enhance VLA models with 3D spatial representations often degrade their inherent semantic reasoning capabilities, and vice-versa.
- The core issue is identified as representation interference, stemming from the coupled optimization of disparate spatial and semantic objectives within shared model parameters.

## Method

- A ==Mixture-of-Transformers== (MoT) architecture with dedicated expert-specific pathways and ==masked joint attention== decouples understanding, perception, and action token optimization.
- A ==sparse spatial perception module== uses query-driven mechanisms to extract crucial spatial priors directly from multi-scale 2D visual features for unified perception tasks.
- A ==three-stage progressive training strategy== (semantic anchoring, perception/planning integration, expert specialization) preserves VLM semantic reasoning while gradually introducing driving-specific capabilities.

## Results

- **UniDriveVLA** achieved a Driving Score of **78.37** and Efficiency of **198.86** on the Bench2Drive closed-loop simulation, demonstrating strong multi-ability driving across interactive scenarios.
- The model attained the lowest average L2 error for open-loop trajectory planning on nuScenes without explicit ego-state inputs, indicating high accuracy in prediction.
- **UniDriveVLA** demonstrated competitive intermediate perception performance on nuScenes (detection mAP of **0.407**, map mAP of **0.535**) and maintained meaningful general VQA capabilities (e.g., **49.9** on RealWorldQA) post-adaptation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decoupling heterogeneous tokens and their optimization pathways is crucial for simultaneously achieving strong spatial perception and robust semantic reasoning within a unified VLA model.
> - The proposed Mixture-of-Transformers architecture effectively mitigates representation interference by assigning distinct parameterizations and controlled interaction patterns to understanding, perception, and action experts.
> - A progressive training methodology can successfully adapt large pre-trained Vision-Language Models to complex, domain-specific tasks like autonomous driving without catastrophic forgetting of their general multimodal understanding.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Autonomous driving research has made progress across various sub-domains, including perception, prediction, and planning. Recently, Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models have emerged as a development direction, aiming to leverage extensive world knowledge and semantic reasoning capabilities to enhance the cognitive functions of driving systems. These VLA models typically build upon existing 2D Vision-Language Models (VLMs), which are pre-trained on large-scale internet image-text datasets.

A challenge in adapting these VLMs for autonomous driving tasks is the trade-off between spatial perception and semantic reasoning. Existing VLA systems generally adopt one of two strategies, each with limitations:
1. **Directly adopting 2D VLMs:** This approach tends to preserve the native semantic reasoning strengths of VLMs but provides limited explicit spatial perception, which is crucial for safe autonomous driving.
2. **Enhancing with 3D spatial representations:** Methods in this category attempt to improve spatial perception by integrating 3D features, either through aligning structured 2D/3D features with language representations or by directly incorporating spatial tokens into a shared decoder. However, these enhancements often lead to a degradation of the VLM's inherent semantic reasoning capabilities.

The paper identifies the core issue as the coupled optimization of spatial perception and semantic reasoning within shared model parameters. When spatial and semantic information are jointly optimized in a single parameter space, representation interference can occur, compromising the distinct strengths of each modality. Many current methods integrate these information types within unified architectures that rely on shared parameters, leading to the observed dilemma. This research positions itself by proposing a framework that seeks to address this fundamental conflict, aiming to achieve both strong spatial perception and robust semantic reasoning simultaneously within a unified VLA model for autonomous driving.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a unified Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model for autonomous driving, named UniDriveVLA, that effectively integrates understanding, perception, and action planning without compromising the performance of these individual capabilities.

The motivation for this objective stems from a recognized dilemma in existing VLA systems for autonomous driving. Current models face a conflict between spatial perception and semantic reasoning. While Vision-Language Models (VLMs) offer rich semantic understanding derived from internet-scale pre-training, they are not inherently designed for explicit spatial perception, which is critical for complex driving scenarios. Efforts to enhance these models with 3D spatial representations often lead to a degradation of their native semantic reasoning abilities.

The authors postulate that this dilemma originates from the joint optimization of disparate objectives—spatial perception and semantic reasoning—within a shared set of model parameters. This shared parameter space can cause "representation interference," where features intended for spatial tasks become entangled with those for semantic tasks, weakening the effectiveness of both. Specifically, although 3D spatial representations can improve perception, their co-optimization with semantic reasoning can undermine the VLM's pre-trained world knowledge.

Therefore, the key motivations are:
- **Overcome the perception-reasoning conflict:** To enable VLA models to possess both strong spatial awareness and robust semantic understanding for autonomous driving.
- **Decouple optimization objectives:** To prevent interference between heterogeneous semantic, spatial, and action tokens, thereby preserving the native reasoning capacity of pre-trained VLMs while enhancing spatial perception.
- **Achieve unified functionality:** To develop a single model capable of performing driving understanding (semantic interpretation), scene perception (spatial awareness), and action planning (trajectory generation) coherently.
- **Maintain native VLM reasoning:** To ensure that the introduction of driving-specific spatial and action capabilities does not lead to "catastrophic forgetting" or degradation of the foundational semantic understanding learned during pre-training.

By addressing these challenges, the research aims to pave the way for more robust and capable VLA systems in autonomous driving that can leverage rich world knowledge effectively without sacrificing critical spatial awareness or planning precision.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

UniDriveVLA addresses the perception–reasoning conflict through a multi-faceted approach involving a specialized architecture, a sparse perception paradigm, and a progressive training strategy.

#### 3.1. UniDriveVLA Architecture: Mixture-of-Transformers (MoT)

The core of UniDriveVLA is a Mixture-of-Transformers (MoT) architecture designed to decouple the optimization of understanding, perception, and action. This prevents representation interference by assigning heterogeneous tokens to expert-specific pathways while allowing controlled interaction.

1. **Token Generation:**
 - **Understanding Tokens (T_und):** Generated from multi-view camera images ($I_{cam}$), historical trajectories ($I_{hist}$), and navigation commands ($L_{nav}$) by a vision-language backbone. These tokens capture high-level semantic information.
 - **Perception Tokens (T_per):** Extracted from visual features by a sparse perception module, encoding critical spatial priors for various perception tasks.
 - **Action Tokens (T_act):** Produced by an action encoder, structured for planning-oriented trajectory modeling. Action inputs are constructed using flow matching interpolation between Gaussian noise and a target velocity sequence.

2. **Expert-Specific Parameterization:** Within each MoT layer, the three token groups are processed by distinct, expert-specific linear projections for queries ($Q_g$), keys ($K_g$), and values ($V_g$), where $g \in \{und, per, act\}$. This ensures that the initial representation space for each expert is decoupled.

3. **Masked Joint Attention:** To facilitate coordinated information flow while maintaining separation, the projected representations from all experts are concatenated ($Q = [Q_{und}; Q_{per}; Q_{act}]$, etc.). Global attention is then computed using a mask matrix ($M$) that governs the visibility pattern between tokens.
 - **Masking Strategy:**
 - Understanding tokens employ causal masking, preventing them from attending to subsequent perception or action tokens, thereby preserving the pre-trained VLM's semantic reasoning autonomy.
 - Perception tokens are allowed to attend to preceding understanding tokens to acquire semantic context.
 - Action tokens can aggregate both semantic information from understanding tokens and spatial information from perception tokens, which is necessary for comprehensive planning.

4. **Expert-Specific Output Processing:** After the masked joint attention, the resulting features are split back into their respective expert groups. Each expert group then undergoes further expert-specific output projection, layer normalization, and a feed-forward transformation.

5. **Unified Objective Function:** The entire system is jointly optimized using a unified objective function: $L_{total} = \lambda_1 L_{ar} + \lambda_2 L_{per} + \lambda_3 L_{act}$. This includes an autoregressive language modeling loss ($L_{ar}$), a perception loss ($L_{per}$) for structured perception tasks, and an action loss ($L_{act}$) based on flow-matching trajectory generation.

#### 3.2. Sparse Spatial Perception

UniDriveVLA employs a sparse spatial perception mechanism, contrasting with methods that use dense Bird's-Eye-View (BEV) representations. This module directly extracts spatial priors from multi-scale 2D visual features.

1. **Query-Driven Paradigm:** Task-specific sparse queries are used to aggregate multi-view image features. These queries are initialized from instance banks (obtained via K-Means clustering on dataset-level data) and dynamically updated.
2. **Unified Perception Module:** A single module jointly models various perception tasks: 3D detection, online mapping, ego-status estimation, and motion forecasting. Occupancy is also modeled as an auxiliary latent branch.
3. **Temporal and Cross-Task Interaction:** The queries are updated through temporal interaction (capturing dynamics), intra-task reasoning (task-specific structure), inter-task communication (cross-task dependencies), deformable feature aggregation, and task-wise refinement.
4. **Semantic Enrichment:** Initial sparse perception outputs are projected into the perception expert's hidden space. Here, they interact with understanding and action branches via masked joint attention, allowing perception tokens to be semantically enhanced by the understanding branch and aggregated by the action branch for planning. The refined features are then projected back to the sparse perception space.

#### 3.3. Three-Stage Progressive Training Strategy

A progressive training strategy is implemented to preserve semantic reasoning while gradually introducing and refining spatial perception and planning capabilities, mitigating catastrophic forgetting.

1. **Stage 1: Semantic Anchoring:** The model's foundational semantic reasoning is established through large-scale multimodal pre-training. This stage utilizes a mix of driving-specific Visual Question Answering (VQA) datasets and general-domain multimodal data, with a dominant sampling ratio for high-quality general data to maintain broad semantic capabilities.
2. **Stage 2: Perception and Planning Integration:** Perception and planning supervision are incorporated. This involves joint optimization of autoregressive language modeling, spatial perception tasks (3D detection, online mapping, occupancy prediction), and flow-matching-based trajectory generation. To protect the VLM's semantic reasoning, Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) is applied to the language model, and its base learning rate is reduced.
3. **Stage 3: Expert Specialization:** The Vision-Language Model is frozen. The Perception Expert and Action Expert are then fine-tuned, with an additional motion prediction objective introduced to provide dynamic priors for the action expert and support motion-aware planning.

This comprehensive methodology aims to build a unified, robust autonomous driving system that handles perception, understanding, and action planning effectively by decoupling their underlying learning processes.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of UniDriveVLA was conducted on widely recognized autonomous driving benchmarks, nuScenes and Bench2Drive, covering both open-loop and closed-loop performance, as well as intermediate perception and understanding tasks.

#### 4.1. Closed-Loop Planning on Bench2Drive

- **Overall Performance:** UniDriveVLA achieved a Driving Score of 78.37 and an Efficiency of 198.86, demonstrating competitive performance among methods not utilizing PDM-Lite (a dataset providing higher-quality expert demonstrations). The model also obtained a Success Rate of 51.82%.
- **Multi-ability Driving:** The model exhibited strong capabilities across various interactive driving scenarios. It achieved 38.75% on Merging and 80.00% on Overtaking, along with a competitive mean score of 51.53% across diverse abilities. These results indicate the model's capacity to handle complex interactive behaviors in closed-loop simulations.

#### 4.2. Open-Loop Trajectory Planning on nuScenes

- **Trajectory Accuracy:** UniDriveVLA showed competitive results in end-to-end trajectory planning on nuScenes, both with and without ego-state inputs.
- **Without Ego-State:** In the more challenging setting where explicit ego-state information is not provided, UniDriveVLA-Large attained the lowest average L2 error under both the ST-P3 and UniAD evaluation protocols, indicating high trajectory accuracy.
- **With Ego-State:** When ego-state inputs were available, UniDriveVLA remained competitive compared to recent VLA methods (e.g., AutoVLA, FSDrive) and established end-to-end driving baselines (e.g., UniAD, VAD). This suggests that the integration of sparse perception priors enhances open-loop trajectory prediction, particularly when ego-state is less explicit.

#### 4.3. Intermediate Perception on nuScenes

- **Detection and Mapping:** UniDriveVLA demonstrated competitive performance in detection and online mapping tasks on the nuScenes validation set. UniDriveVLA-Large achieved a detection mAP of 0.407 and an NDS (nuScenes Detection Score) of 0.460, alongside a map mAP of 0.535. These metrics represent improvements over earlier end-to-end driving methods like VAD, suggesting that the sparse perception design provides a robust spatial foundation.
- **Motion Prediction:** While the motion prediction accuracy of UniDriveVLA did not surpass specialized baselines, the results indicate that the model maintains meaningful multi-task perception capabilities within the unified VLA framework.

#### 4.4. Ablation Study on Planning Components

- **Component Contribution:** A progressive integration of planning components (ego-state, detection, mapping, occupancy, motion) was evaluated.
 - **Ego-state:** Introducing ego-state information led to substantial improvements in both trajectory accuracy (L2 error) and collision rate.
 - **Detection:** Adding detection capabilities further reduced the collision rate to 0.10, indicating the benefit of explicit object-level perception for safety.
 - **Occupancy:** Incorporating occupancy information resulted in the best L2 error of 0.53, suggesting that dense spatial context contributes to trajectory prediction.
 - **Mapping and Motion:** These components yielded marginal gains in the nuScenes open-loop setting, which the authors attribute to the limited headroom for improvement in this specific evaluation context.

#### 4.5. Driving Scene Understanding and General Visual Capabilities

- **Driving-Oriented Understanding:** UniDriveVLA achieved a strong average performance of 51.97% on DriveBench, indicating effective driving-oriented understanding and behavior reasoning.
- **Effect of MoT Decoupling:** A comparison between a shared-weight decoder and the Mixture-of-Transformers (MoT) architecture revealed that MoT consistently improved performance across understanding, perception, and planning tasks, with notable gains in trajectory prediction and collision reduction. Empirical analysis of token similarity showed that MoT maintained low similarity between LLM and perception tokens, supporting the effectiveness of expert decoupling in mitigating the perception-reasoning conflict. In contrast, the shared-weight decoder exhibited increasing similarity, suggesting feature collapse.
- **Preservation of General Multimodal Capability:** Despite adaptation for driving, UniDriveVLA retained meaningful performance on various general VQA benchmarks (e.g., 49.9 on RealWorldQA, 76.3 on AI2D, and 76.3 on ChartQA). This suggests that the training strategy effectively preserved a level of multimodal reasoning ability beyond specific autonomous driving tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents UniDriveVLA, a unified Vision-Language-Action (VLA) framework for autonomous driving, which addresses a fundamental challenge within the field: the conflict between spatial perception and semantic reasoning in monolithic VLA models. The work identifies that the coupled optimization of these disparate objectives within shared model parameters leads to representational interference, hindering the overall performance of driving systems.

The significance of UniDriveVLA lies in its architectural and methodological contributions:
- **Architectural Innovation:** The introduction of a Mixture-of-Transformers (MoT) architecture with dedicated Understanding, Perception, and Action experts offers a method to decouple optimization while maintaining controlled cross-expert interaction via masked joint attention. This approach enables the model to concurrently achieve strong spatial perception and robust semantic reasoning, which existing VLA models have historically struggled to balance.
- **Enhanced Spatial Perception:** The sparse perception paradigm, which extracts spatial priors directly from multi-scale 2D visual features, contributes to improving the model's ability to interpret 3D scenes effectively. This is integrated with a broader context by projecting first-pass perception outputs into the VLM's hidden space for semantic enrichment.
- **Robust Training Strategy:** The three-stage progressive training strategy is designed to anchor semantic reasoning, incrementally incorporate perception and planning capabilities, and specialize experts without sacrificing the foundational semantic understanding of the pre-trained VLM. This addresses the challenge of catastrophic forgetting during domain-specific adaptation.

The potential impact of UniDriveVLA extends across several dimensions:
- **Advancement in Autonomous Driving Systems:** By demonstrating competitive performance in both open-loop evaluations on nuScenes and closed-loop evaluations on Bench2Drive, UniDriveVLA provides a step towards more capable and reliable autonomous driving systems. Its strong performance across perception, prediction, and understanding tasks within a unified framework suggests a more holistic approach to autonomous vehicle control.
- **Bridging Perception and Reasoning:** The model's ability to mitigate the perception-reasoning conflict offers a pathway for VLA models to leverage the rich world knowledge of large language models without compromising the precise spatial awareness required for physical interaction with the environment.
- **Broad Applicability:** The decoupled VLA design proposed in this work is not confined solely to autonomous driving. The principles of expert decoupling and controlled information flow could potentially be extended to other complex robotic manipulation scenarios that necessitate both structured spatial perception and high-level semantic reasoning, such as industrial robotics or household assistance.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** This work contributes to the ongoing evolution of multimodal foundation models by providing a robust architecture and training methodology for domain-specific adaptation, particularly in safety-critical applications. The maintenance of general multimodal capabilities post-adaptation also highlights the potential for developing truly versatile intelligent agents.

In summary, UniDriveVLA represents an advancement in unifying diverse capabilities for autonomous driving by structurally addressing a core conflict, thereby laying groundwork for more integrated and cognitively capable intelligent systems.
