# RISE: Adaptive Imagination for World Action Models

> [!summary] Summary
> RISE is an adaptive-imagination framework for driving World Action Models: a lightweight Scheduler, combining a Latent Evaluator that predicts risk and Future Planning Gain with a cost-aware Rollout Gate, makes per-step Roll/Stop decisions so rollout depth varies by scene. Trained with CounterDrive, a verified counterfactual driving dataset, it reaches 91.5 PDMS on NAVSIM v1 and 90.8 EPDMS on v2 with fewer rollout steps.

## Problem

- Existing world action models fix one imagination budget for every scene, never reassessing after a partial rollout whether more prediction would actually improve the planned trajectory.
- Imagine-and-plan, imagine-then-plan, and no-imagination schedules are chosen globally at design time, so easy scenes pay latency while ambiguous interactive scenes get too little future context.
- Factual driving logs record only the realized future, leaving safety-critical alternatives unseen and giving no supervision for ranking how risky a predicted future prefix is.

## Method

- ==Latent Evaluator==, a ==causal GRU== over pooled latent prefixes, predicts a ==Risk Profile== for each prefix depth and a ==Future Planning Gain Profile== scoring the improvement from continuing rollout to each deeper horizon.
- ==Rollout Gate==, an ==MLP== over pooled tokens, both profiles, normalized depth and cumulative cost, emits a signed score whose sign yields the binary ==Roll/Stop decision== under a ==computation-preference weight==.
- ==Three-stage training== plus **CounterDrive**: Wan-generated counterfactual clips with ==OpenVO==-recovered poses and human incident annotation supply ==risk-ranking and onset-localization losses==, atop a frozen ==V-JEPA 2== encoder, ==action-conditioned Predictor==, and ==diffusion Planner==.

## Results

- Reaches **91.5 PDMS** on NAVSIM v1 and **90.8 EPDMS** on v2, exceeding the strongest baselines by **0.8** and **0.9** points, plus nuScenes average L2 **0.31 m** and collision rate **0.10**.
- Ablations: Scheduler alone gives **91.2 PDMS**, CounterDrive alone **90.5**, both together **91.5** versus **89.7** base; CounterDrive lifts hazard-ranking AUC from **0.49-0.52** to **0.93-0.96** and accident accuracy to **0.96**.
- The Scheduler beats Random Stop (**89.5 EPDMS**) and latent-convergence stopping (**89.7 EPDMS**, **2.98 rollouts**) at **90.8** with **2.40** average rollouts; transferring it into DAWN raises PDMS from **89.1** to **90.3**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Test-time imagination should be budgeted per scene, not globally: 1,248 NAVSIM scenes plan best with zero rollout, where deeper prediction actively degrades scores.
> - Stopping criteria grounded in expected downstream planning gain outperform representation-intrinsic heuristics such as latent convergence, which spend more compute for worse decisions.
> - Verified counterfactual generation supplies risk supervision that factual logs structurally cannot; without it, hazard ranking sits at chance despite strong planning metrics.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of end-to-end autonomous driving has progressed from direct trajectory regression towards models that incorporate reasoning about future events. World Action Models (WAMs) represent a significant development in this area, allowing policies to evaluate actions based on their potential consequences rather than solely on current observations. This capability is particularly relevant for driving, where identical immediate visual contexts can lead to diverse outcomes depending on interactions between the ego vehicle and surrounding agents. Current WAMs, including those utilizing latent future prediction, action-conditioned dynamics, or world-model-based policy learning, typically employ a fixed imagination budget or rollout depth for every scene.

Existing WAM architectures vary in how they integrate future imagination: "imagine-and-plan" models use a shared backbone for prediction and planning, "imagine-then-plan" models complete a future rollout before planning, and some approaches forgo test-time imagination entirely. Despite these structural differences, a common limitation is their fixed inference schedules. These methods do not dynamically reassess after each partial rollout whether the current future prefix is adequate for robust planning or if continued prediction would improve the final decision. This fixed allocation can lead to inefficiencies, as simpler scenes may not require extensive imagination, while complex, safety-critical scenarios might benefit from deeper future reasoning. The absence of a "Future Planning Gain" signal—a metric for the potential improvement in planning quality from continued rollout—represents a gap in existing methodologies. This work addresses this gap by proposing an adaptive imagination framework designed to allocate computational resources more efficiently and effectively.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an adaptive imagination framework for World Action Models (WAMs) that can dynamically adjust the depth of future imagination based on the specific requirements of a given driving scene. This contrasts with existing WAMs that allocate a predetermined, fixed imagination budget, leading to suboptimal performance or inefficient resource use.

The key motivations behind this work are multifaceted:
1. **Optimize Computational Efficiency**: Fixed imagination budgets can result in unnecessary computation for straightforward driving scenarios where immediate observations are sufficient for planning. Conversely, complex scenarios involving ambiguous interactions or high uncertainty may require deeper future prediction to make informed decisions. An adaptive approach aims to "Roll" (continue imagination) only when the expected improvement in planning justifies the additional computational cost, and "Stop" (proceed to planning) otherwise.
2. **Improve Planning Performance and Safety**: By dynamically allocating imagination resources, the system can dedicate more processing to situations where future evolution is ambiguous or critical interactions are unresolved. This is expected to lead to more accurate, safer, and more compliant trajectories, particularly in safety-critical situations.
3. **Address Data Limitations for Risk Learning**: Training models to predict future risk is challenging because factual driving logs record only one realized future, often lacking diverse counterfactual outcomes that could lead to incidents. This limits the ability to supervise a model's sensitivity to potential hazards. The researchers aim to overcome this by creating a synthetic dataset containing varied, safety-critical counterfactual scenarios.
4. **Enhance Generality and Plug-in Capability**: The goal is to develop a modular adaptive imagination framework that can be integrated into various existing WAM architectures without requiring fundamental redesigns of their core prediction and planning components. This would allow for broader applicability and easier adoption.

To achieve these objectives, the researchers propose RISE, which incorporates a Scheduler composed of a Latent Evaluator and a Rollout Gate. The Latent Evaluator is designed to estimate the planning-relevant risk and the potential planning gain of continued imagination, while the Rollout Gate uses these estimations to make dynamic "Roll/Stop" decisions. Concurrently, the CounterDrive dataset is constructed to provide richer supervision for future dynamics and localized risk assessment, especially in safety-critical contexts.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

RISE (Refining Imagination through SElective Rollout) is presented as a plug-in adaptive imagination framework for World Action Models (WAMs). It augments a standard Encoder–Predictor–Planner WAM with a lightweight Scheduler to dynamically control the depth of future imagination.

#### 3.1. World Action Model Architecture

The underlying WAM consists of three main components:
- **Encoder**: A frozen V-JEPA 2 image encoder with a ViT-L backbone processes front-camera observations. Four input frames are converted into two observed latent time steps, each comprising 512 spatial tokens of 1024 dimensions.
- **Predictor**: This component is a frame-causal, ego-motion-conditioned Transformer with 12 layers. It autoregressively generates future latent steps, up to a maximum horizon `H_D`, starting from the observed latent tokens. Ego-motion conditions are represented by relative pose changes.
- **Planner**: A 12-layer diffusion Transformer, conditioned on the observed tokens, the selected future latent prefix (distinguished by learned source embeddings), observed ego-motion history, and current ego kinematics. It generates six candidate trajectories, each consisting of eight future poses, using a variance-preserving diffusion process.

#### 3.2. Adaptive Rollout Scheduler

The core of RISE is its Scheduler, which makes sequential "Roll/Stop" decisions at each rollout depth `h`.
- **Latent Evaluator**: Given the observed latent `z_t` and the imagined prefix `hat_Z_h` (containing `h` future latent steps), the Latent Evaluator (`Eval_theta_E`) predicts two profiles:
 - **Risk Profile (R_h)**: A sequence `[r_1, ..., r_h]` where each `r_k` estimates the trajectory risk associated with the prefix ending at depth `k`. This summarizes the risk already exposed.
 - **Future Planning Gain Profile (B_h)**: A sequence `[b_{h->h+1}, ..., b_{h->H_D}]` where each `b_{h->j}` predicts the planning score improvement achievable by continuing imagination from depth `h` to `j`, relative to stopping at `h`. This quantifies the potential gain from continued rollout.
- **Rollout Gate**: For each depth `h` (from 0 to `H_D-1`), the Rollout Gate (`G_theta_G`) receives input comprising pooled latent features, the predicted Risk and Future Planning Gain Profiles (zero-padded to `H_D`), the current depth, cumulative computation cost `c_h`, the incremental cost `Delta_c_{h+1}`, and a computation preference parameter `lambda`. It outputs a scalar `x_h`. If `x_h > 0` and `h < H_D`, the decision is "Roll," and the Predictor generates one additional latent step. Otherwise, the decision is "Stop," and the current prefix is passed to the Planner. This sequential decision-making allows the effective rollout horizon to adapt dynamically to each scene.

#### 3.3. CounterDrive Dataset

To address the scarcity of safety-critical counterfactual data in factual driving logs, the CounterDrive dataset was constructed:
- **Generation**: For selected scenes from NAVSIM and nuScenes, a Vision-Language Model (VLM) generates a near-field scene description and a one-sentence accident description. These, combined with a fixed camera constraint prompt and a source key frame, are fed to the Wan 2.7 video generation model to create 10-second counterfactual videos.
- **Annotation and Verification**: OpenVO is used to recover ego poses and compute ego-motion actions. Human annotators verify ego-motion consistency, identify the first incident frame, mark generation distortions, and categorize incidents (normal, non-ego caused, ego-caused). Invalid clips are removed.
- **Purpose**: Accepted CounterDrive clips enrich the training of the Predictor for future-latent generation. Crucially, verified factual–counterfactual incident pairs provide temporally localized supervision for the Latent Evaluator's Risk Profile, enhancing its ability to discriminate hazards.

#### 3.4. Three-Stage Training

RISE's training is structured into three stages:
- **Stage I: Predictor and Initial Planner**:
 - The Predictor is trained on both real and accepted CounterDrive sequences to predict future latents (L_Pre).
 - An initial Planner (Π_0) is trained on real data using variable-length future prefixes (L_0_Pla). After this stage, the Encoder, Predictor, and Π_0 are frozen.
- **Stage II: Latent Evaluator and Guided Planner**:
 - The Latent Evaluator's Risk Profile is trained. For real samples, it is supervised by a fixed geometry-based risk evaluator (L_real). For CounterDrive, verified factual-counterfactual pairs provide ranking supervision (L_rank) to enforce higher predicted risk for hazardous counterfactuals after incident onset, and localized calibration (L_loc) to prevent premature risk labeling. The complete objective is L_R.
 - After risk training, a small norm-constrained update refines each non-empty prefix (`hat_Z_h`) into a "risk-refined" prefix (`tilde_Z_h`) by iteratively reducing accumulated prefix risk.
 - A final Planner (Π_1), initialized from Π_0, is trained on these risk-refined real prefixes (L_1_Pla).
 - The Latent Evaluator's Future Planning Gain Profile is then trained, supervised by the actual planning score differences (`q_j - q_h`) observed from Π_1 when evaluating planning at different rollout depths (L_B).
- **Stage III: Rollout Gate**:
 - With all other modules frozen, the Rollout Gate (`G_theta_G`) is trained as a cost-aware stopping policy. For each real sample and valid rollout depth, the best remaining cost-adjusted gain `g_h^*` is computed using the planning scores `q_h` and cumulative costs `c_h`. A binary target `y_h^*` indicates whether this gain is positive. The Gate is trained with a Binary Cross-Entropy loss (L_G).

This comprehensive approach integrates data generation, predictive modeling, adaptive decision-making, and multi-stage training to create a WAM capable of dynamic and efficient future imagination.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted on the NAVSIM and nuScenes benchmarks aimed to validate the effectiveness of RISE, its constituent components, and its generalizability.

#### 4.1. Main Performance Metrics

- **NAVSIMv1 and v2**: RISE achieved leading overall planning performance. On NAVSIM v1, it recorded 91.5 PDMS (Planning Deviation Metric Score), surpassing the previous best by 0.8 points. Notable improvements included 2.9 points in EP (Ego Planning Score) and 1.9 points in TTC (Time To Collision). On NAVSIM v2, RISE obtained 90.8 EPDMS, outperforming the next best by 0.9 points and ranking first or tying for first on seven out of nine component metrics, indicating robust performance across safety, compliance, and planning quality.
- **nuScenes**: RISE demonstrated state-of-the-art results, achieving the lowest average L2 error of 0.31 m and a collision rate of 0.10.
These results suggest that the adaptive rollout mechanism effectively improves planning outcomes across different evaluation settings and benchmarks.

#### 4.2. Ablation Studies

- **Key Components (Scheduler and CounterDrive)**: An ablation study isolated the contributions of the Scheduler and CounterDrive. Using CounterDrive alone improved EPDMS/PDMS from 88.9/89.7 to 89.8/90.5. The Scheduler alone achieved 90.4/91.2. Combining both components yielded the best performance of 90.8/91.5 EPDMS/PDMS. This indicates that CounterDrive provides valuable supervision for future representation learning, while the Scheduler independently enhances planning by dynamically allocating imagination. Their combined effect suggests complementary roles.
- **Adaptive Rollout Method Comparison**: The Scheduler was compared against alternative adaptive rollout strategies:
 - **Random Stop**: Stopping at a random horizon yielded the lowest latency (264.075 ms) but resulted in 89.5 EPDMS.
 - **Latent Margin**: Stopping when consecutive latents converged averaged 2.98 rollouts, incurring 308.532 ms latency, and achieved 89.7 EPDMS.
 - **RISE Scheduler**: Achieved 90.8 EPDMS with 2.40 average rollouts and 287.429 ms latency.
This comparison demonstrates that the Scheduler's planning gain-aware stopping mechanism is more effective than arbitrary stopping or reliance on latent convergence alone, offering a favorable balance between performance and computational cost.

#### 4.3. Further Analysis

- **Necessity of Adaptive Rollout**: An analysis of planning performance at fixed rollout depths revealed that the optimal depth varies significantly across scenes. Many scenes (e.g., 1,248) performed best with zero rollout, while others (e.g., 4,036 and 2,180) benefited most from depths of 3 and 4, respectively. For scenes that performed best at h=0, forcing deeper imagination (to h=4) reduced EPDMS from 89.9 to 88.4. Conversely, for scenes optimal at h=4, reducing imagination to h=0 decreased EPDMS from 91.1 to 88.5. Qualitative examples illustrated that simple, open-road scenes required less imagination, whereas complex scenarios with dense traffic or intersections benefited substantially from deeper future context. This finding supports the argument that a fixed rollout budget is not universally optimal and an adaptive approach is necessary.
- **CounterDrive for Safety-Critical Evaluation**: The impact of CounterDrive on safety-critical evaluation was assessed on a held-out counterfactual test set. Models without CounterDrive exhibited near-random risk ranking (AUC scores of 0.49–0.52) and accident recognition accuracy (0.51). With CounterDrive, the AUC scores increased significantly to 0.93–0.96, and accident recognition accuracy rose to 0.96, indicating a substantial improvement in the model's ability to discriminate hazards.
- **Transferability of RISE**: To evaluate its generality, the RISE Scheduler was integrated into DAWN, a World–Action Interactive Model, without modifying DAWN's Predictor or Planner. This integration improved DAWN's PDMS from 89.1 to 90.3, with consistent gains across metrics like NC, DAC, EP (2.7 points), and TTC (2.3 points), while maintaining a perfect collision score. This result suggests that the Scheduler can function as a plug-in module across different world–action modeling paradigms.
- **Plausibility of Trajectories**: Qualitative comparisons on NAVSIM demonstrated that RISE generates plausible and lane-consistent trajectories across varied road structures and selected rollout depths. Compared to Drive-JEPA, RISE trajectories remained aligned with human references, even in complex intersection and turning scenarios, where Drive-JEPA sometimes exhibited lateral or directional deviations.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents RISE, an adaptive imagination framework that contributes to the field of World Action Models (WAMs) by introducing a dynamic approach to future prediction in autonomous driving.

The primary significance of RISE lies in its ability to replace the fixed-depth imagination prevalent in existing WAMs with a scene-dependent, cost-aware computation allocation. By introducing a Latent Evaluator to estimate current risk and Future Planning Gain, and a Rollout Gate to make sequential "Roll/Stop" decisions, RISE offers a principled method to balance planning quality with inference cost. This adaptive capability is particularly important for autonomous driving, where computational resources are finite and decision-making speed is critical, yet complex, safety-critical scenarios demand thorough future reasoning.

The introduction of CounterDrive, a counterfactual driving dataset, represents an additional contribution. This dataset addresses a fundamental limitation of factual driving logs—the absence of diverse, safety-critical alternative futures. By providing temporally localized risk supervision through verified factual–counterfactual incident pairs, CounterDrive enhances the model's ability to discriminate hazards and learn more robust risk profiles, thereby improving safety-critical world modeling research.

The experimental results demonstrate that RISE achieves competitive planning performance on established driving benchmarks such as NAVSIM and nuScenes, often surpassing previous state-of-the-art methods while reducing unnecessary rollout. Furthermore, the successful transfer of the RISE Scheduler to a different WAM architecture (DAWN) without modifying its core Predictor or Planner components highlights the plug-in generality of the proposed framework. This suggests that RISE is not tied to a specific WAM design but can serve as a modular enhancement, potentially broadening its applicability across various autonomous driving systems.

The potential impact of this work is therefore two-fold:
1. **Enhanced Performance and Efficiency**: By intelligently allocating computational resources, RISE can enable autonomous driving systems to operate more efficiently in simpler scenarios and more robustly in complex or high-risk situations, leading to improved overall performance and potentially reduced latency.
2. **Improved Safety in Critical Scenarios**: The explicit learning of risk from counterfactual data and its integration into the planning process could lead to more proactive and safer decisions, particularly in edge cases where potential incidents are subtle or require deep future reasoning to avoid.

Despite its current focus on autonomous driving, the principle of adaptive rollout for world action models may have broader applicability in other domains requiring sequential decision-making under uncertainty, although this remains to be explored in future work. The limitations include the current domain specificity and the non-comprehensive coverage of CounterDrive due to generation costs, indicating avenues for future research in scaling the counterfactual dataset and extending evaluation to other robotics or control tasks.
