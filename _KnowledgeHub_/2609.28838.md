# Uncertainty-Gated Exploration Noise Suppresses Task Collapse in Online RL Fine-Tuning of a Flow-Matching Vision-Language-Action Policy

> [!summary] Summary
> Online RL fine-tuning of a 450M flow-matching VLA (SmolVLA) on LIBERO-10 often destroys individual tasks while pooled success looks fine, a failure the authors call task collapse. They compare fixed, ReinFlow-style learned, and uncertainty-gated exploration noise, the last setting per-stream SDE noise from random-network-distillation novelty and critic-ensemble competence signals. Only the gated controller collapsed no task in any seed.

## Problem

- Prior online RL fine-tuning of flow and diffusion VLA policies reports only aggregate suite success, never per-task dynamics during training, hiding tasks that die inside a healthy average.
- A flow-matching policy carries no state-dependent exploration width the way a Gaussian policy does, so the Flow-SDE recipe applies one fixed noise scale everywhere.
- A task driven to zero success stops receiving reward and may never recover, and repairing it with imitation contaminates the policy that repair is meant to fix.

## Method

- ==Flow-SDE== **PPO** fine-tuning of **SmolVLA**: Gaussian noise injected at each of ten denoising steps makes log-densities tractable, giving a two-layer MDP over environment and denoising steps.
- The uncertainty-gated controller sets per-stream noise from two binary task-agnostic signals: whitened ==random-network-distillation== novelty, and low competence from the PPO critic value plus five-head ensemble spread.
- Those signals index a four-cell multiplier table on base scale 0.3, yielding noise in {0.12, 0.30, 0.36, 0.42}, recomputed every environment step and applied one step later.

## Results

- Under pooled collapse, the controller lost **no task** in any of three seeds, while fixed noise collapsed tasks in two seeds and learned noise in all three.
- Mean applied noise was **0.235** to **0.244** for the controller against fixed **0.2500**, and low-success tasks drew scales near **0.288** versus **0.180** for high-success ones.
- No arm beat the behavior-cloning tare of **0.648**, and bfloat16 in-place updates left **96.02%** of action-expert elements bit-identical across three consecutive iterations.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Aggregate success hides per-task destruction; report per-task training dynamics and pre-register collapse thresholds, since a definition change moved one run from 4/7 to 7/7.
> - Adaptive exploration preserved skills without lowering average noise, so protection came from redistributing exploration across task streams, not simply from exploring less.
> - Check numerical precision before crediting an algorithmic effect: in-place bfloat16 with no fp32 master copy absorbs most updates and bounds what any arm could show.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Overview

A robot policy that learns from experience on several tasks can quietly lose one skill while its overall success score hides the damage. The authors change how much exploratory randomness the policy injects while it keeps learning online. They replace one noise level applied everywhere with a bounded level that changes from stream to stream, according to signals about familiarity and competence. Across the plotted runs, this controller's success stays near its starting level, while fixed-noise and learned-noise runs decline.

Canonical success rate over outer iterations for K, the uncertainty-gated controller; F, fixed noise 0.25; and R, learned noise. Each panel has traces for seeds 1337, 2337, and 3337, and the dotted line marks the behavior-cloning tare. K traces remain in the vicinity of the tare. F traces diverge and include substantial declines. All R traces decline early, though by different amounts.

### 2. A noise gate for flow policies

A flow-matching VLA generates actions by repeatedly refining noise through denoising steps. Unlike a Gaussian policy, it has no built-in, state-dependent exploration width. The Flow-SDE sampling recipe injects noise, usually at one fixed scale.

The controller instead supplies a per-stream scale from two task-agnostic signal families:

- **Novelty** is a random-network-distillation prediction-error signal on the policy's last-denoising-step feature.
- **Competence** uses the PPO critic's value and the spread of a separate five-head value ensemble.

These are regulation signals, not calibrated confidence estimates.

The combination of the two signals selects one of four scales around a base of 0.3:

SituationScaleNovel, low competence0.42Novel, high competence0.30Familiar, low competence0.36Familiar, high competence0.12

The chosen scale is applied one environment step later. The controller reads no task label and needs no episode boundary.

### 3. Does each task survive?

The tested policy is a 450M SmolVLA, behavior-cloned on LIBERO-10 and fine-tuned with PPO and stochastic sampling. Seven tasks were jointly trained. All three arms had three seeds and reached 200 outer iterations. The arms changed only the exploration-noise policy:

- fixed σ = 0.25;
- one [[2505.22094|ReinFlow]]-style learned-noise network;
- or the controller.

A task counts as collapsed if its success rate falls below half its own behavior-cloning tare on the same evaluation instrument. Under the primary "pooled" definition, the last five complete canonical scans (150 episodes per task) are averaged before applying that threshold.

Pooled collapsed-task counts were:

- **Controller:** 0/7 for each seed.
- **Fixed noise:** 4/7, 1/7, and 0/7.
- **Learned noise:** 4/7, 6/7, and 5/7.

So at least one task collapsed in two of three fixed seeds and all three learned-noise seeds, but not in any controller seed. One controller seed did cross the threshold on a single scan. The seed-level Fisher test for pooled fixed versus controller outcomes gives p = 0.400. With three seeds per arm, the smallest attainable p is 0.100, so this result is not conventionally significant at 0.05.

Aggregate success tells a separate story. Controller seed 1337 pooled at 0.585, versus 0.316 for fixed seed 1337, against a campaign behavior-cloning tare of 0.648. No arm exceeded its own tare.

Table 1: per-seed pooled success rate with Wilson interval and four collapsed-task counts: Pool, Point, Sust., and Ever. All four counts share the half-of-task-specific-tare threshold but use, respectively, the last-five-scan pool, one scan, two consecutive scans, or any scan. The table also includes lower-fixed-noise ablations, single-seed fp32-master pilots, and a local-instrument controller run. Every row is a single seed.

### 4. What the comparison leaves open

Two alternative explanations remain partly open. The first is that the controller simply explores less. Its logged mean noise is close to fixed noise: 0.2444 versus 0.2500 for seed 1337. However, these are incomplete, non-identical logging windows, and the experiment does not isolate adaptation across states from adaptation over time.

The second is that the controller's policy was frozen. It was not: in one controller run, relative expert-weight movement reached 0.19% of its initial norm by iteration 200.

### 5. Contribution and limits

The main result is that task-level preservation and overall success are different questions. In this matched setting, the controller preserved tasks under the pooled definition without raising aggregate success above behavior cloning. The authors frame preservation as necessary, not sufficient, for lifelong learning. The experiment tests simultaneous multi-task training, not sequential lifelong learning.
