# Following the Human Thread in Social Navigation

> [!summary] Summary
> A two-stage reinforcement learning framework, Social Dynamics Adaptation (SDA), enables robots to infer and adapt to human movements for social navigation, achieving higher finding success (0.91) and following rates (0.39) compared to baseline methods in simulated human-centric environments. This allows robots to operate robustly by distilling complex human trajectory information into an adaptable latent representation without needing real-time privileged human data.

## Problem

- Robots face challenges adapting to real-time human motion and inferring human intentions in dynamic, shared environments for tasks like "find and follow".
- High-performing social navigation methods often depend on "privileged information" (e.g., exact human GPS coordinates) during deployment, which is rarely available in real-world scenarios.
- Existing approaches frequently oversimplify human social dynamics, treating humans as simple obstacles or only focusing on collision avoidance, rather than understanding nuanced interactive behaviors.

## Method

- A two-stage ==Reinforcement Learning== (RL) framework, Social Dynamics Adaptation (SDA), is introduced, leveraging the Habitat 3.0 simulator for human-robot interaction.
- Stage 1 trains a social policy to encode complex human trajectories into a latent representation of "social dynamics" using privileged information.
- Stage 2 trains an "Adapter" module to infer these learned latent social dynamics solely from the robot's egocentric state and action history, enabling deployment without real-time privileged sensors.

## Results

- The SDA-S2 model achieved higher Finding Success (**0.91**), Finding Success Weighted by Path Steps (**0.45**), and Following Rate (**0.39**) compared to Habitat **3.0** baseline (**0.76**, **0.34**, **0.29** respectively) in deployment scenarios without privileged information.
- The approach demonstrated robustness against sensor noise, reduced update frequencies, and even in multi-human environments, consistently outperforming baselines under these varied conditions.
- Ablation studies confirmed that adapting human trajectories was effective, whereas attempting to adapt more explicit privileged information like Humanoid GPS (hGPS) resulted in significant performance degradation in the adaptation stage.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Human trajectory information proves more effectively adaptable from robot's egocentric observations than direct Humanoid GPS (hGPS) for inferring social dynamics during deployment.
> - The learned latent social dynamics space organizes into discernible clusters representing key interaction phases such as "Find," "Seek," "Lost," and "Follow."
> - Encoding human trajectories over a sufficient historical window (e.g., 20 steps/0.8 seconds) is critical for extracting robust social dynamics, with shorter windows leading to significant performance degradation.
