# EA4LLM: A Gradient-Free Approach to Large Language Model Optimization via Evolutionary Algorithms

> [!summary] Summary
> EA4LLM introduces a gradient-free method employing Evolution Strategies for optimizing Large Language Models, successfully demonstrating full-parameter pretraining for models up to 32 billion parameters with stable loss reduction. This approach significantly reduces GPU memory consumption by over 4 times for models with 4 billion parameters or more, compared to traditional gradient-based methods.

## Problem

- Gradient-based optimization methods for Large Language Models (LLMs) require substantial GPU memory and computational power, limiting research accessibility.
- The reliance on differentiability in gradient-based approaches restricts the use of potentially promising non-differentiable neural network architectures.
- The prevailing assumption that gradient-based optimization is indispensable for large-scale neural network training hinders exploration of alternative optimization strategies.

## Method

- ==EA4LLM== proposes a gradient-free approach utilizing ==Evolution Strategies (ES)== for full-parameter pretraining of LLMs.
- The method redefines the maximum-likelihood language modeling objective as an ==ES fitness function==, directly linking model output logits to a per-sample reward.
- Fitness evaluation is performed on random mini-batches (==subsampled evaluation==) to ensure computational feasibility and memory efficiency across large datasets.

## Results

- EA4LLM achieved stable loss reduction from the pretraining stage across all tested LLM sizes, ranging from **0.5B** to **32B** parameters.
- GPU memory usage was reduced by over **4x** for models with **4B** parameters and above, with a 32B model requiring **102.97 GB** using EA4LLM compared to **431.76 GB** with Adam.
- Ablation studies confirmed that increasing the evaluation budget and population size led to more accurate optimization signals and stronger loss reduction for a **1.7B** parameter model.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Evolution Strategies can effectively perform full-parameter pretraining of LLMs across various scales (0.5B to 32B parameters), challenging the necessity of gradient-based optimization.
> - Gradient-free optimization through ES can drastically reduce GPU memory footprint, achieving more than a 4-fold reduction for models of 4 billion parameters and above.
> - The inherent characteristics of ES, such as population size and evaluation budget, directly impact optimization signal accuracy and loss reduction, offering guidance for effective scaling.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of artificial neural networks, particularly large language models (LLMs), has been fundamentally tied to **gradient-based optimization algorithms**. For decades, methods like Stochastic Gradient Descent (SGD), Adam, and more recently, Lion, have been the workhorses for training these complex models. These optimizers leverage the differentiability of neural network operations to compute gradients, which then guide parameter updates towards minimizing a loss function.

However, this dominant paradigm comes with significant limitations, which form the crucial context for EA4LLM. Firstly, gradient-based methods impose **stringent hardware requirements**. Computing and storing gradients, alongside model parameters and optimizer states, demands substantial GPU memory (often 3-8 times the model parameter size) and computational power (2-3 times the cost of a forward pass). This high barrier to entry restricts participation in cutting-edge LLM research to well-funded institutions with access to massive computational clusters. Secondly, the reliance on differentiability **excludes many promising non-differentiable architectures**. For instance, certain efficient sparse attention mechanisms that might use non-differentiable operations like FAISS for approximate nearest neighbor hashing cannot be directly integrated into gradient-based training pipelines.

In response to these limitations, researchers have explored **zeroth-order optimization methods**, which do not require explicit gradient calculations. Evolutionary Algorithms (EAs), a broad family of such methods inspired by natural selection, have shown promise. Early explorations by Igel (2003) and Salimans et al. (2017) demonstrated the utility of Evolution Strategies (ES), a type of EA, as an alternative to Markov Decision Process (MDP)-based methods for optimizing neural networks in reinforcement learning (RL) contexts.

More recently, EAs have gained traction for optimizing specific aspects of large models, such as prompt engineering (e.g., Sun et al., 2022a;b; Zhao et al., 2023; Guo et al., 2023) or fine-tuning small parameter spaces (e.g., Jin et al., 2024; Carmona et al., 2024; Huang et al., 2025). A particularly relevant predecessor is the work by Qiu et al. (2025), which successfully extended ES to *full-parameter fine-tuning* of LLMs within the *reinforcement learning stage*. While groundbreaking, these prior works primarily confine ES to RL settings, where effectiveness can heavily depend on the design of the RL reward function, or to smaller-scale optimization tasks.

**EA4LLM significantly pushes the boundaries of this research by applying Evolution Strategies for *full-parameter optimization of LLMs starting from the pretraining stage***, a much more challenging and computationally intensive task than fine-tuning or prompt optimization. This work directly tackles the core limitation of gradient-based methods by demonstrating a viable gradient-free alternative for the most resource-intensive phase of LLM development. It differentiates itself from previous ES applications by explicitly reformulating the maximum-likelihood language modeling objective as an ES fitness function, rather than treating a mini-batch loss as a black-box objective or relying on RL-specific reward functions. Furthermore, the paper highlights its distinction from other zeroth-order optimizers like MeZO by its population-based approach, structured objective integration, and demonstrated scalability for large-scale pretraining. In essence, EA4LLM challenges the prevailing assumption that gradient-based optimization is indispensable for training neural networks and opens new avenues for efficient, resource-conscious deep learning research.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind the development of EA4LLM are multifaceted and driven by the limitations inherent in the current gradient-based paradigm for training large language models:

1. **Address Hardware Requirements:** A central motivation is to overcome the stringent hardware demands of gradient-based optimization. By eliminating the need to compute and store gradients and optimizer states, EA4LLM aims to substantially reduce GPU memory and computational overhead, thereby making LLM training more accessible.
2. **Enable Non-Differentiable Architectures:** Gradient-based methods require all neural network operations to be differentiable. This limitation stifles innovation by preventing the use of promising non-differentiable architectures or components (e.g., certain sparse attention mechanisms). EA4LLM seeks to remove this constraint, opening possibilities for novel, perhaps more efficient, model designs.
3. **Empirically Verify Gradient-Free Full-Parameter LLM Optimization:** The core objective is to, for the first time, empirically demonstrate and verify that evolutionary algorithms (specifically Evolution Strategies) can effectively perform *full-parameter optimization of Large Language Models directly from the pretraining stage*. This involves showing stable loss reduction across a significant range of model sizes, from 0.5B to 32B parameters.
4. **Challenge the Gradient-Based Dogma:** The authors explicitly aim to challenge the prevailing assumption that gradient-based optimization is the *only* viable or practical approach for training neural networks, especially at the scale of modern LLMs.
5. **Democratize Deep Learning Research:** By reducing the computational cost and hardware requirements associated with LLM training, a key motivation is to lower the barrier to entry for deep learning research. This would empower research groups with limited computational resources—including those without access to high-concurrency, high-memory GPUs—to participate meaningfully in LLM development and exploration.
6. **Provide Insights into EA for Neural Networks:** Beyond simply demonstrating feasibility, the work also aims to conduct extensive experiments and provide key insights into *how* evolutionary algorithms can effectively optimize neural networks, laying a foundation for future advancements in this area.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

EA4LLM proposes a gradient-free approach to optimizing Large Language Models by leveraging **Evolution Strategies (ES)**, a type of Evolutionary Algorithm. The methodology is carefully designed to adapt ES for the complex task of full-parameter pretraining of LLMs.

**Core Principles of Evolution Strategies:**
ES operates by iteratively perturbing the parameters of a model, evaluating the "fitness" of these perturbed versions, and then updating the original parameters based on the fitness landscape. Crucially, ES does not require gradient information, making it suitable for non-differentiable problems or when gradient computation is impractical.

**EA4LLM's Specific Approach:**

1. **Linking Logits to ES Fitness:** This is a fundamental innovation. Instead of relying on a black-box loss function, EA4LLM directly connects the model's output logits to a well-defined ES fitness function. For causal language modeling, the fitness is based on the next-token predictive quality.
- **Per-Sample Reward (R(x; θ)):** For a given tokenized sequence `x` and model parameters `θ`, the model produces logits `z_t`. These logits are converted to probabilities via softmax. The reward is defined as the mean log-probability of the true next token, effectively a negative log-likelihood over the sequence. A binary mask `m_t` is used to handle padding and truncation.
- **Overall Fitness (F(θ)):** The overall fitness for the ES algorithm is the average of these per-sample rewards across the entire dataset `D`. This directly aligns with the standard maximum-likelihood objective used in language model pretraining.

2. **Evolution Strategies Operator:**
- **Parameter Perturbation:** For each iteration, a population of `N` noise samples `ϵ_j` is drawn from a standard Gaussian distribution (N(0, I)). These noise samples are used to create perturbed versions of the current model parameters: `θ'_j = θ + σϵ_j`, where `σ` is the noise scale.
- **Fitness Evaluation:** The fitness `r_j = F(θ'_j)` is then evaluated for each perturbed parameter set on a given dataset subset.
- **Weight Computation:** The evaluated fitness values `{r_j}` are converted into normalized weights `{w_j}` to guide the parameter update. EA4LLM supports two methods:
 - **Z-score normalization:** `w_j = (r_j - μ_r) / (σ_r + 10⁻⁸)`, where `μ_r` and `σ_r` are the mean and standard deviation of the fitness values, respectively.
 - **Rank-based shaping:** `w_j = 2 * (rank(r_j) / (N-1)) - 1`, which assigns weights based on the rank of the fitness values, ranging from -1 to 1.
- **Parameter Update:** The next direction for parameter update (`ˆg`) is computed by aggregating the noise samples, weighted by their corresponding `w_j`: `ˆg = (1/N) Σ w_j ϵ_j`. The model parameters are then updated as `θ ← θ + α ˆg`, where `α` is the learning rate. An optional NES-style `1/σ` scaling can also be applied.

3. **Subsampled Evaluation:** To make the approach computationally feasible for large datasets, fitness evaluation is performed on a **random mini-batch (subset D_t)** of the dataset at each iteration, rather than the entire dataset. This `subsampled evaluation` significantly reduces wall-clock time and memory usage. The subset indices are synchronized across parallel processes, ensuring all workers evaluate the same subset. This stochastic ES estimator remains unbiased under uniform sampling.

4. **Implementation Details:** The authors highlight that their implementation simplifies standard ES by explicitly disabling certain variance-reduction and control mechanisms like antithetic sampling, grouped/layered operators, and step-size multipliers in their baseline configuration. This suggests a focus on the most direct application of ES to validate its core capability for LLM pretraining. The method employs a constant population size, noise scale, and step size throughout the experiments.

The algorithm, summarized in Algorithm 1, illustrates a straightforward iterative process: sample a data subset, generate perturbed parameters, evaluate their fitness, compute weights, aggregate updates, and update the model. This systematic approach allows EA4LLM to perform zeroth-order optimization for LLMs across various scales.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted with EA4LLM yielded several significant findings, demonstrating the viability and advantages of using evolutionary algorithms for LLM pretraining:

1. **Cross-Scale Training Validation (0.5B–32B):**
- The most critical finding is the successful empirical verification of full-parameter optimization for LLMs ranging from 0.5 billion to 32 billion parameters.
- Across all tested model sizes (0.5B, 1.7B, 4B, 8B, 14B, 32B), EA4LLM consistently achieved **stable loss reduction** from the pretraining stage. This is visually represented in Figure 1, which shows consistent downward trends in loss curves (Min, Mean, and Max for the population) over 1000 iterations for all model scales. This demonstrates that ES can effectively optimize complex, high-dimensional parameter spaces characteristic of LLMs.

2. **Significant GPU Memory Footprint Reduction:**
- EA4LLM exhibited a remarkable advantage in GPU memory usage compared to traditional gradient-based optimization pipelines.
- By avoiding backpropagation and the storage of gradients and optimizer states, ES consistently used **less memory across all model sizes**.
- The relative memory advantage became even more pronounced as model size increased. For models of **4 billion parameters and above, EA4LLM-based pretraining reduced GPU memory usage by more than 4 times** compared to gradient-based methods. For example, a 32B model, which required 431.76 GB with Adam, only needed 102.97 GB with EA4LLM (Figure 2). This finding is crucial for democratizing LLM research.

3. **Ablation Studies on Evaluation Budget and Population Size:**
- Experiments on a 1.7B model explored the impact of key ES hyperparameters: evaluation samples per iteration (evaluation budget) and population size.
- **Evaluation Budget:** Increasing the number of evaluation samples per iteration (e.g., from 1000 to 7000) led to **more accurate optimization signals and more pronounced loss reduction** (Figure 3). This aligns with the expectation that a larger sample provides a more reliable estimate of the fitness landscape.
- **Population Size:** Similarly, increasing the population size (e.g., from 20 to 40) also resulted in **more accurate optimization signals and stronger loss reduction** (Figure 4). A larger population explores the parameter space more broadly and can provide a better estimate of the update direction.
- These ablation studies confirmed the expected behavior of evolutionary algorithms and guided the selection of optimal hyperparameters (evaluation budget of 5000 and population size of 30) for practical efficiency in other experiments.

In summary, the main findings robustly validate EA4LLM as a viable and highly memory-efficient gradient-free alternative for pretraining large language models, challenging the long-held assumption of gradient-based optimization's sole applicability.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of EA4LLM carries profound significance and potential impact across several dimensions of deep learning research and development:

1. **Paradigm Shift in Neural Network Optimization:** The most significant impact of EA4LLM is its direct challenge to the entrenched belief that gradient-based optimization is the only practical method for training large-scale neural networks. By empirically demonstrating that Evolution Strategies can effectively perform full-parameter pretraining for LLMs up to 32 billion parameters, EA4LLM opens the door for a fundamental rethinking of optimization strategies in deep learning. This could initiate a shift towards exploring and integrating more zeroth-order methods where gradient information is difficult or impossible to obtain.

2. **Democratization of LLM Research and Development:** The substantial reduction in GPU memory footprint is a game-changer. For models of 4B parameters and above, EA4LLM reduces GPU memory usage by over 4x. This dramatically lowers the hardware barrier to entry for LLM research. Smaller research groups, academic institutions, and even individual researchers with limited access to high-end, high-concurrency GPUs can now realistically participate in training and experimenting with significant LLMs. This democratizes the field, fostering greater diversity in research perspectives and potentially accelerating innovation from a broader base of contributors. It even suggests the possibility of conducting meaningful deep learning research without traditional high-memory GPUs, paving the way for more distributed or heterogeneous computing environments.

3. **Enabling Novel Neural Network Architectures:** Gradient-based optimization's requirement for differentiability has implicitly constrained neural network design. EA4LLM's gradient-free nature eliminates this constraint, unlocking the potential for new, non-differentiable architectural components and entire models. This could lead to breakthroughs in areas like efficient sparse attention mechanisms (e.g., those leveraging FAISS), novel memory systems, or even architectures that integrate symbolic reasoning or discrete components more seamlessly. The ability to optimize such models could yield new levels of performance, efficiency, or interpretability.

4. **Robustness to Non-Convexity and Local Minima:** Evolutionary algorithms are generally known for their ability to escape local minima and explore highly non-convex landscapes more effectively than gradient-based methods, which can get stuck. While not explicitly highlighted as a primary finding, this inherent characteristic of ES could lead to more robustly trained models or better generalization, especially in complex LLM landscapes.

5. **Foundation for Future Hybrid Approaches:** While EA4LLM demonstrates a purely gradient-free approach, its success lays the groundwork for future hybrid optimization strategies. Combining the strengths of ES (global exploration, robustness to non-differentiability, lower memory) with the efficiency of gradient-based methods (fast local convergence) could lead to even more powerful and versatile training paradigms. The authors themselves point to exploring hybrid pipelines as future work.

6. **Insights into Evolutionary Algorithm Application:** The work provides valuable empirical insights into the practical application of ES for large-scale, high-dimensional optimization problems. The ablation studies regarding evaluation budget and population size contribute to a deeper understanding of how to configure and scale EAs effectively for deep learning tasks, guiding future research in this intersection.

In conclusion, EA4LLM stands as a landmark achievement, not merely as an alternative optimization method but as a potential catalyst for a broader shift in how we approach the development of artificial intelligence. Its memory efficiency and freedom from differentiability constraints hold the promise of a more inclusive, innovative, and flexible future for LLM research and beyond.
