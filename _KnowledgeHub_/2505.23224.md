# MMBoundary: Advancing MLLM Knowledge Boundary Awareness through Reasoning Step Confidence Calibration

> [!summary] Summary
> MMBoundary introduces a two-stage reinforced fine-tuning framework for multimodal large language models to express fine-grained natural language confidence for each reasoning step. The approach reduces confidence calibration errors by up to 7.5% and improves task accuracy by up to 8.3% across various domains.

## Problem

- Multimodal large language models (MLLMs) frequently generate unreliable responses and hallucinations, often due to errors propagating through multi-step reasoning.
- Existing confidence estimation methods for MLLMs primarily provide an overall response confidence, failing to pinpoint uncertainty at individual reasoning steps.
- A lack of fine-grained confidence awareness makes it difficult for MLLMs to self-correct and for users to identify specific problematic parts of a generated explanation.

## Method

- A ==two-stage reinforced fine-tuning framework== is proposed, consisting of a Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) warm-up stage and a Reinforcement Learning (RL) stage.
- The SFT stage teaches the MLLM to express confidence statements for each sentence using a combined ==Internal Confidence Estimation (ICE)== based on textual and cross-modal uncertainty signals.
- The RL stage refines and calibrates these confidence expressions using PPO with three novel reward functions: ==Knowledge Accuracy, Expected Calibration, and Confidence Self-Calibration==.

## Results

- MMBoundary achieved an average **7.5%** reduction in Multigranularity Expected Calibration Error (MECE) on in-distribution datasets and **6.6%** on out-of-distribution data.
- Task performance improved by up to **8.3%** in overall accuracy and Reasoning Chain F1 scores, with human evaluations confirming enhanced faithfulness, conciseness, and granularity of confidence statements.
- Ablation studies demonstrated the critical role of the RL stage (**9.2%** MECE improvement) and the synergistic contributions of all three reward functions and diverse internal uncertainty estimation methods.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Fine-grained, sentence-level confidence calibration is essential for improving the trustworthiness and robustness of MLLMs in multi-step reasoning tasks.
> - A robust internal confidence estimation can be achieved by combining diverse uncertainty signals, including length-normalized log probability, mean token entropy, TokenSAR, and CLIPScore.
> - Reinforcement learning, guided by rewards for knowledge accuracy, expected calibration, and consistency between internal and expressed confidence, effectively teaches MLLMs to articulate their knowledge boundaries.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The rapid advancements in large language models (LLMs) and multimodal large language models (MLLMs) have revolutionized many AI applications. However, a significant and persistent challenge in this landscape is the issue of **hallucinations**—where models generate factually incorrect or ungrounded information—and their inability to express **calibrated confidence** in their outputs. This problem is exacerbated in MLLMs, where errors can originate from either the visual perception layer or the cross-modal reasoning layer and then propagate through the entire reasoning chain, leading to "hallucination snowballing."

Prior research on uncertainty estimation and confidence calibration for LLMs and MLLMs has primarily focused on the *overall response*. Methods like those relying on self-consistency (Fomicheva et al., 2020; Kuhn et al., 2023), information-based metrics (Malinin and Gales, 2020; Kadavath et al., 2022), or even supervised fine-tuning and reinforcement learning (SaySelf by Xu et al., 2024; R-tuning by Zhang et al., 2024a) typically assign a single confidence score or statement to the entire generated output. While these approaches offer some degree of trustworthiness, they fall short in scenarios requiring complex, multi-step reasoning. If a model provides a multi-sentence answer with an overall high confidence score, but one critical intermediate step is incorrect, this can still be highly misleading.

This paper directly addresses this crucial gap by focusing on **fine-grained, reasoning step confidence calibration** for MLLMs. It acknowledges that effective multimodal reasoning requires assessing confidence at both multi-level (perception, reasoning) and multi-granular (each sentence in a reasoning chain) stages. By enabling MLLMs to express natural language confidence statements for *each sentence* within a generated response, the work pushes the boundaries of explainable and robust AI. This granular approach moves beyond simply flagging an entire response as uncertain; it allows users (or subsequent AI agents) to pinpoint exactly *which part* of the reasoning chain the model is unsure about, thereby enabling potential self-correction or external intervention.

MMBoundary integrates techniques from several active research areas:
- **Uncertainty Quantification:** It leverages multiple textual uncertainty metrics (length-normalized log probability, mean token entropy, TokenSAR) and a cross-modal relevance score (CLIPScore) to form a robust internal confidence signal, moving beyond simple self-consistency methods that can be computationally expensive or less precise for fine-grained analysis.
- **Reinforcement Learning with Human Feedback (RLHF) / Self-Alignment:** The use of multiple reward functions in the RL stage, which incorporate knowledge accuracy, expected calibration, and self-consistency between internal and expressed confidence, aligns with the broader trend of using RL to fine-tune large models for specific behaviors and improved alignment.
- **Reasoning Chain Analysis:** The detailed annotation of reasoning chains, including perception and reasoning levels, reflects a growing interest in understanding and improving the internal thought processes of LLMs, rather than just their final outputs.

In summary, MMBoundary significantly contributes to the evolving landscape of reliable and explainable AI by introducing a novel, granular approach to MLLM confidence calibration, which is vital for complex reasoning tasks and for building more trustworthy AI systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to **advance the knowledge boundary awareness of multimodal large language models (MLLMs) through reasoning step confidence calibration.** This overarching goal is broken down into several specific objectives and is driven by critical motivations stemming from current MLLM limitations.

#### 2.1. Key Objectives

1. **Enable Fine-Grained Confidence Expression:** To train MLLMs to express natural language confidence statements for *each sentence* or reasoning step within their generated responses, rather than merely assigning an overall confidence score to the entire output.
2. **Enhance Reasoning Chain Self-Correction:** To equip MLLMs with the ability to identify and self-correct low-confidence or potentially erroneous steps within their reasoning chains during inference, thereby preventing the propagation and amplification of knowledge errors.
3. **Integrate Complementary Confidence Signals:** To develop a robust method for internal confidence estimation that combines both textual uncertainty signals (from the model's internal states) and cross-modal consistency signals (between generated text and the input image).
4. **Calibrate Model Confidence and Align Knowledge:** To utilize a two-stage training approach (supervised fine-tuning followed by reinforcement learning) that not only teaches the model to express confidence but also actively calibrates these expressions to reflect actual knowledge accuracy and align the model's internal beliefs with its external statements.

#### 2.2. Motivation

**Motivation:**
The motivation for MMBoundary is rooted in the inherent challenges and observed shortcomings of current MLLMs:
- **Unreliable Responses and Hallucinations:** Despite their advanced capabilities in cross-modal reasoning, MLLMs frequently generate responses that are unreliable or contain factual errors (hallucinations). These errors can arise from misinterpretations at the visual perception level or flaws in the subsequent reasoning process.
- **Propagation of Errors ("Hallucination Snowballing"):** A critical problem is that an initial error, particularly at an early stage of reasoning (e.g., visual perception), can cascade and contaminate subsequent reasoning steps. Since models typically lack the ability to express uncertainty about intermediate steps, these errors are amplified, leading to confidently presented but incorrect final answers.
- **Insufficient Existing Confidence Estimation:** Prior methods for estimating model confidence often focus on the overall response. This macro-level confidence is inadequate for multi-step reasoning tasks, as a logically coherent but factually incorrect reasoning chain can still yield high overall confidence, obscuring underlying errors. The example in Figure 1 vividly illustrates this, where misidentifying "drum" as "shield" leads to an incorrect answer, yet the overall confidence remains high.
- **Mimicking Human Cognition:** Humans naturally express varying levels of certainty when reasoning, especially for complex problems or when faced with ambiguous information. Equipping MLLMs with similar fine-grained uncertainty expression can make them more transparent, trustworthy, and easier for human users to interact with and debug.
- **Need for Actionable Feedback:** Granular confidence statements provide actionable feedback. If a model indicates uncertainty about a specific sentence, it creates an opportunity for self-correction, external prompting for clarification, or human intervention, leading to more robust and accurate outcomes.

By addressing these motivations, MMBoundary aims to make MLLMs not only more capable but also more accountable and transparent in their reasoning processes.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

MMBoundary proposes a novel two-stage reinforced fine-tuning framework to advance MLLM knowledge boundary awareness through reasoning step confidence calibration. The framework consists of a **Confidence Expression Warm-Up Stage** (Supervised Fine-Tuning) and a **Reinforcement Learning Stage**.

#### 3.1. Confidence Expression Warm-Up Stage

This initial stage aims to teach the MLLM to naturally generate confidence statements alongside each sentence of its response.

#### 3.2. Internal Confidence Estimation (ICE)

The core of this stage is to estimate the model's internal confidence for each generated sentence by combining multiple uncertainty signals:
- **Text-based Uncertainty Methods:**
 - **Length-normalized log probability (U_LNLP):** Measures the average negative log probability of generated tokens, indicating how likely the sequence is according to the model. Lower values suggest higher confidence.
 - **Mean token entropy (U_MTE):** Calculates the average entropy of token distributions, reflecting the model's certainty about each token choice. Lower entropy indicates higher confidence.
 - **TokenSAR (U_TokenSAR):** A weighted average of negative log probabilities, emphasizing tokens relevant to the entire generated text using a sentence similarity function.
- **Cross-modal Uncertainty Method:**
 - **CLIPScore (U_CLIPScore):** Evaluates the relevance between the generated sentence and the input image using CLIP embeddings. A higher score suggests better alignment and thus higher confidence in the visual grounding of the statement.
These four uncertainty scores are normalized to and then combined using a weighted average (`U_Final`). A mapping from `U_Final` to five discrete confidence levels (1-5, representing uncertain to fully confident) is established based on the distribution of `U_Final` across the dataset (using mean and standard deviation).

#### 3.3. Confidence Score-Statement Mapping

A crucial component is the creation of a pool of diverse, natural language confidence statements for each of the five predefined confidence levels (e.g., "with total certainty," "but I can't be entirely sure"). During data construction for fine-tuning, statements are randomly selected from the pool corresponding to the detected confidence level and inserted directly after the associated sentence in the model's original response. This trains the model to generate human-like confidence expressions. For the RL stage, a reverse mapping (statement to numerical score) is implemented using an encoder model to compute cosine similarity with embedding pools.

#### 3.4. Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT)

The vanilla MLLM (LLaVA-NEXT 7B or Qwen2VL 7B) is fine-tuned on this constructed dataset. The loss function encourages the model to generate both the correct sentences and their associated confidence statements, warm-starting its ability to express fine-grained uncertainty.

#### 3.5. Reinforcement Learning (RL) Stage

The SFT stage provides an initial capability, but the RL stage further refines and calibrates the expressed confidence, aligning it with both the model's internal beliefs and factual accuracy. Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) is employed.
- **Reasoning Chain Annotation:** A key enabler for this stage is the *reference reasoning chain*. For each training sample, GPT-4o is used to generate a detailed, multi-level (perception and reasoning) reference chain, which is then refined and quality-controlled. Each sentence in this chain represents a distinct piece of knowledge.

- **Reward Functions:** Three distinct reward functions are designed to guide the RL process:
 1. **Knowledge Accuracy Reward (R_KA):** This reward assesses the factual correctness of each generated sentence by comparing its knowledge content against the corresponding reference reasoning chain. If a generated sentence matches a piece of knowledge in the reference, it receives a positive reward (1). This ensures the model learns to generate accurate information.
 2. **Expected Calibration Reward (R_EC):** This reward measures the correlation between the model's *expressed confidence* (`EV(c_t)`, converted from the generated confidence statement) and the *ground-truth correctness* of the sentence (`I(z_t)`). It penalizes miscalibration, encouraging the model to express high confidence for correct statements and low confidence for incorrect ones, mirroring the Expected Calibration Error (ECE) concept at a granular level.
 3. **Confidence Self-Calibration Reward (R_CS):** This reward promotes consistency between the model's *internally estimated confidence* (`IV(z_t)`, derived from the ICE module in Stage 1) and its *externally expressed confidence* (`EV(c_t)`). It encourages the model to faithfully articulate its internal belief states, reducing discrepancies between what it "feels" and what it "says."
These three rewards are combined into an overall reward (`R = αR_KA + βR_EC + γR_CS`) with learnable weights. The PPO algorithm then optimizes the model's policy to maximize this combined reward, thereby simultaneously improving knowledge accuracy and confidence calibration at each reasoning step.

**Backbone Model:** LLaVA-NEXT 7B is used as the backbone MLLM, with experiments also conducted on Qwen2VL 7B to demonstrate generalization.
**Datasets:** A-OKVQA (general), ScienceVQA (scientific), and CulturalVQA (cultural) are used to evaluate robustness across diverse domains, including out-of-distribution scenarios.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The empirical evaluation of MMBoundary demonstrates significant improvements across various metrics, showcasing its effectiveness in advancing MLLM knowledge boundary awareness and reasoning capabilities.

- **Superior Confidence Calibration Performance:**
 - MMBoundary significantly reduces confidence calibration errors. It achieves an average **7.5% reduction** in Multigranularity Expected Calibration Error (MECE) on in-distribution datasets (A-OKVQA and ScienceVQA) and a **6.6% reduction** on the out-of-distribution CulturalVQA dataset, highlighting its generalizability.
 - Compared to other strong baselines (e.g., DRL, RCE, SaySelf), MMBoundary consistently performs better, indicating its superior ability to calibrate confidence at the granular, step-by-step level.
 - The AUROC scores (Table 8 in Appendix) further confirm that MMBoundary is more effective at distinguishing between correct and incorrect answers based on its expressed confidence levels.

- **Enhanced Task Performance:**
 - Beyond calibration, MMBoundary also leads to tangible improvements in core task performance. It achieves an **up to 8.3% improvement** in overall task accuracy (Acc) and Reasoning Chain F1 scores, particularly notable on the challenging CulturalVQA dataset.
 - The improved F1 scores indicate that the model not only generates more accurate final answers but also produces higher-quality, more reliable underlying reasoning chains. This suggests that better calibration facilitates better reasoning.

- **Human Evaluation Validation:**
 - Human evaluation, conducted by graduate students, confirms the subjective quality of MMBoundary's outputs. The framework demonstrates statistically significant improvements across dimensions of **faithfulness** (accurately expressing confidence), **conciseness**, and **granularity** (providing step-level confidence) compared to baselines. This reinforces that the natural language confidence statements are intuitive and useful.

- **Effectiveness of Framework Components (Ablation Studies):**
 - **Importance of Reinforcement Learning:** The ablation studies reveal that the RL stage is crucial, providing an average of **9.2% improvement** in the MECE score compared to only using supervised fine-tuning. This confirms that SFT is important for warm-up, but RL is essential for precise calibration.
 - **Contribution of Individual Reward Functions:** Each of the three reward functions (Knowledge Accuracy Reward `R_KA`, Expected Calibration Reward `R_EC`, Confidence Self-Calibration Reward `R_CS`) plays a distinct and important role. Removing any one of them leads to performance drops, indicating their synergistic contribution to comprehensive calibration and knowledge alignment.
 - **Impact of Uncertainty Estimation Methods:** All four chosen internal uncertainty estimation methods (LNLP, MTE, TokenSAR, CLIPScore) contribute positively to the model's performance. TokenSAR is highlighted as having the most significant impact on confidence calibration.
 - **Value of Score-Statement Mapping:** The mutual mapping between numerical confidence scores and natural language statements (`S-S Mapping`) positively impacts confidence calibration, resulting in an average improvement of 4.8%.

- **Efficiency of Internal Confidence Estimation (ICE):**
 - A comparative study with self-consistency-based confidence estimation (SCE) shows that MMBoundary's ICE method yields a small average confidence estimation bias (e.g., 0.0578 on ScienceVQA). This suggests that ICE is highly effective without requiring multiple samples, making it more efficient than some black-box methods.

- **Granular Impact across Reasoning Levels:**
 - MMBoundary significantly improves confidence calibration at the **perception level** (20.4% increase). This foundational improvement cascades, leading to a substantial **38.5% improvement** in the accuracy of the overall reasoning chain.
 - At the **cross-modal reasoning level**, the benefits from strengthened knowledge boundary awareness at the visual understanding stage further boost both confidence calibration (19.7% better than baselines) and reasoning chain F1 scores (27.4% better). This highlights the cascading positive effect of granular calibration from early stages of reasoning.

- **Generalization across Models:**
 - Experiments on Qwen2VL 7B (Table 6 in Appendix) demonstrate that MMBoundary's approach generalizes well to different base MLLMs, maintaining its superior performance in confidence calibration and task metrics.

In summary, MMBoundary presents a robust and effective framework that not only enables MLLMs to express fine-grained uncertainty but also significantly improves their factual accuracy and overall reasoning performance across diverse domains.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The MMBoundary framework represents a substantial leap forward in addressing a critical bottleneck in the reliability and trustworthiness of multimodal large language models: the inability to accurately assess and express confidence at a granular level during complex reasoning. Its significance and potential impact can be elaborated as follows:

- **Enhanced MLLM Trustworthiness and Reliability:** By enabling MLLMs to express fine-grained uncertainty for each reasoning step, MMBoundary makes their outputs far more transparent and interpretable. Users can immediately identify *which specific piece of information* the model is unsure about, rather than receiving a confidently stated but potentially erroneous overall response. This fosters greater trust in MLLM-generated content, crucial for their adoption in high-stakes applications.

- **Mitigation of Hallucination Snowballing:** The core innovation of step-level confidence calibration directly tackles the problem of error propagation. When a model can identify and express uncertainty about an initial perceptual error or a faulty reasoning step, it creates an opportunity for self-correction or external intervention, preventing minor inaccuracies from escalating into significant hallucinations further down the reasoning chain. This leads to more robust and factually accurate outputs.

- **Improved Reasoning Capabilities and Self-Correction:** The framework's ability to align internal confidence with external expression, coupled with rewards for knowledge accuracy, encourages MLLMs to refine their internal reasoning processes. This inherent mechanism for self-correction during inference allows models to become more capable and independent problem-solvers, potentially reducing the need for extensive human supervision to spot and correct errors. The significant improvements in Reasoning Chain F1 scores underscore this impact.

- **Advancement in Explainable AI (XAI):** MMBoundary contributes directly to the field of Explainable AI by making MLLM reasoning processes more transparent. The natural language confidence statements serve as a form of self-explanation, providing insights into the model's certainty about different factual claims and inferential leaps. This interpretability is vital for developers to debug models, for users to understand decisions, and for regulatory bodies to assess AI safety and fairness.

- **Broad Applicability Across Diverse Domains:** The demonstrated effectiveness across general, scientific, and cultural domains, including out-of-distribution scenarios, indicates that MMBoundary is a generally applicable solution. This suggests that the framework can be deployed to enhance the reliability of MLLMs in a wide array of real-world applications, from scientific research assistance and medical diagnostics to cultural heritage interpretation and everyday visual question answering.

- **Foundation for Future Research:** The work lays a strong foundation for future advancements in MLLM development. The authors themselves hint at future work in multimodal self-correction mechanisms and synthetic data pre-training, both of which can further amplify the benefits of granular confidence awareness. This framework provides a robust baseline for integrating uncertainty into even more complex and adaptive MLLM architectures.

In essence, MMBoundary moves MLLMs closer to exhibiting human-like metacognitive abilities, allowing them to not only process and reason about multimodal information but also to understand and articulate the boundaries of their own knowledge. This is a critical step towards building more intelligent, reliable, and trustworthy artificial intelligence systems.
