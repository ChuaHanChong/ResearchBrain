# Safety in Embodied AI: A Survey of Risks, Attacks, and Defenses

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Fudan University and City University of Hong Kong, among others, conducted a comprehensive survey on safety in Embodied AI, proposing a multi-level taxonomy of risks, attacks, and defenses across the perception, cognition, planning, action, and agentic system layers. The work synthesizes over 500 papers, identifying unique challenges and critical research gaps specific to physical-world interactions.

## Problem

- Embodied AI systems deployed in safety-critical physical environments (e.g., autonomous driving, robotics) face unique risks where failures can cause direct physical harm, unlike purely digital AI.
- The existing AI safety research landscape is fragmented, lacking a comprehensive, multi-level taxonomy that addresses the tightly coupled perception, cognition, planning, and interaction within physical constraints.
- Increased autonomy and integration of advanced AI models (like LLMs) into embodied systems lead to an expanded attack surface and novel risks (e.g., jailbreak attacks on planning, tool misuse) that were not systematically cataloged.

## Method

- A multi-level taxonomy is proposed, structuring safety research across five principal components of embodied AI: Perception, Cognition, Planning, Action & Interaction, and Agentic System.
- Insights from over 500 research papers, encompassing both embodied-specific and relevant traditional AI safety literature, are systematically reviewed and synthesized.
- The "capability–risk duality" principle is introduced to explain how increasing agent capabilities at each layer also expands and complicates the attack surface.

## Results

- A comprehensive mapping details specific adversarial, backdoor, and sensor attacks across all five layers of the embodied AI pipeline, illustrating vulnerabilities from physical perception manipulation to jailbreak attacks on LLM-based planning.
- Emerging risks in agentic systems are identified, including the misuse of tools/skills, memory poisoning, data leakage, and potential misalignment or unintended capability expansion in self-evolving agents.
- The analysis highlights how attacks on one layer can cascade, propagating downstream to cause system-wide unsafe physical actions, alongside broader supply chain and infrastructure-dependent failures.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The "capability–risk duality" principle demonstrates that advancements in an embodied agent's functional capabilities at each pipeline layer proportionally expand its attack surface.
> - Embodied AI faces unique safety challenges, such as the fragility of multimodal perception fusion and the instability of planning under jailbreak attacks, which differ from purely digital AI systems.
> - A significant gap exists in current research, with many identified attack types, particularly backdoor attacks on auditory/spatial perception and multi-agent threats, lacking dedicated or comprehensive defense strategies.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Embodied Artificial Intelligence (Embodied AI) focuses on developing autonomous agents capable of perception, cognition, planning, and interaction within physical environments. These systems are increasingly deployed in safety-critical domains such as autonomous driving, collaborative robotics, smart healthcare, and assistive robotics. The unique characteristic of Embodied AI, in contrast to purely digital AI systems, is its operation under conditions of uncertain sensing, incomplete knowledge, and dynamic human–robot interactions, where failures can lead directly to physical harm.

The broader research landscape of AI safety has seen substantial work, particularly in digital domains like vision foundation models, large language models (LLMs), and multimodal large language models (MLLMs). Existing surveys often categorize attacks and defenses in these digital contexts, but they tend to lack a comprehensive examination of the distinct safety challenges that arise when intelligence is embodied and interacts with the physical world. Some prior surveys on Embodied AI safety have explored vulnerabilities, robustness in specific tasks like navigation, or conceptual safety principles. However, these works typically offer limited coverage of defensive strategies, focus narrowly on particular components (e.g., world models or LLM integration), or do not develop detailed, component-level attack–defense taxonomies across the entire Embodied AI pipeline.

This survey distinguishes itself by providing a systematic and comprehensive review of safety research specifically tailored to Embodied AI. It addresses the unique requirements of physical environments, where perception, cognition, planning, and interaction are tightly coupled. The work integrates insights from traditional AI safety research (vision, language, multimodal foundation models) that have direct implications for embodied systems, alongside embodied-specific findings. It positions itself as a unifying framework, moving beyond fragmented analyses to offer a multi-level taxonomy that spans the entire pipeline of embodied agents, from low-level perception to high-level agentic systems. A central concept introduced is the "capability–risk duality," which posits that as an embodied agent's capabilities expand, its attack surface broadens and complicates commensurately.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to provide a comprehensive and structured review of safety research within the domain of Embodied Artificial Intelligence. This overarching goal is supported by several specific objectives and driven by critical motivations arising from the rapid development and deployment of Embodied AI systems.

The key objectives include:
- **Systematic Organization**: To organize existing research on attacks and defenses in Embodied AI into a coherent, multi-level taxonomy. This taxonomy spans the entire operational pipeline of an embodied agent, covering perception, cognition, planning, action & interaction, and agentic systems.
- **Integrated Review**: To synthesize findings from both embodied-specific research and relevant advancements in traditional AI safety domains, such as vision, language, and multimodal foundation models. This involves reviewing a large body of literature (over 500 papers) to draw connections and identify unique embodied challenges.
- **Identification of Gaps**: To identify and summarize overlooked challenges, critical research gaps, and emerging risks within the field of Embodied AI safety.
- **Future Guidance**: To provide a roadmap for future research, aiming to guide the development of embodied agents that are not only capable and autonomous but also robust, reliable, and trustworthy in real-world environments.

The motivation for undertaking this extensive survey stems from several critical factors:
- **Increasing Autonomy and Deployment**: Embodied AI systems are gaining autonomy and being deployed in increasingly open-world and safety-critical environments (e.g., transportation, healthcare, industrial robotics). This expansion necessitates a thorough understanding of their safety implications.
- **Physical Consequences of Failure**: Unlike purely digital AI systems, failures in embodied agents can directly lead to physical harm, accidents, property damage, and erosion of human trust. The consequences of even minor errors are significantly amplified in physical domains.
- **Expanding Attack Surface**: Rapid advancements in areas like multimodal sensing, world modeling, task planning, and human–robot interaction capabilities also broaden and complicate the potential attack surface. Vulnerabilities that might be manageable in digital contexts become severe risks in physical ones (e.g., adversarial examples in vision causing autonomous vehicles to misinterpret signs).
- **Fragmented Research Landscape**: The existing research on AI safety is often fragmented, with many surveys focusing on digital-only systems or isolated components of Embodied AI. There has been a lack of a unified framework that comprehensively addresses the tightly coupled nature of perception, cognition, planning, and interaction within physical constraints.
- **Emergence of Novel Risks**: The integration of advanced AI models (like LLMs) as core components of embodied systems introduces new types of risks, such as jailbreak attacks on planning or tool misuse in agentic systems, which require dedicated analysis within the embodied context.

By addressing these motivations, the authors aim to provide a foundational resource for building safer and more reliable Embodied AI systems for real-world deployment.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors employed a systematic survey methodology to analyze safety research in Embodied AI. Their approach is characterized by:

- **Multi-level Taxonomy Development**: The core of the methodology involves proposing a multi-level taxonomy to categorize and structure the diverse landscape of vulnerabilities and defenses. This taxonomy is organized across five principal components of Embodied AI systems:
 1. **Perception**: Focused on how agents interpret their environment through various sensory modalities.
 2. **Cognition**: Addressing semantic interpretation, logical inference, and internal world models.
 3. **Planning**: Covering the generation of action sequences at various abstraction levels.
 4. **Action & Interaction**: Pertaining to physical execution, control policies, and human-agent interaction.
 5. **Agentic System**: Encompassing higher-level capabilities such as tool use, memory, and self-evolution.

- **Capability–Risk Duality Principle**: A key organizing principle of the survey is the "capability–risk duality." This principle highlights that each layer of the embodied pipeline represents both an expansion of functional capability and a corresponding introduction of new vulnerabilities. The framework illustrates how vulnerabilities at inner layers can cascade and amplify risks in more autonomous, outer layers (e.g., a perceptual error leading to flawed reasoning, unsafe plans, and dangerous actions).

- **Comprehensive Literature Review**: The survey synthesizes insights from over 500 research papers. This extensive review includes:
 - **Embodied-specific research**: Works directly addressing safety within robotics, autonomous systems, and human-robot interaction.
 - **Traditional AI safety research**: Papers from broader AI safety domains (e.g., computer vision, natural language processing, multimodal foundation models) were selected based on their clear relevance and implications for embodied systems.

- **Detailed Categorization within Each Component**: For each of the five main components, the methodology further breaks down attacks and defenses into specific subcategories:
 - **Perception**: Subdivided by sensory modality (Visual, Auditory, Spatial, Motion, Cross-Modal Perception). Attacks are categorized into adversarial attacks (white-box, black-box, digital, physical), backdoor attacks (training manipulation, data poisoning), and sensor attacks (spoofing, jamming). Defenses are organized into robust training (adversarial training, data augmentation, multimodal fusion, formal safety methods) and robust inference (input moderation, output moderation).
 - **Cognition**: Examines instruction understanding (jailbreak attacks and defenses), world models (hallucination, rule violation, contextual risk mitigation), and reasoning (chain-of-thought hĳacking).
 - **Planning**: Covers task planning (adversarial, jailbreak, backdoor attacks and defenses), trajectory planning (adversarial, jailbreak attacks and defenses), and multi-agent planning (Byzantine faults, goal conflicts, potential defenses).
 - **Action & Interaction**: Addresses robot control (adversarial and backdoor attacks/defenses on policies), human-agent interaction (handover safety, trust manipulation), and multi-agent collaboration (infection, collusion).
 - **Agentic System**: Investigates tool and skill use (creation, manipulation, injection attacks and defenses), memory (poisoning, leakage attacks and defenses), self-evolving agents (misalignment, capability expansion risks and alignment defenses), and cascading risks (cross-layer propagation, supply chain, infrastructure failures, and mitigation).

- **Identification of Overlooked Challenges**: Throughout the review, the authors aimed to identify aspects that have received less attention in current research, such as the fragility of multimodal perception fusion, the instability of planning under jailbreak attacks, and the trustworthiness of human–agent interaction in open-ended scenarios.

This systematic and layered approach allows the survey to provide a detailed, mechanism-oriented understanding of Embodied AI safety, situating it within the broader AI safety ecosystem while emphasizing the unique physical-world considerations.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The survey provides a comprehensive mapping of safety challenges, attacks, and defenses across the entire Embodied AI pipeline. It identifies specific vulnerabilities and emerging risks at each layer, synthesizing insights from a large body of literature.

**Perception Layer Findings:**
- **Adversarial Attacks**: Demonstrated widespread vulnerabilities across visual (object classification, detection, tracking, segmentation), auditory (speech recognition, speaker verification), and spatial (3D object detection, SLAM, NeRFs) modalities. These attacks include both digital perturbations and physical manipulations (e.g., adversarial patches on road signs, acoustic injections, LiDAR spoofing, 3D mesh manipulation).
- **Backdoor Attacks**: Identified methods to embed hidden triggers in visual (e.g., via training manipulation or data poisoning for object detectors, vision-language encoders, and ViTs) and auditory models, which activate malicious behavior upon specific prompts or visual cues. Spatial perception (3D object detectors, LiDAR segmentation) is also vulnerable to data-poisoning backdoors.
- **Sensor Attacks**: Highlighted vulnerabilities in motion perception (GNSS, IMU, ultrasonic, mmWave radar) to spoofing (replaying or generating false signals) and jamming (disrupting signals), leading to navigation failures or loss of situational awareness.
- **Cross-Modal Fragility**: A key overlooked challenge is the fragility of multimodal perception fusion. Attacks can exploit inconsistencies between modalities, corrupt fusion mechanisms, or target the weakest channel to compromise the entire perception pipeline (e.g., temporal misalignment, simultaneous camera-LiDAR fooling).

**Cognition Layer Findings:**
- **Instruction Understanding (Jailbreaks)**: Embodied LLMs are susceptible to jailbreak attacks that manipulate natural language instructions to bypass safety constraints and induce harmful physical actions. Both white-box and black-box attacks exist, with some visual perturbations capable of blinding VLMs to safety instructions.
- **World Model Failures**: World models, crucial for prediction and reasoning, exhibit hallucination (generating nonexistent objects or actions) and rule violations (systematically breaking physical laws or safety constraints). These failures can lead to unsafe decisions based on false beliefs about the environment. Attack methods include tail-aware ranking attacks on imagined trajectories and physical-conditioned adversarial attacks on diffusion world models.
- **Reasoning Hĳacking**: Chain-of-thought (CoT) reasoning, while promoting transparency, can be hĳacked by injecting adversarial steps, corrupting the agent's deliberative process and steering it toward harmful conclusions.

**Planning Layer Findings:**
- **Task Planning Manipulation**: LLM-based task planners are vulnerable to adversarial attacks (e.g., visual perturbations misleading navigation systems), jailbreak attacks (crafting inputs to elicit harmful task decompositions), and backdoor attacks (implanting triggers for malicious plan generation).
- **Trajectory Planning Vulnerabilities**: Trajectory prediction and path planning are susceptible to adversarial perturbations that can induce collisions, amplify prediction errors, or degrade trajectory quality. These include both white-box gradient-based attacks and black-box query-based methods, some of which can cause physical-world planner failures.
- **Multi-Agent Coordination Risks**: Distributed planning introduces risks such as Byzantine faults (misbehaving agents corrupting consensus) and goal conflicts (adversarial agents advancing conflicting objectives), which can lead to swarm-level failures.

**Action and Interaction Layer Findings:**
- **Robot Control Attacks**: RL-based control policies are vulnerable to adversarial attacks (perturbing states, actions, observations, environments) and backdoor attacks (embedding triggers to activate unsafe behaviors). These have been demonstrated across various robotic platforms and policy architectures (MLP, Diffusion Policies, Decision Transformers, VLAs).
- **Human-Agent Interaction Risks**: Ensures that even a competent policy operates safely around people. Issues include:
 - **Handover Safety**: Coordinated grasp planning and force regulation can be compromised, leading to drops or collisions during object transfer.
 - **Trust Manipulation**: Miscalibrated trust (over-trust or under-trust) can lead to safety-critical failures. Agents can also be psychologically manipulated through conversation to deliver harmful responses or embed persistent unsafe tendencies.
- **Multi-Agent Collaboration Threats**: Beyond individual agent control, emergent risks arise from:
 - **Infection Attacks**: A single compromised agent can propagate adversarial behaviors through communication and memory-sharing channels.
 - **Collusion Attacks**: Autonomous agents can deliberately coordinate malicious activities, potentially outperforming centralized systems in executing harmful actions.

**Agentic System Layer Findings (Emerging Risks):**
- **Tool and Skill Misuse**: Agents that use external tools (APIs, code interpreters) or skills (reusable behavioral routines) are vulnerable to malicious tool creation, tool manipulation (tricking agents into harmful tool selection/sequencing), and skill injection/poisoning attacks (embedding malicious payloads in skill artifacts).
- **Memory Attacks**: Agent memory is a durable attack surface. Memory poisoning implants malicious records that cause persistent unsafe behavior, while memory leakage extracts private data from logs and conversation histories, posing privacy risks.
- **Self-Evolving Agents (Misalignment & Capability Expansion)**: Agents that autonomously modify themselves face risks of misalignment (parametric drift, unsafe experience accumulation overriding constraints) and unintended capability expansion (acquiring harmful skills beyond design scope).
- **Cascading Failures**: Attacks on one layer can propagate downstream to cause system-wide unsafe physical actions. This includes cross-layer attack propagation (e.g., perception errors affecting steering outputs), supply chain attacks (pre-trained models or third-party plugins compromised before deployment), and infrastructure failures (cloud dependencies compromising safety).

The survey also identifies a significant lack of dedicated defenses for several types of attacks, particularly for backdoor attacks on auditory and spatial perception, and many multi-agent threats, highlighting these as critical research gaps.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This survey holds substantial significance for the field of Embodied AI and its broader societal implications, with potential impacts across research, development, and policy domains.

- **Unified and Comprehensive Framework**: The primary significance lies in providing the first systematic and comprehensive framework for understanding safety in Embodied AI. By organizing disparate research into a multi-level taxonomy spanning perception, cognition, planning, action, and agentic systems, it offers a unified lens through which to analyze vulnerabilities and defenses. This integration of over 500 papers, including those from traditional AI safety, helps consolidate fragmented knowledge.
- **Identification of Unique Embodied Challenges**: The survey explicitly highlights that Embodied AI safety differs from digital AI safety due to its interaction with the physical world, leading to distinct risks like physical harm, uncertain sensing, and dynamic human-robot interactions. It underscores the "capability–risk duality," which is crucial for a nuanced understanding of risk expansion with increased autonomy.
- **Pinpointing Overlooked Research Gaps**: By systematically mapping the current landscape, the authors identify critical overlooked challenges, such as the fragility of multimodal perception fusion, the instability of planning under jailbreak attacks, and the trustworthiness of human-agent interaction in open-ended scenarios. This diagnosis serves as a valuable guide for future research, directing efforts towards areas requiring urgent attention.
- **Roadmap for Future Development**: The identified open problems and future trends provide a clear roadmap for researchers, engineers, and policymakers. This includes the need for:
 - **Safe Evaluation Methods**: Developing high-fidelity digital twins and formal verification methods to test safety without causing real-world harm.
 - **Generalizable Safety**: Creating cross-embodiment safety principles and interfaces to overcome fragmentation.
 - **Robust HRI Protocols**: Designing comprehensive protocols to ensure safety in diverse human-robot interactions, accounting for social, emotional, and adversarial aspects.
 - **Hardware-Aware Safety**: Integrating safety into the physical design of robots (e.g., soft robotics, mechanical fail-safes) to complement software defenses.
 - **Safety for Generalist Foundation Models**: Developing new safety mechanisms for broadly capable models that adapt to new tasks and environments.
 - **Governance and Standards**: Establishing institutional practices, benchmarks, and regulatory frameworks to ensure responsible deployment.
- **Accelerating Trustworthy Deployment**: By systematically addressing risks and outlining pathways for defense, the survey aims to accelerate the development of Embodied AI systems that are not only capable and autonomous but also genuinely robust, reliable, and trustworthy. This is critical for public acceptance and successful integration of robots into various aspects of human life.
- **Fostering Interdisciplinary Collaboration**: The complexity of Embodied AI safety necessitates collaboration across multiple disciplines, including robotics, control theory, machine learning, human factors, ethics, and law. The survey implicitly encourages such interdisciplinary efforts by demonstrating the interconnectedness of these challenges.

In conclusion, this report is poised to become a foundational reference for advancing safety research in Embodied AI, guiding the community toward building intelligent agents that can operate reliably and safely in dynamic human environments.
