# PvP: Data-Efficient Humanoid Robot Learning with Proprioceptive-Privileged Contrastive Representations

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from HK PolyU, LimX Dynamics, and other institutions introduce PvP, a proprioceptive-privileged contrastive learning framework, to improve data efficiency and robustness in humanoid robot Whole-Body Control. This method leverages the complementary nature of proprioceptive and privileged states to learn compact, task-relevant representations, resulting in accelerated learning and superior policy performance on the LimX Oli humanoid robot for velocity tracking and motion imitation tasks.

## Problem

- Whole-Body Control (WBC) for humanoid robots is inherently complex due to intricate dynamics, underactuation, and strong coupling between motor functions, making robust policy design challenging.
- Reinforcement Learning (RL) applications in humanoid WBC face significant sample inefficiency, stemming from complex dynamics, partial observability, and the need to optimize composite reward structures.
- Existing State Representation Learning (SRL) methods for humanoid robots often yield suboptimal representations due to limitations of reconstruction-based approaches or the inability of single-modality contrastive learning to capture comprehensive task-relevant information.

## Method

- Developed **PvP** (==Proprioceptive-Privileged== contrastive learning), a framework using a modified SimSiam architecture to learn compact, task-relevant latent representations by exploiting the complementary information between proprioceptive and privileged states.
- Introduced SRL4Humanoid, an open-source, modular framework for systematic study and integration of various SRL methods (VAE, SPR, SimSiam, PvP) with an RL backbone (PPO), featuring a decoupled SRL and RL training objective with an interval update mechanism.
- Conducted extensive experiments on the LimX Oli humanoid robot for velocity tracking and motion imitation tasks, comparing PvP against existing SRL baselines and validating its effectiveness in both simulation and real-world deployment.

## Results

- PvP demonstrated accelerated learning and achieved superior final policy performance on both velocity tracking and motion imitation tasks for the LimX Oli humanoid robot compared to vanilla PPO and other SRL-enhanced baselines.
- Policies learned with PvP exhibited improved action smoothness (faster convergence of smoothness penalty) and enhanced control precision (better tracking of waist pitch, feet distance, and joint positions) across diverse tasks.
- Real-world testing on the LimX Oli humanoid robot confirmed the practical effectiveness and transferability of PvP-learned policies, successfully executing complex motion imitation and velocity tracking in physical environments.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Leveraging the intrinsic complementarity between proprioceptive and privileged states through contrastive learning (PvP) effectively guides the learning of superior, task-relevant proprioceptive representations, leading to enhanced data efficiency without explicit data augmentations.
> - Applying SRL loss to the policy encoder, rather than the value encoder, generally results in a more stable learning process and improved performance for humanoid WBC tasks.
> - Optimizing the update intervals and data proportions for SRL loss application can significantly enhance training efficiency and prevent premature convergence to local optima, particularly in complex imitation tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Humanoid robots represent a platform for embodied intelligence, offering versatility and adaptability in human-centered environments due to their human-like morphology. A foundational requirement for these capabilities is Whole-Body Control (WBC), which involves coordinating numerous joints and actuators to produce balanced, agile, and safe behaviors. However, designing effective WBC policies is challenging due to complex humanoid dynamics, underactuation, and strong coupling between different motor functions like locomotion and manipulation. Traditional model-based control methods often lack the flexibility and robustness required for real-time control in non-stationary conditions.

Reinforcement Learning (RL) has emerged as a prominent data-driven paradigm for humanoid WBC, demonstrating success in areas such as motion tracking and multi-gait locomotion. Despite these advances, RL applications in this domain are constrained by sample inefficiency. The intricate dynamics, partial observability inherent in humanoid robots, and the necessity to optimize composite reward structures (combining task performance with real-world reliability like energy efficiency) significantly increase the data requirements for training.

To address this sample inefficiency, existing research has explored simulation acceleration, data augmentation, and State Representation Learning (SRL). SRL offers a method to transform high-dimensional sensory inputs into compact, informative latent representations, filtering out noise and redundancy while preserving task-relevant dynamics. This process can improve sample efficiency, generalization, and overall performance in RL. While pioneering work has demonstrated SRL's potential in humanoid robot learning—using reconstruction-based methods (e.g., elevation-based internal maps) and contrastive learning (e.g., perceptive internal models from height maps) to refine state embeddings—the comprehensive integration of SRL techniques into a unified, end-to-end RL framework for humanoid WBC remains an area requiring further exploration. This paper aims to contribute to this underexplored area by investigating an SRL-empowered approach for humanoid robot learning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the data efficiency and robustness of Whole-Body Control (WBC) for humanoid robots, particularly when leveraging Reinforcement Learning (RL). The authors identify sample inefficiency as a significant challenge in RL-based humanoid control, stemming from the complex dynamics, partial observability, and the need to optimize composite reward structures.

The motivation behind the proposed approach, PvP (Proprioceptive-Privileged contrastive learning), arises from several observations regarding existing State Representation Learning (SRL) methods in this domain:

- **Limitations of Reconstruction-Based SRL:** Many current SRL approaches for humanoid robots rely on reconstruction, attempting to predict privileged information (like root linear velocity) from proprioceptive states. These methods often yield suboptimal representation quality and generalization because they prioritize preserving complete state information, including irrelevant details, rather than focusing on extracting only task-relevant features.
- **Limitations of Single-Modality Contrastive Learning:** While some methods employ contrastive learning to achieve more robust representations, they typically operate on a single state modality. This limits their capacity to capture the full spectrum of task-relevant dynamics and information available, leading to less informed representations.
- **Underutilization of Privileged Information:** Privileged states, available during simulation training (e.g., root pose, link velocities, contact indicators), contain rich information that is not directly available or reliable on a real robot. The authors aim to effectively leverage this information to guide the learning of better proprioceptive representations.
- **Need for Robust and Generalizable Representations:** There is a need for SRL methods that can produce compact and task-relevant latent representations without requiring extensive hand-crafted data augmentations, thereby enabling faster and more stable policy learning across diverse tasks.

Based on these motivations, the paper sets forth three main objectives:

1. **Develop PvP:** Propose a contrastive learning framework that leverages the intrinsic complementarity between proprioceptive and privileged states. This framework aims to enhance proprioceptive representations for policy learning by combining information from both modalities, without relying on manual data augmentations, thereby producing consistent improvements across various tasks.
2. **Introduce SRL4Humanoid:** Create a unified, modular, and open-source framework that provides high-quality implementations of representative SRL methods specifically for humanoid robot learning. This framework is intended to support systematic evaluation, facilitate reproducible research, and accelerate community progress by enabling studies on how different SRL methods and configurations influence the efficiency and performance of humanoid WBC learning.
3. **Conduct Comprehensive Validation:** Validate the PvP approach through extensive experiments on the LimX Oli humanoid robot. This validation involves two representative tasks (velocity tracking and motion imitation) to demonstrate that PvP significantly improves sample efficiency and policy performance compared to existing SRL baselines. The study also aims to provide practical insights into the integration of SRL with RL for humanoid WBC.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology of this research centers on two main components: the proposed PvP framework for state representation learning and the SRL4Humanoid framework for systematic evaluation and integration of SRL with Reinforcement Learning (RL).

#### 3.1. PvP Implementation

PvP (Proprioceptive-Privileged contrastive learning) is designed to learn compact and task-relevant latent representations by exploiting the complementary information from both proprioceptive and privileged states.

1. **State Modalities:**
 - **Proprioceptive State (o):** Consists of robot-specific, hardware-measurable signals such as joint positions, joint velocities, base angular velocity, and gravity estimates.
 - **Privileged State (s):** Includes full simulator state information available during training, such as root pose and velocity, per-link poses and velocities, contact indicators, and environment features. The proprioceptive state is a subset of the privileged state.
2. **Contrastive Learning Mechanism:** PvP utilizes a modified SimSiam architecture for contrastive learning.
 - **Data Pair Generation:** The privileged state `s` is treated as one view. A "pseudo augmentation" of the proprioceptive state, denoted `~s`, is derived from `s` by applying a "zero-masking" operation to the privileged information part of `s`, leaving only the proprioceptive observations. This process creates a data pair (`s`, `~s`).
 - **Network Architecture:** A shared policy encoder `f_θ` processes both `s` and `~s` to produce latent representations `z` and `~z`, respectively. A predictor `h_ψ` then processes `z` and `~z` to produce `p` and `~p`.
 - **Loss Function:** The PvP loss (`L_PvP`) is defined as the sum of two negative cosine similarity losses (`D_ncs`). One term computes similarity between `p` and the stop-gradient of `~z` (sg(`~z`)), while the other computes similarity between `~p` and the stop-gradient of `z` (sg(`z`)). The stop-gradient operation is crucial for preventing representational collapse, a common issue in self-supervised learning without negative samples.
3. **Advantages:** This approach enables the policy to access privileged information indirectly through the learned representation. It leverages intrinsic complementarity between modalities without requiring explicit hand-crafted data augmentations, contributing to its versatility and applicability across various tasks.

#### 3.2. The SRL4Humanoid Framework

SRL4Humanoid is an open-source framework designed for the systematic study and integration of SRL methods with RL for humanoid robot learning.

1. **Architecture:**
 - **RL Backbone:** Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) is chosen as the core RL algorithm.
 - **Network Separation:** The policy network receives the proprioceptive state of the robot, while the value network receives the privileged state of the environment.
 - **Decoupled SRL and RL:** The SRL and RL processes are designed to be fully decoupled. The SRL objective can be applied to either the policy encoder or the value encoder.
2. **Implemented SRL Algorithms:** The framework includes high-quality implementations of three representative SRL algorithms:
 - **VAE (Variational Autoencoders):** A reconstruction-based method.
 - **SPR (Self-Predictive Representations):** A dynamics modeling method.
 - **SimSiam:** A contrastive learning method (used as a baseline and extended by PvP).
3. **Joint Optimization:** The overall training objective combines the RL loss (`L_RL`) and the SRL loss (`L_SRL`) with a weighting coefficient `λ`: `L_Total = L_RL + λ * L_SRL`.
4. **Interval Update Mechanism:** To address issues arising from low-quality or repetitive data in early training stages (which can cause SRL to converge prematurely to local optima), an interval update mechanism is employed. The SRL loss is applied only every `T` time steps, controlled by an indicator function `1(T)`. This aims to stabilize training and improve efficiency.
5. **Workflow:** The overall workflow is summarized in Algorithm 1, detailing the initialization, rollout sampling, advantage estimation, mini-batch sampling, loss computation, and network updates.

#### 3.3. Experimental Setup

1. **Robot Platform and Tasks:** Experiments are conducted on the LimX Oli, a full-size humanoid robot with 31 Degrees of Freedom (DoF). Two tasks are designed:
 - **LimX-Oli-31dof-Velocity:** Requires the robot to track a velocity command on flat terrain.
 - **LimX-Oli-31dof-Mimic:** Requires the robot to imitate 20 pre-recorded human animations.
2. **Algorithmic Baselines:** Vanilla PPO is compared against PPO combined with VAE, SPR, SimSiam, and PvP. All methods share the same network architectures and training steps. Hyperparameter searches were conducted for baselines to ensure fair comparison, including data augmentation operations and loss coefficients.
3. **Evaluation Metrics:**
 - Overall task performance (weighted sum of sub-rewards).
 - Key Performance Indicators (KPIs) for each task (e.g., velocity tracking accuracy, position alignment accuracy).
 - Training efficiency (convergence speed).
 - Effectiveness in real-world deployment (e.g., action smoothness reward).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted on the LimX Oli humanoid robot investigated the performance of the PvP algorithm and SRL4Humanoid framework across various conditions.

#### 4.1. Task Performance Comparison (Q1)

- **Overall Reward (Figure 5):** PvP demonstrated accelerated learning and superior final performance compared to vanilla PPO and other SRL-enhanced baselines on both velocity tracking and motion imitation tasks. For velocity tracking, PvP showed significant acceleration, while other SRLs yielded marginal improvements. In motion imitation, PvP achieved the highest performance, though VAE showed a performance degradation, suggesting that simple reconstruction may not be sufficient.
- **Specific Reward Terms (Figures 6 & 7):**
 - **Action Smoothness (Velocity Tracking, Figure 6):** PvP significantly accelerated the convergence of the action smoothness penalty term, indicating that policies learned with PvP produce smoother, more controlled movements, which is beneficial for real-world deployment reliability.
 - **Tracking Performance (Motion Imitation, Figure 7):** PvP improved critical Key Performance Indicators (KPIs) such as waist pitch orientation, feet distance, and joint position tracking, demonstrating enhanced control precision across diverse tasks.

#### 4.2. Impact of Training Time Proportion (Q2)

- **Update Intervals (Figure 8):** Varying the update interval (how often the SRL loss is applied) showed task-dependent effects. For velocity tracking, the impact was minimal. However, for motion imitation, an update interval of 50 generally proved optimal for all SRL methods. This suggests that judiciously applying SRL loss at intervals can improve performance, prevent premature convergence to local optima, and enhance overall training efficiency by reducing computational overhead.

#### 4.3. Impact of Training Data Proportion (Q3)

- **Data Resampling (Figure 9):** The proportion of training data used for SRL also exhibited task-dependent effects. In the velocity tracking task, different data proportions (10%, 50%, 100%) resulted in similar training curves. In contrast, for the motion imitation task, increasing the data proportion generally led to improved performance, particularly for SimSiam and PvP. This indicates that an appropriate allocation of training data can accelerate learning and boost performance, especially in complex imitation tasks.

#### 4.4. Impact of SRL on Value Encoder (Q4)

- **Policy vs. Value Encoder (Figure 10):** Applying SRL loss to the value encoder resulted in slower convergence and, in the velocity tracking task, led to a collapse in training (indicated by a sharp drop in action smoothness before recovery). This suggests that integrating SRL with the policy encoder generally leads to a more stable learning process and improved performance compared to applying it to the value encoder.

#### 4.5. Computational Efficiency Comparison (Q5)

- **Resource Usage (Section 5.2.5):** The SRL4Humanoid framework allows all experiments to run on a single GPU (RTX4090 with 24GB memory). The SRL module operates entirely on the GPU, incurring minimal computational resource cost while effectively accelerating humanoid WBC tasks.

#### 4.6. Real-world Evaluation (Q6)

- **Sim2Sim and Real-robot Testing (Figure 1, 11):** The learned policies were first evaluated in a Sim2Sim setting using the MuJoCo simulator, demonstrating the robot's ability to execute complex motion imitation and velocity tracking tasks. Subsequent real-robot testing on the LimX Oli humanoid robot confirmed the effectiveness of the approach in physical deployment.

#### 4.7. Ablation Studies (Appendix D)

- **Comparison with Teacher-Student Distillation (TSD) (Figures 13, 14, 15):** PvP consistently outperformed student policies trained with Teacher-Student Distillation (TSD). TSD students showed a significant performance gap compared to their teachers, and vanilla PPO (proprioceptive only) failed to learn in these tasks. This highlights PvP's ability to foster a synergistic relationship between representation learning and policy optimization, avoiding limitations of TSD such as teacher quality constraints and over-regularization.
- **Evaluation on Diverse Tasks (Figures 15, 16):** PvP demonstrated strong performance on additional tasks, including velocity tracking on rough terrain (LimX-Oli-31dof-Velocity-Rough) and on a different robot platform (Unitree-G1-29dof-Velocity), excelling in final performance and learning speed.
- **Impact of λ on PvP (Figure 17):** For the motion imitation task, a weighting coefficient `λ = 0.1` for the SRL loss in PvP yielded the best final performance and sample efficiency, suggesting that a relatively smaller `λ` is beneficial.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a framework that addresses the challenge of data inefficiency in humanoid robot Whole-Body Control (WBC) through Reinforcement Learning (RL), offering several implications for the field.

The introduction of **PvP (Proprioceptive-Privileged contrastive learning)** provides a method for learning compact and task-relevant latent representations. By leveraging the intrinsic complementarity between proprioceptive and privileged states, PvP enhances proprioceptive representations for policy learning without relying on hand-crafted data augmentations. This approach has demonstrated an ability to improve both sample efficiency and final policy performance in complex humanoid WBC tasks. The ability to learn effective representations from readily available state information can reduce the engineering effort associated with data preprocessing and augmentation in robot learning.

The development of **SRL4Humanoid**, a unified, modular, and open-source framework, is designed to support systematic evaluation and integration of State Representation Learning (SRL) methods with RL for humanoid robots. This framework aims to facilitate reproducible research and accelerate progress within the community by providing a standardized platform for implementing and comparing different SRL techniques. Its modular design allows researchers to easily experiment with various SRL algorithms and configurations, potentially leading to faster development of more robust control policies.

The **extensive experimental validation** on the LimX Oli humanoid robot, encompassing velocity tracking and motion imitation tasks, provides empirical evidence of PvP's effectiveness. The results indicate that PvP improves learning efficiency and policy performance compared to several baseline SRL methods. Furthermore, the analysis of specific performance indicators, such as action smoothness, suggests that PvP can also contribute to the development of policies that are more suitable for reliable real-world deployment. The insights gained from studying the impact of training time proportion, training data proportion, and the application of SRL to different encoders (policy vs. value) offer practical guidance for integrating SRL with RL in humanoid WBC.

The successful **real-world evaluation** on a full-size humanoid robot in both simulation and physical deployment underscores the practical applicability of the proposed approach. This demonstrates the potential for SRL-empowered RL to produce control policies that transfer effectively from simulation to the real world, a critical step for deploying humanoid robots in diverse, human-centered environments.

While the work presents advancements, the authors acknowledge limitations, such as the potential for exploring additional SRL techniques and integrating multimodal data (e.g., RGB or depth images). Addressing these limitations in future research could further expand the capabilities of humanoid robots in more complex and dynamic environments. Overall, this research contributes to the advancement of data-efficient and robust control for humanoid robots, which is crucial for their eventual widespread adoption.
