# LP-NavOA: Integrated Local Navigation and Obstacle Avoidance for Humanoid Robots under Limited Perception

> [!summary] Summary
> This research introduces LP-NavOA, an integrated framework for humanoid robots that combines local navigation and obstacle avoidance under limited perception. It enables robots to efficiently avoid obstacles and reliably recover sparse, potentially occluded goals, improving on-time arrival by over 50 percentage points and reducing contact brush rates by more than 60 percentage points in simulated cluttered environments.

## Problem

- Humanoid robot navigation in complex, cluttered environments faces challenges in tightly coupling high-level navigation (obstacle avoidance, sparse goal recovery) with low-level whole-body locomotion (balance, agile maneuvers).
- Traditional navigation pipelines introduce latency unsuitable for agile legged robots and simplify planning abstractions, while purely reactive controllers lack memory for post-avoidance goal recovery.
- Limited short-range and partially observable sensor data make it difficult for humanoids to determine when to continue bypassing an obstacle versus resuming pursuit of a hidden long-term goal.

## Method

- **LP-NavOA** employs a two-stage learning process: first, a perception-action PPO locomotion backbone is trained and frozen; then, a recurrent local planner is distilled via ==behavior cloning== from A-star and waypoint teacher-generated rollouts.
- A planner-free inference interface operates solely on local raycast observations and goal state to steer the pre-trained locomotion policy, using an internal recurrent memory without requiring global maps or external planners.
- Dynamic route shaping, a teacher control mechanism, adaptively blends waypoint guidance with sparse goal-seeking based on local obstacle clearances during distillation, enabling obstacle-conditioned bypass and goal recovery behaviors.

## Results

- LP-NavOA significantly improved T-rel. on-time arrival rates, achieving **91.9%** and **96.6%** in open-wall and indoor simulated layouts respectively, compared to **38.3%** and **40.3%** for the baseline backbone-only policy.
- The framework drastically reduced 'any brush' rates from ~70-**77%** to ~23-**24%** and 'contact brush' rates from ~69-**76%** to ~9-**10%**, demonstrating safer and less contact-heavy navigation.
- Real-robot deployment on a Unitree G1 humanoid confirmed the hardware executability of LP-NavOA, successfully performing bypass-and-recover steering commands in various wall-occlusion scenarios at speeds up to **1.0** m/s.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A robot-centered circular heading-speed command interface is critical for training an agile, high-speed (3.0 m/s) locomotion backbone, proving functionally superior to static world-frame goal sequences.
> - Separating training instruments (sophisticated planners, privileged information) from a compact, planner-free inference interface with internal memory allows efficient distillation of complex navigation behaviors into low-latency policies.
> - Dynamic route shaping is essential for teaching the policy to condition bypassing behavior on observed obstacle geometry and intelligently recover sparse goals, avoiding over-reliance on memorized detours.
