# LP-NavOA: Integrated Local Navigation and Obstacle Avoidance for Humanoid Robots under Limited Perception

> [!summary] Summary
> This research introduces LP-NavOA, an integrated framework for humanoid robots that combines local navigation and obstacle avoidance under limited perception. It enables robots to efficiently avoid obstacles and reliably recover sparse, potentially occluded goals, improving on-time arrival by over 50 percentage points and reducing contact brush rates by more than 60 percentage points in simulated cluttered environments.

## Problem

- Humanoid robot navigation in complex, cluttered environments faces challenges in tightly coupling high-level navigation (obstacle avoidance, sparse goal recovery) with low-level whole-body locomotion (balance, agile maneuvers).
- Traditional navigation pipelines introduce latency unsuitable for agile legged robots and simplify planning abstractions, while purely reactive controllers lack memory for post-avoidance goal recovery.
- Limited short-range and partially observable sensor data make it difficult for humanoids to determine when to continue bypassing an obstacle versus resuming pursuit of a hidden long-term goal.

## Method

- **LP-NavOA** employs a two-stage learning process: first, a perception-action PPO locomotion backbone is trained and frozen; then, a recurrent local planner is distilled via ==behavior cloning== from A-star and waypoint teacher-generated rollouts.
- A planner-free inference interface operates solely on local raycast observations and goal state to steer the pre-trained locomotion policy, using an internal recurrent memory without requiring global maps or external planners.
- Dynamic route shaping, a teacher control mechanism, adaptively blends waypoint guidance with sparse goal-seeking based on local obstacle clearances during distillation, enabling obstacle-conditioned bypass and goal recovery behaviors.

## Results

- LP-NavOA significantly improved T-rel. on-time arrival rates, achieving **91.9%** and **96.6%** in open-wall and indoor simulated layouts respectively, compared to **38.3%** and **40.3%** for the baseline backbone-only policy.
- The framework drastically reduced 'any brush' rates from ~70-**77%** to ~23-**24%** and 'contact brush' rates from ~69-**76%** to ~9-**10%**, demonstrating safer and less contact-heavy navigation.
- Real-robot deployment on a Unitree G1 humanoid confirmed the hardware executability of LP-NavOA, successfully performing bypass-and-recover steering commands in various wall-occlusion scenarios at speeds up to **1.0** m/s.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A robot-centered circular heading-speed command interface is critical for training an agile, high-speed (3.0 m/s) locomotion backbone, proving functionally superior to static world-frame goal sequences.
> - Separating training instruments (sophisticated planners, privileged information) from a compact, planner-free inference interface with internal memory allows efficient distillation of complex navigation behaviors into low-latency policies.
> - Dynamic route shaping is essential for teaching the policy to condition bypassing behavior on observed obstacle geometry and intelligently recover sparse goals, avoiding over-reliance on memorized detours.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Humanoid robot navigation in complex, cluttered environments presents significant challenges, primarily due to the tight coupling between high-level navigation decisions and low-level whole-body locomotion. A robot must maintain balance, ensure stable contact, and execute agile maneuvers while simultaneously deciding how to bypass obstacles and recover its path towards a potentially occluded, sparse goal. This must be accomplished under the constraints of short-range and partially observable sensor data.

Traditional robotic navigation systems often rely on a sequential pipeline of mapping, planning, and control. While these systems offer interpretability, they frequently introduce latency and can be ill-suited for the agile and dynamic command-tracking requirements of legged robots. Simplified planning abstractions in such pipelines may not adequately account for factors like body sway or the physical limits of humanoid command execution.

In parallel, reinforcement learning (RL) has advanced the capabilities of legged robots, enabling robust locomotion skills, including agile motor control, blind navigation over challenging terrains, and perceptive locomotion in unstructured environments. Recent developments in rapid motor adaptation and real-world whole-body control have extended these capabilities to humanoid platforms, often leveraging large-scale imitation learning from human motion. The advent of scalable simulators like Isaac Gym and lightweight systems such as mjlab has further accelerated the training of sophisticated policies. This has led to achievements in collision-free high-speed locomotion, extreme parkour, and various locomotion benchmarks.

However, a critical gap remains in the integration of these robust locomotion skills with goal-directed local navigation, particularly the ability to reliably recover a sparse goal *after* an obstacle has been avoided, rather than merely reacting to immediate threats. Existing reactive controllers may lose sight of the long-term goal when an obstacle temporarily occludes it. This research directly addresses this gap by proposing a framework that synthesizes advanced learned locomotion with memory-based, goal-recovery navigation under limited perceptual input, diverging from both purely classical and purely reactive learned approaches. It aims to provide a more integrated and efficient solution for humanoid local navigation that leverages the strengths of RL while mitigating the limitations of sequential pipelines and purely reactive systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an integrated local navigation and obstacle avoidance framework, named LP-NavOA, for humanoid robots operating in cluttered environments under limited perceptual capabilities. The motivation stems from several key challenges inherent in this domain:

1. **Joint Resolution of Navigation Tasks:** Humanoid navigation necessitates simultaneously addressing obstacle avoidance, the recovery of sparse and potentially occluded goals, and the maintenance of stable whole-body locomotion. These tasks are tightly coupled, and a failure in one can compromise the others.
2. **Constraints of Limited Perception:** Humanoid robots often operate with short-range and partially observable sensing modalities, which can make it difficult to determine when to continue bypassing an obstacle versus when to resume pursuit of a hidden goal.
3. **Limitations of Traditional Architectures:**
 - **Explicit Planner-Control Decompositions:** Classical pipelines, which explicitly separate planning from control, can introduce undesirable latency. This latency can hinder the agility of legged systems and create a mismatch between high-level planning abstractions and the real-time command-tracking limits of the robot's whole-body control. Such decompositions, while interpretable, are considered "awkward" for agile legged systems.
 - **Purely Reactive Controllers:** While effective for immediate obstacle avoidance, purely reactive controllers lack memory and foresight. This can lead to scenarios where a robot successfully avoids an immediate obstacle but then loses its way or cannot efficiently reorient towards its original sparse goal once the obstacle is no longer directly in its path.
4. **Desire for Compact and Efficient Deployment:** The authors aim for a deployment interface that is free of global maps, continuous waypoint streams, or external high-level planners, allowing the robot to operate autonomously with minimal, readily available sensor inputs and an internal recurrent memory. This enhances real-world applicability and reduces computational overhead at runtime.

To address these motivations, LP-NavOA sets out to achieve three specific contributions:
- To design a planner-free navigation interface that relies solely on local range observations and goal state to steer a pre-trained, frozen whole-body locomotion policy via simple heading commands.
- To establish a planner-supervised training methodology that combines a circular heading-speed Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) backbone, teacher-generated waypoint supervision, dynamic route shaping, and teacher-active rollouts. This pipeline aims to effectively distill complex obstacle bypassing and post-avoidance goal recovery behaviors into the deployed policy.
- To validate the proposed system comprehensively in a simulated environment (MuJoCo) using various layouts and diagnostic metrics, and to verify its practical executability on a physical Unitree G1 humanoid robot.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The LP-NavOA framework is structured around a two-stage learning process designed to separate the complexities of generating training supervision from the simplicity and efficiency required for real-time inference.

**A. Framework Overview: Planner-Supervised Training and Planner-Free Inference**
The system is developed for a Unitree G1 humanoid in MuJoCo, aiming for sparse planar goal reaching in unknown static-obstacle layouts using only proprioception, short-range raycasts, and relative goal direction at deployment.

1. **Locomotion Backbone Training:** The first stage involves training a `perception-action PPO locomotion backbone (πθ)`. This policy learns to map proprioceptive sensor data, local raycast observations, and a compact 2D command vector `c_t = [Δψ_t, v*_t]` (representing a desired change in heading and a target forward speed) to low-level normalized joint actions (`a_t`). This training maximizes locomotion rewards, focusing on stability, agility, and accurate command tracking. Once trained, this backbone policy is frozen.

2. **Recurrent Local Planner Distillation:** With the backbone frozen, the second stage focuses on distilling navigation intelligence into a `recurrent local planner (fϕ)`. This is achieved through `behavior cloning`. `A-star` and `waypoint teachers` are used to generate expert rollouts by guiding the frozen backbone through obstacle courses. The data from these rollouts, comprising observations, recurrent states, and teacher-generated heading commands, is used to train the local planner. At deployment, this recurrent planner reads inference-time observations and its internal memory, and outputs a bounded steering command. This output `overwrites only the heading component (Δψ_t)` of the `c_t` command, allowing the original whole-body locomotion policy to remain intact.

**Deployment Interface:** At runtime, LP-NavOA operates with limited perception. It takes proprioception, short-range local range sensing (raycasts), and a body-frame goal direction as inputs. Critically, it does not require a global map, a continuous stream of waypoints, or any external planner. A `command-side safety filter` is integrated, which caps forward speed based on front-ray stopping time and dampens unsafe yaw commands. This filter is applied to the command `c_t` before it is passed to the frozen PPO backbone and is shared across all inference conditions.

**B. Perception-Action PPO Training for the Locomotion Backbone**

- **Observation Space (`o_ll_t`):** The backbone receives a comprehensive set of observations including body angular velocity, gravity vector (in body frame), the command vector `c_t`, joint position offsets and velocities, the previous action, gait/order phase features, and three `8x8` normalized raycast grids (`ρ_f_t`, `ρ_ℓ_t`, `ρ_r_t`) representing front, left, and right views. These raycasts provide obstacle distances up to a 4m limit.
- **Action Space:** The policy outputs normalized joint actions (`a_t`) which are then converted to desired joint positions by a PD controller.
- **Asymmetric Critic:** The critic network, used during training, has access to additional privileged information, specifically a yaw-aligned downward terrain scan, to provide richer state evaluation.
- **Circular Goal Command:** A novel `circular goal command` generation strategy is employed. Instead of static world-frame goals, a target heading `(θ_w,cmd_t)` is sampled around the robot's current yaw `(ψ_t)`. The policy then receives the signed shortest heading error `(Δψ_t)` to this target and a target speed `(v*_t)`. This approach allows for training smooth goal-directed locomotion and ensures the command interface is suitable for later obstacle avoidance curricula.
- **Rewards and Curricula:** The backbone's reward function includes exponential terms for yaw-rate and goal-directed velocity tracking, combined with penalties for orientation deviation, joint limits, action rates, foot slip, and body/foot collisions. Training progresses through a staged curriculum: initially flat circular-goal locomotion, followed by A-star-guided visual generalization that introduces obstacle bypassing via intermediate waypoints, and optionally adapting to rougher terrain.

**C. Teacher-Distilled Local Planner for Goal Recovery**

- **Student Observation Space (`o_lp_t`):** The local planner observes a limited set of inputs: yaw encoding, selected joint velocities (for dead-reckoning) or measured body-frame velocity (for odometry), the same front/left/right raycast grids used by the backbone, and a body-frame offset to the current key goal (distance and bearing).
- **Planner Architecture:** The architecture comprises shared CNN encoders (`E_ρ`) for raycast features, an MLP (`φ`) to fuse these features with the state vector, and a Gated Recurrent Unit (`GRUCell`) for processing sequential observations and maintaining recurrent memory (`h_t`). An output MLP (`W_ψ`) predicts the heading command. An optional head predicts forward velocity for the dead-reckoning variant.
- **Route-Shaping Teacher Control:** During distillation, `planner or waypoint teachers` guide the frozen backbone. The teacher's target heading `(Δψ*_t)` is dynamically blended between the key goal bearing `(Δψ_key_t)` and an intermediate waypoint bearing `(Δψ_wp_t)` using a gate `α_t`. This gate is determined by local clearances from raycasts (forward clearance `d_f_t` and goal-side clearance `d_gs_t`). `α_t` is near zero when obstacles are close, indicating the waypoint should dominate for bypass, and transitions to one when clearances permit direct goal recovery. This `dynamic route shaping` ensures that the student policy learns to condition its bypassing behavior on observed obstacle geometry and intelligently recover towards the sparse goal. The `α_t` gate also dictates the heading command injected into the frozen backbone during data collection, thereby shaping the rollout routes and influencing the student's learned state distribution. The student never directly observes `α_t`, learning implicitly from the varied teacher labels.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research provides a detailed evaluation of LP-NavOA through ablations and benchmarks in simulation, along with a real-robot deployment analysis.

**A. Command-Form Ablation for Fast Backbone Training**
This ablation investigated the effectiveness of the command interface for training a high-speed locomotion primitive.
- **Circular Moving Goal:** The proposed robot-centered circular command enabled the backbone to achieve high-speed tracking (3.0 m/s) with a low velocity RMSE (0.52 ± 0.02 m/s), high rollout completion (99.51 ± 0.15%), and minimal fall rate (0.49 ± 0.15%).
- **Fixed Scattered Sequence:** In contrast, a fixed sequence of world-frame goals resulted in significantly poorer performance, with a trackable speed of only 1.44 ± 0.10 m/s, higher RMSE (1.36 ± 0.03 m/s), and much lower rollout completion (40.07 ± 2.95%).
- **Conclusion:** The circular command interface is essential for training the agile, high-speed (3.0 m/s) locomotion backbone, demonstrating its functional importance beyond mere convenience.

**B. Simulation Evaluation of Obstacle Avoidance and Goal-Directed Navigation**
The formal evaluation compared the performance of the backbone-only policy (R1), LP-NavOA with dead-reckoning (OURS DR), LP-NavOA with odometry (OURS Odom), and a teacher-present reference (T1) on open-wall and indoor layouts. Performance was measured by T-rel. on-time arrival, time overhead, and brush/collision rates.
- **Baseline (R1 Backbone-only):** Exhibited low T-rel. on-time arrival (38.3% for open-wall, 40.3% for indoor) and substantial time overhead (approximately +14 seconds). This policy also showed high rates of 'any brush' (70.9-77.7%) and 'contact brush' (69.7-76.5%), indicating indirect and contact-heavy obstacle handling despite the inherent safety filter and perception-action training.
- **LP-NavOA (OURS DR/Odom):**
 - **On-time Arrival:** Both DR and Odom variants significantly improved T-rel. on-time arrival. For open-wall, DR achieved 85.1%, and Odom reached 91.9%. For indoor layouts, DR achieved 88.0%, and Odom reached 96.6%.
 - **Time Overhead:** Overhead was substantially reduced. For open-wall, DR had +7.5s, Odom +6.1s. For indoor, DR had +7.4s, Odom +5.5s.
 - **Brush/Contact:** Rates of 'any brush' and 'contact brush' were drastically lowered. For open-wall, DR reduced 'any brush' to 24.0% and 'contact brush' to 9.5%; Odom further reduced these to 23.7% and 10.1% respectively. Similar reductions were observed for indoor layouts. Hard collisions remained rare across all methods.
- **Conclusion:** The distilled recurrent local planner within LP-NavOA significantly enhances navigation quality by enabling timely goal arrival and reducing contact-heavy progress, moving beyond simple reactive avoidance to incorporate post-avoidance goal recovery. The odometry-assisted variant generally outperformed the dead-reckoning variant.

**C. Ablations on Route-Shaping Control, Odometry, and Safety Filtering**
This ablation focused on the open straight-wall task to isolate the effects of various components, particularly the route-shaping control.
- **Key-goal-only control:** This variant performed poorly, with only 33.9% (DR) and 32.4% (Odom) T-rel. on-time arrival and high 'any brush' rates exceeding 65%, confirming its inability to learn effective bypass strategies.
- **Waypoint-only control:** While better than key-goal-only, achieving 72.6% (DR) and 89.8% (Odom) on-time arrival, this approach showed a tendency for the student to reproduce memorized detour templates rather than grounding avoidance in current obstacle geometry once teacher waypoints were removed.
- **Dynamic Route Shaping (Full):** This control mechanism demonstrated superior performance, achieving 85.3% (DR) and 92.5% (Odom) on-time arrival with lower brush rates, indicating successful conditioning of bypass behavior on observed clearances and subsequent goal recovery.
- **No Teacher Forcing:** Removing the injection of the teacher's target heading into the backbone during data collection for the DR variant significantly increased 'any brush' (from 23.1% to 53.8%) and the PP-NoPP gap, confirming that route shaping must influence the rollout state distribution for effective distillation.
- **No Safety Filter:** Disabling the command-side safety filter resulted in the best simulated timing and brush rates (87.0% on-time, 15.6% brush for DR), suggesting that the learned local planner itself is capable of strong obstacle avoidance in the simulator.
- **Conclusion:** Dynamic route shaping is a critical component for distilling obstacle-conditioned bypass and goal-recovery behavior. The safety filter, while potentially making simulated motion more conservative, serves as a crucial deployment safeguard for real-world scenarios.

**D. Real-Robot Deployment Analysis**
A deployment feasibility check was conducted on a Unitree G1 humanoid robot.
- **Setup:** The robot used the raycast-conditioned locomotion backbone paired with the odometry-assisted local planner.
- **Tasks:** Representative wall-occlusion layouts were tested, including frontal, oblique, and front-rear two-wall barriers. The target was always placed in front of the robot.
- **Observations:** The robot successfully approached obstacles, selected feasible bypass directions, and recovered towards the target, with commanded speeds from 0.5 to 1.0 m/s and a closest observed clearance of 0.281 m.
- **Conclusion:** The real-robot trials confirmed that LP-NavOA's compact inference interface is executable on hardware and can issue effective bypass-and-recover steering commands without requiring continuous joystick guidance, demonstrating its practical viability.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work contributes to the field of humanoid robotics by presenting a framework that integrates high-quality whole-body locomotion with intelligent, memory-based local navigation and obstacle avoidance under realistic perception constraints. Its significance and potential impact are multifaceted:

1. **Addressing a Critical Gap in Humanoid Navigation:** The paper effectively addresses the challenge of sparse-goal recovery after obstacle avoidance, a problem often overlooked by purely reactive controllers. By enabling the robot to not only avoid obstacles but also to intelligently reorient and proceed towards its long-term goal, LP-NavOA enhances the autonomy and operational efficiency of humanoids in cluttered environments.

2. **Novel Training-Inference Separation:** The core methodological contribution lies in its clean separation of training instruments from the inference interface. Leveraging sophisticated planners and privileged information *only* during the training phase to generate rich supervision, while relying on a compact, planner-free interface with minimal sensor inputs and internal memory during deployment, represents an efficient and robust learning paradigm. This design reduces runtime computational overhead and latency, making the system more suitable for real-world application.

3. **Enhanced Navigation Performance and Robustness:** The results demonstrate a significant improvement in navigation quality. The system achieves substantially higher rates of timely goal arrival and drastically reduces undesirable brush and contact events compared to a baseline policy. This indicates that LP-NavOA enables more agile, safer, and direct navigation paths, minimizing time wasted and potential damage.

4. **Key Methodological Innovations:**
 - The **circular heading-speed command interface** is shown to be crucial for training a high-speed (3.0 m/s) and agile locomotion backbone, laying a strong foundation for the navigation layer.
 - **Dynamic route shaping** is a powerful teacher control mechanism. By adaptively blending waypoint guidance with goal-seeking based on local obstacle clearances, it effectively distills complex, context-dependent behaviors into the recurrent local planner, avoiding the pitfalls of over-reliance on static waypoint templates or direct, obstructed goal paths.

5. **Real-World Feasibility Demonstration:** The successful deployment analysis on a Unitree G1 humanoid robot provides empirical evidence for the hardware executability and practical viability of the proposed compact inference interface. This step is critical for bridging the gap between simulated research and real-world robotic applications.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Advancement in Humanoid Autonomy:** LP-NavOA could serve as a foundational component for developing more autonomous and capable humanoid robots in diverse applications, such as assistance in homes or workplaces, logistics, inspection, or exploration in human-scale environments.
- **Enabling Complex Tasks:** The ability to reliably navigate cluttered spaces and recover sparse goals is fundamental for humanoids to perform more complex manipulation and interaction tasks that require precise positioning and movement.
- **Efficient Robot Learning:** The teacher-supervised distillation approach offers a blueprint for how complex behaviors, often learned with privileged information in simulation, can be efficiently transferred into compact, low-latency policies suitable for real-world deployment. This could inspire similar approaches in other domains of robot learning.
- **Future Research Directions:** The modular nature of LP-NavOA (frozen locomotion backbone + distilled steering layer) provides a flexible platform for future research, including adapting to dynamic obstacles, integrating with higher-level semantic understanding, or scaling to more complex and expansive environments.
