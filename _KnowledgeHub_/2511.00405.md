# UME-R1: Exploring Reasoning-Driven Generative Multimodal Embeddings

> [!summary] Summary
> UME-R1, a framework from Xiamen University and WeChat AI, introduces reasoning-driven generative multimodal embeddings, allowing models to generate explicit reasoning and summaries before producing an embedding. This approach consistently outperforms conventional discriminative embedding models on the MMEB-V2 benchmark, achieving an overall score of 60.1 with UME-R1 (Qwen2-VL-2B) compared to VLM2Vec-V2's 58.0, and showing substantial improvements across image, video, and visual document tasks over its discriminative counterpart.

## Problem

- Existing multimodal embedding models, often built on MLLMs, primarily operate in a discriminative manner, encoding inputs directly without leveraging the MLLMs' generative and reasoning capabilities.
- The lack of explicit intermediate reasoning in current embedding models limits interpretability and the potential for more robust representations, unlike successful Chain-of-Thought (CoT) methods in other MLLM applications.
- Optimizing generative embeddings using reinforcement learning is challenging because embedding tasks typically lack definitive 'best answers' or explicit ground-truth outputs for reward computation.

## Method

- **UME-R1** unifies embedding tasks within a ==generative paradigm==, enabling MLLMs to generate both discriminative and reasoning-driven generative embeddings based on user prompts.
- A two-stage training strategy is employed: Supervised Fine-tuning (SFT) provides initial reasoning capabilities, and Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Reward (RLVR) refines the model to produce more beneficial embeddings.
- A novel reward function for RLVR is designed, combining 'Format Reward' for adherence to structure and 'Embedding Reward' which factors in both ranking performance and similarity gap to evaluate embedding quality without explicit best answers.

## Results

- UME-R1 consistently achieved superior overall performance on the MMEB-V2 benchmark (78 tasks), with UME-R1 (Qwen2-VL-**2B**) scoring 60.1 compared to 58.0 for VLM2Vec-V2 (Qwen2-VL-**2B**) and showing up to 11.1 points improvement over a discriminative baseline (DUME).
- An oracle evaluation, selecting the better of discriminative or generative embeddings per instance, further improved overall scores by 4.3 points for UME-R1-**2B** and 3.6 points for UME-R1-**7B**, confirming their complementarity.
- The RL stage enhanced generative embeddings by 1.0 point overall, and the SFT stage, by incorporating generative objectives, improved discriminative embeddings by 3.0 points, indicating a mutually beneficial training process.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly integrating reasoning and summarization into the embedding generation process significantly enhances multimodal embedding performance across various tasks and modalities.
> - Discriminative and reasoning-driven generative embeddings are complementary, suggesting that dynamically selecting or combining them can yield higher 'oracle' performance.
> - Reinforcement learning, even with a custom reward policy and without 'best answers,' can effectively optimize generative embedding models, leading to substantial performance gains and demonstrating a scalable fine-tuning paradigm.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of multimodal embeddings has experienced advancements driven by the success of multimodal large language models (MLLMs). Existing MLLM-based embedding models, such as VLM2Vec (Jiang et al., 2025) and MM-Embed (Lin et al., 2025a), operate primarily in a discriminative manner. They encode multimodal input and extract a final hidden state to form an embedding without generating new tokens or explicit intermediate reasoning. This contrasts with earlier dual-encoder vision-language models like CLIP (Radford et al., 2021).

Concurrently, large reasoning models (LRMs) like GPT-4o (Hurst et al., 2024) and DeepSeek-R1 (Guo et al., 2025) have demonstrated significant capabilities in complex reasoning, often by incorporating chain-of-thought (CoT) prompting (Wei et al., 2022a) to elicit step-by-step reasoning. These advancements have been extended to MLLMs (Shen et al., 2025b; Hong et al., 2025a), enhancing their performance on various multimodal tasks. However, the benefits of reasoning-driven generation have been limited for multimodal embedding models, as their discriminative nature does not inherently support the generation of intermediate content.

Some prior studies (Ouali et al., 2025; Yu et al., 2025a) have integrated next-token prediction loss during training to retain generative capabilities alongside discriminative performance. Despite this, their embeddings are still derived from direct encoding at inference, classifying them as discriminative embeddings.

This paper addresses this gap by exploring "reasoning-driven generative embeddings." It proposes a framework, UME-R1, that aims to unify embedding tasks within a generative paradigm, allowing MLLMs to actively generate reasoning and summaries *before* producing an embedding. This approach seeks to leverage the generative and reasoning capabilities of MLLMs, which are underutilized in conventional discriminative embedding models. The work positions itself as a pioneering effort in this specific area, investigating how MLLMs can produce both discriminative and reasoning-driven generative embeddings and how reinforcement learning can be applied to optimize these generative embeddings, even in the absence of definitive "best answers."

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to pioneer the exploration of reasoning-driven generative embeddings and to unify various embedding tasks within a generative paradigm. The work proposes a framework, UME-R1, designed to enable multimodal embedding models to produce both discriminative and reasoning-driven generative embeddings based on user demand.

The motivation for this research stems from several observations and challenges in the existing multimodal embedding landscape:
- **Limitations of Discriminative Embeddings:** Current MLLM-based multimodal embedding models are inherently discriminative. They compute embeddings by directly encoding multimodal inputs and extracting a representation, typically the final hidden state of the last token, without generating any intermediate content. This approach limits their ability to fully exploit the powerful generative and reasoning capabilities inherent in the MLLMs they are built upon.
- **Untapped Potential of Generative Reasoning:** Large Reasoning Models and MLLMs have demonstrated superior performance on complex tasks through explicit reasoning processes, often employing Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting. The authors hypothesize that integrating such reasoning capabilities into the embedding generation process could lead to more robust and higher-quality embeddings.
- **Desire for Flexible Embedding Generation:** The research is motivated by the question of how to transform a multimodal embedding model into a generative one that can produce embeddings following an explicit reasoning path. This includes enabling the model to generate intermediate reasoning steps and summaries, which could then inform the final embedding representation.
- **Optimizing Generative Embeddings with Reinforcement Learning:** A further motivation is to investigate whether reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) can effectively optimize generative embedding models. This is particularly challenging as embedding tasks, unlike some language generation tasks, lack standard "best answers," necessitating a novel reward policy.
- **Exploring Inference-Time Scalability:** The authors are also motivated to determine if reasoning-driven generative embeddings can benefit from inference-time scaling techniques, such as repeated sampling, to improve performance on downstream tasks.
- **Revealing Complementarity:** The work seeks to explore if discriminative and reasoning-driven generative embeddings are complementary, potentially offering an oracle upper bound that surpasses either type alone.

In summary, the core motivation is to bridge the gap between powerful MLLM reasoning capabilities and multimodal embedding tasks, developing a framework that not only generates embeddings but also explicit reasoning processes to enhance their quality and interpretability.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

UME-R1 is presented as a universal multimodal embedding framework featuring a two-stage training strategy. The backbone models adopted for UME-R1 are Qwen2-VL-2B and Qwen2-VL-7B.

#### 3.1. Architecture for Embeddings

The model is designed to produce both discriminative and reasoning-driven generative embeddings. This is achieved through a specific prompting template:
`USER: <image> <video> {query/target} <disc emb> Represent the above input text, images, videos, or any combination of the three as embeddings. First output the thinking process in <think> </think> tags and then summarize the entire input in a word or sentence. Finally, use the <gen emb> tag to represent the entire input. ASSISTANT: <think> {reasoning} </think> <answer> {summary} <gen emb>`

- **Discriminative Embedding:** Derived from the last-layer hidden state corresponding to the `<disc emb>` token in the prompt. This embedding is generated without any intermediate model generation.
- **Reasoning-Driven Generative Embedding:** Derived from the last-layer hidden state corresponding to the final model-generated `<gen emb>` token. This embedding incorporates the preceding generated reasoning and summary.

#### 3.2. Two-Stage Model Training

**Stage 1: Supervised Fine-tuning (SFT)**
This stage aims to equip the model with initial reasoning capabilities and the ability to generate both types of embeddings.

- **Dataset Construction:**
 - A "cold-start" SFT dataset was created by sampling 50,000 instances from each of 20 in-distribution MMEB-V2 datasets.
 - Training instances from LLaVA-Hound, ViDoRe, and VisRAG datasets were integrated to cover video and visual document modalities, resulting in approximately 1.76 million query-target pairs.
 - The pure-thinking model GLM-4.1V-Thinking (Hong et al., 2025b) was utilized to generate Chain-of-Thought (CoT) rationales and summaries for both the query and target of each pair.
 - Data was filtered to remove instances with excessive token repetition, overly long reasoning (exceeding 8,192 tokens), or invalid format, yielding 1.46 million SFT pairs.
- **Loss Functions:** The SFT stage combines three loss components:
 - **Discriminative Contrastive Loss (`L_dctr`):** An InfoNCE loss applied to the discriminative embeddings of query-target pairs. This maximizes similarity between positive pairs and minimizes it with negative pairs.
 - **Generative Contrastive Loss (`L_gctr`):** An InfoNCE loss applied to the reasoning-driven generative embeddings, where `o_q_i` and `o_t_i` represent the generated reasoning trajectory and summary for query `q_i` and target `t_i`, respectively.
 - **Next-Token Prediction Loss (`L_ce`):** A standard autoregressive cross-entropy loss applied to the generated reasoning trajectories and summaries (`o_q_i` and `o_t_i`), enabling the model's generative reasoning capabilities.
 - The total SFT loss is `L_sft = L_dctr + L_gctr + L_ce`.
- **Training Details:** Temperature (τ) was set to 0.02, batch size to 1,024 (via gradient accumulation), max sequence length to 12,288 tokens, and learning rate to 5e-5 for 5K steps.

**Stage 2: Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Reward (RLVR)**
This stage further refines the model, encouraging the generation of reasoning trajectories that yield more beneficial embeddings.

- **Dataset:** A smaller dataset of 11,136 balanced-sampled pairs was used for RL training, prioritized from instances not included in the SFT data to prevent overly simplistic samples.
- **Algorithm:** Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) (Shao et al., 2024) was employed. GRPO uses the mean reward across multiple sampled outputs as a baseline for optimization.
- **Reward Function:** A two-component reward system was designed:
 - **Format Reward:** A reward of 1 is given if the generated response strictly adheres to the predefined template (`<think>...</think><answer>...<gen emb>`); otherwise, it is 0. This encourages structured and interpretable outputs.
 - **Embedding Reward (`R_emb`):** This component evaluates the quality of the generated embeddings without standard best answers. It combines two aspects:
 - **Ranking:** The proportion of positive targets found within the top G most similar candidates among positive and negative targets (i.e., `|S+ ∩ topG(S+ ∪ S−)| / G`).
 - **Similarity Gap:** The difference between the average similarity of the query with positive targets and the average similarity with negative targets (i.e., `avg(S+) - avg(S−)`).
 - The embedding reward is computed as the product of these two components: `R_emb = Ranking × Similarity Gap`.
- **Training Details:** Group size G = 8, clipping parameter ϵ = 0.2, KL-divergence coefficient β = 0.04. Batch size was 256, learning rate 1e-6, trained for one epoch.

#### 3.3. Evaluation

- **Benchmark:** MMEB-V2 (Meng et al., 2025), encompassing 78 tasks across image, video, and visual document modalities (9 meta-tasks).
- **Metrics:** Hit@1 for video and image tasks, NDCG@5 for visual document tasks.
- **Inference:** Greedy search, max 8,192 newly generated tokens. Reasoning-driven generative embeddings are used by default unless specified.
- **Baselines:** A range of MLLM-based multimodal embedding models including GME, ColPali, VLM2Vec, LamRA, CAFe, and VLM2Vec-V2. A crucial baseline, DUME (discriminative UME), was trained under the same dataset and settings as UME-R1 but exclusively on discriminative embeddings for a direct comparison.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded several findings regarding the performance and characteristics of reasoning-driven generative multimodal embeddings:

- **Superior Performance of UME-R1:** UME-R1 consistently demonstrated superior performance across the MMEB-V2 benchmark (78 tasks across images, videos, and visual documents) compared to conventional discriminative embedding models.
 - For instance, UME-R1 (Qwen2-VL-2B) achieved an overall score of 60.1, outperforming VLM2Vec-V2 (Qwen2-VL-2B) which scored 58.0, despite UME-R1 using approximately two-thirds of the training data.
 - Compared to DUME (discriminative UME) trained with the same data, UME-R1 (Qwen2-VL-2B) showed substantial improvements: 4.1 points for image tasks, 9.0 points for video tasks, and 11.1 points for visual document tasks, indicating the effectiveness of reasoning-driven generative embeddings.
 - The 7B parameter version of UME-R1 also exhibited similar improvements over its discriminative counterpart and other baselines.

- **Complementarity of Embedding Types (Oracle Performance):** An oracle evaluation, which selected the best performance between discriminative and reasoning-driven generative embeddings for each test instance, revealed further performance gains.
 - UME-R1-2B achieved an overall improvement of 4.3 points under the oracle setting, and UME-R1-7B improved by 3.6 points. This suggests that the two embedding types are complementary, and dynamic selection could yield more robust retrieval results in practical applications.

- **Effectiveness of RL Stage and Reward Design:**
 - The reinforcement learning (RL) stage, even with a relatively small dataset (11K pairs) and without contrastive learning, contributed to substantial performance improvements for the generative embeddings (UME-R1 outperformed UME without RL by 1.0 point overall). This indicates that optimizing reasoning paths and summarization via RL is effective.
 - The reward function design, which jointly considered ranking and similarity differences, was found to be critical. Both components played distinct roles: ranking for aligning with downstream tasks, and similarity differences for guiding the model on easier samples where ranking might saturate.
 - An alternative fixed-threshold reward policy was less effective, performing inconsistently across modalities, which was attributed to varying similarity distributions across different task categories.

- **Positive Impact on Discriminative Embeddings:** Training for reasoning-driven generative embeddings during the SFT stage, which includes the next-token prediction objective, also improved the performance of discriminative embeddings.
 - DUME (purely discriminative) achieved 52.7 overall, while UME (UME-R1 without RL) achieved 55.7 using discriminative embeddings. This 3.0-point improvement, particularly noticeable in visual document tasks (+7.5 points), suggests that the generative embedding objective provides richer supervisory signals.
 - The RL stage, while primarily optimizing generative embeddings, did not compromise discriminative embedding performance, showing an additional 0.4-point gain, indicating no conflict during training.

- **Inference-Time Scaling Potential:** Reasoning-driven generative embeddings demonstrated potential for inference-time scaling.
 - Pass@k curves showed that repeated sampling at inference time improved coverage across various image and video datasets for both UME-R1-2B and UME-R1-7B. This suggests that allocating more computational resources at inference can lead to better embedding representations.

- **Self-Generated vs. External-Enhanced Reasoning:** UME-R1's self-generated reasoning and summaries consistently outperformed an approach where an external, stronger model (9B GLM-4.1V-Thinking) generated reasoning for a discriminative embedding model (DUME+Gen).
 - While external reasoning could provide some gains (e.g., +19.7 on K700, +3.9 on MSVD), it could also degrade performance (e.g., -12.3 on CIRR). UME-R1's self-generated approach proved more efficient and effective, indicating the value of integrated, task-specific reasoning.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work makes several significant contributions and suggests potential impacts on the field of multimodal AI:

- **Pioneering Reasoning-Driven Generative Embeddings:** This research represents a novel exploration into reasoning-driven generative embeddings. By unifying embedding tasks within a generative paradigm, UME-R1 demonstrates a new approach to leveraging the advanced reasoning capabilities of MLLMs for representation learning. This departs from the conventional discriminative paradigm and opens new avenues for multimodal embedding research.

- **Enhanced Performance and Interpretability:** The consistently superior performance of UME-R1 on a broad benchmark (MMEB-V2) underscores the effectiveness of integrating explicit reasoning and summarization into the embedding generation process. The ability to produce step-by-step reasoning paths also offers potential for greater interpretability of multimodal embeddings, allowing insights into *why* a particular representation is generated.

- **Scalable Optimization with Reinforcement Learning:** The successful application of rule-based reinforcement learning with a custom-designed reward policy to multimodal embedding tasks is a notable achievement. This demonstrates a scalable optimization paradigm for enhancing generative embeddings, addressing the challenge of lacking definitive "best answers" for such tasks. This method provides a foundation for future advancements in fine-tuning MLLMs for complex, non-generative representation tasks.

- **Inference-Time Performance Scaling:** The finding that repeated sampling can boost the coverage (pass@k) of reasoning-driven generative embeddings highlights their potential for inference-time scalability. This suggests that beyond model size, increasing computational resources at inference can further enhance embedding quality, offering a flexible trade-off between latency and performance.

- **Foundation for Future Research:** UME-R1 establishes a new direction for multimodal embeddings. The identified complementarity between discriminative and reasoning-driven generative embeddings, along with the performance gap relative to an oracle, points to promising future research directions:
 - **Adaptive Embedding Mode Selection:** Developing mechanisms for models to intelligently switch between discriminative and reasoning-driven generative embeddings based on input or task context.
 - **Advanced RL Strategies:** Designing more sophisticated RL datasets and training strategies to encourage even higher-quality reasoning and summaries, potentially by incorporating harder negative examples.
 - **Further Inference-Time Optimization:** Exploring additional techniques for inference-time scaling to maximize the benefits of generative embeddings.

While the higher training and inference costs associated with generative embeddings are acknowledged limitations, the demonstrated performance gains, interpretability, and scalability potential position UME-R1 as a foundational work that could influence the development of more advanced, reasoning-aware multimodal AI systems.
