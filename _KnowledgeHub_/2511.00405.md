# UME-R1: Exploring Reasoning-Driven Generative Multimodal Embeddings

> [!summary] Summary
> UME-R1, a framework from Xiamen University and WeChat AI, introduces reasoning-driven generative multimodal embeddings, allowing models to generate explicit reasoning and summaries before producing an embedding. This approach consistently outperforms conventional discriminative embedding models on the MMEB-V2 benchmark, achieving an overall score of 60.1 with UME-R1 (Qwen2-VL-2B) compared to VLM2Vec-V2's 58.0, and showing substantial improvements across image, video, and visual document tasks over its discriminative counterpart.

## Problem

- Existing multimodal embedding models, often built on MLLMs, primarily operate in a discriminative manner, encoding inputs directly without leveraging the MLLMs' generative and reasoning capabilities.
- The lack of explicit intermediate reasoning in current embedding models limits interpretability and the potential for more robust representations, unlike successful Chain-of-Thought (CoT) methods in other MLLM applications.
- Optimizing generative embeddings using reinforcement learning is challenging because embedding tasks typically lack definitive 'best answers' or explicit ground-truth outputs for reward computation.

## Method

- **UME-R1** unifies embedding tasks within a ==generative paradigm==, enabling MLLMs to generate both discriminative and reasoning-driven generative embeddings based on user prompts.
- A two-stage training strategy is employed: Supervised Fine-tuning (SFT) provides initial reasoning capabilities, and Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Reward (RLVR) refines the model to produce more beneficial embeddings.
- A novel reward function for RLVR is designed, combining 'Format Reward' for adherence to structure and 'Embedding Reward' which factors in both ranking performance and similarity gap to evaluate embedding quality without explicit best answers.

## Results

- UME-R1 consistently achieved superior overall performance on the MMEB-V2 benchmark (78 tasks), with UME-R1 (Qwen2-VL-**2B**) scoring 60.1 compared to 58.0 for VLM2Vec-V2 (Qwen2-VL-**2B**) and showing up to 11.1 points improvement over a discriminative baseline (DUME).
- An oracle evaluation, selecting the better of discriminative or generative embeddings per instance, further improved overall scores by 4.3 points for UME-R1-**2B** and 3.6 points for UME-R1-**7B**, confirming their complementarity.
- The RL stage enhanced generative embeddings by 1.0 point overall, and the SFT stage, by incorporating generative objectives, improved discriminative embeddings by 3.0 points, indicating a mutually beneficial training process.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly integrating reasoning and summarization into the embedding generation process significantly enhances multimodal embedding performance across various tasks and modalities.
> - Discriminative and reasoning-driven generative embeddings are complementary, suggesting that dynamically selecting or combining them can yield higher 'oracle' performance.
> - Reinforcement learning, even with a custom reward policy and without 'best answers,' can effectively optimize generative embedding models, leading to substantial performance gains and demonstrating a scalable fine-tuning paradigm.
