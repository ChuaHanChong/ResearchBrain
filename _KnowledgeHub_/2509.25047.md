# Scaling Synthetic Task Generation for Agents via Exploration

> [!summary] Summary
> AutoPlay introduces a scalable, automated pipeline that uses explicit environment exploration to synthesize diverse, feasible, and verifiable tasks for training multimodal large language model agents. This approach significantly enhances agent performance on UI interaction benchmarks, achieving up to 20.6% higher success rates on AndroidWorld and enabling robust reinforcement learning without human supervision.

## Problem

- Training MLLM-based interactive agents is hampered by the scarcity of high-quality, diverse, and verifiable task-demonstration data.
- Existing synthetic task generation methods often produce generic or hallucinated tasks due to a lack of grounding in actual environment dynamics.
- Human annotation for creating agent training data is prohibitively expensive and does not scale efficiently for complex, open-ended environments.

## Method

- A two-stage pipeline, **AutoPlay**, employs an MLLM-based '==Explorer Agent==' to systematically explore interactive environments and gather rich context through summarized memory.
- An 'Exploration-Conditioned Task Generator' MLLM synthesizes diverse and feasible tasks by combining collected exploration trajectories with predefined task guideline prompts.
- MLLM-based executor and verifier agents automatically generate and filter high-quality task execution demonstrations for Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) and provide reward signals for Reinforcement Learning (RL).

## Results

- AutoPlay-trained agents achieve substantial performance gains, with AutoPlay-**7B** improving success rates by **20.6%** on AndroidWorld and **7.7%** on OSWorld compared to its base MLLM.
- The framework enables effective Reinforcement Learning, with an AutoPlay-**3B** model trained via RL approaching the performance of the SFT-trained AutoPlay-**7B** model (**39.9%** vs. **40.1%** Pass@1).
- AutoPlay tasks demonstrate higher feasibility (executor completes **46.0%** of tasks) and lead to significantly improved downstream agent performance (**9.4%** Pass@1 higher than 'No Exploration' tasks).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicit and systematic environment exploration is crucial for generating a broad range of high-quality, feasible, and diverse synthetic tasks that significantly improve agent performance.
> - MLLM-based verifiers are effective in automatically filtering successful trajectories for SFT and serving as reward models for scalable RL training, reducing reliance on human annotation.
> - Training with AutoPlay's synthetically generated and verified data can lead to agents that outperform the expert policies used for initial data collection, demonstrating improved generalization and robustness.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work is situated within the rapidly evolving landscape of developing intelligent, interactive agents powered by Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs). MLLMs have shown immense promise as foundational models for agents across diverse domains like computer-use, web navigation, video games, and robotics, owing to their broad knowledge and reasoning capabilities. However, a central and pervasive challenge in scaling the training of these agents is the scarcity of high-quality, downstream agentic data. This data needs to comprise two key components: a diverse set of tasks that cover real-world use cases and corresponding task execution trajectories (demonstrations).

Existing approaches to address this data scarcity suffer from significant limitations:
- **Human Annotation:** While generating high-quality tasks and demonstrations, this method is prohibitively expensive and scales poorly, especially for complex, open-ended environments. Many previous efforts have heavily relied on this.
- **MLLM Prompting with Limited Information:** Some prior works attempt to generate tasks by prompting MLLMs with only limited domain knowledge or static textual descriptions of environments (e.g., Trabucco et al., 2025; Zhou et al., 2025). This typically yields generic tasks with limited coverage, and critically, offers no inherent guarantees of feasibility or verifiability. MLLMs, in their ungrounded state, often hallucinate tasks that are not executable or relevant to the current environment state.
- **Iterative Task Proposal and Execution:** Other research focuses on interleaving task proposal with execution, where short-horizon subtasks are proposed, executed, and then summarized into longer-horizon tasks via hindsight relabeling (e.g., Xie et al., 2025; Pahuja et al., 2025). While these methods ground tasks in environment interactions, they are often limited by progressively constrained exploration spaces and can lead to less diverse or easier tasks by stitching simple subgoals.

AutoPlay directly addresses these shortcomings by proposing a scalable, automated pipeline that explicitly **explores interactive environments** to gather rich, dynamic context. By doing so, it grounds task generation in actual environment dynamics and current state information, thereby ensuring tasks are more diverse, feasible, and verifiable. This explicit grounding represents a significant departure from methods that rely on static descriptions or limited iterative interactions. The paper focuses specifically on UI agents, a highly practical domain given the prevalence of digital interfaces, and one where programmatic environments are increasingly available for interaction.

In essence, AutoPlay aims to bridge the gap between powerful MLLMs and the demanding data requirements for training truly capable, general-purpose agents, establishing a novel, scalable paradigm for synthetic data generation that reduces reliance on human effort and enhances the quality and relevance of generated tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core motivation behind the AutoPlay framework stems from a critical bottleneck in the advancement of MLLM-based interactive agents: the prohibitive cost and scalability issues associated with acquiring high-quality training data. While MLLMs offer powerful reasoning and understanding capabilities, their effective post-training for specific agentic domains is severely hampered by the lack of datasets comprising diverse, feasible, and verifiable tasks, along with their corresponding execution demonstrations.

The key objectives of this research are:

1. **Develop a Scalable Task Generation Pipeline:** The primary objective is to create an automated and reliable pipeline that can synthesize a large volume of agentic tasks at scale, effectively overcoming the limitations of human annotation.
2. **Ensure Task Quality:** The generated tasks must possess several crucial properties:
 - **Diversity:** They need to cover a broad spectrum of real-world use cases and functionalities within the target environment.
 - **Feasibility:** Tasks must be realistically executable by an agent within the given environment to generate useful demonstrations. This requires explicit knowledge of the environment's state and interaction possibilities.
 - **Verifiability:** The successful completion of a task must be automatically ascertainable, either for filtering high-quality demonstrations for Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) or for providing reward signals in Reinforcement Learning (RL).
 - **Environment Grounding:** Tasks should be explicitly grounded in the environment's current state and dynamics, rather than being generic or hallucinated.
3. **Enable Large-Scale Demonstration Synthesis:** With a robust task generation pipeline, the next objective is to enable the automatic generation of corresponding task execution trajectories (demonstrations) using MLLM-based executor and verifier agents, without human intervention.
4. **Facilitate Scalable MLLM Agent Post-Training:** Ultimately, the goal is to utilize the synthetically generated tasks and demonstrations to scalably post-train MLLM agents, enhancing their performance and capabilities through both Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) and Reinforcement Learning (RL). This aims to reduce or eliminate the reliance on human-annotated data for agent development.

The motivation arises from the observation that existing methods fall short: human annotation is too slow and expensive, while previous synthetic generation techniques often produce low-quality tasks due to a lack of genuine understanding of the environment's interactive possibilities. AutoPlay seeks to remedy this by introducing an approach that actively explores the environment, gathering the necessary context to generate truly useful and high-quality tasks for training advanced MLLM agents.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

AutoPlay is presented as a scalable, two-stage pipeline for generating diverse, feasible, and verifiable tasks for MLLM-based interactive agents, specifically focusing on UI agents in mobile and computer environments.

#### 3.1. Stage 1: Environment Exploration

This initial stage focuses on systematically uncovering the functionalities and content of a given interactive environment in a goal-agnostic manner.
1. **MLLM Explorer Agent:** An MLLM agent is employed to explore the environment exhaustively. This agent is designed to maximize coverage over novel environment states and functionalities.
2. **Memory Module:** To overcome the finite context window of MLLMs and encourage broad exploration, the explorer agent is equipped with an explicit memory of past interactions. For each exploration turn, a summarizer MLLM (e.g., GPT-4o) processes the trajectory and generates a concise, comprehensive text representation (`m`) of the observed functionalities and data. This summarized memory is then provided to the explorer in subsequent turns.
3. **Trajectory Collection:** The explorer interacts with the environment for a predetermined number of steps (`K`) for each application, generating exploratory trajectories (`τ`). This process is repeated (`M`) times for each of `N` applications, building a rich environment context `E` consisting of these trajectories. The initial state (`s0`) for each exploration run is sampled from the environment's state distribution.

#### 3.2. Stage 2: Exploration-Conditioned Task Generation

In the second stage, the collected exploration trajectories are leveraged to synthesize environment-grounded tasks.
1. **Task Generator MLLM:** A separate MLLM acts as the task generator. Its primary input is an exploration trajectory (`τ`) from Stage 1, which serves as concrete environmental context, implicitly revealing feasible interactions and current state information.
2. **Task Guideline Prompts:** To ensure diversity and alignment with desired task properties, the task generator is also conditioned on a set of "task guideline prompts" (`P`). These domain-specific prompts describe desired task characteristics (e.g., "Feature-Use" for CRUD operations, "Information Retrieval" for data queries, "Feature Composition" for multi-step tasks, "Subtask Repetition" for repetitive actions).
3. **Task Synthesis:** The task generator MLLM combines the task generator prompt, a specific task guideline prompt (`p`), and an exploration trajectory (`τ`) to synthesize a sequence of goals (`g`). Each generated goal, paired with the initial state (`s1`) of its corresponding trajectory, forms a task in the final dataset `D`. This process is iterated over all combinations of trajectories in `E` and guidelines in `P`.

#### 3.3. Training Data Generation and Verification

Once tasks are generated, AutoPlay facilitates the creation of high-quality training data:
1. **Task Executor Agent:** An MLLM-based executor agent attempts to solve the AutoPlay-generated tasks. This agent uses a modular architecture comprising an MLLM planner (e.g., GPT-4o), a reflection tool, and a grounding model (e.g., UI-TARS-1.5 7B for mobile, GTA1-7B for computer-use). The planner takes the task instruction, current screenshot, action history, and reflection trace to generate high-level actions, which the grounding model translates to pixel-level interactions if needed.
2. **Task Trajectory Verifier:** An MLLM-based verifier (e.g., GPT-4o or Qwen2.5-VL) evaluates the success of executed trajectories. It takes the task instruction and the interleaved image-action trajectory, provides a summary of agent actions, Chain-of-Thought reasoning for task completion, and a final "success" or "failure" judgment. This verifier operates without privileged environment information, mimicking human evaluation.
3. **SFT Data Generation:** Successful trajectories, filtered by the verifier, form the dataset for Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) of MLLM agents. AutoPlay generates approximately 20k tasks across 20 Android apps (yielding ~8k successful trajectories) and 10k tasks across 13 Ubuntu apps (yielding ~3.5k successful trajectories).
4. **Reinforcement Learning (RL) Integration:** The generated tasks can also be used for RL training. The MLLM verifier serves as a reward model, assigning a binary reward (1 for success, 0 for failure) to trajectories. This enables scalable RL training on all generated tasks, including those the initial executor might struggle with, improving agent stability and performance.

The approach leverages powerful MLLMs for sophisticated reasoning at multiple stages (exploration, summarization, task generation, execution, and verification), effectively creating a self-sustaining data generation and training loop without extensive human oversight.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

AutoPlay demonstrates significant advancements in scaling synthetic task generation and improving MLLM-based interactive agents.

#### 4.1. Performance on UI Agent Benchmarks

- **Significant Gains over Base Models:** AutoPlay-trained agents consistently and substantially outperform their base Qwen2.5-VL Instruct models across various sizes. For instance, AutoPlay-7B improves success rates by **20.6%** on AndroidWorld and **7.7%** on OSWorld compared to Qwen2.5-VL-7B. Similarly, AutoPlay-72B achieves **12.9%** and **10.1%** gains on AndroidWorld and OSWorld, respectively, over its base. This demonstrates the high relevance and quality of AutoPlay-generated tasks for training capable UI agents.
- **Competitive with Proprietary/Human-Annotated Baselines:** AutoPlay-72B surpasses strong baselines, including UI-TARS-1.5 (trained on large-scale human-annotated GUI data) by **13.7%** on AndroidWorld at the 72B scale and AguVis-72B by **5.0%** on OSWorld. While larger, more complex proprietary models like UI-TARS-2 230B and Seed-VL-1.5 show higher performance, AutoPlay achieves its results without human supervision, highlighting its scalability advantage.
- **Outperformance of its own Executor:** Remarkably, AutoPlay-72B achieves a **4.8% higher Pass@1** and **12.9% higher Pass@5** rate than the GPT-4o + UI-TARS policy used to *collect* the training data. This indicates that the MLLM verifier effectively filters high-quality trajectories, allowing the trained agent to generalize and solve tasks more robustly, even beyond the initial executor's capabilities.
- **Effectiveness for Reinforcement Learning (RL):** AutoPlay-generated tasks enable successful RL training, leading to an additional **5.7% gain** in success rate on AndroidWorld. The AutoPlay-3B model trained with RL even approaches the performance of the SFT-trained AutoPlay-7B model (39.9% vs. 40.1% Pass@1). This validates the utility of AutoPlay for diverse training paradigms.

#### 4.2. Ablation Analysis

- **Importance of Environment Exploration:**
 - **Task Feasibility:** AutoPlay tasks demonstrate higher feasibility, with the executor successfully completing **46.0%** of tasks, compared to only **21.3%** for "No Exploration" (which suffers from hallucination) and **56.4%** for "Iterative Exploration" (which generates easier, short-horizon tasks).
 - **Downstream Agent Performance:** Agents trained with AutoPlay tasks significantly outperform those trained with tasks from "No Exploration" (by **9.4% Pass@1**) and "Iterative Exploration" (by **16.6% Pass@1**). This underscores that explicit, broad exploration leads to more diverse, challenging, and ultimately more effective training data.
- **Importance of Task Guidelines:** The task guideline prompts are crucial for generating high-quality tasks. AutoPlay with guidelines improves the executor's success rate and, more significantly, boosts the downstream agent's performance, indicating that these guidelines steer the generation process towards diverse and performant tasks.
- **Task Coverage and Diversity:** Qualitative analysis (Figure 3) shows AutoPlay generates tasks with broader coverage across different categories (e.g., deleting, editing, retrieving in-app data) compared to "No Exploration," and its task distribution more closely mimics that of real-world benchmarks.
- **Verifier Capabilities:** While a proprietary MLLM (GPT-4o) performs slightly better as a verifier than an open-source one (Qwen2.5-VL 72B) for SFT data filtering, the performance difference is small (**1.5%**). This suggests that open-source MLLMs are sufficiently capable to serve as effective verifiers for scaling RL training, addressing cost implications.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The AutoPlay framework represents a significant leap forward in the development and training of interactive MLLM-based agents, addressing a fundamental bottleneck of data scarcity and quality. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Enabling Scalable Agent Training:** AutoPlay fundamentally shifts the paradigm of agent training data generation from expensive, labor-intensive human annotation to a highly scalable, automated pipeline. By synthesizing tens of thousands of diverse, feasible, and verifiable tasks across multiple applications and platforms, it provides the necessary fuel for continuously improving MLLM agents. This scalability is crucial for developing generalist agents that can operate across a vast number of applications and real-world scenarios.
2. **Reduced Reliance on Human Annotation:** The ability of AutoPlay to generate tasks, collect demonstrations via an MLLM executor, and verify their success with an MLLM verifier – all without human intervention – dramatically reduces the cost and time associated with data collection. This democratizes access to high-quality training data, potentially lowering the barrier for entry in agent development.
3. **Improved Agent Performance:** The consistent and substantial performance gains observed across different model sizes and domains (up to 20.0% on mobile-use and 10.9% on computer-use scenarios) highlight the efficacy of AutoPlay-generated tasks. By grounding tasks in explicit environmental exploration, the framework ensures that agents are trained on relevant, executable, and diverse interactions, leading to more capable and robust downstream agents.
4. **Advancements in Reinforcement Learning for Agents:** AutoPlay's integration with RL training, where the MLLM verifier acts as a reward model, is particularly impactful. This unlocks scalable RL for UI agents without human-designed reward functions or privileged access to environment states, paving the way for more sophisticated learning strategies that can continually improve agent policies on a massive scale. The ability to use open-source MLLMs as effective verifiers further enhances the practicality and accessibility of this approach.
5. **Robustness and Generalization:** The finding that AutoPlay-trained agents can outperform the very expert policy used for data collection (GPT-4o + UI-TARS) indicates that the generated datasets foster better generalization and robustness. This suggests that the synthetic data, combined with effective verification, allows agents to learn more fundamental interactive skills rather than simply mimicking expert trajectories.
6. **Broader Impact on AI Research:** Beyond UI agents, the core principles of AutoPlay – systematic environment exploration for task generation, MLLM-based execution, and automated verification – could be adapted to other interactive domains like robotics, web navigation, and gaming. This could accelerate progress in developing more autonomous and intelligent agents across the board.
7. **Future Development Pathways:** The paper also points to future research directions, such as improving the task executor and diversifying task guidelines, which could further enhance the quality of synthetic datasets and lead to even more capable agents. Addressing current limitations like cross-app interaction, fine-grained text editing, and complex UI interactions identified in the analysis opens fertile ground for further innovation.

In conclusion, AutoPlay is a pioneering work that provides a robust and scalable solution to one of the most pressing challenges in agentic AI. By automating the entire data lifecycle from task generation to verification, it promises to significantly accelerate the development of truly intelligent and autonomous agents capable of interacting with complex digital environments.
