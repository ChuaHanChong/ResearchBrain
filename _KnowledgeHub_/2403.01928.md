# ZSL-RPPO: Zero-Shot Learning for Quadrupedal Locomotion in Challenging Terrains using Recurrent Proximal Policy Optimization

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Huawei Technologies and collaborators developed ZSL-RPPO, a direct deep reinforcement learning method that enables quadrupedal robots to achieve zero-shot simulation-to-reality transfer for robust locomotion across diverse challenging terrains. The approach, which integrates recurrent neural networks and extensive domain randomization, demonstrated superior real-world performance on Unitree A1 and Aliengo robots compared to imitation learning baselines.

## Problem

- Teacher-student imitation learning frameworks suffer from an 'imitation gap' leading to significant performance degradation during sim-to-real transfer, especially under substantial discrepancies.
- Deploying simulation-trained policies on quadrupedal robots in highly challenging and previously unseen terrains remains difficult, often requiring fine-tuning and exhibiting vulnerabilities to sim-to-real discrepancies.
- Existing deep RL methods struggle with partial observability in real-world environments, where policies lack full task parameter knowledge and rely on limited sensory feedback.

## Method

- Introduced Recurrent Proximal Policy Optimization (RPPO), a direct deep ==reinforcement learning== algorithm that frames the problem as a Partially Observed Markov Decision Process (POMDP) and avoids the teacher-student imitation paradigm.
- Employed a Gated Recurrent Policy Network (GRPN) to handle long observational histories and self-adaptively integrate proprioceptive and exteroceptive (Lidar/depth camera) information.
- Implemented comprehensive domain randomization, perturbing intrinsic/extrinsic physical parameters, actuator properties, and observation noises, alongside perception alignment techniques, to bridge the sim-to-real gap.

## Results

- Achieved successful zero-shot sim-to-real transfer for quadrupedal locomotion on Unitree A1 and Aliengo robots across diverse challenging real-world terrains (e.g., stairs, oily surfaces, sand) without any fine-tuning.
- Demonstrated superior real-world success rates (e.g., **100%** on stairs, deformable ground) compared to state-of-the-art teacher-student baselines (T-S, RMA) and Model Predictive Control (MPC), with fully randomized ZSL-RPPO outperforming a weakly randomized version.
- Showed lower mean and standard deviation for position and orientation errors across different A1 robot units during stationary stepping, indicating higher consistency and robustness to hardware variations.
- Successfully deployed the Aliengo robot with ZSL-RPPO for a 2-kilometer, hour-long autonomous campus inspection, navigating various terrains without manual assistance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Directly training recurrent policies with an appropriate RL algorithm (RPPO) can bypass the performance degradation issues associated with the 'imitation gap' in teacher-student frameworks for sim-to-real transfer.
> - Extensive and comprehensive domain randomization, covering a wide range of physical, actuator, and observation parameters, is crucial for achieving robust zero-shot sim-to-real transfer in challenging, unmodeled environments.
> - Recurrent neural network architectures, like GRPN, are effective in utilizing partially observable sensory inputs (including exteroceptive terrain data) to enable adaptive and robust locomotion behaviors in complex terrains.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotics, particularly quadrupedal locomotion, is advancing rapidly due to its potential for navigating complex, unstructured, and hazardous environments where wheeled robots are less effective. Traditionally, quadruped locomotion control has relied on classical methods such as Model Predictive Control (MPC), which often demand precise models and significant expert tuning. While these methods have achieved notable results, their limitations regarding model accuracy and design complexity have motivated the exploration of alternative approaches.

Reinforcement Learning (RL) has emerged as a promising paradigm for robotic control, offering the ability to learn complex policies directly from data through trial and error. Recent advancements in deep reinforcement learning have enabled impressive locomotion capabilities for quadrupedal robots. A common strategy in this area involves a "teacher-student" imitation learning framework. In this setup, a teacher policy is initially trained in simulation with access to "privileged information" (e.g., ground-truth terrain data, robot contact forces). Subsequently, a student policy attempts to imitate the teacher's behavior using only available sensor data, aiming for robustness during simulation-to-reality (sim-to-real) transfer. However, a significant challenge within this paradigm is the "imitation gap," which can lead to a substantial performance degradation of the student policy, particularly when the sim-to-real gap is extensive due to discrepancies between simulated and real-world dynamics and perception.

Overcoming the sim-to-real gap is a critical research challenge. Existing strategies often involve either enhancing simulator fidelity through system identification or developing robust policies that are more likely to transfer. Domain randomization, where various physical and observational parameters are varied during simulation training, is a widely adopted technique for creating policies that are robust to real-world uncertainties. Despite these efforts, deploying simulation-trained policies on quadrupedal robots in highly challenging and previously unseen terrains remains an open problem. Many current approaches, including teacher-student designs, still exhibit vulnerabilities to extensive simulation-to-reality discrepancies, manifesting as performance drops, stability issues, or specific failure modes in real-world scenarios.

This work contributes to the landscape by proposing a direct, teacher-student-free deep RL approach designed to achieve zero-shot sim-to-real transfer. It aims to address the inherent partial observability issues in deep RL for locomotion by integrating recurrent neural networks with a modified policy optimization algorithm. By focusing on extensive domain randomization and direct training, this research seeks to mitigate the performance degradation observed in imitation-based methods and enhance the robustness and generalization of quadrupedal locomotion controllers, particularly in diverse and difficult terrains.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind the ZSL-RPPO research are multifaceted, addressing critical limitations in current quadrupedal locomotion control:

1. **Overcome Limitations of Teacher-Student Imitation Learning:** The authors identify that existing mainstream RL approaches, which often rely on a teacher-student imitation training paradigm, suffer from a significant performance gap between the teacher and student policies. This gap can lead to "catastrophic degradation" in the student's real-world performance, especially during sim-to-real transfer under substantial discrepancies between simulation and reality. A core motivation is to develop a method that bypasses this imitation learning step entirely, thereby avoiding the associated performance decline and simplifying the training pipeline.

2. **Enable Robust, Reliable, and Versatile Locomotion in Challenging Terrains:** A fundamental goal is to generate locomotion controllers that allow quadrupedal robots to navigate a wide range of difficult and disorderly environments, such as slippery surfaces, rough grassy terrains, and stairs. The motivation is to enhance the practical applicability of these robots in real-world scenarios like search and rescue, industrial inspection, and academic research, which often involve unpredictable and complex terrain conditions.

3. **Achieve Zero-Shot Simulation-to-Reality Transfer without Fine-Tuning:** The research aims for a learning architecture that facilitates direct deployment of simulation-trained policies onto real robots without any further fine-tuning or adaptation. This objective is motivated by the desire to significantly reduce maintenance costs, enhance deployment efficiency, and improve the scalability of robot operations, as real-world fine-tuning can be time-consuming, resource-intensive, and potentially damaging to hardware.

4. **Develop a New Algorithm for Training Recurrent Neural Networks in Partially Observable Environments:** The authors recognize that many challenges in deep reinforcement learning, particularly regarding domain randomization, adaptation, and generalization, are inherently partially observable. Policies must operate without full knowledge of task parameters (e.g., simulation specifics, precise hardware calibration) or low-dimensional system states, relying instead on limited sensory feedback (e.g., Lidar or camera observations). This motivates the development of a Recurrent Proximal Policy Optimization (RPPO) algorithm, designed to directly train recurrent networks under such conditions, ensuring more stable and scalable training than teacher-student baselines.

5. **Support Extensive Perturbation Across Sim-to-Real Transfer:** To enhance robustness and generalization, a key objective is to design a learning architecture that can handle extensive randomization of both intrinsic and extrinsic physical parameters during simulation training. This includes varying robot mass, center of mass, sensor parameters, contact friction, restitution coefficients, actuator properties (motor strengths, PD coefficients), and proprioceptive and exteroceptive observation noises. The motivation is to expose the policy to a broad distribution of potential real-world conditions, making it resilient to unmodeled dynamics and hardware inconsistencies.

6. **Learn Visuomotor Action Selection Rules End-to-End:** The approach seeks to learn control policies that can directly map sensory observations (from Lidar or depth cameras) to motor commands. This end-to-end learning paradigm is motivated by its potential to simplify the control architecture and enable adaptive behaviors based on real-time terrain perception, reducing reliance on explicit environment models.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The ZSL-RPPO methodology integrates recurrent neural networks with a modified policy optimization algorithm and extensive domain randomization to achieve zero-shot sim-to-real transfer for quadrupedal locomotion.

**A. System Overview**
The control pipeline processes proprioceptive measurements (internal robot states) and exteroceptive observations (environmental perception) as input to a control policy network. This network outputs a distribution over gait schedule parameters, which are then fed into a Policy Modulated Trajectory Generator (PMTG) and an Inverse Kinematics (IK) module to produce valid control signals for the robot's actuators. The neural network is trained exclusively in simulation using Deep Reinforcement Learning and then directly deployed onto real robots without further training or adaptation. Real-world velocity estimation uses a Kalman filter, and terrain elevation samples are provided by either a solid-state Lidar or a depth camera.

**B. Recurrent Proximal Policy Optimization (RPPO)**
To avoid the limitations of the teacher-student paradigm, the authors propose RPPO. This algorithm frames the reinforcement learning problem as a Partially Observed Markov Decision Process (POMDP). The agent learns a recurrent policy $\pi_{\theta}(\cdot|\cdot, h)$ which selects actions conditioned on observations ($o_t$) and an internal state ($h$). The objective is to maximize the expected cumulative reward.

Similar to Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO), RPPO updates the policy by optimizing an $\varepsilon$-clipped surrogate loss function, which ensures stable policy improvements. The advantage estimates ($\hat{A}_t$) are computed using Generalized Advantage Estimation (GAE). A key aspect of RPPO is its ability to leverage an asymmetry between the critic and actor networks during training: the value (critic) network $V_{\eta}$ is permitted to access latent states ($s_t$) from the simulator for more accurate value estimation, while the policy (actor) network $\pi_{\theta}$ only receives noisy observations ($o_t$), consistent with real-world deployment constraints. The critic network is updated by minimizing an $\varepsilon$-clipped value loss.

**C. Gated Recurrent Policy Network (GRPN)**
The policy network is implemented as a Gated Recurrent Policy Network (GRPN), designed for self-adaptive integration of exteroceptive information. The GRPN incorporates Gated Recurrent Units (GRUs) and gated attention mechanisms within a recurrent belief encoder. The GRU has an input size of 197, a hidden size of 64, and 2 layers. Additional Multi-Layer Perceptrons (MLPs) are used, with specified layer sizes, for processing inputs and outputs. This architecture is chosen for its capacity to handle longer observational histories efficiently compared to temporal convolutional networks.

**D. Observation and Action Space**
- **Proprioception:** These observations are directly available in simulation and reliably measurable in reality. They include: user-specified velocity command (3D), body pose (3D), base linear velocity (3D), base angular velocity (3D), 12-dimensional joint positions and velocities, histories of joint positions (3 timesteps, 36D), joint velocities (2 timesteps, 24D), joint target positions (2 timesteps, 24D), and 13-dimensional PMTG phase information, totaling 133 dimensions.
- **Exteroception:** Terrain information is provided as a rectangle-shaped point grid covering the robot's base and footholds. For a depth camera, elevation data is sampled from the area beneath the robot's head and front legs. For Lidar, elevation data is sampled from the area directly beneath the robot's body using terrain mesh construction based on Lidar odometry.
- **Action Space:** The policy network outputs 16 gait schedule parameters, comprising target phase and joint residual angles for the four legs. These parameters are then fed into the PMTG module, which generates 12-dimensional Degrees of Freedom (DoF) target positions. The PMTG module is utilized to enhance training stability and sample efficiency, resulting in regular gait movements.

**E. Reward Shaping**
The reward function is an extension of previous works, designed to shape the robot's behavior. Key reward terms include linear and angular velocity tracking (to encourage instruction following), velocity constraints, orientation penalties, base height maintenance, torque minimization, DoF acceleration penalties, target smoothness, stand-still rewards, DoF position limits, collision penalties, foot air time management, foot stumble prevention, foot stance rewards (encouraging placement away from stair edges), foot height maintenance, and proximity constraints. A consistent set of rewards is applied across all terrain types.

**F. Zero-shot Transferable Simulation**
To bridge the sim-to-real gap, the approach employs comprehensive domain randomization and perception alignment:
- **Domain Randomization:** Various parameters are randomized during simulation:
 - **Intrinsic physical parameters:** mass offset and center-of-mass shift for the robot and onboard sensors.
 - **Extrinsic physical parameters:** contact friction and restitution coefficients.
 - **Actuator parameters:** motor strength factors and PD control coefficients.
 - **Proprioceptive observations:** noises applied to base linear/angular velocities, DoF positions/velocities.
 - **Exteroceptive observations:** nominal noise on height samples and drift noise on root position (emulating localization errors).
 These randomizations cover a wide distribution of physical properties and potential sensor errors. Actuator and physical parameters are randomized once per simulation episode, while observation noises are applied at every timestep.
- **Perception Alignment:** Pre-processing and 3D reconstruction techniques are used to align simulated and real-world perception. For depth cameras, simulated leg motion noise, Gaussian noise, and random artifacts are introduced to elevation samples. In reality, point clouds are translated to the body frame, and depth hole-filling is performed. For solid-state Lidar, Lidar-IMU fused odometry constructs a terrain mesh for elevation sampling.
- **Terrain Curriculum:** A curriculum learning strategy is implemented within a 10x10 grid-cell simulation environment. The grid includes ten rows of sub-terrain types, each with ten levels of increasing difficulty. Agents are promoted to higher difficulty levels within a row upon successfully traversing a sufficient distance.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several findings based on real-world deployments and simulation evaluations, demonstrating the efficacy of the ZSL-RPPO approach.

**A. Zero-Shot Transfer Success:**
The proposed controller, trained entirely in simulation, was deployed on Unitree A1 and Aliengo quadruped robots without any further fine-tuning. This direct deployment successfully enabled locomotion in various challenging real-world environments.

**B. Real-World Quantitative Evaluation:**
Experiments were conducted on Unitree A1 robots across diverse and challenging terrains, including stairs, oily surfaces, deformable ground, sand, slope lawns, and cobblestones (Fig. 5). The robot equipped with the ZSL-RPPO controller performed five trial runs for each scene, and its success rate was recorded.
- **Overall Performance:** The ZSL-RPPO method (labeled "Ours") consistently achieved higher success rates across all tested scenes compared to state-of-the-art baselines: Teacher-Student (T-S), Rapid Motion Adaptation (RMA), and Model Predictive Control (MPC). For instance, "Ours" achieved 100% success on stairs, deformable ground, slope lawns, and cobblestone, significantly outperforming T-S (60% on stairs, 60% on deformable, 80% on slope lawn) and RMA (0% on stairs, 40% on deformable). On oily surfaces and sand, "Ours" achieved 80% success, while T-S and RMA performed at 20-60%.
- **Impact of Domain Randomization:** A variant of the ZSL-RPPO policy trained with "weak randomization" (disabling randomization of motor strength, Kp, Kd, and root position noise) exhibited a noticeable decline in performance. For example, its success rate dropped from 100% to 40% on stairs and from 80% to 40% on oily surfaces. This observation indicates that comprehensive domain randomization is a critical factor for achieving robust real-world transfer with end-to-end RPPO training.
- **Baseline Limitations:** Teacher-student methods (T-S, RMA) reportedly experienced difficulties under stronger domain randomization due to the inherent imitation gap. The MPC baseline, pre-installed in Unitree robots, showed fluctuating performance across different sub-terrains.

**C. Quantitative Evaluation in Simulation:**
Simulation-based evaluations, performed on a playground with more challenging parameters than training (e.g., stairs with 3-18cm steps vs. 2-12cm during training), complemented the real-world results. All domain randomization and noises were activated.
- **Superiority in Simulation:** The ZSL-RPPO method demonstrated the highest performance across metrics such as average terrain level reached (8.9), distance traversed (72.7m), and success rate (89%), albeit with narrower margins compared to real-world gaps (Table V).
- **Confirmation of Randomization Impact:** Similar to the real-world findings, the "Ours (weak rand.)" policy showed reduced performance in simulation (7.9 terrain level, 64.6m distance, 79% success rate) compared to the fully randomized version, further supporting the importance of extensive randomization for generalization.

**D. Domain Transfer Consistency on Hardware:**
To assess robustness to hardware variations, ZSL-RPPO was compared against teacher-student policies on three different A1 robots. The robots performed stationary stepping motions for 10 seconds, with position and orientation drift errors measured by a motion capture system.
- **Lower Errors and Higher Consistency:** ZSL-RPPO exhibited the lowest mean and standard deviation for both position and orientation errors (Fig. 6). This suggests that the policy achieved minimal behavioral discrepancies between the virtual and real-world domains and maintained a higher level of consistency across different physical robot units, despite inherent variations in motor dynamics and IMU miscalibration among the A1 robots.

**E. Introspective Analysis:**
An analysis of the ZSL-RPPO policy during stair climbing (Fig. 7, top) revealed that the learned policy adapted its foot trajectories based on terrain and body orientation observations. Specifically, higher foothold lifting was observed during stair ascent (Phase 2) compared to approaching or recovering on flat ground (Phase 1 and 3).
- **Influence of Terrain Observations:** Internal state profiles within the GRPN network showed distinct pattern variations in components like z0 and z7 during phase transitions. Policies that did not incorporate terrain observations failed to traverse stairs, providing empirical evidence that the GRPN effectively utilizes exteroceptive terrain observations to modulate its behavior.

**F. Real-Life Application (Campus Inspection):**
The Aliengo robot, equipped with ZSL-RPPO and a depth camera, successfully conducted a 2-kilometer campus inspection over approximately an hour. The robot autonomously navigated various terrains, including stairs, slopes, stone pathways, grassy fields, and cobblestone areas, without manual assistance (Fig. 8), demonstrating the practical utility and robustness of the approach in a sustained real-world operation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The ZSL-RPPO research contributes to the field of quadrupedal robotics and reinforcement learning in several notable ways, with potential impacts on the development and deployment of autonomous legged systems.

**A. Enhanced Robustness and Generalization:** The primary significance lies in the demonstration of a robust and general end-to-end control policy that enables quadrupedal robots to navigate a wide range of challenging and previously unseen terrains. The systematic application of extensive domain randomization, coupled with the RPPO algorithm, addresses critical sim-to-real challenges, making the learned policies more resilient to real-world uncertainties, including variations in physical parameters, actuator characteristics, and sensor noise.

**B. True Zero-Shot Sim-to-Real Transfer:** The achievement of zero-shot transfer—deploying simulation-trained policies directly onto physical robots without any real-world fine-tuning—represents a notable advancement. This capability significantly reduces the often-prohibitive costs and time associated with real-robot experimentation and adaptation. It enhances the efficiency and scalability of deploying robotic solutions, particularly in scenarios where rapid deployment or operation across multiple, varied robot platforms is required.

**C. Advancement in Reinforcement Learning for Partially Observable Environments:** The introduction of the Recurrent Proximal Policy Optimization (RPPO) algorithm provides a direct and effective method for training recurrent neural networks in partially observable environments. By sidestepping the teacher-student imitation learning paradigm, RPPO mitigates the performance degradation commonly observed with student policies, offering a more stable and scalable training approach for visuomotor control tasks where complete state information is unavailable to the agent.

**D. Improved Hardware Consistency and Reliability:** The findings regarding domain transfer consistency on different hardware units suggest that ZSL-RPPO policies are less susceptible to individual robot variations and miscalibrations. This robustness to hardware inconsistencies can lead to more reliable and predictable robot performance across a fleet of robots, reducing the need for individual calibration or specialized tuning for each unit.

**E. Practical Applicability in Real-World Scenarios:** The successful demonstration of autonomous navigation during a 2-kilometer campus inspection showcases the practical readiness of the ZSL-RPPO approach. This indicates that the technology has matured to a point where it can be considered for real-life applications such as industrial inspection, surveillance, logistics in unstructured environments, and search-and-rescue operations where robust and autonomous locomotion is paramount.

**F. Foundation for Future Research:** The work establishes a strong foundation for future research in several directions. The ability to handle challenging terrains using zero-shot learning opens avenues for exploring even more complex environments, such as discontinuous terrains like gullies, as mentioned by the authors. Furthermore, the principles of extensive domain randomization and direct recurrent policy optimization can be applied or extended to other robotic platforms and control tasks facing similar sim-to-real transfer challenges.
