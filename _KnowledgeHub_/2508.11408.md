# On-Policy RL Meets Off-Policy Experts: Harmonizing Supervised Fine-Tuning and Reinforcement Learning via Dynamic Weighting

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Alibaba Group developed CHORD, a framework that unifies Supervised Fine-Tuning and Reinforcement Learning for Large Language Models through dynamic weighting, employing both global and token-wise control. This approach facilitates stable and efficient learning, leading to superior performance on mathematical reasoning and tool-use tasks compared to sequential training methods.

## Problem

- Sequential Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT)-then-Reinforcement Learning (RL) pipelines often yield suboptimal performance compared to pure RL, indicating issues with integrating these paradigms.
- Applying SFT to instruction-tuned Large Language Models (LLMs) with established policies leads to a "shift-readapt-overfit" progression, causing initial performance degradation and eventual overfitting to limited expert data.
- Existing integration strategies struggle with the distributional shift between an LLM's established policy and off-policy expert data, leading to instability and hindering effective knowledge transfer.

## Method

- **CHORD** (Controllable Harmonization of On- and Off-Policy Reinforcement Learning via ==Dynamic Weighting==) reconfigures SFT as a dynamically weighted auxiliary objective within the continuous on-policy RL process, rather than a separate sequential stage.
- A global coefficient (μ) with a dynamic decay schedule is introduced to holistically manage the trade-off between RL and SFT objectives, smoothly transitioning the learning focus from off-policy imitation to on-policy exploration.
- A token-wise weighting function (ϕ(yt∗;πθ)=pt(1−pt)ϕ(y^*_t; \pi_θ) = p_t(1 - p_t)ϕ(yt∗;πθ)=pt(1−pt)) is applied to the SFT loss, selectively down-weighting learning signals for expert tokens that are either highly probable (to prevent overconfidence) or extremely improbable (to avoid disruptive policy shifts).

## Results

- CHORD-ϕ consistently achieved the highest performance across mathematical reasoning (e.g., +4.1 on AMC, +1.1 on AIME24) and tool-use benchmarks (**78.5%** BFCL), outperforming conventional SFT-then-RL and other baselines.
- The framework maintained balanced policy entropy, prevented premature collapse, and showed stable, continuous reward increases, demonstrating a more robust and efficient learning process.
- CHORD proved effective and robust across various settings, including different expert data sources, non-verifiable domains, weaker policy models, and diverse model architectures like Mixture-of-Experts, indicating broad applicability.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The observed "shift-readapt-overfit" progression highlights a fundamental instability in integrating off-policy expert data into LLMs with existing strong policies.
> - Dynamically controlled integration of off-policy data, through both global and fine-grained mechanisms, is crucial for stable learning and avoiding policy disruption or entropy collapse in LLMs.
> - Weighting SFT signals based on the model's uncertainty for a given expert token effectively creates a "learning sweet spot," promoting the integration of novel yet not excessively divergent expert knowledge.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Large Language Models (LLMs) have advanced significantly, partly due to two post-training paradigms: Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) and Reinforcement Learning (RL). SFT involves mimicking patterns from high-quality expert demonstrations, which is effective but sensitive to data quality and quantity. It can also be susceptible to exposure bias and may struggle to generalize beyond memorization. In contrast, RL encourages LLMs to explore actively and learn from direct feedback on their own generations, potentially leading to better generalization. However, RL exploration can be inefficient, sometimes resulting in policy degradation due to entropy collapse or over-exploitation of suboptimal strategies.

A common approach to integrate the strengths of SFT and RL is the sequential SFT-then-RL paradigm. This method aims to use expert patterns learned in SFT to guide RL exploration, and subsequently use on-policy RL to mitigate SFT's exposure bias and prevent overfitting. However, recent empirical observations, including those presented in this paper, indicate that this sequential approach does not consistently outperform pure RL. This suggests that the separate SFT stage can disrupt established response patterns and induce overfitting to expert data, particularly when the expert data significantly diverges from the model's existing policy.

Existing research has explored various ways to combine on-policy and off-policy data in RL for LLMs. These methods include direct data mixing (e.g., SimpleMix, LUFFY), using expert data as generation guidance (e.g., UFT, BREAD), or interleaving RL and SFT steps on predefined or adaptive schedules. A notable distinction of the current work is its focus on fine-tuning *instruction-tuned models* that already possess well-developed response patterns, rather than *base models* with nascent policies. This scenario introduces greater challenges in managing the distributional shift between the model's established policy and external expert data, exacerbating the off-policy correction problem. The paper addresses this specific gap by proposing a method for controllable and harmonious integration of these two paradigms, aiming to maintain training stability and leverage expert knowledge without disrupting the model's existing capabilities.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this work stems from the observed limitations of integrating Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) and Reinforcement Learning (RL) in current post-training pipelines for Large Language Models (LLMs). While both SFT and RL are effective, their sequential application (SFT-then-RL) frequently yields suboptimal performance compared to pure RL. This counter-intuitive outcome indicates that direct or rigid integration strategies can be detrimental.

The authors identify a specific problematic dynamic, termed the "shift-readapt-overfit" progression, which occurs when an LLM with an established policy is trained on off-policy expert data during SFT. This progression consists of three phases:
- **Policy Shift:** Initially, the model's performance declines. This degradation is attributed to a sudden shift in policy as the model attempts to conform to expert demonstrations whose response patterns significantly differ from its own. This disruption is compounded by exposure bias, where the model struggles with self-generated contexts after being trained exclusively on ground-truth expert data.
- **Readaptation:** As SFT continues, the model's policy begins to integrate expert response patterns, and its performance gradually recovers. This phase involves the model adapting to the expert's style and potentially mitigating some aspects of exposure bias.
- **Overfitting:** Prolonged training on a limited set of expert data eventually leads to overfitting. This results in a decline in generalization capabilities and a reduction in output diversity, which can subsequently restrict the model's exploratory capacity during the subsequent RL optimization phase.

These observations highlight the difficulty in precisely controlling the influence of off-policy expert data and the inherent fragility of the SFT-then-RL paradigm, especially when there is a substantial divergence between the expert's patterns and the model's established ones. The existing fixed or binary integration schedules often fail to manage this dynamic effectively.

Given these challenges, the key objectives of this research are:
- To provide a systematic analysis of the training dynamics when off-policy expert knowledge is integrated into LLMs with established policies, specifically identifying and characterizing the "shift-readapt-overfit" progression.
- To unify SFT and RL conceptually by framing SFT as a dynamically weighted auxiliary objective within the continuous on-policy RL process, rather than a separate, discrete stage.
- To propose a novel framework, CHORD (Controllable Harmonization of On- and Off-Policy Reinforcement Learning via Dynamic Weighting), which offers a more flexible and stable mechanism for integrating these two learning paradigms.
- To develop a dual-control mechanism within CHORD:
 - A global coefficient (μ) designed to dynamically manage the overall influence of off-policy expert data throughout the training process, enabling a smoother transition from imitation to exploration.
 - A token-wise weighting function (ϕ(·)) that provides granular control, selectively down-weighting tokens from expert data that are either highly probable (to prevent overconfidence/entropy collapse) or extremely improbable (to avoid disruption), thereby promoting on-policy exploration and mitigating policy shifts.
- To empirically demonstrate that CHORD achieves a stable and efficient learning process, resulting in significant performance improvements over conventional SFT-then-RL and other existing baselines across various practical tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology, CHORD, reframes SFT as a dynamically weighted auxiliary objective within the on-policy RL process, moving away from a separate sequential stage. This unification is achieved through a combined loss function:
$L_{Hybrid}(\theta) = (1 - μ)L_{GRPO}(\theta) + μL_{SFT}(\theta)$

Here, $L_{GRPO}(\theta)$ represents the Group Relative Policy Optimization loss, a PPO-style clipped surrogate objective commonly used in RL for LLMs. It involves sampling multiple responses, evaluating them with a reward function, and updating the policy to maximize expected reward, utilizing token-wise Importance Sampling (IS) ratios. The formulation notably omits a KL divergence term, consistent with some recent RL for LLM studies. $L_{SFT}(\theta)$ is the standard negative log-likelihood loss, minimizing the discrepancy between the model's generated tokens and the expert responses.

CHORD integrates a dual-control mechanism to manage the influence of off-policy expert data:

#### 3.1. Global Coefficient (μ) for Holistic Control

The coefficient $\mu \in$ directly controls the trade-off between the RL and SFT objectives. A larger $\mu$ emphasizes SFT, while a smaller $\mu$ prioritizes RL. Instead of a fixed $\mu$ or a rigid binary switch (as in SFT-then-RL), CHORD employs a dynamic decay schedule for $\mu$. Training begins with a relatively high $\mu$ to encourage learning from expert data, and $\mu$ gradually decreases as training progresses. This aims to smoothly transition the learning focus from off-policy imitation to on-policy exploration, analogous to scheduled sampling techniques that mix expert and self-generated data. This decay schedule is designed to bridge the distributional gap and mitigate the disruptive "shift-readapt" phase observed with static SFT integration. While this dynamic $\mu$ shows improvement, observations suggest it can still lead to some initial disruption and potentially indiscriminate adoption of expert patterns, motivating the need for fine-grained control.

#### 3.2. Token-wise Weighting Function (ϕ(·)) for Fine-grained Stability

To address the limitations of global control, CHORD introduces a token-wise weighting function, $\phi(y^*_t; \pi_\theta)$, which modulates the SFT loss. The original $L_{SFT}(\theta)$ is replaced by $L_{SFT-\phi}(\theta)$:
$L_{SFT-\phi}(\theta) = -E_{(x,y^*) \sim D_{SFT}} \left[ \sum_{t=1}^{|y^*|} \phi(y^*_t; \pi_\theta) \cdot \log \pi_\theta(y^*_t | x, y^*_{<t}) \right]$

The weighting function is defined as $\phi(y^*_t; \pi_\theta) = p_t(1 - p_t)$, where $p_t = \pi_\theta(y^*_t | x, y^*_{<t})$ is the policy's probability of generating the expert token $y^*_t$. This function forms a parabolic curve that peaks at $p_t = 0.5$ and approaches zero as $p_t$ nears 0 or 1.
- **Purpose:** This design down-weights the learning signal for tokens at both extremes of the probability spectrum. Tokens that are highly probable (close to 1) receive low weight, preventing the model from becoming overconfident and preserving its exploratory capacity (avoiding entropy collapse). Tokens that are extremely improbable (close to 0) also receive low weight, preventing disruptive shifts from off-policy data that strongly conflict with the current policy.
- **Information-theoretic interpretation:** $p_t(1 - p_t)$ can be viewed as a measure of the policy's uncertainty for generating token $y^*_t$. By weighting with this term, CHORD biases learning towards tokens where the policy is most uncertain, focusing off-policy learning on novel but not excessively divergent tokens. This creates a "learning sweet spot" that is informative without being disruptive.
- **Contrast with Importance Sampling (IS):** Standard IS re-weights based on probability ratios, often aggressively reinforcing existing high-probability tokens and limiting exploration. $\phi(\cdot)$ differs by also down-weighting highly probable tokens, preventing overconfidence and maintaining policy entropy.

#### 3.3. Experimental Setup

Experiments are conducted on two distinct categories of tasks:
- **Mathematical Reasoning:** Utilizing the OpenR1-Math-220k dataset for SFT and RL, and evaluated on in-domain benchmarks (AIME24, AIME25, AMC) and general reasoning (MMLU-Pro). The policy model is Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, with Deepseek-R1 as the expert, chosen for its divergent response patterns.
- **Tool-use Tasks:** Using single-turn instances from the ToolAce dataset, evaluated on BFCL. The policy model is LLaMA3.2-3B-Instruct, with expert trajectories generated by Deepseek-R1.
Baselines for comparison include the original model, SFT-only variants (light and best), RL-only (GRPO), sequential SFT+RL, and other unified approaches like LUFFY and SASR. Custom hierarchical and binary reward functions are employed for math and tool-use, respectively. Evaluations consider accuracy, response length, and analyses of entropy loss and reward curves. Additional experiments explored varying expert sources, non-verifiable domains, weaker policy models, and diverse model architectures.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several key findings across various experiments, demonstrating the effectiveness and characteristics of the CHORD framework:

#### 4.1. Validation of the "Shift-Readapt-Overfit" Progression

Empirical observations confirm the "shift-readapt-overfit" progression when SFT is applied to an instruction-tuned model. Initial SFT leads to a performance drop (policy shift), followed by recovery as the model adapts (readapt), and then a decline due to overfitting to limited expert data. This validates the core motivation for CHORD’s design, highlighting the instability inherent in rigid SFT integration.

#### 4.2. Overall Performance and Superiority of CHORD

- **CHORD-ϕ** consistently achieved the highest performance across all evaluated tasks. On mathematical reasoning benchmarks, it demonstrated significant improvements over the strong SFT-best+RL baseline (e.g., +4.1 on AMC, +1.1 on AIME24, +0.9 on AIME25) and also maintained higher general reasoning capabilities (MMLU-Pro accuracy of 56.2% vs. 51.3%). For tool-use tasks, CHORD-ϕ achieved the highest overall BFCL score of 78.5%.
- **CHORD-μ**, employing only the dynamic global coefficient, also outperformed the SFT-then-RL paradigm and other baselines. This indicates that a smooth transition from off-policy to on-policy learning, rather than a rigid switch, is beneficial.
- The **SFT-then-RL** paradigm exhibited inconsistent performance, with the optimal timing for the SFT-to-RL transition being task-dependent (e.g., SFT-best+RL better for math, SFT-light+RL better for tool-use). This underscores the fragility and tuning complexity of sequential approaches.

#### 4.3. Response Patterns Analysis

- Expert data (DeepSeek-R1) typically exhibited verbose response patterns. SFT-only models tended to mimic this verbosity.
- Pure RL adjusted response length based on task, favoring more detailed reasoning for math and concise sequences for tool-use.
- CHORD-μ showed an initial increase in response length, aligning with expert patterns, before gradually converging to a lower length as on-policy training progressed.
- **CHORD-ϕ** demonstrated a more nuanced behavior. It produced more comprehensive mathematical reasoning (average 2,444 tokens) while generating concise and efficient responses for tool-use tasks (average 120 tokens). This suggested that the token-wise weighting enabled selective integration of expert patterns in a task-specific manner, avoiding wholesale adoption of potentially suboptimal stylistic elements. Qualitative analysis supported this, showing CHORD-ϕ developing structured reasoning combined with expert-like verification strategies.

#### 4.4. Analysis of μ and ϕ(·)

- **Dynamic μ vs. Fixed μ:** Experiments confirmed that a dynamic decay schedule for μ consistently yielded better performance than fixed μ values, which often resulted in suboptimal outcomes, sometimes even underperforming pure RL. This emphasized the importance of dynamically adjusting the influence of off-policy data.
- **Training Dynamics of CHORD-ϕ:**
 - **Entropy Loss:** CHORD-ϕ maintained a balanced policy entropy, preventing both the premature collapse seen with aggressive importance sampling methods and the large spikes indicative of instability with unweighted off-policy data. This demonstrated its ability to promote exploration without disruption.
 - **Reward Curve:** CHORD-ϕ achieved a stable and continuous increase in rewards, significantly outperforming pure RL.
- **Tuning μ with ϕ(·):** When ϕ(·) was utilized, the specific decay schedule for μ became less critical. CHORD-ϕ proved robust even with a fixed μ value, simplifying its practical application. This indicates that the token-wise control provided stability, reducing sensitivity to the global hyperparameter.
- **Principle of ϕ(·):** The $p_t(1 - p_t)$ formulation effectively implements the principle of down-weighting learning signals for tokens at probability extremes. Ablation studies on various ϕ variants confirmed its robustness and effectiveness.

#### 4.5. Further Analysis

- **Varying Expert Data Source:** CHORD methods consistently outperformed baselines regardless of the expert source (DeepSeek-R1 vs. Qwen2.5-72B-Instruct). Methods more reliant on imitation (SFT+RL, CHORD-μ) showed greater gains when the expert's style was more aligned with the policy model, validating the impact of pattern shift.
- **Non-verifiable Domains:** CHORD generalized effectively to the RaR-Medicine dataset (medical QA), with both CHORD-μ and CHORD-ϕ significantly outperforming pure RL, demonstrating broader applicability.
- **Weaker Policy Models:** For a weaker Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct model, naive SFT led to performance collapse, and SFT+RL was unstable. CHORD-ϕ provided a robust objective, maintaining stability and improving performance, suggesting its particular advantage when models struggle with imitation from limited expert data.
- **Diverse Architectures:** CHORD-ϕ proved effective on a Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) model (Phi-mini-MoE-instruct), boosting performance where naive SFT collapsed, indicating architecture-agnostic efficacy.
- **Instruction vs. Base Models:** The work highlights that training instruction-tuned models with established policies presents greater challenges (e.g., distributional mismatch) than training base models. CHORD is designed for and excels in this more demanding instruction model scenario, where "zero-RL" methods often struggle.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research makes several contributions to the field of Large Language Model (LLM) post-training, with potential implications for enhancing model capabilities and stability.

The work identifies and systematically analyzes the "shift-readapt-overfit" progression, a key challenge arising from integrating off-policy expert data into LLMs with established response patterns. This characterization provides a clearer understanding of why prevalent sequential SFT-then-RL paradigms can yield suboptimal results, thus framing a critical problem for the community.

The proposed CHORD framework offers a novel conceptual unification of SFT and RL, reframing SFT as a dynamically weighted auxiliary objective within the on-policy RL process. This integrated approach, rather than a separate-stage pipeline, represents a shift in how these powerful paradigms can interact. The dual-control mechanism, comprising a global coefficient $\mu$ and a token-wise weighting function $\phi(\cdot)$, provides both holistic and fine-grained control over the influence of expert data. This design is shown to promote a harmonious integration, selectively absorbing expert knowledge while preserving the model's exploratory capacity and established patterns.

Empirical evidence across diverse tasks, including complex mathematical reasoning and practical tool-use, demonstrates that CHORD significantly outperforms existing baselines and sequential pipelines. This performance improvement is notable for its consistency and robustness across different expert data sources, policy model scales, and architectures, including Mixture-of-Experts models. The framework's ability to generalize to non-verifiable domains further expands its potential applicability. CHORD's capacity to maintain training stability, particularly for instruction-tuned models and even weaker policy models that typically struggle with naive SFT, addresses a critical practical concern in LLM development.

The research's findings on task-specific response patterns (e.g., verbose for math, concise for tool-use) suggest that CHORD enables models to adaptively integrate stylistic elements from experts, rather than simply mimicking them. This indicates a more sophisticated learning process that goes beyond rote imitation.

The implications of this work are manifold. It offers a more stable, efficient, and flexible paradigm for fine-tuning LLMs, potentially reducing the need for extensive hyperparameter tuning and careful timing inherent in previous sequential approaches. By mitigating policy disruption and overfitting, CHORD can contribute to the development of more capable and reliable LLMs for a wider range of applications.

Future research avenues highlighted by the authors include exploring further algorithmic advancements to stabilize RL training dynamics, developing more effective adaptive tuning functions for $\mu$ and $\phi(\cdot)$, gaining a deeper understanding of how diverse chain-of-thought (CoT) patterns influence learning, and extending the unified paradigm to incorporate heterogeneous expert data sources. The release of the implementation aims to facilitate further exploration and development in these directions.
