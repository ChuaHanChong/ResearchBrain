# On-Policy RL Meets Off-Policy Experts: Harmonizing Supervised Fine-Tuning and Reinforcement Learning via Dynamic Weighting

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Alibaba Group developed CHORD, a framework that unifies Supervised Fine-Tuning and Reinforcement Learning for Large Language Models through dynamic weighting, employing both global and token-wise control. This approach facilitates stable and efficient learning, leading to superior performance on mathematical reasoning and tool-use tasks compared to sequential training methods.

## Problem

- Sequential Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT)-then-Reinforcement Learning (RL) pipelines often yield suboptimal performance compared to pure RL, indicating issues with integrating these paradigms.
- Applying SFT to instruction-tuned Large Language Models (LLMs) with established policies leads to a "shift-readapt-overfit" progression, causing initial performance degradation and eventual overfitting to limited expert data.
- Existing integration strategies struggle with the distributional shift between an LLM's established policy and off-policy expert data, leading to instability and hindering effective knowledge transfer.

## Method

- **CHORD** (Controllable Harmonization of On- and Off-Policy Reinforcement Learning via ==Dynamic Weighting==) reconfigures SFT as a dynamically weighted auxiliary objective within the continuous on-policy RL process, rather than a separate sequential stage.
- A global coefficient (μ) with a dynamic decay schedule is introduced to holistically manage the trade-off between RL and SFT objectives, smoothly transitioning the learning focus from off-policy imitation to on-policy exploration.
- A token-wise weighting function (ϕ(yt∗;πθ)=pt(1−pt)ϕ(y^*_t; \pi_θ) = p_t(1 - p_t)ϕ(yt∗;πθ)=pt(1−pt)) is applied to the SFT loss, selectively down-weighting learning signals for expert tokens that are either highly probable (to prevent overconfidence) or extremely improbable (to avoid disruptive policy shifts).

## Results

- CHORD-ϕ consistently achieved the highest performance across mathematical reasoning (e.g., +4.1 on AMC, +1.1 on AIME24) and tool-use benchmarks (**78.5%** BFCL), outperforming conventional SFT-then-RL and other baselines.
- The framework maintained balanced policy entropy, prevented premature collapse, and showed stable, continuous reward increases, demonstrating a more robust and efficient learning process.
- CHORD proved effective and robust across various settings, including different expert data sources, non-verifiable domains, weaker policy models, and diverse model architectures like Mixture-of-Experts, indicating broad applicability.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The observed "shift-readapt-overfit" progression highlights a fundamental instability in integrating off-policy expert data into LLMs with existing strong policies.
> - Dynamically controlled integration of off-policy data, through both global and fine-grained mechanisms, is crucial for stable learning and avoiding policy disruption or entropy collapse in LLMs.
> - Weighting SFT signals based on the model's uncertainty for a given expert token effectively creates a "learning sweet spot," promoting the integration of novel yet not excessively divergent expert knowledge.
