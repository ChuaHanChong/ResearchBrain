# Predictive Inverse Dynamics Models are Scalable Learners for Robotic Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> This paper introduces an end-to-end predictive inverse dynamics model that unifies visual prediction and action control for robotic manipulation

## Problem

- Current approaches either focus solely on behavior cloning from robot data or visual pre-training, but don't effectively combine both
- Two-stage approaches that separate visual prediction from action control lack synergy between vision and action
- Existing methods struggle with generalization across novel objects, lighting conditions and environments

## Method

- Proposes **Seer**, an end-to-end model that integrates ==conditional visual foresight== with ==inverse dynamics prediction==
- Uses a ==transformer architecture== with ==unidirectional attention== to process visual states and actions together, allowing action tokens to incorporate both past and future predictive information
- Pre-trains on large-scale robot datasets and fine-tunes on downstream tasks

## Results

- Achieves **13%** improvement on LIBERO-LONG benchmark and **43%** improvement on real-world tasks
- **21%** improvement on CALVIN ABC-D benchmark, setting new state-of-the-art with average length of **4.28**
- Shows robust generalization to novel objects, lighting conditions, and environments, with strong performance even under limited fine-tuning data

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - End-to-end training of visual prediction and action control, where future visual state prediction helps guide action selection, leads to better performance than separated approaches
> - Pre-training on large robot datasets improves generalization capabilities
> - Unidirectional attention enables deep integration of past and future information

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

#### 1.1. Introduction

Robotic manipulation remains one of the most challenging frontiers in artificial intelligence and robotics. While humans effortlessly coordinate vision and motor control to manipulate objects, robots struggle to achieve similar capabilities. Recent research has made significant strides in this area, but creating robots that can reliably manipulate objects in diverse, real-world environments remains a difficult challenge.

The paper "Predictive Inverse Dynamics Models Are Scalable Learners for Robotic Manipulation" introduces Seer, a novel approach that bridges the gap between visual perception and motor control for robots. By developing a model that predicts future visual states and uses these predictions to guide action generation, Seer represents a significant advancement in creating more capable and generalizable robotic manipulation systems.

#### 1.2. Current Approaches and Their Limitations

Current approaches to robotic manipulation can be broadly categorized into two main paradigms:

**Action-Centric Learning**: This approach, exemplified by models like RT-1, Octo, and OpenVLA, focuses on behavior cloning from large datasets of robot actions. While these methods scale well with data, they often fail to fully leverage the rich visual and temporal information available in robot demonstrations.

**Vision-Centric Learning**: These approaches, like R3M, MVP, UniPI, and CLOVER, emphasize pre-training visual representations or generative world models using large visual datasets. However, they typically operate in a two-stage manner, where vision processing is separated from control, potentially hindering the seamless integration of perception and action.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

#### 2.1. The Gap Between Existing Paradigms

Both approaches have shown promise but face limitations in creating truly robust and generalizable manipulation systems. Action-centric models may struggle with novel visual scenarios, while vision-centric approaches might fail to translate their visual understanding into effective actions.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

#### 3.1. Predictive Inverse Dynamics Models

The paper introduces a new paradigm called Predictive Inverse Dynamics Models (PIDM), which aims to bridge the gap between visual perception and motor control. The key insight behind PIDM is that by predicting future visual states and using these predictions to guide action generation, robots can develop a more intuitive understanding of how their actions affect the world.

This approach mimics the way humans coordinate vision and manipulation: we constantly predict the visual outcomes of our actions and adjust our movements accordingly. By integrating visual prediction and action generation in a unified framework, PIDM creates a closed loop between perception and action that enables more robust and generalizable manipulation.

The PIDM approach offers several advantages over existing methods:

1. It leverages visual prediction to guide action generation, creating a more intuitive mapping between perception and control.
2. It integrates visual processing and action generation in an end-to-end framework, allowing for joint optimization.
3. It can leverage large-scale datasets for pre-training, enhancing generalization to novel scenarios.

#### 3.2. Seer: Architecture and Design

Seer, the implementation of PIDM presented in the paper, is a transformer-based architecture that processes visual observations, language instructions, and robot state information to predict actions. The model consists of three main components:

**Multi-Modal Encoder**: This component processes different input modalities, including:
- RGB images from the robot's camera
- Language instructions (when available)
- Robot state information (joint positions, gripper state, etc.)

The encoder uses a transformer architecture to integrate these different input modalities into a unified representation.

**Conditional Visual Foresight Module**: This module predicts future visual states (RGB images) based on the current state and goal. It uses a special foresight token ([FRS]) that aggregates information from the multi-modal encoder to generate visual predictions.

**Inverse Dynamics Module**: This component takes the predicted visual states and generates appropriate actions. It uses a special action token ([INV]) that attends to both the current state and the predicted future states to generate actions that will achieve the desired visual outcomes.

A key innovation in Seer's architecture is the unidirectional attention mask that allows the action token ([INV]) to attend to both past observations and future predicted visual states. This creates a closed loop between visual prediction and action generation, enabling the model to generate actions that are directly informed by predicted visual outcomes.

#### 3.3. Training Methodology

Seer employs a sophisticated training methodology that combines pre-training on large datasets with fine-tuning on specific tasks:

**Pre-training on Large-Scale Datasets**: The model is pre-trained on large robotic datasets like DROID, which contains diverse manipulation demonstrations across different robots and tasks. This pre-training helps the model learn general manipulation skills and visual-motor coordination.

**End-to-End Training**: Unlike two-stage approaches that separate visual processing from action generation, Seer is trained end-to-end, allowing for joint optimization of visual prediction and action generation. This end-to-end training is crucial for developing a tightly integrated visual-motor system.

**Diverse Data Augmentation**: During training, the model is exposed to diverse data augmentations, including variations in lighting, camera angles, and object appearances. This helps improve the model's robustness to variations in the environment.

The paper demonstrates that this training methodology results in a model that can effectively leverage large datasets for pre-training while still adapting to specific downstream tasks with limited data.

```python
# Pseudocode for Seer's forward pass
def seer_forward(rgb_images, robot_state, language_instruction=None):
    # Encode inputs using the multi-modal encoder
    rgb_features = vit_encoder(rgb_images)
    state_features = mlp_encoder(robot_state)

    if language_instruction:
        lang_features = language_encoder(language_instruction)
        combined_features = concat([rgb_features, state_features, lang_features])
    else:
        combined_features = concat([rgb_features, state_features])

    # Add special tokens for foresight and action
    foresight_token = create_token("[FRS]")
    action_token = create_token("[INV]")

    # Create attention mask that allows action token to attend to foresight token
    attention_mask = create_unidirectional_mask(
        allows_inv_to_attend_frs=True
    )

    # Process through transformer with the special attention mask
    transformer_output = transformer(
        tokens=concat([combined_features, foresight_token, action_token]),
        attention_mask=attention_mask
    )

    # Extract outputs for visual prediction and action generation
    foresight_output = extract_token_output(transformer_output, "[FRS]")
    action_output = extract_token_output(transformer_output, "[INV]")

    # Generate visual prediction and action
    predicted_image = visual_decoder(foresight_output)
    predicted_action = action_decoder(action_output)

    return predicted_image, predicted_action
```

### 4. Main Findings and Results

#### 4.1. Experimental Results

The paper evaluates Seer on several benchmarks, including simulation tasks (LIBERO-LONG and CALVIN ABC-D) and real-world robot manipulation tasks. The results demonstrate that Seer consistently outperforms previous state-of-the-art methods:

**LIBERO-LONG**: Seer achieves a 13% improvement in success rate compared to previous state-of-the-art methods. The results also show that pre-training significantly improves performance, especially in low-data regimes.

**CALVIN ABC-D**: On this benchmark, Seer achieves a 21% improvement in success rate and sets a new state-of-the-art average task length of 4.28. This demonstrates the model's ability to perform complex, multi-step tasks.

**Data Efficiency**: An important finding is that Seer is remarkably data-efficient. Even with only 10-20% of the training data, the pre-trained Seer model can match or exceed the performance of previous methods trained on the full dataset.

**Scaling Properties**: The paper also investigates how Seer scales with model size and data. The results show that larger models (with more parameters) consistently perform better, and that pre-training provides significant benefits across all model sizes.

The ablation studies further validate the importance of the key components of Seer:

1. **End-to-End Training**: Comparing to a two-stage version of PIDM, the end-to-end approach leads to significantly better performance.
2. **Visual Prediction**: Removing the visual prediction component results in worse performance, confirming that predicting future visual states helps generate better actions.
3. **Unidirectional Attention Mask**: The specific attention mask that allows the action token to attend to the foresight token is crucial for optimal performance.

#### 4.2. Real-World Performance

One of the most impressive aspects of Seer is its performance on real-world robotic manipulation tasks. The paper demonstrates that Seer can successfully perform a variety of tasks with a real robot arm:

**Task Diversity**: Seer successfully performs a diverse range of tasks, including flipping bowls, stacking cups, pick-and-place operations, and board wiping.

**Precision Manipulation**: The model demonstrates the ability to perform precision tasks like pressing small buttons and inserting objects into specific slots:

**Robustness to Environmental Variations**: The paper evaluates Seer's robustness to various environmental variations, including:

1. **Novel Objects**: Seer maintains high performance even when manipulating objects not seen during training.

2. **Background Variations**: The model performs well even with different backgrounds.

3. **Lighting Conditions**: Seer shows robustness to changes in lighting conditions.

4. **Multiple Objects**: The model can handle scenes with multiple objects, demonstrating its ability to process complex visual inputs.

These results demonstrate that Seer not only performs well in controlled environments but also maintains high performance under realistic, variable conditions. This robustness is crucial for deploying robots in real-world settings.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

#### 5.1. Key Insights and Implications

The paper provides several key insights that have important implications for robotic manipulation:

**Vision-Action Integration**: The success of Seer demonstrates the importance of tightly integrating visual perception and action generation. By predicting future visual states and using these predictions to guide action generation, Seer creates a closed loop between perception and control that mirrors human visual-motor coordination.

**End-to-End Learning**: The superior performance of the end-to-end trained model compared to two-stage approaches highlights the importance of joint optimization of visual processing and action generation.

**Scalable Learning**: Seer demonstrates strong scaling properties, with performance improving as model size and pre-training data increase. This suggests that the PIDM approach can effectively leverage large-scale datasets and computational resources.

**Generalization**: The model's ability to generalize to novel objects, backgrounds, and lighting conditions indicates that the PIDM approach can develop robust representations that transfer well to new scenarios.

**Data Efficiency**: Perhaps most importantly, Seer demonstrates remarkable data efficiency, requiring significantly less task-specific data to achieve high performance. This is crucial for practical robot deployment, where collecting task-specific data can be expensive and time-consuming.

#### 5.2. Future Directions

While Seer represents a significant advancement in robotic manipulation, the paper also points to several promising directions for future research:

**Multi-Modal Inputs**: Extending Seer to incorporate additional sensory modalities, such as tactile feedback or force sensors, could further enhance its manipulation capabilities.

**Long-Horizon Planning**: Building on Seer's visual prediction capabilities to enable longer-horizon planning and more complex sequential tasks is a natural next step.

**Multi-Robot Learning**: Investigating how Seer's architecture can be adapted for multi-robot scenarios, where robots need to coordinate their actions.

**Human-Robot Interaction**: Exploring how Seer can be extended to better understand and respond to human instructions and demonstrations.

**Reinforcement Learning Integration**: Combining Seer's predictive capabilities with reinforcement learning could enable robots to learn from their own experiences and improve over time.

In conclusion, Seer and the broader PIDM paradigm represent a significant step forward in robotic manipulation. By integrating visual prediction and action generation in a unified framework, Seer demonstrates capabilities that are more robust, generalizable, and data-efficient than previous approaches. As this research direction continues to develop, we can expect even more capable robotic manipulation systems that can operate effectively in complex, real-world environments.
