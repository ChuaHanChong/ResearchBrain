# How to pick the domain randomization parameters for sim-to-real transfer of reinforcement learning policies?

> [!summary] Summary
> This research introduces a method to systematically optimize domain randomization parameters for reinforcement learning policies, addressing the previous reliance on manual tuning. It demonstrates that optimizing these parameters significantly improves policy performance in a target environment, achieving 80-102% higher average performance in sim-to-sim transfer experiments for Hopper and Walker tasks compared to non-optimized settings.

## Problem

- Transferring policies learned in simulation to real-world robots (sim-to-real transfer) remains a significant challenge due to data inefficiency and the inherent gap between simulation and reality.
- Domain randomization (DR), a technique to bridge the sim-to-real gap, requires careful selection of parameters that define the distribution of simulated environments.
- These domain randomization parameters are typically chosen manually by human experts, relying on intuition or trial-and-error, which lacks a systematic and principled foundation.

## Method

- The problem of selecting ==domain randomization== parameters (φ) is formulated as a ==bilevel optimization== problem, maximizing real-world policy performance while a policy is trained within the φ-parameterized simulated environments.
- The **Cross Entropy Method (CEM)**, a gradient-free stochastic optimization algorithm, is utilized for the outer loop to optimize the domain randomization parameters (φ).
- **Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO)**, an on-policy reinforcement learning algorithm, is used for the inner loop to train the policy within the randomized simulated environments generated according to the current φ.

## Results

- Policies trained with optimized domain randomization parameters (φ) achieved an average of **102%** higher performance in the Mujoco Hopper environment during sim-to-sim transfer compared to policies using initial, non-optimized parameters.
- For the Walker environment, optimized parameters led to an average performance **80%** higher than policies trained with initial parameters in sim-to-sim transfer.
- Even in less favorable runs, the minimum observed performance improvement was **28%** for Hopper and **19%** for Walker, demonstrating consistent benefits from the optimization approach.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The choice of domain randomization parameters (φ) significantly influences the performance of reinforcement learning policies when transferred to a target real-world (or sim-to-sim equivalent) environment.
> - Systematically optimizing these φ parameters using a bilevel optimization approach can lead to substantial improvements in sim-to-real transfer performance, moving beyond heuristic approaches.
> - Framing the problem as a bilevel optimization, with CEM for the outer loop and PPO for the inner loop, provides a robust and data-driven method for configuring simulated training environments.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Reinforcement Learning (RL) has demonstrated considerable success in developing complex behaviors, particularly within simulated environments. However, a significant challenge in the field of robotics and artificial intelligence is transferring policies learned in simulation to real-world systems (sim-to-real transfer). The data inefficiency inherent in many RL algorithms often bottlenecks their performance when applied directly to physical robots, where data collection can be costly and time-consuming.

Domain randomization (DR) has emerged as a promising approach to address the sim-to-real gap. This technique involves training an RL policy on a diverse distribution of simulated environments, with the objective that if this distribution encompasses sufficient variability, the trained policy will generalize effectively to the actual physical world. Prior successful applications of domain randomization have been observed in tasks such as object pushing and dexterous manipulation.

A key challenge within domain randomization is the selection of the parameters that define the distribution of simulated environments. Historically, these parameters have been chosen manually, often relying on expert intuition, domain knowledge, or extensive trial-and-error processes. This approach lacks a systematic and principled method, potentially limiting the robustness and optimality of the resulting policies.

This research paper directly addresses this gap by proposing a method to optimize the parameters of the domain randomization distribution. It positions itself in contrast to related work that might optimize simulation parameters to match state distributions between simulation and reality, arguing for a direct optimization of the ultimate objective: policy performance in the real environment. The work contributes to the broader effort to improve the reliability and efficiency of sim-to-real transfer, alongside other parallel research directions such as model-based RL and adaptive control for legged robots. By offering a systematic approach to a critical aspect of domain randomization, this paper seeks to enhance the practical applicability of RL for real-world robotic control.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of this research is to develop a method for effectively utilizing a simulator to train policies that exhibit robust performance when deployed in real-world scenarios. Within this broad aim, the authors identify a specific, unaddressed problem in the current practice of domain randomization: the manual and often arbitrary selection of parameters that define the distribution of simulated environments.

The paper highlights that prior work in domain randomization typically relies on hand-tuned distributions, where the parameters (denoted as φ) are chosen by human experts, often through iterative experimentation or based on prior domain knowledge. The authors contend that this approach lacks a principled foundation and may not yield optimal results for sim-to-real transfer.

Consequently, the primary motivation for this work is to demonstrate that:
1. **The choice of the domain randomization parameters (φ) significantly influences the performance of policies in the real environment.** This addresses the underlying assumption that a systematic selection process is warranted. If φ's impact is negligible, then optimization would not be necessary.
2. **These parameters (φ) can be systematically optimized to enhance the performance of the trained policies when transferred to the real environment.** This provides a constructive solution to the identified problem of arbitrary parameter selection.

The authors formalize this concept by framing domain randomization as a bilevel optimization problem. The outer problem aims to maximize the real-world performance of a policy, where the policy itself is trained on a distribution of simulated environments defined by φ. The inner problem then involves training the policy within this φ-parameterized distribution of simulated environments. This mathematical formulation underpins their methodological approach, providing a clear objective for the proposed optimization strategy. By achieving these objectives, the research seeks to move beyond heuristic approaches to domain randomization, offering a more robust and data-driven method for configuring simulated training environments to facilitate effective sim-to-real transfer. The authors also acknowledge that limited or costly access to real systems could be leveraged to provide the necessary signal for this optimization, indicating practical considerations for real-world applicability.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of the methodology revolves around framing the problem of selecting domain randomization parameters as a bilevel optimization problem, which is then solved using specific algorithms for the inner and outer loops.

**Problem Formulation:**
The research formulates domain randomization as a bilevel optimization:
- **Outer Problem (Equation 1):** `argmax_φ J(m_real)(π_θ*(φ))`
 This objective seeks to maximize the expected return `J` of the policy `π_θ*` in the real-world environment `m_real`. The policy `π_θ*` itself is a function of the domain randomization parameters `φ`.
- **Inner Problem (Equation 2):** `such that θ*(φ) = argmax_θ E_m~p_φ [J(m)(π_θ)]`
 This constraint defines how the policy `θ*` is obtained. It represents the optimal policy trained by maximizing the expected return `J` across a distribution of simulated MDPs `p_φ`, which is parameterized by `φ`.

**Optimization Algorithms:**
To address this bilevel optimization, the authors select specific algorithms for each level:
- **For the Outer Problem (Equation 1 - Optimizing φ): Cross Entropy Method (CEM)**
 CEM is chosen for its simplicity as a gradient-free, iterative stochastic optimization method. The process involves:
 1. Initializing the distribution parameters `φ` (e.g., mean and variance of a Gaussian distribution).
 2. Evaluating `φ` by training a policy using the inner loop and assessing its performance in the target environment `m_real`.
 3. Updating `φ` based on the evaluation results, typically by fitting a new distribution to the best performing `φ` values.
 This process is repeated iteratively until convergence or a stopping criterion is met.
- **For the Inner Problem (Equation 2 - Training Policy π_θ): Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO)**
 PPO, an on-policy reinforcement learning algorithm, is used to train the policy `π_θ` within the randomized simulated environments generated according to `p_φ`. PPO is selected for its balance of performance and computational efficiency.

**Experimental Settings (Sim-to-Sim Transfer):**
To provide a controlled proof-of-concept without the complexities and costs of real robot experiments, the authors perform "sim-to-sim" transfer. This involves training policies in one simulator (Dart) and evaluating their performance in a different simulator (Mujoco), with Mujoco serving as the `m_real` equivalent. This approach is recognized in prior literature as a valid testbed for sim-to-real studies.

- **Environments:** The experiments utilize two standard continuous control tasks: Hopper and Walker. These environments involve bipedal robots that must learn to locomote efficiently.
- **Simulation Parameters (m):** The MDPs in both simulators are parameterized by three physical properties:
 - Masses of the robot's links.
 - Damping coefficients of the robot's links.
 - The gravity constant.
 These parameters constitute a 9-dimensional space (R^9) for the Hopper environment and a 15-dimensional space (R^15) for the Walker environment.
- **Distribution Parameterization (φ):** The parameter `φ` for the domain randomization distribution `p_φ` is defined as the mean and variance of a diagonal Gaussian distribution over these simulation parameters. This choice simplifies the optimization problem initially.
- **Initialization:** The initial mean `φ_0` of the Gaussian distribution is set to the default parameters of the Dart simulator (`m_dart`). The initial variance for all parameters is set to 1. These values represent a reasonable baseline without specific domain expertise or extensive trial-and-error, analogous to a common "hand-tuned" starting point.
- **Replication:** Experiments are conducted over 5 different random seeds for each setting to ensure the robustness and statistical significance of the results.

This methodical approach allows the authors to directly test their hypotheses regarding the importance and optimizability of domain randomization parameters within a controlled yet representative simulation environment.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental results obtained from the sim-to-sim transfer experiments between the Dart and Mujoco simulators demonstrate the significance of optimizing domain randomization parameters. The findings directly support the two primary hypotheses articulated by the authors.

**Impact of φ on Policy Performance:**
The experiments consistently showed that the choice of the domain randomization parameters `φ` substantially affects the performance of policies when transferred to the target environment (Mujoco, serving as `m_real`). This validates the premise that `φ` is a critical design choice, contradicting any notion that its specific values might be inconsequential.

**Effectiveness of Optimized φ vs. Initial φ_0:**
The application of the Cross Entropy Method (CEM) to optimize `φ` resulted in marked improvements in policy performance compared to policies trained with an initial, non-optimized `φ_0` (which represented a reasonable default or hand-tuned setting).

- **Hopper Environment:**
 - Policies trained with the optimized `φ` achieved, on average, a 102% higher performance in the Mujoco environment than policies trained with the initial `φ_0`.
 - The improvement across different random seeds varied, showing a standard deviation of 48%.
 - The minimum observed performance improvement was 28%, indicating that even in less favorable runs, optimization yielded benefits.

- **Walker Environment:**
 - Similarly, for the Walker environment, policies trained with the optimized `φ` demonstrated an average performance 80% higher than those trained with the initial `φ_0`.
 - The standard deviation for this improvement was 53%.
 - The minimum observed improvement in performance was 19%.

**Conclusion on Optimality:**
The consistent and substantial performance gains (ranging from a minimum of 19-28% to an average of 80-102%) over the baseline `φ_0` directly support the hypothesis that `φ` can be effectively optimized. These results indicate that manual selection or default settings for domain randomization distributions are suboptimal and can be systematically improved through the proposed optimization framework. The findings are also presented as being consistent with research in domain randomization for supervised learning, which also emphasizes the importance of the sampling distribution.

In summary, the experiments successfully demonstrate that the choice of domain randomization parameters plays a crucial role in sim-to-real transfer success, and that these parameters can be optimized using a bilevel optimization approach to significantly improve policy performance in target environments.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes a significant advancement to the field of reinforcement learning and sim-to-real transfer, particularly in its approach to domain randomization.

**Enhanced Sim-to-Real Transfer Reliability:** The primary significance lies in providing a principled, systematic method for configuring domain randomization parameters. By moving beyond manual, expert-driven, or trial-and-error approaches, this work offers a data-driven strategy to select the distribution of simulated environments. This can lead to more robust and reliable transfer of policies from simulation to real-world robotic systems, a long-standing challenge in robotics.

**Improved Policy Performance:** The demonstrated improvements in policy performance (average gains of 80-102% in the experimental settings) are substantial. This indicates that a well-optimized domain randomization distribution can lead to higher-quality, more generalizable policies, which is critical for tasks requiring precise and adaptive control in physical environments.

**Reduced Human Effort and Expertise Dependency:** Automating the optimization of domain randomization parameters reduces the reliance on extensive human expertise and time-consuming manual tuning. This makes domain randomization more accessible and efficient for researchers and practitioners, accelerating the development and deployment of RL-controlled robotic systems.

**Foundation for Future Research and Practical Applications:** The paper establishes a framework that can be extended in several key directions:
- **Complex Distribution Learning:** The exploration of deep generative models (e.g., VAEs, autoregressive flows) for learning more intricate dependencies and correlations within simulation parameters suggests a path towards even more effective randomization strategies.
- **Advanced Optimization Techniques:** Investigating more sophisticated gradient-free methods (e.g., CMA-ES, Bayesian optimization) or developing gradient-based approaches (by parameterizing `φ` with `ω`) could yield faster and more efficient optimization of `φ`. The mention of asynchronous evolutionary algorithms (AEA) highlights potential for better utilization of computational resources, including limited real-world interaction time.
- **Integration with Off-policy RL:** Leveraging real-world data collected during the outer loop's evaluation for off-policy training in the inner loop presents an opportunity to improve data efficiency and potentially fine-tune policies with real-world experience, enhancing adaptation.
- **Transferability and Real Robot Testing:** A crucial future impact lies in understanding if general principles for `φ` selection or transferable initial `φ` values exist across different tasks and robots. This would significantly reduce the computational cost of solving the bilevel optimization problem from scratch for every new application. Ultimately, the successful application and validation of this approach on physical robots represent the highest level of practical impact, confirming its utility in real-world engineering.

In conclusion, this research not only addresses a key limitation in current domain randomization practices but also lays a groundwork for more principled and efficient development of sim-to-real transfer capabilities in reinforcement learning, with broad implications for autonomous systems and robotics.
