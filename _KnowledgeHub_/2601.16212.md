# Point Bridge: 3D Representations for Cross Domain Policy Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> NVIDIA, NYU, and University of Washington researchers developed POINT BRIDGE, a framework that uses automated 3D point representations to enable zero-shot sim-to-real policy transfer for robotic manipulation. The method achieved up to 44% improvement in transfer success compared to baselines and a 97% success rate on held-out objects, leveraging large synthetic datasets and minimal real-world data.

## Problem

- The scarcity of large-scale real-world robotic manipulation datasets limits the development of generalist agents.
- A significant visual domain gap between simulated and real environments impedes direct sim-to-real policy transfer.
- Prior keypoint-based methods often require manual annotations, primarily address embodiment differences, and are restricted to single-task scenarios.

## Method

- **Point Bridge** distills observations into compact, task-relevant ==3D point-based representations== using ==VLMs== for real-world scene filtering and ==Foundation Stereo== for depth estimation.
- A ==multi-task transformer policy== is trained on these ==domain-agnostic point representations==, leveraging large-scale synthetic data and optionally co-training with small real-world datasets.
- The framework employs an efficient inference pipeline supporting various ==3D sensing strategies== for real-time deployment on physical robots.

## Results

- Point Bridge demonstrated a **39%** improvement in single-task and **44%** in multitask zero-shot sim-to-real transfer over baselines on rigid object manipulation.
- Co-training with only **45** real-world demonstrations boosted real-world performance by up to **30%**, outperforming image-based co-training by **61-66%**.
- Policies generalized to held-out object instances with a **97%** success rate and maintained an **85%** average success rate on soft and articulated objects with only **20** real demonstrations per task.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Unified, domain-agnostic 3D point representations can effectively bridge the visual domain gap between simulation and reality for zero-shot policy transfer.
> - Vision-Language Models (VLMs) can automate the extraction of task-relevant 3D points from real-world scenes, eliminating manual annotation and allowing for diverse object generalization.
> - Co-training with even limited real-world demonstrations significantly enhances policy performance when combined with large synthetic datasets through the proposed point-based approach.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of generalist robotic agents is a significant goal in artificial intelligence and robotics. This endeavor is currently constrained by the limited availability of large-scale, real-world manipulation datasets, a challenge not faced to the same extent in domains like vision and language, which can leverage internet-scale data. Simulation and synthetic data generation offer a scalable alternative for creating vast datasets, but their utility for deploying policies on physical robots is often hampered by the visual domain gap between simulated and real environments.

Existing paradigms for robot policy learning largely rely on large-scale teleoperated datasets, which are expensive and time-consuming to collect. While simulation environments have advanced in fidelity and automated asset generation, methods for training policies on synthetic data and transferring them to the real world still face obstacles. Approaches such as domain randomization require extensive tuning, and techniques enhancing simulation fidelity often demand considerable manual effort. Furthermore, co-training policies with mixed simulation and real data, while demonstrating effectiveness, often necessitate careful alignment between the simulated and real visual domains. Human videos are another data source, but they introduce an embodiment gap and representation mismatch.

A recent direction in addressing the domain gap has involved using task-relevant keypoint representations. These methods abstract both the robot and scene into sets of keypoints, potentially enabling policies to generalize across visual appearances and environmental conditions. However, current keypoint approaches often depend on manual human annotations, concentrate on bridging embodiment differences rather than visual disparities, and are frequently restricted to single-task scenarios. This paper introduces POINT BRIDGE, which aims to advance this line of research by addressing these limitations through automated, domain-agnostic point-based representations, with a specific focus on unlocking synthetic datasets for zero-shot sim-to-real policy transfer and multitask learning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the bottleneck posed by the scarcity of large-scale real-world robotic manipulation datasets in the pursuit of generalist robotic agents. While synthetic data generation in simulation offers a scalable solution, its direct application to real robots is often hindered by the visual domain gap between simulation and reality. Existing methods for sim-to-real transfer frequently require either extensive manual alignment between domains, high-fidelity photorealistic simulators, or a reliance on a substantial amount of real-world data, thereby limiting their scalability and generality.

Against this background, the key objectives of the POINT BRIDGE project are:

- **Enable Zero-Shot Sim-to-Real Policy Transfer:** To develop a framework that allows policies trained exclusively on large-scale synthetic simulation data to be deployed on real robots without any real-world fine-tuning or explicit visual and object-level alignment. This aims to overcome the visual domain gap directly.
- **Leverage Unified, Domain-Agnostic Representations:** To utilize point-based representations that are inherently invariant to visual domain differences. This involves extracting a compact set of task-relevant 3D keypoints from both simulated and real-world observations, providing a consistent input for policy learning across domains.
- **Automate Point Extraction:** To move beyond manual annotation requirements of prior keypoint-based methods by incorporating Vision-Language Models (VLMs) for automated, on-the-fly identification and extraction of task-relevant points from real-world scenes.
- **Support Multitask Learning:** To design a policy architecture capable of learning and executing multiple manipulation tasks within a single framework, leveraging the domain-agnostic point representations and language conditioning.
- **Enhance Performance with Co-training:** To demonstrate that even small amounts of real-world demonstration data, when combined with large synthetic datasets within the POINT BRIDGE framework, can significantly boost real-world policy performance, outperforming existing vision-based co-training methods.
- **Develop an Efficient Inference Pipeline:** To create a lightweight and flexible pipeline for real-time scene extraction during deployment, accommodating various 3D sensing strategies to balance performance and throughput.

By addressing these objectives, POINT BRIDGE seeks to provide a scalable and robust approach to training capable real-world manipulation agents, reducing the dependence on laborious real-world data collection and complex domain adaptation techniques.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

POINT BRIDGE is a framework designed to facilitate cross-domain policy learning by employing unified, domain-agnostic point-based representations. The approach involves three main stages: scene filtering to extract point clouds, transformer-based policy learning, and an efficient inference pipeline.

#### 3.1. Overview

The process begins with a small set of human demonstrations ($D_{src}$) collected in simulation, which is then expanded into a larger synthetic dataset ($D_{sim}$) using synthetic data generation techniques. Optionally, a small set of real-world demonstrations ($D_{real}$) can be incorporated for co-training. All observations from both simulation and reality are converted into a compact, task-relevant 3D point-based representation ($P$). This representation serves as the input to a transformer-based policy that maps observations to robot actions.

#### 3.2. Data Collection and Synthetic Data Generation

For simulated tasks, the MimicLabs suite (Saxena et al., 2025) is used to create atomic tasks involving various object instances. A small number of human demonstrations ($D_{src}$) are collected for each task and then significantly scaled up to a larger dataset ($D_{sim}$) using MimicGen (Mandlekar et al., 2023). MimicGen adapts demonstration segments to new scenes by applying a constant SE(3) transformation that preserves the relative geometry between the end-effector and the object. This allows for generation of diverse object configurations and types from a limited set of initial demonstrations.

#### 3.3. Point Extraction

The core of POINT BRIDGE lies in distilling observations into a compact set of task-relevant 3D keypoints, which serve as the unified, domain-agnostic representation.

- **VLM-Guided Scene Filtering (Real World):** Given a scene image ($I_0$) and a natural language task description ($L$), the pipeline first uses Gemini-2.5-flash to identify task-relevant objects (e.g., "bowl, plate"). Molmo-7B (Deitke et al., 2024) then localizes these objects as pixels ($o_p$) in the image. SAM2 (Ravi et al., 2024) extracts 2D segmentation masks ($m_i^0$) for each identified object, leveraging its memory module for consistent mask propagation and robust object tracking across subsequent frames, even with occlusions.
- **3D Projection of Task Objects (Real World):** A predetermined number of 2D object points ($P_{2D}^t$) are sampled uniformly from each object's segmentation mask, after shrinking the mask inward by 20% to mitigate noise at boundaries. Foundation Stereo (Wen et al., 2025) computes a depth map ($I_d^t$) from a stereo image pair. These 2D points are then lifted to 3D using the depth map, camera intrinsics, and extrinsics. Farthest point sampling is applied to downsample each object to a fixed number (M) of representative points, which are finally transformed into the robot's base frame ($P_{3D}^t$).
- **Considerations for Simulation Data:** In simulation, VLM-based detection is bypassed. 3D points are directly sampled from task-relevant object meshes. To align with real-world observations, mesh points are projected to the image plane using camera extrinsics and intrinsics, and then lifted back to 3D using ground-truth depth, replicating the view-dependent nature of real cameras. Gaussian noise (1 cm standard deviation) is added to simulated point clouds to account for sensor noise in reality.
- **Robot Representation:** The robot end-effector is represented as a set of keypoints on the gripper. These keypoints' positions are derived by applying rigid transformations from the robot's pose at each timestep.

#### 3.4. Policy Learning

The policy learning component utilizes a decoder-only multi-task transformer architecture, inspired by BAKU (Haldar et al., 2024). Robot points ($P_r$) and object points ($P_o$) are combined into a single point cloud ($P$). A PointNet (Qi et al., 2017) encoder processes this point cloud. For multitask learning, a language embedding ($L$), generated by MiniLM (Wang et al., 2020) from Sentence Transformers (Reimers & Gurevych, 2019), is also input. The transformer policy outputs a deterministic action head that predicts the robot end-effector pose and gripper state. Action chunking with exponential temporal averaging is employed to ensure smooth predicted trajectories. The policy is optimized using Mean Squared Error (MSE) between predicted and ground-truth actions.

#### 3.5. Policy Inference

During real-world deployment, the VLM-guided pipeline first extracts 2D object keypoints from the initial scene image and task instruction. These 2D points are then projected to 3D using scene depth and camera parameters. While Foundation Stereo with stereo images is the primary depth estimation method, the framework also supports depth from commodity RGB-D sensors or point triangulation from two RGB cameras. For triangulation, 2D keypoints are transferred between views using MAST3R (Leroy et al., 2024) and tracked over time by Co-Tracker (Karaev et al., 2023), before multi-view triangulation computes the 3D points in the robot's base frame. This flexible pipeline allows deployment across diverse real-world setups.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments were conducted on six real-world tasks (bowl on plate, mug on plate, stack bowls, fold towel, close drawer, put bowl in oven), involving a Franka Research 3 arm with a Franka Hand gripper. Data collection utilized RoboTurk and Open Teach, with sensing provided by Intel RealSense RGB-D and ZED 2i stereo cameras. A total of 1410 real-world evaluations were performed.

#### 4.1. Zero-Shot Sim-to-Real Transfer with Minimal Alignment

POINT BRIDGE demonstrated effectiveness in zero-shot sim-to-real transfer across three simulated tasks: bowl on plate, mug on plate, and stack bowls. Each object was represented with 128 points extracted via the VLM filtering pipeline.
- **Minimal Visual Alignment:** Despite significant visual differences between simulation and real-world setups (e.g., table appearance, backgrounds, lighting), POINT BRIDGE's scene-filtering strategy enabled domain-invariant representations. This resulted in a 39% improvement in single-task transfer and a 44% improvement in multitask transfer compared to baselines. Image-based sim-to-real policies failed to achieve any success in the zero-shot setting under these conditions.
- **Diverse Object Instances:** POINT BRIDGE facilitated policy transfer across novel real-world object instances with visual appearances substantially different from those in simulation. The use of Foundation Stereo for depth estimation contributed to handling visually challenging objects like transparent or reflective items, which typically pose difficulties for commodity RGB-D sensors.
- **Multitask Capability:** The framework supports multitask zero-shot sim-to-real transfer, with the multitask policy conditioned on natural language instructions. The multitask policy achieved performance comparable to or better than single-task policies, demonstrating scalability across diverse tasks. The underlying 3D point extraction pipeline is task-agnostic and reusable.

#### 4.2. Compatibility of POINT BRIDGE with Real Data

The study also investigated the impact of jointly training policies with simulated and real-world data (co-training).
- **Performance Improvement with Co-training:** Supplementing 1200 simulated demonstrations with 45 teleoperated real-world demonstrations (using an 80-20 sim-to-real ratio) consistently boosted real-world performance by up to 30%. POINT BRIDGE outperformed image-based co-training methods by 61% in single-task and 66% in multitask settings, indicating its ability to leverage small amounts of real data effectively alongside large-scale simulation.
- **Soft and Articulated Objects:** POINT BRIDGE policies were trained exclusively on 20 real-world demonstrations per task for tasks involving soft (fold towel) and articulated objects (close drawer, put bowl in oven), without any simulation data. The policies achieved an average success rate of 85% across these tasks (17/20 for fold towel, 18/20 for close drawer, 16/20 for put bowl in oven), demonstrating its applicability beyond rigid-object manipulation.

#### 4.3. System Analysis

Detailed analysis of key design decisions was conducted:
- **Depth Estimation:** Foundation Stereo provided the most reliable depth estimation, operating at 5 Hz and remaining robust on reflective surfaces. Multi-view triangulation suffered from noisy correspondences and slower inference, while RGB-D cameras had issues with noise, missing regions, and reflective objects.
- **Camera View Alignment:** Training with camera-aligned points in simulation (replicating real-world camera extrinsics) significantly improved sim-to-real transfer. While performance decreased when training with randomized camera views in simulation, the system still achieved an average success rate of approximately 47% across three tasks, suggesting potential for robustness with further development.
- **Comparison with Baselines:** POINT BRIDGE’s automated keypoint extraction yielded significantly more robust sim-to-real transfer compared to a PointNet-based unfiltered point cloud baseline (BAKU-PCD) and a point track baseline (Point Policy), which showed limited or zero success in zero-shot sim-to-real settings.
- **Background Distractors:** The scene filtering pipeline in POINT BRIDGE demonstrated robustness to background distractors, maintaining performance similar to distractor-free scenarios, unlike unfiltered point cloud approaches that failed completely.
- **Generalization to Held-Out Objects:** Co-trained multitask POINT BRIDGE policies achieved a 97% success rate on held-out object instances not seen during training, demonstrating strong generalization capabilities to novel objects.
- **Number of Points and Action Representation:** Performance remained consistently high (over 86% success) across different numbers of object points (10, 64, 128). Both pose regression and point track prediction action representations yielded comparable performance, suggesting that with large datasets, the specific action representation might have a reduced impact.
- **Latency and Robustness of VLM Pipeline:** The VLM-guided scene filtering pipeline had an initial overhead of ~9 seconds for model loading. Per-step execution, involving SAM2 tracking and Foundation Stereo depth computation, was approximately 0.115 seconds, enabling real-time policy deployment at 5 Hz. The VLM pipeline exhibited low failure rates (e.g., 1 failure in 20 trials for 'bowl on plate' due to occlusion, 1 for 'stack bowls' due to object misidentification).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

POINT BRIDGE introduces a methodology that addresses critical limitations in scaling robot learning, specifically the scarcity of real-world data and the challenges of sim-to-real transfer.

### 6. Significance

The work demonstrates a viable pathway for leveraging vast synthetic simulation datasets to train generalist robotic agents for real-world deployment. By employing unified, domain-agnostic point-based representations and automating their extraction via Vision-Language Models (VLMs), POINT BRIDGE significantly reduces the dependence on laborious manual data collection and explicit visual domain alignment. This approach marks a departure from prior methods that often require costly real-world data, meticulous simulation fidelity, or extensive human annotation for keypoint-based policies. The framework's ability to perform zero-shot sim-to-real transfer, generalize across diverse object instances, and scale to multitask learning with comparable or superior performance to existing methods, including those co-trained with real data, underscores its technical advancement. The demonstrated robustness to background distractors and held-out objects further validates the effectiveness of the proposed representation.

**Potential Impact:**
The contributions of POINT BRIDGE could have several implications for the field of robotics:
- **Accelerated Robot Development:** By making large-scale synthetic data more readily applicable to real robots, the framework could significantly accelerate the development and iteration cycles for new robotic skills and generalist agents.
- **Reduced Costs and Accessibility:** The reduced reliance on extensive real-world data collection could lower the financial and logistical barriers to entry for robotics research and development, potentially making advanced robotic manipulation more accessible.
- **Deployment in Unstructured Environments:** The improved generalization capabilities across varying visual conditions and object types may facilitate the deployment of robots in less controlled, more unstructured real-world environments.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The work establishes a robust foundation for future research into hybrid representations that combine the efficiency of sparse point-based abstractions with richer scene context, potentially overcoming current limitations in cluttered environments. Further advancements in VLMs are also expected to directly enhance the robustness of the point extraction pipeline.

**Limitations:**
The authors acknowledge several limitations:
1. **Vulnerability to VLM Failures:** The framework's dependence on VLMs and other vision models means its robustness is tied to their current capabilities, and failures in these foundational models can propagate.
2. **Camera Pose Alignment:** While showing some robustness to viewpoint variation, precise camera pose alignment between simulation and reality during training remains important for optimal performance. Future work could explore training with more diverse simulated viewpoints to mitigate this.
3. **Loss of Scene Context:** Point-based abstractions, while aiding generalization, may discard critical scene context, potentially limiting performance in highly cluttered environments. Hybrid representations could be explored to address this.
4. **Control Frequency:** The current policy inference operates at a lower control frequency (5 Hz) compared to some image-based baselines (15 Hz) due to the computational overhead of depth and point processing, which might affect performance in highly dynamic tasks requiring faster feedback.

The authors commit to publicly releasing datasets, training, and evaluation code, promoting reproducibility and further research in this area.
