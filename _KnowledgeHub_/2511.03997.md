# PhysCorr: Dual-Reward DPO for Physics-Constrained Text-to-Video Generation with Automated Preference Selection

> [!summary] Summary
> A framework named PhysCorr enables text-to-video models to generate physically plausible content by employing a dual-reward Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) strategy with automated preference selection. The approach achieved up to a 2.83% increase in mechanics compliance metrics for leading T2V models while largely maintaining visual quality and semantic coherence.

## Problem

- State-of-the-art text-to-video (T2V) models often produce videos that violate fundamental physical laws despite high perceptual quality.
- Current human preference alignment techniques, like RLHF and DPO, struggle to address physical inconsistencies due to limitations in reward models and human preference datasets.
- Reward models typically prioritize aesthetics and text alignment, lacking efficient mechanisms to quantify physical plausibility.

## Method

- **PhysCorr**, a unified framework, models, evaluates, and optimizes physical consistency in T2V generation using a specialized Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) pipeline.
- A lightweight, dual-dimensional Physics ==Reward Model== (**PhysicsRM**) quantifies physical plausibility by assessing subject-consistency and mechanical verification.
- Physics-Specialized DPO (**PhyDPO**) leverages physics-aware reweighting to prioritize learning from more significant physical inaccuracies, guiding the T2V model towards coherent outputs.

## Results

- PhysCorr enhanced overall video quality and semantic consistency across 24-25 out of 32 VBench dimensions for leading T2V models (VideoCrafter2 and Wan2.1).
- The framework achieved significant improvements in VBench2 intrinsic faithfulness metrics, particularly a 2.29-2.83% increase in the critical mechanics metric.
- Ablation studies validated the contributions of the dual-dimensional PhysicsRM and the reweighting mechanism in PhyDPO to physical realism, and qualitative results demonstrated corrected physical interactions and stable object appearances.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Injecting explicit physics-aware mechanisms throughout the T2V optimization pipeline is essential for moving beyond superficial visual quality to intrinsic faithfulness.
> - A dual-dimensional reward model that efficiently assesses both geometric stability and mechanical plausibility can effectively quantify physical realism.
> - Prioritizing preference pairs with larger physical plausibility differences through a reweighted DPO loss significantly accelerates and targets learning for physical consistency.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of text-to-video (T2V) generation has witnessed rapid advancements, driven largely by large-scale diffusion models and transformer architectures. Recent breakthroughs, exemplified by models like Sora (OpenAI 2024), VideoCrafter2 (Chen et al. 2024), and Wan2.1 (Wan et al. 2025), have achieved unprecedented levels of perceptual quality, visual fidelity, and temporal coherence, allowing for the generation of dynamic video content that aligns impressively with complex textual descriptions. These models represent a significant leap towards creating realistic and diverse video content from simple text prompts.

However, despite these visual achievements, a critical and often overlooked limitation persists: the generated videos frequently violate fundamental physical laws. This manifests as "implausible object dynamics, incoherent interactions, and unrealistic motion patterns." Examples provided in the paper, such as waves not rebounding plausibly or knives failing to leave marks on cut meat, highlight these glaring physical inconsistencies. Such failures severely restrict the utility of T2V models in applications requiring high degrees of realism and predictability, such as embodied AI, robotics, scientific visualization, and realistic world simulation.

Current solutions primarily leverage human preference alignment techniques like Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) and Direct Preference Optimization (DPO). While effective in enhancing subjective aspects like visual appeal and text-video coherence, these methods fall short in addressing core physical accuracy for several reasons:
1. **Reward Model Limitations**: Existing reward models (e.g., VideoReward, VIDEORM) often rely on computationally expensive large-scale Vision-Language Models (VLMs) and prioritize frame-level aesthetics and text alignment, largely neglecting metrics for physical plausibility (e.g., rigid body dynamics, fluid continuity).
2. **Dataset Bias**: Human preference datasets, which form the basis for RLHF/DPO, typically focus on subjective quality dimensions and lack explicit annotations for physical violations, leading to a misalignment between training objectives and the need for physical realism.
3. **Algorithmic Constraints**: Standard alignment algorithms with KL-divergence constraints often preserve low-level artifacts, and inference-time guidance struggles to resolve structural physical violations without dedicated physics-aware rewards.

PhysCorr directly addresses this critical gap by introducing a novel framework that specifically models, evaluates, and optimizes for physical consistency. It marks a significant shift in T2V research from solely focusing on perceptual fidelity and semantic alignment to incorporating "intrinsic faithfulness," specifically adherence to physical laws. This work builds upon the foundation of advanced generative models and preference learning but innovates by injecting explicit physics-aware mechanisms throughout the optimization pipeline. By doing so, PhysCorr aims to elevate T2V models from mere content generators to tools capable of producing "physically grounded and trustworthy" simulations, thereby extending their applicability to more demanding, real-world scenarios. This positions PhysCorr at the forefront of the movement towards more intelligent and reliable generative AI systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of PhysCorr is to bridge the significant gap between the impressive perceptual quality of state-of-the-art text-to-video (T2V) generation and its often-lacking adherence to fundamental physical laws. The motivation stems from the recognition that current T2V models, despite their visual prowess, produce content that frequently violates basic physics, rendering them unsuitable for applications demanding high fidelity to real-world dynamics.

The key objectives and motivations can be broken down as follows:

- **Objective 1: Develop a Unified Framework for Physical Consistency.**
 - **Motivation**: There is a distinct lack of comprehensive tools and methodologies for systematically modeling, evaluating, and optimizing physical consistency within the T2V generation pipeline. Existing approaches are piecemeal or focus on other quality metrics. PhysCorr aims to provide an end-to-end solution.

- **Objective 2: Quantify Physical Plausibility with an Efficient Reward Model.**
 - **Motivation**: Current reward models for T2V are either too computationally expensive (relying on large VLMs) or lack the granularity to effectively assess physical laws. They prioritize aesthetics and text alignment over mechanics. PhysCorr seeks to create a "dual-dimensional reward model," PhysicsRM, that is parameter-efficient (e.g., distilling knowledge from larger models) and capable of quantifying both intra-object stability (how objects maintain their form) and inter-object interactions (how objects interact mechanically).

- **Objective 3: Design a Physics-Aware Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) Pipeline.**
 - **Motivation**: Standard DPO and RLHF methods treat all preference pairs equally, regardless of the magnitude of the quality difference. This means that minor physical inconsistencies might receive the same learning signal as egregious violations. Moreover, human preference datasets often lack annotations for physical accuracy, making it challenging for conventional DPO to learn these constraints. PhyDPO is introduced to leverage contrastive feedback and physics-aware reweighting, explicitly prioritizing learning from more significant physical errors to guide generation towards physically coherent outputs.

- **Objective 4: Ensure Model Agnostic and Scalable Integration.**
 - **Motivation**: To maximize impact, the proposed framework should not be limited to a specific T2V architecture. The ability to integrate seamlessly into a wide range of existing video diffusion and transformer-based backbones (like VideoCrafter2 and Wan2.1) is crucial for broad applicability and adoption.

- **Objective 5: Demonstrate Significant Improvements in Physical Realism while Preserving Visual Fidelity.**
 - **Motivation**: The ultimate goal is to generate videos that are not only visually stunning but also physically accurate, without sacrificing aesthetic quality or semantic alignment with the text prompt. This is vital for widespread deployment and user acceptance.

In essence, the motivation for PhysCorr is to address the "physical implausibility crisis" in T2V, transforming generative models from merely visually impressive content creators into reliable simulators of dynamic physical phenomena. This transition is crucial for unlocking their potential in high-stakes applications like embodied AI, robotics, and scientific simulations where physical accuracy is paramount.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

PhysCorr proposes a unified framework designed to model, evaluate, and optimize physical consistency in text-to-video generation through a specialized Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) pipeline. The methodology is structured around two core innovations: the **Physics Reward Model (PhysicsRM)** and **Physics-Specialized DPO (PhyDPO)**.

#### 3.1. Overall Pipeline

The PhysCorr pipeline begins by generating multiple videos for a given text prompt using a target video diffusion model. These videos are then evaluated by PhysicsRM, which assigns a "PhyScore" based on their physical plausibility. The video with the highest PhyScore (physically plausible) and the one with the lowest PhyScore (physically implausible) form a "win/lose" preference pair. These preference pairs, along with their PhyScore differences, are then used to train the PhyDPO, which employs a reweighted loss function to prioritize learning from more significant physical inaccuracies. This entire process is model-agnostic, allowing integration with various T2V backbones.

#### 3.2. Lightweight Physics Reward Model (PhysicsRM)

PhysicsRM is a novel, dual-dimensional reward model specifically designed to quantify the physical plausibility of generated videos efficiently. It integrates two primary components:

1. **Subject-Consistency Module**:
 - **Purpose**: This module assesses the temporal stability of 3D geometric features (e.g., object shape, material integrity) across video frames.
 - **Mechanism**: It extracts features from consecutive frames using DINOv2 embeddings, a self-supervised vision transformer known for learning robust visual representations. Cosine similarity is computed between these features for adjacent frames, and the average similarity is normalized to produce a `s_subj` score. A higher score indicates better subject consistency and geometric stability.

2. **Mechanics Verification Module**:
 - **Purpose**: This module evaluates the mechanical plausibility of a video through a hierarchical two-stage reasoning process.
 - **Mechanism**: It utilizes a physics-oriented question generator (based on Qwen2.5, a conditional language model) to produce two auxiliary physics questions (q1, q2) for a given prompt `p`. These questions are constrained by difficulty (q1 easier than q2), domain (mechanics), and relevance to the prompt. A lightweight, distilled version of the LLaVA-Video-Qwen2-7B model (reduced to 0.5B parameters via LLaVA-MoD distillation, retaining 98% accuracy while significantly reducing latency) is then used to answer these questions based on the input video. The module assigns a `s_mech` score: 0 for a first-level failure (q1 incorrect), 0.5 for a second-level failure (q2 incorrect but q1 correct), and 1 for full mechanical correctness.
 - **Model Architecture**: The LLaVA-Video-Qwen2-Distill model is crucial for computational efficiency, enabling robust physical analysis without the overhead of the full 7B parameter model.

- **PhyScore Calculation**: The final PhyScore (`s_phy`) is a weighted sum of `s_subj` and `s_mech`, controlled by a trainable parameter `λ` (initialized at 0, implying equal weighting via a sigmoid function initially). This dynamic weighting allows the model to balance the importance of geometric stability and mechanical correctness during training.

- **Training PhysicsRM**: PhysicsRM is trained via supervised learning using a custom dataset of 300 prompts (50 physically difficult, 250 neutral) from T2VQA. For each prompt, 10 videos are generated and human-annotated with physical plausibility scores (`s_phy` ∈). To mitigate annotation noise and outliers, the Huber loss function is employed during training.

#### 3.3. Win/Lose Video Selection

To create the preference pairs necessary for DPO, a structured process is followed:
1. **Prompt Curation**: A set of 108 prompts is selected from Vidpro-10k, comprising 36 manually chosen "physically challenging" prompts and 72 randomly sampled "neutral" prompts.
2. **Video Generation**: For each of these 108 prompts, 4 videos are generated using a pre-trained text-to-video model (e.g., VideoCrafter2 or Wan2.1).
3. **Preference Pair Assignment**: PhysicsRM evaluates all generated videos and assigns a PhyScore. For each prompt, the video with the highest PhyScore is designated as `v_win`, and the one with the lowest score as `v_lose`. These pairs, along with the original prompt, form the preference dataset `D_pref`.

#### 3.4. Physics-Specialized DPO (PhyDPO)

PhyDPO addresses a limitation of standard DPO, which treats all preference pairs equally. It prioritizes learning from pairs where there is a clearer and more significant physical difference between the "win" and "lose" videos.

1. **Probability Density Modeling**: The empirical probability density `p_hat(s)` of PhyScores is computed across all generated videos using histogram binning. This helps identify scores that are rare or common.
2. **Weight Assignment**: For each preference pair (`v_win`, `v_lose`) with scores (`s_win`, `s_lose`), a joint sampling probability `P_ij` is calculated as `p_hat(s_win) * p_hat(s_lose)`. A re-weighting coefficient `w_ij` is then derived as `(beta / P_ij)^alpha`, where `beta` is a normalization constant and `alpha` (a hyperparameter) controls the prioritization intensity. This weighting mechanism ensures that pairs with larger PhyScore differences, especially those involving extreme (rare) good or bad scores, receive higher weights, compelling the model to focus on correcting egregious physical inaccuracies.
3. **Physics-Informed Loss Integration**: The calculated `w_ij` is directly integrated into the standard DPO loss function, `L_DPO`, forming the final training objective `L_PhyDPO`. This customized loss guides the T2V model to optimize for improved physical plausibility while maintaining other aspects of video quality.

By combining the lightweight yet powerful PhysicsRM with the intelligent re-weighting strategy of PhyDPO, PhysCorr provides a comprehensive and efficient approach to infuse physical common sense into text-to-video generation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted demonstrate that PhysCorr significantly enhances the physical realism of text-to-video generation without compromising visual fidelity or semantic alignment. The evaluation leveraged two benchmark suites: VBench (for general quality and semantic consistency) and VBench2 (specifically for intrinsic faithfulness and physics compliance).

#### 4.1. PhyScore Distribution

- An initial analysis of the PhyScore distribution (Figure 3) reveals a healthy range of scores across generated videos, indicating PhysicsRM's ability to effectively differentiate between varying levels of physical plausibility. Critically, the distribution of PhyScore differences between "win" and "lose" preference pairs shows significant deltas, validating the premise for PhyDPO's re-weighting mechanism.

#### 4.2. VBench Evaluation (General Quality)

- **Overall Improvement**: PhysCorr consistently improved the overall quality across both baseline models, VideoCrafter2 and Wan2.1. It enhanced performance on 25 out of 32 dimensions for VideoCrafter2 and 24 out of 32 dimensions for Wan2.1.
- **Specific Gains**: Notable improvements were observed in critical metrics such as:
 - **Temporal Style**: Wan2.1 saw a 0.89% increase, and VideoCrafter2 achieved a substantial 2.45% increase.
 - **Imaging Quality**: VideoCrafter2 improved by 0.92%.
 - **Overall Consistency**: Wan2.1 showed a 1.38% increase.
 - **Multiple Objects**: Wan2.1 improved by 0.92%.
- **Model-Agnostic Enhancement**: The framework proved its adaptability, with VideoCrafter2's overall score increasing by 0.08% and Wan2.1's by 0.26%.
- **Minor Trade-offs**: Small decreases were observed in a few dimensions (e.g., spatial relationship for both models, multiple objects for VideoCrafter2). The authors note these as expected during multi-dimensional optimization and are acceptable given the significant gains in physical realism. This also highlights PhysCorr's ability to avoid the "quality-semantic trade-off trap" often seen in single-objective reward models.

#### 4.3. VBench2 Evaluation (Physics Compliance and Intrinsic Faithfulness)

- **Significant Physics Improvement**: PhysCorr demonstrated substantial gains on nearly all VBench2 intrinsic faithfulness metrics (27 out of 32 dimensions), which explicitly include physics compliance.
- **Mechanics Metric**: The most critical improvement was in the **mechanics metric**, where PhysCorr yielded a 2.29% increase for VideoCrafter2 and a 2.83% increase for Wan2.1, directly confirming its effectiveness in enhancing physical plausibility.
- **Motion Rationality**: A significant increase in the motion rationality metric was also observed, further validating the framework's ability to generate more believable object movements.
- **Minor Decreases**: Similar to VBench, a few minor decreases were noted (e.g., human anatomy, human identity, and motion order understanding for VideoCrafter2; composition and material for Wan2.1). However, these were outweighed by widespread improvements across other crucial metrics.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies

Ablation experiments confirmed the essential contributions of PhysCorr's key components:
- **Mechanics Module (MC) in PhysicsRM**: Removing the mechanics module (MC w/o) led to a significant 1.42% degradation in motion, and the dual-dimensional PhysicsRM provided a 1.44% gain on mechanics over single-module variants, underscoring its importance.
- **Reweighting Module (RW) in PhyDPO**: Disabling the reweighting mechanism (RW w/o) reduced overall consistency by 1.53%, highlighting the necessity of prioritizing high-impact preference pairs.
- **Impact of `alpha` in Reweighting**: Varying the `alpha` parameter in PhyDPO's reweighting (Figure 5) showed that higher `alpha` values (indicating stronger prioritization of significant physical errors) led to improved aesthetic quality, mechanics, and thermotics, even if at a slight cost to imaging quality and scene metrics. This validates the design choice of adaptive prioritization.

#### 4.5. Qualitative Results

- **Figure 6** visually illustrates the effectiveness of PhysCorr. Examples show:
 - **Corrected Physical Interactions**: A bouncing ball no longer just shakes but exhibits realistic rebound dynamics; free-falling meat particles demonstrate appropriate acceleration rather than constant speed.
 - **Stable Object Appearances**: Objects like a dog maintain consistent texture across frames, and 3D spatial relationships are preserved, preventing issues like mutual penetration or unreasonable subject appearances.

In summary, the results convincingly demonstrate that PhysCorr achieves state-of-the-art physics alignment across multiple benchmarks, significantly enhancing physical realism while largely preserving or improving overall visual fidelity and semantic alignment in leading text-to-video models.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The PhysCorr framework represents a critical advancement in the field of text-to-video (T2V) generation, addressing a fundamental limitation that has hindered the practical deployment of otherwise impressive generative models. Its significance and potential impact span several key areas:

#### 5.1. Enhancing Trustworthiness and Reliability of Generative AI

Currently, despite their stunning visual fidelity, T2V models often produce "hallucinations" of physical reality, generating content that violates basic laws of physics. This lack of physical plausibility undermines the credibility and trustworthiness of the generated videos. PhysCorr takes a crucial step towards making T2V models more reliable by explicitly enforcing physical constraints. This move from merely "looking good" to "acting real" is essential for broader acceptance and more serious applications of generative AI.

#### 5.2. Enabling Advanced Applications in Embodied AI and Robotics

One of the most significant impacts of PhysCorr lies in its potential to revolutionize embodied AI and robotics. These fields rely heavily on realistic simulations for training agents, testing behaviors, and understanding complex environments.
- **Robotics Simulation**: Robots require physically accurate environments to learn manipulation, navigation, and interaction skills. If generated training videos exhibit physically impossible scenarios, the learned policies may fail in the real world. PhysCorr can provide high-fidelity synthetic data that adheres to real-world physics, accelerating the development and robustness of robotic systems.
- **Embodied AI**: Agents operating in virtual worlds or simulated environments need to understand and interact with objects according to physical rules. PhysCorr's ability to generate physically consistent interactions makes it invaluable for creating more realistic and challenging learning environments for embodied agents.

#### 5.3. Advancing Scientific Visualization and World Simulation

PhysCorr can significantly benefit domains requiring precise visual representation of physical phenomena:
- **Scientific Research and Education**: Generating accurate simulations for complex physical processes (e.g., fluid dynamics, material deformation, celestial mechanics) can be invaluable for research, experimentation, and educational purposes. PhysCorr's focus on mechanics and subject consistency ensures that scientific visualizations are not only visually appealing but also fundamentally correct.
- **World Models for AI**: The concept of building "world models" for AI, allowing agents to predict future states of an environment, critically depends on understanding physical laws. PhysCorr contributes directly to this vision by providing a foundation for generating more coherent and predictive environmental dynamics, moving closer to true "world simulators."

#### 5.4. Improving AI Filmmaking and Content Creation

While current T2V models can generate visually striking scenes, physical inconsistencies can break immersion or lead to "uncanny valley" effects for viewers. By improving mechanics and object consistency, PhysCorr can lead to more believable and high-quality content for entertainment, advertising, and digital media production, reducing the need for extensive manual correction or CGI work.

#### 5.5. Methodological Contributions and Future Research Directions

PhysCorr introduces several novel methodological contributions:
- **PhysicsRM**: The development of a lightweight, dual-dimensional reward model specifically tailored for physical plausibility, leveraging knowledge distillation for efficiency, is a significant contribution. This demonstrates a path for creating specialized reward models for specific, complex attributes without relying solely on general-purpose VLMs.
- **PhyDPO**: The concept of physics-aware reweighting in DPO, prioritizing learning from high-impact physical errors, offers a more efficient and targeted optimization strategy. This could inspire similar approaches for other complex alignment tasks where some errors are more critical than others.
- **Model-Agnostic Design**: Its ability to be integrated into diverse T2V architectures (diffusion, transformers) ensures broad applicability and impact across the rapidly evolving landscape of generative models.

This work sets a precedent for incorporating explicit physics laws into generative models. It opens avenues for future research into other dimensions of "intrinsic faithfulness," such as commonsense reasoning, complex compositional integrity, and causal consistency, moving AI generation beyond surface-level aesthetics to deeper semantic and logical understanding of the world.
