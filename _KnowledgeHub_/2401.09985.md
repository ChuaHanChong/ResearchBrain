# WorldDreamer: Towards General World Models for Video Generation via Predicting Masked Tokens

> [!summary] Summary
> WorldDreamer introduces a world model that redefines general video generation as an unsupervised visual sequence modeling task by predicting masked visual tokens using a Transformer-based architecture. This model achieves versatile video creation and editing, including image-to-video, text-to-video, and action-to-video, with significantly faster inference speeds than existing methods.

## Problem

- Existing world models are largely confined to specific, structured environments (e.g., gaming, robotics), limiting their ability to generalize to diverse "general world" scenes.
- Current visual dynamics models, often relying on RNNs or diffusion, face challenges in effectively capturing nuanced motion and physics across a wide array of general world scenes.
- A need exists for world models that can integrate multiple input modalities (text, action) for diverse, controlled video generation and editing tasks.

## Method

- **WorldDreamer** proposes a Transformer-based general ==world model== that frames world modeling as an unsupervised visual sequence modeling task through masked visual token prediction.
- It utilizes a Spatial Temporal Patchwise Transformer (STPT) with localized spatial attention and spatial-wise ==cross-attention== for effective multimodal prompt integration.
- The model employs a dynamic masking strategy and parallel sampling during inference, enabling significantly faster video generation by predicting multiple tokens concurrently.

## Results

- Demonstrates proficiency across multiple video generation and editing tasks, including image-to-video, text-to-video, video inpainting, video stylization, and action-to-video generation.
- Generates high-fidelity, cinematically smooth videos from single images and aligns generated videos with text prompts across various styles.
- Achieves video generation in approximately 10 steps, resulting in an estimated **3x** to **20x** faster inference speed compared to diffusion or autoregressive methods, generating a 24-frame, 192x320 video in about 3 seconds on an A800 GPU.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Framing world modeling as an unsupervised visual sequence modeling task, similar to large language models' masked token prediction, is an effective paradigm for learning general visual dynamics.
> - A Transformer architecture combining localized spatial-temporal patchwise self-attention with spatial-wise cross-attention can efficiently learn complex spatio-temporal dynamics and integrate multimodal conditioning.
> - A dynamic masking strategy with consistent masks across frames, coupled with parallel sampling, enables substantial acceleration of video generation, moving beyond sequential token prediction.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of artificial intelligence is progressing towards systems capable of comprehending and predicting the dynamic visual world. Within this context, world models are considered essential for learning the motion and physics inherent in general environments, a capability that is particularly relevant for video generation.

Prior explorations into world models, as exemplified by early work and the Dreamer series, primarily focused on specific, structured scenarios such as gaming environments. Subsequent research extended these models to domains like autonomous driving and real-world robotics. However, these existing world models generally operate within the confines of their training scenarios, limiting their ability to generalize to the broader complexity of the "general world," which encompasses diverse natural scenes and human activities. Furthermore, prevalent world models for visual dynamics often rely on Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs) or diffusion-based methods. While these approaches have demonstrated utility in video generation, the paper suggests they face challenges in effectively capturing the nuanced motion and physics within a wide array of general world scenes.

In parallel, the field of video generation itself is characterized by two dominant methodological categories: Transformer-based methods and diffusion-based methods. Transformer-based approaches, drawing inspiration from large language models (LLMs), typically employ autoregressive prediction or parallel decoding of masked tokens. Examples include VideoGPT, GAIA-1 for autonomous driving scenarios, and more recent models like VideoPoet that leverage video tokenizers and parallel decoding for high-quality video synthesis. Diffusion-based methods, on the other hand, build upon 2D image diffusion models by introducing temporal dimensions or leveraging pre-trained text-to-image models for video synthesis, as seen in VideoLDM, Make-A-Video, and Imagen Video. While powerful, diffusion models are noted for potential difficulties in integrating multiple modalities within a single framework and for their computational requirements during inference.

WorldDreamer addresses these gaps by attempting to pioneer a *general* world model for video generation. It seeks to move beyond scenario-specific limitations by adopting a Transformer-based approach that frames world modeling as an unsupervised visual sequence modeling challenge, specifically through the prediction of masked visual tokens. This design choice draws directly from the demonstrated success and scalability recipes of LLMs, aiming to provide a unified framework that can integrate multi-modal prompts and efficiently generate diverse video content, bridging the gap between specialized world models and general video generation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to introduce WorldDreamer, a novel world model designed to achieve a comprehensive understanding of general world physics and motions for enhanced video generation capabilities.

The motivation for this work stems from several limitations observed in the existing landscape of world models and video generation techniques:

1. **Confinement of Existing World Models:** Current world models are largely confined to specific, often structured, environments such as gaming, robotics, or autonomous driving. This specialization limits their capacity to generalize and capture the dynamic complexity, physics, and motions present in a broader range of "general world" environments, including natural scenes and varied human-centric scenarios. The authors identify a need for models that can transcend these specific domains to achieve a more universal understanding of visual dynamics.
2. **Challenges with Current Visual Dynamics Modeling Approaches:** The reliance of existing visual dynamics models on Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs) or diffusion-based methods, while yielding some successes, presents challenges in effectively capturing the motion and physics across diverse general world scenes. The paper implicitly suggests these methods may not be optimally suited for learning complex, long-range temporal dependencies and varied physical interactions in an unsupervised manner across general visual data.
3. **Inspiration from Large Language Models (LLMs):** The success of LLMs in unsupervised sequence modeling, particularly through masked token prediction, serves as a significant motivation. The authors propose that framing world modeling as an unsupervised visual sequence modeling task—by mapping visual inputs to discrete tokens and predicting masked ones—could enable a similar leap in understanding and generating complex visual dynamics. This approach aims to leverage the robust architectures and proven scalability of Transformers.
4. **Desire for Multi-Modal Interaction:** There is a recognized need for world models that can interact with and be controlled by multiple input modalities, such as text and action signals. Integrating these prompts is seen as crucial for enabling diverse and controlled video generation and editing tasks, moving beyond simple unconditional generation or single-condition synthesis.
5. **Efficiency and Speed in Video Generation:** Compared to diffusion-based methods, which typically require multiple iterative denoising steps, and autoregressive Transformer models, which predict tokens sequentially, there is a motivation to develop a faster video generation process. By adopting a parallel decoding approach inspired by masked image modeling, WorldDreamer aims to achieve video generation in significantly fewer steps, thereby enhancing practical applicability.

In summary, the key objective is to develop a general world model that can learn complex visual dynamics across diverse scenarios, leveraging a masked visual token prediction paradigm inspired by LLMs, and supporting multi-modal conditional generation with improved inference speed.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

WorldDreamer approaches general world modeling for video generation by framing it as an unsupervised visual sequence modeling challenge. This is achieved through tokenization of visual inputs, masked token prediction, and the incorporation of multi-modal prompts, all built upon a Transformer-based architecture.

#### 3.1. Overall Framework

The WorldDreamer framework operates in several stages:
1. **Visual Tokenization:** Visual signals (images and videos) are first converted into discrete visual tokens.
2. **Masking:** A portion of these visual tokens undergoes a masking operation.
3. **Multimodal Prompt Encoding:** Textual and action signals are separately encoded into embeddings, serving as multimodal prompts.
4. **Spatial Temporal Patchwise Transformer (STPT) Prediction:** The STPT module processes the masked visual tokens and multimodal embeddings to predict the original masked visual tokens.
5. **Visual Decoding:** Predicted tokens are then decoded by visual decoders to produce videos for various generation and editing tasks.

During training, WorldDreamer utilizes Visual-Text-Action data triplets, with the sole supervision signal being the prediction of masked visual tokens. The model supports training with incomplete multimodal data (e.g., without text or action). At inference time, WorldDreamer can perform tasks such as image-to-video, video stylization, text-to-video, video inpainting, and action-to-video generation by appropriately masking visual tokens and providing conditions.

#### 3.2. Model Architecture

**Preliminaries:**
- **Visual Tokenization:** WorldDreamer employs VQGAN to tokenize visual signals. Given N frames of visual inputs I ∈ R<sup>N×H×W×3</sup>, VQGAN downsamples the resolution by 16× to produce visual tokens T<sub>V</sub> ∈ R<sup>N×h×w</sup> (where h = H/4, w = W/4). The VQGAN has a vocabulary size of 8192 and is pre-trained on a large image dataset.
- **Text Encoding:** Pre-trained T5 is used to map text inputs into high-dimensional embeddings E<sub>T</sub> ∈ R<sup>K×C<sub>T</sub></sup>, which are then repeated for N frames and mapped to the visual embedding channel C<sub>V</sub>.
- **Action Encoding:** A Multi Layer Perception (MLP) encodes action inputs into action embeddings E<sub>A</sub> ∈ R<sup>N×C<sub>V</sub></sup>.
- **Multimodal Prompts:** The processed text embeddings and action embeddings are concatenated to form multimodal prompt embeddings E<sub>M</sub> ∈ R<sup>N×(K+1)×C<sub>V</sub></sup>. These prompts can be empty if not provided.
- **Training Objective:** The model is optimized to predict the masked visual tokens (ˆT<sub>V</sub>) conditioned on the unmasked visual tokens (e<sub>T<sub>V</sub></sub>) and the multimodal prompts (E<sub>M</sub>), using a cross-entropy loss.

**Spatial Temporal Patchwise Transformer (STPT):**
STPT is the core generative component, building on the U-ViT foundation with enhancements for video data.
- **Visual Embeddings:** Input masked tokens (ˆT<sub>V</sub>) are converted into visual embeddings E<sub>V</sub> ∈ R<sup>N×h×w×C<sub>V</sub></sup> using a learnable codebook of size 8193 (one more than VQGAN for masked tokens).
- **3D Convolution:** In each STPT layer, visual embeddings are first processed by a 3D convolutional network.
- **Spatial Temporal Patchwise Self-attention:** These embeddings are spatially partitioned into patches E<sub>P</sub> ∈ R<sup>N×h/s×w/s×C<sub>V</sub></sup> (with a patch stride `s` of 2). Each patch embedding is flattened, and standard self-attention is applied *within* these patches. This mechanism confines attention to localized patches within a temporal-spatial window, designed to facilitate learning of visual dynamics and accelerate training convergence.
- **Spatial-wise Cross-attention:** After patch-wise self-attention, the patches are concatenated and reshaped. Subsequently, spatial-wise cross-attention is applied to enable feature interaction between the visual embeddings (E<sub>V</sub>) and the multimodal embeddings (E<sub>M</sub>), treating the frame number as the batch size.
- **Output:** After `L` layers, the feature dimensionality of E<sub>V</sub> is mapped to the VQGAN codebook size, allowing softmax to predict probabilities for each token.
- **Joint Training:** STPT can be jointly trained on both video and image datasets. For image inputs, the attention weight for the temporal dimension is treated as a diagonal matrix. This strategy expands training data and enhances comprehension of temporal and spatial aspects.

#### 3.3. Mask Strategy

- **Dynamic Masking Rate:** WorldDreamer employs a dynamic masking rate based on cosine scheduling, similar to. In each iteration, a random mask rate `r` ∈ is sampled, and a calculated number of tokens (approximately `(2hw/π) * (1-r^2)^(-1/2)`) are masked in each frame.
- **Consistent Masking Across Frames:** The same token mask is applied across different frames. This choice aims to prevent information leakage that could occur if different masks were used on highly similar adjacent frames.
- **Parallel Sampling Advantage:** This dynamic mask schedule is critical for enabling parallel sampling during inference. Unlike autoregressive methods that predict tokens sequentially, WorldDreamer can predict multiple output tokens in a single forward pass, leveraging a Markovian property assumption where tokens become conditionally independent given others.
- **Inference Process:** The inference also follows a cosine mask schedule. At each step, a fixed fraction of the highest-confidence masked tokens are predicted. These predicted tokens are then unmasked for subsequent steps, iteratively reducing the set of masked tokens. This parallel prediction significantly accelerates video generation, requiring approximately 10 steps compared to ~30 for diffusion models or ~200 for autoregressive methods.

#### 3.4. Datasets

WorldDreamer was trained on a diverse set of images and videos:
- **Deduplicated LAION-2B:** A refined version of the LAION dataset, used for its scale and quality, after addressing issues like duplication and text-image discrepancies.
- **WebVid-10M:** Approximately 10 million short videos, each paired with text. The dataset's inherent watermarks led to further refinement on self-collected data.
- **Self-collected video-text pairs:** 500K high-quality video clips obtained from the internet, preprocessed to detect continuous scenes, filter slow motion, and captioned in detail using Gemini (extracting keyframes and generating descriptive captions).
- **NuScenes:** A dataset for autonomous driving, comprising training and validation videos with front-view footage. Detailed captions were generated using Gemini, including weather, time, road structure, and traffic elements. Yaw angle and velocity of the ego-car were extracted as action metadata.

#### 3.5. Implementation Details

- **Training Stages:**
 1. Initial training on WebVid and LAION datasets: 16 frames (videos) or 16 independent images (LAION) at 256×256 resolution. Trained for 2M iterations with a batch size of 64 using AdamW (learning rate 5 × 10<sup>-5</sup>, weight decay 0.01).
 2. Finetuning on self-collected datasets and NuScenes: Input resolution 192×320, 24 frames per sample. Finetuned for 20K iterations with a batch size of 32 (learning rate 1 × 10<sup>-5</sup>), allowing all 1B parameters of STPT to be trained.
- **Inference:** Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG) is employed to enhance generation quality by interpolating between conditional and unconditional logits. The pretrained VQGAN decoder directly outputs video from predicted visual tokens. WorldDreamer can generate 24 frames at 192×320 resolution in approximately 3 seconds on a single A800 GPU.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

WorldDreamer's experimental evaluation demonstrates its capabilities in generating and editing videos across a range of scenarios, indicating an understanding of visual dynamics in general world environments.

**Key Findings:**

1. **Versatility in Video Generation Tasks:** WorldDreamer exhibits proficiency across multiple video generation and editing tasks, including image-to-video synthesis, text-to-video generation, video inpainting, video stylization, and action-to-video generation. This demonstrates the model's adaptability to diverse input conditions and objectives.

2. **High-Fidelity Image-to-Video Generation:**
 - The model can generate high-quality, cinematic landscape videos from a single initial image input.
 - Generated videos show seamless frame-to-frame motion and maintain consistency with the composition and content of the original image.

3. **Proficient Text-to-Video Generation:**
 - WorldDreamer is capable of generating videos from text prompts across various stylistic paradigms.
 - The generated videos align with the input language descriptions, with text serving as a control mechanism for content, style, and camera motion.

4. **Exceptional Video Editing Capabilities:**
 - **Video Inpainting:** The model demonstrates the ability to perform high-quality video inpainting. By specifying a masked region and providing a text prompt for desired modifications, WorldDreamer alters the original video content within the masked area to produce realistic results.
 - **Video Stylization:** WorldDreamer excels in video stylization. Using a randomly generated visual token mask and a style prompt, the model transforms the original video to achieve specified stylistic effects (e.g., "Autumn style"), resulting in convincing visual outcomes.

5. **Action-to-Video Generation for Autonomous Driving:**
 - In the context of autonomous driving, WorldDreamer can generate future video frames based on an initial frame and specific driving actions (e.g., "Turn Left," "Turn Right").
 - The model produces distinct future video sequences corresponding to different action commands, indicating an ability to understand and simulate physical responses to actions within a dynamic environment.

6. **Inference Speed Advantage:**
 - Compared to diffusion-based methods, which typically require around 30 steps for noise reduction, and autoregressive methods, which may need approximately 200 steps for iterative token prediction, WorldDreamer achieves video generation through parallel prediction of masked tokens in about 10 steps.
 - This parallel decoding capability results in a notable speed advantage, estimated to be approximately 3× to 20× faster than diffusion-based or autoregressive methods, with a 24-frame, 192×320 resolution video generated in approximately 3 seconds on an A800 GPU.

These results collectively indicate that WorldDreamer has acquired an understanding of general visual dynamics, enabling it to produce and manipulate video content effectively across diverse conditions and scenarios.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

WorldDreamer represents a step towards the development of general world models for video generation. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Advancement in General World Modeling:** The introduction of WorldDreamer addresses a limitation in existing world models, which have largely been confined to specific domains (e.g., gaming, autonomous driving). By framing world modeling as an unsupervised visual sequence modeling task, WorldDreamer aims to learn a more comprehensive understanding of general world physics and motions. This generalization capability is crucial for AI systems aspiring to interact with and predict dynamics in varied, real-world environments.

2. **Unified Framework for Diverse Video Tasks:** WorldDreamer demonstrates versatility in executing a wide array of video generation and editing tasks, including text-to-video conversion, image-to-video synthesis, video inpainting, video stylization, and action-to-video generation. This multifunctionality within a single model offers a more integrated and potentially efficient approach compared to specialized models for each task.

3. **Leveraging LLM Success for Visual Domain:** By drawing inspiration from the success of large language models (LLMs) and their masked token prediction paradigms, WorldDreamer applies proven Transformer-based architectures and scaling principles to the visual domain. This methodology suggests a promising avenue for constructing large-scale, general-purpose visual models that benefit from architectural optimizations developed for LLMs.

4. **Efficiency in Video Generation:** The parallel decoding strategy employed by WorldDreamer provides a significant speed advantage over both diffusion-based and autoregressive methods. Generating videos in fewer steps (approximately 10 iterations) can reduce computational costs and inference time, making high-quality video generation more accessible for practical applications and iterative design processes.

5. **Enhanced Controllability with Multimodal Prompts:** The incorporation of multi-modal prompts (text and action signals) facilitates interaction and provides fine-grained control over video generation. This enables users to specify desired content, style, or actions, moving beyond unconditional generation towards more purposeful and context-aware video synthesis.

6. **Foundational for Future AI Systems:** A world model with a profound understanding of general visual dynamics could serve as a foundational component for various advanced AI applications. This includes improved robotics, more realistic simulations for training autonomous agents, advanced content creation tools, and AI systems capable of deeper scene comprehension and interaction with dynamic environments.

In conclusion, WorldDreamer's approach to learning general world dynamics through masked visual token prediction and its demonstrated versatility across multiple video tasks mark it as a notable contribution to the field of generative AI, with potential implications for building more capable and efficient visual intelligence systems.
