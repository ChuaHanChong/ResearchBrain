# A Cyclic Adaptation-Generalization Framework with Uncertainty-Guided Self-Paced Learning for Long-Term Brain-Machine Interfaces

> [!summary] Summary
> A framework named Uncertainty-guided Self-paced Cycling (UnSPC) is introduced to address neural drift in long-term Brain-Machine Interfaces by integrating Domain Adaptation and Domain Generalization with an uncertainty-guided pseudo-labeling strategy. This approach maintains high neural decoding accuracy over extended periods without requiring recalibration, outperforming existing state-of-the-art methods on non-human primate and human datasets.

## Problem

- Invasive Brain-Machine Interfaces (iBMIs) suffer from performance degradation over long-term deployment due to neural drift, necessitating frequent recalibration.
- Existing methods like Domain Adaptation (DA) and Domain Generalization (DG) are often employed in isolation, leading to limitations such as susceptibility to noise or overly conservative generalization.
- Leveraging unlabeled target data for continuous regression-based neural decoding is challenging due to difficulties in confidently estimating pseudo-label reliability and the risk of error propagation from noisy neural signals.

## Method

- RPD initializes the model with robustness by training on clean and masked neural inputs with feature-label and teacher-student consistency constraints.
- UnSPL quantifies predictive uncertainty using ==Monte-Carlo Dropout== and inconsistency metrics to progressively select and assign weights to high-fidelity pseudo-labeled samples from unlabeled target data.
- CycAG iteratively refines the model by alternating between ==Domain Adaptation (DA)== and ==Domain Generalization (DG)== steps, leveraging the reliable pseudo-labeled samples to synergistically adapt and generalize.

## Results

- UnSPC consistently achieved superior neural decoding accuracy compared to static decoders and state-of-the-art DA/DG methods across multiple non-human primate and human datasets, effectively mitigating performance degradation over extended periods.
- Ablation studies confirmed that the Robust Pre-training Decoder, Uncertainty-guided Self-paced Pseudo-labeling, and Cyclic Adaptation-Generalization components each contribute significantly to the framework's overall effectiveness and robustness.
- The framework demonstrated low sensitivity to its hyperparameters and maintained high performance on a human dataset, indicating its robustness and practical applicability for stable, long-term human Brain-Machine Interfaces without frequent recalibration.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A synergistic and cyclic integration of Domain Adaptation and Domain Generalization effectively addresses both target-specific neural drift and generalizability to unseen variations in iBMIs, surpassing the limitations of isolated approaches.
> - Quantifying predictive uncertainty, combining Monte-Carlo Dropout and inconsistency measures, enables robust and progressive pseudo-labeling for continuous regression tasks in noisy neural environments, preventing detrimental error accumulation.
> - A stable pre-training phase incorporating hybrid neural decoding and consistency constraints is crucial for providing a robust model backbone, which significantly enhances the overall performance and long-term generalization of the BMI decoder.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Brain-Machine Interfaces (BMIs) represent a field with potential applications in neurorehabilitation, human performance augmentation, and human-centered robotics by establishing a direct link between brain activity and external devices. Invasive BMIs (iBMIs), which record neural activity from motor cortical areas, offer high-fidelity signals for decoding motor intentions, facilitating control of prosthetic and robotic systems. Significant progress has been made in neural recording technologies and decoding algorithms, improving accuracy and expanding applicability.

However, a fundamental challenge for the long-term deployment of iBMIs is neural drift. This phenomenon, caused by factors such as electrode shifts, neuronal turnover, and neural plasticity, leads to a gradual change in the statistical relationship between neural activity and intended movements. Consequently, decoders trained on initial data often show degraded performance over time when applied to later sessions, necessitating frequent recalibration.

Existing approaches to address neural non-stationarity generally fall into two categories: Domain Adaptation (DA) and Domain Generalization (DG). DA-based methods aim to maintain performance by aligning the decoder with the statistics of new sessions. While effective in some scenarios, DA can be susceptible to negative transfer if driven by noisy features or severe distribution shifts. Conversely, DG-based methods focus on learning invariant representations from source-domain data that are robust to unseen shifts, but they can be overly conservative, potentially failing to capture session-specific neural drifts by ignoring target-domain statistics. A common limitation is that DA and DG are typically treated as isolated paradigms, despite potentially complementary roles.

Furthermore, many previous methods primarily address global marginal distributions, often overlooking fine-grained subdomain shifts, which are relevant for precise neural decoding. Pseudo-labeling is a technique that could provide instance-level supervision for unlabeled target data, but its application in iBMIs, particularly for continuous regression tasks, faces challenges. These include the difficulty in confidently estimating reliability for regression pseudo-labels (unlike discrete classification) and the risk of error propagation due to the inherent noise in neural signals.

This work positions itself within this landscape by addressing these limitations. It argues for a synergistic integration of DA and DG, proposing that DG can provide a stable initialization to protect DA from noise, while DA can leverage unlabeled target data to guide DG. Additionally, it aims to develop a robust regression-based pseudo-labeling framework that can handle the specific challenges of iBMIs, thereby enabling more stable and reliable long-term BMI operation without requiring frequent manual recalibration.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a robust framework that can mitigate the performance degradation of invasive Brain-Machine Interfaces (iBMIs) over long-term deployment, primarily caused by neural drift. This overarching goal is broken down into several specific objectives, motivated by the limitations of current methodologies:

1. **To synergistically integrate Domain Adaptation (DA) and Domain Generalization (DG) within a unified framework:** Existing methods typically employ DA or DG in isolation. The authors argue that these paradigms are complementary; DA provides adaptability to evolving target distributions, while DG enhances robustness to unseen variations. The motivation is to overcome the limitations of single-paradigm approaches, where DA might overfit to noisy target features and DG might be too conservative by ignoring target-specific information.
2. **To effectively address fine-grained subdomain neural drift:** Prior methods often focus on global distribution shifts, neglecting more subtle, subdomain-specific changes in neural activity that are crucial for high-precision neural decoding. The objective is to develop mechanisms that can capture and adapt to these fine-grained shifts.
3. **To establish a robust, regression-based pseudo-labeling mechanism for iBMIs:** Pseudo-labeling is a promising approach for leveraging unlabeled target data, but its application to continuous regression tasks in iBMIs is challenging. The motivation is to overcome two specific hurdles:
 - **Difficulty in confidence estimation:** Unlike classification, where confidence scores are readily available, estimating the reliability of pseudo-labels in a regression context is not straightforward.
 - **Risk of error propagation:** Neural signals are inherently noisy, and incorrect pseudo-labels can lead to detrimental error accumulation during model refinement.
4. **To develop an iterative learning paradigm that progressively refines the decoding model:** The process of adapting to neural drift is continuous. The objective is to create a cyclic framework that can iteratively improve decoding performance by selectively leveraging reliable information from the target domain, gradually adapting to its evolving statistics while maintaining generalizability.

In summary, the motivation stems from the critical need for stable and reliable long-term iBMI operation, which is currently hindered by neural drift and the inadequacy of isolated DA/DG methods and unrefined pseudo-labeling strategies for regression tasks. The research aims to bridge these gaps by proposing a comprehensive framework that unifies these concepts through an uncertainty-guided, self-paced learning approach.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework, named Uncertainty-guided Self-paced Cycling (UnSPC), is designed to mitigate performance degradation in long-term BMIs by progressively adapting to evolving neural distributions while preserving robust and generalizable representations. UnSPC operates in two main stages: Source-session Pre-training and Target-session Refining. A teacher-student paradigm, where the teacher model's parameters are updated via an Exponential Moving Average (EMA) of the student's, is employed throughout to stabilize training.

**A. Source-session Pre-training (Robust Pre-training Decoder - RPD)**
The first stage involves training a Robust Pre-training Decoder (RPD) on the labeled source session data to provide a stable initial model. This stage incorporates:

1. **Hybrid Neural Decoding:** To enhance robustness against electrode instability and channel dropouts, the decoder is trained with both clean and randomly masked neural inputs.
 - **Clean Neural Decoding Loss (L_CND):** Standard mean squared error (MSE) between predictions on clean neural signals and true behavioral labels.
 - **Masked Neural Decoding Loss (L_MND):** MSE between predictions on corrupted (masked) neural signals and true behavioral labels, simulating electrode perturbations.
2. **Consistency Constraints:** These constraints aim to stabilize representation learning and improve cross-session generalization.
 - **Feature-Label Consistency Loss (L_FLC):** Enforces that pairwise distances in the neural feature space align with those in the behavioral (label) space, preserving the geometric relationship between neural activity and movements. Batch normalization is applied to stabilize distance scales.
 - **Teacher-Student Consistency Loss (L_TSC):** Ensures consistency between the teacher model's predictions on clean signals and the student model's predictions on masked signals, further improving robustness to input corruption.
3. **Overall Pre-training Loss (L_PRE):** Combines L_CND, L_FLC, L_MND, and L_TSC with a weighting parameter α to balance the contributions of clean and masked signal-related losses.

**B. Target-session Refining**
After pre-training, the model is refined on the unlabeled target session data through an iterative procedure. This stage involves two key modules:

1. **Uncertainty-guided Self-paced Pseudo-labeling (UnSPL):** This module addresses subdomain drift and label scarcity by quantifying predictive uncertainty to progressively mine high-fidelity pseudo-labeled samples from the unlabeled target domain.
 - **Reliable Sample Selection (RSS):**
 - Pseudo-labels (ˆy_t_i) for target samples (x_t_i) are generated using the teacher model.
 - Prediction uncertainty (u_i) is estimated from two perspectives:
 - **Monte-Carlo (MC) Dropout Uncertainty (u_MC_i):** Computed by averaging the squared differences between multiple stochastic forward passes (with dropout) and their mean prediction.
 - **Inconsistency (IC) Uncertainty (u_IC_i):** Measures the discrepancy between the teacher's prediction on clean input and its prediction on masked input, plus the discrepancy between the teacher's prediction on clean input and the student's prediction on clean input.
 - A combined uncertainty (u_i) is derived by summing batch-normalized u_MC_i and u_IC_i.
 - A self-paced strategy selects a candidate reliable set R(τ) based on a dynamically adjusted uncertainty threshold τ_w. τ_w is chosen at each iteration to ensure a progressively increasing proportion of selected samples (controlled by γ_0 and ∆γ).
 - **Reliable Sample Weighing (RSW):** Samples in the reliable set R_w are ranked by their uncertainty. A weight (z_i) is assigned to each sample based on its uncertainty rank using an exponential decay function (exp(-β * rank/|R_w|)). This mechanism gives higher weights to more reliable samples, mitigating noise propagation.

2. **Cyclic Adaptation-Generalization (CycAG):** This novel iterative optimization scheme alternates between DA and DG steps, leveraging the reliable pseudo-labeled samples from UnSPL.
 - **Domain Adaptation (DA) Step (L_DA):** This step aims to align feature distributions and improve prediction accuracy on the target domain.
 - **Maximum Mean Discrepancy (MMD) Loss (L_MMD):** Aligns feature distributions between the source and target domains.
 - **Weighted Supervised Learning (WSL) Loss (L_WSL):** Utilizes the pseudo-labels and their assigned weights from UnSPL for supervised learning on the reliable set.
 - The overall DA loss (L_DA) combines L_CND, L_FLC (from source pre-training), L_WSL, and L_MMD with weighting parameters α, β, and η.
 - **Domain Generalization (DG) Step (L_DG):** This step focuses on improving the model's robustness to unseen variations.
 - **Teacher-Student Consistency on Target (L_TSCT):** Enforces consistency between the teacher's prediction on clean target signals and the student's prediction on masked target signals.
 - **Weighted Masked Neural Decoding (WMND) Loss (L_WMND):** Applies the masked neural decoding principle to the reliable target samples, using their pseudo-labels and weights.
 - The overall DG loss (L_DG) combines L_CND, L_FLC (from source pre-training), L_WMND, and L_TSCT with weighting parameters α, β, and ϕ.

This cyclic process allows for complementary learning: DA improves target-specific performance, while DG enhances robustness, leading to a more stable and reliable decoding model under neural drift. The teacher model is used for final inference.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The effectiveness and robustness of UnSPC were evaluated through extensive experiments on multiple non-human primate (NHP) and human datasets, using Pearson Correlation Coefficient (CC), Coefficient of Determination (R^2), and Mean Squared Error (MSE) as metrics.

**A. Recalibration Performance Comparison**
Qualitative and quantitative results were presented for three NHP datasets (Chewie, Mihili, Jango), spanning short-term (∼1 day), medium-term (∼1 month), and long-term (∼3 months) neural drift.

1. **Qualitative Results:**
 - **Feature Distributions:** Visualizations of feature distributions (colored by speed and direction) showed that an ideal decoder maintains a radial shape and label-feature consistency. The static decoder's feature distributions exhibited significant deviations. Existing alignment-based methods partially reduced these deviations, but UnSPC achieved feature distributions described as "nearly ideal."
 - **Motor Trajectories:** Trajectories decoded by UnSPC were observed to be closer to the ideal motion trajectories compared to those from static decoders or other alignment-based methods.

2. **Quantitative Results (Table I):**
 - **Static Decoder:** Demonstrated significant performance degradation (e.g., R^2 dropping from 59.24% on C1 to 30.23% on C3 for Chewie) without recalibration, highlighting the challenge of neural drift.
 - **DA and DG Methods:** DA-based methods (DA-DCF, UAN, WDGRL) and the DG-based method (LFDA) showed partial mitigation of performance drop, but their effectiveness was limited due to the absence of target labels or overly conservative generalization.
 - **Semi-Supervised Methods:** SeSA, a semi-supervised method requiring a few labeled target data, generally improved performance over most unsupervised baselines.
 - **UnSPC Performance:** UnSPC consistently and substantially outperformed the static decoder baseline and achieved superior performance compared to state-of-the-art DA and DG methods across all datasets and time spans. Notably, UnSPC surpassed SeSA on most datasets, despite being a fully unsupervised approach. For instance, on Chewie C1, UnSPC achieved an R^2 of 78.40%, compared to SeSA's 69.43% and WDGRL's 72.45%. Similar trends were observed for Jango and Mihili datasets, demonstrating its effectiveness in mitigating cross-session neural drift.

**B. Ablation Study (Table II)**
Experiments on the Chewie C0-C1 dataset validated the contribution of each component of UnSPC.

1. **Robust Pre-training Decoder (RPD):** Incorporating RPD (Baseline+RPD) significantly improved performance (R^2 from 59.24% to 73.04%) compared to a basic source-trained model, confirming its role as a strong and stable backbone initialization.
2. **Uncertainty-guided Self-paced Pseudo-labeling (UnSPL):**
 - Removing Reliable Sample Selection (RSS) or Reliable Sample Weighing (RSW) individually led to performance degradation (R^2 dropping from 78.40% to 77.46% and 77.80% respectively), indicating their importance.
 - Removing UnSPL entirely (i.e., applying pseudo-labels directly without uncertainty guidance) resulted in a severe performance drop (R^2 of 73.30%, lower than Baseline+RPD), confirming that noisy pseudo-labels can be detrimental and UnSPL is critical for effective recalibration.
3. **Cyclic Adaptation-Generalization (CycAG):**
 - Removing either the Domain Adaptation (CycDA) or Domain Generalization (CycDG) component individually resulted in reduced performance (R^2 76.36% for w/o CycDA, 77.01% for w/o CycDG) compared to the full UnSPC, demonstrating their individual contributions.
 - Combining DA and DG without the cyclic iteration ("UnSPC w/o Cyc") yielded competitive results (R^2 77.69%) but was still outperformed by the full CycAG framework (R^2 78.40%). This indicates that the iterative cycling between adaptation and generalization provides synergistic benefits.

**C. Robustness Analysis**

1. **Sensitivity Analysis of Hyperparameters (Table III):** UnSPC demonstrated low sensitivity to its hyperparameters (α, β, η, ϕ) within a reasonably wide range of values. This suggests robust performance across different settings, as optimal performance was maintained even with variations in these parameters. For instance, α values between 1.0 and 2.0 yielded similarly high R^2 values, and β between 0.05 and 0.2 performed well.
2. **Robustness on Human Dataset (Table IV):** When evaluated on a human robotic effector dataset, UnSPC consistently achieved the highest performance across three subsequent target days compared to the static decoder and other SOTA methods (UAN, WDGRL). This indicates its effectiveness and robustness in real-world human BMI scenarios, suggesting its ability to reliably handle cross-day neural variability without labeled target data.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The UnSPC framework addresses a critical and persistent challenge in invasive Brain-Machine Interfaces (iBMIs): the long-term performance degradation caused by neural drift. By proposing a novel methodology that cyclically integrates Domain Adaptation (DA) and Domain Generalization (DG with uncertainty-guided self-paced pseudo-labeling, this work makes several significant contributions to the field.

Firstly, UnSPC is presented as the first unified framework to cyclically integrate DA and DG with pseudo-labeling for regression-based neural decoding. This integration is significant because it recognizes and exploits the complementary nature of DA and DG, enabling the model to simultaneously adapt to evolving target-specific neural distributions and maintain robust, generalized representations against unseen variations. This addresses the limitations of previous methods that often treated these paradigms in isolation, leading to suboptimal performance in dynamic neural environments.

Secondly, the introduction of Uncertainty-guided Self-paced Pseudo-labeling (UnSPL) provides a novel solution for robustly leveraging unlabeled target data in regression tasks. This is particularly impactful for iBMIs, where the lack of a natural confidence metric for continuous predictions and the high noise levels in neural signals can lead to detrimental error propagation in conventional pseudo-labeling. UnSPL's mechanism for quantifying prediction uncertainty and assigning weights based on reliability allows for a progressive, noise-attenuated refinement process, which is crucial for stable self-training.

Thirdly, the Cyclic Adaptation-Generalization (CycAG) scheme represents a significant methodological advance. By iteratively alternating between DA and DG steps, the framework facilitates a synergistic learning process. DA benefits from DG's stable initialization by being shielded from noise-induced overfitting, while DG is guided by the target-specific supervision signals provided by DA, leading to a more comprehensive and stable model refinement.

The extensive experimental validations across multiple NHP and human datasets, demonstrating superior performance against state-of-the-art baselines, underscore the practical potential of UnSPC. Its ability to maintain high decoding accuracy over extended periods without requiring frequent manual recalibration or additional labeled target data makes it a promising computational methodology.

The potential impact of this research is substantial for the practical deployment of iBMIs. By providing a stable and reliable method for long-term neural decoding, UnSPC could facilitate:
- **Enhanced Neurorehabilitation:** Enabling patients with paralysis to consistently control prosthetic limbs or assistive devices over prolonged periods, improving their quality of life.
- **Improved Human-Robot Interaction:** Paving the way for more intuitive and stable brain-controlled robotic systems in various applications.
- **Advancements in Assistive Technologies:** Making BMI-driven devices more user-friendly and reliable for daily use.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The framework's principles for integrating adaptation, generalization, and uncertainty-guided learning could inspire further developments in other domains facing similar challenges of distributional shift and label scarcity.

In conclusion, this work offers a robust and effective solution to a critical bottleneck in BMI technology, moving closer to the realization of stable and practical long-term brain-machine interfaces.
