# Fabrica: Dual-Arm Assembly of General Multi-Part Objects via Integrated Planning and Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Fabrica introduces an autonomous system for dual-arm assembly of diverse multi-part objects directly from CAD models, integrating hierarchical planning with learning-based local control. This approach enables a single generalist policy to perform contact-rich insertions, achieving an 80% step-wise success rate on real robots with zero-shot sim-to-real transfer and demonstrating robust recovery capabilities for complex assemblies.

## Problem

- Traditional robotic assembly systems require extensive manual programming, lack generalizability to novel geometries, and struggle with long-horizon, contact-rich tasks involving multiple parts.
- Coordinating dual-arm robots for complex assemblies presents significant challenges in planning stable grasps, optimal sequences, and collision-free motions.
- Training robust manipulation policies for real-world scenarios is time-consuming and expensive, necessitating effective sim-to-real transfer for complex, multi-step tasks.

## Method

- A ==hierarchical dual-arm planning== framework optimizes assembly sequences, grasp selections for both holding and assembling, and motion paths directly from CAD models, considering stability and kinematics.
- A general single-step assembly policy is learned using reinforcement learning, leveraging ==path-centric coordinate transformations== for ==SE(3)-equivariance== and ==plan-guided residual actions== for robust, compliant insertions.
- The system integrates offline global planning with online reactive RL control for end-to-end execution, incorporating techniques like ==domain randomization== and ==Policy-Level Action Integrator (PLAI)== to achieve zero-shot sim-to-real transfer.

## Results

- The planning framework efficiently generates optimal assembly plans for complex objects within minutes, achieving significantly higher scores for supportive parts and grasp stability compared to random sequences.
- Real-world dual-arm execution achieved an **80%** step-wise success rate without intervention, with out-of-distribution generalist policies performing comparably to specialist policies, demonstrating strong zero-shot transferability.
- For multi-step assemblies, Fabrica achieved **81%** cumulative success with just one human intervention and **95-100%** with two interventions, substantially outperforming baselines lacking planning optimizations.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating a detailed hierarchical planner with a reactive learning-based controller effectively addresses the complexity of long-horizon tasks while maintaining robustness against real-world uncertainties.
> - Path-centric coordinate transformations are crucial for enabling a single, generalist RL policy to generalize across diverse insertion directions and orientations, simplifying the learning problem.
> - Learning residual actions on top of coarse planner outputs provides an effective way to inject prior knowledge, allowing the RL policy to focus on fine-grained adjustments for contact-rich interactions.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Robotic assembly has been a longstanding grand challenge in robotics, crucial for automation in manufacturing and beyond. Traditionally, industrial assembly robots are programmed manually for repetitive tasks in highly structured environments, demanding significant engineering effort and lacking adaptability to new designs or uncertainties.

The broader research landscape for robotic assembly can be segmented into several key areas:
- **Assembly Sequence Planning:** Early work focused on geometric reasoning and sampling-based motion planners to determine the order of parts. More recent efforts incorporate physics-based planning and reinforcement learning (RL) for combinatorial sequence search. However, these often neglect real-world execution uncertainties.
- **Contact-Rich Manipulation and Skill Learning:** Tasks like part insertion are inherently contact-rich, requiring compliant and adaptive control. Reinforcement learning, especially with sim-to-real transfer, has shown promise in learning these skills. However, most prior work focuses on simplified settings like two-part, top-down insertions with fixed grasps, which are insufficient for complex multi-part assemblies with diverse insertion angles and bi-manual operations.
- **Generalization and Adaptability:** A major limitation of existing robotic assembly systems is their domain-specificity. Solutions for assembling IKEA furniture or LEGO blocks, while impressive, often lack the generalizability needed for arbitrary multi-part objects. The goal of developing "generalist" policies that can adapt to varying geometries, assembly paths, and grasp poses remains largely unfulfilled.
- **Dual-Arm Coordination:** Multi-part assembly often benefits from dual-arm manipulation for stability (one arm holding, the other assembling) or complex interactions. Coordinating two arms introduces significant planning and control challenges.
- **Benchmarks:** Robust and generalizable benchmarks for multi-part assembly are crucial for evaluating progress, but existing ones tend to be narrow, limiting the assessment of generalization.

"Fabrica" addresses these gaps by presenting a holistic solution that integrates advances from several sub-fields. It moves beyond domain-specific solutions by aiming for *general* multi-part objects using *dual-arm* robots. Crucially, it tackles the entire pipeline from CAD models to robot execution, combining sophisticated *long-horizon planning* with *robust local control via reinforcement learning*. The novelty lies in its successful integration of hierarchical planning that considers stability and kinematics, with learning-based control that leverages equivariant representations and residual actions for generalization and zero-shot sim-to-real transfer. By introducing a new benchmark, "Fabrica" also contributes to providing a standardized evaluation platform for future research in this challenging domain.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of the Fabrica project is to develop a fully autonomous, end-to-end robotic system capable of assembling *general multi-part objects* using *dual-arm robots*, solely from CAD models, without requiring domain-specific knowledge or human demonstrations. This overarching goal is driven by several key motivations and aims to address specific limitations of existing robotic assembly approaches:

1. **Overcome Challenges of Long-Horizon, Contact-Rich Manipulation:** Multi-part assemblies involve many sequential steps, each requiring precise, contact-rich interactions (e.g., part insertions). Current robots struggle with the combination of long planning horizons and robust execution in uncertain, contact-rich scenarios.
2. **Achieve Generalization Across Complex Geometries:** Existing robotic assembly systems are often tailored to specific objects (e.g., IKEA furniture, LEGOs) and fail to generalize to novel geometries, diverse insertion types (top-down, sideway), and varying grasp poses. Fabrica aims to build a system that can adapt to a wide range of industrial and daily objects.
3. **Integrate Planning and Control:** Robotic assembly demands both high-level task planning (sequencing, grasping, motion) and low-level reactive control for precise execution. The authors are motivated to seamlessly integrate these two aspects, where planning informs and guides learning, and learned policies handle uncertainties during execution.
4. **Enable Dual-Arm Operation:** Multi-part assembly often benefits from collaborative dual-arm manipulation (e.g., one arm holding a sub-assembly, the other inserting a part). This introduces significant coordination challenges that Fabrica aims to solve for improved stability and dexterity.
5. **Facilitate Zero-Shot Sim-to-Real Transfer:** Training complex manipulation policies in the real world is expensive and time-consuming. The project aims for policies trained entirely in simulation to transfer to real robots without additional fine-tuning, thus significantly reducing deployment costs.
6. **Develop a Comprehensive Benchmark:** To rigorously evaluate the performance and generalizability of their system and foster future research, the authors are motivated to create a diverse benchmark suite of multi-part assemblies.

In essence, Fabrica seeks to push robotic assembly from a domain-specific, manually programmed endeavor to a general-purpose, autonomous, and adaptable capability for complex manufacturing and domestic tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Fabrica adopts a highly integrated and hierarchical approach, combining offline global planning with online local reinforcement learning (RL) control for dual-arm assembly. The system takes part meshes and robot hardware configurations as input and outputs a complete assembly execution.

**A. Hierarchical Dual-Arm Planning (Offline)**

The planning component formulates the assembly problem as optimizing assembly-hold sequences, grasps, and robot motions, subject to precedence, kinematic, and collision constraints. To manage the intractability of joint optimization, it decomposes the problem into sequential sub-problems:

1. **Part Precedence Planning (Section 3.1):**
 - **Tier Generation:** Identifies groups of parts that can be disassembled independently using a physics-based motion planner (Assemble-Them-All). Tiers are ordered by disassembly feasibility.
 - **Graph Generation:** Constructs a directed precedence graph `G_prec` where an edge `o_i -> o_j` means `o_i` must be assembled before `o_j` due to blocking disassembly paths. This ensures structural integrity.

2. **Dual-Arm Grasp Filtering (Section 3.2):**
 - **Offline Pre-computation:** To avoid expensive online collision checks, a dense set of grasp candidates is sampled for each part.
 - **Feasibility Checks:** Each candidate grasp is checked for feasibility for both *assembling* (can the robot insert the part along its disassembly path without colliding with preceding parts?) and *holding* (can the robot hold the part while other parts are disassembled without collision or inter-arm collision?). Inverse Kinematics (IK) and collision checks are performed and recorded.
 - **Grasp Pair Filtering:** For every potential dual-arm assembly-hold pair, feasible grasp combinations are identified that avoid inter-arm collisions.

3. **Dual-Arm Sequence-Grasp Optimization (Section 3.3):**
 - **State-Space Search:** Formulates the problem as a state-space search (directed state tree `T_G`) where nodes represent partial assembly states (robot task, assembled parts, grasped part, grasp pose).
 - **Dynamic Programming (DP):** Propagates optimal cumulative scores through the tree using DP to find the best assembly-hold sequence and associated grasps (`phi*`, `sigma*`).
 - **Objective Function:** The cost function evaluates step-wise quality vectors (`f`), considering objectives such as: maximizing supportive held parts, minimizing grasp switches, minimizing approximated torque for assembling grasps, and maximizing contact area (weighted by orientation difference) for holding grasps to ensure stability for downstream control.

4. **Grasp-Aware Pickup Fixture Generation (Section 3.4):**
 - **Automated Design:** Automatically generates a 3D printable fixture for each part, ensuring stable top-down pickup without the need for reorientation or regrasping.
 - **Pose Determination:** Defines pickup pose based on the planned assembly grasp to maintain consistent relative gripper-part transformation.
 - **Layout Optimization:** Uses a 2D bin-packing algorithm to arrange parts on the XY plane, minimizing material usage and workspace, while ensuring collision-free pickup paths for the gripper. Mold cavities are generated based on part geometry and poses.

5. **Motion Planning for Transit and Transfer (Section 3.5):**
 - **RRT-Connect:** Uses the RRT-Connect algorithm to plan collision-free joint-space trajectories for all free-hand transit and grasping/transfer motions, leveraging parallelization given fixed start and goal states from previous stages.

**B. Learning General Single-Step Assembly Policy (Online Control)**

For contact-rich assembly steps (insertions), Fabrica employs a lightweight RL framework, executed online as a reactive controller. This controller complements the open-loop planned motions, providing robustness against uncertainties.

1. **Path-Centric Coordinate Transformation (Section 4.1):**
 - **SE(3)-Equivariance:** Transforms all straight-line assembly motions from the world frame into a canonical "top-down insertion" in a task-centric frame. This allows the RL agent to learn a single generalist policy for various insertion directions and orientations, simplifying the learning task by standardizing observations and actions. The agent observes transformed part position and outputs transformed delta position.
 - **Impedance Control:** A task-space impedance (TSI) controller is used for compliant insertions, with gains transformed into the path-centric frame for consistent behavior.

2. **Plan-Guided Residual Action (Section 4.2):**
 - **Prior Knowledge Injection:** The RL policy learns only the *corrective* (residual) action on top of the coarse open-loop action provided by the planner. This warm-starts learning, allowing the policy to focus on fine-grained adjustments for robustness rather than learning the full motion.

3. **Minimalist Reward Design and Sim-to-Real Transfer (Section 4.3):**
 - **Simple Reward:** A dense reward function, simply the negative L2 distance to the goal position, is used. This is effective because the planner provides initial states already in proximity to the goal.
 - **Sim-to-Real Techniques:**
 - **Domain Randomization:** Noise is added to object poses during simulation training to bridge the sim-to-real gap.
 - **Policy-Level Action Integrator (PLAI):** During real-world deployment, PLAI is used to improve action consistency and reduce error accumulation by incrementally applying policy outputs to the last desired state, rather than the current noisy state.

**C. System Integration and Execution:**

The real-world system alternates between:
- Executing pre-planned open-loop transit and transfer motions (joint space control).
- Executing closed-loop reactive RL policies for contact-rich insertion steps (task-space impedance control guided by learned policy).

This hybrid approach leverages the strengths of both planning (for long-horizon, global consistency) and learning (for local adaptability and robustness to uncertainty).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research paper presents a comprehensive evaluation of Fabrica across simulation and real-world experiments, highlighting its efficiency, optimality, and generalizability.

**A. Planning Multi-Step Assembly in Simulation:**
- **Efficiency:** The planning framework demonstrates impressive efficiency, generating optimal plans for complex assemblies within minutes (e.g., Duct: 592.7s, Stool: 502.7s). This speed is achieved through a hierarchical decomposition and significant parallelization of stages like precedence tier generation and grasp filtering.
- **Optimality:** Comparison with randomly generated sequences shows that the optimized plans achieve significantly better scores across all objectives. For example, the optimal beam assembly had 4.00 supportive parts compared to 0.88 for random, and a higher grasp contact area. This confirms that the sequence-grasp optimization effectively improves metrics related to stability and ease of manipulation.
- **Generality:** The planning framework is shown to be hardware-agnostic, demonstrating successful plan generation for different robot arms (Franka Emika Panda, UFactory xArm7, UR5e) and grippers without needing additional tuning.

**B. Learning Single-Step Assembly in Simulation:**
- **Performance Comparison:**
 - **Open-Loop Tracking:** Showed very low success rates (0-24%) across all assemblies, emphasizing its inability to handle uncertainties without feedback.
 - **Part Specialist (PS) and Assembly Specialist (AS) Policies:** Achieved high success rates (70-100%), demonstrating that RL can effectively learn robust insertion skills for specific parts or within a single assembly. AS policies showed competitive performance to PS, suggesting knowledge transfer between parts within an assembly.
 - **Assembly Generalist (AG) Policy:** While slightly lower, still demonstrated robust performance (58-99%), confirming that a single policy can generalize across diverse assemblies, largely thanks to the equivariant representations.
- **Ablation Studies (Appendix E.1):** The path-centric coordinate transformation and plan-guided residual actions were found to be crucial. Removing either significantly reduced performance, especially for assemblies involving diverse insertion directions (e.g., Plumber Block and Gamepad saw substantial drops in success without path-centric transformation).

**C. Executing Multi-Step Assembly in Real World:**
- **Step-wise Success Rates:**
 - Fabrica (AS and OOD AG policies) achieved an overall 80% success rate without intervention for individual steps.
 - The adapted ASAP baseline, lacking planning optimizations, performed substantially worse at 55%.
 - Open-Loop Tracking performed lowest at 23%, highlighting the necessity of reactive control in the real world.
 - Remarkably, the OOD (Out-of-Distribution) Generalist policy performed comparably to specialist policies, indicating excellent zero-shot transferability to novel, unseen assemblies.
- **Multi-step Cumulative Success Rates:**
 - Due to the challenging nature of real-world contact-rich tasks, all methods achieved near-zero cumulative success rates without *any* intervention. This emphasizes the inherent difficulty of long-horizon robotic assembly.
 - However, with minimal human interventions for failure recovery:
 - Fabrica (AS/AG) achieved 81% cumulative success with one intervention and 95-100% with two interventions, demonstrating remarkable robustness and recoverability.
 - The adapted ASAP baseline only reached 5% with one intervention and 57% with two.
 - Open-loop tracking remained at 0% with one intervention and only 14% with two.
- **Ablation Studies on Real-World Performance:**
 - **`w/o Part Seq Optim`:** Dropped overall step-wise success to 71% and cumulative 1-intervention success to 48%, showing that optimal sequencing is critical for stability and reducing part drift.
 - **`w/o Grasp Optim`:** Dropped overall step-wise success to 64% and cumulative 1-intervention success to 14%, indicating the importance of stable grasp selection for preventing slippage and resisting torques.
 - **`w/o Path-Centric Transform`:** Dropped overall step-wise success to 61% and cumulative 1-intervention success to 43%, confirming its crucial role in generalizing across varying assembly directions.
- **Failure Analysis:** Common failure modes included small parts slipping from grippers, cumulative error in large assemblies, unexpected obstructions from the holding gripper (not modeled in RL), and shifting of previously assembled parts. These highlight the challenges of the sim-to-real gap, where real-world uncertainties often exceed simulated noise.
- **Effect of Number of Trials (Appendix E.2):** Allowing additional trials per step significantly boosted real-world success rates, indicating the stochastic nature of the policy and its ability to refine alignment with repeated attempts.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

Fabrica represents a significant leap forward in autonomous robotic assembly, with profound implications for both academic research and industrial applications.

**1. Pioneering Generalizable End-to-End Assembly:** This work presents the first system capable of truly generalizable, end-to-end multi-part object assembly for dual-arm robots, directly from CAD models, without task-specific programming or human demonstrations. This shifts the paradigm from highly specialized, brittle systems to versatile and adaptive ones.

**2. Seamless Integration of Planning and Learning:** Fabrica effectively bridges the gap between global, long-horizon planning and local, reactive control. The hierarchical planner provides a structured approach for complex tasks, while the learning policies handle the nuances of contact-rich manipulation and real-world uncertainties. This integrated framework is a powerful template for future complex robotic tasks.

**3. Robustness and Sim-to-Real Transfer:** The success of zero-shot sim-to-real transfer, particularly with out-of-distribution generalist policies, is a major achievement. Techniques like path-centric coordinate transformation, plan-guided residual actions, domain randomization, and PLAI are crucial enablers for robust performance in the real world, paving the way for faster deployment of learned skills.

**4. Addressing Core Assembly Challenges:** The system directly tackles critical challenges such as dual-arm coordination, diverse grasp poses, varied insertion directions, and complex part geometries, which were previously major bottlenecks in robotic assembly. The automated fixture generation further streamlines the process by removing manual setup needs.

**5. Contribution of a Comprehensive Benchmark:** The introduction of a diverse benchmark suite for multi-part assemblies is invaluable for the research community. It provides a standardized platform for future work, enabling rigorous and reproducible evaluation of generalization capabilities, a key metric often overlooked in previous benchmarks.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Manufacturing and Logistics:** Fabrica could revolutionize small-batch manufacturing, custom product assembly, and rapid prototyping by significantly reducing the lead time and cost associated with manual programming and specialized tooling. It enables greater flexibility and adaptability on assembly lines.
- **Beyond Industrial Settings:** The techniques developed could extend to other complex manipulation tasks, including repair, disassembly, and even household robotics, where adaptability to novel objects and situations is paramount.
- **Foundation Models for Robotics:** The capability for autonomous data collection in simulation and minimal human intervention in the real world paves the way for generating large-scale, diverse datasets. These datasets could be instrumental in training "foundation models" for robotic manipulation, similar to large language models for text, leading to highly generalizable and intelligent robots.
- **Future Research Directions:** By clearly outlining its limitations (e.g., insertion-only, no subassembly reorientation, fixed grasp sets, light parts, lack of direct vision for bin-picking), Fabrica also sets a clear agenda for future research, inspiring work on increased dexterity, multi-fingered hands, integration of advanced perception systems, and handling more complex interactions like screwing or sliding.

In conclusion, Fabrica is a landmark contribution that pushes the frontier of autonomous robotic assembly, demonstrating an unprecedented level of generalization and robustness for complex, multi-part tasks. Its integrated planning and learning framework, coupled with rigorous evaluation and a new benchmark, will undoubtedly serve as a critical reference point for the next generation of intelligent robotic systems.
