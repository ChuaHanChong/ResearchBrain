# WestWorld: A Knowledge-Encoded Scalable Trajectory World Model for Diverse Robotic Systems

> [!summary] Summary
> WestWorld introduces a scalable trajectory world model for diverse robotic systems, employing a Knowledge-Encoded Embedding Modular (KNEE) to integrate robot morphology and a System-Aware Mixture-of-Experts (Sys-MoE) block to handle varying system dynamics. This approach significantly improves zero-shot generalization, few-shot adaptation, and scalability, outperforming baselines in dynamics prediction and downstream control tasks across simulated and real-world robots.

## Problem

- Existing multi-system trajectory world models struggle with scalability to a large number of diverse robotic systems due to 'gradient conflicts' and 'negative transfer' when using shared parameters.
- Many data-driven models overlook explicit robot morphological information, limiting their 'zero-shot generalization' capabilities to unseen systems by lacking physical inductive biases.
- Developing a unified world model capable of predicting future states for a multitude of robots with varying sensor/actuator types and kinematic structures remains a challenge.

## Method

- A Knowledge-Encoded Embedding Modular (KNEE) integrates explicit robot morphological information by converting kinematic trees into traversal indices, which are then encoded and added to trajectory embeddings.
- A System-Aware Mixture-of-Experts (Sys-MoE) block processes structure-aware embeddings, utilizing a router and specialized experts to implicitly learn distinct system dynamics for scalable joint training across diverse robots.
- The model is trained to minimize a next-token cross-entropy loss over discretized state/action channels, enabling multi-step trajectory prediction in a single forward pass.

## Results

- WestWorld achieved lower Mean Absolute Error (MAE) and Mean Squared Error (MSE) compared to baselines on zero-shot generalization to unseen systems (e.g., MAE of **16.350** on Walker2D vs. **20.122** for TDM).
- The model significantly outperformed baselines in few-shot adaptation tasks on real-world datasets, demonstrating faster adaptation and higher accuracy with limited fine-tuning data (e.g., MAE of **5.316** on Cassie vs. **7.834** for TrajWorld).
- WestWorld's prediction accuracy remained stable as the number of pretraining environments increased, showcasing robust scalability, and achieved the highest accumulated rewards in downstream MPPI control tasks, including successful real-world deployment on a Unitree Go1 robot.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly encoding robot physical structures (morphology) as an inductive bias significantly enhances a world model's ability to generalize to unseen robotic systems.
> - Utilizing a mixture-of-experts architecture with system-aware routing effectively mitigates task interference and negative transfer during joint training of diverse robot dynamics, leading to improved scalability.
> - Predicting multi-step trajectories in a single forward pass, rather than autoregressively, contributes to faster inference times, crucial for real-time control applications.
