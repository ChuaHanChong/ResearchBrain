# WestWorld: A Knowledge-Encoded Scalable Trajectory World Model for Diverse Robotic Systems

> [!summary] Summary
> WestWorld introduces a scalable trajectory world model for diverse robotic systems, employing a Knowledge-Encoded Embedding Modular (KNEE) to integrate robot morphology and a System-Aware Mixture-of-Experts (Sys-MoE) block to handle varying system dynamics. This approach significantly improves zero-shot generalization, few-shot adaptation, and scalability, outperforming baselines in dynamics prediction and downstream control tasks across simulated and real-world robots.

## Problem

- Existing multi-system trajectory world models struggle with scalability to a large number of diverse robotic systems due to 'gradient conflicts' and 'negative transfer' when using shared parameters.
- Many data-driven models overlook explicit robot morphological information, limiting their 'zero-shot generalization' capabilities to unseen systems by lacking physical inductive biases.
- Developing a unified world model capable of predicting future states for a multitude of robots with varying sensor/actuator types and kinematic structures remains a challenge.

## Method

- A Knowledge-Encoded Embedding Modular (KNEE) integrates explicit robot morphological information by converting kinematic trees into traversal indices, which are then encoded and added to trajectory embeddings.
- A System-Aware Mixture-of-Experts (Sys-MoE) block processes structure-aware embeddings, utilizing a router and specialized experts to implicitly learn distinct system dynamics for scalable joint training across diverse robots.
- The model is trained to minimize a next-token cross-entropy loss over discretized state/action channels, enabling multi-step trajectory prediction in a single forward pass.

## Results

- WestWorld achieved lower Mean Absolute Error (MAE) and Mean Squared Error (MSE) compared to baselines on zero-shot generalization to unseen systems (e.g., MAE of **16.350** on Walker2D vs. **20.122** for TDM).
- The model significantly outperformed baselines in few-shot adaptation tasks on real-world datasets, demonstrating faster adaptation and higher accuracy with limited fine-tuning data (e.g., MAE of **5.316** on Cassie vs. **7.834** for TrajWorld).
- WestWorld's prediction accuracy remained stable as the number of pretraining environments increased, showcasing robust scalability, and achieved the highest accumulated rewards in downstream MPPI control tasks, including successful real-world deployment on a Unitree Go1 robot.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly encoding robot physical structures (morphology) as an inductive bias significantly enhances a world model's ability to generalize to unseen robotic systems.
> - Utilizing a mixture-of-experts architecture with system-aware routing effectively mitigates task interference and negative transfer during joint training of diverse robot dynamics, leading to improved scalability.
> - Predicting multi-step trajectories in a single forward pass, rather than autoregressively, contributes to faster inference times, crucial for real-time control applications.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Trajectory world models are fundamental components in robotics for tasks such as dynamics learning, planning, and control, as they enable robots to predict future states based on their actions and sensory data. Historically, many world models have been developed for single robotic systems. Generalizing these models across diverse robotic platforms presents challenges due to varying sensor and actuator types, differing data sampling rates, and distinct kinematic structures, which collectively lead to sensor/actuator heterogeneity and significant system dynamics gaps.

Recent research has begun to explore multi-system trajectory world models. Approaches such as discretizing continuous states and actions into tokens and utilizing Transformer architectures for joint training have enabled pretraining across multiple robot types within a single model. However, these methods often encounter two primary limitations. First, they typically employ a single, dense model with shared parameters across all systems. This can lead to "gradient conflicts" and "negative transfer" as the diversity and number of robotic systems increase, hindering scalability. Second, these models frequently treat trajectories purely as abstract token sequences, often overlooking the explicit morphological information of the robots. This omission can limit their "zero-shot generalization" capabilities to entirely new and unseen robotic systems, as they lack physical inductive biases.

This work, WestWorld, positions itself as an advancement in this landscape by directly addressing these two identified limitations. It aims to develop a trajectory world model that can scale to a large number of diverse robotic systems more effectively than prior methods and incorporate explicit knowledge of robot physical structures to improve generalization, particularly to unseen systems. By integrating a specialized architecture for handling diverse dynamics and injecting structural priors, WestWorld seeks to overcome the scalability and generalization barriers faced by existing generalist world models.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from two challenges observed in existing trajectory world models when applied to diverse robotic systems:

1. **Scalability to a Large Number of Distinct System Dynamics:** Current methods, while attempting to model multiple systems with shared parameters (e.g., a single dense Transformer), frequently encounter issues such as gradient conflicts and negative transfer. This occurs because the optimization process for one robot's dynamics can interfere with learning the dynamics of another, particularly as the number and diversity of robots in the pretraining dataset increase. This limitation restricts the practical scalability of these models to a truly large and varied collection of robotic systems. The objective is to design a model that can learn distinct system dynamics at scale without significant task interference.

2. **Lack of Domain Knowledge of Physical Structures:** Many existing data-driven trajectory world models rely solely on state-action observations and do not explicitly encode the physical morphology or connectivity patterns of robots. Robots with similar physical structures often exhibit shared high-level dynamical behaviors, but without explicitly leveraging this "domain knowledge" or "physical inductive bias," models struggle to generalize efficiently, especially in zero-shot settings to unseen robotic systems. The motivation here is to inject this structural information into the model's representations to enhance its ability to capture underlying system dynamics and improve generalization.

Based on these challenges, the paper outlines two key objectives for WestWorld:

- To develop a novel, system-aware architecture that implicitly learns distinct system dynamics through specialized experts, thereby mitigating task interference and significantly improving scalability for joint training across diverse robotic systems.
- To introduce a mechanism for incorporating robot morphological information as an explicit inductive bias into trajectory representations, enhancing the model's zero-shot generalization capabilities to unseen robotic systems.

The overarching goal is to learn a single, unified trajectory world model that can capture the dynamics of a multitude of robotic systems and environments, providing robust future state predictions conditioned on actions, and subsequently improving performance in downstream tasks like model-based control.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

WestWorld's methodology is structured around two core components designed to address scalability and generalization: a Knowledge-Encoded Embedding Modular (KNEE) and a System-Aware Mixture-of-Experts (Sys-MoE) block.

**Overall Architecture:**
The process begins with channel-wise normalization and discretization of scalar variables into K-bin categorical vectors, followed by learned projections to d-dimensional embeddings. These embeddings are then enhanced by the KNEE Modular, which incorporates robot morphology. The structure-aware embeddings subsequently pass through multiple Sys-MoE blocks for dynamics modeling. A linear decoder finally maps the hidden states to future trajectory predictions.

**Knowledge-Encoded Embedding Modular (KNEE):**
- **Motivation:** To address the limitation of purely data-driven models by incorporating explicit domain knowledge about robot physical structures. The hypothesis is that similar connectivity patterns correlate with shared high-level dynamical behaviors.
- **Trajectory Tokenization:** Each state or action dimension at time *t* is treated as a scalar channel. Channel-wise min-max normalization is applied, followed by discretization into a K-bin categorical vector. This vector is then mapped to a *d*-dimensional embedding. Timestep, channel order index, and modality (state or action) embeddings are added to create *z*<sup>(m)</sup><sub>t</sub>. These per-channel embeddings are stacked into *S*<sub>t</sub> (states) and *A*<sub>t</sub> (actions).
- **Knowledge-Encoded Structural Embedding:**
 1. Each articulated object (robot) is modeled as a rooted kinematic tree, which is converted to a binary tree using the Left-Child-Right-Sibling (LCRS) transformation.
 2. For each body node in the LCRS-converted tree, three traversal indices (pre-order, in-order, post-order) are assigned. An additional object identifier is assigned for scenes with multiple objects (e.g., robot = 0, others by Euclidean distance).
 3. These tuple indices (object identifier, pre-order, in-order, post-order) uniquely identify each robot body node.
 4. Learned structural encoders map each discrete index to a *d*/4-dimensional vector. These vectors are concatenated to form a *d*-dimensional structural embedding, *p*<sup>(i,j)</sup>.
 5. This structural embedding *p*<sup>(i,j)</sup> is added to the corresponding state/action embeddings, yielding structure-aware trajectory embeddings that serve as inputs to the model.

**System-Aware MoE Block:**
- **Motivation:** To enable scalable joint training across diverse robotic systems and mitigate task interference by allowing different system dynamics to be learned by specialized experts. The concept is that complex dynamics can be approximated by composing basis dynamics modules with system-dependent coefficients.
- **Block Design:** Each Sys-MoE block comprises two parts:
 1. **Attention-based Aggregation:** Self-attention is applied to state variables to capture correlations. Cross-attention then conditions these state features on action embeddings, supporting variable state/action dimensionalities. For *k*-step prediction, history state embeddings are concatenated with *k* learnable query embeddings that serve as latent queries for future states.
 2. **System-Aware MoE Layer:** A learnable system embedding *e* is appended to the attention outputs. This augmented sequence is processed by a Mamba-style selective State-Space Model (SSM) layer. The output corresponding to the system embedding (*U*<sub>L+1</sub>) is used by a router, which produces mixture weights (*w*) over *P* experts via a softmax gate. Each expert *E*<sub>p</sub> is an MLP. The final output of the Sys-MoE layer is a weighted combination of expert predictions, *Y*<sup>(m)</sup><sub>1:L</sub>. Multiple Sys-MoE blocks can be stacked to increase model expressivity.

**Objective Function:**
The model is trained by minimizing a next-token cross-entropy loss. For each channel *m*, a linear decoder head produces logits over *K* uniform bins, which are then passed through a softmax to yield a categorical probability distribution *P*<sup>(m)</sup><sub>ℓ</sub>. The loss *L*<sub>CE</sub> penalizes discrepancies between these predictions and the ground-truth categorical representations of future states. During inference, the model operates in a sequence-to-sequence manner, enabling multi-step prediction in a single forward pass.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research involved pretraining WestWorld on a diverse dataset and conducting extensive experiments to evaluate its performance across several metrics.

**Diverse Pretraining Datasets:**
WestWorld was pretrained on 89 complex environments, comprising 80 simulated environments from the UniTraj dataset (which aggregates data from ExORL, RL Unplugged, JAT, DB-1, TD-MPC2, and Modular RL) and 9 real-world robot-arm datasets from Open X-Embodiment.

**Baseline Methods:**
Comparisons were made against:
- **MLP Ensemble:** A probabilistic dynamics model using an ensemble of MLPs.
- **TDM (Transformer Dynamics Model):** A Transformer-based model built on the Gato architecture, using tokenization and one-dimensional attention.
- **TrajWorld:** A Transformer-based trajectory model employing temporal-variate attention for autoregressive rollout.

All baselines were pretrained on the same dataset for fairness.

**Evaluation on Zero-shot Generalization:**
WestWorld was evaluated on three unseen robotic environments: Walker2D, Hopper (from D4RL), and a real-world Franka manipulator dataset. These systems shared structural similarities with pretraining data but were not explicitly seen during training. For 100-step consecutive predictions given a 50-step history:
- WestWorld consistently achieved lower Mean Absolute Error (MAE) and Mean Squared Error (MSE) compared to all baselines across all three environments (e.g., MAE of 16.350 on Walker2D vs. 20.122 for TDM, 13.731 on Hopper vs. 17.634 for TDM, 7.737 on Franka vs. 12.164 for MLP Ensemble).
- Visualizations of trajectory plots indicated that WestWorld tracked ground-truth dynamics more closely over long horizons.
- Errors reported in the original physical space also showed consistent gains for WestWorld. This suggests that the combined Sys-MoE architecture and structural inductive bias facilitated better generalization by selecting appropriate experts and leveraging morphology-aware representations.

**Evaluation on Few-shot Adaptation:**
The model's ability to adapt with limited data was tested on three real-world datasets with significant domain shifts from pretraining: Cassie bipedal jumping, Unitree A1 quadruped locomotion, and UR5 tabletop manipulation. Fine-tuning was performed using only 10 episodes.
- WestWorld significantly outperformed all baselines in MAE and MSE across all three systems (e.g., MAE of 5.316 on Cassie vs. 7.834 for TrajWorld, 4.227 on A1 vs. 5.138 for TrajWorld, 4.925 on UR5 vs. 8.066 for TrajWorld).
- Further analysis showed that pretraining dramatically improved both adaptation speed and final prediction accuracy in few-shot settings, demonstrating the value of learned dynamics representations.

**Evaluation on Scalability:**
Scalability was assessed by varying the number of pretraining environments (N from 1 to 89) while keeping the data budget per environment fixed.
- WestWorld's prediction accuracy remained stable and did not degrade significantly as the number of environments increased.
- In contrast, TrajWorld's performance deteriorated notably with increasing N. This was attributed to WestWorld's Sys-MoE design mitigating gradient interference and negative transfer during multi-system joint learning.
- Visualization of Sys-MoE routing weights confirmed sparse, system-dependent expert activation, indicating that different systems utilized distinct combinations of experts, supporting the model's scalable design.

**Evaluation on Downstream Control Task:**
WestWorld was evaluated as a dynamics predictor within an MPPI (Model Predictive Path Integral) controller for three diverse systems: Walker2D, Hopper (OpenAI Gym), and Unitree Go1.
- Pretraining consistently improved control performance (accumulated episode reward) for nearly all methods and systems compared to training from scratch.
- WestWorld achieved the highest accumulated rewards across all three systems and both training regimes, particularly after pretraining (e.g., 2134.60 for Walker2D, 2253.51 for Hopper, 2.20 for Go1 with pretraining). This indicates that WestWorld's improved dynamics prediction translates to more stable and effective action selection in MPC.
- A real-world deployment on a Unitree Go1 demonstrated that a distilled and fine-tuned WestWorld model successfully completed a straight-walking task, whereas a similarly processed TrajWorld model failed to perform reliably. This confirmed the real-world applicability and robustness of WestWorld's learned dynamics.

**Ablation Studies:**
- **Effect of Knowledge-Encoded Embedding (KNEE) Modular:** Removing the structural embedding ("w/o structural embedding") led to performance degradation, especially on complex morphologies like Hopper and Walker2D, with a smaller effect on Franka. This suggests structural encoding is beneficial for complex unseen systems.
- **Effect of System-Aware MoE Layer:** Replacing the Sys-MoE layer with a dense SSM layer of comparable parameter count ("w/o Sys-MoE") resulted in performance degradation across tasks. This indicates the Sys-MoE's criticality in mitigating inter-task interference for diverse dynamics.
Both KNEE and Sys-MoE components were found to be essential for WestWorld's generalization and scalable pretraining.

**Additional Discussions:**
- **Parameter-efficient fine-tuning:** Fine-tuning only a subset of WestWorld's parameters (e.g., the last two Sys-MoE layers, comprising 21.91% of parameters) was shown to outperform fully fine-tuned TrajWorld, suggesting efficient adaptation capabilities.
- **Computational Cost:** WestWorld demonstrated faster inference latency for long-horizon predictions compared to autoregressive Transformer baselines (e.g., 3.09x faster than TrajWorld for 100-step prediction), primarily due to its ability to predict all future steps in a single forward pass.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

WestWorld represents a notable advancement in the development of trajectory world models for robotics, addressing critical limitations pertaining to scalability and generalization across diverse robotic systems.

**Key Contributions and Significance:**
- **Enhanced Scalability:** The introduction of the System-Aware Mixture-of-Experts (Sys-MoE) block is a significant methodological contribution. By dynamically routing and combining specialized experts, the model effectively learns distinct system dynamics for a wide range of robots without incurring the negative transfer and gradient conflicts common in shared-parameter models. This allows for scalable pretraining on large, heterogeneous datasets.
- **Improved Generalization:** The Knowledge-Encoded Structural Embedding (KNEE) modular provides an explicit inductive bias by incorporating robot morphological connectivity. This domain knowledge allows WestWorld to better align trajectory representations with physical structures, leading to substantial improvements in zero-shot generalization to unseen robotic systems and more robust few-shot adaptation under domain shifts.
- **Demonstrated Performance:** The rigorous experimental evaluation, encompassing zero-shot prediction, few-shot adaptation, scalability tests, and downstream model-based control, consistently shows WestWorld outperforming competitive baselines. This includes its successful deployment on a real-world Unitree Go1 robot, which validates its practical utility in challenging real-world scenarios.
- **Computational Efficiency:** The model's architecture, particularly its single-forward-pass prediction for multi-step horizons, contributes to faster inference times compared to autoregressive methods, which is crucial for real-time control applications. Parameter-efficient fine-tuning also suggests a more resource-conscious deployment strategy.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Accelerated Robot Development:** A general-purpose, scalable world model like WestWorld could significantly streamline the development and deployment of new robotic systems. By leveraging a pretrained model, new robots might require less initial data and training time to learn their dynamics, facilitating faster iteration and broader application.
- **Enhanced Autonomy and Reliability:** Improved prediction accuracy and generalization capabilities contribute to more reliable and robust robot operation in complex and dynamic environments, including those with unseen robot types or novel task configurations. This could enable intelligent robots to adapt more effectively to unforeseen circumstances.
- **Interdisciplinary Research Catalyst:** The explicit integration of physical structure knowledge into neural network architectures could inspire further interdisciplinary research at the intersection of machine learning, classical robotics, and control theory. This could lead to more physically informed and robust AI models for embodied agents.
- **Real-world Applications:** The demonstrated success in real-world control tasks (e.g., Unitree Go1 locomotion) indicates the potential for WestWorld to facilitate the deployment of advanced robotics in various sectors, from industrial automation and logistics to exploration and service robotics.

**Limitations and Future Work (as identified by the authors):**
The current iteration of WestWorld primarily focuses on trajectory modeling and does not explicitly incorporate visual observations. A stated future direction is to extend WestWorld into a multimodal world model that fuses both vision and trajectory signals, which would further enhance its ability to understand and interact with complex environments.
