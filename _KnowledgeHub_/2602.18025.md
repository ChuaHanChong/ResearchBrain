# Cross-Embodiment Offline Reinforcement Learning for Heterogeneous Robot Datasets

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from The University of Tokyo and RIKEN developed a framework for cross-embodiment offline reinforcement learning that effectively utilizes heterogeneous robot datasets, including suboptimal data. Their Embodiment Grouping strategy, which clusters robots by morphological similarity for policy updates, mitigated inter-robot gradient conflicts, leading to a 33.99% average performance improvement on suboptimal datasets compared to standard cross-embodiment offline RL.

## Problem

- The high cost and difficulty of collecting large volumes of high-quality expert demonstration data for each individual robot platform or task.
- Robot learning systems, including emerging robot foundation models, are limited by data scarcity and reliance on expert demonstrations.
- The systematic integration of offline reinforcement learning (offline RL) with cross-embodiment learning to exploit heterogeneous robot data, including suboptimal trajectories, was largely unexplored.

## Method

- Conducted a systematic empirical study combining offline reinforcement learning (==IQL==) with cross-embodiment learning using an extended Universal Robot Morphology-Agnostic (URMA) architecture.
- Developed a new locomotion dataset in MuJoCo featuring 16 distinct robot platforms and varying data qualities (expert, expert replay, 70% suboptimal replay).
- Proposed Embodiment Grouping (EG), a novel strategy that clusters robots based on Fused Gromov-Wasserstein (FGW) distance and updates actor policies group-wise to resolve inter-robot gradient conflicts.

## Results

- Implicit Q-learning (IQL) consistently outperformed Behavior Cloning (BC) on datasets containing suboptimal trajectories, confirming its ability to leverage lower-quality data in a cross-embodiment context.
- Cross-embodiment pre-training with offline RL significantly accelerated the fine-tuning convergence for individual robots compared to training from scratch.
- The proposed Embodiment Grouping (EG) strategy achieved the best average performance, showing a **33.99%** average improvement for IQL+EG over standard cross-embodiment IQL on **70%** Suboptimal datasets.
- Analysis confirmed a strong positive correlation (r=**0.815** for IQL) between a robot's transfer gain and its average gradient cosine similarity, empirically identifying gradient conflicts as the cause of negative transfer.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Offline reinforcement learning (IQL) consistently outperforms Behavior Cloning (BC) when leveraging suboptimal robot interaction data in cross-embodiment settings.
> - Cross-embodiment pre-training significantly accelerates the fine-tuning process for individual robots, indicating positive transfer of general control capabilities.
> - Negative transfer observed in cross-embodiment learning, especially with high proportions of suboptimal data and diverse robots, stems from conflicting policy gradients among different robot morphologies.
