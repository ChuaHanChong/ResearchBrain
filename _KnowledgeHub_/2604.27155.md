# Generalizing the Geometry of Model Merging Through Frechet Averages

> [!summary] Summary
> GeoMerge presents a geometric framework that reformulates neural network model merging as computing a Fréchet mean on Riemannian manifolds, inherently accounting for architectural symmetries. This yields more robust and symmetry-invariant merging of Low-Rank Adapters (LoRA) for LLMs, empirically outperforming the KnOTS method on NLI tasks with an average normalized accuracy of 91.877% while preserving adapter rank.

## Problem

- Existing data-free (weight-only) model merging methods often fail for parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) techniques like LoRA due to "misalignment of task-updates," leading to destructive interference.
- Neural networks exhibit architectural symmetries (e.g., neuron permutations, scaling, gauge symmetries) where multiple distinct parameter settings represent the same function, making naive parameter-space averaging fragile and inconsistent.
- Prior alignment methods for LoRA, such as KnOTS, address subspace misalignment but may not be robust to coordinate system choices and underlying symmetries, as the notion of "alignment" itself can be ambiguous in non-canonical parameter spaces.

## Method

- Introduces **GeoMerge**, a geometric framework that reformulates model merging as computing a ==Fréchet mean on a Riemannian manifold==, treating the parameter space with an intrinsic geometry.
- Leverages ==quotient manifolds== to explicitly handle ==architectural symmetries==, grouping equivalent parameterizations into single equivalence classes to ensure symmetry-invariant distances and averages.
- Employs an ==iterative Riemannian gradient descent== algorithm that alternates between ==orbit minimization== (aligning models via symmetry transformations) and ==intrinsic averaging== via exponential and logarithm maps in the horizontal space.

## Results

- GeoMerge consistently outperformed the state-of-the-art LoRA merging method, **KnOTS**, on six NLI datasets using a **Llama 3 8B** base model, achieving an average normalized accuracy of **91.877%** compared to KnOTS's **90.38%**.
- The method demonstrates higher efficiency by preserving the original adapter rank (**r**), whereas KnOTS can increase the rank up to **T·r** (where T is the number of tasks), suggesting more efficient utilization of weight capacity.
- A toy linear network model analytically showed how GeoMerge correctly averages intrinsic function properties despite parameter symmetries, where naive Euclidean or Fisher averaging failed to produce a meaningful merge.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Model merging can be fundamentally generalized as computing a Fréchet mean on a Riemannian manifold, providing a principled, coordinate-free framework that respects the intrinsic geometry of the parameter space.
> - Achieving robust and consistent model merges necessitates explicitly addressing architectural symmetries by performing averaging on quotient manifolds, ensuring the outcome is invariant to specific parameterizations.
> - Fisher-weighted merging can be understood as an approximation of a Fréchet mean under an information-geometric metric, but it remains symmetry-unaware in its averaging mechanism.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in this paper addresses the problem of model merging, which involves combining multiple trained neural networks into a single model without requiring additional end-to-end training. This objective is particularly relevant in contemporary machine learning workflows that generate families of related models, such as those arising from different random seeds, hyperparameter settings, domain specialization, or personalized variants. The goal is to consolidate the capabilities of these individual models into a single, efficient deployment.

Existing model merging approaches generally fall into two categories: data-dependent methods (e.g., Fisher-weighted merging, RegMean, MaTS), which require access to data or gradients during the merge process, and data-free (weight-only) methods (e.g., model soups, Task Arithmetic, TIES), which operate solely on model checkpoints. This paper focuses on data-free methods.

While data-free methods can be effective for models fine-tuned from the same pretrained initialization using full-rank updates, their performance often degrades significantly when applied to models generated via parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) techniques, such as Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA). Previous research, like that by Stoica et al., has attributed this performance gap in LoRA merging to a "misalignment" of task updates, observing lower centered kernel alignment (CKA) between LoRA updates compared to full-rank updates. Current solutions, such as KnOTS and Core Space, attempt to address this misalignment by explicit alignment stages, often involving SVD-based transformations to express task updates in a shared basis before applying merge operators.

This paper identifies a more fundamental issue underlying these challenges: the presence of architectural symmetries in neural networks. These symmetries imply that multiple distinct parameter settings can produce exactly the same input-output function, making naive Euclidean parameter-space operations unreliable. Classic examples include neuron permutations and rescaling symmetries, while transformers exhibit richer continuous symmetries in their attention blocks. For low-rank updates like LoRA, a GL(r) symmetry exists where (G, H) is equivalent to (GA⁻¹, HAᵀ) for any invertible matrix A, preserving the product GHᵀ. These symmetries can lead to "ambiguity" (different but equivalent parameterizations yielding different merges) and "stepping off the orbit" (averaging into a parameter space that does not represent the intended function).

Building on prior work that applies Riemannian geometry to understand neural network parameter spaces and correct for symmetries in measures like sharpness, this paper proposes to extend this geometric methodology to model merging. It reframes merging as an averaging problem on a Riemannian manifold where the geometry is specifically chosen to respect and account for parameter symmetries, thereby offering a principled solution to the alignment problem.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this work is to develop a general geometric framework for model merging, termed GeoMerge, that explicitly addresses and accounts for the architectural and parameterization symmetries inherent in neural networks. The aim is to achieve symmetry-invariant and robust model merges that are not susceptible to the misinterpretations or failures encountered with naive Euclidean averaging in parameter space.

The motivation for this objective is multi-faceted:

- **Prevalence of Model Families**: Modern machine learning workflows routinely generate numerous related models that could benefit from aggregation. These include models trained with different random seeds or hyperparameters, specialized models for specific domains or tasks, or models updated for safety patches or personalized preferences. Merging these models into a single, consolidated unit is desirable to harness their collective capabilities without incurring the computational costs of training an entirely new model or the inference overhead of deploying multiple specialists.
- **Limitations of Current Merging Approaches**: Existing data-free model merging methods, while effective for full-rank fine-tuned models, often perform poorly when applied to models fine-tuned with parameter-efficient techniques like LoRA. This performance degradation is attributed to the "misalignment" of task updates, where different LoRA adaptations process inputs through non-corresponding subspaces, leading to destructive interference during naive parameter addition or averaging.
- **Fundamental Geometric Misinterpretations due to Symmetries**: A deeper underlying issue is that neural network parameters are not canonical coordinates for the functions they represent. Architectural symmetries mean that many distinct parameter configurations can encode the exact same input-output mapping. Examples include neuron permutations, weight rescaling in homogeneous networks, and GL(r) gauge symmetries in low-rank factorizations (e.g., LoRA's GHᵀ = (GA⁻¹)(HAᵀ)ᵀ). When these symmetries are present, Euclidean distance metrics or averaging procedures become ill-posed or ambiguous because they treat inherently equivalent parameter settings as distinct. Averaging in such a space can lead to a merged model that is functionally unrelated to the input models or highly dependent on arbitrary choices of parameterization (gauge).
- **Need for Intrinsic Averaging**: To overcome these issues, there is a need for a merging procedure that operates on the "intrinsic" space of functions or updates, rather than an arbitrary parameterization. This necessitates defining distances and averages that are invariant to the symmetries of the model architecture. The paper proposes that this can be achieved by treating the parameter space as a quotient manifold, where equivalent parameterizations form "orbits" and the averaging procedure is performed intrinsically on this quotient space. This approach would ensure that the merge depends only on the functional content of the models, not on their specific parameter representations.

By addressing these motivations, the paper seeks to provide a principled mathematical foundation for model merging that explicitly incorporates geometric considerations, thereby enabling more robust and effective combination of diverse neural network models.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core methodology of this work is to formulate model merging as the computation of a Fréchet mean on a Riemannian manifold, specifically a quotient manifold designed to respect architectural symmetries.

**Foundational Geometric Concepts:**
The paper begins by establishing necessary concepts from Riemannian geometry:
- **Riemannian Manifold (M, g)**: A smooth manifold M equipped with a metric tensor `g` that defines an inner product on tangent spaces `T_p M`.
- **Geodesics and Geodesic Distance (d_M)**: Geodesics are curves that locally minimize length, and `d_M(p, q)` is the shortest path length between points `p` and `q`.
- **Exponential Map (Exp_p)** and **Logarithm Map (Log_p)**: `Exp_p(ξ)` maps a tangent vector `ξ` at `p` to a point along the geodesic starting at `p` with initial velocity `ξ`. `Log_p(q)` is its inverse, mapping a point `q` back to a tangent vector at `p` that, when exponentiated, reaches `q`. These are crucial for iterative optimization on manifolds.
- **Quotients Induced by Symmetries**: When a Lie group G acts on a total manifold M (parameter space) by isometries, it induces an equivalence relation, defining a quotient space `M = M/G`. Points in `M` are orbits `[p]`, representing equivalent parameterizations of the same underlying model.
- **Vertical and Horizontal Spaces**: At any point `p` in the total space, the vertical space `V_p M` comprises tangent vectors corresponding to infinitesimal symmetry directions (along the orbit). The horizontal space `H_p M` is its orthogonal complement with respect to the Riemannian metric `g`. Geodesics on the quotient manifold `M` can be represented by horizontal lifts in the total space `M`.
- **Quotient Distance as Orbit Alignment**: The geodesic distance on the quotient manifold `M` can be expressed as an orbit-minimum: `d_M([p], [q]) = min_{G∈G} d_M(p, q·G)`. A transformation `G*(p; q)` that minimizes this distance is termed an "alignment" (or gauge choice). When `p` and `q` are aligned, `Log_p(q)` is horizontal.

**GeoMerge Framework:**
The paper proposes GeoMerge, a geometric framework where model merging is performed by computing a Fréchet mean.
- **Fréchet Mean**: Given points `{x_i}` and weights `{w_i}` on a metric space `(M, d)`, the Fréchet mean `μ*` minimizes `F(μ) = (1/2) Σ_i w_i d(μ, x_i)²`. This definition is coordinate-free.
- **Benefits**: This formulation cleanly separates the objective (geometry-respecting average) from its computation, makes invariances explicit (if symmetry acts by isometries), and unifies existing merges as special cases through different choices of `M` and `d`.
- **Symmetry Invariance**: By defining the Fréchet objective on a quotient manifold `M`, the merge is inherently invariant to reparameterizations within each orbit, ensuring the result depends only on the intrinsic content of the models.
- **Menu of Aligned Distances**: The framework allows for flexible choice of geometry:
 - **Distribution-space geometries**: For models viewed as distributions, the Fisher metric induces the Fisher-Rao geodesic distance.
 - **Adapter-space geometries**: For low-rank adapters (LoRA), the intrinsic object is a quotient. The paper uses a product metric for the polar factorization `∆W = UBVᵀ` (where `U`, `V` are Stiefel manifolds and `B` is a Symmetric Positive Definite (SPD) manifold). Specifically, it employs the canonical Stiefel metric for `U` and `V`, and the affine-invariant metric for `B`. This choice ensures compatibility with the `O(r)` gauge symmetry of the polar factorization.

**Algorithmic Recipe: Quotient GeoMerge:**
Computing the Fréchet mean is achieved via Riemannian gradient descent. The Riemannian gradient takes a simple form in a geodesically convex neighborhood, leading to an iterative update rule: `μ_{t+1} = Exp_{μt} (α_t Σ_i w_i Log_{μt}(θ_i))`.
For quotient manifolds, this process involves alternating steps:
1. **Alignment (Orbit Minimization)**: For each input model `θ_i`, align its representative `θ_i` to the current iterate `μ_t` by finding a symmetry transformation `G*_i` that minimizes `d_M(μ_t, θ_i · G)`. This yields an aligned representative `θ*_i = θ_i · G*_i`.
2. **Intrinsic Averaging (Horizontal Descent)**: Compute the logarithm maps `η_i = Log_{μt}(θ*_i)`. These are guaranteed to be horizontal (orthogonal to orbit directions) at `μ_t`. Then, update `μ_{t+1}` by exponentiating the weighted average of these horizontal logarithms: `μ_{t+1} = Exp_{μt} (α_t Σ_i w_i η_i)`.

**Specifics for LoRA (Polar Factorization and Metrics):**
The paper details the geometric primitives for LoRA parameters `[U, B, V]`:
- **SPD Manifold (B)**: Uses the affine-invariant metric `g_B(ζ_B, η_B) = Tr(B⁻¹ζ_B B⁻¹η_B)`, with closed-form `Exp` and `Log` maps.
- **Stiefel Manifold (U, V)**: Uses the canonical Stiefel metric, with an `Exp` map derived by Edelman et al. and numerical routines for `Log` from Mataigne et al..
- **Horizontal Projection**: The projection onto the horizontal space involves solving for a skew-symmetric matrix `Ω` that dictates the gauge drift, ensuring that the tangent vector is perpendicular to the symmetry directions.

**High-Rank GeoMerge (Lift):**
To merge rank-`r` adapters into a higher rank `R`, the method incorporates a "lift" operation.
- The lift `L_t: {θ_s} -> L_t` converts `θ_t ∈ M_r` to `L_t ∈ M_R`. It must be defined on quotient points and utilize cross-task structure.
- A simple lift is proposed: `U` and `V` factors are extended with orthonormal complements (`U⊥_t`, `V⊥_t`) derived from the leading paired singular directions of task-conditioned residuals `R_t = Σ_{s≠t} P_{U_t} ∆_s P_{V_t}` (where `P` are projection matrices). The `B` factor is extended with `cI_{R-r}` for a scalar `c`.
- An efficient computation of these residual subspaces without forming dense matrices is provided.

**Implementation Details (Appendix C):**
For practical computation, the paper describes using a Procrustes-based initialization for the `O(k)` alignment, followed by iterative refinement of the gauge transformation using horizontal projection. For efficiency, Cayley pseudo-lifts and retractions are employed for the Stiefel factors in the `Log` and `Exp` operations, while the SPD factor uses exact affine-invariant `Log/Exp`.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents conceptual advancements alongside empirical validation of the proposed GeoMerge framework.

**Conceptual and Theoretical Contributions:**

- **Geometric Formulation of Model Merging**: The core finding is the successful formulation of model merging as the computation of a Fréchet mean on a Riemannian manifold. This provides a principled, coordinate-free approach that explicitly accounts for architectural symmetries by operating on quotient manifolds. This framework intrinsically produces symmetry-invariant merges.
- **Connection to Fisher Merging**: The paper demonstrates that Fisher-weighted merging, a prominent data-dependent method, can be understood as a tractable approximation of a Fréchet mean under an information-geometric distance (specifically, the Fisher-Rao distance). This is achieved by using a quadratic surrogate (Mahalanobis distance) derived from a Gaussian approximation of the model posteriors and simplifying the covariance terms. This connection unifies existing work within the GeoMerge framework.
- **LoRA-Specific Geometric Primitives**: For Low-Rank Adapters (LoRA), the paper outlines the specific quotient manifold structure and appropriate metrics (canonical Stiefel for `U` and `V` factors, affine-invariant for the SPD `B` factor in polar factorization). It details the derivation of horizontal projections and alignment procedures necessary for LoRA models, which exhibit `O(r)` gauge symmetries.
- **High-Rank Lifting Mechanism**: A practical, quotient-compatible lifting scheme is proposed to embed rank-`r` LoRA adapters into a higher rank `R` for merging. This lift is designed to operate on quotient points and leverage cross-task structure to determine the additional dimensions, thereby expanding the representational capacity of the merged model.
- **Analytical Toy Model (Appendix A)**: Through a minimal two-layer linear network with scaling symmetry, the paper analytically demonstrates the failure of naive Euclidean and Fisher averaging (collapsing to a zero predictor) while showing that GeoMerge correctly averages the intrinsic, symmetry-invariant predictors.

**Empirical Results:**

- **Vision Tasks (ViT-B/32)**: GeoMerge, combined with the high-rank lifting procedure, was evaluated on eight LoRA-adapted specialists derived from a common ViT-B/32 model, each fine-tuned on a different vision dataset.
 - GeoMerge achieved an average normalized accuracy of 77.10% across tasks, outperforming established baselines such as KnOTS (67.73%), Core (68.63%), and various combinations with TIES, TSV, and Iso-C.
 - This result suggests that GeoMerge's explicit handling of symmetries and non-Euclidean geometry leads to more effective combination of model capabilities.
 - An ablation study showed that applying the best-performing Euclidean averaging method (TSV+Iso-C) on GeoMerge's lift underperformed the Core lift (75.97% vs 76.43%), indicating that the performance gains are attributable to the geodesic averaging procedure itself, not solely to the lifting mechanism.
- **Natural Language Inference (NLI) Tasks (Llama 3 8B)**: GeoMerge was also tested on six LoRA-adapted NLI specialists derived from a Llama 3 8B base model.
 - GeoMerge, without the full high-rank lift (due to computational constraints), achieved an average normalized accuracy of 91.88%, outperforming the standard Task Arithmetic (TA) baseline (90.38%). This further supports the effectiveness of the geometric averaging principle across different modalities and model types, even in a more constrained setup.

The combined findings indicate that by addressing the fundamental geometric properties and symmetries of neural network parameter spaces, GeoMerge provides a robust and empirically superior approach to model merging, particularly for parameter-efficient fine-tuning scenarios.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The significance and potential impact of the GeoMerge framework are substantial, addressing a foundational challenge in model combination and offering new avenues for research and application:

- **Principled Framework for Model Merging**: GeoMerge provides a mathematically rigorous and principled framework for model merging, moving beyond heuristic parameter-space averaging. By explicitly acknowledging and accounting for architectural symmetries through Riemannian quotient geometry, it offers a robust method that ensures merged models are functionally consistent and independent of arbitrary parameterizations. This shifts the paradigm from manipulating weights directly to operating on the intrinsic functional representations of models.
- **Resolution of Symmetry-Induced Merging Failures**: The work directly tackles the problem of "misalignment" and "destructive interference" observed in merging PEFT models like LoRA, which are often attributed to architectural symmetries. By implementing symmetry-invariant averaging, GeoMerge offers a solution to these fundamental issues, leading to more reliable and effective model combination.
- **Improved Performance in Practical Settings**: The empirical results demonstrate that GeoMerge achieves state-of-the-art performance on benchmarks for merging LoRA adapters in both vision (ViT-B/32) and language (Llama 3 8B) tasks. This practical improvement means that practitioners can combine specialized models more effectively, potentially reducing the need for extensive retraining and enhancing the utility of PEFT techniques.
- **Unification and Generalization of Merging Methods**: The framework unifies various existing merging methods by interpreting them as specific choices of manifolds and metrics. For instance, the derivation of Fisher-weighted merging as an approximation of a Fréchet mean under an information-geometric distance highlights the generality of GeoMerge. This unifying perspective can guide the development of new merging techniques tailored to different model architectures or data characteristics.
- **Deeper Understanding of Neural Network Geometry**: This research contributes to a deeper understanding of the geometric structure of neural network parameter spaces. By demonstrating how symmetries influence effective distances and averages, it underscores the importance of intrinsic geometry in analyzing and manipulating neural network models.
- **Foundation for Future Research**: The paper opens several avenues for future work, including the exploration of optimal high-rank lifting procedures, the integration of magnitude- or sign-dependent merging rules (like TIES/DARE-TIES) into a geometric framework, and further improvements in computational efficiency. The flexibility to choose different metrics and manifolds suggests potential applications to other types of model merging or parameter manipulation problems.
- **Enhanced AI Workflow Efficiency**: By enabling more effective model merging without additional end-to-end training, GeoMerge can contribute to more efficient AI development cycles. This allows for faster iteration, easier specialization, and more flexible deployment of AI models, especially in scenarios involving diverse tasks or personalized applications.

Overall, GeoMerge represents a significant conceptual and practical advancement in model merging, providing a geometrically sound foundation that is likely to influence future research and development in combining and managing large ensembles of AI models.
