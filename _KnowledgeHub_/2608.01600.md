# Perception-and-action system for humanoid robot task execution in construction

> [!summary] Summary
> A Vision-based Perception-and-Action (VPA) system allows humanoid robots to learn construction tasks from human demonstrations by translating human motion into humanoid-compatible poses and executing them with stable whole-body control. This system achieved an average MPJPE of 82.45 mm for various construction-related actions on a Unitree G1 robot.

## Problem

- Humanoid robots lack inherent specialized knowledge for complex, dynamic, and contact-rich construction tasks.
- Translating human demonstrations to humanoid robots is challenging due to morphological mismatches between human and robot kinematics.
- Existing methods struggle to convert kinematic pose references into dynamically stable, physically executable whole-body actions for humanoid robots in real-world environments.

## Method

- A **Vision-based Perception-and-Action (VPA)** system, comprising ==Humanoid-PoseNet== and ==Humanoid-ActionNet==, is introduced to enable learning and execution of construction tasks from human demonstrations.
- Humanoid-PoseNet employs a vision-based deep network to translate observed human motions into humanoid-compatible 3D pose trajectories, addressing kinematic mismatches.
- Humanoid-ActionNet utilizes a ==teacher-student reinforcement learning== framework to generate stable whole-body control policies that execute the retargeted poses while maintaining dynamic stability and contact.

## Results

- The Humanoid-PoseNet achieved an average MPJPE of **48.46 mm** for human-to-humanoid pose retargeting and showed adaptability to different humanoid platforms.
- The VPA system successfully executed **eight** construction-related actions on a physical Unitree G1 robot, demonstrating stable balance and consistent performance with sim-to-sim evaluations (average MPJPE **82.45 mm**).
- Humanoid-ActionNet outperformed other whole-body control methods, achieving an average MPJPE of **82.45 mm**, suggesting improved motion tracking accuracy for construction-specific tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A combined triplet, reconstruction, and latent-consistency loss function effectively addresses morphological mismatches for accurate human-to-humanoid pose retargeting.
> - Physics-aware reward function design within a teacher-student reinforcement learning framework enables robust whole-body control for dynamically stable execution of construction-specific actions.
> - Distilling a comprehensive teacher policy into a student policy using only proprioceptive states facilitates reliable sim-to-real transfer for humanoid robots performing complex tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The construction industry is characterized by complex, dynamic, labor-intensive, and hazardous work environments, leading to significant safety risks and a pressing need for automation. Existing efforts in construction robotics have seen the deployment of specialized robots, such as the Semi-Automated Mason (SAM) for bricklaying, the MuLE for heavy material lifting, and the Spot robot for site inspection and monitoring. These robots have demonstrated capabilities in improving efficiency, reducing arduous workloads, and enhancing safety for specific tasks. Concurrently, research has focused on mechanisms to facilitate safe human-robot interaction with these deployed systems.

Beyond these specialized robots, humanoid robots represent another class of robotic solutions with potential for construction applications. Their human-like form factor offers advantages in human-dominated environments where tools, scaffolding, and workspaces are designed to human dimensions, potentially enabling more natural collaboration. Furthermore, their high degrees of freedom (DoF) and dexterity suggest a capability to perform a broader range of tasks compared to single-task robots, such as material delivery, assembly assistance, inspections, and finishing tasks, thereby reducing the need for multiple specialized robots.

Despite these potential advantages, the deployment of humanoid robots in construction faces challenges. These include the requirement for specialized domain knowledge for construction tasks, which robots do not inherently possess, and the difficulty in maintaining stability and balance during contact-rich movements and bipedal locomotion on uneven and varying construction site conditions. Limited research has explored practical methods to enable humanoids to acquire the necessary capabilities for construction tasks, particularly learning from human demonstrations.

This study addresses these gaps by proposing a system that enables humanoid robots to learn construction-related actions from worker demonstrations. It builds upon existing research in vision-based human pose estimation and whole-body control (WBC) of humanoid robots. While advancements in 3D human pose estimation can capture human motion, a critical gap exists in directly translating these human-centric poses to humanoid robots due to morphological mismatches (differences in limb proportions, joint structures, and range of motion). Additionally, pose estimation typically captures only kinematics and lacks information about physical contact, forces, and dynamic stability, which are crucial for real-world task execution. Whole-body control methods are necessary to convert pose references into physically executable actions while maintaining balance and stability, but existing reinforcement learning (RL)-based WBC approaches are primarily developed for general human-like behaviors rather than construction-specific actions and contact conditions. This work aims to bridge these specific technical gaps to enable humanoid robots to perceive, learn, and execute construction tasks effectively and safely.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation for this study stems from the significant challenges in deploying humanoid robots on construction sites, despite their alignment with human-dominated workplaces and multi-tasking potential. The authors identify two primary issues contributing to the absence of humanoids in the field:
1. **Specialized Domain Knowledge:** Construction tasks require specific behaviors, actions, and tool usage that are not intrinsically available to robots.
2. **Maintaining Stability and Balance:** The dynamic, uneven, and contact-rich nature of construction environments poses a significant challenge for humanoid robots, especially for their bipedal locomotion systems.

These challenges lead to the central research question: "How can humanoid robots learn and execute construction-related tasks efficiently and safely?"

Inspired by the structural similarities between human workers and humanoid robots, the study proposes that robots can learn construction-related actions directly from human demonstrations. This approach leverages workers' motions and actions as observable expressions of task execution, providing critical information for robot learning. However, two key technical gaps hinder this direct learning process:
1. **Morphological Mismatch in Pose Translation:** Existing human pose-estimation methods produce human-centric outputs that cannot be directly translated into mechanically feasible poses for humanoid robots due to differences in kinematics, limb proportions, joint structures, and range of motion.
2. **Conversion of Poses to Physically Executable Actions:** Even after pose translation, a method is needed for the humanoid robot to convert these pose references into physically executable whole-body actions that maintain balance, contact consistency, and motion stability in practical construction environments.

To address these motivations and gaps, the study proposes a Vision-based Perception-and-Action (VPA) system with two key objectives:
1. **Develop Humanoid-PoseNet:** Design a vision-based perception module that enables humanoid robots to perceive worker demonstrations and accurately retarget observed human motions into humanoid-compatible 3D pose trajectories, accounting for the kinematic mismatch.
2. **Develop Humanoid-ActionNet:** Create a teacher-student reinforcement learning (RL) framework that learns a whole-body control policy capable of executing the retargeted humanoid poses while maintaining the robot's dynamic stability, balance, and contact consistency, specifically for construction-related actions.

The overarching goal is to enable humanoid robots to learn and perform construction tasks from worker demonstrations, providing an early step toward deploying humanoid collaborators in construction.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed Vision-based Perception-and-Action (VPA) system integrates two deep networks: Humanoid-PoseNet and Humanoid-ActionNet.

#### 3.1. Humanoid-PoseNet: Vision-based Deep Network for Estimating 3D Humanoid Posture from Human Demonstrations

Humanoid-PoseNet is designed to perceive worker demonstrations and translate human motions into humanoid-compatible pose trajectories. It comprises two main components:

1. **3D Human Pose Estimation Module:** This module reconstructs workers' 3D posture from 2D RGB images or videos. The study leverages existing off-the-shelf 3D pose estimation models, such as PoseNet, VideoPose3D, AlphaPose, and 3D-PoseNet, which are fine-tuned on a custom dataset. PoseNet was selected for its performance, achieving the lowest Mean Per Joint Position Error (MPJPE). The input is a sequence of 2D RGB frames, and the output is 3D human keypoints, denoted as $P_{3D_{h,i}}$.

2. **Human-to-Humanoid Motion Retargeting Network:** This network maps the extracted 3D human poses into the humanoid's 3D configuration space. Its architecture consists of two encoders ($E_h$ for human poses and $E_r$ for robot poses) and one decoder ($D_r$ for robot poses), sharing a common latent space. Both encoders and the decoder are implemented as Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) networks with eight fully connected (FC) hidden layers, each with 128 units and ReLU activations. The decoder's output layer generates a 14-dimensional pose vector in the humanoid’s configuration space.

 The training of this network utilizes a triplet objective loss ($L_1$), a reconstruction loss ($L_2$), and a latent-consistency loss ($L_3$).
 - **Triplet Loss ($L_1$):** Inspired by contrastive learning, this loss uses a pose-aware similarity score based on Bone-Direction Angular Error (BAE) to compute similarity between human and robot poses. It pulls latent embeddings of similar cross-domain poses closer and pushes dissimilar ones apart, reducing cross-domain mismatch and improving generalization.
 - **Reconstruction Loss ($L_2$):** This loss ensures that the decoder $D_r$ can accurately reconstruct humanoid configurations from robot-latent inputs by minimizing the discrepancy between a reconstructed humanoid pose (decoded from a robot embedding) and the ground-truth humanoid pose.
 - **Latent-Consistency Loss ($L_3$):** This loss encourages a humanoid pose decoded from a human embedding to be re-encoded into a latent representation consistent with the original human embedding. This promotes cross-domain distribution alignment and prevents decoded poses from drifting into infeasible configurations.
 The overall objective function is a weighted sum of these three losses, minimized using gradient descent. After training, a given human pose $P_{3D_{h,t}}$ is translated into a humanoid target pose $\hat{P}_{3D_{r,t}}$.

#### 3.2. Humanoid-ActionNet: Teacher-Student Reinforcement Learning Framework for Whole-Body Humanoid Task Execution

Humanoid-ActionNet takes the retargeted humanoid pose trajectories ($\hat{P}_{3D_{r,t}}$) from Humanoid-PoseNet and enables the robot to physically execute these behaviors while maintaining dynamic stability and contact interactions. It employs a teacher-student reinforcement learning (RL) architecture:

1. **Teacher Layer ($\pi_{teacher}$):** A teacher policy is trained in simulation using a Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO)-based actor-critic framework. The policy optimizes for actions ($a_t$) (target joint angles) that maximize a physics-aware reward function. The state ($s_t$) for the teacher policy is comprehensive, including:
 - **Privileged Observations ($s_{po_t}$):** DoF/joint differences, keypoint differences, and root velocity (available only in simulation).
 - **Proprioceptive States ($s_{ps_t}$):** DoF positions, velocities, previous action, root angular velocity, and root orientation.
 - **Motion Tracking Targets ($s_{mt_t}$):** Target DoF positions, keypoint positions, root linear/angular velocity, roll, pitch, yaw, and height (derived from Humanoid-PoseNet outputs).
 The reward function (Table 1) is designed with multiple components to encourage accurate motion tracking, dynamic feasibility (balance, smoothness, contact consistency), and task-oriented action consistency for construction behaviors. Reward terms include DoF position tracking, keypoint position tracking, root linear velocity, root velocity direction, body rotation tracking, upper-body coherence, feet air-time shaping, and feet slip penalty. The actor and critic networks are MLPs with specific architectural details to process the diverse input features.

2. **Student Layer ($\pi_{student}$):** After the teacher policy is optimized, it is distilled into a deployable student policy. The student policy operates using only information available on the physical robot, meaning it replaces privileged observations ($s_{po_t}$) with a window of past proprioceptive states ($s_{ps_{t-w+1}}, \dots, s_{ps_t}$; window size w=10). The distillation process uses a loss function that minimizes the squared difference between the actions generated by the student policy and the optimized teacher policy, combined with the DAgger (Dataset Aggregation) technique to improve robustness.

3. **Real-World Deployment:** The output of the deployed student policy (target joint angles) is tracked by a Proportional-Derivative (PD) controller on the physical Unitree G1 humanoid robot to ensure robust and smooth execution. The PD controller generates commanded joint torques based on the difference between target and current joint positions and velocities. The Unitree G1 has 23 actuated DoFs, and the controller is adapted accordingly.

#### 3.3. Case Study and Evaluation

- **Training Data Collection:** Five healthy adult subjects performed 30 construction-related actions (e.g., carrying pipe, lifting boxes) in an OptiTrack motion-capture environment. This generated a synchronized motion-capture (3D human pose at 120 Hz) and RGB video (30 fps) dataset. A 21-joint human skeleton representation and a 14-joint humanoid pose representation (Unitree G1) were used. The dataset included 9000 paired RGB-3D samples for fine-tuning 3D pose estimation and 9000 corresponding manually retargeted Unitree G1 humanoid poses as ground truth for training the human-to-humanoid retargeting module.
- **Evaluation Stages:**
 1. **Retargeting Accuracy:** Assessed on a held-out test set using MPJPE relative to ground-truth retargeted humanoid poses.
 2. **Sim-to-Sim Transfer:** The VPA system was evaluated using eight selected construction actions in two physics engines: IsaacGym (training environment) and MuJoCo (independent evaluation). This assessed robustness across different dynamics implementations and served as a safety buffer before hardware deployment.
 3. **Sim-to-Real Transfer:** Actions that passed sim-to-sim evaluation were deployed on a physical Unitree G1 humanoid robot. Performance was quantified by MPJPE, measuring deviation between reference pose trajectories and executed trajectories.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of the proposed VPA system demonstrated its capability in translating human demonstrations into executable humanoid actions for construction tasks.

#### 4.1. Performance of Humanoid-PoseNet in Human-to-Humanoid Pose Translation

- **3D Human Pose Estimation:** The selected PoseNet model, fine-tuned on the custom dataset, achieved an average MPJPE of 58.3 mm for 3D human pose estimation. Comparative analysis against AlphaPose, VideoPose3D, and 3D-PoseNet indicated PoseNet's superior performance (Table 5).
- **Human-to-Humanoid Retargeting:** The retargeting module of Humanoid-PoseNet achieved an average MPJPE of 48.46 mm across all 14 humanoid joints on the test set (Table 3). Visualizations (Fig. 8, 9) indicated that the retargeted humanoid poses closely mimicked human inter-joint coordination while maintaining humanoid-compatible joint configurations.
- **Ablation Study:** The ablation study on the retargeting module's loss functions ($L_1$, $L_2$, $L_3$) showed that each component contributed to retargeting precision. Removing $L_1$ (triplet loss) resulted in the largest MPJPE increase (106.37 mm), followed by removing $L_2$ (reconstruction loss, 85.79 mm), and $L_3$ (latent-consistency loss, 57.22 mm) (Table 7). This indicates the importance of geometry-aware alignment, accurate reconstruction, and cross-domain distribution alignment.
- **Adaptability:** The retargeting module also demonstrated adaptability by successfully translating human poses to the Unitree H1 kinematic configuration, in addition to the G1, suggesting generalizability across different humanoid platforms (Fig. 13).

#### 4.2. Performance of Humanoid-ActionNet in Construction Actions Execution

- **RL Policy Training:** The PPO-based training for the teacher policy showed stable convergence, with the surrogate loss stabilizing around -0.0037 and the value loss decreasing to 0.061 (Fig. 10). The student policy, distilled from the teacher, achieved a distillation loss of 0.0056, indicating a close match in behavior.
- **Sim-to-Sim Evaluation:** The student policy ($\pi_{student}$) successfully executed all eight selected construction-related actions in simulation (IsaacGym and MuJoCo). The average MPJPE across these actions was 82.45 mm (Table 4, Fig. 11), demonstrating consistent performance across different simulation environments.
- **Sim-to-Real Transfer:** The trained VPA system was deployed on a physical Unitree G1 humanoid robot. The G1 successfully reproduced all eight tested actions (e.g., flagger signaling, carrying a pipe, carrying a concrete block) in the physical environment, maintaining stable balance throughout execution (Fig. 12). The real-world performance was consistent with the sim-to-sim evaluation trends.
- **Comparative Analysis of Whole-Body Control:** Humanoid-ActionNet achieved the lowest average MPJPE (82.45 mm) compared to representative whole-body humanoid control methods such as ExBody (102.75 mm), ExBody with AMP (90.19 mm), OmniH2O (96.35 mm), and ExBody2 (87.48 mm) (Table 8). This suggests that the construction task-specific reward design and teacher-student framework contributed to more accurate motion tracking for the types of tasks evaluated.
- **Failure Cases:** Despite overall success, failure cases were observed during sim-to-sim transfer in MuJoCo, including loss of balance, unstable recovery after perturbations, and tracking breakdown (Fig. 14). These were attributed to challenges in fully capturing contact-rich dynamics and dynamics/actuation mismatches across simulation engines.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This study presents a Vision-based Perception-and-Action (VPA) system that represents a foundational step toward enabling humanoid robots to learn and execute construction tasks from human demonstrations. The work offers several significant contributions and potential impacts:

- **Bridging Human-Humanoid Morphological Mismatch:** The Humanoid-PoseNet effectively addresses a critical challenge by accurately translating human-centric motion into humanoid-compatible pose trajectories. This capability is essential for leveraging the vast amount of human demonstration data for robot learning in construction environments where tools and spaces are human-designed.
- **Enabling Physically Feasible Task Execution:** Humanoid-ActionNet, with its physics-aware reinforcement learning framework, allows the humanoid robot to convert kinematic pose references into dynamically stable and executable whole-body actions. This is crucial for real-world deployment, as construction tasks often involve complex contact interactions and balance requirements.
- **Demonstrated Adaptability to Construction Tasks:** The successful execution of eight diverse construction-related actions (e.g., carrying various materials, signaling, pushing/dragging wheelbarrows) by the Unitree G1 robot in both simulation and physical environments demonstrates the system's potential for multi-task adaptability. This differentiates humanoids from many single-task construction robots and suggests their value for worker-like roles that require a range of coordinated movements.
- **Scalability and Generalizability:** The VPA system's performance across different 3D pose estimation backbones and its adaptability to different humanoid configurations (e.g., Unitree H1 and G1) indicate its potential for broader application to various humanoid platforms and expansion to more complex construction scenarios.
- **Addressing Industry Challenges:** By offering a pathway for humanoids to autonomously or collaboratively perform physically demanding and hazardous tasks, this research directly contributes to mitigating safety risks and addressing skilled labor shortages in the construction industry. The humanoid form factor could facilitate more natural integration into existing human workflows and infrastructure.
- **Future Directions:** The identified limitations, such as the scope of the training dataset, the need for tool and material interaction modeling, and the refinement of reward functions for robust contact dynamics, provide clear directions for future research. Enhancing simulation realism with techniques like 3D Gaussian Splatting and extending to long-horizon, multi-step construction tasks are also critical for practical deployment.

In comparison to other robotic platforms in construction (UAVs for aerial inspection, UGVs for ground transport, robotic arms for precise manipulation, and quadruped robots for uneven terrain inspection), humanoid robots, empowered by systems like VPA, offer a complementary role focused on human-like whole-body coordination and multi-task transitions in human-centric workspaces. This research, therefore, offers a promising direction for accelerating construction automation through next-generation human-robot collaboration.
