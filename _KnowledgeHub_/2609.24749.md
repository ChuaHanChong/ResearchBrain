# D-JEPA: A Decision-Aligned Latent World Model

> [!summary] Summary
> D-JEPA is a decision-aligned latent world model that re-ranks candidate action futures from pretrained JEPA-style predictors. A bounded, permutation-equivariant set Transformer combines goal-relative latent descriptors with scale-free ordinal ranks, supervised by executed candidate outcomes, then writes the aligned order back into future latents so native latent-distance planning recovers it. It reaches 87.89% on PushT, +15.04 points on RoboTwin, and +17 points on physical robots.

## Problem

- Latent world models such as LeWM and TD-JEPA predict well globally, yet predicted goal distance can prefer a failing candidate over an available successful one.
- Predicted-realized cost agreement collapses near the decision: within-start Spearman falls from 0.90 to 0.11 (LeWM) as the full 63-candidate set narrows to the top four.
- Prior planning-aware objectives shape global latent metrics or cost fidelity, but do not learn from executed outcomes which relative distinctions matter among competing candidate futures.

## Method

- **Relational alignment**: a permutation-equivariant ==set Transformer== over all candidates ingests goal-relative latent descriptors plus within-set ordinal ranks, emitting a zero-initialized tanh-bounded correction (ε=0.2) trained with success-mass and local boundary-ordering losses.
- **Predictive plasticity** and calibrated composition: only **TD-JEPA**'s final predictor block and projection are fine-tuned under rank and latent preservation; a calibrated gate admits this proposal over the relational default. Scale-free ranks fuse heterogeneous geometries.
- **Representation lifting**: exact ordinal realization places each terminal future latent at a goal-relative radius proportional to its aligned rank, and bounded ==temporal transport== refines trajectories, so native latent-distance planning recovers aligned choices.

## Results

- On PushT (256 starts), D-JEPA reaches **87.89%** vs **83.59%** LeWM, **85.16%** JEPA-WM and **76.95%** TD-JEPA; Reacher rises to **93.75%** from **86.72%**, beating PEGrad, Var-JEPA and CVaR controls.
- Transfer: RoboTwin VLA success **61.72%** to **76.76%** (**+15.04**), physical PiPER with V-JEPA 2-AC **64%** to **81%**, and Drive-JEPA driving PDMS **57.36** to **95.34** across seven hard scenes.
- Ablations: relational alignment alone gives **+10.16 points** over TD-JEPA versus **+2.74** for predictor plasticity; dual LeWM+TD-JEPA descriptors reach **93.75%** vs **~84%** single-source; ordinal realization matches relational selection at **34 ms**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Global prediction accuracy is an insufficient proxy for planning: near-perfect rank correlation can coexist with fixed regret, so evaluate world models on the few candidates competing for execution.
> - Cheap post-hoc set-wise re-ranking supervised by executed outcomes can beat fine-tuning the predictor; bounded, zero-initialized corrections keep changes local to near-tie decisions and preserve pretrained behavior.
> - Within-set ranks are a scale-free lingua franca for fusing heterogeneous world models, but the supervision assumes counterfactual executions from restored states, which is easy in simulation, costly on real robots.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Overview

D-JEPA addresses a weak point in latent-world-model planning. A planner predicts several possible futures, compares them with a goal, and chooses an action, but the predicted future that looks closest is not always the one that succeeds. The authors' approach is to teach the planner how its available futures relate to one another, using what happened when candidate actions were executed. On PushT, a goal-conditioned pushing task, D-JEPA reaches **87.89% success versus 83.59% for LeWM**, a 4.30 percentage-point gain.

### 2. When the closest future is wrong

A latent world model predicts futures in a learned representation. A goal-conditioned planner ranks those futures by a latent distance or related cost and executes the preferred action. The mismatch matters most among the few candidates near the decision point.

In one PushT example, LeWM assigns the failing candidate a smaller predicted goal distance than a successful alternative. In the recorded LeWM pair, predicted goal RMS distance is 0.2027 for the failing candidate and 0.2167 for the successful alternative.

The PushT audit shows the same local problem. The within-start Spearman correlation between predicted and simulator-realized latent costs falls from 0.90 to 0.11 for LeWM, and from 0.80 to 0.13 for TD-JEPA, when attention narrows from all 63 candidates to the four lowest-cost candidates. These correlations compare latent costs, not binary success labels. They diagnose a local ranking problem rather than proving that every preferred action fails.

### 3. Learning relations among candidates

D-JEPA changes how already-available futures are ranked. It does not generate more candidates or show that the underlying world model's predictions become universally more accurate.

For each available action, pretrained models provide a goal-relative future descriptor and a within-set ordinal coordinate, which is an order derived from that model's native cost. Ranks give scale-free preference evidence, and dense descriptors retain directions within each model's own representation. A permutation-equivariant set transformer processes the candidate collection jointly. Each candidate's correction can therefore depend on its alternatives without depending on their presentation order.

A bounded, zero-initialized, rank-eight correction adjusts the ordinal base score. The bound limits reordering to candidates near the base winner. Executed success/failure outcomes supervise successful-candidate mass and local success-over-failure ordering. A regularizer discourages unnecessary global reordering. At inference, the model receives predictive evidence, not execution labels.

One context and goal feed 63 candidate actions. The "Predictive Plasticity" path trains the predictor's final block and prediction projection under preservation and ordering constraints. In the "Relational Alignment" path, candidate futures and future-to-goal costs become dense descriptors and within-set ordinal ranks. These pass through a shared encoder and a two-layer, four-head permutation-equivariant set transformer, producing a bounded low-rank correction added to an ordinal base score.

### 4. What the evidence shows

Success is the percentage of evaluation starts where the selected candidate's executed outcome succeeds. For the headline result, both methods use the same 256-start confirmation cohort and candidate pool, and D-JEPA uses calibrated composition. This cohort was selected for nontrivial decision boundaries, so the result is evidence on that PushT evaluation, not a guarantee for arbitrary starts or candidate sets. The paired-start 95% bootstrap interval for the gain is +0.39 to +8.20 points (19 gains, 8 losses).

A matched module ablation on the same PushT starts and candidate pools compares the components. Relational alignment reaches 87.11% success, versus 79.69% for predictive plasticity alone, and calibrated composition reaches 87.89%. The authors interpret relational alignment as the strongest individual intervention. The rows differ in predictive evidence and decision readout, however. They support the usefulness of relational alignment in this setup but do not isolate it as the only possible cause of the difference.

(a) Success (%) for Plasticity, Relations and Combined (calibrated composition): 79.7, 87.1 and 87.9. (b) Predictive-source ablation, where Dual is highest. (d) Median latency (ms) for Ordinal, Combined and Relations: 35.0, 52.2 and 34.0. Latency is inference cost, not simulator or end-to-end task time. ### 5. Better choices from shared futures

A predicted future can be plausible yet misleading as a choice. D-JEPA uses executed outcomes to learn which candidate distinctions matter within a local decision set, and this improved action selection on PushT while the predictive model was retained. The gain is established on a confirmation cohort selected for nontrivial decision boundaries, with candidate availability held fixed.
