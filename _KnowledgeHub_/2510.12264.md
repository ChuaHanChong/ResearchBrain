# Reducing Belief Deviation in Reinforcement Learning for Active Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from The Chinese University of Hong Kong and ByteDance propose T3, a method to mitigate "belief deviation" in LLM agents during active reasoning, where internal beliefs diverge from the true problem state. This approach truncates uninformative training trajectories, leading to improved task performance and enhanced training stability across multiple benchmarks.

## Problem

- LLM agents in multi-turn active reasoning settings frequently generate uninformative or repetitive actions, often becoming stuck in unproductive interaction loops.
- Reinforcement Learning (RL) policies for LLM agents can yield suboptimal outcomes and exhibit limited robustness due to imperfect belief tracking in partially observable environments.
- "Belief deviation," where an LLM's internal belief state diverges from the true problem state, contaminates credit assignment during RL training.

## Method

- A theoretical framework models active reasoning as a ==POMDP==, distinguishing between an ideal oracle reasoner and an imperfect LLM reasoner, and introduces a Truth-Anchored Potential Function to quantify task progress.
- T3 (Truncating Belief-Trapped Trajectories) is introduced as an RL meta-wrapper that detects entry into "Belief-Trap Regions" (BTRs) using observable proxy signals, effectively truncating uninformative trajectory tails.
- The T3 condition is instantiated using task-specific proxies, such as reductions in candidate set size for finite hypothesis spaces or uninformative judge feedback for unbounded spaces, to detect stalled epistemic progress.

## Results

- T3 consistently improved performance across 14 out of 18 metrics on five active reasoning tasks, with gains up to 30.1 points in Exact Match on GuessNumbers and 41.0 points in accuracy on MovieRecommendation.
- The method led to more stable training trajectories, preventing reward collapses, and improved token efficiency by reducing average token consumption per rollout by up to **34%**.
- T3 enhanced robustness and generalization, maintaining substantial performance improvements over vanilla PPO in various out-of-distribution scenarios for CircuitDecoding and PreferenceEstimation tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - "Belief deviation" is identified as a fundamental limitation for LLM agents, leading to a loss of awareness about actual task progress and fostering unproductive interactions.
> - The theoretical concept of a "Belief-Trap Region" (BTR) is established, where an agent's expected task progress stalls if its internal potential exceeds a certain threshold.
> - Entering a BTR leads to misguided credit assignment in RL, as uninformative trajectory tails can corrupt the Generalized Advantage Estimator (GAE) and hinder effective policy optimization.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Large Language Models (LLMs) have demonstrated reasoning capabilities across various domains, with performance further enhanced by reinforcement learning (RL) using outcome rewards. A current research direction involves extending the application of RL to long-horizon and multi-turn reasoning, particularly in the context of LLM agents. These agents are designed to perform "active reasoning," a process that requires them to strategically interact with external environments, gather information, and solve problems over multiple turns. This approach stands in contrast to single-turn LLM interactions.

However, a recurring challenge for LLM-based agents in multi-turn and active reasoning settings is their tendency to generate uninformative, redundant, or irrelevant actions, sometimes leading to unproductive loops. Even with standard RL training, policies learned by these agents can yield suboptimal outcomes or exhibit limited robustness to novel tasks.

This work positions itself within this landscape by identifying a fundamental issue underlying these observed failures: "belief deviation." While classical models for sequential decision-making under uncertainty, such as Partially Observable Markov Decision Processes (POMDPs), assume perfect belief estimation, LLM agents approximate belief tracking imperfectly due to their inherent reasoning limitations. This imperfection can cause their internal beliefs to diverge from the true problem state, leading to a loss of state awareness. The paper addresses a gap in current RL approaches for LLMs by explicitly modeling and mitigating the consequences of this belief deviation, particularly how it contaminates credit assignment in RL training.

The paper differentiates itself from existing work on active reasoning that focuses on prompting strategies (e.g., Proactive CoT, UoT) or general multi-turn RL techniques (e.g., rule-based intermediate rewards, potential function-based rewards, reward modeling). Instead, it proposes a mechanism specifically targeted at the issue of distorted credit assignment arising from belief deviation in partially observable environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation for this research stems from an observed limitation in LLM agents operating in multi-turn active reasoning environments. The paper identifies that despite advancements in LLM reasoning and the application of reinforcement learning, these agents frequently exhibit suboptimal behaviors such as generating repetitive or uninformative queries, or becoming stuck in unproductive interaction loops. The authors pose a key research question: "Why do LLM agents get trapped in active reasoning, and how can we mitigate it?"

The objectives and motivations are detailed as follows:

- **Understanding the Role of Belief Tracking:** The paper emphasizes that effective active reasoning necessitates robust "belief tracking"—the agent's ability to maintain an accurate internal representation of the problem's underlying state and the associated uncertainties. This is critical for making informed decisions on what information to seek next.
- **Identifying "Belief Deviation" as a Core Problem:** The authors theorize that LLM agents, due to their finite reasoning capacity, approximate belief updates imperfectly. This imperfection leads to "belief deviation," where the agent's internal belief state diverges from the true problem state. This deviation can result in a loss of awareness about the actual progress and current status of the task.
- **Analyzing the Consequences of Belief Deviation:** The paper highlights several negative consequences of belief deviation:
 - **Uninformative or Repetitive Actions:** Agents generate actions that do not contribute to task progress or revisit already explored avenues.
 - **Entry into "Belief-Trap Regions (BTR)":** The theoretical framework introduces the concept of BTRs, where the agent's internal progress stalls, leading to unproductive interactions.
 - **Compounded Errors in RL Trajectories:** When belief deviation occurs, errors accumulate within the interaction trajectories used for RL training.
 - **Misattributed Credits and Limited Exploration:** The errors in trajectories lead to a distorted credit assignment problem, where rewards are incorrectly attributed to actions. This can hinder the agent's ability to learn effective exploratory strategies and can result in suboptimal policies.
- **Developing a Mitigation Strategy:** Based on this analysis, the primary objective is to propose and evaluate a method, T3 (Truncating Belief-Trapped Trajectories), designed to address the issues arising from belief deviation. The method aims to detect excessive deviation and truncate training trajectories before uninformative tail effects contaminate credit assignment. This is intended to preserve accurate credit for informative early actions and improve policy optimization.
- **Empirical Validation:** A further objective is to empirically demonstrate that T3 can consistently improve training stability, task performance, and token efficiency across a range of challenging active reasoning tasks and different RL algorithms, thus substantiating the theoretical claims about the importance of belief control.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology proposed in this paper, T3 (Truncating Belief-Trapped Trajectories), is grounded in a theoretical analysis of belief deviation in LLM agents and its impact on reinforcement learning. The approach proceeds from formal modeling to practical implementation and empirical evaluation.

#### 3.1. Theoretical Formulations

The authors model active reasoning as a Partially Observable Markov Decision Process (POMDP) defined by (S, A, O, T, O, R, γ), where S is the latent state space, A the action space, O the observation space, T the transition dynamics, O the observation model, R the reward function, and γ the discount factor.
- **Oracle vs. LLM Reasoner:** The framework distinguishes between an ideal "oracle reasoner" that performs exact Bayesian belief updates (B*) and an "imperfect LLM reasoner" with its own update rule (Bθ), parameterized by the LLM.
- **Truth-Anchored Potential Function (Ψ):** Task progress is quantified by Ψ(b) = -log b(s*), which measures the negative log-belief mass assigned to the true latent state s*. Lower Ψ values indicate higher confidence in s*.
- **Belief-Update Discrepancy (cθ(b)):** This metric quantifies the expected difference in Ψ-progress between the LLM agent's update rule and the oracle's Bayesian update.
- **Assumption 1 (Update-Error Growth):** This core assumption posits that for beliefs with high potential (Ψ(b) ≥ U0), the belief-update error cθ(b) grows at least linearly with Ψ(b), indicating that errors are amplified as the agent's deviation from the true state increases.
- **Theorem 1 (Belief Trap Region - BTR):** Based on Assumption 1, the paper theoretically establishes that if the LLM agent's potential Ψt exceeds a certain threshold U, the expected task progress will cease to decrease (E[Ψt+1 | bt] ≥ Ψt). This defines the "Belief Trap Region (BTR)" as an absorbing state where further actions do not reduce uncertainty.
- **Theorem 2 (Misguided Credit Assignment):** This theorem analyzes the Generalized Advantage Estimator (GAE) within the context of BTRs. It demonstrates that once an agent enters a BTR, the uninformative "tail" of the trajectory, characterized by non-positive expected task progress, can contaminate the credit assigned to earlier, potentially informative actions. This contamination can lead to negative expected advantages for early actions, misdirecting policy gradients and hindering effective exploration.
- **Corollary 1 (Value of Truncation):** This corollary provides a theoretical basis for T3, stating that truncating trajectories upon entry into a BTR (at time tS) yields less biased gradient estimates by removing the adverse effects of the uninformative tail.

#### 3.2. From Theory to Practice: T3 (Truncating Belief-Trapped Trajectories)

The challenge in implementing the theoretical truncation rule (at tS) is that the exact belief state (b) and the BTR threshold (U) are unobservable in LLM agents. T3 addresses this through an operational criterion based on observable proxy signals.
- **Definition 2 (T3 Condition):** A trajectory is truncated at step t if, for a window of k consecutive steps [t-k, t), a "refinement measure" d(Hτ, Hτ+1) (quantifying contraction of the hypothesis space Ht) falls below a minimum progress threshold Δmin. This principle aims to detect sustained stalls in epistemic progress, which is the defining characteristic of BTRs.
- **Proposition 1:** This proposition provides a statistical guarantee for the T3 rule, showing that under a biased Gaussian-noise model for the proxy signal, the probability of false truncation on a non-BTR segment can be bounded and reduced by appropriate choices of k, Δmin, and the proxy's properties.
- **Task-Specific Instantiations (Section 3.1):** The T3 condition is instantiated using observable proxies tailored to five active reasoning tasks:
 - **GuessNumbers (GN) and CircuitDecoding (CD):** For tasks with finite, enumerable hypothesis spaces (Ht = set of candidate solutions), the refinement metric d is defined as the reduction in the size of the candidate set (|Hτ| - |Hτ+1|). Truncation occurs if this reduction is non-positive for k=1 (GN) or k=3 (CD) steps, indicating a lack of uncertainty reduction.
 - **SituationPuzzles (SP):** For unbounded hypothesis spaces, stalled refinement is approximated by uninformative judge feedback ("unknown" responses) for k=5 consecutive steps. An alternative proxy based on semantic similarity of questions is also explored.
 - **PreferenceEstimation (PE) and MovieRecommendation (MR):** For continuous latent spaces, the agent's explicit estimate of the preference vector (v_t) is used. The refinement signal is approximated by the change in similarity between v_t and the ground-truth v*. Truncation occurs if this similarity decreases for k=2 consecutive steps. Ground-truth-free alternatives based on the magnitude of internal vector changes are also investigated.
- **Integration with RL Algorithms:** T3 is designed as a meta-wrapper, seamlessly integrating into standard policy optimization frameworks such as PPO, GRPO, and GSPO without requiring modifications to their core algorithms. It operates by modifying the length of collected trajectories passed to the RL optimizer.

#### 3.3. Experimental Setup

- **Baselines:** The effectiveness of T3 is evaluated against direct inference LLMs (e.g., o3-mini, Gemini-2.5-Pro, Qwen-2.5-7B-Inst) and standard RL algorithms (PPO, GRPO, GSPO) without T3.
- **LLMs:** The primary LLM used for RL training is Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct. Additional experiments explore different LLM scales (Qwen-2.5 3B, 14B) and architectures (LLaMA-3.1-8B-Instruct, DeepSeek-R1-Distill-LLaMA-8B).
- **Tasks:** Five challenging active reasoning benchmarks are used: GuessNumbers, SituationPuzzles, CircuitDecoding, PreferenceEstimation, and MovieRecommendation, adapted from AR-Bench and Multi-Turn Puzzles.
- **Rewards:** All RL tasks utilize outcome-based rewards, provided only at the terminal step of a trajectory, consistent with the outcome-based RL setting.
- **Evaluation Metrics:** Task-specific metrics include Exact Match (EM), F1-word, F1-char, and Binary Similarity.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of T3 involved comparisons against various baselines across five active reasoning tasks, analyzing performance, training dynamics, efficiency, and robustness.

#### 4.1. Overall Performance

- **Superiority over Zero-Shot:** All RL-trained agents, both with and without T3, consistently surpassed the performance of zero-shot LLMs, indicating the utility of RL for active reasoning.
- **Consistent Improvements with T3:** Incorporating T3 into RL methods (PPO, GRPO, GSPO) led to performance gains across the majority of tasks and metrics. Specifically, T3 contributed to improvements in 14 out of 18 reported metrics.
 - On the CircuitDecoding (CD) task, PPO with T3 increased Exact Match (EM) by 16.2 points.
 - In GuessNumbers (GN), GRPO with T3 achieved a 30.1-point gain, and GSPO with T3 reached 99.74 EM.
 - In MovieRecommendation (MR), GSPO with T3 showed a 41.0-point improvement in accuracy.
- **Performance on Complex Hypothesis Spaces:** While advanced reasoning LLMs performed strongly on tasks with finite hypothesis spaces (GN, CD), their performance diminished on tasks with large, infinite, or continuous hypothesis spaces (SP, PE). In these more complex scenarios, RL-trained Qwen-7B models equipped with T3 often outperformed the frontier LLMs.

#### 4.2. Training Stability and Optimization Behavior

- Vanilla RL methods exhibited higher variance and instability in their reward curves during training, sometimes experiencing reward collapses after partial convergence.
- The integration of T3 resulted in more stable training trajectories, characterized by monotonic or near-monotonic reward improvement and fewer abrupt performance drops. This suggests that T3 facilitates more effective policy optimization.

#### 4.3. Token Efficiency

- T3 contributed to reduced average token consumption per rollout due to early trajectory truncation, leading to higher training efficiency when measured against token cost. For example, on CD, PPO+T3 achieved a reward level of 0.65 using 66.4% of the tokens compared to vanilla PPO.
- While vanilla methods often plateaued or stagnated, T3 enabled continued reward improvement, reaching higher terminal performance levels.

#### 4.4. Out-of-Distribution (OOD) Analysis

- T3 consistently improved agent performance over vanilla PPO across various OOD scenarios in CircuitDecoding and PreferenceEstimation. These scenarios involved variations in the number of hidden circuits, candidate pool size, reference movie count, and score sampling distributions.
- The gains from T3 remained substantial even in challenging OOD regimes, indicating enhanced robustness and generalization capabilities.

#### 4.5. Ablation Study on Truncation Conditions

- **Window Size (k):** The optimal window size for detecting stalled progress varied by task. Moderate values of k typically yielded the best performance; overly strict truncation (small k) could prematurely end informative trajectories, while overly loose truncation (large k) had limited impact.
- **Alternative Proxies:** Truncation based on question semantic similarity (for SP) also produced performance gains, suggesting that T3 is robust to different proxy signal formulations, provided they effectively approximate BTR entry.
- **Random Truncation:** Even random truncation demonstrated mild improvements, indicating the inherent benefit of mitigating long, uninformative trajectory tails. However, a properly designed BTR-aligned truncation condition significantly outperformed random truncation.
- **Training Dynamics of Early Truncation:**
 - In tasks with unbounded hypothesis spaces (SP, PE), higher and stable truncation ratios from early training were associated with stronger performance, suggesting the benefit of aggressively removing uninformative tails.
 - In tasks with finite, enumerable spaces (CD), a low-to-moderate truncation ratio was preferred, as aggressive truncation could lead to premature termination of potentially informative trajectories.

#### 4.6. Impact of LLM Architecture

- **Model Scale:** Larger Qwen-2.5 models (7B and 14B) benefited more substantially from T3 than the smaller 3B variant. This suggests that LLMs with weaker belief-tracking abilities (potentially corresponding to larger update-error growth parameters) might fall into BTRs more quickly, limiting T3's effectiveness if the base model cannot sustain initial informative interactions.
- **Architecture Type:** Different LLM architectures (LLaMA, Qwen, DeepSeek-distilled LLaMA) showed varying degrees of benefit from T3. The DeepSeek-distilled LLaMA variant with T3-equipped RL achieved the best overall performance and the largest gains, suggesting that distillation processes might improve belief-tracking capabilities, thereby enhancing T3's utility.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research presented in this paper offers several contributions and potential impacts on the development of LLM agents for active reasoning:

- **Identifies a Critical Failure Mode:** The work formally identifies "belief deviation" and subsequent entry into a "Belief-Trap Region (BTR)" as a fundamental limitation that hinders the stability and optimality of RL-trained LLM agents in multi-turn active reasoning tasks. This theoretical framing provides a new lens through which to understand common LLM agent failures (e.g., repetition, unproductive loops).
- **Principled Solution for Credit Assignment:** By proposing T3 (Truncating Belief-Trapped Trajectories), the paper introduces a theoretically grounded mechanism to mitigate distorted credit assignment. T3 directly addresses the issue of uninformative trajectory tails corrupting gradients, thus enabling more effective policy optimization in outcome-based RL settings.
- **Enhanced Training and Performance:** The empirical results demonstrate that T3 consistently improves training stability, yields significant performance gains (up to 30 points in some tasks), and increases token efficiency (up to 34% reduction in token cost). These improvements are observed across various active reasoning tasks and different RL algorithms, suggesting broad applicability.
- **Improved Robustness and Generalization:** The out-of-distribution analysis indicates that T3 enhances the robustness of LLM agents in scenarios that deviate from the training distribution, which is a valuable characteristic for real-world applications.
- **Practical Implementability:** T3 is designed as a "drop-in solution," capable of being seamlessly integrated into existing policy optimization frameworks (PPO, GRPO, GSPO) without altering their core algorithms. This ease of integration facilitates its adoption by practitioners.
- **Guidance for Agent Design:** The findings highlight "belief control" as a key principle for building more reliable and generalizable active reasoning agents. The T3 condition provides a structured, theory-informed approach for designing effective proxy signals to detect epistemic stalling, reducing reliance on ad hoc heuristics.
- **Future Research Directions:** The work opens avenues for future research, including the development of more general-purpose, task-agnostic truncation detectors (e.g., leveraging semantic redundancy signals or internal LLM state representations) and adaptive truncation rules that can dynamically adjust thresholds during training. These explorations could further enhance the applicability and performance of belief control mechanisms.

In summary, this research contributes to a deeper understanding of LLM agent limitations in complex interactive settings and offers a robust, principled method for enhancing their learning and performance by explicitly addressing belief deviation.
