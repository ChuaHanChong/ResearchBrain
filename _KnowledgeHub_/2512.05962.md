# Whatever Remains Must Be True: Filtering Drives Reasoning in LLMs, Shaping Diversity

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from NAVER Labs Europe and an independent researcher introduce Distributional Matching with Verifiable Rewards (DMVR) and a novel α-DPG method to fine-tune Large Language Models. This framework enables explicit management of the trade-off between solution precision (pass@1) and output diversity (pass@k) in reasoning tasks, achieving superior coverage (e.g., best pass@256 scores with α=0.25α=0.25) while maintaining competitive precision on formal theorem proving benchmarks.

## Problem

- Large Language Models (LLMs) fine-tuned for reasoning tasks using Reinforcement Learning from Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) often suffer from reduced output diversity, a phenomenon known as 'mode collapse'.
- RLVR's implicit objective, which is tied to minimizing Reverse Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence, is 'mode-seeking', causing models to concentrate on a narrow set of high-probability solutions and potentially neglect other valid options.
- In formal theorem proving, this lack of diversity hinders the ability to solve complex theorems that may require exploring infrequent or less obvious proof strategies.

## Method

- The **DMVR** framework explicitly defines a desirable ==target distribution== that includes only correct answers (as verified by an external function) while preserving the relative probabilities of these correct answers from the base, untuned model.
- The paper proposes α-DPG, a novel approach utilizing Distributional Policy Gradient algorithms with α-divergences, to approximate this target distribution and provide continuous control over the trade-off between diversity (Forward KL, α→0α \to 0α→0) and precision (Reverse KL, α→1α \to 1α→1).
- The partition function for the target distribution is estimated online by averaging verifier outcomes of sampled responses, avoiding additional computational overhead compared to baselines.

## Results

- α-DPG models successfully spanned a significant portion of the Pareto frontier for pass@1 (precision) versus pass@256 (coverage) on the Lean theorem-proving benchmark, demonstrating controllable balance.
- With α=**0.25**α=0.25α=**0.25**, α-DPG achieved the highest coverage among all tested methods (superior pass@256 scores for k≥4k ≥ 4k≥4) while also improving precision over the base SFT model.
- High-alpha\\alphaalpha α-DPG (e.g., α=**0.999**α=0.999α=**0.999**) and traditional RL methods like GRPO exhibited a 'polarizing effect' on problem difficulty, improving many medium problems but rendering some hard problems unsolvable, whereas low-alpha\\alphaalpha α-DPG better maintained the solvability of harder problems.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The observed diversity loss in RLVR is a consequence of its implicit objective being equivalent to minimizing the 'mode-seeking' Reverse KL divergence to a target distribution that favors correct answers.
> - Fine-tuning LLMs with verifiable rewards primarily reweights and amplifies existing solution pathways present in the base model rather than discovering fundamentally novel ones, as suggested by perplexity analysis.
> - The α-divergence provides a principled and unified framework, allowing continuous interpolation between 'mass-covering' (diversity-promoting) and 'mode-seeking' (precision-emphasizing) objectives, thereby enabling explicit control over the precision-diversity trade-off.
