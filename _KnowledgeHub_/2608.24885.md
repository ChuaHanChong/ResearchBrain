# Do Robotic World Models Really Follow Actions? Diagnosing and Aligning Action-Conditioned Generation for Policy Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> WorldEcho is a benchmark that probes action-conditioned robotic world models with demonstrated and four off-expert action-query types, scoring rollouts by a visual-integrity gate plus SE(3) end-effector trajectory alignment. WorldSync then improves action following via expanded action coverage, an Action-Forcing Expert head, and intervention-effect supervision, reaching the lowest gated error (0.0661 m) and lifting real-robot policy success from 48% to 68%.

## Problem

- Action-conditioned world models are used as learned simulators on the unverified assumption that generated futures faithfully reflect arbitrary valid actions, never directly tested.
- Existing world-model benchmarks stay confined to expert demonstrations, measuring perceptual quality or downstream executability rather than continuous action following across broad off-expert numerical queries.
- Prior perturbation studies judge motion in image space or by task-level failure labels, lacking action-specific SE(3) ground truth for each commanded trajectory.

## Method

- **WorldEcho** benchmark: five action-query categories (demonstrated, cross-state replay, local perturbation, policy rollout, feasible-space sampling), each replayed in RoboTwin to give action-specific ground-truth futures.
- ==Integrity-gated protocol== combines a four-condition visual gate (MUSIQ quality, motion smoothness, SAM gripper visibility, VLM arm integrity) with a ==pose-aware normalized DTW== error, penalising failed rollouts by fixed κ.
- **WorldSync** training recipe: expanded action coverage in a shared end-effector action space, an ==Action-Forcing Expert== decoding future SE(3) states from intermediate video features, and an ==Intervention-Effect loss== aligning paired-action prediction differences.

## Results

- Across six expert-trained world models, integrity-gated error rose **0.029-0.099 m** on off-expert queries, with raw NDTW up **0.010-0.043 m** and visual failure rate up **6.3-28.1 points**.
- On 50 RoboTwin tasks WorldSync got the lowest gated error **0.0661 m** versus CtrlWorld **0.0670** and highest visual pass **84.51%**, though Cosmos-Predict2.5 had better raw NDTW **0.0127**.
- Two matched policy-improvement rounds lifted simulated success **51-52%** to **65%** versus CtrlWorld's **56-57%**, and real-robot cup-stacking **48%** to **68%** versus **56%**; ablation shows Intervention-Effect drives trajectory gains (NDTW **0.0170**).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Benchmarking a simulator only on expert actions hides its failures; evaluation queries must cover the off-distribution states a learned policy will actually visit.
> - Visual plausibility and action fidelity fail independently, so any generative-simulator metric needs both a validity gate and a motion-agreement term to avoid blind spots.
> - Relational supervision on paired counterfactual actions teaches controllability better than per-rollout feature grounding, which improved visual quality yet moved neither action-following metric alone.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic learning has increasingly leveraged Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models and World Action Models (WAM) for achieving high success rates and generalization in diverse robotic tasks, often through large-scale pretraining. Despite these advancements, task-specific online post-training is frequently necessary to optimize downstream performance. This post-training, however, is resource-intensive due to the need for extensive real-world interaction.

Action-conditioned world models (AC-WMs) have emerged as learned simulators to mitigate this challenge by enabling efficient closed-loop policy interaction and providing synthetic experience for policy post-training. The effectiveness of AC-WMs as reliable simulators for policy evaluation and improvement hinges on an assumption that their generated futures faithfully reflect arbitrary valid actions. However, this assumption has largely remained unverified, particularly when policies explore state-action distributions beyond expert demonstrations. Existing benchmarks for AC-WMs primarily focus on perceptual quality, semantic consistency, or similarity to expert behaviors, leaving the evaluation of continuous action following for broad numerical action queries, especially off-expert ones, largely underexplored.

This research addresses this gap by proposing a new diagnostic framework and an improvement method. It builds upon prior work in AC-WM development, their application in policy learning, and existing world model evaluation, but distinguishes itself by specifically scrutinizing the fidelity of action-conditioned generation for actions outside expert demonstrations, using precise SE(3) trajectory alignment as a key metric.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives of this research are:

1. **To introduce a comprehensive evaluation benchmark for action-conditioned world models (AC-WMs):** This benchmark, named WorldEcho, aims to diagnose the extent to which AC-WMs faithfully follow actions, particularly those that deviate from expert demonstrations. It seeks to move beyond evaluations confined to expert behaviors and perceptual quality, incorporating metrics for visual integrity and the fidelity of action-induced robot motion.
2. **To diagnose the failure modes of current AC-WMs under off-expert actions:** The motivation is to identify specific weaknesses of AC-WMs when presented with diverse, feasible actions not present in their expert-dominated training data. This diagnosis aims to reveal whether models maintain visual coherence and accurately predict the consequences of actions that explore beyond typical expert trajectories.
3. **To propose a systematic training methodology to improve action following in AC-WMs:** Based on the identified failure modes, the research aims to develop a training recipe, called WorldSync, that enhances AC-WMs' ability to generate futures consistent with arbitrary valid action inputs. This improvement is intended to result in more reliable learned simulators for robotic policy learning.
4. **To validate the practical utility of improved action following for policy learning:** The ultimate objective is to demonstrate that AC-WMs enhanced by the proposed methodology provide more trustworthy feedback for iterative policy improvement, leading to higher success rates for learned policies in downstream tasks, both in simulation and on real robots.

The motivation for these objectives stems from several critical observations:

- **Unverified Assumption:** Current AC-WMs used as learned simulators often rely on an unverified assumption that they accurately capture world dynamics and respond faithfully to arbitrary valid action inputs. This assumption is crucial for effective policy evaluation and post-training.
- **Limitations of Existing Benchmarks:** Existing benchmarks typically assess perceptual quality, semantic consistency, or general adherence to reference behaviors. They often lack a fine-grained evaluation of continuous action following across broad numerical action queries, especially for off-expert actions, which are essential for policy exploration.
- **Distribution Shift in Policy Learning:** Policies undergoing improvement inevitably generate state-action distributions that extend beyond the expert demonstrations used to train AC-WMs. If AC-WMs fail to accurately model these off-expert actions, their utility as simulators for policy iteration diminishes.
- **Observed Failure Modes:** Initial empirical diagnosis revealed two characteristic failure modes in expert-trained AC-WMs under off-expert actions: (1) visual collapse, where generated videos lose integrity (e.g., distorted robot arms), and (2) action mismatch, where generated videos appear visually plausible but depict motion inconsistent with the commanded actions. These failures highlight the need for both robust evaluation and targeted improvements.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research employs a "diagnose–improve–validate" workflow, comprising WorldEcho for diagnosis, WorldSync for improvement, and downstream policy learning for validation.

#### 3.1. WorldEcho: Benchmarking Action Following

WorldEcho is introduced as an evaluation framework to assess AC-WMs' fidelity to numerical robot actions, particularly beyond expert replay.

- **Action Query Construction:** Five categories of action queries are designed, progressively reducing reliance on expert behavior:
 - **Demonstrated Action:** Replays expert action sequences, serving as an in-distribution baseline.
 - **Cross-State Replay:** Applies an expert action sequence from a different state, testing the model's capture of state-dependent action effects.
 - **Local Perturbation:** Applies small, bounded perturbations to demonstrated actions, probing sensitivity to local variations around the expert manifold.
 - **Policy Rollout:** Uses actions generated by a learned policy, reflecting typical deviations encountered during policy improvement.
 - **Feasible-Space Sampling:** Samples valid actions from the broader robot action space, assessing controllability with minimal reliance on expert data.
 All off-expert queries are filtered for feasibility and executed in a simulator (RoboTwin) to generate ground-truth rollouts.

- **Visual Integrity Assessment:** Trajectory agreement is considered meaningful only for visually valid rollouts. Four complementary aspects of visual integrity are assessed:
 - **Image quality (q):** Measured using MUSIQ for frame-level perceptual fidelity.
 - **Motion smoothness (m):** Evaluates temporal continuity via frame-interpolation consistency.
 - **End-effector visibility (G_EEF):** Tracks grippers throughout the rollout using SAM-based video tracking.
 - **Arm integrity (G_arm):** Detects distorted, broken, or disappearing robot arms using a vision-language evaluator.
 These are converted into binary decisions using thresholds, forming an overall visual-integrity gate (G_vis), which requires all four conditions to be satisfied.

- **End-Effector Trajectory Alignment:** For visually valid rollouts, the robot motion in generated videos is compared to ground-truth videos.
 - A trajectory extractor (Φ) recovers per-frame SE(3) poses (position and orientation) for each end effector.
 - Local pose discrepancy (d_e) between generated and ground-truth frames is calculated, weighting translation and rotation.
 - Pose-aware Normalized Dynamic Time Warping (NDTW) is used to align the two trajectories, accommodating temporal progression differences. The NDTW error is normalized by the alignment-path length, preserving the absolute metric scale of pose discrepancy.

- **Integrity-Gated Evaluation Protocol:** For each query, both the visual-gate result (G_n) and the ungated NDTW error (D_NDTW_n) are retained. The final integrity-gated error (S_n) assigns the NDTW error if the visual gate is passed, or a fixed penalty (κ) otherwise. Results are macro-averaged across tasks.

#### 3.2. WorldSync: Improving Action Following

WorldSync is a training recipe designed to address the identified off-expert support gap and weak action dependence. It leverages three complementary axes: distributional coverage, representational grounding, and intervention-effect alignment, all integrated into a joint training objective based on flow matching.

- **Action Coverage Expansion Strategy:** To broaden the training distribution beyond expert demonstrations, WorldSync incorporates multi-task simulated trajectories, including expert behavior, local perturbations, cross-state replays, policy rollouts, and broad feasible actions. A small amount of real-robot demonstration data from the target domain is mixed in to maintain visual fidelity. Actions are represented as relative Cartesian end-effector pose displacements in the robot base frame, establishing a shared action space for both simulation and real-world data.

- **Action-Forcing Expert (AFE):** This component provides an auxiliary feature-level grounding signal. An AFE module progressively cross-attends to intermediate video features from successive blocks of the AC-WM's backbone and decodes the action-induced future end-effector trajectory in SE(3). A loss (L_AFE) is optimized to minimize the discrepancy between the predicted and ground-truth trajectories. AFE does not directly condition on actions but guides the video backbone's features to encode robot dynamics. It is removed during inference.

- **Intervention-Effect (IE) Supervision:** This provides a relational signal by supervising how predictions should change with action alterations. Paired trajectories are generated with the same initial observation and instruction but different actions. Crucially, both branches use the same noise input at the flow-matching noise endpoint, isolating the action as the sole differing input. An IE loss (L_IE) aligns the predicted intervention effect (difference in flow velocities) with the ground-truth intervention effect (difference in future video latents), ensuring the model learns to associate specific action changes with corresponding changes in the future.

- **Joint Training Objective:** The overall training objective combines the standard flow-matching generation loss (L_FM) with the two auxiliary objectives, AFE and IE, weighted by hyperparameters (λ_AFE, λ_IE). This joint optimization aims to enhance distributional coverage, ground video representations in robot dynamics, and align predicted changes under action interventions.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments were conducted on 50 RoboTwin manipulation tasks and real-robot tasks, using WorldEcho as the evaluation protocol.

#### 4.1. Benchmark Diagnosis (WorldEcho)

- **Off-Expert Performance Gap:** Across six expert-trained baseline models, the integrity-gated error consistently increased by 0.029–0.099 meters when evaluated on off-expert queries compared to demonstrated actions. This indicated that expert-only evaluations systematically underestimated performance errors under broader feasible control.
- **Failure Decomposition:** The performance degradation under off-expert actions was attributed to both trajectory inconsistency and visual degradation. Raw NDTW increased by 0.010–0.043 meters, and the visual failure rate increased by 6.3–28.1 percentage points across models. The relative contributions of these failure modes varied, highlighting the necessity of jointly evaluating both visual integrity and trajectory alignment.
- **Expanded Evaluation Coverage:** PCA visualization confirmed that the WorldEcho off-expert queries (Cross-State Replay, Local Perturbation, Policy Rollout, Feasible-Space Sampling) covered a significantly broader region of the action space compared to expert actions, demonstrating a more comprehensive evaluation protocol.

#### 4.2. Main Action-Following Evaluation

- **Effect of Expanded Action Coverage:** Training with Expanded Action Coverage consistently improved action following across all six baseline backbones. It resulted in lower integrity-gated error and raw NDTW compared to training with Expert Demonstrations. The effect on visual pass rate was mixed, improving for some models and remaining stable or slightly decreasing for others.
- **Comparison with Baselines:** WorldSync achieved the lowest integrity-gated error (0.0661 m) and the highest visual pass rate (84.51%) among all evaluated configurations. While Cosmos-Predict2.5 with Expanded Action Coverage attained a slightly lower raw NDTW (0.0127 m vs. WorldSync's 0.0223 m), WorldSync's overall leading integrity-gated performance was attributed to a strong balance between trajectory alignment and visual integrity.

#### 4.3. Policy Improvement

- **Simulation Results (RoboTwin):** On a RoboTwin bin-dumping task, WorldSync demonstrated superior policy improvement. Starting from similar initial success rates (51-52%), WorldSync-trained policies achieved 65% success after two refinement rounds, representing a 13 percentage point gain. In contrast, CtrlWorld (with either Expanded Action Coverage or Expert Demonstrations) only reached 56-57%, gaining 5 percentage points.
- **Real-Robot Results:** On a real-robot stacking-cups task, both conditions started at 48% success. After two rounds, WorldSync achieved 68% success (a 20 percentage point gain), while CtrlWorld reached 56% (an 8 percentage point gain). These results indicate that WorldSync's enhanced action following translated into more effective policy improvement in real-world scenarios.

#### 4.4. Component Contributions and Interactions (Ablation Study)

- **Expanded Action Coverage:** In isolation (without IE and AFE), expanded training coverage reduced mean gated error and raw NDTW, indicating its direct contribution to improved action consistency, rather than primarily visual quality.
- **Roles of IE and AFE:** Under expanded action coverage, Intervention-Effect (IE) supervision was identified as the primary driver of trajectory alignment, achieving the lowest raw NDTW (0.0170 m). The Action-Forcing Expert (AFE) alone did not significantly improve action metrics but yielded the highest visual pass rate. When AFE was combined with IE, it slightly lowered the gated error and partially recovered the visual pass rate, suggesting AFE contributes by improving the balance between action consistency and visual validity, conditional on IE's trajectory alignment gains.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of robotic learning by systematically addressing an unverified assumption fundamental to the use of action-conditioned world models as learned simulators: their fidelity to diverse, off-expert actions.

The **WorldEcho** benchmark is a significant contribution, providing a robust, multi-faceted framework for evaluating AC-WMs beyond expert demonstrations. By jointly considering visual integrity and SE(3) trajectory alignment across five distinct action-query categories, WorldEcho offers a more comprehensive and diagnostic assessment than previous methods. It has identified critical failure modes—visual collapse and action mismatch—that were previously overlooked by expert-only evaluation protocols. This diagnosis is crucial for understanding the limitations of current AC-WMs.

The proposed **WorldSync** training methodology offers a systematic approach to mitigate these identified failures. By combining action coverage expansion, representational grounding through the Action-Forcing Expert, and intervention-effect alignment, WorldSync enhances the model's ability to generate physically consistent and action-faithful futures. The experimental results consistently demonstrate that WorldSync improves action-following performance across various world-model backbones and evaluation metrics.

The most impactful finding is the translation of these improvements into more effective downstream policy learning. WorldSync-enhanced world models served as more reliable simulators for iterative policy improvement, enabling policies to achieve higher success rates in both simulated and real-robot tasks. This validates the practical utility of faithful action following, reducing the need for costly and time-consuming real-world interactions during policy post-training.

The work's impact extends to:
- **More reliable robotic simulators:** By ensuring AC-WMs faithfully respond to arbitrary actions, this research paves the way for building more trustworthy virtual environments for robot training and testing.
- **Efficient policy learning:** Policies can explore more effectively in a faithful simulated environment, accelerating the learning process and potentially reducing the sim-to-real gap.
- **Guidance for future AC-WM development:** The diagnostic insights and proposed training axes provide clear directions for researchers aiming to develop more robust and controllable world models for robotics.

While WorldEcho substantially broadens evaluation coverage, the authors acknowledge limitations such as the continued challenge of comprehensively probing long-horizon interactions across diverse embodiments and open-world environments, indicating avenues for future research.
