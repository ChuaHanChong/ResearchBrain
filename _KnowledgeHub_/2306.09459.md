# Recurrent Action Transformer with Memory

> [!summary] Summary
> The Recurrent Action Transformer with Memory (RATE) integrates a novel Memory Retention Valve and recurrent components into a transformer architecture, enabling robust long-term memory and effective decision-making in partially observable environments. It outperformed leading transformer and recurrent baselines on memory-intensive tasks like ViZDoom and T-Maze and remained competitive on standard RL benchmarks.

## Problem

- Standard transformer-based reinforcement learning models suffer from quadratic computational complexity, limiting their context window and ability to process very long trajectories.
- Existing transformers struggle in Partially Observable Markov Decision Processes (POMDPs) where critical information for optimal decision-making appears early and falls outside a fixed context window.
- Prior methods for extending context, such as Transformer-XL or sparse attention, often face training instability, poor generalization, or insufficient selective information retention for sparse rewards.

## Method

- The **RATE** model processes trajectories in fixed-length segments, augmenting each segment with learnable ==memory embeddings== and recurrently caching past hidden states.
- A novel Memory Retention Valve (MRV), implemented as a cross-attention module, filters and refines updated memory embeddings to selectively retain critical information and prevent its dilution or overwriting.
- Modality-specific encoders convert returns-to-go, observations, and actions into fixed-dimensional embeddings before they are fed into the segmented transformer architecture.

## Results

- RATE achieved the highest average returns and robust performance on memory-intensive tasks like ViZDoom-Two-Colors and T-Maze, maintaining **100%** success at 9600-step inference in T-Maze where baselines failed.
- It obtained the highest overall average normalized score (9.54) on the POPGym suite, scoring positively on 33 challenging 'memory puzzle' tasks where all other baselines scored below zero.
- RATE matched or outperformed Decision Transformer on Atari games (e.g., significant improvements in Breakout and Qbert) and surpassed it across all 9 D4RL MuJoCo tasks, demonstrating competitive performance on standard RL benchmarks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Combining trajectory segmentation with explicit memory components (embeddings, cached states) allows transformers to scale effectively to very long horizons, overcoming quadratic complexity issues.
> - The Memory Retention Valve (MRV) is crucial for selectively preserving sparse yet critical information over extended periods, preventing catastrophic information loss in long-term dependencies.
> - A hybrid architecture that integrates local transformer processing with global recurrent memory achieves superior performance in memory-intensive POMDPs while maintaining versatility across standard RL tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Transformers have gained prominence in the field of reinforcement learning (RL) by reframing policy learning as a sequence modeling task, particularly in offline RL where agent trajectories are treated as sequences. This approach has demonstrated capabilities in handling long-horizon credit assignment and operating in certain memory-intensive environments. Transformers have been successfully applied across various RL settings, including online, offline, model-based, and in-context learning.

However, a fundamental limitation of standard transformers in this context is the quadratic complexity of their self-attention mechanism with respect to sequence length. This computational constraint restricts the context window, making it challenging for transformers to effectively process very long trajectories or retain information over extended horizons. This limitation is particularly pronounced in partially observable environments (POMDPs), where optimal decision-making often necessitates recalling information from events that occurred hundreds or thousands of steps in the past.

Existing approaches to extend the context window, such as Transformer-XL (TrXL) with its hidden state caching, or methods that introduce sparse attention patterns, often encounter issues such as training instability or a lack of generalization beyond specific Natural Language Processing (NLP) tasks. Memory-augmented transformers have been explored in NLP to address similar long-range dependency challenges, but their direct application to RL is complicated by the structured, modality-rich inputs (observations, actions, rewards), the sequential nature of decision-making, and the frequent sparsity of reward signals and observations characteristic of RL environments. This work positions itself within this landscape by seeking to develop a memory-augmented transformer architecture specifically tailored to overcome these challenges in offline RL, particularly for scenarios demanding long-term memory and generalization.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research was to develop and evaluate a novel transformer-based architecture for offline reinforcement learning, termed the Recurrent Action Transformer with Memory (RATE). This architecture aims to address the inherent limitations of standard transformers in handling long-horizon decision-making and operating effectively in partially observable, memory-intensive environments.

The motivation for developing RATE stems from several challenges observed in current transformer-based RL models:

- **Context Length Limitations in POMDPs:** Standard transformers, such as the Decision Transformer (DT), operate with a fixed context window. While effective for sequences within this window, they struggle in POMDPs where critical information (e.g., an initial cue) might appear early in a trajectory and be required much later, after it has fallen outside the transformer's fixed context. This leads to an inability to make informed decisions based on historical data when long-term dependencies are present.
- **Quadratic Complexity of Self-Attention:** The computational cost of self-attention scales quadratically with sequence length. This makes it impractical to simply expand the context window to arbitrary lengths, especially for very long episodes or tasks requiring recall over thousands of steps.
- **Limitations of Existing Context Extension Methods:** While techniques like Transformer-XL (TrXL) introduce recurrence through hidden state caching, this often involves directly reusing past hidden states without explicit content-based gating. Such static recurrence can lead to error accumulation or the overwriting of relevant, sparse information, proving insufficient for robust long-range recall in sparse reward settings. Other sparse attention patterns, while efficient, may not generalize well beyond their original NLP applications.
- **Need for Selective Information Retention:** In sparse reward environments, critical information might be presented only once at the beginning of an episode. Effective memory mechanisms must not only store this information but also selectively retain it, preventing it from being diluted or overwritten by subsequent, less relevant observations. This necessitates a mechanism that can regulate information flow into and out of memory.
- **Versatility Across Task Types:** The goal was to develop a unified architecture that excels in memory-intensive tasks without compromising performance on standard, less memory-demanding RL benchmarks. This aims to establish a more general-purpose model for offline RL.

In summary, the research was motivated by the need for a robust, scalable, and versatile transformer architecture that can effectively leverage long-term memory to enable effective decision-making in complex, partially observable, and long-horizon offline RL settings, a capability currently lacking in many existing transformer-based and recurrent models.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core methodological approach of RATE involves augmenting a transformer with a recurrent memory mechanism designed to regulate information retention across long sequences. This is achieved by processing trajectories in segments and incorporating three complementary memory components: learned memory embeddings, recurrent caching of past hidden states, and a novel Memory Retention Valve (MRV).

**Architecture Overview (Recurrent Action Transformer with Memory - RATE):**
The RATE model processes a trajectory $\tau_{0:T-1}$ of length $T$, represented by triplets of returns-to-go ($R_t$), observations ($o_t$), and actions ($a_t$).

1. **Modality-Specific Encoders:** Each modality ($R_t$, $o_t$, $a_t$) is first encoded into fixed-dimensional embeddings ($\tilde{R}_t$, $\tilde{o}_t$, $\tilde{a}_t$) using modality-specific encoders.
2. **Trajectory Segmentation:** The encoded sequence is then split into $N$ non-overlapping segments $S_n$ of a fixed length $K$. This mechanism effectively extends the context to $K_{eff} = N \times K$ without increasing the local context window of the self-attention mechanism.
3. **Memory Embeddings:** Learnable memory embeddings, $M_n \in \mathbb{R}^{m \times d}$ (where $m$ is the number of memory tokens and $d$ is the embedding dimension), are prepended and appended to each segment. This structure, $\tilde{S}_n = \text{concat}(M_n, S_n, M_n)$, ensures both "read access" (prefix $M_n$ allows segment tokens to attend to past memory) and "write access" (suffix $M_n$ allows the transformer to update memory based on the current segment).
4. **Recurrent Caching of Hidden States:** Similar to Transformer-XL, hidden activations computed for preceding segments are stored and reused as an extended key-value context when processing the next segment. This provides segment-level recurrence and information flow across boundaries without backpropagating gradients through the cached states.
5. **Memory Retention Valve (MRV):** A central component of RATE, the MRV is a novel cross-attention module designed to filter and refine the updated memory embeddings ($M_{n+1}$) before they are passed to the subsequent segment.
 - **Mechanism:** The MRV takes the incoming memory ($M_n$) as queries and the newly updated memory ($\tilde{M}_{n+1}$ from the transformer's suffix tokens) as keys and values. Specifically, $M_{n+1} = \text{FFN}(\text{MultiHead}(\text{Query}=M_n, \text{Key}=\tilde{M}_{n+1}, \text{Value}=\tilde{M}_{n+1}))$. This allows the previous memory state ($M_n$) to "gate" or "select" which information from the newly generated memory ($\tilde{M}_{n+1}$) is retained for the next step, preventing catastrophic overwriting or dilution of critical information.
 - **Theoretical Guarantee:** A formal theorem (Theorem 1) is presented, demonstrating that the MRV, under an $\alpha$-alignment condition for memory embeddings, guarantees a lower bound on the amount of memory preserved and provides an upper bound on memory loss across updates.

**Experimental Evaluation:**
The effectiveness of RATE was assessed across a diverse set of environments:

- **Memory-intensive tasks:** ViZDoom-Two-Colors, T-Maze, Memory Maze, Minigrid-Memory, and the POPGym suite (46 partially observable tasks). These environments were chosen to specifically test the model's ability to retain and utilize information over long horizons, often with sparse rewards.
- **Standard RL benchmarks:** Atari games and MuJoCo continuous control tasks. These were used to evaluate RATE's versatility and competitiveness against models not specifically designed for memory-intensive settings.

**Baselines for Comparison:**
A comprehensive set of baselines was used, including:
- Transformer-based: Decision Transformer (DT), Recurrent Memory Transformer (RMT), Transformer-XL (TrXL) adapted for offline RL, and Long-Short Decision Transformer (LSDT).
- Classic baselines: Behavior Cloning (BC) and Conservative Q-Learning (CQL) with MLP backbones.
- Recurrent models: BC and CQL with LSTM backbones, Decision LSTM (DLSTM), and Decision GRU (DGRU).
- State Space Models (SSMs): Decision Mamba (DMamba).

**Ablation Studies:**
Detailed ablation studies were conducted to isolate the contributions of RATE's individual components:
- Impact of memory embeddings and cached hidden states (via corruption experiments).
- Upper-bound performance estimation using an "OracleDT" baseline, which receives perfect task-critical information.
- Comparison of different MRV configurations (cross-attention variants, gating mechanisms, GRU/LSTM-based updates) to identify the most effective design for information retention.
- Ablations on transformer parameters (layers, heads, feature size), number of memory tokens, and segment length/count.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation demonstrated that RATE improved performance in memory-dependent settings and remained competitive on standard tasks.

**Performance on Memory-Intensive Tasks:**

- **ViZDoom-Two-Colors:** RATE achieved the highest average return and the lowest imbalance between red and green pillar collection, indicating robust and consistent memory utilization over long horizons (up to 2100 steps, with cues disappearing at step 90). In contrast, models like DT and TrXL showed significant performance degradation when the pillar cue exited their fixed context windows, with DT’s return dropping by nearly 50% in certain scenarios. RATE’s stable performance across different target reward levels and pillar colors further supported its effective memory architecture.
- **T-Maze:** RATE achieved a 100% success rate across all in-distribution corridor lengths and demonstrated strong generalization, maintaining high performance even at 9600-step inference (equivalent to 28,800 tokens). Other transformer baselines (DT, LSDT) performed well on training-length sequences but degraded sharply beyond, collapsing to a 50% success rate. Memory-augmented models like RMT showed some generalization but still deteriorated. Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs) and State Space Models (SSMs) exhibited flat and generally low success rates, indicating difficulty with sparse long sequences. Ablation studies confirmed that the MRV mechanism was critical for preventing information leakage and enabling long-horizon memory.
- **Minigrid-Memory:** When trained on a fixed grid size (41x41) and evaluated on a wide range of unseen grid sizes (11x11 to 501x501), RATE achieved consistently high performance. This demonstrated strong interpolation and extrapolation capabilities across varying spatial and temporal scales within the environment.
- **Memory Maze:** RATE achieved the highest average episode returns (7.64 ± 0.41) compared to baselines such as RMT (7.27 ± 0.21) and DT (6.83 ± 0.51). This performance was attributed to RATE’s ability to capture implicit maze structure and object locations, which is crucial for efficient navigation in this environment.
- **POPGym Suite:** Across 46 partially observable tasks, RATE achieved the highest overall average normalized score (9.54). On the 33 challenging "memory puzzle" tasks, RATE maintained a positive average score (0.45), while all other baselines scored below zero, indicating their failure to exploit long-term dependencies. On "reactive POMDP" tasks, RATE performed comparably to DT and BC-LSTM, suggesting that its memory mechanisms do not impede performance in simpler tasks.

**Performance on Standard Benchmarks (Atari and MuJoCo):**

- **Atari Games:** RATE matched or outperformed DT in 3 out of 4 evaluated Atari games (Breakout, Qbert, Pong, SeaQuest). Notably, in Breakout and Qbert, RATE showed significant improvements over DT, which the authors attributed to the subtle, longer-range dependencies in these games where past actions can significantly alter the present environment state.
- **MuJoCo Tasks:** On the D4RL MuJoCo benchmark, RATE demonstrated competitive performance and, in several datasets (e.g., Hopper-medium-expert, Walker2d-medium-expert), surpassed methods tailored for standard MDP control. It also consistently outperformed DT across all 9 MuJoCo tasks. A novel evaluation strategy was used for MuJoCo, where each segment started with the maximum return, which was found to be beneficial.

**Ablation Study Results:**

- **Impact of RATE Components:** Corrupting memory embeddings in T-Maze caused performance to drop to a 50% success rate, indicating that these embeddings are crucial for storing sparse, discrete, task-relevant cues (e.g., the initial turn instruction). In ViZDoom-Two-Colors, corrupting both memory embeddings and cached hidden states degraded performance, with cached hidden states being particularly important for continuous reward, dense feedback tasks.
- **Performance Upper-Bound:** The OracleDT baseline, which was augmented with perfect prior knowledge, established an empirical upper bound for performance. The gap between OracleDT and RATE quantified the effectiveness of RATE’s memory mechanisms in autonomously discovering, storing, and utilizing task-relevant information. RATE successfully approximated OracleDT's performance in T-Maze for longer sequences, where DT failed.
- **Memory Retention Valve (MRV) Configuration:** Among the five tested MRV variants, the MRV-CA-2 configuration (where incoming memory $M_n$ queries updated memory $M_{n+1}$) consistently demonstrated the best performance in T-Maze. This cross-attention scheme effectively controls information flow, preventing the loss of important context over long sequences by selectively updating memory based on the relevance of new information. Without MRV, RATE’s performance deteriorated significantly on long corridors.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of the Recurrent Action Transformer with Memory (RATE) represents a significant contribution to the field of offline reinforcement learning, particularly in addressing the challenges posed by long-horizon tasks and partially observable environments.

The **significance** of this work lies in several key aspects:

- **Addressing Transformer Limitations:** RATE effectively mitigates the fundamental limitation of traditional transformers in handling long sequence lengths due to the quadratic complexity of self-attention. By introducing segment-level recurrence, memory embeddings, hidden state caching, and the Memory Retention Valve (MRV), RATE enables transformers to process trajectories far exceeding their conventional context windows.
- **Enhanced Memory Capabilities in RL:** The empirical results demonstrate that RATE provides a robust and scalable mechanism for retaining and utilizing critical information over extended temporal horizons. This is particularly crucial for Partially Observable Markov Decision Processes (POMDPs) and tasks with sparse rewards, where early cues or distant events dictate optimal decision-making. RATE's ability to consistently outperform leading transformer and recurrent baselines in various memory-intensive environments underscores its effectiveness.
- **Theoretical Grounding for Memory Retention:** The theoretical analysis of the MRV, providing guarantees for lower-bounded memory preservation, adds a foundational understanding to its functional role in preventing catastrophic information loss. This enhances the model's reliability and provides insights into its robust performance.
- **Versatility as a Unified Architecture:** RATE's competitive performance on standard benchmarks like Atari and MuJoCo, alongside its superior performance on memory-intensive tasks, positions it as a versatile and general-purpose offline RL model. This suggests that its memory mechanisms are beneficial across a wide spectrum of tasks and do not compromise performance in settings where explicit long-term memory is not the primary challenge.

The **potential impact** of RATE is broad:

- **Advancing Complex RL Applications:** By enabling robust long-term memory and generalization, RATE could facilitate the application of transformer-based RL to more complex, real-world scenarios where agents need to reason over extended periods, infer hidden states, and adapt to sparse or delayed feedback. Examples include robotics, autonomous navigation, and medical decision support systems.
- **New Directions for Transformer Design:** The architectural innovations, particularly the Memory Retention Valve, offer a template for future research in designing more efficient and capable memory-augmented transformer models, not just in RL but potentially in other sequence modeling domains requiring extended context.
- **Improved Offline RL Performance:** For domains where large datasets of past experience are available but online interaction is costly or risky, RATE offers a powerful tool for learning robust policies that can handle temporal dependencies and generalization challenges, thereby expanding the applicability of offline RL.
- **Bridging Research Gaps:** RATE helps bridge the gap between traditional recurrent neural networks (which struggle with very long-term dependencies and training stability) and standard transformers (which are context-limited), combining the strengths of both paradigms.

Overall, RATE represents a step towards developing more intelligent and adaptive agents capable of operating effectively in complex, dynamic environments that demand sophisticated memory and reasoning capabilities.
