# In-Hand Object Rotation via Rapid Motor Adaptation

> [!summary] Summary
> A rapid motor adaptation framework enables a multi-fingered robot hand to perform generalized in-hand object rotation around the z-axis. The system, trained in simulation using only cylindrical objects, directly transfers to a real robot and robustly rotates over 30 diverse, unseen objects by estimating object properties solely from proprioceptive feedback.

## Problem

- Achieving generalized in-hand manipulation has been a long-standing challenge, as traditional methods struggle with novel objects due to reliance on precise models or limited training data.
- Reinforcement Learning (RL) methods face sample inefficiency in real-world training, and sim-to-real techniques like Domain Randomization (DR) primarily achieve robustness rather than true generalization to fundamentally different object shapes or dynamics.
- Many dexterous manipulation approaches conventionally require complex sensing modalities such as vision or tactile feedback, increasing system complexity and reducing robustness to real-world uncertainties.

## Method

- The research applies a Rapid Motor Adaptation (RMA) framework, previously used in legged locomotion, to dexterous in-hand manipulation, focusing on continuous z-axis object rotation.
- A two-stage training process is used: a base policy is trained in simulation using privileged object information encoded into a low-dimensional "extrinsics" vector, and then an adaptation module is trained to predict this extrinsics vector online from proprioceptive history.
- The simulation-trained policy and adaptation module are directly deployed to a real Allegro Hand, with the adaptation module continuously estimating object properties (extrinsics) from joint positions and actions, enabling real-time adaptation to diverse objects without real-world fine-tuning.

## Results

- The policy successfully transfers directly from simulation to a real Allegro Hand, robustly rotating over 30 diverse, unseen objects (including heavy, irregular, and soft items) without any real-world fine-tuning.
- Quantitative real-world tests showed superior performance, achieving 23.96 average rotations for heavy objects and 19.22 for irregular objects, with high time-to-fall scores, outperforming baselines such as Domain Randomization and Online Explicit System Identification.
- Analysis revealed that the learned 8-dimensional extrinsics vector correlated with physical object properties like diameter and mass, and objects with similar properties clustered together in the latent space, demonstrating the learned representation's interpretability and utility for generalization.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The Rapid Motor Adaptation paradigm, utilizing low-dimensional "extrinsics" to encode environmental (object) properties, is effective for online adaptation in complex, contact-rich in-hand manipulation tasks, extending its utility beyond locomotion.
> - Proprioceptive sensing alone (robot joint positions and motor commands) is sufficient for estimating crucial object properties and enabling robust generalized in-hand rotation, significantly reducing sensing requirements compared to systems relying on vision or tactile feedback.
> - Natural, stable, and high-clearance finger gaits for object rotation can emerge automatically from reinforcement learning with a well-designed composite reward function, without requiring explicit behavioral heuristics or predefined contact sequences.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Generalized in-hand manipulation has long been recognized as a "grand challenge" in robotics, demanding precise control, understanding of contact dynamics, and rapid adaptation to diverse object properties. This paper makes a significant contribution by addressing the limitations of existing approaches in achieving true generalization for a diverse set of objects in real-world scenarios.

Historically, **classic control methods** for dexterous manipulation relied heavily on analytical models of objects and robots, requiring precise geometric and physical parameters. While capable of complex tasks, these methods inherently struggle with generalization to novel or irregularly shaped objects due to the difficulty of obtaining accurate models and planning in high-dimensional contact spaces for every new object.

More recently, **reinforcement learning (RL)** has gained traction due to its ability to learn complex behaviors without explicit models. However, training RL policies directly in the real world is notoriously sample-inefficient, limiting learned skills to simple tasks or a narrow range of objects. This motivated **sim-to-real transfer** approaches, aiming to leverage high-throughput simulation for training before deploying to physical robots.

Within sim-to-real, **Domain Randomization (DR)** has been a popular strategy. DR involves training policies in simulations with randomized physical parameters (e.g., friction, mass, textures) to make the policy robust to variations and uncertainties in the real world. While DR has enabled impressive feats like solving the Rubik's Cube with a robot hand, it often requires massive computational resources and primarily achieves *robustness* rather than true *generalization* to objects with fundamentally different shapes or dynamics that were not implicitly covered by the randomized parameters during training. It typically still focuses on a limited number of object types.

This work positions itself as a significant departure from pure DR by employing an **adaptive control paradigm**. It draws direct inspiration from the success of **Rapid Motor Adaptation (RMA)** in legged locomotion. In these works, a robot learns to adapt to different terrains by estimating low-dimensional "extrinsics" (compressed representations of terrain properties) from proprioceptive history. This paper is among the first to successfully extend this powerful RMA concept to the intricate domain of *in-hand manipulation*.

The key innovation lies in demonstrating that, similar to terrain properties for legged robots, the crucial physical properties of an object relevant to in-hand rotation (local shape, mass, size) can be compressed into a compact, low-dimensional "extrinsics" representation. By learning to estimate this representation online from *only proprioceptive feedback* (robot joint positions and actions), the policy can rapidly adapt to novel objects, moving beyond the limitations of pre-defined object sets or expensive real-world data collection. This approach fills a critical gap between robustness via randomization and true generalization via adaptation, especially for complex dynamic tasks like dexterous manipulation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop and demonstrate a simple, adaptive control policy capable of **generalized in-hand object rotation** using only the fingertips of a multi-fingered robot hand. Specifically, the task is simplified to rotation around the z-axis, yet it remains challenging due to the need for continuous dynamic or static force closure without palm support.

The core motivations underpinning this objective are:

- **Addressing the Generalization Gap**: Humans effortlessly adapt to manipulating new objects. Current robotic manipulation, especially with multi-fingered hands, often struggles to generalize beyond objects seen during training or those for which specific models are available. The authors aim to bridge this gap, even if it's a "small step towards this grand goal" of generalized in-hand manipulation.
- **Leveraging Rapid Motor Adaptation (RMA)**: Inspired by the success of RMA in legged locomotion, the authors hypothesize that the principle of learning a low-dimensional "extrinsics" representation of environmental properties (in this case, object properties) and using it for online adaptation can be effectively applied to in-hand manipulation. This constitutes a novel application of RMA in a highly dynamic and contact-rich domain.
- **Achieving Sim-to-Real Transfer without Real-World Fine-tuning**: A significant motivation is to train the controller entirely in simulation, using only simple cylindrical objects, and then directly deploy it to a real robot for a diverse set of real-world objects without any further real-world data or fine-tuning. This promises to reduce the prohibitive cost and time associated with real-world robot learning.
- **Minimizing Sensor Requirements**: The work is explicitly motivated to achieve this generalization using *only proprioceptive information* (joint positions and motor commands). This is a strong design choice, challenging the conventional wisdom that vision or tactile sensing is indispensable for dexterous manipulation. Success here implies simpler, potentially more robust, and cost-effective robot systems.
- **Emergence of Natural Behaviors**: The authors also seek to investigate if natural and stable finger gaits – complex coordinated finger movements for manipulation – can automatically emerge from reinforcement learning training without explicitly enforcing such behaviors through heuristics or predefined contact sequences.

In essence, the research aims to demonstrate a highly adaptive, simulation-trained, proprioception-only control policy that can generalize to a wide variety of unseen objects, paving the way for more versatile and intelligent robotic manipulation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology revolves around a novel application of the Rapid Motor Adaptation (RMA) framework, adapted for dexterous in-hand manipulation. It comprises a two-stage training process followed by real-world deployment.

#### 3.1. Hardware and Simulation Setup

- **Robot Hand:** An Allegro Hand (16 Degrees of Freedom, 4 fingers, 4 joints per finger) from Wonik Robotics. Controlled using position control at 20 Hz, with commands converted to torque via a PD controller at 300 Hz.
- **Simulator:** IsaacGym, known for its high-performance GPU-based physics simulation, allowing for 16,384 parallel environments during training.
- **Control Frequency:** 20 Hz, with episodes lasting 400 control steps (20 seconds in simulation).

#### 3.2. Base Policy Training (Simulation Only)

- **Privileged Information:** During simulation training, the policy has access to "privileged information"—an accurate 9-dimensional vector `e_t` containing object properties like position, size, mass, coefficient of friction, and center of mass.
- **Extrinsics Encoder (μ):** Instead of using `e_t` directly, a small 3-layer MLP (`μ`) encodes `e_t` into an 8-dimensional "extrinsics" vector `z_t`. This compressed representation is crucial for generalization.
- **Base Policy (π):** This is a multi-layer perceptron (MLP) that takes the current robot joint positions `q_t` (augmented with past two timesteps for velocity/acceleration), the predicted action from the previous timestep `a_{t-1}`, and the extrinsics vector `z_t` as input. It outputs `a_t`, the target positions for the robot's PD controller.
- **Reward Function:** A carefully designed composite reward function is used, optimized with PPO:
 - `r_rot`: Reward for achieving positive angular velocity along the desired z-axis, clipped to encourage stable rotation rather than maximal speed.
 - `r_pose`: Penalty for deviation from the initial hand configuration, encouraging stable finger gaits.
 - `r_torque`: Penalty for high motor torques, promoting energy efficiency.
 - `r_work`: Penalty for energy consumption (`τ^T * dq`), further encouraging efficiency.
 - `r_linvel`: Penalty for linear object velocity, promoting object stability within the grasp.
 - *Crucially, no explicit finger gaiting heuristics or contact constraints are enforced, allowing gaits to emerge naturally.*
- **Object Initialization & Dynamics Randomization:** The policy is trained *only on cylindrical objects* with varying aspect ratios and masses. Objects and fingers are initialized in a stable precision grasp by randomly sampling positions around a canonical grasp. Extensive domain randomization is applied to mass, center of mass, and friction coefficients, as well as PD controller parameters and external disturbances (see Table 5 in Appendix).

#### 3.3. Adaptation Module Training (Simulation Only)

- **Motivation:** In the real world, `e_t` (and thus `z_t`) is unknown. An "adaptation module" (`φ`) is needed to estimate `z_t` online.
- **Mechanism:** Inspired by previous RMA work, `φ` estimates `_z_t` from the history of robot proprioception (joint positions) and commanded actions.
- **Training `φ`:** After `π` and `μ` are trained and frozen, `φ` is trained in simulation via supervised learning. Trajectories are collected by executing the base policy `π` (which still uses the ground-truth `z_t` from the privileged information). `φ` receives a history window of proprioception and actions (`q_{t-k:t}`, `a_{t-k-1:t-1}`) and is trained to predict the ground-truth `z_t` using an L2 loss. The process is iterative until convergence.
- **Architecture of `φ`:** The adaptation module uses a two-layer MLP for encoding joint position and action pairs per timestep, followed by three 1-D convolutional layers over the time dimension to capture temporal correlations, and finally a linear layer to output `_z_t`. A history length of 30 timesteps (1.5 seconds) is used.

#### 3.4. Real-World Deployment

- The trained base policy `π` and the adaptation module `φ` are transferred directly to the real Allegro Hand.
- During deployment, the adaptation module `φ` continuously estimates the extrinsics `_z_t` online from the robot's proprioception history at 20 Hz.
- This estimated `_z_t` is then fed to the base policy `π`, allowing it to adapt its control strategy in real time to the specific properties of the object currently being manipulated.
- Crucially, *no vision or tactile sensing* is used in the real-world deployment; the system relies solely on proprioception.

This modular, two-stage approach, combined with extensive simulation randomization and the novel application of proprioception-based online adaptation for object properties, forms the backbone of the proposed solution for generalizable in-hand manipulation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents compelling results demonstrating the effectiveness and generalizability of the proposed Rapid Motor Adaptation (RMA) approach for in-hand object rotation.

#### 4.1. Superior Generalization via Adaptation

- **Simulation Performance:** In simulation (Table 1), the "Ours" method with continuous online adaptation consistently achieves the best performance across various metrics (Rotation Reward, Time-to-Fall, Object Linear Velocity, Torque) in both "Within Training Distribution" and challenging "Out-of-Distribution" settings (e.g., larger physical randomization, inclusion of spheres and cubes). It closely matches the "Expert" policy, which has direct access to privileged information, highlighting its ability to effectively infer and leverage object properties.
- **Real-World Transfer Success:** The most significant finding is the successful direct transfer of the simulation-trained policy to a real Allegro Hand without any fine-tuning. The controller robustly rotates over 30 diverse objects, including heavy objects (baseball, fruits, cups, Figure 3) and irregular objects (container with moving COM, objects with concavity, shuttlecock, cube, soft/deformable objects, Figure 4).
 - **Quantitative Real-World Results:** For heavy objects, "Ours" achieves 23.96 average rotations with a near-perfect Time-to-Fall (0.98, meaning it stays in hand for almost the full 30s episode). For irregular objects, it achieves 19.22 rotations with a TTF of 0.78, outperforming all baselines.
 - **Baseline Comparisons (Real-World):**
 - **Domain Randomization (DR):** Learned a conservative, slow policy, resulting in low rotations despite reasonable stability on some objects. It struggled with shape generalization.
 - **Online Explicit System Identification (SysID):** While more agile than DR, it was significantly less stable (lower TTF) and struggled to generalize, suggesting that learning exact physical parameters is harder and less effective than a low-dimensional embedding.
 - **No Online Adaptation (NoAdapt):** Universally failed to transfer to the real world, emphasizing the critical role of continuous online adaptation.
 - **Periodic:** Simple action playback performed poorly even in simulation, demonstrating the task's complexity and the necessity of reactive control.

#### 4.2. Interpretability and Utility of Extrinsics

- **Correlation with Physical Properties:** Analysis of the estimated 8-dimensional extrinsics vector (`_z_t`) during a continuous real-world run (Figure 5) revealed interpretable correlations. For example, one dimension (`z_t,0`) correlated with object diameter, showing lower values for smaller diameters and variations for irregular shapes. Another dimension (`z_t,2`) correlated with object mass, attaining higher values for lighter objects and lower for heavier ones. This demonstrates that the learned embedding captures meaningful physical properties.
- **Extrinsic Clustering:** t-SNE visualization of estimated extrinsics (Figure 6) showed that objects of different sizes and weights tend to cluster in distinct regions of the latent space. Irregular objects resulted in more scattered clusters, reflecting the varying perceived shape during rotation. This low-dimensional structure is identified as crucial for the method's generalization ability.

#### 4.3. Emergent Finger Gaits

- The reward function, which includes penalties for hand pose deviation and energy consumption but no explicit finger gaiting heuristics, led to the spontaneous emergence of natural, stable, and high-clearance finger gaits. The authors note that training with cylindrical objects, rather than purely spherical ones, was important for the emergence of robust gaits that generalize well.

#### 4.4. Proprioception Sufficiency

- A key finding is the "surprising effectiveness" of using *only proprioceptive sensory signals* for object property estimation and adaptive control. The system achieves robust in-hand rotation for a wide range of objects without requiring vision or tactile sensing, simplifying the sensing requirements significantly.

#### 4.5. Efficiency and Training

- The approach can be trained within a few hours (simulation time on IsaacGym, corresponding to 500 million agent steps), suggesting a relatively efficient path to acquiring complex manipulation skills compared to other large-scale RL systems.
- The appendix further shows an almost linear correlation between object mass and the average commanded torque, indicating the policy's adaptive behavior in terms of energy efficiency for different objects (Figure 7).

In summary, the main findings strongly validate the rapid motor adaptation paradigm for in-hand manipulation, demonstrating superior generalization to novel objects purely from proprioception, achieved through efficient simulation training and the emergence of natural control behaviors.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research marks a significant step forward in the field of dexterous in-hand manipulation, addressing a long-standing challenge with a novel and effective approach. Its significance and potential impact can be understood on several fronts:

**Advancing Generalizable Manipulation:** The work provides a compelling solution to a core problem in robotics: how to achieve true generalization in manipulation tasks. By enabling a robot hand to rotate a diverse array of objects (varying shapes, sizes, weights, and materials) without prior knowledge or real-world fine-tuning, it moves beyond the limitations of policies trained for specific objects or narrow distributions. This opens the door for robots to operate more flexibly and autonomously in unstructured environments.

**Validating and Extending Rapid Motor Adaptation (RMA):** The successful application of the RMA paradigm, previously primarily demonstrated in legged locomotion, to the highly complex and dynamic domain of in-hand manipulation is a major conceptual contribution. It shows that the principle of learning low-dimensional "extrinsics" for online adaptation is a powerful and generalizable framework for robotic control across different physical embodiments and tasks. This could inspire further research applying RMA to other intricate robotic skills.

**Reduced Sensing Requirements and Enhanced Robustness:** Demonstrating high performance using *only proprioceptive sensing* is a remarkable achievement. This challenges the common assumption that vision and/or tactile feedback are absolutely necessary for dexterous manipulation. By eliminating reliance on external sensors, the system simplifies hardware, reduces computational overhead, and inherently becomes more robust to visual occlusions, poor lighting, or unreliable tactile data, which are common issues in real-world deployments. This could lead to more robust, cost-effective, and practical robotic manipulators.

**Efficient Sim-to-Real Transfer:** The ability to train a complex, generalizable skill entirely in simulation (using only simple cylindrical objects) and directly transfer it to the real world without any real-world data collection or fine-tuning represents a substantial leap in sim-to-real transfer capabilities. This drastically reduces the time, cost, and safety concerns associated with robot learning, accelerating the development of new robotic skills. The training efficiency, completing in "a few hours" of simulation time, further underscores its practical value.

#### 5.1. Foundations for Future Research

- **Full SO(3) Reorientation:** The authors acknowledge that the current work focuses on z-axis rotation but suggest that multiple policies (for different axes) could achieve full 3D object reorientation, laying a clear path for future extensions.
- **Incorporating Other Modalities:** While demonstrating proprioception's sufficiency, the work also highlights its limitations (e.g., small objects, precise contact points). This provides a strong baseline against which the incremental benefits of incorporating vision or tactile sensing for even more challenging tasks can be precisely evaluated.
- **Understanding Learned Representations:** The interpretability of the learned extrinsics (correlating with mass and size) and the emergence of natural finger gaits invite deeper theoretical analysis into the learned latent spaces and control policies, potentially leading to new insights into human-like dexterity.

**Broader Industrial and Societal Impact:** Improved generalized manipulation capabilities have broad implications across various sectors. This includes flexible manufacturing and logistics (handling diverse product lines), assistive robotics (adapting to various everyday objects), and even space exploration or hazardous environment operations where human intervention is limited. The paper's contribution brings us closer to robots that can truly adapt and operate in unpredictable, human-centric environments.
