# DataFlex: A Unified Framework for Data-Centric Dynamic Training of Large Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> A unified framework named DataFlex facilitates data-centric dynamic training for large language models, consolidating disparate methods for data selection, mixture, and reweighting into a single, extensible system. It demonstrates consistent improvements in MMLU accuracy and perplexity over static baselines, alongside enhanced runtime efficiency compared to original implementations of integrated algorithms.

## Problem

- The landscape of data-centric LLM training methods is fragmented, with diverse strategies implemented in isolated codebases, leading to inconsistent interfaces and protocols.
- Systematic evaluation and fair comparison between different data-centric algorithms are challenging due to varied experimental settings and difficulty in reproducing results.
- Integrating diverse data-centric algorithms into scalable LLM pipelines is complicated by the lack of a unified abstraction for managing model-dependent operations like embeddings or gradients.

## Method

- **DataFlex** introduces a unified framework built upon **LLaMA-Factory**, featuring a ==three-layered modular design== to dynamically control data usage during LLM training.
- Specialized ==Trainer modes== (Select, Mix, Weight) abstract different data-centric paradigms, interacting with pluggable ==strategy Components== to manage algorithm-specific logic.
- The framework standardizes model-dependent operations and supports large-scale distributed training environments, including ==DeepSpeed ZeRO-3==, with mechanisms for ==distributed gradient collection==.

## Results

- Data-centric methods integrated within **DataFlex** consistently outperformed static baselines, with LESS achieving **0.452** MMLU accuracy (vs. **0.394** static) on Mistral-7B and Reweight reaching **0.453** (vs. **0.319** static) on Llama-3.2-3B.
- Dynamic data mixture strategies (DoReMi, ODM) improved MMLU accuracy and corpus-level perplexity on SlimPajama datasets; DoReMi achieved **26.04%** MMLU accuracy on SlimPajama-6B compared to **25.27%** for the static baseline.
- **DataFlex** demonstrated improved runtime efficiency, reducing latency by up to **7.09%** for online selection (LESS) and **1-3.5%** for offline selection (TSDS) compared to their original implementations, while also exhibiting parallel scalability across multiple GPUs.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A modular, unified framework can effectively integrate diverse data-centric strategies for LLM training, simplifying development and enabling systematic comparison.
> - Decoupling algorithmic logic from the core training loop through pluggable components enables high extensibility and rapid iteration for new data-centric methods.
> - Dynamic data management strategies (selection, mixture, reweighting) consistently enhance LLM performance, demonstrating improvements in accuracy and perplexity over static data approaches.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of large language models (LLMs) has seen considerable advancements, driven by developments in model architectures, optimization algorithms, and the increasing scale and quality of training data. As training corpora become more massive and heterogeneous, the management and utilization of this data have transitioned from being a static resource to a dynamic factor influencing training efficiency, generalization, and performance. This shift has led to the emergence of "data-centric training," a paradigm focused on optimizing not only model parameters but also the selection, composition, and weighting of training data during the optimization process.

Current research in data-centric training has explored various strategies, including online and offline data selection, data mixture optimization, and data reweighting. Examples include gradient-based influence methods (e.g., LESS, NICE) for sample selection, multi-armed bandit approaches for dynamic data mixture (e.g., ODM), proxy-model-based methods for static data mixture optimization (e.g., DoReMi), and loss-based sample reweighting. However, these diverse approaches are often implemented in isolated codebases, characterized by inconsistent interfaces, heterogeneous training and evaluation protocols, and sometimes outdated codebases. This fragmentation hinders systematic evaluation, fair comparison under unified experimental settings, and practical integration into production-scale LLM pipelines. The lack of a unified abstraction to manage model-dependent operations (like embedding extraction, inference, and gradient computation) further complicates the integration of diverse data-centric algorithms into scalable workflows, particularly in large-scale distributed training environments.

DataFlex addresses this fragmentation by proposing a unified framework designed to integrate these disparate data-centric methods into a single, cohesive system. It aims to provide a standardized infrastructure for reproducible research, systematic comparison, and practical deployment of data-centric dynamic training strategies for LLMs, positioning itself as a foundational system to bridge the gap between individual algorithmic advancements and their collective application.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind the development of DataFlex are rooted in addressing the challenges posed by the fragmented landscape of data-centric LLM training:

1. **Unification of Data-Centric Paradigms:** A key objective is to consolidate three major paradigms of data-centric optimization—dynamic sample selection, domain mixture adjustment, and sample reweighting—into a single, unified framework. This unification is intended to overcome the current fragmentation where these methods are often developed in isolation, leading to inconsistent implementations and evaluation protocols. By providing a common infrastructure, DataFlex aims to facilitate systematic comparison and practical deployment of these diverse strategies.

2. **Enhancing Reproducibility and Fair Comparison:** The existing situation of method-specific repositories with varying interfaces and protocols makes it difficult to reproduce results or conduct fair comparisons between different data-centric algorithms. DataFlex seeks to provide a standardized environment for experimentation, thereby improving the reproducibility of research outcomes and enabling robust comparisons under controlled settings.

3. **Seamless Integration with Existing LLM Training Workflows:** A significant motivation is to enable data-centric strategies to be integrated directly into modern LLM training pipelines without requiring external orchestration or extensive modifications. DataFlex is designed as a "drop-in replacement" for the training layer of existing frameworks, specifically LLaMA-Factory, ensuring compatibility with established model management, data processing, and optimization components. This minimizes the engineering overhead for researchers and practitioners adopting data-centric approaches.

4. **Promoting Extensibility and Modularity:** The framework aims to be extensible, allowing researchers to implement and integrate new data-centric algorithms with minimal engineering effort. This is achieved through modular abstractions (trainers and pluggable components) that encapsulate algorithm-specific logic while maintaining a common interface within the training framework. This design supports the rapid development and testing of novel data-centric methods.

5. **Standardizing Model-Dependent Operations:** Many data-centric methods require frequent access to model-dependent signals (e.g., sample embeddings, inference outputs, gradients). DataFlex is motivated to unify the management of these operations, providing reusable implementations and ensuring compatibility with large-scale training settings such as DeepSpeed ZeRO-3. This standardization is crucial for the efficient and scalable deployment of dynamic training algorithms.

In essence, DataFlex is motivated by the need for a comprehensive, efficient, and user-friendly system that treats data as an active optimization variable, thereby moving beyond static data management to dynamic, adaptive data utilization throughout the LLM training lifecycle.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

DataFlex is designed as a Data-Centric Dynamic Training System, built upon the LLaMA-Factory framework, and implemented with a modular architecture to support dynamic control over data usage. Its methodology revolves around a three-layered design and a standardized approach to integrating diverse data-centric algorithms.

**System Design and Core Abstractions:**

1. **Base Layer:** This layer inherits the foundational infrastructure from LLaMA-Factory, including model management, data processing, and optimization functionalities. This approach reuses a mature backbone, minimizing the need to re-implement generic training features and ensuring compatibility with existing fine-tuning workflows.
2. **Trainer Layer:** This is the core of DataFlex, where the original LLaMA-Factory trainer is replaced by three specialized dynamic training modes:
 - **Select Trainer:** Manages dynamic selection of a subset of samples based on a specified strategy.
 - **Mix Trainer:** Handles dynamic adjustment of mixture ratios across different data domains or sources during training.
 - **Weight Trainer:** Modifies per-sample training weights dynamically during backpropagation.
 These trainers abstract the different paradigms of data-centric optimization.
3. **Component Layer:** Each trainer interacts with a set of pluggable strategy components:
 - **Selectors:** Used by the Select Trainer (e.g., LESS, NICE, Loss-based, Delta Loss, NEAR, TSDS).
 - **Mixers:** Used by the Mix Trainer (e.g., DoReMi, ODM).
 - **Weighters:** Used by the Weight Trainer (e.g., Loss-based reweighting).
 These components encapsulate algorithm-specific logic and are managed through a centralized registry, enabling easy registration and instantiation of new algorithms.

**Algorithm Integration and Extensibility:**

- **Unified Trainer-Component Interaction:** DataFlex establishes a standardized interaction pattern where trainers invoke their corresponding components at configurable intervals (e.g., `warmup_step`, `update_step`, `update_times`) to obtain control signals. These signals include selected sample subsets, domain mixing ratios, or per-sample weights, which are then fed back into the optimization process. This decoupling of algorithmic logic from the training pipeline facilitates the implementation and comparison of new data-centric methods.
- **Supported Algorithms:** DataFlex integrates a range of representative algorithms:
 - **Data Selection:** LESS, NICE (gradient-based), Loss, Delta Loss (loss-based), NEAR, TSDS (distribution-based).
 - **Data Mixture:** DoReMi (offline), ODM (online), along with static and random baselines.
 - **Data Reweighting:** A loss-based weighter.
 All these are implemented as pluggable components under a unified interface.
- **Configuration and Usability:** DataFlex maintains the YAML-based configuration format of LLaMA-Factory, requiring only a dedicated `dataflex` section to specify the desired data-centric strategy (`train_type`, `component_name`) and its scheduling parameters. This design ensures minimal modification when transitioning from standard LLaMA-Factory usage.

**System Efficiency and Scalability:**

- **Distributed Training Support:** DataFlex inherits support for mixed-precision training, distributed data parallelism, and DeepSpeed integration from LLaMA-Factory. It addresses the challenge of gradient acquisition in model parallelism settings (like DeepSpeed ZeRO-3) by implementing a distributed gradient collection mechanism that reconstructs full gradients and optimizer states from partitioned shards.
- **Overhead Reduction:** To mitigate computational overhead, DataFlex executes gradient-based operations at configurable intervals, caches selection and weighting decisions, and supports lightweight proxy signals (e.g., loss values) when full gradients are not strictly necessary. All data-centric components are designed to operate within the distributed training loop and can be parallelized across workers without centralized coordination, ensuring scalability to multi-node, multi-GPU environments.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of DataFlex covered data selection, data mixture, and efficiency aspects, demonstrating the framework's capabilities and the performance of integrated data-centric methods.

#### 4.1. Data Selection and Reweighting

Experiments were conducted on a subset of Open-Hermes-2.5 (100,000 examples) for training, with MMLU validation and test splits for evaluation. Two LLM architectures, Mistral-7B and Llama-3.2-3B, were used as backbones. Methods evaluated included online selection (LESS, NICE, Loss, Delta Loss, Random), offline selection (NEAR, TSDS), a reweighting method (Reweight), and a static full-data training baseline.

- **Overall Trends:** For both Mistral-7B and Llama-3.2-3B, dynamic data-centric methods generally outperformed the static full-data training baseline in MMLU accuracy, validating the utility of actively managing data during optimization. Gradient-based methods (LESS) and the loss-based reweighting strategy (Reweight) consistently achieved the highest final accuracies.
- **Mistral-7B Results:** LESS achieved the highest MMLU accuracy of 0.452, outperforming the static baseline (0.394) by 5.8 percentage points. Reweight and TSDS followed closely at 0.429. Offline methods (NEAR and TSDS) showed faster initial convergence (steps 200–600) compared to some online methods.
- **Llama-3.2-3B Results:** The performance gap between dynamic methods and the static baseline was more pronounced on the smaller Llama-3.2-3B. The static baseline reached 0.319, while all online methods exceeded 0.427. Reweight achieved the highest accuracy at 0.453, followed by LESS (0.450) and Delta Loss (0.434). Offline methods (NEAR at 0.344, TSDS at 0.345) performed less effectively on this smaller model compared to online methods.

#### 4.2. Data Mixture

Domain mixture optimization was evaluated on SlimPajama-6B and SlimPajama-30B token subsets, using Qwen2.5-1.5B as the target model and Qwen2.5-0.5B as proxy/reference models for DoReMi. Methods compared included DoReMi (offline), ODM (online), and a static baseline with default SlimPajama proportions. Evaluation metrics included MMLU accuracy and corpus-level perplexity (global and per-domain).

- **SlimPajama-6B Results:** Both DoReMi and ODM improved MMLU accuracy over the static baseline (25.27%). ODM achieved 26.04% and DoReMi 25.84%. For overall perplexity, DoReMi yielded the best score (4.134), surpassing the baseline (4.217) and ODM (4.244). DoReMi showed lower perplexity on large domains (CommonCrawl, C4), while ODM performed best on smaller, specialized domains (StackExchange, ArXiv, Book).
- **SlimPajama-30B Results:** DoReMi achieved the highest MMLU accuracy (25.97%), while ODM showed the best overall log perplexity (3.429), outperforming DoReMi (3.562) and the baseline (3.584). ODM demonstrated lower perplexity on 5 out of 7 domains at this scale, indicating its effectiveness in exploring and upweighting minority domains. The static baseline did not achieve the best result on any domain at the 30B scale.

#### 4.3. Efficiency of DataFlex

Runtime efficiency was assessed by comparing DataFlex's implementations of LESS (online selection) and TSDS (offline selection) against their original codebases.

- **Online Data Selection (LESS):** DataFlex consistently achieved lower training latency and maintained or improved accuracy compared to the original LESS implementation. In single-GPU settings, DataFlex reduced runtime by 3.72% at a 0.05 sampling ratio and up to 7.09% at a 0.5 ratio. For large-scale scenarios, DataFlex demonstrated parallel scalability using 8 GPUs, achieving a 57.13% runtime reduction compared to its single-GPU performance at a 1.0 sampling ratio, while also reaching the highest MMLU accuracy of 43.01%.
- **Offline Data Selection (TSDS):** DataFlex's re-implementation of TSDS showed consistent, albeit moderate, runtime improvements of approximately 1–3.5% across varying training and validation set sizes compared to the original TSDS operator. This consistent improvement makes the DataFlex implementation more suitable for iterative experimental setups.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

DataFlex presents a unified framework for data-centric dynamic training of large language models, addressing several limitations in the current research landscape and offering significant potential impacts.

1. **Enhanced Reproducibility and Comparability:** By consolidating diverse data selection, data mixture, and data reweighting algorithms into a single, standardized framework built upon LLaMA-Factory, DataFlex provides a common experimental environment. This standardization is critical for improving the reproducibility of data-centric research and enabling fair, systematic comparisons between different algorithms, which was previously hindered by fragmented codebases and inconsistent experimental setups.
2. **Streamlined Research and Development:** The modular design, with extensible trainer abstractions and pluggable algorithm components, lowers the engineering barrier for researchers. It allows for the rapid implementation and evaluation of new data-centric strategies with minimal overhead, fostering innovation in this domain. The unified management of model-dependent operations (embeddings, inference, gradients) further simplifies development, making dynamic training methods more accessible.
3. **Improved LLM Training Performance and Efficiency:** The experimental results demonstrate that dynamic data-centric methods, when integrated within DataFlex, consistently outperform static full-data training baselines. This includes improvements in MMLU accuracy for data selection, and both MMLU accuracy and corpus-level perplexity for data mixture. The observed runtime improvements over original implementations indicate that DataFlex can facilitate more efficient training, particularly in large-scale and distributed settings. This directly translates to potential cost and time savings in the computationally intensive process of LLM development.
4. **Practical Deployment and Scalability:** DataFlex's compatibility with existing large-scale training infrastructures (like DeepSpeed ZeRO-3) and its ability to manage distributed gradient collection positions it as a practical system for deploying advanced data-centric strategies in production environments. This enables practitioners to leverage dynamic data optimization to enhance their LLMs with a system designed for scalability.
5. **Foundation for Future Data-Centric AI Research:** By treating data as a first-class optimization object, DataFlex provides a flexible foundation for future studies on more adaptive, efficient, and principled data–model interactions. It encourages a shift in focus from solely model-centric improvements to a more holistic approach that considers the dynamic interplay between data and model throughout the training lifecycle. This could lead to new discoveries in how data can be intelligently managed to drive further advancements in LLM capabilities.
