# Mitigating Multimodal Hallucinations via Gradient-based Self-Reflection

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from NVIDIA, Data61, CSIRO, and ANU present Gradient-based Influence-Aware Constrained Decoding (GACD), an inference-based method that leverages gradient information to dynamically rebalance cross-modal influence and mitigate multimodal hallucinations in MLLMs. The approach effectively reduces co-occurrence and text-visual biases by up to 57% on co-occurrence metrics while improving accuracy and preserving informativeness across various MLLM architectures.

## Problem

- Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) frequently generate 'hallucinations' (factually incorrect or ungrounded content), particularly due to text-visual and co-occurrence biases, limiting their trustworthiness and adoption.
- Existing hallucination mitigation strategies often require expensive retraining, rely on auxiliary models, or lack the granularity to address biases at a token or object level during inference.
- Current inference-based methods provide limited insight into the sample-dependent severity of underlying biases, making targeted adjustments challenging.

## Method

- Proposes ==Gradient-based Influence-Aware Constrained Decoding (GACD)==, an inference-time solution that operates without finetuning or auxiliary models.
- Employs ==first-order Taylor expansion== to compute token-wise ==Jacobians (gradients)== that quantify the influence of individual visual features, prompt tokens, and prior output tokens on the next predicted token's logits.
- Implements ==Influence-Aware Constrained Decoding== by dynamically adjusting output logits, partitioning visual tokens into object-related and unrelated groups to target co-occurrence bias, and adaptively amplifying visual influence to counteract text-visual bias.

## Results

- Achieved significant reductions in hallucination rates, including up to **33%** for sentence-level and **57%** for co-occurrence hallucinations on the AMBER dataset, and up to a **92%** increase in accuracy on the LLaVA-QA90 dataset.
- Maintained or enhanced informativeness; recall dropped by an average of only **1.1%** across models, and detailedness improved by up to **45%** on LLaVA-QA90, demonstrating a balanced performance.
- Demonstrated broad applicability and superior performance across various MLLMs (e.g., LLaVA-v1.5, InstructBLIP, mPLUG-Owl2, Intern-VL2, Qwen2-VL) and benchmarks, consistently outperforming leading state-of-the-art mitigation methods.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Gradients can provide a principled, granular, and sample-dependent measure of individual input token influence on MLLM outputs, enabling a 'self-reflective' understanding of model biases.
> - Multimodal hallucinations can be effectively mitigated at inference time by dynamically rebalancing cross-modal contributions and suppressing spurious visual correlations through adaptive logit adjustments.
> - Partitioning visual tokens based on their relevance to previously mentioned objects allows for targeted reduction of co-occurrence bias, while a dynamic amplification factor helps rebalance visual vs. textual influence.
