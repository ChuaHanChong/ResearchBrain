# Reinforcement Learning for Large Model: A Survey

> [!summary] Summary
> A comprehensive survey maps the rapidly expanding landscape of visual Reinforcement Learning (RL) applied to large multimodal models, systematically categorizing over 250 recent works and detailing algorithmic advancements, evaluation methods, and persistent challenges. This work aims to provide a coherent guide for researchers and practitioners in this rapidly evolving interdisciplinary field.

## Problem

- The rapidly growing and fragmented research landscape at the intersection of reinforcement learning (RL) and large multimodal models (LMMs) lacks a comprehensive, up-to-date synthesis, especially for advancements since 2024.
- Significant challenges exist in applying RL to visual domains, including stabilizing policy optimization with complex reward signals, handling high-dimensional visual inputs, and designing scalable reward functions for long-horizon decision-making.
- There is a need for a structured overview to guide future research and practical deployment of RL techniques for visual tasks, covering areas from perception to generation and interaction.

## Method

- Conducted an extensive and systematic literature review, synthesizing over ==250 representative works==, predominantly focusing on research advancements since 2024.
- Formalized visual RL problems by casting text/image generation as an episodic ==Markov Decision Process== and detailing key alignment paradigms (==RLHF==, ==DPO==, ==RLVR==) and policy optimization algorithms (==PPO==, ==GRPO==).
- Organized the vast literature into a clear taxonomy across four thematic pillars: ==MLLMs==, ==Visual Generation==, ==Unified Model Frameworks==, and ==Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models==, analyzing algorithmic design, reward engineering, and benchmarking within each.

## Results

- A structured synthesis identifies RLHF and its variants, along with GRPO, as dominant approaches for aligning and optimizing large multimodal models, providing a clear map of algorithmic evolution.
- The survey successfully categorizes over **250** works into four key application areas, detailing their specific algorithmic designs, reward engineering strategies, and progress in benchmarking for each.
- A principled taxonomy for evaluating visual RL methods is introduced, differentiating between set-level, sample-level, and model-state metrics, alongside a clear articulation of critical open challenges and promising future research directions.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - RLHF principles and its variants, such as Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) and Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR), are profoundly influencing reward signal design for visual RL.
> - Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) emerges as a particularly effective policy optimization algorithm for visual RL, offering stable, low-variance learning signals and reducing memory consumption by discarding the value network.
> - The field's research landscape is structured into four primary thematic areas: MLLMs, Visual Generation, Unified Models, and VLA Models, each presenting unique algorithmic adaptations and reward design considerations.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This survey paper is strategically positioned at the nexus of two of the most impactful and rapidly advancing fields in artificial intelligence: large language models (LLMs) and reinforcement learning (RL). Over the past few years, RL has achieved remarkable success in enhancing LLM capabilities, most notably through techniques like Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF), which aligns model outputs with human preferences, and innovative frameworks such as DeepSeek-R1, which incentivize complex reasoning. These successes in the purely linguistic domain have ignited "explosive interest" in extending RL methodologies to multimodal large models.

The broader research landscape is characterized by a concerted effort to create more generalist AI systems that can not only process and generate text but also perceive, reason about, generate, and act within complex visual and interactive environments. This involves developing Vision-Language Models (VLMs), Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, diffusion-based visual generation models, and unified multimodal frameworks. This survey directly addresses this trend by synthesizing the nascent but rapidly growing body of research that applies RL to these multimodal challenges. It provides a timely and critical overview, focusing specifically on advancements since 2024, indicating its relevance to the cutting edge of current research. By formalizing problems, tracing algorithmic evolution, and categorizing over 250 representative works, the paper serves as a crucial navigational tool for researchers and practitioners attempting to understand and contribute to this complex, interdisciplinary landscape. It highlights how RL is enabling multimodal models to achieve higher semantic coherence, better alignment with human judgments, and improved task-specific performance in diverse applications ranging from robotic manipulation to GUI automation and visual content creation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core motivation behind this survey stems from the "explosive interest" and rapid progress at the intersection of reinforcement learning and multimodal large models. While RL has significantly enhanced LLMs, its application to the visual domain is a newer, burgeoning field that lacks a comprehensive, up-to-date synthesis. The sheer volume and diversity of recent works necessitate a structured overview to guide future research and practical deployment. The authors identify challenges such as stabilizing policy optimization under complex reward signals, managing high-dimensional visual inputs, and designing scalable reward functions for long-horizon decision-making, emphasizing the need for methodological innovations.

The key objectives of this survey are clearly articulated in the abstract and introduction:
1. **Formalize Visual RL Problems:** To provide a foundational understanding by casting text/image generation as an episodic Markov Decision Process and defining relevant notations and problem formulations.
2. **Trace Algorithmic Evolution:** To track the development of policy-optimization strategies, from RLHF and Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) to Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR), and from Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) to Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), highlighting their adaptations for visual domains.
3. **Organize and Categorize Research:** To systematically categorize over 250 representative works into four thematic pillars: Multi-Modal Large Language Models (MLLMs), Visual Generation, Unified Model Frameworks, and Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models.
4. **Examine Key Aspects within Pillars:** For each thematic area, to analyze algorithmic design, reward engineering strategies, and progress in benchmarking.
5. **Distill Emerging Trends:** To identify and discuss prevailing trends such as curriculum-driven training, preference-aligned diffusion, and unified reward modeling.
6. **Review Evaluation Protocols:** To provide a structured review of evaluation methods, distinguishing between set-level fidelity, sample-level preference, and state-level stability metrics.
7. **Identify Open Challenges and Future Directions:** To pinpoint critical open problems in the field, including sample efficiency, generalization, and safe deployment, and to suggest promising avenues for future inquiry.

Ultimately, the overarching goal is "to provide researchers and practitioners with a coherent map of the rapidly expanding landscape of visual RL and to highlight promising directions for future inquiry." The authors also explicitly state three key contributions: a systematic survey of over 200 studies, an analysis of policy optimization, reward modeling, and benchmarking, and the introduction of a principled taxonomy for visual RL methods based on metric granularity and reward supervision.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed in this paper is a comprehensive and systematic literature review, characteristic of a high-quality survey. The authors embark on an extensive synthesis of "more than 250 representative works" (abstract) or "over 200 visual reinforcement learning studies" (contributions), predominantly focusing on research advancements since 2024 to ensure timeliness.

The survey's approach can be broken down into several key components:

1. **Foundational Preliminaries (Section 2):** Before diving into visual applications, the authors establish a strong theoretical groundwork. They formalize the notation for text/image generation as an episodic Markov Decision Process, defining states, actions, policies, and rewards. They then detail three crucial alignment paradigms:
- **Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF):** Explaining its three-stage recipe of supervised policy training, reward model learning from human preferences, and policy optimization via RL.
- **Direct Preference Optimization (DPO):** Presenting it as a more direct method that bypasses the explicit reward model, optimizing a closed-form supervised objective based on pairwise preferences.
- **Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR):** Highlighting its distinctiveness by replacing subjective human preferences with deterministic, programmatically checkable reward signals, often used with GRPO.
 They also review core policy optimization algorithms:
- **Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO):** Detailing its use of importance sampling, immediate rewards from a pre-trained reward model, a learned value network (critic) for advantage estimation, and a KL regularization term.
- **Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO):** Emphasizing its improvements over PPO by discarding the critic network, computing group-relative advantages from multiple outputs for the same prompt, and applying an explicit prompt-level KL penalty.

2. **Categorical Organization of Visual RL (Section 3):** The extensive literature is meticulously organized into a clear taxonomy of four main thematic pillars, visually reinforced by Figure 4:
- **Multi-Modal Large Language Models (MLLMs):** Further subdivided into conventional RL-based MLLMs, Spatial and 3D Perception, Image Reasoning (differentiating "Think about Image" and "Think with Image" approaches), and Video Reasoning.
- **Visual Generation:** Covering Image Generation, Video Generation, and 3D Generation, with a dedicated subsection on reward design and preference modeling.
- **Unified Model:** Distinguishing between Unified RL (single policy and reward for multiple tasks) and Task-specific RL.
- **Vision-Language-Action (VLA) Models:** Divided into GUI Automation, Visual Navigation, and Visual Manipulation.
 For each sub-category, representative works are cited and their specific contributions to algorithmic design, reward engineering, and benchmarking are discussed.

3. **Metrics and Benchmarks (Section 4):** A critical aspect of evaluating progress, this section introduces a principled taxonomy for metrics based on granularity:
- **Task Metric (M_set):** Set-level evaluation over the full prompt set, typically RL-free external benchmarks.
- **Reward/Advantage Metric (M_samp):** Sample-level metrics operating on individual input-output pairs, forming the learning signal for RL.
- **Model-state Metric (M_t_state):** State-level diagnostics tracking training dynamics (e.g., KL divergence, output length drift).
 This framework is then applied to each of the four main application areas, detailing commonly used metrics and public benchmarks (summarized in Tables 2, 3, 4, 5).

4. **Challenges and Future Works (Section 5):** The survey concludes by synthesizing the identified limitations and proposing concrete directions for future research. This includes areas like reasoning calibration, long-horizon RL in VLA, action-space design for "thinking with vision," and the critical need for scalable and faithful reward models in visual generation.

Through this structured and detailed approach, the authors provide a coherent and actionable map of the current state of visual RL, ensuring both breadth of coverage and depth in key technical areas.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

As a comprehensive survey, the "results" are not empirical data but rather a structured synthesis of the research landscape, key trends, and identified challenges. The main findings can be summarized as follows:

1. **Dominance of RLHF and its Variants:** The success of RLHF in LLMs has profoundly influenced visual RL. While direct human feedback is challenging for complex visual tasks, its principles have led to adaptations like DPO and RLVR, where rewards are derived from verifiable signals or model preferences.
2. **Ascendance of Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO):** GRPO is highlighted as a particularly effective policy optimization algorithm for visual RL. Its ability to reduce memory consumption by discarding the value network and provide stable, low-variance learning signals through group-relative baselines makes it well-suited for high-dimensional visual data and comparison-based reward models, especially in the context of verifiable rewards.
3. **Four Thematic Pillars for Visual RL:** The survey successfully categorizes the vast and diverse research into four primary areas, each with distinct characteristics and challenges:
- **Multi-Modal Large Language Models (MLLMs):** RL is applied to enhance alignment, reasoning (both "thinking about images" and "thinking with images"), and perception tasks. Verifiable rewards are shown to be a cost-effective alternative to human feedback, and curriculum learning aids generalization.
- **Visual Generation (Image, Video, 3D):** RL plays a crucial role in steering generative models (especially diffusion models) towards higher realism, better text-image alignment, and user-specific appearance. Reward design is critical here, evolving from human-centric preferences to multimodal reasoning-based evaluations and metric-driven objectives. Temporal coherence and physical plausibility are key challenges for video and 3D.
- **Unified Models:** An emerging trend where a single RL objective and shared policy optimize across heterogeneous multimodal tasks (e.g., understanding and generation), fostering cross-modal generalization and reducing training costs.
- **Vision-Language-Action (VLA) Models:** RL enables agents to perform complex sequential decision-making in interactive environments like GUI automation, visual navigation, and robotic manipulation. Rule-verifiable rewards, group-normalized updates, and memory-guided policies are crucial for long-horizon stability and efficiency.
4. **Structured Evaluation Taxonomy:** The survey introduces a principled taxonomy for evaluating visual RL methods based on metric granularity:
- **Set-level Metrics (M_set):** Traditional, RL-free benchmarks for overall performance.
- **Sample-level Metrics (M_samp):** The direct reward or advantage signals used for policy training (human preferences, verifiable rewards, model preferences).
- **Model-state Metrics (M_t_state):** Diagnostics tracked during training to monitor stability (e.g., KL divergence, output length drift), crucial for debugging and preventing issues like reward hacking.
5. **Persistent Challenges:** Despite rapid progress, several fundamental challenges remain pervasive across all domains:
- **Sample Efficiency:** RL methods often require significantly more data and compute than supervised learning.
- **Generalization:** Models struggle to generalize robustly across diverse domains, viewpoints, and unseen environments.
- **Reward Design:** Crafting scalable, faithful, and hack-resistant reward functions, especially for long-horizon and open-ended tasks, is a significant hurdle.
- **Reasoning Calibration:** Balancing the depth and efficiency of reasoning to avoid excessive latency or overly aggressive pruning of critical information.
- **Action-Space Design:** Representing continuous visual actions (e.g., cropping, sketching) within discrete RL frameworks efficiently.

In essence, the survey reveals a vibrant research field rapidly adapting RL techniques to multimodal settings, driven by innovative reward design and policy optimization strategies, while also clearly outlining the persistent methodological and practical hurdles that require further attention.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This survey paper holds significant importance and is poised to have a substantial impact on the field of artificial intelligence, particularly in the rapidly converging domains of large models, multimodal understanding, and autonomous agents.

1. **Comprehensive Research Guide:** By synthesizing "over 250 representative works" and organizing them into a clear, thematic taxonomy, the paper provides an invaluable, up-to-date "coherent map" for researchers and practitioners. This is crucial for new entrants to the field, offering a structured entry point to a complex landscape, and for seasoned researchers seeking to identify trends, gaps, and potential collaborations. It effectively reduces the overhead of navigating a vast and quickly evolving literature.
2. **Catalyst for Future Research:** The explicit identification of "open challenges" and "promising directions for future inquiry" serves as a direct call to action for the research community. Topics like adaptive horizon policies, meta-reasoning for efficiency, intrinsic sub-goal discovery for long-horizon RL, structured visual skill learning, and scalable reward model design for visual generation are highlighted as critical areas demanding innovation. This forward-looking perspective can directly influence research agendas and foster new breakthroughs.
3. **Standardization and Conceptual Clarity:** The formalization of visual RL problems, the detailed review of key alignment paradigms (RLHF, DPO, RLVR), and policy optimization algorithms (PPO, GRPO), along with the introduction of a principled taxonomy for evaluation metrics (set-level, sample-level, state-level), contribute to standardizing terminology and fostering conceptual clarity. This common framework facilitates more precise discussions, comparisons, and advancements across different sub-fields.
4. **Acceleration of Application Development:** The insights gleaned from the survey, particularly regarding effective reward engineering, stable policy optimization (e.g., GRPO's utility), and robust evaluation protocols, can accelerate the development and deployment of practical AI applications. This includes more capable GUI automation agents, reliable visual navigation systems, sophisticated robotic manipulators, and advanced visual content generation tools that are better aligned with human preferences and real-world requirements.
5. **Interdisciplinary Bridge:** The survey inherently acts as an interdisciplinary bridge, connecting methodologies from reinforcement learning with challenges in computer vision and natural language processing. This integration is vital for the development of truly generalist AI systems that can seamlessly perceive, reason, generate, and interact across modalities, pushing the boundaries towards more holistic artificial intelligence.
6. **Timeliness and Relevance:** The paper's focus on research activity "since 2024" underscores its extreme timeliness. In a field where significant advancements occur annually, providing such an immediate and focused overview ensures that the insights are maximally relevant to current research and development efforts.

In conclusion, this survey is not merely a summary of existing work but a strategic contribution that illuminates the path forward for visual reinforcement learning. By structuring knowledge, clarifying concepts, and pinpointing critical future directions, it significantly strengthens the foundation for the next wave of innovation in intelligent multimodal systems.
