# V2P-Manip: Learning Dexterous Manipulation from Monocular Human Videos

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Zhejiang University, Shanghai Jiao Tong University, and Shanghai AI Laboratory developed V2P-Manip, a framework that learns dexterous robot manipulation from unconstrained monocular human videos by employing a hierarchical perception-refinement-synthesis pipeline. The system provides physically plausible trajectories for residual reinforcement learning, enabling an overall success rate of 0.483 on the OakInk benchmark and transfer to real robotic platforms.

## Problem

- Traditional methods for collecting high-fidelity robotic demonstration data are expensive, hardware-intensive, and lack scalability and diversity.
- Extracting physically plausible and spatially consistent 3D hand-object interaction trajectories from noisy, unconstrained monocular human videos is difficult due to depth ambiguity and occlusion.
- Training dexterous manipulation policies in high-dimensional action spaces with reinforcement learning from scratch is computationally prohibitive, requiring effective initialization from human priors.

## Method

- A unified "==perception-refinement-synthesis==" pipeline for 3D asset acquisition, object reconstruction, and pose estimation from monocular RGB video.
- A two-stage refinement module that first resolves geometric misalignments through hierarchical optimization and then enforces physical consistency via cross-embodiment retargeting and simulation-based stability validation using Task Wrench Space optimization.
- A residual reinforcement learning framework that decomposes control actions into a pre-trained base motion and a learned adaptive residual policy, trained with PPO and a composite reward function in a physics simulator.

## Results

- Achieved an ADD-S score of **80.52%** on the TACO dataset for object pose estimation and an overall success rate of **0.483** on the OakInk benchmark, outperforming baselines.
- Ablation studies confirmed that both geometric (C1) and physical (C2) refinement stages are critical, with the full V2P framework achieving **79.3%** average success compared to **0.0%** (without C1) and **54.4%** (without C2).
- Demonstrated successful generalization by adapting learned skills to five different robot hands (e.g., **82.4%** for Arti-MANO) and executing complex bimanual manipulation tasks on a real UR + XHAND platform.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A hierarchical, two-stage refinement process integrating both geometric alignment and physics-based contact constraints is essential for transforming noisy visual data into physically plausible and executable robot trajectories.
> - Decomposing the control problem into a reference base motion and a learned residual policy effectively bridges the gap between imperfect kinematic priors from human videos and the dynamics of robotic systems in simulation.
> - Robust object pose tracking in unconstrained videos benefits significantly from a multi-hypothesis strategy combined with pixel-level temporal correspondences, mitigating issues like occlusion and motion blur.
