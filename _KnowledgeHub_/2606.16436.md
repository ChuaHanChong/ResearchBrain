# V2P-Manip: Learning Dexterous Manipulation from Monocular Human Videos

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Zhejiang University, Shanghai Jiao Tong University, and Shanghai AI Laboratory developed V2P-Manip, a framework that learns dexterous robot manipulation from unconstrained monocular human videos by employing a hierarchical perception-refinement-synthesis pipeline. The system provides physically plausible trajectories for residual reinforcement learning, enabling an overall success rate of 0.483 on the OakInk benchmark and transfer to real robotic platforms.

## Problem

- Traditional methods for collecting high-fidelity robotic demonstration data are expensive, hardware-intensive, and lack scalability and diversity.
- Extracting physically plausible and spatially consistent 3D hand-object interaction trajectories from noisy, unconstrained monocular human videos is difficult due to depth ambiguity and occlusion.
- Training dexterous manipulation policies in high-dimensional action spaces with reinforcement learning from scratch is computationally prohibitive, requiring effective initialization from human priors.

## Method

- A unified "==perception-refinement-synthesis==" pipeline for 3D asset acquisition, object reconstruction, and pose estimation from monocular RGB video.
- A two-stage refinement module that first resolves geometric misalignments through hierarchical optimization and then enforces physical consistency via cross-embodiment retargeting and simulation-based stability validation using Task Wrench Space optimization.
- A residual reinforcement learning framework that decomposes control actions into a pre-trained base motion and a learned adaptive residual policy, trained with PPO and a composite reward function in a physics simulator.

## Results

- Achieved an ADD-S score of **80.52%** on the TACO dataset for object pose estimation and an overall success rate of **0.483** on the OakInk benchmark, outperforming baselines.
- Ablation studies confirmed that both geometric (C1) and physical (C2) refinement stages are critical, with the full V2P framework achieving **79.3%** average success compared to **0.0%** (without C1) and **54.4%** (without C2).
- Demonstrated successful generalization by adapting learned skills to five different robot hands (e.g., **82.4%** for Arti-MANO) and executing complex bimanual manipulation tasks on a real UR + XHAND platform.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A hierarchical, two-stage refinement process integrating both geometric alignment and physics-based contact constraints is essential for transforming noisy visual data into physically plausible and executable robot trajectories.
> - Decomposing the control problem into a reference base motion and a learned residual policy effectively bridges the gap between imperfect kinematic priors from human videos and the dynamics of robotic systems in simulation.
> - Robust object pose tracking in unconstrained videos benefits significantly from a multi-hypothesis strategy combined with pixel-level temporal correspondences, mitigating issues like occlusion and motion blur.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in "V2P-Manip" addresses the challenge of endowing robots with dexterous manipulation capabilities by leveraging human demonstrations. Historically, acquiring high-fidelity trajectory data for robotic skill learning has relied on costly and hardware-intensive methods such as teleoperation or optical motion capture systems. These traditional approaches are limited by hardware requirements, time-consuming calibration, and restricted diversity of captured scenarios, which impede the scalability of robotic skill acquisition.

In response to these limitations, the research community has increasingly explored the use of more scalable data sources, particularly human manipulation videos from the internet and egocentric datasets. However, extracting reliable and physically plausible trajectories from such unconstrained monocular video data presents significant challenges. Prior work has highlighted that independent estimation of hand and object poses from monocular videos frequently results in spatial inconsistencies and physical violations, such as interpenetration, due to inherent depth ambiguity and occlusion. These inaccuracies negatively impact downstream policy training.

The current research landscape for video-based skill acquisition includes methods focusing on trajectory reconstruction and imitation learning (e.g., DexImit, DexMan). Many existing frameworks often operate under assumptions of structured environments or static-camera settings to maintain tracking stability. This work differentiates itself by explicitly targeting unstructured, in-the-wild monocular videos, including those with egocentric camera motion, which commonly suffer from motion blur, occlusion, and depth ambiguity.

Furthermore, learning dexterous manipulation policies in high-dimensional action spaces with reinforcement learning (RL) from scratch is computationally prohibitive. Therefore, mainstream approaches integrate imitation learning with RL, using human priors to guide exploration. Residual learning, which refines a base motion or policy, has emerged as an effective paradigm in this context. V2P-Manip aligns with this approach by integrating a physics-guided refinement process with a residual policy adaptation framework. This positions V2P-Manip as an effort to bridge the gap between noisy visual priors from monocular video and physically consistent, executable 3D dexterous manipulation for robotic systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of the V2P-Manip research is to develop an efficient framework for acquiring dexterous robotic manipulation skills directly from unstructured monocular RGB human demonstration videos. This objective is motivated by several key challenges in the field of robot learning:

1. **Scalability of Demonstration Data:** Traditional methods for collecting high-fidelity human demonstration data, such as teleoperation and optical motion capture, are constrained by expensive hardware, complex calibration, and limited diversity of scenarios. This hinders the large-scale acquisition of diverse manipulation skills. The vast and readily available pool of human manipulation videos on the internet represents a highly scalable and cost-effective alternative, but effectively utilizing this resource remains a challenge.

2. **Physical Plausibility from Monocular Video:** Extracting usable trajectories from monocular videos is difficult due to inherent limitations in 2D observations. Independent hand and object pose estimators often produce spatially inconsistent and physically implausible interactions, such as interpenetration or unrealistic hovering. These inaccuracies are detrimental to subsequent policy training, as robots cannot learn from physically impossible demonstrations. The framework aims to rectify these perceptual inaccuracies to ensure both geometric alignment and physical feasibility in the extracted trajectories.

3. **Robustness in Unstructured Environments:** Many existing video-based skill acquisition frameworks are confined to structured environments or static-camera settings to maintain tracking stability. However, real-world applications often involve unconstrained conditions, including egocentric camera motions, dynamic viewpoints, motion blur, and occlusions. The objective is to distill executable 3D hand-object interaction trajectories from raw RGB observations under these challenging "in-the-wild" conditions.

4. **Efficient Policy Learning for High-Dimensional Dexterous Hands:** Dexterous robot hands operate in high-dimensional action spaces, making reinforcement learning from scratch computationally prohibitive. While imitation learning can provide priors, there often exist morphology and domain gaps between human demonstrations and robot embodiments, along with inherent noise in human priors. The research seeks a robust foundation for reliable grasping and smooth manipulation trajectories within simulated environments by combining rigorous physical refinement with guided exploration strategies, such as residual learning.

To address these motivations, the authors establish a comprehensive pipeline that transforms raw monocular observations into high-quality robotic demonstrations through a hierarchical two-stage optimization process. This process systematically rectifies spatial misalignments and enforces contact-level constraints, aiming to ensure both geometric alignment and physical feasibility. By building upon these refined motion priors, the framework intends to facilitate robust and efficient policy learning for dexterous manipulation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

V2P-Manip establishes an integrated pipeline for learning dexterous manipulation from monocular RGB videos, following a "perception-refinement-synthesis" workflow. The methodology encompasses 3D asset acquisition, trajectory estimation, and dexterous policy learning.

#### 3.1. Unified Perception and Object Reconstruction

The initial phase involves processing a monocular RGB video sequence to acquire 3D assets and recover scale.
1. **Segmentation and Depth Estimation:** A segmentor is used to extract binary masks of target objects. A depth estimator (MeGo2, based on supplementary information) infers per-pixel depth and camera intrinsics for each frame.
2. **Depth Refinement and Scale Recovery:** Temporal inconsistencies in per-frame intrinsic estimation are mitigated by establishing a canonical intrinsic matrix (global average). Depth maps are then refined using a remapping operation to ensure spatial consistency. Object segments are back-projected into 3D space to recover spatial coordinates. Outliers are filtered using spatial clustering, and the object's absolute physical dimensions (scale `s`) are estimated by defining the length of its longest axis.
3. **Mesh Generation:** SAM3D is employed to generate a coherent 3D mesh from monocular views, which is then uniformly scaled to match `s`, yielding a metric-consistent digital twin of the object (`M_obj`). The pipeline is designed to tolerate some depth bias, tracking trajectories in a scaled virtual space while preserving internal motion structure.

#### 3.2. Pose Estimation and Optimization

This module recovers coordinated hand-object motion through vision-based tracking and a dual-stage refinement layer.

- **Object-Hand Tracking:**
 - **Object Pose:** FoundationPose is used to estimate the 6D object pose (`T_t`). To mitigate tracking drift and abrupt jumps under occlusion or rapid motion, a multi-hypothesis strategy is employed. This strategy integrates robust pixel-level temporal correspondences from SpatialTracker, rigid motion consistency checks, and a candidate pose space formed by motion priors and current visual observations. A confidence score from FoundationPose's scoring network selects the optimal pose, with an iterative drift-detection loop categorizing low-quality frames to filter out unreliable refinement candidates.
 - **Hand Kinematic State:** A robust pose tracker (Dyn-HAMR, based on references) estimates the parameters of the MANO hand model, regressing shape (`β`) and pose (`θ_t`) parameters, along with global wrist orientation (`R^h_t`) and translation (`t^h_t`).

- **Refinement Module:** This module implements a two-stage process to enforce geometric alignment and physical stability.

 - **Geometric Constraint (C1):** This stage resolves spatial misalignments hierarchically.
 1. **Global Optimization:** Systemic biases are rectified by optimizing a unified scale factor and rigid offsets across all contact frames. An anatomically weighted contact loss prioritizes key grasping sites (e.g., thumb, index finger) while preserving the original relative motion structure. This stage minimizes a global objective `L_g` using the L-BFGS-B optimizer over scale factor `s` and rigid transformation offsets (`ΔT_g`, `ΔR_g`).
 2. **Per-Frame Local Refinement:** Building on global alignment, fine-grained, per-frame local refinement of pose and translation parameters is performed. The objective function jointly optimizes localized contact constraints (`L_c,t`), temporal smoothness terms (`L_s`), and a regularization term penalizing deviations from raw estimations. For non-contact frames, optimization increments from the nearest contact neighbor are propagated to maintain temporal coherence.

 - **Physical Constraint (C2):** This stage addresses the morphology gap and ensures physical feasibility.
 1. **Cross-Embodiment Motion Retargeting:** Human MANO model motions are retargeted to robotic hands. This is formulated as a non-linear optimization problem, minimizing the weighted distance between the robot's joints/tips and corresponding targets from the MANO model. Higher weights are assigned to functional digits to preserve manipulation intent.
 2. **Adaptive Trajectory Refinement in Simulation:** Within the Isaac Gym environment, per-frame stability validation is performed. Frames exhibiting instability trigger a physics-grounded optimization process in the Task Wrench Space (TWS). This process samples random external force directions and uses the GJK algorithm to query contact locations and surface normals. A joint objective function minimizes grasp energy (encouraging contact wrenches within friction cones to counteract disturbances) and enforces a critical distance threshold via GJK alignment to maintain contact proximity and prevent interpenetration. This optimization improves physical plausibility while maintaining kinematic consistency.

#### 3.3. Residual Reinforcement Learning

To bridge the gap between kinematic priors and physical execution, a Markov Decision Process (MDP) is formulated.
1. **Action Decomposition:** The control action `a_t` is decomposed into a reference base motion (`a_base,t`) and a learned adaptive residual (`π_ϕ(s_t)`).
2. **Hand Imitator:** The base action is provided by a pre-trained hand imitator, trained on foundational datasets augmented with synthesized data to establish a high-fidelity motion prior.
3. **Residual Policy Learning:** The residual policy `π_ϕ(s_t)` learns to compensate for unmodeled dynamics and domain gaps. Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) is used to train networks within the Isaac Gym simulator, leveraging parallelized environments.
4. **Reward Function:** The reward function `R_t` is a composite of imitation and contact-driven terms. An imitation term `r_imit` enforces reference trajectory tracking via a weighted sum of exponential alignment kernels (for wrist, fingertips, joints, and optionally object pose). A contact term `r_cont` regularizes physical interaction and contact force, activated during residual refinement, ensuring the policy refines the kinematic foundation into a dynamically stable grasp.
5. **Robustness:** Domain Randomization (DR) is applied over physical parameters (gravity, friction, object mass) to bolster robustness. Early termination and curriculum learning schemes are implemented for training stability.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The V2P-Manip framework was evaluated through experiments focusing on object pose estimation, policy learning, ablation studies, and generalization capabilities.

#### 4.1. Object Pose Estimation

- **Qualitative Analysis:** The method demonstrated superior tracking stability compared to the FoundationPose baseline in challenging scenarios involving heavy hand-object occlusions, cluttered backgrounds, and motion blur. V2P-Manip's multi-hypothesis strategy and integration of pixel-level correspondences from SpatialTracker contributed to trajectory continuity and mitigation of environmental noise.
- **Quantitative Evaluation (TACO Dataset):** On the TACO dataset, V2P-Manip achieved higher precision and robustness. It registered an ADD-S score of 80.52% (compared to 76.98% for FoundationPose and 63.65% for SpatialTracker), a lower Failure Rate (FR) of 7.27% (compared to 10.52% and 28.45%), and the most favorable Stability Index (SI) of 0.535 (compared to 0.529 and 0.532), indicating superior temporal consistency. Chamfer Distance (CD) was 1.447 cm, lower than baselines.

#### 4.2. Policy Learning Evaluation

- **OakInk Benchmark:** Evaluation on the OakInk benchmark showed that V2P-Manip achieved faster convergence speed and higher peak success rates compared to the ManipTrans baseline in both synthesized and transfer scenarios.
- **Quantitative Results (OakInk):** V2P-Manip demonstrated an overall Success Rate (SR) of 0.483, outperforming ManipTrans (0.417). It also exhibited lower relative rotation error (E_rR) and translation error (E_rT) for the right hand, and lower translation error for the left hand, while the left hand's relative rotation error was slightly higher. These results indicate that the integration of high-quality trajectory priors and structured exploration facilitates policy learning in complex manipulation tasks.

#### 4.3. Ablation Study

- **Contribution of Geometric (C1) and Physical (C2) Constraints:** An ablation study on XHand across eight synthetic manipulation tasks assessed the individual contributions of the Geometric Constraint (C1) and Physical Constraint (C2).
 - **Without C1:** Removing global pose alignment and hand-object proximity priors resulted in a 0.0% average success rate across all tasks, indicating that most tasks failed to initialize.
 - **Without C2:** Excluding fine-grained contact refinement and force-based optimization led to a significant performance degradation, with an average success rate of 54.4%. This demonstrated the importance of C2 for refining complex interactions, especially in high-precision scenarios (e.g., Hammering at 7% vs. 51% with C2, Using Toothbrush at 14% vs. 33% with C2).
 - **Full V2P Framework:** The complete V2P framework achieved the highest average success rate of 79.3%, confirming the synergistic benefit of geometric alignment and physical optimization for robust dexterous manipulation.

#### 4.4. Generalization and Visualization Results

- **Multi-Embodiment Adaptation:** The framework's adaptability was evaluated across five robotic platforms (XHand, Allegro, Arti-MANO, Inspire, Shadow hands). The extracted motion priors were successfully retargeted and optimized for diverse kinematic structures.
 - **Performance Variance:** Arti-MANO achieved the highest average success rate (82.4%), attributed to its anthropomorphic design closely aligning with the human MANO model. Inspire Hand followed closely at 78.8%.
 - **Challenges:** Allegro Hand (52.0%) and Shadow Hand (61.6%) showed lower average success rates. The high-dimensional configurations of Shadow and Allegro hands increase action space complexity, while Allegro Hand's bulky scale and thicker fingertips presented geometric constraints, leading to self-collisions or mesh penetrations with fine-grained objects and triggering early termination in simulation.
- **Egocentric Video Processing:** The framework successfully extracted robust manipulation trajectories from raw, first-person egocentric videos, demonstrating its efficacy in converting passive observations into executable robotic priors.
- **Real-Robot Trajectory Execution:** Trajectories recovered from monocular human videos were successfully deployed on a real dual-arm UR + XHAND platform. After transforming floating-base formulations into executable arm-hand trajectories via inverse kinematics and verifying feasibility in physics-based simulation, the trajectories enabled the real robot to perform a diverse set of bimanual manipulation tasks, including pouring water, spraying flowers, and lifting/placing objects. This validated the transferability of video-derived behaviors to physical hardware.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The V2P-Manip framework offers a unified pipeline for converting unconstrained monocular human videos into physically feasible dexterous manipulation trajectories. This work contributes to the field of embodied AI and robotics in several ways:

1. **Increased Accessibility and Scalability of Data:** By integrating reconstruction and refinement modules, the approach eliminates the need for specialized tracking hardware or motion-capture suites. This reduces the barrier to entry for large-scale data acquisition, allowing researchers and practitioners to leverage abundant internet video resources for robotic skill learning, thus improving scalability and diversity of acquired skills.

2. **Enhanced Trajectory Quality:** The hierarchical, two-stage refinement process—encompassing geometric alignment and physical consistency—is crucial for bridging the gap between noisy visual perception and physically plausible robotic actions. This rigorous optimization addresses critical issues like spatial inconsistencies and interpenetration, providing higher quality demonstrations for downstream policy learning.

3. **Robust Policy Learning:** The combination of physically grounded trajectories with a residual reinforcement learning scheme enables efficient policy training for complex, contact-rich dexterous manipulation tasks. The base model provides a kinematic foundation, while the residual policy refines it into a dynamically stable grasp, improving exploration efficiency and training convergence. The use of domain randomization further enhances policy robustness.

4. **Generalization Across Embodiments:** The framework demonstrates the adaptability of extracted motion priors across diverse dexterous hand embodiments, despite variations in kinematics, link dimensions, and fingertip geometries. This suggests that the learned skills are not overly specific to a single robotic hand, promoting broader applicability.

5. **Bridging Simulation and Reality:** The successful deployment of video-derived trajectories on a real dual-arm UR + XHAND platform validates the framework's capability to bridge the gap between monocular human video demonstrations and real-world dexterous manipulation. This provides a tangible path for translating human expertise from videos into robot actions.

**Limitations and Future Directions:**
Despite these advancements, the framework currently has limitations. It is tailored for rigid-body objects, with articulated or deformable entities remaining unexplored due to their high degrees of freedom and complex contact physics. The simulation also enforces a floating-base assumption, omitting specific robot arm morphologies and requiring external inverse kinematics solvers for joint reachability. Future work is proposed to directly incorporate full-body kinematic and dynamic constraints into the policy learning pipeline to ensure end-to-end trajectory feasibility. Furthermore, scaling the framework to full arm-hand robot embodiments and extending it to articulated and deformable objects are identified as vital next steps. The authors also note occasional failures when handling extremely thin or flat objects due to monocular depth estimation inaccuracies from egocentric viewpoints.

Overall, V2P-Manip represents a step towards making dexterous robot manipulation learning more accessible and scalable by effectively utilizing readily available human video demonstrations, thereby contributing to the development of embodied intelligence.
