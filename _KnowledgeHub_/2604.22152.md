# dWorldEval: Scalable Robotic Policy Evaluation via Discrete Diffusion World Model

> [!summary] Summary
> dWorldEval introduces a discrete diffusion world model for scalable robotic policy evaluation, which enhances action controllability and spatiotemporal consistency by treating actions as primary tokens. The model demonstrates high correlation with real-world policy performance across diverse tasks and platforms, allowing for reliable and automated assessment of robotic policies.

## Problem

- Evaluating generalist robot policies faces limitations due to the high cost and time of real-world execution, and the asset-heavy nature of physics-based simulators.
- Existing generative world models for robotics struggle with accurately reflecting robot actions (leading to hallucinated successes) and maintaining spatiotemporal consistency over long prediction horizons.
- Prior world model architectures, often adapted from video generation backbones, treat actions as auxiliary conditions, allowing dominant visual priors to override control signals.

## Method

- dWorldEval develops a discrete diffusion world model that unifies all modalities (vision, language, actions) into a single token sequence, treating actions as primary inputs for fine-grained control.
- It incorporates a sparse keyframe memory mechanism to preserve spatiotemporal consistency and mitigate drift over extended imagined rollouts.
- A discrete progress token is jointly predicted with visual outcomes to enable automatic, intrinsic, and reliable detection of task success or failure directly within the world model's output.

## Results

- dWorldEval achieved robust action controllability, faithfully reproducing failure states from suboptimal actions and maintaining consistent Δ\DeltaΔ-LPIPS scores (e.g., 0.315 for success, 0.352 for failure) on LIBERO tasks.
- It demonstrated superior spatiotemporal consistency, exhibiting an LPIPS error of 0.243 at a 20-step horizon in round-trip consistency tests, outperforming baseline models that suffered from significant drift.
- The model served as a reliable policy evaluation proxy, achieving high Pearson correlation coefficients (e.g., ~0.9 to 0.92 across LIBERO, RoboTwin, and real-world setups) and low Mean Multi-Rank Violation, accurately ranking diverse robot policies against real-world performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating actions as primary tokens within a unified discrete diffusion framework, trained from scratch on robotic data, significantly improves action controllability and prevents the hallucination of successes often seen in models adapting video generation architectures.
> - The use of sparse keyframe memory is crucial for maintaining spatiotemporal consistency and bounding cumulative visual drift over long imagined horizons in robotic world models.
> - Jointly predicting a discrete progress token alongside visual outcomes provides an effective and automatic mechanism for task success detection, aligning assessment directly with the generated visual trajectory.
