# dWorldEval: Scalable Robotic Policy Evaluation via Discrete Diffusion World Model

> [!summary] Summary
> dWorldEval introduces a discrete diffusion world model for scalable robotic policy evaluation, which enhances action controllability and spatiotemporal consistency by treating actions as primary tokens. The model demonstrates high correlation with real-world policy performance across diverse tasks and platforms, allowing for reliable and automated assessment of robotic policies.

## Problem

- Evaluating generalist robot policies faces limitations due to the high cost and time of real-world execution, and the asset-heavy nature of physics-based simulators.
- Existing generative world models for robotics struggle with accurately reflecting robot actions (leading to hallucinated successes) and maintaining spatiotemporal consistency over long prediction horizons.
- Prior world model architectures, often adapted from video generation backbones, treat actions as auxiliary conditions, allowing dominant visual priors to override control signals.

## Method

- dWorldEval develops a discrete diffusion world model that unifies all modalities (vision, language, actions) into a single token sequence, treating actions as primary inputs for fine-grained control.
- It incorporates a sparse keyframe memory mechanism to preserve spatiotemporal consistency and mitigate drift over extended imagined rollouts.
- A discrete progress token is jointly predicted with visual outcomes to enable automatic, intrinsic, and reliable detection of task success or failure directly within the world model's output.

## Results

- dWorldEval achieved robust action controllability, faithfully reproducing failure states from suboptimal actions and maintaining consistent Δ-LPIPS scores (e.g., 0.315 for success, 0.352 for failure) on LIBERO tasks.
- It demonstrated superior spatiotemporal consistency, exhibiting an LPIPS error of 0.243 at a 20-step horizon in round-trip consistency tests, outperforming baseline models that suffered from significant drift.
- The model served as a reliable policy evaluation proxy, achieving high Pearson correlation coefficients (e.g., ~0.9 to 0.92 across LIBERO, RoboTwin, and real-world setups) and low Mean Multi-Rank Violation, accurately ranking diverse robot policies against real-world performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating actions as primary tokens within a unified discrete diffusion framework, trained from scratch on robotic data, significantly improves action controllability and prevents the hallucination of successes often seen in models adapting video generation architectures.
> - The use of sparse keyframe memory is crucial for maintaining spatiotemporal consistency and bounding cumulative visual drift over long imagined horizons in robotic world models.
> - Jointly predicting a discrete progress token alongside visual outcomes provides an effective and automatic mechanism for task success detection, aligning assessment directly with the generated visual trajectory.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotics is experiencing rapid advancements in generalist robot manipulation policies. However, evaluating the capabilities of these policies remains a challenge. Traditional evaluation methods, such as real-world execution, are resource-intensive, costly, and time-consuming, making it infeasible to assess policies across a large number of environments and tasks. Physics-based simulators offer an alternative but are often asset-heavy and require significant manual effort for setup and maintenance.

Generative world models have emerged as a data-driven paradigm to address the scalability issues of policy evaluation. These models aim to predict future states based on current observations and actions, serving as a proxy for real-world or simulated environments. However, existing world models for robotics evaluation have faced limitations that hinder their reliability. A primary issue is their difficulty in accurately reflecting robot actions and physical interactions. This often manifests as a failure to generalize to out-of-distribution (OOD) actions, leading to hallucinated successful outcomes when suboptimal actions are executed. Furthermore, these models frequently exhibit physical inconsistencies, such as visual warping of objects or their outright disappearance, due to spatiotemporal drift over long horizons.

The authors attribute these failures primarily to architectural design choices. Many existing approaches adapt backbones originally developed for video generation (e.g., image-to-video models). In these architectures, robotic actions are typically injected as auxiliary conditions (e.g., via cross-attention or adaptive modulation), rather than being treated as fundamental inputs. Since these backbones are often heavily pre-trained on large video datasets, they inherit strong visual priors. Consequently, the action signals often act as weak guidance, frequently overridden by these dominant visual priors, which can lead to hallucinated success or temporal inconsistencies.

Against this backdrop, dWorldEval is presented as an alternative architectural paradigm. It aims to overcome the limitations of current world models by designing a system specifically for robotics evaluation. The work fits into the landscape by proposing a discrete diffusion world model trained from scratch on robotic data, where actions and visual observations are treated as equivalent tokens. This design choice is intended to enhance action controllability and spatiotemporal consistency, thereby improving the reliability of world models as policy evaluation proxies and enabling more scalable assessment of robotic policies. It distinguishes itself from prior world models like WorldEval, WorldGym, and Ctrl-World by integrating actions as primary tokens rather than auxiliary conditions and from discrete diffusion models focused on policy learning by building a world model for prediction and evaluation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a scalable and reliable methodology for evaluating robotics policies. The motivation stems from the inherent impracticality of existing evaluation approaches when dealing with a large number of policies, environments, and tasks.

The key motivations and detailed objectives are as follows:

- **Address Scalability Limitations:** The overarching motivation is that evaluating robotics policies across thousands of environments and tasks is infeasible with current methods. Real-world rollouts are expensive and time-consuming, while physics-based simulators are asset-heavy. Existing world models have not yet provided a sufficiently reliable alternative.
- **Enhance Action Controllability:** A core challenge with current world models is their failure to accurately reflect robot actions, particularly out-of-distribution (OOD) or erroneous ones. This leads to models hallucinating successful outcomes rather than faithfully simulating failures. The objective is to design a world model where predictions precisely reflect the visual changes induced by the input actions, ensuring that generated failures correspond to suboptimal actions.
- **Improve Spatiotemporal Consistency:** Existing models often exhibit physical inconsistencies, such as objects visually warping or vanishing, due to accumulated errors over long imagined horizons. This drift renders long-horizon rollouts unreliable for evaluation. The objective is to develop mechanisms that preserve the spatial layout and visual consistency across extended simulated trajectories.
- **Overcome Architectural Bottlenecks:** The authors hypothesize that the root cause of these issues is architectural. Current world models often adapt video generation architectures where robot actions are secondary inputs, allowing strong visual priors to override control signals. The objective is to propose a new architecture where actions are treated as first-class citizens, equally weighted with visual observations and language instructions, to ensure that control signals directly drive the generated states.
- **Enable Automatic and Reliable Success Detection:** Prior methods often decouple success detection from visual generation, which introduces additional complexity and potential inconsistencies. The objective is to integrate task completion assessment directly into the world model's output, allowing for automatic, intrinsic, and reliable determination of task success or failure based on jointly predicted visual outcomes and progress scores.
- **Establish a Reliable Proxy for Policy Ranking:** Ultimately, the system needs to serve as an accurate proxy for assessing diverse robot policies. The objective is to ensure that the performance estimated via closed-loop rollouts in the world model strongly correlates with actual real-world execution performance and can accurately rank policies across different architectures and training stages.
- **Develop a Diagnostic Metric for Action Controllability:** To objectively quantify the model's ability to adhere to control signals, a specific metric is needed. The objective is to introduce and validate a metric, such as $\Delta$-LPIPS, that measures the perceptual fidelity of state *transitions* induced by actions, rather than just static appearance.

By addressing these objectives, dWorldEval aims to provide a robust, scalable, and reliable tool for robotic policy evaluation, thereby accelerating research and development in robot learning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

dWorldEval proposes a discrete diffusion world model designed for scalable robotics policy evaluation. Its methodology centers on integrating all relevant modalities into a unified token space and employing specific architectural components to ensure action controllability, spatiotemporal consistency, and automatic success detection.

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation

The world model $W_\theta$ is formulated to predict a future visual outcome $\hat{o}_{t+\Delta}$ and a task progress score $\hat{v}_{t+\Delta} \in$ at a given horizon $\Delta$. This prediction is conditioned on a language instruction $l$, a current observation $o_t$, a history $h_t$, and a sequence of future actions $a_t$. The model approximates the joint distribution $({\hat o}_{t+\Delta}, {\hat v}_{t+\Delta}) \sim W_\theta(\cdot | o_t, a_t, h_t, l)$. For reliability, $W_\theta$ must exhibit:
1. **Action Controllability:** Predictions must accurately reflect action-induced visual changes.
2. **Spatiotemporal Consistency:** Spatial layout and consistency must be maintained over long horizons.
3. **Discriminative Task Completion:** Generated states must allow unambiguous success detection.

Policy evaluation is performed via imagined rollouts, where a policy $\pi$ interacts in a closed loop with $W_\theta$ to generate a trajectory $\tau$. Performance is measured by an imagined success rate $J = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^{N} S(\tau_i)$, where $S(\cdot)$ is a binary success indicator.

#### 3.2. World Modeling via Discrete Diffusion: Action Control via Unified Token Sequence

To ensure action controllability, dWorldEval integrates actions as primary tokens within a unified discrete token space.
- **Tokenization:** Heterogeneous modalities are mapped into discrete codes using specialized tokenizers:
 - **RGB Observations:** MAGVIT-v2
 - **Language Instructions:** LLaDA
 - **Continuous Action Chunks $a_t$**: FAST
- **Unified Sequence:** These discrete codes are serialized into a single flattened sequence. A transformer-based denoising network then models the joint distribution of actions, visual observations, and language.
- **Self-Attention:** Within this unified sequence, self-attention mechanisms enable each visual token to directly attend to action tokens. This fine-grained control at the token level is designed to ensure that generated visual states accurately reflect the input actions. Unlike prior work, dWorldEval is trained from scratch on robotic data, preventing pre-trained visual priors from overriding action signals.

#### 3.2.2. Sparse Keyframe Memory for Spatiotemporal Consistency

To mitigate long-horizon drift and maintain spatiotemporal consistency, a sparse keyframe memory is employed.
- **Memory Update:** The memory is updated using a sliding window that samples the $K$ most recent frames (where $K=4$ in experiments) at a fixed stride $\Delta$, aligning with the action chunk size.
- **Context Integration:** These sampled keyframes are tokenized and concatenated with the current observation, language, and action tokens.
- **Temporal Encoding:** Absolute frame indices are encoded as text tokens and prepended to the corresponding history keyframes to explicitly preserve temporal order.
- **Efficiency:** History frames are encoded at a reduced resolution (128x128) and use only a fixed global view (e.g., top-down) to optimize computational efficiency and reduce token usage. The current observation, however, maintains all views at full resolution (256x256) to capture fine-grained interactions.

#### 3.2.3. Discrete Progress Token for Automatic Success Detection

dWorldEval integrates success detection directly into the generation process by jointly predicting visual outcomes and a discrete progress score.
- **Progress Token Definition:** A discrete progress token is introduced to quantify task completion, ranging from 0 to 1.
- **Training Data Generation:** During training, task-specific milestones are defined. An off-the-shelf Vision-Language Model (VLM), SEED-1.5VL, is prompted with few-shot examples and detailed scoring rules to estimate task completion progress from visual observations. The continuous scores are then converted into discrete tokens (e.g., "1.0") and appended to the target sequence.
- **Inference:** At inference, the generated text tokens for progress are decoded into a numeric score $\hat{v}_{t+\Delta}$. Task success is automatically determined when this terminal score equals 1. This unified approach aims to align the predicted score with the generated visual content.

#### 3.3. Joint Visual-and-Progress Denoising

The model employs Masked Discrete Diffusion (MDD) to learn the transition distribution $p(y_{t+\Delta} | c_t)$.
- **Masking Process:** During training, a diffusion level $\lambda \sim U(0, 1)$ is sampled, and the target $y_{t+\Delta}$ is corrupted via a mask-based forward process $\tilde{y}_{t+\Delta} \sim q_\lambda(\tilde{y} | y_{t+\Delta})$. The context $c_t$ remains unmasked.
- **Optimization Objective:** The model is optimized to reconstruct the clean tokens at the masked indices $\Omega_\lambda$ by minimizing a weighted objective:
 $L_{WM} = \mathbb{E}_{\tau, \lambda, \tilde{y}} \left[ -\frac{1}{m(\lambda)} \sum_{j \in \Omega_\lambda} w_j \log p_\theta(y_j | c_t, \tilde{y}_{t+\Delta}, \lambda) \right]$
 where $m(\lambda)$ is the masking probability, and $w_j$ are modality-specific rebalancing weights ($w_j=2$ for progress tokens, $w_j=1$ for visual tokens).
- **Inference:** Iterative parallel decoding is used to sample $y_{t+\Delta}$ conditioned on the unified context $c_t$, simultaneously generating the future visual state and its progress score.

#### 3.4. Experimental Setup

- **Platforms and Data:** Evaluations are conducted on:
 - LIBERO: LIBERO-Object, LIBERO-Spatial, LIBERO-Goal, LIBERO-100 suites. Augmented with 1k failed rollouts in addition to 5.5k expert demonstrations.
 - RoboTwin: ARX arm configuration for contact-rich tableware manipulation (5.5k trajectories across 10 tasks).
 - Real-world Setup: Bimanual AgileX system with three synchronized RealSense cameras (5.2k trajectories, including 1k human-collected failures, across five tasks: Bussing Table, Place Cup, Handover Block, Strike Block, Dual Bottle Pick).
- **Baselines:** WorldEval, WorldGym, and Ctrl-World are used for comparison, with identical training data splits.
- **Target Policies:** Evaluated on checkpoints of a base policy ($\pi_0$) on LIBERO, and heterogeneous policies (e.g., DexVLA, Diffusion Policy) on RoboTwin and real-world.
- **Implementation Details:** Multi-view outcomes at 256x256 resolution. Sparse history of K=4 keyframes (128x128). Prediction horizon $\Delta$ from. Loss weights: $w_j=2$ for progress, $w_j=1$ for visual. 16-step iterative parallel decoding.
- **Evaluation Metric:** A new metric, $\Delta$-LPIPS, is introduced to measure action controllability. It quantifies the perceptual fidelity of state transitions by computing LPIPS between difference images ($\Delta o_t = o_{t+\Delta} - o_t$).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of dWorldEval addresses several research questions, demonstrating its capabilities as a robotic policy evaluator.

#### 4.1. Action Controllability (RQ1)

- **Quantitative Performance:** dWorldEval demonstrated robust action controllability. In experiments on LIBERO, quantified by the proposed $\Delta$-LPIPS metric, dWorldEval maintained consistent performance on both expert success ($D_{succ}$) and suboptimal failure ($D_{fail}$) subsets (0.315 and 0.352, respectively). In contrast, baseline methods like WorldGym showed significant degradation on failure data, with $\Delta$-LPIPS increasing from 0.347 to 0.650, indicating a failure to adhere to incorrect actions.
- **Qualitative Fidelity:** Visualizations confirmed that dWorldEval faithfully reproduced failure states resulting from suboptimal inputs, such as a missed grasp. Baseline models, however, frequently exhibited hallucinated successes or generated non-existent objects, failing to align with the input action sequences.
- **Causal Dependency Validation:** Action shuffling tests demonstrated that randomly corrupting action sequences led to a substantial increase in $\Delta$-LPIPS and a drop in policy ranking correlation, confirming that dWorldEval's dynamics are genuinely sensitive to and dependent on the input actions.

#### 4.2. Spatiotemporal Consistency (RQ2)

- **Long-Horizon Stability:** A variable-horizon round-trip protocol, which involved applying forward actions followed by inverse actions, was used to assess spatiotemporal consistency. The full dWorldEval model, incorporating sparse keyframe memory, effectively preserved consistency. At a horizon of H=20, it maintained an LPIPS error of 0.243 between the initial and final states.
- **Impact of Memory:** An ablation study showed that without the sparse keyframe memory, the model suffered from significant cumulative drift, with LPIPS errors increasing progressively up to 0.411 at H=20. This indicates the memory's role in bounding long-term drift.
- **Comparison with Baselines:** Compared to WorldEval, WorldGym, and Ctrl-World, dWorldEval consistently exhibited superior spatiotemporal consistency. Baselines showed significantly higher round-trip LPIPS errors (e.g., 0.531 for WorldEval at H=20), indicating severe inconsistency attributed to both spatiotemporal drift and insufficient action controllability.

#### 4.3. Automatic Success Detection (RQ3)

- **Discriminative Progress Token:** The discrete progress token, jointly predicted with visual outcomes, was found to be discriminative. It exhibited sharp transitions corresponding to task completion milestones.
- **Alignment with Real Execution:** The automated success rate estimates derived from the predicted progress token (Auto (imag.)) closely tracked real execution success rates (Real) across multiple checkpoints of a base policy on LIBERO suites. This automated metric also closely matched human judgment (Human (imag.)) of generated rollouts. The system could capture even non-monotonic fluctuations in policy performance, confirming that the progress score reflects actual visual changes.

#### 4.4. Reliable Proxy for Policy Evaluation (RQ4)

- **Policy Ranking Accuracy:** dWorldEval demonstrated high reliability as a proxy for policy evaluation, achieving strong Pearson correlation coefficients (r) and low Mean Multi-Rank Violation (MMRV) between imagined and real success rates.
- **Comparison with Baselines:** On LIBERO tasks (single-view), dWorldEval achieved a correlation of approximately 0.9 with an MMRV of 0.013. This performance was superior to existing baselines (WorldGym, WorldEval, Ctrl-World), which exhibited weaker correlations and higher rank violations (MMRV up to 0.039) due to their limited action controllability.
- **Robustness Across Domains:** The model's reliability extended to diverse settings:
 - LIBERO (Multi-view): r = 0.910.
 - RoboTwin with heterogeneous policies: r = 0.927.
 - Real-world tasks: r = 0.918.
 - The ablation without history showed a notable drop in correlation (to 0.786 in multi-view settings), further emphasizing the importance of spatiotemporal consistency for policy ranking.

#### 4.5. $\Delta$-LPIPS as Diagnostic Indicator (RQ5)

- The action shuffling experiments empirically validated $\Delta$-LPIPS as a diagnostic metric. As the probability of action shuffling increased, $\Delta$-LPIPS scores worsened, and simultaneously, the Pearson correlation between estimated and real success rates significantly dropped. This confirmed that higher $\Delta$-LPIPS values correlate with degraded action controllability and unreliable policy evaluation.

#### 4.6. Universal Fidelity

- dWorldEval maintained consistently low $\Delta$-LPIPS scores (approximately 0.31-0.36) across a wide range of platforms (LIBERO, RoboTwin, Real-Robot) and synchronized viewpoints (3rd-Person, Wrist, Top-Down). This consistency, even in real-world scenarios, suggests that the unified tokenization approach is robust to domain gaps.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The dWorldEval research addresses a critical need in the advancement of robotic systems by offering a method for scalable and reliable policy evaluation. Its significance and potential impact can be understood through several points:

- **Scalable Policy Evaluation:** By providing a data-driven world model that can simulate robot-environment interactions faithfully, dWorldEval offers a viable alternative to the costly and time-consuming processes of real-world robot execution and the asset-heavy nature of traditional physics-based simulators. This enables the evaluation of robot policies across a significantly larger number of environments and tasks than previously practical.
- **Enhanced Reliability of World Models:** The work highlights and addresses key limitations of prior world models, specifically their lack of action controllability and spatiotemporal inconsistency. By integrating actions as primary tokens within a unified sequence and employing sparse keyframe memory, dWorldEval produces more faithful predictions, including the correct rendering of failure states rather than hallucinating success. This improvement in fidelity is crucial for a world model to serve as a trustworthy evaluation proxy.
- **Architectural Paradigm Shift:** The proposed architectural approach, which treats all modalities (vision, language, actions) equivalently within a discrete diffusion framework trained from scratch on robotic data, represents a departure from adapting pre-trained video generation models. This design ensures that action signals are not overridden by dominant visual priors, potentially setting a new standard for how world models for robotics are constructed.
- **Automated and Intrinsic Success Detection:** The introduction of a discrete progress token and its joint prediction with visual outcomes streamlines the evaluation process. This enables automatic and intrinsic determination of task success, eliminating the need for external reward functions or manual human grading, which reduces overhead and potential inconsistencies.
- **Accurate Policy Ranking:** The empirical results demonstrate that dWorldEval's estimated success rates correlate strongly with actual real-world execution performance. This capability to accurately rank diverse robot policies across different architectures and training stages is vital for accelerating research and development in robot learning, allowing researchers to efficiently compare and iterate on policy designs.
- **Diagnostic Evaluation Tools:** The introduction and validation of the $\Delta$-LPIPS metric provide a specific diagnostic tool for quantifying action controllability. Such metrics are important for identifying model weaknesses and guiding further improvements in world model design.
- **Broad Applicability:** The method's demonstrated performance across various platforms (LIBERO, RoboTwin, and real-world setups) and viewpoints suggests its robustness and generalizability, indicating that it could be widely applicable to diverse robotic manipulation tasks and environments.

In summary, dWorldEval contributes to making generalist robot policy development more efficient and effective by providing a reliable and scalable evaluation infrastructure. Its advancements in world model design could have a lasting impact on how robotic policies are benchmarked, iterated, and ultimately deployed.
