# Verifier-free Test-Time Sampling for Vision Language Action Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from KAIST and Seoul National University developed MG-Select, a verifier-free test-time sampling framework that improves the precision of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models in robotic control tasks. The method leverages condition-masking distributional confidence and joint training to enhance task success rates, achieving a 168% relative improvement in low-data pick-and-place tasks and a 28% gain in real-world scenarios.

## Problem

- Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, relying on greedy decoding, exhibit a 'precision gap' that limits their effectiveness in robotic tasks requiring fine-grained object manipulation or accurate placement.
- Existing Test-Time Scaling (TTS) approaches for robotics employ external verifiers (value functions or reward models), which require additional training, increase complexity, and often show poor generalization to novel conditions.
- External verifiers struggle to generalize robustly to unseen tasks, objects, or environmental layouts, as their reward modeling is typically tailored to specific datasets.

## Method

- Introduces **MG-Select**, a ==test-time scaling== framework that samples N candidate actions and selects the best by maximizing a novel, verifier-free confidence criterion.
- Proposes a ==condition-masking distributional confidence== metric, computed as the ==KL divergence== between the VLA's predicted action distribution and an 'uncertainty-aware' reference distribution generated by masking VLA input conditions.
- Develops an optional ==joint training== strategy that exposes the VLA to masked inputs during fine-tuning, enabling it to learn meaningful condition-masking distributions, alongside an efficient ==single-prefill== deployment method to reduce inference latency.

## Results

- MG-Select* (with joint training) achieved a **168%** relative improvement in success rate for π₀-FAST on RoboCasa low-data pick-and-place tasks and a **28%** relative gain in average success rate on real-world Franka robot tasks.
- Demonstrated enhanced zero-shot generalization, yielding a **35%** improvement in average success rate on out-of-distribution (unseen) objects in real-world robot experiments.
- The efficient single-prefill deployment strategy reduced inference latency by **45%** for N=4 samples, making MG-Select's inference time comparable to that of single-action inference.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Internal model signals, specifically the KL divergence from an uncertainty-aware, condition-masked reference distribution, can effectively serve as a robust and verifier-free confidence measure for selecting optimal actions in VLAs.
> - Regularizing the condition-masking distribution with a high temperature is crucial for it to accurately represent broad uncertainty, thereby providing a meaningful baseline for KL divergence-based confidence scoring.
> - Jointly training VLAs with masked inputs during fine-tuning allows the model to learn both conditional and unconditional distributions, resulting in more reliable and well-behaved reference distributions for confidence estimation without impacting base performance.
