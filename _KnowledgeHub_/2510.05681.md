# Verifier-free Test-Time Sampling for Vision Language Action Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from KAIST and Seoul National University developed MG-Select, a verifier-free test-time sampling framework that improves the precision of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models in robotic control tasks. The method leverages condition-masking distributional confidence and joint training to enhance task success rates, achieving a 168% relative improvement in low-data pick-and-place tasks and a 28% gain in real-world scenarios.

## Problem

- Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, relying on greedy decoding, exhibit a 'precision gap' that limits their effectiveness in robotic tasks requiring fine-grained object manipulation or accurate placement.
- Existing Test-Time Scaling (TTS) approaches for robotics employ external verifiers (value functions or reward models), which require additional training, increase complexity, and often show poor generalization to novel conditions.
- External verifiers struggle to generalize robustly to unseen tasks, objects, or environmental layouts, as their reward modeling is typically tailored to specific datasets.

## Method

- Introduces **MG-Select**, a ==test-time scaling== framework that samples N candidate actions and selects the best by maximizing a novel, verifier-free confidence criterion.
- Proposes a ==condition-masking distributional confidence== metric, computed as the ==KL divergence== between the VLA's predicted action distribution and an 'uncertainty-aware' reference distribution generated by masking VLA input conditions.
- Develops an optional ==joint training== strategy that exposes the VLA to masked inputs during fine-tuning, enabling it to learn meaningful condition-masking distributions, alongside an efficient ==single-prefill== deployment method to reduce inference latency.

## Results

- MG-Select* (with joint training) achieved a **168%** relative improvement in success rate for π₀-FAST on RoboCasa low-data pick-and-place tasks and a **28%** relative gain in average success rate on real-world Franka robot tasks.
- Demonstrated enhanced zero-shot generalization, yielding a **35%** improvement in average success rate on out-of-distribution (unseen) objects in real-world robot experiments.
- The efficient single-prefill deployment strategy reduced inference latency by **45%** for N=4 samples, making MG-Select's inference time comparable to that of single-action inference.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Internal model signals, specifically the KL divergence from an uncertainty-aware, condition-masked reference distribution, can effectively serve as a robust and verifier-free confidence measure for selecting optimal actions in VLAs.
> - Regularizing the condition-masking distribution with a high temperature is crucial for it to accurately represent broad uncertainty, thereby providing a meaningful baseline for KL divergence-based confidence scoring.
> - Jointly training VLAs with masked inputs during fine-tuning allows the model to learn both conditional and unconditional distributions, resulting in more reliable and well-behaved reference distributions for confidence estimation without impacting base performance.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic control has seen remarkable advancements with the advent of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. These models, trained on vast datasets of robotic demonstrations, leverage principles from large language models (LLMs) and vision-language models (VLMs) to enable robots to understand high-level natural language instructions and translate them into physical actions. Autoregressive VLAs, in particular, have emerged as a dominant paradigm, offering a flexible and powerful framework for sequential decision-making in complex environments.

Despite their impressive capabilities, VLAs face a critical limitation: their inherent "single-inference paradigm," which typically relies on greedy decoding (selecting the highest probability action at each step). While effective for many tasks, this greedy approach often falls short in scenarios demanding high precision, such as fine-grained object manipulation, grasping, or accurate placement. In real-world robotic applications, where millimeter-level accuracy is often paramount for task success or failure, this precision gap becomes a significant bottleneck.

Previous research has attempted to bridge this gap by adopting "Test-Time Scaling (TTS)" approaches, inspired by their success in improving reasoning capabilities in LLMs. These methods typically involve generating multiple candidate actions through repeated stochastic sampling and then selecting the "best" action using an *external verifier*. Such verifiers often take the form of value functions trained with reinforcement learning (RL) objectives on extensive robotic datasets (e.g., Nakamoto et al., 2024; Kwok et al., 2025).

However, these verifier-based TTS approaches introduce significant drawbacks. Firstly, they necessitate *additional training* to learn the verifier, which adds substantial computational overhead, complexity, and time to the model deployment pipeline. This goes against the goal of streamlined, efficient robot control. Secondly, these external verifiers often struggle with *generalization*, failing to perform reliably in unseen conditions, such as novel task instructions, unfamiliar objects, or different environmental layouts. Their reward modeling is frequently tailored to specific datasets, severely limiting their broader applicability and robustness.

This paper steps into this landscape by proposing a novel, *verifier-free* test-time scaling framework called MG-Select. It addresses the core limitations of previous TTS methods by eliminating the need for external verifiers and additional training. Instead, MG-Select ingeniously leverages the *internal properties* of the VLA itself to generate confidence signals for action selection. This approach offers a significant advancement, aiming to provide precision improvements that are more practical, generalizable, and less computationally demanding, thereby pushing VLAs closer to reliable deployment in real-world, high-precision robotic tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to develop an effective and efficient test-time scaling framework for Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models that significantly enhances their precision in robot control tasks, particularly those requiring fine-grained manipulation. The authors are primarily motivated by the recognition that despite the remarkable performance of state-of-the-art VLAs, their reliance on a single-inference (greedy decoding) paradigm fundamentally limits their ability to achieve the high accuracy necessary for many real-world applications.

The specific motivations and objectives driving MG-Select are:

1. **To overcome the "precision gap" in VLAs:** The authors observe that even extensively pre-trained VLAs often fail in tasks like precise grasping or object placement. This gap is critical because even minor inaccuracies can lead to task failure in robotic applications. The goal is to enable VLAs to generate millimeter-level accurate actions.
2. **To eliminate the need for external verifiers:** Previous successful test-time scaling methods for robotics have relied on external value functions or reward models. The primary motivation here is to circumvent the associated problems: the substantial computational overhead of additional training, increased deployment complexity, and poor generalization of these verifiers to novel or out-of-distribution conditions.
3. **To leverage intrinsic model properties for confidence estimation:** Instead of external modules, the authors aim to tap into the VLA's internal representations and learned distributions. They hypothesize that the model itself can provide a reliable "self-certainty" measure, akin to recent advancements in LLM reasoning, to guide optimal action selection.
4. **To develop an uncertainty-aware confidence metric:** The authors note that simple likelihood-based selection from multiple samples is often ineffective because fine-tuned VLAs tend to produce overly concentrated probability distributions, leading to similar candidate actions. This motivates the development of a more sophisticated confidence metric that considers the *uncertainty* or *deviation* from a baseline of maximal uncertainty.
5. **To improve generalization and robustness:** A key objective is to create a framework that performs consistently across diverse tasks, environments, and most importantly, generalizes well to unseen objects and out-of-distribution scenarios, which is a common failing of verifier-based approaches.
6. **To ensure efficiency and practicality:** The framework must be deployable without significant increases in inference latency, which is crucial for real-time robot control. This includes developing optimized implementation strategies.

In essence, the motivation is to make high-precision robot control with VLAs more accessible, robust, and efficient by providing a novel, verifier-free test-time scaling solution that intelligently utilizes the model's own internal signals.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of this research is **Masking Distribution Guided Selection (MG-Select)**, a novel test-time scaling framework designed to enhance the precision of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models without requiring additional training or external modules. The methodology comprises three main components: a general test-time scaling framework, a unique condition-masking distributional confidence metric, and an optional joint training strategy to further improve the quality of confidence signals, along with an efficient deployment strategy.

#### 3.1. Test-Time Scaling Framework

MG-Select follows a standard two-stage test-time scaling paradigm:
1. **Sampling N Candidate Actions:** At each timestep, the autoregressive VLA (πθ) generates multiple diverse action sequences. This is achieved by stochastically sampling `N` candidate actions (denoted as ã(n)) in parallel. To promote diversity among the samples, a sampling temperature `τ > 0` is applied to the predicted action token distribution, `πθ(· | ot, qt, I, ã<j; τ)`. A higher temperature encourages more diverse (less greedy) samples.
2. **Best-of-N Selection:** From these `N` candidate actions, the framework selects the final action, `a*`, that maximizes a predefined criterion `M`. The choice of this criterion is crucial for the framework's effectiveness.

#### 3.2. Condition-Masking Distributional Confidence

This is the key innovation of MG-Select. The authors argue that simple likelihood-based selection is inadequate because VLAs fine-tuned on target tasks often produce overly concentrated probability distributions, leading to redundant samples. Instead, they propose a confidence metric based on the Kullback–Leibler (KL) divergence from an "uncertainty-aware" reference distribution.

- **Confidence over Action Token Distributions:** For each action token `a_i` in an action sequence, a token-level distributional confidence `C_i` is computed as `KL(Q_i || P_i)`. Here, `P_i` is the predicted distribution from the VLA (`πθ(· | ot, qt, I, a<i)`) given the full context, and `Q_i` is the novel *reference distribution*.
- **Action-Level Confidence:** These token-level confidences are aggregated across the action sequence to obtain a final action-level confidence score `C_a = Σi∈I C_i`, where `I` can be the full sequence or a subset of tokens, depending on the action tokenization scheme. The authors empirically found that aggregating over the *first 5 tokens* worked best for their primary VLA (`π₀-FAST`), suggesting early tokens are crucial for precision.
- **Condition-Masking Distribution (Q_i):** The core idea for constructing `Q_i` is to generate a distribution that represents maximum uncertainty while remaining aligned with the target task. This is achieved by using the *same VLA* (`πθ`) but with crucial input conditions randomly *masked* (replaced with a null token `∅`). This simulates "failure modes" where essential information is missing, providing a baseline of expected uncertainty. Three variants are explored:
 - **Text-masking:** `KL(πθ(· | ot, qt, ∅, a<i) || πθ(· | ot, qt, I, a<i))` (Masking the language instruction `I`)
 - **State-masking:** `KL(πθ(· | ot, ∅, I, a<i) || πθ(· | ot, qt, I, a<i))` (Masking the proprioceptive state `q_t`)
 - **Text&State-masking:** `KL(πθ(· | ot, ∅, ∅, a<i) || πθ(· | ot, qt, I, a<i))` (Masking both `I` and `q_t`)
The optimal masking variant is task-dependent; for instance, text-masking is effective when instructions are critical, while state-masking shines when visual observations are sufficient to imply the goal.
- **Regularization Temperature:** An important empirical finding is that applying a high temperature (e.g., `τ = 4.0`) to the condition-masking distribution itself is crucial. This regularization prevents `Q_i` from becoming overly "peaked" around certain actions, ensuring it effectively represents a broad uncertainty and provides a meaningful baseline for KL divergence, thereby improving confidence signals.

#### 3.3. Joint Training Strategy

To ensure that the VLA is prepared to generate meaningful condition-masking distributions, the authors propose a "joint training" strategy (resulting in **MG-Select***).
- **Problem:** Standard VLAs are not explicitly trained with masked inputs, so directly masking during inference might lead to nonsensical outputs.
- **Approach:** During fine-tuning on the target dataset, the VLA is trained with an augmented dataset (`D_augmented`) that includes both all-condition data and data where `q_t` or `I` (or both) are randomly dropped out (e.g., 10% dropout rate for each masking variant).
- **Loss Function:** The joint imitation learning loss `L_Joint-IL` optimizes the policy `πθ` to predict expert actions `a_t` given various input conditions, including the masked ones: `L_Joint-IL(θ; D) = -E_((ot,qt),a_t:t+H,I)~D [ E_((q(m)t,I(m)))∈M [ log πθ(at | ot, q(m)t, I(m)) ] ]`.
- **Benefit:** This strategy allows the VLA to learn both conditional and unconditional (masked) distributions simultaneously. It maintains performance comparable to standard fine-tuning while gaining "awareness" of condition-masking distributions, providing more reliable and well-behaved reference distributions for the confidence metric.

#### 3.4. Efficient Deployment Strategy (Single Prefill)

Recognizing that generating `N` action sequences in parallel can increase inference latency, especially for autoregressive models requiring repeated "prefill" steps, the authors introduce an optimized deployment strategy. This method shares a single prefill computation across all `N` candidates before parallel decoding, significantly reducing the computational overhead and making MG-Select's inference time comparable to that of single-action inference.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted on both simulated and real-world benchmarks consistently demonstrate the effectiveness and robustness of MG-Select in improving the precision and performance of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models.

#### 4.1. Simulation Experiments

MG-Select was evaluated across diverse robotic simulation environments, including RoboCasa, SIMPLER-WidowX, and LIBERO, using `π₀-FAST` and `OpenVLA` as base models.

- **RoboCasa (Nasiriany et al., 2024):** This benchmark focuses on 8 challenging pick-and-place tasks within household kitchen environments.
 - MG-Select consistently improved `π₀-FAST`'s performance across all tasks and demonstration scales (30, 100, and 300 demonstrations).
 - Even without joint training, MG-Select provided reliable precision gains.
 - With the joint training strategy (MG-Select*), the gains were further amplified. Notably, in the low-data regime (30 demonstrations), MG-Select* achieved a remarkable **168% relative improvement** in success rate on pick-and-place tasks over the base model, demonstrating its efficacy in compensating for limited data.
- **SIMPLER-WidowX (Li et al., 2024):** This benchmark evaluates precision in a real-to-sim transfer setting for 4 pick-and-place tasks.
 - MG-Select* improved `π₀-FAST`'s performance across all tasks, showcasing its robustness in enhancing action precision even when the base model struggles or faces domain shifts (e.g., "put eggplant in basket" task with varying backgrounds). The average success rate improved from 46.9% to 50.3%.
- **LIBERO (Liu et al., 2023):** This benchmark assesses generalization across variations in layout, objects, goals, and long-horizon tasks.
 - MG-Select* demonstrated superior average performance over both `π₀-FAST` and `OpenVLA`, confirming its general effectiveness and compatibility with different VLA architectures.
 - Significant gains were observed on LIBERO-Object and LIBERO-Long, which are particularly challenging task suites requiring sustained high-precision actions, highlighting the framework's capability in critical scenarios. `π₀-FAST` with MG-Select* achieved 93.1% average success vs. 92.0% for base.

#### 4.2. Real-World Experiments

To validate generalization beyond simulation, experiments were conducted on a 7-DoF Franka Research 3 robot arm using the `π₀-FAST-DROID` model.

- **In-Distribution (ID) Tasks:** Pick-and-place tasks with seen objects (teddy bear, cube, rigid cup, sponge) after multi-task training with 60 demonstrations per task.
 - MG-Select* outperformed the base model across all tasks, achieving a **28% relative gain** in average success rate (47.9% vs. 37.5%).
 - Qualitative results showed MG-Select generating high-precision actions at critical moments (grasping and releasing), where the base policy often failed.
- **Out-of-Distribution (OOD) Tasks:** Pick-and-place tasks involving unseen objects (lighter cup, roll of tape).
 - MG-Select (without joint training in this specific test) significantly enhanced the zero-shot generalization and precision of the policy, achieving a **35% improvement** in average success rate (71.9% vs. 53.1%). This indicates improved policy robustness on novel objects, especially those difficult to grasp like a roll of tape.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies and Analysis

Detailed ablations on RoboCasa and latency analysis on LIBERO-Object provided further insights:

- **Inference Strategy:** MG-Select (using condition-masking KL) achieved the strongest improvements compared to greedy decoding, simple sampling, likelihood-based Best-of-N, and uniform KL-based Best-of-N, confirming its superior uncertainty signal.
- **Number of Candidates (N):** Performance generally improved from N=1 to N=4, showing that a small number of samples is sufficient for meaningful gains.
- **Condition-Masking Variants:** Text-masking often performed best, while other variants remained competitive and outperformed uniform baselines, emphasizing the task-dependent nature of optimal masking.
- **Effect of Joint Training:** Joint training alone improved over vanilla imitation learning, and its combination with MG-Select yielded further gains, validating its role in generating reliable confidence signals.
- **Regularization Temperature:** Empirically, applying a high temperature (e.g., `τ=4.0`) to the condition-masking distribution was crucial for superior performance, regularizing its concentration to serve as an effective uncertainty reference.
- **Aggregation Strategy:** Summing token-level confidences over the *first 5 tokens* was found to be most effective for `π₀-FAST`, suggesting that the initial, most frequent action tokens are critical for task precision.
- **Efficient Deployment (Single Prefill):** This strategy achieved a **45% reduction in latency** for `N=4` compared to vanilla MG-Select, making the inference time comparable to single-action inference and addressing practical deployment concerns.

In summary, MG-Select consistently and significantly boosts VLA performance, particularly in high-precision tasks and low-data regimes, while maintaining efficiency and strong generalization.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The work presented in "VERIFIER-FREE TEST-TIME SAMPLING FOR VISION LANGUAGE ACTION MODELS" holds significant implications for the field of robot learning and the broader development of generalist robot policies.

Firstly, the most immediate and critical impact of MG-Select is its ability to **address the fundamental precision gap in Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models**. Current VLAs, while powerful, are inherently limited by their single-inference (greedy decoding) paradigm, leading to sub-optimal performance in tasks requiring fine-grained manipulation—a bottleneck for real-world robotic deployment. By enabling VLAs to execute high-precision actions consistently, MG-Select directly enhances the practical applicability of these models in domains such as manufacturing, delicate object handling, and service robotics, where millimeter-level accuracy is non-negotiable.

Secondly, the proposal of a **novel verifier-free test-time scaling (TTS) approach** marks a significant methodological advancement. Unlike prior TTS methods that rely on external verifiers, which demand additional training overhead, introduce complexity, and suffer from poor generalization, MG-Select leverages the *intrinsic properties* of the VLA itself. This eliminates the need for external modules and their associated computational costs and deployment challenges, making advanced precision-enhancing techniques more accessible and streamlined. This simplification could democratize the use of TTS in robotics, allowing more researchers and practitioners to benefit from its advantages without prohibitive resource requirements.

Thirdly, the research demonstrates that **internal model signals can serve as robust and reliable confidence measures**. By conceptualizing an "uncertainty-aware" reference distribution through condition-masking and quantifying confidence via KL divergence, the authors showcase an elegant way to extract meaningful self-certainty from pre-trained models. This insight could inspire future work in developing intrinsic evaluation metrics and self-correction mechanisms for foundation models, not just in robotics but potentially across other AI domains.

Fourthly, the **consistent and substantial performance gains** observed across diverse simulation benchmarks (RoboCasa, SIMPLER-WidowX, LIBERO) and, crucially, in real-world robot experiments underscore the practical efficacy of MG-Select. The improvements in low-data regimes and out-of-distribution scenarios are particularly impactful. Enhancing performance with limited demonstrations addresses a core challenge in robotics—the high cost of data collection—while improved generalization to unseen objects and conditions boosts the robustness and adaptability of VLA policies, making them more resilient to the inherent variability of real-world environments.

Finally, the **optimized deployment strategy** that utilizes a single prefill for parallel sampling directly tackles the practical concern of increased inference latency. By ensuring that MG-Select's computational overhead remains comparable to single-action inference, the method becomes viable for real-time robotic control, a critical requirement for safe and responsive robot operation.

In conclusion, MG-Select represents a significant step forward in making Vision-Language-Action models more reliable, precise, and practical for real-world robotic applications. By offering an efficient, verifier-free approach to enhance precision and generalization, this work not only pushes the boundaries of current VLA capabilities but also lays a strong foundation for future research into intrinsic confidence learning and robust, generalist robot policies.
