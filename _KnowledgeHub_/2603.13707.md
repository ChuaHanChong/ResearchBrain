# REFINE-DP: Diffusion Policy Fine-tuning for Humanoid Loco-manipulation via Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> The REFINE-DP framework from Georgia Institute of Technology presents a method for humanoid loco-manipulation that jointly fine-tunes a high-level diffusion policy planner and a low-level reinforcement learning controller. This approach achieves over 90% success rates in simulation for complex tasks and demonstrates improved data efficiency and motion quality.

## Problem

- Humanoid robots struggle with autonomous execution of complex, human-like loco-manipulation tasks due to difficulties in real-time adaptation to modeling errors and contact uncertainties.
- Pre-trained Diffusion Policies (DPs) suffer from poor out-of-distribution robustness, distribution shift, and compounding errors when relying solely on static offline datasets.
- Decoupling in hierarchical control between high-level motion planners and low-level whole-body controllers leads to poor command tracking and task failures because offline planners do not account for real-time execution dynamics.

## Method

- **REFINE-DP** proposes a three-stage pipeline (Data Collection, ==Diffusion Policy== Pre-training, Joint Optimization) using a hierarchical control architecture with a high-level ==Diffusion== Policy planner and a low-level RL-based whole-body controller.
- It employs Diffusion Policy Policy Optimization (DPPO) for RL fine-tuning, augmenting the environment Markov Decision Process (MDP) to treat each denoising step as a decision step for implicit optimization of the high-level DP.
- The framework jointly optimizes both the high-level DP and the low-level RL controller using dual reward signals, simultaneously enhancing task success, motion quality, and distributional consistency.

## Results

- REFINE-DP achieved over **90%** success rates in simulation for complex loco-manipulation tasks (e.g., 97-**99%** for Joint Optimized), substantially outperforming baselines which achieved 50-**70%**.
- Fine-tuning a Diffusion Policy pre-trained on only 50 trajectories achieved up to **95%** success rate, demonstrating significant data efficiency compared to the 1,000 trajectories required for similar performance from pre-training alone.
- Joint optimization reduced end-effector orientation error by up to **50%** and end-effector velocity by **15%**, indicating improved motion quality, and halved the required training iterations for Diffusion Policy fine-tuning.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Reinforcement learning fine-tuning is crucial for adapting pre-trained Diffusion Policies to unmodeled robot dynamics and OOD conditions, significantly boosting task success rates beyond offline training alone.
> - Jointly optimizing the high-level planner and low-level controller maintains distributional consistency, which improves command tracking accuracy and leads to smoother, less hesitant robot motions.
> - Transformer-based Diffusion Policies demonstrate superior ability to model multimodal action distributions compared to MLP-based DPs, resulting in better pre-trained performance and more effective fine-tuning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Humanoid robots exhibit increasing capabilities in physical behaviors, but still encounter difficulties in autonomous execution of complex, human-like tasks that require coordinated locomotion and manipulation in dynamic environments. Traditional approaches, including model-based methods and learning-based motion imitation, often rely on human supervision or heuristic planning, struggling with real-time adaptation to modeling errors and contact uncertainties inherent in physical interaction.

Recent advancements in learning high-level action generation from demonstrations, particularly Diffusion Policies (DPs), have shown promise in enabling task-level autonomy by capturing complex and multimodal expert behaviors. However, DPs typically rely on offline datasets, which can lead to issues such as poor out-of-distribution (OOD) robustness, distribution shift, and compounding errors. The common strategy of scaling up demonstration data to mitigate these issues is often computationally prohibitive and expensive for high-dimensional humanoid systems, and does not consistently yield robust performance in long-horizon loco-manipulation tasks.

To address these limitations, a line of research explores reinforcement learning fine-tuning (RLFT) to adapt pre-trained policies beyond their initial offline training. While some works have investigated integrating RL with DPs to learn residual policies or directly fine-tune DPs for improved success rates, few have focused on jointly optimizing both a high-level diffusion-based planner and a low-level whole-body controller within a unified framework, particularly for complex humanoid loco-manipulation. This paper positions itself within this gap, proposing a joint optimization method to enhance both task success and motion quality, while also improving data efficiency for high-dimensional humanoid robots.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a robust and efficient framework for humanoid loco-manipulation that overcomes the challenges associated with deploying Diffusion Policies (DPs) in complex, dynamic environments. The authors identify several key motivations and specific goals:

- **Addressing Decoupling Issues in Hierarchical Control:** Humanoid loco-manipulation often employs hierarchical control, where a high-level motion planner is decoupled from a low-level whole-body controller. This decoupling can lead to poor command tracking, compounding distribution shifts, and ultimately task failures because the planner, trained offline, does not account for the real-time execution errors and dynamics of the low-level controller. The research aims to bridge this gap by enabling adaptive interaction between the planner and controller.
- **Mitigating Distribution Shift and OOD Robustness:** Pre-trained DPs, reliant on static offline datasets, often exhibit limited robustness to out-of-distribution scenarios and accumulate errors over long task horizons. Scaling demonstration data for high-dimensional humanoid systems to improve generalization is costly and not always effective. The motivation is to enhance OOD robustness and task success rates without incurring prohibitive data collection and training costs.
- **Improving Motion Quality and Tracking Accuracy:** While existing fine-tuning methods for DPs may improve task success, they do not consistently address the quality of motion, such as tracking accuracy and smoothness, which are crucial for reliable physical interaction. The research aims to improve these aspects by ensuring distributional consistency between the planner's outputs and the controller's inputs.
- **Developing a Data-Efficient Framework:** Given the expense and difficulty of collecting large datasets for humanoid robots, a key motivation is to enable effective learning and adaptation with fewer expert demonstrations, thereby making robot learning more practical.
- **Enabling Autonomous Humanoid Loco-manipulation in Real-World Scenarios:** The overarching goal is to enable humanoid robots to perform complex, long-horizon loco-manipulation tasks, such as door traversal and object transport, autonomously and reliably in dynamic real-world environments, extending beyond controlled laboratory settings.

To achieve these, the paper proposes to:
1. Design a hierarchical framework with a DP as a high-level planner outputting compact, task-relevant commands and an RL-based policy as a low-level whole-body controller.
2. Introduce REFINE-DP, an RL fine-tuning approach that jointly optimizes both the high-level DP planner and the low-level RL controller.
3. Validate this framework on a humanoid robot across a range of complex loco-manipulation tasks, demonstrating improved success rates, motion quality, and robustness.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology, REFINE-DP, employs a three-stage pipeline to enable robust humanoid loco-manipulation: Data Collection, Diffusion Policy Pre-training, and Joint Optimization via Reinforcement Learning Fine-tuning.

#### 3.1. Hierarchical Control Architecture

A hierarchical framework is utilized to manage the complexity of humanoid loco-manipulation.
- **High-level Planner:** A Diffusion Policy (DP), denoted as `¯π_θ`, serves as the high-level autonomous planner. It outputs low-dimensional, task-relevant commands, specifically desired base velocities and hand poses (SE(3) poses in the robot's root frame). This compact command space is designed for intuitive teleoperation and efficient learning.
- **Low-level Controller:** An RL-based policy, `π_loco_manip`, acts as the low-level whole-body controller. It is responsible for executing the high-level commands, maintaining locomotion stability, and ensuring manipulation accuracy.

#### 3.2. Data Collection

Expert demonstrations are collected primarily in IsaacLab, a simulation environment.
- **Source of Demonstrations:** Data is generated through VR teleoperation and rollouts of heuristic planners, both leveraging a pre-trained `π_loco_manip` for whole-body control. This allows for diverse loco-manipulation skills, such as object transportation and door opening.
- **Data Augmentation:** To scale the dataset and mitigate the cost of teleoperation, 50 teleoperated trajectories are augmented with 1000 trajectories generated by heuristic planners designed for specific tasks.
- **State and Action Representation:** Collected trajectories include successful state-action pairs. Observed states encompass robot hand and foot poses in the body frame, gripper state, and object information (from MoCap). Actions specify desired hand poses, gripper state, and a base-velocity command, which is converted into footstep commands by a velocity-to-footstep planner.
- **Randomization:** Initial object configurations and robot states are randomized during data collection to ensure broad scenario coverage.

#### 3.3. Diffusion Policy Pre-training

The collected loco-manipulation data is used to pre-train the Diffusion Policy `¯π_θ`.
- **DP Formulation:** The DP models a conditional action distribution `p_θ(A_0:K_t | S_t)`, where `A_0:K_t` represents an action chunk over `K` denoising steps and `S_t` is a state observation chunk.
- **Forward Diffusion Process:** Gaussian noise is incrementally added to the actions, defining `q(A_k_t | A_k-1_t) = N(√(α_k) A_k-1_t, (1 − α_k)I)`. A closed-form marginal `A_k_t = √(¯α_k) A_0_t + √(1 − ¯α_k) ϵ` allows direct sampling of noise-corrupted actions.
- **Training Objective:** The DP is trained by minimizing the noise prediction error `L_diff(θ) = E_A_0_t,S_t,k,ϵ[∥ϵ − ϵ_θ(A_k_t, S_t, k)∥^2]`, where `ϵ_θ` is the noise prediction network.
- **Inference:** During inference, an initial Gaussian noise sample `A_K_t` is progressively denoised using `ϵ_θ` to produce the clean action `A_0_t`.

#### 3.4. Diffusion Policy Fine-tuning (REFINE-DP)

The pre-trained DP is further fine-tuned in the simulator to enhance task success and motion quality.

- **Motivation for Fine-tuning:** Pre-trained DPs may suffer from accumulated deviations due to unmodeled robot dynamics and execution errors. RL fine-tuning improves task success through trial-and-error and enhances robustness to OOD conditions, facilitating sim-to-real transfer.
- **DPPO for Implicit Policies:** Standard policy gradient methods are not directly applicable to DPs due to their implicit nature. REFINE-DP adopts Diffusion Policy Policy Optimization (DPPO), which augments the environment Markov Decision Process (MDP) by incorporating the denoising process. Each denoising step is treated as a decision step, and a PPO-style policy gradient is applied.
 - **Augmented MDP (`M_DP`):** Expands the environment MDP (`M_ENV`) by inserting a full denoising process within each environment timestep.
 - **Reward Structure:** Intermediate denoising steps (k>0) receive zero reward. Only the final denoised action (`A_0_t`, at k=0) is executed in `M_ENV` and receives the environment reward `R_t(S_t, A_0_t)`.
 - **Optimization:** The objective `∇_θ ¯J(¯π_θ) = E_¯π_θ, ¯P, ¯P_0[∑_t≥0 ∇_θ log ¯π_θ(¯a_t | ¯s_t) ¯r(¯s_t, ¯a_t)]` is optimized using generalized advantage estimation (GAE) and a PPO-style policy gradient.

#### 3.5. Joint Optimization of Diffusion and RL Policies

REFINE-DP proposes jointly optimizing both the high-level DP (`¯π_θ`) and the low-level RL controller (`π_loco_manip`).
- **Dual Reward Signals:** The augmented environment `M_DP` generates two sets of states and rewards:
 - `¯R_t`: Used to update `¯π_θ` for task success.
 - `ˆR_t`: Represents the rewards used in pre-training `π_loco_manip`, now used to further improve motion quality, accuracy, and smooth tracking of DP commands.
- **Optimization Process:** Both the DP and the RL control policy are optimized using PPO. Algorithm 1 details this process, iteratively sampling actions from the DP, executing them, collecting experience for both policies in replay buffers, and then updating `π_loco_manip` and `¯π_θ` using mini-batches.
- **Benefits of Joint Optimization:** This process helps maintain distributional consistency between the planner's evolving outputs and the controller's inputs, thereby reducing command-tracking mismatch, improving motion quality (e.g., smoother tracking, reduced jitter), and enhancing overall task success rates.

#### 3.6. Training the Low-level Loco-manipulation Controller (`π_loco_manip`)

The `π_loco_manip` is a pre-trained RL policy with a decoupled control architecture.
- **Lower-body Locomotion Policy (`π_lower`):** Responsible for dynamic balance and intermediate foot placements. Unlike velocity-tracking controllers, it is a foot-placement tracking controller that takes discrete foot-placement commands as input, leading to improved accuracy and stability. Trained using RL motion imitation on lower-body reference trajectories, conditioned on foot-placement commands (swing-foot indicator, countdown, relative target swing-foot pose) and proprioception. Reward function combines foot tracking and regularization, with randomized arm configurations for disturbance robustness.
- **Upper-body Arm Policy (`π_upper`):** Tracks desired hand pose commands (SE(3) poses). Trained with domain randomization to improve robustness against payload and actuation uncertainties.
- **Combined Output:** Both policies output joint position commands (`a_t = [a_lower_t; a_upper_t]`) as offsets from a default configuration `q_def`. These target joint positions (`q_target = q_def + a_t`) are then tracked by a proportional-derivative (PD) controller.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents quantitative and qualitative results from simulation and hardware experiments, comparing REFINE-DP with several baselines and ablations.

#### 4.1. Experiment Setup

- **Hardware:** Booster T1 humanoid robot (29 degrees of freedom).
- **Software:** Policy computer with AMD 7945HX CPU and NVIDIA RTX 4060 GPU. IsaacLab for simulation.
- **Policy Parameters:** DP (`¯π_θ`) has an observation horizon of 8, action chunk size of 12, 0.1s interval, runs at 10Hz using NVIDIA TensorRT. Loco-manipulation policy (`π_loco_manip`) runs at 50Hz.
- **Perception:** State-based DP relies on pre-processed poses of the robot torso and target objects, captured by a motion capture (MoCap) system streaming at 90Hz.
- **Tasks:** Four loco-manipulation tasks evaluated: Object pickup, Long-horizon pick-and-place, Door opening and traversal, and Stair-assisted object retrieval. These tasks require multiple stages of walking and manipulation, with the DP implicitly inferring task stages from object locations.

#### 4.2. Baselines and Ablation Study

- **DiT Planner:** Pre-trained Diffusion Policy with a transformer backbone (standard DP formulation).
- **MLP Planner:** Pre-trained DP with a deterministic multilayer perceptron (MLP) backbone.
- **MLP-FT:** Fine-tuning of the MLP planner, converted to a stochastic policy during fine-tuning.
- **Residual RL:** Learning a lightweight corrective residual policy on top of a frozen pre-trained DP.
- **DiT-FT (Ours):** Fine-tuning only the pre-trained DiT planner.
- **Joint Optimized (Ours):** REFINE-DP, jointly optimizing the DiT planner and the low-level RL controller.

#### 4.3. Quantitative Results and Analysis (Simulation)

- **Task Success Rate (Fig. 4):**
 - **Superior Performance of REFINE-DP:** Both DiT-FT and Joint Optimized REFINE-DP substantially outperform all baselines across all four tasks, achieving over 90% success rate (e.g., 97-99% for Joint Optimized). This demonstrates that RL fine-tuning significantly improves the success rate from a pre-trained baseline of 50-70%.
 - **Transformer Backbone Advantage:** The transformer-based DiT (pre-trained) consistently shows higher success rates than the MLP-based DP, attributed to its ability to model multimodal action distributions and generate diverse, feasible trajectories, unlike deterministic MLPs that average behaviors.
 - **MLP-FT Improvement:** MLP-FT partially mitigates the MLP's limitations by injecting stochasticity, improving performance compared to the static MLP, but remains limited by the backbone's representational capacity.
 - **Importance of Pre-training:** Fine-tuning is effective when initiated from a moderately capable pre-trained policy (50-70% SR). Fine-tuning from random initialization yielded limited improvements.
- **Efficiency Gains from Fine-tuning and Joint Optimization:**
 - **Motion Quality Improvement (Fig. 5):** Joint optimization (`π_loco_manip`) improves motion quality. It achieves the lowest position error and reduces orientation error by up to 50% compared to the pre-trained `π_loco_manip`. This is accompanied by an average 15% decrease in end-effector velocity, indicating smoother manipulation. Fine-tuning the DP alone can degrade low-level tracking, as the planner might overcommand to compensate for tracking inaccuracies.
 - **Training Efficiency:** Optimizing the RL controller alone using DP rollouts improves SR by 18% for long-horizon tasks. Joint optimization reduces the required iterations for DP fine-tuning by approximately half (20 vs. 40 iterations for 90% SR).
 - **Data Efficiency (Fig. 7):** Fine-tuning significantly reduces the need for extensive pre-training data. A DP pre-trained on only 50 trajectories, followed by fine-tuning, achieves up to 95% SR, whereas pre-training alone requires approximately 1,000 trajectories to reach 90% SR.
 - **Higher Throughput (Fig. 6):** Fine-tuned policies demonstrate higher throughput, reducing task completion time by an average of 15% across tasks (e.g., Task 2, 3, 4), due to improved action efficiency and less hesitant motion.
- **Adaptation to Out-of-Distribution (OOD) Scenarios:**
 - Fine-tuning with a curriculum enabled adaptation to significantly expanded OOD initial robot locations (320% radial distance, 125% polar angle, 600% heading angle compared to pre-training ranges).
 - The pre-trained policy achieved only 7% SR in maximum randomization OOD scenarios. Curriculum-based fine-tuning improved this to over 90% SR, whereas direct fine-tuning without a curriculum achieved only 30% SR.

#### 4.4. Hardware Experiment

- **Performance:** REFINE-DP achieved over 70% SR in the door-opening task and over 50% SR in the box transportation task on the Booster T1 humanoid robot. Locomotion and hand speeds were clamped (0.2 m/s and 0.05 m/s, respectively) for stable execution.
- **Sim-to-Real Gap:** Performance in hardware was lower than in simulation due to noisy object observations (MoCap) and more pronounced trembling/sliding in locomotion.
- **Robustness and Autonomy:** Despite the sim-to-real gap, the fine-tuned policy demonstrated improved robustness. It could adapt to environmental perturbations by re-adjusting plans on-the-fly (e.g., when target object location changed) and re-attempting failed actions (e.g., missing a door handle). Pre-trained policies often became stuck in similar situations.
- **Real-world Efficiency:** REFINE-DP achieved average speedups of approximately 10-20% for box pickup and door-opening tasks in hardware, demonstrating improved task efficiency.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research, presenting REFINE-DP, contributes to the field of humanoid robotics and robot learning by addressing several key limitations of current approaches to loco-manipulation.

- **Enhanced Reliability and Autonomy:** The core contribution is the demonstration that reinforcement learning fine-tuning, particularly through joint optimization of high-level planners and low-level controllers, is critical for achieving reliable and autonomous humanoid loco-manipulation. This moves humanoid capabilities beyond motion tracking towards task- and environment-level awareness, crucial for operating in dynamic and unstructured environments.
- **Improved Data Efficiency:** REFINE-DP significantly reduces the reliance on extensive and costly demonstration data for pre-training. By showing that fine-tuning a policy trained on a small dataset can surpass the performance of policies trained on substantially larger offline datasets, the work offers a more practical pathway for deploying learning-based control on complex robotic systems.
- **Better Motion Quality and Tracking:** The joint optimization approach specifically addresses the distributional mismatch between planner outputs and controller inputs, leading to improved command tracking accuracy and smoother, less hesitant robot motions. This is crucial for precise physical interaction and overall task success in contact-rich tasks.
- **Robustness to Out-of-Distribution Scenarios:** The ability of REFINE-DP to adapt to out-of-distribution initial conditions and environmental perturbations through curriculum-based fine-tuning enhances the generalization capabilities of humanoid policies. This is vital for real-world deployment where unforeseen variations are common.
- **Practical Sim-to-Real Transfer:** The successful deployment on a physical Booster T1 humanoid robot, despite inherent sim-to-real gaps, underscores the practical applicability of the proposed framework. The demonstrated ability to re-adjust plans and recover from failures in real-world settings highlights a significant step towards more resilient robot behaviors.
- **Framework for Complex Tasks:** By enabling autonomous execution of complex, long-horizon tasks like door traversal and object transport without explicit stage supervision, REFINE-DP provides a robust framework for learning and adapting multi-stage loco-manipulation skills.

In summary, REFINE-DP offers a methodology that can enhance the performance, efficiency, and robustness of humanoid robots in complex loco-manipulation tasks, facilitating their progression towards more general-purpose autonomous operation in human environments. Future work could build upon this foundation by incorporating richer sensory modalities like vision and expanding the versatility of whole-body skills in the controller.
