# SkillEvo: Self-Renewing Evolution Gradients from Multi-Turn Interaction Feedback

> [!summary] Summary
> SkillEvo evolves cloud-support agent Skills through a closed loop where multi-turn user simulation acts as a feedback generator rather than an evaluation endpoint, paired with a governance layer that repairs structural degradation. Across 9 production Skills it lifts task success rate by 51.8 points over hand-authored baselines and 15.4 points over single-turn-QA-driven evolution.

## Problem

- Single-turn QA evaluation only patches gaps visible in one exchange, so the evolution gradient decays and Skill improvement saturates after the first round.
- Existing governance relies on a scalar verification score that can reject a degraded Skill candidate but cannot localize or repair its structural cause.
- Prior continual-improvement methods depend on costly human-annotated feedback or treat multi-turn simulation purely as an evaluation endpoint, discarding trajectories instead of feeding them back into Skill revision.

## Method

- ==Trustworthy feedback generation== uses an intent state machine to gate coverage, dual-sided orthogonal evaluation to separate simulator distortion from agent failure, and collective attribution to screen failures into Knowledge Gap, Capability Limit, or Evaluation Noise.
- **Bounded Editing** via a Skill Optimizer updates the Skill using only verified knowledge gaps, anchored to the production baseline so new content never overwrites existing stable facts.
- **Skill Governor** performs graph-structural diagnosis against dual anchors to enforce fact consistency as a hard constraint and repair knowledge bloat, reference breakage, and factual over-generalization as a soft constraint.

## Results

- On 9 production Skills across 6 cloud-service categories and 98 reference files, **SkillEvo**'s task success rate climbs from **30.0%** to **81.8%** over 4 rounds, beating self-reflection by **23.0** points and single-turn QA by **15.4** points.
- Ablation swapping multi-turn interaction for single-turn QA drops TSR to **66.4%**, closing the entire gap and isolating the feedback source as the cause of the improvement; removing governance alone drops TSR only to **78.6%**.
- Governance cuts cumulative knowledge bloat to **+2.8%** versus **+16.2%** without it (nearly **6x** smaller) and lowers cross-round regression rate from **28.2%** to **21.1%** across successive revision rounds.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Evaluation feedback modality, not editing capability or iteration count, sets the ceiling on self-evolving systems: single-turn signals exhaust themselves while multi-turn interaction keeps exposing deeper defects.
> - Treating a knowledge base as a structured graph rather than a flat text blob makes degradation (bloat, broken references, over-generalization) diagnosable and repairable, not just detectable via a scalar score.
> - Separating simulator-side coverage/fidelity from agent-side accuracy is necessary to trust automated feedback; conflating them risks misattributing evaluation noise as genuine capability failure.

## Detailed Report

### 1. SkillEvo: Self-Renewing Evolution Gradients from Multi-Turn Interaction Feedback

#### 1.1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The landscape of language agents is evolving beyond single-turn question answering towards more autonomous capabilities, encompassing multi-turn interaction, knowledge retrieval, and tool invocation. In specialized domains such as cloud services, existing Large Language Model (LLM) applications like RCACopilot, RCAgent, and D-Bot address specific tasks like incident root-cause analysis or database diagnosis. However, these applications primarily focus on single-exchange capabilities and do not comprehensively address the long-term challenge of maintaining and continuously improving "Skills"—portable modules that encapsulate domain knowledge and handling procedures.

Current Skill maintenance largely relies on manual authoring, with failed interactions not being automatically consolidated into reusable knowledge. Prior research in continual improvement often depends on human-annotated feedback, which is resource-intensive and limited in coverage. This creates a bottleneck in scaling and latency for customer support operations.

Existing self-evolution research can be categorized by its feedback source. Approaches like "Self-Refine" perform reflection and editing without external evaluation, lacking the ability to distinguish genuine knowledge gaps from model-specific limitations. Methods driven by single-turn Question-Answering (QA) evaluation, such as "SkillForge," only address deficiencies apparent in a single interaction. Consequently, the evolution gradient for these methods tends to decay rapidly after initial improvements, leaving multi-turn interaction failures unaddressed. Moreover, feedback in these systems often drives revision without adequate attribution screening, which can lead to the mis-encoding of irreparable signals, resulting in document bloat and factual conflicts.

While multi-turn evaluation benchmarks like "τ-bench" and "SAGE" can expose interaction-level defects, they typically treat simulation as an evaluation endpoint, discarding trajectories after judging success or failure rather than converting them into feedback for Skill evolution. Concurrent efforts in skill evolution, including "SkillFoundry," "SkillX," "EvoSkills," "AgentSkillOS," and "Steve-Evolving," tend to derive skills from execution traces or co-evolution signals, not from the layered defects revealed through follow-up questions in multi-turn interactions.

Furthermore, existing governance mechanisms for Skill evolution primarily rely on scalar scores for verification, which can reject a degraded candidate but cannot diagnose or repair the underlying structural causes of degradation, such as knowledge bloat, reference breakage, or factual over-generalization. This leads to an append-only growth without bound, particularly problematic for Skills structured as multi-file directed graphs.

SkillEvo positions itself to address these limitations by introducing a framework that provides trustworthy, self-renewing evolution gradients from multi-turn interaction feedback and implements controllable governance to actively maintain the structural integrity and factual consistency of Skills. It diverges from previous work by converting multi-turn simulation from an evaluation endpoint into a feedback generator and by moving beyond passive scalar-gated rejection to diagnosis-driven active repair of knowledge bases.

#### 1.2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this work stems from observed limitations in current approaches to language agent Skill development and maintenance. Agent Skills, whether hand-authored or generated by LLMs in a single pass, typically lack a closed loop for improvement based on actual interaction failures. This absence prevents Skills from automatically learning and adapting from their operational shortcomings.

The paper identifies two critical deficiencies in existing methods that attempt to close this loop:
1. **Decaying Evolution Gradients from Limited Feedback Modalities:** Previous work that does incorporate feedback, particularly single-turn Question-Answering (QA) evaluation, suffers from a "sharp asymmetry." Once the initial, visible gaps exposed by a single exchange are patched, the evolution gradient diminishes. This leaves defects that only manifest across multiple turns unaddressed, leading to a stagnation of Skill evolution. The motivation here is to develop a feedback mechanism that continuously supplies fresh, reliable evolution gradients, capable of exposing latent defects layer by layer across extended interactions.
2. **Ineffective Governance of Skill Degradation:** Governance in existing systems often relies on a single, end-to-end verification score. While such a scalar gate can reject a degraded candidate, it lacks the ability to localize or repair the structural causes of degradation. This includes issues like factual inconsistencies, knowledge bloat, reference breakage within a structured knowledge base, or factual over-generalization. The motivation is to replace this passive rejection with an active, independent governance layer that can diagnose and repair these structural and factual degradations, ensuring the Skill evolves in a stable and coherent manner.

Based on these observations, the central claim of the research is that the primary constraint on sustained Skill evolution is the quality of the evaluation feedback (its trustworthiness and ability to provide a consistent gradient) and the effectiveness of controllable evolution governance, rather than simply editing capability or the number of iterative revisions.

The key objectives of the SkillEvo framework are therefore:
- To formalize three necessary conditions for trustworthy feedback—coverage, accuracy, and attributability—and recast multi-turn user simulation into a feedback generator that continually uncovers new defects.
- To identify the Skill as a structured knowledge system and develop an independent governance layer capable of actively repairing factual degradation and structural bloat through graph-structural diagnosis, moving from scalar-gated passive rejection to diagnosis-driven active repair.
- To demonstrate, through empirical evaluation on production cloud service scenarios, that this framework significantly improves Skill performance and stability compared to self-reflection and single-turn QA-driven evolution methods.

#### 1.3. Methodology and Approach

SkillEvo is presented as a closed-loop framework for Skill self-evolution, organized around two core pillars: trustworthy feedback generation and controllable Skill governance.

##### 1.3.1. Overall Framework

The framework operates through a sequence of interconnected components (illustrated in Figure 2):
1. **Scenario Synthesizer:** This component generates a constrained user scenario from a real human-handled support ticket. It extracts key information including the user's intent agenda (core requests and follow-ups), behavior facts (information the user possesses, operations attempted, symptoms), emotion trajectory, and a human reference solution. These elements collectively form an evaluation task.
2. **User Agent:** A simulated user agent (U) interacts with a service agent (π(St)) loaded with the Skill being evaluated (St). This multi-turn interaction produces a dialogue trajectory (τt).
3. **Verifier:** The generated trajectory is judged against the human reference solution. The Verifier determines success or failure and generates detailed feedback, including the failure cause and supporting evidence.
4. **Collective Attribution:** Failures identified by the Verifier are classified into three categories: Knowledge Gap (repairable by the Skill), Capability Limit (beyond Skill repair, e.g., permission issues, tooling limits), and Evaluation Noise (e.g., false negatives, scenario distortion). Only "Knowledge Gap" failures are aggregated into the evaluation feedback (Lt).
5. **Skill Optimizer:** This component performs a "bounded" update of the Skill (St+1) using the feedback Lt and anchored to the original production baseline (S0). "Bounded" implies two constraints: an evidence boundary (only verified gaps are patched, no unsupported content introduced) and a reference boundary (new knowledge does not overwrite existing stable facts from S0).
6. **Skill Governor:** After the Skill Optimizer performs a revision, an independent governance layer is invoked. This layer detects structural degradations such as knowledge bloat, reference breakage, and factual over-generalization. It then emits recommendations, which are merged with the attribution signals, to guide subsequent repair and maintain Skill structure and consistency.

##### 1.3.2. Trustworthy Feedback Generation

Trustworthy feedback is defined by three conditions:
- **Coverage:** All key intents are raised by the simulator, and the simulation is faithful.
- **Accuracy:** The simulator judges each exposed intent accurately against the human reference.
- **Attributability:** The root cause of the failure is repairable by the Skill.

These conditions are addressed through specific mechanisms:
- **Trustworthy User Reconstruction:** From human-handled tickets, the system reconstructs a constrained user profile by extracting:
 - *Intent agenda:* Core requests and optional follow-up questions, labeled as key or minor.
 - *Behavior facts:* User's existing information, attempted operations, and observable symptoms.
 - *Emotion trajectory:* Modeled fluctuations in cooperativeness and emotional turning points.
 This reconstruction aims for task-level realism to prevent intent omission, factual fabrication, and answer leakage.
- **Intent State Machine:** This machine tracks the status of each intent (raised and substantively addressed). Normal dialogue termination is only permitted when all intents are satisfied; otherwise, an abandonment-style termination is triggered.
- **Dual-Sided Orthogonal Evaluation:** SkillEvo evaluates the simulator and the agent separately to isolate responsibilities.
 - *Simulator side (Intent coverage, cU):* Measures the fraction of key intents from the agenda that were actually raised. Samples with low coverage are attributed to "Evaluation Noise" and excluded from agent-side evaluation.
 - *Agent side (Skill hit and exposed-intent response accuracy, sC):* "Skill hit" (h) is a binary factor indicating if the Skill was invoked. "sC" is a weighted accuracy score for each raised intent against human "intent–solution" pairs, reflecting the agent's ability to answer exposed intents.
- **Collective Attribution:** The Attributor compares the human handling process, the simulated dialogue, and the Verifier's evidence to classify failures:
 - *Knowledge Gap:* A stable fact present in the human reference was omitted or incorrectly stated by the Skill. Only these are projected into the feedback (Lt).
 - *Capability Limit:* Failures due to permissions, tooling, poor delivery, or infrastructure faults, not repairable by Skill knowledge.
 - *Evaluation Noise:* False negatives or scenario distortions.
 Multiple Knowledge Gap failures within a round that point to the same underlying issue are merged by semantic similarity into a single feedback signal (Lt), focusing revision on commonalities.

##### 1.3.3. Controllable Skill Self-Evolution

This pillar ensures the Skill's integrity and coherence during multi-round revision.
- **Fact Consistency:** This is a hard constraint. A revised Skill (St) must retain at least the stable facts of the production baseline (S0). This is checked by an independent inspector against dual anchors: S0 (to detect cumulative knowledge loss) and St-1 (to detect newly introduced factual errors in the current round). Violations—knowledge loss against S0, process errors against St-1, or global self-contradiction—result in rejection of the candidate and trigger same-round repair.
- **Structural Consistency:** This is a soft constraint addressing degradation in the Skill's knowledge organization, which is modeled as a directed graph. The Skill Governor detects three types of structural degradation:
 - *Knowledge bloat:* Redundancy within nodes that dilutes routing precision.
 - *Reference breakage:* Severed inter-node connectivity (dangling references, orphan files).
 - *Factual over-generalization:* Concrete values/rules decaying into vague statements.
 Unlike fact consistency, structural consistency issues do not immediately reject candidates. Instead, the governance layer merges recommendations for structural repair with attributed knowledge gaps and injects them into the next revision round, leading to gradual dissolution of degradation.

##### 1.3.4. Implementation Details

The Skill Editor (Generator) utilizes `deepseek-v4-pro`, while the Verifier, User Agent, Attributor, and Governor (Evaluator) employ `minimax-m3`. This separation ensures that the Generator and Evaluator belong to different model families, preventing circular dependencies where a model reviews its own edits. The service agent operates in-process headless, with a restricted tool allow-list preventing it from self-modifying code or Skills.

#### 1.4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of SkillEvo was conducted on production technical support scenarios from Tencent Cloud, encompassing six categories of cloud services, 9 production Skills, and 98 skill-reference files. The dataset comprised 2,000 human-escalated tickets, representing failures of existing Skills. Tickets were chronologically split into a development set (75%) for driving evolution and an evaluation set (25%) for measurement.

##### 1.4.1. Overall Task Success Rate (TSR)

The primary metric was Overall TSR, the fraction of tasks solved on the evaluation set. The evaluation compared SkillEvo against three baselines over four rounds:
- **Original Skill:** The initial hand-authored Skill, with 30.0% TSR.
- **Self-Reflection:** A model that self-reflects and edits without external evaluation feedback. This method showed initial improvement to 59.2% TSR in R1 but then oscillated (58.7%, 57.4%, 58.8% in R2-R4), demonstrating no sustained evolution gradient.
- **Single-turn QA:** An evolution method driven by single-turn QA evaluation (representative of SkillForge-style approaches). It improved from 58.9% (R1) to 66.4% (R4), but with sharply diminishing marginal gains, indicating gradient decay after initial visible gaps were patched.
- **SkillEvo:** The proposed framework. It showed a steady and substantial increase in TSR across rounds, from 59.4% (R1) to 81.8% (R4).

SkillEvo demonstrated an improvement of 51.8 points over the Original Skills, 23.0 points over self-reflection-based evolution, and 15.4 points over single-turn-QA-driven evolution. The consistent upward trend of SkillEvo's TSR curve suggests that its multi-turn interaction feedback successfully generates new evolution gradients in each round, unlike the saturating gradients of single-turn methods.

##### 1.4.2. Ablation Study

An ablation study further examined the contributions of multi-turn interaction and governance:
- **Single-turn QA (SkillEvo variant):** Replacing SkillEvo's multi-turn interaction with single-turn QA evaluation, while keeping attribution, revision, and governance, resulted in a TSR of 66.4% at R4. This matched the Single-turn QA baseline, confirming that the 15.4-point performance lead of full SkillEvo is directly attributable to its multi-turn feedback mechanism.
- **SkillEvo without Governance:** Removing the governance layer (while retaining multi-turn feedback, attribution, and revision) led to a TSR of 78.6% at R4, a drop of 3.2 points compared to the full SkillEvo (81.8%). This indicates that while governance does contribute to the overall score, its primary role is not in boosting the absolute TSR but in preventing degradation accumulation across rounds.

##### 1.4.3. Trustworthy Feedback Evaluation

The trustworthiness of the feedback generation mechanism was quantitatively assessed:
- **Intent Coverage (cU):** The simulator achieved 98.9% intent coverage, indicating that key intents were almost exhaustively raised. Low-coverage samples (remaining 1%) were isolated from the revision loop.
- **Simulator Fidelity (ρ):** An external validation by domain experts, comparing 200 simulated dialogues against real tickets, showed 95.3% agreement across intent expression, information-reveal pace, and emotion trajectory. This confirmed that the simulator raised intents in a manner representative of real users.
- **Exposed-Intent Response Accuracy (sC):** The agent's accuracy on already-exposed intents during simulation was 71.1%. This value reflects the Skill's knowledge-coverage gaps and serves as the direct source of the evolution gradient.

These results indicate that the dual-sided orthogonal evaluation effectively separates simulator distortion from agent failures, providing a reliable basis for feedback.

##### 1.4.4. Controllable Governance Evaluation

The effectiveness of the governance layer was measured by its ability to prevent degradation, specifically using Cross-round Regression Rate (RegR) and Knowledge Bloat (Bloat):
- **Cross-round Regression Rate (RegR):** RegR decreased across the three transitions (from 28.2% R1→2 to 21.1% R3→4), with a first-to-last change of -7.1%. This demonstrates that the governance layer successfully constrained the regression of existing capabilities across successive revisions.
- **Knowledge Bloat (Bloat):**
 - With governance (SkillEvo Full): Cumulative bloat was +2.8% relative to the production baseline (S0), stabilizing after the first round of knowledge supplementation.
 - Without governance: Cumulative bloat reached +16.2%, nearly six times larger, showing continuous accumulation.
These findings confirm that the structural-consistency constraint is effective in preventing knowledge bloat. The observation that TSR improved by 51.8 points with only a 2.8% increase in knowledge volume suggests that capability gains were primarily driven by the correct revision of existing knowledge rather than simply expanding the text base.

#### 1.5. Significance and Potential Impact

The SkillEvo framework introduces a significant advancement in the development and maintenance of language agent Skills, with implications across several dimensions:

**Addressing a Fundamental Bottleneck:** The work challenges the conventional focus on editing capability or iteration count in Skill evolution. It empirically establishes that the primary bottleneck for sustained, high-quality Skill evolution is the continuous supply of trustworthy evaluation gradients and effective, controllable governance. This reorientation of focus is fundamental for developing more robust and autonomously improving agent systems.

**Novelty in Feedback Generation:** SkillEvo pioneers the recasting of multi-turn user simulation from a mere evaluation endpoint into an active feedback generator. By allowing follow-up questions to expose defects layer by layer, it creates a "self-renewing" evolution gradient. This mechanism enables continuous learning from latent interaction failures that are invisible to single-turn assessment methods, providing a more comprehensive and effective signal for Skill improvement.

**Robust and Controllable Governance:** The introduction of an independent governance layer that actively diagnoses and repairs factual degradation and structural bloat marks a departure from passive, scalar-gated rejection. By maintaining fact consistency against dual anchors and addressing issues like knowledge bloat, reference breakage, and factual over-generalization through graph-structural diagnosis, SkillEvo ensures that Skills evolve in a stable, coherent, and maintainable manner. This significantly enhances the reliability and structural integrity of agent knowledge bases over multiple revision cycles.

**Empirical Validation and Practical Deployment:** The evaluation on production technical-support scenarios from Tencent Cloud, involving actual customer tickets and Skills, provides strong empirical evidence of SkillEvo's effectiveness. The substantial improvements in Task Success Rate (TSR) and the demonstrated control over regression and knowledge bloat under real operating conditions underscore its practical utility. Its deployment in a production environment further validates its robustness and applicability to real-world challenges.

**Impact on Long-Term Skill Maintenance:** By enabling automatic, closed-loop continual improvement, SkillEvo offers a scalable solution to the long-standing problem of manual Skill maintenance. This can reduce operational costs, increase the efficiency of customer support, and accelerate the development cycle for new agent capabilities, ultimately leading to more autonomous and capable language agents in various service domains.

**Ethical and Safety Considerations:** The framework acknowledges the inherent risks of automated knowledge writing and integrates safeguards. Fact consistency is treated as a hard constraint, preventing the deletion of stable facts. Furthermore, a human confirmation step is mandatory before any revised Skill is rolled out to production, ensuring a necessary human checkpoint for safety and reliability in user-facing systems.

In summary, SkillEvo represents a significant step towards autonomous and continuously improving agent systems by addressing critical deficiencies in feedback generation and governance, offering a pathway for scalable and reliable Skill evolution in complex, real-world applications.
