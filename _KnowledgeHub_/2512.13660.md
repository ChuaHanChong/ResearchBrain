# RoboTracer: Mastering Spatial Trace with Reasoning in Vision-Language Models for Robotics

> [!summary] Summary
> RoboTracer enhances Vision-Language Models with 3D spatial intelligence, enabling the generation of precise 3D positional sequences for robotic control. It introduces the TraceSpatial dataset and benchmark, demonstrating superior performance in 3D spatial reasoning and achieving state-of-the-art results in real-world robotic manipulation tasks.

## Problem

- Embodied robots struggle to interpret and execute complex, spatially constrained natural language instructions in 3D environments due to limited metric-grounded understanding.
- Existing Vision-Language Models (VLMs) lack precise 3D spatial reasoning capabilities and often operate in 2D, failing to capture absolute metric quantities crucial for robotics.
- There is a scarcity of large-scale, high-quality datasets and benchmarks specifically designed for multi-step, metric-grounded 3D spatial tracing in robotics.

## Method

- Introduces RoboTracer, a VLM architecture extended with a ==universal spatial encoder== to integrate geometric inputs and a ==scale decoder== for explicit metric scale prediction.
- Employs a two-stage training paradigm: ==Supervised Fine-tuning (SFT)== for metric alignment and enhancement, followed by ==Reinforcement Fine-tuning (RFT)== using novel ==metric-sensitive process rewards== for multi-step reasoning.
- Develops the ==TraceSpatial dataset== (4.5M samples) for 3D spatial reasoning, and TraceSpatial-Bench, a challenging real-world benchmark for evaluating 3D spatial tracing.

## Results

- RoboTracer-8B-SFT achieved an **8.58%** absolute improvement over Gemini-2.5-Pro and **20.3%** over NVILA-8B in 3D spatial understanding and metric measuring benchmarks.
- On the challenging real-world TraceSpatial-Bench, RoboTracer-RFT achieved an overall success rate **36%** higher than Gemini-2.5-Pro, demonstrating robust 3D spatial tracing with collision-free path validation.
- RoboTracer enabled UR5 and G1 humanoid robots to execute long-horizon, dynamic tasks in cluttered real-world scenes, generating collision-free spatial traces and adapting to changes at **1.5 Hz**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicit integration of 3D geometric inputs via a universal spatial encoder and direct supervision for metric scale prediction are crucial for enhancing a VLM's 3D spatial and metric awareness.
> - Metric-sensitive process rewards, which supervise intermediate perceptual cues in an order-invariant manner, significantly improve the model's ability to perform complex, multi-step compositional reasoning during RFT.
> - A diverse data recipe combining 2D, 3D, and video data within TraceSpatial is essential for learning robust 3D spatial referring, measuring, and physically plausible trace generation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

RoboTracer addresses a critical and evolving challenge at the intersection of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) and embodied robotics: enabling robots to understand and execute complex, spatially constrained instructions in 3D environments. This work directly tackles limitations in existing VLM and Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models that primarily operate in 2D or lack precise metric-grounded understanding.

**Current Landscape and Gaps:**
- **Embodied AI and Robotic Control:** The demand for embodied robots to interpret nuanced human instructions, such as "Water flowers from left to right with watering can hovering 1-5 cm above each flower," is rapidly increasing. Current VLA models struggle with such compositional tasks due often to data scarcity and limited reasoning capabilities.
- **2D vs. 3D Spatial Reasoning:** While modern VLMs have advanced 2D spatial reasoning (e.g., identifying relative positions or generating 2D visual traces), they fundamentally lack the 3D space grounding and absolute metric understanding required for real-world robotic interaction. A 2D pixel coordinate cannot fully capture the volumetric nature of objects or precise distances.
- **Multi-step Metric-Grounded Reasoning:** Robotic tasks often require a sequence of spatially aware decisions. Existing models generally overlook this multi-step nature and the role of intermediate objects in shaping the final trajectory. For instance, correctly "hovering 1-5 cm above each flower" requires precise 3D measurement and relative positioning for each flower in sequence.
- **Data and Benchmarking Deficiencies:** A significant bottleneck is the lack of large-scale, high-quality datasets and benchmarks specifically designed for 3D spatial tracing that incorporate multi-step, metric-grounded reasoning and diverse real-world scenarios.

**RoboTracer's Position:**
RoboTracer directly addresses these gaps by proposing a 3D-aware VLM capable of generating "spatial traces"—3D positional sequences—that serve as an intuitive bridge between natural language instructions and robot actions. It distinguishes itself by:

1. **Enhancing 3D Spatial Understanding:** Unlike existing VLMs that struggle with absolute-scale measurements, RoboTracer explicitly focuses on 3D spatial referring (localizing objects) and 3D spatial measuring (quantifying real-world metric quantities). It integrates explicit geometric information and scale supervision, moving beyond implicit 3D learning from RGB inputs alone.
2. **Advancing Multi-step Metric-Grounded Reasoning:** It goes beyond simple 2D tracing by incorporating reinforcement fine-tuning (RFT) with novel metric-sensitive *process rewards*. These rewards supervise key intermediate perceptual cues (e.g., individual object measurements, specific spatial relations) during the reasoning process, which is crucial for complex, long-horizon tasks.
3. **Introducing Foundational Resources:** The paper contributes TraceSpatial, a large-scale, highly annotated dataset encompassing diverse scenes and complex reasoning processes. It also introduces TraceSpatial-Bench, a challenging real-world benchmark specifically for evaluating 3D spatial tracing, filling a critical void in the evaluation landscape.
4. **Demonstrating Real-World Embodiment:** RoboTracer aims to be a practical solution, showcasing its ability to control diverse robots (UR5, G1 humanoid) in cluttered real-world scenes for long-horizon, dynamic tasks, indicating strong generalization capabilities.

In essence, RoboTracer positions itself as a foundational advancement for embodied AI, moving beyond purely visual or language-based understanding to achieve truly *spatial intelligence* in robots, which is critical for future human-robot interaction and automation. It builds upon prior work in spatial reasoning and RFT, but significantly extends them into the 3D metric-grounded, multi-step domain.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching motivation behind RoboTracer is to enable embodied robots to precisely and reliably execute tasks based on complex, spatially constrained natural language instructions within dynamic 3D environments. The authors identify "spatial tracing" – the generation of a 3D positional sequence – as a pivotal capability for this goal, acting as an intuitive intermediate representation between high-level instructions and low-level robot control.

The key objectives and specific motivations for developing RoboTracer are:

1. **To bridge the gap between complex instructions and 3D robotic actions:** Existing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models often fail when instructions involve intricate spatial constraints (e.g., "hovering 1-5 cm above each flower," "from left to right"). The motivation is to create a robust system that can interpret these nuances and translate them into a concrete 3D plan.

2. **To empower VLMs with precise 3D spatial referring:** Robots need to accurately identify and locate objects involved in a task based on their spatial relationships. Current VLMs, while capable of 2D referring, struggle with unambiguous 3D localization, especially in complex and cluttered scenes. An objective is to imbue the VLM with this capability (e.g., "identifying flowers with their from left to right order and locating them").

3. **To equip VLMs with robust 3D spatial measuring:** Many robotic tasks require understanding absolute, real-world metric quantities (e.g., "1–5 cm height," "physical height"). Existing VLMs largely lack this metric-grounded understanding, making it difficult to generate action trajectories that adhere to specific distance or size constraints. A key objective is to enable precise metric measurement from visual inputs.

4. **To enable multi-step, metric-grounded reasoning:** Complex spatial tasks inherently require a sequence of interconnected perceptual and reasoning steps. Current VLM approaches often overlook this compositional nature and the crucial role of intermediate objects. RoboTracer aims to advance the VLM's ability to perform multi-step reasoning, where each step involves both spatial referring and measuring, to build an accurate overall spatial trace.

5. **To address the scarcity of training data and evaluation benchmarks for 3D spatial tracing:** The lack of large-scale, high-quality datasets that provide detailed annotations for 3D spatial referring, measuring, and multi-step reasoning processes is a significant impediment. Similarly, there is a lack of benchmarks specifically designed to evaluate these complex 3D spatial tracing capabilities in real-world scenarios. A major objective is to create these foundational resources: the **TraceSpatial** dataset and the **TraceSpatial-Bench** benchmark.

By tackling these objectives, RoboTracer aims to overcome fundamental limitations in current embodied AI, moving towards more intelligent, versatile, and human-instruction-following robots that can operate effectively in the unstructured complexity of the real world.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

RoboTracer's methodology is built upon enhancing Vision-Language Models (VLMs) with 3D-aware capabilities, metric-grounded reasoning, and multi-step learning through a novel architecture, a two-stage training paradigm (SFT and RFT), and new large-scale data resources.

#### 3.1. RoboTracer Architecture

The core architecture extends a base VLM (NVILA) with specialized components for 3D spatial understanding:
- **Universal Spatial Encoder:** This component integrates diverse geometric inputs (e.g., camera intrinsics, absolute depth, camera poses) alongside RGB images. It leverages the encoder of MapAnything, a universal feed-forward metric 3D reconstruction model. This encoder is kept frozen during training to preserve its powerful pretrained 3D representations, and its outputs are projected into the LLM's latent space. This design allows for flexible input, adapting to available geometric cues at inference time without architectural changes or retraining.
- **Scale Decoder:** An MLP-based decoder is introduced to explicitly predict a "metric scale factor" from a special `<SCALE>` token embedding. Unlike traditional next-token prediction, this decoder is supervised by a regression loss on the predicted metric scale factor. This direct supervision enhances the VLM's awareness of real-world absolute scales, even when only RGB inputs are available during parts of the training.
- **Multi-Modal Projectors:** Linear connectors are used to align the visual and spatial encoder outputs with the Large Language Model (LLM) backbone, similar to the base NVILA model.

#### 3.2. Training Stages

RoboTracer employs a two-stage fine-tuning process:

- **Stage 1: Supervised Fine-tuning (SFT):**
 - **Metric Alignment:** The first step focuses on aligning the universal spatial encoder and scale decoder with the LLM. Using the **TraceSpatial** dataset (specifically its RGB+X data, where 'X' denotes geometric annotations like depth and camera intrinsics), only the projectors and the scale decoder are trained. The loss function includes the standard next-token prediction loss (`L_ntp`) and a regression loss (L2) for the predicted logarithmic scale factor, encouraging the model to link scale-invariant visual representations to absolute metric scales.
 - **Metric Enhancement:** In this step, all components of the VLM (except the frozen spatial encoder) are fine-tuned. This involves training on both RGB-only and RGB+X inputs from **TraceSpatial**, along with other general instruction-following datasets. This mixed training strategy helps preserve the image encoder's general VQA capabilities while simultaneously enhancing the model's 3D spatial and scale awareness. The SFT-trained model serves as a "cold start" for the subsequent RFT stage.

- **Stage 2: Reinforcement Fine-tuning (RFT):**
 - After SFT, RFT is applied using the GRPO algorithm, leveraging the multi-step reasoning data from **TraceSpatial**. The core innovation here is a sophisticated reward design:
 - **Outcome-Based Rewards:**
 - `R_OF` (Outcome Format Reward): Penalizes/rewards for adherence to structured output formats.
 - `R_P` (Point Reward): Evaluates the consistency of the predicted trace's start and end points with ground truth, using an L2 distance-based similarity.
 - `R_T` (Trace Reward): Measures trajectory-level alignment between predicted and ground-truth traces using a distance metric (e.g., Dynamic Time Warping).
 - **Metric-Sensitive Process Rewards:** These are crucial for supervising intermediate perceptual steps, moving beyond purely outcome-based feedback. They leverage the detailed step-wise annotations in **TraceSpatial**.
 - `R_PF` (Process Format Reward): Enforces a specific format for reasoning steps (e.g., `[Perception Type] [Target Object]: [Value]`).
 - `R_Acc` (Accuracy Reward): Applies to key intermediate steps annotated in TraceSpatial. It measures prediction error using specific metrics (e.g., L1 distance for 3D spatial referring, a ±30% error tolerance for 3D spatial measuring and scale prediction). This reward is order-invariant, allowing flexible reasoning step ordering.
 - The final reward is a sum of these outcome-based and scaled process-based terms, guiding the model to generate accurate spatial traces by reinforcing correct intermediate perceptions.

#### 3.3. Data Resources

- **TraceSpatial Dataset:** A large-scale (4.5M samples, 30M QA pairs) and highly diverse dataset specifically designed for 3D spatial reasoning and tracing.
 - **Diversity:** Covers outdoor, indoor, and tabletop scenes; includes both object-centric and end-effector-centric traces from various single-/dual-arm robot configurations.
 - **Multi-Dimensionality:** Contains a high proportion (48.2%) of metric-grounded QA pairs, supporting 3D spatial measuring, referring, and multi-step tracing with detailed reasoning process annotations (up to 9 steps).
 - **Construction:** Uses a progressive pipeline integrating 2D web images (OpenImages with VLM-generated captions/scene graphs), 3D scanning datasets (CA-1M, ScanNet with precise 3D annotations), and manipulation videos (DROID, AgiBotWorld for physically plausible traces). It employs physics-based simulation for object-centric traces and rigorous filtering for quality.
- **TraceSpatial-Bench Benchmark:** A challenging benchmark for evaluating spatial tracing in real-world, cluttered indoor/tabletop scenes.
 - **Composition:** 100 real-world images with precise geometric annotations (camera geometry, absolute depth, 3D occupancy).
 - **Task Complexity:** Tasks require 3-8 reasoning steps, with annotations for start-point masks, end-point 3D bounding boxes.
 - **Evaluation Metrics:** Includes both 2D (Start/End Success) and 3D metrics (Start/End Success, Overall 3D Success including collision-free path validation).

The methodology of RoboTracer combines architectural enhancements, a meticulously designed training regimen, and the creation of bespoke large-scale datasets and benchmarks to achieve its objective of mastering 3D spatial tracing with reasoning for robotics.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

RoboTracer demonstrates significant advancements across various benchmarks, particularly in 3D spatial understanding, measuring, referring, and spatial tracing, outperforming strong baselines, including proprietary models like Gemini-2.5-Pro.

1. **Superior Spatial Understanding and Metric Measuring (SFT Stage):**
 - RoboTracer-8B-SFT, trained on TraceSpatial, achieves an average success rate of 85.7% on spatial understanding and measuring benchmarks (CV-Bench, BLINK, RoboSpatial, EmbSpatial, Q-Spatial, MSMU).
 - This performance surpasses Gemini-2.5-Pro by an absolute 8.58% and the base model NVILA-8B by 20.3%.
 - The improvements are more pronounced in 3D-related and measurement tasks (23.6% absolute gain) compared to 2D tasks (14.7% absolute gain), validating the effectiveness of the architecture and dataset in enhancing 3D spatial and scale awareness during SFT.

2. **State-of-the-Art 2D Spatial Referring and Visual Trace:**
 - RoboTracer models (both 2B-SFT and 8B-SFT) achieve the best performance on public 2D spatial referring benchmarks (Where2Place, RoboSpatial, RefSpatial-Bench) and 2D visual trace benchmarks (ShareRobot-Bench, VABench-V).
 - This success is attributed to the decoupled (u,v,d) point formulation and the strategic reuse of TraceSpatial data, which allows seamless projection into 2D formats, enabling effective multi-task learning.

3. **Breakthrough in Multi-Step Spatial Tracing (RFT Stage):**
 - On the challenging **TraceSpatial-Bench** (a real-world benchmark requiring multi-step, metric-grounded reasoning), RoboTracer-RFT significantly outperforms all baselines.
 - It achieves an overall success rate (including collision-free path validation) that exceeds Gemini-2.5-Pro by a remarkable 36% absolute margin and its own SFT-trained counterpart by 8%.
 - Baselines, including powerful VLMs, struggle with 3D spatial tracing, often generating "floating" or "colliding" traces due to limited metric depth understanding. RoboTracer-RFT's learned 3D spatial knowledge and compositional reasoning enable more accurate and physically plausible traces.
 - Leveraging explicit geometric information (RGB-I-D input) further boosts performance on TraceSpatial-Bench by up to 6% absolute success rate, confirming that readily available geometry in embodied settings refines metric reasoning.

4. **Preservation of General VLM Capabilities:**
 - Joint training on TraceSpatial (RGB and RGB+X) and general visual instruction datasets allows RoboTracer-2B-SFT to perform on par with or slightly better than the base NVILA-2B on public general VLM benchmarks (MME, MMBench, OK-VQA, POPE). This indicates that the specialized 3D spatial reasoning enhancements do not compromise the model's general common-sense knowledge.

5. **Robustness in Simulation and Real-World Robotics:**
 - **RoboTwin Simulation:** RoboTracer-2B demonstrates strong generalization as a practical tool, outperforming task-specific end-to-end policies and other VLMs by 32.8% on *unseen* hard tasks in the RoboTwin 2.0 simulation environment. This highlights its capability to act as an effective spatial planner.
 - **Real-World Tasks:** RoboTracer enables UR5 and G1 humanoid robots to execute long-horizon, dynamic tasks in cluttered real-world scenes, such as adaptive hamburger placement and flower watering (demonstrated in Figure 1 and 5). It generates collision-free spatial traces and can adapt to dynamic changes (e.g., moving objects) with a rapid update rate of 1.5 Hz.

6. **Ablation Study Insights:**
 - **Data Recipe:** Combining 2D, 3D, and video data in TraceSpatial is crucial; each data type provides unique benefits (scale supervision from 2D/3D, gripper motion from video).
 - **Universal Spatial Encoder:** Essential for enhancing metric-grounded reasoning and multi-step spatial tracing, suggesting its prior 3D knowledge and ability to integrate geometric cues are vital.
 - **Scale Regression Supervision:** Explicitly supervising the metric scale factor with a regression loss is more effective than next-token prediction or no supervision for improving metric awareness.
 - **Metric-Sensitive Process Rewards:** Integrating these rewards significantly improves the overall success rate (4% absolute gain) and 3D metrics during RFT, proving the value of supervising intermediate perceptual steps.

In summary, RoboTracer successfully achieves its objectives, showcasing a VLM capable of sophisticated 3D spatial reasoning, measuring, and tracing, which translates into superior performance in both benchmarks and real-world robotic applications.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

RoboTracer represents a significant step forward in the field of embodied AI and robotics, particularly in bridging the gap between high-level human instructions and precise robotic execution in complex 3D environments. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Enabling Truly Intelligent Embodied Robots:** By mastering spatial tracing with multi-step, metric-grounded reasoning, RoboTracer moves robots closer to understanding and acting upon human commands with a nuanced comprehension of 3D space. This is critical for tasks requiring precise placement, navigation around obstacles, and adherence to specific dimensions or distances, unlocking new levels of autonomy and versatility for robots.

2. **Addressing a Critical 3D Perception Gap in VLMs:** While VLMs excel in many 2D vision-language tasks, their inherent struggle with 3D metric understanding has been a major limitation for robotics. RoboTracer's architectural innovations (universal spatial encoder, scale decoder) and training methodology (scale regression supervision, metric-sensitive rewards) directly address this, providing a blueprint for enhancing VLMs with robust 3D spatial intelligence. This could lead to a new generation of VLMs that are inherently 3D-aware.

3. **Foundational Contributions to Data and Benchmarking:** The introduction of **TraceSpatial** and **TraceSpatial-Bench** is a monumental contribution.
 - **TraceSpatial** provides a rich, large-scale, and diverse dataset specifically designed for 3D spatial referring, measuring, and multi-step tracing. Its detailed annotations, including reasoning processes and metric-grounded information, are invaluable for training future models.
 - **TraceSpatial-Bench** fills a crucial void in evaluation by offering a challenging real-world benchmark for 3D spatial tracing, allowing for rigorous and fine-grained assessment of complex robotic capabilities. These resources will undoubtedly accelerate research and development in this domain.

4. **Advancing Reinforcement Fine-tuning (RFT) Methodologies:** The design of "metric-sensitive process rewards" for RFT is a notable methodological advancement. By supervising key intermediate perceptual cues in an order-invariant manner, RoboTracer shows how to effectively guide reinforcement learning for complex, compositional reasoning tasks, moving beyond generic outcome-based feedback. This approach could be adopted for other multi-step reasoning problems in AI.

5. **Practical Tool for Robotic Applications:** RoboTracer demonstrates strong generalization capabilities across diverse robots (UR5, G1 humanoid) and complex tasks in both simulation and the real world. Its ability to generate collision-free traces and adapt to dynamic scene changes at a reasonable frequency (1.5 Hz) positions it as a practical perception and planning module. This versatility suggests immediate applications in areas such as:
 - **Industrial Automation:** For tasks requiring precise manipulation and assembly.
 - **Service Robotics:** Enabling robots to perform household chores or assist in environments where precise object interaction is needed.
 - **Logistics and Warehousing:** For efficient object handling and stacking in cluttered spaces.
 - **Human-Robot Collaboration:** Allowing robots to understand and execute human instructions more naturally and safely.

While the authors acknowledge limitations such as slow inference for highly dynamic environments and the inherent challenges of scaling 3D metric-grounded capabilities in VLMs, RoboTracer lays a robust foundation. Its architectural innovations, comprehensive data strategy, and superior empirical performance mark it as a pioneering work that will likely inspire and guide future research towards more capable and spatially intelligent embodied AI systems.
