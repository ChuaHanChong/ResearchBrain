# Metamorphic Testing of Vision-Language Action-Enabled Robots

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Mondragon University, University of Seville, and Simula Research Laboratory applied Metamorphic Testing (MT) to Vision-Language-Action (VLA) robots, developing new trajectory-based metamorphic relations (MRs) to expose failures related to motion quality and manipulation robustness that traditional symbolic oracles often miss. Their method detected a substantial number of distinct behavioral inconsistencies, particularly with a medium strictness threshold, providing a more comprehensive evaluation of VLA model performance.

## Problem

- The "test oracle problem" makes it difficult to assess the correctness and quality of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) robot outputs in physical environments.
- Automated generation of symbolic test oracles for VLA models is complex and inflexible due to natural language variability and reliance on predefined templates.
- Existing symbolic oracles primarily evaluate task completion (final state) but overlook critical qualitative aspects like trajectory optimality, manipulation stability, or collision avoidance, missing significant failure classes.

## Method

- Applied ==Metamorphic Testing (MT)== as a complementary evaluation approach for VLA-enabled robots, focusing on verifying expected relationships (==metamorphic relations==) across systematically varied inputs.
- Introduced two novel, task-agnostic ==Metamorphic Relation Patterns (MRPs)==: ==Trajectory Consistency (TC)== for invariant behavior and ==Trajectory Variation (TV)== for predictably changed behavior.
- Derived five concrete ==Metamorphic Relations (MRs)== from these patterns, including synonym substitution, non-interfering object addition, light brightness change, task negation, and target object relocation.

## Results

- At a high strictness threshold, MT detected **3,527** failures exclusively, demonstrating its capability to identify subtle deviations beyond simple task non-completion.
- MT revealed a broader diversity of failure types, particularly in "Motion" (e.g., trajectory sub-optimality, control instability) and "Manipulation" (e.g., unstable grasping), which symbolic oracles largely overlooked.
- The study established that MT and symbolic oracles are complementary, with symbolic oracles assessing task-level correctness and MT exposing execution-level and robustness-related failures, thereby providing more comprehensive coverage.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Metamorphic Testing (MT) effectively detects failures in VLA-enabled robots, complementing symbolic oracles by exposing behavioral deviations often missed by traditional methods.
> - A medium strictness threshold for trajectory comparison provides a favorable balance, correlating with correctness failures while also exposing a considerable number of additional behavioral inconsistencies.
> - Metamorphic Relations based on environment-based transformations (e.g., adding non-interfering objects) and semantic transformations (e.g., task negation) are particularly effective in revealing VLA model weaknesses.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models represent an evolving paradigm in multimodal robotic control, designed to translate natural language instructions and visual observations into low-level control actions for robots operating in physical environments. These models are anticipated to facilitate the development of generalist robots capable of performing diverse tasks in unstructured settings. Despite advancements in VLA model efficiency and effectiveness, their evaluation primarily relies on benchmarks that utilize symbolic oracles.

Current symbolic oracles present two primary limitations within the research landscape. First, their automated generation is heavily dependent on specific task instructions and often requires predefined templates, making them inflexible to the variability of human language and potentially prone to issues like hallucinations if large language models are used for generation. Second, these oracles primarily assess the success of a task based on the final symbolic states of objects and the robot (e.g., whether an object has been picked up), but they fail to capture critical qualitative aspects of task execution. Such qualitative aspects include the optimality of the robot's trajectory, the stability and correctness of object manipulation, or the occurrence of collisions. Consequently, a range of failures related to motion quality, stability, and suboptimal behavior often remain undetected by conventional evaluation methods.

This paper addresses the "test oracle problem," a known challenge in software testing where determining the correctness of program outputs is difficult or impractical. Metamorphic Testing (MT) is a testing approach specifically designed to mitigate this problem by focusing on verifying expected relationships, known as metamorphic relations (MRs), across multiple executions under systematically varied but related inputs. While MT has been applied to various systems, including other autonomous systems like self-driving cars and general Cyber-Physical Systems (CPS), this work distinguishes itself by targeting end-to-end VLA-enabled robotic controllers rather than isolated components. Furthermore, it introduces trajectory-based and task-agnostic MRs, departing from task-specific symbolic oracles to capture qualitative and behavioral properties of robotic task execution. The research positions MT as a complementary approach to existing benchmark-based evaluations that typically report performance based on task success rates, aiming to provide a more comprehensive assessment of VLA behavior by uncovering execution-level and robustness-related failures.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation for this research stems from the inherent "test oracle problem" faced by Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models in robotics. While VLA models are designed to enable flexible and generalist robotic operation, their evaluation is challenging due to the difficulty in comprehensively assessing the correctness and quality of their outputs—the robot's sequence of actions and resulting trajectory.

The paper identifies two core issues with current evaluation practices, which primarily rely on symbolic oracles:
1. **Difficulty in Oracle Generation:** Generating symbolic test oracles automatically is complex and not easily generalizable across the highly variable ways humans instruct robots. Current methods often depend on predefined task and object templates, which limit their applicability to the diverse and often ambiguous nature of natural language commands.
2. **Limited Scope of Assessment:** Symbolic oracles assess task completion based on the final symbolic state (e.g., object in desired location). However, they fail to evaluate critical qualitative aspects of *how* a task is performed. These overlooked aspects include the efficiency or optimality of the robot's trajectory, the stability and safety of object manipulation (e.g., preventing drops or collisions), or the presence of suboptimal behaviors. This omission means that significant classes of failures related to motion quality, robustness, and safety remain undetected.

Given these limitations, the overarching objective of this research is to investigate whether Metamorphic Testing (MT) can alleviate the test oracle problem in the context of VLA-enabled robots.

To achieve this, the authors delineate several specific objectives:
- **Propose Novel Metamorphic Relation Patterns (MRPs):** The research aims to define two new MRPs, Trajectory Consistency (TC) and Trajectory Variation (TV), that specifically leverage the robotic trajectories produced by VLA models. These patterns are intended to guide the systematic derivation of concrete MRs for robotic systems.
- **Derive and Evaluate Specific Metamorphic Relations (MRs):** From the proposed MRPs, the paper intends to define five specific MRs. The objective is to empirically evaluate these MRs to assess their effectiveness in automatically detecting diverse types of failures in VLA-enabled robots, including those beyond simple task non-completion.
- **Assess Generalizability:** A key objective is to demonstrate that the proposed MRs and the MT approach are generalizable, meaning they can be applied across different VLA models, robot platforms, and tasks without requiring task-specific symbolic oracles.
- **Compare MT with Symbolic Oracles:** The study aims to analyze and compare the effectiveness of MT against traditional symbolic oracles in detecting failures. This includes investigating the overlap and distinct contributions of each approach to failure detection.
- **Characterize Failure Types:** The research seeks to identify and categorize the types of failures revealed by MT, particularly those that are not captured by symbolic oracles. This involves developing a taxonomy of VLA failures based on expert analysis to understand the nature of misbehaviors exposed by different evaluation methods.
- **Determine Complementarity:** Ultimately, the motivation is to establish whether MT serves as a complementary tool to symbolic oracles, providing a more comprehensive evaluation of VLA behavior that encompasses both task-level correctness and execution-level robustness.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research employs Metamorphic Testing (MT) to evaluate Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, specifically addressing the test oracle problem. The methodology involves defining novel metamorphic relation patterns (MRPs) and deriving concrete metamorphic relations (MRs) that focus on robotic trajectory analysis.

**A. Metamorphic Relation Patterns (MRPs)**
Two MRPs are introduced:
- **Trajectory Consistency Pattern (TC):** This pattern includes input transformations that should ideally *not* affect the robot's trajectory. It assesses the VLA model's robustness to superficial or semantically irrelevant changes.
- **Trajectory Variation Pattern (TV):** This pattern encompasses input transformations that are expected to induce *predictable changes* in the robot's trajectory. It evaluates the VLA model's adaptability and reasoning capabilities in response to meaningful modifications.

**B. Derived Metamorphic Relations (MRs)**
Five specific MRs are derived:
- **From TC:**
 - **MR1: Synonym Substitution:** The action verb in the prompt is replaced with a synonym (e.g., "Pick the apple" → "Grab the apple"). The expectation is that the Fréchet distance ($d_F$) between the source and follow-up trajectories ($\tau(P_s, E)$ and $\tau(P_f, E)$) should be less than or equal to a defined tolerance $\delta$ ($d_F(\tau(P_s, E), \tau(P_f, E)) \lesssim \delta$).
 - **MR2: Non-Interfering Object Addition:** An irrelevant object is added to the environment far from the target object. The expectation is that the trajectory remains similar to the original one ($d_F(\tau(P, E_s), \tau(P, E_f)) \lesssim \delta$), where $E_s$ and $E_f$ are the source and follow-up environments.
 - **MR3: Light Brightness Change:** The scene illumination is altered slightly. The expectation is that the trajectory remains consistent ($d_F(\tau(P, E_s), \tau(P, E_f)) \lesssim \delta$).
- **From TV:**
 - **MR4: Negation or Task Inversion:** The instruction prompt is modified to negate the original task (e.g., "Pick the apple" → "Don’t pick the apple"). The expectation is a significant change in trajectory or refusal to move, meaning the trajectory distance should be greater than a minimum threshold $\delta$ ($d_F(\tau(P_s, E), \tau(P_f, E)) \gtrsim \delta$).
 - **MR5: Target Object Relocation:** The target object's position is shifted. The expectation is that the robot's trajectory adapts to reach the new location proportionally to the object's translation. This is formalized with lower and upper proportionality bounds, $\alpha$ and $\beta$, relating the trajectory distance to the magnitude of object translation $||\Delta p||$: $\alpha||\Delta p|| \lesssim d_F(\tau(P, E(p_s)), \tau(P, E(p_f))) \lesssim \beta||\Delta p||$.

**C. Empirical Study Setup**
- **Research Questions (RQs):** The study was designed to answer three RQs:
 - RQ1: Effectiveness of MT at detecting failures in VLA-enabled robots.
 - RQ2: Differences in failure detection ability among different MRs.
 - RQ3: Types of failures revealed by MT compared to symbolic oracles.
- **Subject VLA Models:** Five state-of-the-art VLA models were evaluated: OpenVLA, π0, SpatialVLA, GR00T-N1.5, and EO-1. These models were selected for their compatibility with SimplerEnv and their use in related prior work.
- **Environments and Tasks:** Experiments were conducted on the SimplerEnv benchmark using two robotic platforms (Google Robot and WidowX) and four standard manipulation tasks (Pick Up, Move Near, Put On, Put In).
- **Test Case Generation:** A total of 1,864 "source" test cases were selected, defined as those successfully completed according to a symbolic oracle. From these, 9,320 "follow-up" test cases were generated based on the proposed MRs, ensuring that symbolic oracles were also executed on these follow-up cases.
- **Trajectory Comparison:** The Fréchet distance was used to measure the similarity or dissimilarity between source and follow-up trajectories.
- **Strictness Thresholds:** Three percentile-based tolerance thresholds (0.1m for strict, 0.2m for medium, 0.3m for low) were calibrated to define MR violations.
- **Execution Platform:** Experiments were distributed across two computing platforms with different CPU/GPU configurations, running Ubuntu 20.04 LTS, Python 3.10, and CUDA 12.8.
- **Replication Package:** A complete replication package including code and configuration files was provided to ensure reproducibility.

**D. Failure Characterization and Taxonomy Development**
- **Sample Selection:** From 7,899 detected failures, a statistically representative sample of 192 failures was selected using Cochran’s sample size test (p < 0.05). This sample maximized diversity across VLA models, MRs, and task types.
- **Expert Annotation:** Two experienced domain experts analyzed the selected failures, using a web-based application that provided videos of source and follow-up task executions. They described the failure types in natural language.
- **Taxonomy Development:** The first author developed an initial taxonomy based on recurring failure types, which was then validated and refined by another author. Large Language Models (LLMs) like ChatGPT, Gemini, and Grok were used as assistants to suggest refinements, with final decisions made by the human authors to mitigate risks of hallucination. The resulting taxonomy categorized VLA failures into Manipulation, Motion, and Planning and Reasoning, with further sub-categorizations.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The empirical study produced detailed findings regarding the effectiveness of Metamorphic Testing (MT) for Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, the comparative performance of different Metamorphic Relations (MRs), and the distinct types of failures revealed.

**A. RQ1 – Effectiveness of MT at Detecting Failures**
The study assessed MT's effectiveness by comparing its failure detection capabilities with symbolic oracles across three strictness thresholds:
- **High Strictness Threshold (0.1m):** MT detected a substantial number of violations. Out of 7,822 total failures, 3,527 were detected exclusively by MRs, 3,655 by both MRs and symbolic oracles, and 640 exclusively by symbolic oracles. This indicates that a high strictness level allows MT to identify numerous subtle deviations and significantly complements symbolic oracles.
- **Medium Strictness Threshold (0.2m):** At this threshold, the total number of failures decreased to 6,286. Failures were distributed more evenly: 1,991 detected only by MRs, 2,519 by both, and 1,776 only by symbolic oracles. This suggests that the medium strictness level provides a balance, with MT violations aligning more closely with correctness failures while still identifying behavioral inconsistencies.
- **Low Strictness Threshold (0.3m):** The total detected failures dropped to 5,177. At this level, 888 failures were detected only by MRs, 1,418 by both, and 2,877 only by symbolic oracles. This indicates that with low strictness, MT primarily reinforces symbolic oracle detections rather than exposing many new behavioral deviations.

**Answer to RQ1:** Metamorphic Testing is effective in detecting failures in VLA-enabled robots, and its detection capability is significantly influenced by the chosen strictness threshold. A medium strictness threshold offers a favorable trade-off by correlating with failures identified by symbolic oracles while also exposing a considerable number of additional failure cases.

**B. RQ2 – Comparison of MRs**
The study analyzed the violation rates of each proposed MR across different models, tasks, and strictness levels:
- **High Strictness Threshold:** All MRs showed elevated violation rates. MR2 (Non-Interfering Object Addition) generally exhibited the highest violation rate across most tasks and VLA models. This suggests that even seemingly irrelevant environmental additions can lead to performance degradation in applications requiring high precision.
- **Medium Strictness Threshold:** Violation rates decreased, but differences among MRs persisted. MR2 remained highly effective, particularly for "grasp" and "put-in" tasks. MR4 (Negation or Task Inversion) also showed high violation rates, indicating issues in semantic understanding of instructions. MR1 (Synonym Substitution) and MR3 (Light Brightness Change) showed more moderate rates, implying some robustness but also revealing inconsistencies.
- **Low Strictness Threshold:** Violation rates were further reduced, highlighting only the most significant deviations. MR2 consistently showed the highest violation rate in a majority of cases (14 out of 20), emphasizing its effectiveness in detecting failures related to environmental object interference. MR3 and MR4 also proved notable in specific contexts, revealing weaknesses in environmental perception and instruction adaptation, respectively.

**Answer to RQ2:** Environment-based transformations (MR2) and semantic transformations (MR4) are generally the most effective MRs at exposing failures across VLA models and tasks. Robustness-oriented MRs (MR1 and MR3) demonstrate sensitivity to the selected strictness threshold. The findings indicate that combining multiple MRs is beneficial for a comprehensive assessment of VLA-enabled robotic behavior.

**C. RQ3 – Failure Type Characterization**
The research characterized the types of failures detected by MT and symbolic oracles using a developed taxonomy, which organized failures into three high-level categories: Manipulation, Motion, and Planning and Reasoning. Each category was further refined into specific failure types (e.g., Grasping Stability, Trajectory Sub-optimality, Collision, Incomplete Task Execution).
- **Failures Detected by Symbolic Oracles:** These primarily concentrated on the "Planning and Reasoning" category. They frequently identified "Incomplete Task Execution" and "Incorrect Action Sequence" failures, which denote instances where the robot failed to achieve the specified final symbolic goal state. However, symbolic oracles often missed failures that did not impact the final state, such as unstable grasps, minor collisions, or inefficient trajectories.
- **Failures Detected by Metamorphic Testing:** MT revealed a broader diversity of failure types, with a strong presence in the "Motion" and "Manipulation" categories. MT violations frequently exposed "Trajectory Sub-optimality," "Control Instability," and "Collision" failures. For example, MR2 (Non-Interfering Object Addition) identified unnecessary trajectory deviations due to irrelevant objects, indicating sensitivity to non-task-related environmental changes. MR5 (Target Object Relocation) revealed issues where the robot failed to adapt its motion consistently to new target positions, exposing deficiencies in planning and motion safety. These failures are inherently behavioral and cannot be captured by binary symbolic goal conditions.
- **Complementarity:** Failures detected by both oracle types typically represented severe issues impacting both task completion and execution behavior. However, a significant portion of failures was detected exclusively by one oracle type. Symbolic oracles primarily assess *what* the robot accomplishes, whereas MT assesses *how* the robot behaves.

**Answer to RQ3:** Metamorphic Testing uncovers a more diverse array of failure types, particularly those related to motion quality and manipulation robustness, which are largely invisible to symbolic oracles. While symbolic oracles effectively identify task-level failures, MT exposes execution-level and behavioral failures crucial for evaluating the reliability and safety of VLA-enabled robotic systems. This demonstrates that combining both symbolic oracles and MT is necessary for comprehensive failure coverage.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research makes several contributions to the field of robotic systems and software testing, with potential implications for the development and deployment of VLA-enabled robots.

First, the work directly addresses and provides a demonstrated solution to the **test oracle problem** within the context of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. This problem is a fundamental challenge in assessing the correctness of complex AI systems, especially those operating in physical environments. By proposing and validating Metamorphic Testing (MT) as a complementary approach, the paper offers a method to evaluate VLA models even in the absence of explicit, comprehensive correctness criteria.

Second, the study introduces a **more comprehensive evaluation methodology** for VLA-enabled robots. Existing approaches predominantly focus on task-level correctness, typically measured by binary success or failure outcomes from symbolic oracles. This research expands evaluation to encompass qualitative aspects of task execution, such as motion quality, manipulation robustness, and perceptual grounding. Detecting failures related to suboptimal trajectories, control instability, and collisions, which are often missed by traditional methods, is critical for understanding the real-world performance and safety of robotic systems.

Third, the proposed Metamorphic Relation Patterns (MRPs) and specific Metamorphic Relations (MRs) are designed to be **generalizable and task-agnostic**. This attribute significantly enhances their reusability and scalability across different VLA models, diverse robotic platforms, and a wide array of manipulation tasks. This generalizability reduces the effort required to define new test oracles for each specific task or robot, facilitating broader applicability of the testing approach.

Fourth, by identifying and characterizing a broader range of failure types, particularly those related to **execution-level behavior and robustness**, this work contributes to building more reliable and safer VLA-enabled robotic systems. Understanding *how* a robot fails, not just *if* it fails, provides valuable insights for debugging, model refinement, and ensuring predictable behavior in complex, unstructured environments. The developed failure taxonomy serves as a structured framework for categorizing and analyzing these nuanced misbehaviors.

Finally, the empirical findings provide **actionable guidance for testers and developers** of VLA models. The comparative analysis of MRs indicates which types of input transformations (e.g., environment-based additions or semantic negations) are most effective at revealing failures. This information can inform the design of more targeted and efficient testing strategies. The emphasis on combining symbolic oracles with MT underscores that a multi-faceted approach is necessary for a thorough assessment of VLA behavior, ensuring both task achievement and quality of execution. The provision of a complete replication package further supports future research and validation in the community.
