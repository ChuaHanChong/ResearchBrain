# DV-SFT: Direct Vision Supervision for Fine-Grained Visual Understanding

> [!summary] Summary
> DV-SFT introduces a method for directly supervising visual tokens in multimodal large language models using explicit word-based labels, which enhances fine-grained visual understanding and multimodal alignment without requiring architectural modifications. The approach consistently improves performance across various visual question answering and OCR-related tasks, including out-of-domain benchmarks, while maintaining general visual capabilities.

## Problem

- Multimodal large language models (MLLMs) implicitly optimize visual tokens through text generation, leading to coarse-grained visual understanding and potential visual hallucination.
- Existing visual supervision methods often require architectural modifications or additional forward passes, complicating training and limiting practical applicability.
- A lack of precise, explicit, token-level supervision for visual information hinders fine-grained understanding and efficient multimodal alignment in MLLMs.

## Method

- **DV-SFT** introduces Direct Vision Supervised Fine-Tuning, applying explicit, token-level supervision to visual tokens by assigning word-based labels that directly describe their corresponding image patches.
- Fine-grained vision labels are constructed using both image-to-label (OCR processing and alignment) and label-to-image (synthetic document rendering) approaches to establish precise word-to-visual-token mappings.
- A "Vision Smoothing" technique is integrated into the loss function to account for the bidirectional nature of visual encoders, allowing visual tokens to learn from contextual information beyond their immediate patch.

## Results

- DV-SFT consistently outperformed standard SFT and representation-based supervision methods on in-domain benchmarks (DocVQA, InfographicVQA, SQuAD), demonstrating strong generalization to out-of-domain tasks (ChartQA, OCRBench, OCRVQA, TextVQA).
- The approach achieved superior character recognition abilities on both contextual and non-contextual plain text OCR tasks, with performance gains observed even at varying image resolutions.
- Despite being trained primarily on OCR-related data, DV-SFT maintained or slightly improved general visual capabilities on the MME benchmark, indicating that fine-grained understanding generalizes effectively to broader visual scenes.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Direct, interpretable word-based supervision for visual tokens effectively enhances fine-grained visual understanding and improves multimodal alignment efficiency in MLLMs.
> - Vision Smoothing is crucial for accommodating the contextual nature of visual encoders, preventing overly restrictive learning and boosting overall performance.
> - The method's architectural simplicity, requiring no modifications or extra forward passes, enables seamless integration into existing MLLM training pipelines.
