# DV-SFT: Direct Vision Supervision for Fine-Grained Visual Understanding

> [!summary] Summary
> DV-SFT introduces a method for directly supervising visual tokens in multimodal large language models using explicit word-based labels, which enhances fine-grained visual understanding and multimodal alignment without requiring architectural modifications. The approach consistently improves performance across various visual question answering and OCR-related tasks, including out-of-domain benchmarks, while maintaining general visual capabilities.

## Problem

- Multimodal large language models (MLLMs) implicitly optimize visual tokens through text generation, leading to coarse-grained visual understanding and potential visual hallucination.
- Existing visual supervision methods often require architectural modifications or additional forward passes, complicating training and limiting practical applicability.
- A lack of precise, explicit, token-level supervision for visual information hinders fine-grained understanding and efficient multimodal alignment in MLLMs.

## Method

- **DV-SFT** introduces Direct Vision Supervised Fine-Tuning, applying explicit, token-level supervision to visual tokens by assigning word-based labels that directly describe their corresponding image patches.
- Fine-grained vision labels are constructed using both image-to-label (OCR processing and alignment) and label-to-image (synthetic document rendering) approaches to establish precise word-to-visual-token mappings.
- A "Vision Smoothing" technique is integrated into the loss function to account for the bidirectional nature of visual encoders, allowing visual tokens to learn from contextual information beyond their immediate patch.

## Results

- DV-SFT consistently outperformed standard SFT and representation-based supervision methods on in-domain benchmarks (DocVQA, InfographicVQA, SQuAD), demonstrating strong generalization to out-of-domain tasks (ChartQA, OCRBench, OCRVQA, TextVQA).
- The approach achieved superior character recognition abilities on both contextual and non-contextual plain text OCR tasks, with performance gains observed even at varying image resolutions.
- Despite being trained primarily on OCR-related data, DV-SFT maintained or slightly improved general visual capabilities on the MME benchmark, indicating that fine-grained understanding generalizes effectively to broader visual scenes.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Direct, interpretable word-based supervision for visual tokens effectively enhances fine-grained visual understanding and improves multimodal alignment efficiency in MLLMs.
> - Vision Smoothing is crucial for accommodating the contextual nature of visual encoders, preventing overly restrictive learning and boosting overall performance.
> - The method's architectural simplicity, requiring no modifications or extra forward passes, enables seamless integration into existing MLLM training pipelines.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Multimodal large language models (MLLMs) integrate vision encoders with large language models, enabling them to process and understand both visual and textual inputs. These models are typically trained end-to-end using a next-token prediction objective, where supervision signals are exclusively applied to text tokens. Visual tokens, which carry the core visual information, are optimized implicitly as part of the context. This indirect optimization can lead to coarse-grained visual understanding, potential hallucination, and a lack of precision in handling fine-grained visual details.

The current research landscape is actively exploring methods to introduce more direct supervision for visual tokens to enhance the precision of visual understanding in MLLMs. Existing approaches can be broadly categorized into several paradigms:
1. **Indirect Semantic Guidance via Reward Signals:** These methods use reward functions to encourage visually consistent outputs, but the supervision remains indirect.
2. **Representation Alignment:** Approaches in this category distill high-quality features to supervise the forward pass of visual tokens. However, the interpretability of continuous representations can introduce noise and limit reliability.
3. **Next-Visual-Token Prediction:** By treating visual tokens discretely, these methods apply autoregressive generation tasks to supervise them. A notable limitation is the potential misalignment between a generative objective and the goal of visual understanding.
4. **Reconstructing Visual Input:** These studies aim to supervise visual token understanding by reconstructing the original visual input from visual representations. A challenge here is the conflict between pixel-level and semantic features, which can result in suboptimal learning outcomes.

A common limitation across many of these existing methods is the requirement for architectural modifications, such as additional decoders, visual tokenizers, or extra forward passes. These modifications can complicate training, increase computational overhead, and restrict their practical applicability to existing MLLM pipelines.

This paper addresses these limitations by proposing a method that applies direct, explicit, token-level supervision to visual tokens without requiring any architectural changes or additional forward passes. The work is inspired by observations that visual logits in end-to-end trained MLLMs inherently contain semantic information about their corresponding image patches. By focusing on OCR-related scenarios where vision-text correspondence is clear, the authors aim to establish a straightforward and practical approach to improve fine-grained visual understanding and multimodal alignment, thereby filling a gap for a more direct, intuitive, and architecturally simple form of vision supervision.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind the proposed DV-SFT method are rooted in addressing the limitations of current MLLM training paradigms regarding visual understanding:

- **Addressing Implicit Visual Token Optimization:** MLLMs conventionally apply explicit supervision solely to text tokens. Visual tokens, despite being the carriers of visual information, are optimized only implicitly through the context of text generation. This leads to a coarse-grained understanding of visual details, potentially causing MLLMs to overlook fine-grained information or engage in visual hallucination. The core motivation is to move beyond this implicit optimization to explicit, direct supervision for visual tokens.

- **Enhancing Fine-Grained Visual Understanding:** The researchers hypothesize that explicitly supervising visual tokens with semantic labels directly describing their corresponding image patches can substantially improve fine-grained visual understanding. This is crucial for tasks requiring precise interpretation of visual content, such as reading text in images, analyzing documents, or interpreting charts.

- **Developing an Architecturally Simple and Unified Training Paradigm:** Many prior attempts to supervise visual inputs rely on auxiliary components (e.g., additional decoders, visual tokenizers, or extra forward passes), which complicates model architecture and training, hindering practical adoption. A key objective of this work is to develop a direct vision supervision method that requires no architectural modifications and maintains a fully unified, end-to-end training paradigm identical to standard next-token prediction, treating the MLLM as a black box. This aims to maximize practical applicability and minimize implementation complexity.

- **Exploiting Direct Vision-Text Correspondence:** The work is motivated by empirical observations that visual tokens, even without dedicated visual supervision, can extract semantic information from their associated image patches, where top words in visual logits often describe the content. This suggests that a direct vision-text correspondence can be leveraged for supervision. OCR-related scenarios are specifically chosen as a testbed due to their straightforward and explicit alignment between visual content (words in an image) and textual labels, which facilitates the construction of fine-grained vision labels.

- **Improving Multimodal Alignment Efficiency:** By providing direct supervision to visual tokens, the objective is to enable MLLMs to align visual and textual features more efficiently and effectively, especially during early training stages or with limited data, leading to a more robust and generalized visual understanding capability.

In summary, the motivation is to overcome the limitations of implicit visual token supervision in MLLMs by introducing a direct, explicit, and architecturally simple method that leverages inherent vision-text correspondences to foster fine-grained visual understanding and improve multimodal alignment.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, Direct Vision Supervised Fine-Tuning (DV-SFT), introduces explicit, token-level supervision for visual tokens by assigning words that describe image patches as direct labels. This supervision is integrated into the existing next-token prediction objective of MLLMs without requiring architectural modifications or additional forward passes.

#### 3.1. Core Principle

DV-SFT operates under the principle that MLLMs produce logits for all tokens, including visual tokens. By leveraging the observation that visual logits contain semantic features reflecting the content of corresponding image patches, the method aims to explicitly supervise these visual tokens. The objective is to train the model to predict the word present in a visual token's image patch, alongside its standard text generation task.

#### 3.2. Vision Label Construction

The central challenge is to establish a fine-grained, one-to-one mapping between words in an image and specific visual tokens. This is primarily addressed in OCR-related scenarios due to their direct vision-text correspondence. Two complementary approaches are employed:

- **Image-to-Label:**
 1. **OCR Processing:** A text detection model and a text recognition model (e.g., PaddleOCR) are used to perform word-level OCR on an image. This identifies all words and their precise spatial locations.
 2. **Filtering:** Word concatenation errors are identified and discarded if their tokenization length exceeds three. Words with heights exceeding three times the patch size are also discarded to maintain annotation precision.
 3. **Alignment:** Each detected word is aligned to the visual token corresponding to its bottom-right corner. This strategy is chosen due to the autoregressive nature of MLLMs.
 4. **Conflict Resolution:** If multiple vision labels are assigned to the same visual token, they are filtered to ensure a single, unambiguous label, preventing confusion.

- **Label-to-Image:**
 1. **Dataset Conversion:** This approach converts unimodal document question-answering datasets (e.g., SQuAD) into a multimodal format.
 2. **Image Synthesis:** Drawing tools are used to render documents by individually aligning each word onto the patch grid of a blank image. This allows for precise control over the visual token-to-word mapping.
 3. **Autoregressive Assignment:** Similar to the image-to-label method, words spanning multiple visual tokens are assigned as labels only to the last visual token in their span, consistent with the autoregressive property.
 4. **Data Filtering:** For the SQuAD dataset, question-answer pairs with weak relevance to the document or low fluency (measured by LLM perplexity) are filtered out to strengthen the model's reliance on visual information. Diverse format-control instructions are also randomly incorporated.

#### 3.3. Vision Smoothing

The visual encoder in MLLMs often exhibits bidirectional characteristics, meaning visual information can interact across tokens. Consequently, a single visual token may implicitly contain semantic information from adjacent or subsequent tokens. To account for this contextual nature and prevent overly restrictive learning, the Vision Smoothing method is introduced, inspired by label smoothing.
- The vision loss function is modified to allow a visual token to learn its own designated vision label while also distributing some probability mass to other vision labels present within the same image.
- The modified loss function, $L_v$, combines the standard next-token prediction loss for the assigned label $w_i$ with a term that encourages a uniform probability distribution over all other vision labels $t \in V, t \neq w_i$ in the image. This is controlled by a smoothing factor $\beta$.

#### 3.4. Overall Loss Function

The final training objective for DV-SFT combines the standard language modeling loss for text tokens ($L_t$) with the newly introduced vision loss for visual tokens ($L_v$).
- $L = L_t + \lambda L_v$, where $\lambda$ is a hyperparameter that balances the contribution of vision supervision relative to text supervision. The loss on visual tokens is computed using only the first token after tokenizing the vision label, aligning with the first-token preference property of visual logits.

#### 3.5. Experimental Setup

- **Datasets:** DocVQA, InfographicVQA (image-to-label); SQuAD (label-to-image).
- **Benchmarks:**
 - **In-domain:** DocVQA test, InfographicVQA test, SQuAD validation.
 - **Out-of-domain:** ChartQA test, OCRBench, OCRVQA test, TextVQA validation.
 - **Auxiliary:** MME (general visual capability), custom plain text OCR tasks (Contextual and Non-Contextual).
- **Baselines:** Standard Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) (text-only supervision), and a re-implemented BASIC (self-distilled representation supervision).
- **Base Models:** Qwen3-VL-2B/8B-Instruct (pre-trained MLLMs), and Qwen3-1.7B (a unimodal LLM fine-tuned from scratch with a Qwen3-VL-2B visual encoder).
- **Training Details:** Vision encoder parameters are frozen. Qwen3-VL-2B and Qwen3-1.7B are fully fine-tuned; Qwen3-VL-8B is fine-tuned using LoRA. $\beta$ is set to 0.3, and $\lambda$ to 2e-3.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted demonstrate the efficacy of DV-SFT across various benchmarks, highlighting improvements in fine-grained visual understanding and multimodal alignment.

#### 4.1. In-Domain Performance

DV-SFT consistently outperformed standard SFT across all three in-domain benchmarks (DocVQA, InfographicVQA, SQuAD) for all tested base models (Qwen3-VL-2B, Qwen3-VL-8B, Qwen3-1.7B). For instance, with Qwen3-VL-2B, DV-SFT achieved an average accuracy of 84.47%, surpassing SFT's 84.06%. Against BASIC, a representation supervision method, DV-SFT showed more substantial gains, particularly with the 2B model where BASIC experienced performance degradation. This indicates that the direct and interpretable nature of DV-SFT's supervisory signal aligns more effectively with the intrinsic characteristics of language models compared to indirect representation-based supervision.

#### 4.2. Out-of-Domain Performance

DV-SFT also demonstrated consistent advantages on out-of-domain tasks (ChartQA, OCRBench, OCRVQA, TextVQA), suggesting strong domain generalization capabilities. While the Qwen3-VL-2B model showed improvements (e.g., average accuracy of 75.86% for DV-SFT vs. 75.45% for SFT), these were more pronounced and consistent with the larger Qwen3-VL-8B model (e.g., average accuracy of 81.85% for DV-SFT vs. 81.71% for SFT). This pattern suggests that DV-SFT enhances the model's fundamental visual capabilities rather than merely improving performance on specific training tasks.

#### 4.3. Multimodal Alignment Efficiency

An analysis using the unimodal Qwen3-1.7B model, which learns visual features from scratch, revealed that vision supervision offers comprehensive advantages over text-only supervision, especially with limited training data. DV-SFT achieved a higher average improvement on out-of-domain tasks (1.6%) compared to in-domain tasks (0.74%), further supporting the idea that direct vision supervision genuinely enhances visual understanding. Furthermore, in the early stages of training, vision supervision exhibited a more pronounced advantage, particularly on out-of-domain tasks, indicating higher multimodal alignment efficiency.

#### 4.4. Character Recognition Ability

To specifically evaluate improvements in character recognition, DV-SFT was tested on plain text OCR tasks (Contextual and Non-Contextual). DV-SFT achieved superior performance on both tasks, with the advantage being amplified on the Non-Contextual OCR task. This indicates that direct vision supervision enhances the model's fine-grained visual understanding beyond mere task performance. Tests at different image resolutions showed that DV-SFT consistently outperformed SFT, implying that the model effectively acquires visual features rather than mechanically learning word predictions in a one-to-one manner.

#### 4.5. General Visual Capability

Despite being trained primarily on OCR-related scenarios, DV-SFT's impact on general visual understanding was assessed using the MME benchmark, which comprises diverse visual perception and cognition tasks. The results showed that purely word-based vision supervision did not degrade the model's general visual capabilities; instead, it slightly improved overall performance (e.g., on Qwen3-VL-8B, DV-SFT F1 was 88.47 vs SFT F1 88.46). This suggests that the fine-grained visual understanding mechanisms developed through direct vision supervision generalize effectively from text-centric visual tasks to broader visual scenes. Case studies of visual logits in general scenes also indicated that DV-SFT enabled more concrete semantic understanding of visual tokens.

#### 4.6. Ablation Study

- **Vision Supervision ($\lambda$):** The experiments confirmed that incorporating vision supervision consistently improved performance. However, excessively large weights for vision supervision led to negative effects, attributed to factors such as label imbalance (vision labels significantly outnumber text labels), initial gradient discrepancy (vision loss being much higher), and potential conflict with the MLLM's ultimate task objective of generating human-like responses. Proper balancing of the vision loss weight was found to be crucial.
- **Vision Smoothing ($\beta$):** Vision smoothing was found to be critical for the effectiveness of DV-SFT. Without it, the performance gains over SFT were limited. This is due to the bidirectional nature of the visual encoder, which causes visual tokens to contain contextual information beyond their immediate patch. Vision smoothing accommodates this global nature, allowing visual tokens to learn not only their own specific label but also other relevant labels within the image, leading to more effective supervision.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces DV-SFT, a method for direct vision supervision in MLLMs, with several significant contributions and potential impacts:

#### 5.1. Significance

- **Novel Direct Supervision Paradigm:** DV-SFT pioneers a direct, token-level supervision approach for visual tokens in MLLMs. Unlike previous methods, it explicitly constructs interpretable, word-based labels for visual tokens, directly aligning visual information with textual semantics.
- **Architectural Simplicity and Integration:** A key strength of DV-SFT is its seamless integration into existing MLLM architectures. It operates as a black box, requiring no modifications to the model architecture or additional forward passes, thus maintaining a unified, end-to-end training paradigm. This addresses a major limitation of many prior vision supervision techniques.
- **Enhanced Fine-Grained Visual Understanding:** The empirical results consistently demonstrate that DV-SFT improves MLLMs' ability to process fine-grained visual details, particularly evident in character recognition tasks and across various document and chart understanding benchmarks. This precision can mitigate issues like visual hallucination.
- **Improved Multimodal Alignment Efficiency:** The study shows that direct vision supervision leads to more efficient and effective alignment between visual and textual modalities, especially when models are learning visual features from scratch or in early training phases.
- **Generalization Beyond OCR:** While developed within OCR-related scenarios, the method's ability to generalize to broader visual scenes and improve general visual capabilities (as shown by the MME benchmark) suggests that this form of direct supervision fosters a fundamental semantic understanding of visual tokens, not just task-specific memorization.

#### 5.2. Potential Impact

- **Practical Applicability for MLLM Development:** The architectural simplicity and black-box nature of DV-SFT make it highly practical and readily applicable to a wide range of existing MLLM pipelines. This could accelerate the development and deployment of MLLMs with improved visual understanding.
- **Foundation for Future Vision Supervision:** DV-SFT offers a new perspective on MLLM training paradigms, moving beyond purely implicit visual token optimization. It lays groundwork for future research to explore direct vision supervision in more complex and diverse visual domains, potentially leading to more robust and accurate MLLMs across various tasks.
- **Reduced Visual Hallucination:** By providing explicit semantic anchors to visual tokens, DV-SFT could contribute to models that are less prone to visual hallucination, producing responses that are more faithful to the observed image content.
- **Advancements in Document AI and Visual QA:** The significant performance gains in tasks like DocVQA, InfographicVQA, and OCR benchmarks suggest a direct impact on document understanding, visual question answering, and other applications that require precise extraction and interpretation of text and information from images.
- **Deeper Understanding of MLLM Learning:** The work contributes to the scientific understanding of how MLLMs acquire and represent visual semantics, particularly highlighting the role of direct, interpretable supervision signals in shaping fine-grained visual representations.
