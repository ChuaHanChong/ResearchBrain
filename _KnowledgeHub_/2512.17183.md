# Semantic Co-Speech Gesture Synthesis and Real-Time Control for Humanoid Robots

> [!summary] Summary
> An end-to-end pipeline that turns speech into semantically meaningful co-speech gestures executed live on a Unitree G1 humanoid. A residual VQ-VAE tokenizes body and hand motion, an audio-conditioned autoregressive Motion-GPT with LLM-based semantic gesture retrieval generates tokens, General Motion Retargeting bridges the human-robot embodiment gap, and a MotionTracker imitation-learning policy tracks the decoded reference motion while keeping balance.

## Problem

- Semantic gestures are sparse in speech-gesture datasets, so data-driven generators treat them as noise and produce only rhythmic beat gestures lacking meaning.
- Prior gesture work targets virtual characters, stopping at animation and never closing the loop to real-time physical execution on a balancing humanoid.
- Poor retargeting from human skeletons introduces ground penetration, self-intersection, and velocity spikes that degrade downstream control policy robustness.

## Method

- ==Residual VQ-VAE== motion codebook splits body and hand streams and stacks residual quantization layers, expanding capacity for fine detail without codebook collapse.
- Autoregressive ==GPT-2== **Motion-GPT** predicts hand and body tokens from audio features (MFCC, delta, chromagram, onset, tempogram), fused at latent level with LLM-retrieved semantic gesture candidates.
- **MotionTracker** imitation-learning policy predicts ==residual PD offsets== relative to the reference motion, tanh-mapped and per-joint scaled, easing optimization over 29 DoFs.

## Results

- Gesture generation RMSE against ground truth is very low at the root (root_pos_x **0.000592**, root_pos_y **0.000783**, root_pos_z **0.000329**), with wrist joints highest at roughly **0.20-0.23**.
- Simulation tracking RMSE stays small across all DoFs, for example right hip roll **0.0064** and root_pos_z **0.0050**, with worst joints near **0.09-0.10**.
- RVQ-VAE reconstructions track ground-truth joint trajectories closely over a **120-second** sequence, and the physical G1 executes synthesized gestures while maintaining balance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Predicting residual PD offsets around a reference trajectory, rather than absolute targets, compacts the action distribution and makes high-DoF whole-body tracking tractable.
> - Retrieval augmentation is a practical fix for long-tail behaviors: an LLM supplies sparse semantic gestures that a purely generative model would average away.
> - Evaluation here is RMSE and qualitative demos only, with no user study, baselines, or success rates, so expressiveness claims stay unquantified.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented by Gang Zhang integrates two traditionally distinct but converging fields: **co-speech gesture generation** and **humanoid robot motion control**. This paper positions itself at the intersection of these domains, aiming to bridge the gap between abstract communicative intent and concrete physical execution on a robot.

#### 1.1. Co-Speech Gesture Generation Landscape

Historically, co-speech gesture synthesis began with **rule-based approaches**, which were labor-intensive and produced stiff, unnatural movements. The field then transitioned to **data-driven methods**, initially employing statistical models and later deep neural networks (MLPs, CNNs, RNNs, Transformers). These deep learning approaches largely excelled at generating **beat gestures** (rhythmic movements synchronized with speech tempo) but struggled significantly with **semantic gestures** (gestures conveying specific meaning) due to their sparsity in training datasets.

Recent advancements, which this work draws upon, have addressed the semantic gesture challenge through several avenues:
- **Data balancing techniques:** Clustering and resampling to give more weight to sparse semantic gestures.
- **Retrieval augmentation:** Utilizing large language models (LLMs) to retrieve contextually relevant semantic gesture candidates from external libraries based on speech transcripts, as seen in works like "Semantic Gesticulator". This approach allows for richer, more varied semantic gestures.
- **Generative models:** Employing VAEs, VQ-VAEs, and diffusion models to mitigate issues like "mean collapse" and generate diverse outputs, a strategy evident in the paper's use of Residual VQ-VAE and Motion-GPT.
- **Zero-shot learning:** Enabling style-controlled gesture generation without explicit style-specific training data.

This paper advances the gesture generation aspect by explicitly leveraging an **LLM-based retrieval mechanism** within an autoregressive Motion-GPT framework, ensuring the synthesis of both rhythmically coherent and semantically rich gestures.

#### 1.2. Humanoid Robot Motion Control Landscape

Humanoid motion control, particularly motion tracking, focuses on enabling robots to reproduce human-like movements expressively while maintaining balance and agility. Key challenges include the **cross-embodiment gap** (differences in kinematics and morphology between humans and robots), **physical actuator limits**, and the notorious **sim-to-real transfer problem**.

Existing research in this area explores:
- **Foundational motion trackers:** Algorithms designed to handle diverse, highly dynamic, and contact-rich movements, often across extensive datasets like AMASS.
- **Robustness and adaptation:** Addressing real-world disturbances such as varying terrains, external forces, and changes in physical properties (e.g., payloads). Approaches include domain randomization and more advanced "two-stage frameworks" that decouple basic motion execution from dynamics adaptability, such as Any2Track.
- **Imitation learning (IL) and reinforcement learning (RL):** These paradigms are widely used to train controllers that learn complex behaviors from demonstrations or through trial and error in simulated environments, then transfer these skills to physical robots.

This work contributes significantly to humanoid control by implementing a robust **Imitation Learning control policy, MotionTracker**, specifically designed for the Unitree G1 robot. It builds upon contemporary methods for general motion tracking and demonstrates successful real-world deployment, effectively bridging the simulation-to-reality gap for complex, expressive movements. The use of a **General Motion Retargeting (GMR)** method is crucial for addressing the embodiment gap, ensuring that human motion data can be accurately translated into robot-executable trajectories.

By combining these two fields, Gang Zhang's work represents a full **end-to-end pipeline** that takes speech input, generates semantically appropriate gestures, and executes them in real-time on a physical humanoid robot. This holistic approach distinguishes it from systems that focus solely on either gesture synthesis for virtual agents or generic robot locomotion, pushing the boundaries towards truly interactive and anthropomorphic physical robot companions.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enable humanoid robots to communicate naturally and expressively through co-speech gestures, mirroring human interaction. This overarching goal is driven by several specific motivations and aims:

1. **Enhance Human-Robot Communication:** The long-standing aspiration in robotics is to create robots that can interact in a human-like manner. Non-verbal cues, particularly gestures, are crucial for effective, relatable, and engaging communication. The goal is to move beyond mere rhythmic movements to truly meaningful expressions.
2. **Overcome Semantic Gesture Sparsity:** While rhythmic (beat) gestures are relatively easy to synthesize, gestures that convey specific semantic meaning are scarce in datasets. A key motivation is to develop a data-driven system that can reliably generate these semantically rich, yet sparse, gestures.
3. **Bridge the Embodiment Gap:** Human motion data cannot be directly applied to robots due to differences in kinematics, morphology, and degrees of freedom (DoFs). A critical objective is to develop or employ robust methods to accurately retarget human-centric motions to a specific humanoid robot platform (Unitree G1) without introducing artifacts like ground penetration or self-intersection.
4. **Achieve Real-Time, Robust Physical Control:** Generating gestures is only part of the challenge; the robot must be able to execute these complex, dynamic motions in real-time while maintaining balance and agility in a physical environment. This necessitates a robust control policy capable of handling high DoFs and dynamic stability.
5. **Develop a Complete End-to-End Pipeline:** The ultimate motivation is to integrate all necessary components—from speech understanding and semantic gesture generation to physical robot execution—into a seamless, automatic, and real-time framework. This holistic approach addresses the entire chain of challenges, moving towards practical, real-world deployment.

In essence, the paper aims to provide a comprehensive solution that allows a humanoid robot to listen to speech, understand its semantic content, generate appropriate co-speech gestures, and perform them physically in real-time, thereby significantly enhancing its interactive capabilities.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The system presented by Gang Zhang adopts an innovative, end-to-end modular architecture, dividing the process into three core components: motion retargeting, automatic gesture generation, and robust imitation learning control.

#### 3.1. System Overview

The system operates in distinct training and inference phases:
- **Training Phase:**
 1. **Motion Codebook Training:** A Residual VQ-VAE learns a hierarchical discrete latent space to represent motion sequences as compressed tokens.
 2. **Motion Generation Training:** An autoregressive Motion-GPT model is trained to predict future motion tokens, conditioned on preceding motion tokens and synchronized audio features.
 3. **Action Control Training:** A specialized imitation learning policy (𝜋𝑚𝑜𝑡𝑖𝑜𝑛), MotionTracker, is trained using reinforcement learning to accurately track high-fidelity reference motions generated for the target robot.
- **Inference Pipeline:** During real-time operation, speech input is processed, fed into the Motion-GPT to generate gesture tokens, which are then decoded into reference motions. This reference motion guides the 𝜋𝑚𝑜𝑡𝑖𝑜𝑛 controller to output real-time actions for the robot.

#### 3.2. Motion Retargeting (General Motion Retargeting - GMR)

To bridge the embodiment gap between human motion data (e.g., BVH format) and the Unitree G1 robot, the General Motion Retargeting (GMR) method is employed. This is crucial for creating high-quality reference motions, as poor retargeting can introduce artifacts that destabilize policy training. The process involves:
1. **Key Body Matching and Alignment:** Establishing correspondences between human and robot body parts and aligning their rest poses in Cartesian space to prevent artifacts like the "toed-in" effect.
2. **Non-Uniform Local Scaling:** Applying a uniform scaling factor *only* to the root translation to adjust the source motion proportions to the robot, thereby avoiding foot sliding.
3. **Two-Stage Kinematic Optimization:** A differential inverse kinematics (IK) solver is used in two stages:
 - **Stage 1:** Minimizes body orientation and end-effector position errors for stable initialization.
 - **Stage 2:** Fine-tunes the solution by adding position constraints for all key bodies.
The output is G1-specific motion data, including root position/velocity and joint angles for the robot's 29 DoFs.

#### 3.3. Automatic Gesture Generation

This component is responsible for synthesizing semantically appropriate co-speech gestures.
1. **Motion Autoencoder (Residual VQ-VAE):** An RVQ-VAE is used to learn a hierarchical discrete categorical space for motion representation. This model improves upon standard VQ-VAEs by:
 - Separating motion representation into body and hand parts.
 - Employing multiple residual quantization layers in a hierarchical architecture, which iteratively models motion features and expands expressive capability without training instability.
2. **Autoregressive Motion-GPT Model:** Based on the GPT-2 architecture, this model generates future discrete gesture tokens (for both hands and body) autoregressively. It is conditioned on preceding motion tokens and a rich set of audio features (MFCC, delta, chromagram, onset, tempogram).
 - **LLM-based Semantic Retrieval:** To inject semantic meaning, the framework integrates an LLM. This LLM analyzes the speech transcript context to retrieve appropriate semantic gesture candidates from a high-quality gesture library (e.g., SeG dataset). These retrieved gestures are then fused with the rhythmically generated motion at the latent space level via a semantics-aware alignment mechanism, ensuring contextually meaningful and natural animation.

#### 3.4. Imitation Learning Control Policy (MotionTracker)

To enable the physical execution of generated reference motions, the MotionTracker policy is utilized. Developed within a two-stage reinforcement learning framework, MotionTracker is designed to track diverse, highly dynamic, and contact-rich human motions, minimizing the tracking error between the robot's state and the target motion goals.
- **Robustness to High DoFs and Motion Diversity:** Instead of directly predicting absolute Proportional-Derivative (PD) targets, the policy predicts *residual PD offsets* relative to the reference motion. This canonicalization, combined with empirically designed joint-specific scaling and a tanh activation function, allows the policy to predict a compact, multi-joint action distribution effectively.
- This approach ensures high expressiveness and motion tracking fidelity, crucial for executing the complex gestures synthesized by the Motion-GPT module while maintaining the robot's balance and stability.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research provides comprehensive experimental validation across three key stages: motion autoencoder performance, gesture generation quality, and the effectiveness of the imitation learning control policy, culminating in real-world deployment.

#### 4.1. Motion Autoencoder Validation

The Residual VQ-VAE (RVQ-VAE) motion autoencoder's reconstruction capability was validated by comparing original human motion data with its reconstructed version on the G1 robot.
- **Key Finding:** Figure 2 visually demonstrates that the reconstructed motion (red dashed line) closely follows the original (Ground Truth, blue solid line) for six randomly selected joint dimensions over a 120-second sequence.
- **Significance:** This indicates that the RVQ-VAE effectively encodes complex motion information into discrete tokens and can accurately reconstruct it with minimal information loss, providing a solid foundation for the subsequent gesture generation pipeline.

#### 4.2. Gesture Generation Quality

The quality and accuracy of gestures generated by the Motion-GPT model, conditioned on audio input, were assessed against human-recorded ground truth gestures.
- **Key Finding:** Figure 3 illustrates a strong visual correlation between the generated motion (red dashed line) and the Ground Truth motion (blue solid line) for a selection of joints. Quantitative evaluation in Table 1 shows very low Root Mean Square Error (RMSE) across all DoFs. For instance, root position errors (e.g., root\_pos\_x: 0.000592) and many joint angle errors are remarkably low.
- **Significance:** This confirms that the Motion-GPT, augmented with LLM-based semantic retrieval, can successfully generate semantically appropriate and rhythmically coherent gestures that closely resemble natural human movements, thereby overcoming the challenge of semantic gesture sparsity.

#### 4.3. Imitation Learning Control Policy

The effectiveness of the MotionTracker control policy (𝜋𝑚𝑜𝑡𝑖𝑜𝑛) was evaluated in both simulation and real-world deployment.

##### 4.3.1. Simulation Tracking Accuracy

- **Key Finding:** Table 2 presents low RMSE errors between the simulated robot's state and the reference motion across all DoFs. For example, lower body movements like the right hip roll joint show a very low RMSE of 0.0064.
- **Significance:** These results demonstrate that the imitation learning algorithm successfully trained the robot to accurately follow the diverse and dynamic reference motions produced by the gesture synthesis pipeline within a simulated environment.

##### 4.3.2. Real-World Deployment

- **Key Finding:** Figure 4 provides visual evidence of the physical Unitree G1 humanoid robot successfully executing the synthesized gestures in real-time. The robot maintains balance while performing complex hand and body movements.
- **Significance:** This is a crucial validation of the entire end-to-end system. It proves that the combined gesture generation and robust imitation control system can operate effectively in a real-world physical environment, translating abstract speech intent into dynamic and stable robotic actions. The ability to maintain balance during expressive movements highlights the robustness and practical utility of the MotionTracker policy.

In summary, the results consistently demonstrate the high fidelity of each component—motion encoding, gesture generation, and control tracking—and crucially, the seamless integration and robust performance of the complete pipeline on a physical humanoid robot.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research marks a significant advancement in the field of human-robot interaction and humanoid robotics, providing a novel, comprehensive, and practical solution for enabling robots to communicate more naturally.

#### 5.1. Key Contributions and Significance

- **Complete End-to-End Pipeline:** The most significant contribution is the development and successful implementation of a *complete* pipeline from speech input to real-time physical gesture execution on a humanoid robot. This bridges the gap between high-level cognitive understanding (semantics from speech) and low-level physical control, a challenge that historically required complex, disjointed systems.
- **Semantic-Aware Gesture Synthesis:** By integrating LLMs for semantic retrieval with an autoregressive Motion-GPT, the system effectively tackles the long-standing problem of generating semantically meaningful, yet sparse, gestures. This moves beyond simple rhythmic movements to truly expressive communication, making robot interactions more intuitive and engaging.
- **Robust Real-Time Physical Deployment:** The successful deployment on the Unitree G1 robot, with the robot maintaining balance during dynamic and complex gestures, validates the robustness of the imitation learning control policy (MotionTracker) and the efficacy of the General Motion Retargeting (GMR) method. This demonstrates practical feasibility for real-world applications.
- **High Fidelity and Accuracy:** Quantitative and qualitative evaluations consistently show high accuracy in motion reconstruction, gesture generation, and control tracking, affirming the technical soundness of each module within the integrated framework.

#### 5.2. Potential Impact

The implications of this work are far-reaching across several domains:

- **Enhanced Human-Robot Interaction (HRI):** By enabling robots to gesture naturally and contextually, this work will lead to more intuitive, relatable, and trustworthy interactions. Robots could become better companions, educators, and assistants, capable of conveying nuanced meaning beyond just spoken words.
- **Robotics Applications:** This framework can be integrated into various humanoid robot applications, including:
 - **Customer Service & Hospitality:** More engaging and helpful robot greeters or service providers.
 - **Education:** Robots that can effectively explain concepts through speech and gesture.
 - **Entertainment:** Highly expressive robotic performers or characters.
 - **Therapy & Assistance:** Robots providing empathetic and clear communication.
- **Advancement in AI and Robotics Research:** The modular approach, combining advanced generative AI (LLMs, GPT) with robust robot control (imitation learning, reinforcement learning), provides a valuable blueprint for future research in embodied AI, multimodal interaction, and sim-to-real transfer.
- **Commercial Viability:** Originating from China Mobile, this research has a clear trajectory towards commercialization. A ready-to-deploy, end-to-end system significantly lowers the barrier for integrating expressive non-verbal communication into commercial humanoid robot platforms.

#### 5.3. Future Directions

The authors outline promising avenues for future research:
- **Refining LLM integration:** Enhancing the LLM's understanding of speech rhythm and prosody to improve the timing and frequency of semantic gesture retrieval, moving beyond simple stroke phase alignment.
- **Complex merging strategies:** Investigating more sophisticated methods for fusing retrieved semantic gestures with rhythmically generated motion to better preserve details and naturalness.
- **Multi-party conversational scenarios:** Extending the framework to handle interactions with multiple human participants, which would require more complex context awareness, turn-taking, and social gesturing capabilities.

In conclusion, Gang Zhang's work represents a pivotal step towards creating truly anthropomorphic and interactive humanoid robots. By meticulously integrating cutting-edge AI for semantic understanding and gesture generation with robust real-time robotic control, this research significantly pushes the boundaries for robots to communicate in a manner that is both expressive and naturally human-like.
