# Language Models as Zero-Shot Planners: Extracting Actionable Knowledge for Embodied Agents

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from UC Berkeley, CMU, and Google developed an inference-time procedure to enable large language models (LLMs) to serve as zero-shot planners for embodied agents in simulated household environments. Their method, which combines semantic translation and autoregressive correction, dramatically increased the executability of LLM-generated action plans from under 10% to over 70% while maintaining competitive semantic correctness.

## Problem

- Leveraging the vast world knowledge embedded within LLMs for zero-shot planning by embodied agents in interactive environments without specific training.
- Translating free-form, natural language action plans generated by LLMs into concrete, syntactically valid, and admissible actions executable by a robotic agent.
- Improving the executability of LLM-generated plans without relying on extensive domain-specific data collection or computationally expensive fine-tuning.

## Method

- An ==inference-time procedure== extracts actionable knowledge from frozen, pre-trained LLMs, using a single in-context example for zero-shot planning in the VirtualHome environment.
- ==Semantic translation== utilizes a separate language model (e.g., Sentence-RoBERTa) to map LLM-generated natural language action phrases to admissible environment actions based on semantic similarity.
- ==Autoregressive trajectory correction== guides plan generation step-by-step, translating each LLM-generated action phrase into an admissible environment action and appending it to the prompt for subsequent step generation, along with dynamic example selection.

## Results

- Vanilla LLMs (e.g., GPT-3 175B) generated plans with high human-judged semantic correctness (**77.86%**) but very low executability (**7.79%**) in VirtualHome.
- The proposed inference-time procedure significantly boosted executability, increasing it from **18.07%** to **78.57%** for Codex 12B and from **7.79%** to **73.05%** for GPT-3 175B.
- While executability improved, the human-evaluated correctness of translated plans saw a slight decrease (e.g., Translated GPT-3 175B at **66.13%**) compared to vanilla LLM outputs, although remaining competitive with human-written plans (**70.05%**).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Large language models (LLMs) inherently possess extensive common-sense knowledge, enabling them to generate semantically plausible action plans for complex, high-level tasks.
> - A significant gap exists between the semantic correctness of raw LLM-generated plans and their practical executability in constrained, interactive embodied environments.
> - Inference-time procedures, without modifying LLM parameters, can effectively ground free-form natural language plans into admissible actions, substantially improving executability but introducing a trade-off with peak human-judged correctness.
