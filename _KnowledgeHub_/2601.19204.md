# MATA: A Trainable Hierarchical Automaton System for Multi-Agent Visual Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> MATA is a trainable hierarchical automaton system for multi-agent visual reasoning that employs an LLM-based hyper agent to learn dynamic transitions between specialized reasoning agents. It achieves state-of-the-art performance across compositional VQA, external knowledge VQA, and referring expression comprehension benchmarks, while providing enhanced transparency and robustness.

## Problem

- Monolithic Vision-Language Models (VLMs) often produce unexplainable reasoning and hallucinate when dealing with complex visual queries.
- Existing compositional and single-agent methods are rigid, lack robustness to errors, and struggle with multi-step, feedback-driven reasoning or flexible collaboration.
- Previous automaton-based systems relied on hand-crafted, rule-based transition rules, which are inflexible and difficult to scale with increasing complexity or ambiguous agent selection criteria.

## Method

- Developed ==MATA==, a multi-agent system structured as a ==hierarchical finite-state automaton== with an LLM-based 'hyper agent' controlling high-level state transitions.
- Specialized agents (e.g., ONESHOT, STEPWISE, SPECIALIZED) act as top-level states, each with rule-based sub-automata for micro-control, and communicate via an ==append-only shared memory==.
- Introduced a ==data generation pipeline (MATA-SFT-90K)== to create supervised finetuning examples for the hyper agent, enabling it to learn dynamic, context-aware transition policies between agents.

## Results

- MATA achieved state-of-the-art results, including **64.9%** accuracy on GQA (outperforming InternVL3.5 by **1.1%**), **76.5%** on OK-VQA (surpassing InternVL3.5 by **0.8%**), and **96.3%** on RefCOCO (exceeding Florence2-L by **1.2%**).
- Ablation studies confirmed the trainable hyper agent's crucial role, substantially improving accuracy (e.g., **64.9%** for MATA vs. **57.7%** for exhaustive on GQA) and reducing inference time.
- The learned transition policy demonstrated strong cross-task generalization, with a single hyper agent showing robust performance across diverse visual reasoning benchmarks with minimal performance drop.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A trainable hyper agent governing high-level transitions allows dynamic selection and collaboration/competition among specialized agents, enhancing flexibility and robustness in visual reasoning.
> - The hierarchical design, separating learned high-level control from rule-based low-level execution, provides both adaptive decision-making and an explainable, auditable reasoning trace.
> - Supervised finetuning the hyper agent with systematically explored optimal reasoning trajectories yields an efficient and generalizable control policy for multi-agent systems.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of visual reasoning, the process of interpreting and analyzing visual information to support decision-making, has seen rapid advancements with Vision-Language Models (VLMs). However, despite their impressive perceptual abilities, current monolithic VLMs often suffer from implicit, unexplainable reasoning processes and a propensity for hallucinations, especially on complex queries involving spatial relations, attributes, and counting.

To address these shortcomings, the research community has explored several avenues:
- **Compositional Methods:** These approaches decompose complex tasks into sub-problems, typically employing Large Language Models (LLMs) for planning or code generation and Vision Foundation Models (VFMs) as specialized tools. While enhancing interpretability and tool utilization, most non-agentic compositional methods are limited by single-turn reasoning and fixed, manually designed pipelines, making them inflexible and unable to engage in multi-step, feedback-driven reasoning.
- **Agentic Methods (Single-Agent):** Recognizing the need for iterative reasoning, some works frame visual reasoning as a multi-step feedback loop, where a single LLM/VLM agent takes actions based on the current state. However, a single agent often lacks the diverse skills required for highly complex problems.
- **Multi-Agent Systems (for other domains):** In broader AI, multi-agent frameworks exist where agents are assigned disjoint roles and connected via hard-coded collaboration patterns. While offering some benefits, these often propagate errors in a linear fashion and overlook the potential for competition among functionally overlapping agents.
- **Automaton-based Approaches:** More recently, finite-state automata have been explored for their explicit control flow and interpretability. Previous work like NAVER (Cai et al., 2025) leveraged automata but relied on hand-written, rule-based transitions, which are inflexible and difficult to scale as the number of states or agents grows. HYDRA (Ke et al., 2024), while data-driven, focuses on instruction-level planning rather than high-level agent switching.

MATA directly addresses the significant gaps in this landscape. It unifies neuro-symbolic principles with a multi-agent design within a hierarchical automaton framework. Crucially, it moves beyond rigid, hand-coded pipelines and fixed automata by introducing a *trainable hyper agent*. This innovation allows for dynamic, context-aware transitions between collaborative and competitive agents, enabling error handling and flexible re-routing—a capability largely under-explored in visual reasoning and unaddressed by previous automaton-based systems that struggled with defining ambiguous transitions between functionally overlapping agents. By learning these high-level transitions while keeping intra-agent micro-steps rule-based, MATA offers a novel solution that combines the best of both worlds: flexibility and robustness with explainability and verifiable control.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core motivation behind MATA stems from the inherent limitations of existing visual reasoning paradigms:
1. **Hallucinations and Lack of Explainability in Monolithic VLMs:** While powerful, monolithic VLMs often produce unreliable outputs and obscure their reasoning process, hindering trust and debugging.
2. **Rigidity and Insufficient Robustness in Compositional/Agentic Methods:** Fixed pipelines in compositional methods fail to adapt to dynamic contexts or errors. Single-agent systems are overwhelmed by complex tasks, and existing multi-agent systems often use inflexible, error-prone collaboration patterns.
3. **Scalability and Flexibility of Automata:** Hand-crafted transition rules in prior automaton-based systems become unmanageable and inflexible as the system's complexity (number of agents/states) increases, especially when deciding between competitive agents with ambiguous selection criteria.

Given these challenges, the key objectives of MATA are:
- **To develop a multi-agent visual reasoning system that facilitates both collaboration and competition** among specialized agents, moving beyond disjoint roles and fixed pipelines.
- **To introduce a hierarchical finite-state automaton architecture** where agents function as top-level states, each with its own rule-based sub-automaton for reliable micro-control.
- **To design and implement a *trainable hyper agent*** (an LLM-based controller) responsible for learning and executing the high-level transition policy between these agents, making the system adaptive and robust.
- **To ensure transparency and explainability** by maintaining an auditable, append-only shared memory that records the full reasoning process, including intermediate results, feedback, and execution history.
- **To create a data generation pipeline (MATA-SFT-90K)** that systematically explores transition trajectories, scores outcomes, and converts these into supervised finetuning (SFT) examples to train the hyper agent's transition policy.
- **To achieve state-of-the-art performance** across a variety of visual reasoning benchmarks, demonstrating the efficacy of the proposed hierarchical, trainable multi-agent automaton system in handling complex and diverse queries.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

MATA (Multi-Agent hierarchical Trainable Automaton) approaches visual reasoning by modeling the inference process as a hierarchical finite-state automaton with a trainable LLM-based hyper agent governing the high-level transitions.

#### 3.1. Hierarchical Automaton Architecture

- **Hyper Automaton (M_θ):** This is the top-level control mechanism, described as a Mealy machine, with a set of states (S), an initial state (S_0), inputs (Σ) from shared memory, an answer space (Λ), a learned transition function (δ_θ), and an output function (Γ).
- **States (S):**
 - **Agent States (S_agent):** These include `ONESHOT`, `STEPWISE`, and `SPECIALIZED`. Each represents a distinct reasoning capability.
 - `SPECIALIZED Agent`: Handles fast, "System 1-style" perception tasks (e.g., object detection, simple VQA) with built-in verifiers.
 - `ONESHOT Reasoner`: Executes a single-pass program generation and execution workflow for moderately compositional queries.
 - `STEPWISE Reasoner`: Designed for complex, multi-step reasoning, generating and executing Python programs in a sandboxed environment with rigorous verification.
 - **Lifecycle States (S_life):** `INITIAL`, `FINAL`, and `FAILURE`, coordinating the reasoning episode.
- **Rule-based Sub-automata:** Each agent in `S_agent` runs a smaller, *rule-based* sub-automaton for its internal micro-control (e.g., prompting LLMs/VLMs, running verifier checks, handling tool I/O). This design separates clear procedural steps from ambiguous high-level decision-making.
- **Shared Memory (m_t):** An append-only, structured memory is central to MATA. All agents read from and write to it, accumulating intermediate variables, perception results, program history, verifier feedback, and task metadata. This provides an auditable history and crucial context for the hyper agent.
- **Execution Flow:** The system starts in `INITIAL`. At each step `t`, the hyper agent observes `m_t` and selects the next state `s_t+1`. If `s_t+1` is an agent state, that agent's sub-automaton executes one cycle, updates `m_t`, and returns control to the hyper automaton. If `s_t+1` is `FINAL`, the episode terminates; if `FAILURE`, the hyper agent attempts to re-route.

#### 3.2. Trainable Transition Function (Hyper Agent)

- **LLM-based Controller (F_θ):** The `δ_θ` transition function is implemented by a trainable LLM. It takes a textual prompt `x_t` (derived from `m_t` using a `Memory Prompter`) as input and predicts the next state `s_t+1` from a set of candidates.
- **Prompting:** The prompt `x_t` encapsulates `task_description`, `query`, `feedback`, `code`, `variables`, `state_history`, `current_state`, `current_step`, and `state_candidates`. A `Transition Verifier` ensures valid state selection.

#### 3.3. Data Generation for Supervised Finetuning (SFT)

- **Transition Trajectory Tree (T(v, q)):** To train `F_θ`, MATA generates a dataset `D` of transition trajectories. For each image-query pair, it systematically explores a bounded-depth trajectory tree where nodes represent states and branches represent possible next transitions.
- **Bottom-up Node Scoring:** Once leaf nodes (terminal states) are reached, they are scored based on task-specific metrics (e.g., IoU for visual grounding, accuracy for VQA). These scores are then propagated upwards to assign a value `V(s)` to every node, representing the maximum achievable score from that state.
- **Optimal Next State Labeling:** For each decision point `s_t`, the optimal next state `s*_t` is identified as the child node that leads to the highest-scoring subtree (`arg max V(s')`).
- **MATA-SFT-90K Dataset:** The collected `(x_t, s*_t)` pairs (textual memory snapshot and optimal next state) are reformatted into instruction-completion pairs, forming the `MATA-SFT-90K` dataset. This dataset is then used to supervised finetune the LLM-based hyper agent.

#### 3.4. Inference

During inference, the hyper agent `F_θ` iteratively selects the next state based on the current shared memory. In case of an `FAILURE` state reported by an agent, the hyper agent re-selects, temporarily removing the failed agent from candidate states to avoid infinite loops, ensuring robustness.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

MATA demonstrates significant advancements across various visual reasoning benchmarks:

- **State-of-the-Art Performance:**
 - **Compositional VQA (GQA):** MATA (General) achieved 64.9% accuracy, outperforming both prior trainable compositional methods like HYDRA (52.8%) and leading monolithic VLMs such as InternVL3.5 (63.8%) and Qwen2.5-VL (62.4%). This highlights its superior ability in complex compositional reasoning.
 - **External Knowledge VQA (OK-VQA):** MATA (Domain-Specific) reached 76.5% accuracy, surpassing previous compositional systems like DWIM (62.8%) and even strong monolithic VLMs like InternVL3.5 (75.7%) and Qwen2.5-VL (71.8%). This indicates MATA's effectiveness in integrating external knowledge.
 - **Referring Expression Comprehension (RefCOCO, RefCOCO+, RefCOCOg, Ref-Adv):** MATA achieved new state-of-the-art results (e.g., 96.3% on RefCOCO), surpassing both monolithic grounding models (e.g., Florence2-L 95.1%) and compositional baselines (e.g., NAVER 96.2%). Its strong performance on Ref-Adv, a domain-transfer benchmark, is particularly noteworthy as its SFT data did not include Ref-Adv trajectories.
- **Impact of Trainable Hyper Agent:** Ablation studies (Table 5) clearly validated the hyper agent's crucial role. Replacing random transitions or even a non-SFT LLM with a supervised finetuned LLM for the hyper agent led to substantial accuracy gains across all tasks (e.g., 57.7% for exhaustive vs. 64.9% for MATA on GQA), while also significantly reducing inference time compared to exhaustive methods. This confirms that learning the transition policy is critical for effective multi-agent collaboration and competition.
- **Generalizability of the Transition Policy:** Extensive generalization analysis (Table 6 & 7) demonstrated that the learned transition policy is largely task-agnostic. The hyper agent trained on one dataset (e.g., GQA) showed strong performance on others (e.g., OK-VQA), with minimal performance drop (less than 1%) compared to domain-specific training. Joint training on all data (general setting) often achieved performance comparable to, or even better than, domain-specific training, indicating robust cross-task transferability.
- **Efficiency:** MATA proved to be efficient (Table 9). Compared to other agentic methods like HYDRA and exhaustive baselines, MATA significantly reduced inference time, LLM API costs, and maintained moderate vRAM usage. Its inference time was comparable to a monolithic VLM (Qwen2.5-VL 72B), while being far more cost-effective and transparent.
- **LLM Size for Hyper Agent:** Experiments showed that a 4B parameter LLM for the hyper agent offered a near-optimal balance between accuracy and efficiency (Figure 3). While larger models provided marginal gains, smaller models (0.6B/1.7B) performed well with domain-specific SFT, highlighting the efficiency of the SFT approach.
- **Number of Agents:** The current three-agent design (Specialized, Oneshot, Stepwise) provides sufficient capabilities for the evaluated benchmarks. Adding more agents showed diminishing returns beyond two agents (Figure 4), suggesting a well-optimized and balanced agent composition.
- **Robustness to Hallucinations and Errors:** Qualitative examples (Figure 6) illustrated MATA's ability to dynamically re-route control between agents, inheriting context through shared memory, to correct errors and avoid common pitfalls like hallucination or fixed-pipeline failures seen in other models.
- **Comparison with Direct SFT:** MATA's approach of SFT on the hyper agent's transition policy demonstrated superior cross-task generalization compared to directly finetuning a monolithic VLM for answers (Table 10). Direct SFT often harms generalization due to catastrophic forgetting of latent reasoning capabilities, whereas MATA maintains robustness by learning flexible control rather than direct mapping.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

MATA represents a significant leap forward in the field of visual reasoning, offering a robust and interpretable framework that addresses critical limitations of current AI systems. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Pioneering Trainable Hierarchical Control:** MATA is the first system to introduce a trainable hyper agent for learning high-level transitions within a hierarchical finite-state automaton for multi-agent visual reasoning. This paradigm shift moves beyond rigid, hand-crafted pipelines to dynamic, context-aware decision-making, which is crucial for handling the ambiguity and complexity of real-world visual queries.
- **Enhanced Explainability and Transparency:** The explicit states, transitions, and the append-only shared memory provide an auditable trace of the entire reasoning process. This transparency is vital for building trust in AI systems, facilitating debugging, and enabling human oversight, a stark contrast to the opaque "black box" nature of many monolithic deep learning models.
- **Increased Robustness and Flexibility:** By allowing dynamic re-routing between collaborative and competitive agents based on shared memory and feedback, MATA significantly improves the system's ability to recover from errors, mitigate hallucinations, and adapt to diverse and unforeseen reasoning challenges. This makes the system more reliable for real-world applications.
- **Scalable and Modular Design:** The hierarchical automaton and the trainable hyper agent decouple the high-level control logic from individual agent functionalities. This modularity means new specialized agents can be integrated into the system without requiring a complete redesign of the control flow, as the hyper agent can learn to incorporate their capabilities, fostering scalability for future expansions.
- **Efficiency in Complex Reasoning:** MATA achieves state-of-the-art accuracy while maintaining competitive efficiency in terms of inference time, vRAM usage, and API costs, especially when compared to complex monolithic models or other multi-agent approaches. This practical efficiency makes MATA a viable solution for deployment in real-world scenarios.
- **Strong Generalization Capabilities:** The demonstrated ability of the learned transition policy to generalize across different visual reasoning tasks and datasets with minimal negative transfer suggests that a single hyper agent can be trained and deployed for a broad spectrum of visual-linguistic problems. This reduces the need for task-specific fine-tuning and enhances the reusability of the control mechanism.
- **Catalyst for Future Research:** MATA’s success in integrating symbolic control with learned, data-driven decisions provides a compelling blueprint for future neuro-symbolic AI systems. It highlights the potential for combining the strengths of deep learning (perception) with the interpretability and control of symbolic AI (reasoning), which could inspire new architectures for general-purpose AI agents.

The primary limitation, as acknowledged by the authors, is the computational cost of the near-exhaustive transition-trajectory tree expansion during data generation, especially as the number of agents and states increases. Future work could focus on more efficient data generation strategies, such as reinforcement learning or meta-learning approaches, to train the hyper agent without exhaustive search.

Overall, MATA pushes the boundaries of compositional visual reasoning, offering a powerful, interpretable, and adaptable framework that sets a new standard for building robust and explainable multi-agent systems in complex AI domains.
