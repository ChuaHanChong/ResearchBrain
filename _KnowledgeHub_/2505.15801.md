# VerifyBench: Benchmarking Reference-based Reward Systems for Large Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers developed VerifyBench and VerifyBench-Hard, the first benchmarks for evaluating reference-based reward systems in Large Reasoning Models, demonstrating that while leading models achieve over 90% accuracy on standard tasks, performance drops by over 20% on challenging cases, with reference answers enhancing verification by 5-18%.

## Problem

- Existing reward benchmarks for Large Language Models (LLMs) primarily assess reward models through pairwise preference comparisons, which are insufficient for evaluating absolute correctness.
- Specialized Large Reasoning Models (LRMs) increasingly rely on "reference-based" reward systems, where rewards are assigned based on alignment with ground truth, necessitating a distinct evaluation methodology.
- There is an absence of dedicated, standardized benchmarks to systematically evaluate these reference-based reward systems, hindering objective assessment and improvement of verification methodologies.

## Method

- Constructed **VerifyBench**, a benchmark of 2,000 balanced question-answer-completion-correctness tuples across general, logical, and mathematical reasoning, curated from open-source problems and human-annotated.
- Developed VerifyBench-Hard, a more challenging dataset comprising 1,000 tuples derived from contentious cases where multiple top-performing LLMs produced conflicting correctness judgments.
- Evaluated various verification approaches, including rule-based baselines, LLM-as-a-judge models, and specialized model-based verifiers, using accuracy as the primary metric, and conducted ablation studies on the impact of reference answers.

## Results

- Top models like Qwen3-**32B** achieved **95.8%** accuracy on VerifyBench, but performance across all models dropped to a maximum of **72.4%** on VerifyBench-Hard, indicating substantial challenges.
- Smaller models (<**3B** parameters) exhibited notably poorer performance on VerifyBench, with Llama-3.2-**3B**-Instruct scoring only **60.95%**.
- Error analysis revealed common issues in verifying complex numeric values, algebraic expressions (e.g., unsimplified but equivalent forms), multiple-choice problems, and semantic consistency in string responses.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Current state-of-the-art LLMs can act as reliable verifiers for standard reference-based tasks, but all evaluated systems, especially smaller models, struggle significantly on difficult instances.
> - Explicit ground truth reference answers play a crucial role in verification, demonstrably improving LLM performance by approximately 5% to 18% in reasoning-oriented tasks.
> - Verifier performance on VerifyBench positively correlates with greater improvements in downstream mathematical reasoning tasks when used in Reinforcement Learning (RL) training and Rejection Sampling Fine-tuning.
