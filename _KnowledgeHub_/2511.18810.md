# MergeVLA: Cross-Skill Model Merging Toward a Generalist Vision-Language-Action Agent

> [!summary] Summary
> MergeVLA is a framework designed to overcome the challenge of merging independently fine-tuned Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, which previously failed due to structural specializations. The framework achieves robust multi-skill, multi-embodiment, and multi-environment generalization, demonstrating up to a 90.2% average success rate on the LIBERO benchmark and successful application on a real robotic arm.

## Problem

- Directly applying established model merging techniques to independently fine-tuned Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models results in near-zero success rates, indicating fundamental incompatibilities.
- VLA models exhibit destructive Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) parameter interference and architectural incompatibility in their action experts, particularly due to self-attention and task-specific specialization in deeper layers.
- Evaluating merged VLA models in mixed-task settings is challenging, as it requires dynamic task identification at inference time without explicit supervision.

## Method

- **MergeVLA** introduces a merging-oriented VLA architecture featuring ==sparsely activated LoRA adapters== with ==task-specific binary masking== to stabilize VLM merging by selectively activating beneficial parameters.
- The ==action expert== is redesigned by removing ==self-attention layers== and replacing the `tanh` gating function with `sigmoid` to reduce task-specific dependencies, while deeper 'expert head' blocks remain unmerged for each task.
- A ==training-free test-time task router== is implemented, which infers task relevance by projecting initial observation hidden states onto ==value-based principal components== of the action expert to dynamically select appropriate skill components.

## Results

- MergeVLA achieved an average success rate of **90.2%** on the LIBERO cross-skill benchmark, representing a significant improvement over direct merging methods that resulted in **0%** success.
- The framework demonstrated enhanced robustness on LIBERO-Plus, maintaining an average success rate of **62.5%** for merged models under distribution shifts, outperforming other VLA models' single-task performance in shifted conditions.
- MergeVLA successfully generalized across different robotic embodiments and tasks on RoboTwin and performed equivalently to single-task models on a real SO-101 robotic arm, achieving a **90.0%** average success rate for multi-skill tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - VLA non-mergeability stems from divergent LoRA updates in the VLM backbone and action-expert architectural coupling; removing self-attention from the action expert makes shallow layers merge-amenable while deep task-specialized heads stay individual.
> - Sparsely activating LoRA parameters through task-specific masking can effectively preserve pretrained visual-language representations and mitigate cross-task interference during merging.
> - Value projection matrices from the action expert provide stable and discriminative signals for test-time task routing, enabling dynamic selection of skill components based on input features without additional training.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Recent advancements in Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models have enabled robotic agents to perform complex manipulation tasks by fine-tuning large Vision-Language Models (VLMs) using extensive robotic demonstration datasets. These VLA models leverage the broad visual grounding and semantic understanding capabilities inherent in VLMs, reformulating action learning as a language generation or policy decoding problem. They have demonstrated proficiency in settings characterized by a single task or a single robotic embodiment.

However, a significant challenge in the development of real-world generalist agents is the requirement to support multiple skills, diverse embodiments, and varied environments within a single, unified policy. A logical approach to consolidate knowledge from multiple independently fine-tuned VLA models into such a policy is model merging. This technique has shown effectiveness in other domains, such as large language models (LLMs) and vision models, by integrating specialized models without the need for joint retraining or access to original datasets.

Despite the success of model merging in other areas, its direct application to VLA experts, particularly those fine-tuned on distinct manipulation tasks, has resulted in substantial performance degradation, often yielding near-zero success rates. This outcome suggests that the fine-tuning process for VLA models induces a form of structural specialization that is incompatible across tasks, a phenomenon not commonly observed in the merging of conventional VLMs. The current work addresses this identified gap by investigating the underlying causes of VLA model non-mergeability and proposing a framework designed to overcome these limitations, thereby aiming to facilitate the creation of scalable generalist embodied agents.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enable the successful and effective merging of independently fine-tuned Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models into a single, unified generalist agent. This generalist agent is intended to perform a wide range of skills across different robotic embodiments and environments.

The motivation for this work stems from the observed limitations of current VLA models and existing model merging techniques when applied to the robotics domain:

1. **Failure of Direct VLA Model Merging**: While VLA models demonstrate strong performance when fine-tuned for a single embodiment or a specific task family, directly applying established model merging methods (proven effective in large language and vision models) to VLA experts trained on distinct manipulation tasks leads to near-zero success rates. This indicates a fundamental incompatibility or a unique challenge in the VLA domain that prevents consolidation of learned skills.
2. **Identification of Non-Mergeability Sources**: Through an empirical decomposition of learnable parameters in representative VLA architectures, the authors identify two principal sources contributing to this non-mergeability:
 - **Destructive LoRA Parameter Interference**: Fine-tuning processes cause Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) adapters within the VLM backbone to update in divergent, task-specific directions. These divergent updates are beyond the capacity of existing merging methods to unify effectively. When merged, these updates can reactivate irrelevant or contradictory parameters, thereby corrupting shared vision-language subspaces and degrading task-invariant semantics. Analysis showed that a substantial proportion of parameters (exceeding 75% for four merged tasks) are "selfish," meaning they are relevant to only one specific task, leading to severe conflicts during direct merging.
 - **Architectural Incompatibility of Action Experts**: The action decoders, typically trained from scratch, develop strong inter-block dependencies via self-attention mechanisms. This coupling results in the widespread propagation of localized task information across layers, which obstructs modular recombination. Deeper blocks, in particular, become highly specialized to individual tasks, rendering their parameters intrinsically irreconcilable through standard merging schemes.
3. **Challenge of Mixed-Task Evaluation**: Beyond the technical hurdles of merging, previous studies have noted that evaluating merged models in a mixed-task setting (where the task identity is unknown during inference) presents a significant challenge. Many existing approaches rely on explicit task identification (e.g., task identity as input or expert-specific prompts) to achieve adequate performance, which is often not feasible in dynamic real-world scenarios.

Motivated by these identified challenges, the research aims to develop a novel VLA architecture and merging methodology that explicitly addresses these issues by design, enabling robust multi-skill, multi-embodiment, and multi-environment generalization.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology, termed MergeVLA, introduces a merging-oriented VLA architecture designed to overcome the identified challenges of destructive LoRA parameter interference and architectural incompatibility in action experts. MergeVLA integrates three core components: sparsely activated LoRA adapters, a redesigned action expert, and a test-time task router.

#### 3.1. Task Conflicts in LoRA-Finetuned VLM: Stabilizing VLM Merging via Task-Specific Masking

- **Problem Addressed**: The divergence of LoRA updates in the VLM backbone across different tasks, leading to destructive parameter interference upon merging.
- **Approach**: MergeVLA employs a task-specific binary masking strategy to manage LoRA updates.
 - When multiple tasks are merged, a single merged update vector (τ_merge) is initially computed.
 - For each task `m`, a binary mask `S_m` is constructed. This mask is applied element-wise to `τ_merge` to create `Θ_merge^(m) = Θ_0 + S_m ⊙ τ_merge`. This selectively activates parameters that are beneficial for task `m` while suppressing those that could cause conflicts or lead other tasks astray.
 - The mask `S_m` is determined by a parameter-level consistency test: `S_m = I [ |τ_m| > λ |τ_merge - τ_m| ]`. This means a parameter is retained (mask value is 1) if its task-specific update `τ_m` is both significant in magnitude and dominant relative to the residual difference between `τ_merge` and `τ_m`. The parameter `λ` controls the tolerance for disagreement among task updates.
- **Rationale**: This method explicitly addresses the "selfish" nature of many LoRA parameters, which are relevant to only one task. By sparsely activating parameters, MergeVLA aims to preserve pretrained visual-language representations, reduce cross-task interference, and enhance merge stability.

#### 3.2. Redesigning the Action Expert for Mergeability

- **Problem Addressed**: The architectural incompatibility of action experts, particularly the accumulation of strong task-specific dependencies across blocks due to self-attention, making deeper layers unmergeable.
- **Approach**: MergeVLA reconfigures the action expert with two key modifications:
 1. **Removal of Self-Attention**: The self-attention layers within the action expert are eliminated. This forces the expert to rely predominantly on the robust, shared features provided by the VLM backbone, preventing the accumulation of task-specific biases that are difficult to reconcile during merging.
 2. **Replacement of Gating Function**: The `tanh` gating function in the task stream is replaced with a `sigmoid` gate. The `tanh` gate could potentially suppress VLM signals, encouraging the expert to depend on its own scratch-trained (and task-specific) parameters. The `sigmoid` gate ensures that VLM information is consistently preserved and balanced, contributing to greater generalizability.
- **Merging Strategy for Action Experts**:
 - For the shallow blocks of the action expert, a simple weight-averaging strategy is adopted, as these layers exhibit reduced task-specific divergence.
 - However, deeper blocks, collectively referred to as the "expert head" (H_l→L), remain unmerged. These layers, typically the final block(s), exhibit strong task-specific specialization, particularly to the action distribution of their corresponding task. Attempting to average these highly specialized weights would lead to performance degradation. Consequently, each task retains its own expert head.

#### 3.3. Test-Time Task Routing

- **Problem Addressed**: The challenge of performing multi-task inference when the task identity is unknown at test time, necessitating dynamic selection of task-specific components.
- **Approach**: A training-free test-time task router is introduced to infer task relevance from the model’s internal parameter subspaces based on input features.
 1. **Masked VLM Variants**: Given the merged LoRA parameters and a set of `M` task masks, `M` masked VLM variants are generated (`Θ_merge^(m)`).
 2. **Hidden State Extraction**: For each masked VLM variant, the hidden states (`h_l^T, h_l^A`) from a specific block `l` (e.g., the last VLM block before the action expert) are extracted using the initial observation.
 3. **Subspace Projection**: The hidden states are then projected onto the principal components (`P_l^T, P_l^A`) derived from the singular value decomposition (SVD) of the *value projection matrices (V)* of the action expert's `l`-th block. Value-based subspaces are chosen because they empirically provide more stable and discriminative signals for routing, as they encode task-dependent information within the hidden states.
 4. **Activation Strength Measurement**: The activation strength for each task `m` is quantified by the L2 norm of the projected hidden states (`r_T,m`, `r_A,m`).
 5. **Task Selection**: A combined score vector `r` is computed, and `softmax` is applied to obtain routing probabilities (`p_m`). The task `m*` with the highest probability (`arg max_m p_m`) is selected.
- **Inference Execution**: Once `m*` is determined from the initial observation (at `t=0`), the corresponding task mask `S_m*` and expert head `H_l→L_m*` are fixed and used for the entire episode, avoiding repeated routing. This mechanism allows the merged model to adaptively activate the appropriate skill components without additional supervision, incurring minimal computational and parameter overhead.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted across various benchmarks and real-world setups demonstrate the effectiveness and robustness of MergeVLA.

- **Results on LIBERO (Cross-Skill)**:
 - Individually fine-tuned MergeVLA achieves an average success rate of 96.7%, which is comparable to VLA-Adapter (98.5%) and significantly surpasses OpenVLA (76.5%), indicating that the architectural modifications preserve core capabilities.
 - Direct application of merging methods (e.g., Task Arithmetic, TA) to existing VLA models (OpenVLA, VLA-Adapter) results in complete failure (0% success rate). Even with task-specific masks, VLA-Adapter performs poorly (0% success). OpenVLA with task masks improves to 62.4%, but performance remains unbalanced.
 - MergeVLA, when combined with merging methods like TIES and task-specific masks, achieves an average success rate of 90.2% on LIBERO. This performance is consistent across all four task suites (Spatial, Object, Goal, Long), indicating strong multi-task capabilities and efficient knowledge reuse while being only 6.5% below its own single-task fine-tuning performance.

- **Results on LIBERO-Plus (Robustness to Distribution Shifts)**:
 - Evaluations on LIBERO-Plus, which introduces seven types of visual and linguistic distribution shifts, show that single-task fine-tuned MergeVLA exhibits stronger robustness (72.4% average success rate) compared to existing VLA models like OpenVLA (16.3%), π0 (56.3%), and VLA-Adapter (59.0%). This is attributed to MergeVLA's architectural design preserving the inherent robustness of the pretrained VLM.
 - In the model-merging setting, MergeVLA with different merging strategies (e.g., TIES) maintains similar robustness (62.5% average success rate). Notably, the merged MergeVLA model (e.g., using TA or TIES) even surpasses the performance of OpenVLA, π0, and VLA-Adapter in their *single-task* settings under these shifted conditions, indicating effective transfer and preservation of robustness across tasks.

- **Results on RoboTwin (Cross-Embodiment)**:
 - **Setting A (Cross-embodiment, Single task)**: MergeVLA with TIES merging and routing of the final two expert head blocks (H_(L-1)→L) achieves an 88.7% average success rate, comparable to individually fine-tuned policies (88.0%). This suggests that early merged blocks effectively capture transferable knowledge across different robotic embodiments.
 - **Setting B (Cross-embodiment, Cross-task)**: This more challenging setting requires routing of the final three expert head blocks (H_(L-2)→L) and TIES merging to maintain performance. MergeVLA achieves a 70.7% average success rate, demonstrating its ability for cross-embodiment generalization, even for tasks requiring coordinated dual-arm motion. The need for more unmerged deeper layers highlights stronger specialization within action heads due to embodiment-specific differences.

- **Real-World Robotic Experiments (SO101 Robotic Arm)**:
 - On three cube manipulation tasks (PICK & PLACE, PUSH CUBE, STACK CUBE) with a real SO-101 robotic arm, single-task fine-tuned MergeVLA achieves a 90.0% average success rate. It demonstrates robustness to unseen cube colors, indicating strong visual out-of-distribution generalization in physical environments.
 - Merged MergeVLA, particularly with TIES-based merging, achieves an average success rate of 90.0%, matching the performance of single-task models. This indicates that MergeVLA preserves cross-task merging ability and allows for skill component reuse on physical hardware without degradation, suggesting practical applicability for multi-skill real robot systems.

- **Ablation Study**:
 - **Impact of Mask Ratio (λ)**: Performance on LIBERO-Long is significantly affected by `λ`. Small `λ` (e.g., 0.2) leads to severe task interference (near-zero success), while `λ` between 0.6 and 0.9 yields over 70% success. This indicates that moderate sparsity in task masks effectively balances task-specific and merged task vectors, improving merge stability.
 - **Impact of Subspace for Routing**: Using only value (V) projections for test-time task routing resulted in 89.7% average success on LIBERO. In contrast, using only key (K) projections or a combination of K and V led to substantial performance drops (53.6% and 65.1% respectively), with complete failure in some tasks. This suggests that value projections, which capture behavioral semantics, provide a more reliable indicator of task identity than key projections.

- **Preliminary Investigations on OpenVLA (Supplementary Material)**:
 - **Non-mergeable Components**: The language model body (excluding the `lm head`) was identified as the primary non-mergeable component in OpenVLA, leading to complete failure upon merging. Other components (vision backbone, projector, `lm head`) could be merged with less performance impact.
 - **Progressive Block-wise Merging**: A block-wise study of OpenVLA's language model showed that merging deeper transformer blocks progressively degraded performance, confirming that task conflicts intensify with layer depth.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a framework, MergeVLA, that addresses a critical and previously unexplored challenge in the field of robotic learning: the inability to effectively merge independently fine-tuned Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models into a single, cohesive policy. The work provides a detailed empirical analysis of the root causes of this non-mergeability, which were identified as destructive LoRA parameter interference and architectural incompatibility within action experts.

The significance and potential impact of MergeVLA are multi-faceted:

1. **Enabling Scalable Generalist Embodied Agents**: By demonstrating that model merging is feasible for VLAs, MergeVLA offers a scalable pathway toward creating generalist embodied agents. This capability allows for the efficient consolidation of diverse learned skills from multiple specialized VLA models into a single unified agent, circumventing the need for extensive joint retraining on combined datasets. This could significantly reduce computational resources and data requirements for developing versatile robots.
2. **Enhanced Robustness and Generalization**: The architectural redesign, particularly the removal of self-attention in the action expert and the adoption of a sigmoid gate, combined with task-specific masking, contributes to improved out-of-distribution generalization. MergeVLA demonstrates robustness across various shifts, including visual appearances, language descriptions, and even different robotic embodiments. This is crucial for deploying robots in dynamic and unpredictable real-world environments.
3. **Practical Applicability in Real-World Robotics**: The validation of MergeVLA's capabilities on a real SO-101 robotic arm, achieving performance comparable to individually fine-tuned models for multi-skill tasks, underscores its practical utility. This indicates that the proposed framework can be deployed on physical hardware, making it a viable solution for developing more capable and adaptable robotic systems for complex manipulation tasks.
4. **Foundation for Future Research**: The insights derived from analyzing VLA non-mergeability and the solutions proposed by MergeVLA open new avenues for research. Future work can explore the compatibility of MergeVLA with larger VLM backbones, the impact of pretraining on diverse robot datasets, and a deeper understanding of task specialization across visual and linguistic pathways. The methodology contributes to the broader understanding of knowledge transfer and modularity in complex multimodal models for embodied AI.

In conclusion, MergeVLA represents a methodological advancement that transforms the previous limitation of VLA model non-mergeability into a scalable approach for building generalist robotic agents, with implications for efficiency, versatility, and real-world deployment.
