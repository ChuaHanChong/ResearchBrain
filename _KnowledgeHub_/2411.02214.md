# DexHub and DART: Towards Internet Scale Robot Data Collection

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at MIT's Improbable AI Lab developed DART (Dexterous Augmented Reality Teleoperation) and DexHub, an integrated system that uses AR for low-latency teleoperation of virtual robots in cloud simulation and a public data hub, to enable internet-scale robot data collection. This approach achieved 2.1 times faster data collection than real-world methods and generated policies that exhibited greater robustness and zero-shot Sim2Real transfer in diverse environments.

## Problem

- The development of generalist robotic systems is limited by the scarcity of diverse and high-quality data.
- Current robot data collection methods are labor-intensive, suffer from scalability issues, and primarily focus on single-arm robots with narrow scope.
- Existing simulation-based teleoperation systems often face challenges with latency, visual fidelity, and lack intuitive interfaces for large-scale data generation.

## Method

- DART (==Dexterous Augmented Reality Teleoperation==) provides an AR-based platform for controlling robots in cloud-hosted MuJoCo simulations, leveraging local rendering on AR devices (e.g., Apple Vision Pro) and low-latency gRPC communication for hand tracking and simulation state.
- DexHub (Robot Data Hub on Cloud) serves as a continuously growing, open-sourced cloud repository for robot learning data, offering APIs for both contributing and retrieving collected robot interactions.
- The system supports various robots and allows users to import custom scenes, features one-click resets, and enables instant task switching to improve operator efficiency and reduce fatigue.

## Results

- DART achieved **2.1** times faster data collection throughput in user studies compared to real-world teleoperation setups, completing tasks like bolt and nut sorting at **7.8** parts/minute versus **3.6** parts/minute for real systems.
- Policies trained with DART-collected simulation data showed zero-shot Sim2Real transfer and significantly higher success rates in challenging real-world scenarios; for example, achieving **60%** success with background changes (vs. **10%** for real-world policies) and **70%** with unseen distractions (vs. **0%**).
- User feedback indicated DART was more enjoyable, less fatiguing, and offered better visual observation, with intuitive hand tracking being sufficient and preferred over heavier kinematic teacher devices.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - AR-driven teleoperation in cloud simulation significantly increases data collection throughput and reduces operator physical and cognitive fatigue compared to real-world teleoperation.
> - Low-latency communication of hand-tracking data and simulation states, combined with local AR rendering, overcomes the visual latency issues common in streamed simulation environments.
> - Policies trained on aggressively augmented simulation data from DART demonstrate superior robustness and zero-shot Sim2Real transfer compared to policies trained on real-world data, even in novel and challenging environments.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of generalist robotic systems is presently constrained by the availability of diverse and high-quality data. Current approaches to robot data collection largely fall into two categories: compiling existing datasets from various institutions or actively collecting large-scale datasets through real-world teleoperation. Projects such as Open X-Embodiment, RH20T, Bridge Data, and Droid have contributed significant datasets. However, these efforts often face limitations including scalability issues due to labor-intensive collection, narrow scope primarily focused on single-arm robots with parallel jaw grippers, and exclusive reliance on real-world data, which may not fully leverage the potential of simulation for data augmentation and reinforcement learning.

Simultaneously, the use of simulation for collecting robotic datasets has been explored. Prior work has utilized webcams, VR interfaces with exoskeletons, or modern VR/AR devices for hand tracking in simulated environments. These systems commonly rely on stereo rendering streams for visual feedback, which can introduce latency and necessitate trade-offs between visual fidelity and real-time performance. Augmented Reality (AR) has also been applied to robotics, primarily for waypoint-based teaching, visual cues during human motion recording, or guiding real-world teleoperation. However, a comprehensive solution that leverages AR for intuitive, low-latency teleoperation of virtual robots in simulation for large-scale, crowd-sourced data collection, while also providing an accessible public data hub, had not been thoroughly explored.

This work addresses these gaps by proposing a system that integrates Augmented Reality for simulation-based teleoperation with a cloud-hosted data repository. It aims to overcome the physical, logistical, and computational bottlenecks of traditional data collection methods, offering a pathway toward internet-scale data generation and accessibility, which could support a wider range of robot learning paradigms, including those requiring digital twins for exploration and refinement.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to address the scarcity of diverse and high-quality data, which is identified as a bottleneck in the development of generalist robotic systems. The authors aim to establish a scalable and accessible framework for robot data collection that mitigates the limitations of existing methods.

The specific motivations and objectives are detailed as follows:

- **Overcome Scalability Issues in Data Collection:** Current real-world data collection efforts are hindered by requirements for specific robot hardware, complex physical environment setups, and time-consuming, physically demanding resets after each task completion. These factors limit the throughput and diversity of collected data. The research seeks to enable internet-scale data collection, similar to how human language, images, and videos are uploaded and shared globally.
- **Improve Teleoperation Efficiency and Reduce Operator Fatigue:** Traditional teleoperation methods often lead to physical and cognitive fatigue due to repetitive tasks, manual environment resets, and challenges in observing and understanding scenes (e.g., visual occlusions, lack of tactile feedback, network delays in remote operation). The objective is to create an intuitive, low-latency teleoperation experience that increases data collection throughput and reduces operator discomfort.
- **Leverage Cloud-Based Simulation and Augmented Reality:** The paper aims to utilize cloud-based simulation to provide an unlimited number of scenes without physical setup and Augmented Reality (AR) for a detailed, low-occlusion visual feedback mechanism. This approach is intended to circumvent the physical constraints and visual limitations of real-world or streamed simulation environments.
- **Enable Robust Policy Training via Simulation Data:** A key motivation is to demonstrate that policies trained on simulation-generated data can effectively transfer to real-world environments and exhibit greater robustness. This is predicated on the ability to apply diverse data augmentation and randomization strategies in simulation that are not feasible in real-world data collection.
- **Establish a Centralized, Accessible Data Hub:** The authors propose to create a publicly available, cloud-hosted data repository, DexHub, to automatically store all collected data. This aims to foster an ecosystem where data can be easily contributed, shared, and accessed by the broader robotics community, facilitating collaborative research and accelerating robot learning.
- **Broaden Accessibility to Robotics Data Collection:** The system is designed to allow individuals without specialized robotics knowledge or complex hardware setups to participate in data collection, thereby potentially expanding the pool of data contributors.

In essence, the research is motivated by the need for a scalable, efficient, and accessible method to generate diverse robot learning datasets, and it proposes a solution integrating AR-driven teleoperation in cloud-hosted simulation with a centralized data sharing platform.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research introduces two interconnected components: DART (Dexterous Augmented Reality Teleoperation) and DexHub (Robot Data Hub on Cloud).

#### 3.1. DART: Dexterous Augmented Reality Teleoperation

DART is designed as an AR-based teleoperation platform for controlling robots in a cloud-hosted simulation environment.

**System Architecture:**
- **Simulation Assets as AR Objects:** DART utilizes Apple's RealityKit to render all simulation assets, including robots and environments, as photo-realistic AR objects overlaid onto the operator's real-world view. This local rendering on the AR device (e.g., Apple Vision Pro) is intended to eliminate latency associated with transmitting large image data packets and improve the real-timeliness of the simulation by offloading rendering computation.
- **Low-Latency Communication:** Communication between the AR device and the cloud-hosted simulation employs gRPC for low-latency, asynchronous bidirectional data transfer. The AR device transmits compact hand-tracking data, and the simulation asynchronously sends back its state. This approach significantly reduces network load compared to systems streaming raw visual data (e.g., RGB images), with packet sizes over 1,000 times smaller.
- **Cloud-Hosted Simulation:** The robot simulation, powered by MuJoCo, runs dynamically on AWS Elastic Container Registry (ECR). Each user's simulation instance is launched in the cloud, aiming to provide open access and minimize user setup costs. The compact packet sizes are reported to mitigate the latency impact of cloud hosting compared to local hosting.
- **Hand Tracking and Mapping:** DART uses Apple’s ARKit to track hand and wrist keypoints. For robots with parallel-jaw grippers, four finger keypoints are used to determine the robot's end-effector SE(3) pose. For dexterous five-fingered hands, six position-only keypoints (five fingertips and one wrist) are used. Differential Inverse Kinematics (IK) is employed, defining position-only tracking costs for keypoints and incorporating safety constraints like self-collision avoidance.

**System Features:**
- **Pre-Designed Robots and Scenes:** The platform offers support for various robots, including Franka Research 3, UR-5 (with Robotiq 2F-85, Panda Hand, Allegro Hand end-effectors), Unitree Humanoid Series (G1), and ALOHA. High-fidelity MuJoCo models are integrated.
- **Importing Custom Scenes:** Users can upload custom simulation environments and assets via an online portal, extending the platform's capabilities.
- **One-Click Reset:** The simulation environment can be reset with a single click, aiming to reduce operator fatigue and increase data collection throughput by eliminating manual physical resets.
- **Instant Task Switching:** The system allows for quick transitions between different tasks and simulation environments to mitigate mental fatigue from repetitive tasks.

**Capability and Task Diversity:**
DART is designed to support a range of tasks from simple object manipulation to complex, precise, and dexterous maneuvers, including fine motor skills, household chores, and dexterous manipulation (e.g., solving a Rubik’s cube).

#### 3.2. DexHub: Central Data Hub for Robot Learning on the Cloud

DexHub is designed as a cloud-hosted data repository for robot learning, serving as a central hub for demonstrations collected through DART and other setups.

**Purpose and Vision:**
- DexHub aims to be a continuously growing, open-sourced data hub where users can retrieve their own and, upon curation, public datasets.
- It provides an API that allows users to log robot interactions regardless of whether they are using DART. This is intended to enable widespread data contribution.
- A user authentication system attributes contributions, offering a potential mechanism for future reward systems.

**API for End-Users:**
- **Downstream API:** Users can retrieve their collected data via an API key, which returns downloadable links for log files. The API is also intended to provide access to a global dataset comprising contributions from other users, once curated.
- **Upstream API:** A Python-based API (dexhub.log(obs, act)) allows users to contribute robot interactions to DexHub from any robot execution script, even without an AR device. All contributions are logged and attributed.

#### 3.3. Experiments

The methodology includes two main experimental components:
- **User Study (Section IV-A):** Conducted with nine participants lacking prior robotics experience, this study assessed DART's intuitiveness and usability. It compared real-world vs. simulation teleoperation, different visual perception methods (AR vs. stereo/mono RGB stream, active vs. fixed viewpoint), and control interfaces (hand tracking IK vs. kinematic double). Participants organized bolts and nuts for 7 minutes in varying settings.
- **Sim2Real and Generalizability (Section IV-B):** This component evaluated the transferability and robustness of policies trained with DART-collected simulation data to real-world robots. Two RGB vision policies were compared: one trained on real-world data and one on DART simulation data, both with 50 minutes of operator effort using ACT implementation. Real-world datasets had basic augmentations (Gaussian blur, color-jitter), while DART datasets were additionally augmented with camera extrinsic/intrinsic randomization, background replacement, and lighting randomization. Policies were evaluated in six diverse real-world environments (nominal lab, camera pose change, unseen distractions, background change, lighting change, location change).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded findings related to teleoperation efficiency, visual perception, control interfaces, and the robustness of policies trained with simulation data.

#### 4.1. User Study Findings

The user study, involving participants with no prior robotics experience, provided quantitative and qualitative comparisons across different teleoperation setups.

- **Real-World vs. Simulation Teleoperation:**
 - **Throughput:** DART enabled 2.1 times faster data collection throughput compared to real-world teleoperation. For the bolt and nut sorting task, DART averaged 7.8 parts/minute, whereas real-world systems (ViperX arms with kinematic doubles) achieved significantly lower rates (e.g., 3.6 parts/minute). Similar trends were observed across four different bimanual tasks and robot systems (ViperX, RB-Y1), with DART consistently outperforming real-world setups.
 - **Time Allocation:** In real-world data collection, a substantial portion of time was spent on physical environment resets and managing hardware failures. In DART, most of the time was dedicated to actual data collection, reducing unproductive periods.
 - **Operator Feedback:** Participants attributed the throughput gap to physical fatigue during real-world teleoperation and difficulty observing local contact interactions. Qualitative feedback indicated DART was more enjoyable, less physically fatiguing, and offered better visual observation.

- **Effect of Visual Observation on Human Operator’s Performance:**
 - **Latency vs. Fidelity Trade-off:** Methods transmitting simulation renderings over the network (stereo and mono RGB streams) resulted in significant drops in data collection throughput compared to DART, which transmits raw simulation states.
 - **Depth Perception and Nausea:** Mono rendering, which limits depth perception, showed a performance drop compared to stereo rendering. Some participants reported nausea with fixed-IPD stereo rendering, whereas DART's reliance on VisionOS's native rendering, which dynamically adjusts to user IPD, did not elicit similar reports.
 - **Active Perception:** Active viewpoint adjustment (allowing users to move their heads to explore surroundings) was found to be critical, with teleoperation without it reducing the data collection rate by 21.7%.

- **Control Method:**
 - **Kinematic Double vs. Hand Tracking IK:** The study indicated that a kinematically equivalent teacher device (kinematic double) did not significantly improve task success rate over inverse kinematics (IK) using hand tracking keypoints. Users reported that DART's intuitive hand tracking was sufficient or preferable due to reduced weight and strain.

#### 4.2. Sim2Real and Generalizability Findings

Experiments evaluating policies trained on DART-collected simulation data demonstrated robustness and transferability to real-world environments.

- **Zero-Shot Sim2Real Transfer:** Policies trained using DART data exhibited zero-shot Sim2Real transfer in nominal laboratory settings.
- **Enhanced Robustness:** DART-trained policies significantly outperformed policies trained exclusively on real-world data across a range of modified real-world environments. For the "Pick Mug in Basket" task, DART policies achieved a 60% success rate with background changes, compared to 10% for real-world trained policies. In the presence of unseen distractions, DART policies achieved 70% success, while real-world policies achieved 0%. Similar trends were observed for "Sorting Small Items."
- **Impact of Data Augmentation:** The robustness of DART-trained policies was attributed to the diverse data augmentation techniques feasible in simulation (e.g., randomizing camera extrinsics/intrinsics, background replacement with varied textures/images, lighting randomization). These augmentations are not readily applied to real-world collected data. This suggests that simulation-collected data, when aggressively augmented, can be more valuable for learning robust policies than real-world data.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents DART and DexHub as a system designed to address fundamental challenges in scaling robot data collection and democratizing access to robot learning datasets. The findings suggest several areas of significance and potential impact:

- **Increased Data Collection Throughput and Efficiency:** DART's demonstrated capability to achieve 2.1 times faster data collection throughput and significantly lower operator fatigue compared to real-world teleoperation platforms suggests a potential to accelerate the generation of large-scale robotic datasets. This efficiency gain could reduce the cost and time required for data acquisition.
- **Enhanced Accessibility to Robot Learning:** By leveraging AR and cloud-hosted simulation, DART reduces hardware requirements and complex physical setups, potentially enabling a broader range of individuals, including those without specialized robotics expertise, to contribute to data collection. This crowdsourcing model could foster a more diverse and extensive data ecosystem.
- **Improved Policy Robustness and Sim2Real Transfer:** The finding that policies trained on DART-collected data, augmented in simulation, exhibit greater robustness and zero-shot transfer to various real-world conditions is significant. This suggests that simulation can be a primary source for training generalizable robot policies, potentially reducing the need for extensive real-world data collection for robustness.
- **Establishment of a Centralized Data Hub:** DexHub's design as a cloud-hosted, open-sourced data repository with an API for contributions aims to address the fragmentation of robot learning datasets. It could facilitate data sharing, collaboration, and the continuous growth of community-driven datasets, mirroring the growth observed in language and vision domains.
- **Advancements in Teleoperation Interfaces:** The research contributes to the understanding of effective teleoperation interface design, demonstrating the benefits of AR for visual feedback and the sufficiency of intuitive hand tracking over more complex kinematic teacher devices, particularly in reducing physical strain.
- **Complementary Role of Simulation and Real-World Data:** While highlighting the advantages of simulation, the authors acknowledge its current limitations (e.g., simulating complex physics like chopping onions or deformable objects). The proposed system is intended to complement, rather than replace, real-world data collection, offering a balanced approach to leveraging the strengths of both.

Overall, this work contributes a integrated system that offers a pathway toward more scalable, efficient, and accessible robot data collection, with implications for accelerating progress in generalist robotics through robust policy learning and community-driven data initiatives.
