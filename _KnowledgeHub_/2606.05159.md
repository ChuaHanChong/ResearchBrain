# X4Val: Learning Neural Surrogates for Variance-Reduced Policy Evaluation

> [!summary] Summary
> X4Val introduces a framework to reduce the variance of real-world performance metric estimates for robotic systems by learning transferable neural surrogates from heterogeneous, non-paired auxiliary data. It consistently achieved variance reductions of up to 38.4% compared to baselines in autonomous driving and robot manipulation tasks, enabling more efficient and confident system validation.

## Problem

- Rigorous validation of learning-based robotic systems in safety-critical applications requires extensive, expensive, and time-consuming real-world testing to obtain reliable performance estimates.
- Existing control-variate methods for variance reduction are limited by their reliance on explicit, sample-wise pairing between real-world and auxiliary data, which is often unavailable or impractical.
- Abundant auxiliary data (e.g., simulations, historical policy logs, data from related platforms) remains underutilized for rigorous validation due to its non-paired and multi-domain nature, hindering robust statistical estimates.

## Method

- Projects heterogeneous data into a shared embedding space using a function to capture task-relevant structure from diverse sources like sensors, simulators, or feature embeddings.
- Trains a transferable ==neural surrogate== to predict the target metric from the embedded scenario representation, leveraging all available auxiliary datasets and a variance-aligned learning objective.
- Integrates the learned surrogate into a statistically unbiased, variance-reduced ==control-variate estimator==, employing cross-fitting to maximize sample efficiency and provide reliable confidence intervals.

## Results

- In autonomous driving, X4Val achieved 15-**20%** variance reductions for policy validation in new geographic regions and **38.4%** variance reduction for iterative policy development by leveraging historical data.
- For robot manipulation tasks, the framework consistently reduced the variance of real-world success rate estimates by effectively using non-paired simulation data from ManiSkill.
- Across diverse robotic development pipelines, X4Val consistently provided tighter confidence intervals and outperformed standard Monte Carlo and classical control-variate baselines, improving the sample efficiency of rigorous policy evaluation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Statistically valid variance reduction is achievable in robotic validation by learning and leveraging neural surrogates, even when auxiliary data is heterogeneous and lacks sample-wise pairing with target-domain samples.
> - A custom learning objective for the neural surrogate that prioritizes minimizing the variance of the residual term, rather than standard mean-squared error, improves the overall estimator's performance.
> - Cross-fitting (K-fold cross-validation style) allows for efficient utilization of limited labeled target-domain data for both surrogate training and unbiased residual estimation, ensuring statistical independence across samples.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Rigorous evaluation and validation are prerequisite steps for the deployment of learning-based robotic systems, particularly in safety-critical applications such as autonomous driving and robot manipulation. This necessity creates a demand for accurate and high-confidence estimates of policy performance under real-world conditions. However, acquiring sufficient real-world test data to support such estimates is often economically unfeasible and time-consuming, as real-world testing is expensive, may expose systems to safety risks, and data collection rates can be limited.

The broader research landscape addresses this challenge through various variance-reduction techniques in Monte Carlo estimation, including importance sampling, stratified sampling, and control variates. Control variates, which utilize correlated surrogate signals whose expectation is more readily estimable, have found application in empirical validation. Notable approaches like Prediction-Powered Inference (PPI) and its variants leverage prediction models as control variates. In the context of robotics, methods such as SureSim and Sim2Val have applied control variates to reduce real-world sample requirements by exploiting paired evaluations between real-world and simulated interactions. These methods typically rely on the ability to reconstruct real-world scenarios in a high-fidelity simulator, creating a direct, sample-wise pairing between real and surrogate measurements.

A limitation of these existing control-variate methods is their reliance on explicit sample-wise pairing. In many practical scenarios, such pairing is unavailable or impractical. For example, real-world data might originate from older policy versions, simulation data may be generated for different inputs or environments, or data could be collected across multiple hardware platforms lacking direct correspondence. These situations leave abundant, inexpensive auxiliary data sources—such as simulations, historical policy logs, or data from related platforms—underutilized for rigorous validation due to their non-paired and multi-domain nature.

This work differentiates itself by moving beyond the strict pairing requirement. It connects to the reinforcement learning (RL) literature on off-policy, off-environment, and off-domain evaluation, which also seeks to estimate policy performance using auxiliary data, often correcting for distribution shifts using importance sampling or doubly robust estimators. However, X4Val targets a more general "black-box" validation setting, where the system under evaluation might be a complete autonomy stack, the metric an arbitrary scenario-level outcome, and assumptions about action likelihoods or transition models typically made in RL may not hold or be ill-defined.

Furthermore, X4Val integrates concepts from transfer learning, multi-task learning, and meta-learning, which focus on leveraging knowledge from source tasks or domains to improve performance on target tasks. While these machine learning paradigms are broad, X4Val applies them specifically to learn a neural surrogate that acts as a statistically valid control variate, thereby extending the utility of control-variate estimation to a wider range of data availability scenarios encountered in robotic system development.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the sample efficiency and confidence of real-world performance metric estimation for robotic systems by developing a general framework for variance reduction in the presence of non-paired, multi-domain data.

The motivation stems from several practical challenges in robotic system development and deployment:

- **High Cost of Real-World Data:** Rigorous validation requires extensive real-world testing to obtain reliable performance estimates. However, real-world data collection is inherently expensive, time-consuming, and can pose safety risks, making it impractical to gather large volumes of data for every policy iteration or scenario. This constraint limits the statistical power of performance estimates, leading to broader confidence intervals and potentially hindering deployment decisions.
- **Abundance of Underutilized Auxiliary Data:** Practitioners typically have access to diverse auxiliary data sources, including data from high-fidelity simulations, historical logs from previous policy versions, evaluations from related hardware platforms, or data collected in different geographical environments. These data are often cheap and abundant. However, they generally differ from the true deployment setting (e.g., due to sim-to-real gap, policy evolution, or environment differences) and cannot be treated as direct substitutes for real-world measurements. Existing variance reduction techniques, particularly those based on control variates, frequently require a precise, sample-wise pairing between real and surrogate data, rendering much of this auxiliary data unusable.
- **Need for Statistically Rigorous Validation:** For safety-critical systems, validation must be statistically rigorous, providing high-confidence performance estimates that account for uncertainties. Methods that leverage auxiliary data must maintain unbiasedness and provide reliable confidence intervals, even when the auxiliary data exhibits domain shift or lacks direct correspondence to real-world observations.

To address these points, X4Val aims to:

1. **Exploit Non-Paired Heterogeneous Data:** Develop a principled methodology to leverage a wide array of auxiliary data—regardless of whether it's paired with real-world samples or comes from different domains (e.g., simulators, older policies, different regions)—to improve real-world metric estimation.
2. **Learn Transferable Neural Surrogates:** Train a neural network to predict real-world metrics from a shared representation space, effectively learning cross-domain structure. This surrogate must be robust enough to provide a useful signal even when trained on data from domains that differ from the target.
3. **Integrate Surrogates into a Control-Variate Estimator:** Incorporate the learned neural surrogate into a control-variates framework. This allows the framework to benefit from the correlation between the surrogate's predictions and the true metric, thereby reducing the variance of the overall estimator, while a residual correction mechanism maintains statistical unbiasedness.
4. **Generalize and Extend Existing Methods:** Create a framework that generalizes classical paired control-variate approaches by removing the strict pairing requirement, making it applicable to broader and more realistic evaluation scenarios, such as iterative policy development, geographical transfer of policies, and cross-platform evaluations.

By achieving these objectives, X4Val intends to enable more efficient and confident validation of robotic systems, facilitating their development and safe deployment.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

X4Val enhances validation efficiency by learning a neural surrogate of the target metric and subsequently employing this surrogate as a control variate within an estimation framework. The approach is structured into three main stages, designed to leverage heterogeneous auxiliary data while preserving statistical validity.

The problem is formally defined as estimating the expected value of a metric of interest, F, under a target scenario distribution P_X, denoted as E_X∼P_X[F(X)]. The framework assumes access to three types of data:
- `D_lab`: A limited set of `n` labeled target-domain samples `{(X_i, G_i, F_i)}`, where `F_i` is the expensive metric measurement.
- `D_X`: An abundant set of `m` unlabeled target-domain scenario-only samples `{(X_unlab_i, G_i)}`, where `G_i` represents inexpensive scenario inputs and `F_i` is not observed.
- `D_aux_j`: Multiple auxiliary datasets `D_aux_1..., D_aux_J` from related domains, which may include metric measurements or surrogate signals but are not necessarily sampled from P_X.

The three stages of X4Val are:

1. **Projecting Heterogeneous Data into a Shared Embedding Space:**
 To enable learning from diverse data sources (e.g., different platforms, simulators, policy versions, sensor modalities, or environments), X4Val first maps each scenario into a common representation space. A function `ϕ` maps a scenario `X` and any inexpensive signals `G` associated with it to a shared representation `z = ϕ(X, G)`. This `z` can consist of hand-designed features, learned embeddings from sensor observations (e.g., DINOv3 embeddings), foundation-model features, or simulator-derived quantities. The purpose of `ϕ` is to capture task-relevant structure that is predictive of the metric `F`.

2. **Learning a Transferable Neural Surrogate:**
 Following the embedding, a neural surrogate `f_θ: z → bF` is trained to predict the target metric `F` from the embedded scenario representation `z`. This stage is critical for leveraging auxiliary data.
 - **Data Splitting:** The limited labeled target-domain dataset `D_lab` is split into `D_train` for training/adapting the surrogate and `D_est` for estimating the residual correction in the subsequent estimation stage. This ensures statistical independence.
 - **Surrogate Training:** The neural surrogate `f_θ` is trained using `D_train` along with all available auxiliary datasets `D_aux_j`. This training can employ various transfer learning or meta-learning strategies, such as pretraining on auxiliary domains and finetuning on `D_train`, multi-task learning across auxiliary signals, or amortized meta-learning to adapt the surrogate to new domains with few examples.
 - **Learning Objective:** While mean-squared error (MSE) is a common objective, X4Val suggests an objective aligned with variance reduction: `L_X4Val = dVar_Dest[F - f_θ(ϕ(X,G))] + (n_est/m) dVar_DX[f_θ(ϕ(X,G))]`. This objective prioritizes minimizing the variance of the residual term and, if `m` is not significantly larger than `n_est`, also considers the variance of the surrogate's predictions over target-domain scenarios. The surrogate `f_θ` does not need to be an unbiased predictor of the target metric, as its bias is corrected later.
 - **Scaling:** An optional post-hoc scaling `f_θ(z) = β f_θ_tilde(z)` can be applied, where `β` is chosen on held-out target-domain data to minimize the empirical variance.

3. **Variance-Reduced Estimation with a Neural Surrogate:**
 The learned surrogate `f_θ` is then integrated into a control-variate estimator to estimate the target mean `μ = E_X∼P_X[F]`. The X4Val estimator `ˆμ_X4Val` is formulated as:
 `ˆμ_X4Val = (1/m) Σ_(X_unlab_i,G_i)∈D_X f_θ(ϕ(X_unlab_i, G_i)) + (1/n_est) Σ_(X_i,G_i,F_i)∈D_est [F_i - f_θ(ϕ(X_i, G_i))]`
 - **Unbiasedness:** This estimator is unbiased, provided that `D_X` and `D_est` are drawn from the target deployment distribution `P_X`, and `f_θ` is trained independently of the labels in `D_est`. Any systematic bias in `f_θ` is corrected by the second term, the mean of the residuals.
 - **Variance Reduction:** The variance of the estimator `Var(ˆμ_X4Val | f_θ) = (1/m) Var_X∼P_X[f_θ(ϕ(X,G))] + (1/n_est) Var_X∼P_X[F - f_θ(ϕ(X,G))]`. The effectiveness of X4Val relies on `f_θ` reducing the variance of the residual term. When `m` is large, the first term becomes small, and the primary focus is on learning a surrogate highly correlated with the target metric.
 - **Cross-fitting:** To improve sample efficiency and utilize all `n` labeled data points, a cross-validation-style approach (K-fold cross-fitting) is employed. `D_lab` is partitioned into `K` disjoint folds. For each fold, a surrogate is trained on data outside that fold, and the fold's samples are used for residual estimation. The final cross-fitted estimator combines predictions from all fold-wise surrogates and residuals across all labeled samples.
 - **Confidence Intervals:** Approximate `(1-α)` confidence intervals are derived using the Central Limit Theorem. For the cross-fitted estimator, it takes the form `C_cf_α = [ˆμ_X4Val,cf ± z_1-α/2 sqrt(ˆσ_f^2/m + ˆσ_Δ^2/n)]`, where `ˆσ_f^2` is the empirical variance of the fold-averaged surrogate predictions on `D_X`, and `ˆσ_Δ^2` is the empirical variance of the held-out residuals across all labeled target-domain samples.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents both theoretical contributions and empirical evaluations across autonomous driving and robot manipulation tasks to demonstrate the efficacy of X4Val.

**Theoretical Contributions:**
1. **Framework Introduction:** X4Val is introduced as a framework designed for variance-reduced estimation of real-world performance metrics in robotic validation settings, specifically leveraging heterogeneous, non-paired auxiliary data sources.
2. **Learning-Based Control-Variate Estimator:** The paper develops a novel control-variate estimator that incorporates transferable neural surrogates. These surrogates are trained effectively using limited real-world labels alongside abundant data from auxiliary domains.
3. **Statistical Validity and Generalization:** It is shown that the proposed estimator maintains statistical validity (unbiasedness) and generalizes classical paired control-variate approaches. This indicates that X4Val retains the benefits of traditional methods while extending variance reduction capabilities to scenarios where sample-wise pairing is absent.

**Empirical Findings - Autonomous Driving Case Studies:**
The autonomous driving experiments utilized a lightweight motion-planning policy and the NVIDIA PhysicalAI-Autonomous-Vehicles dataset, evaluated using the AlpaSim simulator. Scenario features were based on DINOv3 camera embeddings, ego-vehicle velocity, and acceleration.

1. **Case Study 1: Autonomous Vehicle Deployment to a New Geographic Region (Germany vs. United States)**
 - **Scenario:** Validating an AV policy in a new deployment region (Germany) using limited target-domain evaluation data, while leveraging abundant auxiliary data from prior evaluations in a source region (United States).
 - **Metric:** Final Displacement Error (FDE) at 3 seconds.
 - **Results:** X4Val consistently achieved variance reductions of between 15-20% compared to baselines such as Monte Carlo (MC), Cross-Prediction-Powered Inference (CPPI), and Control Variates with Metric Correlator Function (CV_MCF). This demonstrates its ability to efficiently combine limited target-domain data with auxiliary source-region data. An ablation study with X4Val (MSE), which used a standard mean-squared-error objective instead of the variance-aligned objective, showed reduced performance, supporting the design choice for the learning objective.

2. **Case Study 2: Iterative Policy Development of an Autonomous Driving Policy**
 - **Scenario:** Validating a newly trained policy with limited current-policy data, by leveraging historical evaluation data from five earlier policy versions trained on progressively larger datasets.
 - **Metric:** Closed-loop distance to ground truth trajectory at 3 seconds.
 - **Results:** X4Val reduced the estimator variance by 38.4% relative to the MC baseline. This performance exceeded that of classical CV, CPPI, and CV_MCF, indicating significant gains from incorporating historical, non-paired data from earlier policy versions. The use of an amortized meta-learning strategy for the neural surrogate proved effective in leveraging this diverse historical data. The study also highlighted that proper control-weight optimization is crucial for CV_MCF in this specific scenario, as an un-optimized version performed worse than Simple MC.

**Empirical Findings - Robot Manipulation Experiments:**
The robot manipulation experiments involved a block-stacking task performed by a Franka Panda robot arm in two environments: the ManiSkill simulator and a real-world setup.

1. **Scenario:** Estimating the mean success rate of a real-world visuomotor policy, given limited real-world evaluation data (100 rollouts) and abundant simulation data (5,000 rollouts) from ManiSkill. The setup makes explicit pairing between sim and real-world rollouts challenging.
2. **Methodology:** DINOv2 features were extracted from initial images, and an MLP was trained as a predictor using a pretrain-finetune strategy (pretrained on ManiSkill, finetuned on real-world data). Cross-fitting was applied with varying numbers of folds (K).
3. **Results:** X4Val consistently reduced the variance of the mean success rate estimator compared to the MC baseline across all tested values of K. The amount of variance reduction generally increased with K, which is attributed to larger training data available per fold for the surrogate, leading to more stable predictors. This demonstrates the framework's capability to leverage non-paired cross-platform data (sim-to-real) for robust real-world metric estimation in manipulation tasks.

Across these diverse robotic development pipelines, X4Val consistently provided variance reductions and tighter confidence intervals compared to standard Monte Carlo and classical control-variate baselines, indicating its effectiveness in improving the sample efficiency of rigorous policy evaluation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The X4Val framework presents a significant contribution to the field of robotic system validation, with several potential impacts:

- **Enhanced Sample Efficiency for Robotic Validation:** The core impact of X4Val is its ability to substantially reduce the number of expensive real-world data points required for rigorous performance evaluation. By achieving variance reductions of up to 38.4% in the presented case studies, the framework enables more confident and statistically robust validation with fewer resources. This directly addresses a critical bottleneck in robotic development, where real-world testing is a primary cost and time driver.
- **Broader Applicability of Variance Reduction Techniques:** X4Val extends the applicability of control-variate methods to scenarios where traditional approaches fail due to the absence of sample-wise paired data. This is particularly relevant in practical robotics, where data often originates from heterogeneous sources such as historical policy versions, different geographical locations, or distinct hardware platforms that do not allow for direct one-to-one correspondence.
- **Leveraging Underutilized Data:** The framework provides a principled mechanism for extracting valuable information from abundant, but previously underutilized, auxiliary data sources. This includes simulation data, logs from earlier development stages, or data from related environments. By transforming these disparate data into a shared embedding space and training transferable neural surrogates, X4Val enables their incorporation into statistically valid estimation processes.
- **Facilitating Iterative Development and Deployment:** In contexts like autonomous driving, where policies are continuously updated and evaluated, X4Val can provide tighter confidence intervals for new policies by leveraging historical evaluation data. This acceleration of the validation cycle can expedite iterative development and increase confidence in deployment decisions for safety-critical systems. For geographical transfers or cross-platform evaluations, it offers a method to assess performance in new contexts without requiring extensive new paired data collection.
- **Generalization of Classical Methods:** X4Val successfully generalizes classical paired control-variate approaches, effectively encompassing them as a special case while offering extended capabilities for non-paired data. This evolutionary step allows researchers and practitioners to apply a unified framework across a wider spectrum of validation challenges.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The work opens avenues for future research, such as extending the framework beyond mean estimation to other validation targets like quantiles or tail-risk metrics, which are crucial for safety assessments. Furthermore, the learned neural surrogates could be utilized to guide active data collection, optimizing the selection of scenarios most informative for further reducing validation uncertainty.

In summary, X4Val offers a practical and statistically sound solution to a pervasive challenge in robotics validation, promising to accelerate development, improve the reliability of performance estimates, and contribute to the safer deployment of autonomous systems.
