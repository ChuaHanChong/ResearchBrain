# ThinkGrasp: A Vision-Language System for Strategic Part Grasping in Clutter

> [!summary] Summary
> Northeastern University and Boston Dynamics AI Institute developed ThinkGrasp, a vision-language system that uses GPT-4o for strategic part grasping in heavily cluttered environments. The system achieves an average success rate of 0.980 in general clutter and 0.789 in heavy clutter in simulation, demonstrating enhanced capabilities in handling occluded objects and generalizing to unseen items.

## Problem

- Robotic grasping systems struggle with accurately identifying and manipulating target objects in heavily cluttered environments, where objects are frequently occluded or hidden.
- Existing deep learning and vision-language models often require extensive task-specific data and show limited generalization to diverse, unseen objects under severe occlusion.
- Challenges include interpreting ambiguous natural language instructions, adapting to dynamic scenes, and ensuring safe, stable, and efficient multi-step grasp planning.

## Method

- **ThinkGrasp** employs an iterative, closed-loop vision-language pipeline that integrates GPT-4o for high-level strategic reasoning to select target or obstructing objects.
- It uses a k x k grid strategy to guide grasp region suggestions and specialized segmentation modules (LangSAM/VLPart) for precise object/part masking based on GPT-4o's output.
- The system continuously captures new scene observations and refines its strategy, allowing for progressive occlusion handling and error correction until the task is completed.

## Results

- In simulation, ThinkGrasp achieved an average success rate of **0.980** across cluttered scenes and **0.789** in heavy clutter, outperforming baselines like VLG (**0.753**/**0.511**) and OVGrasp (**0.438**/**0.000**).
- Real-world experiments showed ThinkGrasp successfully completed multi-step tasks with high rates, such as removing an obstruction in 75-**90%** of trials and subsequently grasping the target object in **55.6**-**80%** of trials, exceeding VL-Grasp's performance.
- Ablation studies confirmed that GPT-4o's reasoning and the k x k grid strategy were crucial, with their removal or simplification leading to notable performance degradations.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Leveraging a general-purpose VLM like GPT-4o for high-level contextual reasoning enables strategic decision-making in clutter, such as identifying and removing obstructing objects, without extensive task-specific pretraining.
> - Separating the VLM's high-level reasoning (imagine segmentation, grasp region suggestion) from dedicated, precise segmentation modules ensures robustness by preventing potential VLM errors from propagating to the segmentation process.
> - An iterative, closed-loop system that adapts to scene changes and progressively uncovers occluded objects significantly improves grasping success rates in complex, heavily cluttered environments.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic grasping has advanced significantly, particularly with the integration of deep learning and vision-language models (VLMs). However, robotic manipulation in highly cluttered environments remains a substantial challenge. Traditional robotic grasping methods often rely on hand-crafted features and heuristics, limiting their generalization capabilities and robustness in diverse, unstructured settings. While deep learning approaches, utilizing Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) and reinforcement learning, have improved grasp planning and execution, they typically require extensive task-specific data collection and labeling, which can hinder scalability and effectiveness in novel scenarios.

More recently, vision-language models and large language models (LLMs) have been explored to enhance robotic grasping by bridging visual and linguistic information. Systems such as CLIPort and VL-Grasp have introduced language-conditioned perception and action, enabling robots to follow high-level instructions. Other models like ManipVQA, RoboScript, CoPa, and OVAL-Prompt leverage VLMs and contextual information for broader manipulation tasks, including perception, reasoning, and action planning. Despite these advancements, a persistent limitation across many existing methods is their struggle to effectively handle heavy occlusions, where target objects are severely obstructed or entirely hidden. This deficiency significantly restricts their applicability and performance in complex, real-world cluttered environments.

ThinkGrasp addresses this specific challenge by focusing on strategic grasping in heavily occluded scenes. It aims to overcome the limitations of prior VLM-based systems that do not adequately manage severe clutter, by integrating a powerful general-purpose VLM (GPT-4o) with an occlusion handling system. The work distinguishes itself by relying on the general reasoning capabilities of a foundation VLM rather than requiring task-specific pretraining or large grasp-specific datasets, which contributes to its generalization to diverse and unseen objects.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the persistent difficulties robots face in grasping objects within heavily cluttered environments. In such settings, target objects are frequently occluded or entirely obscured by other items, making direct identification and grasping challenging. Existing robotic grasping systems, including advanced deep learning and vision-language models, often exhibit limitations when confronted with severe occlusions and diverse, previously unseen objects. A robust solution capable of reliably identifying and grasping target objects under these conditions is therefore necessary.

The core problem definition addressed by ThinkGrasp is the selection of an appropriate grasp pose for a target object specified by natural language instructions, particularly when occlusions are prevalent. Specific challenges include:
- **Occlusions:** Objects are often partially or fully hidden, impeding identification and grasp planning.
- **Ambiguity in Natural Language Instructions:** Vague or unclear commands require sophisticated interpretation to discern user intent and locate the correct object among many.
- **Dynamic Environments:** Grasping strategies must adapt to changing object positions and orientations.
- **Safety and Stability:** Grasp poses must be secure to prevent damage to objects or the robot.
- **Efficiency:** Minimizing the number of steps to achieve a successful grasp is crucial for practical application.

To address these challenges, the research established several key objectives:
1. **Develop a plug-and-play vision-language grasping system:** The system should efficiently utilize visual and linguistic information for robotic grasping, specifically designed to handle heavy clutter.
2. **Leverage advanced contextual reasoning:** Integrate a large-scale pre-trained vision-language model, such as GPT-4o, to gain a visual understanding of environmental and object properties (e.g., sharpness, material composition) and use this knowledge to guide grasping strategies.
3. **Strategic Occlusion Handling:** Implement a system that can effectively identify and generate grasp poses for target objects even when they are heavily obstructed or nearly invisible, by strategically guiding the removal of obstructing objects through goal-oriented language.
4. **Enhance Robustness and Safety:** Improve success rates and ensure the safety of grasp poses by enabling the VLM to evaluate object visibility, accessibility, and task relevance at a high-level decision stage.
5. **Achieve Strong Generalization:** Enable the system to generalize effectively to diverse and previously unseen objects and complex environments without requiring additional grasp-specific datasets or task-level pretraining.
6. **Ensure Modularity and Adaptability:** Design the system with a modular, plug-and-play paradigm, allowing independent components (high-level decision-making, visual segmentation, grasp pose generation) to be easily replaced or integrated with different robotic platforms.
7. **Implement a Robust Error-Handling Framework:** Incorporate mechanisms using dedicated segmentation modules (LangSAM, VLPart) to ensure that errors in high-level language reasoning do not propagate to the segmentation process, leading to higher success rates and safer grasp execution.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

ThinkGrasp is an iterative, closed-loop vision-language system designed for strategic part grasping in cluttered environments. Its pipeline integrates a large vision-language model (GPT-4o) for high-level reasoning with dedicated perception and grasp generation modules.

**System Pipeline:**
1. **Input Acquisition:** The system receives an initial RGB-D scene observation (224x224 pixels for simulation, 640x480 for real robot) and a natural language instruction (e.g., "Give me a fruit").
2. **High-Level Decision Making (GPT-4o):**
 - **Imagine Segmentation:** GPT-4o, acting as the primary VLM, processes the visual scene and language instruction. It performs a hypothetical "imagine segmentation," identifying relevant objects or object parts without generating explicit segmentation masks. This process relies on high-level visual features and task context.
 - **Strategic Reasoning:** Based on a structured prompt, GPT-4o evaluates object visibility, accessibility, ease of grasping, task relevance, and safety. It decides whether to directly select the goal object (if visible) or an intermediate obstructing object whose removal is likely to reveal the goal.
 - **Grasp Region Suggestion (k x k Grid Strategy):** For identified objects, GPT-4o suggests suitable grasp locations by partitioning the potential target object's bounding box into a k x k grid (k=3 in this work). It then assigns a discrete index (1 to 9 for a 3x3 grid) indicating the preferred grasping region. This grid-based approach enables region-level reasoning, which is beneficial for low-resolution visual observations and compatible with robotic kinematic constraints.
3. **Segmentation (LangSAM / VLPart):**
 - The object name or object-part name predicted by GPT-4o is passed to a specialized segmentation module.
 - **LangSAM:** Utilized for general object segmentation, particularly effective with low-resolution images. It builds on GroundingDINO and Segment-Anything.
 - **VLPart:** Employed for detailed part-level segmentation when higher-resolution observations are available (e.g., segmenting a "handle").
 - **Fallback Mechanism:** If VLPart fails to accurately segment a part, LangSAM, combined with the k x k grid strategy, serves as a fallback. This division of responsibility ensures that potential errors in GPT-4o's high-level reasoning do not propagate to the segmentation process.
4. **Cropping Region Generation:** All segmented objects or object parts are used to crop a point cloud, focusing the system's attention on the relevant area.
5. **Grasp Pose Generation and Selection:**
 - **Candidate Grasp Poses:** A set of candidate 6-DoF grasp poses is generated from the cropped local point cloud. Graspnet-1Billion is used for simulation experiments, and FGC-Graspnet for real-robot experiments to ensure consistency.
 - **Evaluation:** Candidate grasp poses are evaluated based on their proximity to the preferred location suggested by GPT-4o's k x k grid and their grasp quality scores (using an improved force-closure metric). The pose with the highest score from the top-k candidates is selected.
6. **Grasp Execution:** The selected grasp pose is executed by the robot on the chosen target entity.
7. **Closed-Loop Iteration:** After each grasp attempt, the system captures a new scene observation. GPT-4o updates its "imagine segmentation" and predictions, and the entire pipeline reiterates. This iterative process allows the system to adapt to changes, progressively uncover occluded objects, and correct errors until the task is successfully completed or a maximum number of iterations is reached.

**GPT-4o's Role and Constraint Solver:**
GPT-4o plays a crucial role in target object selection by integrating visual and language information for contextual reasoning. It assesses object relevance, ease of grasping, and potential obstruction, focusing on objects most likely to lead to task completion. Its "imagine segmentation" combined with the k x k grid strategy enables region-level reasoning, which is robust to low-resolution inputs and guides the more precise segmentation modules.

**Robustness and Error Handling:**
The system incorporates an iterative refinement process. If a grasp fails or GPT-4o initially misidentifies an object, the system captures a new image and updates its strategy. The independent segmentation modules (LangSAM/VLPart) are designed to provide precise masks based on the object name from GPT-4o, and their scoring mechanism helps rectify initial VLM misidentifications by selecting the highest-scoring segmentation among matching candidates.

**Modularity:**
The design follows a plug-and-play paradigm, with distinct modules for high-level decision-making, visual segmentation, and grasp pose generation. This modularity allows for easy replacement or integration of components with different robotic platforms and grippers, enhancing versatility and scalability.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The ThinkGrasp system was evaluated through extensive experiments in both simulated and real-world environments, demonstrating its performance and generalization capabilities in cluttered scenarios.

**Simulation Experiments:**
- **Setup:** PyBullet simulator with a UR5 arm, ROBOTIQ-85 gripper, and an Intel RealSense L515 camera. Input images were resized to 224x224 pixels. GraspNet-1Billion was used for grasp generation to ensure fair comparison with baselines.
- **Baselines:** Performance was compared against Vision-Language Grasping (VLG) and OVGrasp. Ablation studies were also conducted, including "GPT4o (only)" (using only GPT-4o for grasp point selection), "no GPT4o," "no 3x3" (using bounding box center instead of grid), and "GPT crop."
- **Metrics:** Task Success Rate (average percentage of successful completions within 15 action attempts over 15 runs), Motion Number (average number of motions per task completion), and Average Success Step.
- **Overall Clutter Performance (Table 1, Figure 5):** ThinkGrasp achieved an average success rate of 0.980, with an average of 3.39 steps per task and 3.32 successful steps. This significantly outperformed VLG (0.753 success rate) and OVGrasp (0.438 success rate).
- **Heavy Clutter Scenarios (Table 1, Figure 3):** In these challenging scenarios involving up to 30 unseen objects and heavily occluded or invisible target objects (up to 50 action attempts allowed), ThinkGrasp achieved an average success rate of 0.789, with 19.35 average steps and 17.06 average success steps. This performance notably exceeded VLG (0.511 success rate) and OVGrasp (0.000 success rate). The results indicate strong generalization capabilities even with diverse, unseen objects and high clutter levels.
- **Ablation Studies (Table 1):** The ablation studies demonstrated the contribution of each component. Removing LangSAM from the pipeline and relying solely on GPT-4o for grasp point selection ("GPT4o (only)") resulted in a significant performance drop (0.713 success rate, 9.826 average steps). The "no 3x3" configuration (using bounding box center) also showed reduced performance (0.973 success rate, 3.40 average steps, but slightly worse success steps at 3.29, and 0.733 success rate in heavy clutter), highlighting the effectiveness of the k x k grid strategy and the integration of specialized segmentation modules.

**Real-World Experiments:**
- **Setup:** UR5 robotic arm (6 DoFs) with a Robotiq 85 gripper, RealSense D455 camera for RGB-D input. FGC-Graspnet was used for downstream grasp generation.
- **Baselines:** Compared against VL-Grasp.
- **Tasks:** Two multi-step tasks: "I want a tape" and "I want to cut something," involving initial removal of an obstructing object followed by grasping the target.
- **Results (Table 3):**
 - **"I want a tape":** ThinkGrasp achieved a Step 1 success rate of 15/20 (75%) for removing the obstructing toy dog and a Step 2 success rate of 12/15 (80%) for grasping the tape. VL-Grasp performed worse, with 11/20 (55%) for Step 1 and 0/11 for grasping the tape (though it could grasp a different object in 6/11 cases).
 - **"I want to cut something":** ThinkGrasp achieved an 18/20 (90%) Step 1 success rate for removing the toy frog and a 10/18 (55.6%) Step 2 success rate for grasping the knife by its handle. VL-Grasp had lower success rates (9/20 or 45% for Step 1 and 2/9 or 22.2% for Step 2).
- **Observed Limitations:** Some real-world failures were attributed to limitations of single-image data, potentially low-quality grasp poses from the downstream model (FGC-GraspNet), and variations in the UR5 robot's stability and control.

**Computational Aspects (Appendix A.6, A.7):**
- ThinkGrasp uses approximately 13GB of GPU memory in simulation.
- Real-world deployment uses a 12GB 2080Ti GPU workstation and a server with dual NVIDIA 3090 GPUs (Flask API for GPT-4o predictions).
- LangSAM requires ~9.38GB GPU memory, while VLPart runs optimally on dual 3090 GPUs.
- The system requires one API request per step, costing approximately $0.01 per step (for 224x224 resolution).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The ThinkGrasp system contributes to the field of robotic manipulation by addressing the significant challenge of grasping in heavily cluttered and occluded environments. Its development and demonstrated performance indicate several areas of significance and potential impact:

1. **Enhanced Robustness in Clutter:** The system significantly improves robotic grasping capabilities in complex scenarios where target objects are heavily occluded or nearly invisible. This advancement is crucial for deploying robots in unstructured environments like warehouses, domestic settings, and disaster zones, where objects are rarely presented in isolation.
2. **Leveraging General VLMs for Robotics:** ThinkGrasp effectively utilizes the advanced contextual reasoning of large-scale, pre-trained vision-language models like GPT-4o without requiring extensive task-specific pretraining or bespoke datasets. This approach reduces the data burden often associated with deep learning methods in robotics, making the system more adaptable to novel objects and tasks.
3. **Strategic Multi-Step Grasping:** The ability to strategically remove obstructing objects to uncover a target object represents a sophisticated form of robotic reasoning. This goes beyond direct single-shot grasping and enables robots to tackle more complex manipulation sequences.
4. **Strong Generalization Capabilities:** The system's performance with diverse and previously unseen objects in heavily cluttered environments indicates its potential for broad applicability. This generalization is vital for robots operating in dynamic, open-world settings where new objects are frequently encountered.
5. **Modular and Adaptable Design:** The plug-and-play architecture, separating high-level decision-making, segmentation, and grasp generation, promotes system flexibility. This design facilitates integration with various robotic platforms, grippers, and future advancements in vision-language models or grasp generation techniques, enhancing scalability and ease of deployment.
6. **Improved Efficiency and Cost-Effectiveness:** Achieving tasks with fewer steps, as demonstrated by the Motion Number metric, translates to more efficient and potentially faster robotic operations. The reported low API request cost also suggests a cost-effective solution for incorporating advanced VLM reasoning into robotic systems.
7. **Foundation for Future Research:** The identified limitations, such as issues with single-view point cloud reconstruction leading to interpenetration or collisions, inability to distinguish identical objects, and current restriction to grasping tasks, provide clear directions for future research. Addressing these could further enhance 3D perception, enable multi-task manipulation, and improve interaction with ambiguous or repetitive scenes.
8. **Potential for Broader Applications:** By overcoming some of the most challenging aspects of robotic grasping, ThinkGrasp could impact various industries, including logistics and supply chain management (bin picking), manufacturing (assembly), and service robotics (household tasks, elder care), where robots need to interact intelligently with real-world clutter.

In summary, ThinkGrasp demonstrates a robust and generalized approach to robotic grasping in cluttered environments by strategically integrating advanced vision-language reasoning, contributing to the development of more autonomous and capable robotic systems.
