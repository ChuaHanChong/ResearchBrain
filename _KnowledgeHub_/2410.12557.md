# One Step Diffusion via Shortcut Models

> [!summary] Summary
> This work from UC Berkeley introduces "shortcut models," a family of generative models that enable high-quality sample generation in significantly fewer steps, including a single forward pass, without increasing training complexity. The approach achieves this by conditioning the model on the desired step size and using a self-consistency training objective, demonstrating strong performance on image generation (e.g., ImageNet-256 with 1-step FID of 10.6) and robotic control tasks.

## Problem

- Generative diffusion and flow-matching models require many (dozens to hundreds) sequential forward passes for high-quality sample generation, making inference slow and computationally intensive.
- Existing methods to accelerate sampling (e.g., distillation) often involve complex multi-stage training processes, generate large synthetic datasets, or use intricate teacher-student architectures.
- Flow-matching models typically fail to produce high-quality samples in very few steps, particularly one-step generation, due to the averaging nature of their learned velocity field when large jumps are taken.

## Method

- A novel family of denoising generative models, **shortcut models**, which build upon ==flow-matching== but condition the neural network on a ==desired step size== to predict a shortcut direction.
- A joint training objective combines a standard ==flow-matching loss== for small step sizes (grounding the model) with a ==self-consistency loss== for larger step sizes.
- The ==self-consistency loss== leverages the property that a single large shortcut step should be equivalent to two consecutive smaller shortcut steps, allowing the model to ==bootstrap== its large-step capabilities.

## Results

- Shortcut models generate high-quality images in **1, 4, or 128 steps**, achieving significantly lower FID scores (e.g., FID-50k) on CelebA-HQ-256 and Imagenet-256 compared to baseline diffusion/flow-matching models and competitive with prior distillation methods.
- The models maintain consistent sample distributions across varying inference budgets, showing graceful degradation rather than mode collapse at low step counts, and their **one-step** generation quality improves with increased model size (e.g., DiT-XL).
- The methodology successfully extends beyond image generation, enabling **one-step** policy generation for robotic control tasks (Push-T, Transport) that maintain performance comparable to iterative policies while reducing inference costs by an **order of magnitude**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Naive flow-matching models struggle with large steps (e.g., one-step generation) because their learned velocity field represents an average direction, leading to blurry outputs or mode collapse.
> - By conditioning the model on the desired step size, a single network can be trained to predict 'shortcut' directions that account for future curvature in the optimal transport path, enabling accurate large steps.
> - A self-consistency objective, where a 2d-step is matched to two d-steps, allows the model to bootstrap its ability to take larger steps from its existing capabilities at smaller step sizes without requiring empirical targets for large steps.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of generative modeling has seen revolutionary advancements with the advent of **iterative denoising methods**, primarily diffusion models (e.g., DDPM, DDIM) and flow-matching models. These models have demonstrated unparalleled capabilities in generating diverse and highly realistic data across various modalities, including images (e.g., Stable Diffusion, Midjourney), video, audio, and even protein structures. Their success stems from their ability to learn a smooth transformation from a simple noise distribution to a complex data distribution, typically by reversing a noising process or learning a continuous-time ordinary differential equation (ODE) that connects noise to data.

Despite their remarkable generative quality, a significant limitation of these models lies in their **expensive inference procedure**. Sampling from a trained diffusion or flow-matching model typically requires dozens to hundreds of sequential neural network forward passes to iteratively denoise a sample from pure noise to a coherent data point. This iterative nature makes generation slow and computationally intensive, hindering their deployment in real-time applications or scenarios with strict latency requirements.

The broader research landscape has actively sought solutions to this "slow sampling" problem:
- **Distillation Techniques:** A prominent line of work involves "distilling" the knowledge from a large, slow teacher diffusion model into a smaller, faster student model capable of fewer-step generation. Examples include Knowledge Distillation, Rectified Flows, Progressive Distillation, and Consistency Distillation. However, these methods often introduce significant complexity: they typically require multiple training phases (pre-training the teacher, then a separate distillation stage), the generation of large synthetic datasets, or intricate teacher-student network architectures that can be fragile to schedule or difficult to optimize.
- **End-to-End Few-Step Models:** Another direction aims to train models that can generate in few steps *from scratch* in a single training run. **Consistency Models** are a notable example, learning to map any partially noised data point directly to the final data point. While a step towards end-to-end training, consistency models often depend on elaborate bootstrapping procedures and careful training schedules (e.g., time discretization schedules, specific loss functions like perceptual loss) to achieve stability and good performance.

"Shortcut models" position themselves directly in this context by aiming to address the limitations of both distillation and prior end-to-end methods. The paper explicitly states its goal: to retain the benefits of diffusion training (high quality, mode coverage) while enabling single-step or few-step denoising using a *single network* and a *single, simplified training phase*. By conditioning the model on the *desired step size*, shortcut models offer a fundamentally different approach to learning the multi-step generation process, integrating the "shortcut" capability directly into the core training objective, rather than as an post-hoc distillation. This makes the work a significant advancement towards more efficient and practical high-quality generative models.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives of the "Shortcut Models" research are directly motivated by the inherent trade-off in existing generative diffusion and flow-matching models: high sample quality versus slow, expensive inference.

**Key Objectives:**
1. **Enable High-Quality Generation in Fewer Steps:** The foremost goal is to significantly reduce the number of neural network forward passes required for sampling, ideally down to a single step, while maintaining or even improving the high quality and diversity characteristic of iterative diffusion models.
2. **Simplify Training Complexity:** To achieve fast generation without the overhead of prior methods, the authors aim to develop a generative modeling objective that allows for end-to-end training in a single phase, using a single network. This means avoiding multi-stage distillation processes, complex training schedules, or the need for multiple teacher-student networks.
3. **Maintain Flexibility in Inference Budget:** The proposed model should ideally be capable of generating high-quality samples across a wide range of inference budgets (e.g., 1, 4, 128 steps) from a single trained checkpoint, providing flexibility at inference time based on computational constraints or desired quality.
4. **Demonstrate Generalizability:** To prove the robustness and broad applicability of the approach, the model should perform well not only on standard image generation benchmarks but also in other domains, such as robotic control.

**Motivation:**
- **The "Slow Inference" Bottleneck:** Diffusion and flow-matching models, despite their generative prowess, are computationally intensive. This limits their real-world applicability in scenarios requiring rapid content creation, interactive systems, or efficient deployment on resource-constrained devices. The motivation is to unlock these applications.
- **Complexity of Existing Solutions:** Previous attempts to speed up sampling (e.g., distillation, consistency models) often introduce considerable training complexity. They might require generating large synthetic datasets, intricate multi-stage training pipelines, or fine-tuned scheduling that can be fragile and time-consuming to optimize. The authors posit that there should be a simpler, more robust objective that achieves the same goal.
- **The "Few-Step Ambiguity" Problem:** Flow-matching models learn an ODE that points towards the average of plausible data points. When large steps are taken, this averaging can lead to mode collapse or blurry outputs, especially at the critical initial steps from pure noise. The motivation is to overcome this inherent limitation by explicitly teaching the model how to "jump" effectively.
- **The "Trifecta" of Generative Models:** Ultimately, the underlying motivation is to move closer to the "ideal generative modelling objective" – one that offers fast sampling, excellent mode coverage (diversity), and high-quality generations, all within a simple and stable training framework.

By tackling these challenges, "Shortcut Models" aim to make powerful generative models more accessible, efficient, and broadly applicable, accelerating their transition from research curiosities to practical tools across various domains.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Shortcut models are a novel family of denoising generative models that build upon the foundation of **flow-matching models** but introduce a key innovation to enable few-step and one-step generation.

**Foundation: Flow-Matching Models**
The paper adopts the optimal transport flow-matching objective (Liu et al., 2022). In essence, flow-matching models learn a neural network to estimate the expected *velocity* `v_t` that transforms a noise point `x_0` into a data point `x_1` over a continuous time `t` (from 0 to 1). This is done by regressing the network's output `v_theta(x_t, t)` to the empirical velocity `(x_1 - x_0)` given `x_t = (1-t)x_0 + t x_1`. During inference, an initial noise `x_0` is iteratively updated by following the learned velocity field `v_theta(x_t, t)` through an ODE solver (e.g., Euler sampling).

**The Challenge: Few-Step Ambiguity in Flow-Matching**
A core insight highlighted by the authors is that naive flow-matching models fail when large sampling steps are taken, particularly in the one-step setting. This is because the learned velocity `v_t` at a given `x_t` represents the *average* direction from `x_t` towards all plausible data points `x_1`. When taking a large step, this average direction can lead the generation off-track, collapsing to the dataset mean or producing blurry, unrealistic samples (as depicted in Figure 2). At `t=0` (pure noise), the model's prediction would point towards the dataset mean, making single-step generation of multi-modal data impossible.

**The Innovation: Shortcut Models**
To overcome this, "shortcut models" introduce a crucial conditioning parameter: the **desired step size `d`**. Instead of just predicting the instantaneous velocity, the neural network `s_theta(x_t, t, d)` is trained to predict a "shortcut" direction that, when scaled by `d`, directly leads to the correct next point `x'_t+d`. This allows the model to "jump ahead" and account for the future curvature of the optimal transport path, enabling accurate large steps. As `d` approaches 0, the shortcut `s(x_t, t, d)` becomes equivalent to the instantaneous flow `v_t`.

**Training Objective: Self-Consistency**
The core of the shortcut model's methodology lies in its novel training objective, which intelligently combines two components:

1. **Flow-Matching Grounding (for `d=0`):** For very small step sizes (approximated as `d=0`), the model is trained with the standard flow-matching loss (Equation 2). This term grounds the shortcut model's behavior at small step sizes, ensuring it develops a base generation capability similar to a standard flow-matching model when queried with many steps. This relies on empirical `(x_0, x_1)` pairings.
2. **Self-Consistency (for `d>0`):** For larger step sizes, generating a target `x'_t+d` by fully simulating the ODE is computationally expensive for end-to-end training. Instead, shortcut models leverage an **inherent self-consistency property**: a single shortcut step of size `2d` should be equivalent to two consecutive shortcut steps of half the size (`d`).
 - The model predicts the first shortcut `s_theta(x_t, t, d)`.
 - A provisional `x_t+d` is computed by taking this predicted step: `x_t+d = x_t + s_theta(x_t, t, d)d`.
 - From this `x_t+d`, the model then predicts a *second* shortcut `s_theta(x_t+d, t+d, d)`.
 - The target for the `2d` shortcut is then `(s_theta(x_t, t, d) + s_theta(x_t+d, t+d, d)) / 2`.
 - This self-consistency loss (Equation 5) allows the model to bootstrap its ability to take larger steps from its existing capabilities at smaller step sizes. Crucially, the second step is queried at the *predicted* `x_t+d` (from the learned ODE), not an empirical data point, avoiding the accumulation of bias seen in some consistency models.

**Training Details and Best Practices:**
- **Joint Optimization:** Both the flow-matching and self-consistency objectives are optimized jointly in a single training run.
- **Batch Composition:** The authors found it beneficial to construct batches with a higher proportion of empirical flow-matching targets (e.g., 75%) than self-consistency targets (25%). This is also computationally efficient as self-consistency targets require two additional forward passes. This leads to a training cost of only ~16% more than a base diffusion model.
- **EMA Weights:** Exponential Moving Average (EMA) of model parameters is used, particularly for generating self-consistency targets, to improve stability and sample quality by smoothing the inherently noisy bootstrap process.
- **Weight Decay:** Crucial for stability, especially early in training, to prevent the model from latching onto noisy, incoherent self-generated targets.
- **Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG):** Applied only when evaluating the shortcut model at `d=0` (many-step generation) but forgone at larger step sizes, where linear approximation of CFG is less appropriate. This is a limitation as the CFG scale must be set before training.
- **Discrete Time Sampling:** During training, `t` is sampled at discrete points (multiples of `d`) relevant to the inference budget, reducing the burden on the network.

This methodology effectively allows the single network to learn how to "teleport" or "skip" through the denoising process, adapting its jump size dynamically based on the inference budget specified at test time.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of shortcut models demonstrates their effectiveness across multiple benchmarks and conditions, confirming their ability to provide high-quality samples with significantly reduced inference steps while simplifying the training process.

1. **Superior Few-Step and One-Step Generation:**
 - **Qualitative Results (Figure 1, Appendix A):** Visual examples clearly show that shortcut models generate high-quality images across 128, 4, and even 1 sampling step. In stark contrast, standard flow-matching and diffusion models rapidly degrade in quality and suffer from artifacts (e.g., blurriness, mode collapse) when forced into few-step or one-step generation.
 - **Quantitative Results (Table 1 - FID-50k):** On CelebA-HQ-256 and Imagenet-256 datasets, shortcut models consistently achieve lower (better) FID scores in the 4-step and 1-step settings compared to:
 - Baseline Diffusion and Flow-Matching models, which show drastically worse FID.
 - Prior end-to-end methods like Consistency Training and Live Reflow.
 - Shortcut models are competitive with or even outperform most two-stage distillation methods (e.g., Reflow, Consistency Distillation) *without* their associated training complexity. Progressive Distillation achieves slightly better 1-step FID but loses the ability for many-step sampling.
 - **Many-Step Performance:** Remarkably, shortcut models do not sacrifice quality at high step counts. Their 128-step generation quality matches or slightly surpasses that of baseline flow-matching models, suggesting that the self-consistency loss might act as an implicit regularizer.

2. **Consistent Behavior Across Varying Inference Budgets (Figure 4):**
 - Shortcut models maintain a consistent sample distribution even as the number of denoising steps decreases. Unlike naive flow-matching models, which exhibit pronounced degradation and mode collapse at low step counts, shortcut models show much more graceful degradation, primarily affecting high-precision details rather than overall structure or mode coverage. This implies that one-step shortcut models can serve as proxies, allowing users to quickly preview generations and then refine them with more steps if needed from the same initial noise.

3. **Scalability with Model Size (Figure 5, Table 2):**
 - The quality of one-step generation from shortcut models continues to improve as the model parameter count increases (e.g., from DiT-B to DiT-XL architectures). This is a crucial finding, as some bootstrap-based methods (e.g., in reinforcement learning) have shown issues with "rank-collapse" or diminishing returns when scaled up. Shortcut models avoid this pitfall, demonstrating that their inherent bootstrapping mechanism is stable and benefits from increased model capacity. On Imagenet-256, a DiT-XL shortcut model achieves a 1-step FID of 10.6 and a 128-step FID of 3.8, competitive with state-of-the-art generative models.

4. **Interpolable Latent Space (Figure 6):**
 - Despite no explicit regularization, the deterministic mapping from noise to data learned by shortcut models results in a qualitatively smooth and semantically plausible latent space. Interpolations between two initial Gaussian noise samples produce coherent intermediate images, indicating a well-structured and meaningful latent representation.

5. **Generality Beyond Image Generation (Figure 7):**
 - The methodology extends successfully to non-image domains. When applied to robotic control tasks (Push-T and Transport), shortcut policies maintain strong performance (comparable to iterative diffusion policies) while reducing inference cost by an order of magnitude (from 100 steps to 1 step). In contrast, a naive 1-step diffusion policy catastrophically fails. This highlights the broad applicability of the shortcut model formulation for learning multi-modal policies.

In summary, the main findings confirm that shortcut models represent a significant advancement, offering a robust, efficient, and flexible approach to generative modeling that overcomes the long-standing challenge of slow inference in diffusion and flow-matching models, all within a simplified training paradigm.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of shortcut models carries substantial significance and potential impact across both academic research and practical applications of generative AI.

**1. Simplification and Democratization of Fast Generative Models:**
- **Simplified Training Pipeline:** The most immediate impact is the drastic simplification of training fast generative models. By enabling end-to-end training in a single phase, with a single network, and *without* fragile schedules or multi-stage distillation, shortcut models offer a "simple recipe" that is easier to implement, optimize, and replicate. This lowers the barrier to entry for researchers and practitioners who wish to leverage accelerated generative models.
- **Reduced Computational Overhead in Training:** Despite its advanced capabilities, training a shortcut model requires only approximately 16% more compute than a base diffusion model. This efficiency, combined with the simplified process, makes high-quality, fast generation more accessible, particularly for groups with limited computational resources compared to large industrial labs.

**2. Unleashing Real-time and Resource-Constrained Applications:**
- **Real-time Content Generation:** The ability to generate high-quality images or other data in a single or very few steps fundamentally transforms potential applications. This opens doors for real-time interactive design tools, rapid prototyping, live animation, and instant avatar generation, where latency is a critical constraint.
- **Edge Device Deployment:** With significantly reduced inference computational demands, shortcut models could enable the deployment of powerful generative AI on edge devices (e.g., smartphones, embedded systems for robotics) where multi-step iterative processes are prohibitive due to power or memory constraints.
- **Efficient Robotic Control:** The successful application to robotic control tasks demonstrates the direct impact on embodied AI. Enabling one-step policy generation means faster decision-making for robots, which is crucial for dynamic environments and real-time interaction, potentially leading to more agile and responsive autonomous systems.

**3. Advancing the Core Generative Modeling Objective:**
- **Bridging Quality and Speed:** Shortcut models make significant progress towards the "ideal generative modelling objective" – a model that simultaneously achieves fast sampling, excellent mode coverage (diversity), and high-quality generations. By showing that many-step performance is not sacrificed and can even slightly improve, they suggest a synergistic relationship between multi-step and one-step learning.
- **Robustness and Scalability:** The demonstration of continued performance improvement with increased model parameter count (DiT-XL) is critical. It suggests that the bootstrapping mechanism employed in shortcut models is robust and avoids the rank-collapse issues observed in some other bootstrap-based learning paradigms, ensuring the method's long-term viability with larger models and datasets.

**4. New Research Directions:**
- The work opens up several avenues for future research, such as exploring how one-step generation training could further improve many-step generation quality, or developing extensions that iteratively adjust the noise-to-data mapping to further close the remaining quality gap between one-step and many-step generation. Investigation into the "implicit regularization" effect of the self-consistency loss is also a promising direction.

In conclusion, "Shortcut Models" represent a significant leap forward in making high-quality generative models truly practical and efficient. By offering a robust, simple, and scalable solution to the slow sampling problem, this research will likely accelerate the adoption of generative AI across a broader spectrum of industries and applications, from creative design to autonomous systems.
