# IMPACT: An Implicit Active-Set Augmented Lagrangian for Fast Contact-Implicit Trajectory Optimization

> [!summary] Summary
> The IMPACT framework from TU Darmstadt proposes an augmented Lagrangian method for contact-implicit trajectory optimization, implicitly identifying contact modes during planning for robots. This approach demonstrated up to 70 times faster optimization compared to baselines and yielded improved control quality metrics, such as lower control variance and effort, in multi-contact dexterous manipulation tasks.

## Problem

- Planning optimal motion for robots in contact-rich environments is challenging due to hybrid and nonsmooth dynamics arising from contact events.
- Contact-Implicit Trajectory Optimization (CITO) problems, often formulated as Mathematical Programs with Complementarity Constraints (MPCCs), are difficult to solve due to their nonsmoothness, non-convexity, and combinatorial nature of contact mode changes.
- MPCCs can cause numerical instability and ill-conditioning in standard Nonlinear Programming (NLP) solvers, leading to unreliable convergence and sensitivity to initial conditions.

## Method

- The **IMPACT** framework utilizes a safeguarded Augmented Lagrangian (AuLa) scheme specifically designed for MPCCs.
- It reformulates the MPCC into a vertical form, treating complementarity constraints explicitly as hard constraints within each inner AuLa subproblem, thereby avoiding direct penalization.
- An inner Block Coordinate Descent (BCD) solver efficiently solves these subproblems by alternating between Gauss-Newton updates for trajectory variables and closed-form minimization for complementarity variables.

## Results

- IMPACT achieved a **100%** success rate on long-horizon CITO benchmarks and demonstrated substantial runtime speedups (**2.9**	imes to 70	imes, geometric mean **13.8**	imes) over baseline solvers.
- Improved control quality in multi-contact dexterous manipulation, with lower control variance (**1.55** ± **0.13**), higher smoothness, and reduced control effort compared to smoothing-based methods.
- Successfully deployed on a real Panda robot for object pushing tasks (**100%** success in 10 trials) and scaled to high-dimensional multi-contact Contact-Implicit Model Predictive Control (CI-MPC) problems.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly enforcing complementarity constraints within an augmented Lagrangian framework, instead of smoothing or relaxing them, provides a principled and robust method for solving MPCC-based CITO with stationarity guarantees.
> - The use of an "implicit active-set" mechanism, achieved through exact minimization over a union of cones for complementarity variables, allows the solver to dynamically discover contact modes.
> - Decomposing the problem into two blocks—trajectory variables and auxiliary complementarity variables—enables efficient optimization with a tailored Block Coordinate Descent inner solver.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Optimal motion planning for contact-rich robotic tasks, such as manipulation and legged locomotion, presents a challenge due to its hybrid and nonsmooth nature. Contact-Implicit Trajectory Optimization (CITO) is a framework that addresses these tasks without requiring a predefined contact-mode schedule. However, CITO problems are typically formulated as Mathematical Programs with Complementarity Constraints (MPCCs), which are known to be numerically ill-conditioned. This degeneracy often violates regularity assumptions required by standard smooth Nonlinear Programming (NLP) constraint qualifications, leading to issues like unbounded Lagrange multipliers and unreliable convergence for off-the-shelf NLP solvers.

Existing approaches to CITO often fall into two categories:
1. **Smooth/Relaxed Contact Models**: These methods modify the rigid complementarity structure through smoothing or relaxation (e.g., compliant contact models, regularized friction, differentiable optimization). While these treatments can improve numerical behavior, they may degrade control quality, affect gradient accuracy, or introduce heuristic update parameters. As these modifications are tightened to approximate the original complementarity, ill-conditioning can re-emerge.
2. **Direct Nonsmooth Treatments**: These approaches handle contact complementarity more directly. Examples include:
 - **Penalty/Continuation Methods**: Introduce slack variables and penalize complementarity violations, often improving reliability through continuation strategies. However, they can become numerically stiff under large penalties and may have slow convergence with heuristic schedules.
 - **Operator Splitting/ADMM**: Exploit the structure by alternating trajectory updates with projections onto contact-feasible sets. These methods often operate on linearized contact/dynamics, produce task-space commands, and may require additional tracking layers for execution.

IMPACT is positioned as an augmented-Lagrangian (AuLa) approach that directly addresses MPCC-based CITO by retaining the original nonsmooth contact constraints. It distinguishes itself from smoothing/relaxation methods by avoiding modifications to the complementarity structure, and from ADMM-based methods by its AuLa formulation for general MPCCs, designed for temporally coupled trajectory optimization and model predictive control settings, without introducing copied variables in its slack updates. The method aims to provide a principled and efficient solver that offers stationarity guarantees, distinguishing it from approaches that may compromise solution quality for numerical robustness.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this work arises from the inherent difficulties associated with Contact-Implicit Trajectory Optimization (CITO) problems. These problems, critical for planning and control in contact-rich robotic tasks, are typically formulated as Mathematical Programs with Complementarity Constraints (MPCCs). Such formulations are fundamentally nonsmooth and numerically ill-conditioned.

Existing methods for CITO face several limitations:
- **Numerical Brittleness**: Standard NLP solvers are often unreliable for MPCCs because complementarity conditions lead to degeneracy, violating regularity assumptions required for their convergence theory. This results in sensitivity to initialization and unreliability at large scales.
- **Compromised Control Quality**: Approaches that smooth or relax the complementarity constraints to improve numerical tractability can degrade the fidelity of the rigid-contact model, potentially leading to suboptimal control performance or inaccurate gradient computations.
- **Heuristic Parameters**: Many treatments introduce additional heuristic parameters, which can be challenging to tune and significantly affect efficiency and reliability. As these modifications are tightened to better approximate the true complementarity, the original numerical issues tend to re-emerge.
- **Lack of Scalable and Principled Solvers**: The absence of systematic and scalable solution strategies that can directly and efficiently handle nonsmooth contact constraints restricts the broader applicability of CITO.

Given these challenges, the key objectives of this research were:
1. **Develop an Augmented-Lagrangian Method for MPCCs**: To propose IMPACT, an augmented-Lagrangian approach specifically designed for solving MPCC-based CITO problems, ensuring stationarity guarantees for feasible accumulation points.
2. **Implicit Contact-Mode Discovery**: To enable the method to identify implicit contact-mode branches dynamically during trajectory optimization iterations, without requiring a predefined contact schedule or smoothing of the contact model. This is achieved by explicitly enforcing the original nonsmooth contact constraints within augmented Lagrangian subproblems.
3. **Efficient Implementation**: To provide an efficient C++ implementation tailored for trajectory optimization and contact-implicit model predictive control (CI-MPC) workloads.
4. **Comprehensive Validation**: To benchmark IMPACT against representative baselines on established open-source CITO and CI-MPC suites, evaluating its performance in terms of speed, robustness, and control quality.
5. **Hardware Demonstration**: To demonstrate the practical applicability of the proposed method on real robotic hardware for a dexterous manipulation task.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, IMPACT (IMPlicit contact ACtive-set Trajectory optimization), addresses CITO by combining a safeguarded augmented-Lagrangian (AuLa) outer loop with a structured block coordinate descent (BCD) inner solver.

**Problem Definition:**
The core problem for both CITO and CI-MPC is formulated as a general Mathematical Program with Complementarity Constraints (MPCC) in generalized coordinates (Problem 1). This formulation includes a sum-of-squares objective, equality constraints (h), inequality constraints (g), and complementarity conditions (0 ≤ G(x) ⊥ H(x) ≥ 0). The complementarity condition implies that for each pair, G(x) ≥ 0, H(x) ≥ 0, and their element-wise product G(x) ◦ H(x) = 0. This structure results in a nonconvex and nonsmooth optimization problem. The formulation is flexible and can accommodate various friction models, such as polyhedral approximations leading to Linear Complementarity Problems (LCPs) or second-order cone representations.

**Vertical Reformulation:**
To facilitate the augmented-Lagrangian treatment, the original MPCC is reformulated into a "vertical form" (Problem 3). This involves introducing auxiliary (slack) variables, Y and Z, such that G(x) = y and H(x) = z. The complementarity constraint is then applied directly to these slack variables: 0 ≤ y ⊥ z ≥ 0. This transformation shifts nonlinearities from the complementarity set into smooth equality constraints while simplifying the complementarity set itself, making it amenable to the chosen inner solver design.

**Safeguarded Augmented Lagrangian (AuLa) Outer Loop (Algorithm 1):**
IMPACT employs a safeguarded AuLa scheme for MPCCs where the complementarity constraints (0 ≤ y ⊥ z ≥ 0) are maintained as hard constraints within the inner subproblems, rather than being absorbed into the augmented Lagrangian function. This design aims to preserve the MPCC structure and mitigate the pathological multiplier growth often seen when complementarity conditions are directly penalized.
The AuLa subproblem (Problem 4) consists of the original objective, quadratic penalty terms for the smooth equality and inequality constraints (including G(x) - y = 0 and H(x) - z = 0 from the vertical reformulation), and linear terms involving approximate Lagrange multipliers (κ and μ) for these constraints. The inner subproblem is a nonlinear least-squares problem subject to the simple complementarity constraints.
Algorithm 1 outlines the outer loop:
- Lagrange multipliers (κ, μ) are updated using a standard rule and clipped to fixed safeguard bounds to ensure their boundedness.
- Penalty parameters (ρ_h, ρ_g) are updated adaptively: they are increased if the combined constraint and KKT residual violations do not decrease sufficiently in an iteration; otherwise, they remain constant.
- The outer loop terminates when variables change minimally and constraint violations are below specified tolerances.
The method provides a stationarity guarantee (Theorem 3): if the inner subproblems are solved to an accuracy that vanishes (ε_k ↓ 0), any feasible accumulation point of the outer iterates is an M-stationary point of the original MPCC.

**Block Coordinate Descent (BCD) Inner Solver (Algorithm 2):**
The AuLa inner subproblem (4) is solved using a two-block coordinate descent (BCD) scheme, alternating between updates for the trajectory variables X and the auxiliary complementarity variables (Y, Z).
- **Block 1: X-update (Gauss–Newton with Globalization)**: With (Y, Z) fixed, the objective becomes a smooth nonlinear least-squares problem in X. This is solved using a damped Gauss-Newton method incorporating an Armijo backtracking line search to ensure sufficient objective decrease.
- **Block 2: (Y, Z)-update (Closed-Form Minimization over a Union of Cones)**: With X fixed, the (Y, Z)-subproblem decouples across individual time steps and complementarity pairs. For each scalar complementarity pair (y_t,i, z_t,i), the problem involves minimizing a quadratic function over the set {(y, 0) | y ≥ 0} ∪ {(0, z) | z ≥ 0}. This subproblem has a closed-form solution: two candidates are computed (one setting z_t,i = 0 and the other setting y_t,i = 0), and the one yielding the smaller objective value is selected. This step effectively determines the active contact branch for each complementarity pair.
Theorem 5 states that this BCD inner solver can achieve ε-stationarity (for a given tolerance ε) in a finite number of iterations, validating its role in the safeguarded AuLa framework. The practical implementation stops the inner iterations when the objective function stagnates, which is empirically shown to correlate with the required stationarity measure. This BCD approach differs from ADMM projections as it directly minimizes the AuLa subproblem with respect to slack variables, allowing the current multipliers and penalty weights to influence the selection of the active mode.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluated IMPACT on two open-source benchmarks: a long-horizon CITO suite and a multi-contact CI-MPC benchmark, along with a real-robot demonstration. The implementation used C++ with Eigen for sparse linear solves and CasADi for symbolic differentiation.

**Long-Horizon CITO Benchmark:**
- **Tasks:** Three contact-rich, hybrid-dynamics problems from the CRISP benchmark: Push Box, Cart Transport, and Push T. These tasks involved long planning horizons and general equality/inequality constraints.
- **Baselines:** Scholtes Relaxation (SR), the Penalty Method (PM) (both implemented with IPOPT), and CRISP (a specialized C++ implementation). All methods used an all-zero initialization.
- **Metrics:** Success rate, total tracking error, iteration count, and wall-clock runtime. A run was deemed successful if constraint violations (including complementarity) were below 10⁻⁵.
- **Results:**
 - **Efficiency:** IMPACT achieved substantial runtime advantages, reporting speedups of 2.9×–70× over baselines, with a geometric mean of 13.8×. Specifically, it was 16.8×, 25.0×, and 34.0× faster than CRISP on Push Box, Push T, and Cart Transport, respectively.
 - **Robustness and Quality:** IMPACT achieved a 100% success rate across all tasks. Its task-level tracking performance (total tracking error) was competitive, ranking second-best on Push Box and Cart Transport, and similar to CRISP on Push T. PM, despite sometimes having fewer iterations, showed larger tracking errors and lower success rates (90-98%), indicating numerical stiffness. SR, while occasionally yielding the lowest tracking error, was comparatively slower.

**Real-Robot Push-T Demonstration:**
- **Task:** A qualitative demonstration of a Panda robot pushing a T-shaped block to a target pose. A full-horizon trajectory was solved offline, and the robot executed it, with online replanning if the object significantly deviated.
- **Results:** IMPACT achieved a 100% success rate across 10 trials with different initial object configurations, demonstrating the method's applicability to hardware.

**Multi-contact High-Dimensional CI-MPC Benchmark:**
- **Task:** Allegro Hand meshable-object reorientation for 17 diverse objects, involving multi-contact dynamics in generalized coordinates. This benchmark assessed IMPACT's scalability for real-time control without smoothing or relaxation of contact constraints.
- **Baseline:** `cfree`, a smoothing-based CI-MPC pipeline. IMPACT used a single-shooting formulation with an MPC horizon of 4.
- **Metrics:** Success rate, control variance, control smoothness, and control effort.
- **Results:**
 - **Success Rate:** IMPACT achieved an average success rate of 91.8% ± 4.1, comparable to `cfree` (e.g., 91.2% ± 4.2 for `cfree`(0.1)).
 - **Control Quality:** IMPACT consistently demonstrated improved control quality across the 17 objects. It yielded lower control variance (1.55 ± 0.13 vs. 2.16 ± 0.21 for `cfree`(0.1)), greater control smoothness (0.029 ± 0.004 vs. 0.044 ± 0.008), and reduced control effort (3.0 ± 1.0 vs. 4.8 ± 2.0).
 - **Control Rate:** IMPACT operated at a control rate of 9.53 ± 0.36 Hz. This is lower than `cfree` (50-60 Hz) but represents real-time performance for a direct, nonsmooth MPCC solution.
 - **Outlier Behavior:** Performance on the "stick" object was comparatively worse for IMPACT, which the authors attributed to the sensitivity of slender geometries to the LCP-based contact modeling.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research presents IMPACT as a method for Contact-Implicit Trajectory Optimization (CITO) and Contact-Implicit Model Predictive Control (CI-MPC) that addresses challenges in contact-rich robotic tasks.

The method's significance is primarily derived from its approach to handling Mathematical Programs with Complementarity Constraints (MPCCs) directly, rather than relying on smoothing or relaxation. By employing a safeguarded augmented-Lagrangian (AuLa) framework that explicitly enforces nonsmooth contact constraints, IMPACT offers a principled solution strategy with stationarity guarantees. This contributes to the mathematical rigor of CITO methods.

The empirical evaluation highlights several areas of potential impact:
- **Computational Efficiency in CITO**: The reported substantial speedups (2.9×–70× over baselines, with a geometric mean of 13.8×) on long-horizon CITO benchmarks indicate that IMPACT can significantly reduce computation time. This efficiency could enable the planning of more complex and extended robotic tasks that were previously computationally intractable.
- **Enhanced Control Quality in CI-MPC**: For high-dimensional, multi-contact CI-MPC tasks, IMPACT demonstrates improved control quality metrics, including lower control variance, greater smoothness, and reduced control effort, at comparable success rates to smoothing-based baselines. This suggests potential for more precise, stable, and dexterous manipulation in real-time applications.
- **Autonomous Contact-Mode Discovery**: The implicit contact-mode selection mechanism removes the need for predefined contact schedules or heuristic continuation, simplifying the planning process for contact-rich tasks and potentially increasing the autonomy of robotic systems.
- **Broadened Applicability**: By providing a robust and efficient solver for MPCCs in contact-implicit settings, IMPACT has the potential to expand the range of robotic applications that can leverage CITO, from intricate manipulation to dynamic legged locomotion, where direct reasoning about contact is crucial.
- **Practical Validation**: The successful demonstration on real robotic hardware for a pushing task underscores the method's practical utility and readiness for experimental deployment beyond simulated environments.

While the paper acknowledges limitations, such as transient contact-mode instability under limited compute and sensitivity to local minima or contact-encouraging costs, the proposed IMPACT method contributes to the development of robust, principled, and efficient strategies for contact-implicit optimization in robotics.
