# IMPACT: An Implicit Active-Set Augmented Lagrangian for Fast Contact-Implicit Trajectory Optimization

> [!summary] Summary
> The IMPACT framework from TU Darmstadt proposes an augmented Lagrangian method for contact-implicit trajectory optimization, implicitly identifying contact modes during planning for robots. This approach demonstrated up to 70 times faster optimization compared to baselines and yielded improved control quality metrics, such as lower control variance and effort, in multi-contact dexterous manipulation tasks.

## Problem

- Planning optimal motion for robots in contact-rich environments is challenging due to hybrid and nonsmooth dynamics arising from contact events.
- Contact-Implicit Trajectory Optimization (CITO) problems, often formulated as Mathematical Programs with Complementarity Constraints (MPCCs), are difficult to solve due to their nonsmoothness, non-convexity, and combinatorial nature of contact mode changes.
- MPCCs can cause numerical instability and ill-conditioning in standard Nonlinear Programming (NLP) solvers, leading to unreliable convergence and sensitivity to initial conditions.

## Method

- The **IMPACT** framework utilizes a safeguarded Augmented Lagrangian (AuLa) scheme specifically designed for MPCCs.
- It reformulates the MPCC into a vertical form, treating complementarity constraints explicitly as hard constraints within each inner AuLa subproblem, thereby avoiding direct penalization.
- An inner Block Coordinate Descent (BCD) solver efficiently solves these subproblems by alternating between Gauss-Newton updates for trajectory variables and closed-form minimization for complementarity variables.

## Results

- IMPACT achieved a **100%** success rate on long-horizon CITO benchmarks and demonstrated substantial runtime speedups (**2.9**	imes to 70	imes, geometric mean **13.8**	imes) over baseline solvers.
- Improved control quality in multi-contact dexterous manipulation, with lower control variance (**1.55** 	extpm **0.13**), higher smoothness, and reduced control effort compared to smoothing-based methods.
- Successfully deployed on a real Panda robot for object pushing tasks (**100%** success in 10 trials) and scaled to high-dimensional multi-contact Contact-Implicit Model Predictive Control (CI-MPC) problems.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly enforcing complementarity constraints within an augmented Lagrangian framework, instead of smoothing or relaxing them, provides a principled and robust method for solving MPCC-based CITO with stationarity guarantees.
> - The use of an "implicit active-set" mechanism, achieved through exact minimization over a union of cones for complementarity variables, allows the solver to dynamically discover contact modes.
> - Decomposing the problem into two blocks—trajectory variables and auxiliary complementarity variables—enables efficient optimization with a tailored Block Coordinate Descent inner solver.
