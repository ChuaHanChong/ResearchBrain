# Flow-GRPO: Training Flow Matching Models via Online RL

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from MMLab, Tsinghua University, Kuaishou Technology, and Shanghai AI Lab developed Flow-GRPO, a framework that integrates online policy gradient reinforcement learning into flow matching models. This method significantly enhances capabilities in compositional image generation, visual text rendering, and human preference alignment, achieving up to 95% GenEval accuracy on SD3.5-M by addressing challenges of determinism and sampling efficiency through an ODE-to-SDE conversion and denoising reduction.

## Problem

- Flow matching generative models struggle with composing complex scenes involving multiple objects and attributes, as well as accurately rendering text within images.
- Applying online reinforcement learning (RL) to these models is challenging due to their deterministic ODE-based generation process, which conflicts with RL's need for stochastic exploration.
- The high computational cost of generating numerous high-quality samples for online RL training makes the process prohibitively slow for large flow models.

## Method

- The iterative denoising process of flow matching models is formulated as a ==Markov Decision Process (MDP)==, and ==Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)== is applied for its memory efficiency.
- The deterministic Flow-ODE is converted into an equivalent ==Stochastic Differential Equation (SDE)== to enable stochastic sampling for RL exploration, preserving the original model's marginal probability density function.
- A =='Denoising Reduction' strategy== uses significantly fewer denoising steps during training sample collection (e.g., 10 steps) to accelerate online RL, while retaining full steps (e.g., 40 steps) for high-quality inference.

## Results

- Flow-GRPO-tuned Stable Diffusion 3.5 Medium (SD3.5-M) improved GenEval accuracy from **63%** to **95%** for compositional generation, surpassing GPT-4o's **84%** performance.
- Visual text rendering accuracy for SD3.5-M increased from **59%** to **92%**, and the framework demonstrated strong generalization to unseen compositional elements and different flow-matching backbones.
- The Denoising Reduction strategy achieved over a **4x** speedup in training sample collection, and KL regularization effectively mitigated reward hacking, preserving image quality and diversity during optimization.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Online policy gradient RL, when properly integrated, can effectively enhance compositional reasoning and text rendering abilities of flow matching models.
> - Injecting stochasticity into deterministic flow models via an ODE-to-SDE conversion provides the necessary exploration for online RL without sacrificing the original model's probabilistic properties.
> - Informative reward signals for online RL can be derived from lower-quality, few-step generated samples, allowing for drastic improvements in training efficiency for large generative models.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This paper addresses a critical, yet relatively unexplored, intersection of flow matching generative models and online reinforcement learning (RL) in the context of text-to-image (T2I) generation.

**Generative Models:** The paper acknowledges that "Flow matching models have become dominant in image generation" due to their strong theoretical foundations and efficiency, often producing high-quality images with fewer steps compared to earlier diffusion models. While diffusion models have seen successful integration with RL, particularly through methods like DPO for *offline* RL alignment, the potential of *online* RL to enhance flow matching generative models remained largely untapped. This work explicitly targets this gap, building upon recent theoretical advancements that unify diffusion and flow models under an SDE/ODE framework, which provides a theoretical basis for the proposed ODE-to-SDE conversion.

**Reinforcement Learning (RL) and Alignment:** The authors highlight the transformative impact of online RL, particularly policy gradient methods like PPO and GRPO, on large language models (LLMs) for improving their reasoning capabilities. Flow-GRPO aims to bring similar reasoning and compositional benefits to image generation. In the broader landscape of T2I alignment, existing efforts fall into categories such as direct fine-tuning with differentiable rewards, Reward Weighted Regression (RWR), Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) and its variants, PPO-style policy gradients, and training-free alignment methods. Flow-GRPO situates itself within the PPO-style policy gradient methods but distinctively applies GRPO to *flow matching models*, making it a pioneering effort in this specific combination. The choice of GRPO over PPO is motivated by its memory efficiency due to the absence of a value network.

The paper also distinguishes itself from concurrent work: applies GRPO to text-to-speech flow models but introduces stochasticity by predicting both mean and variance of velocity, which requires retraining the base model, unlike Flow-GRPO's conversion approach. Another study explores SDE-based stochasticity primarily for inference-time scaling, not for enabling online RL training. This contextualization firmly establishes Flow-GRPO as a novel contribution addressing a specific and important gap in generative AI research.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to leverage online reinforcement learning to enhance the capabilities of flow matching generative models, particularly in complex T2I tasks, while ensuring efficiency and preventing unwanted side effects.

The primary motivations stem from two observations:
1. **Limitations of Flow Matching Models:** Despite their high image quality, flow matching models often "struggle with composing complex scenes involving multiple objects, attributes, and relationships, as well as text rendering." These limitations indicate a need for improved reasoning and fine-grained control over generation.
2. **Success of Online RL in LLMs:** Online reinforcement learning has proven highly effective in "enhancing the reasoning capabilities of large language models (LLMs)," suggesting a promising avenue for improving generative image models.

However, applying online RL to flow matching models presents several critical challenges:
1. **Deterministic Nature vs. RL Exploration:** Flow matching models typically rely on a deterministic generative process based on Ordinary Differential Equations (ODEs). This conflicts with online RL's fundamental requirement for stochastic sampling to explore the environment and learn from varied trajectories.
2. **Sampling Efficiency for Online RL:** Online RL depends on efficient data collection. Flow models, especially large ones, require many iterative steps to generate each high-quality sample, making the data generation process for online RL prohibitively expensive and slow.

Flow-GRPO aims to overcome these challenges to effectively integrate online RL into flow matching models, thereby improving their performance on tasks requiring compositional understanding, accurate text rendering, and alignment with human preferences, all while maintaining high image quality and diversity.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Flow-GRPO introduces a novel framework that integrates Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) into flow matching models, addressing the inherent challenges of determinism and sampling efficiency.

#### 3.1. GRPO on Flow Matching (Denoising as an MDP)

The core idea is to formulate the iterative denoising process of flow matching models as a Markov Decision Process (MDP), as previously explored for diffusion models. In this MDP:
- **State ($s_t$):** Defined by the prompt, current timestep, and the partially denoised image ($c, t, x_t$).
- **Action ($a_t$):** The denoised sample predicted by the model at the next step ($x_{t-1}$).
- **Policy ($\pi(a_t | s_t)$):** The probability of generating $x_{t-1}$ given $x_t$ and prompt $c$.
- **Reward ($R(s_t, a_t)$):** Given only at the final step ($t=0$) based on the quality of the generated image ($r(x_0, c)$), otherwise zero.

Flow-GRPO then adapts the GRPO algorithm to this MDP. GRPO is chosen for its memory efficiency, as it avoids the need for a separate value network. It calculates the advantage ($\hat{A}_i^t$) for each image in a group by normalizing its reward against the mean and standard deviation of rewards within the group. The policy model is optimized using a regularized objective function ($J_{\text{Flow-GRPO}}$) that maximizes expected cumulative reward while incorporating a Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence constraint to keep the updated policy close to a reference policy ($\pi_{\text{ref}}$), preventing drastic deviations and mitigating reward hacking.

#### 3.2. Key Strategies

Flow-GRPO proposes two key strategies to enable and accelerate online RL training for flow models.

#### 3.3. From ODE to SDE (Enabling Stochasticity)

- **Problem:** Flow matching models traditionally rely on deterministic Ordinary Differential Equations (ODEs) for sampling. This determinism conflicts with the need for stochastic exploration in online RL and makes computing the policy's probability ($p_{\theta}(x_{t-1} | x_t, c)$) computationally expensive.
- **Solution:** The authors convert the deterministic Flow-ODE into an equivalent Stochastic Differential Equation (SDE) that *preserves the original model’s marginal probability density function at all timesteps*. This is achieved by deriving a reverse-time SDE formulation (Equation 8), based on theoretical foundations from score-based generative models.
- **Implementation:** Euler-Maruyama discretization (Equation 9) is applied to the SDE, introducing Gaussian noise ($\epsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I)$) at each step, thereby injecting the necessary stochasticity for RL exploration. A scalar hyper-parameter `a` controls the noise level ($\sigma_t = a \frac{t}{1-t}$). This conversion also allows for a closed-form calculation of the KL divergence needed for the GRPO objective.

#### 3.4. Denoising Reduction (Improving Sampling Efficiency)

- **Problem:** Generating high-quality images with flow models typically requires many iterative denoising steps, making data collection for online RL very costly in terms of computational time.
- **Solution:** Flow-GRPO employs a Denoising Reduction strategy where significantly *fewer denoising steps* are used during the *training sample collection* phase (e.g., T=10 steps for training samples), while the *original, full number of steps* (e.g., T=40 steps) is maintained for *inference* to ensure high-quality final outputs.
- **Rationale:** The empirical finding is that even low-quality, few-step samples, while not aesthetically perfect, are sufficient to provide informative reward signals for online RL optimization. This drastically reduces the wall-clock time and computational resources required for generating training data, making online RL practical for large flow models.

By combining these two strategies with the GRPO framework, Flow-GRPO effectively addresses the fundamental challenges of applying online RL to flow matching models, paving the way for improved T2I generation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

Flow-GRPO demonstrates significant empirical effectiveness across various text-to-image tasks, showcasing substantial improvements in targeted capabilities while largely preserving image quality and diversity.

#### 4.1. Performance Gains Across Tasks

- **Compositional Image Generation (GenEval):**
 - Flow-GRPO-tuned Stable Diffusion 3.5 Medium (SD3.5-M) achieves a remarkable increase in GenEval accuracy from 63% to 95%. This benchmark evaluates complex compositional understanding, including object counts, spatial relations, and fine-grained attribute binding.
 - Notably, the enhanced SD3.5-M outperforms the state-of-the-art GPT-4o, which achieved 84% on GenEval, highlighting Flow-GRPO's effectiveness in reasoning-intensive image generation.
 - Qualitative examples (Figure 3) visually confirm superior performance in accurately representing counting, colors, attribute binding, and object positioning.
- **Visual Text Rendering:**
 - Accuracy in rendering specified text within images improves from 59% to 92% for SD3.5-M, significantly boosting the model's ability to generate coherent and accurate text.
- **Human Preference Alignment:**
 - Flow-GRPO also yields substantial gains in aligning models with human preferences, using metrics like PickScore.
- **Generalization:**
 - The model demonstrates strong generalization capabilities, extending improvements to unseen object classes and counts (e.g., generalizing from 2-4 objects during training to 5-6 or 12 objects during evaluation).
 - Flow-GRPO also achieves significant gains on T2I-CompBench++ (Table 3), a benchmark with object classes and relationships substantially different from the GenEval-style training data, further proving its robustness and transferability.
 - The framework is shown to be task-independent and applicable to different flow-matching backbones, as evidenced by positive results on FLUX.1-Dev (Table 7).

#### 4.2. Reward Hacking Mitigation

A crucial finding is that Flow-GRPO achieves these improvements with "very little reward hacking." This means that the increase in task-specific rewards did not come at the cost of reduced overall image quality or diversity.
- **Role of KL Regularization:** The Kullback-Leibler (KL) constraint in the GRPO objective is identified as critical for preventing reward hacking.
 - Without KL regularization, Flow-GRPO showed significant degradation in image quality (Aesthetic Score, DeQA, ImageReward, UnifiedReward) for compositional generation and text rendering tasks.
 - For human preference alignment, omitting KL led to a "collapse in visual diversity," where outputs converged to a single style despite different seeds.
 - Properly tuned KL regularization effectively preserves image quality and diversity while still achieving high task-specific rewards, albeit sometimes requiring slightly longer training (Table 2, Figure 6, Figure 12). The authors emphasize that KL regularization is not empirically equivalent to early stopping.

#### 4.3. Efficiency and Robustness from Ablation Studies

- **Denoising Reduction:** This strategy significantly accelerates training. Reducing denoising steps from 40 to 10 during training sample collection achieved over a 4x speedup across all tasks without impacting the final reward. This confirms that lower-quality, few-step samples are sufficient for effective RL optimization (Figure 7a, Figure 9).
- **Noise Level ($\mathbf{a}$):** A moderate noise level (e.g., $a=0.7$) is optimal for exploration and reward improvement. Too little noise limits exploration, while excessive noise (e.g., $a=1.0$) can degrade image quality and lead to training failure (Figure 7b).
- **Group Size (G):** Larger group sizes (e.g., G=24) lead to more stable training and better performance, as they provide more accurate advantage estimates, reducing variance and preventing training collapse observed with smaller groups (Figure 5).
- **Initial Noise:** Employing different initial random noise for each rollout significantly enhances exploratory diversity and contributes to consistent high rewards during training (Figure 10).

#### 4.4. Comparison with Other Alignment Methods

Flow-GRPO consistently outperforms other alignment methods tested, including supervised fine-tuning (SFT), reward-weighted regression (Flow-RWR), Direct Preference Optimization (Flow-DPO), and their online variants (Figure 4, Tables 5 and 6). It also shows more stable and higher performance than adapted versions of DDPO and ReFL for flow models, particularly avoiding the collapse seen in DDPO during later training stages. This underscores the superior efficacy and stability of Flow-GRPO.

In summary, Flow-GRPO represents a significant advancement, demonstrating that online policy gradient RL can be effectively and efficiently applied to flow matching models, yielding substantial improvements in complex generation tasks while robustly maintaining overall output quality.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

Flow-GRPO marks a pivotal advancement in the field of generative AI, particularly at the intersection of flow matching models and reinforcement learning. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

**1. Pioneering Integration of Online RL for Flow Matching:** Flow-GRPO is the *first method* to successfully integrate online policy gradient RL (specifically GRPO) into flow matching models. This fills a crucial gap in the literature, as prior work had largely focused on offline RL for flow models or online RL for diffusion models. By proving the effectiveness of online RL in this context, Flow-GRPO opens up new avenues for enhancing state-of-the-art flow-based generative systems.

**2. Overcoming Fundamental Technical Hurdles:** The paper brilliantly addresses the core challenges that previously hindered the application of online RL to flow models:
 - **Determinism:** The novel ODE-to-SDE conversion method enables stochastic sampling for exploration while preserving the marginal distributions of the original flow model. This is a critical theoretical and practical contribution.
 - **Efficiency:** The Denoising Reduction strategy dramatically improves sampling efficiency during training, making online RL practical for large, computationally intensive generative models. This innovation highlights that "low-quality but still informative trajectories" are sufficient for effective RL signal extraction, a powerful insight for training efficiency.

**3. Breakthrough Performance on Complex Tasks:** Flow-GRPO achieves remarkable performance gains in areas where traditional flow models struggled:
 - **Compositional Generation:** Surpassing models like GPT-4o on benchmarks like GenEval demonstrates an enhanced ability to understand and render complex, multi-object, attribute-rich scenes. This is vital for creating more accurate and controllable generative AI.
 - **Visual Text Rendering:** Significant improvements in text accuracy within images greatly expand the practical utility of T2I models for applications involving text integration (e.g., posters, memes, infographics).
 - **Human Preference Alignment:** The ability to effectively align with human preferences through model-based rewards like PickScore means generated content can be better tailored to user expectations and aesthetic criteria.

**4. Robustness and Generalizability:** The framework's ability to mitigate reward hacking through KL regularization ensures that performance gains in specific tasks do not compromise overall image quality or diversity – a common pitfall in RL-tuned generative models. Its demonstrated generalization to unseen compositional elements and effectiveness across different flow model backbones (SD3.5-M, FLUX.1-Dev) highlights its robustness and broad applicability.

**5. Enabling a "Simple and General Framework":** Flow-GRPO offers a straightforward and adaptable framework for applying online RL to any flow-based generative model. This simplicity lowers the barrier to entry for researchers and developers seeking to improve their models through online feedback.

**6. Future Impact and Directions:** The paper lays strong groundwork for several exciting future research directions, particularly in the realm of video generation:
 - **Advanced Reward Design:** The need for more sophisticated reward models (beyond simple heuristics or existing VLMs) for multi-objective tasks like video generation (e.g., physical realism, temporal consistency, smoothness, coherence) is critical.
 - **Balancing Multiple Rewards:** Video generation inherently involves balancing competing objectives, posing a complex challenge that Flow-GRPO's framework could potentially address.
 - **Scalability for Video:** Adapting Flow-GRPO to the even more resource-intensive domain of video generation will require further innovations in efficient data collection and training pipelines.
 - **Further Reward Hacking Prevention:** While KL regularization is effective, continued exploration of methods to prevent reward hacking more robustly and with less training overhead remains an open area.

In essence, Flow-GRPO represents a significant leap forward in making online reinforcement learning a practical and powerful tool for enhancing the capabilities, controllability, and alignment of cutting-edge flow matching generative models, with clear implications for the future of text-to-image and potentially text-to-video generation.
