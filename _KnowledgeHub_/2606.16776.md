# JoyAI-Sim: A Simulation-Enabled Interconversion Toolchain for the Embodied Data Pyramid

> [!summary] Summary
> JoyAI-Sim is a simulation-centered toolchain that places a simulator between robot and human data as a Robot-Simulation-Human loop: calibrated Isaac Sim digital twins of real tidy-up tasks give scalable policy screening, while egocentric human videos are lifted, feasibility-checked, and re-rendered as robot-centered data. Simulated and real success rates correlate at r=0.89, and synthetic data plus human filtering lifts real-robot success from 60% to 95%.

## Problem

- Real-robot trials and demonstrations give the most faithful deployment signal but are slow, costly, hard to reproduce, and need safety monitoring, so neither evaluation nor data collection scales.
- Existing simulation benchmarks such as RLBench, LIBERO, and RoboCasa use hand-designed scenes only loosely tied to real deployment instances, so measured success rates need not track physical performance.
- Abundant egocentric human videos are not robot-executable: hands and grippers differ in kinematics, contact geometry, and force profiles, and VLM critics judge semantics rather than embodied physical plausibility.

## Method

- **JoyAI-Sim**'s ==Robot-to-Simulation-to-Human pathway== rebuilds real tidy-up tasks as ==Isaac Sim== digital twins via scene, embodiment, and action alignment (shared URDF/USD, unified DDS control schema, 30 Hz Parquet record-replay), then sweeps predefined generalization axes.
- Four ==SimGen== synthesizers (teleoperation, FSM, IKFlow, RL) produce candidate trajectories; ==Simulation-to-Human consistency calibration== projects them into human-hand space for embodied inspection and naturalness filtering, yielding paired robot-human episodes.
- ==Human-to-Simulation-to-Robot pipeline== uses ==HaMeR== hand-pose priors to instantiate editable sim scenes, constraint-checks robot rollouts, then renders them with ==domain randomization==, ==Cosmos Transfer== reality augmentation, and a hand-inpainting render-and-overlay route.

## Results

- Digital twins reproduced object placements in about **90%** of **100** replayed real-robot trajectories, and paired policy success rates correlate at Pearson **r=0.89**, Spearman **rho=0.78**.
- Simulation on **8** GPUs with **64** concurrent environments ran **128** evaluation episodes per hour versus **40** on hardware (**3.2x**), with success-rate standard deviation **3.16%** versus **5.07%**.
- On tabletop tidy-up, real-only training scored **60%/60%** sim/real; adding SimGen gave **80%/90%**, human filtering **85%/95%**, while RL-mined data reached only **85%/90%**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Rank correlation matters more than absolute fidelity: a twin good enough to order checkpoints can screen policies cheaply before spending scarce real-robot trials.
> - Human embodied feedback catches global strategy flaws (awkward approaches, missing pre-adjustment, abrupt phase transitions) that per-frame metrics and VLM critics miss, worth 5 points here.
> - Automated trajectory mining is not free quality: RL-generated data matched human-filtered data in simulation yet transferred no better than unfiltered synthetic data to hardware.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this work fits into the broader research landscape

The development of generalist robot policies capable of operating in complex manipulation settings relies on two critical resources: trustworthy evaluation methods and scalable, robot-centered training data. Traditional approaches using physical robots, while providing the most faithful deployment signals, encounter significant scaling challenges due to slow execution, high costs, difficulty in reproduction, and safety requirements. This bottleneck necessitates alternative strategies for scaling data collection and evaluation.

Simulation environments offer scalability, control, and parallel execution, but their effectiveness is limited by a "scaling gap" – the discrepancy between simulated performance and real-world deployment faithfulness. Prior research has explored "real-to-sim-to-real" paradigms, which primarily establish a two-level loop between physical robots and simulation to reconstruct real-world scenes, generate data, and evaluate policies.

JoyAI-Sim extends this existing landscape by introducing a more comprehensive "Robot ⇌ Simulation ⇌ Human" framework. This framework explicitly integrates human data as an additional, abundant source of embodied observations, demonstrations, and feedback. It positions simulation not merely as an evaluation environment but also as a central hub for physical consistency filtering and data amplification within an "embodied data pyramid." In this hierarchy, robot data is considered deployment-faithful but scarce, simulation data provides scalable and inspectable intermediate representations, and human data is abundant but often mismatched in terms of embodiment.

The paper contextualizes JoyAI-Sim against various related research directions:
- **Robotic Manipulation Benchmarks:** While real-robot benchmarks (e.g., RT-1, RT-2, Open X-Embodiment) offer direct deployability evidence, they are expensive. Simulation benchmarks (e.g., RLBench, LIBERO) provide scalability but often lack direct ties to real-world tasks. JoyAI-Sim aims for a hybrid approach where real robots define task semantics, and calibrated digital twins provide scalable, aligned simulation evaluation.
- **Digital-Twin Construction (Robot → Simulation):** Prior work (e.g., RialTo, Robo-GS) focuses on reconstructing physical workspaces into editable digital twins. JoyAI-Sim builds on this by aligning household digital twins specifically for real-robot evaluation and synthesizing data inspectable via human feedback.
- **Evaluating Simulated Motions (Simulation → Human):** Some efforts use vision-language models (VLMs) to assess simulated robot actions (e.g., CRISP). JoyAI-Sim distinguishes itself by proposing human embodied inspection to evaluate motion naturalness and physical feasibility, leveraging human physical intuition which VLMs may not fully capture.
- **Human → Simulation → Robot Methods:** Research like EgoHumanoid, X-Sim, and DexMan aims to bridge human demonstrations to robot execution via simulation. JoyAI-Sim organizes this entire process into a cohesive toolchain, encompassing human video parsing, simulation instantiation, feasibility checking, and diversified rendering.
- **Synthetic Data Generation:** Existing methods include teleoperation, finite state machines (FSMs), inverse kinematics (IKFlow), and reinforcement learning (RL) for generating diverse robot trajectories. JoyAI-Sim integrates these complementary approaches within its digital-twin environments.

By integrating these components into a unified, simulation-centered toolchain, JoyAI-Sim seeks to provide a more robust and scalable solution for advancing generalist robot policies.

### 2. Key objectives and motivation

The primary objective of this work is to address the fundamental scaling challenges in developing generalist robot policies, specifically in the areas of trustworthy evaluation and the generation of scalable, robot-centered training data. The authors highlight two core bottlenecks that motivate the development of JoyAI-Sim:

1. **Evaluation Bottleneck:** Real-robot trials are the most reliable way to assess policy performance in deployment, as they expose the full range of physical complexities, including sensing noise, control latency, and contact uncertainty. However, these trials are inherently slow, costly, difficult to reproduce consistently (due to variations in reset conditions, illumination, and object states), and require constant safety monitoring. While simulation offers scalable execution, controllable resets, and parallel rollouts, it cannot be treated as a direct substitute for physical deployment if it lacks fidelity and alignment with real-world conditions.
 - **Motivation:** The need for a scalable evaluation pipeline that initiates from real-world tasks and success criteria, reconstructs them as calibrated digital twins, and leverages simulation as an efficient screening layer before final, costly physical validation. The goal is to obtain evaluations that are both scalable and faithful to deployment.

2. **Data Bottleneck:** Generalist robot policies require vast amounts of diverse training data. Real-robot data is scarce and expensive to collect. Human egocentric videos, conversely, are abundant and cover a wide array of everyday manipulation behaviors. However, these human demonstrations are not directly executable by robots because human hands and robot end-effectors possess distinct kinematic, contact, and control constraints. Directly mapping human motions to robots often results in physically infeasible or unnatural robot actions.
 - **Motivation:** To establish a scalable mechanism for transforming abundant human demonstrations into physically feasible and robot-centered training data. Simulation is positioned as the crucial intermediate layer that can reconstruct human motions and task scenes, check them against robot physical constraints, and convert them into usable robot-centric trajectories and observations. This role extends beyond mere evaluation, enabling simulation to act as a physical consistency filter and data amplifier.

In response to these motivations, JoyAI-Sim sets out to achieve the following specific objectives:

- **Develop a Human-Robot Aligned Model Evaluation Pathway (Robot → Simulation → Human):** This pathway aims to reconstruct real-robot tabletop organization tasks into calibrated digital twins for scalable evaluation. It further incorporates human embodied feedback to inspect and refine the naturalness and physical plausibility of simulated robot motions, ensuring the quality of generated trajectories.
- **Develop a Human-Robot Aligned Data Generation Pathway (Human → Simulation → Robot):** This pathway is designed to lift egocentric human demonstrations into a simulation environment, where they are checked against robot physical constraints. Subsequently, these validated demonstrations are converted into robot-centered trajectories, annotations, and visual observations, making them suitable for robot policy training.
- **Establish Simulation as a Central Alignment Layer:** The toolchain explicitly uses the simulator as both a scalable evaluation layer and a physical consistency filter for robot data generation, effectively bridging the fidelity of robot data with the abundance of human data.
- **Provide a Reusable and Scalable Infrastructure:** The core modules for reconstruction, simulation, rendering, and realism augmentation are packaged as cloud services on JD Cloud. This infrastructural aspect aims to turn JoyAI-Sim into a scalable, accessible, and reusable system for continuous robot data production and model evaluation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

JoyAI-Sim implements a simulation-enabled interconversion toolchain structured around a bidirectional `Robot ⇌ Simulation ⇌ Human` paradigm, underpinned by a cloud-native infrastructure and integration with a unified environment framework.

**A. Robot → Simulation → Human Pathway: Human-Robot Aligned Model Evaluation**

This pathway focuses on transforming real-robot tasks into scalable simulation evaluations and leveraging human feedback for trajectory inspection.

1. **Robot → Simulation (Digital Twin Construction and Evaluation):**
 - **Real-Robot Evaluation:** A benchmark of long-horizon household tidy-up tasks (e.g., Study Room, Living Room) is established using a bimanual humanoid robot platform. This platform logs proprioceptive states, end-effector poses, visual observations from multiple cameras (head-mounted RGB-D, wrist cameras), and task metadata at 30 Hz. The benchmark defines task semantics, object-to-target mappings, success criteria, and serves as the physical reference. It also evaluates policies across various generalization axes (e.g., robot state, object layout, illumination, instruction wording) in controlled real-world settings.
 - **Scene Alignment:**
 - **Sim-Ready Data Preparation:** An extensive asset library of 300+ fine-grained categories, comprising 53,661 asset instances, is created by collecting, normalizing, and reconstructing 3D models into NVIDIA Isaac Sim 5.1 format.
 - **Sim-Ready Scene Preparation:** Virtual scenes (study room, living room) are constructed to meticulously align with the physical workspaces in appearance, geometry, object placement, and task-relevant spatial layouts. High-fidelity scene and object assets are recovered using 3D Gaussian Splatting and image-to-3D generation.
 - **Real-Simulation Scene Alignment:** Paired real-robot and simulation scenes are created to ensure consistency in task-relevant containers, object categories, spatial arrangements, and robot states, maintaining language goals and success criteria across modalities.
 - **Interaction Alignment:**
 - **Embodiment Alignment:** The simulated robot platform uses SDK-consistent URDF and USD models that mirror the physical robot's kinematics, initial state (encoder-based ready pose with settling), sensing geometry (calibrated camera intrinsics and hand–eye extrinsics), and actuator dynamics (calibrated stiffness/damping parameters).
 - **Action Alignment:** A unified Data Distribution Service (DDS) message schema enables seamless control across real and simulated environments. Policy rollouts and teleoperation inputs are recorded as 30 Hz Parquet streams, allowing identical command sequences to be replayed in simulation. Standardized lifecycle commands (prepare, finalize, reset) ensure repeatable episode boundaries.
 - **Simulation Evaluation:** The aligned digital twins serve as a controlled environment for scalable policy evaluation. They enable testing policy sensitivity to specific non-semantic factors (e.g., robot initialization, object placement, illumination) by varying one factor at a time while preserving the task goal. Evaluation is object-centric, checking final stable poses against target regions, and records a comprehensive "failure profile" (e.g., grasp outcome, placement error, collision events) beyond simple success/failure.

2. **Simulation → Human (Trajectory Inspection and Data Alignment):**
 - **Synthetic Data Generation:** JoyAI-Sim employs four complementary methods to generate robot trajectories in simulation:
 - **Teleoperation:** Human operators control the simulated robot using 6-DoF input devices.
 - **FSM-based Method:** Rule-driven expert policies decompose tasks into states (e.g., select, grasp, release) to automatically synthesize trajectories.
 - **IKFlow-based Method:** Augments a small number of seed demonstrations by generating diverse joint-space solutions for end-effector poses through latent-space sampling.
 - **RL-based Method:** Trains manipulation policies in simulation to autonomously generate diverse successful and failed trajectories under randomized conditions.
 - **Consistency Calibration (Human Embodied Feedback):** To improve the naturalness of simulated trajectories, robot end-effector paths are projected into human-hand space. Human operators inspect these mapped trajectories from an embodied perspective, identifying unnatural or physically implausible motion patterns (e.g., overly direct approaches, lack of anticipatory adjustments, abrupt phase transitions). This human feedback forms criteria for quality filtering, removing low-quality trajectories.
 - **Robot-Human Data Alignment:** This process yields paired robot-human data. Each simulated episode can be exported as both a robot-centered trajectory (preserving robot embodiment and observations) and a corresponding human-form trajectory (by projecting robot motion into human-hand space). This aligned data supports representation alignment in policy training.

**B. Human → Simulation → Robot Pathway: Human-Robot Aligned Data Generation**

This pathway focuses on converting abundant human demonstrations into physically feasible and visually realistic robot-centered training data.

1. **Human → Simulation (Extracting and Instantiating Human Demos):**
 - **Human Motion and Task Information:** Egocentric human manipulation videos are processed using methods like HaMeR to extract task-relevant hand pose cues (wrist motion, approach direction, grasp/release phase, interaction regions) rather than full hand articulation.
 - **Task Context Instantiation:** The manipulated object, target container, camera configuration, and robot embodiment are specified or selected in the simulator, aligning with the human demonstration. Uncertain quantities from the video are treated as editable parameters.
 - **Constraint-Aware Robot Rollout:** The human motion priors are instantiated and adapted to the target robot in the simulator. The simulator checks kinematic, safety, and task-level constraints (e.g., joint limits, collisions, grasp stability). Rollouts violating these constraints are adjusted, manually reviewed, or excluded.
 - **Front-end for Render-and-Overlay (Alternative):** For an alternative "render-and-overlay" route, the system inpaints human hands from egocentric videos (diffusion-based models) and uses inverse kinematics (IK) to solve for robot arm DOFs matching demonstrated wrist motion.
 - **Outputs:** Robot-centered simulated episodes containing robot states, poses, commands, camera observations, object poses, and task annotations.

2. **Simulation → Robot (Diversity and Realism):**
 - **Domain-Randomized Simulation Rendering:** Accepted simulated episodes are expanded by randomizing task-relevant (object poses, container locations) and visual factors (object colors, textures, distractor objects) within the simulator. This generates a diverse set of domain-randomized videos with synchronized, physically grounded labels.
 - **Reality Augmentation with Cosmos Transfer:** To bridge the visual gap between simulation and real robot cameras, Cosmos Transfer is used as a reality-augmentation module. It translates simulated clips into more realistic robot-view videos by adapting visual appearance while preserving task content and structural signals (depth, edges).
 - **Appearance-Level Visual Generalization:** The transfer module also generates plausible appearance variants (e.g., tabletop material, illumination, global light tone) from fixed rollouts, complementing physical randomizations.
 - **Render-and-Overlay Alternative:** The IK-solved robot arm is rendered onto the hand-free inpainted real video. A video editing model (e.g., Cosmos Transfer) corrects occlusions and shadows, harmonizing the overlaid robot with the original real background, preserving real-world appearance while providing robot-centered actions.

**C. Scalable Simulation Infrastructure**

JoyAI-Sim is deployed as a cloud-native execution layer on JD Cloud, leveraging services like JoyBuilder 2.0.
- **Cloud Support Services:** This includes a simulation service (parallel physics, distributed rendering), elastic scheduling (GPU/CPU resources, job orchestration), high-performance storage (parallel asset access), and an embodied data lake (metadata indexing, versioning for all generated data).
- **Isaac Lab Arena Integration:** JoyAI-Sim integrates with NVIDIA Isaac Lab Arena, adopting its Scene–Embodiment–Task abstractions for environment construction. This allows validated digital-twin scenes, robot configurations, and task definitions to be registered as reusable components, supporting expansion into diverse variants (e.g., object instances, layouts, appearances, robot embodiments). This one-to-N design facilitates scalable rollout generation and reproducible policy evaluation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of JoyAI-Sim focused on two complementary aspects: model evaluation through digital twins and the impact of simulation-generated and human-filtered data on policy performance.

#### 4.1. Digital-Twin Evaluation: Consistency, Efficiency, and Stability

- **Real-Simulation Consistency:**
 - **Environment-level Outcome Reproduction:** The system demonstrated high fidelity in replicating real-world task outcomes. When 100 real-robot trajectories from a desk-tidying task were replayed in the corresponding digital twin, approximately 90% of the simulation replays reproduced object-placement outcomes consistent with their real-robot counterparts.
 - **Policy-level Evaluation Agreement:** A strong positive linear association was observed between the success rates of various policy checkpoints evaluated in both the digital twin and on the real robot. Pearson’s correlation coefficient was `r = 0.89`, and Spearman’s rank correlation coefficient was `ρ = 0.78`. This indicates that policies performing better in simulation generally exhibited improved performance on the physical robot, affirming the digital twin's utility as a pre-screening tool.
- **Evaluation Efficiency and Stability:**
 - **Evaluation Throughput:** The simulation platform, utilizing 8 GPUs with 8 parallel instances per GPU, achieved a throughput of 128 evaluation episodes per hour. This represents a 3.2x increase compared to the physical-robot evaluation setup, which completed 40 episodes per hour.
 - **Repeated-Run Stability:** Simulation demonstrated greater stability in evaluation results. The standard deviation of success-rate estimates across multiple independent runs was 3.16% in simulation, which is lower than the 5.07% observed on the physical robot. This indicates more repeatable and consistent evaluation outcomes in the simulated environment.

#### 4.2. Policy Gains from Simulation-Generated Data and Human Filtering

Experiments evaluated the impact of different training data configurations on the performance of the JoyAI-RA robot manipulation policy on tabletop organization tasks.

- **Baseline (Real-only):** Training solely with real-robot demonstrations resulted in a 60% success rate in both matched simulation and real-robot environments.
- **Real + SimGen:** Augmenting the real-robot data with simulation-generated trajectories (derived from teleoperation, FSMs, and IKFlow) significantly improved performance. The success rate increased to 80% in simulation (a 20 percentage point gain) and 90% on the real robot (a 30 percentage point gain). This indicates that simulation-generated data is a primary contributor to policy improvement.
- **Real + SimGen + HF (Human Filtering):** Further refinement of the simulation-generated trajectories through human embodied feedback, where unnatural motion patterns were identified and filtered, led to additional performance gains. Success rates rose to 85% in simulation and 95% on the real robot, representing a 5 percentage point improvement over SimGen alone in both settings. This finding highlights the value of human intuition in assessing and improving the naturalness and strategic quality of synthetic manipulation data.
- **Real + SimGen + RL:** Incorporating additional trajectories mined through reinforcement learning yielded an 85% success rate in simulation and 90% on the real robot. While matching the simulation performance of human filtering, this approach did not provide additional gains over SimGen alone in the real-robot evaluation within this benchmark.

In summary, the results demonstrate that simulation-generated data significantly enhances robot policy performance. The `Simulation → Human` pathway, through human filtering, consistently improved these gains, achieving the highest success rates in both simulated and real-robot evaluations. The strong real-robot performance indicates that the simulation-generated experience effectively supports physical robot deployment.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

JoyAI-Sim presents a significant contribution to the field of robotics by addressing critical challenges in scaling robot learning and evaluation. Its potential impact spans several key areas:

- **Bridging the Real-Sim Gap for Evaluation:** By creating calibrated digital twins of real-robot tasks and aligning interactions, JoyAI-Sim enhances the trustworthiness of simulation-based evaluation. This capability allows for scalable, controlled, and repeatable policy screening in simulation before costly and time-consuming physical trials, potentially accelerating the development cycle for robot policies. The demonstrated strong correlation between simulated and real-robot performance validates its utility as a reliable pre-screening tool.
- **Unlocking Abundant Human Data for Robotics:** The `Human → Simulation → Robot` pathway provides a structured method to convert the vast, but often embodiment-mismatched, resource of egocentric human videos into physically feasible and robot-centered training data. This mechanism enables robot learning systems to leverage a much larger and more diverse dataset of manipulation experiences than is typically available from physical robots alone, which is crucial for training generalist robot policies.
- **Improving Synthetic Data Quality with Human Embodied Feedback:** The `Simulation → Human` pathway introduces a novel quality control mechanism that integrates human embodied intuition. By projecting simulated robot trajectories into human-hand space for inspection, the system can filter out unnatural or strategically suboptimal motions. This approach goes beyond automated or VLM-based assessments, leading to higher-quality synthetic training data that yields superior performance on physical robots, as demonstrated by the gains from human filtering.
- **Providing a Scalable and Reusable Infrastructure:** The deployment of JoyAI-Sim's core modules as cloud services on JD Cloud and its integration with NVIDIA Isaac Lab Arena establishes a robust, scalable, and reusable infrastructure. This cloud-native architecture facilitates distributed data generation, policy evaluation, and environment expansion. It can significantly lower the barrier to entry for robotics research and development, allowing researchers and practitioners to efficiently generate data and test policies without managing complex hardware setups.
- **Advancing Generalist Robot Policies:** By offering both a robust evaluation framework and a scalable, high-quality data generation pipeline, JoyAI-Sim contributes directly to the development of more capable and generalist robot policies. The ability to diagnose policy weaknesses through controlled generalization axes and to train on diverse, physically consistent data is fundamental for creating robots that can operate reliably in complex, unstructured real-world environments.
- **Extending the Embodied AI Paradigm:** The `Robot ⇌ Simulation ⇌ Human` paradigm explicitly recognizes the hierarchical relationship within the "embodied data pyramid." This broader framework provides a conceptual foundation for future research in embodied AI, highlighting the synergistic role of different data modalities and feedback loops in training intelligent robotic systems.
