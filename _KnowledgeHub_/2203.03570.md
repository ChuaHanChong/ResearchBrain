# Kubric: A scalable dataset generator

> [!summary] Summary
> Google Research and collaborators developed Kubric, an open-source Python framework that generates large-scale, photo-realistic synthetic image and video datasets with rich ground-truth annotations. It operates at scale across thousands of machines, demonstrating its versatility by creating 13 diverse datasets for various computer vision challenges, often revealing limitations of existing models.

## Problem

- Real-world data is costly to collect and annotate, often lacks diversity, provides limited control over scene parameters, and raises privacy/fairness concerns.
- Existing synthetic data generation tools are fragmented, often "one-off" and task-specific, hindering reuse, reproducibility, and scaling.
- A lack of mature, general-purpose frameworks for synthetic data generation compared to advanced tools for model architecture design and training.

## Method

- **Kubric** is a high-level, open-source Python framework that orchestrates ==Blender (Cycles raytracing)== for photo-realistic rendering and ==PyBullet== for physics simulation.
- It offers an ==object-oriented API== for defining scenes, assets, and behaviors, abstracting away backend complexities for ease of use.
- Designed for scalability, it leverages ==distributed computing== to generate terabytes of data, exported in a standardized ==Scene Understanding Datasets (SunDs)== format for ML pipelines.

## Results

- Successfully generated **13 distinct datasets** for a wide range of computer vision tasks, including object discovery, optical flow, NeRF robustness, and pose estimation.
- A Kubric-generated dataset for optical flow pre-training yielded significantly more accurate results than the widely used FlyingChairs dataset on Sintel and KITTI-15 benchmarks.
- Demonstrated that state-of-the-art models struggle with increased scene complexity, structured outliers (for NeRF), and highly specular surfaces, highlighting specific areas for future research.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - High-fidelity raytracing through Blender's Cycles engine is critical for generating photo-realistic synthetic data that effectively reduces the sim-to-real gap.
> - A unified, open-source framework with standardized outputs (SunDs/TFDS) promotes reproducibility, reusability, and collaborative data generation at scale.
> - Fine-grained control over scene parameters enables researchers to systematically test hypotheses, isolate variables, and create targeted benchmarks for specific machine learning challenges.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of machine learning, particularly deep learning, is critically dependent on vast quantities of high-quality data. However, the paper identifies significant limitations with real-world data: it is expensive and labor-intensive to collect and annotate at scale, often lacks diversity, provides limited control over scene parameters, and raises complex privacy, fairness, and legal concerns.

Synthetic data has long been recognized as a powerful alternative, offering:
1. **Cost-effectiveness:** Cheaper to generate than acquiring and annotating real data.
2. **Rich ground-truth annotations:** Provides perfect, pixel-accurate labels (e.g., depth, optical flow, segmentation masks) that are impossible or prohibitively expensive to obtain from real scenes.
3. **Full control:** Allows precise manipulation of scene content, lighting, object properties, camera parameters, and dynamics.
4. **Bias and privacy mitigation:** Can be designed to address specific biases or privacy concerns inherent in real-world datasets.

Historically, synthetic data has been used for benchmark evaluation (e.g., for optical flow) and increasingly for training models across various tasks (e.g., 3D human pose estimation, semantic segmentation). Early efforts often involved simpler, less realistic synthetic datasets (e.g., FlyingChairs), but the trend is towards greater photorealism to narrow the "generalization gap" between synthetic and real data.

Despite the clear advantages and growing adoption, the authors pinpoint a critical gap: existing software tools for effective data generation are less mature and more fragmented compared to the sophisticated frameworks available for architecture design and model training (e.g., TensorFlow, PyTorch). This often leads to "one-off" and task-specific generation efforts, hindering reuse, reproducibility, and scaling.

Kubric enters this landscape as a comprehensive solution designed to address these shortcomings. It positions itself as a **general-purpose, open-source framework** that can generate photo-realistic image and video data with rich ground-truth annotations, specifically built for **scalability** across thousands of machines. It distinguishes itself from:
- **Specialized pipelines** (e.g., hand-crafted Blender/Unity scripts): These are often tailored for a single task and difficult to adapt.
- **Real-world to simulation pipelines** (e.g., ScanNet, Omnidata): While producing high-quality data, they rely on 3D scans of full scenes, imposing a bottleneck for scaling and composition.
- **Other generic dataset creation pipelines** (e.g., ThreeDWorld, BlenderProc): While some exist, Kubric differentiates itself by its explicit focus on **ray-tracing for high realism** (using Blender's Cycles engine) and **seamless integration with distributed computing environments** (e.g., Google Cloud Platform) and machine learning data loaders (SunDs/TFDS).

By providing a unified, scalable, and open platform, Kubric aims to elevate synthetic data generation to a level of maturity comparable to other stages of the machine learning pipeline, fostering greater collaboration, reproducibility, and innovation in data-driven research.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to introduce **Kubric**, an open-source Python framework designed to streamline the generation of large-scale, photo-realistic synthetic datasets with rich ground-truth annotations.

The core motivations driving the development of Kubric are multi-faceted:

- **Overcoming Real Data Limitations:** The authors emphasize that real-world data, while essential, presents significant challenges. These include the high cost of collection, the difficulty and expense of obtaining high-quality and detailed annotations, issues with data diversity, lack of fine-grained control over scene complexity, and growing concerns around privacy, fairness, and licensing. Kubric aims to circumvent these problems by offering an alternative data source.
- **Leveraging Synthetic Data's Strengths:** Synthetic data inherently offers powerful advantages:
 - **Cost-effectiveness:** It is significantly cheaper to generate programmatic data than to acquire and meticulously annotate real data.
 - **Rich Ground Truth:** Synthetic environments allow for perfect, precise, and comprehensive annotations (e.g., pixel-level segmentation, accurate depth, optical flow, 3D object poses, camera parameters) that are invaluable for training and evaluating machine learning models.
 - **Full Control:** Researchers gain unparalleled control over every aspect of data generation, including scene composition, object properties, lighting conditions, camera movements, and physical interactions. This enables targeted experiments and the creation of specific scenarios.
 - **Bias and Privacy Mitigation:** By constructing data programmatically, researchers can explicitly address and mitigate biases found in real-world datasets and bypass privacy concerns associated with human-derived data.
- **Addressing Tool Fragmentation and Immaturity:** A critical motivation is the observed fragmentation and relative immaturity of existing synthetic data generation tools compared to the advanced frameworks for model architecture design and training. Many generation efforts are "one-off" and task-specific, leading to duplicated work, lack of reusability, and difficulty in scaling. Kubric seeks to provide a general, coherent framework that supports reuse, replication, and shared assets at scale.
- **Enabling Scalable Data Generation:** Machine learning models increasingly demand massive datasets, often in the terabyte range. Kubric is explicitly designed to handle this scale, seamlessly distributing large jobs over thousands of machines.

In summary, Kubric's key contributions are articulated as:
- Introducing a framework for generating photo-realistic synthetic datasets for myriad vision tasks, with fine-grain control over data complexity and rich ground truth annotations.
- Enabling generation at scale, running large jobs over thousands of machines, producing terabytes of data in a standard export format.
- Demonstrating its versatility by creating 13 diverse datasets for new vision challenge problems, ranging from 3D NeRF models to optical flow estimation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Kubric is conceived as a high-level Python library designed to act as an orchestrator, integrating and simplifying the use of specialized back-end tools for rendering, physics simulation, and data export. The core methodology revolves around a set of design principles and a streamlined workflow that enables scalable, realistic, and reproducible synthetic data generation.

#### 3.1. Design Principles

The framework adheres to several key design philosophies:
- **Openness:** Kubric is open-source (Apache2 license) and utilizes software and 3D assets with similarly permissive licenses, fostering free use, reproduction, and modification by the research community.
- **Ease of Use:** It provides a simple, object-oriented Python API that abstracts away the complexities and differing interfaces of underlying graphics and physics engines (Blender and PyBullet). This minimizes friction for researchers.
- **Realism:** To maximize utility, Kubric leverages Blender's Cycles raytracing engine. This choice enables high levels of visual realism, accurately modeling complex phenomena like global illumination, reflections, refractions, subsurface scattering, motion blur, and depth of field, which are crucial for reducing the sim-to-real gap.
- **Scalability:** The framework is built to scale from local prototyping to massive cloud-based jobs involving thousands of machines, capable of generating terabytes of data.
- **Portability and Reproducibility:** A Docker image is provided to ensure easy setup and consistent results across different environments, overcoming common installation challenges and version discrepancies.
- **Rich Data Export:** Kubric automatically exports a comprehensive suite of ground-truth annotations, including RGB images, depth maps, instance segmentation, optical flow, surface normals, object coordinates, 2D/3D trajectories, bounding boxes, velocities, mass, friction, camera parameters, and collision events. It also introduces **SunDs (Scene Understanding Datasets)**, a unified API and data format for standardized ingestion into machine learning pipelines (leveraging TFDS).

#### 3.2. Kubric Worker Workflow

The typical workflow is centered around a "Kubric worker" script:
- **Scene Definition:** Each worker script defines a `kb.Scene` object, which manages global settings (resolution, frames, gravity), camera parameters, and a collection of `kb.Asset` instances (objects, lights, materials).
- **Backend Integration:** When an `Asset` is added to the scene, corresponding representations are automatically created in the connected backends: the **PyBullet physics simulator** and the **Blender renderer**. Kubric maintains synchronization, propagating property changes from the high-level API to these backends.
- **Physics Simulation:** PyBullet is used to simulate rigid-body physics, facilitating tasks like ensuring non-overlapping object placement during scene population and generating object trajectories and collision events.
- **Rendering:** Blender's `bpy` module interfaces with its Cycles raytracing engine to render photo-realistic images and videos. The choice of raytracing prioritizes visual fidelity over raw speed, deemed a worthwhile trade-off for realism.
- **Annotation Generation:** Blender's specialized render passes are exploited to generate pixel-level ground-truth annotations (segmentation, depth, flow, normals, object coordinates). Concurrently, Kubric collects object-centric metadata.
- **Data Export:** All generated images, annotations, and metadata are exported in a standardized format, compatible with the SunDs API, facilitating direct ingestion into ML frameworks.
- **Distributed Execution:** A full dataset is generated by running many independent worker scripts in parallel, allowing for seamless scaling on cloud platforms like Google Cloud.

#### 3.3. Asset Management

Kubric provides access to several preprocessed collections of high-quality 3D assets to simplify scene creation:
- **KuBasic:** A small set of simple primitive shapes for prototyping.
- **ShapeNetCore.v2:** A large dataset of 3D models from 55 categories, extensively pre-processed (e.g., fixing rendering issues, generating watertight collision meshes using ManifoldPlus and VHACD) for compatibility and quality rendering in Blender.
- **Google Scanned Objects (GSO):** A collection of high-quality textured meshes of household objects, also pre-processed with generated collision meshes.
- **Polyhaven:** A public library for HDRI (High Dynamic Range Image) backgrounds and textures for realistic lighting and materials.
Assets are managed via a `manifest.json` file, standardizing metadata and simplifying their integration.

#### 3.4. Scene Understanding Datasets (SunDs)

Kubric introduces SunDs, an API designed to standardize access to scene understanding datasets. It addresses the fragmentation of file formats and conventions by providing a consistent interface. SunDs:
- Standardizes field names, structure, shape, and data types across different datasets, enabling easy switching between synthetic and real data.
- Comprises two sub-datasets: `scenes` (high-level metadata) and `frames` (individual examples like RGB images, bounding boxes).
- Acts as a wrapper around TensorFlow Datasets (TFDS), providing scalability to terabytes, native compatibility with cloud file systems (GCS, S3), and leveraging `tf.data` pipeline capabilities (prefetching, caching).
- Introduces the concept of "tasks" to control which features are decoded and which transformations are applied to the data pipeline, optimizing for specific ML tasks (e.g., NeRF task dynamically generates rays).

This comprehensive methodology, combining robust software engineering, high-fidelity rendering, physics simulation, and scalable data management, positions Kubric as a powerful tool for advancing data-driven machine learning research.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The core "finding" of the paper is the successful development and demonstration of Kubric's capabilities. The authors showcase its versatility and effectiveness by generating **13 distinct datasets** for a wide range of computer vision tasks. These case studies serve as empirical evidence that Kubric can:
1. **Significantly reduce engineering effort** for synthetic data generation.
2. **Facilitate reuse and collaboration** through its open-source nature and standardized outputs.
3. **Enable novel research questions** and benchmarks that are difficult or impossible to address with real-world data or less sophisticated synthetic tools.

Here's a summary of the key findings and results demonstrated through the challenges:

- **Object Discovery from Video (MOVi-A to -E):** Kubric generated datasets of increasing visual and dynamic complexity. Evaluation with state-of-the-art models (SAVi, SIMONe) revealed that existing methods struggle significantly as scene complexity, object count, and camera motion increase, highlighting the challenge for temporally consistent object segmentation in realistic dynamic scenes.
- **Optical Flow:** A Kubric-generated dataset (with manually tuned parameters) for pre-training RAFT (a prominent optical flow model) showed **significantly more accurate results than the widely used FlyingChairs dataset** on Sintel and KITTI-15 benchmarks. This demonstrates the benefit of 3D rendering and increased realism provided by Kubric. It also performed competitively against AutoFlow, which used learned rendering hyperparameters, suggesting further gains are possible with Kubric.
- **Texture-structure in NeRF:** By generating scenes with procedurally textured flat surfaces, Kubric enabled a novel study into Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF). It was found that higher texture frequencies lead to **lower depth variance (better surface approximation) but higher reconstruction error**, suggesting a trade-off and a research gap for NeRFs to robustly model complex textures and surface geometry simultaneously.
- **Pose Estimation:** Synthetic images of diverse human poses (including non-aesthetic ones common in real-life motions) generated with Kubric **improved the performance (Keypoint Mean Average Precision) of the MoveNet model** both in-domain (COCO, Active) and out-of-domain (Yoga test set). This validates the utility of synthetic data for diversifying training distributions.
- **Pre-training Visual Representations:** A pilot experiment using Kubric to render ShapeNet objects for pre-training a ResNet-50 showed promising results. It **halved the performance gap between random initialization and ImageNet pre-training** on standard benchmarks (ImageNet, Pets, CIFAR-100), suggesting synthetic data can be a viable alternative for general backbone pre-training, circumventing issues with web-image datasets.
- **Robust NeRF:** Kubric created datasets ("teleport," "jitter") that explicitly violated NeRF's typical static scene assumption. The results showed that standard NeRF and even L1-robust versions **struggle significantly more with structured outliers** (jittering objects) than unstructured ones (teleporting objects). This highlights the need for NeRF models to be robust to non-static scene elements.
- **Multi-view Object Matting (SOD):** Two challenging multi-view SOD datasets ("easy" and "hard" with clutter) were generated. State-of-the-art single-view SOD models performed poorly, especially in cluttered scenes, indicating that the **lack of 3D understanding is a major bottleneck**. This implicitly demonstrates Kubric's ability to provide the multi-view information necessary for future, more robust SOD models.
- **Complex BRDFs:** Kubric was used to render ShapeNet objects with highly specular materials. Benchmarking existing novel view synthesis models (LFN, PixelNeRF) showed **substantially worse performance on specular surfaces compared to Lambertian ones**. This underscores the difficulty for current models to accurately infer complex BRDFs and light source positions from limited views.
- **Single View Reconstruction:** Kubric enabled the processing of the *entire* ShapeNetCoreV2 dataset (55 categories, ~51,300 models) for training SOFTRAS (a differentiable renderer for 3D reconstruction). The trained model showed **generalization to new object classes** but struggled with objects of highly complex shapes (e.g., headphones, microphones), indicating areas for further research in 2D-to-3D reconstruction.
- **Video Based Reconstruction:** Videos of rigid and non-rigid objects were generated to evaluate LASR (a method for articulated shape reconstruction). Results showed LASR's difficulty with **extrapolation to unseen views and reconstructing non-zero genus shapes** (e.g., torus), and that ground-truth optical flow provided only marginal improvements. This highlights limitations in handling complex deformations and topology.
- **Point Tracking:** Kubric provided long-term 2D point trajectories with occlusion information. A contrastive tracking baseline developed using this data showed **fairly good coarse tracking** but less precision and poor occlusion detection in challenging cases (appearance changes, severe occlusion). This points to the need for algorithms with better global reasoning and robustness to appearance changes.
- **Scene Semantic Segmentation:** Three datasets (KLEVR, ToyBox5, ToyBox13) with direct 2D image and 3D point cloud correspondence were created. Comparison of 2D (DeepLab, NeSF) and 3D (SparseConvNet, NeSF) segmentation models showed that **SparseConvNet, with access to ground truth 3D geometry, achieved significantly higher mean intersection-over-union in 3D**, especially as scene complexity increased.
- **Conditional Novel View Synthesis:** A massive (1 million scenes) photo-realistic dataset with grouped ShapeNet objects and varied backgrounds was generated. Existing methods (LFN, PixelNeRF, SRT) scaled vastly differently, with **significant performance degradation on complex scenes and views far from conditioning views**, especially for LFN. This demonstrates Kubric's ability to create datasets that truly challenge the generalization capabilities of neural scene representations.

Collectively, these results demonstrate Kubric's profound utility in providing controlled, richly annotated, and scalable synthetic data. It acts as a scientific instrument, enabling researchers to isolate variables, test hypotheses, and create benchmarks that push the boundaries of machine learning capabilities across diverse vision tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

Kubric represents a significant step forward in the field of machine learning, particularly computer vision, by addressing the fundamental challenge of data scarcity and quality. Its potential impact is broad and transformative:

#### 5.1. Democratization and Acceleration of Research

- **Lowering the Barrier to Entry:** By providing an open-source, user-friendly framework with a simple Python API, Kubric significantly lowers the technical barrier for researchers to generate custom, high-quality synthetic data. This empowers smaller research groups and individual researchers who may lack the resources for extensive real-world data collection and annotation.
- **Accelerating Innovation:** The ability to rapidly generate diverse, richly annotated datasets tailored to specific research questions allows for faster iteration cycles in model development and evaluation. This can accelerate progress in areas currently bottlenecked by data limitations.
- **Standardization and Reproducibility:** The open-source nature, shared assets, Docker image for portability, and the standardized SunDs export format promote reproducibility of experimental results and facilitate easier sharing and modification of data generation pipelines within the community. This combats the "fragmentation" issue identified in current synthetic data efforts.

#### 5.2. Enabling Novel Research Directions

- **Systematic Hypothesis Testing:** Kubric's fine-grained control over scene parameters allows researchers to systematically test specific hypotheses about model behavior. Examples demonstrated include studying the robustness of NeRF models to non-static elements, the impact of texture frequencies on NeRF reconstruction, or the challenges of rendering complex BRDFs.
- **Targeted Benchmarking:** The framework enables the creation of highly specific and challenging benchmarks that push the limits of existing models, as shown with the MOVi datasets for object discovery, or the multi-view SOD and conditional novel view synthesis challenges. This drives targeted improvements in algorithms.
- **Sim-to-Real Transfer:** By providing photo-realistic data with perfect ground truth, Kubric can facilitate research into sim-to-real transfer learning, where models trained in synthetic environments are deployed in the real world. This is crucial for applications like robotics and autonomous driving.

#### 5.3. Addressing Ethical and Practical Concerns of Real-World Data

- **Privacy and Legal Compliance:** Synthetic data entirely bypasses the privacy concerns and legal complexities (e.g., GDPR, licensing) associated with collecting, storing, and sharing real-world human or personally identifiable data. This makes research in sensitive domains safer and more accessible.
- **Bias Mitigation and Fairness:** While not inherently bias-free (as human designers introduce their own biases), synthetic data offers an unparalleled opportunity to explicitly identify, study, and mitigate dataset biases. Researchers can deliberately create datasets that are balanced, diverse, or test for specific failure modes, leading to more robust and fair AI systems. This is a critical advantage for ethical AI development.
- **Safety-Critical Applications:** For high-stakes applications like self-driving cars or medical AI, synthetic data can be used to generate rare but critical edge cases or dangerous failure modes that are difficult or unsafe to capture in the real world. This allows for rigorous testing and validation before deployment.

#### 5.4. Environmental Considerations

- The authors acknowledge that generating large synthetic datasets can be computationally intensive (e.g., 3 CPU-years for their optical flow dataset). However, they also argue that Kubric's control-based methodology can promote efficiency. By allowing researchers to construct smaller, targeted problems that validate specific behaviors, it can potentially **reduce the need for repeatedly training massive models on huge datasets**, thereby lowering the overall environmental impact of research. This emphasizes a shift towards "thoughtful" data generation.

#### 5.5. Limitations and Future Work

The authors transparently acknowledge limitations, including the current lack of support for volumetric effects (fog, fire), soft-body/cloth simulations, advanced camera effects (depth of field, motion blur), and a rasterizing backend for faster-but-less-realistic rendering. Future plans include adding these features and integrating more asset sources. These limitations suggest ongoing development and the potential for even broader impact as the framework matures.

In conclusion, Kubric is more than just a data generator; it is a foundational infrastructure project that empowers machine learning researchers to overcome significant data-related hurdles. By making high-quality, scalable, and controllable synthetic data generation accessible, it is poised to significantly influence the trajectory of AI research and development, fostering innovation, reproducibility, and more ethically conscious AI systems.
