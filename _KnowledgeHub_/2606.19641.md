# Scaling Self-Play for End-to-End Driving

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Mila, Université de Montréal, Torc Robotics, and NYU Tandon School of Engineering developed a scalable self-play framework for training end-to-end autonomous driving models using raw pixel inputs. The approach achieved state-of-the-art performance on the HUGSIM benchmark, with a DrivoR-Reg variant showing a 12.5 HD-Score point improvement over behavior cloning, alongside enhanced robustness in off-distribution scenarios.

## Problem

- End-to-end autonomous driving models trained with behavior cloning (BC) suffer from limited state coverage and covariate shift, leading to brittle performance in out-of-distribution scenarios.
- Existing self-play simulators designed for high-throughput use vectorized observations, making them incompatible with end-to-end policies requiring raw pixel inputs, while direct pixel-based reinforcement learning is sample-inefficient.
- Bridging the simulation-to-real-world gap for policies trained on abstract renderings requires an efficient adaptation strategy with minimal real-world supervision.

## Method

- Developed **Gigapixel**, a high-throughput, batched driving simulator with simplified bounding-box rendering to provide ego-centric pixel observations for scalable self-play.
- Introduced **self-play DAgger**, a distillation-based training methodology where a robust vectorized teacher policy supervises a pixel-based student policy in Gigapixel, addressing the sample inefficiency of direct pixel-based RL.
- Designed a lightweight **sim-to-real perception adaptation** strategy that fine-tunes only the perception backbone of the student using paired real/sim data, keeping the planning head frozen, to transfer learned behaviors to real-world sensors.

## Results

- Gigapixel demonstrated high throughput, being approximately 1000 times faster than the HUGSIM rasterizer, which is crucial for enabling large-scale self-play from pixel observations.
- Self-play DAgger training consistently outperformed behavior cloning, achieving state-of-the-art performance on HUGSIM with a **38.5** HD-Score and improving the DrivoR-Reg variant's score by **12.5** points over its BC counterpart.
- Policies trained with self-play exhibited more robust and anticipatory behaviors in challenging, safety-critical scenarios in both photorealistic and pseudo-closed-loop benchmarks, improving Stage 2 EPDMS on NAVSIM-v2 by up to **7.1** points for DrivoR-Reg.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Large-scale self-play, enabled by high-throughput pixel-rendering simulators like Gigapixel, is a viable and scalable alternative to behavior cloning for learning robust end-to-end driving policies.
> - Distillation via self-play DAgger significantly improves sample efficiency, making it practical to train complex end-to-end models from pixel observations that would otherwise be computationally prohibitive with direct reinforcement learning.
> - The sim-to-real gap for complex planning behaviors learned in self-play can be effectively bridged with minimal supervision by isolating adaptation to the perception module through a targeted fine-tuning process.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Autonomous driving research has increasingly adopted end-to-end models that directly map raw sensor inputs to planning outputs. The prevailing method for training these models is behavior cloning (BC), which relies on large offline datasets of human driving demonstrations. While BC has facilitated progress, it possesses intrinsic limitations. These include restricted state coverage, meaning policies often fail when encountering situations outside the nominal human driving distribution, and a lack of closed-loop feedback during training. The absence of closed-loop interaction can lead to covariate shift, where the policy encounters states during deployment that differ from its training distribution, resulting in compounding errors. These issues persist even with larger datasets.

An alternative paradigm, self-play in simulation, has emerged as a method to address these limitations. In self-play, agents learn through continuous interaction with copies of themselves within a simulated environment. This process generates diverse experience and allows agents to learn the consequences of their actions, which can enhance robustness to compounding errors. Furthermore, self-play's state coverage can scale with available computational resources, providing a mechanism for extensive and targeted experience generation.

Previous self-play approaches in driving have primarily utilized vectorized Bird’s-Eye-View (BEV) observations in high-throughput batched simulators like Gigaflow and PufferDrive. While effective for learning robust behaviors, these vectorized observations are not directly compatible with end-to-end policies that operate on raw sensor inputs, such as camera pixels. Additionally, direct reinforcement learning (RL) from pixel observations for large end-to-end models is typically sample-inefficient due to high computational costs per step.

This research aims to bridge the gap between scalable self-play training and end-to-end policies operating on pixel observations. It positions itself by developing a high-throughput simulator capable of perspective rendering, integrating a sample-efficient training methodology (DAgger-based self-play), and providing a lightweight sim-to-real adaptation strategy. This approach contrasts with prior works that often rely on slower photorealistic simulators or focus on single-agent training paradigms, thereby offering a more scalable and robust alternative for learning complex multi-agent driving behaviors from raw sensor data.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations guiding this research are:

1. **Overcome the Structural Limitations of Offline Behavior Cloning:** The primary motivation is to address the inherent weaknesses of training end-to-end driving models solely on offline human demonstration datasets. These datasets often provide limited coverage of diverse or safety-critical driving states, leading to policies that are brittle when faced with out-of-distribution scenarios. The lack of closed-loop feedback during BC training also causes covariate shift, where policy-induced states diverge from the training distribution, resulting in compounding errors during deployment. The research seeks a training approach that inherently mitigates these issues.

2. **Develop a Scalable and Principled Training Approach for End-to-End Driving Models:** The paper argues that for end-to-end models to realize their full scaling potential, a training method beyond BC is necessary. Self-play is proposed as a principled alternative that can generate a rich curriculum of diverse, closed-loop interactions, continually exposing the agent to novel scenarios that are rare in human logs.

3. **Enable Large-Scale Self-Play Directly from Pixel Observations:** A significant technical hurdle for applying self-play to end-to-end models is the incompatibility of existing high-throughput self-play simulators (which use vectorized BEV observations) with policies that require raw pixel inputs. A key objective is to develop a simulator that supports batched, high-throughput self-play while providing ego-centric perspective pixel observations. This requires a balance between visual fidelity and computational efficiency.

4. **Address the Sample Inefficiency of Direct Pixel-Space Self-Play Reinforcement Learning:** Training large end-to-end models directly with self-play RL from pixel observations is computationally prohibitive due to the cost of forward and backward passes through complex neural networks combined with RL's typical high sample complexity. The research aims to devise a more sample-efficient training strategy that retains the benefits of closed-loop self-play.

5. **Bridge the Simulation-to-Real-World Gap with Lightweight Adaptation:** Policies trained in simulation on abstract renderings need to perform effectively on real-world sensor data. An objective is to develop a transfer mechanism that is efficient and requires minimal real-world supervision, ideally avoiding human trajectory labels. This involves isolating the sim-to-real gap to the perception module, while preserving the complex planning behaviors learned in self-play.

6. **Empirically Demonstrate the Scalability and Practicality of Self-Play for End-to-End Driving:** The research aims to provide evidence that increasing the scale of self-play training directly translates into proportional gains in policy performance on established benchmarks, thus establishing self-play as a practical and effective strategy for training end-to-end autonomous driving models.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology for training end-to-end driving models via self-play involves three main stages: training a vectorized teacher in an abstract simulator, distilling this teacher into a pixel-based student using a novel self-play DAgger approach in a new high-throughput simulator, and finally adapting the student to real-world sensor data.

#### 3.1. Policy Formulation

The policy is formulated as π(τ |I, C), where τ is a trajectory, I represents raw image observations, and C is additional ego context (e.g., ego state, navigation command). The policy decomposes into a perception backbone f_θ_per(I, C) producing features E_per, and a planning head f_θ_plan(E_per, C) mapping these features to a trajectory.

#### 3.2. Gigapixel Simulator

A central component is **Gigapixel**, a high-throughput batched driving simulator designed for scalable self-play directly from pixel observations.
- **Design Philosophy**: It renders a simplified bounding-box world that preserves essential scene structure (agent bounding boxes, static map polylines, traffic light states) rather than pursuing photorealism. This enables high throughput while supporting pixel inputs.
- **Architecture**: Gigapixel extends PufferDrive and integrates the Madrona rendering engine. Madrona provides GPU-accelerated, batched perspective rendering using an entity-component-system (ECS) design, allowing thousands of environments to render in parallel on a single GPU.
- **Rendering**: Supports both rasterization and ray tracing. Ray tracing is the default for improved visual fidelity, as rendering ceases to be the bottleneck when policies involve complex neural networks.
- **Observations**: Each agent receives ego-centric observations comprising an ego-state vector (speed, acceleration, dimensions, collision flag), a high-level navigation command (LEFT, STRAIGHT, RIGHT, UNKNOWN derived from logged paths), and raw pixel observations.
- **Action Space and Dynamics**:
 - **Action-based**: For the vectorized teacher, a discrete set of 12 longitudinal and lateral jerk values controls a kinematic bicycle model, similar to Gigaflow.
 - **Trajectory-based**: For the pixel-based student, the policy outputs a 4-second future trajectory as a sequence of SE(2) waypoints. This trajectory is executed by a receding-horizon LQR controller for the ego vehicle. Other agents are controlled by the student’s parallel action head using native jerk dynamics.
- **Reward Functions**: A multi-objective reward function for RL training encompasses safety (collisions, off-road), rule compliance (traffic lights, lane direction, speed limits), progress (velocity, goal reach), and comfort (harsh acceleration/jerk, lane centering). Reward coefficients are randomized per agent during training (reward conditioning) to foster diverse driving personas and fixed to a "careful-driver" setting for inference.

#### 3.3. Self-Play DAgger Training

To address sample inefficiency, the paper introduces **self-play DAgger**, a distillation-based approach.
- **Vectorized Teacher Training**:
 - A compact (2.7M-parameter) permutation-invariant policy (π_teacher), inspired by Gigaflow, is trained via decentralized PPO on vectorized BEV observations in an abstract simulator (re-implementation of Gigaflow).
 - Training involves 25 billion agent steps and utilizes reward conditioning to generate a diverse population of driving behaviors.
 - Advantage prioritization is used to upsample high-magnitude advantage events during minibatch sampling, focusing learning on informative transitions.
- **Pixel-Based Student Training**:
 - The robust vectorized teacher is distilled into a pixel-based end-to-end student policy (π_student) in Gigapixel using a self-play DAgger objective.
 - All agents in the simulation are controlled by π_student.
 - Supervision is provided by π_teacher: for each student-controlled state S, a parallel simulator instance is forked, and the teacher is rolled out in self-play for a horizon H, generating target trajectories τ_i for each agent i.
 - The student is trained to minimize a planning loss L_plan between its predicted trajectory and the teacher's target trajectory. The student's reward conditioning vector c_i is matched to the teacher's to ensure consistent persona imitation.
 - A mixed-policy rollout schedule is employed, where actions are sampled from the teacher (with probability β, annealed from 1 to 0) and the student, to guide exploration during early training.
 - Training data is curated by upweighting rare turn commands and filtering out trajectories containing infractions.
 - Two student architectures are trained: a multimodal DrivoR planner and a single-mode regression variant (DrivoR-Reg). Rendering resolutions are gradually increased during training.

#### 3.4. Sim-to-Real Perception Adaptation

To enable deployment on real sensor data, a lightweight perception adaptation strategy is used.
- **Mechanism**: The sim-to-real gap is framed as an image-to-image perceptual adaptation task. Only the perception backbone (f_θ_real_per) of the Gigapixel-trained student policy is fine-tuned, while the planning head (f_θ_plan) remains frozen.
- **Data**: A dataset of paired observations D_paired = {(o_real, o_pix)} is used, where o_pix is a Gigapixel rendering reconstructed from the abstract state corresponding to the real camera image o_real.
- **Objective**: A frozen teacher policy (π_sim), operating on o_pix, supervises a trainable student policy (π_real), operating on o_real. The loss function combines the planning loss (L_plan) from self-play DAgger and an L2 perceptual loss (L_perc) that aligns the feature representations (register tokens) of the real-image-adapted backbone with the frozen reference backbone. For multimodal DrivoR, a mode-aligned distillation objective is used where the student's k-th proposal is regressed towards the teacher's k-th proposal.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several findings and results demonstrating the effectiveness and scalability of the proposed self-play approach for end-to-end driving.

- **Gigapixel Simulator Throughput**: Gigapixel demonstrates high throughput, with its rasterizer being approximately 1000 times faster than the Gaussian-splatting-based HUGSIM simulator and 4000 times faster than the rasterization-based RAP at 512x512 resolution. This throughput capability is identified as crucial for enabling large-scale self-play training from pixel observations.

- **Self-Play DAgger Efficiency**: Empirical comparison within Gigapixel shows that self-play DAgger training is substantially more sample-efficient than direct pixel-based self-play RL. Self-play DAgger achieves a Gigapixel Driving Score of 60 in roughly 3000 times fewer steps, justifying its adoption for training compute-intensive end-to-end models.

- **Scaling Performance with Experience**: Self-play DAgger training consistently improves policy performance with increasing experience. Beyond 10 million steps, it outperforms both single-agent DAgger and behavior cloning (BC). Behavior cloning notably plateaus around 100 million steps, attributed to its inability to generate diverse, on-policy experience. Self-play DAgger’s superior performance is linked to its ability to generate varied multi-agent interactions and leverage data from all agents in a rollout.

- **HUGSIM Benchmark Performance (Closed-loop, Photorealistic Simulation)**:
 - Gigapixel-DrivoR achieves state-of-the-art performance on HUGSIM, surpassing all baselines in both average Route Completion (RC) (50.1) and HUGSIM Driving Score (HD-Score) (38.5) without relying on human trajectory supervision.
 - Compared to behavior-cloned DrivoR, Gigapixel-DrivoR shows a 2.8 HD-Score point improvement. The regression-based Gigapixel-DrivoR-Reg variant exhibits a more substantial 12.5 HD-Score point improvement (a 60% relative gain) over its BC counterpart.
 - Analysis suggests that while behavior-cloned DrivoR shows a higher HD-Score in the "Extreme" difficulty tier, this is correlated with a high-velocity bias and less safe driving style (2.7x higher average collision velocity), rather than superior closed-loop reasoning. Gigapixel-DrivoR adopts a more cautious approach.

- **NAVSIM-v2 Benchmark Performance (Pseudo-closed-loop, Real Logs)**:
 - On the NAVSIM-v2 navhard split, Gigapixel-DrivoR and Gigapixel-DrivoR-Reg models outperform their behavior-cloned counterparts in Extended Predictive Driver Model Score (EPDMS) without human trajectory supervision or SimScale recovery data.
 - The improvements are concentrated in Stage 2 EPDMS, which evaluates robustness in perturbed, off-distribution ego poses (a proxy for closed-loop robustness). Gigapixel-DrivoR-Reg improves by 7.1 points, and Gigapixel-DrivoR by 4.1 points in Stage 2 over their BC equivalents.
 - Gigapixel-DrivoR's Stage 2 score (63.5) nearly matches DrivoR (w/ SimScale) (64.6), despite the latter being trained with explicitly curated high-EPDMS trajectories.

- **Ablation Study on Perception Adaptation**: The ablation study on HUGSIM confirms the importance of the proposed sim-to-real perception adaptation strategy. Removing the perceptual loss significantly reduces HD-Score (from 33.2 to 18.5), and further unfreezing the planning head leads to an additional drop (to 15.8), indicating that both components are essential for preserving learned closed-loop behaviors.

- **Qualitative Behavior Analysis**: Visual comparisons illustrate that self-play trained policies exhibit more robust and anticipatory behaviors in challenging scenarios. For example, when approaching a decelerating lead vehicle, the self-play trained DrivoR plans a safe lateral maneuver, while the BC trained DrivoR rear-ends the vehicle. Similarly, in a recovery state near the road edge, the self-play policy successfully corrects towards the lane, whereas the BC policy drifts off-road. These observations suggest that self-play effectively exposes policies to safety-critical and corrective states that are underrepresented in offline human logs.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a significant contribution to the field of autonomous driving, particularly concerning the training of end-to-end models. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Validation of Self-Play as a Viable End-to-End Training Strategy**: The work establishes self-play as a practical, scalable, and effective alternative to traditional behavior cloning for training end-to-end driving policies. By demonstrating competitive, and in some cases superior, performance on benchmarks without human trajectory supervision, it validates self-play's ability to learn robust driving behaviors.

2. **Addressing Core Limitations of Behavior Cloning**: The paper provides a methodology that explicitly addresses the fundamental limitations of behavior cloning, namely limited state coverage and the absence of closed-loop feedback. Self-play DAgger in Gigapixel allows policies to learn from a continually evolving distribution of self-generated interactions, improving robustness in out-of-distribution and safety-critical scenarios that are rare in human logs.

3. **Introduction of Gigapixel for Scalable Pixel-Based Self-Play**: The development and integration of Gigapixel, a high-throughput batched perspective-rendering simulator, is a key enabler. This simulator provides the necessary computational scale for pixel-based end-to-end self-play, which was previously a bottleneck due to the slowness of photorealistic simulators or the incompatibility of abstract simulators with pixel inputs. Gigapixel opens new avenues for research into large-scale closed-loop learning from visual inputs.

4. **Enhancing Policy Robustness and Safety**: The qualitative and quantitative results demonstrate that policies trained with this self-play methodology exhibit more robust and safer driving behaviors in complex, multi-agent scenarios, such as anticipating and reacting to decelerating lead vehicles or recovering from off-nominal road edge positions. This directly impacts the reliability and deployability of autonomous driving systems.

5. **Reduced Reliance on Costly Human Supervision**: By achieving strong performance without requiring human trajectory supervision, the method reduces the dependence on expensive and time-consuming human data collection and annotation. This can lower the barrier to entry for developing and continually improving autonomous driving systems.

6. **Enabling Continual Improvement**: The self-play paradigm inherently supports continual improvement through the generation of synthetic experience. As agents learn, they explore new states, which can then be used to further refine their policies, creating a virtuous cycle of learning and performance enhancement that scales with available compute.

7. **Foundation for Future Research**: The work lays a foundation for several promising future research directions, including the integration of human supervision (e.g., via KL-regularization), the use of generative models to synthesize even more diverse and challenging initial scenarios, and the exploration of direct pixel-based RL with native trajectory outputs to overcome the teacher-student asymmetry. These extensions could further enhance the capabilities and generalization of autonomous driving policies.
