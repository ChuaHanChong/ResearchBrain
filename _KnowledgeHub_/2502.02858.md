# Dexterous Safe Control for Humanoids in Cluttered Environments via Projected Safe Set Algorithm

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Carnegie Mellon University developed a Projected Safe Set Algorithm (p-SSA) for humanoid robots to ensure dexterous safety in cluttered environments by robustly resolving multi-constraint Quadratic Program (QP) infeasibility. This method consistently reduced collision severity and control constraint violations in simulation and on a real Unitree G1 humanoid, operating effectively without requiring manual parameter tuning for safety-performance trade-offs.

## Problem

- Traditional robot collision avoidance simplifies robot geometry, making humanoids overly conservative and limiting their dexterity in cluttered environments.
- Multi-body safety in dense, cluttered environments generates a large number of safety constraints, frequently leading to infeasible Quadratic Programs (QPs) in safe control algorithms.
- Existing safe control methods lack practical solutions for resolving QP infeasibility in high-dimensional, multi-constraint problems involving highly articulated robots.

## Method

- The work proposes a ==multi-constraint QP formulation== for "dexterous safety" using ==limb-level geometric models== for both robot links and obstacles.
- It introduces the **Relaxed Safe Set Algorithm (r-SSA)** which utilizes ==slack variables== to allow constraint relaxation, ensuring QP feasibility at the cost of sensitive parameter tuning.
- It develops the **Projected Safe Set Algorithm (p-SSA)** which ==decouples constraint relaxation from task optimization==, first minimizing constraint violations, then optimizing the task using the minimally relaxed constraints.

## Results

- Both r-SSA and p-SSA significantly reduced safety constraint violations (collisions) and improved control constraint satisfaction compared to a naive Safe Set Algorithm (SSA) baseline.
- p-SSA consistently outperformed r-SSA in minimizing constraint violations and achieved comparable or better task performance without requiring task-specific parameter tuning for safety-performance trade-offs.
- p-SSA successfully enabled safe teleoperation of a Unitree G1 humanoid robot in cluttered environments, both in simulation and on real hardware, by effectively preventing collisions even with unsafe operator inputs.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Multi-constraint QPs for dexterous safety are frequently infeasible due to inherent physical limitations, conflicting control law designs, or kinematic limits of the robot.
> - Decoupling the optimization for feasibility from the task objective, as in p-SSA, eliminates the need for sensitive, task-specific parameter tuning while achieving a robust balance between safety and performance.
> - Principled and minimal relaxation of safety constraints is crucial for maintaining real-time operability and reducing safety violations when strict constraint satisfaction is unattainable.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The deployment of highly articulated humanoid robots in real-world environments, such as factory automation, service, and healthcare, necessitates robust safety mechanisms. A critical challenge in this context is ensuring collision avoidance without unduly restricting the robot's dexterity and task performance, especially in cluttered environments where interactions are complex and involve close proximity.

Traditional approaches to robot collision avoidance often simplify the robot's geometry (e.g., using a single bounding cylinder for the entire body). While computationally efficient, these simplified models are overly conservative for humanoids, severely limiting their feasible motion and task execution, particularly for "dexterous safety" problems which involve limb-level geometric modeling.

Existing safe control methods, broadly categorized as indirect (model-based) or direct (model-free), have typically focused on simpler scenarios. Indirect methods, such as Safe Set Algorithms (SSA), Control Barrier Functions (CBFs), and Hamilton–Jacobi reachability, derive explicit control laws from robot dynamics and safety specifications. These methods are generally favored for their interpretability and adaptability but have primarily been applied to problems involving simplified robot geometries in sparse environments, or multi-body scenarios in sparse environments by reducing safety to a single energy function (e.g., maximizing the closest distance to an obstacle).

The current research landscape for indirect safe control faces significant challenges when applied to "multi-body safety with densely distributed obstacles"—a scenario the authors define as "dexterous safety in cluttered environments." In such complex situations, a single energy function is often insufficient to capture all potential collisions, as addressing one potential collision might immediately lead to another. This necessitates the use of multiple energy functions, each defining a control constraint. When these multiple constraints are combined into an optimization problem (typically a Quadratic Program, QP), the problem frequently becomes infeasible, either due to physical impossibility, conflicting constraints from the control law design, or kinematic limits of the robot. Prior work on multi-constraint safe control has not adequately addressed QP infeasibility for high-dimensional, highly articulated robots in densely cluttered settings, often focusing on inherently consistent constraints or small problem dimensions.

This work addresses this specific gap by proposing methods that can resolve QP infeasibility in high-dimensional, multi-constraint dexterous safety problems, allowing humanoids to operate robustly in cluttered environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to ensure the safety of humanoid robots operating in cluttered environments without compromising their dexterous performance. This specific challenge is termed "dexterous safety," characterized by enforcing limb-level geometry constraints to avoid both external and self-collisions in environments with numerous obstacles.

The motivation for this work stems from several critical observations and unmet needs in the field of robot safety:

- **Limitations of Simplified Safety Models:** Current safety approaches often rely on simplified geometric models of robots and environments (e.g., single bounding shapes in sparse environments). While these simplify computations, they are overly conservative for highly articulated humanoids, severely restricting their operational capabilities and preventing them from utilizing their full dexterity in complex, cluttered spaces. For humanoids to fulfill their potential in tasks requiring close-proximity interaction (e.g., in factories or healthcare), fine-grained, limb-level geometry modeling is essential.
- **Complexity of Multi-Body, Dense Environments:** When considering dexterous safety, the robot's numerous links and their interaction with multiple, densely distributed obstacles (and self-collision) generate a large number of safety constraints. Unlike simpler scenarios where safety can be quantified by a single energy function (e.g., minimum distance to the closest obstacle), multi-body safety in dense environments is fundamentally a multi-objective problem. A motion that resolves one potential collision might inadvertently cause another.
- **Infeasibility of Multi-Constraint Optimization:** The application of established indirect safe control methods (like SSA or CBF) to such multi-constraint problems typically results in a Quadratic Program (QP). When many detailed constraints are simultaneously active, these QPs frequently become infeasible. The authors categorize these infeasibilities into three types:
 - **Inherent Infeasibility:** Physically unavoidable collisions, where no control input can prevent impact (e.g., being trapped by dynamic obstacles).
 - **Method Infeasibility:** Conflicts arising from the design of multiple energy functions, where the control laws derived from different safety indices require contradictory actions (e.g., needing to move in opposite directions simultaneously).
 - **Kinematics Infeasibility:** Situations where the robot's kinematic limits prevent it from executing the required safety-critical motions, even if physically possible in principle (e.g., an arm in an extended pose having limited joint velocity to avoid an obstacle).
- **Lack of Practical Solutions for Infeasible QPs:** Current theoretical tools generally aim for persistently feasible QPs, but for high-dimensional, multi-constraint problems like dexterous safety, constructing such QPs is deemed intractable. There is a need for a practical method that can be quickly deployed on humanoids and minimize safety violations when QPs inevitably become infeasible, rather than simply failing or resorting to unsafe fallback behaviors.

The core objective is to develop a robust and practical safe control method that can handle hundreds of limb-level safety constraints, effectively address QP infeasibility by principled constraint relaxation, and achieve a balance between safety and task performance without extensive parameter tuning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology extends classical safe control algorithms to handle multi-constraint scenarios, specifically focusing on resolving the frequent infeasibility that arises in dexterous safety problems. The approach is based on an energy-function framework, where safety is quantified by multiple safety indices, each leading to a control constraint.

**A. Safe Control Preliminaries**
The robot dynamics are modeled as a control-affine system: $\dot{x} = f(x) + g(x)u$, where $x$ is the system state and $u$ is the control input, bounded by $u^- \le u \le u^+$. Robot tasks involve minimizing an objective function $J(x, u)$ while satisfying safety constraints, represented by staying within a safe set $X_S$, defined by an energy function $\phi_0(x) \le 0$.

Standard indirect safe control methods (SSA, CBF) derive control laws to ensure forward invariance of a safe set $X_{\text{safe}} \subseteq X_S$. This involves designing an $n$-th order safety index $\phi = \phi_0 + \sum_{i=1}^n k_i \phi_0^{(i)}$ and deriving a control constraint $\dot{\phi}(x, u) \le -\eta$ if $\phi(x) \ge 0$, where $\eta > 0$. This typically results in a Quadratic Program (QP) to find the control $u$ that minimizes $J(x,u)$ subject to the safety constraint and control limits.

**B. Dexterous Safety Formulation**
For dexterous safety in cluttered environments, a single $\phi_0$ is insufficient. The approach considers a set of $M$ constraints, with $\phi_{0,i}$ for each robot body-obstacle or self-collision pair. Each $\phi_{0,i}$ corresponds to a safety index $\phi_i$, leading to a multi-constraint QP:
$\min_u J(x, u)$
s.t. $\dot{\phi}_i(x, u) \le -\eta_i$ if $\phi_i(x) \ge 0$, for $i=1, \dots, M$
$u^- \le u \le u^+$

This multi-constraint QP formulation is the source of frequent infeasibility, as discussed in the motivation. The paper assumes a quadratic objective $J(x, u) = \|u - u_{\text{ref}}\|_{2,Q}^2$, minimizing deviation from a nominal control $u_{\text{ref}}$, resulting in a standard QP structure. The Lie derivatives $L_f \phi(x)$ and $L_g \phi(x)$ are computed to express $\dot{\phi}(x, u)$ as $L_f \phi(x) + L_g \phi(x)u$.

**C. Relaxed Safe Set Algorithm (r-SSA)**
To address QP infeasibility, r-SSA introduces positive slack variables $s \in \mathbb{R}^M$ for each safety constraint, allowing them to be relaxed. The objective function is augmented to minimize both the deviation from the nominal control and the magnitude of the slack variables:
$\min_{u,s} \|u - u_{\text{ref}}\|_{2,Q}^2 + \frac{1}{p} (\|s\|_{p, Q_s^{\text{rssa}}})^p$
s.t. $L_f \phi(x) + L_g \phi(x)u \le -\eta + s$ if $\phi(x) \ge 0$
$u^- \le u \le u^+$
$s \ge 0$
Here, $Q_s^{\text{rssa}}$ is a diagonal matrix of positive coefficients for weighting the slack variables. This formulation guarantees a feasible solution by allowing violations, but it optimizes performance and safety objectives concurrently. This can lead to conflicts where performance is prioritized over safety if $Q$ and $Q_s^{\text{rssa}}$ are not properly tuned.

**D. Projected Safe Set Algorithm (p-SSA)**
p-SSA improves upon r-SSA by decoupling the optimization for feasibility from the task objective, removing the need for balancing weighting parameters. It operates in two phases:

1. **Phase I (Constraint Projection):** This phase focuses solely on minimizing constraint violations to find the "nearest" feasible set. It solves an optimization problem to compute an optimal slack variable $s^*$ that relaxes the constraints by the minimum amount required to make them feasible, subject to control limits:
 $\min_s \frac{1}{p} (\|s\|_{p, Q_s^{\text{pssa}}})^p$
 s.t. $L_f \phi(x) + L_g \phi(x)u \le -\eta + s$ for some $u$ satisfying $u^- \le u \le u^+$ (this implicitly requires finding a $u$ that is feasible given $s$)
 $s \ge 0$
 The paper simplifies this by saying "compute an optimal slack variable $s^*$ ... to ensure that for this $s^*$, there exists *some* $u$ such that the full set of constraints are feasible." In practice, this means finding the minimum $s$ such that the control constraint $L_f \phi(x) + L_g \phi(x)u \le -\eta + s$ *can be satisfied by some $u$ within the control limits*. The solution $s^*$ represents the minimal necessary relaxation. $Q_s^{\text{pssa}}$ (set to identity in this paper) allows balancing the relative importance among different constraints, not between safety and performance.

2. **Phase II (Task Optimization):** With the minimal relaxation $s^*$ determined, this phase solves for the control input $u_{\text{safe}}$ that best achieves the task objective, knowing that the constraints are now guaranteed to be feasible:
 $\min_u \|u - u_{\text{ref}}\|_{2,Q}^2$
 s.t. $L_f \phi(x) + L_g \phi(x)u \le -\eta + s^*$ if $\phi(x) \ge 0$
 $u^- \le u \le u^+$
 Since $s^*$ from Phase I ensures feasibility of the constraint set, Phase II is guaranteed to have a solution. This decoupling ensures that p-SSA always prioritizes maximal safety (minimal violations) while still trying to achieve the task, without the explicit performance-safety trade-off seen in r-SSA.

**E. Energy Function Design and System Modeling**
The paper uses first-order safety indices ($\phi = \phi_0$) for collision avoidance, given the first-order integrator model of the robot dynamics ($\dot{x}=u$). $\phi_{\text{env},i} = d_{\text{min,env}} - d_i$ for robot-obstacle collisions and $\phi_{\text{self},i} = d_{\text{min,self}} - d_i$ for self-collisions, where $d_i$ is the distance between collision spheres. The humanoid's collision bodies are modeled as spheres, and obstacles as spheres or planes. The Lie derivatives $L_f \phi(x)$ and $L_g \phi(x)$ are derived based on these distance functions and the forward kinematics and Jacobian of the robot's links.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments were conducted in both MuJoCo simulation and on a real Unitree G1 humanoid robot, assessing the proposed methods (r-SSA and p-SSA) against a naive Safe Set Algorithm (SSA) that does not handle infeasibility. The Unitree G1 robot was modeled in two configurations: `G1FixedBase` (upper body only, 17 DoFs) and `G1WholeBody` (floating base, 20 DoFs). Tasks involved tracking 3D goal positions for wrists/base while avoiding collisions, with static (SO) or dynamic (DO) obstacles at two difficulty levels (V0, V1). Collision bodies were modeled as spheres for both robot links and obstacles. Safety margins were $d_{\text{min,env}}=0.05$m for external obstacles and $d_{\text{min,self}}=0.01$m for self-collision.

**Evaluation Metrics:**
- **J (Goal Tracking Performance):** Measures how well wrists/base track their goals. Higher is better.
- **C (Control Constraint Satisfaction Score):** Evaluates minimal violation of control constraints *when the QP is infeasible*. Higher (closer to 1) is better.
- **S (Safety Constraint Satisfaction Score):** Evaluates violation of actual safety margins (collisions). Lower (closer to 0) is better.
- **R_Feas (QP Feasibility Rate):** Empirical probability of the QP being feasible. Higher is better.

**Overall Performance (Answering Q1, Q2, Q3):**

1. **Q1: How often does (9) become infeasible?**
 - The naive SSA (9) frequently became infeasible, particularly in environments with more obstacles (V0) and for the `G1FixedBase` configuration due to reduced mobility. Dynamic obstacles (DO) also increased infeasibility. The `R_Feas` metric for r-SSA and p-SSA was often lower than naive SSA. This is attributed to r-SSA and p-SSA actively keeping the robot close to obstacles without collision, leading to a higher number of "active" constraints and thus more frequent detection of (or need to relax) infeasible QPs. Naive SSA, by contrast, might let the robot pass through obstacles if infeasible, reducing active constraints.

2. **Q2: Do r-SSA and p-SSA mitigate infeasible QPs and improve humanoid safety?**
 - Yes, both r-SSA and p-SSA significantly improved safety compared to naive SSA. The naive SSA, upon encountering an infeasible QP, directly passed the unsafe nominal control, resulting in negligible C scores (high control constraint violations) and poor S scores (frequent collisions). In contrast, r-SSA and p-SSA consistently achieved higher C scores, indicating they effectively reduced violations to control constraints even when infeasible. Consequently, they achieved lower S scores, signifying fewer and less severe safety constraint violations (i.e., less penetration into safety margins or fewer collisions). This demonstrates their effectiveness in mitigating the impact of infeasible QPs.

3. **Q3: How does r-SSA compare to p-SSA in terms of parameter tuning and performance?**
 - **Minimizing Violations:** p-SSA consistently outperformed r-SSA in terms of minimizing control constraint violations (C) and, consequently, safety constraint violations (S). This is because p-SSA independently minimizes constraint violations in its first phase, whereas r-SSA jointly optimizes performance and safety, potentially trading off safety for better goal tracking.
 - **Parameter Tuning:** An ablation study on r-SSA with various relaxation weights ($Q_s^{\text{rssa}}$) showed that r-SSA's performance-safety trade-off is highly sensitive to this parameter. Small $Q_s^{\text{rssa}}$ values allowed larger constraint violations for better performance, while larger values improved safety but degraded task performance. Each task configuration resulted in a different Pareto front, requiring tedious and task-specific tuning for r-SSA to achieve an optimal balance. p-SSA, by design, automatically achieved a position on the Pareto front that balanced safety and performance well across all tasks, and notably, it often matched or surpassed the best-tuned r-SSA performance without any parameter tuning. This highlights p-SSA's robustness and practical advantage.

**Safe Teleoperation on Unitree G1 Humanoid (Answering Q4):**

- **Simulation:** In a teleoperation task where the humanoid tracked human wrist motions to manipulate objects inside a cabinet, p-SSA effectively rejected unsafe nominal controls. For instance, if the operator's input would guide the robot's arms through cabinet walls, p-SSA intervened to keep the arms inside, demonstrating its ability to prevent collisions even with erroneous inputs. Purple lines in visualizations indicated where control constraints were relaxed.
- **Real Hardware:** The Unitree G1 humanoid, teleoperated via Apple Vision Pro (AVP), successfully performed safe manipulation inside a cabinet. p-SSA prevented collisions arising from various improper operator actions, such as overly aggressive motions near the cabinet opening or attempting to squeeze both arms into too small a space. The occurrence of purple lines (relaxed constraints) in the real-time AVP view confirmed that p-SSA was actively mitigating infeasible QPs and ensuring safe operation in a cluttered, confined space.
- This demonstrates that p-SSA is a reliable and practical approach for dexterous safety in real-world scenarios, effectively handling complex collision avoidance in challenging teleoperation tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a significant contribution to the field of robotic safety, particularly for highly articulated humanoids operating in complex, cluttered environments.

The key significance and potential impact are as follows:

1. **Enabling Dexterous Operation in Cluttered Environments:** The work directly addresses a critical barrier to deploying humanoids in real-world settings that demand high dexterity and interaction in confined spaces. By providing a robust method for "dexterous safety" with limb-level geometry modeling, it moves beyond overly conservative safety approaches, allowing humanoids to perform complex tasks like manipulation within cabinets or in close proximity to obstacles and humans.
2. **Practical Resolution of QP Infeasibility:** The core contribution of r-SSA and especially p-SSA is their principled approach to resolving the frequent infeasibility of multi-constraint quadratic programs in safety-critical control. Instead of failing or reverting to unsafe modes, these algorithms relax constraints minimally, ensuring that a feasible control solution is always found. This makes safe control algorithms more robust and deployable in dynamic and unpredictable environments.
3. **Tuning-Free Performance with p-SSA:** The Projected Safe Set Algorithm (p-SSA) stands out by decoupling the optimization for constraint feasibility from the task objective. This eliminates the need for tedious, task-specific parameter tuning (which is a common challenge with relaxation-based methods) while consistently achieving a favorable balance between safety and performance. This makes p-SSA highly practical for real-world applications where rapid deployment and adaptability are crucial.
4. **Enhanced Safety in Human-Robot Interaction and Teleoperation:** The successful demonstration of p-SSA in teleoperation tasks, both in simulation and on real hardware, highlights its direct applicability to human-robot interaction scenarios. By acting as a robust safety filter, p-SSA can prevent accidental collisions caused by operator error, motion retargeting imperfections, or unforeseen environmental changes. This can improve user confidence and expand the range of tasks humanoids can perform safely alongside or guided by humans.
5. **Generalizability:** The framework, based on energy functions and QP optimization, is compatible with various energy-based safe control methods (SSA, CBF). The demonstration on different robot configurations (fixed-base vs. whole-body) and obstacle types (static vs. dynamic) suggests its generalizability across a range of humanoid tasks and environmental conditions.
6. **Foundation for Future Research:** While the current work acknowledges that relaxation implies a lack of formal safety guarantees, it opens avenues for future research into adaptive constraint modification or dynamic guarantee restoration strategies post-infeasibility. The insights into various sources of infeasibility (inherent, method, kinematics) also provide a deeper understanding of the challenges in multi-body safe control.

In conclusion, this research provides a practical, robust, and tuning-free solution (p-SSA) to a significant challenge in robot safety, enabling advanced humanoid dexterity in cluttered environments and moving closer to reliable real-world humanoid deployment.
