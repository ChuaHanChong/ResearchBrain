# RUKA: Rethinking the Design of Humanoid Hands with Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> A tendon-driven robotic hand design (RUKA) combines 3D-printed components with learning-based control through motion capture data, achieving human-like dexterity with a 10/10 Kapandji score and 6.03kg payload capacity while maintaining affordability through off-the-shelf components and open-source plans.

## Problem

- Existing robotic hands struggle to balance precision, compactness, strength, and affordability
- Tendon-driven systems offer advantages but are complex to control due to nonlinearities and uncertainties
- Collecting accurate training data for learning-based hand control traditionally requires expensive joint encoders, limiting the affordability of data-driven approaches to tendon-driven hand control

## Method

- Open-source design using 3D-printed parts and off-the-shelf components for accessibility
- Learning-based control system using LSTM+MLP models trained on motion capture data
- Anthropomorphic form factor enabling human demonstration learning

## Results

- Perfect 10/10 score on Kapandji test vs 9/10 for existing hands, with 2.74N pinch force and 6.03kg payload capacity
- 40Hz direct control rate and 25Hz learned controller operation
- Successful demonstration of complex tasks including cube flipping and object manipulation

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Motion capture using MANUS gloves enables control without expensive joint encoders
> - Temporal information processing through LSTM networks improves control accuracy
> - Combined approach of affordable hardware and learned control achieves human-like capability

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

- **Problem Area:** Dexterous robotic manipulation is a long-standing challenge in robotics. Creating robotic hands that can replicate the dexterity and adaptability of human hands is crucial for robots to effectively perform tasks in human-centric environments.
- **Existing Approaches and Limitations:** The paper situates itself within the context of existing robotic hand designs, categorizing them into direct-drive, tendon-driven, soft robotic, and biomimetic hands.
 - **Direct-drive hands (e.g., LEAP, Allegro):** Offer precise control but are often bulky, heavy, and expensive due to integrated motors and encoders.
 - **Tendon-driven hands (e.g., Shadow Hand):** Prioritize compactness and strength by placing actuators remotely but suffer from control complexities due to nonlinearities and uncertainties in tendon transmission.
 - **Soft robotic hands (e.g., RBO3, ADAPT):** Provide compliance and natural grasps but are often less precise and challenging to simulate.
 - **Biomimetic hands (e.g., FLLEX hand):** Mimic human tendon actuation but are complex and difficult to reproduce.
- **Gap Addressed:** The paper identifies the trade-offs inherent in existing designs, particularly the difficulty of achieving a balance between precision, compactness, strength, affordability, and usability simultaneously. The central argument is that learning-based approaches offer an opportunity to overcome some of these limitations, especially in the context of tendon-driven actuation using low-cost materials.
- **Related Research:** The paper references a wide range of research areas relevant to the work:
 - Autonomous dexterous policies: It acknowledges the progress made in robotic learning for complex manipulation.
 - Sim-to-real transfer: Leveraging simulated environments for training robot controllers.
 - Imitation learning: Learning from human demonstrations (teleoperated or human hand tracking).
 - Controller Learning: Addresses the challenge of collecting ground-truth data for supervised learning.
 - Motion Capture: Addresses the challenge of how to gather accurate training data for robot hands.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

- **Primary Objective:** To design and develop a novel, open-source, tendon-driven humanoid hand (RUKA) that is compact, affordable, capable, and easier to control through learning-based methods.
- **Motivations:**
 - **Hardware limitations:** Existing robotic hands often make compromises on key features, hindering their ability to perform diverse human-like tasks.
 - **Potential of learning:** Learning-based control offers a way to address the complexities of tendon-driven systems and low-cost materials.
 - **Accessibility:** To provide a readily available and modifiable platform for robotics research, the hand should be affordable and open-source.
 - **Anthropomorphism:** Matching the size and morphology of a human hand facilitates learning from human data and integration into human environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

- **Hardware Design:**
 - **Tendon-driven actuation:** Uses tendons connected to remotely located actuators to achieve compactness and strength.
 - **3D-printed parts:** Lowers cost and enables rapid prototyping and customization.
 - **Off-the-shelf components:** Ensures affordability and ease of assembly.
 - **Humanoid form factor:** Mimics the size, shape, and degrees of freedom of a human hand.
- **Data-Driven Control:**
 - **Motion capture using MANUS gloves:** Leverages off-the-shelf motion capture gloves to obtain fingertip and joint position data without relying on expensive joint encoders.
 - **Autonomous data collection:** Generates a large dataset of (fingertip/joint position, actuation command) pairs by randomly sampling actuation commands.
 - **Learning joint-to-actuator and fingertip-to-actuator models:** Trains machine learning models to predict actuation commands required to achieve target positions. Specifically, they use a Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) network to process the temporal information of the past 10 finger states and an MLP head to predict the motor position based on the output of the LSTM.
- **Evaluation:**
 - **Reachability tests:** Assesses the hand's range of motion and ability to achieve various grasps.
 - **Durability tests:** Evaluates the hand's ability to operate continuously without overheating or performance degradation.
 - **Strength tests:** Measures the hand's pinch force, payload capacity, and resistance to slipping.
 - **Teleoperation tasks:** Demonstrates the hand's ability to perform dexterous movements under human control.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

- **Successful Design:** RUKA achieves a balance between key design principles, offering a compact, affordable, capable, and anthropomorphic hand.
- **Superior Performance:** RUKA outperforms existing robotic hands (LEAP, Allegro) in reachability, durability, and strength tests. Specifically, RUKA scored 10/10 on the Kapandji test, whereas Allegro and LEAP scored 9/10. On the strength test, RUKA achieved a pinch force of 2.74N, a payload of 6.03kg, a DIP/PIP force of 3.02N, and an MCP force of 16.15N.
- **Effective Learning-Based Control:** The learned joint-to-actuator and fingertip-to-actuator models enable dexterous movements and teleoperation capabilities. Comparing the LSTM+MLP model against a MLP model, the k-NN model, and a search-based model, the LSTM+MLP model performs the best when it comes to generalizing to data gathered from a human hand.
- **Teleoperation Demonstrations:** RUKA can be successfully used for teleoperation tasks. The system supports control at 40Hz by directly controlling the hand via motor positions and 25Hz when using the learned controllers.
- **Policy Learning Demonstrations:** RUKA can be used for policy learning tasks such as Cube Flipping and Bread Pick and Drop.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

- **Accessibility and Affordability:** RUKA's open-source design and low cost make it accessible to a wider range of researchers and developers, potentially accelerating progress in robotic manipulation.
- **Advancement of Learning-Based Control:** The data-driven control approach offers a viable solution for addressing the challenges of tendon-driven hands and can be extended to other complex robotic systems.
- **Human-Robot Interaction:** The hand's anthropomorphic design and dexterity make it well-suited for applications involving close interaction with humans and human environments.
- **Applications:**
 - **Teleoperation:** Enables remote operation of robots in various scenarios, such as disaster response or surgery.
 - **Dexterous manipulation:** Facilitates the development of robots capable of performing intricate tasks, such as assembly, packaging, and healthcare.
 - **Imitation learning:** Providing a platform for training robots from human demonstrations.
- **Future directions:**
 - **Tactile sensing:** Improve performance in complex dexterous tasks by providing feedback for more precise and adaptive manipulation.
 - **Alternative motion capture techniques:** Improve accessibility by exploring methods beyond the Manus glove.
 - **MCP Abduction:** Incorporate this into the design to enable dynamic tasks.
