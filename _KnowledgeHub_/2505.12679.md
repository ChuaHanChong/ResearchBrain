# Dribble Master: Learning Agile Humanoid Dribbling through Legged Locomotion

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Tsinghua University developed NVDS+^{+}+, a neural framework that stabilizes flickering depth maps from single-image models, achieving hundreds of times faster inference than test-time methods while improving accuracy and temporal consistency. This framework is supported by VDW, a large-scale and diverse video depth dataset for natural scenes.

## Problem

- Humanoid robots face significant challenges in achieving agile, continuous, and robust dribbling due to the need for dexterous ball manipulation, dynamic balance, and complex robot-ball interactions.
- Real-world ball perception for humanoids is often discontinuous and inaccurate due to camera limitations and occlusions, making continuous visual tracking difficult.
- Existing methods, including rule-based and prior learning-based approaches, struggle with adapting to dynamic ball behavior, generalizing across terrains, and effective sim-to-real transfer for full-sized humanoids.

## Method

- A two-stage curriculum reinforcement learning framework, built on PPO, was developed to progressively acquire skills, starting with basic locomotion and ball chasing before moving to fine-grained ball manipulation.
- A virtual camera model was implemented in simulation with active sensing mechanisms, encouraging the robot to tactically move its head to maintain ball visibility and reacquire it if lost.
- Extensive sim-to-real transfer techniques, including domain randomization of physical parameters, training on rough terrains, and simulating motor delays and perception inaccuracies, were employed to enhance robustness.

## Results

- The method achieved an **86.7%** success rate in the "Dribbling to Target" task and a **93.3%** success rate in the "Obstacle Avoidance" task on a physical Booster T1 humanoid robot, significantly outperforming ablated baselines and tele-operation.
- In simulation, the system demonstrated precise ball velocity tracking, with average direction change errors of 2-**3%** for 45 and 90-degree turns and a speed tracking error of approximately **10%** for a 1 m/s target.
- The learned policy showcased strong generalization, successfully deploying on different bipedal robot platforms in simulation and performing robustly on a physical robot across diverse real-world terrains including artificial grass, potholed ground, and asphalt.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A progressive two-stage curriculum effectively enables reinforcement learning to acquire complex skills like humanoid dribbling by addressing conflicting objectives of stable locomotion and agile manipulation separately.
> - Integrating a realistic virtual camera model and active sensing within the learning framework is crucial for overcoming challenges posed by discontinuous visual perception in dynamic real-world tasks.
> - Robust sim-to-real transfer, achieved through comprehensive domain randomization and simulation of real-world constraints, allows policies to generalize effectively across diverse terrains and different humanoid hardware.
