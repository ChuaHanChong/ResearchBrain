# Robotic Visual Instruction

> [!summary] Summary
> Li et al. introduce Robotic Visual Instruction (RoVI), an object-centric, hand-drawn symbolic language, and the Visual Instruction Embodied Workflow (VIEW) pipeline to guide robotic tasks. This approach enables robots to interpret precise 2D visual instructions and translate them into 3D actions, achieving an 87.5% success rate on unseen real-world manipulation tasks, surpassing language-conditioned baselines.

## Problem

- Natural language instructions for robots often lack spatial precision, leading to ambiguity, verbosity, and unsuitability in quiet environments.
- Existing visual instruction methods, such as goal images or full trajectories, are often impractical or unintuitive for users to provide.
- There is a need for a communication method that balances user-friendliness, interpretability, and direct spatio-temporal alignment for robotic manipulation tasks.

## Method

- Researchers designed **Robotic Visual Instruction** (RoVI), a hand-drawn symbolic language using object-centric primitives (arrows, circles) augmented with colors and numbers to encode spatial and temporal information.
- ==The RoVI Book== dataset, comprising 15,000 image-text question-answer pairs, was curated to fine-tune Vision-Language Models (VLMs) for robust RoVI comprehension, enabling learning with minimal computational resources.
- The Visual Instruction Embodied Workflow (VIEW) pipeline was developed, consisting of a VLM for high-level planning, a specialized keypoint module to extract 2D spatial-temporal constraints, and a keypoint-conditioned low-level policy to execute 3D actions.

## Results

- The VIEW pipeline, particularly with LLaVA-13B fine-tuned on RoVI Book, achieved an **87.5%** success rate across 8 unseen real-world tasks, outperforming language-conditioned policies like Voxposer (**43.8%**) and CoPa (**45%**).
- In challenging simulated cluttered environments, VIEW reached a **76.6%** success rate, significantly outperforming end-to-end Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models such as RT-1-X (**20%**) and Octo-language (**3%**).
- A specialized YOLOv8-based keypoint module achieved a mean average precision (mAP) of **1.00** for RoVI symbol detection and mean distance errors of **6.83** pixels, demonstrating superior performance over general open-vocabulary detectors.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Object-centric, hand-drawn visual symbols offer a precise, intuitive, and silent alternative to natural language and existing visual modalities for robotic instruction, effectively conveying both path and end-state information.
> - Specialized VLM fine-tuning on a curated dataset like RoVI Book, coupled with a dedicated keypoint extraction module, is crucial for robust interpretation and execution of novel visual instructions by robots.
> - A structured "Geometric Style" for drawing RoVI symbols significantly improves VLM comprehension and overall task performance compared to a casual "Loose Style".
