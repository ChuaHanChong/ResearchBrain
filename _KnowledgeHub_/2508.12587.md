# Multimodal Chain of Continuous Thought for Latent-Space Reasoning in Vision-Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Harvard Medical School and Knovel Engineering Lab introduced MCOUT, a framework that enables Vision-Language Models to perform iterative reasoning directly in a continuous latent space. This approach improved multimodal reasoning accuracy by up to 8.23% on MMMU and 4.79% on ScienceQA, with a 1B-parameter model often outperforming larger VLM baselines.

## Problem

- Traditional Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting, designed for text-only models, struggles with dynamically aligning and integrating heterogeneous visual and textual information in multimodal contexts.
- Current VLM reasoning methods often rely on discrete token sequences and static vision features, leading to computational inefficiency and hindering truly dynamic cross-modal interaction.
- Existing VLM approaches lack a continuous, iterative refinement process in a unified latent space, unlike human reflective cognition where thoughts are continuously validated against sensory inputs.

## Method

- MCOUT introduces a framework for iterative reasoning directly within a VLM's ==continuous latent space==, refining ==thought embeddings== rather than generating discrete language tokens.
- Two variants are proposed: ==MCOUT-Base== directly reuses the language model's last hidden state as the continuous thought, while ==MCOUT-Multi== integrates multimodal latent attention to explicitly combine the hidden state with visual embeddings.
- The reasoning process involves N_t iterative steps where each newly generated thought embedding is appended to the input sequence and fed back into the language model for further refinement, guided by an ==auxiliary loss on intermediate thoughts==.

## Results

- MCOUT-Base achieved **58.86%** accuracy on ScienceQA, representing a **4.79%** increase over the baseline, and **27.53%** accuracy on MMMU, an **8.21%** increase.
- The **1B**-parameter MCOUT variants consistently outperformed several larger models (e.g., LLaVA-7B/13B, MiniGPT-4-13B, Kosmos-2) on ScienceQA, MMMU, and MMStar benchmarks.
- An auxiliary weight of **0.3** for intermediate thought losses yielded the highest performance gains, and full finetuning of both VLM components with LoRA provided additional performance boosts across benchmarks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Continuous latent reasoning provides a robust and efficient paradigm for enhancing VLM performance, demonstrating that iterative refinement in a hidden space can be more effective than language-bound discrete CoT.
> - The 1B-parameter MCOUT models achieved strong performance, frequently matching or exceeding much larger state-of-the-art VLMs, highlighting the efficiency and scalability benefits of latent continuous thought.
> - Analysis revealed a 'modality collapse' in MCOUT-Multi, where the multimodal attention mechanism, despite normalization, provided a static, low-variance contribution from visual inputs, suggesting challenges in truly integrating diverse visual context for continuous reasoning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Vision-language models (VLMs) have revolutionized multimodal tasks such as visual question answering (VQA), image captioning, and complex reasoning, by integrating visual and textual data. Despite their success, achieving robust and efficient reasoning in VLMs remains a significant challenge. Current reasoning techniques, particularly those adapted from large language models (LLMs) like Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting, are often suboptimal for multimodal contexts.

The research landscape can be broadly categorized into several areas:

- **Discrete Token-based Reasoning (CoT and variants):** Initially popularized by Wei et al., CoT prompting has been highly effective in LLMs by generating explicit, intermediate reasoning steps in natural language (e.g., for arithmetic or logical tasks). Variants like Self-consistency, Tree of Thoughts (ToT), and Graph of Thoughts (GoT) have further refined this approach. While adapted for VLMs (e.g., Multimodal CoT), these methods struggle with two key limitations in multimodal settings:
 1. **Dynamic Cross-Modal Alignment:** They often rely on discrete text tokens and static vision features, making it difficult to dynamically align and integrate heterogeneous visual and textual information throughout the reasoning process.
 2. **Computational Inefficiency:** Generating verbose natural language sequences for reasoning steps incurs significant computational overhead and token usage.

- **Training Techniques (Reinforcement Learning):** Approaches like InstructGPT and Group relative policy Optimization (GRPO) use reinforcement learning (RL) to fine-tune LLMs, improving reasoning and alignment with human feedback. RL has also been applied to align visual and textual features in VLMs. However, these methods often depend on static embeddings, which limits their capacity for dynamic and iterative reasoning.

- **Emerging Latent Reasoning Paradigms:** To overcome the limitations of discrete, token-based approaches, a newer paradigm has emerged, shifting reasoning from explicit token sequences to continuous latent spaces. For LLMs, pioneering works like COCONUT demonstrated the efficiency of using the last hidden state as a "continuous thought" for iterative refinement, reducing token overhead. Other efforts like Latent Reasoning Skills (LaRS) and recurrent depth approaches further explored latent representations for faster and more scalable reasoning in text-only contexts.

- **Latent Reasoning in VLMs:** While nascent, the application of latent reasoning to VLMs is gaining traction. Researchers have explored diffusion processes for text-image aligned latent spaces, multimodal diffusion models for coherent generation and reasoning, augmenting VLMs with latent visual tokens, and stacked latent attention. However, many of these still implicitly or explicitly rely on discrete token-based reasoning or static vision features, limiting truly dynamic and continuous cross-modal interaction. Challenges like "modality collapse" where attention mechanisms fail to properly integrate multimodal information, or "entropy collapse" in transformer training, further highlight the difficulties in this domain.

This paper, by proposing MCOUT, positions itself as a pioneering effort to bridge the efficiency and scalability of latent continuous reasoning, inspired by COCONUT, with the inherent complexity of multimodal inputs in VLMs. It directly addresses the shortcomings of language-bound CoT by enabling reasoning to occur in a joint latent space, thus aiming for more dynamic cross-modal alignment and reduced computational overhead, especially for image-based tasks. The work attempts to tackle fundamental challenges in multimodal reasoning by drawing inspiration from human reflective cognition, where thoughts are iteratively refined and validated against sensory inputs.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core motivation behind the Multimodal Chain of Continuous Thought (MCOUT) framework stems from the observed limitations of existing reasoning techniques in vision-language models (VLMs), particularly those adapted from large language models (LLMs). The authors identify several critical issues:

- **Suboptimality of Language-Bound CoT in Multimodal Contexts:** Traditional Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting, while effective for text-only LLMs, struggles to seamlessly integrate and dynamically align disparate modalities (e.g., visual, textual, audio). Its reliance on discrete word sequences for expressing reasoning steps leads to a disconnect when processing continuous and rich visual information.
- **Computational Inefficiency and Static Feature Reliance:** Current VLM reasoning methods often use discrete token sequences for intermediate thoughts and rely on static vision features. This results in high computational overhead, especially for complex, multi-step reasoning, and hinders the dynamic interaction required for fine-grained multimodal understanding. The inability to dynamically revisit and validate thoughts against input data, a characteristic of human cognition, is a significant limitation.
- **Lack of Reflective Reasoning:** Human reasoning is inherently iterative and reflective. We generate intermediate thoughts, validate them against available evidence (e.g., re-examining an image or text), and refine our understanding. Existing VLM approaches often lack this continuous, iterative refinement process in a unified latent space.

Given these challenges, the **key objectives** of this work are:

1. **Propose an Alternative Reasoning Paradigm:** Develop a novel framework, MCOUT, that enables reasoning directly within a joint latent space, moving beyond natural language-bound reasoning.
2. **Mimic Human Reflective Cognition:** Design MCOUT to represent the reasoning state as a continuous hidden vector that is iteratively refined and aligned with multimodal (visual and textual) embeddings, inspired by how humans reflectively validate thoughts against input data.
3. **Enhance Cross-Modal Alignment:** Improve the dynamic alignment between visual and textual information during the reasoning process, which is a major weakness of token-based CoT methods in multimodal settings.
4. **Reduce Computational Overhead:** Achieve reasoning with greater efficiency by operating in a continuous latent space, thereby avoiding the generation of verbose token sequences.
5. **Demonstrate Performance Gains:** Validate the effectiveness of MCOUT on diverse and challenging multimodal reasoning benchmarks, showing measurable improvements in accuracy and qualitative metrics over strong baselines, especially with a focus on smaller VLMs.
6. **Explore Architectural Variants:** Investigate two distinct approaches within MCOUT—MCOUT-Base and MCOUT-Multi—to understand the impact of directly reusing the language model's hidden state versus integrating explicit multimodal latent attention.

In essence, the authors are motivated to develop a more biologically plausible, computationally efficient, and dynamically interactive reasoning framework for VLMs that can truly leverage the richness of multimodal data, rather than being constrained by a language-first approach.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The Multimodal Chain of Continuous Thought (MCOUT) framework introduces a novel paradigm for reasoning in Vision-Language Models (VLMs) by performing iterative refinements directly in a continuous latent space. This approach is inspired by human reflective cognition, where thoughts are continuously generated, validated, and refined against incoming sensory data.

#### 3.1. Model Architecture

MCOUT is built upon a standard VLM architecture, specifically using **SilVar** as its base. This VLM comprises two main components:

- **Visual Encoder (V):** A pre-trained **CLIP** model is used to process input images (x_v ∈ R^(H×W×C)) into visual embeddings (e_v ∈ R^(S_v ×D)), where S_v is the sequence length of visual tokens and D is the embedding dimension.
- **Language Model (L):** **Llama 3.2 1B** serves as the language model, processing tokenized text inputs (x_t) to generate contextual embeddings (e_t ∈ R^(S_t ×D)). The authors explicitly state their choice of a small (1B parameter) Llama model to focus on latent reasoning for smaller VLMs, emphasizing efficiency and scalability.

The key innovation lies in how MCOUT generates and refines "thought embeddings" for iterative reasoning. Two variants are proposed:

- **MCOUT-Base:** This variant simplifies the thought generation process by directly reusing the language model's last hidden state (h_l ∈ R^(B×D)) as the continuous thought embedding (h_t). It relies entirely on the LM's internal state to encapsulate and refine the reasoning state.
 - `h_t = h_l`

- **MCOUT-Multi:** This variant enhances cross-modal alignment by integrating the language model's last hidden state (h_l) with multimodal input embeddings (e_m, typically visual embeddings e_v for image-based tasks). This integration occurs via a **multimodal latent attention module**:
 1. The last hidden state (h_l) is projected into a query space.
 2. Multi-head attention (with N_h = 8 heads) is applied, attending to the multimodal input embeddings (e_m).
 3. The output is normalized and projected back to produce the thought embedding (h_t ∈ R^(B×1×D)).
 - `h_t = Norm(Proj_back(MultiHeadAttn(Proj(h_l), e_m⊤)))`
 This module aims to enrich the continuous thought with explicit visual context, enabling stronger cross-modal alignment.

#### 3.2. Multimodal Latent Reasoning Process

The MCOUT framework executes reasoning through an iterative process of generating and refining continuous thought representations in the latent space.

1. **Initial Input Processing:** Preprocessed, interleaved input embeddings (e_inter, containing both visual and textual features) along with an attention mask (m) are fed into the language model (L) to compute initial hidden states (h).
 - `h = L(e_inter, m)`
2. **Extracting Last Hidden State:** The last hidden state (h_l) corresponding to the last non-padded token is extracted from `h`. This `h_l` represents the initial "thought" or current reasoning state.
3. **Iterative Thought Generation (N_t steps):** For a predefined number of latent reasoning steps (N_t), MCOUT iteratively produces refined thought embeddings (h_t^(k)):
 - **MCOUT-Base:** The previous iteration's last hidden state (h_l^(k-1)) directly becomes the current thought embedding (h_t^(k)).
 - `h_t^(k) = h_l^(k-1)`
 - **MCOUT-Multi:** The multimodal latent attention module processes the previous last hidden state (h_l^(k-1)) and the multimodal input embeddings (e_m) to generate the current thought embedding (h_t^(k)), enriching it with visual context.
 - `h_t^(k) = MultimodalLatentAttention(h_l^(k-1), e_m)`
4. **Sequence Augmentation:** Each newly generated thought embedding (h_t^(k)) is appended to the current input sequence (`e_inter^(k-1)`) and the attention mask (`m^(k-1)`). This augmented sequence effectively incorporates the continuous thought into the model's input for the next iteration.
 - `e_inter^(k) = [e_inter^(k-1), h_t^(k)]`
 - `m^(k) = [m^(k-1), 1_B×1]`
5. **Feedback Loop:** The updated sequence (`e_inter^(k)`) is fed back into the language model (L) to compute new hidden states, and the process repeats for N_t iterations.
6. **Final Output Generation:** After N_t iterations, in the final step (k = N_t + 1), the language model generates the final output sequence (x_a) using a standard generation process.

#### 3.3. Loss Function and Training

The training objective incorporates both intermediate thought supervision and final output prediction:

- **Total Loss:** `L_total = Σ (μ * L_aux^(k)) + L_final`
 - `L_aux^(k)` is the language modeling loss (cross-entropy) for the k-th intermediate thought, encouraging coherence in the continuous reasoning steps.
 - `L_final` is the cross-entropy loss for the final generated output.
 - `μ` is an auxiliary weight that balances the contribution of intermediate thought losses.

**Training Details:**

- **Datasets:** Pretrained on VQAv2, fine-tuned on ScienceQA and MMMU, and tested on MMStar.
- **Computational Setup:** 8-bit precision, vision model frozen, LoRA (rank 64, alpha 16) for efficient adaptation of the LM. Trained on a single CUDA device with a batch size of 4.
- **Hyperparameters:** Number of latent thoughts (N_t) experimented with 5 and 10. Temperature for inference set to 0.1. Auxiliary weight (μ) was subjected to an ablation study (0, 0.3, 0.5, 0.8). Full finetuning of both VLM components with LoRA was also explored.
- **Norm Balancing:** An important detail revealed in the analysis section is the initial norm imbalance between the LM's last hidden state and the multimodal attention's initial thought embedding. Normalization layers were introduced in the attention module (Equation 1) to align scales and stabilize thought embeddings, mitigating risks of unstable fusion.

This methodology provides a comprehensive framework for continuous, iterative reasoning in VLMs, offering an alternative to discrete, language-bound approaches and aiming for more dynamic and efficient multimodal intelligence.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of the MCOUT framework was conducted across four diverse vision-language datasets: VQAv2 (pretraining), MMMU (fine-tuning/validation), ScienceQA (fine-tuning/test), and MMStar (testing), utilizing accuracy and BLEU scores as primary metrics. The experiments compared MCOUT-Base and MCOUT-Multi against a baseline VLM (without latent reasoning) and various state-of-the-art larger models from the literature.

#### 4.1. Performance on Benchmarks

- **ScienceQA (Table 1):**
 - MCOUT-Base with N_t=10 achieved the highest accuracy at 58.86%, representing a significant 4.79% increase over the baseline.
 - MCOUT-Multi with N_t=5 demonstrated the best BLEU score at 52.60, an improvement of 2.18% over the baseline, suggesting its strength in generating coherent textual explanations, especially in image-rich scientific reasoning tasks.
 - Notably, the 1B-parameter MCOUT variants consistently outperformed several much larger models, including Kosmos-2 (1.7B, 32.70%), LLaVA-7B/13B (41.10%–47.74%), and MiniGPT-4-13B (47.71%). They also closely matched or slightly exceeded InstructBLIP-7B (8B, 54.10%) and LLaMA-2-13B (55.78%), demonstrating remarkable efficiency for a small VLM.

- **MMMU (Table 2):**
 - MCOUT-Base with N_t=5 yielded the highest gains, with accuracy reaching 27.53% (↑ 8.21%) and BLEU at 27.54 (↑ 8.31%) compared to the baseline.
 - MCOUT-Multi with N_t=10 also showed strong improvements (7.54% accuracy, 7.58% BLEU).
 - Again, the 1B-parameter MCOUT models outperformed Kosmos-2 and MiniGPT-4 variants and were competitive with larger models like OpenFlamingo-9B and Qwen-VL, underscoring MCOUT’s effectiveness in complex, college-level multimodal reasoning.

- **MMStar (Table 3):**
 - MCOUT-Base with N_t=10 improved both accuracy and BLEU by 3.98% over the baseline (26.13% accuracy, 26.14 BLEU).
 - MCOUT-Multi with N_t=10 showed similar gains of 3.74%.
 - MCOUT outperformed Kosmos-2, MiniGPT-4 variants, and PandaGPT-13B, and closely rivaled OpenFlamingo-9B and LLaVA-7B, indicating its strong capability in challenging fine-grained visual reasoning tasks despite its small size.

#### 4.2. Multimodal Latent Reasoning Analysis (Figure 3)

The latent distribution analysis provided crucial insights into the internal workings and comparative performance of MCOUT-Base and MCOUT-Multi:

- **Norm Imbalance Mitigation:** Prior to training, a significant norm imbalance was identified between the language model’s last hidden state (103.90) and the initial thought embedding from the multimodal attention module (26.48). This was successfully mitigated by introducing normalization layers within the attention module, aligning the scales and stabilizing thought embeddings, thus preventing unstable fusion in MCOUT-Multi.

- **MCOUT-Base Stability:** For MCOUT-Base, the mean of the last hidden layer remained stable around -0.02197, with a consistent standard deviation of approximately 2.23 across iterations. This stability reflects a robust and effective reasoning process, underpinning its substantial performance gains.

- **MCOUT-Multi's Modality Collapse:** While MCOUT-Multi's last hidden layer means and standard deviations closely tracked MCOUT-Base, indicating minimal disruption from multimodal fusion, a critical limitation was observed in its "mixed embeddings." These embeddings, derived from the multimodal latent attention, exhibited a constant mean (0.002418) and a negligible standard deviation (0.001866), with a uniformly low mixed standard deviation (0.198925) across all iterations. This "static, low-variance contribution from the multimodal input" suggests a **modality collapse** or **entropy collapse**. Despite the normalization to prevent catastrophic failure, the multimodal attention mechanism failed to extract diverse visual context, causing MCOUT-Multi's performance to closely align with MCOUT-Base. This finding resonates with recent studies on visual attention sink and entropy collapse, where activation imbalances favor certain modalities (e.g., text) over others.

#### 4.3. Ablation Study (Table 4)

- **Impact of Auxiliary Weight (μ):**
 - An auxiliary weight of `μ = 0.3` for the intermediate thought losses yielded the highest performance, leading to 4.33% accuracy gains on ScienceQA and 8.23% on MMMU (for MCOUT-Base, N_t=5). This highlights the importance of an appropriate balance in supervising intermediate reasoning steps.
 - Higher `μ` values (0.5, 0.8) resulted in reduced gains, suggesting that overemphasizing intermediate thoughts might disrupt the optimization of the final output.
 - Even with `μ = 0` (no auxiliary loss), moderate improvements were observed, indicating the inherent benefit of iterative continuous thought.

- **Full Finetuning:**
 - Fully finetuning both the vision encoder and language model with LoRA (instead of freezing the vision model) significantly boosted overall performance for both the baseline and MCOUT variants, with improvements ranging from 3.06% to 5.88% across benchmarks.
 - The performance gap between MCOUT-Base and MCOUT-Multi remained small even with full finetuning, further confirming the robustness of the MCOUT framework under different optimization settings and the persistent challenge of effective multimodal fusion in MCOUT-Multi.

In summary, MCOUT consistently and significantly improves multimodal reasoning, often outperforming much larger models, especially with the base variant. However, the advanced MCOUT-Multi variant, despite its design for enhanced multimodal fusion, encounters "modality collapse," limiting its superior performance and providing critical insights into current VLM challenges.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research presented in this paper, "Multimodal Chain of Continuous Thought for Latent-Space Reasoning in Vision-Language Models," marks a significant advancement in the field of multimodal AI, particularly for Vision-Language Models (VLMs). Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Pioneering Latent Continuous Reasoning for VLMs:** This work is a pioneering effort to explore and implement a continuous latent reasoning framework for VLMs. By moving beyond the discrete, language-bound nature of traditional Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting, MCOUT offers an alternative paradigm that more closely aligns with human reflective cognition. This shift to a continuous latent space represents a fundamental change in how VLMs can process and integrate multimodal information, laying a promising foundation for future research.

- **Enhanced Efficiency and Scalability:** MCOUT demonstrates notable efficiency gains. It achieves significant performance improvements (up to 8.23% accuracy gains on MMMU and 4.79% on ScienceQA) using a relatively small 1B-parameter VLM. This is crucial for applications where computational resources are constrained, such as edge devices, mobile platforms, or specialized domains like medical imaging. The ability to outperform or rival much larger models (e.g., LLaVA-7B/13B, MiniGPT-4-13B, Kosmos-2) with a smaller footprint highlights MCOUT's potential for developing scalable and deployable multimodal AI solutions.

- **Improved Multimodal Reasoning Capabilities:** The consistent performance improvements across diverse and challenging benchmarks (ScienceQA, MMMU, MMStar) affirm MCOUT's ability to bolster multimodal reasoning. The iterative refinement of continuous thought embeddings allows the model to delve deeper into complex reasoning tasks, leading to more accurate and coherent outputs, as evidenced by improved accuracy and BLEU scores.

- **Valuable Insights into Modality Collapse:** The detailed latent distribution analysis, particularly the observation of "modality collapse" in MCOUT-Multi's mixed embeddings, provides a critical contribution to the VLM research community. Identifying that the multimodal attention mechanism, despite normalization, struggles to extract diverse visual context (resulting in static, low-variance contributions) offers a tangible explanation for why advanced multimodal fusion mechanisms sometimes fail to yield expected superior performance. This insight is vital for understanding current limitations in multimodal AI and guides future research directions in designing more robust and truly interactive cross-modal attention mechanisms. It emphasizes the need to address issues like activation imbalances and entropy collapse in multimodal training.

- **Inspiration for Human-like Reflective Inference:** MCOUT’s design, inspired by human reflective cognition, offers a more intuitive and potentially more powerful approach to reasoning. The iterative validation and refinement of thoughts against input data are key aspects of human intelligence, and MCOUT's continuous latent space framework provides a scalable model for approximating this in AI systems. This could lead to VLMs that not only answer questions but also "understand" and "deliberate" in a more human-like manner.

- **Future Research Directions:** The paper explicitly points to future work, particularly investigating more effective multimodal attention mechanisms and alternative methods for multimodal alignment within continuous latent reasoning to overcome the observed modality collapse. This open-endedness fosters further exploration and innovation in the field, building directly on the strengths and identified weaknesses of MCOUT.

In summary, MCOUT represents a significant step towards more efficient, robust, and reflectively intelligent VLMs. Its practical benefits for smaller models, coupled with its theoretical contributions regarding continuous latent reasoning and insights into modality collapse, position it as an impactful contribution that will likely influence the development of next-generation multimodal AI systems across various application domains.
