# Belief-Based Offline Reinforcement Learning for Delay-Robust Policy Optimization

> [!summary] Summary
> DT-CORL introduces a belief-based offline reinforcement learning framework that enables training policies robust to observation and actuation delays using only static, delay-free datasets. The approach jointly optimizes a transformer-based belief model with a constrained policy, achieving superior performance on delayed environments across D4RL locomotion, AntMaze, and Adroit manipulation tasks compared to augmentation-based and decoupled belief-based baselines.

## Problem

- Autonomous systems frequently encounter observation and actuation delays, violating the Markov assumption and hindering policy deployment in real-world scenarios.
- Existing online Delayed RL methods are often sample-inefficient or prone to compounding errors when adapted to offline settings, which struggle with out-of-distribution actions.
- A key challenge is learning policies robust to delays solely from delay-free offline data, to bridge the sim-to-real gap without expensive online interaction with delayed systems.

## Method

- **DT-CORL** (Delay-aware Transformer-belief Constrained Offline RL) proposes a ==belief-based policy iteration== framework that maps the delayed MDP back to the original state space via belief estimation.
- A transformer architecture is used to learn the belief function, predicting the current delay-compensated state from a history of past observations and actions.
- The policy is optimized jointly with the belief model, using a constrained offline RL objective that regularizes the learned policy towards the dataset's augmented behavior policy.

## Results

- DT-CORL consistently outperformed augmentation-based and decoupled belief-based baselines across D4RL MuJoCo locomotion, AntMaze, and high-dimensional Adroit manipulation tasks.
- The method demonstrated greater robustness to increasing delay lengths (e.g., Δ=4 to **16**) and performed more consistently under stochastic delay distributions compared to baselines.
- Ablation studies confirmed that joint training of the belief model and policy, along with the transformer architecture, were critical for achieving higher returns and stable performance in delayed environments.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Directly augmenting state for delays leads to high dimensionality and distribution mismatch; a belief-based mapping back to the original state space is more efficient.
> - Jointly optimizing the belief model and policy is crucial to mitigate compounding prediction errors and distribution mismatch, outperforming decoupled, two-stage approaches.
> - Transformer architectures effectively learn temporal dependencies in augmented state histories, providing accurate and stable multi-step state predictions for robust delay compensation.
