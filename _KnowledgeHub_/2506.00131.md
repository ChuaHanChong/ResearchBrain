# Belief-Based Offline Reinforcement Learning for Delay-Robust Policy Optimization

> [!summary] Summary
> DT-CORL introduces a belief-based offline reinforcement learning framework that enables training policies robust to observation and actuation delays using only static, delay-free datasets. The approach jointly optimizes a transformer-based belief model with a constrained policy, achieving superior performance on delayed environments across D4RL locomotion, AntMaze, and Adroit manipulation tasks compared to augmentation-based and decoupled belief-based baselines.

## Problem

- Autonomous systems frequently encounter observation and actuation delays, violating the Markov assumption and hindering policy deployment in real-world scenarios.
- Existing online Delayed RL methods are often sample-inefficient or prone to compounding errors when adapted to offline settings, which struggle with out-of-distribution actions.
- A key challenge is learning policies robust to delays solely from delay-free offline data, to bridge the sim-to-real gap without expensive online interaction with delayed systems.

## Method

- **DT-CORL** (Delay-aware Transformer-belief Constrained Offline RL) proposes a ==belief-based policy iteration== framework that maps the delayed MDP back to the original state space via belief estimation.
- A transformer architecture is used to learn the belief function, predicting the current delay-compensated state from a history of past observations and actions.
- The policy is optimized jointly with the belief model, using a constrained offline RL objective that regularizes the learned policy towards the dataset's augmented behavior policy.

## Results

- DT-CORL consistently outperformed augmentation-based and decoupled belief-based baselines across D4RL MuJoCo locomotion, AntMaze, and high-dimensional Adroit manipulation tasks.
- The method demonstrated greater robustness to increasing delay lengths (e.g., Δ=4 to **16**) and performed more consistently under stochastic delay distributions compared to baselines.
- Ablation studies confirmed that joint training of the belief model and policy, along with the transformer architecture, were critical for achieving higher returns and stable performance in delayed environments.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Directly augmenting state for delays leads to high dimensionality and distribution mismatch; a belief-based mapping back to the original state space is more efficient.
> - Jointly optimizing the belief model and policy is crucial to mitigate compounding prediction errors and distribution mismatch, outperforming decoupled, two-stage approaches.
> - Transformer architectures effectively learn temporal dependencies in augmented state histories, providing accurate and stable multi-step state predictions for robust delay compensation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research addresses a convergence of challenges within reinforcement learning (RL) that are critical for deploying autonomous agents in real-world scenarios. It primarily sits at the intersection of **Offline Reinforcement Learning (Offline RL)** and **Delayed Reinforcement Learning (Delayed RL)**, with implications for **Sim-to-Real Transfer**.

Real-world autonomous systems frequently encounter observation and actuation delays due to computational or communication latencies, which fundamentally violate the Markov assumption central to standard RL formulations. This issue is the domain of Delayed RL. Existing approaches to Delayed RL typically fall into two categories:
1. **State augmentation methods**: These extend the state representation with past observations and actions to restore the Markov property. While conceptually simple, they suffer from severe sample inefficiency and scalability issues as the delay (and thus the augmented state dimension) increases.
2. **Belief-state methods**: These compress historical information into a compact latent belief state, which acts as a proxy for the unobservable true state. While more sample-efficient, they are susceptible to compounding prediction errors and distribution mismatch during deployment. Critically, most existing Delayed RL methods assume online interaction with a delayed environment for training.

Concurrently, Offline RL aims to train effective policies from static, pre-collected datasets without further environment interaction. This is appealing for safety-critical or data-costly applications. However, offline policies often struggle with out-of-distribution (OOD) actions, leading to unstable value estimates and suboptimal performance if the learned policy deviates significantly from the data-collecting behavior policy.

The specific challenge addressed by this paper is learning delay-robust policies *offline*, using only datasets collected from *delay-free* environments (e.g., simulators or idealized hardware logs), for deployment in *delayed* real-world environments. This problem is less explored, as prior work generally assumes either online interaction in delayed settings or offline learning in delay-free settings, but rarely the combination. The paper draws an analogy to classic control theory techniques like the Smith Predictor, which leverage delay-free models for robust operation under delayed dynamics, suggesting a similar principle for RL.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this work is to develop a framework capable of training a robust policy *offline*, utilizing only pre-collected, *delay-free* trajectories, such that the policy performs reliably when executed in a *delayed* real-world environment. This objective is driven by two fundamental deployment challenges:

1. **The Sim-to-Real Gap**: Real-world systems inherently exhibit physical imperfections, such as latency, that are often absent or simplified in simulators used for data collection. Policies trained in idealized, delay-free simulations may catastrophically fail when deployed in environments with realistic delays. Bridging this gap without expensive or risky real-world data collection under delayed conditions is a critical need.
2. **The Interaction Gap / Cost of Online Data**: Collecting online interaction data in delayed, potentially safety-critical environments is often impractical, costly, or unsafe. Many systems, however, possess large datasets from delay-free simulations or legacy controllers. The motivation is to leverage these abundant delay-free resources to train policies robust to delays without requiring any online interaction with the delayed system.

The paper identifies specific deficiencies in current approaches that motivate its proposed solution:
- **Limitations of existing Delayed RL methods**: These methods largely rely on online interaction, which is precisely what offline RL seeks to avoid. When adapted to an offline setting, state augmentation leads to sample inefficiency and dimensional blow-up due to the expanded state space. Belief-state methods, if trained separately, are prone to compounding errors and distribution mismatch because the belief model is "frozen" and cannot adapt to policy shifts during deployment.
- **Limitations of existing Offline RL methods**: While offline RL addresses the data scarcity problem, it struggles with OOD generalization. Combining this with delay handling exacerbates these issues, as naive augmentation can create artificial delay distributions that are poorly covered by static data.

Therefore, the key motivation is to devise an offline RL framework that can:
- Effectively infer latent states from delayed observations, even when trained on delay-free data.
- Mitigate out-of-distribution errors typical in offline RL.
- Achieve robust performance in environments with significant observation and actuation delays (both fixed and stochastic).
- Avoid the computational and data inefficiencies of naive state augmentation.
- Overcome the compounding errors and distribution mismatch of decoupled belief-based approaches.

Achieving these aims promises safer development cycles, reduced sample complexity, and smoother sim-to-real transfer for latency-sensitive robotic and autonomous systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, **DT-CORL (Delay-aware Transformer-belief Constrained Offline RL)**, is a novel belief-based framework designed to learn delay-robust policies from static, delay-free offline data. The approach integrates a transformer-based belief model with a constrained policy optimization objective within an offline RL setting.

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation

The problem is initially formulated as a finite-horizon Markov Decision Process (MDP) for delay-free environments. For delayed scenarios, the problem is reformulated as a Delayed MDP by augmenting the state space `X` with a history of past actions and observations `x_t = {s_{t-Δ}, a_{t-Δ}..., a_{t-1}}`. The objective is to maximize the expected return in this delayed MDP.

To address OOD issues inherent in offline RL, a policy-constrained learning objective is adopted, similar to BRAC. This involves a policy evaluation step (Equation 1) and a policy improvement step (Equation 2), where the learned policy `π_Δ` is regularized towards the dataset's behavior policy `μ_Δ` (lifted to the augmented space) by bounding a divergence measure `D(π_Δ, μ_Δ)`.

#### 3.2. Belief-Based Policy Iteration

DT-CORL departs from directly optimizing in the augmented state space, instead proposing a **belief-based policy iteration framework**. The motivation for this shift stems from three drawbacks of the augmented-state approach:
1. **High dimensionality**: The state dimension grows significantly with delay `Δ`, increasing sample complexity.
2. **Distribution mismatch**: Augmented trajectories from delay-free data may not accurately reflect true delayed online trajectories.
3. **Loss of temporal information**: Treating action histories as distinct augmented state inputs might obscure inherent temporal relationships.

DT-CORL aims to cast the augmented state space problem back into the original state space via belief estimation. The constrained optimization problem (Equations 1 and 2) is first converted into an unconstrained formulation with regularization constants `α_1` and `α_2`. The core of the belief-based approach is to map the delayed policy `π_Δ` and its Q-function `Q_Δ` back to their delay-free counterparts `π` and `Q_π` using a belief distribution `b_Δ(s|x)`.

Lemmas 4.1 and 4.2 provide bounds for performance and Q-value differences between policies in delayed and belief-induced state spaces, quantifying the relationships needed for this mapping. This leads to a new policy-iteration framework (Equations 3 and 4) operating in the original state space, where `λ_1` and `λ_2` are constants and `μ_Δ` is the behavior policy after data augmentation. Proposition 4.3 theoretically demonstrates that this iterative application monotonically increases the Q-value after each update, ensuring convergence.

A key aspect highlighted is the superiority of this integrated approach over a two-stage pipeline (pre-train belief model, then freeze and apply offline RL). DT-CORL avoids:
- **Biased value targets**: The Bellman target in DT-CORL is computed through `b_Δ(·|x)`, ensuring the critic learns on states the policy will encounter, unlike a frozen belief model which introduces unmodeled noise.
- **Distribution mismatch**: Joint training keeps the critic, policy, and belief model aligned, preventing divergence between training and deployment distributions.
- **Inferior sample efficiency in stochastic MDPs**: DT-CORL reuses transitions to generate multiple latent states, providing richer supervision.

#### 3.3. Practical Implementation

1. **Belief Prediction**: A transformer architecture (Vaswani et al., 2017) is employed to learn the belief function `b_Δ`. Given an offline trajectory `{(s_t, a_t, r_t, s_{t+1})}` from a delay-free environment, augmented states `x_t = {s_{t-Δ}, a_{t-Δ}..., a_{t-1}}` are manually constructed. The transformer takes `x_t` as input and predicts a sequence of states `ˆs_{t-Δ+1}` to `ˆs_t`. The transformer is updated using either Mean Squared Error (MSE) for deterministic MDPs or Maximum Likelihood Estimation (MLE) objectives for stochastic MDPs. This design is intended to reduce compounding prediction errors.

2. **Belief-based Policy Optimization**: The policy improvement step (Equation 4) approximates the policy-behavior divergence `D(π, μ_Δ)` using the mean-squared error between actions sampled from the learned policy and the augmented behavior policy, `α ||a - ˆa||^2_2`. This pragmatic choice avoids explicit training of an augmented behavior model while providing a quadratic penalty for regularization. For deterministic MDPs, immediate rewards `r(s_t, a_t)` can be directly read from the dataset.

3. **Online Adaptation**: During deployment, a circular action buffer of length `Δ` stores past actions. At each timestep `t`, the agent receives a delayed observation `o_t`, updates the action buffer with `a_{t-1}`, and constructs the augmented input `x_t = {o_t, a_{t-Δ}..., a_{t-1}}`. This `x_t` is fed into the trained belief transformer to estimate the current delay-compensated state `ˆs_t`. The policy `π(· | ˆs_t)` then selects `a_t`. Special [MASK] tokens are used to handle initial timesteps where the buffer is not yet full.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The empirical evaluation of DT-CORL was conducted on the D4RL benchmark suite, including MuJoCo locomotion and AntMaze goal-conditioned tasks, under various deterministic and stochastic delay regimes (`Δ` ∈ {4, 8, 16}).

1. **AntMaze Tasks**:
 - DT-CORL consistently outperformed baseline methods across `umaze` and `umaze-diverse` environments, showing less performance degradation with increasing delay lengths compared to augmentation-based methods (Augmented-BC, Augmented-CQL, Augmented-COMBO).
 - State-of-the-art online delayed RL methods (DBPT-SAC) demonstrated complete failure in the offline setting, validating the incompatibility of online approaches with static datasets.
 - Augmentation-based methods degraded more under stochastic delays, while DT-CORL exhibited robust performance.
 - On harder tasks like `medium-play` and `large-play`, all methods, including DT-CORL, showed low overall performance, suggesting the need for further goal-conditioned modifications.

2. **Belief-Based Comparison (MuJoCo Locomotion Tasks)**:
 - DT-CORL demonstrated a clear performance gap over naive offline RL implementations (CQL, IQL) and decoupled belief-based methods (Belief-CQL, Belief-IQL).
 - Naive CQL and IQL were ineffective under delayed settings.
 - Belief-IQL's performance was sensitive to inaccuracies in the latent belief state, leading to unstable policy updates.
 - Belief-CQL, which trained its Q-function on precomputed belief embeddings without joint optimization, yielded suboptimal value estimates, especially with longer delays.
 - DT-CORL's joint alignment of belief prediction with policy evaluation and optimization resulted in superior performance by allowing co-adaptation and effective mitigation of delay-induced distribution shifts.

3. **Ablation Studies**:
 - **Transformer Architecture**: A comparison of transformer, ensemble MLPs, and diffusion models for belief prediction showed that the transformer offered a balance of multi-step prediction accuracy, stable long-horizon predictions, and fast inference suitable for online deployment. Ensemble MLPs accumulated error rapidly, while diffusion models incurred high computational costs. Further ablation revealed that a moderately sized transformer (e.g., 256-dim, 8-10 layers) provided the best balance of predictive accuracy and sample efficiency.
 - **Trajectories Availability**: DT-CORL consistently achieved higher returns than baselines across varying proportions of available offline data (25% to 100%). The performance gap between DT-CORL and baselines widened with more data, suggesting that DT-CORL scales more effectively.
 - **Joint Training Benefits**: Empirical comparison between DT-CORL (joint training) and a variant using a separately pretrained belief model confirmed that joint optimization yielded significantly higher returns, highlighting its role in reducing distribution mismatch and stabilizing delayed-policy learning.
 - **Delay Robustness**: DT-CORL maintained robust performance under long-horizon delays (32-64 steps) and across different delay distributions (Gaussian, exponential, binomial, uniform) that shared the same expected delay. Performance naturally decreased with increasing delays, but DT-CORL showed graceful degradation and robust generalization across distributions.

4. **Dexterous Manipulation Tasks (Adroit Hand)**:
 - In high-dimensional, contact-rich manipulation tasks (Pen, Door, Hammer), DT-CORL consistently attained the highest performance across all tested delay levels. It exhibited more graceful degradation compared to augmentation-based (Aug-CQL) and decoupled belief-based (Belief-CQL) baselines, particularly on tasks like Hammer where baselines failed. This indicates the belief model's capacity to capture fine-grained temporal structure crucial for complex manipulation under latency.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

DT-CORL represents a contribution to the fields of offline and delayed reinforcement learning by addressing a critical challenge: enabling delay-robust policy learning solely from static, delay-free offline data. This approach has several significant implications:

1. **Bridging the Sim-to-Real Latency Gap**: The framework explicitly tackles the common discrepancy between idealized simulation environments (delay-free) and real-world deployment (delay-prone). By learning to predict latent states from delayed observations based on delay-free data, DT-CORL offers a method to enhance the realism and robustness of sim-to-real transfer, potentially reducing the need for extensive real-world testing in delayed environments.

2. **Enhanced Safety and Efficiency in Data Collection**: In domains where online interaction with a delayed system is unsafe, costly, or impractical (e.g., robotics, autonomous vehicles, high-frequency trading), DT-CORL allows for leveraging readily available delay-free datasets. This could lead to safer development cycles, reduced sample complexity, and a lower barrier to entry for deploying RL agents in latency-sensitive applications.

3. **Overcoming Limitations of Existing Methods**:
 - It effectively avoids the **dimensional explosion** and **sample inefficiency** inherent in naive state augmentation methods for handling delays, making it more scalable for longer delays.
 - By introducing **joint belief-policy optimization**, DT-CORL mitigates the issues of **compounding prediction errors** and **distribution mismatch** that plague decoupled belief-based pipelines, leading to more stable and performant policies.
 - The use of a **transformer-based belief model** allows for robust learning of temporal dependencies from sequential data, which is essential for accurate state estimation under various delay regimes, including stochastic delays.

4. **Broader Applicability**: The empirical validation across diverse tasks, from locomotion and goal-conditioned navigation (D4RL MuJoCo, AntMaze) to high-dimensional dexterous manipulation (Adroit Hand), suggests the generalizability of the DT-CORL framework. Its consistent superiority over baselines across different delay lengths, types (deterministic/stochastic), and data availability levels underscores its robustness.

**Potential Future Impact**:
- The framework's ability to learn from delay-free data could accelerate the development and deployment of autonomous systems in real-world settings where obtaining data under realistic delay conditions is prohibitive.
- It could serve as a foundational method for incorporating other real-world imperfections (e.g., sensor noise, partial observability) into offline RL, paving the way for more robust and reliable autonomous agents.

**Open Questions and Future Work (as identified by the authors)**:
- **Handling unknown/time-varying delays**: The current work assumes known, fixed, or uniformly distributed delays. Extending the framework to adapt to unknown, time-varying, or heterogeneous delays remains an important challenge.
- **Scaling to high-dimensional perceptual domains**: Applying DT-CORL to environments with high-dimensional observations (e.g., vision-based manipulation) may require further architectural advancements such as spatial attention mechanisms or structured world models.

In summary, DT-CORL presents a method for addressing a critical gap in the field, offering a principled approach to building delay-robust RL agents using only existing, idealized datasets.
