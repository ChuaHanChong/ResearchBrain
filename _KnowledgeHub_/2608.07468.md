# SimWAM: A Simple World Action Model for End-to-End Autonomous Driving

> [!summary] Summary
> SimWAM is a World-Action Model for end-to-end autonomous driving that achieves state-of-the-art Predictive Driver Model Score (PDMS) on the NAVSIM benchmark. It co-trains a video expert and an action expert to leverage future-scene dynamics during training, while maintaining efficient, real-time inference through a decoupled architecture and refining the policy with reinforcement learning.

## Problem

- Existing "imagine-then-act" World-Action Models (WAMs) for autonomous driving incur high inference latency due to computationally intensive real-time future-scene generation.
- End-to-end planners relying solely on imitation learning implicitly capture traffic semantics, limiting explicit understanding and direct optimization for overall driving quality.
- Many current Vision-Language-Action (VLA) and driving WAM approaches incorporate future-scene generation components that remain loosely coupled with action prediction, leading to additional training stages or sequential inference.

## Method

- **SimWAM** co-trains a pre-trained video ==Diffusion Transformer (DiT)== as a "video expert" alongside a lightweight "action expert" (another DiT) using a joint ==flow matching== objective over video and trajectory modalities.
- An ==isolated attention mask== in the co-training framework prevents the action expert from directly attending to future-frame tokens, allowing the entire video branch to be discarded after training for efficient inference.
- ==Reinforcement learning== refines the imitation-based policy by transforming the deterministic flow ODE into a stochastic SDE, enabling exploration and optimizing trajectory generation with compositional driving rewards on challenging scenarios.

## Results

- **SimWAM** achieved a state-of-the-art Predictive Driver Model Score (PDMS) of **91.5** on the NAVSIM `navtest` benchmark with a single front camera, outperforming existing VLM-based and "imagine-then-act" WAM planners by up to **2.4** PDMS points.
- The system demonstrated substantially lower inference latency compared to other world-model-based planners by discarding the video expert post-training, validating its efficient training-time world modeling approach.
- **SimWAM** exhibited zero-shot generalization to the nuScenes open-loop planning benchmark, achieving the lowest average collision rate of **0.04%** and a competitive L2 error of **0.96m**, without any domain-specific fine-tuning.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The primary benefit of video-based world modeling for action prediction can be effectively realized during training through representation learning, eliminating the need for expensive future imagination at inference time.
> - A decoupled architecture with an isolated attention mechanism allows for efficient, real-time inference by retaining only a lightweight action expert, while still leveraging rich spatiotemporal priors learned from a video expert during training.
> - Integrating reinforcement learning as a refinement stage, particularly by focusing on challenging scenarios and updating only LoRA adapters, significantly improves driving quality metrics beyond pure imitation by balancing safety, compliance, and progress.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

End-to-end autonomous driving (E2E AD) systems aim to map raw sensor observations directly to a planned trajectory through a unified network, reducing the need for hand-crafted interfaces and mitigating error propagation common in modular perception-prediction-planning pipelines. While contemporary E2E planners have shown improvements in planning accuracy, they primarily function as imitation policies. This means they reproduce behaviors from logged trajectories, often capturing complex traffic semantics, user intent, and scene dynamics only implicitly.

To address the limitations in semantic understanding, Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models have emerged, adapting pre-trained vision-language models to driving tasks. These models leverage semantic knowledge and high-level reasoning to enhance scene understanding and better connect trajectory generation with user intent. Some VLA approaches further generate explicit rationales to improve interpretability, particularly in complex or instruction-conditioned scenarios. Recent developments in this area have also incorporated future-scene generation or latent reasoning to improve spatiotemporal understanding. However, these components frequently remain loosely coupled with action prediction, often necessitating additional training stages or sequential inference, thus modeling motion and temporal evolution indirectly.

World models represent an alternative paradigm by providing an explicit prior regarding how the environment evolves under motion. Building on this concept, World-Action Models (WAMs) in embodied intelligence, such as DreamZero and LingBot-VA, jointly predict future observations and actions using pre-trained video-generation backbones. This world-action paradigm has been extended to autonomous driving, with methods like DriveLaW and DriveWAM training a video predictor alongside a planner to allow anticipated scene dynamics to inform trajectory generation. A common characteristic of existing driving WAMs is their "imagine-then-act" pipeline, where the planner conditions its output on generated future frames. This design introduces costly video synthesis into the real-time planning loop, leading to substantial increases in inference latency, which presents a challenge for real-world deployment.

The SimWAM research builds upon the observation, previously highlighted by Fast-WAM, that video co-training benefits action prediction primarily through training-time representation learning rather than requiring test-time future imagination. SimWAM aims to integrate the advantages of explicit world modeling for learning traffic dynamics during training while circumventing the high computational cost associated with generating future frames at inference time. Furthermore, the paper positions its work in relation to reinforcement learning (RL) for autonomous driving. While imitation learning is limited to expert demonstrations, RL offers a refinement stage to optimize policies directly for task-level rewards. SimWAM integrates RL to optimize its action expert, moving beyond pure imitation. By combining efficient world-model-based training with RL refinement, SimWAM seeks to establish a practical and robust baseline for autonomous driving.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop SimWAM, presented as a simple yet effective World-Action Model (WAM) for end-to-end autonomous driving. This objective is driven by several key motivations:

1. **Addressing High Inference Latency in Existing WAMs:** Prior driving WAMs typically follow an "imagine-then-act" paradigm, requiring real-time future-scene generation during inference to inform trajectory planning. This process is computationally intensive and results in high latency, which is prohibitive for safety-critical real-time autonomous driving applications. The motivation is to achieve the benefits of world modeling without incurring this inference cost.
2. **Effective Transfer of Traffic Dynamics Priors:** The researchers aim to leverage the capabilities of pre-trained video generation models to provide a rich prior on traffic dynamics. This prior, learned from general video data, is intended to enhance the planner's understanding of scene evolution without needing dedicated driving-specific motion prediction modules or explicit future generation at deployment.
3. **Achieving Efficient and Direct Trajectory Prediction:** A core motivation is to design a system where video generation serves purely as a training signal. This design allows the entire video branch to be discarded after training, leaving a self-contained and lightweight action expert that directly predicts trajectories from current sensor inputs. This approach is intended to significantly reduce inference latency, making the system more suitable for real-time operation.
4. **Enhancing Architectural Flexibility and Scalability:** The research is motivated by the desire to create a modular architecture that supports independent advancements. This includes the ability to replace the video backbone with more advanced or domain-relevant video generators without altering the action expert or inference pipeline. Additionally, the decoupled nature aims to allow for independent scaling of the video and action experts to meet diverse performance and computational budgets.
5. **Optimizing Driving Quality Beyond Imitation Learning:** While imitation learning is effective for reproducing expert behavior, it can be limited by the quality and diversity of demonstrations and only indirectly optimizes for overall driving quality metrics. The motivation here is to integrate reinforcement learning (RL) as a refinement stage. By transforming the deterministic flow ODE into a stochastic SDE and optimizing with compositional driving rewards, the goal is to enable the action expert to explore more diverse maneuvers and directly improve safety, compliance, and progress, thereby surpassing the limitations of pure imitation.

In summary, SimWAM is motivated by the need for an autonomous driving system that combines the rich spatiotemporal understanding gained from video-based world modeling with the real-time efficiency and direct policy optimization necessary for practical deployment.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

SimWAM is designed as a World-Action Model (WAM) for end-to-end autonomous driving, integrating video generation during training to inform trajectory planning while maintaining efficient inference. The methodology involves a co-training framework, a specific attention mechanism, and a reinforcement learning refinement stage.

#### 3.1. Model Architecture and Problem Formulation

The system takes a front-camera observation ($o_t$), ego state ($s_t$, including velocity, acceleration, and yaw rate), and a navigation command ($l$) as input. The objective is to predict an ego trajectory ($a_{t+1:t+H}$) comprising a sequence of waypoints.

Unlike existing "imagine-then-act" driving WAMs that factorize the planning process to first synthesize future driving-scene latents ($z_{t+1:t+N}$) and then condition trajectory generation on them (e.g., $p_{\theta}(a_{t+1:t+H} | o_t, s_t, l, z_{t+1:t+N})$), SimWAM adopts a direct policy interface: $p_{\theta}(a_{t+1:t+H} | z(o_t), s_t, l)$. Here, $z(o_t)$ is a representation derived solely from the current observation. Traffic dynamics knowledge is acquired entirely during training, allowing inference without future-scene latents or auxiliary motion modules.

The core architecture comprises two main components:

- **Video Expert:** A video Diffusion Transformer (DiT) initialized from Wan2.2-5B, along with its associated Video VAE (for mapping frames to latent tokens) and T5 text encoder (for processing navigation commands). This expert is trained with a standard video-generation objective, reconstructing noised future frames via flow matching, conditioned on the current frame. Its role is to supply the action expert with a traffic-aware motion prior.
- **Action Expert:** A lightweight Diffusion Transformer with a hidden size of 1024. This expert is conditioned on a concatenation of current observation latents ($z(o_t)$), ego state ($s_t$, embedded by a small MLP), and navigation command ($l$). It predicts the trajectory velocity field ($v_{\theta_a}(a_{\tau_{t+1:t+H}}, \tau, c)$) using flow matching. During inference, only this lightweight action expert is retained, and its predicted velocity field is integrated to generate the planned trajectory.

#### 3.2. Co-training and Isolated Attention Mask

The two experts are co-trained using joint flow matching over video and trajectory modalities. The joint objective is defined as $L = L_{act}^{FM} + \lambda L_{vid}^{FM}$, where both terms instantiate a flow matching loss (Eq. 1) for the action trajectory and future-frame latents, respectively, and $\lambda$ balances their contributions. This co-training allows future-scene prediction to influence the observation representation used by the planner.

A critical component is the **isolated attention mask**. This mask is introduced to decouple the action expert from direct reliance on future frames during inference. In the shared attention stream, which includes current observation latents ($z(o_t)$), future frame latents ($z_{t+1:t+N}$), and action tokens, both future frame tokens and action tokens are allowed to attend to $z(o_t)$. However, the isolated attention mask prevents future frame tokens and action tokens from attending to each other. This ensures that the action expert learns from the shared observation representation but does not directly depend on future frame tokens for its predictions. Consequently, after training, the video DiT and future-frame decoder can be entirely discarded, resulting in an efficient inference pipeline.

#### 3.3. Reinforcement Learning (RL) Refinement

Following the imitation-based co-training phase, reinforcement learning is applied to optimize trajectory generation directly for driving quality. The original deterministic flow ODE is transformed into a stochastic SDE (Eq. 2), preserving marginal distributions while enabling stochasticity for maneuver exploration and providing tractable log-likelihoods for policy optimization.

- **Exploration:** The SDE allows for sampling a group of $G$ candidate trajectories for each scenario.
- **Reward:** Each candidate trajectory is evaluated using the compositional NAVSIM Predictive Driver Model Score (PDMS) reward, which combines metrics like No Collision (NC), Drivable Area Compliance (DAC), Ego Progress (EP), Time-to-Collision (TTC), and Comfort (C).
- **Policy Update:** Group-relative advantages derived from these rewards are used for a clipped policy update, following principles similar to GRPO.
- **Targeted Optimization:** The RL stage focuses on challenging `navtrain` scenarios where the imitation policy exhibits lower PDMS.
- **Parameter Updates:** To preserve the distilled motion prior and maintain a lightweight planner, only LoRA (Low-Rank Adaptation) adapters on the attention projections of the action expert are updated during RL, rather than modifying the full network weights.

#### 3.4. Flexibility Aspects

The decoupled architecture and shared attention interface contribute to SimWAM's flexibility:

- **Video Generator Flexibility:** Because the action expert only interacts through the shared observation representation and is independent of the video expert's internal parameterization, the video generator can be replaced with different pre-trained models (e.g., newer or domain-specific ones) without altering the action expert or the inference pipeline.
- **Scale Flexibility:** The video and action experts can be scaled independently. A larger video generator can provide a richer motion prior during training without increasing deployment costs. Conversely, the action DiT's width and depth can be adjusted to meet specific latency or performance targets.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of SimWAM was conducted primarily on the NAVSIM benchmark, with zero-shot generalization to nuScenes.

#### 4.1. Overall Performance on NAVSIM

- **State-of-the-Art PDMS:** SimWAM achieved a Predictive Driver Model Score (PDMS) of 91.5 on the NAVSIM `navtest` benchmark, utilizing only a single front camera. This result surpassed existing state-of-the-art planners in end-to-end autonomous driving.
- **Comparison with VLM-based Planners:** SimWAM outperformed SGDrive (91.1 PDMS) by 0.4 points and ExploreVLA (90.4 PDMS) by 1.1 points.
- **Comparison with World-Model-based Planners:** SimWAM consistently outperformed other "imagine-then-act" WAM planners, achieving 2.4 points higher than DriveLaW (89.1 PDMS) and 1.4 points higher than DriveWAM (90.1 PDMS) under the same single-camera setup.
- **Metric Breakdown:** Among world-model-based planners, SimWAM attained the highest Drivable Area Compliance (DAC) and Ego Progress (EP) scores, while maintaining competitive No Collision (NC) and Time-to-Collision (TTC) metrics.
- **Efficiency:** Crucially, SimWAM achieved these performance levels with substantially lower inference latency compared to other world-model-based planners (as depicted in Figure 1 of the paper), validating its training-time world modeling approach for efficient inference.

#### 4.2. Component Analysis (Table 2)

- **Action-only Baseline:** A standalone action DiT achieved 86.6 PDMS.
- **Benefit of Video Co-training:** Joint training with the video expert improved the PDMS to 90.3, demonstrating that future-video supervision effectively transfers traffic-dynamics priors to the observation representation, enhancing the action expert's scene understanding.
- **Benefit of Reinforcement Learning:** Subsequent reinforcement learning (RL) further increased the PDMS to 91.5. This improvement indicates that direct optimization towards a compositional driving reward effectively balances safety, compliance, and progress beyond imitation learning alone.

#### 4.3. Attention Mask Analysis (Table 3)

- The proposed isolated attention mask achieved a PDMS of 90.3, which was comparable to or slightly better than bidirectional attention (90.2 PDMS) and action→video attention (90.1 PDMS). This result supports that exposing the action branch to future video tokens provides no measurable performance benefit in this configuration, while the isolated design enables efficient inference.

#### 4.4. Video Backbone Flexibility (Table 4)

- SimWAM demonstrated architectural flexibility by accommodating various pre-trained video generators. Wan2.1-1.3B and Wan2.2-5B yielded comparable PDMS values (90.2 and 90.3, respectively).
- Cosmos-Predict2.5, pre-trained on driving videos, achieved the highest PDMS of 90.4, suggesting that domain-relevant video priors can further enhance performance. The quality of the video prior was found to be influential, as a lightweight model like LTX-Video resulted in a lower PDMS of 88.7.

#### 4.5. Action Expert Scalability (Table 5)

- The ability to scale the action expert independently was validated. Increasing the action DiT size from 0.21B to 1.02B consistently improved PDMS from 89.9 to 90.3, demonstrating complementary scaling dimensions for the two experts.

#### 4.6. Cross-dataset Generalization (Table 6)

- SimWAM, trained solely on NAVSIM, exhibited zero-shot generalization capabilities on the nuScenes open-loop planning benchmark. It achieved the lowest average collision rate of 0.04% and a competitive average L2 error of 0.96 m, without any fine-tuning or auxiliary annotations specific to nuScenes. This indicates that the learned dynamics prior transfers effectively across domains.

#### 4.7. Ablation Studies

- **Exploration Sampler (Table 7):** The SDE-based exploration sampler for RL resulted in a PDMS of 91.5, outperforming native random noise perturbations (91.3 PDMS). The SDE was found to generate diverse yet plausible trajectories suitable for policy optimization.
- **RL Training Dynamics (Figure 3):** Training RL on a subset of challenging `navtrain` scenes (imitation PDMS below 90) led to better overall PDMS (peak 91.5) compared to training on the full `navtrain` set, indicating that focused optimization on difficult scenarios yields more informative reward signals.
- **Prediction Horizon and Frame Density (Table 8):** A 4-second prediction horizon at 2 Hz (8 future frames) achieved the best performance (90.3 PDMS). Broad temporal coverage (4s) was found to be more critical than dense frame sampling for learning traffic dynamics.
- **Input Resolution (Table 9):** A resolution of 384×672 provided the most favorable balance between planning accuracy and inference efficiency, yielding 90.3 PDMS with 518ms latency. Higher resolutions offered diminishing returns for increased computational cost.
- **Number of Sampling Steps (Table 10):** Ten sampling steps for the action flow sampler yielded the highest PDMS of 90.3. Five steps recovered most of the performance, while twenty steps provided no further improvement, suggesting convergence of the sampler.

#### 4.8. Qualitative Results (Figure 4)

- Qualitative comparisons showed that the RL-refined model produced more decisive and farther-progressing trajectories at intersections and along narrow streets, while maintaining adherence to drivable areas and safe clearances, compared to the imitation-trained model.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

SimWAM introduces a notable approach to end-to-end autonomous driving by decoupling the benefits of world modeling during training from the computational burden of future scene generation during inference. This work has several significant implications and potential impacts:

1. **Enabling Efficient World-Action Modeling:** The core contribution of SimWAM is its "training-time world modeling" paradigm. By using video generation purely as a training signal and discarding the video expert at inference, it effectively addresses the high latency issue prevalent in previous "imagine-then-act" WAMs. This efficiency is critical for deploying autonomous driving systems in real-time, safety-critical environments.
2. **Improving Planning Performance:** SimWAM achieves state-of-the-art PDMS on the NAVSIM benchmark with a single camera, demonstrating that explicit learning of traffic dynamics through video co-training significantly enhances planning quality. This suggests that incorporating powerful, pre-trained video generation models can provide richer priors that translate to more capable driving policies.
3. **Enhancing Generalization and Robustness:** The zero-shot transferability to the nuScenes benchmark, particularly its low collision rate without any domain-specific fine-tuning, indicates that the learned dynamics prior is robust and generalizable across different datasets. This property is crucial for developing autonomous driving systems that can operate reliably in varied real-world conditions.
4. **Architectural Flexibility and Scalability:** The decoupled design, allowing independent replacement of the video backbone and scaling of the action expert, positions SimWAM as a highly flexible and future-proof framework. It enables the system to readily benefit from advancements in video generation research without requiring major modifications to the core planner or inference pipeline. This adaptability supports different performance-to-computation trade-offs for various deployment scenarios.
5. **Integration of Imitation and Reinforcement Learning:** The successful integration of reinforcement learning to refine the imitation-trained policy highlights a promising direction for autonomous driving. By optimizing directly for compositional driving rewards, SimWAM can transcend the limitations of pure imitation, leading to policies that better balance safety, comfort, and progress, and can explore a broader range of plausible maneuvers.
6. **Establishment of a Practical Baseline:** SimWAM is positioned as a "simple yet solid WAM baseline." Its architectural clarity, efficiency, and strong performance make it an accessible and effective starting point for future research in areas such as leveraging larger video foundation models, exploring more advanced RL techniques, or integrating multi-modal sensor inputs within this decoupled framework.

Overall, SimWAM offers a practical and effective solution to a key challenge in end-to-end autonomous driving, demonstrating that world models can enhance driving intelligence without compromising real-time performance.
