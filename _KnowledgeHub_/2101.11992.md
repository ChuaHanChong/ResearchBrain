# Acting in Delayed Environments with Non-Stationary Markov Policies

> [!summary] Summary
> Delayed-Q formulates execution-delay MDPs without state augmentation: an approximate one-step forward model is rolled out m times to infer the future state, on which a DDQN-style Q-network acts, yielding a non-stationary deterministic Markov policy. Theory proves such policies suffice for optimality; empirically it beats oblivious and augmented baselines in 39 of 42 tabular, physical, and Atari experiments with delays up to 25.

## Problem

- Standard MDPs assume actions execute instantly; real latency in robotics, cloud, and finance breaks this, and prior delayed-RL work mostly quantified degradation rather than fixing it.
- The dominant remedy, concatenating pending actions to the state, blows up the state space exponentially in delay, and no formal cost of that augmentation existed.
- Prior augmentation-free planning assumed near-deterministic transitions, required finite discretized states, and ran offline, so it could not scale to continuous control or image domains.

## Method

- ==Execution-Delay MDP (ED-MDP)== formalism keeps the original state space, treating the pending-action queue as an exogenous variable, plus a ==non-stationary Bellman-type recursion== for the delayed value function.
- Policy-class theorems: deterministic non-stationary Markov policies suffice for optimality under delay, stationary ones are provably sub-optimal, and matching lower/upper iteration bounds are derived for ==augmented policy iteration (mA-PI)==.
- **Delayed-Q** algorithm: a learned ==one-step forward model== rolled out m times gives a most-likely future state fed to a ==DDQN== Q-network, with an m-step ==replay-buffer shift== aligning actions to execution time.

## Results

- Delayed-Q beats Oblivious-Q and Augmented-Q in **39 of 42** experiments across maze, CartPole, Acrobot, and 8 Atari games, with delays up to **m=25**.
- Training episodes needed to reach **0.5** maze reward grow linearly in delay for Delayed-Q but exponentially for Augmented-Q, which exhausts Q-table memory beyond **m=15**.
- Swapping the learned forward model for the true simulator on Noisy CartPole raises return by **20-30%**, while a delay-unaware RNN policy fails to beat either baseline.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Under execution delay, non-stationarity matters more than history: state augmentation buys expressiveness the optimal policy never needs, at exponential state-space cost.
> - Predicting the future state with a one-step model decouples the agent from the delay value it trained on, so misspecified or varying delays degrade gracefully.
> - Most-likely state estimates work only when dynamics stay near-unimodal; under multi-modal stochastic dynamics a point estimate lands where probability mass never is.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of reinforcement learning (RL) and Markov Decision Processes (MDPs) traditionally assumes that actions selected by an agent are executed instantaneously. This assumption, however, often does not hold in real-world applications where system latency can lead to delays in action execution. Such delays are prevalent in various domains, including robotic manipulation, cloud computing, financial trading, and autonomous systems, and can result in significant performance degradation or system failures.

Existing literature on handling delays in decision-making systems is diverse. Control theory has extensively studied delay in linear time-invariant systems, but these approaches typically assume known system dynamics, which differs from the model-free or model-agnostic nature of discrete-time MDPs. Within the RL context, some works have considered delayed reward signals, such as in multi-armed bandits or specific MDP settings. Other studies have addressed execution or observation delays in MDPs, often employing a state-augmentation method. This method involves concatenating relevant past information (e.g., pending actions) to the current state to preserve the Markov property, thereby transforming the delayed problem into a larger, standard MDP. However, a significant drawback of state augmentation is the exponential growth of the state space with increasing delay, rendering it computationally intractable for substantial delays.

Previous attempts to avoid state augmentation, such as Model-Based Simulation (MBS) by Walsh et al. (2009), typically focused on planning in environments with delayed feedback, assuming near-deterministic transitions or finite/discretized state spaces. These methods often operated offline, estimating a surrogate MDP before solving it, which limits their applicability to large, continuous domains. Recent works have also explored concurrent control settings where multiple actions are sampled between observations, effectively reducing the problem to a small, fixed execution delay. Policy-gradient (policy-based) methods have been modified to account for delays, but these approaches often still rely on some form of state augmentation or are evaluated on relatively small delay values.

This paper positions itself by specifically addressing the challenge of execution delay in MDPs without resorting to the computationally expensive state-augmentation approach. It aims to develop a theoretical framework and a practical value-based (Q-learning style) algorithm that can effectively learn and plan in environments with significant execution delays, thereby expanding the applicability of RL to a broader range of real-world scenarios.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the disparity between the standard theoretical formulation of MDPs and the practical realities of real-world systems. The standard MDP framework's reliance on the immediate execution of chosen actions is often an idealized assumption. In actual deployments, delays arise from various sources, including the computational complexity of modern decision systems, the physical latencies of actuators, or network communication lags. Ignoring these delays, which can be substantial, can lead to suboptimal performance or even catastrophic failures in critical applications such as robotic control, autonomous driving (e.g., braking decisions), financial trading, and cloud resource management. The paper highlights that observation delays (in sensing the environment) and execution delays (in applying actions) are often equivalent in their treatment, emphasizing the broad relevance of the problem.

Given this fundamental motivation, the key objectives of this work are:

1. **Develop a New Formalism for Delayed MDPs:** To introduce a rigorous mathematical framework for learning and planning in MDPs where chosen actions are executed with a fixed delay of `m` steps, without relying on the state-augmentation approach that exponentially increases state space complexity.
2. **Analyze Theoretical Properties of Optimal Policies:** To investigate the characteristics of optimal policies in these delayed environments. Specifically, to determine if standard Markov policies are sufficient for optimality and if non-stationarity is necessary, as opposed to the stationary policies often sufficient in non-delayed MDPs. This also involves quantifying the trade-off between environmental stochasticity and delay effects.
3. **Quantify Complexity of State Augmentation:** To provide a formal analysis of the computational complexity of the conventional state-augmentation method, particularly for Policy Iteration, establishing tight upper and lower bounds that demonstrate its exponential dependency on the delay value `m`.
4. **Devise a Novel Algorithm for Delayed Execution:** To propose and implement a model-based, Q-learning style algorithm that can efficiently solve delayed execution tasks without needing to augment the state space. This algorithm should address the partial observability introduced by the delay by inferring future states.
5. **Empirically Validate the Proposed Algorithm:** To evaluate the performance of the new algorithm against standard and state-augmented baselines across diverse environments (tabular, physical, and complex image-based domains like Atari), with varying and substantial delay values, to demonstrate its effectiveness, robustness, and scalability.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed in this paper involves a combination of theoretical analysis and empirical evaluation.

**A. Formalism and Theoretical Analysis:**

1. **Delayed MDP (ED-MDP) Formulation:** The paper introduces a formal definition for an MDP with `m`-step execution delay (ED-MDP). In this setup, an action chosen at time `t` is executed at `t+m`. It assumes a queue of `m` actions is initially awaiting execution. This contrasts with the `m`-Augmented MDP (m-AMDP), which is a standard MDP where the state space `X_m` is augmented to include the current state `S` and the `m` pending actions `A^m` (`X_m = S x A^m`).
2. **Complexity of State Augmentation:** The paper analyzes the classic Policy Iteration (PI) algorithm applied to the `m`-AMDP (mA-PI). It proves that the number of iterations required for mA-PI to converge is `Ω(|S||A|^m)`, demonstrating exponential complexity in `m`. This lower bound is shown to be tight, with convergence guaranteed in at most `|S||A|^m(|A|-1) * log(1/gamma)^-1 * log(1/(1-gamma))` iterations. This analysis highlights the computational intractability of state augmentation for larger delays.
3. **Policy Optimality in ED-MDPs:**
 - The paper proves that for ED-MDPs, the stochastic process induced is generally non-Markovian even for stationary policies, unlike standard MDPs.
 - A key theoretical finding is that deterministic Markov policies (which are not necessarily stationary) are sufficient for attaining maximal reward in ED-MDPs. This is an important distinction from standard MDPs where stationary deterministic policies are sufficient.
 - Conversely, it is demonstrated that restricting to stationary policies generally leads to sub-optimal performance in delayed environments. An example (two-state MDP) analytically quantifies how optimal return decreases with delay and increases with environmental certainty for stationary policies.
 - A non-stationary Bellman-type recursion for the delayed value function is derived, which is satisfied by these optimal non-stationary Markov policies.

**B. Delayed-Q Algorithm:**

To address delayed execution without state augmentation, the authors propose "Delayed-Q," a model-based, Q-learning style algorithm.

1. **Two-Stage Decision Process:**
 - **Future State Inference:** When deciding on an action `a_t` at current state `s_t`, which will be executed `m` steps in the future (at `s_t+m`), the algorithm first infers the likely future state `s_t+m`. This is achieved by iteratively applying an approximate forward model `m` times, using the current state `s_t` and the queue of `m` pending actions `(a_{t-m}, ..., a_{t-1})`. This effectively "un-delays" the Q-function. The forward model predicts `s_{k+1}` from `s_k` and `a_k`.
 - **Action Selection:** Once the predicted future state `s_t+m` (denoted `s_t+m`) is obtained, the agent selects an action `a_t` based on this predicted state using a Q-network, i.e., `a_t = argmax_a Q(s_t+m, a)`.
2. **Policy Type:** This procedure implicitly generates a non-stationary Markov policy because the decision rule at time `t` depends on the current state `s_t` (through `s_t+m`) and the time-dependent action queue, but not on the full history. The Q-function itself does not take past actions as input, distinguishing it from the augmentation approach.
3. **Forward Model Implementation:**
 - For tabular environments, the forward model is tabular, predicting the most frequently visited next state given a current state-action pair.
 - For continuous and image-based environments, the forward model is implemented as a neural network. It has a similar architecture to the Q-network and is trained concurrently with it, learning to predict the next state `s_{k+1}` from `s_k` and `a_k`. For Atari games, the simulator itself is used as a perfect forward model due to the complexity of learning image-based dynamics.
4. **Training Mechanism:** To train the Q-network, a sample buffer of length `m` is maintained. The chosen action `a_t` (selected at `s_t` for `s_t+m`) is associated with the reward `r_t+m` and next state `s_t+m+1` that result from its *actual execution* `m` steps later. This `(s_t+m, r_t+m, a_t, s_t+m+1)` tuple is then inserted into the replay buffer for standard Q-learning updates.

**C. Experimental Setup:**

1. **Baselines:**
 - **Oblivious-Q:** Standard Q-learning that ignores the delay and assumes immediate action execution.
 - **Augmented-Q:** Q-learning applied to the `m`-AMDP, where the state includes the original state and the `m` pending actions.
2. **Environments:**
 - **Tabular:** Maze (N x N, with and without action noise).
 - **Physical (Continuous):** CartPole and Acrobot (with and without mass perturbations).
 - **Atari (Image-based):** 8 different games (e.g., MsPacman, Enduro, Qbert).
3. **Delay Values:** Experiments were conducted with delays `m` ranging from 0 to 25 (`m ∈ {0, 5, 15, 25}`).
4. **Metrics:** Episodic return (cumulative reward) and convergence speed were evaluated over multiple random seeds.
5. **Robustness Checks:** The sensitivity of Delayed-Q to model accuracy (comparing learned vs. perfect forward models) and to misspecified delay values (training with one `m`, evaluating with another) was also investigated. A comparison to RNN-based policies was also performed in one Atari environment.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded both theoretical insights and empirical evidence supporting the proposed approach for delayed environments.

**A. Theoretical Findings:**

1. **Complexity of State Augmentation:** The theoretical analysis demonstrated that Policy Iteration on state-augmented MDPs (m-AMDPs) has a computational complexity that scales exponentially with the delay value `m` (`Ω(|S||A|^m)`). This formally confirms the practical limitations of this "brute-force" approach, especially for substantial delays.
2. **Sufficiency of Non-Stationary Markov Policies:** A core theoretical result is that deterministic Markov policies are sufficient to achieve optimal performance in execution-delayed MDPs (ED-MDPs). This is notable because, unlike standard MDPs where stationary policies are typically optimal, the optimal policies in ED-MDPs are generally non-stationary due to the inherent time-dependence introduced by the action queue.
3. **Sub-Optimality of Stationary Policies:** Conversely, the paper proved that restricting to stationary policies generally leads to sub-optimal solutions in delayed environments. This was analytically quantified in a two-state MDP example, showing a monotonic decrease in optimal return with increasing delay `m` for stationary policies.
4. **Bellman-Type Recursion:** A non-stationary Bellman-type recursion was derived for the delayed value function, providing a theoretical foundation for value-based methods in this context.

**B. Empirical Findings:**

The experimental evaluation across tabular, physical, and Atari domains demonstrated the superior performance and robustness of the Delayed-Q algorithm compared to the baselines.

1. **Overall Performance:** Delayed-Q outperformed both Oblivious-Q and Augmented-Q in 39 out of 42 experiments spanning the three environment categories and various delay values (up to `m=25`). It consistently converged quickly to higher performance levels, even for substantial delays.
2. **Performance of Baselines:**
 - **Oblivious-Q:** This approach, which ignores the execution delay, exhibited catastrophic performance degradation for `m > 0`. In the Maze domain, it consistently failed for any delay, and in physical domains, it produced very poor results even for `m=5`.
 - **Augmented-Q:** While conceptually sound, its performance was severely limited by its exponential complexity. In the Maze environment, it converged slowly with increasing `m` and became intractable due to memory limitations for `m > 15`. In continuous physical domains, it performed moderately up to `m=15` but struggled or failed for larger delays. For Atari games, the performance gap between Augmented-Q and Delayed-Q increased with `m`, with Augmented-Q sometimes performing similarly to Oblivious-Q at `m=25`.
3. **Delayed-Q Specifics:**
 - **Scalability with Delay:** In the Maze environment, Delayed-Q exhibited a linear increase in training complexity with `m`, a significant advantage over the exponential scaling of Augmented-Q. It maintained high performance even at `m=25`.
 - **Robustness to Stochasticity:** Delayed-Q performed well even in noisy environments (e.g., Noisy Cartpole, Noisy Acrobot), although a clear trade-off between noise and delay was observed, indicating increased sensitivity to stochasticity at higher delays.
 - **Sensitivity to State-Space Size:** While superior to augmentation, tabular Delayed-Q showed an exponential performance drop with increasing maze size, indicating its reliance on accurate forward model predictions.
 - **Forward Model Accuracy:** Experiments comparing a learned forward model to a perfect simulator indicated that performance could potentially improve by 20-30% with a more accurate model, highlighting an area for future work.
 - **Robustness to Misspecified Delay:** When trained with `m=10` and evaluated on different delay values (`m ∈`), Delayed-Q showed robustness. Performance variations were attributed to the inherent impact of execution delay rather than the model being "unaware" of the actual delay value during evaluation. For evaluation delays shorter than the training delay, performance even improved, suggesting a decoupling of the algorithm from the specific training delay value.
 - **Comparison to RNN Policies:** An additional experiment with an RNN-based policy in an Atari game demonstrated that recurrent policies, if unaware of the delay structure, do not significantly improve upon Augmented-Q or Oblivious-Q. This supports the theoretical finding that historical states beyond `s_t-m` do not provide additional information for action selection.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research provides both fundamental theoretical advancements and practical algorithmic solutions for a critical challenge in reinforcement learning: acting effectively in environments with execution delays.

**Theoretical Significance:**
- The formal proof that deterministic non-stationary Markov policies are sufficient for optimality in delayed MDPs is a significant contribution to RL theory. It clarifies the class of policies required for optimal control under delay, contrasting with the stationary policies often sufficient in non-delayed settings.
- The rigorous complexity analysis of state augmentation for Policy Iteration formally quantifies why this standard approach is intractable for non-trivial delays, thus justifying the need for alternative methodologies.
- The demonstration of stationary policies' sub-optimality in delayed contexts further underlines the unique characteristics of these problems.

**Practical Significance and Potential Impact:**
- **Enabling Real-World RL:** The development of Delayed-Q offers a practical, scalable algorithm that overcomes the limitations of traditional state augmentation. This is crucial for deploying RL agents in real-world applications where delays are unavoidable and substantial. This includes complex robotic systems, autonomous vehicles, high-frequency trading platforms, and cloud computing resource management, where immediate decision execution is a fiction.
- **Improved Performance and Stability:** By intelligently inferring future states to compensate for execution delay, Delayed-Q achieves significantly higher performance and stability compared to methods that ignore delay or struggle with state-space explosion. This can lead to more reliable and efficient autonomous systems.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The work opens several avenues for further research:
 - **Advanced Forward Models:** Incorporating more sophisticated probabilistic future-state estimators or learning image-based action-dependent forward models could further enhance performance.
 - **Handling Unknown/Varying Delays:** The algorithm's inherent decoupling from a fixed delay value (as shown in robustness experiments) suggests potential for extension to scenarios with unknown or dynamically varying delays, which are common in real systems.
 - **Policy-Gradient Methods:** Extending the framework to policy-gradient-based methods could enable application to continuous control tasks with inherent observation delays.
- **Broader Impact on RL Community:** By providing both theoretical guarantees and an empirically validated algorithm, this paper encourages the RL community to systematically address the pervasive issue of execution delay, moving closer to developing agents truly capable of operating effectively in complex, asynchronous real-world environments.
