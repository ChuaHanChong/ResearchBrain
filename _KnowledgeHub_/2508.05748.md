# WebWatcher: Breaking New Frontier of Vision-Language Deep Research Agent

> [!summary] Summary
> WebWatcher is a multimodal deep research agent developed by Alibaba's Tongyi Lab, integrating vision-language reasoning with autonomous multi-tool interaction to process complex web information. The agent achieves state-of-the-art performance on several challenging multimodal benchmarks, including a new BrowseComp-VL benchmark designed for multi-page web browsing and fine-grained visual grounding.

## Problem

- Existing deep research agents are primarily text-centric, overlooking crucial visual information essential for many real-world web and research tasks.
- Current multimodal Visual Question Answering (VQA) benchmarks and models often lack the depth of reasoning, multi-step planning, and cross-modal integration required for advanced agent capabilities.
- There is a scarcity of high-quality, complex multimodal training data and benchmarks that demand autonomous, multi-tool interaction for deep research agents.

## Method

- Developed **WebWatcher**, a multimodal ==Deep Research Agent== equipped with external tools like web image search, web text search, webpage visiting, and a code interpreter, along with internal OCR.
- Introduced BrowseComp-VL, a novel VQA benchmark and a scalable data generation pipeline for creating synthetic, high-quality multimodal training data with complex multi-hop reasoning requirements.
- Trained the agent using a two-stage approach: Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) on auto-annotated, quality-filtered tool-use trajectories, followed by Reinforcement Learning (RL) using Group-Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO).

## Results

- WebWatcher-**32B** achieved a state-of-the-art score of **13.6%** Pass@1 on Humanity’s Last Exam (HLE-VL), outperforming GPT-4o's direct inference (**6.5%**) and RAG-based workflows (**12.3%**).
- On the new BrowseComp-VL benchmark, WebWatcher-**32B** scored **27.0%** Pass@1, significantly higher than GPT-4o RAG (**13.4%**) and OmniSearch (**16.3%**), demonstrating superior performance on complex web browsing tasks.
- Analysis of tool calls showed WebWatcher adaptively uses tools, with high reliance on Web Text Search and Visit for BrowseComp-VL (**62.1%** and **23.3%** respectively) and a shift towards Web Image Search for visually-centric tasks like LiveVQA and MMSearch.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - An SFT-based cold start is critical for stable and effective Reinforcement Learning, providing a robust foundation for agents to learn complex, multi-step tool-use sequences.
> - WebWatcher demonstrates adaptive utilization of its diverse set of multimodal tools, dynamically adjusting its strategy based on the specific demands and modality of different tasks.
> - The use of a multi-stage quality control process for both generated VQA data and tool-use trajectories is essential for producing high-quality supervision signals and enabling deep reasoning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work is strategically positioned at the intersection of **deep research agents** and **multimodal understanding**, addressing a critical gap in the current AI landscape.

In recent years, "deep research agents" have emerged as a new frontier in AI. These agents, often powered by Large Language Models (LLMs), can autonomously perform complex, multi-step tasks such as issuing search queries, browsing the web, reading documents, and iteratively refining answers. Pioneering efforts by entities like OpenAI (DeepResearch) and Google (Gemini Deep Research), along with open-source initiatives like WebDancer, WebThinker, R1-Searcher, WebSailor, and WebShaper, have demonstrated superhuman abilities in navigating intricate information environments. However, the paper critically notes that **most of these advancements remain primarily text-centric**. This fundamental limitation means they overlook the rich visual information that is omnipresent and often crucial in real-world scenarios, from interpreting scientific diagrams and analyzing graphics to navigating visually-rich web interfaces.

Concurrently, research in **Multimodal Visual Question Answering (VQA)** has made strides, but existing VQA benchmarks predominantly assess single-step perception or shallow retrieval. Datasets like OK-VQA, A-OKVQA, MMT-Bench, MicroVQA, Open3DVQA, Dyn-VQA, MMMU, and MMMU-Pro, while advancing multimodal understanding, often lack the depth of reasoning, multi-step planning, cross-modal integration, large scale, and automated quality control needed for advanced agent capabilities. They struggle with tasks requiring agents to synthesize information across modalities, perform multi-hop reasoning, and strategically plan tool use in open-ended, real-world web environments.

WebWatcher directly addresses these two shortcomings by introducing a **multimodal Deep Research Agent**. It aims to integrate robust visual-language reasoning with the autonomous, multi-tool interaction capabilities of advanced web agents. By moving beyond text-only or perception-limited multimodal approaches, WebWatcher attempts to redefine the scope of what deep research agents can achieve, making them more aligned with human-like information seeking that naturally combines visual and textual cues. The introduction of **BrowseComp-VL**, a novel benchmark, further contributes to the landscape by providing a more challenging evaluation framework specifically designed for these advanced multimodal reasoning tasks, mirroring the complexity of existing text-based agent benchmarks like BrowseComp.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind the development of WebWatcher are driven by the recognition of significant limitations in current AI agents:

1. **Bridging the Modality Gap in Deep Research Agents:** The most significant motivation is to overcome the **text-centric bias** of existing deep research agents. While these agents excel at processing textual information, they largely ignore the critical visual cues present in real-world web environments. This limits their ability to tackle many research-centric and everyday tasks that inherently demand integrated vision-language reasoning, such as understanding complex charts, interpreting images, or navigating dynamic visual interfaces.

2. **Enhancing Reasoning Capabilities for Complex Multimodal Tasks:** Current multimodal agents often rely on rigid, template-driven pipelines or visual tools focused purely on perception (e.g., OCR, bounding box extraction). This results in a lack of flexible reasoning, particularly in tasks requiring deep textual understanding, cross-modal inference, and the integration of information from diverse sources (both visual and textual). The authors aim to develop an agent that can perform genuine multi-step reasoning, not just retrieval or simple perception.

3. **Enabling Effective Multi-tool Integration:** To solve complex, real-world problems, agents need to go beyond simple search or visual tools. They require the ability to flexibly coordinate and utilize a diverse set of external tools, including web search, image retrieval, webpage browsing, and code interpreters. A key objective is to equip WebWatcher with this sophisticated multi-tool integration capability, where the agent can strategically select and sequence tools based on the task's demands.

4. **Developing High-Quality Training Data and Benchmarks:** A major challenge for training advanced multimodal agents is the scarcity of data that combines high-quality visual content with complex, multi-step reasoning trajectories. Existing VQA datasets often lack the planning complexity and reasoning depth needed for agent capabilities. Therefore, a crucial objective is to develop a scalable pipeline for generating synthetic, high-quality multimodal training data and introduce a new, challenging benchmark (BrowseComp-VL) to properly evaluate agents on these advanced tasks.

In essence, the motivation is to create a more **cognitively complete and versatile AI agent** that can perceive, understand, and interact with the world in a human-like multimodal fashion, moving beyond narrow, modality-specific problem-solving.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

WebWatcher's methodology comprises three main pillars: constructing high-quality multimodal data and a challenging benchmark, generating effective reasoning trajectories, and training the agent model through a combination of supervised fine-tuning and reinforcement learning.

#### 3.1. Data Preparation and BrowseComp-VL Benchmark Construction

The authors introduce **BrowseComp-VL**, a new VQA benchmark designed for advanced multimodal reasoning agents, categorized into five major domains and two difficulty levels:
- **Level 1 (199 VQA pairs):** Requires multi-hop reasoning with explicit entities, necessitating information integration across multiple sources.
- **Level 2 (200 VQA pairs):** Features intentionally obscured or fuzzified entities and attributes, demanding contextual reasoning, planning, comparison, and synthesis.

The data generation pipeline for BrowseComp-VL and the training data involves:
1. **Generate QA (Textual):**
 - **Level 1:** Inspired by CRAWL-QA, this involves recursively traversing hyperlinks from authoritative sources (arXiv, GitHub, Wikipedia) to build knowledge graphs, then using GPT-4o to synthesize multi-hop question-answer pairs.
 - **Level 2:** Following WebSailor, this uses a two-stage framework:
 - *Nodes Selecting:* GPT-4o generates base QA from a Wikipedia page (root entity), then expands a hyperlink graph (depth 3, branching factor 3) to form subgraphs with N entities, defining unique reasoning paths.
 - *Query Generating:* GPT-4o transforms standard QA into "fuzzed" versions by replacing precise references with ambiguous descriptions, pushing models to infer answers.
2. **QA-to-VQA Conversion:**
 - **Visual Context Construction:** For each filtered target entity, relevant web images are retrieved via Google SerpApi (K=2 images per entity), ensuring authentic visual grounding.
 - **Entity Masking and Question Transformation:** GPT-4o rewrites original text questions by masking target entities with visual reference tokens (e.g., "the object in the image"), creating multimodal VQA queries.
3. **Quality Control:** A three-stage filtering pipeline ensures high-quality VQA samples:
 - **Selector:** Filters out trivial VQA queries (e.g., same as original question, explicit entity mentions) and assesses visual context alignment and plausibility using GPT-4o.
 - **Examiner:** GPT-4o answers image-query pairs using only visual content and captions; failures indicate weak retrieval or poor queries.

#### 3.2. Trajectory Generation and Post-Training

This phase focuses on equipping WebWatcher with robust tool-use capabilities and refining its decision-making:

1. **Multimodal Tools:** WebWatcher is equipped with a set of external tools:
 - **Web Image Search:** Google SerpApi for retrieving images, captions, and URLs.
 - **Web Text Search:** Google SerpApi for open-domain information seeking.
 - **Visit:** Jina for navigating URLs and summarizing webpage content based on a specified goal.
 - **Code Interpreter:** For symbolic computation, data analysis, and content extraction.
 - **OCR:** An internal tool invoked via prompt and SFT data to extract text from images.

2. **Automated Trajectory Annotation:** Given a VQA instance, GPT-4o automatically constructs ReAct-style (Thought-Action-Observation) tool-use trajectories. Each trajectory simulates human-like problem-solving, with the language model generating intermediate reasoning, tool invocations, and processing environment feedback.

3. **Trajectory Filtering and Quality Assurance:** To ensure high-quality supervision data for training, three stages of filtering are applied to the generated trajectories:
 - **Final Answer Matching:** Only trajectories leading to a correct ground truth answer are retained.
 - **Step-by-Step Consistency Check:** GPT-4o verifies the logical consistency of each intermediate step, discarding trajectories with hallucinations or unjustified tool calls.
 - **Minimum Tool Usage Requirement:** Trajectories with fewer than three tool calls are removed to encourage multi-step reasoning.

4. **Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) as Cold Start:** The filtered, high-quality tool-use trajectories are used for SFT. The agent is trained to predict the correct tool-use action at each step, maximizing the log-likelihood of the target action given the input image, question, and context history. This stage teaches the agent meaningful tool use and structured reasoning.

5. **Reinforcement Learning (RL):** Following SFT, **Group-Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)**, a ranking-based variant of PPO, is applied for further optimization.
 - For a given VQA query, the policy generates a group of K trajectories, each assigned a scalar return R.
 - A group-relative advantage is computed by normalizing rewards within the group, removing the need for a separate value function.
 - The GRPO objective uses a clipped surrogate loss to promote stable updates and explore trajectories with higher relative returns.
 - The total reward R for a trajectory combines a binary format score (r_f) for schema conformance and an LLM-graded semantic accuracy score (r_a) for the final answer. This reward function encourages well-structured tool calls while prioritizing task completion.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

WebWatcher demonstrates significant performance improvements across various challenging multimodal benchmarks, consistently outperforming proprietary and open-source baselines.

#### 4.1. Overall Performance Superiority

- **Humanity’s Last Exam (HLE-VL):** WebWatcher-32B achieves a state-of-the-art score of 13.6% (Pass@1 average), surpassing direct inference models (e.g., GPT-4o at 6.5%), RAG-based workflows (GPT-4o RAG at 12.3%), and even reasoning-specialized models like Gemini-2.5-Pro (15.8% for reasoning model, but WebWatcher-32B is 13.6% for full agent on HLE-VL, showing strong competitiveness when considering multimodal tasks). It particularly shows significant gains in domains like Biology and Physics.
- **BrowseComp-VL (BC-VL):** WebWatcher-32B achieves 27.0% (Pass@1 average), notably higher than GPT-4o RAG (13.4%) and OmniSearch (16.3%). This highlights its effectiveness on a benchmark specifically designed for complex, multi-page web browsing and fine-grained visual grounding.
- **LiveVQA:** WebWatcher-32B scores 58.7%, outperforming GPT-4o RAG (34.0%) and OmniSearch (40.9%).
- **MMSearch:** WebWatcher-32B achieves 55.3%, significantly higher than GPT-4o RAG (24.1%) and OmniSearch (49.7%).
- **SimpleVQA (Perception Benchmark):** Even on this perception-focused benchmark, WebWatcher-32B achieves a competitive score of 59.0%, demonstrating robust visual reasoning capabilities beyond knowledge-intensive tasks.

#### 4.2. Adaptive Tool Usage

- Analysis of tool calls across benchmarks (Figure 5) reveals that WebWatcher flexibly adapts its tool-use strategy to the specific demands of each task.
 - **HLE:** Shows diverse tool usage (Web Text Search, Web Image Search, Code Interpreter, Visit all around 15-35%), reflecting the benchmark's varied task types.
 - **BrowseComp-VL:** Heavily relies on Web Text Search (62.1%) and Visit (23.3%), emphasizing information retrieval and web navigation.
 - **LiveVQA, MMSearch, SimpleVQA:** Shift focus towards Web Image Search (around 30-40%), while Web Text Search and Visit act as auxiliary tools, reflecting the visual-centric nature of these tasks.
- The Code Interpreter is invoked sparingly, only when genuine calculation is needed, indicating the agent's cost- and context-awareness.

#### 4.3. Importance of SFT-based Cold Start

- Experiments comparing RL training with an "Instruct" start (public instruction-following data) versus an "SFT-based cold start" (trained on high-quality trajectories) demonstrate the critical role of cold start.
- Without SFT, the agent struggles, with initial performance near zero due to tool-call format errors and strict grading.
- SFT-based cold start significantly lifts initial scores (e.g., 0.12 on HLE, 0.30 on BC-VL), providing a stable foundation for subsequent GRPO optimization.

#### 4.4. Pass@k Analysis on HLE

- The Pass@k curve for WebWatcher on HLE shows that even a single attempt (k=1) yields 13.6%, outperforming many baselines.
- Performance increases steeply with more rollouts: 20.3% at k=3 (a 50% relative improvement), 35.7% at k=16, and 41.9% at k=32.
- This indicates that WebWatcher generates diverse and mutually informative trajectories, allowing substantial accuracy boosts with multiple trials, showcasing the power of its agentic paradigm.

In summary, WebWatcher establishes itself as a leading multimodal deep research agent, demonstrating robust reasoning, adaptive tool use, and superior performance across a range of challenging benchmarks, validated by comprehensive analysis of its internal mechanisms.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

WebWatcher represents a significant step forward in the field of artificial intelligence, particularly in the realm of agentic AI and multimodal understanding. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Paving the Way for Truly Autonomous Multimodal Agents:** By effectively integrating complex vision-language reasoning with multi-tool interaction, WebWatcher moves beyond the limitations of text-centric AI agents. This brings AI closer to replicating human-like cognitive abilities, where information-seeking and problem-solving inherently involve processing both visual and textual cues. This is crucial for developing agents that can autonomously operate in dynamic, real-world digital environments, which are inherently multimodal.

2. **Elevating the Standard for Deep Research and Information Seeking:** The ability of WebWatcher to handle highly challenging, multi-hop, and visually-rich information-seeking problems opens new avenues for automated research. It can potentially assist humans in tasks requiring interpretation of scientific diagrams, analysis of complex graphics, or navigating intricate web interfaces to gather comprehensive, cross-modal evidence—tasks previously beyond the scope of purely text-based systems. This could revolutionize areas like scientific discovery, market research, and intelligent personal assistants.

3. **Advancing Multimodal Reasoning Capabilities:** The methodology developed for WebWatcher, particularly the focus on generating high-quality synthetic multimodal trajectories and leveraging a sophisticated SFT-then-RL training pipeline, provides a robust framework for enhancing multimodal reasoning. This approach trains models not just to perceive but to reason deeply, plan strategically, and synthesize information across modalities, contributing significantly to the general understanding and development of advanced multimodal LLMs.

4. **Introducing a Challenging New Benchmark (BrowseComp-VL):** The creation of BrowseComp-VL is a critical contribution to the research community. By providing a benchmark that mirrors the complexity of real-world web browsing and emphasizes both perception and superhuman information-gathering, it sets a new, higher standard for evaluating multimodal agents. This benchmark will drive future research by challenging models to develop more sophisticated planning, cross-modal inference, and tool-use capabilities.

5. **Practical Applications in Alibaba's Ecosystem:** Given its origin in Alibaba's Tongyi Lab, WebWatcher's advancements have strong potential for practical deployment within Alibaba's vast ecosystem. This could include enhancing search functionalities, improving product recommendations based on visual attributes, automating customer support by interpreting visual context, or assisting in data analysis from visually-rich reports, ultimately leading to more intelligent and efficient services.

6. **Insights into Agentic Training Paradigms:** The detailed analysis of tool usage, the indispensable role of SFT-based cold start for stable RL training, and the effectiveness of the Pass@k strategy offer valuable insights for designing and training future agentic AI systems. These findings help optimize resource allocation and training methodologies for developing robust and scalable AI agents.

In conclusion, WebWatcher is not merely an incremental improvement; it represents a foundational shift towards truly intelligent, autonomous, and multimodal AI agents, poised to tackle real-world problems with unprecedented flexibility and reasoning depth.
