# ActionMesh: Animated 3D Mesh Generation with Temporal 3D Diffusion

> [!summary] Summary
> ActionMesh, a two-stage, feed-forward model, generates animated 3D meshes with consistent topology by extending 3D latent diffusion models with temporal consistency and a temporal 3D autoencoder that predicts vertex deformations. It produces 16-frame animations in approximately 2 minutes, improving motion fidelity by up to 39% compared to previous methods.

## Problem

- Generating animated 3D objects from user inputs often results in topologically inconsistent meshes across frames, complicating downstream tasks like texturing and rigging.
- Existing 4D generation methods are computationally expensive, typically requiring long optimization loops (30-45 minutes) per scene.
- Prior approaches are often limited to specific input modalities or object categories, lacking versatility for diverse content creation workflows.

## Method

- **ActionMesh** introduces a two-stage, feed-forward generative model that leverages 3D latent ==diffusion==, specifically adapting a VecSet-based backbone like TripoSG.
- Stage I implements "temporal 3D diffusion" with inflated attention and rotary positional embeddings to generate temporally consistent 4D meshes (sequences of independent 3D shapes).
- Stage II employs a "temporal 3D autoencoder" to convert the 4D mesh into an animated 3D mesh by predicting topology-consistent vertex deformations of a reference mesh.

## Results

- ActionMesh generated 16-frame animated 3D meshes in approximately 2 minutes on a single H100 GPU, demonstrating a 10-fold speed-up compared to previous optimization-based methods (15-45 minutes).
- The model achieved a **35%** improvement in 4D reconstruction quality (CD-4D of 0.081) and a **39%** improvement in motion fidelity (CD-M of 0.148) compared to state-of-the-art approaches.
- It produces rig-free, topology-consistent animated meshes with high geometric fidelity, outperforming baselines qualitatively on the Consistent4D benchmark and generalizing to real-world videos despite synthetic training.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Extending single-frame 3D diffusion models with inflated attention and relative positional embeddings effectively enables the generation of temporally consistent 3D sequences from videos.
> - A separate feed-forward autoencoder can successfully translate a sequence of independently generated 3D shapes into a topology-consistent animated mesh by predicting vertex deformations of a fixed reference.
> - Masked generative modeling allows for conditioning on known 3D shapes, enhancing control and enabling diverse applications like motion transfer and animation extrapolation.
