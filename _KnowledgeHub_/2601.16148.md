# ActionMesh: Animated 3D Mesh Generation with Temporal 3D Diffusion

> [!summary] Summary
> ActionMesh, a two-stage, feed-forward model, generates animated 3D meshes with consistent topology by extending 3D latent diffusion models with temporal consistency and a temporal 3D autoencoder that predicts vertex deformations. It produces 16-frame animations in approximately 2 minutes, improving motion fidelity by up to 39% compared to previous methods.

## Problem

- Generating animated 3D objects from user inputs often results in topologically inconsistent meshes across frames, complicating downstream tasks like texturing and rigging.
- Existing 4D generation methods are computationally expensive, typically requiring long optimization loops (30-45 minutes) per scene.
- Prior approaches are often limited to specific input modalities or object categories, lacking versatility for diverse content creation workflows.

## Method

- **ActionMesh** introduces a two-stage, feed-forward generative model that leverages 3D latent ==diffusion==, specifically adapting a VecSet-based backbone like TripoSG.
- Stage I implements "temporal 3D diffusion" with inflated attention and rotary positional embeddings to generate temporally consistent 4D meshes (sequences of independent 3D shapes).
- Stage II employs a "temporal 3D autoencoder" to convert the 4D mesh into an animated 3D mesh by predicting topology-consistent vertex deformations of a reference mesh.

## Results

- ActionMesh generated 16-frame animated 3D meshes in approximately 2 minutes on a single H100 GPU, demonstrating a 10-fold speed-up compared to previous optimization-based methods (15-45 minutes).
- The model achieved a **35%** improvement in 4D reconstruction quality (CD-4D of 0.081) and a **39%** improvement in motion fidelity (CD-M of 0.148) compared to state-of-the-art approaches.
- It produces rig-free, topology-consistent animated meshes with high geometric fidelity, outperforming baselines qualitatively on the Consistent4D benchmark and generalizing to real-world videos despite synthetic training.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Extending single-frame 3D diffusion models with inflated attention and relative positional embeddings effectively enables the generation of temporally consistent 3D sequences from videos.
> - A separate feed-forward autoencoder can successfully translate a sequence of independently generated 3D shapes into a topology-consistent animated mesh by predicting vertex deformations of a fixed reference.
> - Masked generative modeling allows for conditioning on known 3D shapes, enhancing control and enabling diverse applications like motion transfer and animation extrapolation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The generation of animated 3D objects from user inputs is a challenging problem in computer vision with applications in video games, animated movies, commercials, and augmented/virtual reality. Previous research in this area typically exhibits several limitations: specific to limited setups (e.g., predefined input modality or object categories), reliance on long optimization loops (30-45 minutes), and output quality that may not meet production standards. A common challenge in generating dynamic 3D content (4D) is maintaining consistent topology across frames, which is crucial for downstream tasks like texturing and rigging.

The broader research landscape includes several categories of approaches:
- **3D Foundational Models:** Works such as 3DShape2VecSet, Trellis, Craftsman, CLAY, LRM, Hunyuan3D, and TripoSG have advanced single-frame 3D object generation, often employing latent diffusion over various 3D representations (neural fields, Gaussian splats, meshes). While these models provide strong frame-level geometric priors, they typically process each 3D instance independently, making it difficult to enforce temporal consistency and constant topology for animated sequences.
- **Video-to-4D via Optimization:** Methods like SV4D, CAT4D, DreamMesh4D, V2M4, and LIM focus on synthesizing dynamic 4D content by first generating multi-view or multi-frame videos and then optimizing a 4D representation. These approaches often achieve high-quality reconstructions but necessitate per-scene optimization or training, resulting in long runtimes. They frequently output representations like deformable 3D Gaussians or neural fields, which may lack explicit vertex-to-vertex correspondences or a single, consistent mesh topology.
- **Feed-forward 4D Reconstruction:** A complementary line of work aims to reconstruct or generate 4D content in a single forward pass to avoid test-time optimization. Examples include Motion2VecSets, L4GM, and 4DGT, which primarily produce sequences of 3D Gaussians or neural fields. Concurrent work like ShapeGen4D extends pretrained 3D generative models with temporal attention for video-conditioned 4D mesh prediction but does not explicitly enforce a single, globally consistent mesh topology across the sequence.
- **Animation-Ready 4D Assets:** Other research focuses on making existing 3D assets animatable by predicting rigs, skinning weights, or deformation fields (e.g., Make-It-Animatable, MagicArticulate, RigAnything, RigGS, Animate3D, Drive Any Mesh, AnimateAnyMesh). These methods concentrate on the animation aspect of pre-existing 3D models rather than the reconstruction of an animated mesh directly from raw video.

ActionMesh aims to address the limitations of existing methods by proposing a feed-forward generative model that is fast, produces production-ready 3D meshes that are rig-free and topology-consistent, and is capable of generating animated 3D objects from diverse inputs. It integrates temporal consistency directly into 3D diffusion models and explicitly generates mesh deformations to maintain a single topology, distinguishing it from approaches that rely on slower optimization or produce topologically inconsistent outputs.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this work is to automatically generate animated 3D objects from various user inputs in a computationally efficient and high-quality manner. This overarching goal is broken down into specific aims motivated by the identified limitations of existing approaches:

1. **Generate Production-Ready Animated 3D Meshes:** The model aims to produce 3D meshes "in action" that meet quality criteria suitable for professional applications like video games, animated movies, and AR/VR. This includes ensuring geometric fidelity and smooth, plausible motion.
2. **Achieve Fast, Feed-Forward Generation:** A key motivation is to overcome the long runtimes associated with optimization-based methods (often 30-45 minutes per scene). ActionMesh aims for a rapid, feed-forward process, targeting generation times in the order of minutes. This enables faster iteration cycles for content creators.
3. **Ensure Rig-Free and Topology-Consistent Outputs:** Many existing 4D generation techniques result in mesh sequences where the topology changes across frames, which complicates essential downstream tasks like consistent texturing, retargeting, and integration into existing animation pipelines. Manual rigging for complex shapes can also be challenging or impossible. ActionMesh seeks to produce outputs that maintain a constant topology throughout the animation and do not require a predefined rig, simplifying their practical application.
4. **Support Diverse Input Modalities:** Current models are often restricted to specific inputs (e.g., video-to-4D only) or object categories. A motivation for ActionMesh is to be versatile, accepting various inputs such as monocular videos, text descriptions, images combined with text prompts, or 3D meshes combined with text prompts for animation. This broadens its applicability for different content creation workflows.
5. **Leverage Existing 3D Priors:** Given the scarcity of comprehensive 3D animated datasets, the research is motivated by the need to effectively utilize the strong 3D priors available from pretrained 3D generative models. By building upon these foundational models, ActionMesh aims to achieve high quality outputs while balancing the data limitations.

To achieve these objectives, ActionMesh proposes a core video-to-4D solution that extends existing 3D diffusion models with a temporal axis, separating the independent 3D shape generation from the animation prediction of a reference mesh.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

ActionMesh operates as a two-stage, feed-forward generative model designed to produce animated 3D meshes with consistent topology from diverse inputs. The approach builds upon the 3D latent diffusion framework, specifically leveraging a VecSet-based backbone like TripoSG.

**Terminology:**
- **3D mesh (M = (V, F))**: A single 3D object represented by vertices (V) and faces (F).
- **4D mesh ({(V_k, F_k)})**: A sequence of time-varying 3D meshes where the topology (F_k) can change across frames.
- **Animated 3D mesh ({(V_k, F)})**: The target output, a sequence of 3D meshes sharing a constant topology (F), with varying vertex positions (V_k) representing deformation.

**Core Backbone: 3DShape2VecSet (and TripoSG Adaptation)**
The model extends the 3DShape2VecSet latent diffusion paradigm. This paradigm consists of:
1. **Variational Autoencoder (VAE)**: Comprising an encoder (E_3D) and a decoder (D_3D) to encode 3D shapes into a compact latent space (`z`). E_3D uses cross-attention and self-attention layers to process 3D points (or query vectors) into `z`. D_3D takes `z` and generates 3D occupancy (or signed distance) fields, which are then converted to meshes via marching cubes.
2. **Diffusion Model (G_3D)**: A diffusion transformer (e.g., Diffusion Transformer (DiT)) that predicts latents conditioned on an input signal, typically an image encoded by a frozen visual encoder (e.g., DINOv2).
ActionMesh specifically uses TripoSG as its backbone due to its performance and open-source nature, leveraging its rectified flow-based diffusion process and Fourier-embedded timestep injection.

**Stage I: Temporal 3D Diffusion**
This stage aims to generate a 4D mesh (sequence of independent meshes) from an input video while ensuring cross-frame consistency. A naive per-frame application of image-to-3D models often leads to inconsistencies like inconsistent 3D orientations or flickering geometric details.
To address this, two primary modifications are introduced to pretrained 3D diffusion models:
1. **Inflated Attention**: The self-attention layers within the diffusion model are modified to process a sequence of `N` frames simultaneously. The input tensor `X` (N frames x T tokens x D dimensions) is reshaped to flatten the frame and token dimensions (1 x N\*T tokens x D dimensions). Standard self-attention is applied, and the output is reshaped back. This allows tokens from different frames to attend to each other, promoting cross-frame synchronization. FlashAttention2 is used to manage the increased computational complexity.
2. **Rotary Positional Embedding**: To mitigate jittering and generate smoother motions, relative frame position information is injected into the inflated attention layers via rotary positional embeddings.
3. **Masked Generation**: This feature enables the model to incorporate known 3D shapes into the generation process.
 - **Training**: During training, a subset (`N_S`) of 3D latents in a sequence are randomly selected and kept noise-free (by setting their flow matching step to 0), and the diffusion loss is not applied to them.
 - **Inference**: Given initial source meshes, their latents are computed via E_3D. Before each denoising step, these "clean" source latents are copied into the noisy latent sequence, allowing the model to condition on known 3D information.
For video-to-4D inference, an off-the-shelf image-to-3D generator (like TripoSG) is first applied to a selected reference frame to obtain an initial 3D mesh, which then serves as a source for the masked temporal 3D diffusion model.

**Stage II: Temporal 3D Autoencoder**
The 4D mesh output from Stage I, while temporally consistent, still lacks a constant topology across frames. This stage converts the sequence of independent 3D shapes into an animated 3D mesh with a shared topology by predicting vertex deformations of a reference mesh. This is achieved via a feed-forward autoencoder.
1. **Encoder (E_3D)**: The frozen E_3D from the 3DShape2VecSet backbone encodes each independent mesh (from Stage I) into its corresponding shape latent `z_k`. This maintains consistency between the two stages.
2. **Decoder (D_4D)**: This decoder takes the entire sequence of shape latents `Z = {z_k}` as input. It predicts a 3D deformation field for the vertices of a predefined reference mesh.
 - It processes all tokens with self-attention layers (also inflated, with rotary embeddings for temporal consistency).
 - It predicts the displacement from a source time step (`t_i`) to a target time step (`t_j`) for a query 3D point. The time steps (`t_i`, `t_j`) are Fourier-embedded and concatenated as an extra token.
 - During training, query points are randomly sampled from the source mesh surface. During inference, the query points are the 3D vertex positions of the reference mesh.
 - Query points are augmented with their surface normals, which assists in disambiguating spatially close but topologically distinct points.

**Applications**
The masked generative modeling capability of ActionMesh enables several generative tasks beyond core video-to-4D:
- **{3D+text}-to-animation**: An input 3D mesh is rendered to an image, a video model generates an animation video based on a text prompt, and ActionMesh then animates the input 3D mesh using the generated video and the initial 3D mesh as input.
- **{Image+text}-to-4D**: An off-the-shelf image-to-3D model converts the input image to a 3D mesh, which is then used in the {3D+text}-to-animation pipeline.
- **Text-to-4D**: This can be achieved by either generating a video from text using a video model and feeding it to ActionMesh, or by generating an image from text with an image generator and then following the {image+text}-to-4D pipeline. The latter is preferred in practice.
- **Motion Transfer / Retargeting**: By applying the {3D+video}-to-animation process with a 3D object and a video of a different object, motion can be transferred if semantic correspondences are plausible.
- **Animation Extrapolation**: ActionMesh can extend animations beyond its training sequence length by splitting long videos into chunks and recursively feeding the 3D output of the last frame of a processed chunk as input for the next chunk's generation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of ActionMesh was conducted through both quantitative and qualitative comparisons against state-of-the-art methods.

**Evaluation Benchmarks and Metrics:**
- **Consistent4D**: Utilized for qualitative comparison against existing video-to-4D models.
- **ActionBench**: A new quantitative benchmark introduced by the authors, consisting of 128 animated scenes from Objaverse, released to support reproducible evaluation.
- **Quantitative Metrics (on ActionBench)**:
 - **CD-3D (Chamfer Distance - 3D)**: Measures per-frame 3D reconstruction quality. Each predicted mesh is aligned to its ground-truth counterpart using ICP, and the Chamfer distance is computed. Lower is better.
 - **CD-4D (Chamfer Distance - 4D)**: Evaluates overall 4D reconstruction quality. The entire predicted mesh sequence is aligned using a global ICP based on the first frame, and the average Chamfer distance over all frames is reported. Lower is better.
 - **CD-M (Chamfer Distance - Motion)**: Quantifies motion fidelity. After global ICP alignment, nearest-neighbor correspondences are established on the first frame and propagated across the sequence. The bidirectional distance between corresponding points in subsequent frames is then averaged. Lower is better.

**Quantitative Comparison to State-of-the-Art (Table 1):**
ActionMesh demonstrated superior performance across all quantitative metrics when compared to contemporary video-to-4D methods such as LIM, DreamMesh4D (DM4D), V2M4, ShapeGen4D (SG4D), and per-frame image-to-3D models like TripoSG and TRELLIS.
- **Speed**: ActionMesh achieved significantly faster inference times, completing a 16-frame video in approximately 2 minutes on a single H100 GPU. This is roughly a 10-fold speed-up compared to prior works, which ranged from 15 to 45 minutes.
- **Geometric Accuracy (CD-3D)**: ActionMesh achieved a CD-3D score of 0.053, representing a 5% improvement over the best prior result (0.056 from TripoSG/SG4D).
- **4D Reconstruction Quality (CD-4D)**: The model achieved a CD-4D score of 0.081, demonstrating a 35% improvement compared to the best prior (0.126 from LIM).
- **Motion Fidelity (CD-M)**: ActionMesh scored 0.148 for CD-M, an improvement of 39% over the best prior (0.243 from LIM).

**Qualitative Comparison (Figures 5 and 9 - Consistent4D):**
Qualitative results on the Consistent4D benchmark indicated that ActionMesh produces reconstructions with higher geometric fidelity, sharper details, and fewer temporal artifacts compared to baselines. While LIM and DreamMesh4D often yielded coarse geometries, and V2M4 and ShapeGen4D sometimes exhibited artifacts or partial temporal drift, ActionMesh consistently maintained stronger temporal coherence and motion fidelity.

**Real-World Video Performance (Figures 6 and 10 - DAVIS Dataset):**
Despite being trained exclusively on synthetic data, ActionMesh demonstrated robustness and generalization capabilities on real-world videos from the DAVIS dataset. It was able to capture complex motions (e.g., jumping horse, walking bear) and handle multi-part animations without requiring rigging, as illustrated by an aquarium scene.

**Motion Transfer (Figure 4):**
The model exhibited an ability to transfer motion from a source video to a different target 3D object (e.g., animating a 3D dragon with the motion of a flying bird), even without explicit training for this task. This functionality proved effective when semantic correspondences between the objects could be established.

**Comparison with AnimateAnyMesh (Figure 8 - Supplementary):**
ActionMesh demonstrated superior motion expressiveness compared to AnimateAnyMesh, a text-driven feed-forward mesh animation model. For instance, in "panda doing martial art" and "armadillo casually walking" prompts, ActionMesh generated more coordinated and natural full-body motions, attributed to its leverage of strong motion priors from video diffusion models.

**Ablation Studies (Tables 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7):**
- **Contribution of Stage II**: The temporal 3D autoencoder (Stage II) effectively preserves the 3D reconstruction quality of Stage I while producing topology-consistent animated meshes, enabling the CD-M metric.
- **Criticality of Stage I**: Removing both stages (equivalent to per-frame TripoSG) showed a significant degradation in 4D reconstruction quality, confirming that the temporal 3D diffusion (Stage I) is crucial for accurate 4D outputs.
- **Backbone Impact**: While the approach is adaptable to different VecSet backbones (e.g., Craftsman), TripoSG yielded better overall performance, indicating the influence of the underlying 3D generative model.
- **Temporal 3D Denoiser Components**: Removing rotary positional embeddings or the masked generation mechanism in Stage I led to degradations in CD-3D and CD-4D, emphasizing their importance for stable and coherent 4D generation.
- **Temporal 3D Autoencoder Components**: Augmenting query points with surface normals and injecting source/target timesteps as additional tokens for self-attention (rather than query features) improved motion fidelity.
- **Sequence Length and Autoregressive Design**: Training with a larger number of keyframes in Stage I significantly improved metrics, while Stage II was less sensitive. For autoregressive generation of long sequences, a context window of c_w = 1 was found to maximize efficiency without sacrificing reconstruction quality.
- **Reference Frame Selection**: Using the first frame as the reference generally yielded slightly better results compared to the middle or last frame, possibly due to optimal visibility for the initial 3D reconstruction.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

ActionMesh contributes to the field of 3D content generation by offering a methodology for creating animated 3D meshes with specific advantages over previous approaches.

**Technical Contributions:**
The introduction of "temporal 3D diffusion" marks a technical advancement in extending pretrained single-frame 3D diffusion models to generate temporally consistent 3D sequences. The design of a feed-forward temporal 3D autoencoder that translates independent 3D shapes into topology-consistent deformations of a reference mesh is a novel approach for achieving animation with fixed connectivity. This two-stage framework effectively leverages strong pre-existing 3D priors while addressing the challenge of temporal consistency.

**Practical Utility:**
- **Speed and Efficiency:** By achieving high-fidelity shape and motion generation in approximately 2 minutes, ActionMesh significantly reduces the iteration time compared to optimization-based methods (which often take 15-45 minutes). This speed can enable more rapid prototyping and content creation workflows in industries such as video game development and film production.
- **Production-Ready Assets:** The generation of rig-free and topology-consistent animated meshes simplifies downstream processes. Constant topology ensures that mesh attributes like textures remain coherent throughout the animation, and the absence of a required rig broadens the applicability to complex or unconventional shapes. These properties are beneficial for seamless integration into various 3D applications.
- **Versatile Input Modalities:** The ability to generate animated 3D meshes from diverse inputs—including text prompts, images with text, 3D models with text, and videos—positions ActionMesh as a flexible tool for a wider range of content creation scenarios. This multi-modal capability can reduce the barriers for users with different starting assets or preferences.
- **Enhanced Motion Expressiveness:** The model's capacity to produce animated motions with greater expressiveness, attributed to its use of strong motion priors derived from video diffusion models, offers a higher quality of animation compared to some prior feed-forward methods.

**Broader Impact and Future Directions:**
ActionMesh demonstrates a viable path toward automating the generation of complex animated 3D assets, potentially contributing to the democratization of 3D content creation. By effectively "lifting" everyday videos into 4D representations, the work opens avenues for learning robust geometric motion priors directly from large-scale video corpora. This could foster deeper integration between video understanding and mesh-native reasoning, facilitating more generalized 4D understanding and generation.

**Acknowledged Limitations:**
The authors identify two main limitations:
1. **Topological Changes:** The current model assumes fixed connectivity and cannot inherently model changes in topology (e.g., an object breaking apart or merging). Addressing this could involve exploring topology-aware latent updates or mesh surgery techniques.
2. **Strong Occlusions:** While the model can hallucinate missing parts, it sometimes struggles with reconstructing occluded regions, particularly when these regions are absent in the initial reference frame or become fully obscured during complex motion. Future work might focus on improved occlusion handling or robust inference under partial observations.
