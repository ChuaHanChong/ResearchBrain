# ArtiTwinSplat: Interactable Digital Twin Reconstruction via Gaussian Splatting from RGB-D videos

> [!summary] Summary
> ArtiTwinSplat builds articulated, photorealistic digital twins of objects from handheld RGB-D video with no CAD models or manual labels. It reconstructs a canonical 3D Gaussian Splatting scene, detects the moving part by appearance change, propagates SAM2 masks backward in time, fits revolute or prismatic joints by 4D RANSAC on 3D point tracks, then fine-tunes Gaussians conditioned on those joints, exporting simulation-ready URDFs for Isaac Sim.

## Problem

- Off-the-shelf NeRF and 3D Gaussian Splatting reconstruction assumes static scenes, so it fails on objects whose parts move during capture.
- Prior articulated reconstruction needs manual part annotations, pre-segmented inputs, or multi-state and multi-view rigs rarely available in real robot deployment.
- Learning-based articulation inference depends on large annotated datasets and generalizes poorly to novel real-world scenes outside training categories.

## Method

- Change detection compares rendered canonical views against post-change frames, seeding ==SAM2== semi-supervised video segmentation propagated in reverse time from the final, least-occluded frame.
- Joint parameter estimation lifts masked pixels to ==TAPIP3D== 3D point trajectories, then ==4D RANSAC== fits revolute and prismatic hypotheses recovering type, axis, pivot, per-frame variable, and motion range.
- ==Two-phase articulation-conditioned Gaussian optimization==: Phase I trains part and interior Gaussians under the joint transform with photometric, depth, and consistency losses; Phase II unfreezes all Gaussians to refine global radiance.

## Results

- Evaluated on **3** real household IKEA Kallax scenes captured by handheld iPhone Pro via Record3D, covering revolute, prismatic, and mixed articulation with no capture rig.
- Renderings at novel joint configurations unseen during optimization stay photorealistic in RGB and depth; prismatic drawers reconstruct most accurately thanks to persistent interior visibility.
- Reconstructions export directly as URDFs with collision meshes and joint constraints, loading into Isaac Sim; revolute interiors show minor artifacts from occlusion-induced incomplete masks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Reverse-time mask propagation from the frame where the part is most visible yields cleaner temporal segmentation than forward tracking from ambiguous early frames.
> - Motion observed during a casual handheld capture is itself sufficient supervision for part discovery, replacing annotations, part labels, and category-specific kinematic priors.
> - Pipelines stacked on foundation models inherit their failure modes: weak texture or small motion degrades segmentation and tracking, corrupting downstream joint estimates.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The reconstruction of articulated objects, such as drawers, doors, and cabinets, is an area of increasing importance in both computer vision and robotics. These objects are prevalent in human environments, making their accurate modeling essential for tasks ranging from scene understanding to physical manipulation by robots. High-fidelity digital twins that capture an object's appearance, geometry, and articulation are considered valuable for applications like robust sim-to-real transfer in embodied AI and learning-based reasoning in interactive 3D environments.

However, articulated object reconstruction presents challenges due to the interconnected nature of geometry, appearance, and part-level motion. Recovering these aspects jointly from dynamic observations is an ill-posed problem. Existing off-the-shelf reconstruction methods, such as Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) and 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS), are typically designed for static scenes and do not generalize directly to articulated settings. Even when assuming rigid-body part motion, estimating part geometry and kinematic parameters from a single moving camera remains a significantly underconstrained problem.

Prior approaches addressing articulated object reconstruction often rely on specific conditions or external data. Some methods use parametric or skeleton-based representations that require prior knowledge of joint structures. Learning-based approaches like Real2Code and ArticulateAnything infer articulation parameters from visual input but often depend on large, annotated datasets and may exhibit limited generalization to novel real-world scenarios. Other techniques, such as PARIS, aim to jointly model geometry and motion by decomposing objects into parts, but they typically require pre-segmented inputs or access to multiple distinct object states, which are conditions not always met in unconstrained dynamic captures. More recent 3DGS-based methods, including ArtGS and SplArt, achieve high-quality articulation modeling but similarly necessitate multi-view or multi-state observations and explicit part supervision. Extensions to 3DGS, such as Deformable 3DGS and Shape-of-Motion, adapt the framework for non-rigid and dynamic settings by tracking 3D point trajectories.

ArtiTwinSplat positions itself within this landscape by addressing the gap of reconstructing articulated objects automatically from unconstrained RGB-D videos without requiring manual annotations, pre-segmented inputs, or discrete articulation states. It aims to provide a pipeline that unifies appearance-based motion discovery, joint parameter estimation, and articulation-conditioned Gaussian optimization to produce interactive and simulation-ready articulated digital twins from real-world observations.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of ArtiTwinSplat is to develop a framework that can automatically construct articulated, photo-realistic digital twins of objects directly from RGB-D videos. This goal is motivated by several challenges and needs in robotics and embodied AI:

1. **Addressing the bottleneck of model construction:** Deploying robots in unstructured real-world environments necessitates accurate, interactive models of objects. However, constructing these models at scale currently represents a significant bottleneck for robotic system integration. The authors aim to provide a practical and scalable pathway for digital twin construction to alleviate this issue.

2. **Eliminating reliance on external data or manual effort:** Existing methods often require CAD models, simulation assets, or manual annotations for part labels, segmentation, or joint structures. A key motivation for ArtiTwinSplat is to remove these prerequisites, making the process fully automatic and annotation-free.

3. **Joint recovery of geometry and kinematics:** The problem of recovering both the geometric structure and the kinematic parameters (articulation) of objects simultaneously from dynamic observations is inherently complex and often ill-posed. The framework aims to solve this by discovering part structure and joint kinematics solely from observed motion.

4. **Achieving high fidelity and realism:** The objective is to preserve both geometric fidelity and photometric realism in the reconstructed digital twins. This is crucial for their utility in downstream applications that demand accurate visual and physical representations.

5. **Enabling interactive and queryable models:** The reconstructed digital twins are intended to be stable and queryable, supporting real-time rendering, viewpoint control, and interactive manipulation. This functionality is vital for tasks such as robot planning and human-robot collaboration.

6. **Providing simulation-ready outputs:** A specific motivation is to produce digital twins that are immediately usable by downstream robot planning and learning systems. This includes the capability to export models in formats compatible with physics simulators, thereby facilitating robust sim-to-real transfer for embodied agents.

In summary, the core motivation is to lower the integration barrier for articulated object manipulation in embodied AI and human-robot collaboration contexts by offering a robust, automated, and scalable method for creating functional digital twins from readily available RGB-D video data.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

ArtiTwinSplat employs a four-stage pipeline to reconstruct articulated digital twins from RGB-D videos. The process begins with a static pre-change RGB-D sequence and a dynamic post-change RGB-D video, culminating in an interactive, articulated 3DGS digital twin.

**Stage I: Canonical Scene Reconstruction**
The initial step involves establishing a static representation of the scene. A standard Structure-from-Motion (SfM) pipeline is applied to the static pre-change RGB-D sequence to estimate camera poses and generate a sparse point cloud. This point cloud serves as the basis for initializing a 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) model, which functions as both the geometric and photometric reference for all subsequent stages. Additionally, the pre-change sequence establishes a canonical coordinate frame. The camera poses of the dynamic post-change frames are then localized against this canonical point cloud to ensure a consistent coordinate system across both sequences.

**Stage II: Change Detection and Part Tracking**
This stage identifies and tracks the moving articulated part within the dynamic sequence.
1. **Change Detection:** Changes indicative of articulation are detected by comparing rendered views from the canonical 3DGS model against frames from the post-change RGB-D video. This comparison is performed in both appearance and geometry domains, leveraging dense feature embeddings as described in. This process yields an initial, coarse change mask, typically generated at the final frame of the post-change sequence.
2. **Part Tracking:** To achieve temporally consistent part masks across the entire dynamic sequence, including instances of occlusion or partial visibility, SAM2 is utilized in a semi-supervised video object segmentation mode. The segmentation process is initialized with the coarse change mask obtained from the final post-change frame (t=T) and propagated backward in time to t=0. This reverse propagation strategy is adopted because the articulated part is often most distinctly visible and least occluded at its final manipulated state. The output is a series of dense, per-frame object masks for the articulated component.

**Stage III: Joint Parameter Estimation**
This stage extracts the kinematic parameters of the articulated object.
1. **3D Point Trajectory Generation:** Using the per-frame SAM2 masks, query points are sampled within the identified articulated region. These points are then tracked across the entire RGB-D sequence using TAPIP3D, a method designed to produce dense 3D point trajectories, denoted as p_i(t) ∈ R^3, for each point i over time t. These trajectories represent persistent 3D correspondences in world coordinates.
2. **Joint Hypothesis Fitting:** The spatio-temporal trajectories are subsequently processed by a 4D RANSAC procedure. This algorithm jointly fits both revolute (hinge-like) and prismatic (sliding) joint hypotheses. It operates by sampling minimal sets of trajectories, computing inlier consensus for potential joint models, and selecting the model that exhibits the highest inlier ratio and the lowest residual error.
3. **Parameter Output and Stabilization:** The estimator automatically outputs the identified joint type (revolute or prismatic), the joint axis (a), the pivot point (c), the per-frame articulation variable (q(t)), and the estimated range of motion ([q_min, q_max]). This entire process is performed without requiring manual annotation, part labels, or category-specific priors. Raw estimates undergo further refinement through outlier filtering and temporal smoothing to enhance stability before being passed to the final optimization stage.

**Stage IV: Articulation-Conditioned Gaussian Optimization**
The recovered joint parameters inform a two-phase fine-tuning of the 3DGS model, which was initially reconstructed in Stage I.
1. **Phase I (Dynamic Supervision):** The dynamic post-change RGB-D frames are used for supervision. The scene Gaussians are partitioned into three subsets: background (G_bg), interior (G_interior), and moving part (G_part). Only the Gaussians belonging to G_interior ∪ G_part are designated as trainable. The Gaussians within the moving part (G_part) are transformed at each timestep t according to the recovered joint parameters:
 - For revolute joints: x'(t) = R_a,q(t)(x - c) + c
 - For prismatic joints: x'(t) = x + q(t) * a
 where R_a,q(t) is the rotation matrix around axis a by angle q(t), x is the original Gaussian position, and c is the pivot point. These transformed Gaussians are then optimized using a combination of photometric, depth, and joint consistency losses, primarily within the region defined by the SAM2 mask.
2. **Phase II (Global Scene Refinement):** Both the static pre-change and dynamic post-change frames are utilized to refine the global scene radiance. All Gaussian subsets (G_bg, G_interior, G_part) are unfrozen and optimized under weak photometric supervision. During this phase, the learned articulation parameters {a, c, q(t)} remain fixed. This ensures that consistent illumination and appearance continuity are maintained across the entire scene.

The final output is an articulated 3DGS model that supports real-time rendering and interactive joint control at arbitrary configurations. The model can also be exported as a Universal Robot Description Format (URDF) file, making it compatible with physics simulators like Isaac Sim.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of ArtiTwinSplat was conducted on real-world household scenes to assess its performance in reconstructing articulated digital twins.

**Evaluation Setup:**
- **Scenes:** Three distinct real-world household scenes were used, each featuring IKEA Kallax furniture units. These scenes represented common articulated object types: revolute joints (hinged cabinet doors), prismatic joints (drawers), and combinations thereof.
- **Data Capture:** Data was captured using a handheld Apple iPhone Pro and the Record3D application. This setup provided synchronized RGB frames, metric depth maps, and camera poses via ARKit, without requiring specialized capture rigs.
- **Recording Protocol:** Each scene involved two separate recordings: a static "pre-change" sequence (before any object interaction) and a "dynamic post-change" sequence (during which the articulated object was manipulated by hand). Standard SfM-based refinement was applied to the pre-change sequence to establish a canonical coordinate frame.

**Qualitative Results (as shown in Figure 2):**
- **Articulation Isolation:** The method consistently demonstrated the ability to reliably isolate the moving components of the articulated objects from the background.
- **Joint Parameter Recovery:** Physically consistent joint parameters (type, axis, pivot, range of motion) were recovered across all three scenes, encompassing both revolute and prismatic joint types.
- **Photorealistic Rendering:** The reconstructed models were capable of synthesizing photorealistic renderings even under novel articulation configurations that were not observed during the optimization process. This indicates a generalization capability in appearance reconstruction.
- **Geometric Fidelity:**
 - **Prismatic Joints:** Reconstruction of prismatic joints (e.g., drawers) showed notable accuracy, which the authors attribute to the consistent visibility of interior surfaces throughout their motion.
 - **Revolute Joints:** Revolute joints (e.g., hinged doors) were reconstructed faithfully on their exterior surfaces. However, occasional minor artifacts were observed on interior surfaces. These artifacts are suggested to stem from partial occlusion during rotation, which can lead to incomplete SAM2 mask coverage and thus insufficient supervision for the Gaussians representing those interior regions.
- **Annotation-Free Operation:** The entire process was conducted directly from handheld RGB-D capture without the need for manual annotations, demonstrating an automatic workflow.

**Simulation-Ready Output (as shown in Figure 3):**
- **URDF Export:** A significant finding is the direct exportability of the reconstructed models as URDFs (Universal Robot Description Format) that are compatible with physics simulators such as NVIDIA Isaac Sim.
- **Functionality in Simulation:** The exported digital twins included collision meshes, correctly defined revolute and prismatic joint constraints, and kinematic parameters derived from ArtiTwinSplat. This capability provides a practical workflow for generating deployment-ready robotic assets directly from real-world RGB-D captures.

In summary, the results indicate that ArtiTwinSplat can reconstruct articulated objects from unconstrained video data, producing models that are visually realistic, kinematically accurate, and directly usable in robotic simulation environments.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

ArtiTwinSplat represents a contribution to the field of articulated object reconstruction and digital twin creation, particularly for applications in robotics and embodied AI. Its significance stems from several key aspects:

1. **Automation and Scalability:** The framework provides a fully unsupervised and automatic pathway for constructing articulated digital twins. By eliminating the need for CAD models, manual annotations, pre-segmented inputs, or controlled multi-view capture setups, it addresses a critical bottleneck in scaling the creation of object models for robotic systems. This automation has the potential to accelerate the development and deployment of robots in complex, unstructured environments.

2. **Practical Workflow for Robotics:** The ability to export reconstructed models directly as URDFs compatible with physics simulators like Isaac Sim creates a practical and streamlined workflow from real-world RGB-D captures to simulation-ready robotic assets. This functionality is crucial for robotic manipulation, planning, and learning systems, enabling more robust sim-to-real transfer and facilitating the development of learning-based agents that operate in interactive 3D environments.

3. **Advancement in Reconstruction from Dynamic Observations:** The method unifies appearance-based motion discovery, joint parameter estimation, and articulation-conditioned Gaussian optimization within a single pipeline. This integration allows for the joint recovery of geometry and kinematics from dynamic RGB-D videos, tackling an inherently ill-posed problem without relying on strong priors or supervision.

4. **Accessibility of High-Fidelity Models:** By leveraging readily available handheld RGB-D capture devices (e.g., iPhone Pro), ArtiTwinSplat makes the creation of high-fidelity, interactive digital twins more accessible. This lowers the integration barrier for researchers and developers working on articulated object manipulation in contexts like embodied AI and human-robot collaboration.

**Limitations and Future Work:**
The paper acknowledges several limitations, primarily inherited from its reliance on upstream foundation models:
- **Segmentation Accuracy:** SAM2, while powerful, may exhibit over- or under-segmentation in regions with low texture or weak visual boundaries, impacting the accuracy of part masks.
- **Tracking Robustness:** TAPIP3D can produce noisy correspondences under conditions of low texture or minimal motion, which can degrade the precision of joint parameter estimation.
- **Interior Surface Reconstruction:** Reconstructing interior surfaces that become visible only during revolute articulation remains challenging due to limited visibility during capture and insufficient Gaussian supervision.
- **Geometric Fidelity of Thin Structures:** Sensor depth noise can reduce the geometric fidelity for thin structures, affecting the accuracy of the digital twin.

Future work outlined by the authors includes addressing these limitations through:
- Joint multi-part optimization, which could potentially handle more complex articulated objects.
- Custom Gaussian densification strategies, aimed at improving reconstruction quality, particularly for challenging regions like interior surfaces.
- Tighter integration of 3D segmentation priors, which could enhance the robustness and accuracy of part tracking and identification.

Overall, ArtiTwinSplat offers a method for creating functional digital twins from real-world observations, with the potential to improve the efficiency and effectiveness of robotics research and development in articulated object interaction.
