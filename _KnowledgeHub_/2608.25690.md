# Trust-Aware Sequential Decision Making and Rollout Planning for Resilient Multi-Robot Systems

> [!summary] Summary
> Trust-aware sequential planning for online multi-robot routing under localization spoofing. A monitor fuses probabilistic localization trust, calibrated on real GPS spoofing data, with behavioral evidence from pickups, expirations, and assignment churn to classify agents and remove adversaries from the planning state before one-at-a-time rollout. On San Francisco taxicab demand this restores routing stability and rollout's empirical advantage over the IA-RA base policy.

## Problem

- Routing and rollout methods assume reported agent states are reliable and that agents execute prescribed actions, so compromised agents break both planning assumptions at once.
- Existing routing attack models ignore monitor-aware adversaries that deliberately bound their reported displacement to stay undetectable while still attracting and then dropping assignments.
- Localization-integrity detection is studied separately from planning: a detector emits an integrity score, but the planner keeps treating every reported agent as cooperative.

## Method

- Monitor-aware ==distance-constrained spoofing== model with tiered target prioritization and coordinated ==bipartite matching==, letting adversaries maximize assignment influence subject to a bounded reported-position displacement.
- A fused monitor combining ==localization trust==, calibrated from GPS signal features by ==Gaussian== and ==Beta== fits with windowed temporal aggregation, and ==Beta-posterior== behavioral trust over pickups, expirations, and assignment churn.
- ==Trust-aware one-at-a-time rollout== over a trusted planning state: classified adversaries are removed from the active fleet before lookahead, restoring planner-execution consistency in the simulated dynamics.

## Results

- A single adversarial agent destabilizes a **35-agent** cooperative fleet otherwise stable under IA-RA, with policy cost growing throughout the **2880-step**, two-day simulation averaged over **100** demand realizations.
- The fused monitor yields the lowest routing cost and fewest adversaries remaining active across spoofing bounds of **0.1** and **0.5** graph diameter; the behavior-only monitor produces **zero** cooperative false positives.
- Horizon and capacity govern recovery: **H=10** stays unstable even when monitored, while **H=30** with **49** cooperative agents lets trust-aware rollout beat monitored IA-RA on cost, backlog, and cancellations.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Trust pays off when it edits the planner's state representation, not when it only emits a detection score beside an unchanged planning loop.
> - Lookahead amplifies model error: one undetected non-executing agent can make rollout worse than its own base policy, voiding the usual cost-improvement property.
> - Detection is not repair. Backlog accrued before removal persists, so recovery needs surplus fleet capacity and a long enough planning horizon.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work is positioned at the intersection of several critical areas in robotics and artificial intelligence: adversarial multi-robot routing, localization spoofing and trust estimation, rollout-based sequential decision-making, and resilient multi-robot systems.

Traditional sequential decision-making algorithms for multi-robot systems, prevalent in applications such as autonomous transportation and logistics, typically assume reliable planning information and cooperative agent execution. However, real-world scenarios present vulnerabilities such as cyber-attacks, localization spoofing, and hardware faults, which can compromise these assumptions. This introduces a significant mismatch between the system model used for planning and the actual physical execution, leading to inefficiencies, delays, and system instability.

Existing literature addresses various aspects of this problem:
- **Resilient task allocation** often focuses on environmental uncertainty, robot failures, or degraded execution, but generally does not account for adversaries actively manipulating information for assignment decisions.
- **Information manipulation** in online routing, particularly localization spoofing, has been shown to destabilize reassignment-based policies. However, prior adversarial models often do not consider "monitor-aware" adversaries that strategically limit their spoofing magnitude to reduce detectability.
- **Localization spoofing detection** methods typically treat integrity estimation and downstream planning as separate problems. A detector provides an integrity estimate, but the planning algorithm continues to operate independently, often without adjusting its model based on this trust information.
- **Rollout-based planning**, a model-based reinforcement learning approach, relies on its predictive model accurately representing the executing system. When compromised agents violate this consistency, the nominal cost-improvement property of rollout may not hold, potentially leading to suboptimal or degraded performance compared to its base policy.

This paper addresses a gap by developing a comprehensive framework that integrates trust estimation directly into the sequential planning loop. It moves beyond treating trust solely as a detection score and instead uses it to construct a "trusted planning state." This approach explicitly tackles the planner-execution mismatch by filtering out unreliable agents from the planning process, thereby allowing model-based planning algorithms like rollout to operate on a more accurate representation of the active, cooperative fleet. The introduction of a monitor-aware adversarial model further refines the understanding of sophisticated attacks, where detectability and influence are strategically balanced.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the disparity between the idealized assumptions of multi-robot planning algorithms and the realities of operating in potentially compromised environments. When planning information is unreliable or agents deviate from prescribed actions, a "planner-execution mismatch" arises, which can lead to inefficient task allocation, wasted resources, increased service delays, and system instability, particularly in online routing where decisions are continuously recomputed.

The paper identifies three key challenges that motivate its specific objectives:

1. **Developing a threat model for monitor-aware adversaries:** Existing adversarial models often assume unconstrained spoofing. The authors are motivated to model adversaries who recognize that large localization discrepancies increase detectability. Therefore, a new model is needed that captures adversaries strategically operating under spoofing constraints, balancing their influence over routing decisions with their desire to remain undetected.
2. **Integrating complementary evidence for trust estimation:** Relying solely on localization evidence may be insufficient to detect subtle or adaptive adversarial deviations. The motivation is to combine probabilistic localization trust, calibrated using real-world data, with behavioral evidence derived from task execution outcomes (e.g., successful pickups, assignment churn, request expirations). This multi-modal evidence aims to provide a more robust and comprehensive assessment of agent reliability.
3. **Incorporating trust estimates into the planning loop:** Traditionally, trust estimation and planning are separate. The authors are motivated to design a framework where trust assessments directly influence subsequent planning decisions. Specifically, agents deemed unreliable should be removed from the active planning set to prevent them from corrupting future routing and lookahead processes, thereby restoring consistency between the planning model and physical execution.

Against this background, the key objectives of this work are to:

- **Introduce a monitor-aware, distance-constrained spoofing model and a tiered bipartite matching strategy** for adversaries, which quantifies the trade-off between adversarial influence on routing and the detectability of spoofing.
- **Develop a closed-loop trust-aware sequential planning framework** for resilient online multi-robot routing. This framework aims to combine localization and behavioral trust to classify agents (cooperative, suspect, adversarial) and construct a "trusted planning state" by removing detected adversaries from subsequent planning steps.
- **Analyze the interaction between trust and rollout-based planning.** The objective is to demonstrate how undetected adversaries cause a planner-execution mismatch, invalidating rollout's nominal cost-improvement behavior, and to show how trust-aware removal of adversaries can restore this consistency, thereby recovering rollout's empirical advantage over its base policy.
- **Empirically validate the proposed framework** using real GPS spoofing datasets and large-scale autonomous pickup-and-delivery simulations based on San Francisco taxicab demand data, across diverse adversarial capabilities, fleet sizes, monitoring configurations, and rollout horizons.

Overall, the work aims to enhance the resilience of multi-robot systems by proactively adapting the planning model based on real-time reliability assessments, rather than reactively dealing with the consequences of misinformed decisions.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The paper proposes a **trust-aware sequential planning framework** designed to mitigate planner-execution mismatch in multi-robot systems operating under localization spoofing. The methodology consists of a closed-loop architecture with three interacting functions: information assessment, trust-aware state construction and planning, and execution.

**A. Trust-Aware Sequential Planning Framework (Section IV)**
The framework introduces a **trusted planning state ($\tilde{x}_t$)**, which is derived from the perceived system state ($\hat{x}_t$) after a trust-aware filtering operation ($F$). This filtering step uses agent trust classifications ($\chi_i(t) \in \{C, S, A\}$ for cooperative, suspect, adversarial) to determine which agents remain in the planning set. The sequential planner then computes actions based on this filtered state, and the execution outcomes provide new evidence for subsequent trust updates, forming a feedback loop.
For online routing, the filtering operation constructs an **active planning fleet ($L_t^+$)** by removing agents classified as adversarial from the currently participating fleet ($L_t$).

**B. Monitor-Aware Adversarial Spoofing Model (Section V)**
To evaluate the framework under strategic interference, a specialized adversarial model is introduced:
- **Distance-Constrained Spoofing:** Adversarial agents ($a \in A_t$) manipulate their reported location ($\hat{\nu}_a^t$) but restrict its displacement to be within a maximum graph distance ($d_{max}$) from their true location ($\nu_a^t$), i.e., $d_G(\nu_a^t, \hat{\nu}_a^t) \le d_{max}$. This models monitor-aware adversaries who trade routing influence for reduced detectability.
- **Coordinated Adversarial Strategy:** Adversaries target "cooperative-feasible" requests ($R_{cf}^t$) which are unpicked requests reachable by at least one cooperative agent. They prioritize these targets using a **tiered matching strategy**:
 1. Maximize the number of simultaneously targeted requests.
 2. Among maximum-cardinality matchings, prioritize requests in lower distance tiers (closer to cooperative service, $\alpha_r(t)$).
 3. Minimize the total localization displacement to realize the attack.
This lexicographic optimization is solved as a weighted bipartite matching problem (Equation 22). If an adversary is matched to a request, it spoofs its location to the request's pickup location; otherwise, it reports its true location. The attack adapts at each decision epoch.

**C. Trust Evidence and Online Monitoring (Section VI)**
The framework combines two complementary sources of evidence for agent classification:
- **Localization Trust:**
 - Models reliability of reported positions using **Beta distributions** ($Beta(\alpha_{loc}, \beta_{loc})$).
 - **Calibration:** Trained on real GPS spoofing data (aerial and ground vehicles). Signal-level features are modeled with class-conditional Gaussian distributions, posterior confidence scores are derived via Quadratic Discriminant Analysis (QDA), and these scores are fit to class-specific Beta distributions (Fig. 4).
 - **Discrepancy-Dependent Trust:** In online operation, localization trust is interpolated between a cooperative and an adversarial Beta distribution based on the normalized localization discrepancy ($\Delta_{i,t} = d_G(\nu_{i,ref}^t, \hat{\nu}_i^t) / D(G)$). This makes smaller spoofing deviations result in trust observations closer to nominal.
 - **Temporal Aggregation:** Individual localization trust observations ($z_{loc}_{i,t}$) are averaged over a sliding window ($W_{loc}$) to produce a score ($\bar{z}_{loc}_{i,t}$), which is then compared against predefined thresholds ($\kappa_{loc}^{adv}, \kappa_{loc}^{coop}$) to yield a **localization classification ($\chi_{loc}_i(t)$)**.
- **Behavioral Trust:**
 - Evaluates an agent's consistency with cooperative service based on **request-level outcomes**.
 - Each agent maintains a Beta distribution ($Beta(\alpha_{beh}, \beta_{beh})$), where $\alpha_{beh}$ accumulates evidence of compliant behavior and $\beta_{beh}$ accumulates evidence of harmful behavior.
 - **Responsibility Attribution:**
 - Successful pickup: servicing agent receives positive evidence ($w_{pick}$).
 - Request expiration: most recently assigned agent receives a penalty ($w_{exp}$).
 - Assignment churn: agents who were assigned and should have picked up a request before its termination, but did not complete service, receive a penalty ($w_{churn}$).
 - A **behavioral classification ($\chi_{beh}_i(t)$)** is assigned based on the posterior mean of the Beta distribution, considering accumulated evidence weight and posterior confidence thresholds.
- **Trust Fusion and Agent Classification:** The local classifications from both branches are fused: an agent is classified as **Adversarial (A)** if strong evidence from *either* localization or behavioral trust suggests it; **Cooperative (C)** if *both* branches agree; otherwise, it remains **Suspect (S)**.
- **Enforcement:** Agents classified as adversarial are permanently removed from the active planning fleet ($L_t^+$). The perceived state ($\hat{x}_t$) is then restricted to this trusted fleet to form $\hat{x}_t^+$, which is the routing-specific trusted planning state.

**D. Trust-Aware Rollout Planning (Section VII)**
The planning component uses **Rollout** with **Instantaneous Assignment with Reassignment (IA-RA)** as the base policy.
- **Rollout Mechanism:** At each decision epoch, after monitoring and fleet update, rollout evaluates candidate actions ($u_t$) by simulating future system dynamics over a finite horizon ($H$) using multiple Monte Carlo demand scenarios ($M$). IA-RA is used to generate actions in subsequent simulated stages. The cost-to-go ($\hat{Q}_H$) is estimated, and the action minimizing this cost is selected.
- **Planner-Execution Mismatch in Rollout:** A central point is that if undetected adversarial agents remain in $L_t^+$, the rollout's internal model assumes they will execute commanded actions, while in reality, they may deviate. This divergence between simulated and physical dynamics can degrade rollout's performance.
- **Trust-Aware One-at-a-Time Rollout:** To manage computational complexity, a one-at-a-time rollout procedure is used. It starts with the IA-RA action and iteratively improves the action for each agent in $L_t^+$ while holding others fixed.
- **Effect of Trust:** Trust-aware removal of adversaries from $L_t^+$ aims to align the agents represented in the rollout model with those expected to execute cooperatively. This reduces the planner-execution mismatch, ideally restoring rollout's ability to improve upon the base policy.

The entire process is summarized in Algorithm 3, emphasizing the closed-loop nature where observations feed trust updates, which then inform the planning state, leading to executed actions that generate new observations.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The empirical evaluation of the trust-aware planning framework was conducted using a pickup-and-delivery simulation on a San Francisco road network, driven by real taxicab demand data, and leveraging real GPS spoofing datasets for trust calibration.

- **Empirical Validation of Localization-Trust Evidence (Section VIII-B):**
 - Analysis of real GPS spoofing data demonstrated that signal-level features produce distinct localization-confidence distributions for cooperative and spoofed operations.
 - However, increasingly "stealthy" attacks, designed to minimize observable deviations, resulted in localization-trust distributions that were less distinguishable from cooperative behavior, highlighting the limitation of relying solely on localization evidence.

- **Routing Impact and Detectability of Monitor-Aware Spoofing (Section VIII-C):**
 - Without monitoring, IA-RA policies exhibited **sustained growth in policy cost (outstanding and canceled requests)**, indicating a loss of stability, even with a single adversarial agent.
 - Monitor-aware, distance-constrained spoofing (Fig. 8) still allowed adversaries to significantly degrade routing performance, demonstrating that restricting spoofing radius does not eliminate the disruptive impact.
 - The experiments confirmed the **influence-detectability trade-off**: smaller spoofing distances (e.g., $d_{max}=0.1D(G)$) resulted in adversarial localization trust values closer to those of cooperative agents, making them more difficult to detect from localization information alone.

- **Complementary Value of Localization and Behavioral Evidence (Section VIII-D):**
 - Comparison of localization-only, behavioral-only, and fused monitors (Figs. 9-11) showed that the **fused monitor achieved the best performance**, resulting in the lowest routing cost and the fewest active adversarial agents remaining.
 - Localization evidence was effective for detecting large spoofing deviations earlier.
 - Behavioral evidence provided a complementary detection capability, identifying adversaries whose localization deviations were subtle but who consistently failed to execute assigned tasks, thus compensating for the limitations of localization-only approaches against monitor-aware attacks. The fused monitor maintained a low cooperative-agent false-positive rate.

- **Trust-Aware Rollout Under Planner-Execution Mismatch (Section VIII-E):**
 - **Adversarial Removal and Planning-Execution Alignment:** Monitoring effectively reduced the number of active adversarial agents over time, directly modifying the planning problem by progressively aligning the rollout model with the actual cooperative fleet (Fig. 12).
 - **Outstanding Demand:** Trust-aware rollout and monitored IA-RA generally reduced outstanding requests compared to their unmonitored counterparts. Trust-aware rollout could achieve comparable or improved outstanding request levels relative to monitored IA-RA (Fig. 13), indicating recovery in current service responsiveness.
 - **Cancellations and Transient Mismatch:** Unmonitored policies showed continuous accumulation of cancellations. Monitoring stabilized cancellation rates after adversarial removal, but the cumulative effect of early service failures persisted in the overall cost (Fig. 14).
 - **Routing Cost and Recovery:** Trust-aware rollout demonstrated improved stability and reduced overall policy cost (current backlog plus cumulative cancellations) compared to unmonitored rollout. The initial accumulated cost during the detection phase could be substantial, influencing the overall reported cost even after adversarial removal and subsequent recovery (Fig. 15).

- **Effect of Planning Horizon and Cooperative Capacity (Section VIII-F):**
 - **Planning Horizon:** Longer rollout horizons (e.g., H=20 vs. H=10) were crucial for achieving stability and effective recovery. Short horizons (H=10) could remain unstable even with monitoring, as the planner might not adequately account for downstream consequences (Fig. 16).
 - **Cooperative Capacity:** With increased cooperative fleet capacity (e.g., 49 agents instead of 35), trust-aware rollout achieved lower routing costs, outstanding requests, and cancellations compared to monitored IA-RA, demonstrating a more pronounced benefit from rollout when greater resources are available to recover from initial disruption (Fig. 17).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research offers a significant contribution to the field of resilient multi-robot systems and sequential decision-making, particularly in the context of cyber-physical attacks.

**Significance:**

- **Integrated Resilience Framework:** The paper introduces a novel, closed-loop framework that explicitly integrates real-time reliability assessment (trust) into the planning process. Unlike previous approaches that treat detection and planning as separate, this work demonstrates how trust can be operationalized to modify the *planning representation itself*, rather than merely serving as an auxiliary detection signal. This is a foundational shift in designing resilient decision-making systems.
- **Addressing Planner-Execution Mismatch:** The core theoretical and empirical contribution lies in identifying and mitigating the "planner-execution mismatch" caused by unreliable agents. By demonstrating how undetected adversaries degrade rollout's performance and how trust-aware removal restores consistency, the work highlights a critical vulnerability in model-based planning and provides a mechanism to address it.
- **Complementary Trust Evidence:** The dual approach of combining probabilistic localization trust (calibrated from real-world GPS spoofing data) with behavioral trust from task execution outcomes offers a more robust and adaptive mechanism for detecting sophisticated adversaries, especially those that are "monitor-aware" and seek to minimize their detectability.
- **Realistic Adversarial Modeling:** The introduction of a distance-constrained, monitor-aware adversarial spoofing model with a tiered matching strategy provides a more realistic and challenging benchmark for evaluating resilient systems, emphasizing the trade-off between attack influence and detectability.
- **Empirical Validation with Real-World Data:** The extensive empirical validation using real GPS spoofing datasets and San Francisco taxicab demand data grounds the theoretical framework in practical, large-scale scenarios, enhancing the credibility and applicability of the findings.

**Potential Impact:**

- **Enhanced Resilience in Autonomous Systems:** The framework has direct implications for improving the resilience of critical autonomous multi-robot applications, such as autonomous transportation, warehouse logistics, and aerial delivery, against various forms of cyber-physical attacks and agent unreliability. It can lead to more robust systems that can detect and adapt to compromised agents without human intervention.
- **Guidance for Model-Based Planning:** For researchers and practitioners utilizing model-based sequential decision-making methods (e.g., rollout, model predictive control, approximate dynamic programming), this work provides crucial insights into how to maintain the integrity of their internal system models in adversarial or uncertain environments. It encourages designers to consider dynamic adaptation of planning models based on real-time reliability assessments.
- **Broader Applicability:** While demonstrated in localization spoofing for online routing, the underlying principle of trust-aware state construction is generalizable. It can be adapted to other forms of information unreliability (e.g., perception confidence, communication quality, sensor consistency) and behavioral inconsistencies (e.g., missed deadlines, execution failures) across a wide range of robotic and multi-agent systems.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The identified limitations, such as the need for robust external integrity sources, more expressive behavioral attribution, dynamic intervention strategies beyond permanent removal, and formal stability guarantees, pave the way for substantial future research. This includes exploring more nuanced trust-dependent actions (e.g., temporary quarantine, reduced priority) and analytical characterizations of system performance under trust-aware planning.

In conclusion, this research significantly advances the understanding and engineering of resilient multi-robot systems by demonstrating a practical and empirically validated approach to integrate trust directly into the decision-making loop, thereby addressing fundamental challenges posed by unreliable agents and information.
