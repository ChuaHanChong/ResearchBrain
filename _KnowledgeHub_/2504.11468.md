# SFT or RL? An Early Investigation into Training R1-Like Reasoning Large Vision-Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> A comparative study examines the effectiveness of Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) versus Reinforcement Learning (RL) for training reasoning-capable vision-language models, revealing that SFT can impede subsequent RL performance by inducing superficial reasoning patterns, while direct RL training through Group Relative Policy Optimization achieves state-of-the-art performance on multimodal reasoning benchmarks.

## Problem

- Traditional "SFT then RL" training paradigm's effectiveness for vision-language models remains unexamined
- Lack of understanding about how SFT and RL distinctly contribute to multimodal reasoning capabilities

## Method

- Created ==VLAA-Thinking dataset== with carefully curated SFT and RL splits based on presence of self-reflective reasoning
- Implemented ==mixed reward GRPO framework== combining rule-based and open-ended rewards
- Conducted systematic comparison of SFT, RL, and combined approaches across multiple model scales

## Results

- VLAA-Thinker-Qwen2.5VL-**3B** achieves **top performance** among 4B-scale models on Open LMM Reasoning Leaderboard
- Models trained directly with GRPO show emergence of authentic self-reflective reasoning
- SFT followed by GRPO performs **worse** than GRPO alone, challenging the conventional training paradigm

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - SFT can induce "pseudo reasoning paths" that hinder genuine reasoning development
> - Increasing SFT data volume or model size does not mitigate adverse effects
> - Direct RL training through GRPO consistently outperforms SFT-based approaches

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

- **Reasoning in Large Language Models (LLMs) and Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs):** The paper directly addresses the burgeoning research area of equipping LLMs and LVLMs with strong reasoning capabilities. This is motivated by the success of models like OpenAI's models and DeepSeek-R1 in text-based reasoning.
- **Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) vs. Reinforcement Learning (RL):** The paper investigates the dominant paradigm of "SFT then RL" used for training these models. It challenges the assumption that SFT is a necessary precursor to RL for reasoning, suggesting that SFT can be detrimental in some cases.
- **Chain-of-Thought (CoT) Reasoning:** The work builds upon CoT reasoning, where models generate intermediate reasoning steps before providing a final answer. The paper critiques the superficial imitation of reasoning traces learned through SFT, terming them "pseudo reasoning paths."
- **Reward Modeling:** The paper contributes to the field of reward modeling in RL, exploring different reward structures (rule-based, open-ended, mixed rewards) to effectively train LVLMs for reasoning tasks. It leverages Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), which is an approach that does not rely on value critics.
- **Multimodal Datasets:** The paper introduces a new multimodal dataset, VLAA-Thinking, specifically designed for studying SFT and RL in LVLMs. This addresses the need for high-quality datasets with step-by-step reasoning traces in the vision-language domain.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

- **Objective 1: Investigate the distinct effects of SFT and RL in multimodal reasoning.** The paper aims to understand how each training method contributes to (or hinders) reasoning abilities in LVLMs.
- **Objective 2: Question the necessity of the "SFT then RL" paradigm.** The authors want to determine if SFT is truly essential for developing reasoning in LVLMs, or if alternative approaches (like direct RL) can be more effective.
- **Motivation:** The primary motivation stems from the observation that while the "SFT then RL" approach has been successful in text-based LLMs, its effectiveness in LVLMs is not fully understood. The authors hypothesize that SFT may induce superficial reasoning patterns that hinder genuine reasoning advancement through RL.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

- **VLAA-Thinking Dataset Creation:** The authors created a new dataset, VLAA-Thinking, following a six-step pipeline:
 - **Metadata Collection:** Gathering data from diverse vision-language datasets (CLEVR-Math, Math PUMA, ArxivQA, DocVQA, VizWiz, ALLaVA, COCO, VisualGenome, and GeoQA170K).
 - **Visual Input and Additional Information:** Generating detailed image captions using GPT-4o and incorporating dataset-specific information to enrich the context.
 - **Reasoning Answer Distillation:** Using DeepSeek-R1 (a strong text-only reasoning model) to generate reasoning traces in a "think-then-speak" format.
 - **Answer Rewriting:** Enhancing the fluency and clarity of the reasoning traces using GPT-3.5-turbo, removing unnecessary phrases and ensuring a clean, instruction-following format.
 - **Automated Verification:** Implementing an automated verifier to ensure the correctness of the generated reasoning answers.
 - **Curating Splits for SFT and RL:** Creating non-overlapping SFT and RL splits based on the presence of "aha moments" (self-reflective cues) in the reasoning traces. The SFT split excludes samples with "aha moments," while the RL split contains them.
- **Experimental Setup:** The authors conducted extensive experiments comparing SFT, RL (specifically GRPO), and their combinations.
 - **Models:** Qwen2VL (2B and 7B), Qwen2.5VL (3B and 7B)
 - **SFT Training:** Fine-tuning the models on the VLAA-Thinking-SFT split, varying the dataset size (25K and 126K samples) and the presence of "aha moments."
 - **RL Training (GRPO):** Implementing a novel mixed reward function within the GRPO framework, combining rule-based rewards (digit matching, multiple-choice, math expression matching, IoU) and open-ended rewards (using XComposer-2.5-RM as a scorer).
 - **Evaluation:** Evaluating the models on six math reasoning benchmarks from the Open LMM Reasoning Leaderboard using VLMEvalKit.
- **Ablation Studies:** The authors performed ablation studies to investigate the impact of different reward components, hyperparameters, and the interaction between SFT and GRPO.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

- **SFT Can Undermine Subsequent RL:** SFT, while improving instruction following, can induce "pseudo reasoning paths" that hinder genuine reasoning advancement in RL.
- **"Aha Moments" in SFT Can Degrade Performance:** Fine-tuning on data containing "aha moments" can lead to worse performance compared to the base model, suggesting that SFT merely teaches the model to mimic rather than generalize self-reflective reasoning.
- **More SFT Data Does Not Necessarily Improve Performance:** Counterintuitively, increasing the size of the SFT dataset often harms performance, implying that simply scaling up SFT data does not boost generalizable reasoning skills.
- **Larger Models Are Not Immune to SFT Degeneration:** Scaling up model size does not mitigate the adverse effects of excessive SFT.
- **Mixed Reward GRPO Improves Multimodal Reasoning:** The novel mixed reward function within the GRPO framework effectively enhances reasoning capabilities in LVLMs.
- **SFT Is Incompatible with GRPO:** Applying SFT before GRPO training hurts performance, suggesting that SFT compromises the instruction-following ability crucial for effective GRPO training.
- **Response Length, Reward, and Model Performance Are Not Necessarily Related:** Longer responses and higher initial rewards do not always correlate with better reasoning performance, challenging previous observations.
- **Direct GRPO Training Boosts Model Performance:** Models trained directly with GRPO consistently outperform their respective base models.
- **Emergence of Authentic "Aha Moments":** GRPO training can induce authentic self-reflection processes, as evidenced by the emergence of "aha moments" during training.
- **VLAA-Thinker Achieves State-of-the-Art Performance:** The VLAA-Thinker-Qwen2.5VL-3B model achieves top-1 performance on the Open LMM Reasoning Leaderboard among 4B-scale LVLMs, surpassing the previous state-of-the-art.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

- **Challenges the Conventional SFT-Then-RL Paradigm:** The paper's central finding that SFT can be detrimental to subsequent RL challenges a widely accepted paradigm in LLM/LVLM training. This could lead to a shift in research focus towards alternative training approaches.
- **Provides Insights into the Roles of SFT and RL:** The study offers valuable insights into the distinct effects of SFT and RL in multimodal reasoning, helping researchers better understand how each method contributes to (or hinders) reasoning abilities.
- **Introduces a High-Quality Multimodal Dataset:** The VLAA-Thinking dataset provides a valuable resource for researchers working on reasoning in LVLMs. The dataset's focus on step-by-step reasoning traces and its separation into SFT and RL splits make it well-suited for studying the impact of different training methods.
- **Demonstrates the Effectiveness of Mixed Reward GRPO:** The paper's successful implementation of a mixed reward function within the GRPO framework provides a promising approach for training reasoning-capable LVLMs. This could lead to further research on reward modeling techniques.
- **Achieves State-of-the-Art Performance:** The VLAA-Thinker model's top performance on a challenging reasoning benchmark demonstrates the effectiveness of the proposed approach and provides a new benchmark for future research.
- **Potential Impact:** The findings of this paper have the potential to significantly impact the development of reasoning-capable LVLMs. By challenging the necessity of SFT and demonstrating the effectiveness of direct RL with a carefully designed reward structure, the authors pave the way for more efficient and effective training methods. This could lead to LVLMs that are better able to solve complex visual reasoning tasks, with applications in areas such as education, robotics, and medical diagnosis.
