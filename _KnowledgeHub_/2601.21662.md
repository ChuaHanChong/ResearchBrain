# Epistemic Uncertainty Quantification for Pre-trained VLMs via Riemannian Flow Matching

> [!summary] Summary
> REPVLM quantifies epistemic uncertainty in frozen vision-language models by estimating embedding density on the unit hypersphere. Conditional Riemannian Flow Matching learns a modality-conditioned vector field transporting a uniform base distribution to CLIP's image and text embedding distributions; negative log-likelihood is the uncertainty score. It reaches Spearman correlation 1.000 with prediction error across shifted benchmarks at 0.55 GFLOPs overhead.

## Problem

- Contrastive VLMs map inputs to single fixed points, with no intrinsic mechanism representing how confident the model is about its own representations.
- Existing VLM uncertainty methods mostly model aleatoric data ambiguity via probabilistic embeddings, leaving model ignorance, epistemic uncertainty, without a principled measure.
- Classical epistemic estimators, deep ensembles and Monte Carlo dropout, are computationally prohibitive at VLM scale, and perturbation scores depend on the candidate batch rather than the input.

## Method

- Theoretical three-link chain (==local encoder linearity==, ==first-order optimality==, ==alignment-and-uniformity==) motivating ==negative log-density== of an embedding as a monotone proxy for Bayesian epistemic uncertainty.
- **Conditional Riemannian Flow Matching**: one ==AdaLN== residual network learns a modality-conditioned tangent vector field along ==spherical-linear-interpolation geodesics==, carrying a uniform hypersphere base to the image and text embedding distributions.
- Inference integrates the reverse ODE with a ==Riemannian Euler scheme== plus renormalization, estimating divergence via a tangent-projected ==Hutchinson trace estimator== to get exact log-density in five steps.

## Results

- On CLIP ViT-B/32, REPVLM is the only method with Spearman **1.000** across all nine distribution-shift cells, and leads ImageNet-1K Acc@90 at **0.725-0.733** versus Conformity's **0.686-0.713**.
- Baselines collapse where REPVLM holds: GMM's Spearman falls to **-0.908** on Food101 and **-1.000** on ImageNet-Sketch; ProbVLM drops to **0.290** on ImageNet-1K under the DataComp proxy.
- Ablations: Riemannian beats Euclidean variants **0.733** versus **0.697-0.716** Acc@90; performance converges at **five** ODE steps; total cost **9.80** GFLOPs versus **92.50** for MC-Dropout.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Respect the manifold: geodesic paths on the hypersphere beat straight-line Euclidean interpolation for density over L2-normalized embeddings, worth 3-5 points of Acc@90.
> - A density model over frozen embeddings buys uncertainty almost free, sidestepping parameter-space posteriors that ensembles and dropout sample expensively.
> - Density conflates rarity with ignorance: the method degrades on SigLIP's multi-objective geometry, and flagging rare-but-valid inputs poses fairness risks for automated curation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Overview

A pretrained vision-language model (VLM) such as CLIP can classify images without task-specific training. It ordinarily maps each input to a single embedding, though, rather than reporting how well-supported that representation is. When a zero-shot VLM assigns a label to a new image, that single embedding does not say whether to trust the label.

The authors check whether the image's representation falls in a well-populated region learned from example embeddings. Low density means higher *estimated* epistemic uncertainty: the model's ignorance, as distinct from ambiguity in the data. Their method, REPVLM, uses this score to rank predictions for review.

The main test is on CIFAR-100, using the OpenCLIP ViT-B/32 setup and a Conceptual Captions proxy. Rejecting the 90% most uncertain predictions leaves an accuracy of 0.873 ± 0.010. The strongest density-aware competitor, Conformity, reaches 0.804 ± 0.000. Spearman's S is 1.000 ± 0.000 for REPVLM and 0.891 ± 0.000 for Conformity.

Two metrics describe these results:

- **Acc@90% rejection** is top-1 accuracy on the 10% most confident predictions.
- **S** is the Spearman correlation between rejection fraction and accuracy of the remaining set, with 1 indicating a perfect monotonic increase.

These are selective-classification results. They do not claim that REPVLM improves the classifier's original accuracy or proves true epistemic knowledge.

REPVLM rises strongly in many panels but is not top on every task/metric; Food101 and ImageNet-Sketch show meaningful exceptions.

### 2. A density score on the sphere

The image and text encoders produce L2-normalized vectors on a unit hypersphere. A proxy dataset supplies image and caption embeddings, distinct from downstream task data.

REPVLM starts from a uniform distribution on the sphere. It learns a time-dependent vector field, conditioned on modality, that transports that base toward the proxy embedding distributions. Training regresses the learned field onto the tangent direction of geodesic paths between base and proxy embeddings. A geodesic is a shortest curved path on the sphere.

At inference, REPVLM integrates the reverse ODE and the field's divergence to estimate log-density. A tangent-projected Hutchinson trace estimate makes divergence computation cheaper. The uncertainty score is negative log-density, $U_{\text{ep}}(x)=-\log p(z\mid c)$, so embeddings in sparse regions receive higher uncertainty.

This estimates support under the proxy distribution. It is not a direct posterior over VLM parameters. The underlying framework, [Riemannian Flow Matching](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2302.03660), provides manifold-native flow training. REPVLM's contribution is applying it to frozen VLM embeddings and conditioning the vector field on image versus text modality.

Overview of REPVLM: text and image inputs, encoders, modality-specific density depictions, and a REPVLM block showing the uniform spherical base and learned flow. Yellow marks high-density and purple low-density regions; these colors are illustrative, not measured values. Negative log-density serves as a proxy for uncertainty.

### 3. Does the score find errors, and does geometry matter?

The main setup uses frozen OpenCLIP ViT-B/32, prompt-based zero-shot classification, and six benchmarks. Evaluation labels are not used to train either the VLM or REPVLM.

Across the grid, REPVLM is near-perfect in S on standard benchmarks and strong under shift. Baselines sometimes lead on particular metrics or datasets, and the authors report that Food101 is an Acc@90 exception.

The ablation asks whether the gain comes from respecting spherical geometry rather than merely fitting some density model. It uses ImageNet-1K with Conceptual Captions and compares three flows:

FlowAcc@90SEuclidean with Gaussian base0.697 ± 0.0020.893 ± 0.052Euclidean with uniform-on-sphere base but straight-line interpolation0.716 ± 0.0030.973 ± 0.012Riemannian REPVLM0.733 ± 0.0040.993 ± 0.006

This comparison supports the value of manifold-aware interpolation. It does not prove that geometry is the only cause of performance.

### 4. A useful warning light

REPVLM turns an embedding's location in proxy-data space into a usable ranking signal. The geometry ablation supports modeling the sphere rather than ignoring it.

The boundary is conceptual. The authors' density-to-ignorance argument includes empirical assumptions, and they say a formal density-preservation result for non-invertible contrastive encoders remains open. Density also confounds rarity with ignorance, so valid underrepresented samples may be flagged. For fairness- or safety-critical settings, the authors recommend subgroup checks or human review rather than automatic deletion.
