# OpenVLThinkerV2: A Generalist Multimodal Reasoning Model for Multi-domain Visual Tasks

> [!summary] Summary
> OpenVLThinkerV2, developed by UCLA researchers, is a generalist multimodal reasoning model that introduces Gaussian GRPO (G²RPO) and task-level shaping to stabilize RL training and balance perceptual grounding with multi-step reasoning. The model demonstrates state-of-the-art performance among open-source models and frequently surpasses leading proprietary models across 18 diverse multimodal benchmarks.

## Problem

- Extreme variance in reward topologies across diverse visual tasks during Reinforcement Learning (RL) post-training, leading to gradient explosion and imbalanced updates in Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs).
- Existing Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) variants fail to robustly handle reward heterogeneity, suffering from issues like intra-task imbalance, inter-task imbalance, or vulnerability to heavy-tail outliers and momentum lag due to linear normalization.
- Difficulty in simultaneously maintaining robust fine-grained perception and multi-step reasoning capabilities within MLLMs due to costly data annotations or substantial computational overhead from current enhancement methods.

## Method

- ==Gaussian GRPO (G2RPO)== (G²RPO), a new RL objective, replaces linear reward standardization with non-linear distributional matching (1D Optimal Transport via CDFs) to transform empirical reward distributions into a standard normal distribution (N(0,1)).
- Task-level Response Length Shaping applies a customized trapezoidal reward envelope to dynamically encourage elaborate responses for reasoning tasks and concise outputs for vision-centric tasks, guided by task-specific length thresholds.
- Task-level ==Entropy Shaping== introduces a strict regularization envelope to bound the model's exploration within an optimal, task-specific zone, thereby preventing both entropy collapse and explosion across diverse task topologies.

## Results

- OpenVLThinkerV2 achieves new state-of-the-art performance among open-source models and frequently outperforms leading proprietary models (e.g., GPT-4o, Gemini **2.5** Pro) across **18** diverse benchmarks spanning six major task categories.
- The model demonstrates strong general reasoning capabilities, scoring **71.6**% on MMMU, **88.2**% on MMBench, **73.8**% on MMStar, and **79.5**% on MathVista.
- It achieves state-of-the-art performance in document understanding (**96.7**% DocVQA, **911** OCRBench, surpassing specialized models) and strong localization on grounding tasks (**93.4**% RefCOCO), demonstrating balanced capabilities.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Non-linear distributional matching via G²RPO effectively mitigates statistical fragilities, such as heavy-tail outliers and inter-task gradient imbalances, by strictly enforcing a Gaussian topology on reward distributions, ensuring stable and equitable gradient updates.
> - Scalable task-level shaping mechanisms for response length and entropy can effectively guide MLLMs to balance fine-grained perception and multi-step reasoning, avoiding expensive data annotations or architectural modifications.
> - Combining a reward-stabilizing objective like G²RPO with behavioral guidance through task-level shaping mechanisms yields synergistic performance gains, crucial for developing robust and generalist multimodal models.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Recent advancements in Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) have increasingly leveraged Reinforcement Learning (RL) as a mechanism to enhance their performance across various visual tasks, from complex reasoning to fine-grained object detection. Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) has emerged as a prominent RL objective within this domain, driving improvements in MLLMs.

However, the application of GRPO to open-source multimodal generalist models faces two primary challenges. First, there is significant variance in reward topologies across diverse visual tasks. Rewards can range from sparse, binary signals (e.g., in math visual question answering or multiple-choice questions) to dense, continuous metrics (e.g., Intersection-over-Union scores in grounding tasks). This variability creates intra- and inter-task update imbalances, which can destabilize optimization and lead to issues such as gradient explosion during large-scale training. Prior attempts to address this, such as Dr.GRPO, removed sample-wise normalization, which led to inter-task imbalance where high-variance tasks dominated updates. EMA-GRPO attempted to mitigate this by using task-wise moving averages of reward variance, but these linear transformations preserve higher-order distributional shapes, leaving the optimization vulnerable to "heavy-tail outliers" where anomalous rewards can suppress learning signals.

Second, MLLMs struggle to maintain robust capabilities across both fine-grained perception and multi-step reasoning simultaneously. Existing approaches to enhance perception often involve perturbing visual inputs for auxiliary training objectives or integrating explicit vision anchors. These methods typically require extensive data annotations or incur substantial computational overhead from additional modules, which limits their scalability and makes their efficacy difficult to verify across diverse, multi-domain benchmarks.

The paper aims to contribute to this landscape by introducing a novel RL objective and complementary training mechanisms to address these identified limitations, particularly in the context of developing a robust, general-purpose MLLM capable of handling a broad spectrum of visual tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives of this research are to:

1. **Develop a novel RL training objective to ensure inter-task gradient equity and mitigate reward variance issues in multi-domain MLLM training.** The motivation stems from the observation that traditional GRPO and its variants (e.g., EMA-GRPO) are susceptible to instability when faced with the extreme diversity of reward topologies in multimodal tasks. This instability arises from imbalanced gradient updates, vulnerability to outliers, and the inability of linear scaling methods to normalize higher-order distributional shapes. The goal is to create an objective that robustly handles these variations, thereby stabilizing the RL optimization process for generalist MLLMs.

2. **Introduce task-level shaping mechanisms to balance fine-grained perception and multi-step reasoning capabilities within MLLMs.** The authors observe that existing RL post-training methods often struggle to maintain strong performance in both perception-centric and reasoning-centric tasks. This imbalance can lead to issues such as hallucination in vision-centric tasks or incoherent text generation in reasoning tasks. The motivation is to provide a scalable and simple approach to guide the model's behavior, encouraging appropriate response lengths and maintaining an optimal exploration/exploitation balance across different task types without requiring costly external annotations or computational overhead.

3. **Construct and evaluate a robust, general-purpose multimodal reasoning model leveraging these advancements.** The overarching motivation is to develop an open-source MLLM that can perform competitively, and in some cases surpass, both strong open-source baselines and leading proprietary models across a wide array of visual tasks, thereby pushing the capabilities of generalist MLLMs. This involves integrating the novel RL objective and shaping mechanisms into a coherent training framework for a foundation MLLM.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research introduces Gaussian GRPO (G²RPO) as a novel RL training objective and complements it with two task-level shaping mechanisms: response length shaping and entropy shaping. These components are integrated to form OpenVLThinkerV2.

#### 3.1. Gaussian GRPO (G²RPO)

G²RPO is proposed to address the statistical fragility of linear normalization in existing GRPO variants. Instead of standardizing rewards using mean and variance, G²RPO employs non-linear distributional matching. The core idea is to mathematically force the empirical reward distribution of any given task to strictly converge to a standard normal distribution, N(0, 1).

- **Optimal Transport Formulation:** G²RPO frames advantage estimation as a 1D Optimal Transport problem. This involves finding a transport map that precisely transforms the empirical reward distribution of a task to the N(0, 1) target distribution.
- **Closed-Form Solution via CDFs:** In 1D, the optimal transport map has an efficient closed-form solution using Cumulative Distribution Functions (CDFs). G²RPO assigns advantages by mapping the relative rank of a response directly to the inverse CDF (quantile function) of the N(0, 1) distribution.
- **Advantage Calculation:** For a set of empirical rewards $R_{\tau} = \{R_1, \ldots, R_N\}$ for a task $\tau$, the advantage $bA_i$ for reward $R_i$ is calculated as $\Psi(R_i, R_{\tau}) = \Phi^{-1}(F_{R_{\tau}}(R_i))$, where $\Phi^{-1}$ is the inverse CDF of N(0, 1) and $F_{R_{\tau}}$ is the empirical CDF of the task's rewards.
- **Rank-Based Probability:** The empirical CDF is approximated by calculating a uniform probability $p_i = \frac{\text{rank}(R_i) - 0.5}{N}$ for each reward, where $\text{rank}(R_i)$ is the rank of $R_i$ among sorted rewards.
- **Tie-Breaking Strategy:** To ensure identical rewards receive identical learning signals, a tie-breaking strategy is applied. If multiple rewards are identical, their assigned quantiles from the inverse CDF are averaged, and this average is assigned to all tied values.
- **Theoretical Benefits:** By enforcing a strict Gaussian topology, G²RPO aims to:
 - **Cap outliers:** Mathematically limits the influence of extreme reward spikes.
 - **Smooth bimodal distributions:** Transforms discontinuous reward functions (e.g., binary rewards) into smooth, symmetrically balanced Gaussian tails.
 - **Ensure inter-task gradient equity:** Guarantees that every task's advantage distribution converges to N(0, 1), inherently providing zero mean and unit variance for policy updates across diverse tasks, without relying on momentum hyperparameters.

#### 3.2. Task-level Length and Entropy Shaping

These mechanisms are introduced to balance perception and reasoning by dynamically shaping the model's response generation and exploration behavior.

- **Task-Level Response Length Shaping:**
 - **Observation:** The authors observed that reasoning-centric tasks benefit from longer, more elaborate responses, while vision-centric tasks (e.g., OCR, grounding) require concise, direct outputs to avoid hallucinations and bolster visual grounding. GRPO training initially decreases response length for reasoning tasks before converging to longer chains, and consistently reduces length for vision tasks.
 - **Mechanism:** A customized trapezoidal reward envelope, $R_{length}(y)$, is defined based on the response token length $|y|$. This reward encourages responses to fall within an "optimal length plateau" defined by task-specific hyperparameters ($L_{low}$, $L_{high}$), while softly penalizing responses that are too short ($L_{min}$) or too long ($L_{max}$). This shapes the model to generate appropriate lengths for different task types.

- **Task-Level Entropy Shaping:**
 - **Observation:** In multi-task RL, varying task difficulties can lead to unstable exploration patterns. Reasoning-centric tasks may exhibit "entropy explosion" (inflating entropy, exploring incoherent tokens), while vision-centric tasks are prone to "entropy collapse" (over-exploiting high-probability tokens, abandoning necessary exploration). Complex or out-of-distribution tasks can exacerbate entropy explosion.
 - **Mechanism:** A margin-based penalty, $L_{ent\_reg}$, is applied to the average entropy loss ($H_{task}$) for a given task. This penalty is activated only when the entropy falls outside a predefined optimal exploration interval, bounded by a minimum entropy threshold ($H_{min}$) to prevent collapse and a maximum entropy threshold ($H_{max}$) to prevent explosion. This regularization term is added to the overall optimization objective, scaled by a hyperparameter $\lambda_{ent}$.

#### 3.3. Training Setup

OpenVLThinkerV2 is initialized from Qwen3-VL-Instruct-8B and trained using a filtered subset of the OneThinker-600k dataset. The optimization uses AdamW for one epoch, with a batch size of 128 and a learning rate of $2 \times 10^{-6}$. KL regularization is disabled, and dynamic data filtering is employed to discard uniformly correct or incorrect rollouts. The maximum generation length is 4096 tokens.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

OpenVLThinkerV2 was evaluated across 18 diverse benchmarks spanning six major task categories, consistently demonstrating performance improvements over baselines and competitive results against proprietary models.

#### 4.1. Visual Reasoning Tasks (General VQA, Math VQA, Chart VQA)

- OpenVLThinkerV2 consistently outperformed strong open-source baselines, including OneThinker-8B, and improved upon standard GRPO and GDPO variants under controlled conditions.
- **MMMU:** Achieved 71.6%, surpassing GPT-4o (70.7%).
- **MMBench:** Reached 88.2%.
- **MMStar:** Achieved 73.8%, also surpassing GPT-4o (65.1%).
- **MathVista:** Scored 79.5%.
- **MathVerse:** Scored 65.8%.
- **MathVision:** Scored 53.4%.
- **AI2D:** Reached 87.5%.
- **ChartQA:** Achieved 87.4%, exceeding Gemini 2.5 Pro (83.3%).
- **CharXiv(RQ):** Scored 53.0%.

#### 4.2. Document Understanding Tasks

- OpenVLThinkerV2 achieved state-of-the-art performance across all three benchmarks in this category.
- **DocVQA:** Scored 96.7%.
- **OCRBench:** Attained 911, outperforming specialized models like DeepEyesV2 (882) and frontier proprietary models such as GPT-5 (810) and Gemini 2.5 Pro (866).
- **InfoVQA:** Scored 86.4%.

#### 4.3. Spatial Reasoning Tasks

- The model demonstrated high capacity for granular visual perception and complex multimodal logic.
- **EmbSpatial:** Achieved the highest performance at 83.1%.
- **RefSpatial:** Scored 44.6%, marginally underperforming the fine-tuned expert model RoboRefer-SFT (48.4) but outperforming GPT-5 (23.8) and Gemini 2.5 Pro (36.5).
- **RoboSpatial:** Scored 63.2%, performing comparably to specialized expert models like SpatialRGPT (66.7).

#### 4.4. Grounding Tasks

- OpenVLThinkerV2 showed state-of-the-art localization capabilities.
- **RefCOCO:** Achieved 93.4%.
- **RefCOCO+:** Achieved 88.2%.
- **RefCOCOg:** Achieved 90.4%.
- It consistently outperformed previous baselines, including the specialized visual grounding expert, Grounding DINO.

#### 4.5. Ablation Study

An ablation study confirmed the contributions of each component:
- Integrating G²RPO alone yielded the most substantial initial performance improvement across all six main domains (average performance increase from Qwen3-VL-Instruct-8B baseline).
- Adding task-level entropy shaping further boosted performance, particularly in reasoning-centric tasks, with more modest gains in saturated or out-of-distribution tasks.
- Applying task-level length reward provided broader improvements, outperforming entropy shaping alone.
- Combining both shaping mechanisms with G²RPO achieved the highest overall gains, indicating a synergistic effect between the components.

#### 4.6. Training Stability (Appendix A)

The appendix provided further results on training stability, showing G²RPO's early convergence and consistent improvement in average accuracy, length, format, and structure rewards on the validation set compared to GRPO and GDPO baselines, which exhibited more oscillation or convergence to lower rewards.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of OpenVLThinkerV2 and its underlying methodologies contributes to the field of multimodal AI in several significant ways:

- **Enhanced RL Optimization Stability:** G²RPO addresses a fundamental challenge in multi-task RL for MLLMs by providing a mechanism to normalize highly diverse reward distributions. By mathematically enforcing convergence to a standard normal distribution, it intrinsically mitigates the impact of outliers, ensures symmetric updates for rewards, and guarantees uniform variance across tasks. This stability is critical for scaling RL fine-tuning to broad generalist models.
- **Balanced Perception and Reasoning:** The introduction of task-level response length and entropy shaping offers a scalable approach to fine-tune MLLMs to simultaneously excel at both fine-grained visual perception and complex multi-step reasoning. These mechanisms address observed training instabilities (e.g., entropy explosion, entropy collapse, inappropriate response lengths) without requiring expensive data annotations or auxiliary modules, thereby improving the model's robustness and mitigating issues like hallucination.
- **Competitive Generalist MLLM:** OpenVLThinkerV2 establishes new state-of-the-art results among open-source models and demonstrates competitive, often superior, performance against leading proprietary frontier models (e.g., GPT-4o, Gemini 2.5 Pro, GPT-5) across a wide array of visual reasoning, document understanding, spatial reasoning, and grounding benchmarks. This suggests that the proposed techniques are effective in building highly capable general-purpose MLLMs.
- **Broader Applicability:** The paper posits that G²RPO's principle of harmonizing divergent reward topologies extends beyond multimodal tasks to other LLM applications suffering from reward heterogeneity, such as software engineering (SWE) coding or Graphical User Interface (GUI) tasks. This suggests a potential for wider impact in stabilizing RL optimization across various domains.
- **Open-Source Contribution:** As an open-source model, OpenVLThinkerV2 provides a valuable resource for the research community, encouraging further development and exploration of stable, scalable, and equitable RL optimization paradigms for foundation models.

The findings indicate that the combination of distributionally-matched advantage estimation and behavioral shaping mechanisms offers a robust framework for improving the generalization and capabilities of multimodal large language models.
