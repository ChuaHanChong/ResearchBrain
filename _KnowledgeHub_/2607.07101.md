# GeoProp: Grounding Robot State in Vision for Generalist Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> GeoProp, a lightweight adapter, addresses the structural decoupling between visual observations and proprioceptive feedback in robot learning by explicitly grounding 3D robot kinematics within 2D visual feature maps. This method consistently improved manipulation policy success rates by up to 8.7 percentage points in simulation and 11.2 percentage points in real-world household tasks, particularly for precision-oriented actions.

## Problem

- Current robot learning architectures often treat low-dimensional proprioceptive feedback as an ungrounded global state, structurally decoupled from high-dimensional visual observations.
- Implicit fusion of proprioception with visual features can be suboptimal or even detrimental, sometimes leading policies to underperform vision-only baselines due to spurious correlations.
- Existing methods for aligning proprioception often distribute signals broadly or require full 3D reconstruction, lacking a lightweight, localized, and state-conditioned modulation within the policy's feature stack.

## Method

- **GeoProp** analytically projects the 3D end-effector state onto the 2D image plane to generate image-grounded visual tokens.
- It uses localized, state-conditioned ==Feature-wise Linear Modulation (FiLM)== to apply geometric priors to intermediate visual features, ensuring the robot's configuration inherits scene semantics.
- ==Predictive kinematic sampling== incorporates short-horizon motion intent by estimating and sampling a future end-effector waypoint, providing look-ahead visual context for impending actions.

## Results

- Achieved an average success rate of **75.3%** with Diffusion Policy (ResNet-18) in simulation, an **8.5 percentage point** improvement over vanilla proprioception fusion and **7.2 percentage points** over vision-only baselines across **63 tasks**.
- Improved real-world success rates on Mobile ALOHA tasks by an average of **11.2 percentage points** with Diffusion Policy (ResNet-18), reaching **50.0%** compared to **38.8%** for vanilla and **28.8%** for vision-only.
- Showed consistent gains on precision-oriented tasks, with an average improvement of **22.3 percentage points** across both backbones in specific MetaWorld tasks, and demonstrated robustness to mild calibration errors.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicit geometric grounding of 3D robot kinematics in 2D visual features serves as an effective inductive bias, enhancing the performance and robustness of robot manipulation policies.
> - Ungrounded proprioceptive input can be counterproductive, leading to suboptimal performance, sometimes worse than vision-only baselines, underscoring the need for direct state-vision correspondence.
> - The GeoProp adapter is lightweight and plug-and-play, demonstrating compatibility and consistent improvements across diverse policy architectures (Diffusion Policy, π0) and vision backbones with minimal parameter overhead (2-3%).

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation has seen the development of various architectures, including diffusion-based controllers, transformer-based action predictors, and large-scale Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. These advancements enable robots to learn policies from high-dimensional visual observations and perform tasks with increasing semantic generalization. However, a consistent challenge across these frameworks is the structural decoupling between high-dimensional visual observations and low-dimensional proprioceptive feedback.

Current methods typically treat proprioception as an ungrounded, global state embedding. Fusion approaches often involve simple concatenation or cross-attention mechanisms, which require the model to implicitly learn the correspondence between 3D kinematics and 2D visual features. This implicit learning often overlooks the robot's inherent geometric relationship with the visual scene. Prior research indicates that such ungrounded proprioceptive input can sometimes be detrimental, leading policies to underperform even vision-only baselines due to the introduction of spurious correlations.

Related work has explored limitations of ungrounded proprioceptive fusion, suggesting that isolated state vectors can lead to embodiment or trajectory-specific overfitting. Approaches to align proprioception have included learned objectives, alternative state parameterizations (e.g., contrastive losses, discrete codebooks), and dense robot-centric positional encoding (RC-PE). While RC-PE projects the end-effector onto the image plane to inject a relative-coordinate embedding, it distributes this signal across the entire feature grid. Other methods operate at the input or planning level, such as predicting visual traces or overlaying robot reticles on raw input. The presented work, GeoProp, distinguishes itself by operating within the policy's feature stack, utilizing localized, state-conditioned modulation of intermediate visual features via 2D projection, without requiring full 3D reconstruction or external VLM planners. This approach aims to provide a lightweight, robot-state-centric alternative to existing methods, filling a gap in explicit proprioception-vision alignment.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from an identified representational limitation in current robot learning architectures: the structural decoupling between visual observations and proprioceptive feedback. Despite the sophistication of modern generalist robot policies, proprioception is frequently treated as an isolated, low-dimensional vector without explicit alignment to visual tokens. This implicit approach often fails to leverage the intrinsic geometric relationship between the robot's physical state (e.g., end-effector pose) and its visual manifestation in the scene. Empirical observations by the authors suggest that without direct correspondence, manipulation policies may struggle to effectively ground the robot's state, leading to suboptimal performance, potentially worse than vision-only approaches.

The key objectives of this work are:
1. **To demonstrate the issue of ungrounded proprioception:** Empirically show that treating proprioception as a global, ungrounded vector can result in modality misalignment and degraded manipulation performance, sometimes falling below vision-only baselines. This establishes the necessity for explicit, geometry-based state-vision correspondence.
2. **To introduce GeoProp:** Propose a lightweight, plug-and-play adapter designed to bridge the representational gap between 3D robot kinematics and 2D scene semantics. GeoProp aims to achieve this by transforming 3D proprioceptive information into image-grounded visual tokens.
3. **To establish explicit geometric grounding:** Implement this grounding through analytical projection of the end-effector state onto the image plane, localized feature sampling, and spatially-aligned feature modulation. This process is intended to enable the robot's configuration to inherit scene semantics within a consistent visual latent space.
4. **To incorporate motion intent:** Augment the grounded state representation with predictive spatial tokens, estimated from short-horizon kinematics, to provide look-ahead visual context for imminent motion.
5. **To validate GeoProp's generality and impact:** Demonstrate that this approach consistently improves performance across distinct policy architectures (e.g., Diffusion Policy, π0) in both simulation and real-world environments, confirming explicit geometric grounding as an efficient and effective inductive bias for embodied AI.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

GeoProp is designed as a lightweight, plug-and-play adapter that aligns 3D proprioception with 2D visual tokens. It operates at the interface between perception and state representation, making it compatible with various policy frameworks without requiring modifications to the backbone architecture. The method assumes access to camera intrinsics (K), camera extrinsics (R, t), intermediate spatial feature maps from the vision backbone, and the 3D position of the robot's end-effector.

The GeoProp architecture comprises three main components:

**1. Geometric Projection and Feature Grounding:**
- The robot's proprioceptive state at time *t*, denoted as p_t, includes the end-effector position, orientation, and gripper state.
- The 3D end-effector position, r_t = [x_t, y_t, z_t]^T, is projected onto the 2D image plane using the known camera intrinsics (K) and extrinsics (R, t) via a perspective projection function, yielding image coordinates q_t = (u_t, v_t).
- These image coordinates are then mapped to a continuous coordinate ̄q_t on the feature grid, consistent with the visual backbone's feature map resolution.
- A "grounded state token," τ_t, is subsequently formed by performing bilinear sampling on the modulated, aggregated feature map (F_mod) at the continuous feature grid coordinate ̄q_t. This token effectively localizes the robot's interaction point within the visual latent space.

**2. Spatially-Aligned Feature Modulation:**
- To inject the geometric prior earlier into the visual processing hierarchy, GeoProp applies state-conditioned Feature-wise Linear Modulation (FiLM) locally.
- For each intermediate feature map F^ℓ_enc from the vision encoder at level ℓ, the corresponding grid cell c^ℓ_t aligned with the projected end-effector location ̄q_t is identified.
- A two-layer MLP processes the proprioceptive state p_t to predict channel-wise FiLM parameters, γ^ℓ_t and β^ℓ_t, for each feature level. The last layer of this MLP is zero-initialized, ensuring an identity transform at initialization.
- The modulation is applied only to the feature cell c^ℓ_t: F^ℓ_mod[c] = (1 + γ^ℓ_t) ⊙ F^ℓ_enc[c] + β^ℓ_t. All other cells in the feature map remain unmodulated.
- These modulated multi-scale features are then aggregated using a Feature Pyramid Network (FPN) to produce the final modulated feature map, F_mod, which is later used for sampling the grounded state token.

**3. Predictive Kinematic Sampling:**
- To capture future motion intent beyond the instantaneous state, GeoProp incorporates a predictive spatial token.
- A short-horizon look-ahead waypoint, r̂_t+1, is estimated by fitting a quadratic polynomial to a temporal window of recent 3D end-effector positions ({r_t-k..., r_t}, typically k=3, using a 4-frame history).
- This predicted waypoint is projected onto the image plane and mapped to a continuous look-ahead feature grid coordinate ̄q_pre_t+1, similar to the current end-effector projection.
- A "predictive token," τ_pre, is constructed by sampling the *unmodulated* FPN-aggregated feature map (F_raw, derived from the original encoder features) at ̄q_pre_t+1. Sampling from unmodulated features ensures this token serves as a spatial preview of anticipated motion, rather than an additional modulation site.

**Policy Input Integration:**
The final input to the downstream policy, z_t, is formed by concatenating the grounded state token (τ_t), the predictive token (τ_pre), and the global visual tokens (h_global) produced by the vision backbone. For tasks involving language instructions, language tokens are also concatenated. In bimanual robot setups, GeoProp is applied independently to each arm, generating separate grounded and predictive tokens for each end-effector, which are then concatenated.

**Implementation Details:**
The method utilizes FPN for multi-scale feature aggregation, producing a dense sampling map (e.g., 14x14 for 224x224 inputs). For ResNet-18, features from four residual stages are aggregated, with a stride-16 FPN output. For ViT-based backbones, intermediate transformer features are reshaped to spatial grids and aggregated with the same FPN interface.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of GeoProp was conducted across 67 tasks, including 63 in simulation and 4 in the real world, utilizing two distinct policy families: Diffusion Policy (DP) and π0.

**Simulation Experiments:**
- **Diffusion Policy (DP):** GeoProp was evaluated on 63 simulation tasks from MetaWorld (50 tasks), RLBench (6 tasks), and RoboTwin (7 tasks).
 - **ResNet-18 Backbone:** GeoProp achieved an average success rate of 75.3%, demonstrating an absolute improvement of 8.5 percentage points (pp) over the Vanilla baseline and 7.2 pp over the No-Proprio baseline.
 - **ViT-Base Backbone:** GeoProp obtained an average success rate of 71.0%, which translates to a 9.0 pp gain over Vanilla and an 8.7 pp gain over No-Proprio.
 - The improvements were particularly noticeable on harder MetaWorld task subsets, suggesting enhanced benefit in precision-oriented manipulation.
 - A recurring observation was that the Vanilla proprioception fusion often performed comparably to or worse than the No-Proprio (vision-only) baseline, indicating the potential for ungrounded state vectors to introduce ineffective or detrimental signals.
 - GeoProp introduced a parameter overhead of 2.3–3.0%.
- **π0 VLA Model (RoboTwin subset):** GeoProp was tested on the 7-task RoboTwin benchmark with the π0 model, which employs deep cross-modality attention.
 - GeoProp improved the average success rate from 64.0% (Vanilla π0) to 68.0%, representing a 4.0 pp gain. It outperformed the Vanilla baseline on 5 out of 7 tasks.
 - The parameter overhead for π0 was approximately 1.98%.
 - Data scaling experiments on RoboTwin showed GeoProp's benefits persisted across different demonstration counts, with gains of +2.3, +4.0, and +2.8 pp at 25, 50, and 100 demonstrations per task, respectively.
 - On an expanded 15-task RoboTwin suite, GeoProp showed a +4.5 pp improvement over Vanilla π0.

**Ablations and Analysis:**
- **Component Contributions (15 MetaWorld tasks):** Replacing vector-based proprioception with geometric spatial grounding alone improved mean success from 61.2% to 64.8%. Adding predictive kinematic sampling and localized FiLM modulation further increased performance to 68.5% (full model), suggesting that geometric grounding is the primary contributor, with the other two components providing complementary gains.
- **Alternative Spatial-Grounding Baselines (15 MetaWorld tasks):** Simple coordinate priors like Heatmap injection (61.7%) and RC-PE (60.8%) showed improvement over No-Proprio (59.5%) but were outperformed by GeoProp-Base (64.8%). This indicates that co-located visual feature sampling is important beyond just providing location cues.
- **Task-Type Patterns:** GeoProp exhibited its largest gains on tasks requiring precision with small objects (e.g., MetaWorld Basketball, Hand Insert, Pick Place, Sweep Into), showing an average improvement of 22.3 pp across both backbones. Performance gains were reduced or inverted in cases where the manipulated object occluded the projected end-effector.
- **Parameter-Matched Control:** Increasing the parameter count of the proprioceptive encoder in baselines to match GeoProp's overhead yielded negligible performance gains (+0.5 pp for DP, +0.1 pp for π0), reinforcing that GeoProp's benefits are due to its geometric alignment, not just increased capacity.
- **Calibration Robustness:** GeoProp's performance degraded gradually under synthetic test-time extrinsic perturbations, remaining above Vanilla and No-Proprio baselines for translation errors up to 2.5 cm and rotation errors up to 1.5°.

**Real-World Experiments (Mobile ALOHA):**
- **Diffusion Policy (ResNet-18):** Across four household manipulation tasks, GeoProp improved the average success rate from 38.8% (Vanilla) to 50.0% (+11.2 pp), and from 28.8% (No-Proprio) to 50.0% (+21.2 pp). In some instances, Vanilla underperformed No-Proprio (e.g., Coffee Retrieval: 35% vs. 40%), consistent with simulation findings regarding ungrounded state fusion.
- **π0:** GeoProp similarly improved average real-world success rates from 43.8% (Vanilla π0) to 53.8% (+10.0 pp).
- Gains were most pronounced on tasks demanding accurate local contact and gripper-object alignment, such as Coffee Retrieval and Paper Toss.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces GeoProp, an adapter addressing a representational limitation in robot learning where proprioceptive feedback is often decoupled from visual observations. By explicitly grounding 3D robot kinematics within 2D visual feature maps, GeoProp offers a method to enhance the performance and robustness of robot manipulation policies.

The significance of GeoProp lies in several aspects:
- **Addressing Fundamental Decoupling:** It tackles a core challenge in robot learning by providing an explicit geometric link between the robot's physical state and its visual context, rather than relying on implicit learning through neural networks. This makes the proprioceptive signal more consistently interpretable and useful for the policy.
- **Generality and Compatibility:** GeoProp is designed as a lightweight, plug-and-play adapter, demonstrated to be effective across different policy architectures (Diffusion Policy and π0) and vision backbones (ResNet, ViT, SigLIP). This suggests its applicability across a broad spectrum of generalist robot policies, including those based on diffusion and large-scale vision-language models.
- **Consistent Performance Gains:** The consistent improvements in success rates observed across 67 diverse simulation and real-world tasks (up to +8.7% in simulation, +10.6% in the real world) suggest that explicit geometric grounding is a valuable inductive bias for embodied AI. This impact is particularly notable in precision-oriented tasks and in real-world scenarios affected by sensor noise and clutter.
- **Efficiency:** The minimal parameter overhead (2–3% of the base model) makes GeoProp an efficient addition to existing policies, offering performance improvements without substantially increasing model complexity or computational cost.
- **Stabilizing Proprioception Use:** The findings that ungrounded proprioception can sometimes be counterproductive (underperforming vision-only baselines) highlight GeoProp's role in stabilizing and making proprioceptive information reliably beneficial for policy learning.

**Potential Impact:**
GeoProp could contribute to the development of more robust and capable robot manipulation systems, particularly in applications requiring precise hand-eye coordination. Its ability to enhance generalist policies with minimal overhead suggests that explicit geometric grounding could become a standard component in future robot learning frameworks. This could lead to improved performance in tasks involving intricate object interaction, fine motor control, and adaptability to varied real-world conditions.

**Limitations identified by the authors:**
- **Calibration Dependency:** GeoProp relies on accurate camera intrinsics and extrinsics. While it exhibits some robustness to mild calibration drift, significant errors necessitate recalibration, which may not always be feasible in dynamic environments.
- **Limited Kinematic Representation:** The method primarily grounds the 3D end-effector position. It does not explicitly represent richer kinematic structures (e.g., individual links, joints, fingertips, contact patches), which might limit its applicability to highly dexterous or whole-body manipulation tasks.
- **Predictive Sampling Assumptions:** Predictive sampling assumes locally smooth end-effector motion. Abrupt changes in motion or contact transitions could lead to inaccurate look-ahead points, potentially reducing its effectiveness.
- **Task-Specific Performance:** Gains may be marginal or inverted in tasks where the manipulated object occludes the projected end-effector, or when the baseline policy is already near saturation.
- **Camera Setup:** The evaluation primarily used a single fixed primary camera per scene. Its effectiveness in multi-view fusion or moving-camera setups remains an area for future investigation.
- **Scope of Evaluation:** The evaluation covered three robot embodiments and two imitation learning policy families. Further work is needed to assess its transferability to humanoids, highly dexterous hands, or reinforcement learning paradigms, as well as to develop calibration-aware alignment mechanisms and multi-point kinematic grounding.
