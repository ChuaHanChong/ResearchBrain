# KILVO: Kinematic-Inertial-LiDAR-Visual Odometry with Robust Multimodal Adaptation for Humanoid Robots

> [!summary] Summary
> KILVO presents a robust kinematic-inertial-LiDAR-visual odometry system for humanoid robots, achieving high accuracy and a 1 kHz output rate by seamlessly adapting to sensor failures and degradation. The system integrates an efficient, sensor-agnostic contact estimation module, enabling reliable state estimation across challenging real-world scenarios.

## Problem

- Proprioceptive odometry in humanoid robots suffers from significant accumulated drift, particularly on unstable platforms and over long durations.
- Existing multisensor fusion systems for humanoid robots often assume continuous data streams, making them fragile to real-world sensor degradation, failures, or interruptions.
- Reliable and efficient foot contact estimation, crucial for kinematic updates, typically relies on specialized hardware or lacks robustness across diverse terrains and gait patterns.

## Method

- An asynchronous-sequential hybrid ==error-state iterated Kalman filter (ESIKF)== integrates high-rate kinematic and inertial data with slower LiDAR and visual measurements, forming **KILVO**.
- A ==multimodal adaptation== mechanism continuously monitors sensor health and measurement reliability to seamlessly switch between available sensor modalities (e.g., KILV, KIL, KI, LIV, LI) during degradation or failure.
- A compact, sensor-agnostic ==contact estimation module== reuses kinematic, inertial, and map cues to dynamically detect foot contact and non-contact, improving efficiency and robustness without dedicated sensors.

## Results

- KILVO achieved an average end-to-end translation error of **0.0145 m** on real-world datasets, outperforming or matching state-of-the-art methods in accuracy and stability under aggressive motion and diverse terrains.
- The system maintained continuous localization and mapping during simulated unrecoverable sensor failures (camera and LiDAR), completing a **9-minute** task with a translation error of **0.3338 m**.
- Its contact estimation module demonstrated over **95%** accuracy and an average **3.67%** false positive rate, along with a **76%** efficiency improvement over comparable methods.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The hybrid ESIKF architecture allows for high-frequency (1 kHz) state outputs driven by proprioceptive data, complemented by asynchronous exteroceptive corrections for drift mitigation.
> - Adaptive noise covariance for kinematic measurements and active sensor health monitoring provide a robust mechanism to handle non-ideal ground contacts and gracefully degrade or recover from sensor issues.
> - Efficient and accurate contact estimation can be achieved by leveraging dynamic initialization, normalized deviations from motion statistics, and reusing existing sensor data for ground patch fitting.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Odometry and Simultaneous Localization and Mapping (SLAM) are fundamental capabilities for autonomous robots, enabling them to estimate their pose and reconstruct maps within unknown environments. For humanoid robots, these capabilities are crucial for high-level tasks such as exploration and planning, as well as low-level functions like locomotion control.

Early approaches to state estimation for legged robots often relied primarily on proprioceptive sensors, such as Inertial Measurement Units (IMUs) and joint encoders, often referred to as proprioceptive odometry. These methods, typically implemented with nonlinear filters like Extended Kalman Filters (EKFs) or invariant observers, offer high-rate state feedback with low computational overhead. Examples include extensions of StarETH state estimators and methods utilizing multiple IMUs. However, a significant limitation of proprioceptive-only systems is their tendency to accumulate drift over time, particularly under non-ideal conditions like challenging terrains or dynamic motions. This issue is more pronounced in humanoid robots due to their vertical chain structure, low-redundancy contacts, and complex locomotion, which can amplify measurement noise and constraint uncertainty, leading to unobservability of yaw and position.

To mitigate drift and enhance accuracy, the robotics community has increasingly focused on fusing proprioceptive data with exteroceptive sensors, primarily LiDAR and cameras. Recent works have integrated LiDAR and camera data with proprioception for humanoid robots, addressing real-time requirements and accuracy. These include approaches that use feature-based or direct visual methods, as well as LiDAR-inertial-kinematic odometry systems like LIKO and HR$^2$-KILO. While these fusion techniques have advanced the field, several challenges remain for reliable autonomy in humanoid robots.

One primary challenge is the fragility of tightly coupled systems to sensor degradation and failure. Humanoid platforms are inherently unstable, and real-world environments introduce complexities such as body vibrations, measurement degradation (e.g., due to lighting variations or textureless scenes for cameras, sparse environments for LiDAR), and data interruptions caused by physical impacts or software issues. Many existing fusion pipelines assume continuous sensor availability and lack robust mechanisms for multimodal adaptation, potentially leading to system failure or robot damage.

Another area of ongoing research is reliable contact estimation. Accurate knowledge of foot contact with the ground is essential for kinematic updates in legged robots. Traditional methods often rely on empirically thresholding ground reaction forces (GRFs) or using dedicated contact sensors, which can be limited in robustness across diverse terrains and gait patterns. Learning-based approaches offer an alternative but are dependent on comprehensive training data. Some systems integrate contact estimation with state estimation, but often suffer from limitations such as coupled bipedal contact estimation or reliance on specific sensor modalities.

KILVO addresses these gaps by proposing a robust multisensor fusion framework specifically tailored for humanoid robots, aiming to provide high-accuracy, high-rate state estimation while explicitly handling sensor failures and integrating an efficient, sensor-agnostic contact estimation module.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a robust and accurate kinematic-inertial-LiDAR-visual odometry system, named KILVO, for humanoid robots. This system aims to fully leverage common onboard sensors—joint encoders, IMU, LiDAR, and camera—within a unified framework to overcome the limitations of existing odometry solutions for humanoid platforms.

The motivation behind KILVO stems from several critical challenges identified in the current state of robotic state estimation:
- **Addressing Drift in Proprioceptive Odometry:** While proprioceptive methods offer high-rate feedback, they suffer from significant accumulated drift over long durations or in challenging environments, especially on unstable humanoid platforms. KILVO seeks to counteract this by tightly integrating exteroceptive measurements.
- **Enhancing Robustness to Sensor Degradation and Failure:** Existing multisensor fusion systems often assume continuous data streams from all modalities. However, real-world operation of humanoid robots is prone to sensor degradation (e.g., motion blur, poor illumination, sparse LiDAR returns) and outright failures (e.g., data interruption from collisions). A key motivation is to design a system that can gracefully adapt to such events, maintaining consistent state estimation even when specific sensors are compromised or unavailable.
- **Achieving High Accuracy and Efficiency Simultaneously:** Humanoid robots require accurate pose information at high rates for both perception and control. KILVO aims to deliver competitive localization accuracy while providing 1 kHz state outputs, a critical requirement for real-time locomotion control.
- **Developing a Compact and Reliable Contact Estimation:** Contact information is vital for kinematic updates, but current methods often depend on additional hardware (e.g., force/torque sensors) or suffer from robustness issues across varied terrains and gaits. KILVO is motivated to integrate a stable and efficient contact estimation module that reuses existing sensor data, reducing hardware dependency and simplifying deployment.
- **Comprehensive Evaluation and Community Contribution:** The authors identify a need for robust systems validated across diverse scenarios and humanoid platforms. They are motivated to conduct extensive experiments on public and real-world datasets, including challenging environments and sensor failure cases, and to release their code and datasets to the research community.

In summary, KILVO's core objectives are to build a resilient, accurate, and efficient odometry solution that supports the anytime-anywhere autonomy of humanoid robots by robustly fusing all available sensor modalities and adapting to real-world complexities like sensor failures and degradation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

KILVO operates as a multisensor fusion odometry system, integrating data from joint encoders, an IMU, a LiDAR, and a camera within an asynchronous-sequential hybrid error-state iterated Kalman filter (ESIKF) framework. The system is designed with explicit multimodal adaptation capabilities and a compact contact estimation module.

#### 3.1. System Overview

Measurements from all sensors are fed into the system at their respective frequencies. Before processing, a data health check is performed. In full modality, IMU data drives state prediction, leg kinematics are processed asynchronously at a high rate, and LiDAR and visual updates are performed sequentially. A unified hash-indexed voxel map is used for both exteroceptive updates and contact estimation.

#### 3.2. State Definition and Prediction

The system state includes body rotation, position, and velocity, IMU biases (gyroscope and accelerometer), contact foot position, gravity vector, and inverse camera exposure time. The IMU provides high-frequency angular velocity and acceleration measurements. State prediction (from $t_i$ to $t_{i+1}$) is performed using a discretized process model based on IMU readings, with process noise including measurement noise and bias noise. The predicted state and covariance are propagated using Jacobian matrices in the ESIKF framework.

#### 3.3. Asynchronous Phase: Kinematic Update (1 kHz)

For a foot detected as being in contact with the ground (determined by the contact estimation module), kinematic measurements are constructed to update the state.
1. **Kinematic Measurements:** Three types of measurements are utilized:
 - **Contact foot velocity ($h_{cv}$):** Modeled as zero in the world frame.
 - **Contact foot position ($h_{cp}$):** Constrains the foot position to remain at a fixed point relative to the estimated contact point.
 - **Contact foot height ($h_{ch}$):** Constrains the foot to be on an estimated ground plane.
 These measurements are derived from forward kinematics relative to the IMU frame and the estimated contact foot position/ground patch in the world frame.
2. **Iterative Update:** The kinematic measurement model is linearized using a first-order Taylor expansion to form a residual. The ESIKF framework then iteratively updates the state and covariance.
3. **Robust Noise Adaptation:** To handle ground impacts and uncertainties, the nominal kinematic measurement noise covariance is adaptively adjusted. A normalized deviation score, calculated from the current kinematic residual and reference statistics (obtained from contact estimation), is used to re-weight the covariance matrix. This provides a mechanism for robustly handling the non-ideal kinematic constraints.

#### 3.4. Sequential Phase: Exteroceptive Updates (10 Hz)

When LiDAR and visual data are acquired, they are processed sequentially after being synchronized.
1. **LiDAR Measurement:**
 - **Preprocessing:** LiDAR scans are recombined and distortion-corrected using states from a state buffer (which stores high-rate updated states from the asynchronous phase).
 - **Measurement Model:** For each undistorted point, a point-to-plane measurement model is constructed. This involves finding the corresponding plane parameters (normal and center point) from the unified hash-indexed voxel map.
2. **Visual Measurement:**
 - **Preprocessing:** Visual map points are selected from the voxel map and projected onto the current image.
 - **Measurement Model:** Sparse-direct visual measurements are formulated based on photometric errors between current and reference image patches. This involves comparing pixel intensity values, considering inverse exposure times and affine warping for robustness.
3. **Iterative Update:**
 - **LiDAR Update:** The LiDAR measurement model is linearized, and the state and covariance are iteratively updated using ESIKF.
 - **Visual Update:** The LiDAR-updated state and covariance serve as the prior for the visual update. The visual measurement model is then linearized, and the state is further refined using ESIKF.
 After updates, LiDAR points are appended to the map to update geometric structure, and images update reference patches for visual map points.

#### 3.5. Multimodal Adaptation

KILVO features a modality switcher and data synchronization manager for robust adaptation to sensor failures and degradation.
1. **Health Status Monitoring:** Sensor health is determined by:
 - **Data-stream integrity:** Monitoring the time difference between a global check time and each sensor's last update time. Exceeding a threshold indicates sensor loss.
 - **Measurement reliability:** Integration of degradation indicators (e.g., PCA-based eigenvalue ratio for LiDAR spatial distribution, number of valid map points for visual).
2. **Adaptive Transitions:** If a sensor is deemed lost or degraded, KILVO seamlessly switches to a feasible modality (e.g., KILV, KIL, KI, LIV, LI, where K=kinematic, I=inertial, L=LiDAR, V=visual).
 - In KIL modality (camera unavailable), mapping uses geometry only.
 - In KI modality (LiDAR and camera unavailable), historical contact foot positions are cached for ground patch fitting instead of the map.
 - If leg kinematics are unavailable (LIV/LI modality), contact estimation is suspended, and the state buffer is cleared, with distortion correction based on IMU predicted states.
3. **Recovery:** The system continuously monitors sensor health, allowing recovery to richer modalities once sensors become available and measurement quality improves (e.g., acceleration normalized deviation below a threshold to avoid low-quality measurements during impacts).

#### 3.6. Contact Estimation

A compact contact estimation module is developed, reusing existing kinematic, inertial, and map cues without additional sensors.
1. **Dynamic Initialization:** At system startup, static reference statistics (for acceleration, foot velocity, and kinematic residual) are computed during an initial stationary period. These are used to detect the first lift-off and touchdown, establishing swing-reference statistics (S_sw). A sliding window of foot clearances (C) is also maintained, computed using ground patches from the voxel map or by fitting historical contact foot positions if the map is unavailable.
2. **Contact/Non-contact Detection:**
 - **Normalized Deviations:** Frame-wise normalized deviations for body acceleration ($d_a$) and foot velocity ($d_{fv}$) are calculated using the swing-reference statistics.
 - **Detection Logic:**
 - For a non-contact foot, if the acceleration cue ($d_a$) is activated (above a threshold for a duration), the foot with the larger signed area between its clearance trajectory and reference lines is declared in contact. If the foot velocity cue ($d_{fv}$) deactivates and the recent clearance trend indicates descent, it's also set to contact.
 - For a contact foot, if the foot velocity cue ($d_{fv}$) is activated, it's declared non-contact.
 - **Robustness:** Thresholds and settling periods (e.g., $d_a$ below threshold for N frames) are used to minimize false detections caused by coupled fluctuations in $d_a$ and $d_{fv}$ during practical contact events. The contact states and deviations are then used for kinematic update covariance reweighting.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research paper presents extensive experiments across public datasets and real-world scenarios, demonstrating KILVO's performance in contact estimation, state estimation accuracy, efficiency, and robustness.

#### 4.1. Contact Estimation

KILVO's contact estimation module was evaluated against GRF thresholding, a learning-based method (DCE), and HR$^2$-KILO's module (HRC).
- **Accuracy:** KILVO achieved the highest accuracy and lowest false positive rate (FPR) in 7 out of 8 sequences, maintaining over 95% accuracy and an average FPR of 3.67%. It showed superior detection stability, especially in challenging scenarios like "fwd run" with an emergency stop and "soft terrain" where other methods struggled (e.g., GRF becoming threshold-dependent and failing, DCE degrading due to out-of-distribution events).
- **Efficiency:** KILVO demonstrated a 76% efficiency improvement over HRC, with an average processing time of approximately 0.02 ms per frame. This is attributed to retrieving map points and building ground patches only at foot-contact moments, reusing this information within a contact event rather than frame-by-frame processing.

#### 4.2. Accuracy Evaluation

1. **Public Dataset Benchmark:** KILVO, operating in its KIL modality, was compared with LIO-SAM, FAST-LIO2, FAST-LIVO2*, LIKO, and HR$^2$-KILO.
 - **LIKO Dataset:** KILVO achieved the lowest Absolute Trajectory Error (ATE) RMSE in 3 of 5 sequences (average RMSE 0.0151 m) and the lowest Relative Trajectory Error (RTE) RMSE across all sequences.
 - **HR$^2$-KILO Dataset:** KILVO maintained end-to-end Z-axis errors below 1 cm, achieving optimal results in sequences like "long corr" and "spinning."
 These results indicate KILVO's competitive, often superior, accuracy on established benchmarks.
2. **Real-world Experiments:** Evaluated against LIO-SAM, FAST-LIO2, R$^3$LIVE, FAST-LIVO2, LIKO*, and HR$^2$-KILO across 15 diverse sequences.
 - KILVO achieved the best overall accuracy with an average end-to-end translation error of 0.0145 m. It obtained the highest accuracy in 8 sequences and ranked second in 2 others.
 - The results indicated that KILVO consistently outperformed or matched other state-of-the-art methods, particularly demonstrating stability under aggressive motion, unstructured scenes, and difficult terrains, where other methods like LIO-SAM (due to disrupted feature association), R$^3$LIVE (during aggressive motion), and LIKO/HR$^2$-KILO (due to unavailable/incorrect kinematic constraints or contact estimation degradation) experienced accuracy loss or failures.
 - Ablation studies showed that while individual sensors might experience degradation (e.g., camera images from impacts/illumination, kinematic constraints from slippage), KILVO's tightly coupled fusion system effectively resisted such compound disturbances.

#### 4.3. Efficiency Evaluation

- KILVO maintained a competitive average processing time of 14 ms, while providing a 1 kHz output rate, for fusing four distinct sensor modalities.
- This performance compares favorably with R$^3$LIVE (average ~137 ms) and HR$^2$-KILO (~27 ms). While FAST-LIO2 and FAST-LIVO2 had lower average times (~10 ms), their output rate was limited by LiDAR/camera frequency, unlike KILVO's 1 kHz.
- Detailed breakdown showed kinematic processing typically below 0.2 ms, LiDAR processing below 10 ms, and visual processing around 5 ms, affirming real-time capabilities.

#### 4.4. Robustness Performance

1. **Unrecoverable Sensor Failure:** On a 9-minute "robust h01" sequence with simulated unrecoverable camera and LiDAR failures:
 - KILVO seamlessly transitioned from KILV to KIL (camera loss at 350 s), then to KI (LiDAR loss at 500 s), maintaining continuous localization and mapping (though mapping became geometric without color).
 - The system completed the task with an end-to-end translation error of 0.3338 m. The degradation was attributed primarily to proprioceptive drift from prolonged exteroception loss, which was considered an acceptable trade-off for improved robustness.
2. **Sensor Failure & Recovery:** On a "robust h02*" sequence with sequential, temporary failures and recoveries of encoders, LiDAR, and camera:
 - KILVO demonstrated graceful degradation and recovery. It adapted to LIV (encoder loss, reducing output rate from 1 kHz to 10 Hz), then to KI (LiDAR loss, suspending mapping), and KIL (image loss), subsequently reverting to full modality upon sensor recovery.
 - The system completed the task with an end-to-end error of 0.0165 m, showcasing its ability to tolerate and recover from sensor failures without drastic localization jumps.
3. **Measurement Degradation:** KILVO's multimodal adaptation mechanism was also tested against degraded measurements:
 - **LiDAR Degradation:** The system intermittently transitioned modalities to handle degraded point clouds (detected by PCA-based eigenvalue ratio) and continuously relied on KI modality during periods of severe degradation.
 - **Visual Degradation:** When the robot approached a non-textured wall, leading to a vanishing number of valid visual map points, KILVO transitioned to KIL mode without visual updates.
 These tests confirmed KILVO's flexible support for different degradation indicators to drive modality adaptation, maintaining robustness to low-quality measurements.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

KILVO represents an advancement in robust state estimation for humanoid robots, with several significant contributions and potential impacts:

1. **Enhanced Reliability for Humanoid Autonomy:** By explicitly addressing sensor degradation and failures through seamless multimodal adaptation, KILVO provides a more reliable and resilient odometry solution. This enhanced robustness is critical for deploying humanoid robots in real-world, unstructured, and dynamic environments where sensor reliability cannot be guaranteed. It moves towards enabling "anytime-anywhere autonomy" for these complex platforms.
2. **High-Rate and Accurate State Estimation:** The tightly coupled asynchronous-sequential hybrid ESIKF architecture delivers competitive localization accuracy while maintaining a 1 kHz output rate. This high-frequency, accurate state feedback is essential for advanced locomotion controllers and dynamic tasks, improving the robot's ability to maintain balance and execute complex movements.
3. **Reduced Hardware Dependency for Contact Estimation:** The compact and efficient contact estimation module, which reuses existing kinematic, inertial, and map data, eliminates the need for dedicated contact sensors. This reduces hardware complexity and cost, making the system more accessible and easier to deploy across various humanoid robot platforms.
4. **Comprehensive Validation and Open-Source Contribution:** The extensive evaluation on both public and newly collected real-world datasets, covering diverse gaits, scenarios, and sensor failure modes, provides a rigorous validation of the system's performance. The release of the code and datasets to the community fosters further research and development in this domain.
5. **Foundation for Future Humanoid Robotics Applications:** A robust and reliable state estimation system like KILVO is foundational for numerous high-level tasks in humanoid robotics, including exploration, manipulation in cluttered environments, human-robot interaction, and disaster response. Its ability to operate in degraded sensing conditions expands the potential application areas for humanoid robots.

The work also opens avenues for future research, such as integrating learning-based modules to further improve adaptability, which could enhance the system's ability to handle even more complex and unforeseen scenarios.
