# Model Merging in Pre-training of Large Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Pre-trained Model Average (PMA), developed by researchers at ByteDance Seed, Peking University, and The University of Hong Kong, consistently improves performance across large language models up to 200 billion parameters by merging checkpoints during pre-training. This method can predict final annealing performance from constant learning rate phases and enhances training stability, notably by recovering training from otherwise irrecoverable loss spikes.

## Problem

- Large Language Model (LLM) pre-training faces immense computational costs, unpredictable performance scaling, and inherent training instability.
- While model merging is applied in post-training, its potential during the pre-training phase, especially for very large models, remains largely unexplored and undocumented publicly.
- Independent researchers find it practically difficult to evaluate pre-training merging due to limited access to intermediate checkpoints from extensive training runs.

## Method

- Proposed ==Pre-trained Model Average (PMA)==, a novel model-level weight merging strategy, specifically designed for the LLM pre-training phase.
- Conducted extensive experiments on independently trained dense and ==Mixture-of-Experts LLMs== ranging from **411 million** to **200 billion** parameters.
- Evaluated different merging variants (SMA, EMA, WMA) and performed ablation studies on key hyperparameters like merging interval and the number of models.

## Results

- Pre-trained Model Average yielded consistent performance improvements across a wide range of LLMs, from **411 million** to **200 billion** parameters.
- PMA-init enhanced training stability, leading to reduced loss spikes and successful recovery from otherwise irrecoverable training collapses.
- Optimal merging intervals showed a scaling relationship with model size (e.g., **~8B tokens** for 1.3B models), and merging a larger number of models consistently improved performance in later training stages.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Pre-training with a constant learning rate combined with PMA can effectively match the performance of an annealed model, potentially eliminating the need for a separate learning rate annealing phase.
> - PMA-merged models ("PMA-init") can serve as robust initialization weights for continual training and supervised fine-tuning, requiring less learning rate tuning.
> - Model merging is effective when individual checkpoints have explored complementary regions in the loss landscape, allowing the average to find a better optimum.
