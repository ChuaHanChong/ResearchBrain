# Model Merging in Pre-training of Large Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Pre-trained Model Average (PMA), developed by researchers at ByteDance Seed, Peking University, and The University of Hong Kong, consistently improves performance across large language models up to 200 billion parameters by merging checkpoints during pre-training. This method can predict final annealing performance from constant learning rate phases and enhances training stability, notably by recovering training from otherwise irrecoverable loss spikes.

## Problem

- Large Language Model (LLM) pre-training faces immense computational costs, unpredictable performance scaling, and inherent training instability.
- While model merging is applied in post-training, its potential during the pre-training phase, especially for very large models, remains largely unexplored and undocumented publicly.
- Independent researchers find it practically difficult to evaluate pre-training merging due to limited access to intermediate checkpoints from extensive training runs.

## Method

- Proposed ==Pre-trained Model Average (PMA)==, a novel model-level weight merging strategy, specifically designed for the LLM pre-training phase.
- Conducted extensive experiments on independently trained dense and ==Mixture-of-Experts LLMs== ranging from **411 million** to **200 billion** parameters.
- Evaluated different merging variants (SMA, EMA, WMA) and performed ablation studies on key hyperparameters like merging interval and the number of models.

## Results

- Pre-trained Model Average yielded consistent performance improvements across a wide range of LLMs, from **411 million** to **200 billion** parameters.
- PMA-init enhanced training stability, leading to reduced loss spikes and successful recovery from otherwise irrecoverable training collapses.
- Optimal merging intervals showed a scaling relationship with model size (e.g., **~8B tokens** for 1.3B models), and merging a larger number of models consistently improved performance in later training stages.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Pre-training with a constant learning rate combined with PMA can effectively match the performance of an annealed model, potentially eliminating the need for a separate learning rate annealing phase.
> - PMA-merged models ("PMA-init") can serve as robust initialization weights for continual training and supervised fine-tuning, requiring less learning rate tuning.
> - Model merging is effective when individual checkpoints have explored complementary regions in the loss landscape, allowing the average to find a better optimum.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of Large Language Models (LLMs) has been a transformative force in AI, demonstrating remarkable capabilities across diverse tasks. However, their pervasive adoption is hindered by several critical challenges: exorbitant pre-training costs, the limited effectiveness of domain-specific post-training, unpredictable performance scaling, and inherent instability during large-scale training. Model merging has emerged as a promising technique to mitigate some of these issues.

Historically, the benefits of model merging have been predominantly explored and applied in the **post-training stage**. Here, the technique involves combining several models fine-tuned on different downstream tasks into a single, more versatile model. Examples include Task Arithmetic, Ties-Merging, DARE, MetaGPT, Fisher Merging, and RegMean, which aim to integrate diverse capabilities (e.g., merging WizardLM with WizardMath for significant performance gains on math benchmarks).

In contrast, **research on model merging during the pre-training phase remains relatively scarce and underexplored.** Existing work in this area is limited; for instance, LAWA (Latest Weight Averaging) has shown some promise in accelerating LLM training by merging intermediate checkpoints from a single training trajectory. Similarly, Sanyal et al. noted that checkpoint averaging combined with high learning rates can lead to faster convergence. While major players like DeepSeek and LLaMA-3 have indicated their internal use of model merging techniques during pre-training, detailed methodologies have not been publicly disclosed, leaving a significant knowledge gap for the broader research community.

This paper directly addresses this gap. By focusing specifically on model merging within the pre-training paradigm, the authors aim to provide the first detailed technical insights into scaling these methods to significantly larger model sizes (up to 200 billion parameters). This work represents a crucial step towards making large-scale LLM pre-training more efficient, stable, and cost-effective, directly impacting the practical development and deployment of next-generation AI models. It seeks to transition model merging from a primarily post-training optimization to a fundamental pre-training strategy.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind this research stems from the aforementioned challenges in LLM development: the immense computational costs, unpredictable performance, and inherent instability of large-scale pre-training. While model merging has shown promise in post-training, its potential during pre-training, particularly for very large models, has been largely unexplored. The authors highlight the practical difficulty for independent researchers to evaluate pre-training merging due to limited access to intermediate checkpoints from extensive training runs.

Against this backdrop, the key objectives of the paper are:

- **Introduce Pre-trained Model Average (PMA):** To propose a novel, model-level weight merging strategy specifically designed for the pre-training phase of LLMs.
- **Comprehensive Evaluation of PMA's Performance:** To systematically assess the impact of PMA on LLM performance across a diverse range of model sizes (from 411M to 70B Dense, and 0.7B/7B to 20B/200B Mixture-of-Experts) and learning rate schedules (Warmup-Stable-Decay). The goal is to demonstrate consistent performance improvements.
- **Predictive Power for Annealing Behavior:** To investigate whether merging checkpoints from a constant learning rate phase can accurately predict, or even surpass, the performance achieved through a traditional learning rate annealing (decay) phase, thereby enabling more efficient model validation and reduced training costs.
- **Ablation Studies on Merging Strategies and Hyperparameters:** To provide a detailed analysis of different merging variants (SMA, EMA, WMA) and critical hyperparameters (interval between merged checkpoints, number of models involved), offering insights into their optimal configurations and underlying mechanisms.
- **Novel Applications for Downstream Training and Stability:** To explore if PMA-merged models can serve as more effective initialization weights ("PMA-init") for subsequent Continual Training (CT) and Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) stages, specifically investigating their impact on training stability (e.g., mitigating loss spikes) without compromising performance.
- **Provide Practical Guidelines:** To distill comprehensive experimental findings into actionable guidelines for the open-source community, facilitating the effective adoption of model merging techniques in LLM pre-training.

In essence, the paper aims to transform model merging into a reliable, cost-effective, and performance-enhancing tool for the very foundational stage of LLM development.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research employs a rigorous experimental approach to comprehensively evaluate model merging during LLM pre-training, introducing their proposed strategy, **Pre-trained Model Average (PMA)**.

**Model Architectures and Training Setup:**
The authors independently trained a diverse set of LLMs from scratch, encompassing both dense architectures (ranging from 411 million to 70 billion parameters) and Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) architectures (with activated/total parameters from 0.7B/7B to 20B/200B). This broad range of models ensures the generalizability of their findings across different scales and architectural paradigms.
For training, they utilized a **Warmup-Stable-Decay (WSD)** learning rate scheduler, which includes an initial warmup, an extended period of stable training with a constant learning rate, and a concluding cosine decay phase. Learning rates were determined based on scaling law guidelines, optimized for an internal pre-training corpus comprising trillions of tokens. While specific model architectures and datasets are not publicly disclosed, the authors posit that their conclusions are not strongly tied to these particular choices, given the extensive scope of their experiments.

**Evaluation:**
Model performance was primarily evaluated on a comprehensive suite of open-source benchmarks, including ARC-Challenge, BBH, DROP, WinoGrande, HellaSwag, MMLU, C-Eval, TriviaQA, Ape210K, GSM8K, MATH, MBPP, HumanEval, AGIEval, GPQA, and MMLU-Pro. A weighted average score across these benchmarks served as the primary performance metric. For downstream tasks like Supervised Fine-tuning (SFT), additional in-house evaluation sets (OOD, Reasoning, Instruction Following) were used.

**Model Merging Variants:**
The core of PMA involves combining sequential checkpoints along a training trajectory. The general formula for merging *N* models with parameters *Mᵢ* and weights *wᵢ* is *M_avg = Σ wᵢMᵢ*. The paper specifically investigated three popular weighting schemes:
- **Simple Moving Average (SMA):** Assigns uniform weights (wᵢ = 1/N) to all models, treating them equally.
- **Exponential Moving Average (EMA):** Emphasizes more recent models by assigning exponentially decaying weights (wᵢ = α(1-α)^(N-i)), where α is a smoothing factor.
- **Weighted Moving Average (WMA):** Prioritizes recent models using linearly increasing weights (e.g., wᵢ = i), normalized over the sum of weights.

**Experimental Questions and Ablation Studies:**
The methodology was structured around addressing six key questions:
1. **Performance Impact:** How does PMA affect performance during both constant learning rate and cosine annealing phases? This involved comparing merged vs. unmerged models and also exploring if PMA with constant LR could mimic or exceed annealing.
2. **Merging Method Comparison:** Systematically evaluating EMA, WMA, and SMA to determine their impact on final performance.
3. **Optimal Hyperparameters:** Ablating the effect of the *interval (V)* between selected models and the *number of models (N)* to merge across different model sizes.
4. **Downstream Training Benefits:** Investigating whether PMA-merged pre-trained models provide better initialization weights (dubbed "PMA-init") for Continual Training (CT) and Supervised Fine-tuning (SFT), assessing loss curves and performance.
5. **Training Stability Improvement:** Studying if PMA-init can enhance training stability, particularly in recovering from "loss spikes" by resuming training from merged checkpoints saved before a collapse. This was reproduced on small models to manage costs.
6. **Mechanisms of Model Merging:** Providing qualitative and quantitative analyses, including a second-order Taylor expansion of the loss function and visualizations of weight distributions relative to performance contours, to explain why model merging is effective.

This comprehensive set of experiments, spanning a wide range of model sizes, architectures, and training stages, allowed the authors to provide detailed insights and practical guidelines for pre-training model merging.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded several significant findings, demonstrating the efficacy and versatility of model merging during the pre-training phase of LLMs:

- **Consistent Performance Improvements in Pre-training:**
 - Merging checkpoints from the **constant learning rate phase** resulted in "significant performance improvements across multiple downstream tasks" for both dense and MoE architectures, ranging from 411M to 200B parameters. The improvements were robust and consistent across model sizes.
 - During the **cosine annealing phase**, PMA-merged models at early annealing stages achieved performance comparable to, or even superior to, models that underwent full natural annealing.

- **Efficient Annealing Prediction and Cost Reduction:**
 - A crucial finding is that pre-training with a constant learning rate combined with PMA can effectively **match the performance of an annealed model** at any point in the training process, eliminating the need for a separate learning rate annealing phase. This capability significantly accelerates model validation and reduces computational resource demands by allowing early performance simulation.

- **Impact of Merging Strategies:**
 - In the early phases of training, when model weights are still undergoing significant changes, **Weighted Moving Average (WMA) delivered the best results**, suggesting that prioritizing more recent checkpoints with more training tokens is beneficial.
 - However, as training advanced and model weights stabilized, the performance differences among SMA, EMA, and WMA "gradually become negligible." For its simplicity and stability, **SMA was primarily used in subsequent experiments.**

- **Optimal Hyperparameters for Merging:**
 - The **optimal merging interval (V)** between checkpoints exhibits a clear scaling relationship with model size. For instance, an interval of ~8B tokens was optimal for 1.3B/13B models, while ~80B tokens was optimal for 10B/100B models, aligning with larger models using larger batch sizes. Merging with large intervals too early in training (when weights are unstable) can negatively impact performance.
 - The **number of models (N)** involved in merging also impacts performance. While incorporating too many unstable models early in training can be detrimental, **merging a larger number of models consistently improved performance once training was completed**. For practical purposes, N=10 was chosen as a balance.

- **PMA-init for Downstream Training:**
 - **Continual Training (CT) Stage:** Initializing CT with PMA-merged weights ("PMA-init") consistently achieved marginally lower loss in the initial phase and slightly outperformed baselines early on. Although performance converged to comparable levels by the end of CT, PMA-init did not require extensive learning rate tuning.
 - **Supervised Fine-tuning (SFT) Stage:** While PMA-init occasionally yielded performance improvements, these gains were not consistently observed across all model sizes. However, importantly, PMA-init *did not adversely affect* downstream training outcomes and was deemed a viable strategy.

- **Improved Training Stability with PMA-init:**
 - PMA-init models demonstrated a "notably more stable GradNorm metric" and a "reduced frequency of loss spikes" during SFT.
 - Crucially, when training experienced "irrecoverable loss spikes" (simulated on small models), applying PMA-init over preceding stable checkpoints successfully **resumed and stabilized the training process**, preventing the need to restart from scratch and thus substantially reducing wasted computational resources. This is highlighted as a novel application.

- **Insights into Merging Mechanisms:**
 - Through mathematical analysis (second-order Taylor expansion) and visualization of weight distributions, the authors explain that model merging is effective when individual model checkpoints have "explored different local regions or directions within the parameter space" that exhibit "complementarity" concerning the loss landscape's geometric structure (i.e., deviation vectors pointing in somewhat opposing directions).
 - The averaged weight position of a merged model is often situated closer to a region of higher performance (a better optimum) than many individual checkpoints, smoothing out idiosyncrasies. This also explains why benefits diminish when models are already tightly converged (e.g., at very low learning rates), as merging points within an already narrow basin offers little room for significant improvement.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research marks a substantial contribution to the field of large language model development by pioneering a comprehensive and detailed investigation of model merging within the challenging pre-training phase. Its significance and potential impact are multifaceted:

- **Enhanced Efficiency and Cost Reduction:** The most direct and impactful contribution is the demonstration that PMA can deliver significant performance gains while simultaneously enabling the accurate prediction of annealing behavior. This means that costly and time-consuming cosine decay phases can potentially be skipped or shortened, allowing for "more efficient model development" and "significantly lower training costs." For the highly resource-intensive process of LLM pre-training, this translates into substantial savings in compute and time.
- **Improved Training Robustness and Reliability:** The introduction of "PMA-init" as a strategy to stabilize training dynamics, particularly in recovering from severe and otherwise irrecoverable loss spikes, is a critical innovation. This capability can prevent the catastrophic loss of multi-million-dollar training runs, offering a practical "alternative solution to avoid retraining the model from scratch." This makes LLM pre-training a more reliable and less risky endeavor.
- **Practical Guidelines for the Open-Source Community:** By thoroughly ablating various merging strategies, optimal hyperparameters (interval and number of models), and detailing their performance across a wide range of model sizes and architectures, the paper provides concrete, actionable "practical pre-training guidelines." This demystifies the application of model merging at scale, empowering smaller research groups and individual practitioners who lack the resources for extensive trial-and-error.
- **Deeper Theoretical Understanding:** The mathematical analysis using Taylor expansion and the visualization of weight distributions offer valuable insights into the underlying mechanisms of why model merging works. This moves beyond empirical observation to provide a more principled understanding of the loss landscape and how averaging can lead to better optima, fostering future methodological advancements.
- **Catalyst for Future Research:** The paper explicitly acknowledges limitations, such as not extensively exploring the impact of learning rate on merging (suggesting higher LRs might be better for merging) and not investigating model merging in post-training scenarios like Reinforcement Learning (RL) training. These limitations open clear avenues for future research, indicating that the potential of model merging is far from fully exhausted.
- **Economic Monitoring of Pre-training:** The ability of PMA to "flexibly simulate the ultimate model performance after annealing" means it can serve as a "reliable and economic monitor for the pre-training process." This allows researchers to get early indicators of a model's potential performance without having to complete the full training run, enabling faster iteration and more informed decision-making.

In summary, this research provides both a robust methodological framework and a deeper understanding of model merging in pre-training, poised to significantly impact the efficiency, stability, and accessibility of large language model development globally.
