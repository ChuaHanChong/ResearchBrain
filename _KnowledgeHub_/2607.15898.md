# Orbis 2: A Hierarchical World Model for Driving

> [!summary] Summary
> Orbis 2, a hierarchical world model from the University of Freiburg, enhances long-horizon prediction stability for autonomous driving by factorizing future prediction into abstract and detailed levels with distinct temporal scales. It achieves superior internal representations and robust action controllability, demonstrating state-of-the-art long-horizon rollout stability and reduced computational costs compared to existing driving world models.

## Problem

- Existing driving world models typically operate at a single level of abstraction, leading to accumulated errors and degradation in long-horizon predictions for complex driving scenarios.
- Latent spaces in current models are often optimized for fine visual details (pixel reconstruction), which is suboptimal for capturing coarse, slowly varying semantic properties essential for robust long-horizon forecasting.
- Standard teacher forcing training objectives often yield weaker internal representations, limiting the model's ability to generalize to out-of-distribution contexts and maintain stability over time.

## Method

- **Orbis 2** introduces a ==hierarchical architecture== with an abstract long-horizon predictor (operating at a coarse 2Hz in a ==DINO-aligned latent space==) and a detailed short-horizon predictor (operating at a finer 10Hz in a pixel-aligned latent space).
- It employs a novel two-stage training paradigm: pre-training with ==Diffusion Forcing== for robust representation learning, followed by fine-tuning with ==Teacher Forcing== to align with future prediction and improve autoregressive rollout stability.
- Action conditioning on steering commands is integrated into the top-level predictor via ==adaptive layer normalization (AdaLN)==, allowing for controllable generation of original and counterfactual trajectories.

## Results

- Orbis 2 achieves state-of-the-art long-horizon rollout stability (best FVD-slope metric) and competitive FVD scores for 6-second predictions, outperforming larger baselines while being significantly more computationally efficient (trained in under **6k H100-GPU-hours**).
- The model demonstrates superior internal representation quality, achieving the highest scores on semantic segmentation (**58.29 mIoU**) and depth estimation (**4.157 RMSE**) via linear probing among compared driving world models.
- It exhibits strong action controllability, achieving the lowest Average Displacement Error (ADE) on original and **three out of four** counterfactual steering trajectories, showcasing robust responsiveness to diverse agent commands.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decomposing future prediction across hierarchical levels with distinct temporal resolutions and specialized latent representations significantly improves long-horizon stability and reduces error accumulation in driving scenarios.
> - Learning semantically rich internal representations, particularly those aligned with DINO embeddings, is critical for building robust and stable world models capable of effective long-term forecasting.
> - A two-stage training strategy combining Diffusion Forcing for pre-training and Teacher Forcing for fine-tuning effectively enhances internal representation quality, leading to more generalizable and stable predictive models.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

World models, which are learned dynamical systems approximating environmental evolution in response to an agent's actions, are a foundational component for tasks such as planning, simulation, and policy learning, particularly in complex domains like autonomous driving. Current driving world models typically operate at a single level of abstraction, predominantly prioritizing perceptual fidelity. This often results in a lack of explicit spatial reasoning and semantic understanding required for effective long-horizon prediction in real-world scenarios.

The existing landscape of driving world models can be broadly categorized into two approaches:
1. **Discrete token space models:** These employ autoregressive transformers over quantized visual tokens, exemplified by models such as GAIA-1, DrivingWorld, DrivingGPT, and Cosmos. These models scale to large-scale video pretraining but can sometimes exhibit brittleness in long-horizon predictions.
2. **Continuous latent space models:** These typically utilize diffusion or flow-matching objectives, including models like Vista, DriveDreamer, GenAD, MagicDrive-V2, Epona, and Orbis (the predecessor to Orbis 2). Previous work, particularly Orbis, indicated that continuous representations are often less brittle and more effective for long-horizon prediction compared to discrete tokens.

A common limitation across both paradigms is the single-level abstraction of their predictors, which often generates rollouts frame-by-frame. This approach accumulates error over time and hinders explicit reasoning about long-horizon scene evolution. Furthermore, latent spaces are frequently shaped by reconstruction objectives that preserve fine visual details, which may be suboptimal for long-horizon forecasting where coarse, slowly varying semantic properties are more relevant.

The concept of hierarchical planning is established in reinforcement learning, where it is used to learn high-level skills and detailed control. Examples include Director, which learns a two-level hierarchy for subgoal selection and execution, and recent vision-language-action models that employ language-based planning at an abstract level and fine-grained policies for low-level control (e.g., Hi Robot, HiLAM). In the context of world models with abstract latent spaces, THICK explores multi-scale temporal abstractions for hierarchical model-based reinforcement learning, and DINO-WM uses DINO features as latents for its predictor. Concurrent work by Zhang et al. also explores hierarchical planning with predictors at two levels, though they share the same latent space.

Orbis 2 positions itself within this landscape by addressing the single-level abstraction limitation and the suboptimal nature of existing latent spaces for long-horizon prediction. It introduces a hierarchical approach inspired by cognitive science, where humans plan over distant goals using simplified, abstract representations and refine details as actions approach execution. The work aims to combine high perceptual fidelity with robust spatial and semantic representations, explicitly addressing long-horizon stability and action controllability, while also improving the quality of learned internal representations through a novel training paradigm.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind the development of Orbis 2 are rooted in overcoming the identified limitations of current driving world models and enhancing their capabilities for real-world autonomous driving applications.

1. **Address Single-Level Abstraction and Accumulation of Error:** Current world models typically predict future frames sequentially at a single temporal scale. This frame-by-frame generation accumulates errors over time, limiting the model's ability to reason effectively about extended scene evolution and leading to degradation in long-horizon rollouts. Orbis 2 aims to decompose future prediction into distinct levels of abstraction and temporal scales to mitigate this issue.

2. **Improve Spatial Reasoning and Semantic Understanding:** The latent spaces in many world models are often optimized for pixel reconstruction, preserving fine visual details. While useful for short-horizon fidelity, this can be suboptimal for long-horizon forecasting, which is primarily governed by coarse, slowly varying semantic properties. The motivation is to create representations that strongly capture spatial structure and semantic understanding, enabling more robust long-horizon dynamics estimation.

3. **Incorporate Principles from Cognitive Science:** The authors draw motivation from cognitive science, which suggests that humans plan over distant goals using simplified, abstract representations and only refine details closer to the point of action execution. This principle motivates the hierarchical decomposition of prediction in Orbis 2.

4. **Achieve High Perceptual Fidelity and Strong Representations Concurrently:** The goal is to develop a world model that can produce high-fidelity, detailed predictions for short horizons while simultaneously maintaining strong spatial and semantic representations for stable, long-horizon forecasting. This involves finding a balance between preserving visual details and capturing abstract dynamics.

5. **Enhance Internal Representation Quality through Training Strategy:** The researchers observed that the standard teacher forcing objective, where models predict the next frame from clean ground-truth context, yields weaker internal representations because the model is not exposed to out-of-distribution context during training. A key motivation is to devise a training paradigm that produces substantially richer internal representations, which are crucial for generalization to unseen scenarios.

6. **Improve Long-Horizon Rollout Stability:** Beyond basic visual fidelity, a critical objective is to ensure that the generated video sequences remain coherent and realistic over extended temporal horizons without significant degradation. This stability is measured by metrics that capture the progressive loss of fidelity over time.

7. **Ensure Robust Action Controllability:** For a driving world model to be practical, it must be responsive to agent actions. A specific objective is to enable conditional generation based on steering commands, not only for original trajectories but also for counterfactual scenarios (e.g., over/understeering), to evaluate the model's ability to truly follow commands rather than merely mimicking context.

8. **Develop an Efficient and Scalable Model:** The aim includes developing a model that is computationally efficient in terms of training hours and inference speed, while also requiring less VRAM, making it more accessible for research and deployment compared to other large-scale driving world models.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Orbis 2 introduces a hierarchical driving world model that factorizes future prediction across two levels of abstraction and distinct temporal scales. The methodology integrates flow matching, a novel two-stage training paradigm, and specialized latent representations for each hierarchical level.

#### 3.1. Hierarchical World Model Architecture

The core of Orbis 2 is a two-level hierarchy for future prediction, composed of an abstract long-horizon predictor and a detailed short-horizon predictor (Figure 1 in the paper).

- **Tokenizer Latent Representations:** For each observed driving frame `o`, two complementary latent representations are extracted:
 - **Fine-grained latent (`x`):** Produced by a tokenizer encoder `E_L`, trained with a reconstruction objective (similar to VQGAN). This latent preserves pixel-aligned details for the low-level predictor.
 - **Abstract latent (`z`):** Produced by an abstract encoder `E_H`, optimized to capture semantic and structural information. `E_H` is trained by aligning its representation with a frozen DINO embedding through a learned projection head `P`. A weak reconstruction regularizer (`λ_rec = 0.1`) is applied via a lightweight decoder `D_H` to retain task-relevant visual information without degrading semantic alignment. This results in a compact 16-dimensional DINO-aligned latent space.

- **Abstract Long-Horizon Predictor (`F_H`):**
 - Operates in the abstract latent space (`z`) at a coarse temporal resolution (e.g., 2Hz).
 - Takes a strided context of past abstract latents (`Z_k^N`) and the current ego action (`a_N`) as input.
 - Predicts the abstract latent `k` steps into the future.
 - During inference, `F_H` advances with a sliding window of 1 frame over a 6-frame window (3 seconds).

- **Detailed Short-Horizon Predictor (`F_L`):**
 - Operates in the fine-grained latent space (`x`) at a finer temporal resolution (e.g., 10Hz).
 - Responsible for generating `k` intermediate fine-grained frames.
 - Conditioned on its own short, high-frequency context window of `m` frames and the abstract subgoal (`ˆz_N+k`) predicted by `F_H`.
 - During inference, `F_L` advances with a sliding window of 5 frames over a 10-frame window (1 second).

#### 3.2. Flow Matching Objective

Both predictors utilize the flow matching objective to model the distribution of future frames in latent space. A noisy interpolant `x_τ^N` is constructed, and a velocity network `v(x_τ^N; x_0:N-1)` is trained to regress the ground-truth velocity field. Inference involves integrating the learned velocity field from noise to data.

#### 3.3. Two-Stage Training Paradigm for Predictive Representation Learning

A key methodological contribution is a generic two-stage training paradigm for the predictors, particularly for `F_H`:

1. **Pre-training with Diffusion Forcing (DF):** This objective independently corrupts each frame in the full sequence `x_0:N` with its own noise level `τ_i` and trains the model to predict the velocity for all frames jointly. This phase is found to yield substantially richer internal representations, as the model learns to handle corrupted contexts.
2. **Fine-tuning with Teacher Forcing (TF):** Following DF pre-training, the model is fine-tuned with the standard teacher forcing objective. In this stage, context frames are always provided as clean ground-truth observations, and the velocity is predicted only for the target frame. This fine-tuning aligns the predictor with the downstream future-prediction objective and improves the stability of autoregressive rollouts, retaining the representational quality gained from DF.

This training strategy is applied to the abstract predictor `F_H`. The detailed low-level predictor `F_L` is trained directly with a teacher-forcing objective, conditioned on the high-level predictor's output (ground-truth abstract latents during training, predicted latents during inference).

#### 3.4. Action-Conditioning

The world model is initially pre-trained unconditionally. Subsequently, it is fine-tuned for conditional generation on planar trajectories. The embedded steering vector is incorporated by adding it to the `τ` embedding and feeding it into the predictor via adaptive layer normalization (AdaLN) layers. Action conditioning is applied only to the top-level predictor branch.

#### 3.5. Model Design and Datasets

- **Tokenizers:** Two hybrid tokenizers are trained, following. The high-level tokenizer prioritizes DINOv2B embedding reconstruction (`λ_rec = 0.1` for pixel reconstruction). The low-level tokenizer is trained primarily for pixel-aligned reconstruction (VQGAN objective).
- **Predictor Backbone:** An ST-Transformer backbone (extension of DiT block) with 512M parameters is used for the abstract predictor. The detail predictor adds 43M parameters through spatially aligned conditioning via AdaLN. The full hierarchical predictor totals 1067M parameters.
- **Training Data:** Both tokenizers are trained on a mix of 2.6 million frames from BDD100K, OpenDV, Honda HAD/HDD, ONCE, nuScenes, and nuPlan. The hierarchical predictors are trained for one epoch over 5,890 hours of video data from OpenDV, BDD100k, PhysicalAI-AV, and NATIX. For steering-conditional generation, the model is fine-tuned on a 500-hour subset of the NVIDIA PhysicalAI AV dataset.

#### 3.6. Evaluation Metrics

- **Representation Quality:** Assessed via linear probing on semantic segmentation (Cityscapes, mIoU) and depth estimation (KITTI, RMSE) using the model's frozen intermediate features.
- **Predictive Performance:** Evaluated using Fréchet Video Distance (FVD) on Waymo, nuPlan, and nuPlan-turns benchmarks for 6-second rollouts.
- **Long-horizon Stability:** Measured by chunked-FVD (FVD over consecutive 4-second windows) and a newly introduced FVD-slope metric (`(FVD_T - FVD_1) / (T-1)`) to quantify temporal degradation.
- **Action Controllability:** Evaluated by Average Displacement Error (ADE) between estimated and ground truth trajectories, for both original and counterfactual steering inputs, estimated using an external inverse dynamics model (VGGT). Trajectories are scaled to remove global scale error before ADE calculation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several key findings across model performance, representation quality, and architectural design choices.

#### 4.1. Comparison to State-of-the-art

- **Long-horizon Rollout Performance:** Orbis 2 demonstrates competitive FVD scores for 6-second rollouts. While Cosmos-v2.5 achieves lower FVD on nuPlan benchmarks, it is approximately 2x larger in parameters and trained on 3x more data. Orbis 2 outperforms Cosmos-v2.5 on the Waymo benchmark. More significantly, Orbis 2 achieves the best FVD-slope, indicating superior long-horizon rollout stability compared to all baselines, including Cosmos-v2.5, Epona, Vista, GEM, and Orbis (v1). This highlights a reduced rate of video quality degradation over time.
- **Conditional Trajectory Evaluation:** Orbis 2 exhibits strong action controllability. It achieves the lowest Average Displacement Error (ADE) on the original steering trajectories and for three out of four counterfactual scenarios (speed×1.5, yaw rate×0.5, yaw rate×1.5) compared to Orbis and Epona. Epona shows slightly better performance for the speed×0.5 counterfactual case. This demonstrates the model's ability to follow diverse steering commands, including those deviating from standard driving patterns.

#### 4.2. Strong Predictor Representations Lead to Stable World Models

- **Representation Quality:** Orbis 2 achieves the highest scores on both semantic segmentation (mIoU on Cityscapes) and depth estimation (RMSE on KITTI) via linear probing among all compared driving world models. This indicates that Orbis 2 learns richer semantic and geometric internal representations.
- **Correlation with Stability:** A clear correlation is observed between stronger semantic and geometric understanding (higher mIoU, lower RMSE) and more stable long-horizon prediction (lower FVD-slope). Orbis 2's superior representation quality aligns with its best FVD-slope performance, suggesting that robust internal representations are critical for long-term predictive stability.

#### 4.3. Comparison between Hierarchical Variants

A study on different hierarchical design choices revealed the following:

- **Flat (Single-branch) Baselines:**
 - Detail-level model (Exp. 1): Achieves strong initial FVD but experiences progressive degradation and learns weak internal representations.
 - Abstract-level model (Exp. 2): Yields substantially richer representations and highly stable long-horizon rollouts (low FVD-slope), but with high absolute FVD due to lack of fine detail.
- **Hierarchical Configurations:**
 - **Proposed Hierarchical Design (Exp. 3, Orbis 2 final):** An abstract top predictor operating at 2Hz and a detailed bottom predictor operating at 10Hz. This configuration achieved the best balance of overall FVD, best FVD-slope, and strong representation quality for the abstract branch.
 - **Other Hierarchical Variants:**
 - Top predictor operating in detailed latent space at 2Hz (Exp. 4): Resulted in a high FVD-slope, indicating that the top predictor, in a detailed latent space, caused the model to drift out-of-distribution over long horizons.
 - Top predictor operating in abstract space at high temporal resolution (10Hz, Exp. 5): Led to "stopping behavior" in rollouts and high FVD-slope, attributed to the abstract latent space being insufficiently detailed to capture small motion at high frame rates, resulting in a loss of motion signal.
 - Bottom predictors consistently showed weaker representation learning due to their detailed predictive task with strong conditioning.

#### 4.4. Training Strategy: Diffusion Forcing + Teacher Forcing

- **Impact on Representation Quality:** The proposed two-stage training strategy significantly improves the internal representation quality of the abstract predictor. Diffusion forcing pre-training alone improves semantic segmentation mIoU and depth estimation RMSE over teacher forcing alone. Subsequent teacher forcing fine-tuning further enhances these gains, resulting in the best overall probing performance for the abstract predictor.
- The final abstract predictor in Orbis 2 outperforms the DINOv2-B-based abstract tokenizer alone in both semantic segmentation (58.29 mIoU vs. 57.19 mIoU) and depth estimation (4.157 RMSE vs. 4.574 RMSE).
- Diffusion forcing also consistently improves intermediate representations across all blocks of predictors trained on pixel-reconstruction-based tokenizer latents (Figure 5).

#### 4.5. More Analysis

- **Model Efficiency:** Orbis 2, with 1.07B parameters, was trained in under 6k H100-GPU-hours, a substantially lower compute cost than comparable driving world models (e.g., GAIA-1, Epona, Orbis v1). It also achieves the fastest inference speed (3.64 FPS) and lowest VRAM requirement (19 GB) among baselines, being at least 5x faster.
- **Abstract Latent Design (Compressed vs. Non-compressed DINO):** Direct prediction in the full 768-dimensional DINO space resulted in rapid degradation of frame quality over the prediction horizon. The compressed 16-dimensional DINO latent space, retaining DINO's semantic structure while reducing dimensionality, was found to be essential for stable long-horizon prediction with the flow matching objective.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of Orbis 2 represents an advancement in the field of autonomous driving world models, primarily through its hierarchical architecture and novel training methodology.

1. **Improved Long-Horizon Prediction and Stability:** The hierarchical decomposition of prediction across abstract and detailed latent spaces, combined with distinct temporal resolutions, addresses a significant limitation of existing world models: the degradation of predictions over long time horizons. Orbis 2 demonstrates substantially greater stability in long-horizon rollouts, which is critical for planning and decision-making in autonomous vehicles that require foresight beyond immediate actions.

2. **Enhanced Internal Representation Quality:** The proposed two-stage training paradigm, leveraging diffusion forcing for pre-training and teacher forcing for fine-tuning, is shown to yield significantly richer and more robust internal representations. This is a generalizable finding that extends beyond the specific architecture of Orbis 2, suggesting a method for improving the semantic and geometric understanding of predictive models. High-quality representations are foundational for a world model's ability to generalize to novel and unseen scenarios.

3. **Advanced Action Controllability:** The model's demonstrated ability to accurately follow diverse steering commands, including counterfactual scenarios, indicates progress towards world models that are genuinely responsive to agent actions rather than merely reproducing observed patterns. This is essential for policy learning and closed-loop simulation in autonomous driving.

4. **Computational Efficiency:** Orbis 2's reduced training compute, faster inference speed, and lower VRAM requirements compared to other state-of-the-art models enhance its practical applicability. Lower computational barriers can accelerate research and development in world models, making advanced capabilities more accessible.

5. **Foundation for Future Research:** The work opens several avenues for further investigation:
 - **Understanding Diffusion Forcing:** A deeper analysis of why diffusion forcing improves representations, particularly regarding implicit data augmentation and learning from varied noise patterns, is suggested.
 - **Interactions between Abstraction Levels:** Exploring more sophisticated connections and interactions between the abstract and fine-grained latent spaces could further enhance the model's capabilities.
 - **Multi-Camera Data Integration:** The authors note plans to extend the approach to multi-camera driving data, which is crucial for a comprehensive understanding of the driving environment.

In summary, Orbis 2 contributes a more robust, stable, and efficient world model for autonomous driving, advancing the state-of-the-art in long-horizon prediction, representation learning, and action controllability. These advancements hold potential for improving the reliability and performance of autonomous driving systems by providing more accurate and generalizable internal models of the environment.
