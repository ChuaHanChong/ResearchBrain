# Sparse Subspace-to-Expert Sharing for Task-Agnostic Continual Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> A new framework, SETA, enables Large Language Models to learn new tasks sequentially while retaining prior knowledge by dynamically allocating sparse, task-specific, and shared expert modules. This approach demonstrated improved overall performance and significantly reduced catastrophic forgetting across diverse benchmarks, requiring minimal parameter expansion.

## Problem

- Large Language Models face the "plasticity-stability dilemma" in continual learning, struggling to acquire new knowledge without catastrophically forgetting previously learned information.
- Existing continual learning methods often treat model parameters uniformly, failing to effectively distinguish between generalizable features and task-specific knowledge.
- Parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) techniques, especially sparse ones, are susceptible to knowledge overwriting during sequential task learning.

## Method

- **SETA** employs ==gradient-driven sparse subspace selection==, primarily targeting the ==Value (V) projection== in attention mechanisms, to identify and fine-tune only the most task-relevant sub-matrices.
- It dynamically partitions the sparse subspace for new tasks into ==unique experts== (frozen, task-specific memory) and ==shared experts== (plastic, generalizable knowledge) using a ==Split-on-Share (SoS)== evolution mechanism.
- Forgetting is mitigated through ==orthogonal subspace isolation== of unique experts and ==adaptive elastic anchoring==, combined with routing-aware gating regularization and adaptive gating expansion for task-agnostic inference.

## Results

- **SETA** achieved competitive or superior overall performance (e.g., Qwen3-4B OP **43.30%**) and the lowest average forgetting (Qwen3-4B F_T **15.42%**) compared to baselines.
- The framework demonstrated sub-linear capacity expansion, with later tasks discovering fewer novel blocks, resulting in significant parameter savings (e.g., Qwen3-4B's cumulative unique block count was **48%** of a non-reusing baseline).
- Unique experts exhibited substantially higher L2 norms (approximately **five times** greater) compared to shared experts, indicating strong task-specific updates for unique knowledge and a well-regularized cross-task knowledge store for shared knowledge.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Leveraging block-wise sparsity within LLMs allows for structural decoupling of novel feature acquisition from historical representation retention, preventing conflicting updates.
> - A dynamic mechanism can automatically differentiate and manage reusable shared knowledge and task-specific unique knowledge within a unified framework, reducing the need for manual hyperparameter tuning.
> - Task-agnostic inference can be achieved by preserving routing decision boundaries during expert splitting and using a unified gating network that dynamically selects experts based on input tokens alone.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses the critical challenge of continual learning in Large Language Models (LLMs), specifically the "plasticity-stability dilemma." This dilemma refers to the inherent trade-off between an LLM's ability to rapidly acquire new knowledge (plasticity) and its capacity to retain previously learned information without experiencing catastrophic forgetting. Catastrophic forgetting occurs when learning new tasks overwrites historical knowledge, a problem exacerbated in parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) regimes dueants to increased sparsity that makes models susceptible to overwriting.

Existing continual learning strategies generally fall into three categories:
1. **Replay-based methods**: These involve re-training on a subset of historical data.
2. **Regularization-based techniques**: These penalize updates to parameters deemed critical for prior tasks.
3. **Parameter Isolation approaches**: These physically separate task-specific modules.

A common limitation among these approaches is their tendency to treat parameters uniformly within their respective subspaces. This uniform treatment often fails to differentiate between generalizable features and task-specific knowledge, leading to suboptimal compromises between stability and plasticity.

The paper posits that Continual Sparse Fine-Tuning offers a mechanism for uncovering the internal knowledge structure of LLMs. Unlike dense PEFT methods like low-rank adapters (e.g., LoRA) that project updates through opaque matrices, sparse tuning identifies specific high-utility attention sub-blocks, particularly in the Value (V) projection, based on gradient signals. This characteristic forms the foundation for the proposed framework, aiming to leverage this sparse, block-wise knowledge to create a more effective continual learning paradigm that explicitly distinguishes between shared and unique task knowledge. The work positions itself as a structural solution to the shared subspace dilemma, where parameters important for multiple tasks conflict during sequential learning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind this research is to resolve the plasticity-stability dilemma in LLMs during continual learning by moving beyond uniform parameter treatment. The authors identify three fundamental research questions (RQs) that drive the design of their proposed framework, Mixture of Sparse Experts for Task Agnostic Continual Learning (SETA):

- **RQ1: How can sparse parameter patterns be leveraged to decouple the acquisition of novel features from the retention of historical representations?**
 - **Motivation**: Existing methods struggle to isolate task-specific knowledge from generalizable knowledge, causing conflicts when new tasks are learned. The paper argues that by exploiting the inherent block-wise sparsity of gradients, the model's knowledge can be decomposed into distinct expert modules. This structural separation aims to ensure that conflicting updates for different tasks operate within isolated subspaces, rather than competing for the same parameter blocks, thereby preventing overwriting. This forms the basis of SETA's Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) paradigm in a sparse continual learning context.

- **RQ2: Is it possible to automatically differentiate between reusable shared knowledge and task-specific unique knowledge within a single unified framework?**
 - **Motivation**: A key challenge is automatically identifying which model parameters are essential for multiple tasks (shared knowledge) and which are unique to a specific task. Current methods often require manual distinctions or rigid regularization. SETA addresses this with its Split-on-Share (SoS) mechanism. This mechanism dynamically assigns parameters based on their recurrence across tasks: overlapping parameters become "shared experts" that are protected, while non-overlapping parameters become "unique experts" that are frozen as immutable memory. This dynamic differentiation aims to stabilize shared knowledge through adaptive elastic anchoring and routing-aware regularization, preventing semantic drift without introducing additional hyperparameters.

- **RQ3: How can the relevant expert modules be dynamically retrieved during inference without relying on external task identifiers?**
 - **Motivation**: In a continual learning scenario, a model must not only learn tasks sequentially but also be able to infer on any past or present task without explicit external signals indicating the task ID. Many continual learning approaches require such identifiers, limiting their practical deployment. SETA resolves this by implementing adaptive gating expansion with logit invariance, which preserves existing routing decision boundaries during expert splitting. Additionally, a routing-aware regularization shapes the gate's decisions during training to minimize interference with shared experts. This enables a unified gating network to evolve alongside the experts, dynamically selecting the appropriate expert combination based solely on the input tokens, thus facilitating task-agnostic inference.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

SETA is a modular framework designed for sparse subspace adaptation in continual learning. It integrates gradient-driven sparse parameter selection with a dynamically evolving Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) architecture and adaptive regularization strategies.

#### 3.1. Sparse Subspace Selection

SETA fine-tunes only the most task-relevant sparse sub-matrices. This is achieved through a block-sparse optimization boundary defined by gradient-driven selection:
- **Block Partitioning**: Pre-trained weight matrices are partitioned into fixed-size sub-blocks, where the block size is an architecture-aware greatest common divisor of the model's matrix dimensions.
- **Dynamic Warm-up Stopping**: A warm-up phase, executed before each task, monitors the per-step gradient contribution ratio. Training halts when this ratio falls below its coefficient of variation, adapting the warm-up length to task-specific gradient dynamics.
- **Layer-Normalized Scoring**: Raw block importance scores are computed as the mean absolute gradient within each block. These scores are then standardized per layer to prevent dominant layers from monopolizing block selection.
- **Cross-Task Divergence Scoring**: For subsequent tasks, the normalized score is augmented with a divergence signal that rewards blocks exhibiting novel gradient responses relative to prior tasks. A convex combination of the current and historical average scores is used, with a decaying alpha (α = 1/(1 + log t)) that automatically reduces the influence of current task novelty for later tasks without additional hyperparameters.
- **Adaptive Block Budget**: The block budget, a fraction (ρ) of total available blocks, is allocated per-layer inversely proportional to historical coverage. Selection can expand beyond a base budget until the discovery rate of previously unselected blocks stabilizes.
- **V-Projection Restriction**: The block selection is primarily restricted to the Value (V) projection in attention mechanisms, as empirical analysis (Figure 7) showed approximately 95% of high-magnitude gradients concentrate there, attributed to Softmax Saturation.

#### 3.2. Design of Unique and Shared Experts

The sparse subspace P_t for a new task T_t is partitioned into two functional sets based on its overlap with the cumulative historical subspace P_1:t-1:
- **Unique Experts (E_u)**: Formed from the disjoint sparse subspace (P_t \ P_1:t-1). These parameters are exclusive to the current task T_t and are designed to be frozen after their task is learned, acting as immutable memory.
- **Shared Experts (E_s)**: Occupy the intersection subspace (P_t ∩ P_1:t-1). These parameters capture structural feature overlap between tasks, serving as a basis for positive backward transfer and are plastic during training.
The effective sparse subspace W is represented as the summation of the frozen base model W_0 and the functional deltas from unique and shared experts.

#### 3.3. Split-on-Share (SoS) Expert Evolution

The SoS algorithm governs the dynamic structural evolution of experts when new tasks introduce overlapping sub-blocks. It uses layer-wise topological filters:
- **Raw Intersection and Remainder**: For each layer *l*, the raw intersection I_l (P^(l)_t-1 ∩ P^(l)_t) and raw unique remainder R_l (P^(l)_t-1 \ P^(l)_t) are computed.
- **Thresholds**: Two fixed topological thresholds are used:
 - **Expert Creation Threshold (τ_ect)**: Filters small overlaps; if |I_l| is below τ_ect, it's considered stochastic noise and reintegrated into the unique subspace.
 - **Tiny Remainder Threshold (τ_trt)**: Merges tiny fragments; if 0 < |R_l| < τ_trt, these negligible unique residuals are absorbed into the shared manifold to prevent architectural fragmentation and preserve efficiency.
- **Expert Formation**: The validated intersection I_l instantiates the plastic shared expert E_s using weight inheritance. The stabilized residuals R_l form the frozen unique expert E^(t-1)_u. Indices exclusive to the new domain initialize the new task expert E^(t)_u. This mechanism allows sub-linear capacity expansion by reusing functionalities.

#### 3.4. Catastrophic Forgetting Prevention

Forgetting is mitigated through:
- **Orthogonal Subspace Isolation**: All prior unique experts E^(k)_u (k < t) are strictly frozen during training of task *t* by enforcing a zero-gradient constraint. Optimization is limited to the plastic shared expert E_s and the current unique expert E^(t)_u.
- **Adaptive Elastic Anchoring**: Shared experts, while plastic, are protected from semantic drift by a per-expert dynamic regularization coefficient, λ_i(t). This coefficient adapts at every training step based on:
 - **Accumulated Task History (ω_i)**: Scales protection proportionally to the number of prior task IDs encoded in expert *i*.
 - **Live Weight Displacement (δ_i(t))**: Monitors drift relative to a pre-task snapshot, strengthening the penalty as the expert drifts.
The regularization loss L_reg is a sum of squared differences between current and anchored weights, scaled by λ_i(t).

#### 3.5. Adaptive Gating Expansion

To maintain stability during expert splitting, SETA preserves pre-activation logits through:
- **Logit Invariance via Weight Inheritance**: When a historical expert is split, the gates for the new plastic shared expert and frozen task-specific expert are initialized to approximately preserve existing routing logits. A small perturbation is added to the unique expert's gate to break symmetry, preventing cold-start errors.

#### 3.6. Routing-Aware Gating Regularization

Complementary to L_reg, a routing-aware gating loss L_gate shapes the gating network's decisions *before* drift accumulates. It penalizes high routing probabilities towards at-risk shared experts, using the same adaptive λ_i coefficients as L_reg without introducing new hyperparameters. This provides a two-layer defense against forgetting.

#### 3.7. Objective Function

For task T_t (t > 1), the composite objective function minimizes the cross-entropy loss for the current task, augmented by the adaptive elastic anchoring loss (L_reg) and the routing-aware gating regularization (L_gate):
L = - Σ log P(y|x; Θ_t) + L_reg + L_gate, where Θ_t represents the active parameter configuration.

#### 3.8. Task-Agnostic Inference

SETA achieves task-agnostic inference via a unified content-based routing network. A single gating network (W_g) maps input *x* to a global logit vector over all experts. The output is a weighted superposition of the frozen backbone and retrieved sparse functional deltas, allowing the model to dynamically activate multiple experts based on input tokens without requiring explicit task IDs.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments were conducted using LLaMA-2 7B and Qwen3-4B models on a sequence of six diverse, domain-specific benchmarks: C-STANCE, FOMC, MeetingBank, ScienceQA, NumGLUE-cm, and 20Minuten. Performance was evaluated using Overall Performance (OP), Average Forgetting (F_T), and Backward Transfer (BWT).

#### 4.1. Comparative Analysis of Continual Learning Methods

- **Overall Performance and Forgetting (Table 1)**: SETA demonstrated competitive or superior performance.
 - On LLaMA-2 7B, SETA achieved an OP of 28.72%, surpassing I-LoRA (27.32%). Its F_T was 19.10%, competitive with I-LoRA (18.30%).
 - On Qwen3-4B, SETA achieved the highest OP of 43.30% and the lowest F_T of 15.42%, outperforming I-LoRA (OP 38.15%, F_T 15.54%) and other baselines (Seq-LoRA, EWC, GEM).
- **Domain Adaptation and Retention (Tables 1, 2, 3)**: SETA exhibited stronger initial task acquisition and significantly greater resistance to catastrophic forgetting compared to baselines.
 - For example, on LLaMA-2 7B, while I-LoRA's accuracy on FOMC degraded to 8.1% by the final step, SETA retained 26.41%. Similarly, SETA preserved 20.81% on MeetingBank versus 14.4% for I-LoRA.
 - On Qwen3-4B (Table 2), SETA showed a stable retention profile, with Task 1 accuracy (C-STANCE) rising to 63.64% by the final step, while EWC and I-LoRA experienced substantial collapses. SETA also maintained high plasticity for new tasks (e.g., 75.90% on Task 4). This balance of stability and plasticity generalized to LLaMA-2 7B (Table 3).

#### 4.2. Layer-wise Selection and Unique Subspace Discovery Rate

- **Non-Uniform Selection (Figure 2, 7)**: Both LLaMA-2 7B and Qwen3-4B exhibited non-uniform, task-sensitive block selection patterns across transformer layers without architecture-specific tuning. Analysis showed approximately 95% of high-magnitude gradients concentrated in the Value (V) projection, supporting the decision to restrict block selection to V-projection.
- **Sub-linear Capacity Expansion (Figure 4, 8, 9, 11)**: As the task sequence grew, each new task progressively discovered fewer novel blocks, leading to a monotonic degradation in later-task discovery rates. This was consistent across both LLaMA-2 7B and Qwen3-4B, confirming sub-linear capacity expansion as a model-agnostic property of SETA. For Qwen3-4B, by Task 6, the cumulative unique block count was 48% of what a non-reusing baseline would require, demonstrating significant parameter savings.
- **Final Task Block Composition (Figure 10)**: Even for the final task, a mixture of novel blocks and blocks shared with prior tasks was identified in most layers, confirming that SETA prevents expert saturation and allocates dedicated unique experts while leveraging shared knowledge.

#### 4.3. Expert Creation Threshold Analysis (Figure 3, 14)

- Increasing the block budget shifts the intersection distribution, creating larger overlaps. The Expert Creation Threshold (ECT) must be calibrated relative to the block budget to avoid creating spurious shared experts from incidental overlap. The chosen ECT values (e.g., 4 for 13% budget) were located at the "elbow" of the approval curve, balancing noise rejection with genuine knowledge sharing.

#### 4.4. Parameter Efficiency (Table 4)

- SETA maintained a minimal memory footprint. Trainable parameters increased marginally from 6.34M (Task 1) to 8.10M (Task 6) on LLaMA-2 7B, representing 0.98% to 1.25% of the total model capacity. This was comparable to or lower than I-LoRA (approx. 8.39M for rank 8), indicating efficient adaptation by modifying approximately 1% of parameters. However, the authors note that this extreme sparsity introduces network saturation limits, potentially creating a plasticity bottleneck for future tasks.

#### 4.5. Ablation Study: Routing-Aware Gating Regularization (Table 5)

- Disabling the L_gate loss resulted in lower average accuracy (38.48% vs. 43.30%) and higher forgetting (BWT -15.70 vs. -12.67).
- L_gate significantly improved the retention of early tasks (e.g., C-STANCE accuracy at final step rose from 53.64% to 63.64%) and boosted plasticity on intermediate tasks (e.g., MeetingBank diagonal accuracy improved from 38.04% to 46.69%). This confirmed L_gate's role in steering the gating network away from at-risk shared experts, complementing weight regularization.

#### 4.6. Expert Weight Analysis (Figure 12, 13)

- Unique experts exhibited substantially higher L2 norms (mean approx. 30.8) compared to shared experts (mean approx. 6.4), a five-fold difference. This indicated that unique experts underwent strong task-specific updates, while shared experts remained closer to the pre-trained base, functioning as a regularized cross-task knowledge store, consistent with the SoS design and forgetting prevention mechanisms.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The SETA framework contributes to continual learning in LLMs by offering a structural and adaptive solution to the plasticity-stability dilemma. Its primary significance lies in:

- **Mitigating Catastrophic Forgetting**: By adaptively decomposing knowledge into unique (frozen, task-specific) and shared (plastic, generalizable) sparse expert modules, SETA effectively isolates conflicting updates and preserves historical knowledge. This is a direct advancement over methods that treat parameters uniformly.
- **Enabling Task-Agnostic Inference**: The unified content-based routing network, combined with adaptive gating expansion and routing-aware regularization, allows the model to dynamically retrieve relevant experts without external task identifiers. This capability is crucial for deploying LLMs in real-world, dynamic environments where task labels are not always available at inference time.
- **Parameter Efficiency and Sub-linear Capacity Growth**: SETA demonstrates efficient adaptation by modifying a minimal percentage of parameters (around 1%) and achieves sub-linear growth in its parameter footprint over a sequence of tasks. This is a significant advantage for scaling continual learning to large models and long task sequences, reducing computational and memory overhead compared to approaches that require linear capacity expansion.
- **Improved Balance of Stability and Plasticity**: The experimental results show that SETA consistently achieves competitive or superior performance in terms of overall accuracy, backward transfer, and reduced forgetting across diverse benchmarks and LLM architectures. This indicates a more effective balance between learning new tasks and retaining old knowledge.

**Potential Impact**: SETA's approach could facilitate the deployment of LLMs in dynamic, real-world applications requiring continuous adaptation, such as evolving conversational AI, personalized assistants, or specialized domain models. By allowing LLMs to learn sequentially without significant forgetting and operate without explicit task labels, it moves towards more robust and versatile lifelong learning systems.

**Limitations Acknowledged by Authors**:
- The experiments covered models up to 14B parameters, leaving the scalability of expert specialization and routing to significantly larger models unexplored.
- Validation was restricted to standard attention-based transformers; behavior on alternative architectures remains an open question.
- Evaluation followed a fixed task ordering across six benchmarks. The effects of longer sequences, greater task diversity, or different task orderings are yet to be investigated.
- The shared expert pool expands without a pruning or consolidation mechanism, which might limit scalability in very long-horizon continual learning settings. These aspects are identified as avenues for future work.
