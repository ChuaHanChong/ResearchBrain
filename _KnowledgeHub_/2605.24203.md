# Afford-VLA: Action-Aligned Visual Planning via Internalized Affordance

> [!summary] Summary
> Afford-VLA, a framework for robot manipulation from Fudan University and collaborators, enhances spatial reasoning in Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models by internalizing task-conditioned affordance as an action-aligned visual planning interface, achieving a 97.4% average success rate on LIBERO and improved zero-shot robustness.

## Problem

- Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models often exhibit limited spatial reasoning, struggling to precisely identify interaction regions in complex visual scenes for robot manipulation.
- Existing visual planning methods in VLA systems either rely on abstract representations or external perception modules, leading to weak coupling between planning and downstream action prediction.
- A lack of integrated visual planning approaches that jointly satisfy locality, visual grounding, internal generation, and direct action alignment within VLA frameworks.

## Method

- The **Afford-VLA** framework internalizes ==task-conditioned affordance== as an explicit, action-aligned ==visual planning interface== directly within the VLA model, rather than as an auxiliary output.
- It generates ==affordance masks== using dedicated ==learnable query tokens== and a lightweight ==query-patch grounding decoder==, then converts these masks into a compact ==affordance embedding==.
- This affordance embedding directly conditions the ==action prediction head==, optimized through a two-stage training strategy that includes a ==straight-through (ST) gradient== pathway for mask pooling.

## Results

- Afford-VLA achieved a new state-of-the-art average success rate of **97.4%** on the LIBERO benchmark, outperforming previous methods across all task suites.
- The framework demonstrated superior zero-shot robustness on the LIBERO-Plus benchmark with a **78.1%** average success rate, particularly under visual perturbations.
- In real-world tabletop manipulation, the system achieved **80%** success on "Cup-to-Plate" and **70%** on "Fork-in-Bowl," surpassing strong VLA baselines.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Internalizing affordance as a direct action-aligned visual planning interface significantly enhances the spatial reasoning and precision of VLA models for robot manipulation.
> - A tightly coupled perception-action pathway, enabled by internal affordance generation and an action-aligned training strategy, is crucial for effective visual planning and robust robot control.
> - A two-stage training approach, starting with ground-truth affordance warm-up and transitioning to model-predicted affordance with ST pooling, effectively aligns the visual planning with downstream action generation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Vision-language-action (VLA) models have demonstrated capabilities in generalist robot manipulation by translating visual observations and language instructions into robotic actions. However, a persistent challenge in these systems is their limitations in spatial reasoning, specifically in determining precise interaction regions within complex visual scenes. This challenge is crucial for successful manipulation tasks, which often require accurate spatial grounding.

Existing approaches to visual planning in VLA systems can be categorized into several types, each with its own limitations:
- **Geometry-based methods** (e.g.,) utilize explicit or implicit 3D cues, such as point clouds or depth information, to provide global or scene-level spatial context. While useful for general spatial awareness, these methods often lack the precision required for task-conditioned local interaction.
- **Symbolic-based methods** (e.g.,) convert visual observations into abstract symbolic representations, such as textual descriptions or structured tokens. This introduces an indirect mapping to action control, potentially detaching the planning from direct visual evidence.
- **Visually grounded methods** (e.g.,) attempt to capture task-relevant interaction regions more explicitly through visual cues like masks or heatmaps. However, these methods frequently rely on external perception modules or treat key-region localization as an independently supervised objective. This often results in a weak coupling between the visual planning component and downstream action prediction, limiting the direct influence of the planned regions on action generation. Some prior works, like CoA-VLA and RT-Affordance, have explored affordance-style reasoning for VLA guidance, but often treat affordance as an auxiliary signal or an externally produced representation.

Afford-VLA aims to address these identified gaps by proposing a unified framework that internalizes affordance as an explicit, action-aligned visual planning interface. This positions the work as an advancement within the visually grounded planning paradigm, specifically by strengthening the perception-action coupling and integrating affordance learning directly within the VLA model's decision-making process.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the spatial reasoning capabilities of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, particularly concerning the identification of precise interaction regions for robot manipulation tasks. The authors argue that current VLA systems often implicitly infer task-relevant interaction regions from global representations, which can lead to insufficient precision for complex manipulation.

To achieve more effective visual planning, the authors propose that such a system should satisfy four key properties:
1. **Locality:** The planning mechanism should enable VLA models to concentrate on specific, task-relevant interaction regions within a visual scene, rather than relying solely on global scene understanding.
2. **Visual Grounding:** The planning information should be directly tied to visual evidence, avoiding reliance on abstract intermediate representations that might lose spatial fidelity.
3. **Internal Generation:** The visual planning signals should be learned and generated within the VLA model itself, rather than being produced by separate, external perception modules. This is motivated by the desire for a more integrated and tightly coupled perception-action system.
4. **Action Alignment:** The generated planning information should be directly consumable by the action model, allowing it to explicitly influence downstream decision-making and action generation. This ensures that the "where to interact" information is directly actionable.

The motivation behind these objectives stems from the observation that existing visual planning methods for VLA systems often fall short in jointly satisfying these properties. For instance, geometry-based methods may offer global context but lack precise local cues, while symbolic methods introduce an indirect guidance pathway. Visually grounded methods show promise but often externalize the planning process or weakly couple it with action learning.

By internalizing task-conditioned affordance—which naturally captures local and visually grounded interaction regions—the authors aim to create an explicit visual planning interface that inherently possesses these four properties. This approach is intended to bridge the gap between high-level perception (understanding the task and scene) and low-level control (executing precise actions), thereby improving the robustness and effectiveness of VLA systems in robot manipulation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Afford-VLA proposes a unified framework that integrates task-conditioned affordance as an internal visual planning interface within VLA models. The methodology involves internal generation of affordance masks, their conversion into compact embeddings, and direct conditioning of action prediction.

#### 3.1. Formulating Affordance as Action-Aligned Visual Planning

Instead of treating affordance as an auxiliary output, Afford-VLA positions it as an internal intermediate representation, denoted as M\_t, which localizes task-relevant interaction regions. The policy is conditioned on both standard inputs (image I\_t, language x, proprioceptive state s\_t) and the predicted affordance M\_t. This means affordance directly guides the action model on *where* to interact.

#### 3.2. Internal Affordance Mask Generation

Afford-VLA generates affordance masks without external perception modules.
- **Input Processing:** Given an RGB observation I\_t and a language instruction x, a Vision-Language Model (VLM) converts them into image tokens Q\_img and language tokens Q\_text.
- **Affordance Query Tokens:** A small set of learnable `<AFF>` query tokens (Q\_aff) are introduced into the multimodal sequence. These tokens participate in the VLM's self-attention mechanism, allowing them to aggregate visual and language information.
- **Contextualized States:** The VLM backbone processes the augmented sequence `[Q_img, Q_text, Q_aff]`, yielding contextualized hidden states for the original tokens (H\_t) and the `<AFF>` tokens (A\_t). The states A\_t are thus conditioned on both the visual observation and the instruction.
- **Affordance Head (D_aff):** An affordance head takes the contextualized `<AFF>` states (A\_t) and dense visual patch features (P\_t) from the image encoder. It decodes these inputs to predict patch-level affordance logits (G\_t), which represent the affordance map. The affordance head is designed as a lightweight query-patch grounding decoder, intuitively matching what interaction evidence is needed (from A\_t) with where it appears (from P\_t).

#### 3.3. Affordance-Conditioned Action Prediction

The predicted affordance logits G\_t are converted into an action-consumable representation.
- **Mask Pooling:** The logits G\_t are flattened, and the top-k patches with the highest affordance scores are selected. This creates a binary selection mask m\_t.
- **Affordance Embedding:** The visual features P\_t corresponding to the selected patches (indicated by m\_t) are aggregated (averaged) and then projected into the VLM hidden dimension to form a compact affordance embedding r\_t. This embedding summarizes the local visual evidence from the interaction-relevant region.
- **Action Conditioning:** The affordance embedding r\_t is appended to the original VLM hidden states H\_t, forming an action-conditioning sequence Z\_t = `[H_t; r_t]`. This sequence, along with the proprioceptive state s\_t, is then fed into the action head.
- **Action Head:** The action head employs a flow-matching design (similar to Isaac-GR00T) to predict continuous action chunks. Its architecture remains largely unchanged, with the affordance embedding simply augmenting its input.

#### 3.4. Action-Aligned Training

To ensure the affordance pathway is optimized for downstream action generation, a specialized training strategy is used.
- **Action-to-Affordance Gradient Pathway:** The hard top-k selection in mask pooling is non-differentiable. To allow gradients from the action loss to propagate back to the affordance head, a straight-through (ST) variant of mask pooling (Φ\_ST) is used during joint training. This replaces the non-differentiable selection with a soft surrogate in the backward pass.
- **Training Objectives:**
 - **Affordance Grounding Loss (L\_aff):** Uses `BCEWithLogits` between the predicted affordance logits G\_t and ground-truth affordance masks Y\_t (obtained from a Joint Affordance & Action Dataset).
 - **Action Prediction Loss (L\_act):** Uses a flow-matching objective (ℓ\_FM) between the predicted action chunk and the ground-truth action chunk.
 - The total objective during joint training is L\_joint = L\_act + L\_aff.
- **Two-stage Training:**
 - **Stage 1 (Warm-up):** The affordance head is initially trained for 4K steps using only L\_aff, with the VLM and action head frozen. During this stage, ground-truth affordance masks are used for region pooling to provide stable initial spatial grounding.
 - **Stage 2 (Joint Training):** The model is then trained with the joint objective L\_joint. Crucially, mask pooling is performed using the *model's own predicted affordance logits* G\_t via Φ\_ST, reducing train-inference mismatch.
- **Inference:** At inference time, hard top-k mask pooling is used, and ground-truth affordance masks are not required.

#### 3.5. Extensions and Implementation Details

- **Multi-view Extension:** The approach extends naturally to multi-view observations. Affordance masks and embeddings are generated independently for each view, and all view-level affordance embeddings are concatenated with the VLM hidden states before being fed to the action head.
- **Implementation:** The system uses Qwen3-VL-4B-Instruct as the VLM backbone and a GR00T-style flow-matching action head. The affordance branch employs 4 learnable `<AFF>` queries per view and a lightweight two-way decoder. Hard Top-K pooling selects 16 patches. Training is done with AdamW optimizer, cosine learning rate schedule, and mixed precision with DeepSpeed ZeRO-2 on NVIDIA H200 GPUs.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents extensive experimental validation across simulation benchmarks and real-world robot manipulation tasks, demonstrating the effectiveness of the Afford-VLA framework.

#### 4.1. Simulation Benchmarks

- **LIBERO Benchmark:** Afford-VLA achieved an average success rate of 97.4%, setting a new state-of-the-art. It consistently outperformed previous methods across all four task suites (Spatial, Object, Goal, Long). Specifically, it reached 97.8% on Spatial, 99.6% on Object, and 97.6% on Goal tasks, indicating strong performance in tasks requiring precise spatial grounding and interaction-region localization.
- **SimplerEnv Benchmark:** Afford-VLA achieved the best overall average success rate of 58.1% on SimplerEnv, a benchmark for realistic tabletop manipulation. While it exhibited weaker performance on some tasks like block stacking (potentially due to greater reliance on trajectory dynamics), its overall average remained superior.

#### 4.2. Zero-Shot Robustness on LIBERO-Plus

- On the LIBERO-Plus benchmark, which introduces various perturbations, Afford-VLA achieved an average success rate of 78.1%, outperforming all baselines in a zero-shot setting (trained on LIBERO, evaluated directly on LIBERO-Plus).
- The improvements were particularly significant under perturbations that challenge visual planning, such as Layout (78.9%), Background (97.0%), Noise (80.9%), and Light (96.8%). This suggests that affordance-guided pooling enables the policy to robustly re-localize task-relevant objects and interaction regions despite visual appearance shifts or scene reconfigurations.

#### 4.3. Main Ablation Studies

Ablation studies were conducted on LIBERO to evaluate the contributions of internalized and action-aligned affordance:
- A baseline VLA model (without affordance) achieved 95.4%.
- Integrating externally generated affordance with direct action conditioning improved performance to 96.5%, demonstrating the benefit of explicit interaction region exposure.
- Internal affordance generation without direct action conditioning showed a limited gain (95.9%), indicating auxiliary supervision alone is insufficient.
- The full Afford-VLA design (internal, action-aligned affordance) achieved the best performance at 97.4%, validating the combined importance of internal generation and action-aligned integration.
- Further ablations on mask pooling designs confirmed that Sparse Top-K Straight-Through (ST) Patch Pooling was optimal (97.4%), outperforming Hard Region Pooling (91.3%) and Dense Soft Mask Pooling (96.0%). This highlights the necessity of both differentiability and focused spatial readout for action-aligned learning.
- Ablations on training strategies (one-stage vs. two-stage warmup) confirmed that the two-stage approach, which provides stable initial affordance conditioning and end-to-end alignment, yielded the best results (97.4%).

#### 4.4. Real-World Experiments

Afford-VLA was evaluated on two real-world tabletop manipulation tasks: "Cup-to-Plate" and "Fork-in-Bowl."
- It achieved 80% success on "Cup-to-Plate" and 70% on "Fork-in-Bowl," outperforming strong VLA baselines like OpenVLA-OFT and π0.
- Qualitative examples and additional experiments on tasks such as "Open the Microwave," "Pick Up the Pot," and "Remove the Lid" further demonstrated the policy's ability to localize small interaction regions and maintain accurate spatial alignment.

#### 4.5. Affordance Visualization

Visualizations of the learned affordance masks showed that Afford-VLA consistently predicted masks concentrating on action-relevant interaction regions (e.g., graspable object parts, target containers) across diverse tasks, objects, and camera views in both simulated and real-world scenes. This supports the claim that the model learns strong task-conditioned visual grounding.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Afford-VLA framework contributes to the field of robot learning by addressing a critical limitation in current Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models: insufficient spatial reasoning, particularly in identifying precise interaction regions. The work introduces a novel paradigm for visual planning that emphasizes locality, visual grounding, internal generation, and direct action alignment of affordance.

#### 5.1. Significance

- **Improved Spatial Reasoning:** By explicitly modeling "where to interact" through internalized, task-conditioned affordance, Afford-VLA enhances the ability of VLA systems to perform precise manipulation. This is demonstrated by consistent state-of-the-art performance across various simulation benchmarks, especially on tasks requiring strong spatial grounding.
- **Enhanced Robustness:** The method exhibits notable zero-shot robustness to visual perturbations (e.g., changes in layout, background, lighting, noise) on the LIBERO-Plus benchmark. This suggests that focusing on task-relevant interaction regions makes the policy less sensitive to irrelevant visual distractions, a crucial aspect for deployment in varied real-world environments.
- **Tightly Coupled Perception-Action Pathway:** The design that allows affordance to be both generated and utilized within the VLA model, coupled with an action-aligned training strategy (straight-through gradient estimation), creates a more integrated and effective perception-action loop. This ensures that visual planning directly influences and is optimized for downstream control.
- **Foundation for Embodied AI:** The research provides a promising direction for improving spatial reasoning and control in more general embodied AI systems, beyond the specific manipulation tasks evaluated.

#### 5.2. Potential Impact

- **Advancement in Robot Manipulation:** The increased precision and robustness offered by Afford-VLA could directly benefit applications in industrial automation, logistics, and assistive robotics, where robots need to interact accurately with diverse objects and environments.
- **More Interpretable Policies:** By explicitly visualizing affordance masks, the system offers a degree of interpretability, showing which regions the robot is attending to for a given task. This could aid in debugging and understanding policy behavior.
- **Inspiration for Future VLA Designs:** The insights regarding the four properties of effective visual planning (locality, visual grounding, internal generation, action alignment) could guide the development of future VLA architectures.
