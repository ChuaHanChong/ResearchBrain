# Delving into RL for Image Generation with CoT: A Study on DPO vs. GRPO

> [!summary] Summary
> A systematic study compares Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) and Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) for autoregressive image generation with Chain-of-Thought reasoning. The research demonstrates that DPO excels in-domain, while GRPO offers superior out-of-domain generalization, and highlights the critical influence of reward model generalization and tailored scaling strategies on performance.

## Problem

- Applying reinforcement learning (RL) and Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning to autoregressive image generation presents challenges distinct from text-based LLMs, such as ensuring text-image consistency and aesthetic quality.
- Image generation demands robust generalization across widely varying prompt styles, from detailed descriptions to concise templates, creating a significant domain gap.
- A comprehensive, systematic investigation into the characteristics and scaling behaviors of different RL algorithms like DPO and GRPO within the visual CoT domain was lacking.

## Method

- The study systematically investigates the application of GRPO and DPO for ==autoregressive image generation==, using Janus-Pro as the baseline model.
- It evaluates performance on both in-domain (T2I-CompBench) and out-of-domain (GenEval) benchmarks to assess efficacy and generalization.
- The research explores the impact of various reward models (human preference, VQA-based, metric rewards) and three scaling strategies: sampled images per prompt, in-domain training data volume, and iterative training paradigms.

## Results

- Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) consistently outperforms GRPO on in-domain tasks (T2I-CompBench) by an average of **11.53%**.
- Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) consistently exhibits better generalization capabilities on out-of-domain tasks (GenEval), surpassing DPO by an average of **1.14%**.
- Iterative DPO training substantially enhances in-domain performance but can significantly reduce out-of-domain generalization after two iterations, while increasing in-domain data diversity for DPO consistently improves both in-domain and out-of-domain performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) is more sensitive to the specific characteristics and biases of the chosen reward model, especially concerning generalization performance.
> - The intrinsic generalization capabilities of reward models directly correlate with the generalization performance of both GRPO and DPO.
> - Scaling strategies exhibit distinct trade-offs: GRPO achieves efficient in-domain gains by scaling sampled images per prompt, while iterative training for DPO boosts in-domain performance but can significantly reduce out-of-domain generalization.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper is situated at the intersection of two rapidly advancing fields: large language models (LLMs) with Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning and autoregressive image generation.

**LLMs and CoT:** Recent breakthroughs in LLMs (e.g., GPT-4o, Claude, Gemini) have showcased remarkable reasoning capabilities, often attributed to their ability to perform multi-step, CoT reasoning. This emergent reasoning is frequently enhanced and aligned through post-training using reinforcement learning (RL), with algorithms like Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) and Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) being prominent. DPO offers computational efficiency and strong performance for simpler reasoning, while GRPO excels in complex, intricate reasoning tasks due to its iterative, self-generated data approach, albeit at a higher computational cost.

**Autoregressive Image Generation:** Parallel to LLMs, autoregressive models in image generation (e.g., Janus-Pro, LlamaGen) process visual tokens sequentially, akin to a "visual Chain-of-Thought." These models have demonstrated competitive performance with diffusion models. However, applying RL techniques, particularly DPO and GRPO, to this visual domain presents unique challenges distinct from text-based LLMs:

1. **Text-Image Consistency and Aesthetic Quality:** Image generation demands high fidelity to textual prompts, aesthetic appeal, and realistic composition, often necessitating complex learned reward models rather than simpler, rule-based rewards used in many LLM tasks (e.g., math problem solving where answers are verifiable).
2. **Domain Gap:** Image generation prompts can vary widely from detailed descriptions (in-domain) to concise, templated formats (out-of-domain), requiring models to generalize robustly.
3. **RL Strategy Analysis:** While some initial works have explored RL for image generation, a comprehensive, systematic investigation into the characteristics and scaling behaviors of different RL algorithms (like DPO and GRPO) in this specific context has been lacking.

**Bridging the Gap:** This paper directly addresses this gap. It recognizes the interpretability of autoregressive image generation as a sequential CoT process and undertakes the *first comprehensive investigation* of GRPO and DPO within this domain. By systematically evaluating their performance, generalization, sensitivity to reward models, and response to scaling strategies, the authors aim to transfer the successful RL-for-CoT paradigm from language to vision, while accounting for the inherent distinctions and challenges of the visual domain. This work significantly advances our understanding of how to effectively train and optimize generative models for complex, multi-step visual synthesis.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of this research is to thoroughly investigate the application and comparative performance of two leading reinforcement learning algorithms, GRPO and DPO, in the context of autoregressive image generation. This extends the successful paradigm of using RL to enhance Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning from large language models (LLMs) to the visual domain.

The central motivating question driving this study is: **"How does the performance of GRPO compare to that of DPO in autoregressive image generation, and which aspects may influence their optimal performance, e.g., reward models and scaling strategies?"**

To answer this question, the authors delineate three key objectives:

1. **Comparative Analysis of In-Domain Performance vs. Out-of-Domain Generalization:** The first objective is to rigorously compare the efficacy of DPO and GRPO in handling text-to-image generation tasks. This involves evaluating their performance on data similar to their training distribution (in-domain) and their ability to generalize to unseen or distinctly structured prompts (out-of-domain). This distinction is crucial because image generation often faces significant domain shifts in prompt styles and complexity.
2. **Impact of Different Reward Models:** Unlike LLM tasks which sometimes allow for rule-based or easily verifiable rewards, image generation relies on sophisticated learned reward models to capture subjective qualities like aesthetics and text-image alignment. Therefore, a critical objective is to understand how the choice of different reward models, and particularly their intrinsic generalization capabilities, influences the performance, and especially the generalization potential, of the applied RL algorithms (GRPO and DPO).
3. **Investigation of Effective Scaling Strategies:** A common approach to improve model performance in deep learning is scaling. This study aims to systematically explore the impact of three prevalent scaling strategies—scaling the number of sampled images per prompt, scaling the diversity and volume of in-domain training data, and implementing iterative training paradigms—on both the in-domain performance and out-of-domain generalization of GRPO and DPO in the autoregressive image generation context. The motivation here is to identify efficient and effective ways to enhance the capabilities of these RL-tuned generative models.

The motivation behind these objectives stems from the unique characteristics of visual CoT reasoning, which differs significantly from textual reasoning. Factors such as the need for accurate text-image consistency, high aesthetic quality, and the complexity of designing robust reward signals in the visual domain necessitate a dedicated and comprehensive investigation. Existing efforts to apply RL to image generation have largely overlooked these distinctions, making a systematic study of RL strategies, their dependencies, and scaling behaviors vital for advancing the field.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research employs a systematic empirical investigation framework to compare GRPO and DPO in autoregressive image generation, building upon an established baseline model and carefully controlled experimental settings.

**Task Formulation:**
The study focuses on autoregressive image generation models, which transform images into discrete tokens using quantized autoencoders and sequentially predict visual tokens, mirroring the token-by-token generation of LLMs. This formulation allows for the direct application and adaptation of preference-based RL objectives, specifically DPO and GRPO.

**Baseline Model:**
**Janus-Pro** is selected as the baseline autoregressive generative model. Janus-Pro is described as a "latest autoregressive image generation model with advanced capabilities," providing a strong foundation for the experiments.

**Experimental Settings:**
To evaluate the models comprehensively, performance is assessed on both in-domain and out-of-domain benchmarks:
- **In-Domain Evaluation:** **T2I-CompBench** is used. This benchmark features "long, detailed prompts designed to compose complex scenes with multiple objects, displaying various attributes and relationships," reflecting challenging, composition-heavy scenarios.
- **Out-of-Domain Generalization:** **GenEval** is used. This dataset utilizes "short, templated prompts starting with 'a photo of,'" designed to assess the model's robustness and generalization to concise and standardized textual prompts, representing a different distribution from the complex in-domain prompts.

**RL Algorithm Implementation:**

- **GRPO (Group Relative Policy Optimization):**
 - **Nature:** On-policy RL algorithm, which refines policies using self-generated data iteratively. It improves upon PPO by eliminating the learned value critic and estimating advantages directly through group-wise normalization of rewards across multiple responses per query.
 - **Training Data:** For fair comparison, GRPO is trained using the official 5.6k prompts from the T2I-CompBench dataset. As an on-policy method, it does not require pre-collected chosen/rejected pairs; it samples from the current policy.
 - **Hyperparameters:** A group size of four completions per query is used, with the iteration times set to 1 for the baseline.

- **DPO (Direct Preference Optimization):**
 - **Nature:** Off-policy RL algorithm, directly optimizing a policy against a reference policy using static, pre-collected preference data (chosen and rejected pairs).
 - **Training Data Curation:**
 - **Image Generation:** To maintain comparable computational costs with GRPO, DPO generates the same number of images per prompt as GRPO's group size (four in baseline setting).
 - **Preference Pair Construction:** For each prompt, the highest-scoring image and the lowest-scoring image from the generated set (as determined by a chosen reward model) are selected as the "chosen" and "rejected" images, respectively. This yields 5.6k ranking pairs from the T2I-CompBench prompts.
 - **Loss Adaptation:** DPO's maximum likelihood objective is directly adapted to the cross-entropy loss used for autoregressive image generation.

**Reward Model Design and Evaluation:**
The study investigates the impact of various reward models, categorizing them into three types:
- **Human Preference Models:** HPS and ImageReward. These are vision-language models (VLMs) trained on human-annotated image rankings to assess aesthetic appeal and text-image alignment.
- **Visual Question Answering Models:** UnifiedReward, Fine-tuned ORM. These leverage multimodal large language models (MLLMs) like LLaVA for scenario-based evaluations, emphasizing detailed reasoning.
- **Metric Reward:** Utilizes the official evaluation protocols from T2I-CompBench for specific attributes, providing a more objective, domain-specific signal.

To understand the intrinsic generalization of these reward models independently of RL training, a "best-of-N" (specifically best-of-4) strategy is employed on the GenEval dataset, where the reward model acts as an Outcome Reward Model (ORM) to select the best image.

**Scaling Strategies Investigation:**
Three critical dimensions of scaling are explored:
1. **Scaling Sampled Images per Prompt:**
 - **GRPO:** Increasing the "group size" of real-time samples during RL training (e.g., from 4 to 8, 12, 16).
 - **DPO:** Increasing the number of images generated per prompt for preference pair selection, enhancing the discriminative power of preference pairs.
2. **Scaling In-Domain Training Data:**
 - A structured prompt generation pipeline leveraging GPT-4o is developed to expand the T2I-CompBench dataset. This involves creating category-specific meta-prompts with strict constraints (e.g., category-aware syntactic templates, semantic novelty enforcement) to generate genuinely novel compositions (double or triple the original dataset size).
3. **Implementing Iterative Training Paradigms:**
 - **GRPO-Iter:** Extends the standard GRPO by updating the reference model with the policy model's parameters after each training round, aligning the KL penalty with the current policy.
 - **DPO-Iter:** Follows established iterative DPO protocols, where preference pairs are re-sampled and re-evaluated after each training cycle, and the reference model is updated.

**Computational Setup:**
All experiments are conducted using 8 A100 GPUs. Detailed computational time records are maintained for DPO (image generation, scoring, training) and GRPO, ensuring a fair comparison of computational costs across different configurations and scaling ratios. This meticulous methodology ensures that the comparative analysis is robust, controlled, and provides actionable insights.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The study presents a comprehensive set of findings, offering distinct insights into the strengths and weaknesses of GRPO and DPO in the autoregressive image generation domain, as well as the critical influences of reward models and scaling strategies.

**A. In-Domain Performance vs. Out-of-Domain Generalization:**

- **DPO excels in in-domain tasks:** DPO consistently outperforms GRPO on the T2I-CompBench (in-domain) dataset, demonstrating an average improvement of 11.53% across various reward models. This highlights DPO's effectiveness and robustness when optimizing for complex, detailed prompts similar to its training data.
- **GRPO shows superior out-of-domain generalization:** Conversely, GRPO consistently exhibits better generalization capabilities on the GenEval (out-of-domain) dataset, surpassing DPO by an average of 1.14%. This suggests GRPO's on-policy nature and iterative refinement allow it to adapt better to novel prompt distributions.

**B. Impact of Different Reward Models:**

- **DPO is more sensitive to reward model choice:** DPO's generalization performance on GenEval shows larger fluctuations and higher variance (range 2.72, variance 0.9547) compared to GRPO (range 1.81, variance 0.5486). This indicates that DPO's generalization is more dependent on the specific characteristics and biases of the chosen reward model.
- **Stronger reward model generalization boosts RL generalization:** A key finding is that the intrinsic generalization capabilities of the reward models directly correlate with the generalization performance of both GRPO and DPO. Reward models that inherently generalize better (e.g., Unified Reward, which showed the best intrinsic generalization via best-of-4 selection on GenEval) lead to better out-of-domain performance for the RL algorithms themselves. This suggests that improving reward models' generalization is crucial for enhancing overall RL performance in this domain.

**C. Investigation of Effective Scaling Strategies:**

- **GRPO Scaling Trends:**
 - **Sampling efficiency for in-domain gains:** Scaling the number of sampled images per prompt (group size) for GRPO tends to yield more computationally efficient in-domain performance gains compared to scaling data or iterative training. Larger group sizes lead to improved advantage estimation and more stable policy updates.
 - **Generalization vs. Overfitting:** Moderate scaling of sampling size (e.g., to 4 or 8) and in-domain data quantity (doubling or tripling) improves GRPO's generalization. However, excessive scaling in either dimension can lead to a slight decline in generalization, indicating overfitting to in-domain characteristics.

- **DPO Scaling Trends:**
 - **Iterative training for in-domain, but at a cost to generalization:** Iterative training (DPO-Iter) substantially enhances DPO's in-domain performance. A single iteration can outperform tripling training data, with further iterations yielding more gains. However, this comes at a significant cost to out-of-domain generalization, which declines substantially after two iterations, likely due to overfitting to the training preference data.
 - **Moderate sampling for optimal contrast:** Scaling the sample size for DPO's preference pair selection yields non-monotonic effects. Moderate sampling (e.g., 8 samples) optimizes preference contrast, leading to improvements in both in-domain and generalization performance. Conversely, excessive sampling (e.g., 16 samples) induces bias, causing a decline in both.
 - **Data scaling mitigates bias and boosts performance:** Increasing the diversity and quantity of in-domain data for DPO consistently improves both in-domain and out-of-domain performance. This strategy helps mitigate biases introduced by the limited preference scope of smaller datasets, leading to more robust models.

In summary, DPO is adept at refining models for specific, complex in-domain tasks, while GRPO demonstrates a stronger capacity for broad generalization. The quality and generalization of the reward model are paramount for both, and tailored scaling strategies are necessary to optimize performance, with distinct trade-offs between in-domain excellence and out-of-domain robustness for each algorithm.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research marks a significant step forward in the application of reinforcement learning to generative AI, particularly for visual synthesis. Its contributions have several layers of significance and potential impact:

1. **First Comprehensive Empirical Study in Visual CoT:** The paper provides the *first comprehensive empirical study* systematically comparing GRPO and DPO for autoregressive image generation. This is crucial because it goes beyond mere application, offering in-depth analysis of their distinct behaviors, strengths, and weaknesses in a domain (visual CoT) that is fundamentally different from LLM-based reasoning. This foundational comparison will serve as a critical reference for future research in this nascent field.

2. **Guiding Reward Model Development:** A key insight is the demonstrated correlation between the intrinsic generalization capacity of a reward model and the generalization performance of the RL algorithms it guides. This finding is highly significant, as it provides a clear direction for improving RL-based image generation: invest in developing reward models that are not just accurate on training data but also robust and generalizable across diverse visual concepts and prompt styles. This shift in focus for reward model design can unlock more powerful and versatile generative models.

3. **Actionable Insights for Scaling Strategies:** The systematic investigation of three critical scaling dimensions—sampled images, training data volume/diversity, and iterative training—yields highly practical and actionable insights. Researchers and practitioners can now make informed decisions about how to efficiently scale their RL-based image generation efforts:
 - For **GRPO**, scaling the number of sampled images per prompt is identified as a computationally efficient path to in-domain gains, while careful moderation of sampling and data scale is needed to preserve generalization.
 - For **DPO**, iterative training boosts in-domain performance but carries significant risks to generalization, while moderate sampling and increasing the diversity of in-domain data are beneficial for both in-domain and generalization performance by mitigating preference bias. These insights enable more strategic resource allocation and optimization.

4. **Paving a New Path for Robust Visual CoT:** By meticulously dissecting the performance characteristics of GRPO and DPO, and identifying the factors that influence their effectiveness, the study "paves a new path for inspiring future work on developing more effective RL algorithms to achieve robust CoT reasoning in the realm of autoregressive image generation." This could lead to:
 - Development of hybrid RL algorithms that combine the in-domain strengths of DPO with the generalization capabilities of GRPO.
 - Novel reward models specifically designed for generalization in multimodal contexts.
 - More sophisticated scaling techniques that balance performance trade-offs.
 - Generative models that exhibit genuinely multi-step, interpretable "visual reasoning," leading to more controlled, consistent, and creative image synthesis.

5. **Enhanced Generative Capabilities:** Ultimately, the findings will contribute to the development of more capable and versatile image generation models. The ability to fine-tune models with RL to achieve higher text-image consistency, better aesthetic quality, and stronger generalization across diverse prompts has direct implications for various applications, including content creation, design, and virtual reality, making generative AI tools more powerful and user-friendly. The release of code further ensures reproducibility and facilitates further research and practical deployment.
