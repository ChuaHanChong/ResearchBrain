# Reinforcement Mid-Training

> [!summary] Summary
> Reinforcement Mid-Training (RMT) formalizes a critical third stage in large language model development, applying reinforcement learning on unlabeled pre-training data to systematically enhance complex reasoning capabilities. The method achieves up to +64.91% higher language modeling accuracy compared to prior RL-based mid-training approaches while reducing reasoning response length by up to 79%.

## Problem

- The conventional two-stage LLM training (pre-training and post-training) often leaves a gap in systematically developing complex reasoning capabilities before specialized fine-tuning.
- Existing RL-based mid-training methods suffer from 'overthinking,' generating excessively long and verbose reasoning chains that lead to high computational costs and inefficiency.
- Current approaches disregard imbalanced token entropy distribution, leading to unstable learning, and underutilize extensive information contained within low-entropy tokens in unlabeled data.

## Method

- Introduces a ==dynamic token budget== (DTB) mechanism that adaptively constrains the length of the reasoning process, incentivized by a triangular-profile length reward function.
- Employs a curriculum-based adaptive sampling (CAS) strategy to dynamically adjust sampling probabilities based on token entropy, progressing from easier to harder tokens during training.
- Utilizes a dual training strategy combining token-selective reinforcement learning for high-entropy tokens with token-inclusive next-token prediction for the majority of low-entropy tokens.

## Results

- RMT-Q3 achieved an average accuracy improvement of +**64.91%** over the prior state-of-the-art RL-based mid-training (RPT) on language modeling tasks.
- Models trained with RMT demonstrated superior transferability and post-training performance, showing an +**18.76%** accuracy improvement over strong baselines after continual post-training.
- RMT reduces reasoning response length to just **21%** of RPT's and **12%** of traditional next-token reasoning, leading to drastic improvements in generation time and computational efficiency.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating reinforcement learning into a dedicated 'mid-training' stage between pre-training and post-training provides a principled way to build complex reasoning skills from unlabeled data.
> - Dynamic control over reasoning length via a token budget and specific reward shaping significantly improves computational efficiency without compromising model effectiveness.
> - An adaptive curriculum that prioritizes tokens based on their learning difficulty, coupled with a dual training objective, enables stable learning and comprehensive utilization of all token information.
