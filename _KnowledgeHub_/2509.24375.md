# Reinforcement Mid-Training

> [!summary] Summary
> Reinforcement Mid-Training (RMT) formalizes a critical third stage in large language model development, applying reinforcement learning on unlabeled pre-training data to systematically enhance complex reasoning capabilities. The method achieves up to +64.91% higher language modeling accuracy compared to prior RL-based mid-training approaches while reducing reasoning response length by up to 79%.

## Problem

- The conventional two-stage LLM training (pre-training and post-training) often leaves a gap in systematically developing complex reasoning capabilities before specialized fine-tuning.
- Existing RL-based mid-training methods suffer from 'overthinking,' generating excessively long and verbose reasoning chains that lead to high computational costs and inefficiency.
- Current approaches disregard imbalanced token entropy distribution, leading to unstable learning, and underutilize extensive information contained within low-entropy tokens in unlabeled data.

## Method

- Introduces a ==dynamic token budget== (DTB) mechanism that adaptively constrains the length of the reasoning process, incentivized by a triangular-profile length reward function.
- Employs a curriculum-based adaptive sampling (CAS) strategy to dynamically adjust sampling probabilities based on token entropy, progressing from easier to harder tokens during training.
- Utilizes a dual training strategy combining token-selective reinforcement learning for high-entropy tokens with token-inclusive next-token prediction for the majority of low-entropy tokens.

## Results

- RMT-Q3 achieved an average accuracy improvement of +**64.91%** over the prior state-of-the-art RL-based mid-training (RPT) on language modeling tasks.
- Models trained with RMT demonstrated superior transferability and post-training performance, showing an +**18.76%** accuracy improvement over strong baselines after continual post-training.
- RMT reduces reasoning response length to just **21%** of RPT's and **12%** of traditional next-token reasoning, leading to drastic improvements in generation time and computational efficiency.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating reinforcement learning into a dedicated 'mid-training' stage between pre-training and post-training provides a principled way to build complex reasoning skills from unlabeled data.
> - Dynamic control over reasoning length via a token budget and specific reward shaping significantly improves computational efficiency without compromising model effectiveness.
> - An adaptive curriculum that prioritizes tokens based on their learning difficulty, coupled with a dual training objective, enables stable learning and comprehensive utilization of all token information.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of state-of-the-art large language models has conventionally been understood as a two-stage process: pre-training and post-training. This paper fundamentally challenges and expands this established paradigm by proposing and formalizing a crucial, emerging third stage: "Reinforcement Mid-Training."

**Pre-training**, the first stage, involves training models on vast, unlabeled web-scale data using self-supervised objectives like next-token prediction. This stage endows LLMs with foundational linguistic competence and extensive world knowledge, exemplified by models like BERT, T5, and GPT-3. The research on scaling laws (Kaplan et al., 2020; Hoffmann et al., 2022) has further guided the efficient development of pre-trained models such as LLaMA and GPT-4.

**Post-training**, the second stage, focuses on refining and aligning pre-trained models with specific human objectives or downstream tasks. This typically involves using high-quality, specialized datasets and methods such as supervised fine-tuning (SFT) for instruction following (Wang et al., 2022; Chung et al., 2024) or reinforcement learning, particularly Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) (Ouyang et al., 2022; Bai et al., 2022). Recent advancements in RL for post-training have also tackled stability and computational efficiency limitations of algorithms like PPO, introducing methods like GRPO (Shao et al., 2024), DAPO (Yu et al., 2025), and VAPO (Yue et al., 2025).

The concept of **mid-training** has recently emerged as an intermediate phase positioned between pre-training and post-training. Prior efforts (Wake et al., 2024; OLMo et al., 2025; Wang et al., 2025) have explored this stage, characterizing it as an intermediate phase in terms of both computational and data demands, designed to enhance complex capabilities (e.g., mathematical reasoning) to better prepare models for post-training. Crucially, existing mid-training approaches aim to leverage *unlabeled pre-training data* but with more targeted objectives than general pre-training, distinguishing it from post-training which often relies on domain-specific data with labels or explicit reward signals.

This paper's contribution lies in formalizing **Reinforcement Mid-Training** (RMT), specifically integrating reinforcement learning into this intermediate stage. It acknowledges the pioneering work of "Reinforcement Pre-Training" (RPT) (Dong et al., 2025), but critically points out that RPT, despite its name, functions as a mid-training method given the established instruction-following and reasoning capabilities of its base model. The broader research gap RMT addresses is the prohibitive computational cost of applying reinforcement learning over massive unlabeled corpora during mid-training. By providing an efficient, adaptive, and unified framework, RMT aims to unlock the full potential of RL in systematically enhancing LLM reasoning capabilities using readily available pre-training data, thereby creating a stronger foundation for subsequent post-training and more capable LLMs.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to formally introduce, define, and establish "Reinforcement Mid-Training" as a critical and distinct intermediate stage in the lifecycle of large language models. The authors aim to leverage reinforcement learning on large-scale unlabeled pre-training data to systematically enhance complex reasoning capabilities in LLMs, thereby creating models that are better prepared for the subsequent, often more specialized, post-training phase and downstream tasks.

This overarching objective is driven by several key motivations, which also highlight the limitations of existing LLM training paradigms:

1. **Bridging the Gap between Pre-training and Post-training:** The conventional two-stage process often leaves a significant gap in systematically developing complex capabilities like mathematical reasoning. Pre-training provides general knowledge, while post-training focuses on alignment or specific tasks. Mid-training is motivated by the need for an intermediate phase that can robustly build these complex capabilities using readily available pre-training data, without the need for expensive human annotations or verified rewards typically required for post-training.

2. **Addressing Inefficiency of Existing RL-based Mid-training (Overthinking):** The paper identifies that current approaches, specifically RPT, suffer from inefficient reinforcement learning due to unconstrained "overthinking." Models tend to generate excessively long and verbose reasoning chains (as illustrated in Figure 1a), leading to substantial computational overhead during both training and inference. This inefficiency makes the application of RL to massive datasets impractical.

3. **Overcoming the Disregard of Imbalanced Token Entropy Distribution:** Tokens within pre-training data possess varying levels of uncertainty and learning difficulty, reflected in their entropy. Existing methods often sample challenging tokens indiscriminately, which can overwhelm the model during early training stages before it has developed sufficient capacity. This leads to unstable learning and suboptimal performance. The motivation is to create an adaptive learning trajectory that progresses from easier to harder tokens.

4. **Mitigating Underutilization of Token Information:** As depicted in Figure 1b, the vast majority of tokens in unlabeled pre-training data exhibit low entropy. Current RL-based mid-training often focuses exclusively on high-entropy tokens, neglecting the extensive information contained within low-entropy tokens. This exclusion results in significant information loss and missed learning opportunities, hindering the model's comprehensive language understanding. The motivation is to develop a unified approach that leverages all available token information.

By addressing these challenges, the authors aim to propose RMT, a framework that is not only efficient and adaptive but also unified, ensuring that LLMs can acquire enhanced reasoning capabilities effectively and economically, setting a stronger foundation for their application in various complex domains.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

To achieve its objectives and overcome the identified challenges, the RMT (Reinforced Mid-Training) framework integrates three innovative components: a dynamic token budget mechanism, a curriculum-based adaptive sampling strategy, and a dual training strategy combining token-selective reinforcement learning with token-inclusive next-token prediction. The overall framework is designed to be efficient, adaptive, and unified.

**Problem Definition:** The core problem is defined as optimizing a policy model (πθ) using reinforcement learning on large-scale unlabeled pre-training data (S) to enhance subsequent post-training performance. This involves partitioning tokens into two disjoint sets: ΦRL for reinforcement learning and ΦNTP for standard next-token prediction, where |ΦNTP| ≫ |ΦRL|. For RL tokens, the model generates chain-of-thought reasoning (cm) and predicts the next token (ym), receiving a reward (r) based on exact match to the ground truth (τm). For NTP tokens, the model maximizes likelihood. The central challenge is to reduce RL computational cost while ensuring concise and efficient reasoning.

#### 3.1. Dynamic Token Budget Mechanism (DTB)

To address the "inefficient overthinking" challenge, RMT introduces a dynamic token budget (`B_t`) that adaptively constrains the length of the reasoning process.
- **Adaptive Budget:** The budget `B_t` decays exponentially with training progress (t), defined as `B_t = max(B_min, floor(B_0 * γ^(t/T)))`, where `B_0` is the initial budget, `B_min` is the minimum budget, and `γ` is the decay factor. This encourages conciseness as the model matures.
- **Budget-Aware Instruction:** During training, an instruction is injected into the input prompt, explicitly requesting the model to utilize exactly `B_t` tokens within its reasoning segment (e.g., `<think></think>`).
- **Length Reward Function:** A triangular-profile length reward `r_len(ℓ; B_t)` is designed. It yields `r_max` when the generated reasoning length `ℓ` equals `B_t`, linearly penalizes deviations (`0 ≤ ℓ < B_t` or `B_t < ℓ ≤ 2B_t`), and provides zero reward for severely over-budget reasoning (`ℓ > 2B_t`). This incentivizes precise adherence to the budget while allowing some flexibility.

#### 3.2. Curriculum-Based Adaptive Sampling (CAS)

To tackle the "imbalanced token entropy distribution" problem, RMT employs a curriculum-based adaptive sampling method.
- **Difficulty Categorization:** Tokens are categorized into "easy," "medium," and "hard" based on their entropy values (a measure of uncertainty/difficulty).
- **Dynamic Sampling Probabilities:** Tokens are sampled from these categories with probabilities (`p_t`) that evolve dynamically over the training steps (T).
- **Piecewise Linear Interpolation:** The sampling probabilities transition smoothly across two curriculum transition points (`t1` and `t2`). In the early phase (t < t1), easier tokens are emphasized. During the transition phase (t1 ≤ t < t2), exposure to medium and hard tokens gradually increases. In the late phase (t ≥ t2), challenging high-entropy tokens are prioritized to boost performance. This ensures stable learning and gradual mastery of complex reasoning.

#### 3.3. Dual Training Strategy (Token-Selective RL and Token-Inclusive NTP)

To resolve the "underutilization of token information," RMT combines two complementary training objectives.
- **Token-Selective Reinforcement Learning:**
 - **Targeted Learning:** RL is applied only to a *small set* of selected tokens (C), chosen via curriculum-based adaptive sampling. These are typically higher-entropy tokens critical for reasoning.
 - **Response Generation & Reward:** For a selected token `τ` at position `pos`, the policy model `π_θ` generates `G` responses `o_i^pos = (c_i^pos, y_i^pos)` (chain-of-thought and prediction). A verifiable matching reward `r_i^pos` (1 for exact match, 0 otherwise) is combined with the dynamic length reward `r_i^len` (from DTB) using a trade-off weight `w` to form a composite reward `r_i`.
 - **Stable RL Algorithm:** GRPO is used to compute a group-relative, whitened advantage (`A_i`) from these rewards, providing a stable learning signal.
 - **RL Objective:** The RL objective `L_RL(θ)` is the expectation of the advantage-weighted log-likelihood of sampled outputs, with a KL-divergence penalty to prevent policy collapse (`− β KL(π_θ || π_ref)`).
- **Token-Inclusive Next-Token Prediction (NTP):**
 - **Comprehensive Information Use:** For the *majority of low-entropy tokens* (`Φ_NTP`) not selected for RL, standard next-token prediction is performed. This ensures that all token information contributes to language understanding.
 - **Masking:** Tokens already used in RL are masked to avoid redundant training.
 - **NTP Objective:** The `L_NTP(θ)` maximizes the likelihood `log p_θ(τ_pos | τ_<pos)` for unmasked tokens, using supervised fine-tuning with teacher forcing.
- **Unified Training Objective:** The total loss `L` is a combination of the reinforcement learning loss and the next-token prediction loss: `L = L_RL(θ) + λ ⋅ L_NTP(θ)`, where `λ` is a trade-off weight. This synergistic approach allows RL to focus on complex reasoning over challenging tokens, while NTP ensures broad language understanding across all tokens.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The extensive experiments conducted demonstrate RMT's significant superiority in both effectiveness and efficiency across language modeling and continual post-training tasks.

#### 4.1. Language Modeling Performance

RMT achieves state-of-the-art performance in language modeling, evaluated on the OmniMATH dataset across easy, medium, and hard difficulty tokens.
- **Superior Accuracy:** RMT consistently outperforms baselines, including the strong pre-trained models (R1-Distill-14B, Qwen3-14B in NTP mode) and the prior state-of-the-art RL-based mid-training method, RPT.
 - RMT-R1 (using R1-Distill-14B as backbone) achieves an average improvement of +30.74% over RPT.
 - RMT-Q3 (using Qwen3-14B as backbone) demonstrates an even more remarkable average improvement of +64.91% over RPT.
- **Amplified Gains with Stronger Base Models:** The results show that stronger backbone models (e.g., Qwen3-14B) further amplify the performance gains achieved by RMT, with RMT-Q3 outperforming RMT-R1 across all difficulty levels.
- **Ineffectiveness of Naive Reasoning:** Notably, traditional "next token reasoning" (NTR) methods without RMT's specific designs perform poorly across all difficulty levels, underscoring the necessity of RMT's structured approach to instill effective reasoning capabilities.

#### 4.2. Continual Post-Training Performance

RMT demonstrates strong transferability, where checkpoints obtained after reinforcement mid-training significantly benefit subsequent post-training on the Skywork mathematical reasoning dataset.
- **Substantial Post-Training Gains:** All models show substantial performance increases after continual post-training. However, RMT-trained models exhibit superior foundations.
- **Highest Overall Performance:** RMT-Q3 achieves a remarkable jump from 25.17% accuracy before post-training to 64.33% accuracy after, showcasing its effectiveness in preparing models for downstream tasks.
- **Superior Transferability:** Compared to the strongest baseline, RMT-Q3 yields an improvement of +8.63% over RPT before post-training and a significant +18.76% improvement over Qwen3-14B after post-training. This indicates that RMT creates more robust and generalizable model checkpoints.

#### 4.3. Response Length and Generation Time Analysis (Efficiency)

RMT drastically improves computational efficiency without sacrificing performance.
- **Reduced Reasoning Length:** In language modeling, RMT-R1 and RMT-Q3 generate significantly shorter responses (188 and 186 tokens, respectively). This represents a reduction to just 21% of RPT's response length (872 tokens) and 12% of Qwen3-14B's (1577 tokens).
- **Dynamic Adaptation:** During continual post-training, RMT dynamically adapts its response length. While starting with shorter reasoning chains than RPT, it adaptively increases length for more complex tasks, yet remains more efficient than RPT throughout.
- **Faster Generation Time:** Consistent with reduced response lengths, RMT exhibits substantially lower generation times, confirming its computational efficiency.

#### 4.4. Ablation Study

The ablation studies confirm the synergistic contributions of all proposed components:
- **NTP Loss is Crucial:** Removing the next-token prediction (NTP) loss leads to the most significant performance drop, highlighting its essential role in incorporating comprehensive token information and maintaining language understanding.
- **DTB and CAS Contributions:** Removing the dynamic token budget (DTB) mechanism or the curriculum-based adaptive sampling (CAS) also results in performance degradation, validating their effectiveness in enhancing model efficiency and learning trajectory.
- **Synergistic Effect:** The full RMT framework consistently achieves the best results, demonstrating that the integration of DTB, CAS, and the dual training strategy provides a synergistic effect, leading to superior overall performance.

#### 4.5. Case Study

A qualitative case study further illustrates RMT's effectiveness. For a given mathematical reasoning problem, RMT-Q3 correctly predicts the next token ("probability") with a concise (165 tokens), coherent, and grammatically correct reasoning trace. In contrast, RPT produces a significantly longer (1,376 tokens), verbose, inconsistent, and ultimately incorrect reasoning, leading to the wrong prediction ("same"). This highlights RMT's ability to promote both accuracy and conciseness in reasoning.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "Reinforcement Mid-Training" (RMT) framework represents a significant advancement in the development and understanding of large language models, with profound implications for their future design and application.

**1. Establishment of a New LLM Development Paradigm:** The most significant contribution is the formal definition and empirical validation of reinforcement mid-training as a crucial, distinct third stage in the LLM lifecycle. By positioning it between general pre-training and specialized post-training, RMT provides a principled approach to leverage vast unlabeled data for systematic enhancement of complex capabilities. This paradigm shift could redefine the roadmap for building highly capable and robust LLMs, moving beyond the traditional two-stage view.

**2. Enhanced Efficiency in Reasoning:** RMT directly addresses a critical bottleneck in RL-based reasoning: computational overhead due to excessively long and verbose reasoning chains. The dynamic token budget mechanism ensures concise and efficient reasoning without sacrificing quality, which is crucial for scaling LLMs to more complex tasks and larger datasets. This efficiency gain makes sophisticated reasoning capabilities more practical and economically viable to integrate into LLM training.

**3. Systematic Improvement of Complex Capabilities:** By combining targeted reinforcement learning with comprehensive next-token prediction, RMT enables LLMs to systematically acquire and refine complex reasoning skills, such as mathematical problem-solving, using readily available unlabeled data. This ability to enhance core reasoning abilities before fine-tuning for specific tasks creates a more powerful and versatile foundation model.

**4. Improved Transferability and Generalization:** The experimental results clearly show that models mid-trained with RMT provide a stronger foundation for subsequent post-training. This means RMT-trained models are not just better at their mid-training objectives but also exhibit superior transferability and generalization to diverse downstream tasks. This leads to more robust and adaptable LLMs that can excel across a wider range of applications after further specialization.

**5. Optimal Utilization of Data Resources:** RMT's dual training strategy effectively utilizes both high-entropy (challenging) and low-entropy (common) tokens in unlabeled data. This holistic approach avoids information loss associated with focusing solely on difficult tokens, ensuring that the model benefits from all available information for comprehensive language understanding and targeted reasoning improvement.

**6. Plug-and-Play Versatility:** The framework's demonstrated compatibility with different backbone models and reinforcement learning algorithms (e.g., GRPO) enhances its versatility and potential for broad adoption across various LLM architectures and training pipelines.

**Potential Impact:** This work opens up promising new directions for future research. It encourages deeper exploration into optimal curriculum design for LLM training, novel RL algorithms tailored for efficiency, and the synergistic combination of diverse learning objectives. Reinforcement Mid-Training has the potential to become a standard practice in the LLM development pipeline, leading to a new generation of more intelligent, efficient, and adaptable language models capable of tackling increasingly complex real-world challenges with greater accuracy and less computational burden.
