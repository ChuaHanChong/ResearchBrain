# HYPERmotion: Learning Hybrid Behavior Planning for Autonomous Loco-manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Istituto Italiano di Tecnologia developed HYPERmotion, a hierarchical framework enabling high-DoF humanoid robots to autonomously perform complex loco-manipulation tasks by integrating reinforcement learning for skill acquisition, whole-body optimization for dynamic feasibility, and foundation models for intelligent planning and morphology selection. The system was validated on a centaur-like robot, demonstrating zero-shot online planning and robust execution of multi-step tasks in real-world environments.

## Problem

- Integrating locomotion and manipulation into a cohesive, adaptable whole-body control framework for high-DoF humanoid robots remains a significant challenge.
- Learning-based methods face exponential increases in training costs for high-DoF humanoids and considerable difficulties for deployment on real hardware under dynamic constraints.
- Existing efforts to integrate Large Language Models (LLMs) and Visual Language Models (VLMs) into robotics often struggle to extend to complex humanoid platforms for intricate whole-body motion control.

## Method

- The **HYPERmotion** framework employs a hierarchical and modular pipeline integrating task-oriented whole-body motion learning via ==Reinforcement Learning (PPO)== for upper and lower body parts, with subsequent mapping to full 38-DoF through a whole-body optimizer.
- OpenAI's GPT-4o acts as the LLM planner, performing logical reasoning to decompose free-text commands into sub-tasks and sequencing skills from a motion library into a hierarchical Behavior Tree.
- OpenAI's GPT-4v serves as the VLM-based morphology selector, utilizing onboard camera images and distilled spatial geometric information to intelligently choose the most suitable locomotion (wheeled/legged) and manipulation (single/dual-arm) modes.

## Results

- The framework successfully learned and deployed individual motion primitives (e.g., opening a drawer, opening a door) on a real centaur-like robot, with the whole-body controller effectively ensuring dynamically feasible and smooth motions.
- The VLM-based morphology selector achieved a high average success rate across ten manipulation and ten locomotion scenarios in both simulation and real environments, with spatial geometric information substantially improving accuracy.
- The LLM-based planner demonstrated the ability to execute complex loco-manipulation tasks from human instructions on the real robot with a success rate of **60%** or higher, showcasing versatility for multi-step, long-horizon tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Combining RL-learned low-dimensional trajectories with a non-linear optimal control whole-body trajectory generator effectively bridges the gap between simplified learning and real-world dynamic constraints, producing smooth and dynamically feasible motions.
> - Grounding high-level planning from foundation models with rich spatial geometry from onboard sensors significantly improves the accuracy and robustness of intelligent morphology selection in diverse manipulation and locomotion scenarios.
> - A modular skill library and hierarchical planning via Behavior Trees, driven by an LLM, enables zero-shot online planning from open-ended verbal instructions for complex, long-horizon tasks without extensive retraining.
