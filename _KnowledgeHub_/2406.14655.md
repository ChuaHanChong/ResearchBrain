# HYPERmotion: Learning Hybrid Behavior Planning for Autonomous Loco-manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Istituto Italiano di Tecnologia developed HYPERmotion, a hierarchical framework enabling high-DoF humanoid robots to autonomously perform complex loco-manipulation tasks by integrating reinforcement learning for skill acquisition, whole-body optimization for dynamic feasibility, and foundation models for intelligent planning and morphology selection. The system was validated on a centaur-like robot, demonstrating zero-shot online planning and robust execution of multi-step tasks in real-world environments.

## Problem

- Integrating locomotion and manipulation into a cohesive, adaptable whole-body control framework for high-DoF humanoid robots remains a significant challenge.
- Learning-based methods face exponential increases in training costs for high-DoF humanoids and considerable difficulties for deployment on real hardware under dynamic constraints.
- Existing efforts to integrate Large Language Models (LLMs) and Visual Language Models (VLMs) into robotics often struggle to extend to complex humanoid platforms for intricate whole-body motion control.

## Method

- The **HYPERmotion** framework employs a hierarchical and modular pipeline integrating task-oriented whole-body motion learning via ==Reinforcement Learning (PPO)== for upper and lower body parts, with subsequent mapping to full 38-DoF through a whole-body optimizer.
- OpenAI's GPT-4o acts as the LLM planner, performing logical reasoning to decompose free-text commands into sub-tasks and sequencing skills from a motion library into a hierarchical Behavior Tree.
- OpenAI's GPT-4v serves as the VLM-based morphology selector, utilizing onboard camera images and distilled spatial geometric information to intelligently choose the most suitable locomotion (wheeled/legged) and manipulation (single/dual-arm) modes.

## Results

- The framework successfully learned and deployed individual motion primitives (e.g., opening a drawer, opening a door) on a real centaur-like robot, with the whole-body controller effectively ensuring dynamically feasible and smooth motions.
- The VLM-based morphology selector achieved a high average success rate across ten manipulation and ten locomotion scenarios in both simulation and real environments, with spatial geometric information substantially improving accuracy.
- The LLM-based planner demonstrated the ability to execute complex loco-manipulation tasks from human instructions on the real robot with a success rate of **60%** or higher, showcasing versatility for multi-step, long-horizon tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Combining RL-learned low-dimensional trajectories with a non-linear optimal control whole-body trajectory generator effectively bridges the gap between simplified learning and real-world dynamic constraints, producing smooth and dynamically feasible motions.
> - Grounding high-level planning from foundation models with rich spatial geometry from onboard sensors significantly improves the accuracy and robustness of intelligent morphology selection in diverse manipulation and locomotion scenarios.
> - A modular skill library and hierarchical planning via Behavior Trees, driven by an LLM, enables zero-shot online planning from open-ended verbal instructions for complex, long-horizon tasks without extensive retraining.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotics is rapidly progressing towards creating versatile and autonomous embodied agents, particularly humanoid robots capable of interacting with complex, unstructured environments. This paper addresses a critical challenge within this landscape: enabling humanoid robots to perform **hybrid motions**, encompassing both locomotion and manipulation, in a truly autonomous and adaptable manner for long-horizon tasks.

Traditionally, research in humanoid robotics has often focused on either robust locomotion or dexterous manipulation using fixed-base arms. While impressive advancements have been made in both domains, integrating these capabilities into a cohesive, adaptable whole-body control framework for high-DoF humanoid robots remains a significant hurdle. Existing humanoid whole-body control methods often struggle with versatility and adapting to new tasks and environments, limiting their ability to execute long-horizon instructions. The high degrees of freedom (DoF) inherent in humanoids lead to exponential increases in training costs for learning-based methods like reinforcement learning (RL) and pose considerable difficulties for deployment on real hardware under dynamic constraints.

In parallel, the rise of Large Language Models (LLMs) and Visual Language Models (VLMs) has revolutionized high-level planning and reasoning in robotics. LLMs have demonstrated capabilities in task decomposition, code generation, and logical sequencing, while VLMs, by integrating multimodal observations, can enhance robot perception and decision-making. However, most efforts to integrate foundation models into robotics have focused on fixed robotic arms, with few successful attempts to extend these to complex humanoid platforms. The challenges stem from the intricate dynamics, precise coordination required between many body parts, and the need to ground abstract language commands into concrete whole-body movements. Prior work on LLM-based humanoid control often either lacks cooperative mobility or is limited to simulation.

HYPERmotion positions itself at the intersection of these domains, aiming to bridge the gap between high-level semantic understanding provided by foundation models and the low-level, dynamic, whole-body control of complex humanoid robots. It addresses the limitations of both pure learning-based approaches (high DoF, deployment complexity) and model-based control (lack of adaptability, predefined trajectories) by combining their strengths. By introducing a modular RL-based skill learning framework with a whole-body optimizer, and integrating LLMs and VLMs for intelligent task planning and morphology selection, the paper pushes the boundaries of autonomous humanoid loco-manipulation in unstructured environments. The focus on zero-shot online planning and the use of onboard sensing to ground spatial knowledge for morphology selection represents a significant step towards truly autonomous and adaptive humanoid robots.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enable humanoid robots to **autonomously perform hybrid motions (locomotion and manipulation) in diverse environments** for long-horizon tasks, such as material handling, household chores, and work assistance. This overarching goal is driven by several key motivations and specific objectives:

- **Enhance Versatility and Adaptability:** Current humanoid control methods often lack the versatility and adaptability required for new tasks and environments, limiting their utility for complex, open-ended instructions. The authors aim to overcome this by learning, selecting, and planning behaviors dynamically.
- **Achieve Whole-Body Coordination:** The complex configuration and high degrees of freedom of humanoid robots make whole-body coordination a significant challenge. The research seeks to exploit the robot's intrinsic motion capabilities and diversify its behaviors while inferring affordances and spatial geometric constraints.
- **Improve Learning Efficiency and Real-world Deployment:** Direct simulation training for whole-body trajectories of high-DoF systems is inefficient and impractical. A key motivation is to develop a decomposed training strategy that reduces complexity and allows for efficient learning and robust deployment on real robots under dynamic constraints.
- **Integrate High-Level Planning with Low-Level Execution:** While LLMs offer remarkable capabilities in logical reasoning and hierarchical planning, they struggle with the intricate details of whole-body motion control and precise coordination. The objective is to effectively leverage LLMs for high-level planning while grounding their outputs into executable, low-level robotic actions.
- **Enable Intelligent Morphology Selection:** Humans naturally adapt their movement strategies based on task requirements and environmental context. The authors aim to endow robots with a similar capability, allowing them to autonomously select appropriate manipulation (e.g., single-arm, dual-arm) and locomotion (e.g., legged, wheeled) morphologies based on task context and distilled spatial information.
- **Facilitate Zero-Shot Behavior Planning from Free-Text Commands:** A core motivation is to achieve high autonomy, allowing the robot to interpret free-text instructions from users and perform complex tasks through online, zero-shot planning without extensive retraining or demonstrations for each new task.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The HYPERmotion framework addresses the challenges of autonomous loco-manipulation by integrating reinforcement learning, whole-body optimization, and foundation models (LLMs and VLMs) into a hierarchical and modular pipeline. The framework is divided into four interrelated sectors: Motion Generation, User Input, Task Planning, and Morphology Selector.

1. **Motion Generation:**
 - **Task-Oriented Whole-Body Motion Learning (RL):** To manage the high DoF of the humanoid, the robot's upper body (manipulation) is separated from its legs (locomotion) for training. Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) is employed to learn task-specific skills. The action space is reduced (e.g., 14 DoF for single-arm tasks, 19 DoF for dual-arm tasks, including arm joints, floating base translation/rotation, torso yaw, and gripper control). Observations include target states and upper-body joint states. A generalized reward function encourages reaching, orientation alignment, grasping, task completion, and penalizes excessive actions.
 - **Whole-Body Motion Generation (Optimization):** The lower-dimensional trajectories learned from RL are then mapped to the full 38-DoF whole-body joint space. A unified whole-body trajectory generator, based on solving a non-linear optimal control problem, ensures the dynamic feasibility of the motion. This optimization enforces whole-body dynamics, joint limits, velocity limits, and contact force constraints, while minimizing the difference between the RL-generated upper-body trajectory and the optimized whole-body trajectory. This process acts as a filter, producing smooth and safe movements.
 - **Motion Library:** Learned whole-body policies, along with their attribute and functional descriptions, are stored in a motion library. This library serves as a catalog of primitive skills that the LLM can access and sequence.

2. **User Input:** This sector provides a structured interface for human interaction. It comprises:
 - **Basic Prompts:** Descriptions of the task background, robot characteristics, user command interpretation, and output format requirements.
 - **Motion Library Catalog:** A list of available learned skills and their descriptions.
 - **Function Options:** Specifications for additional functions (e.g., morphology selection, failure detection/recovery) that can be enabled or disabled to guide the LLM's planning.

3. **Task Planning (LLM):** OpenAI's GPT-4o is utilized as the LLM planner. Upon receiving a free-text command from the user, the LLM performs logical reasoning to decompose complex instructions into sub-tasks. It then selects appropriate skills from the motion library and arranges them into a **hierarchical task graph**. This task graph is converted into an XML file, which constructs a **Behavior Tree (BT)**. The BT provides a robust, hierarchical structure for guiding the robot's actions and making decisions, translating high-level instructions into executable low-level skill sequences.

4. **Morphology Selector (VLM):** OpenAI's GPT-4v serves as the VLM. Inspired by human common sense, this component enables the robot to select the most suitable locomotion or manipulation morphology based on environmental context and task requirements.
 - **Manipulation Morphology Selection:** For manipulation tasks, the robot uses its head camera (2D and depth images). Object detection and pose estimation algorithms extract the 6D pose of the target object. The VLM, fed with the current task state, 2D images, object pose, and descriptions of the robot's structure/affordances (p_V), determines the optimal manipulation mode (e.g., single-arm or dual-arm).
 - **Locomotion Morphology Selection:** For locomotion, the pelvis depth camera generates a point cloud, which is down-sampled into a voxel grid. This spatial information, combined with 2D images, task state, and robot affordance prompts, allows the VLM to select the appropriate locomotion mode (e.g., wheeled or legged motion).

**Hardware and Software:** The framework is deployed on a centaur-like humanoid robot with 38 actuated joints, featuring two arms (one with a claw gripper) and four wheeled legs. Two RealSense Depth Camera D435i units provide onboard sensing (head and pelvis). Xbot facilitates real-time communication, and Isaac Gym is used for RL training, while Gazebo is used for validating whole-body motion.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted in both simulation and the real world validate the HYPERmotion framework's ability to learn, plan, and select behaviors for diverse loco-manipulation tasks.

1. **Whole-body Trajectory Learning:**
 - The paper demonstrates the successful learning and deployment of individual motion primitives, such as opening a drawer and opening a door.
 - Comparison of end-effector position trajectories across Isaac Gym (training), Gazebo (simulation validation), and the real robot (Fig. 5) shows effective tracking. The whole-body controller, acting as a filter, produces smoother and dynamically feasible motions on the real robot, effectively bridging the gap between simplified RL outputs and complex real-world dynamics. This confirms the robustness of the combined RL-optimization approach for real-world deployment.

2. **Morphology Selection Towards Different Scenarios:**
 - The VLM-based morphology selector was evaluated in ten manipulation and ten locomotion scenarios in both simulated and real environments.
 - Results (Fig. 6) indicate a high average success rate in determining the optimal manipulation (single-arm vs. dual-arm) and locomotion (wheeled vs. legged) modes.
 - Crucially, integrating spatial geometric information (from depth sensors) significantly improved the selector's accuracy and robustness, especially for complex locomotion tasks involving obstacles and for adapting to varied manipulation scenarios, compared to using only 2D image input. This highlights the importance of grounding knowledge with rich spatial data.

3. **Loco-manipulation Tasks with Language Model Planner:**
 - **Tasks with Human Instructions:** The LLM-based planner was tested on four representative loco-manipulation tasks ("Open the drawer and pick up the drill," "Find the door and open it," "Pick up the cracker and put it into the box," "Pick up the box and put it on the table").
 - The framework achieved a desired success rate (≥ 60%) on the real robot (Fig. 8), demonstrating that the LLM could effectively plan semantic instructions into sequences of learned skills.
 - Analysis of failure modes revealed that increased task complexity and the invocation of multiple functional modules (e.g., both locomotion and manipulation morphology selectors) could increase planning difficulty. Execution errors often arose from intricate dynamic constraints or misalignments between floating sensing and robot execution.
 - **Long-horizon Task:** A complex long-horizon task involving navigation, object identification (using AprilTags), and multi-step manipulation was successfully demonstrated (Fig. 1, 7). The framework synthesized sequences of motion primitives, accurately inferred semantic logic, and autonomously selected appropriate locomotion and manipulation morphologies based on the environment and task state. This qualitative result underscores the framework's versatility and adaptability for complex tasks, surpassing many existing methods limited to shorter horizons or less unstructured environments.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

HYPERmotion represents a significant advancement towards truly autonomous and intelligent humanoid robots. Its primary significance lies in providing a **comprehensive, hierarchical framework** that effectively bridges the gap between high-level human language commands and complex, low-level whole-body control for high-DoF humanoid robots in the real world.

**Key Contributions and Significance:**

- **Holistic Approach:** The framework integrates learning, planning, and morphology selection, offering a more complete solution for humanoid autonomy than previous works often focused on isolated aspects.
- **Bridging Abstraction Levels:** By combining LLMs/VLMs for semantic understanding and planning with RL for skill acquisition and whole-body optimization for dynamic feasibility, HYPERmotion successfully translates abstract instructions into concrete, safe, and effective robot actions.
- **Real-world Embodiment:** A critical achievement is the successful deployment and validation of the framework on a complex, high-DoF centaur-like humanoid robot in real-world scenarios. This addresses a major challenge in robotics—sim-to-real transfer for sophisticated behaviors.
- **Intelligent Adaptability:** The morphology selector, enhanced by distilled spatial geometry from onboard sensors, allows the robot to adapt its physical actions (e.g., using wheels vs. legs, single vs. dual arm) to the specific task and environmental context, improving robustness and versatility.
- **Zero-Shot Planning:** The LLM-driven planning, leveraging a modular skill library and Behavior Trees, enables the robot to execute new, complex tasks from open-ended verbal instructions without requiring explicit demonstrations or extensive retraining for each new scenario.

**Potential Impact:**

- **Expanding Humanoid Applications:** HYPERmotion's capabilities could unlock new applications for humanoid robots in domains requiring complex loco-manipulation, such as industrial material handling, logistics, assistive robotics in homes, disaster response, and exploration in hazardous environments.
- **Accelerating Robot Development:** The modular design of skill learning and planning could streamline the development and integration of new robot behaviors, allowing researchers and engineers to build upon existing primitives rather than starting from scratch for every new task.
- **Advancing Embodied AI:** This work contributes significantly to the broader goal of embodied artificial intelligence, demonstrating how robots can understand, reason, and act in the physical world in a more human-like, adaptive manner.

**Limitations and Future Work:**

Despite its achievements, the authors acknowledge several limitations:
- **Limited Motion Library:** The current size of the motion library restricts the range and complexity of tasks the robot can perform.
- **Generalization of Skills:** Learning new skills often requires separate training optimization, hindering the generalization of existing actions to novel scenarios.
- **Robustness to Disturbances:** The system currently struggles with handling unexpected external disturbances and collisions, which is crucial for real-world safety and reliability.
- **Lack of Real-time Interaction and Re-planning:** The framework does not yet support real-time linguistic interaction during task execution or dynamic re-planning in response to unforeseen events or task changes.

Future work will focus on enriching the robot's action skills to expand the motion library, enhancing the LLM's dynamic planning ability for more reactive and flexible execution, and improving robot navigation and perception capabilities to achieve robust closed-loop humanoid motions and safe human-robot collaboration. Addressing these limitations will be key to realizing the full potential of autonomous humanoid agents in diverse and dynamic environments.
