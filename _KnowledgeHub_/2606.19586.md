# One Demo is Worth a Thousand Trajectories: Action-View Augmentation for Visuomotor Policies

> [!summary] Summary
> 1001 DEMOS is an offline data-augmentation framework that turns a single eye-in-hand fisheye demonstration into thousands of training episodes. It reconstructs the scene as a fisheye-adapted 3D Gaussian Splatting model plus point cloud, generates smooth collision-free trajectories via trajectory optimization, and renders matching novel fisheye views. Augmented policies gain 55-56% success-rate improvement and reach 100% obstacle-avoidance success in the real world.

## Problem

- Visuomotor policies break under small shifts in initial robot configuration or unseen obstacles, since such observations fall out of distribution and cascade into compounded execution failures.
- Covering spatial variation demands humans repeat the same skill many times under different configurations, a tedious and costly manual collection process that scales poorly.
- Image-editing augmentation changes appearance without adjusting actions, and state-based methods defer visual realism to state estimators and learned or simulated dynamics models.

## Method

- ==Fisheye 3D Gaussian Splatting== replaces the pinhole ray sampler with a ==KB8 fisheye sampler==, retiling rays into 256-per-tile CUDA blocks to keep fast rasterization under wide-FoV distortion.
- ==Trajectory optimization== combines a ==delta funnel loss== converging to the original pre-contact pose, a ==TSDF collision loss==, a ==render loss== keeping poses near training views, and a ==smoothness term==.
- ==Contact-aware augmentation== splits demos at the first gripper-point-cloud collision frame, augmenting only pre-contact motion; obstacles sampled from Objaverse seed ==RRT*== initial guesses for collision-free paths.

## Results

- On RoboMimic square with fisheye wrist views, augmentation raised average success rate **56%** over no augmentation, trailing the ground-truth-rendering oracle by only **8-11%**.
- Against baselines, the method beat action-only perturbation by up to **35%** and single-step NeRF-based corrective augmentation by **15%** average, peaking at **18%**.
- Real-world cup serving: free-space augmentation gained **55%** over no augmentation; obstacle augmentation hit **100%** versus **10%** and **5%**, and **10/10** on challenging obstacles where baselines scored **0**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Augmenting whole trajectories rather than single steps supplies recovery behaviors, so policies acquire skills such as obstacle avoidance never present in the original human demonstrations.
> - With eye-in-hand cameras, perturbing actions without re-rendering views destroys action-view consistency, because tiny end-effector pose changes cause drastic visual shifts.
> - Augmentation diversity trades off against render fidelity: success plateaued at a 50 degree viewpoint cone, beyond which sparse view coverage degrades reconstruction quality.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated within the field of robot learning, specifically focusing on visuomotor policies trained through imitation learning. While these policies have demonstrated potential in modeling complex robotic behaviors, a significant limitation is their fragility to minor environmental variations. Small changes in the robot’s initial configuration or the presence of unseen objects can lead to out-of-distribution (OOD) observations, which in turn cause compounding execution errors and task failures. Historically, improving the spatial robustness of these policies has required extensive, manual data collection, involving numerous demonstrations of the same skill under varying spatial configurations. This process is both costly and labor-intensive.

The paper addresses these challenges by introducing a data augmentation framework designed to enhance the spatial robustness of visuomotor policies. Data augmentation is a standard practice in other domains, such as computer vision, for improving model generalization. However, applying data augmentation to real-world robot manipulation data presents unique difficulties:
- **Action-View Consistency**: Robot learning requires paired observation and action data. Augmentation must generate diverse visuals and actions while ensuring their consistency.
- **Physical Constraints**: Augmented action data must adhere to physical laws, including 3D collision avoidance and appropriate object contact dynamics.
- **Visual Coverage from Limited Data**: Real-world robot demonstrations are often limited in viewpoint coverage, necessitating methods to maximize the utility of each collected trajectory.

Previous work in image augmentation for visual invariance has explored techniques like color jittering, image filtering, and generative models for object modifications. However, these methods typically alter global appearance or minor viewpoints without corresponding robot trajectory adjustments, limiting their applicability to policies requiring action-view consistency. State-based data augmentation techniques, which inject noise into keypoint-based representations or leverage learned dynamics, often rely on accurate state estimation or simulation models. These approaches defer visual invariance to state estimation and depend on a dynamics model for physical consistency.

More directly related are visual-action augmentation methods. Some prior work generates third-person pinhole augmentations in simulation or real-world via novel-view/3D configuration synthesis. Other approaches adapt egocentric pinhole observations using neural radiance fields (NeRFs) or 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) with model predictive control (MPC). However, these often suffer from limitations such as being restricted to single-step augmentation, inability to handle wide Field-of-View (FoV) cameras, or failure to produce trajectory-level, obstacle-avoiding data. Concurrent works, while enabling trajectory-level visual-action augmentation, are typically restricted to demonstrations from static, third-person, pinhole cameras or rely on point cloud inputs.

The current work distinguishes itself by integrating a novel fisheye ray sampler into a 3D Gaussian Splatting formulation. This adaptation allows it to gracefully handle wide FoV fisheye cameras commonly used in eye-in-hand robotic setups, while preserving the speed and editability inherent to 3DGS. By doing so, the framework generates trajectory-level, obstacle-avoiding data for eye-in-hand observations, a capability that aims to produce visuomotor policies robust across diverse camera viewpoints and capable of complex collision avoidance behaviors.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the spatial robustness of visuomotor policies for robotic manipulation. This is motivated by the observed brittleness of existing policies, which are prone to failure when faced with minor deviations from their training data distribution. Specifically, changes in the robot’s initial configuration or the introduction of unseen obstacles can lead to out-of-distribution observations, resulting in task failures.

The current method for improving policy robustness, which involves human demonstrators repeatedly executing tasks under varied spatial conditions, is described as tedious and expensive. The research aims to overcome this limitation by developing an automated data augmentation framework. This framework seeks to generate additional, high-quality real-world robot trajectory data from a limited set of existing human demonstrations, thereby expanding the spatial coverage of the training data without the need for exhaustive manual collection.

To achieve this overarching goal, the authors identified and addressed three specific challenges intrinsic to augmenting real-world robot manipulation data:

1. **Maintaining Action-View Consistency**: Robot policies require synchronized observation and action pairs. Therefore, any data augmentation must not only increase visual and action diversity but also critically ensure that the generated visual observations align physically and temporally with the corresponding augmented action trajectories.
2. **Respecting Physical Constraints of Actions**: Augmented action data cannot be arbitrarily generated. It must adhere to real-world physical constraints, including the avoidance of 3D collisions with the environment and objects, and accurately reflecting object contact dynamics (e.g., only augmenting pre-contact phases to preserve original contact behaviors).
3. **Maximizing Visual Coverage from Limited Demonstrations**: Given that real-world robot demonstration data is inherently limited, the framework must effectively leverage each collected trajectory. This means maximizing the visual information extracted and utilized from a given number of views during data collection to enable extensive and diverse augmentations.

To address these challenges, the authors propose "1001 DEMOS," a data augmentation framework featuring a trajectory-level action-view augmentation algorithm. This algorithm reconstructs the 3D scene, generates physically feasible and collision-free action trajectories via optimization, and then renders spatially matching, visually realistic observations using an editable 3D Gaussian Splatting model.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The "1001 DEMOS" framework is an offline data augmentation system designed for visuomotor policies. It operates on real-world eye-in-hand demonstrations captured with a portable parallel gripper equipped with a single fisheye camera. The system generates thousands of visually realistic image sequences and corresponding physically feasible action trajectories from a limited number of initial demonstrations.

The overall workflow, as illustrated in Fig. 2 of the paper, consists of three main modules:

#### 3.1. 3D Scene Reconstruction from Eye-in-Hand Fisheye Video

The initial step involves reconstructing the 3D environment from fisheye video sequences.
- **Data Collection Protocol**: The system utilizes the UMI framework for data collection, leveraging an iPhone for capturing fisheye videos. ARKit VIO provides metric 6D end-effector poses, and gripper-opening width is measured via fiducial markers. Each dataset episode consists of sequences of eye-in-hand fisheye RGB images and corresponding 6D end-effector poses (a_ee) and gripper-opening widths (a_gp).
- **Scene Point Cloud Reconstruction & Contact Detection**: COLMAP is employed to reconstruct high-fidelity 3D point clouds from the fisheye image sequences captured during an initial scene scanning phase. For each demonstration, contact events are detected by identifying the first frame where the gripper exceeds a collision threshold with the reconstructed point cloud, allowing the separation of pre-contact and post-contact phases. Augmentation is primarily performed during the pre-contact phase to preserve original contact dynamics.
- **Fisheye 3D Gaussians**: The framework adapts the 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) formulation to handle wide FoV fisheye cameras. The standard 3DGS ray sampler is replaced with a KB8-based fisheye ray sampler. For each fisheye pixel, its ray direction is computed and then projected through the camera intrinsics to obtain pinhole coordinates, effectively mapping fisheye rays to 3D-Gaussian splat locations on a 2D image plane. Tile assignments for GPU rasterization are redistributed from pinhole to fisheye rays, maintaining the CUDA block-thread structure for fast rendering while accurately modeling fisheye distortion. The fisheye 3DGS is optimized using pixel-wise losses between rendered and ground-truth fisheye images.

#### 3.2. Action Generation via Trajectory Optimization

Utilizing the reconstructed 3D scene point cloud and the original demonstration trajectory, the system generates diverse novel action trajectories. This module produces two types of augmented trajectories:
- **Free-Space Trajectory Generation**: Trajectories are generated within the existing scene but start from different initial camera poses, which are sampled randomly in the free space.
- **Obstacle-Avoiding Trajectory Generation**: Trajectories are planned to navigate around augmented obstacles introduced into the scene.
- **Trajectory Optimization Formulation**: Trajectory generation is framed as an optimization problem to minimize a composite loss function:
 `argmin_{{x_k}} (L_funnel(X, O_ee) + L_collision(X, tsdf(P_scene)) + L_render(X, O_ee) + L_smooth(X))`
 subject to `x_1 = x_init`, `x_T = x_goal`, and `X ∩ convhull(P_obstacle) = ∅`.
 - `L_funnel`: A delta funnel loss is used to ensure new trajectories converge consistently to the same pre-contact pose of the original demonstration, preserving the original contact dynamics. This involves temporally dependent weights.
 - `L_render`: This loss optimizes each generated pose `x_k` to remain close to the 6D pose distribution of the original demonstration and scanning views. This is critical for mitigating rendering artifacts (floaters, blurriness) that can occur in 3DGS when rendering viewpoints deviate significantly from the training distribution.
 - `L_collision`: This loss penalizes collisions with the environment, using a Truncated Signed Distance Function (TSDF) of the scene point cloud.
 - `L_smooth`: This term penalizes velocity jerkiness to ensure the generated trajectories are kinematically smooth.
- **Initialization for Trajectory Optimization**:
 - For free-space augmentation, the initial guess trajectory is a linear interpolation between the newly sampled start pose (`x_init`) and the pre-contact goal pose (`x_goal`).
 - For obstacle augmentation, obstacles (sampled from Objaverse) are added to the scene, and their convex hull updates the scene's TSDF. An RRT* algorithm is used to generate an initial guess trajectory that connects `x_init` and `x_goal` while avoiding the added obstacle. This RRT*-generated path serves as the initialization for the subsequent trajectory optimization, which then explicitly includes the collision constraint `X ∩ convhull(P_obstacle) = ∅`.

#### 3.3. View Generation via Fisheye 3D Gaussian Splatting

The generated action trajectories are paired with corresponding visual observations.
- **Free-Space View Generation**: For free-space trajectories, the trained Fisheye 3DGS model of the scene renders the visual observations from the optimized novel camera poses.
- **Obstacle-Scene View Generation**: To create obstacle-aware scenes, the original Fisheye 3DGS scene is augmented by integrating a separately trained Fisheye 3DGS model of an obstacle (obtained from Objaverse). The corresponding visual fisheye images are then rendered from the obstacle-avoiding trajectories within these augmented scenes.

#### 3.4. Action-View Augmentation for Visuomotor Policies

- **Policy Training**: A Diffusion Policy is trained on a combined dataset comprising the original human demonstrations (D) and the newly generated augmented data ($\tilde{D}$). A CLIP-pretrained ViT-B/16 encoder with a relative action representation is utilized for visual processing.
- **Data Compilation**: For augmented data, fisheye observations are segmented by SAM2, and the robot gripper is overlaid onto the rendered images. The trajectory-optimized 6D poses serve as the augmented end-effector actions, while the original gripper-opening width is retained.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted in both simulation and real-world environments addressed three core questions regarding the effectiveness and implementation of the "1001 DEMOS" framework.

#### 4.1. Does Action-View Augmentation Help?

- **Improved Robustness and Sample Efficiency in Free Space**:
 - **Simulation (RoboMimic "square" task)**: When evaluated on a peg-in-hole task, policies trained with "1001 DEMOS" free-space augmentation demonstrated an average of 56% improvement in task success rate compared to policies trained without any augmentation (referred to as "No Aug"). The performance of the augmented policies closely tracked an "oracle" policy that used ground-truth novel-view rendering (only possible in simulation), showing an 8-11% performance gap.
 - **Real-world (Cup serving task)**: In a real-world cup serving task, the "FreeSpace Aug" policy exhibited a 55% performance boost over the "No Aug" policy, validating the benefits of augmentation for handling out-of-distribution (OOD) initial robot and object states.

- **Enhanced Obstacle Avoidance Capabilities**:
 - **Real-world (Cup serving task with obstacles)**: The "Obstacle Aug" policy, trained with free-space and obstacle-distractor augmentations, achieved a 100% success rate when required to avoid obstacles. This significantly outperformed the "FreeSpace Aug" policy (10% success rate) and the "No Aug" policy (5% success rate) in scenarios with unseen obstacles. This indicates that the framework successfully imbues visuomotor policies with collision avoidance behaviors not present in the original obstacle-free demonstrations.
 - **Challenging Obstacles (Appendix A.2)**: In a harder set of obstacle scenarios involving more complex placements and shapes, the "Obstacle Aug" policy maintained a 100% success rate across 10 trials, while both "No Aug" and "FreeSpace Aug" policies failed completely (0% success).

#### 4.2. How to Augment? (Comparison to Baselines)

- **Comparison to Action-Only Augmentation**:
 - The "Aug Action Only" baseline, which perturbs only proprioceptive and gripper actions while retaining original visual data, showed a 29% average success rate improvement over "No Aug" in simulation. However, this approach is less effective for eye-in-hand observations where small end-effector pose changes lead to significant visual shifts. "1001 DEMOS" outperformed "Aug Action Only" by up to 35%, especially in high-data regimes, highlighting the importance of joint action-view augmentation.

- **Comparison to Single-Step Augmentation**:
 - The "SPARTN" baseline, which performs single-step visual-action augmentation using NeRF, improved policies' performance for OOD camera views by 41% over "No Aug" in simulation. However, its single-step nature limits the generation of smooth, trajectory-level collision-avoidance behaviors. "1001 DEMOS," with its trajectory-level augmentation, outperformed "SPARTN" by an average of 15% (peaking at 18%), indicating the benefit of generating full, consistent trajectories for learning robust behaviors.

#### 4.3. How Much to Augment?

- **Optimal Augmentation Range**: The study investigated the trade-off between increasing data diversity (achieved by larger rotation bounds for initial pose sampling) and maintaining rendering quality (which can degrade with viewpoints too far from the training distribution). For the RoboMimic "square" task, it was found that success rates plateaued at a 50-degree quaternion cone rotation bound for initial camera orientations. This indicated an optimal balance, and this parameter was subsequently adopted for other experiments.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "1001 DEMOS" framework represents a significant step in addressing a fundamental challenge in robotic manipulation: the brittleness of visuomotor policies to out-of-distribution observations. By providing an automated and effective data augmentation method, this work has several implications:

- **Enhanced Spatial Robustness**: The primary impact is enabling robots to handle variations in initial configurations and the presence of unforeseen obstacles without requiring exhaustive manual demonstrations. This directly translates to more reliable and deployable robotic systems in real-world environments.
- **Improved Data Efficiency**: The framework demonstrates that complex robotic behaviors, including obstacle avoidance, can be learned from a limited set of human demonstrations. By synthesizing thousands of diverse, physically feasible, and visually realistic augmented trajectories, the need for costly and time-consuming manual data collection is substantially reduced. This could democratize access to advanced robot learning by lowering data acquisition barriers.
- **Advancements in 3D Scene Representation for Robotics**: The novel adaptation of 3D Gaussian Splatting for wide FoV fisheye cameras is a technical contribution. This allows for fast, high-fidelity novel view synthesis from eye-in-hand cameras, which are common in robotic setups, while maintaining the editability essential for introducing virtual obstacles.
- **Enabling Novel Robot Capabilities**: The ability to generate obstacle-avoiding trajectories and corresponding views allows policies to acquire behaviors (such as collision avoidance) that were entirely absent from the initial, obstacle-free human demonstrations. This expands the scope of skills robots can learn through imitation.
- **Foundation for Future Research**: The identified limitations, such as the view coverage of single moving cameras for dynamic scenes and the multi-view inconsistency of 3DGS, suggest clear avenues for future work. Integrating multi-camera rigs, ToF sensors, or more advanced dynamic reconstruction methods, alongside exploring view-consistent representations like 2DGS, could further enhance the framework's capabilities. Additionally, incorporating downstream robot kinematic constraints into trajectory optimization could lead to embodiment-aware skill transfer.

Overall, this research contributes to making visuomotor policies more robust, data-efficient, and capable, thereby accelerating the deployment of autonomous manipulation systems in complex and dynamic real-world settings.
