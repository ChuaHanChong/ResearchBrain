# Rhythm: Learning Interactive Whole-Body Control for Dual Humanoids

> [!summary] Summary
> The Rhythm framework enables physically coupled, interactive whole-body control for dual-humanoid systems in real-world environments. It achieves robust interaction capabilities on physical Unitree G1 humanoids across diverse tasks, supported by novel data generation and reinforcement learning techniques.

## Problem

- Extending single-humanoid whole-body control capabilities to robust, physically coupled multi-humanoid interactions in real-world scenarios.
- Addressing the scarcity of feasible interaction data for humanoids, as direct retargeting of human-human interaction data often leads to kinematic conflicts due to morphological differences.
- Mastering complex coupled dynamics between two active agents and bridging the sim-to-real gap for multi-humanoid systems, which involves challenges like global vs. ego-centric observability and synchronization.

## Method

- **Rhythm** combines **Interaction-Aware Motion Retargeting (IAMR)**: a decoupled optimization of individual and unified manifolds to generate kinematically feasible and topologically consistent interaction references for humanoids from human demonstration data.
- **Interaction-Guided Reinforcement Learning (IGRL)**: a multi-agent reinforcement learning module based on ==MAPPO==, which learns coupled dynamics through ==graph-based rewards== (interaction graph, contact graph) and robust training strategies including curriculum-based adaptive sampling and dual-agent domain randomization.
- A Real-World Deployment System manages state estimation via ==LiDAR-fused localization== (POINT-LIO, GICP, Kalman Filter) and ensures inter-agent synchronization using a soft proportional feedback mechanism.

## Results

- IAMR achieved **0%** inter-penetration and superior contact fidelity (e.g., F1-Strict **0.932** in Intensive Contact) compared to baselines, generating physically feasible and high-utility references for policy learning.
- The full IGRL policy demonstrated robust physical coupling with high interaction success rates (above **75%**) and contact success rates (above **77%**) across diverse scenarios in simulation, outperforming ablated variants.
- The Rhythm framework achieved real-world success rates up to **86.7%** in complex tasks like 'Hug' on Unitree G1 humanoids and exhibited resilience to aggressive external disturbances, maintaining interaction topology and balance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Resolving kinematic conflicts in motion retargeting requires adaptively decoupling self-motion and interaction constraints to simultaneously preserve individual motion style and precise interaction geometry for humanoids.
> - Explicitly modeling both geometric interaction topology and physical contact states through graph-based rewards in reinforcement learning is crucial for mastering complex, physically coupled dynamics in multi-humanoid systems.
> - Robust real-world deployment for multi-humanoid interaction necessitates reliable relative state estimation and an adaptive inter-agent synchronization mechanism to effectively bridge the sim-to-real gap.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of humanoid robotics has made progress in single-agent capabilities, including dynamic locomotion and whole-body control. This research addresses the challenge of extending these capabilities to multi-humanoid systems, specifically focusing on physically coupled interactions.

Current research in multi-agent interaction is often limited. In computer graphics, physics-based animation can simulate multi-character interactions, but these methods frequently prioritize visual fidelity over the strict physical constraints necessary for real-world robotic deployment. In robotics, while human–robot collaboration and competitive sports have been explored, these scenarios typically involve a compliant human partner or passive objects. Explicit multi-humanoid interaction remains largely confined to virtual environments or simulation-only validations. Consequently, achieving robust, physically coupled whole-body control on real multi-humanoid hardware represents a significant gap.

Two primary challenges hinder the transition of dual-humanoid systems from simulation to reality: the scarcity of feasible interaction data and the complexity of the training-to-deployment paradigm. Human-human interaction data often leads to kinematic conflicts when transferred to robots due to morphological differences, and standard retargeting methods may not preserve both individual motion style and precise interaction geometry. Furthermore, existing tracking policies typically treat agents in isolation, failing to model intricate coupled dynamics. The disparity between global observability in simulation and the asynchronous, ego-centric, partially observable reality of physical hardware also poses a challenge for deployment. This work aims to bridge these gaps by providing a unified framework for real-world dual-humanoid interaction.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to introduce Rhythm, a unified framework for achieving interactive whole-body control for dual-humanoid systems, enabling their deployment for complex, physically plausible interactions in real-world scenarios.

The motivation for this work stems from the limitations observed in the current state of humanoid robotics:

1. **Enabling Complex Collaboration:** While individual humanoids have become more agile and robust, their potential for complex collaborative tasks in shared environments remains largely untapped. Realizing physically coupled multi-humanoid interaction is considered a critical step towards advanced embodied intelligence beyond isolated agents.
2. **Addressing Data Scarcity and Kinematic Mismatches:** Existing human-human interaction (HHI) datasets, while rich in motion information, present significant challenges for direct transfer to robots. Morphological differences between humans and humanoids cause kinematic conflicts, where a motion suitable for a human might lead to physically impossible or unnatural postures for a robot. Standard motion retargeting methods often fail to simultaneously preserve the robot's individual motion style and the precise interaction geometry, leading to suboptimal or infeasible motion references. The objective is to develop a method that can generate feasible and consistent interaction references for humanoids from human data.
3. **Mastering Coupled Dynamics:** Policies trained for individual robot control typically do not account for the intricate, mutually influencing dynamics inherent in close physical interaction between two active agents. These coupled dynamics require a learning approach that explicitly models the relationship and potential contact between robots. The goal is to develop a reinforcement learning policy capable of mastering these complex, coupled dynamics.
4. **Bridging the Sim-to-Real Gap for Multi-Humanoid Systems:** Deploying multi-humanoid interaction policies from simulation to real-world hardware introduces several challenges. Simulation environments often provide global, perfect state observability and synchronous execution, which contrast with the noisy, ego-centric, and asynchronous nature of real-world robotic systems. This disparity makes robust real-world deployment difficult. The objective is to develop a system that can account for these real-world limitations, including state estimation and inter-agent synchronization, to ensure stable transfer.
5. **Establishing a Unified Framework:** The work aims to integrate solutions for these distinct challenges—data generation, coupled dynamics learning, and real-world deployment—into a single, coherent framework. This unified approach is intended to provide a comprehensive solution for multi-humanoid interaction, moving beyond ad-hoc or isolated solutions.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Rhythm is a unified framework composed of three integrated modules: Interaction-Aware Motion Retargeting (IAMR), Interaction-Guided Reinforcement Learning (IGRL), and a Real-World Deployment system.

#### 3.1. Interaction-Aware Motion Retargeting (IAMR)

IAMR addresses the challenge of generating kinematically feasible and topologically consistent interaction references for humanoids from human demonstration data, resolving kinematic conflicts arising from morphological differences.

- **Problem Formulation:** The goal is to synthesize trajectories for two humanoids that preserve individual motion style and dense interaction geometry. The core issue is that a single reference manifold cannot simultaneously satisfy both self-motion constraints (e.g., limb proportions, ground contact) and interaction constraints (e.g., precise hand-holding distance) due to embodiment mismatch.
- **Interaction Mesh and Laplacian Coordinates:** The framework uses a volumetric Interaction Mesh to model the coupled multi-agent system as a graph. Laplacian coordinates encode local geometric details.
- **Topological Partitioning:** To resolve kinematic conflicts, the system's graph edges are partitioned into:
 - **Intra-Agent Edges (E_self):** Connections within a single robot, encoding self-motion topology. These align with an **Individual Manifold (M_ind)**, constructed by scaling each human with individual ratios to match robot height. This preserves valid self-motion Laplacians.
 - **Inter-Agent Edges (E_inter):** Connections between key joints of the two robots, encoding relative interaction topology. These align with a **Unified Manifold (M_uni)**, constructed by applying a single global scale to the entire scene. This preserves relative interaction edges.
- **Decoupled Optimization:** A hybrid energy function is minimized to find the optimal joint configuration.
 - **Self-Motion Objective (E_self):** Aligns the Laplacian geometry of each robot to its M_ind, preserving individual motion quality.
 - **Interaction Objective (E_inter):** Treats inter-agent edges as extrinsic constraints driven by M_uni, enforcing relative interaction geometry. A distance-aware dynamic stiffness function (ω_ij) is used, which stiffens during close contact to prevent penetration and becomes compliant at a distance.
 - **Constraints:** Joint limits, collision avoidance (using signed distance fields), foot contact (zero-velocity constraint), and trust region for step size.
- **Topological Interaction Priors:** IAMR extracts cross-agent graph representations: an Interaction Graph (from E_inter) for binary connectivity, and a Contact Graph (via collision detection) for binary physical contact states. These graphs serve as interaction priors for subsequent policy learning.
- **MAGIC Dataset:** A high-fidelity motion capture dataset (∼3 hours) covering diverse interaction categories (Coordinated, Intimate/Care, Contact, Social Rituals, Competitive) was created to address the lack of clean human-human interaction data suitable for robot transfer. Both raw motion capture and IAMR-retargeted humanoid trajectories will be released.

#### 3.2. Interaction-Guided Reinforcement Learning (IGRL)

IGRL is a multi-agent reinforcement learning module designed to master the coupled dynamics of dual-humanoid interaction, leveraging graph-based rewards and robust training strategies.

- **Multi-Agent Policy Design:** The problem is formulated as a Multi-Agent Markov Decision Process (MA-MDP), using the Centralized Training with Decentralized Execution (CTDE) paradigm via MAPPO.
 - **Interaction-Centric Observation:** The policy receives a compact observation space (o_t) including:
 - **Proprioception (o_prop):** Agent's internal state (joint positions, velocities, base angular velocities, previous action).
 - **Peer Perception (o_peer):** Peer's state relative to the ego agent (joint positions, relative root transform).
 - **Reference Motion (o_ref):** Future reference trajectories and relative states.
 - **Network Architecture:** A hierarchical policy architecture uses 1D-CNN temporal encoders for historical observations and future references, feeding latent features into an MLP action decoder.
- **Graph-based Rewards:** These rewards translate the topological interaction priors from IAMR into learning signals.
 - **Interaction Graph Reward (r_inter):** Penalizes deviations between simulated and reference interaction edges, using the dynamic weights from IAMR to prioritize critical geometry.
 - **Contact Graph Reward (r_contact):** Regularizes physical interaction with two goals: Contact Consistency (penalizing mismatches between simulated and reference contact states) and Force Regularization (constraining contact forces to realistic ranges when active, penalizing non-zero forces during non-contact). This explicitly discourages interpenetration and encourages compliant interaction.
- **Robust Training Strategy:**
 - **Curriculum-based Adaptive Sampling:** An error-aware sampling strategy is used, based on a multi-objective landscape combining failure, tracking, and interaction metrics. The curriculum dynamically shifts focus from stability to tracking and interaction precision as the policy matures.
 - **Dual-Agent Domain Randomization:** To bridge the sim-to-real gap, wireless latency is simulated via noisy, delayed peer observations, and initial state perturbations are applied to enforce recovery from physical misalignment.

#### 3.3. Real-World Deployment

The deployment system addresses the Sim-to-Real Gap by managing state estimation and ensuring inter-agent synchronization.

- **State Estimation and Relative Localization:** A robust localization system is employed, fusing POINT-LIO (for high-frequency local odometry) and GICP (registering real-time point clouds against a pre-built map for global positioning). A Kalman Filter fuses these estimates. Robots broadcast global poses, allowing ego-agents to derive consistent peer perceptions.
- **Inter-Agent Synchronization:** A soft synchronization mechanism is implemented based on proportional feedback. Agents exchange their motion phase (ϕ), and the ego agent dynamically modulates its phase progression rate (˙ϕ_ego = 1.0 + k(ϕ_peer − ϕ_ego)) to maintain temporal alignment without hard resets.
- **Hardware and Control:** The system is validated on Unitree G1 humanoids. All computations are performed onboard using ONNX Runtime for policy inference (less than 3ms latency at 50Hz control loop). A high-level policy (50Hz) computes desired joint positions, which are translated into motor torques by a low-level PD controller (500Hz).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments were designed to evaluate IAMR's retargeting quality (Q1), IGRL's policy efficacy (Q2), and the framework's real-world robustness (Q3).

#### 4.1. Retargeting Quality (Q1)

IAMR was evaluated against baselines (GMR, OR, DOR) on the MAGIC and Inter-X datasets using metrics for safety (Inter-Penetration Rate - IPR, Max Penetration Depth - MPD), fidelity (Interaction Edge Error - IEE, Contact F1 Score), and utility (Downstream Success Rate - DSR).

- **Safety:** IAMR achieved 0% IPR and 0 cm MPD across all interaction categories, strictly eliminating penetration. In contrast, isolated baselines (GMR, OR) exhibited significant IPR (e.g., OR 47.3% in Intensive Contact), rendering motions physically infeasible.
- **Fidelity:** IAMR demonstrated superior contact fidelity, achieving the highest F1-Strict scores (e.g., 0.932 in Intensive Contact on MAGIC, 0.843 on Inter-X). The coupled baseline DOR, while safe, compromised fidelity under anthropometric mismatch (F1-Strict 0.589 on Inter-X). IAMR's adaptive decoupling balanced self-motion and interaction objectives effectively.
- **Utility:** IAMR showed superior downstream utility, with a DSR of 78.3% in contact-rich tasks, indicating that its generated references are highly suitable for policy learning.
- **Qualitative Observations:** On the Inter-X dataset, IAMR successfully preserved precise interaction geometry, preventing "air handshakes" in transient contacts and avoiding penetration or unnatural stiffness in continuous contacts (like "Hugs"), which were observed in baselines.

#### 4.2. Policy Efficacy (Q2)

IGRL was assessed against ablated variants (Single Agent, w/o Peer Obs, w/o Contact Rew, w/o Interaction Rew) using metrics for interaction performance (Interaction Edge Error - IEE, Interaction Success Rate - ISR) and contact performance (Contact Success Rate - CSR, Contact Error Rate - CER).

- **Interaction Awareness:** The "Single Agent" baseline, lacking interaction awareness, failed to coordinate effectively (e.g., 18.7% ISR in Collaborate tasks). Removing peer observations ("w/o Peer Obs") also led to physical coupling collapse (e.g., CSR dropped to 18.6% in Light Contact), confirming the necessity of explicit inter-agent perception.
- **Functional Hierarchy of Rewards:**
 - **Interaction Reward:** The interaction reward was found to be foundational. Removing it ("w/o Interaction Rew") significantly degraded performance across all metrics (e.g., ISR dropped to 58.1% in Collaborate, CSR to 28.1% in Light Contact), as agents failed to establish spatial proximity for interaction.
 - **Contact Reward:** While the interaction reward established spatial proximity, the contact reward was critical for physical realism. The "w/o Contact Rew" variant achieved high geometric precision (e.g., 93.4% ISR in Collaborate) but exhibited a "ghosting" phenomenon, failing to maintain valid physical contact (e.g., 52.1% CSR in Light Contact). This indicated that policies could exploit interpenetration to minimize geometric error without true physical coupling.
- **Full IGRL Performance:** The full IGRL method integrated these components effectively, achieving robust physical coupling with high ISR (above 75%) and CSR (above 77%) across diverse scenarios.
- **Qualitative Observations:** In tasks like "Greeting," the "Single Agent" drifted into collisions. "w/o Contact Rew" maintained geometric alignment but allowed "ghosting" of hands. The full method enforced valid physical contact while maintaining precision, aligning with quantitative results.

#### 4.3. Real-World Robustness (Q3)

The Rhythm framework was deployed on Unitree G1 humanoids to evaluate generality, quantitative success rates, and robustness to disturbances.

- **Framework Generality:** The system demonstrated versatility across diverse interaction modalities, successfully handling contact-rich tasks (e.g., Hug, Shoulder-to-Shoulder) by preserving fine-grained contact geometry, and coordinated interactions (e.g., synchronized dancing) by maintaining spatiotemporal coherence.
- **Quantitative Success Rate:** Rhythm consistently outperformed the "Single Agent" baseline in real-world trials. For instance, in the "Hug" task, Rhythm achieved an 86.7% success rate compared to 26.7% for the baseline. In the "Greeting" task, Rhythm achieved 82.2% success, significantly higher than the baseline's 12.2%, demonstrating its ability to compensate for accumulated drift in long-horizon tasks.
- **Robustness to Disturbances:** During physical execution, the robots exhibited resilience to aggressive external perturbations, such as pushing, pulling, and kicking. The agents successfully recovered balance and actively adjusted their relative states to restore interaction topology, indicating that the policy learned robust closed-loop synchronization and real-time recovery strategies.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents Rhythm, a unified framework that facilitates physically coupled interactive behaviors for dual-humanoid systems in real-world environments. This work addresses several fundamental challenges in multi-robot interaction and contributes to the broader field of embodied intelligence.

The significance of this work can be understood through its contributions:

1. **First Robust Real-World Dual-Humanoid Interaction:** The framework is reported to achieve the first successful and robust transfer of complex, physically coupled interactive behaviors to physical dual-humanoid hardware. This advances the state of the art beyond simulation-only validations or simplified human-robot interaction scenarios.
2. **Addressing Kinematic Conflicts in Data Generation:** The Interaction-Aware Motion Retargeting (IAMR) module provides a method to generate kinematically feasible and topologically consistent humanoid-humanoid interaction references from heterogeneous human data. This resolves a critical data scarcity issue for multi-humanoid systems by making existing human motion capture data usable for robots despite morphological differences.
3. **Mastering Coupled Dynamics with Graph-based Rewards:** The Interaction-Guided Reinforcement Learning (IGRL) policy effectively learns complex coupled dynamics by incorporating graph-based rewards. These rewards explicitly model interaction geometry and physical contact, which is crucial for achieving physically plausible and stable interactions between active agents, distinguishing it from policies that treat agents in isolation.
4. **Bridging the Sim-to-Real Gap for Multi-Agent Systems:** The robust real-world deployment system, encompassing state estimation (LiDAR-fused localization) and inter-agent synchronization, addresses key challenges in transferring policies from simulation to reality. This enables reliable operation in real-world conditions characterized by noisy sensor data and asynchronous communication.
5. **Release of a Multi-Humanoid Interaction Dataset:** The creation and planned public release of the MAGIC (Multi-Humanoid Geometric Interaction Dataset) dataset, comprising high-fidelity raw and retargeted interaction data, provides a valuable resource for future research in multi-agent embodied intelligence, fostering further development in this domain.

The potential impact of this research includes unlocking a range of complex collaborative capabilities for humanoid robots, allowing them to operate effectively in shared environments beyond isolated tasks. This could lead to advancements in areas such as assistance robotics, manufacturing, and logistics where multi-robot physical collaboration is beneficial. The methodologies developed, particularly the graph-based formulation, are presented as theoretically general, potentially supporting extensions to larger multi-agent systems and more complex open-world scenarios in future work. The identification of current limitations, such as reliance on pre-built maps for state estimation, also guides future research toward fully ego-centric perception for map-free collaboration.
