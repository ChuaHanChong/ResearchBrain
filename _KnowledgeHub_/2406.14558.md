# CooHOI: Learning Cooperative Human-Object Interaction with Manipulated Object Dynamics

> [!summary] Summary
> CooHOI trains physically simulated humanoids to cooperatively carry large objects using a two-phase pipeline: single-agent AMP-based imitation carrying, then MAPPO fine-tuning where shared object dynamics serve as implicit inter-agent communication. Achieves 89.5% two-agent success without any multi-humanoid motion capture data.

## Problem

- Multi-humanoid collaborative object transport, like carrying beds or sofas, remains under-investigated despite progress in single-character physics-based human-object interaction.
- Prior tracking-based multi-character methods require costly motion capture of multi-person interactions and struggle to generalize to varying object sizes or agent counts.
- Training multi-agent policies from scratch expands the action sampling space, slowing convergence so much that cooperative carrying policies fail to train.

## Method

- Single-agent ==AMP==-based skill learning augments observations with manipulated-object dynamics, bounding-box vertices, rotation, velocity, angular velocity, as feedback for carrying tasks.
- ==Stand points== and ==held points== reward shaping guide the agent to the shortest object edge and to grip the geometric center of each end for reliable lifting.
- Two-phase skill transfer replicates the single-agent policy across agents, then fine-tunes with ==centralized-training decentralized-execution== ==MAPPO==, using object dynamics changes as implicit communication.

## Results

- Two-agent CooHOI reaches **89.54%** success carrying boxes **15-40kg** over **2-20m**, versus **0%** for a from-scratch multi-agent baseline trained without the two-phase pipeline.
- Single-agent CooHOI matches InterPhys baseline success, **96.48%** versus **94.3%**, while extending carry distance to **20m** and improving precision to **6.9cm**.
- Ablations show removing stand points, dynamic observation, reverse-walk data, or single-agent initialization each collapses training into deadlock or standstill failure modes.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Object dynamics can substitute for explicit multi-agent communication channels, letting agents implicitly coordinate by observing shared physical state changes.
> - Bootstrapping multi-agent policies from a well-shaped single-agent skill avoids the exploration blowup that causes from-scratch multi-agent training to fail entirely.
> - A framework that needs only single-agent motion capture data can still generalize to multi-agent and multi-object cooperative tasks, cutting data collection cost sharply.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper addresses a critical, yet underdeveloped, area within robotics and AI: enabling humanoid robots to perform complex cooperative human-object interaction (HOI) tasks. This work operates at the intersection of several rapidly advancing fields: physics-based character control, human-object interaction synthesis, multi-character animation, and multi-agent reinforcement learning.

Historically, significant advancements have been made in **physics-based human-object interaction** for single agents. Frameworks like Adversarial Motion Priors (AMP) have allowed physically simulated characters to perform diverse actions such as sitting, carrying objects, and interacting with scenes in a natural and plausible manner. These methods often rely on imitation learning from human motion capture (MoCap) data, using deep reinforcement learning to optimize policies for realistic and goal-oriented behaviors.

However, the leap from single-agent HOI to **multi-character control and multi-humanoid collaboration** presents substantial challenges. Existing approaches for multi-character animation often fall into two categories:
1. **Kinematic-based methods:** These primarily focus on generating visually plausible motions but often lack physical realism and struggle with true interaction dynamics. They also heavily depend on high-quality, task-specific multi-character interaction datasets, which are scarce and costly to acquire.
2. **Tracking-based physics methods:** While some methods have explored multi-character interactions (e.g., carrying long boxes), they still require explicit motion capture data of multi-humanoid interactions. This limits their scalability to different numbers of agents, diverse object types, and novel scenarios, as generating comprehensive datasets for every possible cooperative task is impractical.

A straightforward alternative, directly training a **multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL) policy from scratch** for cooperative HOI, faces severe efficiency issues. The joint action space of multiple agents is exponentially larger, making training convergence prohibitively slow and sample-inefficient.

CooHOI strategically positions itself to overcome these limitations. It acknowledges the strengths of single-agent learning (especially with frameworks like AMP) and aims to build upon them for multi-agent scenarios. The key innovation is its two-phase learning paradigm and the novel use of **manipulated object dynamics as an implicit communication channel** between agents. By doing so, CooHOI effectively:
- **Bypasses the need for costly multi-humanoid MoCap data:** It leverages existing, more readily available single-agent motion data.
- **Addresses the efficiency challenge of MARL:** It uses a skill transfer mechanism to leverage pre-learned individual competencies, rather than starting from scratch.
- **Provides a scalable solution:** The reliance on object dynamics and a centralized training/decentralized execution (CTDE) scheme makes it adaptable to varying numbers of agents and object types.

Therefore, CooHOI represents a significant step towards practical multi-robot cooperation in complex physical interaction tasks, bridging the gap between highly realistic single-agent behaviors and the nascent field of scalable, data-efficient multi-agent collaborative control.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching motivation for this research stems from the long-standing dream within humanoid robotics communities: to enable humanoid robots to perform complex tasks in human environments, such as cleaning rooms or assisting with household chores. However, many practical tasks, especially those involving large or heavy items like furniture, necessitate the **collaboration of multiple humanoids**.

The authors identify two primary obstacles preventing the realization of this dream:
1. **Data Scarcity:** There is a severe lack of high-quality motion capture data specifically for multi-humanoid collaboration. Collecting such data is intrinsically costly, time-consuming, and difficult to generalize across diverse scenarios, objects, and numbers of agents. Traditional tracking-based methods, while effective for specific scenarios, are bottlenecked by this data dependency.
2. **Efficiency Challenges in Multi-Agent Learning:** Directly training multi-agent policies from scratch using reinforcement learning is computationally inefficient. The combinatorial explosion of the action space for multiple agents makes policy convergence exceedingly difficult and slow, rendering this approach impractical for complex, high-dimensional tasks like cooperative object transportation.

Against this backdrop, the **key objectives** of the CooHOI framework are:
- To develop an **efficient and robust framework** that can train physically simulated characters to execute cooperative object transporting tasks.
- To enable natural-looking and precise **multi-agent human-object interaction** without requiring specific motion capture data of multi-humanoid collaborations.
- To achieve **implicit communication and coordination** between teammates by leveraging the shared dynamics of the manipulated object.
- To design a framework that is **scalable** to include more participants (agents) and a wider range of object types.
- To move beyond "tracking-based methods" to inherently more generalizable and efficient learning paradigms for multi-humanoid HOI.

By addressing these objectives, CooHOI aims to significantly advance the capabilities of humanoid robots, making them more capable collaborators in real-world scenarios requiring physical coordination. The motivation is deeply rooted in practical applications, envisioning a future where humanoid robots can seamlessly assist humans with tasks that are currently beyond the scope of single-agent systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

CooHOI proposes a novel **two-phase learning paradigm** inspired by how humans acquire cooperative skills: first mastering individual tasks, then adapting for collaborative efforts. The framework combines physics-based character control, imitation learning, and multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL).

#### 3.1. Preliminary Concepts

- **Physics-based Character Control:** The agents are physically simulated characters controlled by joint torques, trained via goal-conditioned reinforcement learning. The **Adversarial Motion Priors (AMP)** framework is adopted, where a discriminator encourages generated motions to align with reference motion datasets, providing a "style reward" in addition to task-specific rewards.
- **Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning (MARL):** Cooperative tasks are framed as Partially Observable Markov Decision Processes (POMDPs). Each agent acts based on its local observation, with a shared reward function. The goal is to jointly optimize accumulated rewards. The paper utilizes the **Centralized Training and Decentralized Execution (CTDE)** scheme, specifically with the **Multi-Agent Proximal Policy Optimization (MAPPO)** algorithm.

#### 3.2. Phase 1: Single Agent Carrying Skills Training

This phase focuses on training individual agents to proficiently interact with and carry objects. It introduces several enhancements over previous single-agent methods:

- **Enriched Goal Feature with Manipulated Object Dynamics as Feedback:** Crucially, the observation space for the agent is augmented to include detailed dynamics of the manipulated object. This includes the eight vertices of the object's bounding box, its rotation angle, linear velocity, and angular velocity. This dynamic feedback ensures agents are continuously aware of the object's state and their impact on it, preparing them for multi-agent scenarios where object dynamics will convey teammates' actions.
- **Enriched Task Design facilitating Efficient Skill Transfer:** The carrying process is decomposed into three sub-tasks, each with a corresponding reward component:
 - **`r_walk` (Walking towards objects):** Agents are encouraged to approach "stand points" – randomly allocated positions directly in front of the object's faces. This helps agents learn to orient themselves correctly for grasping, especially for long objects where the "short side" is preferred for multi-agent setup.
 - **`r_held` (Lifting the object):** Agents are incentivized to align their hands with "held points" – the geometric centers of the object's ends. This guides agents to appropriate grip points for lifting, especially for large objects.
 - **`r_target` (Carrying to destination):** Agents are rewarded for transporting the object to its intended final location and orientation.

#### 3.3. Phase 2: Cooperation Strategy Training

After mastering individual carrying skills, these abilities are transferred and refined for cooperative tasks.

- **Efficient Skill Transfer Using Dynamics Information:**
 - The single-agent policy is replicated for all agents and then fine-tuned.
 - Agents are encouraged to observe the dynamics information (state and velocity of the bounding box) *at their respective ends* of the long object. This allows them to effectively "simulate carrying a smaller box" localized to their interaction point, leveraging their pre-trained single-agent skills.
 - The previously designed "stand points" and "held points" further facilitate this smooth skill transfer in a cooperative context.
- **Implicit Communication Channel:** The key mechanism for cooperation is the manipulation of object dynamics. When one agent acts, it changes the object's state (position, velocity, orientation). Because all agents observe these object dynamics as feedback, they implicitly perceive and react to their teammates' actions, leading to emergent coordination without explicit communication protocols. This design is inherently adaptable to varying numbers of agents.
- **Cooperation Training using CTDE Scheme:** The MAPPO algorithm is employed for cooperative strategy training. The value function network is updated using accumulated trajectories shared among all agents.
- **Parameter Sharing:** Given the homogeneous roles of agents in object transportation, parameter sharing is applied across both policy and value networks. This common practice in MARL enhances training efficiency and performance for cooperative behaviors.

#### 3.4. Experiment Setup

- **Simulation Environment:** Isaac Gym, known for its high-performance GPU-based physics simulation, enabling large-scale parallel environments.
- **Motion Data:** Primarily the AMASS dataset (SMPL format). A small set of basic reference motions (walk, pick up, carry, put down) were collected (26 motions in total). Critically, "sidewalk" and "reverse carry" motions were added to ensure agents possess basic movements necessary for multi-person collaboration (e.g., walking backward).
- **Robustness:** Object weight, size, and destination distances were randomized during training to enhance policy robustness.
- **Metrics:** Success Rate (object within 0.2m of destination) and Precision (average distance to target for successful tasks), averaged over 4096 environments and 4 random seeds.

This methodology provides a robust and innovative approach to tackling the complex problem of multi-humanoid cooperative object manipulation, leveraging both single-agent intelligence and the physical properties of the environment for emergent coordination.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted validate the effectiveness, efficiency, and scalability of the CooHOI framework for cooperative human-object interaction tasks.

#### 4.1. Comprehensive Evaluation on Box Carrying

- **Single-Agent Performance:** CooHOI demonstrates high success rates (96.48%) and good precision (6.9 cm) for single-agent box carrying, even when handling objects with randomized weights (2-13 kg) and distances (1-20 m). When augmented with weight information in observations (similar to InterPhys), CooHOI achieves comparable success rates but with improved accuracy (4.8 cm) over a larger distance range (1-20m) compared to previous work like InterPhys. This suggests CooHOI's individual skill learning is competitive or superior to existing methods, even with simpler discrete motion data.
- **Two-Agent Performance:** For cooperative box carrying (15-40 kg, 2-20 m), CooHOI achieves an impressive 89.54% success rate with 3.86 cm precision. Crucially, a direct multi-agent training approach "From Scratch" (without CooHOI's two-phase learning and skill transfer) results in a **0% success rate**, highlighting the necessity and effectiveness of CooHOI's paradigm.

#### 4.2. Extension to Diverse Daily Life Objects

- CooHOI successfully generalizes to a variety of real-world objects beyond simple boxes, including Tables, Armchairs, HighStools (single-agent), and Sofas (two-agents).
- **Single-agent tasks:** High success rates (97.07% for Table, 97.26% for Armchair, 99.21% for HighStool) and low precision errors (around 4-5 cm) are observed.
- **Two-agent tasks:** For the heavier and more complex Sofa, CooHOI achieves an 84.17% success rate with 10.12 cm precision. The slight drop in accuracy compared to boxes is attributed to the greater variability in sofa shapes and weights, which are challenging to represent with a simple bounding box input and limited data. This demonstrates the framework's practical applicability across a range of common items.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies and Robustness Analysis

Detailed ablation studies clearly demonstrate the critical contribution of each component within the CooHOI framework:

- **Single-Agent Robustness:**
 - **Weight Limit:** A single agent can handle approximately 13 kg. Beyond this, success rate significantly drops (e.g., 30% at 20 kg), indicating physical limitations without density information in observations.
 - **Object Size:** The policy is sensitive to extreme object sizes. Very large or very small widths are challenging due to limited arm reach and lack of dexterous hands. However, the policy is robust to variations in object length and height when width is kept reasonable (e.g., 97.8% success when width is 1x, length/height 1.5x).
 - **Distance Variation:** The policy shows robust performance regardless of the transportation distance.
- **Multi-Agent Robustness:**
 - **Weight Capacity:** Two agents can collectively lift much heavier objects than a single agent, emphasizing the value of cooperation.
 - **Object Size Sensitivity:** Similar to single agents, extremely large or small objects pose challenges, but multi-agent coordination makes the task feasible. Restricting object width to 1x while scaling length and height to 1.5x increased success from 0% (for arbitrary scaling) to 88.67% in multi-agent scenarios.
- **CooHOI Framework Components:**
 - **Stand Point:** Essential for guiding agents to the correct grasping face of the object. Without it, agents often approach the longest edge, leading to failed lifts.
 - **Dynamic Observation:** Crucial for inter-agent communication and coordination. Without object dynamics in the observation, agents fail to react to each other and stand still, unable to proceed.
 - **Reverse Walk:** Including motion data for backward movement is vital. Without it, agents can lift the object but get into "deadlock" states by pushing against each other, unable to collectively move backward if needed.
 - **Initialization with Single-Agent Policy:** Fine-tuning a multi-agent policy initialized with pre-trained single-agent skills is indispensable. Training a multi-agent policy "From Scratch" completely fails, even with extended training duration (Figure 8).
- **Robustness to Noise (Appendix D):** Experiments show that CooHOI maintains reasonable performance under observational noise. For a 10kg box with one agent, a noise level of 2 (standard deviation) still yields 78.56% success. For a 20kg box with two agents, the success rate remains high (87.98%) at a noise level of 2, demonstrating good stability.

In summary, CooHOI demonstrates highly effective and efficient learning of cooperative object transportation. Its ability to generalize across diverse objects, robustly handle variations, and critically leverage pre-trained skills and implicit communication through object dynamics marks a significant advancement in multi-humanoid control.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The CooHOI framework represents a substantial contribution to the fields of robotics, artificial intelligence, and character animation, with far-reaching significance and potential impact:

#### 5.1. Scientific and Technical Advancements

- **Novel Paradigm for Multi-Agent HOI:** CooHOI introduces a pioneering two-phase learning paradigm (individual skill learning followed by policy transfer and cooperative fine-tuning) that effectively bridges the gap between sophisticated single-agent control and complex multi-agent collaboration. This approach offers a powerful alternative to traditional, data-intensive or computationally prohibitive methods for multi-robot tasks.
- **Data Efficiency and Scalability:** A core breakthrough is the ability to achieve multi-humanoid cooperative object transportation using *only single-agent motion capture data*. This sidesteps the severe challenges and costs associated with collecting multi-humanoid interaction datasets, making advanced cooperative behaviors more accessible. The framework's design, particularly the implicit communication via object dynamics, allows for seamless extension to more agents and diverse object types.
- **Implicit Communication through Object Dynamics:** The innovative use of manipulated object dynamics as an implicit communication channel is a key technical contribution. This elegant solution allows agents to perceive and react to each other's actions without explicit message passing, leading to emergent and natural coordination. This principle could be highly influential in designing future multi-agent systems where direct communication is costly or unreliable.
- **Robust and Generalizable Controllers:** The comprehensive task design, including enriched observations and specialized reward functions (stand points, held points), facilitates the learning of robust individual skills that readily transfer to cooperative settings. The system demonstrates strong generalization to various object categories and resilience to parameter variations (weight, distance), and even reasonable robustness to observation noise.

#### 5.2. Potential Practical Impact

- **Advancing Humanoid Robotics:** CooHOI moves humanoid robots closer to practical deployment in real-world environments. Tasks like collaboratively moving furniture, carrying heavy equipment, or assisting in construction, which are currently challenging even for specialized robots, become more feasible. This has direct implications for home assistance, logistics, elderly care, and disaster relief.
- **Reducing Development Costs:** By eliminating the need for expensive multi-humanoid MoCap data, CooHOOI significantly lowers the barrier to entry for developing and testing cooperative robotic behaviors. This could accelerate research and development in multi-robot systems.
- **Enhanced Human-Robot Collaboration:** The natural and precise interactions demonstrated by CooHOI could lead to more intuitive and effective human-robot collaboration scenarios. If robots can move objects with human-like coordination, they can integrate more smoothly into human workflows.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The limitations acknowledged by the authors (lack of dexterous hands, simple object representation, simulation-only deployment) naturally pave the way for exciting future research directions. Integrating advanced manipulation capabilities, sophisticated object perception (beyond bounding boxes), and real-world deployment on physical robots are clear next steps that could build upon CooHOI's success. This framework provides a solid foundation for exploring more complex human-robot and robot-robot physical interactions.

In conclusion, CooHOI offers a significant advancement by presenting an efficient, data-light, and scalable method for training cooperative humanoid behaviors. Its innovative use of environmental dynamics for implicit communication opens new avenues for creating robust and adaptable multi-agent systems, pushing the boundaries of what humanoid robots can achieve collaboratively.
