# CurEvo: Curriculum-Guided Self-Evolution for Video Understanding

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Huazhong University of Science and Technology, La Trobe University, and Beijing Institute of Computer Technology and Applications introduce CurEvo, a framework for video understanding that leverages curriculum learning to guide iterative self-improvement without human supervision. The model achieves consistent accuracy gains of up to +4.05% on various VideoQA benchmarks by progressively structuring learning from perceptual grounding to higher-level reasoning.

## Problem

- Traditional video understanding methods, particularly for VideoQA, rely heavily on large-scale human annotations, which can lead to models exploiting superficial correlations.
- Existing self-evolving video understanding frameworks lack explicit control over the learning process, resulting in variable quality of self-generated supervision and unstructured progression.
- The multi-level cognitive nature of video understanding (perception, semantic recognition, reasoning) amplifies the complexity of effectively organizing and reusing self-generated supervision.

## Method

- **CurEvo** introduces an iterative feedback loop integrating ==Curriculum-Guided Self-Evolution==, ==Multi-Dimensional Question Generation==, and ==Type-Adaptive Evaluation==.
- Generates questions across three cognitive dimensions: ==structural perception== (self-supervised), ==semantic recognition== (confidence-based), and ==reasoning understanding== (LLM-inferred confidence).
- Employs ==adaptive sampling ratios== and ==sample weighting== to dynamically adjust learning emphasis, with type-adaptive evaluation to filter unreliable Q&A pairs.

## Results

- **CurEvo** consistently improved accuracy across **7 Video-LLM backbones** and **4 VideoQA benchmarks**, with gains up to **+4.05%** (LLaVA-OneVision on NExT-QA).
- Ablations confirm all core modules contribute significantly; iterative refinement is fundamental.
- Over iterations, retained samples progressively shifted from perception toward recognition and reasoning, indicating effective curriculum progression.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Structuring self-generated supervision into a progressive curriculum, spanning perception to reasoning, provides a more stable and effective path for autonomous video understanding.
> - Dynamically adjusting the emphasis on different cognitive dimensions during training, based on the model's evolving competence, is crucial for balanced skill acquisition.
> - Robust quality assessment of self-generated questions and pseudo-labels via a type-adaptive evaluation mechanism is essential for ensuring reliable supervision and preventing overfitting to noise.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Traditional video understanding methods, particularly those for Video Question Answering (VideoQA), have largely relied on large-scale human annotations and supervised optimization paradigms. While multimodal large language models (MLLMs) and Video-LLMs have advanced cross-modal alignment, their performance remains constrained by the static nature and potential biases of annotated data. Such datasets can lead models to exploit superficial visual-text correlations rather than developing robust temporal or causal reasoning abilities. Furthermore, the fixed training distributions of supervised learning limit adaptability, preventing models from continual improvement beyond their initial pre-training.

To address these limitations, recent research has explored self-evolving video understanding frameworks. These approaches aim for autonomous optimization by employing iterative cycles of generation, evaluation, and retraining, leveraging self-generated supervision to enable improvement without human-labeled data. However, existing self-evolution paradigms often lack explicit control over the learning process. The quality and difficulty of self-generated supervision can vary significantly, evaluation standards may drift, and the training process can be susceptible to noisy or unbalanced pseudo-labels. This can result in models overfitting to unreliable supervision or stagnating, making it challenging to establish a clear learning trajectory from low-level perception to higher-level semantic and reasoning capabilities.

CurEvo positions itself within this landscape by integrating curriculum learning principles into self-evolving video understanding. It proposes to transform weakly controlled self-evolution into a structured and progressive learning process. By dynamically regulating task difficulty, refining evaluation criteria, and balancing data diversity according to model competence, CurEvo aims to provide a more stable and effective path for autonomous video understanding, thereby bridging the gap between general self-evolution and structured, progressive skill acquisition. This work contributes to the development of label-free multimodal reasoning systems with enhanced self-improvement mechanisms.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation for CurEvo stems from the challenges associated with applying self-evolution to video understanding, particularly in VideoQA. While self-evolution offers a promising route to improve models without extensive manual annotations, the inherently multi-level cognitive nature of video understanding—ranging from perceptual grounding to semantic recognition and high-level reasoning—amplifies the complexity of self-generated supervision. The core problem is not merely generating more supervision, but effectively organizing, assessing, and reusing it to foster stable and progressive model improvement.

Specifically, the authors identify three interconnected challenges in existing self-evolving VideoQA systems:

1. **Variable Quality of Self-Generated Supervision:** The question-answer pairs generated during self-evolution can exhibit substantial variations in quality across iterations. Questions might be ambiguous, answers weakly grounded in visual evidence, or pseudo-labels noisy. This necessitates a mechanism to identify and retain reliable supervision from the overall generated set.
2. **Lack of Structured Learning Progression:** Current self-evolution pipelines typically treat all generated supervision as an undifferentiated pool. They do not explicitly structure learning to progress from easier perceptual signals to more complex reasoning tasks. The authors propose that supervision should be explicitly organized across cognitive dimensions (perception, semantic recognition, reasoning) to facilitate a curriculum-based approach.
3. **Ineffective Adaptive Reallocation of Learning Emphasis:** Even when supervision from different cognitive dimensions is available, existing models lack a mechanism to dynamically adjust the emphasis placed on each type of signal as their competence evolves. Without such adaptive reallocation, training might disproportionately focus on already-mastered perceptual tasks or inadequately support the development of weaker semantic and reasoning capabilities.

These challenges collectively underscore the need for a more structured and controlled self-evolution process. The primary objective of CurEvo is to address these limitations by introducing a framework that:
- Controls the quality of self-generated supervision through robust evaluation and filtering.
- Organizes this supervision into a multi-dimensional curriculum spanning perception, semantic recognition, and reasoning.
- Dynamically reallocates learning emphasis across these dimensions based on the model's evolving competence, ensuring a progressive and balanced acquisition of video understanding skills.

By integrating curriculum learning principles, CurEvo aims to transform the often trial-and-error nature of conventional self-evolution into a structured, feedback-driven optimization loop for autonomous and progressive video understanding.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

CurEvo is a curriculum-guided self-evolution framework designed to improve video question answering models without human supervision. It operates through an iterative feedback loop comprising three core stages: Curriculum-Guided Self-Evolution, Multi-Dimensional Question Generation, and Type-Adaptive Evaluation.

#### 3.1. Curriculum-Guided Self-Evolution

This module establishes the iterative learning cycle, integrating generation, evaluation, selection, and retraining to foster progressive model improvement.

**Self-Evolution Cycle:**
At each iteration *t*, the framework performs the following steps:
1. **Question and Pseudo-Label Generation:** The base model generates candidate questions (*qᵢ*) and initial pseudo-labels (˜*aᵢ*) from unlabeled video fragments, forming a preliminary curriculum dataset *C*⁽*ᵗ*⁾.
2. **Question Assessment:** An evaluation model, *g*Φ, assigns a quality score *Qᵢ* to each generated question. Ambiguous or low-confidence questions are filtered out to ensure reliability.
3. **Answer Generation and Evaluation:** For the retained questions, the base model *f*Θ generates answers (ˆ*aᵢ*). The evaluation model *g*Φ then computes semantic confidence scores *Eᵢ* for these answers.
4. **Adaptive Training Pool Formation:** Only question-answer pairs satisfying *Eᵢ* > *τ*⁽*ᵗ*⁾*d* (a dimension-specific threshold) are preserved, forming the adaptive training pool *D*⁽*ᵗ*⁾.
5. **Model Update:** The base model is incrementally updated by minimizing a weighted loss *L*⁽*ᵗ*⁾, aiming to improve its reasoning and generalization capabilities.
6. **Curriculum Adjustment:** After training, statistical feedback *A*⁽*ᵗ*⁾*d* (evaluator-verified performance for each dimension *d*) is used to adjust dimension-specific parameters for the next iteration. This feedback reflects the current model's proficiency in handling perception, semantic, or reasoning supervision. The updated model parameters become Θ⁽*ᵗ*⁺¹⁾, and a refined curriculum *C*⁽*ᵗ*⁺¹⁾ is constructed based on new generations from *f*Θ⁽*ᵗ*⁺¹⁾.

**Progressive Curriculum Design:**
This component governs the selection and prioritization of learning signals.
1. **Adaptive Sampling Ratio:** Although an equal number of candidate questions are generated for each dimension (perception, semantic, reasoning), only a subset is used for training. An adaptive sampling ratio *r*⁽*ᵗ*⁾*d* is calculated for each dimension *d*. This ratio is updated based on the previous iteration's accuracy feedback *A*⁽*ᵗ*⁾*d* and a sensitivity parameter *λd*:
 *r*⁽*ᵗ*⁺¹⁾*d* = (*r*⁽*ᵗ*⁾*d* (1 + *λd* (1 - *A*⁽*ᵗ*⁾*d*))) / (Σ*d'*( *r*⁽*ᵗ*⁾*d'* (1 + *λd'* (1 - *A*⁽*ᵗ*⁾*d'*))))
 This mechanism increases the sampling ratio for underperforming dimensions, shifting training emphasis towards weaker capabilities over time.
2. **Sample Weighting:** Once *D*⁽*ᵗ*⁾ is formed, each sample *i* is assigned a weight *w*⁽*ᵗ*⁾*i* for optimization. This weight combines three factors: the dimension's sampling ratio *r*⁽*ᵗ*⁾*d*(*i*), the evaluator confidence *Eᵢ*, and an informativeness proxy *Uᵢ* (derived from answer length and richness).
 *w*⁽*ᵗ*⁾*i* = (*r*⁽*ᵗ*⁾*d*(*i*) * Eᵢ* * Uᵢ*) / (Σ*j*∈*D*⁽*ᵗ*⁾ (*r*⁽*ᵗ*⁾*d*(*j*) * Eⱼ* * Uⱼ*))
 This ensures that underrepresented dimensions, high-confidence answers, and informative cases receive appropriate gradient emphasis.
3. **Training Objective:** The final training objective for iteration *t* is the weighted cross-entropy loss:
 *L*⁽*ᵗ*⁾ = Σ*i*∈*D*⁽*ᵗ*⁾ *w*⁽*ᵗ*⁾*i* L*CE*(*aᵢ*, ˆ*a*⁽*ᵗ*⁾*i*)
 where *aᵢ* is the pseudo-label (for perception tasks) or the selected answer (for semantic consistency), and ˆ*a*⁽*ᵗ*⁾*i* is the model's prediction.

#### 3.2. Multi-Dimensional Question Generation

This module structures the curriculum by generating questions across three cognitive levels, facilitating progressive learning:

1. **Structural Perception Level:** Questions focus on directly observable physical and temporal cues, such as frame counting, motion consistency, temporal localization, and order recovery. Pseudo-labels (˜*aᵢ*) are automatically derived from video frame metadata or geometric relations, making these questions fully self-supervised with explicit correctness signals.
2. **Semantic Recognition Level:** Questions transition to content understanding, addressing objects, attributes, and actions. These cannot be directly labeled from raw video. Instead, the language evaluation model provides confidence-based semantic judgments (*s*⁽*sem*⁾*i*) reflecting correctness and coherence, offering richer supervision than the perception level.
3. **Reasoning Understanding Level:** Questions involve higher-order inference like causal, temporal, and intent reasoning, requiring cross-fragment integration. Lacking ground-truth supervision, evaluation relies entirely on the language model's inferred confidence (*s*⁽*reason*⁾*i*), which encodes internal consistency and factual plausibility. This layer functions as an open-ended learning component driven by LLM-based evaluative signals.

#### 3.3. Type-Adaptive Evaluation

This mechanism unifies heuristic and model-based scoring for questions and answers, providing consistent supervision.

1. **Question Assessment:** Estimates the quality of each generated question *qᵢ* before answer generation using a weighted combination of heuristic and semantic signals:
 *Qᵢ* = *αH*ᵩ*ᵢ* + (1 - *α*) *S*ᵩ*ᵢ*
 where *H*ᵩ*ᵢ* measures grammatical soundness and clarity, and *S*ᵩ*ᵢ* evaluates semantic alignment via the evaluator. Questions above a dimension-specific threshold *τ*d,*k* are included.
2. **Answer Evaluation:** Determines the reliability of predicted answers. For semantic and reasoning questions, a confidence score *Eᵢ* is computed:
 *Eᵢ* = *ηH*ᵃ*ᵢ* + (1 - *η*) *L*ᵃ*ᵢ*
 where *H*ᵃ*ᵢ* assesses fluency and structural correctness, and *L*ᵃ*ᵢ* measures semantic consistency. These scores modulate sample weights during training. Perception-level questions do not require answer evaluation as their pseudo-labels are deterministically verifiable. This differential evaluation reflects the distinct supervision characteristics of the three dimensions.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The effectiveness of CurEvo was evaluated across four VideoQA benchmarks using multiple backbone models, with Qwen2.5-VL(7B) serving as the default evaluation model.

#### 4.1. Experimental Setup

- **Backbones:** Seven different Video-LLM backbones were used, including Video-LLaVA(7B), LLaVA-OneVision(7B), VILA(7B), Video-LLaMA3(7B), InternVL2.5(8B), Qwen2.5-VL(7B), and Qwen3-VL(8B). All models were fine-tuned with LoRA.
- **Benchmarks:** ActivityNet-QA (long-range temporal, multi-stage event), NExT-QA (temporal, causality, event ordering), MSRVTT-QA (short-video grounding, fine-grained recognition), and MSVD-QA (short-video grounding, fine-grained recognition). These benchmarks collectively cover the perception, semantic recognition, and reasoning dimensions of CurEvo.
- **Metrics:** Standard benchmark accuracy and an evaluator-based semantic score (assessing correctness, relevance, completeness, semantic consistency).

#### 4.2. Performance Comparison

**Overall Improvements (Table 1):**
CurEvo consistently improved both standard accuracy and the evaluator-based semantic score across all seven backbone models and all four datasets.
- On **ActivityNet-QA**, accuracy gains ranged from +0.61% (Qwen3-VL) to +3.92% (LLaVA-OneVision).
- On **MSRVTT-QA**, gains ranged from +0.85% (Qwen3-VL) to +3.60% (LLaVA-OneVision).
- On **MSVD-QA**, gains ranged from +0.85% (Qwen3-VL) to +3.80% (LLaVA-OneVision).
- On **NExT-QA**, gains ranged from +0.77% (Qwen3-VL) to +4.05% (LLaVA-OneVision).
These results suggest that the curriculum-guided self-evolution framework generalizes across various model architectures and capability levels.
- **MSRVTT-QA and MSVD-QA:** Consistent gains indicate improved short-term visual grounding and semantic recognition.
- **ActivityNet-QA:** Clear gains suggest strengthened temporal grounding and multi-step event understanding.
- **NExT-QA:** Improvements on this benchmark, which emphasizes higher-level reasoning, suggest that the framework benefits these complex capabilities.

**Impact of Evaluation Model Quality (Table 2):**
The performance of CurEvo was influenced by the quality of the evaluation model relative to the base model.
- When the evaluation model was comparable to or stronger than the base model, CurEvo generally yielded more substantial and consistent gains. For example, Qwen2.5-VL as an evaluator showed strong improvements for most base models.
- When the evaluation model was clearly weaker than the base model (e.g., VILA or InternVL2.5 as evaluators for Qwen2.5-VL or Qwen3-VL as base models), the gains diminished and could even become negative. This indicates that reliable feedback from the evaluator is a prerequisite for effective curriculum-guided self-evolution.

#### 4.3. Ablation and Analysis

**Ablation of Components (Table 4):**
An ablation study on ActivityNet-QA demonstrated the contribution of CurEvo's modules:
- **Self-Evolution Iteration:** Removing this core iterative refinement caused the largest performance drop, confirming its fundamental role (+67.84% baseline vs. 62.48% w/o Self-Evolution).
- **Multi-Dimensional Question Generation:** Removing this module reduced accuracy (65.96%), indicating its importance in structuring supervision.
- **Type-Adaptive Evaluation:** Its removal also degraded performance (66.37%), highlighting its role in ensuring data reliability.
- **Curriculum-Related Modules (combined):** Removing both the multi-dimensional question generation and type-adaptive evaluation modules resulted in a larger drop (65.02%), demonstrating their complementary contribution beyond the basic self-evolution loop.

**Ablation of Question Dimensions (Table 3):**
Analyzing the impact of removing specific question dimensions (perception, recognition, understanding) showed:
- Removing any single dimension led to performance reduction. For example, "w/o Perception" resulted in 67.88% accuracy on ActivityNet-QA compared to 69.91% full model.
- Removing two dimensions simultaneously caused a more significant performance decrease. For instance, "w/o Perc. + Rec." resulted in 65.72% on ActivityNet-QA.
- The degradation patterns were consistent with benchmark characteristics; MSRVTT-QA and MSVD-QA were more sensitive to the recognition dimension, while ActivityNet-QA and NExT-QA relied more on perception and understanding. These results support the rationale for CurEvo’s multi-dimensional curriculum design.

**Analysis of Retained Samples and Training Iteration (Figure 5):**
- **Retained Samples:** Over successive iterations, the proportion of retained samples progressively shifted from perception-level questions towards recognition and understanding questions. This aligns with the adaptive curriculum's objective to gradually increase task difficulty.
- **Accuracy Improvement:** The overall accuracy across the four benchmarks showed a consistent upward trend across iterations, with final iterations outperforming the baseline. This indicates that CurEvo facilitates a controlled learning trajectory, progressing from lower-level perception to higher-level reasoning.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

CurEvo addresses critical limitations in existing self-evolution frameworks for video understanding by introducing a curriculum-guided approach. Its significance lies in transforming weakly controlled and unstructured autonomous learning into a more organized and progressive process.

The framework demonstrates that integrating curriculum learning principles, specifically through multi-dimensional question generation, type-adaptive evaluation, and adaptive sampling/weighting, can lead to consistent and measurable improvements in video understanding capabilities. This is evidenced by the across-the-board performance gains observed on diverse VideoQA benchmarks and across multiple backbone models, without requiring additional human annotations.

Potential impacts of this work include:
- **Enhanced Autonomous Learning:** CurEvo provides a scalable pathway toward label-free multimodal reasoning systems that can continuously improve. This could reduce the reliance on costly and time-consuming human data annotation, making the development of advanced video understanding models more efficient and accessible.
- **Robust Skill Acquisition:** By structuring learning from basic perception to complex reasoning, CurEvo encourages models to develop a more fundamental understanding of video content, potentially mitigating the tendency to rely on superficial correlations often seen in purely supervised models.
- **Generalizability Across Architectures:** The consistent improvements observed across various Video-LLM backbones suggest that the curriculum-guided self-evolution paradigm is broadly applicable and can enhance diverse model families.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** This work establishes a foundation for further exploration into advanced curriculum strategies, dynamic task sequencing, and more sophisticated self-assessment mechanisms in multimodal learning. The authors explicitly plan to extend CurEvo to more diverse question types, including long-form video understanding and cross-scene reasoning, indicating avenues for continued development.

While the study identifies a dependency on the quality of the evaluation model as a potential limitation, the overall approach represents a notable advancement in self-supervised video understanding, offering a structured path for models to autonomously acquire and refine complex reasoning skills.
