# Generative World Modelling for Humanoids: 1X World Model Challenge Technical Report

> [!summary] Summary
> Team Revontuli secured first place in the 1X World Model Challenge by developing and optimizing generative models for humanoid robots, achieving state-of-the-art performance in both future frame prediction and latent code compression. Their solutions demonstrated superior pixel-level accuracy with 23.0 dB PSNR for sampling and a lower cross-entropy loss of 6.6386 for compression, while also achieving training times significantly faster than competitors.

## Problem

- Equipping humanoid robots with internal simulators capable of predicting environmental dynamics and the consequences of their actions to enable more autonomous and efficient operation.
- Accurately forecasting future high-resolution image frames (pixel space) from a robot's visual observations.
- Efficiently predicting future discrete latent codes that represent compressed versions of video frames, crucial for computationally tractable planning in robotics.

## Method

- For the sampling track, they adapted a pre-trained Wan 2.2 TI2V-5B flow-matching generative video model, fine-tuning it with LoRA and integrating robot states using ==AdaLN-Zero==.
- For the compression track, they developed a Spatio-Temporal Transformer, inspired by Vision Transformers, which efficiently processes video sequences in latent space by alternating between spatial and temporal attention blocks.
- During inference, they employed ensemble averaging for the sampling track to boost PSNR performance and utilized greedy decoding for efficient, high-probability sequence generation in the compression track.

## Results

- Achieved 1st place in both the Sampling and Compression tracks of the 1X World Model Challenge.
- Secured **23.0** dB PSNR on the test set for the Sampling track, outperforming the second-place entry by **1.44** dB, and achieved a Top-500 Cross-Entropy Loss of **6.6386** on the test set for the Compression track.
- Demonstrated high computational efficiency, completing training for the sampling model in 36 hours and the compression model in under 17 hours, significantly faster than other competitors.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Large pre-trained generative video models can be effectively fine-tuned for specialized robotics world modeling tasks using techniques like LoRA and state conditioning via AdaLN-Zero.
> - Ensemble averaging of multiple predictions during inference proved crucial for maximizing PSNR in future frame prediction, effectively leveraging predictive uncertainty to smooth outputs.
> - The Spatio-Temporal Transformer architecture offers an efficient way to model video sequences in compact latent spaces by decoupling spatial and temporal attention, which is vital for scalability in world models.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work is positioned at the intersection of several rapidly advancing fields within artificial intelligence: **generative AI, world models, and embodied AI/robotics**.

- **World Models Paradigm:** The paper explicitly frames its contribution within the "world models" paradigm, a concept pioneered by researchers like David Ha and Jürgen Schmidhuber. World models aim to equip intelligent agents (such as humanoid robots) with internal simulators of their environments, allowing them to predict future observations or latent states, plan actions, and anticipate consequences without direct real-world interaction. This reduces the need for costly and time-consuming real-world experimentation, making robot learning more efficient and safer.
- **Evolution of Architectures:** The authors contextualize their approach by tracing the evolution of world model architectures. Early efforts largely relied on recurrent neural networks (RNNs) and multilayer perceptrons (MLPs). More recently, generative modeling techniques, especially those based on **transformers** (operating over discrete latent spaces) and **diffusion/flow-based models**, have become dominant. These models, often trained at "scale," form the basis of powerful "foundation models" capable of generating highly realistic and accurate video predictions. This paper leverages a state-of-the-art flow-matching generative video model (Wan 2.2 TI2V-5B) for the sampling track and a spatio-temporal transformer for the compression track, placing it firmly within the most advanced architectural trends.
- **1X World Model Challenge:** The most immediate context for this work is its direct participation in the "1X World Model Challenge." This challenge provides an open-source benchmark specifically designed for evaluating world models in the context of real-world humanoid interaction. By excelling in both the "sampling" (forecasting future image frames) and "compression" (predicting future discrete latent codes) tracks, Team Revontuli contributes significantly to this benchmark. Their methods establish new state-of-the-art results, effectively setting a new bar for performance and efficiency in this specific domain. The challenge itself is a crucial effort to standardize evaluation and drive progress in building practical world models for robotics.
- **Embodied AI and Humanoid Robotics:** The ultimate goal of world models in this context is to enhance the capabilities of humanoid robots. By providing robust predictive capabilities, this research directly contributes to enabling humanoids to operate more autonomously, intelligently, and safely in complex, dynamic environments. The focus on visual observations and robot states underscores its relevance to real-world robot perception and control.

In summary, this work represents a cutting-edge application of large-scale generative models to fundamental problems in embodied AI, specifically addressing the computational and predictive challenges of world modeling for humanoid robots through a rigorous, benchmark-driven approach.

The overarching objective of this research, as framed by the 1X World Model Challenge, is to advance the state-of-the-art in **generative world modeling for humanoid robots**. This breaks down into several specific objectives:

- **Develop Internal Simulators for Agents:** The primary motivation behind world models is to equip intelligent agents (specifically humanoid robots in this challenge) with an internal simulator of their environment. This internal model allows robots to "imagine" future states, predict the consequences of their actions, and refine their decision-making processes without needing constant real-world interaction, which is often expensive, time-consuming, or even hazardous.
- **Address Core Predictive Challenges in Robotics:** The 1X World Model Challenge itself is motivated by "core problems when using world models in robotics." Team Revontuli's objectives align directly with these:
 - **Sampling Track:** To accurately forecast future high-resolution image frames (pixel space) observed by a humanoid robot. This is crucial for visual perception, understanding complex scene dynamics, and generating realistic visual feedback for planning.
 - **Compression Track:** To predict future discrete latent codes that represent compressed versions of video frames. This objective addresses the need for computationally tractable and efficient representations of environmental dynamics, enabling faster planning and reasoning in a compact latent space.
- **Achieve State-of-the-Art Performance in the 1X World Model Challenge:** Team Revontuli's explicit objective was to perform strongly in the competition. Their successful attainment of 1st place in both tracks demonstrates their ability to develop highly effective solutions that push the boundaries of current capabilities on this specific benchmark.
- **Leverage Modern Generative AI Techniques:** A key motivation was to adapt and optimize recent advancements in generative modeling (e.g., flow-matching models, spatio-temporal transformers, large-scale pre-training) for the specialized domain of robotic world models, demonstrating their applicability and efficacy.
- **Investigate Efficient Training and Inference Strategies:** The team was also motivated to explore and implement efficient training methodologies (e.g., LoRA fine-tuning, large-batch training on cutting-edge GPUs) and inference strategies (e.g., ensemble predictions, greedy decoding) to optimize both performance and computational resource utilization. Their achievement of first place with significantly faster training times underscores this sub-objective.
- **Shape Future Research Efforts:** By delivering strong performance and a detailed technical report, the team aims for their "methods [to] shape future efforts" in world modeling for robotics, providing valuable insights and baseline architectures for subsequent research.

In essence, Team Revontuli's motivation was to provide robust, high-performance, and computationally efficient solutions to fundamental world modeling tasks, thereby contributing to the practical deployment of intelligent humanoid robots.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Team Revontuli adopted distinct yet complementary methodologies for the two tracks of the 1X World Model Challenge, both leveraging state-of-the-art generative modeling techniques and optimized training strategies.

#### 3.1. Sampling Challenge Methodology

The sampling track required predicting future image frames in pixel space (512x512 pixels) based on past frames and a complete sequence of robot states.

- **Base Model Adaptation:** The core of their approach was to adapt **Wan 2.2 TI2V-5B**, a pre-trained flow-matching generative video model with a 30-layer DiT (Diffusion Transformer) backbone. This foundation model was originally designed for text-image-to-video generation.
- **Video-State Conditioning:**
 - **Video-to-Video Transformation:** They modified the masking of input latents to transition the model from image-to-video to video-to-video generation, effectively fixing multiple initial frames as conditioning context.
 - **Robot State Integration:** Robot states (continuous angle and velocity, 25 dimensions) were incorporated using **AdaLN-Zero** within Wan's DiT blocks. This involved:
 1. Downsampling robot states to match the video's temporal resolution.
 2. Augmenting continuous states with sinusoidal features.
 3. Projecting states through an MLP to a hidden dimension (r\_dim=256).
 4. Compressing these projected features along the temporal dimension using a 2-layer 1D convolutional network to align with the Wan-VAE's video compression.
 5. Feeding the compressed features into an MLP to generate modulation parameters for AdaLN-Zero layers, which were then combined with the flow matching timestep modulation. This allowed the robot states to modulate specific latent slices corresponding to their respective frames.
- **Training:**
 - **Fine-tuning with LoRA:** The model was post-trained using LoRA (Low-Rank Adaptation) with a rank of 32 on the Wan 2.2 DiT backbone. This allowed efficient fine-tuning of a large pre-trained model without modifying all its parameters.
 - **Optimizer and Learning Rate:** AdamW optimizer with a constant learning rate of 4 × 10⁻⁴.
 - **Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG):** Experiments were conducted with and without CFG during training, though minimal PSNR improvement was observed from CFG.
 - **Computational Resources:** Training was conducted on a DataCrunch instant cluster equipped with 4 nodes, each with 8 NVIDIA B200 GPUs, using an effective batch size of 1024. The B200's 184GB VRAM capacity was crucial for handling the memory requirements of video generation models.
- **Inference:**
 - **Ensemble Predictions:** To optimize for the PSNR metric, which penalizes sharp errors, the team found that averaging an ensemble of predictions (sampling multiple times from the model and averaging the outputs) significantly improved PSNR. This strategy created a smoothing effect, particularly in areas of high motion.
 - **Hyperparameter Tuning:** They experimented with different numbers of ensemble samples, inference steps, and classifier-free guidance weights to maximize PSNR.

#### 3.2. Compression Challenge Methodology

The compression track involved predicting future discrete latent tokens, generated by the Cosmos 8x8x8 tokeniser, based on past token grids and robot states.

- **Model Architecture: Spatio-Temporal Transformer (ST-Transformer):**
 - Inspired by Genie, they built a world model based on the Vision Transformer (ViT).
 - The core innovation was the **Spatio-Temporal Transformer** design, which alternates between spatial and temporal attention blocks to reduce the quadratic memory cost of standard Transformers.
 - **Spatial Attention:** Attends over 1 × 32 × 32 tokens per frame.
 - **Temporal Attention:** Applies a causal mask and attends across T × 1 × 1 tokens over time. This design ensures spatial attention scales linearly with the number of frames, enhancing efficiency for video generation.
 - **Stability Enhancements:** Pre-LayerNorm and QKNorm were applied for improved training stability.
 - **Positional Embeddings:** Learnable absolute embeddings were used for both spatial and temporal tokens.
 - **Configuration:** The transformer used 24 layers, 8 heads, an embedding dimension of 512, and a sequence length of T=5.
- **State Conditioning:** Robot states were integrated as additive embeddings. The state vector was processed by an MLP, a 1D convolution, enriched with absolute position embeddings, and then combined with video tokens.
- **Training:**
 - **Implementation:** Implemented in PyTorch, using the fused AdamW optimizer.
 - **Weight Tying:** Input and output embeddings were tied, a technique known to reduce memory footprint and often improve performance, especially in sequence models like GPT-2.
 - **Objective:** Minimizing cross-entropy loss between predicted and ground-truth tokens at future timesteps.
 - **Teacher Forcing:** Teacher forcing was used during training to enable parallel computation across timesteps.
 - **Learning Rate Schedule:** A linear learning rate schedule was applied, with a peak of 8 × 10⁻⁴ after a 2000-step warmup.
 - **Computational Resources:** Automatic mixed precision (bfloat16) was used for training efficiency on the same DataCrunch B200 cluster, with an effective batch size of 160.
- **Inference:**
 - **Autoregressive Generation:** The model generated sequences autoregressively.
 - **Greedy Decoding:** Instead of sampling, greedy decoding (selecting the token with the highest probability at each step) was used. This produced deterministic, high-probability sequences, which they found to be both effective and efficient. Experiments with scheduled sampling to bridge the teacher-forced/autoregressive gap did not yield meaningful improvements.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

Team Revontuli achieved outstanding results, securing **1st place in both the Sampling and Compression tracks** of the 1X World Model Challenge.

#### 4.1. Sampling Challenge Results

- **Quantitative Performance:**
 - **PSNR (Peak Signal-to-Noise Ratio):** Achieved 23.0 dB on the test set and 25.53 dB on the validation set. This significantly surpassed the second-place entry (21.56 dB), demonstrating superior pixel-level accuracy in future frame prediction.
 - **Other Metrics (Validation Set):**
 - SSIM (Structural Similarity Index Measure): 0.773
 - LPIPS (Learned Perceptual Image Patch Similarity): 0.158
 - FID (Fréchet Inception Distance): 69.25
- **Impact of Inference Strategy:**
 - **Ensemble Averaging:** Crucially, averaging multiple samples during inference (exploiting predictive uncertainty) proved highly effective for boosting PSNR scores. Increasing the number of ensemble samples from 1 to 20 improved PSNR from 22.63 dB to 24.88 dB on the validation set. This strategy smoothed out predictions, which PSNR tends to reward.
 - **Post-Training on Validation Data:** Training on the combined train + validation raw dataset led to a +0.58 dB increase in PSNR on the public leaderboard, demonstrating the benefits of additional data.
 - **CFG:** Classifier-free guidance did not yield significant PSNR improvements in their experiments.
- **Training Efficiency:** The team managed to achieve their top-ranked sampling performance in only **36 hours** of training time, an order of magnitude faster than the runner-up, highlighting the efficiency of their setup and methodology.

#### 4.2. Compression Challenge Results

- **Quantitative Performance:**
 - **Top-500 Cross-Entropy Loss (CE loss):** Achieved 6.6386 on the test set and 4.92 on the validation set. This was substantially lower (better) than the second-place entry (7.50), indicating superior prediction accuracy for discrete latent tokens.
- **Training Dynamics:**
 - **Teacher Forcing vs. Autoregressive:** Training curves showed a gap between the teacher-forced validation loss (which acts as a lower bound for an ideal model) and the autoregressive validation loss. Efforts to reduce this gap using scheduled sampling did not lead to meaningful improvements.
 - **Greedy Decoding:** Greedy autoregressive inference proved to be both effective and efficient for generating high-probability sequences in the latent space.
- **Training Efficiency:** The Spatio-Temporal Transformer model for the compression track was trained in under **17 hours**, further emphasizing the team's ability to achieve top-tier performance with impressive computational efficiency.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The work by Team Revontuli for the 1X World Model Challenge holds significant implications for the fields of generative AI, robotics, and embodied intelligence.

- **Setting New Benchmarks for World Models:** By securing 1st place in both the sampling and compression tracks, Team Revontuli has established new state-of-the-art performance levels on a challenging open-source benchmark for humanoid interaction. This provides strong baselines and validates their architectural and training choices, guiding future research.
- **Validation of Foundation Models in Robotics:** The successful adaptation and fine-tuning of a large, pre-trained internet-scale video generation foundation model (Wan 2.2 TI2V-5B) for a specialized robotics task demonstrate the immense power and transferability of such models. This suggests that pre-training on vast, diverse datasets can effectively provide a powerful starting point for embodied AI applications, reducing the need for extensive domain-specific data from scratch.
- **Computational Efficiency and Accessibility:** Achieving top performance in remarkably short training times (36 hours for sampling, under 17 hours for compression) is a major contribution. This efficiency, partly enabled by advanced hardware (NVIDIA B200 GPUs via DataCrunch) and optimized methodologies (LoRA, Spatio-Temporal Transformer), makes the development and iteration of complex world models more practical and potentially accessible for researchers with limited time or resources.
- **Methodological Innovations for World Modeling:**
 - **Effective State Conditioning:** The robust integration of robot states into generative video models using AdaLN-Zero provides a blueprint for how action and state information can effectively guide visual predictions, a critical component for intelligent agents.
 - **Strategic Inference for Metrics:** The use of ensemble predictions to leverage predictive uncertainty and optimize for metrics like PSNR highlights the importance of tailored inference strategies beyond simple greedy decoding, even if it presents trade-offs with other visual quality metrics (LPIPS, FID). This opens a discussion about ideal inference strategies for downstream robotics tasks.
 - **Efficient Latent Space Modeling:** The Spatio-Temporal Transformer demonstrates an efficient way to model video sequences in compact latent spaces. Predicting discrete latent tokens is often preferred in reinforcement learning and control, as it reduces dimensionality and can simplify planning by focusing on meaningful abstract states rather than raw pixels.
- **Advancing Humanoid Robotics:** The ultimate impact of this research is on empowering humanoid robots with more sophisticated cognitive capabilities. Robust world models enable:
 - **Advanced Planning:** Robots can simulate potential future scenarios resulting from their actions.
 - **Anticipation:** Better prediction of environmental changes and object movements.
 - **Improved Decision-Making:** More informed choices without constant real-world trial and error.
 - **Faster Learning:** Reduces the need for real-world interactions, accelerating the development of robotic skills.
- **Future Research Directions:** The report thoughtfully points to future work, such as determining the "most suitable inference strategy for downstream decision-making," acknowledging that optimizing for a single metric like PSNR might not always align with practical robotic needs. This invites further research into perception-action loops, where predictive quality is directly linked to task success.

In conclusion, Team Revontuli's work provides a compelling demonstration of how state-of-the-art generative AI, combined with efficient computational strategies, can yield highly effective world models for complex humanoid robotics challenges. Their achievements pave the way for more autonomous, intelligent, and capable robots in the future.
