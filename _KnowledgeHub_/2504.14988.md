# Benchmarking Large Vision-Language Models on Fine-Grained Image Tasks: A Comprehensive Evaluation

> [!summary] Summary
> The paper introduces FG-BMK, a comprehensive benchmark for evaluating Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) on fine-grained image tasks, demonstrating that current LVLMs exhibit struggles with subtle distinctions, pronounced knowledge biases due to training data imbalance, and generally underperform specialized fine-grained models.

## Problem

- A critical gap exists in systematically evaluating Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) on fine-grained image tasks, which demand distinguishing subtle visual cues between subordinate categories.
- Existing holistic and specialized benchmarks do not adequately assess LVLMs' ability to perform at this granular level.
- The true capabilities and limitations of LVLMs in fine-grained domains, crucial for applications like biodiversity monitoring or product retrieval, remained largely unexplored.

## Method

- Introduces ==FG-BMK==, a comprehensive fine-grained evaluation benchmark comprising 1.01 million questions and 0.33 million images sourced from 12 established fine-grained datasets.
- Employs a ==human-oriented evaluation paradigm== to assess conversational understanding and knowledge recall through attribute recognition, knowledge bias estimation, and hierarchical granularity recognition tasks.
- Utilizes a ==machine-oriented evaluation paradigm== to directly assess the quality of visual feature representations using image retrieval and classification tasks within and across meta-categories.

## Results

- LVLMs' accuracy declines significantly as the granularity of recognition questions increases, showing struggles with excessively fine-grained categories (e.g., species vs. class level distinctions).
- LVLMs exhibit pronounced knowledge biases across fine-grained categories, primarily attributed to uneven representation in their vast, general training data.
- Current LVLMs consistently underperform state-of-the-art specialized fine-grained models on classification tasks and show significantly higher susceptibility to adversarial perturbations in fine-grained settings. Benchmark includes **1.01 million** questions and **0.33 million** images from **12** datasets.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The granularity mismatch between fine-grained visual content and coarse-grained textual descriptions in alignment data can impair the fine-grained discriminability of visual features.
> - Models trained with contrastive loss exhibit superior fine-grained discriminability compared to those using generative or reconstruction-based paradigms.
> - Data quality and effective training paradigms are more important than sheer data quantity or vision encoder size for achieving robust fine-grained capabilities in LVLMs.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of artificial intelligence has seen substantial progress with the emergence of Large Language Models (LLMs), such as GPT-4, demonstrating advanced text comprehension, reasoning, and generation capabilities. Building on this foundation, Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs), including models like GPT-4o, Qwen, InternVL, and LLaVA-1.5, have rapidly evolved, exhibiting strong multimodal reasoning and perception abilities across a variety of tasks.

In response to these advancements, a range of evaluation benchmarks has been developed to assess the capabilities of LVLMs. These include holistic evaluations that measure general multimodal perception and reasoning, such as LVLM-eHub and MMBench. Additionally, specialized benchmarks focus on particular tasks, including ChartQA for chart understanding, GQA for visual reasoning, DocVQA for document perception, CAPability for caption evaluation, and assessments for optical character recognition (OCR) and adversarial robustness, as well as complex expert-level problems in MathVista and MMMU.

Despite the breadth of existing evaluations, the authors identify a critical gap: the absence of a systematic and comprehensive evaluation of LVLMs on *fine-grained image tasks*. Fine-grained image analysis involves distinguishing between subordinate categories of visual objects (e.g., different species of birds, different models of cars) and is considered fundamental to many computer vision applications. Existing efforts to evaluate LVLMs in fine-grained domains, such as those by Geigle et al. (2024) and Zhang et al. (2024c), are limited, often focusing primarily on classification or involving a restricted number of questions.

This research directly addresses this gap by introducing FG-BMK, a new, comprehensive benchmark specifically designed for fine-grained image tasks. By systematically evaluating LVLMs on these tasks, the work aims to extend the understanding of their capabilities and limitations beyond generic multimodal perception, providing insights into their performance in a domain crucial for applications like biodiversity monitoring, object retrieval, and product recommendation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the observation that while Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) have demonstrated significant advancements in multimodal perception, their performance and capabilities on fine-grained image tasks remain largely unexplored and poorly understood. Fine-grained image tasks require models to discern subtle, discriminative visual patterns and leverage specialized knowledge to differentiate between closely related subordinate categories. The current landscape of LVLM evaluation lacks a dedicated and comprehensive benchmark for these tasks.

The key objectives of this work are:

- **To address the evaluation gap in fine-grained image tasks:** The authors explicitly state their aim to fill the void of systematic evaluation for LVLMs in fine-grained domains, which are considered fundamental to computer vision and crucial for various real-world applications.
- **To introduce a comprehensive fine-grained evaluation benchmark (FG-BMK):** A central objective is to develop and present FG-BMK, a robust benchmark comprising 1.01 million questions and 0.28 million images, designed to provide a thorough testing environment for LVLMs.
- **To systematically examine LVLMs from multiple perspectives:** The benchmark is designed to evaluate LVLMs from both human-oriented (conversational interaction) and machine-oriented (direct feature assessment) perspectives, focusing on their semantic recognition and fine-grained feature representation capabilities. This dual approach aims to provide a holistic understanding of model performance.
- **To uncover key findings and limitations of current LVLMs:** Through extensive experiments using the FG-BMK benchmark, the authors intend to identify specific strengths and weaknesses of existing LVLMs, particularly regarding the influence of training paradigms, modality alignment, susceptibility to perturbations, and their ability to perform fine-grained category reasoning.
- **To provide guidance for future LVLM development:** The insights gained from this comprehensive evaluation are intended to offer practical guidance for future data construction and model design strategies, ultimately contributing to the development of more advanced and robust LVLMs tailored for fine-grained visual understanding.

In essence, the motivation is to illuminate the "capability boundaries" of contemporary LVLMs in a challenging and critical area of computer vision, thereby informing and guiding subsequent research efforts.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

To comprehensively evaluate Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) on fine-grained image tasks, the researchers developed and utilized a new benchmark named FG-BMK. The methodology encompasses data curation, a dual evaluation paradigm, and experiments with a selection of representative models.

#### 3.1. Data Curation

The FG-BMK benchmark comprises 1.01 million questions and 0.28 million images. To ensure data quality, diversity, and mitigate issues prevalent in web-sourced images such as inconsistent quality or mislabeling, images were sourced from 12 established fine-grained datasets. These datasets cover a range of meta-classes, categories, and sample counts, including CUB-200-2011 (birds), Stanford Dogs, Stanford Cars, Food-101, FGVC Aircraft, DeepFashion, and iNat2021. For human-oriented evaluation, questions were constructed based on the test sets of these datasets, while for machine-oriented evaluation, original labels were used.

Question templates were manually designed for each task within the human-oriented evaluation to ensure diversity and comprehensive coverage. An experiment with an expanded set of 10 human-written prompts for multiple-choice questions showed only minor changes in accuracy, indicating that the impact of template quantity on results is negligible as long as the question is clearly stated.

#### 3.2. Evaluation Paradigms

The evaluation framework is divided into two paradigms: human-oriented and machine-oriented.

##### 3.2.1. Human-oriented Evaluation

This paradigm assesses an LVLM's ability to understand visual content and apply domain-specific knowledge through conversational interactions.
- **Question Types:** Three types of questions are employed: true/false, multiple-choice, and short-answer. A response is deemed correct if it includes the ground truth.
- **Tasks:**
 - **Attribute Recognition:** Evaluates the model's ability to identify specific visual attributes (e.g., size, color, length, shape, pattern). This task uses true/false and multiple-choice questions. Negative samples for true/false questions are generated by pairing an image with an incorrect attribute.
 - **Knowledge Bias Estimation:** Utilizes true/false questions to identify potential knowledge biases across different fine-grained categories. Positive samples pair an image with its correct fine-grained label. Negative samples are created by pairing an image with a randomly selected incorrect fine-grained label from other subcategories within the same super-category. Accuracy is calculated for each category to measure understanding.
 - **Hierarchical Granularity Recognition:** Assesses the LVLMs' capacity to leverage domain-specific knowledge to identify object categories across various hierarchical levels (e.g., class, genus, species). This task employs true/false, multiple-choice, and short-answer questions. Negative samples for true/false questions are generated by pairing images with incorrect labels at the same hierarchical level (e.g., Aves vs. Insecta). Multiple-choice options are drawn from categories within the same parent category of the hierarchy. Short-answer questions prompt direct generation of responses.

##### 3.2.2. Machine-oriented Evaluation

This paradigm directly measures the quality of LVLMs' visual feature representations through fundamental vision tasks.
- **Tasks:**
 - **Image Retrieval:** Measures the ability to retrieve images belonging to multiple subordinate categories of a meta-category by calculating the similarity of their visual features.
 - **Image Classification:** Assesses the recognition of images into fine-grained categories, either within a single meta-category (e.g., animal species) or across multiple meta-categories. For multi-meta-category classification, subcategories from different meta-categories are combined into a unified training/testing set.
- **Metrics:** Mean Average Precision (mAP) is used for image retrieval, and Top-1 accuracy is used for image classification, consistent with the DINOv2 approach.

#### 3.3. Models Under Evaluation

The study evaluates twelve representative LVLMs and VLMs. This selection includes nine widely used open-source models (InternVL3-7B, InternVL-Chat, LLaVA-1.5-7B, Qwen2.5-VL-7B, Qwen-VL-Chat, BLIP-2-XL, EVA-CLIP, BEiT3, CoCa), two closed-source models (GPT-4o-1120, Gemini-2.0-flash), and one purely visual model (DINOv2). These models were chosen to represent diverse training strategies, including different combinations of contrastive, generative, image-text matching, reconstruction, and distillation losses, as well as varying vision encoder sizes and language models. For machine-oriented evaluations, earlier model versions were often used to better isolate the effects of distinct characteristics. Official configurations were followed for inference, with open-source model temperatures set to 0.

#### 3.4. Specific Analyses

Beyond standard performance measurement, the methodology includes specific analyses to understand influencing factors:
- **Influence of Training Paradigms:** Comparing performance based on the type of loss function used (contrastive, generative, reconstruction).
- **Impact of Modality Alignment:** Investigating how aligning visual and textual features affects fine-grained discriminability, particularly concerning granularity consistency.
- **Robustness to Perturbations:** Assessing the models' susceptibility to feature perturbations using projected gradient descent.
- **Scaling Effects:** Examining the impact of vision encoder size and the scale of training data on fine-grained discriminability.
- **Comparison with Specialized Models:** Benchmarking LVLMs against state-of-the-art fine-grained tailored models to contextualize their performance.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The comprehensive evaluation of LVLMs on fine-grained image tasks using the FG-BMK benchmark yielded several key findings:

- **Difficulty with Fine-Grained Granularity:** LVLMs demonstrated a struggle to distinguish between excessively fine-grained categories. In human-oriented hierarchical granularity recognition tasks, models like InternVL3 showed a notable decline in accuracy as the level of granularity increased. For example, accuracy on CUB-200-2011 dropped from approximately 99% at the class level to about 62% at the species level, indicating difficulty in differentiating closely related species. Similar trends were observed across other evaluated LVLMs.

- **Knowledge Bias Attributed to Training Data Imbalance:** LVLMs exhibited inconsistent recognition accuracy across different fine-grained categories. For instance, LLaVA showed performance ranging from nearly 90% for some categories to approximately 30% for others in true/false questions. This inconsistency was hypothesized to stem from uneven representation of fine-grained knowledge in the training data, rather than inherent category difficulty. Fine-tuning LVLMs on datasets with balanced category occurrences significantly improved consistency across categories, supporting the hypothesis that training data distribution is a primary cause of knowledge bias, potentially inherited from the underlying language models.

- **Limited Attribute Recognition Capabilities:** LVLMs showed significant room for improvement in recognizing fine-grained visual attributes. While attributes like pattern, size, and length were recognized with relatively higher accuracy (e.g., InternVL3 and Qwen2.5-VL achieving around 50% for pattern recognition), recognition of attributes such as shape was considerably weaker (around 30%). Some attributes scored as low as 10%. Although recent LVLMs showed advancements in pattern and length recognition, improvements in color and shape recognition were more limited.

- **Underperformance Compared to Fine-Grained Tailored Models:** Despite their general advancements, LVLMs did not outperform specialized fine-grained tailored models in fine-grained classification tasks. Whether evaluated via short-answer questions or linear classifiers on visual features, LVLMs achieved lower accuracy than state-of-the-art fine-grained specific models. This disparity is attributed to LVLMs being primarily optimized for general tasks, lacking the specialized architectural designs or training objectives focused on fine-grained distinctions found in tailored models.

- **Superiority of Contrastive Training Paradigms:** In machine-oriented evaluations, vision encoders trained with contrastive learning paradigms (e.g., EVA-CLIP, InternVL, DINOv2) consistently outperformed those trained with generative (Qwen) or reconstruction-based (BEiT3) paradigms in fine-grained image retrieval and classification tasks. Nemenyi statistical tests confirmed these models performed significantly better. Contrastive models also maintained higher accuracy when classifying across multiple meta-categories, with smaller performance drops compared to generative/reconstruction models. This suggests that contrastive training effectively enhances the fine-grained discriminability of visual features.

- **Detrimental Effect of Modality Alignment Inconsistencies:** The alignment strategy crucial to LVLMs was found to potentially impair the fine-grained discriminability of visual features. LLaVA's original visual features exhibited superior classification accuracy (an average of 3.39% higher) compared to features that had undergone alignment with textual features. This performance decline was linked to inconsistencies between visual and textual feature spaces, particularly "granularity inconsistencies" where fine-grained visual objects were often paired with coarse-grained textual descriptions in the alignment data. Retraining the alignment module with text descriptions matching the fine-grained granularity of images led to an improvement in classification accuracy, confirming the negative impact of granularity mismatch.

- **Limited Gains from Scaling Model Size or Data Volume:** Increasing the vision encoder size (e.g., DINOv2 from B to L to G) yielded only marginal improvements in average classification accuracy (0.6% and 0.3% respectively). Similarly, models trained on a larger scale of web data (e.g., EVA-CLIP with 2 billion samples or InternVL with 6 billion samples) did not necessarily outperform models trained on smaller, curated datasets (e.g., DINOv2 with 142 million samples) in fine-grained classification and retrieval. This suggests that simply scaling model parameters or raw data volume, without corresponding attention to data quality and fine-grained relevance, offers limited benefits for enhancing fine-grained discriminability.

- **Increased Susceptibility to Perturbations in Fine-Grained Tasks:** LVLMs' visual features were found to be more vulnerable to perturbations in fine-grained tasks compared to generic vision tasks. For instance, perturbing EVA-CLIP's features resulted in a substantial accuracy drop on CUB-200-2011 (from 88.95% to 24.94%), while the drop on the generic CIFAR-100 dataset was less pronounced (from 93.05% to 50.76%). This vulnerability is attributed to coarse-grained or noisy training data limiting the development of robust, discriminative features for subtle fine-grained differences. Vision Transformers trained on ImageNet with cross-entropy loss demonstrated superior robustness.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of artificial intelligence and computer vision by providing a systematic and comprehensive evaluation of Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) on fine-grained image tasks, an area previously underexplored. The introduction of the FG-BMK benchmark, comprising 1.01 million questions and 0.28 million images, offers a valuable and robust resource for future research.

The key findings from this work hold several significances and potential impacts:

- **Identifies Critical Limitations:** The study systematically uncovers specific limitations of current LVLMs in handling fine-grained visual distinctions. These limitations span various aspects, including their ability to discern subtle category differences, recognize fine-grained attributes, mitigate knowledge biases, and maintain robustness under feature perturbations. This provides a clearer understanding of where current LVLMs fall short.
- **Informs Model Design and Training Strategies:** The insights derived from the evaluation offer practical guidance for the development of future LVLMs.
 - The observation that contrastive training paradigms enhance fine-grained discriminability suggests prioritizing such approaches in model pre-training.
 - The identified issues with modality alignment, particularly granularity inconsistencies, highlight the necessity of curating more precisely aligned image-text data or developing alignment mechanisms that preserve fine-grained details.
 - The vulnerability to perturbations in fine-grained tasks indicates a need for improved robustness techniques, potentially through adversarial training or the incorporation of higher-quality, fine-grained specific data.
- **Highlights Areas for Specialized Improvement:** The finding that LVLMs currently lag behind fine-grained tailored models underscores the potential for integrating specialized architectural components or training objectives into general-purpose LVLMs. This could lead to hybrid models that combine broad multimodal capabilities with strong fine-grained discrimination.
- **Guides Data Curation Efforts:** The evidence of knowledge bias stemming from uneven representation in training data emphasizes the importance of balanced and representative fine-grained datasets for pre-training LVLMs, as well as the need for better methods to inject expert-level fine-grained knowledge.
- **Facilitates Reproducibility and Further Research:** By open-sourcing the FG-BMK code, the authors enable the research community to reproduce their findings, conduct comparative studies, and extend the benchmark. This fosters transparency and accelerates advancements in evaluating and developing LVLMs for fine-grained understanding.
- **Addresses Ethical Considerations:** The explicit statement regarding the ethical compliance of the datasets used, ensuring anonymization and adherence to licenses, sets a standard for responsible AI research, particularly when dealing with sensitive domains like medical images (e.g., SkinCon).

Overall, this research lays a foundational groundwork for advancing LVLM performance on fine-grained visual tasks by clearly identifying current challenges and providing data-driven directions for future improvements in model architecture, training methodologies, and data curation practices.
