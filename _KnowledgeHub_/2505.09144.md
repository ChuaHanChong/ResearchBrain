# Latent Theory of Mind: A Decentralized Diffusion Architecture for Cooperative Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Stanford University and the National University of Singapore developed Latent Theory of Mind (LatentToM), a decentralized diffusion policy architecture for cooperative robot manipulation. LatentToM achieves robust multi-robot coordination, performing comparably to state-of-the-art centralized policies, by integrating sheaf theory for numerical consistency with cognitively-inspired constraints for expressive consensus representations.

## Problem

- Centralized diffusion policies, while effective for single or dual-arm tasks, suffer from scalability issues, increased training complexity, and fragility in multi-robot systems.
- Decentralized multi-robot systems struggle to achieve coherent and cooperative behavior due to partial observations, domain shifts, and difficulty in maintaining a consistent understanding of shared task states.
- Existing multi-agent learning methods lack a robust, expressive, and numerically consistent consensus representation that can effectively integrate local information for complex cooperative manipulation tasks, especially in out-of-distribution scenarios.

## Method

- **LatentToM** introduces a decentralized ==diffusion policy== architecture where each arm processes ego-centric and consensus observations through separate encoders, creating a structured latent embedding.
- ==Sheaf theory== is applied to formalize the dual-arm system, introducing a ==Sheaf Consistency Loss (L_nc)== to enforce numerical alignment and a coherent global state between the consensus embeddings of different arms.
- To ensure expressiveness and prevent trivial consensus, a ==Theory of Mind (ToM)-inspired constraint (L_tom)== compels each arm's consensus embedding to predict the other arm's ego-centric embedding, alongside a ==directional consensus mechanism (L_conf)== that uses learned confidence scores to guide consistency.

## Results

- In the "Push T-shaped Block" task with asymmetric friction, LatentToM with the optional Sheaf Laplacian (communicative mode) achieved **full** task completion, matching the performance of a centralized diffusion policy and significantly outperforming naive decentralized baselines.
- For the "Pouring Coffee Beans" task, LatentToM (without communication) showed strong performance (**13/15** fully successful), while LatentToM with Sheaf Laplacian achieved **14/15** fully successful tasks, comparable to the centralized baseline's perfect score and outperforming naive decentralized methods.
- The proposed architecture demonstrates robust and reliable cooperative behavior in complex manipulation tasks, effectively managing coordination and adapting to dynamic environmental factors without requiring full centralized control or extensive explicit communication.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Sheaf theory provides a mathematically principled framework for building numerically consistent consensus representations from locally distributed observations in decentralized multi-agent systems.
> - Integrating a ToM-inspired cross-agent prediction loss in the latent space forces the consensus representation to encode rich, semantically meaningful information about other agents' states and the global context, preventing collapse into uninformative states.
> - Combining these structural consistency and expressiveness constraints enables decentralized diffusion policies to achieve cooperative manipulation capabilities that rival centralized approaches, even in challenging out-of-distribution scenarios.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research sits at the intersection of several rapidly evolving fields within robotics and artificial intelligence: robotic manipulation, diffusion policies, and multi-agent systems, particularly decentralized control.

**Robotic Manipulation with Diffusion Policies:**
The paper acknowledges the significant advancements in robotic arm manipulation, which is critical for diverse applications from industrial automation to medical procedures. Recently, "Diffusion Policy" has emerged as a state-of-the-art solution, excelling in generating smooth, multi-modal, and long-horizon trajectories. These models, leveraging the power of diffusion models to process high-dimensional data, have shown impressive performance in various manipulation tasks, including dual-arm scenarios. This work builds directly on this foundation, aiming to extend the capabilities of diffusion policies.

**The Challenge of Centralized Control in Multi-Agent Systems:**
A key limitation identified by the authors is that most current successful applications of diffusion policies, especially for multi-arm tasks, rely on centralized frameworks. While effective for up to two arms with fused data and actions, centralized policies suffer from inherent fragilities: poor scalability as the number of agents grows, increased difficulty in training, and sensitivity to single-point failures. Moreover, there's a scarcity of specialized multi-arm datasets that explicitly capture compelling examples of collaboration, making centralized training harder. This motivates a shift towards decentralized methods, a widely recognized trend in the broader multi-agent learning community, where robustness and flexibility are paramount.

**Decentralized Collaboration and Consensus Learning:**
Achieving coherent and cooperative behavior in a decentralized manner is a non-trivial challenge. Each robot operates with partial observations, potentially leading to inconsistencies and failures, especially in delicate cooperative tasks. The core problem is designing a "consensus representation" that can capture shared, overlapping information across agents despite their local and incomplete observations. The paper highlights this gap: how to enable decentralized policies to align partial information while maintaining agent independence for complex, cooperative tasks.

**Novel Integration of Sheaf Theory and "Theory of Mind":**
The distinguishing aspect of this work is its innovative use of **sheaf theory** and the concept of **Theory of Mind (ToM)** in a latent space. Sheaf theory provides a principled mathematical framework for integrating locally distributed information into a globally consistent structure, naturally aligning with decentralized learning paradigms where agents operate on partial observations. This mathematical rigor offers a novel approach to formalizing global task representation and extracting consistent intermediate embeddings. Furthermore, the "Theory of Mind" concept, usually associated with cognitive science and advanced AI, is adapted here to ensure the expressiveness of the consensus representation, preventing it from collapsing into trivial or uninformative states. This combination is particularly novel in the context of diffusion policies for cooperative manipulation, aiming to provide a robust and theoretically grounded solution to decentralized control.

In summary, this work addresses a crucial limitation of existing state-of-the-art robotic manipulation policies (diffusion policies) by decentralizing them. It introduces sophisticated mathematical tools (sheaf theory) and cognitive-inspired mechanisms (latent ToM) to achieve robust, scalable, and coherent cooperative behavior in multi-robot systems, pushing the boundaries of what is possible in learning-based multi-agent robotics.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a decentralized diffusion policy architecture, named **Latent Theory of Mind (LatentToM)**, for collaborative robot manipulation. This architecture aims to enable multiple manipulators, each equipped with its own perception and computational capabilities, to work together towards a common task goal. A crucial aspect of this objective is to achieve this collaboration **with or without explicit communication** between the agents.

The motivation for pursuing this objective stems from several key challenges and limitations observed in the current state of robotic manipulation:

- **Scalability and Robustness of Centralized Policies:** While centralized diffusion policies have demonstrated impressive performance in single and dual-arm manipulation tasks, they inherently suffer from limitations in scalability, training complexity, and fragility when dealing with larger numbers of agents or more complex, dynamic environments. A single point of failure or an inability to adapt to local disturbances can lead to system-wide failure.
- **Difficulty of Multi-Agent Data Collection:** Training centralized policies for multi-arm tasks is often hampered by the scarcity and difficulty of collecting comprehensive multi-agent training data that explicitly captures collaborative behaviors.
- **Maintaining Coherence in Decentralized Systems:** Decentralized frameworks offer advantages in robustness and flexibility, but achieving coherent and cooperative behavior is challenging. Each agent operates with partial observations and may experience domain shifts, making it difficult to maintain a consistent understanding of the global task state and other agents' intentions. Slight inconsistencies can lead to task failure in delicate cooperative tasks.
- **Need for an Expressive and Consistent Consensus Representation:** To overcome the challenges of partial observations and local inconsistencies, there is a fundamental need to design a robust consensus representation. This representation must effectively capture overlapping information across all agents, allowing them to form a unified understanding of the shared environment and task, even if their individual observations are incomplete. This consensus representation must not only be numerically consistent but also retain sufficient information richness to guide complex actions.

In essence, the authors are motivated to bridge the gap between the powerful action generation capabilities of diffusion models and the inherent advantages of decentralized control for multi-robot systems. They aim to achieve this by introducing novel mechanisms that foster deep, shared understanding among robots without constant explicit communication, thereby paving the way for more robust and scalable multi-robot applications.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

LatentToM proposes a novel decentralized diffusion policy architecture designed for cooperative manipulation, with an emphasis on robust consensus building among agents. The methodology can be broken down into several key components:

#### 3.1. Model Training: LatentToM Architecture

The architecture is designed for `N` independent robotic arms, demonstrated with a two-arm system.

- **Structured Observation and Latent Embeddings:** Each arm `u` has its own observations `o_u`. These are explicitly divided into two subspaces:
 - `o_ego_u`: Ego-centric observations, such as the arm's end-effector camera image and pose, representing private, robot-specific information.
 - `o_con_u`: Consensus observations, corresponding to a shared third-person view of the workspace, capturing global scene information.
 Two distinct visual encoders, `ϕ_ego_u` and `ϕ_con_u`, process these observations separately to produce:
 - `h_ego_u = ϕ_ego_u(o_ego_u)`: The ego embedding.
 - `h_con_u = ϕ_con_u(o_con_u)`: The consensus embedding.
 The complete embedding for arm `u` is `h_u = [h_ego_u, h_con_u]`.

- **Sheaf Theory for Numerical Consistency:**
 - The dual-arm system is formalized as a cellular sheaf `F` over a graph `G` where each arm is a node.
 - The vector space assigned to each node `u` is its consensus embedding: `F(u) = h_con_u`.
 - For an edge `e=(u,v)` between two arms, the overlapping subspace `F(e)` is assumed to be `R^d_con`, the consensus embedding space. Restriction maps `ρ_u→e` and `ρ_v→e` project the consensus embeddings to this shared space.
 - A **Sheaf Consistency Loss (L_nc)** is introduced, derived from the first-order cohomology of the sheaf:
 `L_nc = Σ_e=(u,v)∈E ||ρ_u→e(h_con_u) - ρ_v→e(h_con_v)||^2_2`
 - Minimizing `L_nc` encourages numerical consistency and a coherent global state where consensus embeddings across all nodes are aligned.

- **Structural Constraints for Expressiveness:** To prevent the consensus embedding `h_con_u` from collapsing into a trivial, uninformative representation, two additional constraints are introduced:
 - **Theory of Mind (ToM)-inspired Constraint (L_tom):** Each arm `u` is trained to predict the ego embedding `h_ego_v` of the other arm `v` using its *own* consensus embedding `h_con_u`. This is achieved through a cross-agent prediction function `ψ_u→v` (a multi-head attention architecture, as described in Appendix A.1).
 `L_tom = Σ_(u,v)∈E ||h_ego_v - ψ_u→v(h_con_u, h_ego_v)||^2_2 + (v ↔ u)`
 This loss forces `h_con_u` to retain rich semantic information about the global context, allowing it to infer aspects of the other agent's internal state.
 - **Directional Consensus Mechanism (L_conf):** Acknowledging that observation quality might differ, each arm `u` learns a confidence score `c_u ∈` for its `h_con_u` using a lightweight confidence predictor (shared trunk, head-specific bias, Appendix A.2). This enables a one-way consistency, where the lower-confidence embedding is guided towards the higher-confidence one.
 `L_conf = (1 + |∆c|) * [1_(c_u≥c_v) * ||h_con_v - h_con_u|| + 1_(c_v≥c_u) * ||h_con_u - h_con_v||] + λ_ent * [H(c_u) + H(c_v)]`
 The `difference-weighting` term penalizes alignment errors based on confidence disparity, while an `entropy term` `H(c)` regularizes confidence scores to prevent collapse and stabilize learning.

- **Total Auxiliary Loss:** The overall training objective combines these losses: `L_tot = αL_nc + βL_tom + γL_conf`, where `α, β, γ` are hyperparameters.

#### 3.2. Model Inference: Decentralized vs. Communicative Execution

The trained LatentToM policy allows for two modes of inference:

- **Fully Decentralized (No Communication):** Each robot independently generates trajectories based on its partial observations, inferring consensus without explicit information exchange. The paper notes this can lead to issues like livelocks if consensus embeddings drift.
- **With Communication (Sheaf Laplacian):** To enhance stability and mitigate decision-making deviations, an optional **Sheaf Laplacian** is introduced as an online adjustment mechanism. This involves a classic bidirectional consistency synchronization operator (Equation 5, shown to be a low-order sheaf Laplacian step in Appendix B) that iteratively adjusts `h_con_u` and `h_con_v` by sharing them once per inference step. This promotes consistency at the cost of minimal communication.

The core approach is to build a robust, expressive, and consistent shared understanding (consensus embedding) among decentralized agents through a theoretically grounded mathematical framework (sheaf theory) combined with cognitive-inspired constraints (ToM) and a practical mechanism for handling uncertainty (directional consensus).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluates LatentToM against several baselines in two cooperative manipulation tasks: "Push T-shaped Block" and "Pouring Coffee Beans." The baselines include a Centralized Diffusion Policy (CDP), a Naive Decentralized Diffusion Policy (NDDP), and a Naive Consensus-based Decentralized Diffusion Policy (NCDDP). LatentToM is tested in both non-communicative and Sheaf Laplacian-enhanced (communicative) modes.

#### 4.1. Task 1: Push T-shaped Block

This task requires two arms to push a T-shaped block while maintaining its initial orientation. The key challenge is introduced by using out-of-distribution (OOD) asymmetric friction coefficients on the block's underside, demanding precise coordination to counteract these dynamics.

- **Centralized Diffusion Policy (CDP):** Performed near-perfectly, successfully accounting for the asymmetric dynamics due to its complete information.
- **Naive Decentralized Diffusion Policy (NDDP):** Failed significantly with large rotational errors, highlighting its inability to coordinate actions effectively against the OOD dynamics.
- **Naive Consensus-based Decentralized Diffusion Policy (NCDDP):** Also failed, suggesting that merely attempting a shared consensus embedding without the proposed structural constraints (ToM, directional consensus) is insufficient to capture informative environmental changes.
- **LatentToM (without communication):** Partially completed the task, indicating an improved ability to coordinate compared to naive decentralized methods.
- **LatentToM with Sheaf Laplacian (SL):** Achieved full task completion, comparable to the CDP. This demonstrates that the combined mechanisms (ToM, directional consensus ensuring an informative embedding, and Sheaf Laplacian for noise mitigation) enable agents to robustly account for OOD changes and achieve high levels of consensual collaboration.

#### 4.2. Task 2: Pouring Coffee Beans

This task involves two arms collaboratively pouring coffee beans from a cup into a small pot and then returning to a resting position. Success depends on real-time interpretation of intentions and tightly coupled coordination to avoid spillage or collisions.

- **Centralized Diffusion Policy (CDP):** Achieved perfect performance (15/15 Fully Successful, FS), completing the task without errors.
- **Naive Decentralized Diffusion Policy (NDDP):** Performed the worst (7 FS, 8 Clear Failures, CF). This confirms frequent miscoordination and highlights the difficulty of independent policies in complex cooperative tasks.
- **Naive Consensus-based Decentralized Diffusion Policy (NCDDP):** Showed an improvement over NDDP (9 FS, 4 CF, 1 Arm Collision, AC, 1 Spill Out, SO), indicating that basic consensus attempts can reduce failures, but still lacked robust recovery and fully coherent behavior.
- **LatentToM (without communication):** Demonstrated strong performance with 13 FS and only 2 instances of "No Return" (NR), where an arm failed to fully retract but without collisions or major spillage. This shows robust and reliable behavior even without explicit communication.
- **LatentToM with Sheaf Laplacian (SL):** Achieved 14 FS and only 1 minor "Spill Out" (SO), attributed to bean dynamics rather than coordination failure. This performance is highly comparable to the state-of-the-art centralized policy (CDP), confirming its effectiveness and stability.

**Overall Findings:**
- LatentToM, especially with the Sheaf Laplacian for online adjustment, achieves cooperative performance comparable to state-of-the-art centralized diffusion policies for bi-manual manipulation.
- It significantly outperforms naive decentralized approaches, demonstrating the critical role of structured coordination mechanisms (sheaf theory, ToM-inspired constraints, directional consensus) in fostering effective collaboration.
- The method is shown to be naturally robust to temporary robot failure or delays (implied by the successful handling of bean dynamics and non-return cases), a key advantage over centralized policies that can catastrophically fail under such conditions.
- The ability to operate without explicit communication (LatentToM without SL) while still achieving high success rates underscores its potential for resource-constrained environments.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The LatentToM architecture represents a significant step forward in the field of multi-robot cooperative manipulation, particularly in extending the capabilities of powerful diffusion policies to decentralized settings. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Enabling Scalable and Robust Multi-Robot Systems:** The most immediate impact is providing an effective pathway for scaling diffusion-based control policies beyond single or dual-arm centralized systems to larger and more complex multi-robot deployments. By addressing the inherent fragility and scalability limitations of centralized approaches, LatentToM paves the way for more robust and fault-tolerant multi-robot applications in real-world scenarios. The demonstrated robustness to OOD dynamics and temporary failures is a crucial advantage for practical deployment.

- **Bridging Theoretical Foundations with Practical Applications:** The novel integration of **sheaf theory** provides a principled mathematical framework for achieving consensus learning in decentralized systems. This rigorous foundation, combined with the practical application of diffusion policies and a cognitively inspired **"Theory of Mind"** concept in latent space, offers a powerful paradigm for designing intelligent, cooperative agents. This methodological innovation could inspire further interdisciplinary research combining abstract mathematical concepts with deep learning for robotics.

- **Reducing Communication Overhead in Multi-Agent Systems:** LatentToM offers the flexibility of operating with or without explicit communication. The ability to achieve high performance in a fully decentralized, no-communication mode is highly impactful for scenarios with limited bandwidth, intermittent connectivity, or privacy concerns. When communication is available, the minimal, one-step exchange facilitated by the Sheaf Laplacian provides a lightweight yet effective mechanism for enhancing stability, offering a practical trade-off between robustness and communication cost.

- **Advancing Cooperative Manipulation Capabilities:** The experimental results demonstrate LatentToM's ability to tackle complex cooperative tasks that require precise coordination, real-time intention inference, and adaptation to unexpected environmental factors (e.g., asymmetric friction, dynamic pouring). This directly contributes to developing more advanced manipulation capabilities essential for next-generation robotic systems, including those involving dexterous humanoid robots.

- **Foundation for Future Research:** The paper openly discusses its limitations, such as current testing on only two arms and reliance on a fixed third-person camera. These limitations, however, highlight clear avenues for future research, including:
 - **Scaling to Larger Agent Numbers:** Exploring the scalability of LatentToM to many robots, potentially through hierarchical or sub-system training approaches.
 - **Enhanced Perception and Robustness:** Developing methods to fuse multiple third-person views or create view-invariant representations to improve robustness in environments with occlusions or limited sensor coverage.
 - **More Complex Collaborative Tasks:** Applying the framework to tasks requiring even more intricate and dynamic multi-robot interaction.

In essence, LatentToM offers a significant contribution by presenting a robust, scalable, and theoretically grounded decentralized control architecture for cooperative robot manipulation. Its success in matching centralized baselines while offering the benefits of decentralization could accelerate the deployment of intelligent multi-robot systems across various critical domains.
