# VTAM: Video-Tactile-Action Models for Complex Physical Interaction Beyond VLAs

> [!summary] Summary
> VTAM (Video-Tactile-Action Models) integrates high-resolution tactile sensing with multi-view visual observations into a predictive world model for robotic manipulation. This framework improves robustness and stability in complex, contact-rich tasks, achieving success rates of 90% in chip pick-and-place, 85% in cucumber peeling, and 95% in whiteboard wiping.

## Problem

- Vision-only robot control models (VAMs, VLAs) often fail in contact-rich manipulation due to partial observability or occlusion of critical interaction states like force or slip.
- Existing tactile integration methods frequently suffer from 'modality collapse', where dominant visual signals suppress the influence of high-frequency tactile inputs, or require models to implicitly infer complex contact physics in semantic spaces.
- Generative world models typically rely on visual prediction, providing only indirect access to critical physical interaction signals at the contact interface, making it difficult to anticipate failure modes.

## Method

- VTAM develops a unified visuo-tactile world action model that projects multi-view visual and high-resolution tactile streams into a shared latent space via a pretrained VAE, leveraging a multi-view diffusion process within a video transformer for temporal dynamics.
- A deformation-aware regularization mechanism, utilizing a 3D virtual force proxy derived from tactile optical flow, is introduced to counteract modality collapse and ensure tactile signals influence action policy gradients.
- The model is trained with a two-stage optimization objective: first fine-tuning for joint visuo-tactile latent dynamics, then optimizing the control policy with a conditional joint action-state-force denoising process.

## Results

- VTAM achieved success rates of 90% for chip pick-and-place, 85% for cucumber peeling, and 95% for whiteboard wiping, significantly outperforming vision-only and naïvely integrated tactile baselines.
- Ablation studies confirmed the critical role of the hierarchical visuo-tactile world modeling and the virtual-force regularization, with their removal leading to substantial performance drops (0% and 10% success rates respectively in chip pick-and-place).
- Qualitative analysis demonstrated VTAM's ability to perform tactile-aware grasping (verifying successful contact), maintain stable contact during peeling, and regulate consistent forces during wiping despite visual ambiguities.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Directly integrating tactile sensing into a predictive world-model framework allows for explicit modeling of contact dynamics and better correction of visual estimation errors in physical interaction.
> - A geometrically grounded virtual force proxy, derived from optical flow in tactile data, provides effective auxiliary supervision to prevent visual dominance and ensure tactile signals are influential in multimodal learning.
> - A two-stage training approach effectively adapts a pretrained visual backbone to learn coherent visuo-tactile world representations before optimizing a control policy with joint action, state, and virtual force denoising.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of embodied intelligence has seen notable advancements through the development of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models and Video-Action Models (VAMs). These models leverage large-scale multimodal alignment and implicit world dynamics learned from visual streams to achieve generalist robot control and long-horizon task execution. By embedding visual observations and language instructions into shared latent spaces, VLA models have demonstrated generalization across diverse manipulation tasks and environments.

However, existing VLA and VAM architectures exhibit limitations in scenarios requiring fine-grained physical interaction, particularly those characterized by contact-rich manipulation. Visual observation alone often provides only partial observability of critical interaction states, such as precise force modulation, incipient slip, or delicate object deformation. These phenomena are challenging to infer accurately from visual cues, leading to unstable or imprecise robotic behaviors when handling fragile, deformable, or slippery objects.

Current approaches to integrating tactile information into VLA architectures typically involve projecting tactile embeddings into a pre-trained vision-language latent space or concatenating tactile features with language-conditioned visual representations in the downstream policy. While these methods expose the model to tactile inputs, they often impose a substantial burden on representation learning. The models are required to implicitly infer contact physics within semantic embedding spaces that are primarily optimized for visual alignment and static scene description, rather than dynamic physical prediction. This can necessitate extensive annotated data and does not guarantee the faithful capture of high-frequency contact dynamics or the causal relationships between successive tactile frames, which are crucial for anticipating failure modes. Furthermore, many VLA backbones prioritize semantic alignment, potentially underutilizing tactile signals for detailed spatial and temporal reasoning.

Generative world models, a related area, aim to forecast future environment states for planning and policy learning. While significant progress has been made in this domain, most existing world models primarily encode environmental dynamics through visual prediction. Visual forecasting captures object motion and scene evolution but offers indirect access to the direct physical interaction signals occurring at the contact interface. Weakly observable or visually occluded phenomena like slip, deformation, and force transfer are critical for delicate interactions, and models relying solely on visual dynamics may struggle to predict these failure modes.

VTAM addresses these identified limitations by integrating tactile sensing into a *predictive world-model framework*. Unlike reactive tactile fusion methods, VTAM focuses on jointly predicting the future evolution of visual and tactile streams. This approach aims to explicitly model contact dynamics within the generative backbone, providing a more physically grounded representation that can correct visual estimation errors and improve robustness in contact-rich manipulation. The work positions itself as a scalable method for physically grounded embodied foundation models by leveraging efficient cross-modal representation learning and addressing the issue of modality collapse, thereby enhancing the capabilities of generalist robot control in complex physical environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a generalist Video-Tactile Action Model (VTAM) capable of robust and stable control in complex, contact-rich robotic manipulation tasks. The underlying motivation stems from the inherent limitations of current vision-centric robot control models, particularly Video-Action Models (VAMs) and Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, when confronted with scenarios demanding precise physical interaction.

The key motivations driving the development of VTAM are:

1. **Addressing Visual Limitations in Contact-Rich Scenarios:** While VAMs demonstrate proficiency in tasks relying on visual reasoning, they often falter in contact-rich manipulation. Critical interaction states, such as fine-grained force modulation, object deformation, and contact transitions (e.g., slip, incipient contact), are frequently partially observable or entirely occluded from visual perspectives. Vision alone provides indirect access to the physical dynamics governing these interactions, making it insufficient for delicate or force-sensitive tasks.
2. **Leveraging Direct Physical Feedback:** Tactile sensing offers direct access to the instantaneous contact dynamics between the robot and its environment. This modality provides high-frequency, localized information about pressure, shear, and surface deformation, which is crucial for tasks involving fragile, deformable, or slippery objects. The motivation is to fully integrate this essential physical feedback to augment the robot's perception and control capabilities.
3. **Overcoming Shortcomings of Existing Tactile Integration Methods:** Current methods for integrating tactile data often treat it as a supplementary input, either by projecting tactile embeddings into a pre-trained vision-language space or concatenating features reactively. These approaches frequently require models to implicitly infer complex contact physics within a semantic space optimized for visual data, leading to a heavy burden on representation learning. They may also struggle to capture temporal dynamics or lead to "modality collapse," where dominant visual signals suppress the influence of tactile inputs during training.
4. **Enabling Predictive Reasoning for Contact Dynamics:** Effective contact manipulation requires not only recognizing current contact states but also anticipating their evolution over time. The research is motivated by the need for a framework that can learn temporally consistent visuo-tactile features and perform predictive reasoning over force trends, allowing the robot to anticipate critical transitions and potential failure modes, particularly for brittle objects.
5. **Achieving Scalable and Efficient Multimodal Learning:** The goal is to develop an efficient cross-modal representation learning framework that integrates tactile streams with a pretrained video transformer. This integration should ideally occur without requiring extensive tactile-language paired data or independent, large-scale tactile pretraining, thereby contributing to the development of scalable embodied foundation models.
6. **Stabilizing Multimodal Fusion:** A significant challenge in multimodal learning is modality collapse, where one modality (often vision) dominates, diminishing the influence of others (like tactile feedback). The motivation is to devise mechanisms, such as regularization techniques, to enforce balanced cross-modal attention and ensure that tactile signals remain influential in the action model.

In summary, the key objective is to advance robotic manipulation capabilities in physically interactive scenarios by developing VTAM, which directly incorporates tactile perception into a predictive world modeling framework, thereby improving robustness, precision, and stability in contact-rich tasks where vision-only models are inadequate.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

VTAM employs a unified visuo-tactile world action model designed for contact-rich manipulation, integrating high-resolution tactile sensing with multi-view visual observations. The methodology can be broken down into three primary components: Vision-Tactile Latent World Modeling, Deformation-Aware Regularization, and a two-stage Optimization Objective.

#### 3.1. Vision–Tactile Latent World Modeling via Multi-View Diffusion

- **Multimodal Latent Space Projection:** VTAM begins by projecting both multi-view visual observations (e.g., first-person and third-person camera feeds) and high-resolution tactile streams (e.g., GelSight sensor data) into a shared continuous latent space. This is achieved using a pretrained Variational Autoencoder (VAE), specifically a video VAE encoder. The reconstruction-oriented objective of the VAE is leveraged to preserve fine-grained spatial details, such as subtle surface deformations and texture variations crucial for encoding shear, slip, and pressure from tactile sensors. This approach allows for efficient modality transfer without the need for a specialized tactile-specific backbone.
- **Temporal Dynamics Modeling:** To capture the temporal evolution of interactions, the encoded latents are processed through a multi-view diffusion process within a high-capacity video transformer. This architecture is designed to model both intra-frame deformation structures and inter-frame contact evolution. The model performs predictive reasoning over force trends, which is intended to enable anticipation of critical transitions in contact.
- **Alternating Attention Blocks:** The latent representations from different modalities and views are processed through a sequence of alternating attention blocks. Each block first applies intra-view self-attention independently to each modality (e.g., vision from camera 1, vision from camera 2, tactile) to capture modality-specific spatial structures. Subsequently, these updated tokens are concatenated across all views, and a cross-view self-attention operation is applied to model inter-modal interactions. This iterative process gradually builds a dense visuo-tactile representation of the joint scene and contact dynamics.

#### 3.2. Deformation-Aware Regularization via Virtual Force Prediction

- **Addressing Modality Collapse:** A central challenge in jointly optimizing visual and tactile modalities is modality collapse, where dominant visual gradients suppress the influence of localized, high-frequency tactile signals. This can lead to the policy becoming overly reliant on vision and neglecting crucial tactile feedback.
- **Virtual Force Proxy:** To counteract this, VTAM introduces a deformation-aware auxiliary objective that provides direct supervision to the tactile pathway. Instead of relying on external force-torque sensors, which impose hardware constraints, a 3D virtual force proxy `F_v_t = [f_x, f_y, f_z]` is derived directly from the optical flow (`u_t = (u_x, u_y)`) between a no-contact reference frame and the current tactile frame.
 - `f_x` and `f_y` represent the spatial expectations of the optical flow components, encoding tangential shear.
 - `f_z` approximates normal compression via the divergence of the optical flow (`∇ ⋅ u_t`), leveraging the property that pressing a deformable elastomer induces an outward expansion of its surface pattern.
 - This derived signal serves as a geometrically grounded proxy for force rather than a physically calibrated force measurement.
- **Integration into Action Head:** This compact virtual force proxy acts as an auxiliary supervision signal during action training. It is incorporated as an additional component in the joint denoising target of a conditional flow-matching objective. This mechanism aims to bind the control gradients to the tactile representations, preserving deformation-sensitive information in the latent space and maintaining balanced multimodal gradients throughout optimization.

#### 3.3. Optimization Objective

VTAM employs a two-stage training strategy to adapt the pretrained visual backbone for multimodal visuo-tactile modeling:

- **Stage I: Multi-View Visuo–Tactile Latent Flow Matching:**
 - The goal of this stage is to fine-tune the backbone exclusively to model joint visuo-tactile latent dynamics, establishing a coherent multimodal world representation.
 - The flow matching formulation is applied to model the forward dynamics of future VAE-encoded latent sequences (`z_0`), encompassing both camera views and the GelSight stream.
 - The loss `L_stage1` is applied only to future prediction frames, excluding the initial conditioning frames. This stage adapts the backbone to capture the physical interplay between macroscopic visual dynamics and microscopic tactile deformations.
- **Stage II: Conditional Joint Action–State–Force Denoising:**
 - Following the establishment of a robust visuo-tactile world model in Stage I, the control policy is optimized.
 - Action generation is formulated as a conditional flow-matching process. The joint denoising target (`z_0`) is constructed by concatenating the robot's future action (`a`), the virtual force (`f`), and the proprioceptive state (`s`). Actions are represented as 6-DoF end-effector pose and 1D gripper width, while the proprioceptive state is 16-dimensional.
 - The network predicts the joint velocity field conditioned on the current state token.
 - The flow-matching objectives for action (`L_action`) and state (`L_state`) components track optimal denoising trajectories.
 - These are integrated with the virtual force regularization (`L_force`) from Section 3.2 to form the complete Stage II objective `L_stage2 = L_action + λ1*L_state + λ2*L_force`.
 - Flow matching's regression of a normalized velocity field is noted to naturally scale target variances across action, state, and force dimensions, reducing the need for aggressive hyperparameter balancing. Joint state prediction further introduces a dynamics-consistency constraint.

#### 3.4. Experimental Setup and Data Collection

Experiments are conducted on a 6-DoF xArm6 robotic manipulator with a parallel gripper, a GelSight Mini tactile sensor on the gripper, and two Intel RealSense D455 RGB-D cameras. Data collection and action execution run at 30 Hz. Real-world visuo-tactile datasets are collected through manual teleoperation for three tasks: potato chip pick-and-place (100 trajectories), cucumber peeling (61 trajectories), and whiteboard wiping (105 trajectories).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of VTAM was conducted on real-world contact-rich manipulation tasks, comparing its performance against several baselines and analyzing its architectural components through ablation studies.

#### 4.1. Quantitative Results on Real-World Tasks (Q1)

VTAM demonstrates superior performance across all evaluated contact-rich manipulation tasks. Across 20 trials per task (80 total real-world trials per model), VTAM achieved consistently high success rates:

- **Chip Pick-and-Place:** VTAM achieved a 90% success rate, indicating robustness in handling brittle objects that require precise grasp force regulation and contact detection.
- **Cucumber Peeling:** VTAM achieved an 85% success rate, confirming the importance of tactile feedback for maintaining stable contact and regulating shear forces during interaction with deformable objects. Baselines largely failed on this task.
- **Whiteboard Wiping:** VTAM achieved a 95% success rate across both flat and tilted surfaces, showcasing its ability to maintain consistent contact and regulate normal forces.

In contrast, the baseline models—Genie Envisioner (a state-of-the-art video foundation model), π0.5 (a generalist Vision-Language-Action policy), and π0.5 + Naïve Tactile Injection (a multimodal extension of π0.5 with tactile treated as an additional visual view)—exhibited significantly lower success rates, often 0% or in low single digits for all tasks. This outcome highlights the limitations of vision-only or naively integrated multimodal approaches in scenarios demanding fine-grained force modulation and contact awareness.

#### 4.2. Qualitative Examples and Failure Mode Analysis

Qualitative analysis provided insights into the behavioral differences between VTAM and baselines:

- **Chip Pick-and-Place:** Vision-only baselines frequently failed to verify successful grasps due to hand-induced visual occlusion, proceeding to the placement stage even when the chip was not secured. VTAM, leveraging tactile deformation, could confirm successful contact before lifting and reattempt a grasp if no tactile signal was detected, demonstrating tactile-aware grasping and recovery.
- **Cucumber Peeling:** Baselines struggled to maintain stable contact with the deformable cucumber, often losing contact as they followed vision-driven trajectories that prioritized object curvature over consistent force regulation. VTAM, however, established and maintained stable contact with appropriate force, allowing for consistent peeling motions despite varying cucumber thickness.
- **Whiteboard Wiping:** Baselines exhibited unstable wiping behaviors. The π0.5 variants applied excessively large contact forces, potentially struggling to infer correct end-effector height from ambiguous visual observations on tilted surfaces. The Genie Envisioner showed unstable contact and irregular motion on tilted boards. VTAM, by contrast, maintained moderate and stable contact forces on both flat and tilted surfaces, enabling consistent wiping and stain removal by effectively using tactile information to handle visually ambiguous contact.

#### 4.3. Prediction Visualization

Visualizations of the backbone video model's predictions demonstrated its capability to forecast future multi-view visual and tactile streams. The predictions largely preserved cross-view temporal consistency and in-view dynamics, with only minor blurring in details deemed irrelevant to manipulation. This indicates that the visuo-tactile world model generates reliable predictions of the environment and contact states for action generation.

#### 4.4. Ablation Study on Chip Pick-and-Place Task (Q2 & Q3)

An ablation study on the chip pick-and-place task further elucidated the contribution of VTAM’s architectural components:

- **Vision-only baseline:** Achieved a 0% success rate, confirming that visual depth estimation suffers from severe occlusion, preventing the perception of subtle contact transitions critical for this task.
- **Late-Fusion Tactile (tactile signals injected only at the action head):** Also resulted in a 0% success rate. This demonstrates that raw force injection without the hierarchical visuo-tactile world modeling employed by VTAM is insufficient for robust performance (addressing Q2).
- **No Virtual-Force Regularization:** Performance dropped significantly to a 10% success rate. This finding supports the claim that the deformation-aware virtual force regularization is critical for preventing "vision modality dominance" and ensuring that tactile signals effectively influence the entire denoising process during action optimization (addressing Q3).
- **Full VTAM Model:** Achieved a 90% success rate, validating the combined effectiveness of its predictive world modeling and regularization strategies.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of VTAM marks a contribution to the field of embodied intelligence, particularly in the domain of robotic manipulation requiring intricate physical interactions. Its significance and potential impact can be understood through several key aspects:

- **Enhanced Robustness in Contact-Rich Manipulation:** VTAM directly addresses a critical limitation of existing vision-centric VLA and VAM models, which often struggle with tasks demanding precise force modulation, detection of subtle contact changes, and interaction with deformable or fragile objects. By effectively integrating tactile feedback into a predictive world model, VTAM significantly improves success rates and interaction stability across challenging contact-rich scenarios such as handling potato chips, peeling vegetables, and wiping surfaces. This translates to more reliable and adaptable robotic systems in environments where physical contact is unavoidable and critical.
- **Development of Physically Grounded Embodied Foundation Models:** The framework provides a scalable approach for developing physically grounded embodied foundation models. By enabling efficient cross-modal representation learning without requiring extensive tactile-language paired data or independent tactile pretraining, VTAM offers a practical pathway for incorporating direct physical interaction signals into large-scale robot learning architectures. This is crucial for bridging the gap between high-level semantic understanding (from vision and language) and low-level physical interaction.
- **Advancement in Multimodal Fusion Strategies:** The research introduces a novel two-stage training strategy and a deformation-aware virtual force regularization objective that effectively mitigates the common problem of modality collapse. This ensures that tactile signals, despite their localized and high-frequency nature, maintain their influence throughout the control pathway, preventing visual dominance. This methodological contribution has implications for the design of future multimodal learning systems where balancing information from disparate sensory modalities is essential.
- **Predictive Reasoning for Contact Dynamics:** By integrating tactile sensing into a generative video backbone, VTAM enables predictive reasoning over visuo-tactile dynamics. This allows the model to anticipate the evolution of contact states and potential failure modes, a capability that is crucial for proactive control, especially when dealing with brittle objects or dynamic interactions where errors can accumulate rapidly. This moves beyond purely reactive tactile fusion towards a more anticipatory control paradigm.
- **Broader Applicability in Robotics:** The demonstrated capabilities of VTAM in tasks involving delicate object handling, stable surface contact, and interaction with deformable materials suggest its potential applicability to a wide range of robotic tasks. These include assembly, food handling, medical procedures, and household robotics, where precise and compliant physical interaction is paramount.
- **Future Research Directions:** The findings highlight the importance of direct physical interaction signals in world action models and provide a foundation for further research into advanced multimodal perception, learning, and control. This could include exploring different tactile sensor modalities, more sophisticated methods for virtual force estimation, and integrating these physically grounded models with higher-level planning and reasoning systems.

In conclusion, VTAM represents a significant step towards more robust and capable robots that can perform complex physical interactions with a nuanced understanding of contact dynamics, moving beyond the limitations of solely vision-based approaches.
