# VTAM: Video-Tactile-Action Models for Complex Physical Interaction Beyond VLAs

> [!summary] Summary
> VTAM (Video-Tactile-Action Models) integrates high-resolution tactile sensing with multi-view visual observations into a predictive world model for robotic manipulation. This framework improves robustness and stability in complex, contact-rich tasks, achieving success rates of 90% in chip pick-and-place, 85% in cucumber peeling, and 95% in whiteboard wiping.

## Problem

- Vision-only robot control models (VAMs, VLAs) often fail in contact-rich manipulation due to partial observability or occlusion of critical interaction states like force or slip.
- Existing tactile integration methods frequently suffer from 'modality collapse', where dominant visual signals suppress the influence of high-frequency tactile inputs, or require models to implicitly infer complex contact physics in semantic spaces.
- Generative world models typically rely on visual prediction, providing only indirect access to critical physical interaction signals at the contact interface, making it difficult to anticipate failure modes.

## Method

- VTAM develops a unified visuo-tactile world action model that projects multi-view visual and high-resolution tactile streams into a shared latent space via a pretrained VAE, leveraging a multi-view diffusion process within a video transformer for temporal dynamics.
- A deformation-aware regularization mechanism, utilizing a 3D virtual force proxy derived from tactile optical flow, is introduced to counteract modality collapse and ensure tactile signals influence action policy gradients.
- The model is trained with a two-stage optimization objective: first fine-tuning for joint visuo-tactile latent dynamics, then optimizing the control policy with a conditional joint action-state-force denoising process.

## Results

- VTAM achieved success rates of 90% for chip pick-and-place, 85% for cucumber peeling, and 95% for whiteboard wiping, significantly outperforming vision-only and naïvely integrated tactile baselines.
- Ablation studies confirmed the critical role of the hierarchical visuo-tactile world modeling and the virtual-force regularization, with their removal leading to substantial performance drops (0% and 10% success rates respectively in chip pick-and-place).
- Qualitative analysis demonstrated VTAM's ability to perform tactile-aware grasping (verifying successful contact), maintain stable contact during peeling, and regulate consistent forces during wiping despite visual ambiguities.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Directly integrating tactile sensing into a predictive world-model framework allows for explicit modeling of contact dynamics and better correction of visual estimation errors in physical interaction.
> - A geometrically grounded virtual force proxy, derived from optical flow in tactile data, provides effective auxiliary supervision to prevent visual dominance and ensure tactile signals are influential in multimodal learning.
> - A two-stage training approach effectively adapts a pretrained visual backbone to learn coherent visuo-tactile world representations before optimizing a control policy with joint action, state, and virtual force denoising.
