# EgoLoc: A Generalizable Solution for Temporal Interaction Localization in Egocentric Videos

> [!summary] Summary
> EgoLoc is a zero-shot method for temporal interaction localization in egocentric video: it localizes hand-object contact and separation timestamps via hand-dynamics-guided sampling plus a VLM-based closed-loop localization-and-feedback loop. It outperforms TAL and mask-based baselines on EgoPAT3D-DT and two new benchmarks, and boosts real and simulated robot pick-and-place success.

## Problem

- Temporal action localization methods detect coarse verb-noun action spans but rely on category annotations and lack precision for fine-grained hand-object contact/separation moments.
- Mask-based hand-object distance methods suffer from inaccurate object grounding, 2D scale aliasing between foreground/background, and occlusion of target objects in cluttered scenes.
- Zero-shot TAL and VLM localization pipelines run open-loop, so timing errors from hallucination or coarse uniform sampling cannot be corrected using posterior evidence.

## Method

- ==Self-adaptive sampling strategy== extracts 3D hand wrist velocities/accelerations from ==HaMeR== keypoints and depth registration, sampling anchor frames near zero-acceleration points via a velocity-weighted distribution.
- ==VLM-based localization module== uses a VLM discriminator on ==GroundingDINO==-cropped boundary frames to classify contact/separation/neither, then a VLM localizer reasons over a tiled grid image to output first-round timestamps.
- ==Closed-loop feedback mechanism== has a VLM checker assess visual plausibility of first-round results and, if rejected, feeds the wrong frame as an in-context negative example for second-round refinement.

## Results

- On EgoPAT3D-DT, EgoLoc (Nadj=2) reaches SR(gamma=5)=**0.819** and MAE=**3.713** versus T-PIVOT's best **0.755**/**6.471**, and on DeskTIL achieves SR(gamma=5)=**1.000** and MAE=**0.611**.
- Ablations show 3D self-adaptive sampling beats 2D (SR **0.819** vs **0.726**) and no-sampling (**0.412**), and full closed-loop feedback lifts DeskTIL SR(gamma=5) from **0.921** to **1.000** with MAE dropping from **1.485** to **0.611**.
- Robot manipulation deployment using EgoLoc-labeled contact/separation timestamps to train MMTwin reaches **8/10** simulated and **10/10** real-world task success, versus **6/10** and **2/10** for a thresholding baseline.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Grounding temporal localization in 3D physical hand dynamics (velocity/acceleration minima) rather than raw RGB appearance yields more reliable candidate timestamps than uniform or appearance-only sampling.
> - Decomposing VLM reasoning into a coarse discriminator plus a closed-loop checker with in-context negative examples curbs single-pass hallucination better than one-shot open-loop VLM localization.
> - Object-mask-free, category-agnostic hand-centric localization generalizes better than mask- or taxonomy-dependent pipelines, and transfers directly to cross-embodiment human-to-robot policy timing.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of egocentric vision has seen advancements in Hand-Object Interaction (HOI) analysis, primarily focusing on understanding "how to interact" within the spatial domain. This includes research on HOI reconstruction, synthesis, hand mesh recovery, and interaction region extraction. However, the more granular problem of "when to interact"—specifically, localizing the precise timestamps of hand-object contact and separation—remains less explored. This temporal localization of critical interaction moments is crucial for developing immersive experiences in mixed reality (VR/AR) and for advanced robotic motion planning, such as pick-and-place tasks.

The paper formulates this problem as Temporal Interaction Localization (TIL), distinguishing it from existing Temporal Action Localization (TAL) methods. TAL typically focuses on detecting broader action segments (e.g., "move the bottle") and action transitions, often relying on category-specific annotations during training. These methods are generally coarse-grained and struggle to precisely identify microscopic interaction states. Furthermore, conventional TAL methods primarily process 2D RGB images, lacking 3D spatial information that could enrich HOI understanding, and often operate in an open-loop manner, which limits post-hoc refinement.

Another approach in human-robot skill transfer involves using semantic masks of hands and objects to infer spatial relationships. However, these mask-based solutions face inherent limitations such as inaccurate object grounding in cluttered scenes, scale aliasing from 2D observations that can lead to misinterpretations of 3D spatial relationships, and challenges with occluded target objects.

This work positions itself to address these limitations by proposing EgoLoc, a novel zero-shot approach to TIL for egocentric videos. It aims to provide a generalizable solution that overcomes the granularity, generalization, perception, and closed-loop capability issues of TAL, as well as the inaccuracies of mask-based methods. EgoLoc seeks to enhance HOI analysis by focusing on fine-grained temporal events with 3D hand dynamics and leveraging Vision-Language Models (VLMs) in a zero-shot, category-agnostic manner. The paper is an extension of a preliminary version, incorporating new elements such as relaxed assumptions, refined components (e.g., more comprehensive hand motion analysis), an additional benchmark (ManiTIL), and validation on a broader range of downstream applications.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a robust and generalizable method for Temporal Interaction Localization (TIL) in egocentric videos. This entails accurately identifying the precise timestamps when a human hand first makes contact with an object and when it separates from it.

The motivation for pursuing this objective stems from several identified gaps and challenges in existing research:

1. **Addressing a Critical Temporal Granularity Gap:** While existing Hand-Object Interaction (HOI) research in egocentric vision has made progress in understanding "how to interact" (e.g., reconstructing hand-object poses or synthesizing interactions), there is a significant lack of focus on "when to interact." This temporal aspect, particularly the precise moments of contact and separation, is crucial for developing natural and immersive experiences in augmented and virtual reality (AR/VR) and for enabling effective robot action planning and human-robot skill transfer. For instance, a robot executing a pick-and-place task requires precise knowledge of when to grasp and release an object.

2. **Overcoming Limitations of Temporal Action Localization (TAL) Methods:** Current TAL methods are designed for coarser-grained action detection (e.g., identifying when an action like "moving a bottle" begins and ends). They are ill-suited for the microscopic precision required by TIL because:
 - **Granularity Mismatch:** TAL models struggle to distinguish subtle changes in hand-object interaction states at the exact moments of contact/separation.
 - **Lack of Generalization:** Most TAL methods rely on extensive, labor-intensive category annotations during training, limiting their ability to generalize to novel HOI categories in a zero-shot manner.
 - **Insufficient Perception:** Standard TAL typically processes 2D RGB input, failing to incorporate 3D spatial information (e.g., 3D hand dynamics) which is vital for understanding physical interactions with absolute scale.
 - **Open-Loop Inference:** TAL methods generally operate without feedback, meaning any initial timing deviations cannot be corrected, leading to accumulated errors in fine-grained localization tasks.

3. **Mitigating Inherent Flaws of Mask-Based HOI Solutions:** Many approaches, particularly in human-robot skill transfer, use semantic masks of hands and objects to infer interaction. However, these methods suffer from:
 - **Inaccurate Object Grounding:** Visual grounding models often produce inaccurate or incomplete masks for target objects, especially with vague text prompts or irregularly shaped objects, leading to incorrect interaction inference.
 - **2D Scale Aliasing:** 2D observations can be misleading; objects or hands might appear to overlap in 2D while being spatially distant in 3D, or vice versa, leading to false positives or negatives.
 - **Occlusion Issues:** In cluttered scenes, hands or other objects can occlude the target object, making it challenging to extract accurate hand-object relationships using masks.

To address these motivations, the key objectives of EgoLoc are:
- To propose a novel zero-shot TIL paradigm that can accurately localize contact and separation timestamps without requiring object masks or specific action taxonomies.
- To integrate 3D hand dynamics (velocities and accelerations) into the temporal localization process, enhancing spatial awareness and providing higher-quality initial temporal guesses.
- To implement a closed-loop feedback mechanism leveraging Vision-Language Models (VLMs) to refine localization results and reduce the uncertainty inherent in VLM outputs.
- To demonstrate the effectiveness and generalizability of the proposed method through comprehensive experiments on public and newly introduced datasets, as well as its practical utility in downstream VR/AR applications and robotic manipulation tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

EgoLoc is designed as a zero-shot Temporal Interaction Localization (ZS-TIL) paradigm for egocentric videos, focusing on localizing precise hand-object contact and separation timestamps. The methodology consists of three main stages: a self-adaptive sampling strategy, a VLM-based localization module, and a closed-loop feedback mechanism.

#### 3.1. Task Definition

Given an untrimmed egocentric video comprising sequential RGB images ($I$) and depth images ($D$), EgoLoc aims to localize contact timestamps ($T_c$) and separation timestamps ($T_s$) for single-hand, non-impulsive HOI processes. These timestamps collectively represent interaction transition moments.

#### 3.2. Self-Adaptive Sampling Strategy (SASS)

This module aims to efficiently generate high-quality initial temporal guesses (anchor frames) that approximate interaction transition timestamps, rather than relying on inefficient frame-by-frame processing or unstable uniform sampling.

- **Hand Motion Extraction:**
 1. **2D Hand Keypoint Detection:** HaMeR is used to extract 2D positions of the hand wrist keypoints from the input RGB images.
 2. **3D Wrist Position Calculation:** Using camera intrinsics and sequential depth images, 3D wrist positions in the camera coordinate system are calculated.
 3. **Global Coordinate System Alignment:** Sequential 3D point clouds from the depth images are registered to obtain relative 6-DOF camera poses. These poses are then used to transform the 3D wrist positions into a fixed global coordinate system (the first frame's camera coordinate system), eliminating ambiguity caused by camera egomotion.
 4. **Hand Dynamics Analysis:** 3D wrist velocities and accelerations are computed from the global 3D wrist positions. A Savitzky–Golay filter is applied to the velocities, and a cubic spline is fitted. Based on the observation that hand velocity is typically lower with near-zero acceleration during contact or separation, local minima of the velocity spline (corresponding to zero acceleration) are identified as potential interaction points.

- **Weighted Sampling:**
 1. For each identified zero-acceleration timestamp, a set of $N_{ac}$ symmetrical anchor candidates (frames around the zero-acceleration point) is extracted.
 2. A weighted sampling scheme is applied to these candidates. Frames with lower hand velocities are assigned higher sampling weights, reflecting the increased likelihood of interaction at slower movements.
 3. One anchor frame is adaptively sampled. Once an anchor frame leads to a valid interaction transition, it is excluded from future resampling to avoid redundancy. In cases where no local velocity minimum exists, candidates are uniformly sampled.

#### 3.3. VLM-Based Localization Module

This module utilizes a Vision-Language Model (VLM), specifically GPT-4o by default, to perform initial interaction attribute identification and first-round temporal localization.

- **Interaction Attribute Identification (VLM Discriminator):**
 1. **Initial Guess Refinement:** $N_{adj}$ adjacent frames around the sampled anchor frame are selected to form an enriched set of initial guesses ($I_{adj}$).
 2. **Hand Region Extraction:** GroundingDINO is applied to the first and last boundary frames of $I_{adj}$ using the prompt "hand" to obtain hand bounding boxes. These bounding boxes are expanded slightly to include surrounding object information.
 3. **Attribute Classification:** The VLM Discriminator takes the expanded boundary frames (concatenated visually) and a tailored text prompt (Fig. 4a) to classify the interaction attribute as "Contact," "Separation," or "Neither." "Neither" indicates no clear transition and triggers resampling a new anchor frame from SASS. This explicit attribute identification creates attribute-specific prompts for the next stage, enhancing VLM reasoning coherence.

- **First-Round Temporal Interaction Localization (VLM Localizer):**
 1. **Grid Image Construction:** All $N_{adj}$ frames from $I_{adj}$ are tiled into a single grid image ($G_k$), ordered chronologically, which serves as a compact visual prompt.
 2. **Localization:** The VLM Localizer receives $G_k$ and an attribute-specific text prompt (Fig. 4b) (determined by the VLM Discriminator) to pinpoint the earliest contact or separation timestamp within the grid. This is a single-step inference, optimized for efficiency by the high-quality initial guesses from SASS.

#### 3.4. Closed-Loop Feedback Mechanism

This mechanism addresses potential VLM hallucinations in the first-round localization, aiming to refine results and reduce inference uncertainty.

- **Visual Cue Assessment (VLM Checker):**
 1. The egocentric image corresponding to the first-round TIL result and a specific text prompt (Fig. 4c) are fed to the VLM Checker.
 2. The VLM Checker assesses the visual cues to determine if the first-round result is plausible (e.g., if a "contact" prediction genuinely shows contact). It outputs "Yes" (accepted as final) or "No" (rejected).

- **Second-Round Temporal Interaction Localization (In-Context Learning):**
 1. If the VLM Checker outputs "No," indicating an erroneous first-round result, a second-round localization is triggered.
 2. The rejected first-round image frame is incorporated as an additional negative example into the text prompt for the VLM Localizer (Fig. 6). This in-context learning guides the VLM Localizer to mitigate its hallucination.
 3. The VLM Localizer then performs a second-round inference using the enriched text prompt, the original grid image, and the negative example, producing a refined TIL result. EgoLoc typically uses a single feedback round for a balance between accuracy and efficiency.

The entire process iteratively loops through zero-acceleration timestamps, performing sampling, first-round localization, and refinement until all potential interaction transition timestamps in the video are identified. EgoLoc's design allows for zero-shot implementation by being hand-centric, avoiding object masks, and using action-category-agnostic prompts.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluates EgoLoc's performance on a public dataset, EgoPAT3D-DT, and two newly introduced benchmarks: DeskTIL and ManiTIL. DeskTIL features varying levels of desk clutter and single HOI processes, while ManiTIL consists of longer videos with multiple HOI processes across four complex tasks. Evaluation metrics include Mean over Frames (MoF), Intersection over Union (IoU), Mean Absolute Error (MAE), and Success Rate (SR) at different tolerance ranges ($\gamma$).

#### 4.1. Comparative Performance

- **Overall Superiority:** EgoLoc consistently outperforms several baselines across all datasets and nearly all metrics. Baselines include Threshold (distance-based heuristic), VideoChat (a chat-centric video understanding approach), GreedyVLM (brute-force VLM on full video), T-PIVOT (a zero-shot Temporal Action Localization method adapted for TIL), and HOIMask (a mask-based approach tested on ManiTIL).
- **Limitations of Baselines:**
 - Threshold and VideoChat exhibit poor performance, indicating that simple heuristics or general video understanding models are insufficient for fine-grained TIL.
 - GreedyVLM and T-PIVOT, while VLM-based, show suboptimal results, attributed to the lack of an effective sampling strategy (GreedyVLM processes all frames at once) or an open-loop inference design (T-PIVOT).
 - HOIMask performs poorly, highlighting the inherent challenges of mask-based methods in accurately selecting active object instances and generating reliable masks in cluttered scenes.
- **EgoLoc's Efficiency and Granularity:** EgoLoc demonstrates superior accuracy, particularly in distinguishing fine-grained interaction transitions in cluttered environments and localizing multiple HOI events in long videos, which T-PIVOT struggles with. EgoLoc also achieves its performance with improved efficiency, completing TIL inference in approximately 0.53x to 0.77x the time required by T-PIVOT.
- **Impact of Neighboring Frames ($N_{adj}$):** Counterintuitively, EgoLoc with $N_{adj}=2$ (two adjacent frames for grid image) often achieves the best results, outperforming configurations with larger $N_{adj}$. This suggests that a larger spatial-temporal receptive field can cause the VLM to distribute attention across irrelevant frames, hindering precise localization.

#### 4.2. Ablation Studies

- **Self-Adaptive Sampling Strategy (SASS):**
 - Removing SASS (using random sampling) leads to a significant degradation in localization performance, highlighting the necessity of a guided sampling approach.
 - Using 2D hand dynamics (SASS-2D) for sampling performs better than random but worse than 3D hand dynamics (SASS-3D). This confirms that incorporating 3D perception and analyzing 3D hand dynamics provides higher-quality initial guesses and avoids issues like depth ambiguity and scale aliasing from 2D observations.
- **Interaction Attribute Identification:**
 - Utilizing boundary frames (first and last frames of the initial guess) for the VLM discriminator yields the best accuracy in identifying contact/separation attributes.
 - Using only the single anchor frame or the entire grid image for attribute identification results in lower accuracy. This indicates that a single frame lacks temporal context, while too many visually similar frames in the grid can introduce ambiguity for attribute classification.
- **Closed-Loop Feedback Mechanism:**
 - The full EgoLoc (with both visual cue assessment and in-context learning) achieves the highest performance, including a 100% success rate (SR) at $\gamma=5$ on DeskTIL.
 - Removing the feedback mechanism entirely leads to the lowest accuracy, underscoring the impact of VLM hallucinations in open-loop systems.
 - Using only visual cue assessment to trigger re-localization (without in-context learning) improves performance but is less effective than the full feedback loop, which uses rejected results as negative examples to guide subsequent refinement.
- **Inference Uncertainties:** EgoLoc with the closed-loop feedback mechanism demonstrates significantly lower standard deviations across all evaluation metrics compared to baselines and EgoLoc without feedback. This indicates that the feedback mechanism enhances the stability of the localization process, which is critical for practical applications.
- **VLM Selection:** GPT-4o is identified as the best-performing VLM for EgoLoc, followed by Gemini 2.5 Pro. While smaller models like Janus-Pro-7B show lower accuracy, they still outperform several baselines, suggesting that EgoLoc's systematic design can compensate for reduced VLM parameter scale.

#### 4.3. Deployment on Downstream Tasks

- **Robotic Manipulation (Human-Robot Policy Transfer):**
 - EgoLoc's automatically generated interaction transition timestamps are used to train MMTwin, a state-of-the-art hand motion forecasting approach, to predict 3D hand trajectories and gripper states (open/close).
 - **Simulated Environment (ViperX 300):** For a "put the blue block onto the cloth" task, EgoLoc's annotations enable the robot to achieve an 8/10 success rate, performing close to manual labeling (9/10) and significantly better than Threshold (6/10) and HOIMask (4/10).
 - **Real-World Environment (Franka Emika Research 3):** For an "open the cabinet door" task, EgoLoc-guided policy achieves a 10/10 success rate, matching manual labeling, and substantially outperforming baselines (Threshold 2/10, HOIMask 4/10).
 - **Conclusion:** EgoLoc effectively facilitates human-robot skill transfer by providing precise timing for gripper actions, reducing reliance on costly tele-operation.
- **Egocentric Vision Tasks:**
 - **VR Overlay Hint:** EgoLoc's annotations for time-to-contact can be used to provide real-time VR cues, offering potential for human motor skill training, preemptive warnings, and collaborative VR.
 - **Egocentric Action Recognition:** Integrating EgoLoc's time-to-contact features into RU-LSTM (a widely used action recognition algorithm on Epic-Kitchens-55) improves Top-5 action recognition accuracy for both RGB and flow branches. This indicates that TIL provides valuable information for modeling human motion patterns.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents EgoLoc, a novel zero-shot Temporal Interaction Localization (TIL) paradigm that addresses a previously underexplored but critical aspect of egocentric vision: precisely identifying "when" hand-object interactions occur. Its significance and potential impact span several areas:

1. **Advancement in Egocentric Vision:** EgoLoc shifts the focus of egocentric HOI analysis from solely "how to interact" to also include "when to interact." This fine-grained temporal understanding provides a richer context for analyzing human activities and opens new avenues for research in first-person perception.

2. **Generalizability and Robustness:** As the first zero-shot TIL paradigm, EgoLoc eliminates the need for labor-intensive, category-specific annotations and explicit object masks. This makes it highly generalizable across diverse HOI scenarios and robust to challenges like inaccurate object grounding, cluttered scenes, and occlusions that plague mask-based methods. Its reliance on universal, action-category-agnostic VLM prompts enhances its applicability to novel interactions.

3. **Enhanced Perception through 3D Dynamics:** The incorporation of 3D hand dynamics, extracted from both 2D RGB and depth information, is a key contribution. This enriches interaction signals by overcoming the limitations of 2D observations, such as depth ambiguity and scale aliasing, leading to more accurate temporal localization of physical interactions.

4. **Increased Reliability with Closed-Loop Feedback:** The innovative closed-loop feedback mechanism, which uses visual cue assessment and in-context learning to refine VLM outputs, significantly reduces VLM hallucinations and inference uncertainties. This iterative refinement process leads to more precise and stable localization results, which is crucial for real-world deployment in safety-critical or high-precision applications.

5. **New Research Resources:** The introduction of the DeskTIL and ManiTIL benchmarks is a valuable contribution to the research community. These datasets, designed to evaluate TIL across varying levels of clutter, video lengths, and HOI counts, will facilitate future research and allow for more comprehensive assessment of TIL methods.

6. **Broad Practical Applications:** EgoLoc demonstrates significant potential across multiple downstream applications:
 - **Mixed Reality (VR/AR):** It can enable more immersive and natural user experiences by providing optimal contact timing for skill training, issuing preemptive warnings about impending interactions, and facilitating collaborative VR by labeling predicted actions.
 - **Robotic Manipulation:** By accurately localizing interaction transitions, EgoLoc provides precise timing for robot gripper actions (opening and closing) in human-robot policy transfer. This can streamline the process of learning robot skills from human demonstrations, reducing the need for costly manual tele-operation and improving the efficacy of robot action planning in both simulated and real-world environments.
 - **Egocentric Action Recognition:** The integration of EgoLoc's temporal interaction insights can enhance existing action recognition frameworks by providing richer information about human motion patterns, leading to more accurate classification of activities.

In conclusion, EgoLoc represents a significant step forward in egocentric vision by offering a principled, generalizable, and robust solution for temporal interaction localization. Its contributions are poised to foster a new generation of advancements in mixed reality, robotic intelligence, and human activity understanding. Future work focusing on bimanual interactions and rapid actions will further expand its applicability.
