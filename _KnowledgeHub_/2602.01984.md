# Enhancing Multi-Image Understanding through Delimiter Token Scaling

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Sogang University, KAIST, and Tübingen University developed a method to improve multi-image understanding in Large Vision-Language Models by scaling the hidden states of image delimiter tokens. This approach effectively mitigates cross-image information leakage and strengthens intra-image interactions, yielding consistent performance improvements across various benchmarks and models without incurring any additional computational cost.

## Problem

- Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) exhibit degraded performance when processing multiple images due to an inability to accurately distinguish and reason across distinct visual inputs.
- A primary issue is "cross-image information leakage," where information from one image blends with others, hindering reliable multi-image understanding.
- Existing image delimiter tokens, despite their presence, prove insufficient in fully preventing this leakage, and their mechanistic function was not well understood.

## Method

- Empirically analyzed the ==attention mechanisms of existing image delimiter tokens== in LVLMs to understand their function and limitations in separating distinct visual contexts.
- Identified two key properties of effective delimiters: ==localized attention absorption== from their corresponding image and reinforcement of intra-image interactions through an "image tagging" mechanism.
- Proposed =="delimiter token scaling"==, a simple, training-free method that scales the hidden states of delimiter tokens by a factor to strengthen their boundary function.

## Results

- The method consistently improves multi-image understanding, reducing cross-image attention by approximately **50%** while maintaining intra-image performance across diverse LVLMs (e.g., Qwen2.5-VL, InternVL3, LLaVA-OneVision) and benchmarks.
- Delimiter token scaling incurs no additional training or inference costs, demonstrating superior computational efficiency and resource footprint compared to previous training-free approaches.
- The technique successfully generalizes beyond multi-image settings, yielding performance improvements in text-only multi-document summarization and multi-table question answering tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Image delimiter tokens are indispensable for LVLMs to distinguish individual images, but they are imperfect and allow significant cross-image information leakage.
> - Delimiter tokens function as localized attractors of attention, primarily receiving strong attention from tokens within their corresponding image, distinguishing them from global sink tokens.
> - This localized attention enables delimiters to act as an "image-specific tag," which provides a common bias to strengthen intra-image interactions.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) have demonstrated proficiency in tasks involving single images. However, a known challenge is their diminished performance when multiple images are provided as input, a phenomenon attributed to "cross-image information leakage" where the model struggles to differentiate information between individual images.

Existing research has explored various approaches to address this multi-image understanding deficit. These can generally be categorized into training-based and training-free methods:
- **Training-based approaches** often involve constructing specialized multi-image instruction datasets and employing supervised fine-tuning. While these methods can yield performance improvements, they are limited by the high costs associated with curating high-quality data and requiring substantial computational resources for training.
- **Training-free approaches** aim to mitigate multi-image issues without extensive retraining. Examples include AVAM (Zeng et al., 2025), which selects relevant visual regions to reduce redundancy, but introduces architectural complexity with external modules. Another method, FOCUS (Park et al., 2025), uses a contrastive decoding strategy to separate outputs based on image-specific contexts; however, it incurs high inference costs due to requiring `n + 1` forward passes for `n` images.

This paper positions itself as a training-free approach that avoids architectural modifications, additional training, and inference-time overhead. It focuses on analyzing and enhancing the functionality of existing image delimiter tokens, which are special tokens used by LVLMs to mark the boundaries of individual images. The role and mechanisms of these tokens have been largely unexplored in previous literature, despite their common usage.

The work also relates to studies on "sink tokens" in Large Language Models (LLMs), where certain tokens (e.g., `<BOS>`) exhibit high activations and influence attention patterns. While image delimiter tokens similarly receive high attention, this paper highlights their distinct, localized behavior compared to the global influence of conventional sink tokens in LLMs, especially in the context of multi-image LVLMs. This localized sink-like behavior in multi-image settings has not been thoroughly investigated previously. The paper builds on prior recognition of cross-image information leakage (Park et al., 2025) by providing a detailed analysis of its underlying causes through the lens of attention patterns involving delimiter tokens.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the capability of Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) to understand and reason accurately over multiple input images. This objective is motivated by a critical limitation observed in current LVLMs: a significant degradation in performance when presented with multiple images compared to single-image scenarios.

The detailed motivations and specific objectives are as follows:

1. **Addressing Cross-Image Information Leakage:** The core problem identified is "cross-image information leakage," where LVLMs inadvertently mix or confuse information from different input images, leading to inaccurate or conflated outputs. This phenomenon is a major contributor to performance decline in multi-image tasks.
2. **Unexplored Role of Delimiter Tokens:** While existing LVLMs already incorporate special "delimiter tokens" (e.g., `<|vision_start|>` and `<|vision_end|>`) to delineate individual images within the input sequence, their precise role and underlying mechanism in facilitating image separation have remained largely unexamined in the literature. The authors hypothesize that these tokens, despite their presence, are not fully effective in preventing cross-image leakage.
3. **Detailed Analysis of Delimiter Token Behavior:** A key objective is to empirically investigate how these delimiter tokens function within the model's attention mechanism. This involves analyzing attention score maps to understand how the presence or absence of these tokens affects the model's ability to form distinct boundaries between image contexts and to identify why leakage persists.
4. **Identification of Key Properties:** Through this analysis, the research aims to identify specific properties of delimiter tokens that contribute to (or limit) their effectiveness in distinguishing images. These properties are intended to form the basis for an improved mechanism.
5. **Development of an Enhanced Mechanism:** Based on the identified properties, the overarching objective is to propose a simple, yet effective, method to augment the functionality of delimiter tokens. The proposed method should aim to:
 - Reinforce intra-image interaction, ensuring that information within a single image is processed coherently.
 - Limit undesired cross-image interactions, preventing information from one image from interfering with another.
 - Enable the model to distinguish between images more effectively, leading to more accurate reasoning.
6. **Efficiency and Practicality:** A significant motivation for the proposed solution is to achieve these performance enhancements without incurring additional computational costs, such as extra training time or increased inference latency and memory usage. This emphasizes the development of a practical and easily deployable approach.
7. **Generalizability:** The research is also motivated by the potential for the proposed method to generalize beyond multi-image understanding, extending to other multi-instance input scenarios where distinct separation of information is crucial, such as multi-document or multi-table processing.

In summary, the work is motivated by a recognized performance gap in multi-image LVLMs stemming from information leakage, a gap that the authors believe can be effectively addressed by a deeper understanding and targeted enhancement of the models' inherent image delimiter mechanisms, prioritizing efficiency and broad applicability.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology of this research involves a two-pronged approach: an analytical investigation into the behavior of image delimiter tokens in LVLMs, followed by the development and empirical validation of a novel method based on insights from this analysis.

#### 3.1. Analysis of Image Delimiter Tokens

The initial phase focused on understanding the functionality and limitations of existing image delimiter tokens (e.g., `<|vision_start|>`, `<|vision_end|>`) in LVLMs.
- **Functionality Assessment:** To determine if these tokens serve their intended purpose, the researchers conducted experiments where delimiter tokens were either removed or replaced with other generic special tokens (e.g., `<|im start|>`, `<|endoftext|>`).
 - **Observation:** When delimiter tokens were present, attention maps exhibited distinct triangular block patterns, visually delineating image boundaries (Figure 1a). The removal or replacement of these tokens caused these patterns to disappear, making it difficult to differentiate image contexts (Figure 1b, 1c, Appendix A.1, Figure A1).
 - **Performance Impact:** This structural change correlated with a performance drop of approximately 10 percentage points, confirming that delimiter tokens play a critical role in distinguishing images.
- **Limitations Identification:** Despite their role, attention map analysis revealed persistent, albeit reduced, cross-image interaction (highlighted in Figure 1a). This indicated that their distinguishing effect was incomplete.
- **Identification of Key Properties:** A detailed analysis of attention patterns led to the identification of two key properties:
 - **Property 1: Localized Strong Attention:** The *i*-th image delimiter token receives strong attention predominantly from tokens belonging to the *i*-th image. This localized attention pattern creates a clear one-to-one mapping between images and their respective delimiter tokens, distinct from the global attention patterns of general sink tokens (Figure 2a).
 - **Property 2: Intra-image Interaction Reinforcement (Image Tagging):** Each delimiter token acts as an image-specific "tag." Tokens within an image assign a dominant attention weight (`p_d_i`) to their corresponding delimiter (`d_i`). This causes a common additive term (`p_d_i * v_d_i`) to appear in the attention output for all tokens within that image, effectively functioning as a localized bias that strengthens intra-image interactions (Equation 1, Figure 2b).

#### 3.2. Proposed Method: Delimiter Token Scaling

Based on the identified properties, the researchers proposed a method to strengthen the model's ability to discriminate between images without compromising intra-image interactions: scaling the hidden states of delimiter tokens.
- **Mechanism:** For a given hidden state `h_t^(l)` of token `t` at layer `l`, it is scaled by a factor `λ > 1` if `t` is an image delimiter token, otherwise it remains unchanged (Equation 2).
- **Enhancement of Properties:**
 - **Enhancing Property 1:** Scaling the hidden states of delimiter tokens amplifies their activation, making them stronger attractors of attention. Due to the softmax normalization in the attention mechanism, this increased attention to delimiter tokens implicitly reduces the attention allocated to tokens from other images, thereby suppressing cross-image interaction (Figure 3a).
 - **Enhancing Property 2:** The method assumes (and provides empirical support in Appendix A.2) that attention from tokens within an image increases most significantly toward *their corresponding* delimiter. Concurrently, scaling also increases the magnitude of the value vectors (`v_d`) of all delimiter tokens (Figure 3b). The combined effect amplifies the `p_d_i * v_d_i` term for the correct image, reinforcing the image-tagging effect and preserving intra-image attention, mitigating the suppressive effect of softmax on intra-image interactions (Figure 3c).
- **Computational Benefits:** This approach modifies hidden states rather than the attention mechanism itself, allowing compatibility with optimized attention kernels like FlashAttention (Dao, 2023). This avoids significant memory and computational overhead that would be incurred by directly modifying attention scores.
- **Text-Image Interaction Preservation:** Empirical analysis indicates that the method has a minimal impact on text-to-image interaction, with only a marginal reduction (approximately 10%) in text-to-image interaction scores, suggesting robust preservation of multimodal alignment.

#### 3.3. Empirical Validation

The method's effectiveness was validated using Qwen2.5-VL-3B by analyzing attention patterns and quantifying interaction changes:
- **Reduced Cross-Image Leakage:** Attention maps (Figures 4a, 4b) and quantitative analysis demonstrated a reduction of approximately 50% in average attention scores between tokens from different images (Figure 5a).
- **Preserved Intra-Image Interaction:** Attention scores *within* individual images, such as Image 3, remained largely unaffected (Figure 5a), supporting the claim that intra-image interactions are preserved. This was further supported by evidence of enhanced image tagging (Figure 5b).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents quantitative and qualitative results demonstrating the effectiveness and generalizability of the proposed delimiter token scaling method across various benchmarks and model architectures.

#### 4.1. Results on Multi-Image Understanding

The method was evaluated on four multi-image benchmarks: Mantis, MuirBench, MIRB, and QBench2, using three families of LVLMs: Qwen2.5-VL (3B, 7B, 32B), InternVL3 (1B, 2B, 8B, 14B), and LLaVA-OneVision (0.5B, 7B).
- **Consistent Performance Gains:** Table 1 shows consistent performance improvements across all tested models and benchmarks. For instance, the Qwen2.5-VL-3B model on MuirBench improved from 37.31 to 42.42, and the InternVL3-2B model on Mantis improved from 52.07 to 54.38. These gains were observed across models of varying sizes, from 0.5B to 32B, indicating scalability.

#### 4.2. Results on Multi-Document and Multi-Table Understanding

To assess generalizability beyond visual tasks, the method was applied to text-only multi-instance benchmarks: MultiNews and WCEP-10 for multi-document summarization, and TQABench for multi-table question answering. Models used included Qwen2.5 (3B, 7B) and Phi-1.5.
- **Cross-Modal Generalization:** Tables 2, 3, and 4 report consistent improvements in ROUGE scores for multi-document summarization and accuracy for multi-table QA. Notably, the Qwen2.5-3B model with delimiter scaling on TQABench achieved higher accuracy (37.84) than the baseline Qwen2.5-7B model (37.50), suggesting that the method can provide benefits comparable to or exceeding increases in model scale.

#### 4.3. Qualitative Results

Qualitative analysis (Figure 6) demonstrated how the method mitigates cross-image information leakage.
- **Disentangled Reasoning:** In examples where the baseline model conflated information across images (e.g., incorrectly stating a man on a bike was in both images, or reversing the order of animals for respective environments), the proposed method enabled the model to accurately distinguish between images and provide correct, disentangled responses.

#### 4.4. Comparison with M-RoPE

A comparison with M-RoPE, a temporal positional embedding method for videos, was conducted. M-RoPE was adapted to inject temporal embeddings into each image to aid distinction.
- **Superiority of Scaling:** Table 5 shows that M-RoPE alone performed worse than the baseline. When combined with image delimiter tokens, M-RoPE improved over the baseline but still lagged behind the proposed delimiter scaling method. This suggests that while mechanisms to distinguish images are beneficial, the simple hidden state scaling is more effective than complex temporal embedding strategies for this task.

#### 4.5. Few-Shot Evaluation with Interleaved Examples

The method was evaluated in a few-shot setting using 4-shot interleaved inputs on single-image datasets (OKVQA and VizWiz).
- **Effectiveness in Few-Shot Learning:** Table 6 indicates consistent performance improvements across Qwen2.5-VL and InternVL3 models, demonstrating applicability to interleaved data and downstream tasks where image-text interaction is crucial.

#### 4.6. Performance on Larger-Scale Models

The method was tested on larger models: Qwen2.5-VL-72B and InternVL3-78B.
- **Scalability:** Table 7 shows performance improvements on the Mantis benchmark for both large models, indicating that the approach remains effective as model scale increases.

#### 4.7. Inference Cost

Empirical measurements confirmed that the method introduces no additional inference cost.
- **Efficiency:** As detailed in Table 8, average and peak VRAM usage, as well as inference time, remained nearly identical to the baseline, confirming compatibility with FlashAttention and efficient operation.

#### 4.8. Hyperparameter Sensitivity

Experiments with varying scaling factor (λ) values demonstrated robustness.
- **Robustness:** Figure 7 illustrates consistent performance improvements over the baseline across a range of λ values, indicating that the method is not overly sensitive to this hyperparameter.

#### 4.9. Ablation Studies (Appendix A)

- **Individual Q, K, V Scaling:** Scaling hidden states implicitly scales Q, K, and V. Ablations showed that scaling Key vectors individually yielded a larger gain than Query or Value, but simultaneous scaling of Q, K, and V (as done by scaling hidden states) achieved the highest performance (Table A2).
- **Alternative Token Scaling:** Scaling non-delimiter tokens or scaling with a factor less than 1 either led to performance degradation or less effective mitigation of cross-image leakage (Tables A3, A4; Figures A3, A4). This confirms the specificity of delimiter tokens for the observed benefits.
- **Layer Selection:** Selecting early layers (e.g., 0-3 in a 36-layer model) for scaling yielded the best performance, aligning with prior work suggesting early layers are important for grounding (Table A5).
- **Comparison with FOCUS:** The proposed method consistently outperformed FOCUS, while requiring substantially less memory and inference time (Tables A6, A7).
- **Attention Entropy Analysis:** The method reduced attention entropy in image regions, confirming suppression of cross-image attention while preserving entropy in text regions (Figure A5).
- **Entropy-based Extension:** An extension dynamically scaling based on attention entropy provided marginal additional gains but incurred significant computational overhead, rendering it impractical (Tables A8, A9).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a technically significant contribution to the field of multi-modal machine learning by addressing a fundamental limitation in Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) when processing multiple images. The findings and the proposed method have several implications:

- **Mitigation of Cross-Image Information Leakage:** The primary significance lies in effectively tackling the "cross-image information leakage" problem. This phenomenon is a known barrier to robust multi-image understanding in LVLMs, and the proposed "delimiter token scaling" method provides a mechanism to reduce this confusion, leading to more accurate and reliable reasoning across distinct visual contexts.
- **Enhanced Understanding of LVLM Mechanics:** The detailed analysis of image delimiter tokens provides new insights into their previously "largely unexplored" role. By identifying their localized attention (Property 1) and intra-image tagging effect (Property 2), the work contributes to a deeper understanding of how LVLMs process and segment multi-modal inputs, distinguishing their behavior from conventional sink tokens in text-only LLMs.
- **Computational Efficiency:** A notable impact of this work is the achievement of performance improvements without incurring any additional training or inference costs. This is a practical advantage over many prior methods (e.g., training-based approaches, FOCUS), which often require substantial computational resources or increased inference latency. Its compatibility with optimized attention kernels like FlashAttention further enhances its practical applicability and efficiency.
- **Broad Generalizability:** The demonstrated effectiveness across diverse multi-image benchmarks (Mantis, MuirBench, MIRB, QBench2), different LVLM architectures (Qwen2.5-VL, InternVL3, LLaVA-OneVision), and various model sizes (from 0.5B to 78B) indicates the robustness and wide applicability of the method. Furthermore, its successful application to multi-document and multi-table understanding tasks highlights its generalizability to any multi-instance input where clear logical separation is required, suggesting potential impact beyond the visual domain.
- **Practical Deployment Potential:** The method's simplicity (a single scaling factor applied to hidden states), ease of integration, and lack of computational overhead make it a strong candidate for immediate adoption in existing LVLMs, offering a "plug-and-play" enhancement.
- **Sustainability Implications:** By improving performance without additional training or inference cost, the method indirectly contributes to sustainability efforts in AI research. It reduces the need for extensive retraining and large-scale data curation, potentially lowering energy consumption and research expenditures.

**Limitations and Future Directions:**
- The method's current limitation is its inapplicability to videos that lack explicit frame-separating tokens. Future work aims to extend the approach to the video domain by incorporating mechanisms for modeling temporal transitions between frames.
- While applicable to open-source models by direct hidden state modification, its direct implementation for proprietary models is limited by external user access to hidden states. However, the authors suggest it is straightforward for model developers to integrate internally.

In conclusion, this research offers an efficient and effective solution to a critical challenge in multi-modal AI, providing both practical improvements for current LVLMs and theoretical insights into their internal mechanisms, with a broader potential impact across various multi-instance data processing tasks.
