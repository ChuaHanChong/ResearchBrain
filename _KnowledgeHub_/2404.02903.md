# LidarDM: Generative LiDAR Simulation in a Generated World

> [!summary] Summary
> LidarDM generates realistic, layout-conditioned, temporally coherent 4D LiDAR videos by first building the underlying world, then sensing it. A latent diffusion model synthesizes intensity-infused static 3D scenes from bird's-eye-view map layouts, separate generators create vehicle and pedestrian meshes, trajectories animate them, and physics-based raycasting with learned stochastic raydrop produces the final point cloud sequences, outperforming baselines on temporal consistency.

## Problem

- Existing LiDAR generative models produce only single frames, offering no semantic controllability and no temporal consistency across a driving sequence.
- LiDAR resimulation methods depend on artist-curated or real-world-reconstructed 3D assets, which is costly, limits scene diversity, and blocks generation of unseen environments.
- Classic simulators raycast hand-built worlds, so realism suffers from both asset design gaps and physics simulation gaps between simulation and reality.

## Method

- ==Latent diffusion== scene generator: encoder-decoder compresses a ==truncated signed-distance function== plus intensity volume, with map layout encoded as conditioning and ==classifier-free guidance== sampling.
- 4D world composition: **GET3D** generates vehicle meshes and **AvatarClip** generates rigged pedestrians, then extended Waymax trajectories with an ==Intelligent Driver Model== pose actors per timestep.
- Physics-informed sensing: ==dual marching cubes== meshing plus multi-beam raycasting, ==Lambertian cosine-law== intensity for actors, and a ==U-Net== stochastic raydrop sampled with ==Gumbel sigmoid==.

## Results

- On KITTI-360 unconditional generation, achieves best JSD of **0.119** and second-best MMD of **1.67e-4**, while uniquely supporting intensity, control, and temporal consistency.
- Beats a sequence-diffusion baseline on temporal coherence: average ICP energy **0.014** versus **0.078**, outlier rate **7.12%** versus **20.56%**, Chamfer distance **0.17m** versus **0.39m**.
- Pretraining CenterPoint on generated data lifts detection mAP from **58.2%** to **61.3%**; planner collision rate drops from **1.65%** to **1.12%**, a **32%** relative cut.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Factorizing generation into world creation then sensor simulation buys temporal consistency and occlusion correctness for free, instead of learning them from sequence data.
> - Physics-only intensity models are insufficient; a learned distribution match cut Wasserstein distance from 0.0813 to 0.0197 against real returns.
> - Asset-free generative simulation yields labeled data whose sim-to-real gap is small enough to pretrain both perception and planning models usefully.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Generative models have demonstrated capabilities across various domains, including image and video synthesis, 3D content creation, and data compression. More recently, their application has extended to robotics, where they facilitate the generation of realistic scenarios and sensory data for training and validating embodied agents. This application aims to reduce the reliance on costly real-world experiments and data collection.

Within this context, the specific task of generating scenario-specific, realistic LiDAR point cloud sequences for autonomous driving applications remains an area of ongoing research. Existing approaches to LiDAR generation broadly fall into two categories:

1. **LiDAR Generative Modeling Methods:** These methods typically focus on single-frame generation and often lack semantic controllability and temporal consistency. Examples include approaches utilizing range image representations with GANs, VAEs, or diffusion models, and voxel-based representations with VQGANs. Such methods have not addressed the generation of coherent sequences or conditioning on specific driving scenarios.
2. **LiDAR Resimulation:** This category relies on pre-existing assets, either manually created (e.g., in simulators like CARLA or AirSim) or reconstructed from real-world data (e.g., as meshes or neural fields). While these methods can produce physically plausible LiDAR, they are constrained by the high cost of asset creation or reconstruction, which limits diversity, scalability, and broader applicability to unseen environments. The "sim2real" gap, concerning both asset design and physics simulation, also presents a challenge.

LidarDM aims to address the limitations of these existing paradigms by introducing a novel approach that combines a generative 4D world model with physics-informed sensor simulation. It seeks to bridge the gap between uncontrollable single-frame generation and asset-dependent re-simulation by enabling the generation of realistic, layout-aware, physically plausible, and temporally coherent LiDAR videos from a purely generative process. This positions LidarDM as a contribution towards developing asset-free, controllable, and temporally consistent synthetic data generation for autonomous driving.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives of the LidarDM research are to develop a generative model capable of producing realistic and useful LiDAR data sequences for autonomous driving applications. This overarching goal is driven by several key motivations:

1. **Controllable LiDAR Synthesis Guided by Driving Scenarios:** A significant motivation is to enable the generation of LiDAR data that is explicitly conditioned on specific driving scenarios, represented by semantic layouts. Current generative LiDAR models generally lack this level of semantic control. The ability to specify a scenario (e.g., road structure, presence of other vehicles, lane markings) and generate corresponding LiDAR data is crucial for testing and training autonomous driving systems, particularly for evaluating performance in diverse and safety-critical situations. This capability allows for the creation of targeted test cases that might be rare or dangerous to collect in the real world.

2. **4D LiDAR Point Cloud Generation for Temporally Coherent Sequences:** Existing generative LiDAR models are largely limited to producing individual, static frames. However, autonomous driving systems operate in dynamic environments and require temporally coherent sensor data to train and evaluate their perception and planning modules effectively. Generating realistic sequences of LiDAR point clouds that maintain physical plausibility and consistency over time is a core objective. This includes ensuring that objects move realistically, scenes evolve coherently, and sensor readings reflect these dynamics accurately across frames.

3. **Overcoming Limitations of Existing LiDAR Generation and Simulation:**
 - **Cost and Diversity of Assets:** Traditional simulators and re-simulation methods are heavily reliant on manually created or real-world reconstructed 3D assets. This process is expensive, time-consuming, and inherently limits the diversity and novelty of the generated environments. LidarDM aims to eliminate this dependency by generating scenes and objects in a purely synthetic manner, allowing for the creation of environments unseen during training, such as the example of Champs-Élysées.
 - **Lack of Controllability and Temporal Coherency in Generative Models:** Prior generative LiDAR models, while capable of producing realistic single frames, do not offer mechanisms for scenario-specific control or the generation of temporally consistent sequences. LidarDM seeks to integrate these missing capabilities.
 - **Reducing the Sim2Real Gap:** The disparity between simulated and real-world data (the "sim2real" gap) can hinder the transferability of models trained solely on synthetic data. By generating more realistic, physically plausible, and noisy (through raydrop simulation) LiDAR data, LidarDM aims to reduce this gap, thereby improving the performance of perception and planning models when deployed in real environments.

4. **Enabling Data Augmentation for Autonomous Driving Modules:** The ultimate motivation is to provide a tool for augmenting training datasets for 3D perception models (e.g., object detectors) and planning models. By generating large volumes of diverse, labeled synthetic LiDAR data with known ground truth, LidarDM can potentially improve model robustness and accuracy, especially in low-data regimes or for underrepresented scenarios, without incurring the high costs of real-world data collection and manual annotation.

In summary, LidarDM is motivated by the need for a comprehensive, controllable, and scalable generative framework that can produce realistic and temporally coherent LiDAR data, thereby enhancing the development and validation of autonomous driving technologies.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

LidarDM operates on the principle of generating a complete 4D virtual world first, and then simulating realistic sensory observations within this environment. The methodology can be broken down into several stages:

#### 3.1. A. Problem Formulation and 4D World Representation

The problem is formulated as sampling from a joint distribution $p(X, P, W|I)$, where $X$ is the LiDAR point cloud video, $P$ is the set of trajectories, $W$ is the 4D world, and $I$ is the input bird’s-eye-view semantic layout. This joint distribution is factorized into sequential generation tasks:
1. Static scene and object generation: $p(s|I) \cdot \prod_i p(o_i|I)$
2. Trajectory generation: $\prod_t p(\tau_t|\tau_{<t}, W, I)$
3. Sensor simulation: $\prod_t p(x_t|\tau_t, W)$

The 4D world $W$ is represented as a static scene geometry $s$ and dynamic objects $o_0, \dots, o_N$. Both are primarily represented as occupancy grids. Dynamics are modeled by trajectories $P = \{\tau^0, \dots, \tau^T\}$, where $\tau^t$ includes the poses of the ego vehicle and all actors. A composed scene at time $t$ is denoted $W_t = \pi(W, \tau_t)$, where $\pi$ is the composition operator. The static scene $s$ also encodes truncated signed distance function (TSDF) and LiDAR intensity values in a 2-channel representation $R^{2 \times L \times W \times H}$.

#### 3.2. B. Scene, Object, and Trajectory Generation

This stage focuses on creating the underlying 4D world components.

1. **Scene Generation:**
 - **Goal:** To generate the static, intensity-infused background scene $s$ conditioned on an input map layout $I$.
 - **Model:** A latent diffusion model (LDM) is employed. The high-dimensional scene $s$ is encoded into a continuous latent representation $z$ using a scene encoder $E_\theta(s)$, and decoded back to $\tilde{s}$ by $D_\theta(z)$. Similarly, the high-definition map layout $I$ is encoded into a compact latent space $c = M_\theta(I)$.
 - **Sampling:** Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG) sampling is used, employing a probabilistic denoising diffusion model $F_\theta(z, c)$. Langevin dynamics iteratively denoise a noisy latent sample $z_k$ until a clean sample $z_0$ is obtained, which is then decoded to recover the 3D scene $s$.
 - **Training:** The model is trained on a dataset of scene geometry-map pairs. Since dense ground truth scene geometry is not readily available, a pseudo-ground truth is recovered from real LiDAR sequences using NKSR, a textured 3D reconstruction method. Dynamic objects are removed from these reconstructions to ensure only static scene elements are learned. The autoencoders for scene geometry and map layout are trained with reconstruction and KL divergence losses, while the LDM is trained using a score matching loss.

2. **Object Generation:**
 - For dynamic traffic participants, two established object generation frameworks are utilized:
 - **GET3D:** Used to generate diverse vehicle meshes. These meshes are then rescaled to fit target bounding boxes specified by the layout.
 - **AvatarClip:** Used for pedestrian generation, conditioned on SMPL pose and shape parameters. Mixamo is then used to animate these rigged models, producing realistic 4D human walking motions.

3. **Trajectory Generation:**
 - The Waymax data-driven 2D BEV traffic simulator is extended to generate and control actor behaviors.
 - **Conditional Generation:** For specific scenarios, real-world ego-vehicle and agent trajectories from the Waymo Open Motion Dataset (WOMD) are replayed. An Intelligent Driver Model (IDM) is incorporated to update agent accelerations and prevent collisions.
 - **Unconditional Generation:** Trajectories are sampled from a pre-computed trajectory bank derived from the Waymo Open dataset.
 - Heuristics are applied to ensure physical feasibility (e.g., no hovering) and collision avoidance among agents and with the scene.

#### 3.3. C. Physics-Informed LiDAR Generation

This final stage transforms the 4D world into realistic LiDAR point clouds.

1. **Scene Composition:** The TSDF channel from the generated static scene $s$ is used to extract a 3D mesh via Dual Marching Cubes. The generated actor meshes are transformed to world coordinates based on their respective poses and the ego car's pose at each timestep $\tau_t$. These are then composed with the static scene to form the full world geometry $W_t$. For pedestrians, forward kinematics articulates their body shape to simulate animated movement.

2. **Physics-based Ray Casting:** LiDAR point clouds are generated by simulating the physical process of LiDAR sensors. For each time step, beams of light are projected from the virtual sensor (ego-vehicle) into the composed scene $W_t$. The intersection points $\{x, y, z\}$ are calculated based on the sensor configuration (matching real-world LiDARs like KITTI HDL-64E and Waymo specifications, including elevation and azimuth angles) using raycasting.

3. **Intensity Modeling:**
 - For points hitting the static background scene, intensity values are queried directly from the intensity channel of the generated scene volume $s$.
 - For points hitting dynamic objects, a physics-based approach utilizing Lambert's Cosine Law is applied: Intensity = $\alpha + \beta (I_{it} \cdot N_t^o)$, where $I_{it}$ is the vector from the sensor to the point, $N_t^o$ is the normal vector of the object surface at that point, and $\alpha, \beta$ are hand-tuned constants to match empirical intensity distributions from real datasets.

4. **Stochastic Raydrop:** To introduce realism and simulate environmental/sensor noise, a "raydrop" mechanism is incorporated. For each raycasted scan, it is projected onto a 2D spherical range image. A U-Net architecture predicts the raydrop probability for each pixel in this image. This U-Net is trained on real-world LiDAR scan raydrop masks, requiring only a range map as input. Finally, sampling from this probability mask (using Gumbel sigmoid) produces the noisy, final LiDAR scan $x_t$.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted to evaluate LidarDM covered unconditional single-frame generation, map-conditioned multi-frame generation, and the utility of generated data for perception and planning models.

#### 4.1. A. Unconditional Single-Frame Generation (KITTI-360 Dataset)

- **Quantitative Metrics:** LidarDM was compared against several baselines using metrics such as MMD\_BEV (Maximum Mean Discrepancy on Bird's Eye View) and JSD\_BEV (Jensen-Shannon Divergence on Bird's Eye View) histogram of voxel occupancy. LidarDM achieved MMD\_BEV of 1.67e-4 and JSD\_BEV of 0.119, performing favorably against state-of-the-art methods like UltraLiDAR (9.67e-5, 0.132) and LidarGen (2.95e-4, 0.136). The paper noted that UltraLiDAR was directly trained on single-frame generation, which is the task of this benchmark.
- **Qualitative Observations:** Visual comparisons indicated that LidarDM generated samples with more detailed salient objects (e.g., cars, pedestrians), sharper 3D structures (e.g., walls), and realistic road layouts compared to competing unconditional methods.

#### 4.2. B. Map-conditioned Multi-Frame Generation (Waymo Open Dataset)

1. **Temporal Consistency:**
 - **Quantitative Metrics:** LidarDM's temporal consistency was assessed by aligning consecutive frames using ICP (Iterative Closest Point) and measuring the average point-to-plane energy, total ICP energy, and outlier percentage. LidarDM achieved a significantly lower average ICP energy (0.014 m vs. 0.078 m for a sequence diffusion baseline), total ICP energy (916.94 m vs. 3616.58 m), and outlier percentage (7.12% vs. 20.56% with a 0.5m threshold). Chamfer distance was also lower (0.17 m vs. 0.39 m).
 - **Qualitative Observations:** Accumulated LiDAR point clouds over multiple frames visually demonstrated LidarDM's temporal coherence and consistency with the map conditioning. The physics-based simulation ensured realistic occlusion patterns and LiDAR sweep structures.

2. **Layout-aware LiDAR Generation:**
 - **Quantitative Metrics:** The semantic correlation between generated LiDAR and ground-truth layouts was evaluated using CenterPoint (a 3D object detector trained on real data). LidarDM achieved a mean Average Precision (mAP) of 56.4% for vehicle detection on its generated data, comparable to 59.7% achieved on real LiDAR. The mAP agreement between LidarDM's generated LiDAR and real LiDAR was 81.1%, indicating strong semantic alignment and realism.

3. **Intensity Evaluation:**
 - **Quantitative Metrics:** The realism of generated LiDAR intensity values was measured using Wasserstein Distance (WD) against real-world intensity distributions. LidarDM achieved a WD of 0.0197, significantly outperforming a physics-based baseline (WD of 0.0813), suggesting a high degree of intensity realism.

#### 4.3. C. Augmenting Real Data with LidarDM

LidarDM's capability to generate layout-conditioned data was tested for its utility in data augmentation for autonomous driving models.

1. **Perception Model Sim2Real (CenterPoint):**
 - A LiDAR-based 3D object detection model (CenterPoint with PointPillars backbone) was pre-trained on 70,000 LidarDM-generated frames, paired with object labels. This pre-trained model was then fine-tuned on 35,000 real Waymo frames.
 - **Result:** The LidarDM-augmented model achieved an mAP score of 61.3%, which was a 3.1% improvement over the model trained solely on real data (58.2%). This indicates that LidarDM-generated data is effective for improving perception model accuracy and reducing the domain gap.

2. **Planning Model Sim2Real (NMP-inspired planner):**
 - A learning-based motion planner was trained first on 92,000 LidarDM-generated snippets and then fine-tuned on 9,200 real sequences.
 - **Result:** The model pre-trained with LidarDM data demonstrated improved planning performance, particularly in safety-critical aspects. The collision rate after 3 seconds was reduced by 32% (relative) compared to a model trained only on real data. L2 trajectory errors also showed minor improvements across 1.0s, 2.0s, and 3.0s predictions. This outcome suggests that conditional LiDAR generation can positively impact end-to-end motion planners.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

LidarDM represents an advancement in the field of generative modeling for autonomous driving applications due to its capabilities and the architectural choices made.

The core significance lies in its ability to **generate realistic, layout-aware, physically plausible, and temporally coherent LiDAR videos**. This addresses several limitations present in prior work:
1. **Controllable Data Generation:** Unlike previous LiDAR generative models that primarily focus on single-frame, unconditional generation, LidarDM offers semantic control through map conditioning. This allows for the synthesis of specific driving scenarios, which is crucial for targeted testing and training of autonomous systems, especially for long-tail or safety-critical events that are difficult and expensive to acquire in real-world data.
2. **4D Temporal Consistency:** The ability to generate coherent sequences of LiDAR point clouds over time, rather than isolated frames, marks a notable contribution. This temporal consistency is vital for training perception and planning modules that operate on dynamic, time-series data.
3. **Asset-Free Simulation:** By generating the entire 4D world (static scenes and dynamic actors) in a purely generative manner, LidarDM mitigates the need for costly, manually created, or real-world reconstructed 3D assets. This approach enhances scalability, diversity, and allows for the creation of novel environments not seen in real-world datasets, such as the example of Champs-Élysées. This also contributes to reducing the "sim2real" gap often associated with synthetic environments.

The potential impact of LidarDM is substantial, particularly for the development and validation of autonomous driving technologies:
- **Enhanced Training and Testing of Perception Models:** The generated data, with its realism and semantic labels, can serve as a potent augmentation strategy for training 3D object detectors and other perception modules. The experiments demonstrated an improvement in mAP for a detector pre-trained with LidarDM data.
- **Improved Autonomous Vehicle Planning and Control:** The findings indicate that LidarDM-generated data can positively influence the training of motion planners, leading to a reduction in collision rates. This suggests that complex behaviors and decision-making processes in autonomous vehicles could be robustly trained using synthetic data.
- **Cost Reduction in Data Acquisition:** By providing a method for synthesizing high volumes of diverse and realistic LiDAR data, LidarDM can potentially reduce the reliance on expensive and resource-intensive real-world data collection and manual annotation efforts.
- **Exploration of Diverse and Safety-Critical Scenarios:** The controllable nature of LidarDM facilitates the generation of a wide array of scenarios, including challenging edge cases or hazardous situations that are unsafe to simulate in physical testing. This enables more thorough testing and validation of autonomous systems under various conditions.
- **Foundation for Generative World Models:** The integrated 4D world generation framework employed by LidarDM contributes to the broader research direction of generative world models, which aim to create comprehensive, dynamic virtual environments for training and evaluating intelligent agents.

In conclusion, LidarDM represents a step towards fully generative, controllable, and realistic LiDAR simulation, offering a tool for advancing autonomous driving research and development by addressing key challenges in data acquisition, diversity, and temporal consistency.
