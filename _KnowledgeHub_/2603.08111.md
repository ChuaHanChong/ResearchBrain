# DeReCo: Decoupling Representation and Coordination Learning for Object-Adaptive Decentralized Multi-Robot Cooperative Transport

> [!summary] Summary
> DeReCo, a multi-agent reinforcement learning framework, decouples object-dependent representation learning from multi-agent coordination to enable robust decentralized cooperative transport of diverse, unseen objects. This approach achieved an average 0.80 success rate on unseen objects in simulation and demonstrated successful real-robot transfer with 9 out of 10 transports completed for previously unencountered objects.

## Problem

- Multi-robot cooperative transport systems struggle to generalize to objects with diverse shapes and physical properties, especially under decentralized control and partial observability.
- Traditional end-to-end multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL) methods suffer from 'structural coupling,' where inaccuracies in object representation learning interfere with multi-agent coordination learning, leading to unstable and inefficient training.
- MARL systems face challenges with non-stationarity, as each robot's learning is destabilized by the simultaneous policy evolution of other agents in the cooperative task.

## Method

- **DeReCo** introduces a three-stage training strategy within a Centralized Training with Decentralized Execution (CTDE) framework built upon Multi-Agent Proximal Policy Optimization (MAPPO); Stage 1 (MARL with Privileged Object Information) initially trains stable coordination policies by providing explicit object properties (privileged information) to both actor and critic.
- Stage 2 (Adaptive Encoder Learning) independently trains a supervised adaptive encoder to reconstruct object-dependent representations from local observations, decoupling this from coordination learning.
- Stage 3 (MARL with Adaptive Encoder) fine-tunes coordination policies by integrating the frozen adaptive encoder into the actor for decentralized execution, while the critic continues to leverage privileged information for stable value estimation.

## Results

- DeReCo achieved an average success rate of **0.91** on seen objects and **0.80** on unseen objects in simulation, outperforming baselines which struggled with generalization (e.g., MAPPO w/o PI: **0.70** seen, **0.58** unseen).
- Real-robot experiments with two HSRs demonstrated successful sim-to-real transfer, with DeReCo completing 5/5 transports for one unseen object and 4/5 for another, in contrast to the best-performing baseline (MAPPO w/o AE) which failed all 10 real-robot trials.
- The framework exhibited higher tracking rewards and improved training stability in simulation compared to end-to-end joint optimization baselines, indicating greater sample efficiency during policy learning.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly decoupling object-dependent representation learning from multi-agent coordination learning significantly mitigates bidirectional interference, leading to more stable and sample-efficient training.
> - Utilizing privileged object information during an initial MARL training stage is effective for establishing robust cooperative behaviors before tackling the challenge of inferring object properties from partial observations.
> - Training an adaptive encoder in a supervised manner enables reliable inference of object features from limited local sensory input, which is crucial for achieving generalization and decentralized execution without direct access to global object properties.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in this paper addresses a challenge in the field of multi-robot cooperative transport: achieving generalization across objects with diverse shapes and physical properties while operating under decentralized control and partial observability. This area lies at the intersection of multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL), robot manipulation, and robust control.

Existing approaches to multi-robot cooperative transport can broadly be categorized into model-based and model-free methods. Model-based approaches often rely on explicit object dynamics models and control strategies (e.g., constrained optimal control, MPC, adaptive control) to ensure stability and convergence. However, these methods typically assume rigid contact between robots and the object, which may not hold in real-world, non-rigid grasping scenarios.

Model-free MARL-based methods have demonstrated success in cooperative transport for various robot platforms (e.g., mobile manipulators, quadrupeds, bipeds, aerial platforms) by learning control policies without requiring explicit dynamics models. A common strategy in MARL is Centralized Training with Decentralized Execution (CTDE), where global information is leveraged during training to stabilize learning, but individual robots execute policies based solely on local observations. While these MARL methods have advanced cooperative transport capabilities, most have been evaluated in single-object settings, limiting their applicability to scenarios involving diverse or novel objects.

Another line of research focuses on using reinforcement learning (RL) for object transport with diverse objects, often employing domain randomization during training to encourage generalization. These methods typically involve jointly optimizing object-dependent representations and control policies in an end-to-end manner. Some work has explored multi-stage learning, separating representation learning from policy learning, but this has primarily been applied to single-robot object transport tasks.

The present work identifies a significant gap: the integration of robust object generalization with stable multi-robot coordination under decentralized execution. The authors pinpoint two key challenges: (1) learning object-dependent representations from partial local observations, and (2) managing non-stationarity inherent in MARL coordination learning. They argue that the typical end-to-end joint optimization of these two aspects leads to a "structural coupling" where inaccuracies in representation destabilize coordination, and MARL non-stationarity degrades representation learning, resulting in inefficient and unstable training. DeReCo aims to address this structural coupling by explicitly decoupling representation learning from coordination learning, thereby contributing a novel MARL framework for object-adaptive multi-robot cooperative transport.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a robust and generalizable multi-robot cooperative transport system that can handle objects with diverse shapes and physical properties under decentralized execution. The core motivation stems from the limitations of current approaches and the inherent challenges in achieving this objective.

The specific key objectives and motivations are detailed as follows:

1. **Overcome Object Generalization under Partial Observability:** In real-world decentralized multi-robot scenarios, robots operate with local observations and often lack direct access to critical object-dependent properties such as shape, mass, and friction during execution. Existing methods frequently struggle to generalize to unseen objects because their policies are not designed to infer these properties from limited sensory inputs. The motivation is to enable robots to learn and adapt to diverse objects by inferring necessary object-dependent representations, even when only local, partial observations are available.

2. **Mitigate Non-stationarity in Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning (MARL):** Cooperative transport necessitates intricate coordination among multiple robots. In MARL, the learning process for each robot is challenged by non-stationarity, as the policies of other agents simultaneously evolve. This dynamic environment complicates stable policy learning. The motivation is to achieve stable coordination learning in MARL, reducing the destabilizing effects of this non-stationarity.

3. **Address Bidirectional Interference from Structural Coupling:** A central argument of the paper is that traditional end-to-end MARL approaches, which jointly optimize object-dependent representations and coordinated policies, suffer from "bidirectional interference." Specifically, inaccurate or unstable representations derived from partial observations can destabilize the coordination policies, while the non-stationarity of MARL can, in turn, degrade the quality of representation learning. This structural coupling leads to sample-inefficient and unstable training processes. The key motivation is to decouple these two learning processes—representation learning and coordination learning—to mitigate this interference, thereby achieving more stable and sample-efficient training.

4. **Improve Sample Efficiency and Generalization:** By addressing the structural coupling, the overarching objective is to improve the efficiency with which the multi-robot system learns effective policies and to enhance the robustness and generalization capabilities of these policies across a wider range of objects and transport scenarios, including those not encountered during training. The goal is not just to perform the task but to do so reliably for novel objects, a crucial aspect for practical deployment.

In summary, the motivation is to build a more effective, adaptable, and deployable multi-robot cooperative transport system by structurally reorganizing the learning process to handle the intertwined challenges of object generalization and multi-agent coordination.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework, DeReCo, is designed to decouple object-dependent representation learning from coordination learning in MARL for object-adaptive decentralized multi-robot cooperative transport. It adopts a three-stage training strategy within a Centralized Training with Decentralized Execution (CTDE) framework, built upon Multi-Agent Proximal Policy Optimization (MAPPO).

**A. Problem Setting and Assumptions**
The problem is formulated as a Decentralized Partially Observable Markov Decision Process (Dec-POMDP) involving two mobile manipulators (HSRs). The objective is to grasp and transport objects of diverse shapes and physical properties to a specified goal pose.

- **Local Observations:** Each robot receives local observations, including its previous action, arm joint angles, gripper states, distance to goal, object position/orientation relative to its end-effector, and ternary force signals from its gripper.
- **Actions:** Each robot controls its planar base velocity, arm joint velocities, and gripper torque.
- **Assumptions:**
 - Robots observe their end-effector and object poses.
 - Other robots' grasping poses and contact forces are not available.
 - Object shape and physical properties (mass, friction) are available during training (privileged information, PI) but not during decentralized execution.

**B. Multi-Agent Proximal Policy Optimization (MAPPO)**
DeReCo uses MAPPO as its underlying MARL algorithm. In the CTDE setting:
- **Critic:** Trained with access to the global state (or joint observations and privileged information) to estimate the state-value function $V_\phi(s_t)$.
- **Actor:** Each robot $i$ has a decentralized policy $\pi_{\theta_i}(a_i_t | o_i_t)$, trained using only its local observations $o_i_t$. Policies are optimized using a clipped surrogate objective, and the critic is optimized with a clipped value loss.

**C. DeReCo's Three-Stage Training Strategy**

The core of DeReCo is its three-stage training process:

**1. Stage 1: MARL with Privileged Object Information**
- **Purpose:** To establish stable coordination policies by isolating coordination learning from the complexities of inferring object properties from partial observations.
- **Process:**
 - Centralized training of the MAPPO algorithm is performed.
 - The **critic** learns a centralized value function, leveraging joint observations ($o_i_t, o_j_t$) and explicit privileged object information (p) (mass, friction, shape of the object). This rich information helps stabilize value estimation in the non-stationary MARL environment.
 - The **actor** for each robot $i$, parameterized as $\pi_{\theta_i}(a_i_t | o_i_t, p)$, is trained using its local observation $o_i_t$ *and* the privileged object information $p$.
 - By providing privileged information, this stage ensures that robots have reliable object-dependent representations, allowing them to focus solely on learning effective cooperative behaviors without the added challenge of inferring object properties.

**2. Stage 2: Adaptive Encoder Learning**
- **Purpose:** To independently learn an adaptive encoder that can reconstruct object-dependent representations from local observations, enabling decentralized inference without privileged information. This stage decouples representation learning.
- **Process:**
 - **Data Collection:** Policies trained in Stage 1 are rolled out for diverse objects (sampled shapes, masses, friction coefficients). For each robot $i$ at each timestep $t$, pairs of $(o_i_t, g_i_t)$ are recorded. Here, $g_i_t$ represents the ground-truth object-dependent representation, which would be the output of the encoder used by the actor in Stage 1 when provided with privileged information.
 - **Supervised Learning of Adaptive Encoder:** An adaptive encoder $e_\psi$ is trained in a supervised manner. This encoder takes only the local observation $o_i_t$ as input and aims to reconstruct the object-dependent representation, $\hat{g}_i_t = e_\psi(o_i_t)$. The encoder is typically implemented as an LSTM network.
 - The training objective is to minimize the Mean Squared Error (MSE) $L(g_i_t, \hat{g}_i_t)$ between the reconstructed representation $\hat{g}_i_t$ and the target representation $g_i_t$ collected during data collection. This stage ensures that each robot can infer object-relevant features from its limited sensory input, mirroring the information it had in Stage 1, but without direct access to privileged information.

**3. Stage 3: MARL with Adaptive Encoder**
- **Purpose:** To fine-tune the coordination policies for decentralized execution by progressively removing privileged information and integrating the adaptive encoder.
- **Process:**
 - The actor and critic networks are initialized with the trained weights from Stage 1.
 - The **adaptive encoder** $e_\psi$ learned in Stage 2 is *frozen* and incorporated into the actor's architecture. Now, the actor for robot $i$ receives its local observation $o_i_t$, passes it through the frozen adaptive encoder to obtain $\hat{g}_i_t$, and then uses this inferred representation to select an action: $\pi_{\theta_i}(a_i_t | o_i_t)$.
 - The **critic** continues to leverage joint observations and privileged information ($o_i_t, o_j_t, p$) during training to maintain stability in the CTDE framework. This allows the critic to provide accurate value estimates to the actors, while the actors learn to rely on their locally inferred representations.
 - This stage enables the policy to adapt from using explicit privileged information (Stage 1) to implicit inference from local observations (via the adaptive encoder), leading to robust policies suitable for decentralized execution.

By separating the complex task of learning object representations from the challenge of multi-agent coordination, DeReCo aims to reduce the interference between these two learning objectives, leading to more stable, sample-efficient, and generalizable policies.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The effectiveness of DeReCo was evaluated through cooperative transport experiments with two Human Support Robots (HSRs) in both simulation and real-robot setups. The experiments aimed to answer three research questions (RQ1-RQ3).

**A. Experimental Setup**
- **Simulation Environment:** NVIDIA Isaac Sim, utilizing 512 parallel environments for training.
- **Robots:** Two HSRs.
- **Observations:** Local observation $o_i$ for each robot (27-dimensional vector including previous action, arm joint angles, gripper state, goal distance, object pose relative to end-effector, and ternary force signals).
- **Actions:** 6-dimensional vector for planar base velocities, arm joint velocities, and gripper torque.
- **Reward:** Standardized reward function for cooperative transport.
- **Success Criterion:** Euclidean distance between object and goal less than 0.1 m at the end of an episode.
- **Objects:**
 - **Training (Seen) Objects (3 shapes):** Bar, Cylinder, Board. Masses randomized (0.2-1.0 kg), friction coefficients (0.5-1.0).
 - **Generalization (Unseen) Objects (6 shapes):** Hexagon, Triangle, L bar, Thick bar, Octagon, Semi-ellipse. Masses and friction randomized within the same ranges.
- **Compared Methods (Baselines):**
 - **MAPPO w/o PI:** End-to-end MAPPO without privileged information (PI), using only local observations.
 - **MAPPO w/o PI + LSTM:** Recurrent variant of MAPPO w/o PI, with an LSTM to infer representations.
 - **MAPPO w/o AE:** End-to-end MAPPO where the critic uses PI and joint observations, but the actor does not use an adaptive encoder and relies solely on raw local observations.
 - **MAPPO w/o AE + LSTM:** Recurrent variant of MAPPO w/o AE.
 - **MAPPO w PI:** End-to-end MAPPO where both actor and critic use PI and local/joint observations (served as an upper bound/reference).
- **Network Architecture:** Standard FC layers for actor/critic; a single-layer LSTM (128 units) for the adaptive encoder in DeReCo. Stage 3 networks initialized from Stage 1.

**B. RQ1: Can DeReCo Improve Training Performance?**
- **Result (Figure 4 - Tracking Reward):** DeReCo (represented by its Stage 3 learning curve) exhibited higher tracking rewards compared to MAPPO w/o AE. MAPPO w PI achieved the highest rewards, as expected due to its access to privileged information throughout training and execution. MAPPO w/o PI showed lower rewards, indicating the difficulty of learning without PI. Both LSTM-integrated baselines (MAPPO w/o PI + LSTM and MAPPO w/o AE + LSTM) performed poorly, suggesting that merely adding recurrence to end-to-end training is insufficient to resolve the structural coupling.
- **Interpretation:** The results indicate that decoupling representation learning from coordination learning, as in DeReCo, mitigates the interference between these two processes, leading to more stable and sample-efficient training and ultimately higher rewards compared to baselines that attempt joint optimization without an explicit decoupling mechanism.

**C. RQ2: Does DeReCo Generalize to Diverse Objects with Different Shapes and Physical Properties?**
- **Result (Table I - Success Rates):**
 - **Seen Objects:** DeReCo achieved an average success rate of 0.91, comparable to MAPPO w PI (0.91), and significantly outperformed MAPPO w/o PI (0.70) and MAPPO w/o AE (0.82). This confirms DeReCo's ability to achieve high coordination performance even when inferring object representations from local observations.
 - **Unseen Objects:** DeReCo consistently surpassed all baselines, achieving an average success rate of 0.80. This was notably higher than MAPPO w/o PI (0.58), MAPPO w/o AE (0.68), and the LSTM variants. MAPPO w PI performed poorly on unseen objects (0.45 average success rate) because it was given random one-hot vectors for unseen object IDs, demonstrating its lack of generalization when direct privileged information is mismatched or absent.
- **Result (Figure 5 - Failure Analysis on Unseen Objects):** For all methods, transport failures (object lifted but not reaching goal) were the most common type of failure on unseen objects. MAPPO w PI exhibited a disproportionately high rate of grasp-and-lift failures on unseen objects, attributed to its reliance on privileged object IDs during training that were randomly assigned for unseen objects, leading to inappropriate grasp strategies. DeReCo showed generally lower failure rates across all categories.
- **Interpretation:** DeReCo's strong performance on unseen objects demonstrates its improved generalization capability. This is attributed to its explicit mechanism for learning and reconstructing object-dependent representations from local observations, which is crucial for adapting to novel object geometries and physics without prior exposure.

**D. RQ3: Can the Trained Policy Successfully Transport Unseen Objects in the Real World?**
- **Setup:** Real-robot experiments were conducted with two HSRs and two unseen objects (Board, Frame), comparing DeReCo with the best-performing simulation baseline, MAPPO w/o AE. Five trials per object.
- **Result (Table II & Figure 7 - Real-Robot Comparisons):**
 - **MAPPO w/o AE:** Achieved 0/5 successful transports for both the Board and Frame. While robots could grasp and lift, they failed to transport the Board within the 0.1m goal distance and the Frame often tipped over after lifting. Average final errors were 0.18m for Board and 0.34m for Frame.
 - **DeReCo:** Successfully transported the Board in 5/5 trials and the Frame in 4/5 trials. Achieved average final object-goal distance errors of 0.09m for both objects, meeting the success criterion.
- **Interpretation:** DeReCo exhibited successful sim-to-real transfer and significantly outperformed the strongest baseline in hardware experiments. This validates its robustness and practical applicability for object-adaptive decentralized multi-robot cooperative transport in physical environments with unseen objects.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The DeReCo framework presents a notable contribution to the field of multi-robot cooperative transport, particularly in scenarios requiring adaptation to diverse and previously unseen objects under decentralized control.

**Significance:**
- **Addressing Fundamental Challenges:** The work directly tackles the structural coupling between object-dependent representation learning under partial observability and coordination learning in non-stationary MARL environments. By explicitly decoupling these two processes, DeReCo offers a more stable and sample-efficient approach to training complex multi-agent policies.
- **Enhanced Generalization:** A key significance is the demonstrated superior generalization capability to unseen objects with varied shapes, masses, and friction coefficients. This extends the applicability of MARL-based cooperative transport beyond single-object or limited-set scenarios.
- **Practical Real-World Applicability:** The successful transfer of policies from simulation to real HSRs for unseen objects highlights the practical relevance of DeReCo. This capability is critical for deploying multi-robot systems in dynamic and unstructured environments where precise knowledge of every object's properties is often unavailable.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Flexible Multi-Robot Deployment:** DeReCo could enable multi-robot systems to be deployed in a wider range of applications, such as logistics, manufacturing, disaster response, or assistance in diverse human environments, where robots must handle varied payloads without extensive pre-programming or re-training for each new item.
- **Reduced Development Costs:** By improving sample efficiency and generalization, DeReCo can potentially reduce the time and computational resources required for developing and deploying multi-robot cooperative transport systems. This would alleviate the burden of generating extensive training data for every possible object configuration.
- **Advancement in MARL for Partial Observability:** The decoupling strategy offers a modular paradigm that could influence the design of MARL algorithms for other complex tasks involving partial observability and interdependent learning objectives. It suggests that explicit architectural separation of concerns can lead to more robust learning outcomes.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The work establishes a strong baseline for future research into scaling generalization to even broader object diversity, larger teams of robots, and more complex interaction dynamics.

**Limitations and Future Work (as identified by the authors):**
The authors acknowledge two primary limitations and corresponding directions for future work:
1. **Scalability to Broader Object Diversity:** While DeReCo generalizes to unseen objects, extending this to an even wider range of shapes and physical properties might still be challenging due to the increased training cost associated with extensive domain randomization. Future work should explore methods to scale to a broader spectrum of object properties.
2. **Adaptation to Varying Numbers of Robots:** The current approach builds on MAPPO, whose policy input dimensionality is dependent on the number of robots, limiting its direct applicability to teams with a different number of agents. Future work aims to integrate the decoupling approach into decentralized MARL formulations that are inherently robust to variations in team size, followed by real-robot validation of scalability.

Despite these limitations, DeReCo represents a step towards robust, adaptable, and deployable multi-robot cooperative transport systems, addressing a critical need for generalization in real-world robotics.
