# R2PS: Worst-Case Robust Real-Time Pursuit Strategies under Partial Observability

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from the University of Chinese Academy of Sciences developed R2PS, a method for generating worst-case robust, real-time pursuit strategies for graph-based pursuit-evasion games under partial observability. R2PS achieved robust zero-shot generalization to unseen real-world graphs and consistently outperformed direct training methods on challenging adversarial evaders, while maintaining real-time inference speed of approximately 0.01 seconds per timestep on large graphs.

## Problem

- Solving graph-based pursuit-evasion games (PEGs) is computationally expensive, especially when environmental structures change dynamically.
- The inclusion of partial observability in PEGs increases problem complexity to PSPACE-hardness, severely limiting real-time applicability of traditional methods.
- Existing reinforcement learning approaches for large-scale PEGs lack robust zero-shot generalization to diverse environments and struggle in scenarios with partial information or strong, predictive evaders.

## Method

- The work extended a ==Dynamic Programming== (DP) algorithm to prove optimal pursuit and evasion strategies for Markov PEGs, valid for both synchronous and asynchronous evader moves with predictive capabilities.
- An efficient `˜O(|V|)` belief preservation mechanism was introduced for pursuers to maintain a set of possible evader positions and a belief distribution, enabling informed decision-making under partial observability.
- The belief mechanism was integrated into the Equilibrium Policy Generalization (EPG) framework, training a Graph Neural Network (GNN)-based pursuer policy via cross-graph reinforcement learning against the asynchronous-move optimal evader.

## Results

- Extended DP pursuers (`DP_belief`) achieved success rates up to **0.94** against an optimal asynchronous evader with an observation range of 2, significantly outperforming a shortest-path strategy.
- The R2PS policy demonstrated robust zero-shot generalization, achieving success rates between **0.20**-1.00 against the optimal asynchronous evader, consistently outperforming PSRO (**0.00**-**0.88**) trained directly on test graphs.
- R2PS maintained real-time inference performance of approximately **0.01** seconds per timestep on real-world graphs with over 2000 nodes, while retaining success rates of **0.33**-**0.76** against strong evaders.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Belief-averaged policies (`DP_belief`) consistently provided more effective and less pessimistic pursuit behaviors under partial observability compared to policies maximizing over possible positions.
> - Policy guidance from the extended DP pursuers during reinforcement learning training significantly enhanced efficiency and stability in learning robust generalized strategies.
> - The GNN-based RL pursuer policy achieved real-time inference speeds of `O(n^2 m)` on large, dynamically changing graphs, a substantial improvement over the `˜O(n^(m+1))` complexity of traditional DP recomputation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Pursuit-evasion games (PEGs) constitute a significant area of research within robotics and security, offering abstractions for real-world scenarios such as law enforcement operations or intruder defense. Graph-based PEGs, in particular, are utilized to model complex and large-scale environments where agent movements are abstracted as transitions between vertices.

Historically, determining optimal strategies for graph-based PEGs has been computationally intensive. Problems arise when real-world factors such as dynamic graph structures (e.g., changing urban street conditions) or partial observability are introduced. Partial observability, where agents have imperfect information about opponent positions, escalates the complexity to PSPACE-hardness, severely limiting the real-time applicability of traditional mathematical programming methods.

Reinforcement Learning (RL) has emerged as an alternative, offering potential for scalability and generalization. Previous RL applications in large-scale PEGs have primarily focused on scalability rather than robust generalization across diverse environments. Methods like MT-PSRO and Grasper have shown limited few-shot generalization to unseen opponent strategies or initial conditions and reportedly struggle with rapid changes in graph structures. The state-of-the-art Equilibrium Policy Generalization (EPG) framework introduced zero-shot generalization across graph structures but was restricted to scenarios with perfect information. Furthermore, existing works often do not account for scenarios where an evader possesses stronger observational capabilities or can predict pursuer actions.

This research addresses these limitations by extending the applicability of robust policy generalization to partially observable settings and considering an adversarially strong evader. It seeks to develop pursuit strategies that are both robust against worst-case evader behaviors and deployable in real-time within dynamically changing, partially observable environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the computational challenges and practical limitations of existing methods for solving pursuit-evasion games (PEGs) in realistic scenarios.

The motivations include:
- **Computational Expense:** Exactly solving graph-based PEGs is computationally expensive. Even minor structural changes in the environment necessitate time-consuming recomputations of worst-case robust pursuit strategies, hindering real-time application.
- **Real-world Factors:** The inclusion of real-world factors such as partial observability significantly increases problem difficulty, leading to PSPACE-hardness in determining optimal strategies within practical time limits.
- **Limitations of Current RL Approaches:** While reinforcement learning (RL) offers promise for generalization, existing state-of-the-art methods like EPG and Grasper are restricted to perfect information settings. They do not account for scenarios where pursuers operate with imperfect knowledge of the evader's position or where the evader can predict pursuer actions, which is crucial for real-world security applications.
- **Need for Robustness and Real-Time Applicability:** There is a specific absence of real-time applicable pursuit strategies for graph-based PEGs under partial observability, especially when considering a worst-case evader capable of predicting pursuer actions.

Given these motivations, the key objectives of this work are articulated as follows:
- **Develop Worst-Case Robust Real-Time Pursuit Strategies (R2PS) under Partial Observability:** The overarching objective is to introduce the first approach that provides pursuit strategies combining worst-case robustness with real-time applicability in partially observable environments.
- **Extend Dynamic Programming (DP) for Asynchronous Moves and Partial Observability:**
 - To theoretically analyze a traditional DP algorithm for Markov PEGs, proving its optimality even when the evader moves asynchronously and can predict pursuers' actions.
 - To propose a belief preservation mechanism that extends these DP pursuit strategies to a partially observable setting, allowing pursuers to make informed decisions despite imperfect information.
- **Integrate Belief Preservation with Cross-Graph Reinforcement Learning:** To embed the developed belief preservation mechanism into the state-of-the-art Equilibrium Policy Generalization (EPG) framework. This integration aims to enable the learning of a real-time, generalized pursuer policy through cross-graph reinforcement learning. This policy is trained against asynchronous-move DP evasion strategies.
- **Demonstrate Zero-Shot Generalization and Performance Superiority:** To verify that the learned policy achieves robust zero-shot generalization to unseen real-world graph structures. This includes demonstrating consistent outperformance compared to policies directly trained on test graphs using existing game RL approaches, thereby validating the effectiveness and practical utility of R2PS.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology involves a multi-stage approach, combining theoretical analysis of dynamic programming (DP) algorithms, development of a belief preservation mechanism for partial observability, and integration into an adversarial reinforcement learning framework for cross-graph generalization.

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation

The work models pursuit-evasion games as infinite-horizon two-player zero-sum Markov games on a graph G = ⟨V, E⟩. The state `s` comprises the positions of `m` pursuers (`s_p`) and one evader (`s_e`). Actions involve moving to an adjacent vertex or staying put. A positive reward is received upon evader capture, and a discount factor `γ` encourages quick capture. The solution concept is a Nash Equilibrium for synchronous moves.

To reflect real-world adversarial conditions, two extensions are introduced:
- **Asynchronous Moves:** The evader can observe or predict the pursuers' actions `a` before making its own move, transforming the evader's policy into `ν(s, a)`.
- **Partial Observability:** Pursuers have limited observation capabilities, only detecting the evader within a specified range. The pursuer's policy `μ(o)` depends on the history of local observations `o`. The evader, however, is assumed to possess global information.

#### 3.2. Dynamic Programming for Markov PEGs

The core is a DP algorithm (Algorithm 1) that computes a distance table `D(s)`, representing the worst-case number of timesteps for pursuers to capture the evader from state `s`. This algorithm generates minimax policies `μ*(s)` for pursuers and `ν*(s)` for evaders under synchronous moves. Theorem 1 establishes that `(μ*, ν*)` constitutes a pure-strategy Nash equilibrium if one exists.

#### 3.3. Extending DP Policies to Asynchronous Moves and Partial Observability

- **Asynchronous-Move Setting:** The evader's DP policy is redefined as `ν*(s_p, s_e, n_p)`, utilizing the pursuers' chosen next position `n_p`. Lemma 1 reveals that `D(n_p, n_e) = min_{s_p} max_{s_e} D(s_p, s_e) + 1` for `D(n_p, n_e) > 0`. This result is used to prove Theorem 2, which states that `μ*` ensures capture within `D(s)` steps against any evasion strategy, and `ν*` avoids capture in less than `D(s)` steps against any pursuit strategy. Corollary 1 concludes that both `μ*` and `ν*` are optimal strategies in the asynchronous-move setting. Theorem 3 further confirms that if `D(s) = ∞`, the evader `ν*` can never be captured.

- **Partially Observable Setting:** The pursuers' observation range limits their knowledge of the evader's exact position.
 - **Belief Preservation Mechanism:** To handle partial observability, the pursuers maintain a set `Pos` of possible evader positions and a belief distribution `belief` over these positions. `Pos` is initialized with the known evader starting position and updated at each timestep: if observed, `Pos` is the evader's current location; if unobserved, `Pos` becomes the set of one-step neighbors of previous `Pos` nodes, excluding any currently observed locations. The `belief` function is updated by spreading probability from `belief_old` to `Neighbor(Pos)` using a default uniform evader policy `ν` (since the true `ν` is unknown to pursuers). The computational complexity of this mechanism is `˜O(|V|)`.
 - **Observation-Based Pursuer Policies:** Two policies are proposed based on the DP distance table `D`:
 - `μ(s_p, Pos)` (position-extended): A minimax policy across `Pos`, assuming full observability after the current step.
 - `μ(s_p, belief)` (belief-averaged): Averages `D` values weighted by the belief distribution, promoting more effective pursuit when `Pos` is large.
 - Lemma 2 proves that both policies reduce to `μ*` under perfect information (when `Pos` is a singleton).

#### 3.4. Finding Robust Real-Time Pursuit Strategies (R2PS) via Adversarial Reinforcement Learning across Graphs

To achieve real-time applicability and generalization, the belief preservation mechanism is integrated into the Equilibrium Policy Generalization (EPG) framework.

- **Cross-Graph Reinforcement Learning Pipeline:** A diverse training set of graphs with precomputed `D` tables and their induced `(μ*, ν*)` policies is used. In each training iteration, a graph `G_i` is sampled. `μ*_i` serves as a reference policy for guidance, and `ν*_i` (the asynchronous-move optimal evader policy) acts as an adversary.
- **Policy Learning:** A Graph Neural Network (GNN)-represented pursuer policy `π_θ` is trained using Soft Actor-Critic (SAC) as the backbone RL algorithm. The policy loss `L(θ|s) = J_π(θ|s) + β D_KL(μ*(s), π(s))` combines the standard SAC policy loss `J_π` with a KL-divergence term that guides `π_θ` towards the reference policy `μ*` (or its observation-based variants `μ(s_p, Pos)` or `μ(s_p, belief)` under partial observability).
- **Implementation and Complexity Analysis:** The GNN architecture employs a decentralized, parameter-sharing design with multi-head self-attention and adjacency matrix masks for state encoding, followed by a decoder and pointer network for graph-based action output. The overall inference time complexity for the RL pursuer policy at each timestep is `O(n^2 m) + ˜O(n) = O(n^2 m)`, where `n` is the number of vertices and `m` is the number of pursuers. This is significantly faster than recomputing DP policies, which has a complexity of `˜O(n^(m+1))` for dynamically changing graphs.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluations assessed both the extended DP pursuers and the generalized RL pursuers (R2PS) under partial observability, assuming two pursuers (m=2) against a single evader. The evader was configured as the provably optimal DP evader with global observations and asynchronous moves. Test environments included Grid Maps, Scotland-Yard Map, Downtown Map, and seven real-world locations (e.g., Times Square, Sydney Opera House).

#### 4.1. Evaluations of Extended DP Pursuers

- **Performance Comparison:** The shortest-path strategy, which assumes full observability, demonstrated low success rates (0.00-0.29) against the optimal DP evader. In contrast, the extended DP pursuers, `DP_Pos` (position-extended) and `DP_belief` (belief-averaged), achieved significantly higher success rates (0.24-0.94) despite an observation range of 2.
- **Effectiveness of Belief Averaging:** `DP_belief` consistently outperformed `DP_Pos` across all test graphs. This suggests that averaging timesteps using belief distribution, rather than simply maximizing over possible positions, leads to less pessimistic and more effective pursuit behaviors.
- **Impact of Observation Range:** Success rates for `DP_belief` monotonically increased with an expanded observation range, reaching 100% when the range exceeded 5. This indicates that the belief-based approach effectively leverages increased observability.

#### 4.2. Evaluations of Generalized RL Pursuers (R2PS)

- **Training and Generalization:** The R2PS policy was pretrained on a synthetic dataset (150 graphs) and a large dataset of random urban locations (150 graphs), for a total of 300 graphs. It was then evaluated for zero-shot generalization on unseen real-world test graphs. Policy guidance (β=0.1) from extended DP pursuers during RL training enhanced efficiency compared to pure reinforcement learning (β=0).
- **Comparison with PSRO:** The R2PS policy was compared against a Policy-Space Response Oracles (PSRO) policy directly trained on the test graphs.
 - **Against `Stay` Evader:** Both R2PS and PSRO achieved high success rates (0.93-1.00).
 - **Against `DP_sync` (Synchronous DP Evader):** R2PS consistently achieved high success rates (0.90-1.00), while PSRO showed variability (0.00-1.00), failing completely on some graphs.
 - **Against `DP_async` (Asynchronous Optimal DP Evader):** R2PS demonstrated significant superiority (0.20-1.00) over PSRO (0.00-0.88), particularly on more challenging graphs. This highlights R2PS's robustness against a stronger, predictive evader.
 - **Against `BR_async` (Best-Responding Asynchronous Evader):** Even against a best-responding evader specifically trained against R2PS, the R2PS policy maintained success rates above 50% in half of the test graphs, indicating considerable worst-case robustness.

#### 4.3. Scalability Tests and Ablation Studies

- **Scalability:** R2PS demonstrated real-time pursuit capability on larger, more complex real-world graphs (up to 2065 nodes). While DP computation times ranged from 6 to 139 seconds per timestep for these larger graphs, R2PS inference time remained consistently low, around 0.01 seconds per timestep using GPU acceleration. The RL policy maintained desirable performance (0.33-0.76 success rates against `DP_async`) even on these larger graphs.
- **Belief Update Impact:** Ablation studies on belief updates confirmed their importance.
 - Utilizing known opponent information for belief updates improved success rates against the best-responding evader.
 - Decreasing the frequency of belief updates (e.g., from every step to every 2 or 3 steps) led to a decline in pursuit success rates. This substantiates the benefits of continuous belief updates.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a notable advancement in the domain of pursuit-evasion games, particularly by addressing the practical challenges of partial observability and real-time deployment in dynamic environments.

### 6. Significance

- **Novelty in Partial Observability and Robustness:** This work introduces the first approach to develop worst-case robust real-time pursuit strategies (R2PS) under partial observability. This fills a critical gap in existing literature, where state-of-the-art RL methods were previously limited to perfect information settings.
- **Unified DP Framework:** The theoretical analysis unifying optimal strategies for both synchronous and asynchronous evader moves using a single DP algorithm simplifies the foundation for robust strategy design.
- **Efficient Belief Preservation:** The proposed belief preservation mechanism offers an efficient method (`˜O(|V|)`) to abstract evader information from observation histories, enabling pursuers to operate effectively despite incomplete information without incurring the prohibitive costs of full history tracking in partially observable stochastic games.
- **Zero-Shot Cross-Graph Generalization:** By embedding belief preservation into the EPG framework, the research successfully achieves robust zero-shot generalization of pursuit policies to unseen real-world graph structures. This significantly enhances the adaptability and deployability of learned strategies across varied environments.
- **Real-Time Applicability:** The development of GNN-based RL policies with `O(n^2 m)` inference time complexity demonstrates a substantial practical improvement over traditional DP recomputation (`˜O(n^(m+1))`), making these strategies viable for real-time applications where environmental graphs can change dynamically.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Enhanced Real-World Security Applications:** The R2PS framework has direct implications for security and defense scenarios, such as guiding law enforcement in urban areas or deploying robotic guards to secure facilities. The ability to function with imperfect information and adapt to changing environments increases the practical utility of autonomous agents in these critical applications.
- **Advancement in Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning:** This work contributes to the broader field of multi-agent reinforcement learning by demonstrating a successful integration of game-theoretic analysis (DP), belief state management, and advanced neural network architectures (GNNs) for complex, adversarial problems.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The methodology provides a robust foundation for subsequent research on PEGs, especially concerning scenarios with higher degrees of uncertainty, more complex agent dynamics, or broader real-world security challenges. Future work could explore more sophisticated belief models, learning evader policies, or extensions to non-zero-sum games.
