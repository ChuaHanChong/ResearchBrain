# R2PS: Worst-Case Robust Real-Time Pursuit Strategies under Partial Observability

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from the University of Chinese Academy of Sciences developed R2PS, a method for generating worst-case robust, real-time pursuit strategies for graph-based pursuit-evasion games under partial observability. R2PS achieved robust zero-shot generalization to unseen real-world graphs and consistently outperformed direct training methods on challenging adversarial evaders, while maintaining real-time inference speed of approximately 0.01 seconds per timestep on large graphs.

## Problem

- Solving graph-based pursuit-evasion games (PEGs) is computationally expensive, especially when environmental structures change dynamically.
- The inclusion of partial observability in PEGs increases problem complexity to PSPACE-hardness, severely limiting real-time applicability of traditional methods.
- Existing reinforcement learning approaches for large-scale PEGs lack robust zero-shot generalization to diverse environments and struggle in scenarios with partial information or strong, predictive evaders.

## Method

- The work extended a ==Dynamic Programming== (DP) algorithm to prove optimal pursuit and evasion strategies for Markov PEGs, valid for both synchronous and asynchronous evader moves with predictive capabilities.
- An efficient `˜O(|V|)` belief preservation mechanism was introduced for pursuers to maintain a set of possible evader positions and a belief distribution, enabling informed decision-making under partial observability.
- The belief mechanism was integrated into the Equilibrium Policy Generalization (EPG) framework, training a Graph Neural Network (GNN)-based pursuer policy via cross-graph reinforcement learning against the asynchronous-move optimal evader.

## Results

- Extended DP pursuers (`DP_belief`) achieved success rates up to **0.94** against an optimal asynchronous evader with an observation range of 2, significantly outperforming a shortest-path strategy.
- The R2PS policy demonstrated robust zero-shot generalization, achieving success rates between **0.20**-1.00 against the optimal asynchronous evader, consistently outperforming PSRO (**0.00**-**0.88**) trained directly on test graphs.
- R2PS maintained real-time inference performance of approximately **0.01** seconds per timestep on real-world graphs with over 2000 nodes, while retaining success rates of **0.33**-**0.76** against strong evaders.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Belief-averaged policies (`DP_belief`) consistently provided more effective and less pessimistic pursuit behaviors under partial observability compared to policies maximizing over possible positions.
> - Policy guidance from the extended DP pursuers during reinforcement learning training significantly enhanced efficiency and stability in learning robust generalized strategies.
> - The GNN-based RL pursuer policy achieved real-time inference speeds of `O(n^2 m)` on large, dynamically changing graphs, a substantial improvement over the `˜O(n^(m+1))` complexity of traditional DP recomputation.
