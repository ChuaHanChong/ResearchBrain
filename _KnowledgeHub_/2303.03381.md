# Real-World Humanoid Locomotion with Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> A fully learning-based controller for the full-sized Digit humanoid robot was developed using a causal transformer architecture trained in randomized simulation. The robot demonstrated robust, zero-shot locomotion in diverse real-world outdoor environments and emergent adaptive behaviors to varying terrains and disturbances.

## Problem

- Classical control methods for humanoids struggle with generalization and adaptation to novel, unstructured real-world environments without extensive re-engineering.
- Humanoid robots are high-dimensional, unstable, and complex systems, making robust and adaptable control a formidable challenge for traditional approaches.
- The aspiration for general-purpose humanoids capable of autonomous operation in diverse settings is limited by controllers that lack inherent adaptability to unexpected conditions.

## Method

- A fully learning-based controller utilizing a causal transformer architecture processes historical proprioceptive observations and actions to generate control commands.
- A two-step policy learning procedure involves training a "teacher" state policy followed by a "student" observation policy, optimized via PPO with an initial imitation objective that anneals to zero.
- Large-scale reinforcement learning in a highly randomized ==NVIDIA Isaac Gym== simulation environment, combined with high-fidelity simulator validation, enables zero-shot transfer to the physical Digit robot.

## Results

- The Digit humanoid demonstrated robust, fall-free locomotion across diverse real-world outdoor environments, including various terrains and under external disturbances, over a week of testing.
- The learned controller outperformed Agility Robotics' classical controller in simulation, especially on challenging terrain, exhibiting emergent recovery and in-context adaptation - changing gait on slopes, recovering from foot-trapping - confirmed via neural activity analysis.
- Ablation studies confirmed the transformer's superior performance over MLPs, TCNs, and LSTMs, and validated the benefits of longer context windows and the joint RL + imitation training objective.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Causal transformer models effectively utilize observation-action history for in-context adaptation, allowing humanoid robots to adjust behavior without weight updates or explicit environment models.
> - Complex and adaptive motor skills, such as human-like arm swings, terrain-specific gait changes, and foot-trapping recovery, can emerge naturally from reinforcement learning.
> - Robust zero-shot sim-to-real transfer for full-sized humanoids is achievable through large-scale domain randomization and a joint teacher-student policy training approach.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The dream of creating general-purpose humanoid robots capable of performing diverse tasks in unstructured, real-world environments has long been a central aspiration in robotics. Historically, this goal was pursued through **classical control methods**, exemplified by early bipedal robots like WABOT-1 and later the impressive feats of Boston Dynamics' Atlas. These methods, often relying on detailed dynamic models, optimization-based strategies (e.g., whole-body control, model predictive control), and pre-programmed gaits, have achieved remarkable stability and agility in specific, often controlled, scenarios (e.g., backflips, parkour). However, as the authors note, a significant limitation of classical approaches is their **difficulty in generalizing and adapting to novel, previously unseen environments and disturbances** without extensive manual tuning or re-design.

In recent years, the robotics landscape has seen a paradigm shift towards **learning-based methods**, particularly leveraging deep reinforcement learning (RL). This trend has been highly successful in areas like dexterous manipulation (e.g., OpenAI's Rubik's Cube solver, Dex-Net) and quadrupedal locomotion (e.g., ETH Zurich's ANYmal, MIT Cheetah, Unitree robots). These learning-based approaches can learn complex, dynamic behaviors directly from data, often in simulation, and have demonstrated impressive robustness and adaptability.

For legged locomotion, specifically, prior learning-based work has shown success with quadrupedal robots (e.g., "Rapid Motor Adaptation" for dynamic terrains, learning to walk in minutes with parallel RL) and increasingly with bipedal robots. However, most bipedal RL work has focused on smaller, simpler platforms (like Cassie) or has adopted **hybrid approaches** that combine learning with classical controllers. Full-sized humanoids, with their higher degrees of freedom, more complex dynamics, and greater instability, present a significantly harder challenge for purely learning-based control.

This paper positions itself as a significant advancement by presenting a **"fully learning-based approach for real-world humanoid locomotion"** on a full-sized robot (Agility Robotics' Digit). A key distinguishing factor is the use of a **causal transformer** architecture, which is novel in the context of real-world humanoid control. While transformers have revolutionized fields like natural language processing (e.g., GPT-3) and computer vision, their application to continuous robot control, particularly for exploiting observation-action history for in-context adaptation, is a relatively new and promising direction. Previous learning-based legged locomotion often employed memory-based networks like LSTMs or explicit environment estimators via TCNs. By hypothesizing that a powerful transformer can implicitly use historical data for in-context adaptation, this work pushes the boundaries of how general-purpose, adaptable controllers can be designed without explicit state estimation or environment modeling. The reliance on **large-scale model-free reinforcement learning in randomized simulations for zero-shot real-world deployment** is a well-established and increasingly effective sim-to-real transfer strategy, but its successful application to full-sized humanoids without falls marks a substantial milestone.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of this research is to advance the state-of-the-art in humanoid robot locomotion towards the long-held dream of **general-purpose machines capable of autonomous operation in diverse, unstructured real-world environments**. The authors explicitly state the potential applications, including addressing labor shortages in factories, assisting the elderly, and even colonizing new planets.

The primary motivations for pursuing a fully learning-based approach, as opposed to traditional classical control, stem from the inherent limitations of the latter:
- **Generalization and Adaptation:** Classical controllers, while precise, are notoriously challenging to generalize and adapt to novel environments, terrains, or unexpected disturbances without extensive manual re-engineering or parameter tuning. This limits their deployability in the complex and unpredictable real world.
- **Complexity of Humanoid Control:** Humanoid robots are high-dimensional, unstable, and inherently complex systems, making the control problem a formidable challenge. Learning-based methods offer a pathway to discover complex, dynamic behaviors that might be difficult to hand-design.

Specifically, the paper's core hypothesis and objective revolves around the capabilities of transformer models:
- **Hypothesis:** The "observation-action history contains useful information about the world that a powerful transformer model can use to adapt its behavior in-context, without updating its weights." This is a crucial element, suggesting that the robot can *implicitly* infer environmental properties or disturbance characteristics from its own past interactions and adjust its gait accordingly, akin to the "in-context learning" observed in large language models.
- **Goal:** To develop and demonstrate a fully learning-based controller for a full-sized humanoid robot (Digit) that achieves:
 - Reliable locomotion in real-world outdoor environments.
 - Robustness to external disturbances and varying payloads.
 - Ability to traverse different types of challenging terrains.
 - Crucially, **in-context adaptation** to novel situations without requiring explicit retraining or environment models.
 - Comparison of the learning-based approach favorably against state-of-the-art classical controllers.

By addressing these points, the authors aim to lay foundational work for scalable and adaptable humanoid robotics.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research employs a sophisticated, fully learning-based approach centered on a **causal transformer** architecture trained with **large-scale model-free reinforcement learning** in simulation for **zero-shot real-world deployment**.

**Robot Platform:** The study utilizes **Digit**, a full-sized general-purpose humanoid robot developed by Agility Robotics. Digit stands at approximately 1.6 meters tall, weighs 45 kilograms, and possesses 30 degrees of freedom, with 16 actuated joints (8 in each leg, 4 in each arm). The authors specifically mention the challenges posed by Digit's underactuated knee-shin-tarsus and tarsus-toe joints, which form closed kinematic chains.

**Controller Architecture:** The core of the controller is a **causal transformer**.
- **Input:** A history trajectory of proprioceptive observations (e.g., joint positions, velocities, IMU data) and past actions over a context window of length `l` (set to 16 timesteps).
- **Output:** The next action, specifically PD setpoints for 16 actuated joints and predicted PD gains for 8 actuated leg joints. Toe motors are set to default positions with fixed PD gains.
- **Mechanism:** Each observation-action pair is treated as a "token" and embedded using Multi-Layer Perceptrons (MLPs). Sinusoidal positional encodings are added to capture temporal information. The self-attention mechanism, central to transformers, allows the model to weigh the importance of different historical tokens, effectively extracting structural information and highlighting relevant past observations/actions. The "causal" aspect means the model only attends to preceding tokens, ensuring real-time deployability.
- **Scale:** The transformer model has four blocks, an embedding dimension of 192, 4 multi-heads, and contains 1.4 million parameters.

**Policy Learning Procedure (Two-Step Approach):**
To address the challenges of direct RL in high-dimensional observation spaces and improve sample efficiency, the authors adopt a two-step training strategy:
1. **Teacher State Policy Training:** An initial "teacher" policy ($\pi_s$) is trained assuming full observability of the environment (i.e., operating in the state space). This step is faster and more resource-efficient, allowing for rapid iteration and tuning of the reward functions. This policy is an MLP with hidden sizes of.
2. **Student Observation Policy Learning:** The main "student" policy ($\pi_o$, the transformer) is then trained using a **joint optimization objective**. This objective combines:
 - **Reinforcement Learning (RL) Loss ($L_{RL}$):** Optimized using the Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) algorithm, maximizing cumulative reward.
 - **Kullback-Leibler (KL) Divergence ($D_{KL}$):** A term that encourages the student policy to imitate the teacher policy ($\lambda D_{KL}(\pi_o \parallel \pi_s)$). The weighting factor $\lambda$ is gradually annealed to zero over training, allowing the student to initially leverage the teacher's knowledge but eventually surpass it by optimizing directly for rewards in the observation space.
This approach does not require pre-computed trajectories or offline datasets, as both supervisions are optimized through on-policy learning.

**Simulation Environment and Domain Randomization:**
- **Simulator:** **NVIDIA Isaac Gym** is used for massively parallel training, leveraging GPUs for high-performance physics simulation.
- **Closed Kinematic Chains:** A notable challenge was accurately simulating Digit's underactuated closed kinematic chains (knee-shin-tarsus, tarsus-toe) in Isaac Gym. The authors addressed this by introducing a "virtual spring" model with high stiffness and an alternating simulation sub-step method to maintain nominal link lengths.
- **Domain Randomization:** Critical for sim-to-real transfer. Various elements were randomized:
 - **Dynamics properties:** Robot parameters (mass, inertia).
 - **Control parameters:** Joint limits, actuator properties.
 - **Environment physics:** Friction, restitution.
 - **Noise and Delay:** Added to observations and actions to mimic real-world sensor imperfections and communication latencies.
 - **Terrain types:** Smooth planes, rough planes, smooth slopes.
 - **Walking commands:** Randomly resampled omnidirectional velocity commands (linear x/y, angular z).

**Sim-to-Real Transfer Pipeline:**
The pipeline consists of three stages:
1. **Isaac Gym Training:** Large-scale training of the policy.
2. **Agility Simulator Validation:** Policies trained in Isaac Gym are first validated in Agility Robotics' high-fidelity simulator. This simulator accurately models Digit's specific dynamics, including closed kinematic chains and sensor noises, serving as a crucial intermediate step to filter out unsafe policies before hardware deployment. No neural network parameters are changed at this stage.
3. **Real Robot Deployment:** The validated policy is deployed zero-shot to the physical Digit robot. The neural network policy runs at 50 Hz, controlling joint PD controllers operating at 1 kHz, using data from joint encoders and IMU.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents compelling results demonstrating the effectiveness of their learning-based approach for real-world humanoid locomotion, particularly highlighting its robustness, generalization, and emergent adaptive behaviors.

#### 4.1. Real-World Zero-Shot Deployment and Robustness

- **Outdoor Locomotion:** The controller successfully enabled Digit to walk reliably across a wide range of outdoor environments, including plazas, walkways, sidewalks, running tracks, and grass fields. These terrains varied in material properties (concrete, rubber, grass) and conditions (dry, wet), none of which were explicitly encountered during simulation training. Remarkably, over a week of full-day testing, **no falls were observed**, even without a safety gantry.
- **External Disturbances:** The robot demonstrated significant robustness to sudden external forces, including being hit by a large yoga ball, pushed with a wooden stick, and pulled from the back while walking. The controller stabilized the robot in fractions of a second, preventing falls.
- **Rough Terrain Traversal:** Digit successfully traversed various rough terrains in the laboratory, such as surfaces covered with rubbers, cloths, cables, and bubble wraps, despite relying solely on proprioceptive sensing (no exteroceptive sensors like cameras) and the potential for entanglement or slipping. It also successfully navigated slopes up to 8.7% grade.
- **Payload Carrying:** The robot maintained stable locomotion while carrying diverse payloads, including an empty backpack, a loaded backpack, a cloth handbag, a loaded trash bag, and a paper bag. Impressively, it adapted to a loaded trash bag attached to its arm, even though its policy leverages arm swings for balancing, indicating in-context behavioral modification.

#### 4.2. Comparison to State-of-the-Art

- In simulation, the authors compared their controller against Agility Robotics' proprietary state-of-the-art controller for Digit.
- **Performance:** Their learned controller showed comparable performance on slopes but significantly **outperformed the company controller on challenging terrains like steps and unstable ground**.
- **Emergent Recovery:** The company controller struggled with foot-trapping on steps, often shutting down, a behavior replicated on hardware. In contrast, the learned controller demonstrated an **emergent ability to recover** from foot-trapping events, despite not being explicitly trained on steps. This highlights the adaptability of the learning-based approach.

#### 4.3. Natural Walking Behaviors

- **Omnidirectional Locomotion:** The controller accurately followed various velocity commands (linear x/y, angular z), enabling omnidirectional walking (forward, backward, turning). It also generalized to continuously changing commands supplied via joystick in real time, a condition different from training.
- **Dynamic Arm Swing:** A striking emergent behavior was the **human-like arm swing motion**, coordinating contralaterally with leg movements (e.g., right arm swings forward when the left leg lifts). This behavior was not explicitly enforced by the reward function or reference trajectories but emerged naturally, potentially suggesting energy efficiency benefits, similar to human biomechanics.
- **Fast Walking:** The robot achieved and accurately tracked high walking speeds (e.g., 1 m/s) from rest, demonstrating dynamic and agile locomotion.

#### 4.4. In-Context Adaptation

This is a key validation of the authors' hypothesis regarding the transformer's ability to adapt in-context.
- **Emergent Gait Changes based on Terrain:** When commanded to walk over a course with flat ground, a downward slope, and then flat ground again, the controller **autonomously changed its gait**. On the downward slope, it transitioned from normal walking to taking smaller steps without lifting its legs much, then reverted to normal walking on flat ground. This adaptation was emergent and not pre-specified.
 - **Neural Activity Analysis:** Analysis of the transformer's hidden states revealed that specific neurons correlated with gait patterns and terrain types, and dimensionality reduction (PCA, t-SNE) showed clear clustering of hidden states based on terrain, indicating that the model implicitly represents and adapts to environmental context.
- **Emergent Recovery from Foot-Trapping:** When hitting a discrete step obstacle (not seen during training), the robot detected the foot-trapping event and **adapted its subsequent actions by lifting its legs higher and faster** to clear the obstacle. This recovery behavior was consistent and emergent.
 - **Neural Activity Analysis:** Similar to gait changes, neural activity patterns showed clear changes, including distinct spikes, during foot-trapping events, suggesting the transformer implicitly detects and reacts to these critical situations.

#### 4.5. Ablation Studies

- **Neural Network Architecture Comparison:** The transformer model significantly **outperformed MLP, TCN, and LSTM** architectures in terms of mean success rate across challenging scenarios (slopes, steps, unstable ground), validating the choice of transformer for this task.
- **Context Length:** The performance of the transformer-based controller **improved with larger context lengths** (e.g., up to 16 timesteps), supporting the hypothesis that historical information is crucial for adaptation.
- **Training Objective:** The **joint optimization objective (imitation learning + reinforcement learning)** consistently outperformed training with either imitation learning or pure reinforcement learning alone, demonstrating the synergistic benefits of the two-step approach.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research marks a significant stride towards realizing the dream of general-purpose humanoid robots operating autonomously in complex, unstructured environments. Its significance and potential impact are multi-faceted:

- **Pioneering Full-Sized Humanoid Control:** It is among the first to demonstrate a **fully learning-based controller for real-world, full-sized humanoid locomotion** (on the Digit robot) that achieves zero-shot sim-to-real transfer without falls over extended periods in diverse outdoor settings. This moves beyond smaller bipedal platforms or hybrid control approaches, addressing a more challenging problem.
- **Validation of Transformer Architectures in Robotics:** The work provides compelling evidence that **causal transformer models are highly effective for high-dimensional, dynamic robot control**, particularly for exploiting historical observation-action data for in-context learning. This is a novel and impactful application of transformers outside their traditional domains (NLP, vision), suggesting a powerful new direction for robot policy design.
- **Demonstration of Robustness and Generalization:** The ability of the robot to handle significant external disturbances, traverse various rough terrains (unseen in training), and carry diverse payloads without prior knowledge or retraining is a critical step towards practical real-world deployment. This level of robustness is crucial for safety and reliability.
- **Emergent Adaptive Behaviors:** The observation of emergent, unprogrammed behaviors like natural arm swing, gait changes based on terrain, and intelligent recovery from foot-trapping is highly significant. It suggests that complex, adaptive motor skills can arise from general learning objectives, rather than requiring meticulous hand-design or explicit environmental models. This paves the way for robots that can learn to "figure things out" on their own.
- **Advancement in Sim-to-Real Transfer:** The successful zero-shot transfer, despite the inherent complexities of simulating underactuated systems like Digit's closed kinematic chains, highlights the efficacy of their comprehensive domain randomization strategy and two-step policy learning. This contributes to the growing body of knowledge on bridging the reality gap in robotics.
- **Scalability Potential:** Given the established scaling properties of transformer models in other domains (e.g., language models improving with more data and compute), the authors suggest that transformers could facilitate future progress in scaling learning-based approaches for even more complex humanoid behaviors and environments. This implies a roadmap towards truly generalist humanoid agents.
- **Inspiration for Future Research:** The work encourages further exploration into general learning-based controllers for humanoids. Potential extensions include integrating additional sensory modalities (e.g., vision) into the transformer input, enhancing fault tolerance, and improving simulation fidelity for underactuated systems.
- **Societal Impact:** By pushing the boundaries of humanoid locomotion, this research directly contributes to the vision of robots that can augment human capabilities, addressing critical societal needs such as labor shortages in various industries, providing assistance for the elderly and those with disabilities, and enabling exploration in hazardous or remote environments like space.

In summary, this paper presents a landmark achievement in humanoid robotics, showcasing how modern deep learning architectures, combined with robust simulation and transfer techniques, can unlock unprecedented levels of robustness, adaptability, and emergent intelligence in complex robotic systems. It sets a new benchmark for learning-based control of full-sized humanoids and lays strong groundwork for the future of general-purpose embodied AI.
