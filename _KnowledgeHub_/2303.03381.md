# Real-World Humanoid Locomotion with Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> A fully learning-based controller for the full-sized Digit humanoid robot was developed using a causal transformer architecture trained in randomized simulation. The robot demonstrated robust, zero-shot locomotion in diverse real-world outdoor environments and emergent adaptive behaviors to varying terrains and disturbances.

## Problem

- Classical control methods for humanoids struggle with generalization and adaptation to novel, unstructured real-world environments without extensive re-engineering.
- Humanoid robots are high-dimensional, unstable, and complex systems, making robust and adaptable control a formidable challenge for traditional approaches.
- The aspiration for general-purpose humanoids capable of autonomous operation in diverse settings is limited by controllers that lack inherent adaptability to unexpected conditions.

## Method

- A fully learning-based controller utilizing a causal transformer architecture processes historical proprioceptive observations and actions to generate control commands.
- A two-step policy learning procedure involves training a "teacher" state policy followed by a "student" observation policy, optimized via PPO with an initial imitation objective that anneals to zero.
- Large-scale reinforcement learning in a highly randomized ==NVIDIA Isaac Gym== simulation environment, combined with high-fidelity simulator validation, enables zero-shot transfer to the physical Digit robot.

## Results

- The Digit humanoid demonstrated robust, fall-free locomotion across diverse real-world outdoor environments, including various terrains and under external disturbances, over a week of testing.
- The learned controller outperformed Agility Robotics' state-of-the-art classical controller in simulation, especially on challenging terrains like steps and unstable ground, exhibiting emergent recovery behaviors.
- The robot exhibited in-context adaptation, autonomously changing its gait on slopes and recovering from foot-trapping events, with neural activity analysis confirming implicit environmental detection.
- Ablation studies confirmed the transformer's superior performance over MLPs, TCNs, and LSTMs, and validated the benefits of longer context windows and the joint RL + imitation training objective.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Causal transformer models effectively utilize observation-action history for in-context adaptation, allowing humanoid robots to adjust behavior without weight updates or explicit environment models.
> - Complex and adaptive motor skills, such as human-like arm swings, terrain-specific gait changes, and foot-trapping recovery, can emerge naturally from reinforcement learning.
> - Robust zero-shot sim-to-real transfer for full-sized humanoids is achievable through large-scale domain randomization and a joint teacher-student policy training approach.
