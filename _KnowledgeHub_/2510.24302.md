# Lookahead Tree-Based Rollouts for Enhanced Trajectory-Level Exploration in Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Nanjing University, the University of Southern California, and Fudan University introduced Lookahead Tree-Based Rollouts (LATR), a novel strategy that explicitly promotes trajectory-level diversity in Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR). LATR improved Pass@1 accuracy by an average of 3.8-4.5% on reasoning benchmarks, accelerated policy learning by 2-3x, and reduced average solution lengths by 8-22%.

## Problem

- Existing Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) pipelines suffer from insufficient diversity in sampled trajectories during the rollout phase, particularly when using token-level stochastic sampling.
- Homogeneous reasoning paths within rollout groups diminish the informativeness of return signals, leading to less discriminative relative advantages and hindering effective policy learning for large language models (LLMs).
- Current solutions like increasing sampling temperature or post-hoc group filtering offer limited improvements as they do not explicitly ensure trajectory-level divergence or incur high computational costs.

## Method

- Lookahead Tree-Based Rollouts (**LATR**) maintains rollouts in a ==tree structure==, iteratively performing Branching, Lookahead Simulation, and Pruning to enforce trajectory-level diversity.
- Branching uses absolute and relative probability thresholds to create child branches from plausible alternative tokens, focusing on 'reasoning crossroads' where the policy shows uncertainty.
- Lookahead Simulation extends new branches for a fixed 'r' tokens to assess potential divergence, while similarity-aware Pruning removes branches lacking meaningful divergence (based on normalized edit distance) within the lookahead window.

## Results

- LATR consistently improved Pass@1 accuracy by an average of **4.5%** on the Countdown dataset and **3.8%** on mathematical reasoning datasets compared to stochastic sampling.
- Policy learning was significantly accelerated, achieving peak performance 2-3 times faster (e.g., **3x** speedup on Countdown, **2x** on math tasks) compared to standard methods.
- The average length of generated reasoning trajectories at test time was reduced by approximately **22%** on Countdown and **8.3%** on mathematical datasets, indicating more concise solutions.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly promoting trajectory-level diversity during rollouts significantly enriches learning signals, leading to faster policy convergence and improved final task performance in RLVR.
> - Controlled branching guided by policy probabilities and dynamic, similarity-aware pruning are crucial for efficiently managing exploration and mitigating redundant reasoning paths.
> - Trajectory-level lookahead for divergence assessment is more effective than token-level checks, ensuring genuine distinction between reasoning sequences.
