# EAGOR: Embodied Reasoning in Omni-direction

> [!summary] Summary
> A framework called EAGOR enhances 360° directional reasoning for embodied AI by decoupling Vision-Language Model outputs from spherical geometric state estimation using a Spherical Harmonic Belief Field. This approach yields robust, motion-consistent, and accurate target direction estimates, outperforming conventional 2D projection methods and larger standalone models in tasks like active visual search and map-free navigation.

## Problem

- Current Vision-Language Models (VLMs) process 360° panoramic data by projecting it onto 2D planes, introducing geometric distortions (seam discontinuities, latitude-dependent scaling) that lead to inconsistent spatial relationships.
- These distortions result in unreliable target direction updates, particularly during agent motion and camera view transformations, which is critical for continuous embodied tasks.
- Existing reasoning frameworks often treat VLM spatial outputs as deterministic 2D coordinate predictions, neglecting inherent uncertainty and accumulating errors in sequential observations.

## Method

- **EAGOR** decouples semantic perception (using a frozen VLM) from geometric state estimation, modeling the dynamic agent-to-target direction as a continuous belief on the spherical manifold.
- It employs a novel ==Spherical Harmonic Belief Field (SH-BF)== for continuous, globally defined, and rotation-equivariant representation of target direction belief directly on the sphere.
- VLM-generated response maps are lifted to the spherical domain as log-likelihood fields, projected into SH coefficients, and then recursively combined with motion-propagated prior beliefs via ==Bayesian updates==.

## Results

- EAGOR achieved average relative gains of **+34.4%** on the HOS dataset and **+45.6%** on the OSR-Bench dataset for active visual search, with EAGOR + Qwen2.5-VL-7B outperforming the larger standalone Gemma-3-12B.
- In map-free navigation, EAGOR demonstrated a **94%** success rate and a **24.5%** reduction in mean angular error compared to baselines, while significantly reducing temporal inconsistency to **2.6°** per step.
- Real-world experiments with a Unitree-Go2 robot confirmed EAGOR's ability to maintain a coherent target direction belief despite sensor noise, ego-motion, and temporary target disappearance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Reframing dynamic target localization as recursive Bayesian estimation directly on the sphere effectively avoids geometric distortions introduced by 2D planar projections of 360° data.
> - Spherical Harmonics provide a mathematically robust and continuous basis for representing and propagating spatial beliefs, ensuring geometric consistency and equivariance under agent motion.
> - The framework's training-free and model-agnostic nature allows it to leverage existing frozen VLMs for semantic evidence while independently handling precise geometric reasoning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of embodied AI is increasingly focused on enabling intelligent agents, such as robots, to perceive, understand, and interact with their physical surroundings. A fundamental aspect of this is directional reasoning – the ability to accurately estimate, track, and update the spatial direction of targets relative to the agent's own egocentric frame. Omni-directional (360°) cameras are a key technology in this domain, providing a holistic view of the environment, which is advantageous for tasks like navigation and object search.

However, a prevalent challenge in processing 360° visual data for embodied AI tasks lies in its representation. Current Vision-Language Models (VLMs) commonly process 360° data by projecting it onto 2D planar images, typically using equirectangular projection (ERP). This approach, while convenient for adapting architectures designed for perspective images, introduces several geometric distortions, including seam discontinuities, latitude-dependent scaling, and interpolation errors. These distortions lead to inconsistencies in spatial relationships and direction estimates when the camera view transforms due to agent motion. For an embodied agent, this translates to unreliable target direction updates, particularly critical in dynamic tasks like map-free navigation or active visual search where continuous, motion-consistent directional awareness is required.

Existing approaches in omnidirectional perception for navigation often attempt to mitigate ERP distortions by decomposing panoramas into perspective crops, incorporating panorama-aware attention, or using spherical positional encodings. However, benchmarks indicate that Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) still face difficulties with reference-frame transformations, suggesting that semantic recognition alone does not guarantee geometric correctness in direction estimation. Furthermore, prior reasoning frameworks frequently treat VLM spatial outputs as deterministic coordinate predictions, neglecting inherent uncertainty and potentially relying on single noisy observations. In sequential embodied settings, where target direction must be continuously updated under egocentric rotation, this can lead to accumulated errors and temporal inconsistencies.

This research addresses a representation gap in embodied omni-directional reasoning. It posits that while VLMs provide robust semantic observations in image space, the underlying geometry necessitates a representation that respects the observer-centered spherical nature of 360° data. Instead of treating directional reasoning as a per-frame recognition problem, this work frames it as a geometric state estimation problem on the sphere, continuously maintained under motion. This approach differentiates it from prior probabilistic methods that operate in 2D planar image space, positioning EAGOR as a method that leverages spherical harmonic representations to perform recursive Bayesian estimation directly on the sphere, integrating VLM semantic evidence into a geometrically consistent belief.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the limitations of current approaches in directional reasoning for embodied AI using omni-directional cameras. The core problem identified is that existing Vision-Language Models (VLMs), when applied to 360° panoramic data, typically project this spherical information onto a 2D equirectangular plane. This projection, while facilitating compatibility with standard VLM architectures, introduces significant geometric distortions such as seam discontinuities, latitude-dependent scaling, and interpolation errors. These artifacts lead to direction estimates that are inconsistent and unreliable under camera view transformations or agent motion, which is crucial for embodied tasks.

The paper highlights that for an embodied agent, the essential output is not merely a 2D pixel location in a panorama but a continuous, egocentric direction to a target that remains consistent as the agent moves and rotates. Current methods often suffer from an accumulation of interpolation errors over time due to camera rotation, causing the predicted target direction to drift. This reveals a "representation gap" where powerful VLM semantic observations are not adequately integrated with geometrically correct, motion-consistent spatial reasoning.

Given these challenges, the key objectives of this research are:

1. **To develop a geometry-aware framework for embodied 360° directional reasoning:** The aim is to move beyond pixel-coordinate prediction in ERP images and instead model the dynamic agent-to-target directional relationship as a continuous belief on the spherical manifold.
2. **To ensure geometric consistency and equivariance under agent motion:** The framework should be capable of propagating the target direction belief robustly and accurately as the agent moves and rotates, mitigating the inconsistencies caused by ERP distortions.
3. **To achieve this without requiring task-specific training of backbone VLMs:** The intention is to create a "training-free" and "model-agnostic" framework that can leverage existing, frozen VLMs for semantic perception while handling the geometric reasoning independently. This implies decoupling semantic evidence extraction from geometric state estimation.
4. **To introduce a novel belief representation for spherical data:** A continuous, globally defined, and rotation-aware basis is needed for target direction estimation directly on the sphere, avoiding artifacts inherent in ERP. This objective leads to the development of the Spherical Harmonic Belief Field (SH-BF).
5. **To validate the framework across multiple embodied tasks and real-world scenarios:** The research seeks to demonstrate that the geometrically consistent directional reasoning provided by the framework translates into improved performance in practical applications such as map-free navigation, active visual search, and dynamic tracking on a physical robot.

In summary, the motivation is to bridge the gap between powerful VLM semantic understanding and the geometric fidelity required for robust embodied spatial reasoning in 360° environments, specifically by reframing the problem as recursive Bayesian estimation on a sphere.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework, EAGOR (Embodied reAsoninG in Omni-diRection), is designed as a training-free and geometry-aware solution for embodied 360° directional reasoning. Its core methodological principle is the decoupling of semantic perception, performed by a frozen VLM, from geometric state estimation, managed by a novel Spherical Harmonic Belief Field (SH-BF).

**Overall Framework Overview:**
At each timestep *t*, an agent receives a full 360° panoramic image *I_t* and a target query. The objective is to estimate the egocentric agent-to-target direction *ω*^*t* ∈ S², represented as a unit vector. EAGOR processes this information through the following stages:

1. **Panoramic Observations to Directional Likelihoods:**
 - **VLM Response Map:** A pre-trained, frozen VLM (e.g., Qwen2.5-VL or Gemma-3) takes the panoramic image *I_t* and the target query as input. It generates a target-conditioned response map *ℓ_t*(u, v) over the ERP image. This map indicates the likelihood of the target appearing at each pixel location (u, v). The response map is normalized to a range.
 - **Lifting to Spherical Domain:** The 2D pixel coordinates (u, v) are transformed into 3D unit direction vectors *ω*(u, v) on the unit sphere S². This conversion uses standard equirectangular projection inverse formulas: azimuth *θ* = 2πu/W − π and elevation *ϕ* = π/2 − πv/H, where W and H are image width and height, respectively. This converts *ℓ_t*(u, v) into a directional likelihood field *ℓ_t*(ω) : S² →.
 - **Log-Likelihood Transformation:** To enable additive evidence accumulation across timesteps, *ℓ_t*(ω) is converted into a log-likelihood field *r_t*(ω) = log(*ℓ_t*(ω) + ϵ), where *ϵ* is a small constant to prevent numerical instability. *r_t*(ω) serves as the current directional evidence from the panorama.

2. **Spherical Harmonic Belief Field (SH-BF):**
 - **Belief Representation:** EAGOR maintains a continuous belief *f_t*(ω) over possible target directions on the sphere. This belief field is represented using real Spherical Harmonics (SH) basis functions *Y*^m*ℓ*(ω). The belief is encoded by a set of SH coefficients *c*^(t)*ℓm*, where *L* is the SH bandlimit (set to 7 in this work):
 *f_t*(ω) = Σ*L*ℓ=0 Σ*ℓ*m=−ℓ *c*^(t)*ℓm* *Y*^m*ℓ*(ω)*
 - **Current Observation in SH Space:** The log-likelihood field *r_t*(ω) from the current panorama is projected onto the SH basis to obtain observation coefficients *b*^(t)*ℓm*. This projection involves an integral over the sphere and includes a *cos ϕ* term to correct for the latitude-dependent area distortion introduced by ERP:
 *b*^(t)*ℓm* = ∫S² *r_t*(ω)*Y*^m*ℓ*(ω)dΩ
 - **Propagated Prior:** As the agent moves from timestep *t* − 1 to *t*, its egocentric frame rotates. The previous posterior belief coefficients *c*^(t−1)*ℓm* are rotated into the current viewer-centric frame to form the propagated prior coefficients *˜c*^(t)*ℓm*. This rotation is achieved using Wigner-D matrices *D*^ℓ*mm'*(*R_t*) corresponding to the agent's rotation *R_t* (e.g., yaw change *∆ψ_t* for planar navigation):
 *˜c*^(t)*ℓm* = Σ*ℓ*m' =−ℓ *D*^ℓ*mm'*(*R_t*)*c*^(t−1)*ℓm'*
 - **Belief Update:**
 - For the first timestep (*t* = 1), the posterior belief is initialized directly from the first observation: *c*^(1) = *b*^(1)*.
 - For subsequent timesteps (*t* > 1), the current observation coefficients *b*^(t)*ℓm* are additively combined with the propagated prior coefficients *˜c*^(t)*ℓm* to update the posterior belief coefficients *c*^(t)*ℓm*. This constitutes a log-space Bayesian update:
 *c*^(t)*ℓm* = *˜c*^(t)*ℓm* + *b*^(t)*ℓm*
 - **Direction Decoding:** After updating the belief, a single egocentric target direction *ω*^*t* is extracted. This is done by computing the spherical mean (Fréchet mean on S²) of the belief field *f_t*(ω). Critically, for a unimodal belief, this degenerates to the Maximum A Posteriori (MAP) estimate and can be computed analytically from the degree-1 SH coefficients:
 *ˆω*^*t* = *m_t* / ||*m_t*||, where *m_t* = ∫S² *f_t*(ω) *ω* dΩ*
 The magnitude of *m_t* relative to the integral of *f_t* provides a confidence measure.

**Key Features:**
- **Training-Free:** The framework does not require fine-tuning the VLM backbone for specific embodied tasks. It operates by interpreting VLM outputs geometrically.
- **Geometry-Aware:** By representing belief directly on the sphere using SH-BF, EAGOR avoids the artifacts of ERP, ensures consistent propagation under rotation, and maintains a continuous belief.
- **Recursive Bayesian Estimation:** Evidence from successive panoramic observations is accumulated probabilistically over time, allowing the system to refine its target direction estimate.
- **Efficiency:** The operations in SH space (projection, rotation, addition) are computationally efficient, allowing real-time application. The chosen bandlimit *L*=7 provides a balance between angular precision and computational cost.

This methodology constructs a robust, persistent, and geometrically accurate directional belief, moving beyond frame-wise, distorted pixel predictions to support continuous, informed decision-making for embodied agents.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of EAGOR was structured around three experimental questions, demonstrating its performance in simulated environments and real-world deployment across various embodied omni-directional reasoning tasks.

**EQ-1: Active Visual Search (HOS and OSR-Bench)**
EAGOR consistently improved target-direction estimation in active visual search, a task where an agent actively searches for a queried object in 360° environments.
- **Humanoid Object Search (HOS) Dataset:**
 - Across various frozen VLM backbones (3B to 12B parameters), EAGOR achieved significant gains. For instance, with Gemma-3 4B, EAGOR improved accuracy from 20.60% to 24.80% (+4.2% absolute gain). With Gemma-3 12B, accuracy increased from 25.67% to 36.55% (+10.88% absolute gain).
 - Using Qwen2.5-VL 3B, EAGOR boosted accuracy from 20.71% to 26.40% (+5.69% absolute gain). With Qwen2.5-VL 7B, accuracy improved from 27.24% to 40.10% (+12.86% absolute gain).
 - Overall, EAGOR achieved average relative gains of +34.4% on HOS, demonstrating 1.7×–2.2× gains over corresponding standalone VLM models.
 - Notably, Qwen2.5-VL-7B with EAGOR (40.10%) surpassed the performance of the larger standalone Gemma-3-12B (25.67%), indicating that explicit spherical belief reasoning can provide advantages over merely increasing VLM capacity.
- **OSR-Bench Dataset (Multi-object Egocentric Spatial Reasoning):**
 - EAGOR also yielded substantial improvements on OSR-Bench. EAGOR+Qwen2.5-VL-7B achieved an accuracy of 25.2%, significantly outperforming its canonical backbone (16.8%) and even the much larger Qwen2.5-VL-72B (18.1%).
 - The framework was efficient, converging in 1.4× fewer views than the baseline, with accuracy improving monotonically from 8.5% to 40.1% across turns.

**EQ-2: Geometrically Equivariant Directional Reasoning in Simulated 360° Environments (Habitat-Sim)**
EAGOR's ability to maintain geometrically consistent directional belief under egocentric motion and seam crossings was evaluated through Waypoint Following and Map-Free Navigation tasks.
- **Waypoint Following:**
 - The mean angular error (MAE) of EAGOR was consistently lower across four challenging trajectory segments (L1–L4) compared to ERP-space baselines (Centroid, Centroid-Circ, Grid).
 - Baselines exhibited significant error spikes after turns and near seam boundaries. For instance, the Centroid baseline showed a 40.3° degradation across segments, while EAGOR's degradation was 25.4°.
 - EAGOR demonstrated improved robustness under occlusion and seam crossings, achieving MAE of 15.5° and 22.9° respectively, significantly lower than baselines (e.g., Centroid: 31.1° and 59.1°).
 - Temporal inconsistency (TC) was substantially reduced by EAGOR, from 7.3°–19.2°/step in baselines to 2.6°/step.
- **Map-Free Navigation:**
 - In closed-loop navigation from challenging initial headings, EAGOR consistently achieved superior performance across all metrics.
 - It demonstrated the highest success rate (SR) of 94% (compared to 82% for Centroid and Grid baselines), and the highest path efficiency (SPL) of 56.8.
 - EAGOR completed navigation in the fewest steps (50.2) and exhibited the lowest mean angular error (33.8°), significantly reducing steps by 17.7% and MAE by 24.5% compared to the best baselines.
 - Crucially, EAGOR showed substantially stronger seam robustness (70.6) compared to baselines (e.g., Cent-Circ: 33.5).

**EQ-3: Consistent Omni-Directional Reasoning in Real-World Settings**
Real-world experiments with a Unitree-Go2 robot equipped with an Insta360-X3 camera confirmed EAGOR's generalization capabilities.
- **Target Direction Estimation under Stationary and Mobile Operation:**
 - Qualitative evaluations demonstrated that EAGOR maintained a coherent target direction, preserving a consistent belief despite real sensor noise, viewpoint changes, ego-motion, and temporary target disappearance. This was observed for both scenarios: a stationary robot tracking a moving target and a moving robot tracking a stationary target.

**Ablation Study (SH Bandlimit) and Runtime Analysis:**
- An ablation on the Spherical Harmonic (SH) bandlimit *L* indicated that *L*=7 provides an approximate 25° angular resolution, balancing precision with avoiding excessive Gibbs ringing (oscillations) at wider angular separations.
- EAGOR incurred a marginal increase in inference time, requiring 0.6× more time than the VLM baseline (∼1.6s vs. ∼1.0s with Qwen2.5-VL-7B), with negligible additional GPU memory.

**Failure Mode Analysis:**
- A failure analysis on the HOS dataset revealed that EAGOR's main limitations arise from semantic ambiguity and perception errors of the underlying VLM, such as OCR/fine-grained text queries, rare targets, multi-instance confusion, and VLM false detections. However, the cross-view belief accumulation mechanism partially mitigated ambiguous observations, as evidenced by better performance on multi-instance cases compared to single-frame detection.

In summary, the findings consistently demonstrate that EAGOR's geometrically aware, training-free framework significantly enhances directional reasoning in 360° embodied AI tasks by providing stable, motion-consistent, and accurate target direction estimates, outperforming conventional ERP-based methods and even larger standalone VLMs.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces EAGOR, a framework that addresses a fundamental challenge in embodied AI: robust and geometrically consistent directional reasoning using omni-directional cameras. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Bridging the Representation Gap:** The paper effectively identifies and addresses the "representation gap" inherent in processing 360° spherical data with 2D-centric Vision-Language Models (VLMs). By reframing dynamic target localization as recursive Bayesian estimation directly on the sphere, EAGOR provides a geometrically correct interpretation of VLM outputs, moving beyond the limitations of equirectangular projection (ERP) distortions. This fundamental shift improves the fidelity of spatial understanding for embodied agents.

2. **Enhanced Robustness and Consistency:** EAGOR demonstrates a notable improvement in the robustness and consistency of directional estimates. Its Spherical Harmonic Belief Field (SH-BF) allows for continuous belief representation and equivariant propagation under agent motion and camera rotation. This directly translates to reduced temporal inconsistency, improved performance during seam crossings (a common failure point for ERP-based methods), and stable tracking even with sensor noise and temporary target occlusions. Such consistency is critical for reliable long-term autonomy in dynamic environments.

3. **Improved Task Performance in Embodied AI:** The quantitative results show substantial performance gains across a range of embodied tasks:
 - **Active Visual Search:** Significant accuracy improvements (+34.4% on HOS, +45.6% on OSR-Bench) indicate a superior ability for agents to locate and track objects in complex 360° scenes.
 - **Map-Free Navigation:** Enhanced navigation success rates (+14.6%), reduced step counts (by 17.7%), and lower angular errors (by 24.5%) demonstrate that geometrically consistent directional reasoning directly leads to more efficient and reliable autonomous navigation without reliance on pre-built maps.
 - **Waypoint Following:** Superior performance in maintaining target direction under viewpoint changes and challenging turns validates the framework's geometric correctness.

4. **Training-Free and Model-Agnostic Approach:** A key strength of EAGOR is its training-free nature. It leverages existing, frozen VLM backbones for semantic perception and integrates their outputs into a geometric reasoning pipeline. This makes it highly adaptable and generalizable, allowing smaller VLMs combined with EAGOR to outperform significantly larger standalone VLMs (e.g., Qwen2.5-VL-7B + EAGOR vs. standalone Gemma-3-12B). This potentially reduces computational resources and data requirements for deploying high-performance embodied perception systems.

5. **Foundation for Future Embodied Perception:** The SH-BF provides a novel and effective method for representing and updating spherical beliefs, which can serve as a foundation for future research in embodied perception. Its ability to accumulate probabilistic evidence over time naturally handles uncertainty and allows for refinement of estimates, aligning with human-like continuous spatial awareness.

6. **Real-World Applicability:** Validation on a physical legged robot (Unitree-Go2) demonstrates the practical applicability of EAGOR in real-world environments, enduring sensor noise and dynamic conditions. This suggests its potential for deployment in various robotic applications requiring advanced spatial intelligence.

The potential impact of EAGOR lies in advancing the capabilities of embodied agents to perceive and interact with complex 360° environments more intelligently and robustly. It contributes a critical component for building more reliable navigation systems, enabling precise object interaction, and fostering a deeper understanding of agent-environment relationships in the context of embodied AI. Future work, addressing current limitations such as semantic ambiguity and multi-instance confusion, could further enhance its capabilities and broaden its influence across diverse applications.
