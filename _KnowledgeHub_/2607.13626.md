# Unifying Decision-Making and Trajectory-Planning in Unsignalized Intersections Using Time-Varying Potential Fields

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Politecnico di Torino introduced a unified framework for autonomous vehicle decision-making and trajectory planning at unsignalized intersections, utilizing a Time-Varying Artificial Potential Field (TV-APF) within a Finite Horizon Optimal Control Problem. This approach generated safe and adaptive trajectories, demonstrating emergent behaviors like yielding and proceeding dynamically from the optimization process across diverse intersection scenarios.

## Problem

- Unsignalized intersections present a highly complex challenge for autonomous vehicles due to the absence of traffic signals, the need to infer intentions of other road users, and the combinatorial nature of interactions.
- Traditional hierarchical architectures for decision-making and trajectory planning often lead to inconsistencies, infeasible trajectories during mode transitions, and limited adaptability in dynamic traffic situations.
- Infrastructure-based coordination methods for intersections are frequently impractical in mixed-traffic environments, as they demand pervasive vehicle-to-infrastructure (V2I) communication and fully compliant, connected vehicles.

## Method

- The problem of integrated decision-making and trajectory planning is formulated as a unified ==Finite Horizon Optimal Control Problem (FHOCP)== that is periodically solved.
- An intersection-specific **Time-Varying Artificial Potential Field (TV-APF)** is incorporated directly into the FHOCP's cost function, with its amplitude dynamically modulated by a predicted ==global occupancy coefficient== for the conflict zone.
- The system includes mechanisms for selecting relevant interacting actors, performing ==short-horizon motion predictions==, and computing a smooth occupancy coefficient to scale the potential field's influence.

## Results

- The framework successfully generated safe and adaptive trajectories in varied unsignalized intersection scenarios, including an irregular three-way junction requiring a left turn through traffic and a congested four-way intersection with multiple right-of-way vehicles.
- In a left-turn scenario, the ego vehicle dynamically yielded to oncoming traffic by smoothly decelerating to **0 m/s**, then accelerated to **11 m/s** to exploit a safe gap.
- In a congested four-way scenario, the ego vehicle progressively decelerated and yielded to **three** right-of-way vehicles, then resumed speed tracking to **11.5 m/s**, demonstrating effective adherence to right-of-way rules and safe navigation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Complex driving behaviors, such as yielding, stopping, and accelerating, can emerge naturally and continuously from a single optimization problem without relying on pre-defined discrete decision logic or mode switching.
> - Dynamically adjusting the potential field's strength based on predicted intersection occupancy allows for continuous adaptation to evolving traffic conditions, leading to smoother and more responsive vehicle control.
> - Unifying decision-making and trajectory planning within a single optimization framework effectively mitigates the inconsistencies and rigidities inherent in traditional hierarchical control architectures.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Autonomous navigation through unsignalized intersections presents a complex challenge for automated vehicles due to the absence of traffic signals, the necessity to infer intentions of other road users, and the combinatorial nature of potential vehicle interactions. The broader research landscape for autonomous vehicle navigation at intersections can be broadly categorized into infrastructure-based coordination methods and ego-centric approaches.

Infrastructure-based coordination methods, such as reservation-based schemes and cooperative scheduling algorithms, involve a centralized roadside controller or intersection manager that assigns crossing orders and time slots. While these methods can theoretically guarantee collision-free traversal and optimize throughput, their applicability is often limited by the requirement for full vehicle-to-infrastructure (V2I) communication and the assumption of compliant, connected vehicles. This renders them less suitable for mixed-traffic environments where human-driven vehicles or unconnected autonomous vehicles are present.

Ego-centric planning methods operate from the perspective of the individual autonomous vehicle. Within this category, several distinct approaches have been explored. Game-theoretic formulations model strategic interactions among multiple vehicles, aiming to capture coupled decision processes. However, these often involve computationally intensive equilibrium calculations, which can be challenging for real-time application. Reinforcement learning (RL) and deep learning (DL) methods attempt to learn flexible navigation policies from data or simulation. While offering adaptability, they typically demand extensive training data, may lack formal safety guarantees, and often focus on high-level behavioral decisions rather than comprehensive, integrated trajectory planning. Optimization-based approaches, including Model Predictive Control (MPC) and Finite-Horizon Optimal Control Problems (FHOCP), explicitly encode vehicle dynamics, safety constraints, and comfort objectives, making them suitable for real-time trajectory planning in interactive scenarios. Artificial Potential Fields (APFs) are a class of methods used within optimization-based approaches, where obstacles generate repulsive fields and goals generate attractive fields, offering a computationally efficient way to embed collision avoidance and goal-seeking within a planning objective. Some recent work has used APFs as auxiliary reward-shaping signals within sampling-based MPC for unsignalized intersections.

A common issue across many ego-centric approaches, particularly rule-based, game-theoretic, and RL/DL methods, is the hierarchical or staged architecture used for decision-making (DM) and trajectory planning (TP). In such systems, a high-level module (e.g., a finite-state machine or a learned policy) first selects a behavioral mode (e.g., yield, proceed, stop). A downstream planner then generates a trajectory consistent with this choice. This modular decomposition, while simplifying design, can introduce inconsistencies where discrete mode transitions lead to infeasible trajectories under dynamic constraints. It also limits continuous adaptation as traffic situations evolve.

This paper addresses the challenge of integrating DM and TP into a unified optimization problem, moving beyond hierarchical architectures and discrete behavioral switching. It specifically extends the concept of Time-Varying Artificial Potential Fields (TV-APF), previously applied to highway and country-road scenarios, to the more complex environment of unsignalized intersections. The contribution lies in developing an intersection-specific TV-APF that directly encodes right-of-way rules and potential conflicts into the cost function of an FHOCP, allowing behaviors to emerge naturally from optimization.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a novel, unified framework for integrated Decision-Making (DM) and Trajectory Planning (TP) for automated vehicles operating in unsignalized intersections. This framework aims to generate safe and feasible reference trajectories by addressing the specific complexities of these environments within a single optimization problem.

The motivation for this work stems from several challenges inherent in unsignalized intersections and the limitations of existing autonomous driving solutions:

1. **Complexity of Unsignalized Intersections:** Unsignalized intersections are identified as one of the most demanding scenarios for autonomous vehicles. The absence of traffic signals necessitates reliance on local priority rules and real-time observation of surrounding traffic. Multiple vehicles with unknown intentions, approaching from various directions, create a combinatorial explosion of possible interactions and entry-exit branch combinations, significantly straining both safety and efficiency requirements for DM and TP systems.

2. **Limitations of Infrastructure-Based Methods:** While centralized coordination approaches can offer theoretical benefits in terms of safety and throughput, their practical application is often constrained. They typically require pervasive vehicle-to-infrastructure (V2I) communication and the assumption that all vehicles are connected and compliant. This renders them less effective or completely inapplicable in mixed-traffic environments, where human-driven vehicles or unconnected autonomous vehicles coexist, or in situations where communication infrastructure is degraded or unavailable.

3. **Limitations of Existing Ego-Centric Approaches:**
 - **Computational Expense:** Game-theoretic formulations, which model strategic interactions, often involve computationally expensive equilibrium calculations, making real-time implementation challenging.
 - **Safety Guarantees and Training Requirements:** Reinforcement learning and deep learning methods can learn flexible policies but usually require extensive training and may lack formal safety guarantees, often focusing on high-level decisions rather than integrated trajectory generation.
 - **Hierarchical DM/TP Inconsistencies:** A significant motivation is to overcome the drawbacks of staged or hierarchical architectures, common in many ego-centric planners. In these systems, a high-level DM module selects a discrete behavioral mode (e.g., stop, yield, proceed), which is then passed to a downstream TP module. This separation can lead to inconsistencies where discrete mode transitions result in infeasible trajectories, or where rigid mode boundaries prevent continuous adaptation to evolving traffic situations. Such issues are particularly pronounced at unsignalized intersections where continuous adjustments are critical.

4. **Desire for Unified Optimization:** The paper explicitly states that the integration of DM and TP into a single optimization problem, without reliance on discrete behavioral switching, remains a largely open challenge. The authors are motivated to provide a solution that allows behaviors like yielding, stopping, decelerating, or crossing to emerge naturally from the optimization process, rather than being pre-defined by a finite set of discrete modes. This approach is expected to lead to more robust, adaptable, and smoother driving behaviors.

5. **Extension of Previous Work:** The research builds upon a previously introduced Time-Varying Artificial Potential Field (TV-APF) framework developed for highway and country-road scenarios. The motivation here is to extend and adapt this proven framework to the specific demands of unsignalized intersections by designing an intersection-specific TV-APF whose amplitude is modulated by predicted conflict-zone occupancy, thereby addressing the unique interaction dynamics of these environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology presented in this paper focuses on unifying Decision-Making (DM) and Trajectory Planning (TP) for automated vehicles at unsignalized intersections through a Finite Horizon Optimal Control Problem (FHOCP) enhanced with a Time-Varying Artificial Potential Field (TV-APF). The overall approach integrates a global path reference, actor prediction, and an intersection-specific potential field within a periodically solved optimization problem.

**A. Core Optimization Framework:**
The DM/TP problem is formulated as an FHOCP, which is solved periodically every $T_P$ seconds over a prediction horizon of $N_P$ steps. The core of this framework is based on the authors' previous work, which outlines a hierarchical control architecture. In this architecture:
1. A global planner provides a smooth reference path ($\Pi \in C^2$) and intersection metadata (e.g., center position $P_{int,c}$, branch descriptions, ego vehicle's intended exit branch $b_{out,ego}$).
2. A local planner, which is the focus of this paper, solves the TV-APF-based FHOCP at each replanning step.
3. A motion controller tracks the resulting optimized trajectory.

**B. Vehicle Modeling and State Representation:**
The ego vehicle's motion is predicted over the horizon using a standard kinematic model. The vehicle state $\chi$ includes position, orientation, and velocity, while the control input vector $u$ consists of longitudinal acceleration and steering rate. The discrete-time dynamic model is given by $\chi(k+1) = f(\chi(k), u(k))$. Two reference frames are used: a global Cartesian frame (X, Y) for environmental representation, and a local Frenet frame (s, d) aligned with the reference path $\Pi$, simplifying distance calculations from lanes and actors.

**C. FHOCP Objective Function:**
The FHOCP aims to minimize a cost function $J$ subject to vehicle dynamics, admissible state, input, and input-rate bounds, safety constraints, and terminal conditions. The objective function is a weighted sum of several terms over the prediction horizon:
$J(U, \chi, \Xi) = \sum_{l=0}^{N_P-1} (K_v W_v + K_b W_b + K_l W_l + K_c W_c + K_o O)$
Where:
- $W_v$: Penalizes deviations from the desired speed profile.
- $W_b$: Enforces lane boundary constraints.
- $W_l$: Promotes occupation of the rightmost free lane.
- $W_c$: Penalizes high lateral acceleration.
- $O(\chi, \Xi, \ell)$: Accounts for obstacle avoidance, including the TV-APF contributions from surrounding actors. $\Xi$ represents the predicted states of all $n_o$ surrounding actors.

**D. Intersection-Aware Actor Selection and Occupancy Estimation:**
To effectively manage intersection interactions, the methodology includes specific mechanisms for actor selection and conflict zone occupancy estimation:

1. **Selection of Interacting Actors:** Only a subset of surrounding vehicles (actors) is considered relevant for intersection interaction. These are categorized into:
 - **Right-of-Way Actors ($A_{ROW}$):** Vehicles that have priority over the ego vehicle (ego must yield). These are identified based on an angular threshold $\rho_e$ calculated from the ego's intended exit branch $b_{out,ego}$. Actors whose polar angle $\alpha_j$ (measured around the intersection center) falls within this threshold are classified as right-of-way vehicles.
 - **Potentially Interfering Actors ($A_X$):** Vehicles that do not have priority over the ego but whose predicted trajectory may still intersect the ego's path. These are identified if their intended exit branch $b_{out,j}$ is less than or equal to the ego's $b_{out,ego}$, and they are not stationary (i.e., not already yielding to the ego).
 The set of selected actors ($A_{sel}$) is the union of $A_{ROW}$ and $A_X$.

2. **Short-Horizon Motion Prediction:** For the selected actors in $A_{sel}$, their positions are predicted over a short horizon ($H_P = N_P T_s$) using a simplified constant-velocity model. This model approximates the actor's position $p_j(k+1) = p_j(k) + T_s v_j$.

3. **Signed Distance to Conflict Zone:** A signed distance $d_{int,j}(k)$ from the intersection center $P_{int,c}$ is computed for each actor $j$. This distance is negative while approaching and positive while leaving the intersection.

4. **Intersection Occupancy Coefficient:** The signed distance is then mapped to a smooth occupancy coefficient $o_{int,j}(k) \in$ using a generalized Gaussian function. This function's parameters ($d_{pre}$, $d_{post}$, $c_{occ}$, $\epsilon_{occ}$) determine the influence range before and after the intersection center, allowing for asymmetry if needed. A global occupancy metric $o^*_{int}(k)$ is then derived as the maximum $o_{int,j}(k)$ over all selected actors at each time instant $k$. This coefficient scales the potential field, strengthening it when any relevant actor is predicted to be near the intersection and smoothly decreasing it as the conflict zone clears.

**E. Intersection TV-APF Formulation:**
The intersection interaction is modeled as a TV-APF, $W_{int}(k)$, consistently with the established framework. This potential is a two-dimensional Gaussian function defined in Frenet coordinates ($s, d$) relative to the intersection center. Its amplitude is dynamically modulated by the predicted global occupancy coefficient $o^*_{int}(k)$:
$W_{int}(k) = K_{int} o^*_{int}(k) \exp \left( - \left( \frac{s - s_{int}}{\gamma_{int,s}} \right)^{c_{int}} - \left( \frac{d}{\gamma_{int,d}} \right)^{c_{int}} \right)$
Where:
- $K_{int}$: Determines the overall strength of the field.
- $c_{int}$: Controls the field's shape.
- $\gamma_{int,s}$, $\gamma_{int,d}$: Decay rates based on desired spreads and tail values, controlling the field's extent in longitudinal and lateral directions.
This continuously differentiable penalty term is directly incorporated into the FHOCP cost function, $J$, allowing the optimizer to trade off between various behavioral alternatives (e.g., slowing down, yielding, stopping, or proceeding) in a unified manner, avoiding discrete decision logic.

**F. Implementation Details:**
The simulation studies are conducted in MATLAB/Simulink, utilizing the hierarchical control architecture described in. Traffic scenarios are generated using the Automated Driving Toolbox. The FHOCP is implemented using CasADi and solved with the Ipopt solver.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The simulation results demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed unified Decision-Making (DM) and Trajectory Planning (TP) framework for automated vehicles at unsignalized intersections. The core finding is that the integration of an intersection-oriented Time-Varying Artificial Potential Field (TV-APF) directly into the Finite Horizon Optimal Control Problem (FHOCP) enables the generation of safe, feasible, and adaptive trajectories, with complex behaviors emerging naturally from the optimization process without recourse to discrete decision switching.

Specifically, the simulations illustrate:

1. **Unified DM and TP:** The framework successfully integrates high-level decision-making (such as yielding or proceeding) with low-level trajectory planning. Behaviors like slowing down, yielding, stopping, and accelerating through the intersection are not pre-programmed as discrete modes but rather emerge as solutions to the single optimization problem, driven by the dynamic potential field.

2. **Adaptive Behavior Modulation:** The time-varying nature of the APF, modulated by the predicted intersection occupancy coefficient, allows the ego vehicle to continuously adapt its behavior to evolving traffic conditions. When the conflict zone is predicted to be occupied by relevant actors, the repulsive field strengthens, prompting the ego vehicle to slow down or stop. As the zone clears, the potential smoothly diminishes, allowing the vehicle to accelerate.

3. **Scenario A: Left Turn Using Traffic Gap:**
 - In an irregular three-way unsignalized intersection, the ego vehicle was tasked with making a left turn onto a secondary road, requiring it to find a safe gap in main road traffic.
 - Initially, the ego vehicle's speed ($v_{ego}$) progressively decreased from its reference of 10 m/s to 0 m/s. This deceleration was a direct result of the TV-APF penalizing entry into the occupied intersection, effectively causing the vehicle to yield.
 - At approximately t = 60s, the updated motion prediction indicated a sufficiently large gap in traffic. Consequently, the FHOCP, influenced by the reduced potential field, generated a trajectory that included prompt acceleration, allowing the ego vehicle to reach its reference speed of 11 m/s and safely complete the left turn. This demonstrates the framework's ability to identify and exploit safe gaps dynamically.

4. **Scenario B: Congested Four-Way Intersection:**
 - The ego vehicle approached a congested four-way unsignalized intersection at t = 125s, facing a more complex right-of-way scenario. It had to yield to three vehicles approaching from its right, while having precedence over two other vehicles (classified as potential interferers) from other branches.
 - The FHOCP successfully generated a behavior of progressive deceleration. This was attributed to the combined effect of the TV-APF, which strengthened due to the right-of-way vehicles and the potential interferers.
 - At around t = 140s, as the three right-of-way actors had passed and the two potential interferers were predicted to be approaching a full stop, the potential field subsided. The ego vehicle then resumed speed tracking to its reference of 11.5 m/s, safely crossing the intersection without incident. This scenario highlights the framework's capacity to manage multiple interacting vehicles and adhere to right-of-way rules through continuous optimization.

5. **Safety and Smoothness:** Across both scenarios, the approach generated feasible and safe trajectories, ensuring collision avoidance. The use of continuously differentiable potential fields contributed to smooth behavioral modulation, as evidenced by the ego vehicle's speed profile (Fig. 8), which showed gradual decelerations and accelerations rather than abrupt changes.

In summary, the simulation results validate that the proposed framework is capable of effectively handling diverse types of unsignalized intersection scenarios and traffic conditions, ensuring ego vehicle safety through an integrated DM-TP approach that avoids discrete decision logic.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The work presented in this paper holds significance for the field of autonomous driving by addressing a fundamental challenge: the integrated management of decision-making (DM) and trajectory planning (TP) in complex, interactive environments. Its potential impact spans several key areas:

1. **Advancement in Autonomous Driving Capabilities:** By proposing a unified framework for DM and TP at unsignalized intersections, the research contributes to the development of more robust and intelligent autonomous driving systems. Unsignalized intersections are a known bottleneck and safety concern for autonomous vehicles, and a reliable solution in this domain represents a step forward for broader autonomous vehicle deployment.

2. **Enhanced Safety and Reliability:** The direct encoding of safety constraints and right-of-way interactions into a single optimization problem through the Time-Varying Artificial Potential Field (TV-APF) mechanism inherently promotes collision-free operation. This approach can lead to higher safety guarantees compared to purely rule-based systems or those relying on discrete mode switching, which may struggle with edge cases or dynamic inconsistencies. The emergent behaviors, rather than pre-defined ones, may also enhance reliability in unforeseen scenarios.

3. **Improved Efficiency and Comfort:** The continuous and smooth modulation of behavior, arising from the differentiable potential fields and unified optimization, can result in more efficient and comfortable driving experiences. This avoids the abrupt maneuvers or overly conservative actions that can occur with hierarchical systems that transition between discrete behavioral states. Efficient navigation through intersections can contribute to overall traffic flow improvement.

4. **Applicability in Mixed-Traffic Environments:** The ego-centric nature of the proposed framework makes it particularly relevant for mixed-traffic environments, where a full deployment of connected and autonomous vehicles (CAV) and vehicle-to-infrastructure (V2I) communication cannot be assumed. The system relies on local sensor observations and short-horizon predictions of surrounding vehicles, making it adaptable to scenarios involving human-driven vehicles.

5. **Overcoming Limitations of Hierarchical Architectures:** The most significant theoretical impact is in mitigating the inherent inconsistencies and inflexibilities of traditional hierarchical DM/TP architectures. By allowing behaviors to emerge from a single optimization problem, the framework avoids issues like infeasible trajectory generation during mode transitions and rigid behavioral boundaries that restrict adaptation to dynamic traffic situations. This shift could simplify system design and enhance overall system robustness.

6. **Computational Feasibility for Real-time Applications:** The use of Artificial Potential Fields (APFs) is noted for its computational efficiency compared to some other complex interaction modeling techniques (e.g., extensive game-theoretic calculations). While the FHOCP itself involves optimization, the APF formulation provides a lightweight way to embed complex interaction rules, potentially making the approach suitable for real-time applications, especially with the simplified constant-velocity prediction model for actors.

7. **Foundation for Future Research:** This unified framework could serve as a foundational building block for developing even more sophisticated integrated planning and control systems. Future work might explore the integration of more complex actor behavior prediction models, adaptive parameter tuning for the TV-APF, or application to other challenging urban driving scenarios beyond intersections, such as roundabouts or complex merging zones.

In conclusion, this research presents a method for autonomous vehicle navigation that offers a unified, safe, and adaptive approach to handling unsignalized intersections, addressing several critical challenges in the field and contributing to the advancement of autonomous driving technology.
