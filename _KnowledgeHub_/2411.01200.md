# GarmentLab: A Unified Simulation and Benchmark for Garment Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> GarmentLab is a GPU-accelerated garment-manipulation environment and benchmark built on Isaac Sim, combining PBD and FEM simulation, ClothesNet garments plus ShapeNet scenes, and ROS/MoveIt/teleoperation interfaces. It defines 20 tasks across five interaction groups (garment-garment, fluid, FEM, rigid, avatar), adds the first real-world deformable benchmark plus three visual sim-to-real alignment methods, and shows current vision and RL methods generalize poorly.

## Problem

- Existing deformable-object simulators support only one physics method, restricting the object types and multi-material interactions that garment manipulation research can actually study.
- Prior benchmarks are largely CPU-based with missing garment meshes and narrow task sets, limiting parallel data collection and long-horizon, multi-media task design.
- No standardized real-world deformable-object benchmark exists, and prior platforms lack integrated sim-to-real algorithms, leaving a large visual and action gap for garments.

## Method

- **GarmentLab Engine** on Isaac Sim/PhysX5 couples ==PBD== for large garments and fluids with ==FEM== for small elastic items, plus ray-traced multi-camera rendering and ROS/MoveIt integration.
- **GarmentLab Assets** aggregates 11 ClothesNet garment categories, 20 scenes and 9000+ ShapeNet/PartNet objects, multiple end-effectors (gripper, suction, dexterous hands), human models, and material parameter sets.
- ==Sim2real framework== adds ==keypoint embedding alignment== via ==InfoNCE== self-play, point-cloud noise augmentation, ==chamfer-optimized affine point-cloud alignment==, plus teleoperation and MoveIt-generated trajectories.

## Results

- On large-piece folding, UniGarmentManip scored **61.5/62.1/59.8** for tops/trousers/skirts while PPO RL-State reached only **14.8/12.5/9.8** and RL-Vision **6.7/8.2/3.2**.
- Method strengths inverted by garment size: DIFT led small-piece hat hanging at **64.7/61.2** versus UniGarmentManip's **31.8/29.2**, but trailed badly on large-piece unfolding.
- Real-world tests gave UniGarmentManip **10/15** on T-shirt folding and hat hanging; ablating point-cloud alignment collapsed these to **2/15** and **5/15**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Multi-physics coupling matters: pairing particle-based and finite-element solvers lets one platform cover garments, fluids, elastic objects, and their interactions realistically.
> - Dense visual correspondence beats reinforcement learning on deformables, since high-dimensional partial observations and tangling trajectories block RL convergence on garment dynamics.
> - No single visual representation dominates across object scales; robustness to rotation and folding understanding trade off, so benchmarks must span garment sizes.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of next-generation home-assistant robots capable of intricate daily tasks, such as laundry or dressing, hinges on their ability to manipulate a diverse array of objects, including rigid, articulated, and especially deformable items like garments. Garment manipulation stands out as one of the most challenging areas in robotics and computer vision due due to the inherent complexities of fabric physics and topology.

Prior research has explored reinforcement learning and vision-based methods for deformable object manipulation. However, the authors argue that the progress in these areas has been severely constrained by the limitations of existing simulation environments and benchmarks. Current benchmarks often suffer from:
1. **Limited Diversity:** They typically offer a narrow range of garment types, tasks, or interaction scenarios (e.g., only simple folding, or single-object manipulation).
2. **Unrealistic Simulation:** Many platforms rely on single simulation methods (e.g., only Position-Based Dynamics (PBD) or Finite Element Method (FEM)), lack robust multi-physics interaction capabilities (garment-fluid, garment-rigid, garment-human), or are CPU-based, limiting parallelism and realism.
3. **Significant Sim-to-Real Gap:** There is a persistent challenge in transferring policies learned in simulation to real-world robots, often due to discrepancies in visual fidelity, physical dynamics, and lack of effective sim-to-real methodologies.

GarmentLab directly addresses these critical gaps. It positions itself as a foundational advancement, moving beyond existing environments like Softgym, Fluidlab, Sapien, Habitat, or Mujoco, which typically focus on specific aspects (e.g., only deformable objects, only fluids, or primarily rigid body interactions) or suffer from performance limitations (CPU-bound, lack of sophisticated rendering). By providing a unified, GPU-accelerated, multi-physics simulation with a content-rich benchmark and integrated sim-to-real solutions, GarmentLab aims to unlock the full potential of learning-based methods for garment manipulation, thereby accelerating the development of truly capable home-assistant robots within the broader embodied AI landscape.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind GarmentLab is to overcome the significant hurdles in developing intelligent agents for garment manipulation, which is crucial for advanced home-assistant robotics. The authors identify three core challenges (C1, C2, C3) that current approaches struggle with:

- **C1: Complex Garment Dynamics and Infinite States:** Each garment possesses nearly infinite self-deform states and complex kinematic/dynamic properties. Models must comprehend these diverse states, which necessitates vast amounts of training data.
- **C2: Multi-Physical Interactions:** Garment manipulation often involves interactions with various object types—rigid (e.g., hangers), articulated (e.g., wardrobes), fluids (e.g., washing clothes), and human bodies (e.g., dressing up). Understanding these multi-modal interactions is critical.
- **C3: Persistent Sim-to-Real Gap:** The high complexity of garment manipulation strategies and the challenges in visual perception (due to diverse states and textures) exacerbate the sim-to-real gap, making real-world deployment difficult.

Against this backdrop, the key objectives of GarmentLab are:
1. **To develop a unified, content-rich, and realistic simulation environment:** This involves supporting diverse garment types, robotic systems, and manipulators, as well as enabling sophisticated multi-physics interactions.
2. **To establish a comprehensive benchmark:** This benchmark should encompass a wide array of tasks, including novel, long-horizon scenarios that test agents' understanding of physics and planning capabilities.
3. **To significantly narrow the sim-to-real gap:** By integrating multiple simulation methods, proposing dedicated sim-to-real algorithms, and offering the first reproducible real-world benchmark for deformable objects.
4. **To evaluate state-of-the-art algorithms:** The aim is to highlight current limitations, particularly in generalization and long-horizon planning, thereby guiding future research directions.
5. **To foster community growth:** By open-sourcing the environment and providing detailed analysis, the authors intend to accelerate research and development in garment and deformable object manipulation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

GarmentLab is structured around three main components: the GarmentLab Engine, GarmentLab Assets, and GarmentLab Benchmark, complemented by a novel sim-to-real framework and a real-world benchmark.

#### 3.1. GarmentLab Engine

Built upon NVIDIA's Omniverse Isaac Sim, the GarmentLab Engine is a highly parallelized, GPU-based simulator providing realistic rendering and extensive sensor support.
- **Multi-Physics Simulation:** It uniquely integrates various simulation methods tailored to different object types:
 - **Position-Based Dynamics (PBD):** Used for large garments (e.g., dresses) and fluids, offering efficiency and stability.
 - **Finite Element Method (FEM):** Applied to small elastic garments (e.g., gloves, socks) and everyday deformable objects (e.g., toys, sponges) for accurate mechanical behavior.
 - **PhysX:** Utilized for rigid and articulated body simulation (robots, furniture) for precise force control and dynamics.
 - **Flow Models:** Integrated to simulate wind and fluid flow effects.
- **Data Pipeline:** Features both a Visual Data System (RGB-D, ground-truth semantic labels, 2D/3D bounding boxes, normals, instance segmentation) and an RL-Training System leveraging IsaacGym for efficient, parallelized GPU training.
- **Rendering & Sensors:** Supports multiple camera angles (eye-on-hand, eye-on-base) and GPU-enabled ray tracing for enhanced realism, crucial for visual algorithms and reducing the sim-to-real gap. It provides access to robot joints, cloth particles, and object poses.
- **ROS Integration:** Includes Robot Operating System (ROS) and MoveIt for motion planning, which are widely used in real-world robotics, further aiding in bridging the sim-to-real gap.

#### 3.2. GarmentLab Assets

This is a comprehensive dataset of simulation-ready content, integrating models from state-of-the-art repositories.
- **Garments and Cloth:** 11 categories of daily garments from ClothesNet, including diverse physical parameters (particle size, stiffness, friction).
- **Robots:** A variety of systems, including Franka manipulators with parallel grippers, UR5 with suction, RidgebackFranka mobile robot, and ShadowHands dexterous hands.
- **Rigid and Articulated Objects:** Over 9000 high-quality 3D models from ShapeNet, PartNet, YCB, and PartNet-Mobility, covering scenes and furniture.
- **Human Models:** Avatars from Actorcore, integrated with articulated joints and skin meshes for realistic human-robot collaboration tasks.
- **Materials:** Carefully selected Omniverse Base Materials to achieve optimal rendering and diverse physical properties.

#### 3.3. GarmentLab Benchmark

The benchmark proposes 20 tasks, categorized into five groups based on physical interactions, to test an agent's understanding of physics, long-horizon planning, and real-world transferability.
- **Task Categories:**
 - **Garment-Garment:** Fundamental manipulation (folding, unfolding, clothes pile retrieval).
 - **Garment-Fluid:** Interactions with fluids/flow (washing, drying with hairdryer).
 - **Garment-FEMObjects:** Manipulating deformable objects (packing clothes, dexterous grasp of plush toys).
 - **Garment-Rigid:** Common interactions with rigid/articulated bodies (hanging, putting into washing machine).
 - **Garment-Avatar:** Human-robot collaboration (dressing a person).
- **Long-Horizon Tasks:** Complex tasks like "organizing clothes," "wash clothes," "make up table," and "dress up," requiring holistic planning and multi-skill integration.

#### 3.4. Real-World Benchmark

GarmentLab introduces the first reproducible real-world benchmark for deformable objects and garments.
- **Object Selection:** Objects are chosen for diversity (shapes, sizes, transparencies, deformabilities, textures), durability, and accessibility (e.g., Uniqlo clothing, Disney plush toys).
- **Scanning Pipeline:** A detailed process for converting real-world objects into simulation assets, involving scanning models wearing clothes, post-processing (down-sampling, texture, hole repair), and manual keypoint annotation.
- **Manipulation Protocols:** Standardized experimental setups and procedural guidelines for tasks like clothes-hanging and scarf-wearing.

#### 3.5. Sim2Real Framework

The paper proposes and integrates methods to mitigate the vision and action gaps between simulation and reality.
- **Sim-Real Vision Alignment:**
 - **Keypoint Embedding Alignment:** Utilizes InfoNCE loss with detected keypoints (via markers and self-play) to align corresponding point representations between real and simulated garments.
 - **Noisy Observation:** Adds salt-and-pepper and Gaussian noise to depth images during training to make models more robust to real-world sensor noise.
 - **Point Cloud Alignment:** Optimizes an affine matrix using Chamfer distance to align point cloud distributions from real-world scans with simulation data.
- **Real-World Motion Generation:**
 - **Teleoperation:** A lightweight, vision-based (Leap Motion) system allows humans to control dexterous hands and grippers in both real and simulated environments, facilitating data collection for offline training.
 - **MoveIt Integration:** Leverages MoveIt for advanced motion planning and obstacle avoidance, enabling the generation of more realistic and collision-free robot trajectories in simulation that are closer to real-world execution.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The authors conducted extensive experiments in both simulation and the real world to evaluate state-of-the-art vision-based and reinforcement learning (RL) algorithms on various garment manipulation tasks.

#### 4.1. Simulation Experiment Results

- **Vision-Based Algorithms (UniGarmentManip (UGM), DIFT, Affordance):**
 - **UGM:** Performed best on large-piece clothing (tops, trousers, skirts) for tasks like folding and unfolding. This is attributed to its emphasis on cross-deform and cross-object consistency, aiding in understanding garment deformation and topology. However, performance was slightly lower than original UGM due to the added complexity of scenes and robot interactions.
 - **DIFT:** Showed superior performance with small-piece clothing (hats, gloves) in hanging/placing tasks. Its robustness to rotation, stemming from pre-trained large diffusion models, was key for small, less deformable items. Conversely, DIFT struggled with large garment unfolding due to its 2D pretrained correspondence's inability to fully grasp complex 3D garment deformations.
 - **Affordance:** Performed well in tasks not requiring precise point selection, such as hanging large garments, but less effectively with folded items.
 - **Generalization:** Overall, vision-based algorithms demonstrated limited generalization capabilities, with performance significantly affected by the initial state of objects and complexities like wrinkles.

- **Reinforcement Learning (RL) Algorithms (RL-State, RL-Vision):**
 - **Overall Poor Performance:** Both RL-state-based and RL-vision-based methods performed significantly worse than vision-based algorithms on garment manipulation tasks.
 - **Challenges:** RL agents often generated "abnormal trajectories" leading to collisions, clothes tangling with the robot arm, or being pushed away. They struggled with long-horizon planning due to large reward fluctuations and difficulty in maintaining previously completed sub-tasks.
 - **Vision-Based RL:** Performed even worse than state-based RL, mainly due to the higher dimensionality and partial nature of visual observations, which hindered the model's ability to converge on effective strategies and accurately detect object positions, especially for thin garments.
 - **Dexterous/Mobile Tasks:** The larger action and search spaces in these more complex tasks resulted in suboptimal performance for RL.

#### 4.2. Real-World Experiments

- **Validation:** Real-world experiments, primarily conducted with vision-based algorithms (due to the safety risks of RL actions), largely aligned with simulation findings, confirming the realism and utility of the GarmentLab environment for real-world applications.
- **Sim2Real Effectiveness:** Ablation studies on the proposed sim2real methods (point cloud alignment, noise augmentation, keypoint embedding alignment) demonstrated significant improvements in real-world performance. Removing any of these components led to a substantial drop in success rates, indicating their crucial role in enhancing representation smoothness and accuracy for real-world transfer. For instance, UGM's T-shirt folding success rate dropped from 10/15 to 2/15 without point cloud alignment.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

GarmentLab represents a significant leap forward in the field of deformable object and garment manipulation, addressing long-standing limitations in existing research platforms.

- **Unprecedented Realism and Diversity:** By unifying multiple advanced physics simulation methods (PBD, FEM, PhysX, Flow) within the high-fidelity Isaac Sim environment, GarmentLab offers an unparalleled level of realism and content diversity. This enables the simulation of complex multi-physics interactions (garment-fluid, garment-human) and a broad range of garment types, moving beyond the scope of previous single-focus environments.
- **Comprehensive Benchmarking:** The introduction of 20 diverse tasks, including novel, complex long-horizon scenarios, provides a rigorous testbed for developing and evaluating advanced manipulation algorithms. This rich benchmark will drive innovation in areas like hierarchical planning, multi-modal sensing, and dexterous control.
- **Pioneering Real-World Benchmark:** GarmentLab establishes the first reproducible real-world benchmark for deformable objects and garments. This is a critical contribution, providing a standardized platform for transparent performance evaluation, international comparison, and crucial sim-to-real validation, which was previously lacking for these challenging object categories.
- **Actionable Sim2Real Solutions:** The integrated and validated sim-to-real framework, encompassing both vision alignment and motion generation techniques (teleoperation, MoveIt integration), directly tackles one of the biggest bottlenecks in robotics research. These automated and self-supervised methods offer practical pathways for more effective transfer of learned policies from simulation to physical robots.
- **Guiding Future Research:** By rigorously evaluating state-of-the-art methods, GarmentLab clearly highlights current algorithmic deficiencies, particularly in generalization across diverse garment states and robust long-horizon planning. These insights provide valuable guidance for the research community, pointing towards key areas for future development.
- **Accelerated Innovation (Open-Source):** The commitment to open-sourcing the GarmentLab environment and its comprehensive analysis will democratize access to this advanced platform. This will significantly lower the barrier to entry for researchers, fostering rapid iteration and collaborative advancement in garment manipulation and, by extension, the broader field of home-assistant robotics and embodied AI.

The potential societal impact of GarmentLab is overwhelmingly positive, as it paves a solid way for the development of highly capable home-assistant robots that can perform complex daily tasks, thereby enhancing human quality of life and potentially assisting in various industrial applications. The authors have not identified any significant negative potential impacts.
