# Toward Ultra-Long-Horizon Agentic Science: Cognitive Accumulation for Machine Learning Engineering

> [!summary] Summary
> ML-Master 2.0 is an autonomous agent employing a Hierarchical Cognitive Caching (HCC) architecture to manage context across ultra-long-horizon machine learning engineering tasks. The system dynamically distills transient execution details into stable knowledge and reusable wisdom, achieving a 56.44% medal rate on OpenAI’s MLE-Bench and outperforming previous LLM-based agents.

## Problem

- Large Language Models (LLMs) struggle with "ultra-long-horizon autonomy" in scientific discovery due to overwhelming high-dimensional data, delayed feedback, and vast execution details.
- Existing context management for LLM-based agents lacks explicit mechanisms for differentiating and dynamically managing raw interaction traces versus stable strategic insights, focusing primarily on context window extension or linear aggregation.
- LLMs fail to consolidate sparse feedback into consistent, long-term strategic guidance, hindering sustained coherence over prolonged experimental cycles in real-world research environments.

## Method

- Introduces **ML-Master 2.0**, an autonomous agent for ultra-long-horizon machine learning engineering tasks, built upon the principle of ==cognitive accumulation==.
- Develops the ==Hierarchical Cognitive Caching (HCC)== architecture, a multi-tiered context management system comprising ==L1 (Evolving Experience)==, ==L2 (Refined Knowledge)==, and ==L3 (Prior Wisdom)== caches.
- Implements ==dynamic context migration== mechanisms (prefetching, retrieval, promotion) to distill transient execution traces into stable knowledge and cross-task wisdom, effectively separating immediate execution details from long-term strategic planning.

## Results

- ML-Master 2.0 achieved an average medal rate of **56.44%** on OpenAI’s MLE-Bench, demonstrating a **92.7%** relative improvement over its predecessor and an **11.2%** relative improvement over the leading proprietary LLM-based method.
- The agent consistently ranked first in medal rates across all task complexities (**75.8%** for low, **50.9%** for medium, and **42.2%** for high), maintaining a **95.6%** valid submission rate and outperforming **50%** of human participants in **63.1%** of tasks.
- Ablation studies confirmed that all three HCC layers are critical for performance: L1 prevents context proliferation during debugging, L2 synthesizes complex solutions for top-tier results, and L3 provides strong initialization to reduce ineffective exploration.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Ultra-long-horizon autonomy requires a "cognitive accumulation" process that hierarchically refines and stabilizes experience from raw traces to reusable wisdom, rather than simple context window expansion.
> - A multi-tiered caching architecture, inspired by computer memory systems, can dynamically manage context to prevent saturation while maintaining strategic coherence over extended experimental cycles.
> - Differentiating between transient execution details (L1), refined phase-level insights (L2), and task-agnostic wisdom (L3) is crucial for sustaining strategic focus and enabling knowledge transfer across diverse and complex machine learning engineering tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work situates itself within the evolving field of artificial intelligence, specifically focusing on the transition from AI as a passive assistant to AI as an autonomous agent capable of scientific discovery, termed "agentic science." This paradigm shift is supported by recent initiatives such as DeepMind’s AlphaEvolve, OpenAI’s FrontierScience, and Google’s AI co-scientist efforts.

A primary challenge identified in this transition is achieving "ultra-long-horizon autonomy." This refers to an agent's ability to maintain strategic coherence and perform iterative corrections over prolonged experimental cycles, potentially spanning days or weeks. Existing Large Language Models (LLMs), while demonstrating capabilities in short-horizon reasoning, often encounter difficulties in real-world research environments. These environments are characterized by high-dimensional data, delayed feedback, and a large volume of execution details, which can overwhelm LLMs and hinder their capacity to consolidate sparse feedback into consistent long-term guidance.

To address this challenge, the authors position their research within the "AI-for-AI (AI4AI)" paradigm. AI4AI involves AI systems autonomously advancing AI itself, offering a computational substrate for accelerated experimentation with immediate feedback and reduced costs, thereby facilitating the development of ultra-long-horizon autonomy without physical constraints. Within AI4AI, machine learning engineering (MLE) tasks, particularly those presented in OpenAI’s MLE-Bench (a collection of 75 real-world Kaggle competitions), are highlighted as a representative challenge. These tasks necessitate navigating vast, unstructured search spaces through extended trial and error and accumulating experience across iterations, rather than relying on single-step correctness.

The paper also addresses limitations in existing context management strategies for LLM-based agents. Prior approaches have largely focused on extending context length through architectural means (e.g., hierarchical context buffering, external memory paging as seen in MemGPT, HiAgent, G-Memory, HippoRAG). These methods primarily emphasize resource allocation and retrieval mechanisms. Concurrently, experience-driven approaches (e.g., Reflexion, Memento, ReasoningBank) aim to abstract execution trajectories and reasoning traces into reusable feedback or knowledge. However, these often operate with flat or loosely structured memory stores and lack explicit mechanisms for governing memory growth and lifecycle, particularly how raw interaction traces should be selectively retained or discarded over time. The paper identifies a gap in frameworks that jointly regulate short-term working context, accumulated execution experience, and abstracted memory within a unified control process, specifically concerning structured policies for context accumulation, promotion, or eviction.

In the realm of autonomous machine learning, earlier benchmarks and platforms (e.g., MLAgentBench, OpenHands) highlighted the need for tight execution feedback loops. Subsequent methods (e.g., AIDE, R&D-Agent) introduced iterative refinement and planning, but typically relied on linear aggregation or summarization for context, failing to structurally differentiate between transient execution details and stable strategic insights. More advanced approaches incorporating search and evolutionary strategies (e.g., AIRA, AutoMLGen, FM Agent) enabled knowledge sharing but often treated "knowledge" as a homogeneous entity, lacking the cognitive differentiation required to distill raw experience into reusable wisdom and maintain focus over prolonged discovery processes. This work seeks to bridge these identified gaps by introducing a system that structurally differentiates and manages context across various temporal scales.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an autonomous agent capable of effectively mastering ultra-long-horizon machine learning engineering (MLE) tasks. These MLE tasks are presented as a representative microcosm of scientific discovery, characterized by their complexity, delayed feedback, and extended experimental cycles.

The core motivation stems from the inherent difficulties current Large Language Models (LLMs) face when attempting to operate autonomously in real-world research settings. While LLMs exhibit proficiency in short-horizon reasoning, they tend to become overwhelmed by the vast amounts of execution details generated in high-dimensional, delayed-feedback environments. This limitation prevents them from consolidating sparse feedback into coherent, long-term strategic guidance, thereby bottlenecking the advancement of agentic science towards true ultra-long-horizon autonomy.

The authors propose that overcoming this bottleneck requires reframing context management. Instead of merely expanding the context window or linearly aggregating historical information, they advocate for a process of "cognitive accumulation." This conceptualization views an agent's experience as undergoing an evolutionary process of refinement, stabilization, and reuse. Specifically:
- Agents initially generate extensive "raw experience."
- Only a validated fraction of this experience is distilled into reusable "knowledge."
- Further abstraction and cross-task stability transform this knowledge into higher-level "wisdom."

This hierarchical differentiation of context is motivated by the need to sustain strategic coherence over extended scientific workflows. Short-term experience is crucial for immediate decision-making and debugging, while more stable knowledge is essential for maintaining strategic consistency throughout prolonged exploration. Abstracted wisdom, in turn, facilitates transfer and reuse across different tasks.

This necessity to distinguish transient processing from stable state draws inspiration from the multi-level cache hierarchies found in computer systems. Similar to how computer caches manage finite resources by separating frequently accessed, short-lived data from stable, long-term reusable states, the proposed agent aims to manage its cognitive state.

The specific objectives for ML-Master 2.0, the agent introduced in this work, are:
- To implement a multi-tiered context management architecture, termed Hierarchical Cognitive Caching (HCC), which is inspired by computer systems.
- To dynamically distill transient execution traces into stable knowledge and cross-task wisdom, effectively decoupling immediate execution details from long-term experimental strategy.
- To overcome the scaling limitations inherent in static context windows, thereby enabling autonomous exploration in complex scenarios that extend beyond human-precedent levels.
- To empirically demonstrate that structured cognitive accumulation is a critical factor for autonomous agents to succeed in long-horizon, trial-and-error research environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors introduce ML-Master 2.0, an autonomous agent designed for ultra-long-horizon MLE tasks, built upon the principle of "cognitive accumulation." This approach redefines context management through a multi-tiered architecture called Hierarchical Cognitive Caching (HCC).

**Hierarchical Cognitive Caching (HCC)**
HCC is presented as a coordinated design comprising two main components:
1. **Hierarchical Caching (HC):** This structural architecture organizes context into multiple tiers based on their temporal stability and reuse value.
2. **Context Migration (CM):** This dictates the protocols for dynamically promoting, consolidating, or discarding information across these tiers as the agent's exploration progresses.

The HCC framework separates transient experience from stable knowledge and reusable wisdom using a three-level cache system:

- **L1 Cache: Evolving Experience:**
 - **Function:** Serves as the agent's working memory, storing high-fidelity execution traces essential for immediate reasoning. This includes the current research plan, code patches, and terminal outputs (e.g., error messages, metric logs).
 - **Content:** Contains all events prior to reaching the initial code and its results (E_{t0-1}), high-level plan proposals at phase boundaries (P_{p-1}), and raw execution traces of the currently active plan (E_{t_{p-1}+1:t}).
 - **Mechanism:** Retains raw traces only for active execution to preserve fidelity for debugging and execution-aware decision-making while preventing context saturation during prolonged exploration.

- **L2 Cache: Refined Knowledge:**
 - **Function:** Acts as the agent's mid-term strategic memory. It stores intermediate, stabilized cognitive elements distilled from *completed* exploration phases. These include key judgments (e.g., "feature X is harmful"), experimental insights (e.g., "CV leakage observed under split Y"), and condensed progress summaries that retain decision rationales but remove verbose execution details.
 - **Content:** Consists of compact knowledge summaries (κ_{i:j}) derived from raw event segments through a phase-level context promotion operator.
 - **Mechanism:** Facilitates long-horizon planning by allowing the agent to access validated decisions and insights without carrying extensive execution logs, thus stabilizing strategic reasoning over tens of hours.

- **L3 Cache: Prior Wisdom:**
 - **Function:** Represents the agent's long-term memory, storing task-agnostic, transferable strategies distilled from *previously solved* machine learning tasks. Examples include robust model templates, reusable preprocessing pipelines, and stable hyperparameter priors.
 - **Content:** Stored as a set of embedding-value pairs {(h_n, w_n)}, where h_n is the semantic embedding of a past task descriptor (d_n), and w_n is the corresponding distilled task-level wisdom text.
 - **Mechanism:** Persistent across tasks and updated only upon task completion via context promotion. Enables efficient transfer of learning across tasks and provides strong priors for initial exploration phases, enhancing long-horizon autonomy.

**Context Migration Mechanisms**
To facilitate cognitive accumulation within this hierarchical structure, ML-Master 2.0 employs cache-like mechanisms for context migration:

1. **Initialization via Context Prefetching:**
 - **Process:** Before starting a new task, a compact textual descriptor (d_τ) of the current task is generated by an LLM and embedded. This embedding (q) is then used to query the L3 (Prior Wisdom) cache. Relevant wisdom units (w_n) are retrieved if their embeddings (h_n) exhibit a cosine similarity above a predefined threshold (δ) with q.
 - **Output:** The initial environment event (e0) for the agent is constructed by concatenating the task description, user instructions, and the retrieved prior wisdom (Ω_τ).
 - **Effect:** Provides a strong and relevant starting context, which enhances initial understanding and informs exploration.

2. **Retrieval via Context Hit:**
 - **Policy:** The agent's context construction function (g(·)) prioritizes retrieving raw events from the L1 (Evolving Experience) cache. If a specific event is not found in L1, the system falls back to retrieving its compact summary from the L2 (Refined Knowledge) cache.
 - **Effect:** This policy ensures that all plan events and active-phase traces are accessed in their raw, detailed form, while completed phases are represented by concise, refined units. This strategy prevents context saturation while maintaining strategic continuity.

3. **Consolidation via Context Promotion:**
 - **Mechanism:** An LLM-based retrospective abstraction process (P) compresses execution traces into knowledge (P1) or wisdom (P2).
 - **Phase-level promotion (P1) to L2:**
 - **Trigger:** Upon the completion of each exploration phase, which corresponds to the execution of a hierarchical research plan.
 - **Process:** Raw parallel exploration trajectories (σ) generated within the phase are summarized into a refined knowledge unit (κ_p) using an LLM.
 - **Update:** The generated summary (κ_p) is written to the L2 cache, and the corresponding raw trajectories are removed from the L1 cache.
 - **Effect:** Incrementally distills noisy exploration traces into compact, reusable knowledge.
 - **Task-level promotion (P2) to L3:**
 - **Trigger:** When the agent completes an entire task at the maximum step limit.
 - **Process:** A task-level wisdom representation (w_τ) is produced by the LLM, synthesizing information from the task descriptor (d_τ), the final contents of L1 and L2, and the final solution code.
 - **Update:** The resulting wisdom (w_τ) is embedded and stored in the L3 cache.
 - **Effect:** Progressively crystallizes transient execution-level context into persistent, retrieval-ready wisdom, completing the cognitive accumulation loop.

**Agent Execution Loop**
For a given ML task, the agent retrieves prior wisdom to form an initial context and attempts to generate an initial functional code. Subsequently, it proposes a hierarchical research plan outlining multiple exploration directions with concrete implementation suggestions. These suggestions are executed, generating code, interacting with the environment, and refining approaches based on feedback. Upon completion of all directions in a plan, the agent reaches a phase boundary, consolidates the phase's experience into L2, and proposes the next research plan. This iterative process continues until task completion or a predefined time budget (24 hours in experiments) is reached.

**Technical Details**
- **LLM Backbone:** Deepseek-V3.2-Speciale is used for coding and research, while Deepseek-V3.2 (with thinking) is sparingly used for context promotion.
- **Warm-up Data:** 407 Kaggle competitions (excluding those in MLE-Bench) were used to rapidly build an initial L3 (Prior Wisdom) cache.
- **Evaluation:** Performance is measured on OpenAI’s MLE-Bench, comprising 75 real-world Kaggle machine learning competitions. The primary metric is the average medal rate (percentage of tasks achieving Bronze, Silver, or Gold performance), along with valid submission rate, above median score rate, Silver+ and Gold medal rates.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of ML-Master 2.0 on OpenAI’s MLE-Bench demonstrated its capabilities in ultra-long-horizon machine learning engineering.

**Overall Performance:**
- ML-Master 2.0 achieved an average medal rate of 56.44%, which was the highest among all evaluated methods. This represents a 92.7% relative improvement compared to its predecessor, ML-Master.
- The agent also outperformed the previously leading proprietary LLM-based method (Leeroo), which had a medal rate of 50.7%, indicating an 11.2% relative improvement.

**Performance Across Task Complexities:**
- ML-Master 2.0 exhibited consistent performance across all difficulty levels defined by MLE-Bench, securing the top rank in medal rates:
 - Low-complexity tasks: 75.8% medal rate (improved from 48.48% for ML-Master).
 - Medium-complexity tasks: 50.9% medal rate (improved from 20.18% for ML-Master).
 - High-complexity tasks: 42.2% medal rate (improved from 24.44% for ML-Master).
- This indicates the agent's ability to handle a diverse range of challenges effectively, without significant performance degradation in more complex scenarios.

**Robustness and Submission Quality:**
- ML-Master 2.0 maintained an average valid submission rate of 95.6%, which is comparable to other leading approaches.
- It achieved a 63.1% rate of outperforming 50% of human participants in tasks, which was also the highest among all methods. This suggests a notable level of robustness and a high performance floor for the solutions generated.

**Ablation Study of HCC Architecture (on MLE-Bench-Lite):**
An ablation study was conducted to evaluate the contribution of each hierarchical caching layer:
- **Full HCC Implementation:** Achieved 95.5% valid submissions, 81.8% above median score, and 72.7% any medal rate.
- **Removal of L1 (Evolving Experience):** Resulted in a substantial performance decline, with the valid submission rate dropping to 54.5% and the medal rate to 22.7%. This indicates the critical role of L1 in managing ultra-long-horizon tasks.
- **Exclusion of L2 (Refined Knowledge):** Led to a decrease in the "any medal" rate to 59.1%. While retaining raw context allowed for average performance, L2 was found necessary for synthesizing complex solutions required for top-tier results.
- **Omission of L3 (Prior Wisdom):** Caused degradation in both the "above median" rate (72.7%) and the "any medal" rate (54.5%). This suggests that L3 provides a strong initialization that reduces ineffective exploration and is crucial for competitive performance. These results collectively support the synergistic design of the HCC architecture.

**Context Length Management:**
- Observations on a specific ultra-long-horizon task (random-acts-of-pizza) demonstrated that without HCC, the context length could proliferate rapidly to over 200,000 tokens, especially with debugging processes.
- With the HCC architecture, ML-Master 2.0 effectively limited the peak context length to approximately 70,000 tokens while retaining critical insights from past attempts. The agent successfully secured a medal during the fourth iteration of its research plan proposal and verification process. This illustrates HCC's ability to manage context efficiently.

**Performance Over Time:**
- The agent consistently improved its solution quality over time. As the iteration time increased, ML-Master 2.0 produced progressively better solutions, demonstrating the effectiveness of its HCC architecture in enabling sustained progress throughout extended task execution.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The work presented by ML-Master 2.0 contributes to the field of artificial intelligence by addressing the challenge of ultra-long-horizon autonomy in agentic science, particularly within machine learning engineering.

**Conceptual Advancement:** The research introduces a conceptual framework for "cognitive accumulation," reframing ultra-long-horizon autonomy not merely as an issue of context window expansion but as an evolutionary process. This process involves the structural differentiation of transient experience into validated knowledge and abstract wisdom. This provides a theoretical basis for autonomous agents to sustain coherent reasoning over extended scientific workflows.

**Architectural Contribution:** The development of the Hierarchical Cognitive Caching (HCC) architecture is a key technical contribution. Inspired by multi-tiered memory systems in computer architecture, HCC provides a dynamic context management system that coordinates the promotion and consolidation of information. This design effectively decouples high-frequency execution feedback from long-term strategic planning, addressing the critical challenge of context saturation that often limits current LLM-based agents.

**Empirical Validation:** The achieved state-of-the-art performance on OpenAI’s MLE-Bench, with a 56.44% medal rate and superior results across various task complexities, provides empirical evidence for the efficacy of structured cognitive accumulation. These results suggest that the ability to evolve and manage context hierarchically is a critical enabler for autonomous agents to master the long-horizon, trial-and-error processes characteristic of real-world scientific research.

**Potential for Agentic Science:** ML-Master 2.0 offers a scalable blueprint for AI systems to move towards full autonomy in scientific discovery. By enabling agents to manage complex, multi-day experimental cycles and learn from extensive interaction histories, the framework suggests a pathway for AI to orchestrate scientific investigations that may exceed human-precedent complexities. This could accelerate discovery in various scientific domains by providing AI with the capacity to explore, learn, and adapt over prolonged periods, overcoming limitations related to human cognitive load and time constraints.

The findings also suggest a generalizable method for agents to evolve their context and sustain strategic focus, potentially extending beyond machine learning engineering to other computationally intensive or iterative scientific domains. This work helps to overcome the inherent limitations of LLMs being overwhelmed by execution details and struggling to consolidate sparse feedback into actionable long-term guidance, thus moving AI closer to autonomous scientific research.
