# Perception-R1: Pioneering Perception Policy with Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers demonstrate a reinforcement learning framework that enhances the visual perception capabilities of multimodal large language models (MLLMs), achieving state-of-the-art performance across multiple benchmarks including a 31.9% AP on COCO2017val object detection - the first time a pure MLLM has surpassed the 30% threshold.

## Problem

- Current MLLMs struggle with visual perception tasks despite strong language reasoning abilities
- Existing approaches lack effective methods for training perception policies in MLLMs
- RL techniques successful for language reasoning do not directly transfer to visual perception, whose distinct characteristics require a revised understanding of how RL should be applied

## Method

- Rule-based reinforcement learning framework using ==GRPO== algorithm without requiring a critic model
- Task-specific reward functions based on visual discrimination metrics like IoU and edit distance
- Multi-subject reward matching using Hungarian algorithm for handling multiple objects

## Results

- Achieved +**4.2%** improvement on RefCOCO+, +**17.9%** on PixMo-Count, and +**4.2%** on PageOCR
- First pure MLLM to exceed **30%** AP (**31.9%**) on COCO2017val object detection benchmark
- Demonstrated that scaling up rollout samples enhances reward optimization, and that reward matching improves exploration for multi-subject visual perception tasks

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicit chain-of-thought reasoning processes are not necessary for effective perception policies
> - Performance improvements from RL scale with task complexity compared to supervised fine-tuning
> - Carefully designed reward functions are crucial for healthy learning curves and pattern discovery

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research falls into the rapidly evolving landscape of multimodal large language models (MLLMs) and reinforcement learning (RL). It specifically addresses the challenge of enhancing the perception capabilities of MLLMs using RL techniques. The paper builds upon the success of DeepSeek-R1, which demonstrated the potential of rule-based RL for improving reasoning in language models.

**Key trends in the field that this work addresses:**

- **Transition from non-reasoning to reasoning LLMs:** The paper acknowledges the shift in the LLM landscape from models like GPT-4 to those with strong reasoning capabilities, exemplified by DeepSeek-R1.
- **Limitations of unimodal reasoning models:** The authors highlight the restriction of current reasoning models being mostly focused on the linguistic domain, limiting their ability to interact with the world in a truly perceptive way.
- **RL for post-training:** The paper acknowledges the growing interest in using RL as a post-training technique to improve LLMs, particularly in alignment with human preferences.
- **Vision-language models:** This work builds upon the existing research in vision-language models and explores how RL can enhance visual reasoning.

**Distinguishing Factors:**

- **Focus on perception policy learning:** This paper differentiates itself by specifically focusing on perception policy learning within MLLMs, a relatively underexplored area compared to language-centric applications of RL.
- **Emphasis on visual perception:** The authors emphasize the distinct characteristics of visual perception tasks compared to natural language tasks, leading to a revised understanding of RL's role in the context of visual perception.
- **Rule-based reward modeling:** The paper proposes a simple, effective, and scalable RL framework based on rule-based reward modeling and GRPO, inspired by successful language reasoning models.
- **Demonstrated performance on core vision tasks:** The paper achieves impressive results on challenging visual perception benchmarks, including object detection, showcasing the potential of RL for improving vision capabilities of MLLMs.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The key objectives of this research are:

- **Explore the potential of RL for perception policy learning in MLLMs:** The primary motivation is to bridge the gap between reasoning LLMs and the ability to interact with the world in a perceptive way through multimodal models.
- **Re-examine the role of RL in visual perception tasks:** The authors aim to understand how RL can be effectively utilized to improve visual perception, considering the distinct characteristics of vision compared to language.
- **Develop a scalable RL framework for perception policy learning:** The goal is to create a framework that can be efficiently applied to post-train MLLMs and improve their performance on various visual perception tasks.
- **Establish a strong baseline for perception policy learning:** The researchers aim to set a new standard for perception policy learning by achieving significant improvements on multiple visual perception benchmarks.
- **Provide insights into the design of reward functions and training strategies for RL-based perception learning.**
- **Stimulate future research:** The authors hope to inspire more research on perception policy learning with RL.

The motivation behind this research is to address the limitations of current LLMs, which are primarily focused on the linguistic domain and lack the ability to engage with the world in a truly perceptive way. By exploring the potential of RL for perception policy learning, the authors aim to create more intelligent and versatile MLLMs that can effectively understand and interact with the visual world.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology and approach of this research involve:

- **Rule-based Reinforcement Learning (RL):** The authors employ the GRPO algorithm, a rule-based RL technique that eliminates the need for a separate critic model. GRPO uses group relative rewards to optimize the policy.
- **Perception Policy Learning:** This research trains the models to extract visual information, perform reasoning on this information and act upon the environment.
- **Reward Modeling:** The authors design a reward system anchored in visual discrimination, using metrics such as IoU, Euclidean distance, and edit distance to provide objective feedback to the model.
- **Multi-Subject Reward Matching:** To handle scenarios with multiple objects, the authors employ the Hungarian algorithm to solve a bipartite graph matching problem, ensuring accurate reward assignment for each predicted attribute.
- **Task-Specific Training:** The authors conduct separate post-training for each visual perception task, using a subset of samples extracted from relevant datasets.
- **Curriculum Sampling:** The authors use a curriculum sampling strategy, starting with easier data and gradually transitioning to more challenging examples.
- **Experimental Evaluation:** The authors evaluate Perception-R1 on a range of mainstream visual perception tasks, including visual grounding, OCR, visual counting, and object detection. They compare the performance of Perception-R1 with existing models and conduct ablation studies to analyze the contributions of different components.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The main findings and results of this research are:

- **Perception-R1 achieves state-of-the-art (SoTA) performance on multiple visual perception benchmarks:** The proposed framework demonstrates significant improvements on RefCOCO, PixMo-Count, PageOCR, and COCO2017val.
- **Explicit thinking processes (CoT) are not necessary for current perception policy:** The model without a thinking process performs better than the one with a thinking process.
- **Reward design plays a pivotal role in perception policy learning:** An appropriate reward function leads to a healthier learning curve and explores stronger perceptual patterns of MLLMs.
- **Perceptual perplexity determines RL superiority over SFT:** RL can bring more significant improvement compared to SFT on more complex visual tasks, e.g., object detection.
- **Reward matching enhances the explorability of multi-subject visual perception.**
- **Scaling up rollout enhances reward optimization and final performance.**

**Specifically, Perception-R1 achieves:**

- +4.2% on RefCOCO+
- +17.9% on PixMo-Count
- +4.2% on PageOCR
- 31.9% AP on COCO2017val (first time for a pure MLLM)

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The significance and potential impact of this research are:

- **Pioneering work in RL for perception policy learning:** This paper represents a significant step towards enabling MLLMs to effectively understand and interact with the visual world.
- **New SOTA performance on challenging benchmarks:** The achieved results demonstrate the potential of RL for improving vision capabilities of MLLMs.
- **Improved visual perception:** The authors offer a new RL framework to improve computer vision, especially in MLLMs. This allows the models to be more accurate, robust, and efficient.
- **Scalable RL framework:** The proposed framework provides a practical and efficient approach for post-training MLLMs to improve their perception capabilities.
- **Insights into RL for visual perception:** The findings regarding the role of reward design, explicit thinking processes, and perceptual perplexity provide valuable insights for future research in this area.
- **Potential for broader applications:** The techniques developed in this research can be applied to a wide range of visual perception tasks, including object recognition, image understanding, and visual reasoning.

The potential impact of this research is substantial, as it can lead to the development of more intelligent and versatile MLLMs that can be used in various applications, such as robotics, autonomous driving, medical image analysis, and human-computer interaction. The achievement of surpassing the 30% AP threshold on the COCO 2017valset with a pure MLLM is particularly significant, as it demonstrates the potential of general foundation models to compete with specialized expert models in mainstream visual tasks. The insights provided by this research can guide future efforts in developing more refined reward functions and training strategies for RL-based perception learning, ultimately leading to more capable and robust MLLMs.
