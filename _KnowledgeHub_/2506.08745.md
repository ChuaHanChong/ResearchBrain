# Consistent Paths Lead to Truth: Self-Rewarding Reinforcement Learning for LLM Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Zhejiang University, Alibaba Cloud Computing, and Nanyang Technological University introduce CoVo, a self-rewarding reinforcement learning framework that enhances LLM reasoning by deriving intrinsic rewards from the consistency and volatility of intermediate reasoning states. This approach operates without external supervision, achieving performance comparable to supervised methods and outperforming other self-rewarding techniques across various reasoning benchmarks.

## Problem

- Existing reinforcement learning methods for improving LLM reasoning heavily depend on costly human-annotated labels or potentially biased pre-trained reward models, limiting scalability.
- Prior self-rewarding LLM approaches, which evaluate only the final answer, are susceptible to 'reward hacking,' where models learn superficial shortcuts without genuine reasoning improvement, leading to performance collapse.
- There is a need for a robust and scalable method to autonomously develop and refine LLM reasoning capabilities without external supervision.

## Method

- **CoVo**, a self-rewarding RL framework, defines an ==intrinsic reward== based on two trajectory features: 'Consistency' (proportion of intermediate states closest to the trajectory's own final answer) and 'Volatility' (deviation from the own final answer).
- It computes this intrinsic reward using a robust vectorial aggregation of consistency and volatility from multiple sampled trajectories for a given final answer.
- A curiosity reward, based on token probabilities between intermediate states, is integrated to encourage diverse exploration and prevent solution collapse, and the combined reward is used in a policy gradient framework like Reinforce++.

## Results

- CoVo achieves reasoning performance on par with or surpassing supervised RL methods (e.g., Qwen2.5-**3B**-Instruct achieves **68.2%** on MATH-500, outperforming GRPO's **67.4%**) and consistently outperforms other self-rewarding baselines.
- The framework demonstrates strong domain generalization, showing performance gains not only in mathematical reasoning but also in commonsense and scientific reasoning, despite being trained solely on mathematical data.
- Empirical analysis validates that correct reasoning trajectories exhibit significantly higher consistency and lower volatility (e.g., **0.832** consistency and **0.271** volatility for correct MATH-500 responses vs. **0.215** consistency and **0.821** volatility for incorrect ones).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The quality of an LLM's reasoning can be reliably assessed by analyzing the 'consistency' (convergence to its own final answer) and 'volatility' (deviation from its own final answer) of its intermediate reasoning steps.
> - Deriving intrinsic reward signals from these intermediate reasoning state features provides a more robust self-supervisory mechanism, mitigating 'reward hacking' issues common in self-rewarding methods that rely solely on final answer evaluation.
> - Combining this process-based intrinsic reward with a curiosity bonus encourages diverse and stable exploration of reasoning paths, leading to improved and generalized reasoning abilities without external supervision.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research directly addresses a critical challenge in the rapidly evolving field of large language models (LLMs) and their reasoning capabilities, particularly as enhanced by Reinforcement Learning (RL).

Historically, LLMs have shown impressive emergent reasoning abilities, especially when fine-tuned with RL, as demonstrated by works like DeepSeek-R1. However, a significant bottleneck for these RL approaches is their heavy reliance on **external supervision**. This supervision typically comes in two forms:
1. **Human-annotated labels:** Costly, time-consuming, and difficult to scale to diverse, open-ended reasoning tasks.
2. **Pretrained reward models:** Often suffer from biases or distributional mismatches, limiting their general applicability and robustness.

To circumvent these limitations, the concept of **self-rewarding LLMs** has emerged. Prior works in this area, such as TTRL and EMPO, attempt to estimate reward signals internally, typically by evaluating the final answers generated by the model (e.g., via majority voting over sampled responses or semantic clustering). While promising, these methods are susceptible to **reward hacking**, where the model learns superficial shortcuts to obtain high rewards without genuinely improving its reasoning quality (e.g., consistently outputting a common incorrect answer that happens to gain a majority vote). This leads to performance collapse as training progresses, as shown in the paper's analysis.

CoVo distinguishes itself by fundamentally shifting the focus of self-reward generation from **final answers** to **intermediate reasoning states**. The core insight is that the *process* of reasoning, not just the outcome, holds valuable self-supervisory signals. By analyzing the consistency and volatility of these intermediate states, CoVo aims to build a more robust and reliable self-rewarding mechanism. This approach positions CoVo as a significant advancement within the self-rewarding LLM paradigm, moving beyond superficial outcome-based evaluation to a deeper, process-oriented understanding of correct reasoning. It represents a step towards truly autonomous learning for reasoning LLMs, mitigating the scalability and reliability issues inherent in externally supervised or purely outcome-based self-rewarding systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this work is to **enhance the reasoning capabilities of Large Language Models (LLMs) through a novel self-rewarding reinforcement learning framework that operates without external supervision.**

The motivation for this objective stems from several critical challenges in current LLM reasoning advancements:
- **Scalability Limitations of External Supervision:** Existing RL-based methods for improving LLM reasoning, while effective, heavily depend on human-annotated labels or pre-trained reward models. Obtaining high-quality human labels for complex reasoning tasks is prohibitively expensive and time-consuming, severely limiting the scalability and applicability of these methods across diverse and emerging domains. Pre-trained reward models, on the other hand, often introduce biases or struggle with out-of-distribution scenarios.
- **Vulnerability of Current Self-Rewarding Methods to Reward Hacking:** While self-rewarding approaches have attempted to address the supervision bottleneck, most existing methods (e.g., TTRL, EMPO) assign rewards solely based on the final answers generated by the LLM. This "outcome-only" evaluation overlooks the critical intermediate steps of the reasoning trajectory. As a result, models can learn to exploit statistical patterns or superficial shortcuts that yield seemingly correct final answers without engaging in sound logical reasoning, leading to unreliable performance and "reward hacking."
- **Underexplored Potential of Intermediate Reasoning States:** The authors hypothesize that the *process* of reasoning itself, specifically the patterns observed in intermediate states of a reasoning trajectory, contains rich intrinsic signals about correctness. They observe that correct reasoning trajectories tend to be "consistent" (intermediate states converge towards the correct final answer) and "low volatility" (minimal deviation towards incorrect alternatives), whereas incorrect trajectories are erratic.
- **Need for Autonomous Reasoning Development:** To move towards more capable and generalizable LLMs, there is a strong need for frameworks that allow models to learn and refine their reasoning abilities autonomously, minimizing human intervention.

In summary, the key motivation is to overcome the limitations of external supervision and reward hacking in self-rewarding LLMs by leveraging the intrinsic properties of intermediate reasoning trajectories, thereby enabling a more scalable, robust, and autonomous development of LLM reasoning capabilities.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

CoVo (Consistency and Volatility), the proposed self-rewarding reinforcement learning framework, addresses the identified challenges by integrating an intrinsic reward mechanism derived from intermediate reasoning states with a curiosity-driven exploration bonus.

#### 3.1. Conceptualizing Reasoning Trajectories and Distance Matrix

The framework formalizes an LLM's reasoning process as a trajectory ($ \tau $) composed of discrete intermediate reasoning states ($ s_i $) and a final answer ($ y $). To analyze these trajectories, a "distance" is defined between an intermediate state ($ s_i $) and a potential final answer ($ y_k $) using the negative log-likelihood of generating $ y_k $ given $ s_i $.
$ d(s_i, y_k) = - \frac{1}{|y_k|} \sum_{j=1}^{|y_k|} \log \pi_\theta(y_k[j] | s_i, y_k[:j]) $
When multiple trajectories are sampled for a prompt, yielding $K$ distinct answers, a **Distance Matrix $D \in \mathbb{R}^{T \times K}$** is constructed for each trajectory, where $D[i,k]$ denotes the distance from $s_i$ to $y_k$. This matrix is central to deriving the intrinsic reward signals.

#### 3.2. Trajectory Features: Consistency and Volatility

Based on empirical observations, the paper identifies two crucial features that distinguish correct from incorrect reasoning trajectories:
- **Consistency ($ \text{Con}(\tau) $):** Measures the proportion of intermediate states ($ s_i $) within a trajectory that are "closest" (i.e., have the minimum distance) to the trajectory's *own* final answer ($ y $) compared to all other candidate answers ($ y' \neq y $).
 $ \text{Con}(\tau) = \frac{1}{T} \sum_{i=0}^{T-1} I(D[i,0] = \min_{0 \le k < K} D[i,k]) $
 High consistency indicates a deliberate and focused reasoning process.
- **Volatility ($ \text{Vol}(\tau) $):** Quantifies how much an intermediate state deviates from its *own* final answer, specifically identifying the *last* state where deviation occurred. It is defined as the ratio of this last deviating state's index to the total number of states.
 $ \text{Vol}(\tau) = \frac{1}{T} \max \{ i \in [0, T-1] \mid D[i,0] \neq \min_{0 \le k < K} D[i,k] \} $
 Lower volatility implies a more stable and less erratic reasoning path.

#### 3.3. Intrinsic Reward for Self-Evaluation

To convert these features into a reward signal, CoVo processes a batch of sampled trajectories. Trajectories are first grouped by their final answers. For each group (sharing the same final answer), the intrinsic reward is computed by aggregating the Consistency and Volatility of its constituent trajectories.
- **Linear Aggregation ($ r^L_{\text{int}} $):** A straightforward sum of ($ \text{Con}(\tau_i) - \text{Vol}(\tau_i) $) for all trajectories ($ \tau_i $) in a group, averaged. This is intuitive but susceptible to outliers.
 $ r^L_{\text{int}} = \frac{1}{G} \sum_{i=0}^{G-1} (\text{Con}(\tau_i) - \text{Vol}(\tau_i)) $
- **Vectorial Aggregation ($ r^V_{\text{int}} $):** This is CoVo's preferred approach for robustness. Each trajectory's (Consistency, Volatility) pair is encoded as a vector:
 $ v_i = \text{Con}(\tau_i) \cdot [\cos(\text{Vol}(\tau_i)), \sin(\text{Vol}(\tau_i))] $
 These vectors are then summed within a group ($ V_{\text{group}} = \sum v_i $), and the magnitude of the combined vector, averaged by group size, serves as the reward:
 $ r^V_{\text{int}} = \frac{1}{G} \|V_{\text{group}}\| $
 The vectorial approach is theoretically proven to be more robust to outliers while maintaining monotonicity with consistency and volatility changes.

#### 3.4. Curiosity Reward for Self-Exploration

To prevent the model from collapsing to homogeneous solutions and encourage diverse exploration, CoVo adds a curiosity bonus ($ r_{\text{cur}} $). This reward is based on the average token probabilities ($ d(s_i, s_{i+1}) $) of transitions between intermediate states, penalized by the KL divergence from a uniform distribution ($ p_{\text{KL}} $). This incentivizes the model to explore paths where its predictions are less certain.
$ r_{\text{cur}} = d(s_i, s_{i+1}) - p_{\text{KL}} $
The total reward for Reinforcement Learning is $ r_{\text{covo}} = r_{\text{int}} + r_{\text{cur}} $.

#### 3.5. Reinforcement Learning Algorithm

CoVo integrates these reward signals into a standard policy gradient framework. Specifically, Reinforce++ is used as the backbone RL algorithm, which combines clipped updates with normalized advantage values.

#### 3.6. Theoretical Analysis

The paper provides theoretical backing, interpreting CoVo's self-rewarding RL as performing approximate variational inference over latent reasoning trajectories. It also theoretically demonstrates how majority-voting-based reward models can lead to "model collapse" and offers a convergence analysis for CoVo, showing an inverse relationship between convergence time and the initial probability of a correct (consistent, low-volatility) trajectory under the base policy.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

CoVo's effectiveness is demonstrated through extensive experiments on diverse reasoning benchmarks and comparisons with various baselines.

#### 4.1. Performance Comparison (Accuracy)

- **Comparable to or Surpassing Supervised RL:** CoVo consistently achieves performance on par with or even surpasses supervised RL methods that rely on ground-truth labels for reward computation (e.g., GRPO, RLOO, Reinforce++).
 - **Llama3.2-3B-Instruct:** CoVo obtains 51.2% accuracy on MATH-500, comparable to GRPO's 51.8%. On CommonsenseQA, CoVo (74.4%) slightly outperforms Reinforce++ (74.1%).
 - **Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct:** CoVo achieves 68.2% on MATH-500, outperforming supervised methods like GRPO (67.4%) and RLOO (66.8%). It also shows strong performance across other benchmarks.
 - **Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct:** Even with increased model parameters, CoVo maintains competitive performance, securing 78.4% on MATH-500, slightly higher than GRPO (78.2%).
- **Outperforming Other Self-Rewarding Methods:** CoVo consistently outperforms other self-rewarding RL baselines (IRPO, EMPO, TTRL), highlighting the robustness and effectiveness of its intermediate-state-based reward mechanism.
- **Significant Improvement over In-Context Learning:** CoVo provides substantial gains over traditional in-context learning methods like Zero-shot CoT, Few-shot CoT, and CoT+SC@4, demonstrating the power of RL fine-tuning.

#### 4.2. Domain Generalization

Despite being trained solely on mathematical reasoning data (from Open-Reasoner-Zero), CoVo exhibits strong generalization capabilities. Performance gains are observed not only in mathematical benchmarks (GSM8K, AMC-23, Olympiad Bench) but also in commonsense reasoning (MMLU-Pro, CommonsenseQA) and science reasoning (GPQA) tasks. This indicates that the learned reasoning abilities effectively transfer to unseen general problem sets, highlighting the intrinsic nature of the acquired improvements.

#### 4.3. Reasoning Diversity

CoVo promotes more diverse reasoning strategies compared to rule-based RL methods. Visualizations (UMAP plots) show that CoVo's reasoning paths are more dispersed, in contrast to the concentrated clusters observed in GRPO. Quantitative metrics (Average Cosine Similarity, Entropy, Self-BLEU) further support this, with CoVo showing higher Entropy and lower ACS/Self-BLEU, indicating broader solution space exploration. This suggests that CoVo avoids collapsing into stereotypical or homogeneous solutions, a common problem in RL fine-tuning.

#### 4.4. Empirical Analysis of Trajectory Features

The initial empirical analysis, which motivated CoVo, is validated with detailed statistical data across diverse reasoning domains. Tables and 2D distribution plots clearly show distinct patterns:
- **Consistency:** Correct responses consistently exhibit significantly higher consistency (e.g., 0.832 for MATH-500) than incorrect responses (e.g., 0.215 for MATH-500).
- **Volatility:** Correct responses show substantially lower volatility (e.g., 0.271 for MATH-500) than incorrect ones (e.g., 0.821 for MATH-500).
These strong discrepancies validate Consistency and Volatility as reliable intrinsic indicators for distinguishing reasoning quality.

#### 4.5. Training Dynamics

CoVo's training process demonstrates greater stability and robustness against reward hacking compared to majority-voting-based reward signals. Its reward accuracy remains consistently high throughout training, whereas majority-voting methods can exhibit misleading or unstable reward signals, leading to performance collapse (as predicted by Proposition 1).

#### 4.6. Ablation Study

The ablation study confirms the complementary and positive contributions of both the intrinsic reward and the curiosity reward. The full CoVo framework (intrinsic + curiosity) consistently outperforms configurations using only one reward component. Furthermore, the vectorial aggregation strategy for the intrinsic reward ($ r^V_{\text{int}} $) is found to be superior to linear aggregation ($ r^L_{\text{int}} $), especially on mathematical and scientific tasks, supporting the claim of its enhanced robustness to sample noise.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

CoVo represents a significant step forward in the development of autonomous and highly capable large language models, particularly for complex reasoning tasks. Its potential impact spans several critical areas:

- **Scalability and Reduced Data Dependency:** By eliminating the need for costly human-annotated labels or pre-trained reward models, CoVo offers a highly scalable solution for improving LLM reasoning. This is crucial for developing and fine-tuning LLMs in new domains or for niche tasks where high-quality labeled data is scarce or non-existent. It democratizes access to advanced RL fine-tuning by significantly lowering the data annotation barrier.
- **Robustness to Reward Hacking:** The core innovation of leveraging intermediate reasoning states for reward generation makes CoVo inherently more robust to "reward hacking." Unlike methods that rely solely on final answers, CoVo evaluates the *process* of reasoning, making it harder for models to achieve high rewards through superficial shortcuts. This leads to more reliable and genuinely improved reasoning capabilities.
- **Autonomous LLM Development:** CoVo moves LLMs closer to truly autonomous learning for reasoning. By deriving reward signals directly from the model's internal reasoning patterns, it enables a self-improving loop where LLMs can refine their own logical processes, fostering more sophisticated and less brittle reasoning.
- **Generalizability Across Domains:** The demonstrated ability of CoVo, trained solely on mathematical reasoning, to generalize effectively to commonsense and scientific reasoning tasks is highly impactful. It suggests that the "consistent path to truth" principle captures fundamental aspects of logical thinking that transcend specific domains, paving the way for more universally capable reasoning LLMs.
- **Improved Interpretability (Implicitly):** While not explicitly an interpretability method, CoVo's focus on intermediate states and their consistency might indirectly contribute to more interpretable models. A model that consistently follows a logical path towards a correct answer is inherently more transparent than one that randomly arrives at a solution.
- **Applicability in Resource-Constrained Scenarios:** The efficiency gains from self-rewarding (avoiding external reward model training or extensive human labeling) make CoVo particularly relevant for applications in resource-constrained environments or for smaller LLMs, enabling them to achieve advanced reasoning on par with larger, more heavily supervised models.

**Limitations and Future Works:**
The authors acknowledge two key limitations and propose future research directions:
1. **Generalizability to Visual Language Models (VLMs):** The current framework focuses on textual reasoning. Extending CoVo to VLMs, which involve both linguistic and perceptual understanding, presents a challenging yet promising avenue.
2. **Effectiveness in Semi-Supervised Scenarios:** While fully self-supervised, exploring CoVo's performance and benefits in semi-supervised settings (where a limited amount of labels might be available) could further enhance its practical applicability and bridge the gap between purely unsupervised and fully supervised approaches.

In conclusion, CoVo offers a novel and effective paradigm for developing LLM reasoning. By grounding RL rewards in the inherent consistency and volatility of reasoning trajectories, it bypasses critical limitations of existing methods, paving the way for more scalable, robust, and autonomously learning AI systems.
