# MultiCrafter: High-Fidelity Multi-Subject Generation via Disentangled Attention and Identity-Aware Preference Alignment

> [!summary] Summary
> MultiCrafter, developed by researchers from Zhejiang University and Huawei Technologies, introduces a decoupled, two-stage training framework for high-fidelity multi-subject image generation. This approach resolves attention entanglement and aligns with human preferences, achieving a 28.3% relative improvement in Face-Sim score for multi-human generation and outperforming state-of-the-art methods across benchmarks.

## Problem

- Existing multi-subject text-to-image generation methods struggle with subject fidelity, exhibiting "attribute leakage" or "attention bleeding" between subjects.
- Current approaches use a "highly coupled training paradigm" with a single, indirect reconstruction loss, forcing a suboptimal compromise between subject fidelity and alignment with human aesthetic preferences.
- Online reinforcement learning has largely been unexplored for the specialized challenge of multi-subject driven generation, particularly in addressing multi-dimensional human preferences.

## Method

- A decoupled, two-stage training framework built on a ==Diffusion Transformer== (DiT) architecture, separating fidelity learning from preference alignment.
- Stage 1 (Fidelity-Focused Pre-training) introduces Identity-Disentangled Attention Regularization to explicitly guide attention to correct spatial regions, combined with Mixture-of-Experts LoRA (MoE-LoRA) to enhance capacity for diverse spatial layouts.
- Stage 2 (Identity-Preserving Preference Optimization - IPPO) employs an online reinforcement learning framework (GSPO) with a composite reward model that includes novel Multi-ID Alignment Reward (using Hungarian matching for human faces and DINOv2 for objects) to align with aesthetics and text-image fidelity while preserving identity.

## Results

- MultiCrafter achieved the highest overall score in comprehensive benchmarks, demonstrating state-of-the-art performance in multi-human and multi-object customized generation.
- For multi-human generation, it showed a **28.3%** relative improvement in Face-Sim score (**0.5284**) over the next-best method (XVerse at **0.4117**), alongside high DINO-I (**0.8294**) and CLIP-I (**0.8524**) scores.
- Ablation studies confirmed the effectiveness of Identity-Disentangled Attention Regularization in enhancing subject fidelity and MoE-LoRA in handling diverse spatial layouts, while IPPO improved aesthetics and text alignment without sacrificing fidelity.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decoupling the learning objectives of subject fidelity and human preference alignment into distinct training stages effectively mitigates the "proxy-objective mismatch" of coupled training.
> - Explicit spatial attention regularization is crucial for preventing attribute leakage and maintaining high subject fidelity in multi-subject generation.
> - Online reinforcement learning, with a carefully designed composite reward that includes a novel multi-ID alignment component, can stably optimize for complex, multi-dimensional human preferences while robustly preserving subject identities.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of text-to-image generation has experienced rapid advancements, particularly with the development and adoption of Diffusion Transformers (DiT). This progress has led to an increasing demand for personalized image generation, a category that includes the complex task of multi-subject image generation. Multi-subject generation involves synthesizing an image containing multiple user-provided subjects while maintaining their individual identities, adhering to the provided text prompt, and aligning with human aesthetic preferences.

Previous research efforts, largely based on In-Context-Learning (ICL) methods such as UNO and OmniGen, have demonstrated progress in adapting to transformer architectures and improving multi-subject generation quality. However, these methods have exhibited limitations, including suboptimal subject fidelity and issues like "attribute leakage" or "attention bleeding" where features from different subjects become entangled. The paper attributes these shortcomings to a "highly coupled training paradigm." In this paradigm, existing methods attempt to achieve both high subject fidelity and alignment with multi-dimensional human preferences simultaneously, relying on a single, indirect reconstruction loss during training. This approach is argued to compel the model to find a suboptimal compromise between these two distinct objectives.

Furthermore, current reinforcement learning (RL) techniques, such as Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO), Direct Preference Optimization (DPO), and Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) variants (e.g., Flow-GRPO, DanceGRPO, MixGRPO), have predominantly focused on basic text-to-image generation tasks. Their application to the more specialized and challenging domain of multi-subject driven generation, particularly with online reinforcement learning, has remained largely unexplored.

MultiCrafter positions itself within this landscape by addressing the identified limitations. It proposes a decoupled, two-stage training framework to separate the learning of subject fidelity from the alignment with human preferences. This approach aims to resolve the issues of attention entanglement and proxy-objective mismatch that constrain existing coupled training methods, thereby advancing the state of multi-subject personalized image generation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of MultiCrafter is to generate images that feature multiple user-provided subjects with high fidelity, ensure prompt consistency, and align with human aesthetic preferences. The motivation for this work stems from the identified challenges and limitations of existing multi-subject image generation methods.

The authors articulate two distinct and challenging objectives that current In-Context-Learning (ICL) based methods struggle to achieve simultaneously:
1. **High Subject Fidelity**: Accurately preserving the distinct visual characteristics of each user-provided subject within a single generated image.
2. **Alignment with Human Preferences**: Generating images that are aesthetically pleasing, semantically accurate, and precisely aligned with the textual prompt.

The core motivation for MultiCrafter is rooted in the argument that existing ICL-based methods, characterized by a "highly coupled training paradigm," are inherently limited in achieving these two objectives concurrently. The paper posits that relying on a single, indirect reconstruction loss for both goals forces models into a suboptimal compromise. This coupled learning objective leads to two major failures:

- **Failure in Subject Fidelity**: Current methods do not effectively resolve the spatial entanglement between subject features and their intended locations in the generated image. This leads to a phenomenon referred to as "attention bleeding," where the attention regions for different subjects become intertwined (as exemplified in Fig. 2 by methods like UNO). This entanglement results in attribute leakage, where characteristics from one subject erroneously transfer to another, significantly compromising individual subject fidelity.
- **Failure in Human Preference Alignment**: The single reconstruction loss used in current methods constitutes a "severe proxy-objective mismatch." A reconstruction loss is not designed to directly optimize for multi-dimensional human preferences, which encompass subjective qualities like aesthetic appeal, the semantic accuracy of the scene, and the precise alignment between the generated image and the textual prompt. Consequently, models trained with such a loss often fall short in delivering outputs that consistently meet human expectations across these various dimensions.

To address these limitations, MultiCrafter proposes a "divide-and-conquer" strategy. The key objectives of the proposed framework are:
- To decouple the learning process into two specialized stages: one focused on achieving subject fidelity and another on aligning with human preferences.
- In the fidelity-focused stage, to introduce a mechanism that explicitly resolves spatial entanglement and attention bleeding, thereby enhancing subject fidelity.
- In the preference-optimizing stage, to develop an online reinforcement learning framework that directly aligns the model with multi-dimensional human preferences while robustly preserving the subject fidelity established in the first stage.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

MultiCrafter employs a decoupled, two-stage training framework built upon the Flux model, a Diffusion Transformer (DiT) utilizing Flow Matching. This framework separates the objectives of achieving high subject fidelity and aligning with human preferences.

#### 3.1. Stage 1: Fidelity-Focused Pre-training

This stage focuses on enhancing subject fidelity by mitigating attribute leakage.

- **Identity-Disentangled Attention Regularization**:
 - The core observation is that "double blocks" within the Flux architecture are critical for determining the spatial layout of reference subjects. In existing methods, attention within these blocks for different subjects can become entangled, leading to attribute leakage.
 - To address this, an explicit positional supervision mechanism is introduced during training. For each reference subject, its latent features are processed to obtain a sequence of 1D tokens. An attention map (`m_i^k`) is computed from the `k`-th double block, representing the correlation between the `i`-th subject's query and the noisy image's latent tokens.
 - These attention maps from multiple double blocks are averaged and normalized to create a mean attention map (`M_hat_i`).
 - Ground-truth masks (`M_i`) corresponding to the precise spatial region of each subject in the generated image are obtained via pre-annotation. For human subjects, specifically facial regions are masked, and for objects, Florence-2 and SAM2 are used.
 - The Dice loss (`L_attn`) is applied to minimize the discrepancy between `M_hat_i` and `M_i`. This regularization explicitly guides the model's attention to concentrate on the correct spatial regions for each subject, thereby forcing spatial disentanglement and reducing attribute leakage.
 - The overall loss for this stage is `L = L_diff + λ * L_attn`, where `L_diff` is the standard Flow Matching loss and `λ` is a balancing factor.

- **Mixture-of-Experts LoRA (MoE-LoRA)**:
 - The task of mastering diverse spatial layouts (influenced by various prompts and subjects) is complex and can exceed the capacity of a standard Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) module.
 - To expand model capacity efficiently, an MoE-LoRA architecture is integrated into the output Feed-Forward Network (FFN) layers of Flux. Other layers use standard LoRA.
 - A lightweight gating network dynamically routes the input vector to a small subset (e.g., top-k=1) of `N_e` experts (e.g., `N_e=4`), each being an independent LoRA module. This allows different experts to specialize in handling different spatial layouts, alleviating the challenges posed by varied compositions.

#### 3.2. Stage 2: Identity-Preserving Preference Optimization (IPPO)

This stage refines the model to align with human preferences across aesthetics and text-image alignment, while preserving subject fidelity.

- **Online Reinforcement Learning Framework**:
 - The framework adapts MixGRPO, an efficient online reinforcement learning method that confines stochastic optimization to a sliding time window.
 - To address potential instability in GRPO when training MoE models (due to expert routing fluctuations), the more stable **Group Sequence Policy Optimization (GSPO)** is employed. GSPO replaces token-level policy ratios with a sequence-level policy ratio, reflecting the overall policy shift for the entire sequence within the optimization window, leading to more stable gradients. The model is fine-tuned using gradient ascent on the GSPO objective.

- **Composite Reward Model**:
 - A critical component of reinforcement learning, the reward model, is designed to evaluate generation quality across three dimensions. The total reward `R` for a generated image is a weighted sum: `R = w_text * R_text + w_aes * R_aes + w_id * R_id`.
 - `R_text` (Text Alignment Reward): Derived from a pre-trained CLIP model, measuring the cosine similarity between the text prompt embedding and the image embedding.
 - `R_aes` (Aesthetic Reward): Obtained from an aesthetic predictor such as HPSv2.
 - `R_id` (Multi-ID Alignment Reward): This novel reward is specifically designed to accurately measure multi-subject fidelity using the Hungarian matching algorithm.
 - **For human subjects**: A face detector extracts facial embeddings from reference images and all detected faces in the generated image. A pairwise cosine similarity matrix is constructed. The Hungarian algorithm then finds an optimal assignment matrix to maximize the total similarity between reference faces and generated faces, ensuring each face is matched at most once. This mechanism prevents "reward hacking" by deterring the generation of multiple "average faces."
 - **For object subjects**: Each reference object has an associated text prompt. Florence-2 and SAM2 are used to locate corresponding objects in the generated image. DINOv2 embeddings are computed for the segmented regions and the reference objects, and their cosine similarity provides the object fidelity score.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presented comprehensive experimental evaluations across multi-human and multi-object customized generation tasks, comparing MultiCrafter against several state-of-the-art methods including MS-Diffusion, MIP-Adapter, OmniGen, UNO, OmniGen2, DreamO, and XVerse.

#### 4.1. Experimental Setup

- **Resolution**: Generated images were 512x512 pixels, while reference images were 320x320. LoRA modules had a rank of 512. The MoE-LoRA used 4 experts, activating 1 per forward pass.
- **Reinforcement Learning**: Configured with 16 sampling steps, a window size of 2, a shift interval of 50, and a window stride of 1.
- **Datasets**:
 - **Multi-human**: A custom dataset of 200k cross-pair data points was curated from OpenHumanVid video datasets using an automated pipeline for reference image extraction, segmentation masks, and text prompt generation via Qwen2.5-VL. For RL, a collection of 80 celebrities and 80 non-celebrities (12,720 data points) was used, with 1,000 randomly selected as a benchmark.
 - **Multi-object**: The public MUSAR-Gen dataset was utilized, with Florence-2 and SAM for object localization, filtered by Qwen2.5-VL. A benchmark of 1,000 unseen samples was created.
- **Evaluation Metrics**:
 - **Text Fidelity**: CLIP-T (cosine similarity between CLIP embeddings of prompt and image).
 - **Aesthetics**: HPSv2 score.
 - **Subject Fidelity**: CLIP-I and DINO-I (cosine similarity between generated and reference images in CLIP and DINO spaces, respectively, after precise subject localization). For multi-human, Face Similarity (Face-Sim) using a face recognition model was additionally employed, combined with the Hungarian algorithm for multi-face assignment.

#### 4.2. Quantitative Comparison

As presented in Table 1, MultiCrafter achieved the highest overall score across the benchmarks.
- **Multi-human Generation**: MultiCrafter demonstrated a substantial lead in subject fidelity. It achieved the highest Face-Sim score (0.5284), representing a 28.3% relative improvement over the next-best method (XVerse at 0.4117). High scores were also observed for DINO-I (0.8294) and CLIP-I (0.8524). The model remained competitive in CLIP-T (0.2753). While its aesthetic score (0.2915) was not the highest among all methods, subsequent analysis attributed this to the quality of the open-source training data rather than an inherent model limitation.
- **Multi-object Generation**: MultiCrafter consistently ranked among the top performers, achieving the highest scores in CLIP-T (0.3380) and DINO-I (0.7824), and the second-highest in aesthetics (0.2748).

These results indicate that the decoupled framework successfully balances subject fidelity with text-image alignment and aesthetics across diverse generation tasks.

#### 4.3. Qualitative Comparison

Qualitative examples (Figure 4, 7-12) illustrate MultiCrafter's ability to preserve subject identity effectively. For multi-human generation, MultiCrafter visibly avoided attribute confusion and leakage, even for subjects of the same gender or in complex interactive scenarios, a common failure mode for ICL-based methods like UNO, OmniGen, and OmniGen2. For multi-object generation, the method maintained good subject fidelity and overall image quality. The ability to generate complex scenes with accurate multi-subject integration was also demonstrated.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies

Detailed ablation studies validated the effectiveness of each component of the MultiCrafter framework, primarily using multi-human customization metrics.
- **Identity-Disentangled Attention Regularization (`L_attn`)**: Introducing `L_attn` with a single LoRA module resulted in a substantial improvement in subject fidelity. For instance, the Face-Sim score increased dramatically from a baseline UNO's 0.1474 to 0.4983. This regularization effectively disentangled attention regions for different subjects (Figure 2). However, it initially led to a decrease in text alignment and struggled with generating complex spatial layouts (e.g., "a teddy bear riding on a motorcycle," Figure 5) when using a single LoRA.
- **MoE-LoRA**: Integrating the MoE-LoRA architecture significantly mitigated the limitations observed with single LoRA. It further boosted subject fidelity, *and* crucially, improved text alignment. Qualitative examples (Figure 5, 6) demonstrated its capacity to correctly generate previously challenging combinatorial spatial layouts. Analysis of expert routing (Supplementary Material Figure 5) confirmed that different experts were activated for distinct spatial prompts, supporting the hypothesis that MoE-LoRA effectively learns to handle diverse spatial scenarios.
- **Identity-Preserving Preference Optimization (IPPO)**: The introduction of IPPO in the post-training stage demonstrably improved text alignment and aesthetic metrics while simultaneously enhancing subject fidelity. This confirmed its efficacy in aligning the model with multi-dimensional human preferences without compromising the fidelity established in the pre-training phase.

#### 4.5. Impact of Dataset Quality (Supplementary Material)

An analysis revealed that the aesthetic scores of MultiCrafter and a retrained UNO baseline were influenced by the aesthetic quality distribution of the custom-curated training data, which had a relatively low mean HPSv2 score. Critically, IPPO was shown to successfully recover and improve the aesthetic quality despite the suboptimal underlying training data, reaching levels comparable to official benchmarks.

#### 4.6. User Study

A user study (Supplementary Material Figure 4) corroborated the quantitative findings. MultiCrafter achieved the highest scores in subject fidelity, realism, and overall quality, and performed strongly in text alignment and aesthetics compared to five state-of-the-art baselines. This indicated that the decoupled training framework aligns well with human preferences.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

MultiCrafter introduces a framework that addresses a fundamental limitation in multi-subject personalized image generation: the suboptimal performance resulting from the coupled optimization of subject fidelity and human preferences using a single loss function. By decoupling these objectives into distinct training stages, the work provides a systematic approach to improve both aspects.

The **significance** of this work is demonstrated through several key contributions:
- **Enhanced Subject Fidelity**: The Identity-Disentangled Attention Regularization, combined with MoE-LoRA, effectively resolves "attention bleeding" and spatial entanglement. This leads to a substantial improvement in preserving the unique visual characteristics of individual subjects, a critical requirement for personalized generation. The quantitative results, particularly the commanding lead in Face-Sim scores for multi-human generation, underscore this achievement.
- **Targeted Preference Alignment**: The introduction of Identity-Preserving Preference Optimization (IPPO), incorporating the novel Multi-ID Alignment Reward and stable GSPO, represents the first online reinforcement learning framework tailored for multi-subject generation. This allows for direct and stable alignment with multi-dimensional human preferences (aesthetics, text-image alignment) without sacrificing subject identity.
- **Robustness to Diversity**: The integration of MoE-LoRA expands the model's capacity to handle the wide variety of spatial layouts and scenarios encountered in multi-subject generation, enhancing the framework's robustness and versatility.
- **State-of-the-Art Performance**: The comprehensive experimental validation against existing methods confirms MultiCrafter's superior performance, achieving state-of-the-art results in overall evaluation and particularly in subject fidelity across both multi-human and multi-object generation tasks.

The **potential impact** of MultiCrafter extends to various applications requiring high-quality, personalized visual content generation. This includes:
- **Content Creation**: Facilitating the generation of complex scenes featuring multiple specific individuals or objects for media, advertising, and entertainment, with greater control and fidelity.
- **Personalized AI Avatars and Virtual Characters**: Improving the creation of virtual representations that accurately retain the identity of multiple users within a shared digital space.
- **Image Editing and Synthesis**: Providing a more precise tool for intricate image manipulation tasks involving multiple subjects, where identity preservation is paramount.

Looking forward, the identified limitations, such as the scale and quality of publicly available multi-subject training datasets and the validation primarily on two-subject scenarios, indicate clear avenues for future research. The framework's design, particularly the attention regularization and the Hungarian algorithm-based reward, is noted to be scalable to scenarios with more subjects, suggesting future work could explore and validate its performance in more complex multi-entity compositions. The methodology offers a foundation for developing more capable and robust multi-subject generation systems.
