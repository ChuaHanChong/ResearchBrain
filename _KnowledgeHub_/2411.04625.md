# Sharp Analysis for KL-Regularized Contextual Bandits and RLHF

> [!summary] Summary
> This work presents a sharp sample complexity analysis for KL-regularized contextual bandits and Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF), demonstrating that KL-regularization can improve sample complexity from an O(1/ϵ2)O(1/\epsilon^2)O(1/ϵ2) rate to an O(1/ϵ)O(1/\epsilon)O(1/ϵ) rate. Researchers at UCLA and University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign developed a two-stage mixed sampling algorithm that achieves this efficiency while showing an additive dependence on a novel data coverage coefficient.

## Problem

- The theoretical understanding of KL-regularization's impact on sample complexity in RL/RLHF was limited, often yielding O(1/ϵ2)O(1/\epsilon^2)O(1/ϵ2) bounds similar to unregularized methods, despite its practical benefits.
- The quantitative influence of the reference policy's coverage on the sample complexity of online RLHF algorithms had not been extensively characterized.
- Existing theoretical analyses for policy optimization with regularization often relied on strong assumptions (e.g., exact gradients, uniform value approximation errors) not fully reflecting general sample-based RL settings.

## Method

- Defined the KL-regularized contextual bandit problem and extended it to RLHF with preference feedback using the ==Bradley-Terry== model, incorporating a novel data coverage condition (quantified by D2D^2D2).
- Proposed a Two-stage Mixed-Policy Sampling (TMPS) algorithm that first collects data using a reference policy, then refines it using an intermediate learned policy to improve reward function estimation.
- Developed a novel suboptimality decomposition technique that leverages the strong convexity induced by KL-regularization, directly relating suboptimality to the squared error of the estimated reward function.

## Results

- Established a lower bound for sample complexity of Ω(min⁡(η/ϵ,1/ϵ2)log⁡NR(ϵ))\Omega(\min(\eta / \epsilon, 1/\epsilon^2) \log \mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{R}}(\epsilon))Ω(min(η/ϵ,1/ϵ2)logNR​(ϵ)) for both KL-regularized contextual bandits and RLHF, suggesting potential O(1/ϵ)O(1/\epsilon)O(1/ϵ) efficiency.
- The TMPS algorithm achieved an upper bound of O(max⁡(η2D2,η/ϵ)log⁡NR(ϵ/δ))O(\max(\eta^2 D^2, \eta/\epsilon) \log \mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{R}}(\epsilon/\delta))O(max(η2D2,η/ϵ)logNR​(ϵ/δ)) for contextual bandits and O(max⁡(η2D2,η/ϵ)eBlog⁡NR(ϵ/δ))O(\max(\eta^2 D^2, \eta/\epsilon) e^B \log \mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{R}}(\epsilon/\delta))O(max(η2D2,η/ϵ)eBlogNR​(ϵ/δ)) for RLHF.
- These bounds confirm the O(1/ϵ)O(1/\epsilon)O(1/ϵ) sample complexity when ϵ\epsilonϵ is sufficiently small, demonstrating a quantifiable improvement over prior O(1/ϵ2)O(1/\epsilon^2)O(1/ϵ2) analyses and highlighting an additive dependence on the data coverage coefficient D2D^2D2.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - KL-regularization can fundamentally shift the theoretical sample complexity from O(1/ϵ2)O(1/\epsilon^2)O(1/ϵ2) to O(1/ϵ)O(1/\epsilon)O(1/ϵ) for achieving an ϵ\epsilonϵ-suboptimality gap in contextual bandits and RLHF.
> - The introduced data coverage coefficient D2D^2D2 allows for an *additive* dependence on coverage in the sample complexity bounds, indicating that strong reference policy coverage can provide efficiency gains without significant multiplicative overhead.
> - The two-stage mixed sampling approach efficiently collects data that is well-aligned with the evolving policy, enabling the achievement of these sharper theoretical bounds in practice.
