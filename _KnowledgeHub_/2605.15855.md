# Do Less, Achieve More: Do We Need Every-Step Optimization for RL Fine-tuning of Diffusion Models?

> [!summary] Summary
> AdaScope is an RL-enhanced plug-in for diffusion models that adaptively scopes Reinforcement Learning fine-tuning to optimal denoising stages, leading to a 59% reduction in computational cost while improving generation quality by mitigating issues like temporal misalignment and reward hacking.

## Problem

- Diffusion models are primarily optimized for data reconstruction, limiting their ability to align with higher-level, task-specific, or human-centric preferences.
- Mainstream RL fine-tuning applies a single final reward signal uniformly across all denoising steps, causing temporal misalignment, high variance in early stages, and reward hacking in late stages.
- The uniform application of RL across the entire denoising trajectory incurs substantial computational overhead and inefficient policy updates.

## Method

- **AdaScope** is developed as an RL-enhanced plug-in that adaptively selects optimal ==training intervals== during the diffusion model's denoising process.
- A theoretical analysis of timestep correlations is provided, showing that uncertainty in latent variables monotonically decreases, justifying varying RL impact across stages.
- Adaptive mechanisms determine the start of RL fine-tuning based on semantic-structure stabilization (ΔSta S_tΔSt) and the end based on reward-gain saturation (ΔPta P_tΔPt).

## Results

- AdaScope reduces computational costs by **59%** while enhancing overall performance by **66%**, achieving superior sample efficiency and faster convergence to target reward scores.
- The method consistently improves generation quality across various metrics, including semantic alignment, aesthetic appeal, and diversity, as supported by t-SNE analysis, Silhouette Coefficient, and human/VLM evaluations.
- Effectiveness is demonstrated across multiple Stable Diffusion backbones (SDv1.4, v1.5, v2.1-turbo, SDXL) and diverse reward objectives, showcasing strong generalizability.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The impact and utility of RL optimization for diffusion models vary significantly across denoising stages, with early stages being noisy and late stages prone to diminishing returns and reward hacking.
> - Focusing RL optimization exclusively on 'high-value' intervals—where semantic structures are stable and reward gains are pronounced—is crucial for improving both generation quality and computational efficiency.
> - Dynamically perceiving trends in semantic-structure alignment and preference reward gain provides effective signals for adaptively starting and ending RL intervention.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Diffusion models have emerged as a prominent framework for general-purpose image generation, demonstrating capabilities in distribution modeling and achieving high-quality visual outputs across various domains such as vision, speech, and 3D generation. These models primarily operate by progressively denoising a latent representation to approximate a target data distribution. However, their foundational optimization objective is centered on data reconstruction. This design inherently limits their ability to align with higher-level, task-specific, or human-centric preferences, such as aesthetic qualities or specific user intentions, which often extend beyond mere data fidelity.

To address this limitation, recent research has explored the integration of Reinforcement Learning (RL) for fine-tuning diffusion models. RL allows the direct incorporation of explicit reward signals, derived from human feedback or preference models, to guide the generation process toward goal-oriented optimization. This paradigm shifts the focus from reconstruction-based optimization to preference-based optimization, aiming to achieve task-level alignment.

Despite the potential of RL fine-tuning, several challenges persist within the existing framework. A primary issue is the sparse nature of reward signals, as rewards are typically computable only on the final, fully generated image. To enable policy optimization, mainstream approaches frequently backpropagate this single final reward signal to all preceding denoising steps. While this strategy partially mitigates the sparse feedback problem and ensures RL functions across the entire trajectory, it introduces several complications:
- **Temporal Misalignment and High Variance**: Early denoising stages involve rapid structural changes and are dominated by noise. Assigning the final reward to these stages can lead to a weak correlation between actions and their perceived rewards, resulting in high variance and inefficient updates.
- **Reward Hacking**: As training progresses and images stabilize in later denoising stages, the marginal gains from further RL optimization diminish. Continued training in these stages can lead to overfitting to local details or exploiting superficial cues within the reward function, a phenomenon known as "reward hacking," where the model maximizes the reward without genuinely improving quality or aligning with true preferences.
- **Computational Overhead**: The uniform application of RL across the entire denoising trajectory, coupled with the inefficiencies arising from temporal misalignment and diminishing returns, incurs substantial computational costs.

This research aims to systematically address these challenges by re-evaluating the necessity of "every-step optimization" for RL fine-tuning in diffusion models. It proposes an adaptive strategy to identify and focus optimization on the most impactful stages of the denoising process, seeking to improve both generation quality and computational efficiency.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to enhance the effectiveness and efficiency of Reinforcement Learning (RL) fine-tuning for diffusion models, specifically addressing limitations associated with applying RL uniformly across the entire denoising trajectory. The authors are motivated by the observation that the impact and utility of RL optimization vary significantly across different denoising stages.

The key motivations and objectives can be detailed as follows:

- **Mitigate Temporal Misalignment and Inefficient Updates in Early Denoising Stages**:
 - **Motivation**: In the initial phases of the denoising process, the image latent representations are highly noisy and lack stable semantic structures. Assigning the final, global reward to these early steps creates a "temporal misalignment" because the actions taken at these stages are distantly correlated with the ultimate reward. Standard reward backpropagation, while providing a signal, often leads to high variance in gradient estimations and inefficient policy updates due to the chaotic nature of the early stages and the weak causal link to the final outcome.
 - **Objective**: To adaptively identify and skip these early, unstable denoising stages where structural formation is incomplete and noise predominates. By intervening only when semantic structures begin to stabilize, the goal is to alleviate action-reward attribution mismatch and promote more stable and effective policy gradient estimation.

- **Counteract Reward Hacking and Diminishing Returns in Late Denoising Stages**:
 - **Motivation**: In the latter stages of denoising, the generated image's structural and feature representations become highly stable, and the model has nearly converged to a clean image. At this point, the marginal benefit of further RL training for optimizing the final preference reward becomes minimal. Continued optimization in these saturated stages can lead to "reward hacking," where the model overfits to local details or superficial aspects of the reward function, generating images that score highly on the reward metric but deviate from true human preferences, potentially reducing realism, diversity, or overall quality.
 - **Objective**: To dynamically terminate RL fine-tuning once the denoising process converges and marginal reward gains saturate. This aims to suppress low-return training intervals and prevent the amplification of reward hacking tendencies by avoiding unnecessary overfitting to the reward model.

- **Reduce Computational Cost**:
 - **Motivation**: The issues of temporal misalignment in early stages and diminishing returns/reward hacking in late stages not only degrade the quality of generated images but also lead to substantial waste of computational resources. Optimizing every step of the denoising process without regard for its current state or potential return results in inefficient updates and prolonged training times.
 - **Objective**: To achieve a "dual benefit" by significantly reducing computational overhead alongside improving generation quality. This is pursued by focusing RL optimization exclusively on "high-value" intervals where semantic structures are stable and training returns are high, thereby avoiding inefficient or counterproductive updates.

In essence, the paper proposes **AdaScope** as a solution to these challenges, with the overarching objective of developing an RL-enhanced plug-in that adaptively scopes the application of RL fine-tuning to the most critical denoising stages, resulting in both improved generation performance and reduced computational expenditure.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of this work is AdaScope, an RL-enhanced plug-in designed to adaptively select optimal training intervals during the diffusion model's denoising process. The methodology comprises a formal problem formulation, theoretical grounding for varying timestep correlations, and an adaptive mechanism for determining the start and end points of RL intervention.

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation

The denoising process of a text-to-image diffusion model is formulated as a Markov Decision Process (MDP).
- **States ($s_t$)**: Represent the intermediate image states $(z, x_{T-t})$ during denoising, where $z$ is the text prompt and $x_{T-t}$ is the latent image at reverse timestep $T-t$.
- **Actions ($a_t$)**: Correspond to the denoised latent representation $x_{T-t-1}$, which is the result of noise prediction and new noise sampling at timestep $t$.
- **Policy ($\pi_\theta$)**: The parameterized policy $p_\theta(x_{T-t-1} | x_{T-t}, z)$ that governs the transition from one latent state to the next.
- **Reward ($R(s_t, a_t)$)**: In its original sparse form, the reward is only obtained at the final timestep ($t = T-1$), where $r(x_0, z)$ is the preference score of the fully generated image $x_0$. For all other intermediate steps, the reward is zero.
- **Existing Compromise ($R_{real}(s_t, a_t)$)**: Mainstream RL fine-tuning methods address the sparse reward problem by backpropagating the final reward $r(x_0, z)$ to all denoising steps, treating it as a constant reward for every action. This aims to provide consistent optimization signals but, as the authors argue, leads to temporal misalignment.

The objective of RL fine-tuning remains to maximize the expected reward of generated images under the prompt distribution: $\max_\theta E_{p(z)} E_{p_\theta(x_0|z)} [r(x_0, z)]$.

#### 3.2. Adjoint Pearson Correlation View of RL-Diffusion Interaction

To provide a theoretical basis for the varying impact of RL across denoising stages, the authors analyze the correlation between latent variables at different timesteps.
- **Theorem 1 (Forward–Reverse Consistency)**: This theorem establishes that the probability distributions and joint distributions of latent variables in the forward noising process are equivalent to those in a perfectly trained reverse (generative) process. This implies that analyzing correlations in the forward process can inform understanding of the generative process.
- **Theorem 2 (Correlation Coefficient Between Timesteps)**: Provides a formula for the correlation coefficient between components of latent variables $x_t$ and $x_{t+\tau}$ in both forward and reverse processes. This formula highlights the dependency on noise schedules ($\bar{\alpha}_t$) and the covariance of the initial data ($\Sigma$).
- **Lemma 1**: Based on Theorem 2, this lemma states that for typical diffusion schedules, $1 - \text{Corr}(x_t^{(i)}, x_{t+\tau}^{(i)})$ is a decreasing function during the generation process. This "uncertainty score" implies that as denoising progresses (from $T$ to $0$), the uncertainty in latent variables monotonically decreases, meaning early steps are chaotic and noisy, while late steps are stable. This lemma provides theoretical justification for the empirical observation that different denoising stages have distinct characteristics regarding structural stability and information content (as visually represented in Figure 1).

#### 3.3. Adaptive RL Scope Selection

AdaScope employs two mechanisms to dynamically determine the optimal start and end points for RL fine-tuning:

#### 3.3.1. Determining the Start of RL Training via Semantic-Structure Trend Perception

This mechanism identifies when the semantic structure of the image begins to stabilize, making it suitable for RL intervention.
- **Structural Gain ($\Delta S_t$)**: The authors define $f(x_t) = \text{CLIP}(\hat{x}_0(x_t), z)$ as a measure of semantic-structure alignment, where $\hat{x}_0(x_t)$ is the reconstructed clean image from latent $x_t$ using the model's noise prediction, and $z$ is the prompt. $\Delta S_t = f(x_{t-1}) - f(x_t)$ quantifies the change in semantic alignment between adjacent denoising steps.
- **Start Point ($t_{start}$)**: RL training commences at the first timestep $t_{start}$ where the rate of change of $\Delta S_t$ approaches zero ($\lim_{\Delta t \to 0} \frac{\Delta S_{t+\Delta t} - \Delta S_t}{\Delta t} \to 0$). This signifies that the semantic structure under the given prompt $z$ has largely formed and stabilized, reducing the reward-attribution mismatch that would occur in earlier chaotic stages.

#### 3.3.2. Determining the End of RL Training via Reward-Gain Trend Perception

This mechanism identifies when further RL optimization yields diminishing returns, signaling the appropriate time to terminate training.
- **Preference Gain ($\Delta P_t$)**: The authors define $g(x_t) = \text{Reward}(\hat{x}_0(x_t), z)$ as the preference score (e.g., aesthetic or human preference) of the reconstructed clean image from latent $x_t$. $\Delta P_t = g(x_{t-1}) - g(x_t)$ measures the change in preference score between adjacent denoising steps.
- **End Point ($t_{end}$)**: RL training terminates at the first timestep $t_{end}$ where the rate of change of $\Delta P_t$ approaches zero ($\lim_{\Delta t \to 0} \frac{\Delta P_{t+\Delta t} - \Delta P_t}{\Delta t} \to 0$). This indicates that the preference optimization for the given prompt has reached saturation, and further fine-tuning is unlikely to yield significant improvements, thereby preventing overfitting and reward hacking.

The adaptive RL fine-tuning interval is thus defined as $[t_{start}, t_{end}]$. This interval is designed to focus optimization on the region where semantic structures are stable and reward gains are pronounced, concurrently minimizing computational overhead and enhancing fine-tuning quality.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents AdaScope as an effective plug-in for RL fine-tuning of diffusion models, demonstrating improvements in both computational efficiency and generation quality across various experimental settings.

#### 4.1. Plugin Computational Efficiency

- **Overall Cost Reduction**: AdaScope consistently reduces computational costs across different state-of-the-art (SOTA) baselines. Specifically, it achieves a reported reduction in computational cost by 59% while improving performance by 66% (as stated in the abstract).
- **Time per Batch (Time-PB)**: When integrated with baselines like DDPO, DPOK, D3PO, and TDPO, AdaScope significantly lowers the time required per training batch. For instance, DDPO's Time-PB of 4.55 minutes is reduced to 2.65 minutes with AdaScope.
- **Time to Reach Specific Reward Scores**: AdaScope enables models to achieve target aesthetic or PickScore levels (e.g., Aesthetic Score=7 or PickScore=22) in less time. For example, DDPO+Ours reaches Aesthetic Score=7 in 5.16 hours compared to DDPO's 12.3 hours.

#### 4.2. Sample Efficiency for Reward Learning

- AdaScope demonstrates superior sample efficiency, achieving improved reward learning with fewer optimization steps. This is attributed to its strategy of focusing on the "moderate uncertain stage" and avoiding over-uncertain or over-certain intermediate samples, leading to a "Do Less, Achieve More" outcome.

#### 4.3. Transfer Study

- **Multiple Backbones**: AdaScope's effectiveness is demonstrated across a range of Stable Diffusion backbones, including SDv1.4, SDv1.5, SDv2.1-turbo, and SDXL. The method consistently shows improved performance, although gains on SDXL were noted as less pronounced, potentially due to the inherent robustness of larger models.
- **Multiple Reward Objectives**: The plug-in maintains promising effectiveness when applied with diverse reward objectives, such as JPEG compressibility, incompressibility, and a combined Aesthetic Score + PickScore (AES+PS) for multi-objective assessments.

#### 4.4. Ablation Study

- The ablation study compared AdaScope's dynamic approach to fixed-scope optimization strategies. Variants with fixed starting and ending steps, or ranges around these averages, generally underperformed AdaScope. This indicates that the adaptive selection of the RL optimization interval, tailored to specific prompts and denoising dynamics, is crucial for achieving optimal performance.

#### 4.5. Distribution Visualization

- **t-SNE Analysis**: Using t-SNE to visualize DINOv2 features of generated images, AdaScope's outputs exhibit a broader and more diverse distribution in the feature space compared to baselines (e.g., DDPO). Baselines tend to produce more compact clusters, suggesting reduced diversity and potential reward hacking.
- **Silhouette Coefficient (SC)**: Quantitative analysis with SC confirmed that baselines show higher SC, indicating denser clusters and reduced diversity, while AdaScope maintains a wider generative distribution. This finding supports the claim that AdaScope mitigates reward hacking and prevents excessive diversity loss.

#### 4.6. Qualitative Evaluation

- **Semantic Alignment**: Visual results (Figure 9) indicate that AdaScope achieves improved semantic alignment in terms of composition, color, object count, and location when evaluated against specific prompts.
- **Complex Unseen Prompts**: For complex, real-world prompts, AdaScope's generated images (Figure 10) demonstrate superior alignment and aesthetic impression compared to other SOTA methods, particularly with the SDXL backbone.
- **Generation Diversity**: Visual comparisons (Figure 13 in supplementary material) show that AdaScope generates images with greater structural and textural diversity (e.g., posture, color, form, style) while maintaining prompt-image alignment, further supporting its ability to mitigate overfitting and reward hacking.
- **Subjective Study**: Human and Vision-Language Model (VLM) evaluations (Figure 12 in supplementary material) across metrics like Structural Faithfulness, Aesthetic Appeal, Fine-grained Detail, Semantic Alignment, and Prompt Responsiveness consistently rank AdaScope's generated images higher.

In summary, AdaScope demonstrates a dual benefit by significantly improving generation quality across various metrics and backbones, while concurrently reducing the computational resources required for RL fine-tuning by adaptively focusing optimization on the most impactful denoising stages.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of AdaScope presents several significant implications for the field of generative AI, particularly concerning the fine-tuning of diffusion models with Reinforcement Learning.

- **Enhanced Efficiency in RL Fine-tuning**: A primary impact is the significant reduction in computational cost, reported as a 59% cut, while simultaneously improving performance. This addresses a critical barrier to the widespread adoption and scalability of RL-based fine-tuning for large diffusion models, making such methods more accessible and practical for researchers and developers with limited computational resources. By making training more efficient, it enables faster iteration cycles and potentially larger-scale experimentation.

- **Improved Generation Quality and Robustness**: AdaScope directly tackles known challenges in RL fine-tuning: temporal misalignment, high variance in early stages, and reward hacking in late stages. By adaptively identifying and focusing on "high-value" denoising intervals, it fosters more stable policy learning, reduces overfitting to reward models, and preserves generative diversity. This leads to higher-quality images that better align with human preferences and prompt instructions, as evidenced by improved aesthetic scores, semantic alignment, and reduced diversity loss.

- **Generalizability and Plug-and-Play Compatibility**: The design of AdaScope as a plug-in, demonstrated to be effective across diverse Stable Diffusion backbones (SDv1.4, v1.5, v2.1-turbo, SDXL) and various reward objectives (Aesthetic Score, PickScore, multi-objective rewards), highlights its broad applicability. This modularity suggests that AdaScope can be readily integrated into existing and future RL fine-tuning pipelines without requiring extensive modifications, thereby accelerating progress in the field.

- **Deeper Understanding of Diffusion Dynamics**: The theoretical analysis of timestep correlations and the derivation of Lemma 1 (describing decreasing uncertainty during generation) contribute to a more profound understanding of the internal dynamics of diffusion models. This theoretical foundation supports the empirical observations and provides a scientific basis for adaptive optimization strategies, potentially informing future research into the architecture and training of generative models.

- **Mitigation of Reward Hacking**: The evidence from t-SNE visualizations and Silhouette Coefficient analysis suggests that AdaScope helps in maintaining a broader generative distribution and reducing the tendency of models to overfit the reward function. This contributes to the development of more robust and aligned generative models that produce outputs genuinely reflecting desired qualities rather than merely exploiting superficial reward signals.

- **Future Research Directions**: AdaScope's success in adaptive optimization may inspire further exploration into dynamic, state-aware training methodologies for generative models. This could extend beyond RL fine-tuning to other training paradigms, prompting a re-evaluation of fixed, uniform optimization schedules across sequential processes. The approach of perceiving structural evolution and semantic consistency could be adapted to other generative tasks or model types.

In conclusion, AdaScope offers an efficient and scalable solution for aligning diffusion models with human preferences, contributing to both the practical utility and theoretical understanding of RL in generative AI. Its dual benefits of reduced cost and enhanced quality mark a notable advancement in the fine-tuning of text-to-image diffusion models.
