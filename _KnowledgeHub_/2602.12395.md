# What does RL improve for Visual Reasoning? A Frankenstein-Style Analysis

> [!summary] Summary
> This study provides a mechanistic analysis of how Reinforcement Learning (RL) improves Vision-Language Models (VLMs) for visual reasoning tasks. It reveals that RL's primary effect is a focused refinement of mid-to-late transformer layers, which in turn enhances vision-to-reasoning alignment and overall reasoning capabilities.

## Problem

- Despite empirical gains from Reinforcement Learning (RL) in Vision-Language Models (VLMs), the specific capabilities RL enhances for visual reasoning remain ambiguous.
- End-to-end benchmark evaluations conflate various factors, making it difficult to attribute performance improvements to distinct skills like visual perception or language-side reasoning.
- Fine-grained metrics for vision ability, reasoning ability, and vision-to-reasoning alignment do not consistently show monotonic improvements from supervised fine-tuning to RL.

## Method

- Proposing a =="Frankenstein-style analysis framework"== to mechanistically dissect VLM behavior and causally trace RL's alterations across model architectures.
- Localizing vision and reasoning-related computations within transformer layers through ==causal probing== using ==vision-token interventions== and ==layer-wise causal ablations==.
- Characterizing parameter updates from supervised fine-tuning and RL by analyzing their energy and diversity using ==Frobenius norms== and ==singular value spectra==, and validating their causal importance and transferability via ==model merging== and ==freezing experiments==.

## Results

- End-to-end benchmark accuracy improves monotonically from base to RL models, but fine-grained vision ability and standalone reasoning ability do not show consistent monotonic improvements.
- Vision computations are predominantly localized in early and mid-transformer layers, while reasoning computations are concentrated in late layers.
- Hybrid models incorporating RL-refined mid-to-late layers show improvements in vision-to-reasoning alignment and reasoning versus SFT models, and freezing late layers during RL training causes substantial degradation in both, confirming their causal necessity.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - RL consistently induces an increased attention from reasoning tokens to visual tokens, concentrated specifically in the mid-to-late transformer layers.
> - RL applies less diverse, more focused parameter refinements in the mid-to-late layers compared to supervised fine-tuning, indicating targeted optimization.
> - RL-induced improvements in vision-to-reasoning alignment and reasoning abilities are modular and transferable, demonstrably encoded within the mid-to-late architectural segments.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of large language models (LLMs) has seen significant advancements, particularly through a two-stage post-training paradigm involving supervised fine-tuning (SFT) as an initialization (IN) followed by reinforcement learning (RL) with verifiable rewards. This IN+RL approach has been highly effective in eliciting robust reasoning behaviors in language-only contexts. Building on these successes, the same paradigm has been increasingly adopted for vision-language models (VLMs), with numerous reports of substantial gains on visual reasoning benchmarks across tasks like spatial reasoning and multimodal understanding.

Despite these empirical gains, a critical gap exists in the understanding of *what specific capabilities* RL actually improves in VLMs. Current end-to-end benchmark evaluations often conflate multiple factors, making it challenging to attribute performance improvements to distinct skills. Prior research has highlighted several ambiguities:
- Vision capabilities do not always reliably benefit from increased inference length or verbose reasoning, which can sometimes even exacerbate visually grounded errors by over-relying on linguistic priors.
- Aggregate benchmark accuracy often fails to distinguish between improvements in core visual perception, enhanced vision-to-reasoning alignment, or pure language-side reasoning.

This paper directly addresses this ambiguity. It positions itself as a crucial contribution to the emerging line of inquiry that moves beyond black-box performance metrics to investigate the internal mechanisms of RL's impact. While some previous works have started exploring RL from a parameter-space perspective in LLMs, this study extends such detailed analysis to VLMs, which introduce the complexity of multimodal interactions. By providing a "Frankenstein-style" analysis framework, the authors aim to mechanistically dissect VLM behavior, revealing *where* RL changes occur within the model's architecture and *what* functional roles these changes play. This work therefore fills a vital void by offering a fine-grained, causal understanding of RL's contribution to visual reasoning, thereby challenging the sufficiency of benchmark-only evaluations.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to answer the concise question: "What is consistently improved by RL for visual reasoning across training recipes?" This overarching question is motivated by several critical observations and ambiguities in the current understanding of RL's role in VLMs:

1. **Ambiguity of Visual Reasoning Improvements**: Despite the apparent and consistent improvements shown on end-to-end visual reasoning benchmarks when applying RL after supervised fine-tuning (Base Model → IN Model → RL Model), these gains do not reliably translate into monotonic improvements across fine-grained capabilities. The authors demonstrate that metrics specifically designed to evaluate vision ability ($M_{vis}$), language-side reasoning ability ($M_{rea}$), and vision-to-reasoning alignment ($M_{v2r}$) often do not show consistent increases from IN to RL models. This discrepancy highlights that aggregate accuracy masks qualitatively different internal changes, making it impossible to discern which functional components are genuinely strengthened by RL.

2. **Inconsistent Fine-Grained Improvements**: The paper explicitly shows that neither vision ability nor standalone reasoning ability consistently improves monotonically across training stages when evaluated with their proposed fine-grained metrics. This finding reinforces the limitations of relying solely on end-to-end benchmarks and underscores the need for a more granular analysis.

3. **Consistent Attention Patterns with Unclear Functional Role**: In contrast to the inconsistent fine-grained performance, the authors identify a consistent inference-time change induced by RL across diverse training recipes: an increased attention from reasoning tokens to visual tokens, predominantly observed in the mid-to-late transformer layers. The functional significance of this consistent pattern, however, remains unclear without further mechanistic investigation.

To address these motivations, the paper's primary objective is to develop and apply a systematic analytical framework—dubbed "Frankenstein-style analysis"—to dissect VLMs. This framework aims to causally trace the specific alterations introduced by RL, localizing them within the transformer architecture, characterizing their nature, and assessing their functional implications for visual reasoning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research employs a novel "Frankenstein-style analysis framework" to mechanistically dissect how RL impacts VLMs for visual reasoning. This framework consists of three main components, complemented by a validation step:

#### 3.1. Functional Localization via Causal Probing

The first step involves establishing a functional reference frame by localizing vision- and reasoning-related computations within the transformer layers of the *base* VLM (Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct). This ensures that subsequent analyses of RL effects are interpreted against the intrinsic functional layout of the model.

- **Localizing Vision Regions**: To identify layers critical for visual processing, a *vision-token intervention strategy* is used. Paired images, differing in exactly one visual attribute (e.g., text in OCR, object count, location, presence of an object), are constructed. For each image pair $(i, i')$, visual tokens at a specific transformer layer $\ell$ from one image are swapped with those from the paired image. A "Change Rate" (proportion of predictions that change) is computed. High change rates indicate layers actively processing the swapped visual information.
 - **Tasks**: Optical Character Recognition (OCR), Object Counting, Object Grounding, Object Recognition.
 - **Findings**: Simple vision processing (recognition) is concentrated in Early layers. More complex vision (OCR, grounding, counting) involves Early to Mid layers.

- **Localizing Reasoning Regions**: Unlike vision, reasoning lacks explicit tokens for swapping. Therefore, a *layer-wise causal ablation strategy* (layer skipping) is employed. For each transformer layer, its input is directly passed as its output, effectively removing its computational contribution. The resulting increase in error rate on textual reasoning benchmarks quantifies the layer's causal importance for reasoning.
 - **Tasks**: Textual math problems (GSM8K, MATH500).
 - **Findings**: Reasoning computations are primarily concentrated in Late layers, with minimal dependence on early layers.

Based on these localizations, the transformer layers are coarsely partitioned into Early (first third), Mid (middle third), and Late (last third) functional regions.

#### 3.2. Update Characterization via Parameter Comparison

This component analyzes the geometric properties of parameter updates introduced by IN and RL within the identified functional regions.

- **Update Energy**: The Frobenius norm (∥∆W∥F) of layer-wise parameter updates (∆W = W_trained - W_base) is computed to quantify the total optimization magnitude.
 - **Findings**: Both IN and RL concentrate the majority of their update energy in the Mid layers, reflecting their role in bridging vision and reasoning.

- **Update Diversity**: The singular value spectrum of ∆W is analyzed. Singular values are log-normalized and plotted. A steeper decay in the spectrum indicates that optimization is concentrated in a smaller number of dominant directions (focused, low-dimensional refinements), while a flatter spectrum suggests more diffuse updates.
 - **Findings**: RL updates exhibit a consistently *steeper spectral decay* in the Mid-Late layers compared to IN updates, implying that RL induces less diverse, more focused refinements in these regions.

#### 3.3. Transferability Test via Model Merging

To determine if RL-induced improvements are localized and transferable, a model merging approach is used.

- **Procedure**: Hybrid models are constructed by taking Early, Mid, and Late layer regions from either the IN or RL checkpoints. For example, "IN:RL:RL" means the Early region comes from the IN model, while the Mid and Late regions come from the RL model. Parameters within the chosen layers are directly copied, and no further training is performed.
- **Evaluation**: These hybrid models are evaluated using the fine-grained metrics ($M_{vis}$, $M_{v2r}$, $M_{rea}$) across the selected training recipes (OpenMMReasoner, MMR1, Revisual).
- **Findings**: Hybrid models preserving RL-refined layers in the Mid-Late regions (e.g., IN:RL:RL) consistently show improvements in vision-to-reasoning alignment ($M_{v2r}$) and reasoning ($M_{rea}$) relative to the IN model. This suggests that RL-induced refinements in these layers encode transferable functional behaviors.

#### 3.4. Necessity Validation via Model Freezing

This final step causally verifies the importance of Mid-Late layer refinements for RL gains.

- **Procedure**: During RL training, parameters in one specific region (Early, Mid, or Late) are frozen, preventing their optimization, while other layers are updated normally.
- **Evaluation**: Fine-grained and benchmark metrics are compared for standard RL training versus RL training with frozen regions.
- **Findings**: Freezing the Late layers during RL training leads to a substantial degradation in reasoning performance and vision-to-reasoning alignment, as well as benchmark metrics. Conversely, freezing Early layers has a much weaker effect. This demonstrates that Mid-Late refinement is not just correlated but *causally necessary* for RL improvements.

The study is conducted on three representative VLM training recipes (OpenMMReasoner, MMR1, Revisual) which follow the dominant IN+RL paradigm and provide reproducible checkpoints, ensuring generalizability of the findings within this common setup.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The "Frankenstein-style analysis" yields several key findings that collectively clarify the functional impact of Reinforcement Learning on visual reasoning in VLMs:

1. **Discrepancy Between End-to-End and Fine-Grained Performance**: While end-to-end benchmark accuracy consistently shows monotonic improvements from Base models to IN models and then to RL models, fine-grained evaluation metrics reveal a different picture. Neither the pure vision ability ($M_{vis}$) nor the standalone language-side reasoning ability ($M_{rea}$) consistently improves monotonically across these training stages. This highlights a fundamental limitation of aggregate benchmarks in diagnosing specific skill improvements.

2. **Consistent Inference-Time Attention Shift**: Despite the inconsistencies in fine-grained performance, RL consistently induces a notable shift in inference behavior across diverse training recipes: an increased attention from reasoning tokens to visual tokens. This effect is concentrated specifically in the **mid-to-late transformer layers**, while early layer attention patterns remain largely unchanged. This pattern provides an initial clue about *where* RL is exerting its influence.

3. **Layer-wise Functional Localization**:
 - **Vision-related computations** are predominantly localized in the **Early and Mid transformer layers**. Simple visual processing like recognition is strong in Early layers, while more complex visual tasks like OCR, grounding, and counting require contributions from Early to Mid layers.
 - **Reasoning-related computations** are concentrated in the **Late layers**, with earlier layers having a comparatively minor role. This establishes a functional stratification within the VLM's transformer backbone.

4. **Distinct Update Characterization for RL**:
 - Both supervised fine-tuning (IN) and RL concentrate their parameter update energy (Frobenius norm) in the **Mid layers**. This suggests these layers are crucial for both stages of post-training.
 - However, RL and IN exhibit complementary optimization diversity. RL updates, particularly in the **Mid-Late layers**, show a *steeper singular value decay* compared to IN updates. This indicates that RL applies *less diverse and more focused refinements* to these critical layers, concentrating optimization energy in a smaller number of dominant directions.

5. **Transferable Improvements in Mid-Late Layers**: The model merging experiments demonstrate that RL-induced refinements are modular and transferable. Hybrid models that incorporate RL-refined layers from the **Mid-Late regions** (e.g., keeping RL's Mid and Late layers while taking Early from IN, denoted as IN:RL:RL) consistently retain and even show improvements in:
 - **Vision-to-Reasoning alignment ($M_{v2r}$)**
 - **Reasoning performance ($M_{rea}$)**
 Relative to models relying solely on IN-trained layers. This strongly suggests that RL's contribution to these specific capabilities is encoded within these mid-to-late architectural segments.

6. **Necessity of Mid-Late Refinement for RL Gains**: The parameter freezing experiments during RL training causally validate the importance of the Mid-Late layers.
 - Freezing the **Late layers** during RL training leads to a substantial degradation in both fine-grained metrics (reasoning and vision-to-reasoning alignment) and overall benchmark performance.
 - In contrast, freezing the **Early layers** has a much weaker impact, often leading to better performance than freezing Mid or Late layers.
 This crucial finding confirms that refinement of the Mid-Late layers is *not merely correlated* but *causally necessary* for RL gains to emerge in visual reasoning.

In summary, the main findings consistently point to RL's primary impact being a systematic, focused refinement of computations in the mid-to-late transformer layers, which in turn significantly improves vision-to-reasoning alignment and reasoning capabilities, rather than a uniform enhancement of early-stage visual perception.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research offers significant contributions to the understanding of Vision-Language Models and the role of Reinforcement Learning in their development, with several potential impacts:

1. **Demystifying RL's Impact on VLMs**: The most significant contribution is moving beyond "black-box" performance gains to provide a mechanistic, layer-wise understanding of *what* RL consistently improves in VLMs for visual reasoning. This clarifies that RL's primary reliable contribution is not a uniform boost in visual perception, but a **systematic refinement of mid-to-late transformer computations** that enhances vision-to-reasoning alignment and reasoning performance. This deeper insight is crucial for advancing VLM research and development.

2. **Challenging Benchmark-Only Evaluation**: The paper convincingly demonstrates the limitations of relying solely on end-to-end benchmarks for evaluating multimodal reasoning improvements. By showing that monotonic gains in aggregate accuracy do not translate into consistent improvements in fine-grained abilities, it advocates for the adoption of more diagnostic and causally-grounded evaluation frameworks. This could lead to a paradigm shift in how VLM performance is assessed and understood.

3. **Providing a Principled Analytical Framework**: The proposed "Frankenstein-style analysis framework" (comprising functional localization, update characterization, transferability tests, and necessity validation) offers a novel and powerful methodology for dissecting and diagnosing complex behaviors in large neural networks. This framework is not limited to RL in VLMs but could be adapted to analyze other post-training techniques, model architectures, or modalities, thereby impacting the broader field of mechanistic interpretability.

4. **Informing Future VLM Architecture and Training Design**: By localizing RL's consistent effects to the mid-to-late transformer layers and characterizing the nature of these refinements (focused, less diverse updates), the research provides concrete guidance for future VLM design. Engineers and researchers can now consider:
 - **Targeted Optimization**: Developing RL algorithms or fine-tuning strategies that specifically target and optimize these crucial mid-to-late layers, potentially leading to more efficient and effective training.
 - **Architectural Design**: Designing VLM architectures that might better facilitate these specific refinements or have dedicated components for vision-to-reasoning alignment.
 - **Diagnostic Tools**: Integrating similar causal probing and parameter analysis tools into the development pipeline to gain real-time insights into model evolution.

5. **Enhancing Interpretability and Trust**: A clearer understanding of how VLMs learn and improve under RL contributes to greater interpretability. This is vital for building more trustworthy AI systems, particularly in sensitive applications where understanding the "why" behind a model's decision is as important as the decision itself.

6. **Highlighting the Importance of Multimodal Alignment**: The emphasis on "vision-to-reasoning alignment" as a key area of RL improvement underscores the complex interplay between different modalities within VLMs. Future research can focus more intently on the mechanisms and representations that facilitate this alignment, leading to more robust and less hallucination-prone multimodal models.

In conclusion, this paper serves as a significant step forward in our mechanistic understanding of RL's role in advancing visual reasoning in VLMs. By dissecting the internal workings of these models, it not only clarifies existing ambiguities but also paves the way for more informed architectural designs, targeted training methodologies, and sophisticated evaluation practices in the future.
