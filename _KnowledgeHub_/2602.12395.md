# What does RL improve for Visual Reasoning? A Frankenstein-Style Analysis

> [!summary] Summary
> This study provides a mechanistic analysis of how Reinforcement Learning (RL) improves Vision-Language Models (VLMs) for visual reasoning tasks. It reveals that RL's primary effect is a focused refinement of mid-to-late transformer layers, which in turn enhances vision-to-reasoning alignment and overall reasoning capabilities.

## Problem

- Despite empirical gains from Reinforcement Learning (RL) in Vision-Language Models (VLMs), the specific capabilities RL enhances for visual reasoning remain ambiguous.
- End-to-end benchmark evaluations conflate various factors, making it difficult to attribute performance improvements to distinct skills like visual perception or language-side reasoning.
- Fine-grained metrics for vision ability, reasoning ability, and vision-to-reasoning alignment do not consistently show monotonic improvements from supervised fine-tuning to RL.

## Method

- Proposing a =="Frankenstein-style analysis framework"== to mechanistically dissect VLM behavior and causally trace RL's alterations across model architectures.
- Localizing vision and reasoning-related computations within transformer layers through ==causal probing== using ==vision-token interventions== and ==layer-wise causal ablations==.
- Characterizing parameter updates from supervised fine-tuning and RL by analyzing their energy and diversity using ==Frobenius norms== and ==singular value spectra==, and validating their causal importance and transferability via ==model merging== and ==freezing experiments==.

## Results

- End-to-end benchmark accuracy improves monotonically from base to RL models, but fine-grained vision ability and standalone reasoning ability do not show consistent monotonic improvements.
- Vision computations are predominantly localized in early and mid-transformer layers, while reasoning computations are concentrated in late layers.
- Hybrid models incorporating RL-refined mid-to-late layers consistently show improvements in vision-to-reasoning alignment and reasoning performance compared to supervised fine-tuned models.
- Freezing late layers during RL training leads to substantial degradation in reasoning and vision-to-reasoning alignment, confirming their causal necessity for RL's gains.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - RL consistently induces an increased attention from reasoning tokens to visual tokens, concentrated specifically in the mid-to-late transformer layers.
> - RL applies less diverse, more focused parameter refinements in the mid-to-late layers compared to supervised fine-tuning, indicating targeted optimization.
> - RL-induced improvements in vision-to-reasoning alignment and reasoning abilities are modular and transferable, demonstrably encoded within the mid-to-late architectural segments.
