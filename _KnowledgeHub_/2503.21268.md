# ClimbingCap: Multi-Modal Dataset and Method for Rock Climbing in World Coordinate

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers developed AscendMotion, a large-scale, multi-modal dataset for rock climbing, and introduced ClimbingCap, a human motion recovery method specifically designed for off-ground activities. ClimbingCap reconstructs continuous 3D human climbing motion in world coordinates, achieving an MPJPE of 75.45 on horizontal scenes and 88.92 on vertical scenes within the new dataset, demonstrating improved accuracy over existing methods.

## Problem

- Human Motion Recovery (HMR) for off-ground activities like rock climbing is underdeveloped due to a lack of specialized datasets and robust methods.
- Existing climbing datasets are limited in dimensionality (2D), scale, or the complexity of motions captured, hindering comprehensive analysis.
- Accurately recovering complex, extreme off-ground poses and global positions in world coordinates presents unique challenges for traditional HMR methods, often leading to accumulated errors.

## Method

- ==The AscendMotion== dataset was created, offering a large-scale, multi-modal collection (RGB, LiDAR, IMU) of 3D human climbing poses and global trajectories with meticulous annotation.
- **ClimbingCap**, a multi-modal HMR method, was developed to reconstruct continuous 3D climbing motion using Separate Coordinate Decoding for both camera and global coordinate systems.
- A post-processing stage with specialized optimization losses (Limb Weight Differentiation, Speed Direction Smoothing, Visible Limb Repair) was integrated to refine motion, ensure physical plausibility, and handle human-scene interactions, along with a semi-supervised training framework.

## Results

- AscendMotion dataset annotations, refined with global optimization losses (L_GR and L_ST), achieved low acceleration errors (**1.54** m/s² for horizontal, **1.60** m/s² for vertical scenes), confirming high precision and physical consistency.
- ClimbingCap consistently outperformed nine state-of-the-art HMR methods on AscendMotion, achieving an MPJPE of **75.45** for horizontal and **88.92** for vertical scenes, substantially improving upon methods like GVHMR (MPJPE **107.09** and **124.60** respectively).
- The method demonstrated strong generalization capabilities, outperforming other methods on the CIMI4D dataset without retraining, achieving an MPJPE of **84.03** compared to **152.56** by GVHMR.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A multi-stage global optimization pipeline, utilizing scene context and body part-specific weighting, is effective in correcting IMU drift and generating precise 3D annotations for challenging climbing movements.
> - The fusion of RGB and LiDAR data through separate coordinate decoding is crucial for robustly reconstructing off-ground human motion in both camera and world coordinate systems.
> - Novel post-processing losses that account for human-scene interaction, limb-scene contact, and motion consistency are essential for reducing errors and ensuring physically plausible motion recovery in complex climbing environments.
