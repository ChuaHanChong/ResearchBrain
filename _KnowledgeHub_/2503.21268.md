# ClimbingCap: Multi-Modal Dataset and Method for Rock Climbing in World Coordinate

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers developed AscendMotion, a large-scale, multi-modal dataset for rock climbing, and introduced ClimbingCap, a human motion recovery method specifically designed for off-ground activities. ClimbingCap reconstructs continuous 3D human climbing motion in world coordinates, achieving an MPJPE of 75.45 on horizontal scenes and 88.92 on vertical scenes within the new dataset, demonstrating improved accuracy over existing methods.

## Problem

- Human Motion Recovery (HMR) for off-ground activities like rock climbing is underdeveloped due to a lack of specialized datasets and robust methods.
- Existing climbing datasets are limited in dimensionality (2D), scale, or the complexity of motions captured, hindering comprehensive analysis.
- Accurately recovering complex, extreme off-ground poses and global positions in world coordinates presents unique challenges for traditional HMR methods, often leading to accumulated errors.

## Method

- ==The AscendMotion== dataset was created, offering a large-scale, multi-modal collection (RGB, LiDAR, IMU) of 3D human climbing poses and global trajectories with meticulous annotation.
- **ClimbingCap**, a multi-modal HMR method, was developed to reconstruct continuous 3D climbing motion using Separate Coordinate Decoding for both camera and global coordinate systems.
- A post-processing stage with specialized optimization losses (Limb Weight Differentiation, Speed Direction Smoothing, Visible Limb Repair) was integrated to refine motion, ensure physical plausibility, and handle human-scene interactions, along with a semi-supervised training framework.

## Results

- AscendMotion dataset annotations, refined with global optimization losses (L_GR and L_ST), achieved low acceleration errors (**1.54** m/s² for horizontal, **1.60** m/s² for vertical scenes), confirming high precision and physical consistency.
- ClimbingCap consistently outperformed nine state-of-the-art HMR methods on AscendMotion, achieving an MPJPE of **75.45** for horizontal and **88.92** for vertical scenes, substantially improving upon methods like GVHMR (MPJPE **107.09** and **124.60** respectively).
- The method demonstrated strong generalization capabilities, outperforming other methods on the CIMI4D dataset without retraining, achieving an MPJPE of **84.03** compared to **152.56** by GVHMR.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A multi-stage global optimization pipeline, utilizing scene context and body part-specific weighting, is effective in correcting IMU drift and generating precise 3D annotations for challenging climbing movements.
> - The fusion of RGB and LiDAR data through separate coordinate decoding is crucial for robustly reconstructing off-ground human motion in both camera and world coordinate systems.
> - Novel post-processing losses that account for human-scene interaction, limb-scene contact, and motion consistency are essential for reducing errors and ensuring physically plausible motion recovery in complex climbing environments.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Human Motion Recovery (HMR) is a field focused on reconstructing human poses and movements from various sensor inputs. The current landscape of HMR research largely concentrates on ground-based activities such as running, walking, interacting, and dancing. Methods developed in this area often rely on assumptions about ground contact and typical human-environment interactions.

Research on capturing and analyzing off-ground motions, such as rock climbing, is less developed. This is primarily due to two factors: the scarcity of dedicated climbing motion datasets and the inherent complexity of climbing movements. Existing climbing datasets like SPEED21 and CIMI4D have limitations in terms of dimensionality (SPEED21 is 2D), scale, or the complexity of the motions recorded (CIMI4D includes many trivial movements from casual climbers). This data insufficiency has hindered the development of HMR methods specifically tailored for climbing.

Furthermore, accurately recovering climbing motions presents unique challenges. Climbers exhibit complex, extreme poses and dynamic interactions with non-planar surfaces (rock walls). Capturing these movements requires precise estimation of both the intricate body pose and the global position and trajectory of the climber within a world coordinate system. Existing global HMR methods, which attempt to maintain consistency in world coordinates, often struggle with the off-ground nature of climbing, accumulating errors over long sequences, and failing to model the distinct human-scene interactions involved. The ambiguity between camera and global coordinate systems also poses difficulties for rigid transformations.

This work addresses these identified gaps by providing a large-scale, multi-modal dataset specifically for climbing and proposing a new HMR method designed to handle the specific requirements of off-ground climbing motion recovery in a global coordinate system.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The research paper outlines two primary objectives, each motivated by identified limitations in the field of Human Motion Recovery (HMR), particularly concerning off-ground activities like rock climbing:

**Objective 1: To address the insufficiency of climbing motion datasets.**
- **Motivation:** The availability of high-quality, large-scale datasets is critical for advancing research in any data-driven field. For climbing motion, existing datasets are limited. SPEED21 is a 2D dataset, meaning it lacks the crucial 3D spatial information necessary for comprehensive motion analysis. CIMI4D, while offering 3D motions and global trajectories, is relatively small in scale and primarily captures motions from casual climbers, which may not represent the full range and complexity of challenging climbing movements. These limitations mean that the research community lacks the foundational data required for an in-depth understanding of skilled climbing motions and for training robust HMR models capable of handling such complexity. The absence of diverse, multi-modal data, including synchronized RGB, LiDAR, and IMU measurements with accurate 3D labels and global trajectories, has impeded progress in developing precise climbing motion capture technologies.

**Objective 2: To develop a robust HMR method capable of reconstructing continuous 3D human climbing motion in a global coordinate system.**
- **Motivation:** Rock climbing is an off-ground activity characterized by unique biomechanical demands, extreme limb extensions, and continuous interaction with varying, often non-planar, environments. Traditional HMR methods are predominantly designed for ground-based activities (e.g., walking, running) where assumptions about ground contact and gravitational forces can simplify the problem. These methods are suboptimal for climbing, as they often fail to accurately capture the intricate poses and the dynamic, multi-directional movements involved. Specifically, accurately estimating the climber's global position and trajectory in world coordinates, along with precise body pose, presents a significant challenge. Existing global HMR approaches often suffer from accumulated errors over long sequences and cannot effectively bridge the inherent ambiguities between camera and global coordinate systems when dealing with arbitrary off-ground motion. The motivation is to overcome these limitations by designing a method that explicitly considers the multi-modal nature of climbing data and the unique constraints imposed by human-scene interaction in a 3D environment.

In summary, the paper aims to provide both the necessary data foundation and a specialized methodological solution to enable accurate and reliable 3D human motion recovery for the challenging domain of rock climbing.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research introduces a multi-modal dataset, AscendMotion, and a corresponding human motion recovery method, ClimbingCap, designed for rock climbing in world coordinates.

#### 3.1. AscendMotion Dataset Collection and Annotation

The AscendMotion dataset was developed to address the scarcity of comprehensive climbing motion data.

- **Data Collection Hardware:** The system integrates three primary sensor types:
 - **LiDAR:** Ouster-OS1 (128 beams), capturing 3D point clouds at 20 frames per second (FPS). This sensor provides dense 3D environmental and human body data.
 - **RGB Camera:** Hik 1080P, recording video at 20 FPS. This provides visual information for texture and context.
 - **IMU Motion Capture System:** Xsens MVN with 17 IMUs, recording at 60 FPS. This system was used for collecting labeled data, providing initial pose and translation information.
 - **Scene Reconstruction:** A Trimble X7 3D laser scanner was utilized to reconstruct high-resolution RGB point cloud scenes (approximately 80 million points) for each climbing environment.
 - **Body Shape:** The IPNet method was employed to obtain the SMPL shape parameters (β) for each climber.
- **Dataset Scale and Content:** The dataset comprises 344 minutes of labeled data and 441 minutes of unlabeled data, totaling 412,000 frames. It features 22 skilled climbing coaches performing bouldering, speed climbing, and lead climbing across 12 distinct indoor and outdoor rock walls. Unlabeled data was collected without the IMU system. Human motion is represented using SMPL parameters: global translation (T), pose (θ), and shape (β).
- **Annotation Pipeline:** To ensure accuracy, especially correcting IMU drift, a three-stage annotation pipeline was developed:
 1. **Multi-modal Data Preprocessing:**
 - **Time Synchronization:** Precision Time Protocol (PTP) was used to synchronize RGB camera and LiDAR. IMU data was post-synchronized with LiDAR and RGB using anchor frames.
 - **Calibration:** LiDAR point clouds were registered with high-precision scanned scenes, establishing the LiDAR coordinate system as the world coordinate. IMU measurements were transformed into this world coordinate system, followed by frame-level calibration among all modalities.
 2. **Multi-stage Global Optimization:** This stage automatically refines initial IMU-derived poses and translations using scene context.
 - **Global Refit Loss (L_GR):** A modified Chamfer distance calculated between the SMPL model vertices and the human body point cloud. This loss term applies different weights to different body parts (e.g., torso vs. limbs) based on distance thresholds to the point cloud, recognizing that limbs are typically in closer contact with the rock surface during climbing (d_limb ≤ d_torso).
 - **Scene Touch Loss (L_ST):** This loss prevents unrealistic intersections between the SMPL model and the reconstructed scene mesh. It measures penetration depth, penalizing negative values where SMPL vertices penetrate the scene.
 3. **Manual Annotation and Verification:** Four observers manually reviewed and adjusted automatically generated annotations using an SMPL annotation tool. Ambiguous limb movements observed in RGB views were manually refined and marked as key frames, with poses propagated for global consistency and accuracy.

#### 3.2. ClimbingCap Method

ClimbingCap is a multi-modal HMR method designed to reconstruct continuous 3D human climbing motion in both camera and global coordinate systems. It consists of three main parts:

1. **Separate Coordinate Decoding (SCD):** This stage extracts features from multi-modal inputs and decodes human motion parameters in their respective coordinate systems.
 - **Input and Feature Extraction:** RGB image sequences and LiDAR point cloud sequences are the primary inputs. LiDAR data is first transformed from world to camera coordinates. RGB and transformed point cloud data are processed by ViT-based (RGB Extract) and PointNet++-based (PC Extract) modules, respectively, to obtain visual and geometric features.
 - **Camera Coordinate Decoder:** This module takes combined features to iteratively decode SMPL parameters (pose θ, shape β, camera translation ∆c) in the camera coordinate system.
 - **Global Coordinate Decoder:** This module uses features to iteratively predict global translation parameters (Γ_trans) of the human body in the world coordinate system, capturing the full motion trajectory.
 - **Loss Function:** The total loss includes L_kp3d (3D keypoint loss), L_kp2d (2D keypoint loss), L_smpl_θ and L_smpl_β (SMPL parameter losses), and L_traj (global trajectory loss).

2. **Post-processing:** This stage refines the motion recovery results from the SCD stage, leveraging the direct transformability between camera and world coordinates due to LiDAR data.
 - **Coordinate Consistency:** Poses from the camera coordinate system are converted to the world coordinate system using the inverse extrinsic matrix, enabled by the LiDAR modality.
 - **Optimization Losses (Adam optimizer):**
 - **L_LWD (Limb Weight Differentiation Loss):** Assigns different weights to SMPL vertices based on their body parts (limbs vs. torso) to optimize the human body's position in world coordinates, emphasizing contacts with the rock wall.
 - **L_SDS (Speed Direction Smoothing Loss):** Optimizes joint positions by smoothing changes in the velocity direction of the human body relative to the scene, promoting physically plausible motion consistency.
 - **L_VLR (Visible Limb Repair Loss):** Addresses difficult-to-estimate limb end poses by utilizing the spatial position of the point cloud, correcting unexpected movements.

3. **Semi-supervised Training:** This component utilizes readily available unlabeled climbing data to enhance model robustness.
 - **Teacher-Student Framework:** A teacher model, initially trained using the labeled data and the SCD/post-processing stages, generates pseudo-labels for unlabeled climbing motion data. A student model, which clones the teacher's parameters, is then trained using these pseudo-labels, leveraging the larger pool of unlabeled data to improve performance.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of AscendMotion and ClimbingCap involved quantitative assessment of dataset quality, comparative analysis with state-of-the-art HMR methods, and an ablation study on ClimbingCap's components.

#### 4.1. Dataset Evaluation (AscendMotion)

The quality of the AscendMotion dataset annotations, generated through the multi-stage global optimization pipeline, was quantitatively evaluated. The scenes were categorized into horizontal and vertical rock walls, acknowledging that vertical movements present greater challenges.
- **Metrics:** MPJPE (Mean Per-Joint Position Error), PA-MPJPE (Procrustes Aligned MPJPE), and Accel (acceleration error in m/s²) were used.
- **Ablation Study on Annotation Losses:** An ablation study on the Global Refit Loss (L_GR) and Scene Touch Loss (L_ST) demonstrated their individual and combined effectiveness. The inclusion of both L_GR and L_ST resulted in the lowest error metrics across both horizontal and vertical scenes. For instance, the acceleration error (Accel) was reduced to 1.54 m/s² for horizontal scenes and 1.60 m/s² for vertical scenes when both losses were applied, indicating precise and physically consistent annotations. This confirmed the effectiveness of the global optimization pipeline in generating high-quality labels.

#### 4.2. Comparison on Global Motion Recovery

ClimbingCap was benchmarked against nine existing HMR methods, categorized by their primary modality (RGB, LiDAR, or RGB+LiDAR), on both the AscendMotion and CIMI4D datasets. Evaluation protocols followed established metrics for camera-space (ACCEL, MPJPE, PA-MPJPE, PVE, PCK0.3) and world-space (WA-MPJPE, W-MPJPE, RTE, Jitter, T-Error) motion recovery.

- **Results on AscendMotion Dataset:**
 - ClimbingCap consistently demonstrated superior performance across most metrics in both horizontal and vertical climbing scenarios.
 - For **horizontal scenes**, ClimbingCap achieved an MPJPE of 75.45 and WA-MPJPE of 62.95, notably outperforming the next best methods (e.g., GVHMR with MPJPE 107.09 and WA-MPJPE 105.15).
 - For **vertical scenes**, which are identified as more challenging, ClimbingCap’s advantage was even more pronounced. It achieved an MPJPE of 88.92 and WA-MPJPE of 85.26, while methods like GVHMR showed significantly higher errors (MPJPE 124.60, WA-MPJPE 1002.11). This indicates that methods relying on assumptions suitable for ground-based motions or horizontal velocity prediction struggle with the predominantly upward movements in vertical climbing.
 - RGB-based methods (e.g., WHAM, GVHMR) generally performed well in camera coordinate metrics but showed reduced accuracy in world coordinate metrics for climbing motions. LiDAR-based and other RGB+LiDAR fusion methods also performed inferiorly, suggesting they did not fully account for global trajectories and complex human-scene interactions in climbing.
- **Results on CIMI4D Dataset (Generalization):**
 - To assess generalization capabilities, methods were evaluated on the CIMI4D dataset without retraining or fine-tuning. ClimbingCap maintained a strong performance, generally outperforming other methods across most metrics. For instance, it achieved an MPJPE of 84.03 compared to 152.56 by GVHMR. This indicates that ClimbingCap's design principles, while specifically tuned for challenging AscendMotion data, transfer effectively to other climbing datasets, even those with less challenging motions.

#### 4.3. Ablation Experiment

An ablation study on the AscendMotion dataset investigated the contribution of each component within the ClimbingCap framework.
- **RGB Input Only:** Using only RGB imagery without LiDAR resulted in the worst overall performance, highlighting the critical role of LiDAR for accurate global position estimation and the post-processing stage.
- **Excluding L_LWD (Limb Weight Differentiation Loss):** Removing this loss led to a minor decrease in performance (MPJPE 80.46 vs. 75.45 for full model), suggesting its beneficial but non-essential role in guiding optimization based on global point clouds.
- **Excluding L_SDS (Speed Direction Smoothing Loss):** The removal of this loss significantly degraded global metrics (e.g., WA-MPJPE 109.35 vs. 62.95), indicating its importance for ensuring motion consistency and physical plausibility on the scene.
- **Excluding L_VLR (Visible Limb Repair Loss):** Similar to L_SDS, removing L_VLR resulted in a significant drop in global metrics (e.g., WA-MPJPE 88.59 vs. 62.95), underscoring its role in correcting and constraining limb movements.
- **Excluding SS (Semi-supervised Training):** Omitting the semi-supervised training module led to a slight performance decrease (MPJPE 77.43 vs. 75.45), demonstrating its contribution to improving the robustness of the network training, albeit with a modest effect on the final metrics.

The full ClimbingCap model, incorporating RGB and LiDAR inputs, all proposed loss terms in post-processing, and the semi-supervised training framework, consistently achieved the best results, validating the efficacy of its integrated design.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of Human Motion Recovery (HMR) by addressing a specific and challenging domain: rock climbing. The work presents both a significant data resource and a methodological advancement, with several potential impacts.

### 6. Significance of AscendMotion Dataset

- **Addressing Data Scarcity:** AscendMotion provides a large-scale, multi-modal, and comprehensively annotated dataset for climbing motion. This addresses a recognized data insufficiency that has hindered progress in off-ground HMR.
- **Complexity and Quality:** The dataset features movements from skilled climbers on diverse rock walls, offering a wider range and greater complexity of challenging poses and interactions compared to previous datasets. Its meticulous annotation pipeline, incorporating multi-stage global optimization and manual verification, ensures high accuracy and reliability of 3D poses and global trajectories.
- **New Research Opportunities:** The availability of this detailed dataset, including synchronized RGB, LiDAR, and IMU data with world-coordinate labels, can stimulate further research into:
 - Developing more robust HMR methods for complex, off-ground human activities.
 - Understanding human-scene interaction in dynamic 3D environments.
 - Investigating biomechanical principles of climbing.
 - Exploring multi-modal data fusion techniques for challenging scenarios.

### 7. Significance of ClimbingCap Method

- **Specialized Solution for Off-Ground HMR:** ClimbingCap offers a dedicated HMR framework tailored for the unique challenges of rock climbing, explicitly accounting for extreme poses, global localization, and continuous human-scene interaction. This fills a gap where existing ground-based HMR methods are largely inadequate.
- **Robust Multi-Modal Fusion:** The method effectively integrates RGB and LiDAR modalities through separate coordinate decoding, enabling accurate pose estimation in both camera and global coordinate systems.
- **Innovative Post-processing and Training:** The inclusion of post-processing stages with specific losses (Limb Weight Differentiation, Speed Direction Smoothing, Visible Limb Repair) enhances the physical plausibility and consistency of reconstructed motions. The semi-supervised training framework leverages abundant unlabeled data, improving model robustness.
- **Performance Benchmark:** ClimbingCap establishes a new benchmark for climbing motion recovery, demonstrating superior accuracy and robustness compared to state-of-the-art methods in both camera and world coordinate metrics.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Sports Science and Training:** The ability to accurately capture and analyze climbing motions can have direct applications in sports science. Coaches and athletes could benefit from detailed feedback on technique, performance analysis, injury prevention strategies, and personalized training programs. This could include identifying inefficiencies, optimizing movement patterns, and tracking progress over time.
- **Robotics and Human-Robot Interaction:** Insights gained from understanding complex human movements in challenging environments could inform the development of more agile and adaptable robots, particularly for tasks in unstructured or off-ground settings.
- **Virtual Reality (VR) and Augmented Reality (AR):** For applications in virtual climbing simulations or AR-enhanced training, accurate 3D human avatars and realistic interactions with virtual environments are crucial. ClimbingCap could contribute to more immersive and effective VR/AR experiences.
- **General HMR Advancements:** The architectural design and loss functions introduced in ClimbingCap, particularly those addressing global consistency and complex scene interactions, could provide insights for developing HMR methods for other challenging human activities beyond climbing.
