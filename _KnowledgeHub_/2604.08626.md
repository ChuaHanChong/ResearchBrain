# WildDet3D: Scaling Promptable 3D Detection in the Wild

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from the Allen Institute for AI and University of Washington developed WildDet3D, a unified monocular 3D object detection system that generalizes to thousands of object categories in diverse real-world scenes, supporting multiple prompt types and optional depth input. The accompanying WildDet3D-Data dataset provides over 3.7 million 3D annotations across 13.5 thousand categories, enabling the model to achieve state-of-the-art performance on benchmarks like WildDet3D-Bench and Omni3D.

## Problem

- Existing monocular 3D object detection methods largely focused on closed-set categories and controlled environments, limiting their applicability in diverse, real-world 'in-the-wild' scenarios.
- Prior methods frequently specialized in a single input prompt type (e.g., text or 2D bounding boxes), lacking a unified framework for flexible user interaction.
- The generation of large-scale, open-vocabulary 3D datasets has been a bottleneck due to the resource-intensive nature of obtaining metric depth and calibrated camera intrinsics.

## Method

- Introduction of **WildDet3D**, a unified monocular 3D detector that integrates ==dual-vision encoders==, a ==depth fusion module==, and a promptable detector supporting text, 2D points, and 2D bounding box inputs.
- Creation of **WildDet3D**-Data, a large-scale dataset featuring over 1 million images and 3.7 million human-verified or VLM-selected 3D annotations across 13.5 thousand object categories, curated via a semi-automatic pipeline.
- Development of a ==deeply-supervised 3D detection head== with multi-source information aggregation, one-to-many matching, and unambiguous rotation normalization for robust 3D box parameterization and training.

## Results

- WildDet3D achieved **22.6** AP3D (text prompts) and **24.8** AP3D (box prompts) on the new WildDet3D-Bench, representing a **9.8x** improvement over prior methods, with additional gains up to **47.2** AP3D when ground-truth depth was available.
- The model set new state-of-the-art performance on the Omni3D benchmark (**34.2** AP3D with text, **36.4** AP3D with box) and demonstrated strong zero-shot generalization, improving ODS by **+16.5** on Argoverse 2 and **+17.4** on ScanNet compared to baselines.
- WildDet3D successfully deployed on consumer hardware (iPhone, Meta Quest 3) for real-time interactive 3D perception and integrated into robotic manipulation and Vision-Language Models, showcasing its practical applicability.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The integration of auxiliary 2D detection and comprehensive geometric supervision is critical, with ablation studies showing significant performance drops when these components are removed or simplified.
> - WildDet3D's architecture effectively leverages optional depth cues at inference time, leading to substantial accuracy gains in 3D localization, particularly in resolving inherent scale and occlusion ambiguities.
> - A semi-automatic, multi-stage data curation pipeline (candidates generation, rule-based filtering, human/VLM selection) can effectively scale 3D annotation to open-vocabulary and in-the-wild scenarios.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work by Huang et al. addresses challenges in monocular 3D object detection, a field focused on recovering the position, extent, and pose of objects from a single RGB image. This capability is considered foundational for spatial intelligence in applications such as robotics, embodied AI, autonomous driving, and augmented/virtual reality (AR/VR).

The broader research landscape has seen significant advancements in open-vocabulary 2D object recognition, largely driven by large-scale vision-language models. However, bringing comparable flexibility and generalization to 3D perception has remained a challenge. Existing monocular 3D detection methods often present limitations in three key areas:
1. **Generalization in diverse, open-world settings:** Current 3D datasets typically cover a limited number of categories in controlled environments, which restricts models' ability to generalize to the long-tail and open-ended object categories encountered in real-world scenarios.
2. **Support for multiple prompt modalities:** Many methods are designed for a single type of input prompt (e.g., text queries or fixed geometric inputs like 2D bounding boxes), limiting their versatility for different downstream applications. A unified framework that supports text, 2D points, and 2D boxes is not widely available.
3. **Integration of auxiliary geometric cues:** Practical deployments may sometimes offer additional geometric information, such as sparse LiDAR data or partial depth maps. Many existing models lack a native mechanism to leverage these cues to enhance 3D localization when available.

This research aims to bridge these gaps by developing both a generalized model architecture and a large-scale dataset. It builds upon advancements in open-vocabulary 2D perception and promptable segmentation systems by extending these concepts to 3D detection, while also addressing the challenges of data scarcity and noise inherent in 3D annotation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives of this research were twofold:
1. **Develop a unified, geometry-aware monocular 3D object detection architecture:** The motivation was to create a single model capable of operating effectively in open-world settings, generalizing beyond closed-set categories, and supporting diverse interaction modalities. Specifically, the authors aimed for a model that could:
 - **Generalize "in the wild":** Detect objects from a wide, long-tailed, and open-ended range of categories, including those not explicitly seen during training.
 - **Support multiple prompt modalities:** Accept different forms of user input, including text queries (e.g., "mug"), 2D point clicks (tapping on an object), and 2D bounding boxes (from an upstream 2D detector), all within a single architecture. This flexibility is critical for various interactive applications.
 - **Leverage auxiliary geometric cues:** Incorporate additional depth signals (e.g., from LiDAR, stereo, or RGB-D sensors) at inference time when available, to improve the accuracy of 3D localization, while gracefully degrading to monocular mode when such cues are absent. The inherent scale and occlusion ambiguities of pure RGB-based 3D detection motivated the optional integration of depth for better metric grounding.

2. **Construct a large-scale, diverse, and human-verified 3D detection dataset for open-world scenarios:** Existing 3D datasets are constrained by limited scale (typically <100 categories) and narrow domain coverage (e.g., autonomous driving or indoor scenes), which hinders open-world transfer. The motivation was to address this data bottleneck by creating a dataset that offers:
 - **Broader vocabulary coverage:** Substantially increase the number of object categories compared to existing benchmarks.
 - **Greater visual diversity:** Include diverse real-world scenes beyond controlled environments.
 - **Human-verified 3D annotations:** Mitigate the noise and inaccuracies often associated with purely automatic 3D annotation pipelines by incorporating human judgment for quality assurance. This aims to enable robust generalization for the trained models.

By addressing these model and data limitations simultaneously, the authors aimed to advance the state-of-the-art in monocular 3D object detection towards a more general-purpose, interactive, and open-world capable system.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research introduces WildDet3D, a model and an accompanying dataset, WildDet3D-Data.

#### 3.1. WildDet3D Model Architecture

WildDet3D is designed as a unified, geometry-aware architecture that takes an RGB image, optional camera intrinsics, optional partial/full depth map, and a user-specified prompt (text, 2D point, or 2D box) to predict 3D bounding boxes. The architecture comprises three main components:

- **Dual-vision Encoder:** This component handles diverse visual and geometric inputs.
 - **Image Encoder:** A ViT-H backbone with a SimpleFPN neck, initialized from a segmentation-pretrained checkpoint (SAM 3), extracts high-resolution, multi-scale semantic features from the RGB image. Most blocks are frozen during training to preserve pre-trained features.
 - **RGBD Encoder:** A DINOv2 ViT-L/14, initialized from a depth completion model (LingBot-Depth), processes 4-channel RGBD input (where depth is optional, filled with zeros if not provided) and generates depth latents through a ConvStack neck. A stochastic strategy (70% monocular, 20% patch-masked depth, 10% full depth) is used during training to support optional depth input.
 - **Depth Fusion Module:** This module combines the depth latents from the RGBD encoder with the semantic features from the image encoder. It uses a ControlNet-style residual design where depth latents are bilinearly interpolated, normalized, and projected via a zero-initialized 1x1 convolution before being added element-wise to the visual features. This modular design aims to keep semantics and geometry largely decoupled until fusion.

- **Promptable Detector:** This module conditions the fused, geometry-aware visual features on user-supplied prompts.
 - **Prompt Types:** It accepts text (category name), 2D points (positive/negative clicks), 2D boxes (bounding box coordinates), and exemplar prompts (a 2D box identifying an object visually similar to others to be detected).
 - **Prompt Encoding:** Text prompts are tokenized and encoded by a causal text Transformer (CLIP-style). Box and point prompts are encoded by a geometry encoder that combines coordinate projection, ROI-aligned/grid-sampled features, and sinusoidal positional encoding, refined by a Transformer. Exemplar prompts reuse box encoding but are differentiated by a special token and multi-target matching.
 - **Per-prompt Batching:** Training batches are constructed at the per-prompt level, aggregating all images containing a queried category or instance, which is intended to enable fine-grained multi-instance supervision.

- **Deeply-supervised 3D Detection Head:** This head lifts 2D query features into 3D bounding box predictions through `L` Transformer decoder layers, each producing its own predictions.
 - **Multi-source Information Aggregation:** Each decoder layer enhances hidden states with camera geometry (using spherical harmonic-encoded camera ray features via cross-attention) and depth information (fusing depth latents via cross-attention).
 - **3D Box Parameterization:** A two-layer MLP predicts a 12-dimensional encoding for each 3D box, including 2D center offset, log-depth, log-dimensions (width, height, length), and a 6D rotation representation (first two rows of the 3x3 rotation matrix).
 - **Unambiguous Rotation Normalization:** A two-step process (dimension ordering and yaw folding) is applied to ground-truth and predicted rotations to resolve inherent ambiguities and ensure a unique representation for training.
 - **3D Confidence Prediction:** A parallel MLP branch predicts a scalar 3D detection quality score (`s_3D`). This score is trained with a soft target that combines depth prediction quality and 3D IoU, emphasizing depth accuracy. At inference, `s_3D` combines additively with the 2D objectness score for final ranking.

- **Multi-task Learning:** The model is trained with a composite loss function (`L = L_3D + L_conf + L_geom + L_2D`).
 - **3D Regression Loss (`L_3D`):** L1 loss on the encoded 3D parameters for matched prediction-target pairs.
 - **3D Confidence Loss (`L_conf`):** IoU-aware focal BCE loss for the confidence branch.
 - **Auxiliary Geometry Loss (`L_geom`):** Combines L1 and scale-invariant logarithmic depth losses, affine-invariant point-map losses (global/local alignment, edge loss), confidence mask BCE for depth validity, and L2 loss for camera ray directions.
 - **Auxiliary 2D Detection Loss (`L_2D`):** IoU-aware BCE for box classification, L1 and Generalized IoU (GIoU) for 2D box regression, and per-category presence loss.
 - **One-to-Many (O2M) Matching:** Each ground-truth object is matched to its top-k (k=4) scoring predictions, providing denser supervision.
 - **Ignore-region Suppression:** During training, negative classification loss is suppressed for predictions overlapping with 2D-annotated "ignore" regions, aligning with evaluation protocols.

#### 3.2. WildDet3D-Data Dataset Creation

WildDet3D-Data is a large-scale, in-the-wild dataset covering over 1M images, 3.7M valid 3D annotations, and 13.5K object categories. Its creation involves a three-stage pipeline:

- **Candidates Generation:**
 - **Data Sources:** Leverages 2D bounding box annotations from large-scale 2D datasets: COCO, LVIS, Objects365, and V3Det.
 - **Monocular Depth and Camera Estimation:** Applies 4x image super-resolution, MoGe-2 for metric depth maps, and PerspectiveFields/WildCamera for camera pose/intrinsics estimation. The depth map is reprojected into a 3D point cloud.
 - **Multi-model Candidate Generation:** Five complementary methods generate initial 3D box candidates for each 2D annotation: 3D-MOOD (open-vocabulary text-based), DetAny3D (direct 3D regression from 2D box), SAM-3D (3D mesh reconstruction from mask/depth), RANSAC-PCA (geometric fitting), and LabelAny3D (single-image 3D reconstruction).
 - **3D Box Optimization:** Each candidate undergoes refinement via translation optimization (aligning depth to estimated depth map) and rotation optimization (PCA-based gravity alignment and 2D projection constraints).

- **Rule-based Filtering:** Multi-stage filtering is applied to candidates and annotations.
 - **Geometric Filters:** Discard candidates based on criteria like edge contact ratio, occlusion ratio, or implausible 3D-to-2D projection size ratios.
 - **Depicted Object Filter:** A VLM-based classifier identifies and removes annotations of depicted objects (e.g., pictures, reflections).
 - **Composite Image Filter:** Removes images composed of multiple sub-images that often yield inaccurate depth maps.
 - **LLM-estimated Size and Geometry Filter:** GPT-4.1-mini estimates expected physical dimensions and ratios for each category, used to filter out implausible box dimensions based on absolute size, depth-to-width ratio, and axis proportion.
 - **Small Object Upgrade:** Small objects initially filtered are re-evaluated using VLM criteria and upgraded if qualified.

- **Candidate Selection:** Final 3D annotations are obtained through two parallel paths:
 - **Human Selection:** For a sampled subset, crowdsourced annotators evaluate up to five candidates per object, selecting the best and rating its quality (good\_fit, acceptable, unacceptable). Quality control measures are in place.
 - **VLM Selection:** For the remaining images, a fine-tuned Molmo2 VLM automatically scores each candidate on six perceptual criteria (category, scale, translation, shape, rotation, vertical tilt accuracy). Candidates with a total score exceeding a threshold (10) are retained.

#### 3.3. Training Details

Training is performed in three stages using AdamW, with pre-trained backbones partially frozen (28/32 ViT blocks for SAM3, 21/24 DINOv2 blocks for LingBot-Depth).
- **Stage 1:** Trains on Omni3D for 12 epochs.
- **Stage 2:** Fine-tunes on a mixture of Omni3D, other supplementary 3D datasets (CA-1M, Waymo, 3EED, FoundationPose), and WildDet3D-Data (human and synthetic), for 12 epochs.
- **Stage 3:** Further fine-tune on Omni3D and WildDet3D-Data (human) with mask-guided point/box training for 3 epochs, leveraging 2D segmentation masks.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents quantitative and qualitative results across multiple benchmarks, demonstrating the performance of WildDet3D.

#### 4.1. In-the-Wild Evaluation: WildDet3D-Bench

On the newly introduced WildDet3D-Bench, an open-vocabulary benchmark with over 700 categories:
- **Performance:** WildDet3D trained on Omni3D, Others, and WildDet3D-Data achieved 22.6 AP$_{3D}$ with text prompts and 24.8 AP$_{3D}$ with box prompts. This represents a substantial improvement over prior methods (e.g., 3D-MOOD's 2.3 AP with text prompts when trained on Omni3D only).
- **Impact of GT Depth:** Incorporating ground-truth depth at test time significantly increased performance, reaching 41.6 AP$_{3D}$ (text) and 47.2 AP$_{3D}$ (box) for the full model, indicating the architecture's ability to utilize geometric cues.
- **Frequency Splits:** The model showed strong generalization, with performance improvements observed across rare, common, and frequent categories. The largest gains were on rare categories (47.4 AP$_{rare}$ with depth for text prompt vs. 2.4 for 3D-MOOD).

#### 4.2. Results on Omni3D

On the standard Omni3D benchmark (98 categories):
- **State-of-the-Art:** WildDet3D achieved 34.2 AP$_{3D}$ with text prompts (outperforming 3D-MOOD Swin-B at 30.0 AP) and 36.4 AP$_{3D}$ with box prompts (outperforming DetAny3D at 34.4 AP).
- **Training Efficiency:** These results were achieved with 12 training epochs, significantly fewer than the 80–120 epochs required by competing approaches, attributed to strong pre-trained backbones.
- **Sparse Depth:** Providing sparse depth at inference time further boosted performance to 41.6 AP$_{3D}$ (text) and 45.8 AP$_{3D}$ (box), with notable gains in indoor datasets (SUNRGBD, Hypersim, ARKitScenes).

#### 4.3. Zero-Shot Evaluation

For cross-dataset generalization, training on Omni3D and testing zero-shot on Argoverse 2 (AV2) and ScanNet:
- **Strong Generalization:** WildDet3D achieved 40.3 ODS on AV2 and 48.9 ODS on ScanNet, outperforming 3D-MOOD Swin-B by +16.5 ODS and +17.4 ODS, respectively.
- **Error Metrics:** The model demonstrated improved orientation estimation (lower mAOE) and translation accuracy (lower mATE) compared to baselines.
- **GT Depth Impact:** Ground-truth depth yielded a +1.3 ODS improvement on ScanNet (indoor scenes), while its impact on AV2 (outdoor driving) was marginal, suggesting robust monocular depth estimation in that domain.

#### 4.4. In-the-Wild Evaluation with Real Depth (Stereo4D)

On the Stereo4D dataset with real stereo depth maps, in a zero-shot setting:
- **Real Depth Leverage:** Monocular WildDet3D (7.5 AP$_{3D}$) was competitive with other methods. However, with real depth provided, performance significantly improved to 27.7 AP$_{3D}$, a 2.8x gain over the best monocular baseline (OVMono3D-LIFT), confirming the effective use of real depth signals.

#### 4.5. Ablation Studies

- **Joint 2D+3D Detection:** Removing the 2D head and directly predicting 3D boxes caused a substantial performance drop (30.2 AP to 11.1 AP), indicating the criticality of 2D spatial priors for 3D regression.
- **3D Confidence Head:** Its removal led to a 0.8 AP decrease, highlighting its role in refining 3D localization quality.
- **One-to-Many (O2M) Matching:** Contributed a 2.5 AP gain, particularly beneficial for scenes with dense, similarly-sized objects.
- **Geometry Loss:** Explicit geometric supervision from depth and camera-ray losses provided a 1.7 AP improvement, especially in indoor scenes.
- **Deep Supervision and Ignore-aware Suppression:** Offered smaller but consistent gains (0.3 AP and 0.2 AP, respectively).
- **Sparse Depth at Test Time:** Integration of sparse depth from sensors at inference time yielded substantial gains (e.g., +9.4 AP for box prompts on Omni3D), confirming the model's ability to accommodate additional geometric signals.

#### 4.6. Qualitative Results

Qualitative comparisons against OVMono3D, DetAny3D (box prompts), and 3D-MOOD (text prompts) showed WildDet3D producing more accurate 3D localization, tighter bounding boxes, and more realistic object placement, orientation, and relative positions across diverse and cluttered scenes, including fine-grained objects and animals.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work presents a notable contribution to the field of 3D computer vision by addressing two key bottlenecks in monocular 3D object detection: the need for a general-purpose, promptable model and the scarcity of diverse, high-quality 3D training data.

The significance of **WildDet3D** lies in its unified architecture, which provides flexibility in interaction and robust performance in challenging, open-world settings. By natively supporting text, point, and box prompts, the model caters to various application needs, from language-driven robotics to interactive AR/VR interfaces. Its ability to gracefully incorporate optional depth cues at inference time means it can benefit from richer 3D information when available, while still functioning effectively in purely monocular scenarios, making it adaptable to diverse deployment environments. The model's efficiency in achieving competitive performance with fewer training epochs, attributed to its use of strong pre-trained backbones, may also contribute to faster research cycles and practical deployment.

The creation of **WildDet3D-Data** is a substantial contribution, offering the largest open 3D detection dataset to date, with over 1 million images and 13.5 thousand categories. This dataset significantly expands the vocabulary and scene diversity available for monocular 3D detection research, moving beyond the limitations of previous benchmarks. The hybrid annotation pipeline, combining multi-model candidate generation with VLM-based filtering and human verification, demonstrates a scalable approach to curating high-quality 3D annotations, which has implications for future dataset creation efforts in 3D vision.

The **demonstrated applications** (iPhone app, Meta Quest 3 integration, robotic manipulation, and vision-language model integration for spatial reasoning) highlight the practical utility and versatility of WildDet3D. It can function as a "plug-and-play" 3D perception module, bridging high-level language understanding with precise spatial localization for real-world tasks. This could foster new developments in areas requiring spatial intelligence, such as enhancing human-computer interaction in AR, enabling more intuitive robotic control, and improving grounded reasoning in embodied AI systems.

**Limitations** acknowledged by the authors include reliance on predicted camera intrinsics (when not provided) which can degrade 3D localization, the inherent ambiguity of monocular depth for distant or occluded objects, challenges in rotation estimation for symmetric or low-visibility objects, and the computational cost of the dual-backbone design for on-device real-time inference without optimization. The performance on rare categories, while improved, still lags frequent ones. Additionally, the applications are presented as research prototypes, emphasizing that the model does not provide guaranteed error bounds and is not intended for safety-critical systems. These limitations provide avenues for future research and development.

In summary, WildDet3D and its accompanying dataset advance the generalizability and practical applicability of monocular 3D object detection, laying a foundation for more interactive and robust spatial AI systems.
