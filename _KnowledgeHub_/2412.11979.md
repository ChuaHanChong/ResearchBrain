# AlphaZero Neural Scaling and Zipf's Law: a Tale of Board Games and Power Laws

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Goethe University Frankfurt demonstrated that AlphaZero agents produce state-frequency distributions adhering to Zipf's law, with the Zipf exponent non-linearly correlating with neural scaling law exponents. This work also mechanistically explains inverse scaling in AlphaZero by showing that larger models selectively overfit numerous, less strategically important late-game states, causing performance degradation on crucial early-game situations.

## Problem

- A universal understanding of neural scaling laws (NSLs) remains elusive, especially their mechanisms in reinforcement learning (RL) like AlphaZero, compared to supervised learning (LLMs).
- The connection between Zipf's law, observed in natural language and other domains, and NSLs in RL, particularly AlphaZero, was not thoroughly investigated.
- The phenomenon of inverse scaling, where larger RL models perform worse, lacked a mechanistic explanation rooted in learning dynamics and data distribution.

## Method

- AlphaZero agents were trained and analyzed on four board games (Connect Four, Pentago, Oware, Checkers) to measure state-frequency distributions and their adherence to ==Zipf's law==.
- The policy temperature of the Monte-Carlo Tree Search (MCTS) was varied during inference to systematically alter state distributions and correlate changes in Zipf exponents with AlphaZero's performance scaling exponents.
- Loss optimization order on states was analyzed, and inverse scaling was investigated by examining state-turn distributions, split-loss between early and late game, and learning dynamics to propose a mechanism involving selective forgetting.

## Results

- AlphaZero agents consistently produced state-frequency distributions following Zipf's law, with exponents between **0.8** and 1.0, and optimized loss on more frequent (though computationally harder) states first.
- Varying MCTS policy temperature revealed a non-linear correlation between the Zipf tail exponent and AlphaZero's size-scaling exponent, while policy quality remained constant.
- Inverse scaling in Checkers and Oware correlated with 'forked' turn distributions and a 'bump' in the Zipf curve from frequent late-game states; larger models showed improved late-game loss but degraded early-game loss due to selective forgetting.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - AlphaZero agents' training and inference data exhibit Zipf's law for state frequencies, whose smooth power-law arises from the interaction of agent policies with the inherent game-tree structure.
> - A causal, non-linear relationship exists between the Zipf tail exponent of the state-frequency distribution and the size-scaling exponent of AlphaZero's Elo score.
> - Inverse scaling in AlphaZero is linked to unusual state-frequency distributions dominated by high-frequency late-game states, where larger models selectively overfit these states, leading to 'selective forgetting' of crucial early-game information due to non-stationary targets.
