# Task aware Dynamic Movement Primitives for failure detection and recovery in contact rich manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> A stage-aware framework for peg-in-hole assembly that couples Dynamic Movement Primitives learned from one teleoperated demonstration with Quadratic Discriminant Analysis stage classification over goal-relative position, force and velocity features. A Mahalanobis distance on force features flags contact anomalies online, triggering a spiral search that realigns the peg before resuming the DMP. Reaches 95% stage accuracy and recovers from 3 mm misalignment.

## Problem

- Standard DMP formulations reproduce demonstrated shapes but degrade under varied object pose, contact location or grasp configuration, causing failures in contact-rich phases.
- Prior Hidden Markov Model stage estimation struggles online near transitions, since future observations are unavailable and adjacent stages share overlapping force-position characteristics.
- Residual-equation stage definitions suit constrained robotic assembly but generalize poorly to human demonstrations because of natural execution variability across trials.

## Method

- Per-axis **DMPs**, a second-order ==spring-mass-damper== with ==Gaussian-basis forcing term== and a decaying canonical phase variable, encode the single teleoperated demonstration for x, y, z.
- **QDA** fits per-stage multivariate Gaussians over a ==nine-dimensional feature vector== of goal-relative end-effector position, measured forces and velocity, labeling approach, alignment and insertion online.
- Failure declared when ==Mahalanobis distance== over force features alone exceeds the stage mean plus six sigma, then a bounded Cartesian ==spiral search== realigns under contact until forces settle.

## Results

- QDA stage classification reaches **95%** overall accuracy, with predicted intervals within **0.7 s** of the true boundaries; six trials average **91.8%** plus-minus **5.4%**.
- Shifting the goal from **(-400, 0, 200)** to **(-400, -100, 200)** preserves correct three-stage segmentation with no retraining, confirming the goal-relative feature generalizes.
- Alignment-stage failure drives axial force above **100 N** and Mahalanobis distance past the six-sigma threshold; spiral search (k = **0.0002 m/s**, **8 mm** radius) restores insertion at **3 mm** offset.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Encode classifier features in the same goal-relative frame the motion primitive uses, so stage recognition inherits the primitive's generalization instead of needing retraining per goal.
> - Split feature sets by purpose: position tracks well even during failure, so anomaly detection on force alone is more physically meaningful than all-feature monitoring.
> - Detection accuracy degrades mainly at stage boundaries where force-position signatures overlap, so transition-adjacent decisions need caution or extra evidence.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Overview

A peg must enter a close-fitting hole. At that point, contact can turn a small alignment error into a jam: a trajectory that looks on track in position can still jam under contact. A robot can learn this peg-in-hole insertion from one teleoperated demonstration and replay the motion. Replaying the motion alone does not tell the robot whether contact is going wrong. In this work, the robot recognizes which stage of the task it is in and watches for unusual force. When it detects a problem, it pauses and searches for alignment before continuing. The authors report successful recovery from a lateral offset of up to 3 mm. This is a result on their experimental setup, and it is not evidence of robustness to arbitrary misalignment or other failure types.

### 2. From replay to recovery

The task has three stages: approach (move toward the hole), alignment (initial contact and adjustment to the hole axis), and insertion. From one teleoperated example, the system learns the motion as a Dynamic Movement Primitive (DMP). A DMP is a motion representation that preserves a demonstrated trajectory while allowing changes such as start and goal position. The system learns separate DMPs for the three spatial dimensions.

From the same example, it trains a stage classifier on goal-relative end-effector position, force, and velocity. Goal-relative position is position measured relative to the target, and it is intended to let the classifier transfer when the goal changes. The classifier uses Quadratic Discriminant Analysis (QDA). QDA models the feature distributions for each stage and selects the most likely stage.

During execution, the robot runs the following loop:

1. It predicts the stage online.
1. It compares force features with the nominal force distribution for that stage using Mahalanobis distance, a measure of how unusual a feature vector is relative to that distribution.
1. It declares an anomaly above the demonstration-derived mean plus six standard deviations. This threshold was chosen empirically and has not been established as universal.
1. After an anomaly, the DMP pauses and the robot searches in the horizontal plane with an expanding spiral.
1. It uses reduced lateral force and near-zero, stable axial force to infer alignment, and then resumes the DMP.

According to the authors' interpretation, the DMP can keep tracking position during failure while the problem appears in contact force.

### 3. What the tests establish

The physical test uses a 33 × 33 mm square peg and a 35 × 35 mm hole, giving 1 mm clearance per side.

Stage-classification accuracy is the proportion of stage labels predicted correctly against the manually labeled stages. It measures stage labels, not task success. Across six recorded execution trials, the mean accuracy is 91.8% ± 5.4%. The abstract and results report a separate 95% figure, and that figure is not the six-trial mean. Accuracy varies, and errors are concentrated near transitions, where sensor characteristics overlap.

In one shifted-goal test, the goal changes from (−400, 0, 200) to (−400, −100, 200). The paper reports that the classifier still identifies the stages without retraining. This single test does not show generalization to every unseen goal or task.

End-effector z position (mm) over time (s) in the shifted-goal run. The predicted stage trace moves from approach (blue) to alignment (orange) to insertion (green), with vertical dashed transition markers.

For the recovery test, the authors induced an approximately 3 mm lateral offset, which caused a failure during the alignment stage. The spiral search was bounded to an 8 mm maximum radius. The authors report restored insertion up to the tested offset. The paper does not report the recovery-trial count or compare recovery against a baseline. It also reports no ablation isolating QDA, force monitoring, or the spiral search.

Mahalanobis distance over time (s), with a red dashed failure-threshold line. The trace is low for much of the displayed interval, then rises sharply above the threshold and later drops. The paper identifies this crossing as the failure phase.

### 4. Closing

The framework keeps the learned motion and uses stage context and contact force to interrupt it, search for alignment, and resume it. On this peg-in-hole setup, the authors report recovery from up to 3 mm of lateral offset. The paper says future work will address more complex failure modes and tighter tolerances.
