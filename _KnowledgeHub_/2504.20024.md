# SpatialReasoner: Towards Explicit and Generalizable 3D Spatial Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> SpatialReasoner enhances large vision-language models with explicit 3D spatial reasoning by integrating precise 3D object locations and orientations as a shared interface. The model achieves a new state-of-the-art accuracy of 60.3% on the 3DSRBench, demonstrating improved generalization over existing models.

## Problem

- State-of-the-art large multi-modal models (LVLMs) often struggle with true 3D spatial reasoning, relying on implicit understanding or exploiting superficial 2D correlations (the "2D shortcut" problem).
- Current LVLMs lack a deep, explicit grasp of 3D geometry, object properties, and metric spatial relationships, leading to failures on seemingly simple human-level 3D questions.
- Existing 3D-aware LVLMs, while improved by fine-tuning on 3D-related data, often exhibit limited generalization to out-of-distribution or compositionally novel 3D spatial queries.

## Method

- Introduces ==explicit 3D representations== (locations and orientations) as a unified interface for 3D perception, 3D computation, and 3D reasoning within a calibrated camera 3D space.
- Employs a ==two-stage post-training pipeline==: **Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT)** to enable the LVLM to predict explicit 3D representations and **Reinforcement Learning (RL)** to foster generalizable 3D thinking.
- Utilizes a ==composite reward scheme== in RL, including ==outcome-based rewards== (accuracy, format) and ==process-based rewards== (reasoning steps, 3D-aware terms) to guide the model's reasoning process.

## Results

- **SpatialReasoner** achieves a new state-of-the-art mean accuracy of **60.3%** on the 3DSRBench, outperforming leading proprietary models like Gemini 2.0 Flash (**49.8%**) and Claude 3.5 Sonnet (**48.2%**).
- The model demonstrates strong generalization, with RL-trained variants significantly outperforming SFT-only models on novel multi-object questions (**46.6%** vs. **40.0%**) when multi-object training data is removed.
- Ablation studies confirm that explicit 3D representations are essential for performance (**51.9%** vs. **58.3%** without them) and reveal that SFT scales better with noisy data while RL benefits more from high-quality data.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicit 3D representations, such as object locations and orientations, are crucial for enabling accurate and interpretable 3D spatial reasoning in large vision-language models.
> - A multi-stage training strategy combining Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) and Reinforcement Learning (RL) improves generalization to novel 3D spatial questions, with RL fostering more robust and adaptive reasoning.
> - The primary bottleneck for overall 3D spatial reasoning accuracy in LVLMs is often the initial 3D perception (accurately estimating 3D properties), rather than the subsequent 3D computation or reasoning steps.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of artificial intelligence has seen monumental advancements with the rise of large multi-modal models (LVLMs), such as OpenAI's GPT-4o, Google's Gemini 2.0, and Alibaba Cloud's Qwen2.5-VL. These models exhibit impressive capabilities in general visual understanding and language generation, enabling diverse applications from image captioning to complex visual question answering. However, despite their progress, this paper identifies a critical frontier where state-of-the-art models still struggle: **true 3D spatial reasoning**.

Current LVLMs often perform 3D spatial reasoning in an "implicit" manner. They might correlate visual patterns in 2D images with learned language associations to answer 3D questions, but they lack a deep, explicit understanding of 3D geometry, object properties, and spatial relationships in a metric sense. This limitation often leads to failures on questions trivial to humans, especially when spurious 2D correlations are present (as highlighted by the "2D shortcut" problem discussed in the appendix, referencing works like). Proprietary models like Gemini 2.0 have shown progress in 3D parsing by predicting bounding boxes, but even they can fail on complex reasoning due to a lack of reliable 3D computation.

The paper positions itself directly against this backdrop by proposing a paradigm shift from implicit to **explicit 3D representations**. This approach harks back to earlier research in neural-symbolic systems and structured visual modules that emphasize interpretability and compositional reasoning by grounding AI in symbolic or explicit representations. While previous 3D-aware LVLMs like SpatialVLM, SpatialBot, SpatialLLM, and SpatialRGPT have explored fine-tuning on synthetic 3D-related QA data, SpatialReasoner aims to go beyond by explicitly integrating 3D locations and orientations as a shared interface across perception, computation, and reasoning stages. This is a crucial distinction, as it allows for precise 3D calculations and interpretable reasoning traces, addressing the "black box" nature of many implicit models.

Furthermore, the work contributes to the burgeoning area of **post-training strategies** for large models. Supervised fine-tuning (SFT) and reinforcement learning (RL) have become standard for aligning pre-trained models with specific downstream objectives. This paper investigates how different combinations of SFT and RL impact generalization, particularly in the context of complex 3D tasks. It empirically tests the hypothesis (e.g., from) that SFT tends to lead to memorization, while RL can foster more robust and generalizable reasoning abilities. By providing a detailed analysis of their combined SFT+RL strategy, the authors offer a valuable "recipe" for future development of 3D-aware LVLMs.

Finally, the paper contributes to **benchmarking and evaluation**. It leverages and contributes to benchmarks like 3DSRBench, CVBench-3D, and GQA to thoroughly assess 3D spatial reasoning. The identification and analysis of the "2D shortcut" problem in CVBench-3D demonstrate the necessity for carefully curated benchmarks that genuinely test 3D understanding, pushing the community to develop more robust evaluation methodologies.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation behind SpatialReasoner is to enable large vision-language models to achieve human-level intelligence in **3D spatial reasoning**, moving beyond superficial visual cues to genuine understanding of three-dimensional space. The authors identify several key objectives:

1. **Address the limitations of state-of-the-art LVLMs in 3D spatial reasoning:** Current models, despite their impressive multi-modal capabilities, often fail on seemingly simple 3D spatial questions, relying on implicit reasoning or 2D shortcuts. The paper aims to rectify this fundamental shortcoming.

2. **Introduce explicit 3D representations as a coherent interface:** The core objective is to integrate explicit 3D information, such as object locations and orientations, directly into the model's reasoning process. This explicit representation is designed to serve as a consistent and accurate interface shared between all stages of spatial reasoning: 3D perception (parsing), 3D computation (e.g., distances, angles), and 3D reasoning (deriving answers from computed properties). This addresses the inefficiency and inaccuracy of relying solely on natural language descriptions for 3D properties, as seen in models like Gemini 2.0.

3. **Enhance generalization to novel 3D spatial reasoning questions:** A major challenge for data-driven approaches is generalization beyond training data. The authors aim to develop a model that can apply its learned 3D understanding to unseen or compositionally novel question types, rather than just memorizing patterns. This goal is tackled through a multi-stage training strategy incorporating reinforcement learning.

4. **Improve "3D thinking" and "3D computation":** The paper explicitly articulates the need for models to decompose complex 3D questions into manageable steps ("3D thinking") and solve these steps accurately ("3D computation"). SpatialReasoner is designed to facilitate this structured approach.

5. **Enable interpretability and study failure modes:** By forcing the model to generate explicit 3D representations as intermediate results, the authors aim to make the reasoning process transparent. This interpretability allows for a detailed analysis of where and why LVLMs fail in 3D tasks, pinpointing whether errors originate from inaccurate 3D perception or flawed 3D computation/reasoning.

6. **Provide valuable insights for future 3D-aware LVLM development:** Beyond building a high-performing model, the research aims to conduct extensive ablations and analyses on various design choices, including data quality, training strategies (SFT vs. RL), and the role of explicit representations. The goal is to provide empirical findings and a "recipe" to guide the community in developing more robust and intelligent 3D-aware LVLMs.

Ultimately, the motivation is to bridge the gap between powerful language models and accurate 3D visual understanding, pushing towards more grounded, interpretable, and generalizable AI systems crucial for applications in robotics, embodied AI, and beyond.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

SpatialReasoner introduces a novel Large Vision-Language Model (LVLM) design centered around **explicit 3D representations** and a **multi-stage training strategy** to achieve robust and generalizable 3D spatial reasoning.

#### 3.1. Core Design: Explicit 3D Representations

The foundational idea is to integrate explicit 3D information directly into the model's reasoning process, acting as a shared interface.
- **Definition:** Explicit 3D representations include 3D locations (as vectors, e.g., (x, y, z) coordinates) and 3D orientations (e.g., Euler angles or quaternions) of objects.
- **Calibrated Camera 3D Space:** These representations are defined within a *calibrated camera 3D space*. This is a crucial detail, as it simplifies subsequent 3D computations. In this space, the z-axis aligns with the world's z-axis (e.g., vertical direction), and the origin is near the ground plane. This means z-coordinates directly correspond to object heights, and relationships like "above"/"below" are simplified. It also allows many 3D spatial relationships to be reduced to simpler 2D problems on a ground plane.
- **Unified Interface:** These explicit representations serve as a coherent interface across three stages of spatial reasoning:
 - **3D Perception (Parsing):** The model predicts object locations and orientations from image inputs.
 - **3D Computation:** It then uses these predicted 3D representations to compute explicit metrics like distances, angles, or relative positions.
 - **3D Reasoning:** Finally, it aggregates this explicit 3D information to answer complex spatial questions. This allows for compositional reasoning, where simple 3D computations are chained to solve more complex queries.

#### 3.2. Training Strategies: Multi-Stage Post-Training

SpatialReasoner adopts a two-stage post-training pipeline, building upon a pre-trained LVLM (Qwen2.5-VL-7B in this case).

- **Stage I: Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT)**
 - **Purpose:** To equip the LVLM with the ability to understand and predict explicit 3D representations. It's a critical initialization step.
 - **Mechanism:** Optimizes a maximum likelihood objective on curated 3D-annotated datasets.
 - **Outputs:** Enables the model to identify objects, predict their 3D locations and orientations, and expose interpretable intermediate reasoning traces (e.g., "The red box is at (X, Y, Z)").
 - **Limitation (addressed by Stage II):** SFT alone can lead to memorization of training patterns and limited generalization to novel compositions.

- **Stage II: Reinforcement Learning (RL)**
 - **Purpose:** To develop robust and generalizable 3D thinking capabilities, building upon the explicit 3D representations learned in Stage I.
 - **Mechanism:** Treats the spatial reasoning task as a sequential decision process. Policy gradients are optimized using GRPO.
 - **Reward Scheme:** A composite reward scheme is designed:
 - **Outcome Rewards:**
 - Accuracy Reward: Positive feedback for correct final answers (multiple-choice evaluation).
 - Format Reward: Encourages structured and readable outputs.
 - **Process Rewards (Explored for diagnostics/ablation):**
 - Reasoning Steps Reward: Promotes structured indicators (e.g., "First," "Next").
 - 3D-aware Process Reward: Checks for necessary spatial terms (e.g., "distance," "orientation") in intermediate steps.
 - **Benefit:** Empirically shown to substantially improve generalization to out-of-distribution 3D spatial questions and foster adaptive reasoning. The authors note that the KL divergence term in GRPO was removed in their final setting, as it was found to harm training accuracy.

#### 3.3. Training Data Generation

A critical component of the methodology is the generation of high-quality, 3D-aware training data. The authors extend existing pseudo-annotation pipelines using the OpenImages dataset.

- **Pseudo 3D Ground-truths:**
 - **Process:** Generates object category, 3D location, and 3D pose annotations on unlabeled OpenImages. Rule-based methods then derive ground-truth labels for 3D spatial relationships.
 - **Quality Control:** Recognizes the susceptibility of pseudo-annotations to factual errors (e.g., missing objects, inaccurate poses). Employs aggressive filtering steps:
 - Removing densely cluttered scenes.
 - Excluding hard-to-segment/pose-estimate object categories.
 - Discarding ambiguous boundary cases.

- **Human Verification (for a subset):** A smaller, higher-quality dataset was created by manually verifying the correctness of 3D pseudo-annotations to assess the impact of data quality.

- **Training Data Variants (for fine-tuning):**
 - **Basic3D-QA:** Contains simple 3D perception and 3D computation question-answering data, used to learn explicit 3D representations.
 - **SR-QA:** Standard visual question-answering pairs about various 3D spatial relationships, similar to previous 3D-aware datasets.
 - **SR-CoT (Chain-of-Thought):** Extends SR-QA by providing step-by-step reasoning with explicit 3D representations, guiding the model on how to decompose and solve problems.

This comprehensive approach combines robust model design with sophisticated training strategies and meticulous data curation, all aimed at fostering explicit, interpretable, and generalizable 3D spatial reasoning in LVLMs.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

SpatialReasoner achieves significant advancements in 3D spatial reasoning, supported by extensive experimental validation and insightful analyses:

- **State-of-the-Art Performance on 3DSRBench:** SpatialReasoner sets a new state-of-the-art with a mean accuracy of **60.3%** on the challenging 3DSRBench. This notably outperforms leading proprietary models like Gemini 2.0 Flash (49.8%) and Claude 3.5 Sonnet (48.2%), showcasing substantial gains across various 3D reasoning categories, including Location (+6.3%), Orientation (+14.6%), and Multi-Object (+8.2%) relative to the next best model. This confirms the effectiveness of its explicit 3D representations and multi-stage training.

- **Strong Generalization on Other Benchmarks:** The model also maintains strong performance on CVBench-3D and GQA. It achieves **87.3%** accuracy on depth-related questions in CVBench-3D and shows improved performance in the 'Compare' category of GQA, indicating enhanced multi-object reasoning.

- **Generalization Abilities: SFT vs. RL:**
 - **RL fosters better zero-shot generalization:** When multi-object training data is entirely removed, SpatialReasoner-Zero (RL-only) significantly outperforms SpatialReasoner-SFT (SFT-only) on novel multi-object questions (46.6% vs. 40.0%). This supports the finding that RL encourages the learning of transferable principles rather than simply memorizing training distributions.
 - **SFT benefits from direct supervision but can overfit:** When multi-object examples *are* present in training, SFT (SpatialReasoner-SFT) performs well (50.3%), suggesting it can memorize these structures. However, its performance drops sharply when these examples are removed (40.0%), confirming its tendency to overfit.
 - **SFT+RL (SpatialReasoner) is the best overall strategy:** The combined approach (SpatialReasoner) generally yields the highest performance across various benchmarks, balancing SFT's grounding with RL's adaptive reasoning.

- **Importance of Explicit 3D Representations:** An ablation study demonstrated that training SpatialReasoner-SFT *without* explicit 3D representations leads to a significant performance drop (51.9% vs. 58.3%), highlighting the crucial role of explicit intermediate 3D information and chain-of-thought reasoning in enhancing 3D spatial reasoning.

- **Training Strategy Insights (Data and Computation Scaling):**
 - **RL thrives on high-quality data:** When using only 1.2K human-verified 3D data, RL-trained models (SpatialReasoner) perform best.
 - **SFT scales better with noisy data:** SFT (SpatialReasoner-SFT) proves more robust to and benefits more from larger quantities of potentially noisy pseudo-annotated data (24K unverified data). This suggests SFT is more scalable when high-quality 3D data is scarce.
 - **SFT overfitting with more steps:** With increased training steps, SpatialReasoner-SFT shows signs of overfitting and performance degradation, while SpatialReasoner (SFT+RL) maintains stable and competitive performance.
 - **RL benefits over repeated SFT:** Applying a second round of SFT (SFT+SFT) degrades performance (54.7%) compared to the SFT+RL strategy (60.3%), reinforcing that RL promotes generalizable reasoning where repeated SFT exacerbates overfitting.

- **Process Reward Modeling:** Ablation studies reveal that 3D-aware process rewards can effectively induce interpretable multi-step reasoning trajectories without relying on costly human-curated chain-of-thought supervision, leading to a slight performance improvement (54.6% vs. 54.0% for SpatialReasoner-Zero).

- **KL Divergence Effect in RL:** Surprisingly, including a KL divergence term in GRPO (a common practice for stability) significantly impairs learning and leads to an eventual collapse in training accuracy for SpatialReasoner-Zero. Removing it allows for stable improvements.

- **Interpreting Failure Modes (3D Perception vs. 3D Reasoning):** By evaluating models on separate 3D perception and 3D reasoning tasks, the authors find that the majority of errors in downstream VQA tasks stem from **3D perception failures** (e.g., accurately estimating 3D locations and orientations from visual features). 3D reasoning (computing distances, angles, etc., given 3D information) is significantly more accurate. This suggests that improving the visual parsing capabilities of LVLMs is a key bottleneck for overall 3D spatial reasoning performance.

- **The "2D Shortcut" Problem:** The paper highlights that many baseline LVLMs exploit 2D shortcuts on benchmarks like CVBench-3D (e.g., using 2D bounding box distances to infer 3D distances). While this might lead to artificially high performance on such datasets, SpatialReasoner, by consistently applying explicit 3D reasoning, shows lower performance on these "shortcut" questions but demonstrates superior and more robust performance on genuinely challenging 3D spatial reasoning problems like those in 3DSRBench where 2D shortcuts are absent. This finding calls for more robust benchmark design.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

SpatialReasoner represents a crucial step forward in the capabilities of Large Vision-Language Models, with broad significance and potential impact across several domains:

- **Advancing True 3D Spatial Understanding:** The most immediate impact is the significant leap in enabling LVLMs to perform genuine 3D spatial reasoning. By moving beyond implicit, shortcut-prone methods to explicit, metric-based 3D representations, SpatialReasoner demonstrates that models can indeed grasp and utilize the complexities of three-dimensional space. This moves AI closer to human-level intelligence in perceiving and interacting with the physical world.

- **Enhanced Interpretability and Trustworthy AI:** The design choice of explicit 3D representations is paramount for interpretability. Unlike black-box models, SpatialReasoner provides clear, step-by-step reasoning traces that include predicted 3D locations, orientations, and computed relationships. This transparency is vital for understanding model behavior, diagnosing errors, and building more trustworthy AI systems, particularly in safety-critical applications.

- **Improved Generalization for Real-World Applications:** The demonstrated superior generalization capabilities, especially on novel 3D question types, are critical for deploying AI models in diverse and unpredictable real-world environments. A model that can reason compositionally about unseen combinations of objects and relationships will be far more robust and applicable than one that merely memorizes training examples.

- **Catalyst for Robotics and Embodied AI:** Accurate 3D spatial reasoning is a fundamental requirement for robotics and embodied AI systems. Robots need to precisely understand the 3D positions, orientations, and relationships of objects to perform tasks like grasping, navigation, and manipulation. SpatialReasoner's advancements directly contribute to building more intelligent and capable robots that can robustly operate in complex physical environments, aligning with research efforts referenced in the paper (e.g.,).

- **Guidance for Future LVLM Development:** The comprehensive ablation studies and analyses provide invaluable empirical insights into effective training strategies for 3D-aware LVLMs. The findings regarding the interplay of SFT and RL, the benefits of explicit representations, the impact of data quality (high-quality vs. noisy pseudo-annotations), and the often-overlooked implications of KL divergence offer a "recipe" for researchers and developers. This guidance can accelerate the development of the next generation of spatial intelligence models.

- **Refining AI Benchmarking:** The identification and detailed analysis of the "2D shortcut" problem in existing benchmarks serve as a crucial warning and a call to action for the AI community. It highlights the need for more rigorously designed evaluation datasets that genuinely test sophisticated reasoning abilities rather than allowing models to exploit superficial correlations. This will lead to more meaningful progress and prevent misleading performance metrics.

- **Bridging Vision and Language with Geometry:** SpatialReasoner successfully bridges the gap between sophisticated 3D parsing capabilities (often the domain of specialized computer vision models) and the powerful reasoning abilities of large language models. This synergistic integration of geometric understanding with linguistic reasoning paves the way for more holistic and intelligent multimodal AI systems.

In conclusion, SpatialReasoner not only sets a new benchmark in 3D spatial reasoning but also provides a deeper understanding of how to build and train such intelligent systems, fostering interpretability, generalization, and robust real-world applicability.
