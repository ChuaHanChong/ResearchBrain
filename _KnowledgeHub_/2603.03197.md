# Specificity-aware reinforcement learning for fine-grained open-world classification

> [!summary] Summary
> SpeciaRL is a reinforcement learning framework that fine- tunes Large Multimodal Models to generate more specific and correct predictions for fine-grained open-world image classification. The framework, employing a dynamic, specificity-aware reward system, improved the harmonic mean of specificity and correctness by up to 0.093 on fine-grained and 0.138 on very fine-grained benchmarks compared to the base model.

## Problem

- Reasoning Large Multimodal Models (LMMs) often produce overly generic predictions for fine-grained concepts in open-world image classification, despite possessing intrinsic domain knowledge.
- Naively encouraging more specific predictions through prompting or standard fine-tuning typically leads to a decrease in prediction correctness, highlighting a significant specificity-correctness trade-off.
- Existing reinforcement learning methods for LMMs are often tailored for tasks with programmatically verifiable solutions, not directly addressing the nuanced, open-ended nature of fine-grained classification.

## Method

- Developed **SpeciaRL**, a specificity-aware reinforcement learning framework based on ==Group Relative== Policy Optimization (GRPO), to fine-tune LMMs for open-world fine-grained image classification.
- Introduced a novel dynamic reward mechanism that adapts per sample, deriving a 'minimal specificity requirement' from the LMM's online 'Best-of-N' prediction for that sample to encourage achievable specificity.
- Employed an LLM-as-a-judge approach to categorize the relationship between LMM predictions and ground-truth labels into six ordered levels, enabling objective evaluation and reward assignment for both correctness and specificity.

## Results

- SpeciaRL achieved the highest Harmonic Mean (HM) across fine-grained (**0.883**) and very fine-grained (**0.830**) test sets, demonstrating the best balance between specificity and correctness, outperforming all baselines including SFT and RFT.
- The framework substantially reduced generic predictions while increasing specific and less specific correct predictions, improving specificity by **0.178** on fine-grained and **0.263** on very fine-grained datasets compared to the base model.
- SpeciaRL exhibited strong out-of-domain generalization and achieved state-of-the-art performance on general-purpose LMM evaluation metrics (Text Inclusion, Semantic Similarity, Concept Similarity), validating its robust advantage in enhancing LMM reasoning.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - LMMs possess substantial latent fine-grained domain knowledge, which is not consistently expressed in single-shot inferences, as evidenced by 'Best-of-N' rollouts yielding significantly more specific and correct answers.
> - A dynamic, adaptive reward strategy that leverages the LMM's current intrinsic capabilities (via online Best-of-N sampling) is crucial for simultaneously improving specificity and preserving correctness during reinforcement learning.
> - The LLM-as-a-judge method provides a robust and scalable way to evaluate the nuanced relationship between open-ended predictions and ground-truth labels, proving effective even with some classification errors.
