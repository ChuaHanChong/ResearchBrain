# Specificity-aware reinforcement learning for fine-grained open-world classification

> [!summary] Summary
> SpeciaRL is a reinforcement learning framework that fine- tunes Large Multimodal Models to generate more specific and correct predictions for fine-grained open-world image classification. The framework, employing a dynamic, specificity-aware reward system, improved the harmonic mean of specificity and correctness by up to 0.093 on fine-grained and 0.138 on very fine-grained benchmarks compared to the base model.

## Problem

- Reasoning Large Multimodal Models (LMMs) often produce overly generic predictions for fine-grained concepts in open-world image classification, despite possessing intrinsic domain knowledge.
- Naively encouraging more specific predictions through prompting or standard fine-tuning typically leads to a decrease in prediction correctness, highlighting a significant specificity-correctness trade-off.
- Existing reinforcement learning methods for LMMs are often tailored for tasks with programmatically verifiable solutions, not directly addressing the nuanced, open-ended nature of fine-grained classification.

## Method

- Developed **SpeciaRL**, a specificity-aware reinforcement learning framework based on ==Group Relative== Policy Optimization (GRPO), to fine-tune LMMs for open-world fine-grained image classification.
- Introduced a novel dynamic reward mechanism that adapts per sample, deriving a 'minimal specificity requirement' from the LMM's online 'Best-of-N' prediction for that sample to encourage achievable specificity.
- Employed an LLM-as-a-judge approach to categorize the relationship between LMM predictions and ground-truth labels into six ordered levels, enabling objective evaluation and reward assignment for both correctness and specificity.

## Results

- SpeciaRL achieved the highest Harmonic Mean (HM) across fine-grained (**0.883**) and very fine-grained (**0.830**) test sets, demonstrating the best balance between specificity and correctness, outperforming all baselines including SFT and RFT.
- The framework substantially reduced generic predictions while increasing specific and less specific correct predictions, improving specificity by **0.178** on fine-grained and **0.263** on very fine-grained datasets compared to the base model.
- SpeciaRL exhibited strong out-of-domain generalization and achieved state-of-the-art performance on general-purpose LMM evaluation metrics (Text Inclusion, Semantic Similarity, Concept Similarity), validating its robust advantage in enhancing LMM reasoning.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - LMMs possess substantial latent fine-grained domain knowledge, which is not consistently expressed in single-shot inferences, as evidenced by 'Best-of-N' rollouts yielding significantly more specific and correct answers.
> - A dynamic, adaptive reward strategy that leverages the LMM's current intrinsic capabilities (via online Best-of-N sampling) is crucial for simultaneously improving specificity and preserving correctness during reinforcement learning.
> - The LLM-as-a-judge method provides a robust and scalable way to evaluate the nuanced relationship between open-ended predictions and ground-truth labels, proving effective even with some classification errors.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research addresses a specific challenge within the field of image classification, particularly focusing on fine-grained concepts in open-world settings. Traditionally, image classification models operate in a closed-world paradigm, where all possible semantic categories are predefined. However, real-world applications often necessitate handling novel or emerging categories, a task addressed by open-world classification. The advent of large pre-trained vision-language models (VLMs) and Large Multimodal Models (LMMs) has facilitated advancements in this area, allowing models to generate candidate concepts from vast textual corpora or through open-ended prompts.

Recent studies have evaluated LMMs as image classifiers in open-world contexts, noting their strong visual understanding capabilities. However, these models, particularly reasoning LMMs, tend to produce predictions that are often overly generic when classifying fine-grained visual concepts (e.g., "flower" instead of "daisy"). A central challenge in this domain is to enhance the specificity of predictions without compromising their correctness, as naive attempts (e.g., explicit prompting for specificity or certain fine-tuning approaches) often lead to an increase in incorrect outputs. This trade-off between specificity and correctness remains an understudied area.

Reinforcement Learning (RL) has emerged as an effective post-training strategy for improving the reasoning capabilities of LMMs. Approaches like RL from Human Feedback (RLHF) and RL with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) have been employed to align models with desired behaviors. While RLVR is typically applied to tasks with structured, programmatically verifiable solutions (e.g., mathematics or code generation), recent work has extended its applicability to other domains through model-based verifiers. This paper contributes to this broader landscape by adapting RLVR for the nuanced evaluation and improvement of LMMs in open-world fine-grained image classification, introducing a novel dynamic reward mechanism to balance specificity and correctness. It leverages an LLM-based verifier to assess the quality of open-ended predictions, moving beyond simple string matching or fixed taxonomies.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the specificity of predictions generated by reasoning Large Multimodal Models (LMMs) for fine-grained open-world image classification, while concurrently preserving or improving the correctness of these predictions. The authors aim to overcome the observed limitation of LMMs producing overly generic outputs in such tasks.

The motivation for this work stems from several key observations:
- **LMMs' Tendency Towards Genericity:** Existing reasoning LMMs exhibit a strong capacity for visual understanding but frequently generate high-level, generic classifications when presented with fine-grained visual concepts. For instance, an LMM might correctly identify an image as "flower" but fail to classify it as a specific type, such as "daisy." Preliminary analysis by the authors confirmed this tendency, especially in very fine-grained datasets, where a significant majority of predictions (e.g., 74.5% for Qwen2.5VL-7B) were categorized as generic.
- **The Specificity-Correctness Trade-off:** Naively encouraging more specific predictions, such as through direct prompts like "be specific" or standard fine-tuning methods like supervised fine-tuning (SFT) or reinforcement fine-tuning (RFT), often results in a decrease in correctness. Models, when pushed for specificity, may produce more incorrect labels. This highlights a non-trivial challenge in achieving both attributes simultaneously.
- **Intrinsic Domain Knowledge of LMMs:** A critical insight gained from the authors' preliminary analysis is that LMMs do possess substantial intrinsic fine-grained domain knowledge. This was evidenced by the "Best-of-N" (BoN) experiment, where taking the most informative prediction from multiple inference rollouts (e.g., 64 attempts) for a given image often yielded significantly more specific and correct answers than a single-shot inference. This suggests that the issue is not a fundamental lack of knowledge within the model, but rather an inefficiency in its sampling process or reasoning path that biases it toward generic outputs.
- **Need for a Balanced Fine-tuning Strategy:** Given that LMMs possess the latent capability for fine-grained classification but struggle to consistently express it, the motivation shifted to developing a fine-tuning strategy that could "steer" the model's behavior. This strategy needed to elicit the model's maximum specificity potential without forcing it beyond its capabilities, thereby preventing the introduction of incorrect predictions. Reinforcement learning was identified as a suitable paradigm for this goal due to its effectiveness in shaping model behavior and improving reasoning performance.

In essence, the research is motivated by the gap between LMMs' latent fine-grained understanding and their expressed generic predictions, and the challenge of bridging this gap without sacrificing accuracy.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology presented in this paper, named SpeciaRL, is a specificity-aware reinforcement learning framework designed to fine-tune reasoning LMMs for fine-grained open-world classification. The approach comprises several key components:

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation

The task is defined as open-world image classification, where an LMM `Φ_θ_LMM` maps an image `I` to a semantic concept `s` from a vast, unconstrained natural language semantic space `S`. The generative nature of LMMs and the open-world setting allow for multiple correct predictions at varying levels of specificity. The objective is to promote predictions that are both correct and maximally specific.

#### 3.2. Prediction Evaluation

A core component is the method for automatically assessing both the correctness and specificity of LMM predictions against ground-truth labels `y`.
- **Prediction Categorization:** An LLM-as-a-judge approach is employed, using a robust Large Language Model `Ψ_LLM` (Llama3-72B for evaluation, Qwen3-30B for training) to categorize the relationship between a prediction `p` and ground-truth `y`. Six mutually exclusive categories are defined, ordered by informativeness:
 - **Wrong (W):** Incorrect prediction.
 - **Abstain (A):** Refusal to answer.
 - **Generic (G):** Correct but significantly broader (e.g., "dog" for "samoyed").
 - **Less Specific (S-):** Correct but a closely related parent category (e.g., "warbler" for "golden-winged warbler").
 - **Specific (S):** Exact match or direct synonym.
 - **More Specific (S+):** More specific than the ground-truth.
- **Specificity and Correctness Measures:**
 - **Correctness:** Calculated as the percentage of non-Wrong predictions.
 - **Specificity:** Computed as the average normalized score across non-Wrong predictions, where each category (A, G, S-, S, S+) is assigned a numerical score (1, 2, 3, 4, 4 respectively), reflecting its informativeness.
 - **Harmonic Mean (HM):** A combined metric that balances specificity and correctness, providing an overall performance score.

#### 3.3. Specificity-aware Reinforcement Learning (SpeciaRL)

SpeciaRL builds upon the Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) paradigm, specifically using the Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) algorithm.
- **GRPO Foundation:** GRPO generates multiple diverse outputs (N rollouts) for each input and optimizes the policy to favor responses with higher rewards within each group.
- **Dynamic Reward Design:** Unlike standard RLVR, which typically uses a binary reward for exact matches, SpeciaRL introduces a novel specificity-aware dynamic reward `r*I(p, y)`. This reward is adapted per sample based on the LMM's current capabilities.
 - **Reference Level `c*`:** For each image `I` and ground-truth `y`, a "minimal specificity requirement" `c*` is determined. `c*` is derived from the "Best-of-N" (BoN) prediction (`BoN_y(I)`) among `N` rollouts generated by the *current policy model*. This `BoN` prediction identifies the highest specificity level the model can intrinsically achieve for that specific sample.
 - **`c*` Adjustments:**
 - If `BoN`'s category `c_best` is `S+`, `c*` is set to `S` (to avoid pushing for overly specific predictions beyond the ground-truth granularity).
 - If `c_best` is `W`, `c*` is set to `A` (to reward abstention when the model struggles significantly).
 - Otherwise, `c*` is set directly to `c_best`.
 - **Reward Assignment:** A prediction `p` receives a positive reward (`1`) if its categorized specificity level `c_y(p)` is equal to or more informative than `c*`; otherwise, it receives a `0` reward. This dynamic reward encourages the model to achieve its current maximal potential for specificity without penalizing for inherent limitations on difficult samples, thereby protecting correctness.
- **Online Computation:** The `BoN` prediction and, consequently, `c*` are computed online during training using the `N` rollouts generated by GRPO, integrating seamlessly and efficiently into the RL framework.

#### 3.4. Experimental Setup

- **Base Model:** Qwen2.5VL-7B, a reasoning LMM.
- **Training Data:** 3000 samples from the CUB dataset (bird species), intentionally chosen to be out-of-domain from the main evaluation datasets to assess generalization.
- **Evaluation Data:** Fine-grained datasets (Flowers102, Food101, OxfordPets) and very fine-grained datasets (StanfordCars, FGVCAircraft).
- **Optimizations:** The vLLM inference engine and a caching mechanism for the LLM-as-a-judge were implemented to manage the computational demands of LMM inference and LLM-based evaluation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presented several findings, both from preliminary analysis and from the evaluation of the proposed SpeciaRL framework.

#### 4.1. Preliminary Analysis on LMM Behavior

- **Overly Generic Predictions:** The initial analysis confirmed that reasoning LMMs, specifically Qwen2.5VL-7B, exhibit a notable tendency to produce generic predictions in open-world fine-grained classification. On the fine-grained benchmark, 39.4% of predictions were Generic, increasing to 74.5% on the very fine-grained set. This indicates a significant gap in expressing fine-grained details.
- **Intrinsic Fine-grained Knowledge:** The "Best-of-N" (BoN-64) experiment, which sampled the best prediction from 64 rollouts, demonstrated that the LMM possessed substantial intrinsic fine-grained knowledge. BoN-64 significantly improved both specificity and correctness compared to single-shot inference (e.g., for fine-grained, HM increased from 0.790 to 0.933; for very fine-grained, from 0.692 to 0.868). This suggested that the limitation was in reliable knowledge expression rather than a lack of knowledge.
- **Specificity-Correctness Trade-off:** Simple prompting ("Be specific") or fine-tuning (SFT, RFT) to encourage specificity often led to a reduction in correctness, underscoring the challenge this work addresses.

#### 4.2. SpeciaRL Performance (Table 1)

- **Overall Best Trade-off:** SpeciaRL achieved the highest Harmonic Mean (HM) across both the fine-grained and very fine-grained test groups, indicating the best balance between specificity and correctness.
 - On fine-grained datasets, SpeciaRL achieved an HM of 0.883, improving upon the base Qwen2.5VL-7B (HM=0.790), SFT (HM=0.866), and RFT (HM=0.825). Notably, it improved both specificity (0.920 vs. 0.742) and correctness (0.848 vs. 0.846) compared to the base model.
 - On very fine-grained datasets, SpeciaRL achieved an HM of 0.830, outperforming Qwen2.5VL-7B (HM=0.692), SFT (HM=0.814), and RFT (HM=0.821). While its correctness was slightly lower than the base model (0.855 vs. 0.919), its substantial gain in specificity (0.818 vs. 0.555) resulted in a higher overall HM.
- **Outperformance of Baselines:** SpeciaRL consistently surpassed zero-shot LMMs, retrieval-based methods (CaSED), and other fine-tuned baselines (SFT, RFT) in terms of HM.
- **Categorical Distribution Shift:** SpeciaRL significantly reduced the proportion of Generic predictions and increased Specific and Less Specific predictions compared to the base model, demonstrating its effectiveness in pushing for finer-grained outputs.

#### 4.3. Performance on General-Purpose Benchmark (Table 2)

- When evaluated using a general-purpose LMM benchmark protocol (Conti et al.), SpeciaRL achieved state-of-the-art performance on three out of four metrics (Text Inclusion, Semantic Similarity, Concept Similarity) for both fine-grained and very fine-grained test sets. This further validates its robust advantage.

#### 4.4. Qualitative Results (Figure 4, 11)

- Qualitative examples illustrated that SpeciaRL produced more specific and fine-grained predictions. Its reasoning traces often incorporated fine visual details to deduce specific classes, indicating an enhanced, goal-oriented reasoning process, whereas the base model might identify details but still output a generic label.

#### 4.5. Ablation Studies

- **Dynamic Reward Effectiveness:** The specificity-aware dynamic reward was found to be crucial. SpeciaRL consistently achieved a higher HM than all static reward variants, with the standard binary reward performing worst among them (Table 3). This highlights the importance of adaptively rewarding predictions based on the model's achievable potential.
- **Optimal Rollout Size (N):** Varying the number of online rollouts `N` during training revealed that `N=10` and `N=5` yielded the best performance, while `N=15` led to degraded results (Table 4). This suggests an optimal range for `N` to balance exploration and computational efficiency.
- **Training Data Generalization:** SpeciaRL trained on CUB (birds) demonstrated strong out-of-domain generalization across diverse fine-grained datasets (Table 9). Performance was generally best on in-domain data, but positive transfer was observed (e.g., CUB-trained outperforming in-domain on Flowers102).
- **Training Data Size Impact:** Performance, particularly correctness, improved with increasing training data size, showing saturation in specificity around 2000 samples (Table 10, 11).
- **RL Algorithm Compatibility:** The dynamic reward proved compatible and effective across different on-policy RL algorithms (GRPO, Dr.GRPO, DAPO), consistently improving performance over their static reward counterparts (Table 13).
- **LLM-as-a-Judge Robustness:** The LLM-as-a-judge showed high agreement rates (Cohen’s κ > 0.81) with alternative large LLM verifiers and robustness to prompt variations. SpeciaRL training was largely insensitive to moderate levels of injected judge classification errors (up to 10%), indicating the robustness of the reward signal (Table 14, 15).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research introduces SpeciaRL, a framework that addresses a notable limitation of current Large Multimodal Models (LMMs) in open-world fine-grained classification. The significance and potential impact of this work are multifaceted:

- **Advancement in Fine-grained Classification:** By demonstrating a method that promotes specificity without compromising correctness, SpeciaRL directly advances the state-of-the-art in fine-grained open-world image classification. This is critical for applications demanding detailed object recognition beyond broad categories.
- **Enhanced LMM Utility:** The ability to consistently generate more specific and accurate predictions makes LMMs more useful for a wider range of real-world tasks where fine distinctions are important. This includes scientific image analysis (e.g., distinguishing species), detailed product cataloging, medical imaging interpretation, and specialized content moderation.
- **Novel Reinforcement Learning Paradigm:** The introduction of a dynamic, specificity-aware reward signal, anchored to the model's online best performance, represents a novel contribution to reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR). This approach extends RLVR beyond tasks with strictly verifiable solutions, offering a blueprint for other open-ended generative tasks where qualitative or hierarchical assessment of outputs is necessary.
- **Improved Reasoning Capabilities:** The qualitative results suggest that SpeciaRL not only refines the final output but also guides the LMM's internal reasoning process to be more goal-oriented and focused on fine-grained visual evidence. This implies a deeper integration of specificity into the model's cognitive architecture, rather than merely a superficial output modification.
- **Robust Generalization:** The demonstrated out-of-domain generalization capability, where a model trained on bird species effectively improves performance across diverse domains like flowers, food, pets, cars, and aircraft, indicates that SpeciaRL learns transferable principles for specificity and correctness. This suggests a true enhancement of reasoning ability rather than mere memorization of training categories.
- **Methodological Robustness:** The comprehensive ablation studies, including variations in reward settings, rollout sizes, training data configurations (domain, scale, diversity), and compatibility with different RL algorithms, establish the robustness and broad applicability of the SpeciaRL approach. The validation of the LLM-as-a-judge against alternative models and prompt variations further reinforces the reliability of the evaluation mechanism.
- **Open Science Contribution:** The public availability of code and models contributes to reproducibility and encourages further research and development in this domain. This can accelerate the exploration of specificity-aware fine-tuning across different LMM architectures and applications.

In summary, SpeciaRL offers a significant step towards developing LMMs that are not only capable of understanding visual information but can articulate that understanding with high precision and reliability, addressing a long-standing challenge in computer vision and artificial intelligence.
