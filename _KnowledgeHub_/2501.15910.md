# The Sample Complexity of Online Reinforcement Learning: A Multi-model Perspective

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at the Max Planck Institute for Intelligent Systems and Inria Paris introduce a multi-model framework for online reinforcement learning in continuous state-action spaces with nonlinear dynamics and non-episodic settings. The framework provides frequentist policy-regret guarantees across finite, general, and parametrized model classes, while ensuring closed-loop stability.

## Problem

- Achieving sublinear policy regret in online, non-episodic reinforcement learning for continuous state-action spaces with general nonlinear dynamics.
- Addressing the exploration-exploitation dilemma in systems where dynamics are unknown and state resets are not possible, leading to time-correlated information.
- Providing robust, nonasymptotic frequentist performance guarantees for algorithms over a finite time horizon, moving beyond asymptotic or Bayesian guarantees.

## Method

- A suite of algorithms employs ==Hedge-type updates==, sampling models from a distribution based on their one-step prediction error.
- Certainty-equivalent control is applied using the selected model's policy, augmented with an explicit Gaussian excitation signal to ensure sufficient information for model discrimination.
- Model identification is decoupled from control policy synthesis, enabling pre-computation of feedback policies offline and reducing online computational burden.

## Results

- Achieved policy regret bounded by `O(d_u ln(N)/∆)` for finite model sets, `O(sqrt(N))` for general bounded L-Lipschitz functions, and `O(sqrt(d_u N p))` for parametrically defined models, matching known theoretical lower bounds up to logarithmic factors.
- Demonstrated rapid convergence of the selected model to the true (or best approximating) system dynamics, with the probability of misidentification decaying at `1/(k-M)^2` for iteration `k`.
- Ensured uniform boundedness of the second moment of state trajectories `E[|x_k|^2]` over time, providing guarantees for closed-loop stability and benign transient behaviors even under exploration.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A multi-model perspective, integrating elements from Hedge-type updates and posterior sampling, effectively addresses the exploration-exploitation dilemma in continuous nonlinear systems.
> - The explicit addition of excitation during online learning is crucial for satisfying persistence of excitation conditions and enabling rapid, provable model identification.
> - By leveraging Lyapunov stability analysis and concentration inequalities, sublinear policy regret and uniform boundedness of state trajectories can be guaranteed for broad classes of nonlinear systems.
