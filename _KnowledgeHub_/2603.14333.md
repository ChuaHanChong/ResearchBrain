# Data-Driven Physics Embedded Dynamics with Predictive Control and Reinforcement Learning for Quadrupeds

> [!summary] Summary
> An RL-MPC framework integrates Lagrangian Neural Networks (LNNs) with an inverse-dynamics planning formulation to enable real-time, physics-consistent locomotion for quadrupeds. This approach achieved up to 4x faster inference latency on a Unitree Go1 robot compared to forward-dynamics LNNs while maintaining robust performance across complex terrains.

## Problem

- Existing data-driven dynamics models in RL-MPC for quadrupeds suffer from compounding errors over long horizons and lack physical interpretability, limiting generalization.
- Purely physics-based models are computationally demanding for real-time control of high-DoF legged robots with complex contact dynamics.
- Prior Lagrangian Neural Network (LNN) implementations for model-based control are often too computationally intensive for real-time inference on systems like quadrupeds.

## Method

- Integrates Lagrangian Neural Networks (LNNs) into an RL-==MPC== framework to learn physically consistent, interpretable dynamics for quadrupedal locomotion.
- Develops an encoder and physics-informed Dreamer module to provide full-state estimates and generate predicted trajectories for the expert actor.
- Implements an inverse-dynamics infinite-horizon Model Predictive Control (MPC) scheme for real-time deployment, which optimizes joint trajectories directly and bypasses computationally expensive mass matrix inversions.

## Results

- The framework achieved a **4x** reduction in inference latency, running at approximately 30 ms for a 16-step horizon, enabling real-time 50 Hz control, while maintaining competitive episodic returns.
- Prediction errors over long horizons were significantly reduced and more stable compared to opaque neural networks and simplified CoM models, aligning with performance of full LNN forward dynamics.
- Validated on a Unitree Go1 robot, the system exhibited successful sim-to-real transfer and robust locomotion on challenging terrains (e.g., stairs, gravel) with consistent error tracking over 1200 time steps.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Embedding Lagrangian mechanics into neural network dynamics (LNNs) provides strong physical inductive biases that improve sample efficiency and reduce long-horizon prediction errors.
> - An inverse-dynamics planning formulation can drastically reduce computational overhead during real-time MPC inference by avoiding explicit mass matrix inversions, making high-frequency control feasible.
> - Combining physics-consistent LNNs with an inverse-dynamics MPC allows for a Pareto optimal trade-off between computational speed and control performance, crucial for real-time deployment on quadrupeds.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of quadrupedal locomotion has seen significant advancements by integrating Model Predictive Control (MPC) with Reinforcement Learning (RL). This combination enables complex motion planning and adaptive behaviors across various terrains. Traditional approaches for modeling system dynamics in robotics rely on first principles, yielding interpretable and physically consistent models. However, these methods often require precise system parameters and can be computationally intensive, especially for complex, multi-degree-of-freedom (DoF) systems like legged robots.

Conversely, data-driven machine learning methods, often referred to as Opaque Neural Network (ONN) dynamics, learn system behavior from large datasets without explicit physics. While flexible, these models typically lack interpretability, struggle with generalization to unseen scenarios, and accumulate errors over long prediction horizons, which can degrade control performance.

This limitation has led to increased interest in hybrid approaches that merge the strengths of physics-based modeling with data-driven learning. Lagrangian Neural Networks (LNNs) and Hamiltonian Neural Networks (HNNs) are examples of such frameworks. LNNs, in particular, embed Lagrangian mechanics into learned dynamics by parameterizing the system's Lagrangian and deriving equations of motion via the Euler-Lagrange formulation. This approach aims to produce physically consistent, interpretable predictions that improve generalization and reliability for planning. Previous work, such as Deep Lagrangian Networks (DeLaN), has demonstrated the viability of LNNs in model-based control for lower DoF systems, and LNNs have also been applied in model-based RL. However, their computational cost for real-time inference has limited their widespread adoption in high-DoF systems like quadrupeds, particularly when dealing with contact dynamics and underactuation.

Existing RL-MPC frameworks often employ a Dreamer module to learn ONN dynamics, a reward estimator, a policy, and a value function for long-horizon control. During inference, these systems solve an approximation of an infinite-horizon trajectory optimization problem, warm-started by an RL policy to expedite convergence. While effective, these ONN-based dynamics still suffer from generalization issues and long-horizon errors. This research aims to address these challenges by integrating physics-consistent LNNs into an RL-MPC framework for quadrupedal locomotion, striving for a balance between computational efficiency and physical consistency for robust real-time planning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations for this research are:

- **Addressing Compounding Errors and Limited Interpretability in State-of-the-Art Approaches:** Current RL-MPC methods for quadrupedal locomotion, while capable of complex motions, often accumulate errors over long prediction horizons and lack interpretability due to the absence of physical inductive biases. The authors aim to mitigate these issues.
- **Integrating Physically Consistent Dynamics Learning:** The core motivation is to incorporate Lagrangian Neural Networks (LNNs) into an RL-MPC framework. This integration is intended to enable the learning of physically consistent dynamics, which can improve generalization, reduce long-horizon errors, and provide more interpretable models compared to unstructured neural dynamics.
- **Improving Computational Efficiency for Real-Time Deployment:** LNNs, while offering physical consistency, can be computationally intensive, especially for real-time inference in high-DoF systems like quadrupeds. The authors aim to develop an inverse-dynamics infinite-horizon MPC scheme that avoids costly mass matrix inversions during deployment, thereby enhancing computational efficiency.
- **Enhancing Sample Efficiency of Dynamics Models:** The paper seeks to improve the sample efficiency of the dynamics model during training by leveraging physics priors embedded in LNNs.
- **Reducing Rollout Errors During Deployment:** By learning physically consistent dynamics, the research intends to reduce the accumulation of errors during long-horizon rollouts, which is critical for stable and accurate control.
- **Enabling Real-World Viability:** A key motivation is to demonstrate the practical applicability and robustness of the proposed framework on physical hardware, specifically the Unitree Go1 robot, across diverse terrain types, to confirm its real-world viability.
- **Benchmarking and Validation:** The research aims to conduct extensive empirical validation through multiple ablations of the proposed LNN and its variants, comparing performance against various baselines to highlight the advantages in sample efficiency, long-horizon error reduction, and real-time planning speed.

In summary, the motivation stems from the need for quadruped locomotion systems that are not only agile and adaptable but also exhibit long-term stability, physical consistency, and computational efficiency for real-time operation in complex, unstructured environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework integrates Lagrangian Neural Networks (LNNs) with an RL-MPC pipeline for real-time quadrupedal locomotion. The methodology consists of three main technical components: the LNN dynamics model, an encoder and physics-informed Dreamer module built upon it, and a deployment-time inverse-dynamics planner.

#### 3.1. Preliminaries and Problem Formulation

The problem is formulated as an infinite-horizon discounted Partially Observable Markov Decision Process (POMDP).
- **State (S):** Full quadruped state, including generalized positions `q` and generalized velocities `q̇` (18 DoF each, 36 total).
- **Observation (O):** Proprioceptive signals including joint angles (`Θ`), joint velocities (`Θ̇`), projected gravity (`g_p`), and previous action (`a_t-1`), augmented with desired linear and angular velocity commands (`v_cmd`).
- **Action (A):** Joint angle offsets applied to nominal joint angles. These are converted to joint torques (`u`) via an actuator network.
- **Reward Function:** Designed to promote tracking of commanded velocities, desired body height, and stable body orientation.

#### 3.2. RL Training

The training design is based on an Asymmetric Actor-Critic formulation of model-free RL.
- **Asymmetric Actor-Critic:** An expert actor receives augmented inputs from a Dreamer module, while the critic uses privileged observations (e.g., height field, external forces, robot's linear velocity, unobservable data). This addresses information asymmetry.
- **Key Changes:**
 1. The encoder is trained to output full-body state estimates.
 2. The Dreamer module is physics-informed through LNNs.
- **Deferred Planning:** Planning (MPC) is deferred to the deployment phase to avoid the high computational and memory costs of running MPC during thousands of parallel training rollouts.

#### 3.3. Lagrangian Neural Networks (LNNs)

The LNNs embed physical structure into a learnable dynamics model by parameterizing the Lagrangian `L = 1/2 q̇ᵀM(q)q̇ - V(q)`. The Euler-Lagrange equations are used to derive the equations of motion: `M(q)q̈ + C(q, q̇)q̇ + G(q) = Bu + H(q, q̇)`.
- **Mass Matrix Parameterization:** The mass matrix `M(q)` is parameterized as a symmetric positive-definite (SPD) matrix using `M_φ(q) = Y_φ(q)Y_φ(q)ᵀ + εI`, where `Y_φ(q)` is a learnable lower triangular matrix from a Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP).
- **Next State Prediction (Forward Dynamics):** The continuous-time dynamics `d/dt [q, q̇] = [q̇, M_φ⁻¹(q) (Bu + H_φ(q, q̇) - C_φ(q, q̇)q̇ - G_φ(q))]` are discretized using first-order integration to obtain `[q_t+1, q̇_t+1] = f_φ(q_t, q̇_t, u_t)`. This `f_φ` is the forward dynamics map.

#### 3.4. Encoder & Dreamer Module

The Dreamer module is used to augment the expert actor's input with predicted future states. It comprises a learned dynamics model (`d_φ`), policy (`π_φ`), reward function (`r_φ`), and value function (`V_φ`), all operating on full state information `z_t = (q_t, q̇_t)`.
- **Full-State Estimation:** An encoder network `E_φ: O_M → R³⁶` maps a history of proprioceptive observations `o_t-M:t` to the full physical state `z_t`. It is trained via supervised regression: `L_Encoder = ||E_φ(o_t-M:t) - z_t||²`.
- **Physics-Informed Dreaming (Algorithm 1):** The estimated state `z_t` is used to generate a predicted trajectory `y_t`. Future states are obtained by rolling out the Lagrangian dynamics model `d_φ` (which is `f_φ` combined with an actuator network) under the dreamer policy `π_φ`. The dreamt trajectory `y_t` then augments the expert actor's input.
- **Supervised Training of Dreamer Module:** All networks within the Dreamer module are trained using supervised targets from the replay buffer. The combined loss function `L_Dreamer` minimizes the squared difference between predicted and ground-truth values for dynamics, reward, value, and policy, ensuring sample-efficient training without backpropagation through imagined rollouts. The Dreamer policy serves as a warm-start for MPC at inference and is not executed as a standalone controller.

#### 3.5. Deployment: Planning with Inverse Dynamics

During inference, a planning approach is adopted where the optimization objective aims to maximize accumulated rewards and future value over a horizon `H` (Equation 2).
- **Computational Challenges (Forward vs. Inverse Dynamics):** Direct application of forward dynamics MPC policies on quadrupeds is computationally intensive due to repeated mass matrix inversions, particularly for sampling-based optimization algorithms.
- **Inverse Dynamics Formulation (Equation 3):** To address this, the authors propose an inverse dynamics optimization formulation that operates directly on joint positions `q`. The state evolution becomes a simple linear rollout, encapsulating nonlinearities in the reward calculation via `u`. The inverse dynamics model `g_φ` computes `Bu = M_φ(q)q̈ + C_φ(q, q̇)q̇ + G_φ(q) - H_φ(q, q̇)`. Velocity and acceleration are approximated using a finite-difference operator `F`.
- **Algorithm 2 (Inverse Dynamics-Based ∞ Horizon MPC):** The MPC solver uses a warm-start from previous MPC solutions and Dreamer rollouts. It samples joint trajectories, computes corresponding torques using `g_φ`, evaluates them using learned rewards and value functions, and updates the distribution for the next sampling round via a Gaussian fit to elite trajectories. A penalty term `λ · RootForcePenalty(Bu)` is included to handle underactuation.
- **Pros and Cons of Inverse Dynamics:** This formulation avoids explicit matrix inversions, significantly reducing computational cost. However, for underactuated systems, ensuring zero root forces in the optimal solution is challenging with sampling-based methods. The approach addresses this by discarding trajectories with high root forces, noting that sensitivity to the penalty weight `λ` requires further study.

#### 3.6. Baselines

The framework is compared against:
- **ONN (Opaque Neural Network):** An unstructured baseline that directly maps current observations and actions to the next observation without physics priors.
- **LNN Forward:** Standard LNN where the mass matrix is fully learned and inverted during training and inference.
- **DeLaN (Deep Lagrangian Network):** Models dynamics using the Lagrangian, but diagonalizes the mass matrix for computational efficiency.
- **LNN Inverse:** Uses an inverse dynamics formulation during training to predict torques.
- **CoM LNN:** Simplifies the system to a single rigid body, modeling Center of Mass (CoM) dynamics with Lagrangian structure.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research provides a comprehensive evaluation of the proposed framework, including training performance, deployment-time effectiveness, and real-world validation.

#### 4.1. Training and Sample Efficiency

- **ONN:** Exhibited the lowest per-iteration compute cost (≈ 4.5 seconds) but maintained significantly higher training loss throughout, indicating poor sample efficiency.
- **LNN-based methods (including Ours):** Demonstrated faster convergence and lower training loss compared to ONN.
- **LNN Forward:** Achieved the lowest overall training loss but incurred the highest computational cost per iteration (≈ 22 seconds) due to full mass matrix inversion, limiting its suitability for real-time applications.
- **Our method (equivalent to DeLaN in training):** Achieved lower training loss due to physics priors while maintaining a relatively low per-iteration cost (≈ 17.5 seconds) through diagonalization. It showed comparable loss to LNN Inverse, with additional advantages in inference speed and planning robustness.
- **Conclusion:** Incorporating physical priors and Lagrangian structure improves sample efficiency and long-term accuracy, compensating for increased computational overhead during training.

#### 4.2. H-step Prediction Error

- **ONN:** Showed the highest prediction error, with severe compounding over longer horizons.
- **CoM LNN:** Provided moderate improvements but still accumulated noticeable drift.
- **Our method:** Maintained stable error profiles with increasing horizons, closely matching the performance of LNN Forward and LNN Inverse, indicating effective long-horizon prediction accuracy essential for MPC.

#### 4.3. Balancing Inference Speed and Performance

- **ONN:** Achieved the lowest inference latency across all horizons, but its returns deteriorated significantly for planning horizons beyond H=8 due to compounding errors and lack of physical priors.
- **LNN Forward:** Consistently achieved the highest returns but suffered from computational bottlenecks due to full-matrix inversion, leading to poor scalability and making real-time deployment challenging.
- **DeLaN:** Showed strong performance, with stable rollouts even at high horizons, but its inference time increased steeply, reaching over 80 ms per control step at H=16, which is unsuitable for high-frequency MPC (> 50 Hz).
- **CoM LNN:** Reduced computational cost compared to DeLaN but still experienced increasing latency with horizon due to forward integration and mass matrix inversion. Performance struggled at H > 10, indicating limitations of simplified dynamics.
- **Our method:** Achieved significantly lower inference latency (≈ 30 ms at H=16), nearly 4x faster than LNN Forward and DeLaN, by employing an inverse dynamics-based rollout that optimizes directly over joint positions without matrix inversions. Despite this efficiency, it maintained competitive returns across all horizons, outperforming ONN and CoM LNN at H > 10, and closely matching DeLaN and LNN Forward up to H=12.
- **Specific Trade-off (H=9):** On demanding stair terrain, our method achieved 98% of DeLaN’s episodic return (7.18 vs. 7.31) while running approximately 4x faster, making it capable of sustaining 50 Hz control, representing a deliberate Pareto trade-off for real-time deployment.

#### 4.4. Multi-Terrain Performance (Simulation)

- **DeLaN:** Achieved the highest rewards across various terrains (flat ground, slopes, rough terrain, stairs) among planning-based controllers, attributed to its structured formulation and stability under forward rollouts.
- **Our method:** Performed competitively, closely matching DeLaN on flat ground and outperforming CoM LNN and ONN on stairs. It maintained effective long-horizon planning capability due to sufficient predictive consistency despite using inverse dynamics.
- **ONN:** Showed degraded performance in complex terrains due to the absence of physical priors.
- **HIMLoco (non-planning RL baseline):** Performed reasonably but lacked the interpretability and planning benefits.

#### 4.5. Hardware Validation

- **Deployment on Unitree Go1:** The method was successfully deployed on a Unitree Go1 robot and tested in unstructured outdoor environments (loose gravel, ramps, flat pavement, staircases).
- **Error Tracking (Fig. 6):** Our method consistently maintained lower and more stable absolute hardware rollout error (averaging ∼0.1) over 1200 time steps compared to ONN and other LNN variants, which exhibited higher variance and drift.
- **Robustness:** The controller demonstrated reactivity to terrain irregularities (e.g., foot slippage, variable stair heights) due to its 50Hz on-device rollout speed and predictive consistency. It recovered from foot perturbations during stair climbing without pre-programmed heuristics.
- **Conclusion:** The hardware validation demonstrated successful sim-to-real transfer and the robustness of the proposed inverse-dynamics-based controller in real-world scenarios.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a significant advancement in quadrupedal locomotion control by addressing critical limitations of existing data-driven and physics-based approaches. The integration of Lagrangian Neural Networks (LNNs) into an RL-MPC framework offers several important contributions:

- **Enhanced Physical Consistency and Interpretability:** By embedding Lagrangian mechanics, the learned dynamics models are physically consistent and more interpretable. This characteristic can lead to models that generalize better to novel situations and exhibit more predictable behavior, which is crucial for safety and reliability in robotic systems.
- **Improved Sample Efficiency:** The use of physics-informed models during training contributes to higher sample efficiency. This means the system can learn effective locomotion policies with less training data, reducing the computational resources and time required for development.
- **Reduced Long-Horizon Error Accumulation:** The physically consistent dynamics models significantly mitigate the problem of compounding errors over long prediction horizons, a common challenge in purely data-driven models. This stability is vital for robust and sustained autonomous operation of quadrupeds.
- **Real-Time Computational Efficiency for Deployment:** The novel inverse-dynamics planning formulation is a key innovation. By optimizing directly over joint trajectories and avoiding costly mass matrix inversions during inference, the method achieves up to a 4x reduction in latency compared to forward-dynamics LNN planners. This efficiency enables real-time control at frequencies like 50 Hz, making it suitable for deployment on resource-constrained hardware and agile locomotion tasks.
- **Robustness and Generalization Across Diverse Terrains:** Both simulation and hardware validation on a Unitree Go1 robot demonstrate the framework's ability to maintain performance and stability across varied and challenging terrains, including slopes, rough ground, and stairs. This robust generalization capability is essential for practical applications of quadruped robots in unstructured environments.
- **Practical Applicability for Legged Robotics:** The successful hardware validation signifies the practical viability of the proposed approach for real-world quadrupedal robots. The ability to perform complex locomotion tasks with minimal tuning and maintain consistent error tracking in actual environments highlights its potential for industrial, exploration, and service robotics.

The potential impact of this work lies in enabling the development of more intelligent, robust, and autonomous legged robots. By bridging the gap between data-driven learning and physical principles, this framework provides a pathway for quadruped systems to execute complex, long-duration tasks in dynamic and uncertain environments with higher reliability and less computational overhead. Future research can build upon this foundation by further refining inverse dynamics, incorporating advanced contact dynamics, and integrating perception for even more sophisticated locomotion capabilities.
