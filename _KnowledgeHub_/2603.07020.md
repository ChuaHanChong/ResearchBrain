# RESCHED: Rethinking Flexible Job Shop Scheduling from a Transformer-based Architecture with Simplified States

> [!summary] Summary
> RESCHED presents a deep reinforcement learning framework for Flexible Job Shop Scheduling (FJSP) that employs a simplified state representation and a Transformer-based policy network. The framework achieves superior makespan performance and strong generalization across diverse FJSP, JSSP, and FFSP benchmarks, outperforming existing DRL and classical baselines in many instances.

## Problem

- The Flexible Job Shop Scheduling Problem (FJSP) is an NP-hard combinatorial optimization problem critical for various industries, where traditional methods often lack adaptability.
- Existing Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL) approaches for FJSP rely on complex, handcrafted state features, leading to redundancy, computational overhead, and limited generalization.
- Dominant graph-biased neural architectures in DRL for scheduling can struggle with long-range dependencies and complex non-local interactions inherent in scheduling, restricting broad applicability.

## Method

- Formulates FJSP as a ==Markov Decision Process== (MDP) and employs a construction-type neural framework using a Transformer-based policy network trained via reinforcement learning.
- Introduces a simplified, minimal state representation condensed to four essential node features and graph structures for operation-to-operation (O2O) and operation-to-machine (O2M) dependencies.
- Employs a tailored decoder-only Transformer architecture with specialized components like Rotary Positional Embedding (RoPE) for O2O, direct edge feature integration for O2M, and self-based cross-attention to handle operation-machine imbalance.

## Results

- RESCHED outperformed all baselines in **14** out of **16** cases on in-distribution FJSP synthetic datasets (SD1 and SD2) and generalized strongly, surpassing DRL baselines in 6 of 8 larger instances.
- Achieved the best performance in 7 out of 8 Brandimarte and Hurink FJSP benchmark cases, yielding competitive makespan solutions with faster inference times than non-learning baselines.
- Demonstrated superior generalization on JSSP benchmarks (Taillard), outperforming L2D and RL-GNN in 7 out of 8 test sizes, and achieved competitive results on FFSP across various sizes.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Optimal scheduling decisions for FJSP exhibit a Markov property, suggesting that a minimal, current-state-only representation is sufficient and avoids the need for complex historical features.
> - Transformer-based architectures, with tailored adaptations, can effectively capture complex non-local dependencies in scheduling problems, offering an alternative to traditional GNN-centric approaches.
> - Generalization across diverse scheduling problem variants (FJSP, JSSP, FFSP) and problem sizes can be achieved from a single model by simplifying state representations and adopting flexible neural architectures.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The Flexible Job Shop Scheduling Problem (FJSP) is identified as a fundamental combinatorial optimization problem (COP) with relevance across various domains, including manufacturing, edge computing, and logistics. It is a generalization of other scheduling problems, such as the Job Shop Scheduling Problem (JSSP) (when machine assignments are fixed) and the Flexible Flow Shop Scheduling Problem (FFSP) (when jobs share processing stages but retain machine flexibility). The complexity of FJSP, being NP-hard, necessitates the use of heuristic and metaheuristic approaches for practical instances.

Traditional methods for FJSP include priority dispatching rules (PDRs), which are known for their simplicity and fast decision-making but often lack adaptability across diverse problem instances. More recently, Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL) has emerged as a promising avenue for constructing scheduling heuristics. Existing DRL approaches typically model scheduling problems using disjunctive graphs, where nodes represent operations and machines, and edges encode constraints. These methods often rely on heavily engineered state representations, frequently incorporating over 20 handcrafted features, and predominantly employ graph-biased neural architectures like Graph Attention Networks (GATs) or other Graph Neural Networks (GNNs).

This paper addresses perceived limitations in current DRL-based scheduling research:
- **Complex state representations:** Many existing methods utilize numerous handcrafted features and retain historical dependencies, which can introduce redundancy, computational overhead, and potentially hinder learning and generalization.
- **Graph-biased architectures:** GNNs, while effective for graph structures, may require deep stacking to capture long-range dependencies and might struggle with complex non-local interactions inherent in scheduling. Heuristic action pruning, sometimes used to boost efficiency, can also limit policy generalization.

RESCHED aims to advance the field by proposing a more generalized and simplified DRL framework for FJSP. It departs from the complexity of feature-engineered states and rigid GNN architectures, advocating for a minimalist approach that emphasizes essential information and a more flexible, Transformer-based neural model. This positions RESCHED as an effort to simplify DRL models for scheduling while improving their generalizability and performance across a spectrum of scheduling problems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a DRL framework, named RESCHED, that offers a more generalizable and less complex solution for the Flexible Job Shop Scheduling Problem (FJSP) and its variants. The authors are motivated by several challenges and perceived inefficiencies in existing DRL-based approaches:

1. **Reducing Modeling Complexity:** Current DRL methods for FJSP frequently employ intricate feature-engineered state representations, often necessitating over 20 handcrafted features. This complexity can lead to redundant information, increased computational overhead, and difficulties in scaling and generalizing across different problem instances. The authors argue that incorporating historical construction data into the state can degrade learning and that heuristic-based action pruning, while intended for efficiency, might limit policy generalization and lead to suboptimal solutions. A key motivation is to simplify the state representation to include only essential, Markov-sufficient information.

2. **Enhancing Generalizability:** Many DRL scheduling policies are designed for specific problem variants (e.g., JSSP or FFSP) or require significant adaptation. The reliance on rigid inductive biases in architectures like GATs, which may need deep stacking for long-range dependencies, can further restrict broad applicability. The authors seek to design a framework that can naturally generalize across different problem sizes, datasets, and fundamental scheduling variants (FJSP, JSSP, FFSP) from a single unified model. This is driven by the desire for a "general construction policy."

3. **Advancing Neural Architecture for Scheduling:** The dominant use of GNN-based architectures in DRL for scheduling is identified as having limitations. Capturing complex non-local scheduling interactions with linear attention mechanisms in GNNs can be challenging. The motivation is to explore a more flexible and expressive neural architecture, specifically Transformer blocks, which have demonstrated effectiveness in capturing dependencies in other sequential data. However, applying Transformers to scheduling presents specific challenges, such as handling process constraints without extra parameters and managing operation-machine imbalances, which necessitates architectural modifications tailored to scheduling tasks.

In summary, the core motivation is to simplify the DRL formulation and model design for scheduling problems to create a framework that is simultaneously more efficient, robust, and broadly applicable, moving towards a "minimalist DRL framework that rethinks both the scheduling formulation and model design."

### 3. Methodology and Approach

RESCHED proposes a construction-type neural framework that formulates scheduling as a Markov Decision Process (MDP) and utilizes a Transformer-based policy network trained via reinforcement learning. The methodology focuses on two main innovations: a simplified state representation and a tailored Transformer architecture.

#### 3.1. State Representation

The framework aims for a minimal yet sufficient state representation.

- **Revisiting Scheduling Formulation:** An FJSP instance is represented using a heterogeneous disjunctive graph. A solution involves both operation assignment to machines and sequencing. The makespan calculation, defined as the maximum finish time across all operations, is central to evaluating solution quality. The formulation explicitly tracks finish times of preceding operations (`FT_i(j-1)`) and machine available times (`AT_m^t`) along with processing durations (`D_m^ij`). This formulation is adaptable to JSSP and FFSP by modifying machine assignment rules.

- **MDP Formulation with Minimal Representation:** The scheduling process is modeled as a sequential decision-making problem, where an agent iteratively selects an operation-machine pair.
 - **State (`S_t`):** The state at decision step `t` is condensed to four essential node features and a graph structure. It is defined to uniquely determine: (1) available times of all machines, (2) completion status of all jobs (finish times of most recently scheduled operations), (3) operation-to-operation (O2O) precedence, and (4) operation-to-machine (O2M) connections with durations.
 - **State-dependent Optimality (Proposition 1):** The paper posits that the optimal decision at any step `t` depends only on the current state `S_t`, not on the full trajectory history. This is supported by a formal proof in Appendix D, establishing the Markov property for the problem.
 - **Subproblem Formulation & Refinements:** Each scheduling step is treated as a subproblem.
 - **Relative Available Time:** Operation and machine available times are normalized by subtracting the global minimum available time at each step. This prevents unbounded time values and mitigates generalization issues.
 - **O2O Connection:** Instead of bidirectional O2O edges, the framework uses backward-looking edges. Hop connections from an operation to all its successors are introduced to provide direct access to job-level future constraints without relying on multi-layer message passing. As operations are scheduled, they and their associated connections are removed, simplifying the graph for the next subproblem.
 - **Final State Features:** The compact state representation comprises: 1) Operation Available Time; 2) Machine Available Time; 3) Duration (edge feature); and 4) Minimum Duration (a proxy for operation difficulty). O2O and O2M relations are encoded as graph structure (masks).

- **Action:** An action at step `t` is the selection of an operation-machine pair (`(ij, m)`). The framework avoids heuristic action pruning beyond natural precedence constraints.

- **Transition:** Taking an action deterministically transitions the environment to a new state (`s_t+1`), updating operation and machine statuses based on Equation (2).

- **Reward:** The reward `r_t` is defined as the negative difference between the estimated lower-bound makespan before and after taking an action (Equation 3). This lower-bound makespan is calculated iteratively based on preceding operation finish times and minimum durations across candidate machines.

#### 3.2. Policy Network Architecture

RESCHED uses a decoder-only neural architecture consisting of a feature extraction network and an MLP-based policy head.

- **Feature Extraction Network:** Composed of two branches, both built upon standard Transformer layers with adaptations:
 - **Operation Branch with RoPE:** This branch models O2O dependencies using self-attention. Rotary Positional Embedding (RoPE) is incorporated to directly model relative intra-job distances without additional learnable parameters. RoPE is applied exclusively within jobs to capture positional awareness in intra-job attention patterns.
 - **Machine Branch with Edge in Attention:** This branch uses a cross-attention mechanism where operations attend to candidate machines. Edge features (processing durations) are directly embedded into the value vectors, influencing both attention weights and aggregated representations (Equation 5). Projection weights for edge features are shared for efficiency.
 - **Machine Branch with Self-based Cross-attention:** To address the severe operation-machine imbalance (many operations per machine), each machine node attends to its own representation during attention weight computation (Equation 6). This helps preserve critical machine-level information by adaptively weighting the machine's own embedding.

- **Decision-Making:** A multi-layer perceptron (MLP) takes concatenated operation, machine, and edge embeddings as input, producing a scalar score for each feasible operation-machine pair. A softmax function converts these scores into a probability distribution. Global embeddings are omitted.

- **Training Algorithm:** The policy is optimized using the REINFORCE algorithm to isolate the impact of state and architecture design from algorithmic improvements. (Appendix E also presents a PPO version of RESCHED for comprehensive evaluation).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents extensive experiments comparing RESCHED against classical dispatching rules, state-of-the-art DRL methods, and strong non-learning baselines across FJSP, JSSP, and FFSP.

- **Performance on FJSP (Section 5.1):**
 - **In-Distribution Performance:** RESCHED generally outperforms all baselines on synthetic datasets SD1 and SD2, achieving superior results in 14 out of 16 cases. The performance advantage is particularly notable on SD2 instances and results in significant gap reductions compared to DANIEL on SD1 instances.
 - **Generalization Performance:** RESCHED demonstrates strong generalization. On larger synthetic instances (30x10 to 40x10), it outperforms DRL baselines in 6 out of 8 cases, and in some instances (e.g., SD2 40x10), it surpasses OR-Tools. On Brandimarte and Hurink benchmarks (trained on SD1), RESCHED achieves the best performance in 7 out of 8 cases across greedy and sampling strategies. It produces competitive solutions within short inference times, contrasting with the longer computation times of non-learning baselines like 2SGA and OR-Tools. Sampling-based decoding further improves performance without additional training.
 - **Running Time Analysis:** RESCHED exhibits runtime efficiency comparable to existing DRL-based approaches while yielding superior scheduling quality.

- **Ablation Study and Robustness Analysis (Section 5.2):**
 - **Ablation Study (Table 5, Appendix B.3):** Studies on removing individual components—minimal representation elements (O2O connection, relative available time, current time) and architectural enhancements (RoPE, Edge in Attention, Self-based Cross-attention)—demonstrate that all proposed components contribute positively to the overall performance of RESCHED.
 - **Robustness Analysis (Table 6, Appendix B.3):** Evaluation across multiple random seeds shows minimal variation (less than 1%) in performance, indicating the stability and consistency of RESCHED's improvements.

- **Performance on JSSP and FFSP (Section 5.3):**
 - **JSSP:** Trained on synthetic 10x10 instances, RESCHED is directly tested on Taillard benchmark instances (15x15 to 100x20). It outperforms L2D and RL-GNN in 7 out of 8 test sizes, even surpassing their in-distribution performance. The CP-SAT solver from OR-Tools (with a 3600-second time limit) provides strong upper bounds but with significantly longer computation. RESCHED also shows consistent superiority on synthetic JSSP data and the DMU benchmark (Tables 7 and 8 in Appendix B.3).
 - **FFSP:** Trained solely on size 20 instances (unlike MatNet which trains separate models for different sizes), RESCHED achieves competitive results in most settings across varying sizes (20, 50, 100) under both greedy and sampling strategies (Table 9 in Appendix B.3).

- **PPO Version of RESCHED (Appendix E.3):** RESCHED-PPO demonstrates faster convergence and further improves in-distribution and out-of-distribution performance compared to RESCHED-REINFORCE. Both RESCHED versions consistently match or outperform DANIEL under matched training budgets, suggesting the benefits stem from the proposed framework design.

- **Reward Design and Discount Factor (Appendix F):** An ablation on reward formulations shows that the estimated lower-bound reward used in RESCHED yields a balance between synthetic and benchmark performance, avoiding potential overfitting. The method is also shown to be insensitive to the discount factor `gamma` (0.99 vs 1.0).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The RESCHED framework presents a number of advancements and implications for the field of combinatorial optimization and scheduling:

- **Reduced Model Complexity:** By demonstrating that a minimal state representation (just four essential features and explicit graph structures for O2O/O2M dependencies) can lead to superior performance, RESCHED challenges the convention of using extensive, hand-engineered features in DRL for scheduling. This simplification has the potential to reduce data preprocessing complexity, memory requirements, and computational overhead during training and inference.
- **Enhanced Generalization Capabilities:** A key contribution is RESCHED's ability to generalize across different problem sizes, datasets, and fundamental scheduling variants (FJSP, JSSP, FFSP) without architectural changes or size-specific tuning. This indicates that the proposed minimalist state design and flexible Transformer architecture effectively capture underlying scheduling principles, making it a more versatile tool.
- **Advancement in Neural Architectures for COPs:** The successful adaptation of Transformer blocks with specific modifications (RoPE for O2O dependencies, direct edge feature integration, and self-based cross-attention for O2M interactions) demonstrates the applicability and effectiveness of Transformer-based models for complex combinatorial problems beyond their traditional NLP and vision domains. This suggests a potential shift away from purely GNN-centric approaches in DRL for scheduling.
- **Competitive Performance:** RESCHED establishes competitive performance against both classical heuristics and state-of-the-art DRL and non-learning methods on various benchmarks. Its ability to achieve high-quality solutions within relatively short inference times makes it practical for real-world applications where rapid decision-making is critical.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The framework's modularity and simplified design provide a robust baseline for future research. The authors note an opportunity for further theoretical acceleration through Key-Value (KV) caching, which could significantly boost inference speed. Additionally, while currently encoding only O2O/O2M interactions, the framework could be extended to model explicit machine-to-operation (M2O) feedback, further enriching its representational power. The generic nature of RESCHED also implies its potential applicability to a broader range of complex resource allocation and task-offloading problems beyond traditional shop scheduling.
