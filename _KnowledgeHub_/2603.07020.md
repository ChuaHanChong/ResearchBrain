# RESCHED: Rethinking Flexible Job Shop Scheduling from a Transformer-based Architecture with Simplified States

> [!summary] Summary
> RESCHED presents a deep reinforcement learning framework for Flexible Job Shop Scheduling (FJSP) that employs a simplified state representation and a Transformer-based policy network. The framework achieves superior makespan performance and strong generalization across diverse FJSP, JSSP, and FFSP benchmarks, outperforming existing DRL and classical baselines in many instances.

## Problem

- The Flexible Job Shop Scheduling Problem (FJSP) is an NP-hard combinatorial optimization problem critical for various industries, where traditional methods often lack adaptability.
- Existing Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL) approaches for FJSP rely on complex, handcrafted state features, leading to redundancy, computational overhead, and limited generalization.
- Dominant graph-biased neural architectures in DRL for scheduling can struggle with long-range dependencies and complex non-local interactions inherent in scheduling, restricting broad applicability.

## Method

- Formulates FJSP as a ==Markov Decision Process== (MDP) and employs a construction-type neural framework using a Transformer-based policy network trained via reinforcement learning.
- Introduces a simplified, minimal state representation condensed to four essential node features and graph structures for operation-to-operation (O2O) and operation-to-machine (O2M) dependencies.
- Employs a tailored decoder-only Transformer architecture with specialized components like Rotary Positional Embedding (RoPE) for O2O, direct edge feature integration for O2M, and self-based cross-attention to handle operation-machine imbalance.

## Results

- RESCHED outperformed all baselines in **14** out of **16** cases on in-distribution FJSP synthetic datasets (SD1 and SD2) and generalized strongly, surpassing DRL baselines in 6 of 8 larger instances.
- Achieved the best performance in 7 out of 8 Brandimarte and Hurink FJSP benchmark cases, yielding competitive makespan solutions with faster inference times than non-learning baselines.
- Demonstrated superior generalization on JSSP benchmarks (Taillard), outperforming L2D and RL-GNN in 7 out of 8 test sizes, and achieved competitive results on FFSP across various sizes.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Optimal scheduling decisions for FJSP exhibit a Markov property, suggesting that a minimal, current-state-only representation is sufficient and avoids the need for complex historical features.
> - Transformer-based architectures, with tailored adaptations, can effectively capture complex non-local dependencies in scheduling problems, offering an alternative to traditional GNN-centric approaches.
> - Generalization across diverse scheduling problem variants (FJSP, JSSP, FFSP) and problem sizes can be achieved from a single model by simplifying state representations and adopting flexible neural architectures.
