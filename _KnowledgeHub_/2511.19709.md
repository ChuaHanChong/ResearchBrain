# Whole-Body Inverse Dynamics MPC for Legged Loco-Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at ETH Zurich developed a real-time whole-body inverse dynamics Model Predictive Control (MPC) framework for legged robots with manipulators, enabling complex loco-manipulation tasks like pulling heavy loads. The framework achieved an 80 Hz update rate on hardware, demonstrating unified motion and force planning while ensuring physical consistency and respecting actuation limits.

## Problem

- Traditional hierarchical control structures for legged loco-manipulation suffer from inconsistencies between simplified planning models (e.g., SRB) and full robot dynamics, particularly with heavy manipulators.
- Existing centroidal MPC implementations often rely on separate low-level whole-body controllers, retaining hierarchical complexity and potentially leading to unsafe torques.
- Real-time application of full whole-body torque-level MPC for loco-manipulation, especially tasks involving significant environmental interaction, faced computational challenges due to high-dimensional, nonlinear optimization.

## Method

- A whole-body ==inverse dynamics== MPC framework directly optimizes joint torques by incorporating full rigid-body equations of motion (Recursive Newton-Euler Algorithm) as path constraints within a single predictive layer.
- Computational efficiency is achieved through adaptive time steps, the use of inverse dynamics over forward dynamics, and integration with the specialized Fatrop interior-point solver, compiled via CasADi code generation.
- The system uses a 22-degrees-of-freedom model (Unitree B2 quadruped + Z1 arm) with objectives for desired states, input regularization, and constraints for contact, swing, end-effector force/velocity, and joint limits.

## Results

- The MPC was deployed on a Unitree B2 with Z1 manipulator at 80 Hz, demonstrating stable locomotion and accurate end-effector tracking with a low-level PD controller on hardware.
- The robot successfully performed physically interactive tasks, including pulling a 10 kg load (exceeding arm's rated payload), pushing a box, and wiping a whiteboard, showcasing simultaneous management of end-effector force and motion targets.
- Benchmarking showed the inverse dynamics formulation yielded the fastest solve times, while accounting for computation time delay, adaptive time steps reduced base drift to **0.4** cm/s compared to **1.2** cm/s with fixed steps.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Whole-body inverse dynamics formulation significantly reduces computational load, enabling faster solve times (12.5 ms) compared to forward dynamics and centroidal models, crucial for real-time MPC.
> - Unified torque-level optimization allows for emergent, dynamically consistent whole-body behaviors where the robot automatically coordinates all degrees of freedom for complex loco-manipulation tasks and disturbance rejection.
> - Adaptive time steps effectively balance fine temporal resolution for immediate control with computational tractability for longer horizons, reducing state drift during locomotion.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotics is rapidly advancing, with legged robots and mobile manipulators increasingly capable of performing complex tasks in diverse environments. A significant challenge in this domain is **loco-manipulation**, where a robot must simultaneously maintain stable locomotion while precisely manipulating objects or interacting physically with its surroundings. This inherently couples the dynamics of the robot's base and legs with those of its manipulator arm, demanding sophisticated control strategies.

Historically, control of legged robots has relied on a **hierarchical structure**. High-level planners often use simplified models, such as the Single Rigid Body (SRB) model, to determine contact locations, forces, and coarse trajectories for the robot's center of mass (CoM) or base. These plans are then passed to a lower-level Whole-Body Controller (WBC) that translates them into joint commands, often using inverse kinematics and prioritized task execution. While computationally efficient, this layered approach can suffer from inconsistencies between the simplified planning model and the robot's full dynamics, especially with heavy manipulators, leading to challenges in real-world deployment and tuning.

**Centroidal dynamics models** emerged as an improvement, offering a more accurate representation of the robot's full kinematic structure while retaining some computational efficiency by focusing on CoM momentum. These models have been successfully applied to quadruped and humanoid locomotion and, more recently, to loco-manipulation tasks on platforms like ANYmal and CENTAURO. However, many centroidal MPC implementations still rely on a separate low-level WBC to ensure joint-level dynamic feasibility, thus retaining a degree of hierarchical complexity. While some approaches compute joint torques directly from centroidal MPC, they often assume consistency with whole-body dynamics, which might not hold in practice, potentially leading to unsafe torques.

The current paper directly addresses the limitations of these hierarchical and partially unified approaches by proposing a **whole-body torque-level Model Predictive Control (MPC) framework**. This paradigm represents the cutting edge in robotic control, aiming for a single, unified optimization layer that directly considers the robot's full rigid-body dynamics and optimizes joint torques. This approach promises greater physical consistency, explicit respect for actuation limits, and emergent behaviors that inherently account for the strong coupling between locomotion and manipulation.

Prior work on whole-body torque-level MPC has shown success in pure quadruped and humanoid locomotion. However, its application to **real-time loco-manipulation**, especially tasks involving significant environmental interaction (like pushing or pulling heavy objects), has been largely unexplored due to the formidable computational challenges of solving high-dimensional, nonlinear optimization problems in real-time. Existing full whole-body formulations for loco-manipulation have typically been applied offline. This paper fills a critical gap by demonstrating the feasibility and effectiveness of such a framework in real-time hardware experiments, thereby advancing the state-of-the-art in unified control for mobile manipulators.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core problem motivating this research is the intricate challenge of **loco-manipulation**, where legged robots equipped with arms must execute precise end-effector tasks and apply/resist substantial forces while simultaneously maintaining locomotion stability. This introduces a strong, inherent coupling between the manipulator arm and the robot's base and legs, making coordinated whole-body motion critical.

The key objectives and motivations of this work are:

1. **Unified Motion and Force Planning:** To develop a control framework that can simultaneously reason about both motion generation and force application/resistance across the entire robot body. This contrasts with traditional approaches that often separate planning for locomotion from planning for manipulation.
2. **Direct Torque Optimization through Inverse Dynamics:** To move away from hierarchical control stacks that rely on reduced-order planning and separate whole-body tracking controllers. Instead, the authors aim to directly optimize joint torques by incorporating full-order inverse dynamics within a single predictive layer. This ensures that the planned motions are physically consistent and explicitly respect the robot's actuation limits.
3. **Emergent, Dynamically Consistent Behaviors:** By unifying planning and control at the torque level, the objective is to enable the robot to generate complex, physically realistic whole-body behaviors without extensive manual tuning of hierarchical layers or heuristic-based trajectory generation. These behaviors should naturally account for the system's dynamics and physical constraints.
4. **Real-Time Performance on Hardware:** A crucial objective is to overcome the computational challenges traditionally associated with whole-body torque-level MPC, particularly for high-dimensional loco-manipulation systems. The goal is to achieve real-time performance (e.g., 80 Hz) suitable for practical hardware deployment.
5. **Robustness to Physical Interaction:** To demonstrate the controller's ability to handle demanding loco-manipulation tasks involving significant physical interaction with the environment, such as pulling heavy loads, pushing objects, and performing precision tasks like wiping, while maintaining stability and safety.
6. **Open-Source Implementation:** To facilitate further research and development by building the MPC framework using open software tools (Pinocchio, CasADi) and sharing the code, allowing others to benchmark, extend, and deploy similar solutions.

In essence, the authors are motivated to create a more direct, robust, and physically grounded control solution for mobile manipulators that can perform versatile and dynamic tasks in the real world, bridging the gap between theoretical optimal control and practical hardware deployment.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology centers on a **whole-body inverse dynamics Model Predictive Control (MPC) framework** for legged loco-manipulation.

**A. System Model:**
The robot system, comprising a quadruped (Unitree B2) and a manipulator arm (Unitree Z1), is modeled using a floating-base representation. The generalized coordinates include base position and orientation (unit quaternion) and joint angles. The state vector `x` combines generalized coordinates and velocities, while the control input `u` consists of joint torques, generalized accelerations, and contact forces. The arm's last two joints are locked, resulting in a 22-degrees-of-freedom model for experiments.

**B. Optimal Control Problem (OCP) Formulation:**
The MPC formulation minimizes a finite-horizon optimal control problem at each time step.
- **Cost Function:** A quadratic stage cost penalizes deviations from desired states (nominal configuration, commanded base velocities, zero joint velocities) and control inputs (zero accelerations, zero joint torques, weight distribution for contact forces).
- **Dynamics Constraints:** The crucial aspect is imposing the **full rigid-body equations of motion (inverse dynamics)** as *path constraints* at each time step. The Recursive Newton-Euler Algorithm (RNEA) is used to compute these inverse dynamics. This choice, as opposed to forward dynamics, has been shown to result in faster solve times and greater robustness. Quaternions are handled using local increments in the tangent space to avoid explicit unit-norm constraints. Explicit Euler integration discretizes the kinematic state dynamics.
- **Contact and Swing Constraints:** A fixed gait schedule dictates contact modes.
 - **Contact feet:** Enforced non-negative normal forces, friction cone constraints, and zero linear velocity to prevent lifting or slipping.
 - **Swing feet:** Zero contact force, with vertical velocity tracking a cubic spline reference (horizontal foot velocities are left unconstrained for MPC to optimize step locations).
- **Arm Constraints:** Desired end-effector linear force and velocity are enforced as equality constraints, suitable for tasks like pulling or pushing with specified interactions.
- **State and Input Bounds:** Joint position and velocity limits are enforced throughout the horizon. Joint torque limits are applied only for the first two time steps, after which they are removed from decision variables for computational efficiency, relying on the RNEA constraint for dynamic feasibility in the base.

**C. Computational Efficiency Strategies:**
- **Adaptive Time Steps:** A geometric time step scheme (`δt_k = γ^k δt_0`) is employed. This provides fine temporal resolution early in the prediction horizon for accurate control while reducing the number of nodes later in the horizon to maintain computational tractability for long-term planning.
- **Inverse Dynamics:** The choice of inverse dynamics (RNEA) over forward dynamics is a key enabler for speed and robustness, as validated in their benchmarking.

**D. Implementation Details:**
- **Software Frameworks:** The MPC is implemented using **Pinocchio** for symbolic expressions of system dynamics (mass matrix, Coriolis/gravity terms, Jacobians) and **CasADi** for automatic differentiation and interfacing with numerical solvers.
- **Solver:** The **Fatrop** interior-point (IP) solver is used. Fatrop is specifically designed for optimal control problems, leveraging the block-sparse structure of stage-wise constraints through a structure-exploiting linear solver (Riccati recursion). This provides significant speedup compared to generic IP solvers like IPOPT.
- **Warm-Starting:** The solver is warm-started with the solution from the previous MPC iteration, which reduces solve times by 15-20%. Contact force guesses are reinitialized to mitigate misalignment due to adaptive time steps and mode switches.
- **Code Generation:** CasADi's code generation feature compiles the Python-implemented MPC into a C shared library, improving execution speed (2-2.5x speedup) and portability for hardware deployment.

**E. Deployment Pipeline (Unitree B2 + Z1):**
The MPC runs at 80 Hz on an off-board PC. It receives tracking commands (base velocity, arm end-effector velocity/force), a fixed gait schedule, and the initial robot state (generalized positions/velocities from a state estimator). The MPC outputs desired joint position, velocity, and torque trajectories. These trajectories are then linearly interpolated at 500 Hz and fed into a low-level PD controller on the robot to compute the final motor torques, compensating for unmodeled dynamics and actuator limitations. The state estimator uses IMU and joint encoder readings, inferring foot contact from the planned gait schedule.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded significant findings across benchmarking, simulation, and hardware experiments, demonstrating the efficacy and real-time capability of the proposed whole-body inverse dynamics MPC.

**A. Benchmarking Dynamics Models:**
- **Computational Efficiency:** The whole-body inverse dynamics MPC achieved the **fastest solve times** among all evaluated dynamics formulations, including forward dynamics and various centroidal models. It converged to a solution in **12.5 ms post-compilation**, enabling real-time control at 80 Hz on the Unitree B2+Z1 system. This was attributed to the inverse dynamics RNEA constraint having the lowest number of mathematical instructions.
- **Formulation Impact:** The Fatrop solver performed significantly better when dynamics were imposed as **path constraints** rather than embedded within the state transition function. This approach, which allows joint optimization over states and inputs, led to faster convergence and lower variance in solve times.

**B. Simulation Experiments:**
- **Stable Loco-Manipulation (Without Time Delay):** The MPC achieved stable locomotion and accurate end-effector tracking with pure feed-forward torques, even without additional feedback gains.
- **Whole-Body Adaptability:** The controller demonstrated emergent coordinated full-body behaviors. While trotting, the robot automatically adjusted its base pose and leg motions to accurately track challenging arm end-effector trajectories (e.g., reaching low or high targets), showcasing the unified optimization's ability to coordinate all degrees of freedom.
- **Disturbance Rejection:** The MPC successfully stabilized the robot against significant external velocity disturbances (0.8 m/s lateral perturbation to the base) while maintaining high arm tracking accuracy. It ensured that joint torques remained within operational limits, highlighting the benefits of torque-level control in enforcing safety constraints and adapting footstep placements for recovery.
- **Adaptive Time Steps:** When accounting for computation time delay (requiring a damping gain, K_d=5), the adaptive time step strategy significantly reduced base drift while trotting (0.4 cm/s) compared to fixed time steps (1.2 cm/s) for the same horizon length.

**C. Hardware Experiments (Unitree B2 + Z1):**
- **Real-Time Deployment:** The compiled MPC, leveraging the Fatrop solver, was successfully deployed on the Unitree B2 quadruped with a Z1 manipulator arm, running in real-time at **80 Hz** off-board.
- **Low-Level Controller Integration:** While simulation allowed pure feed-forward torques, hardware deployment required a velocity feedback gain (K_d ≈ 20) for the quadruped to mitigate motor oscillations and a small proportional term for arm joints due to noisy sensor readings. The MPC's torque solution was effective enough that no position feedback was needed for the quadruped.
- **Challenging Loco-Manipulation Tasks:** The robot successfully performed several physically interactive tasks:
 - **Pulling a 10 kg load** while in stance and walking. This significantly exceeded the Z1 arm's rated payload (3-5 kg), pushing the robot to its operational limits (though overheating limited even heavier loads than simulated).
 - **Pushing a box** against a wall while trotting in place.
 - **Wiping a whiteboard** with accuracy.
 - **Compliant human interaction** was demonstrated by reducing arm PD gains, indicating potential for safe collaboration.
- These tasks collectively showcased the MPC's ability to simultaneously manage end-effector force and motion targets on real hardware.

**D. Sim-to-Real Discrepancies:**
- **Solve Time Variability:** Hardware solve times showed greater variability, occasionally exceeding the average simulation times (up to 20 ms vs. 12.5 ms).
- **Contact Mode Mismatch:** Significant differences were observed in joint torque and velocity profiles during contact transitions, particularly delayed ground contact on hardware, leading the MPC to temporarily reduce torque.
- **Model Inaccuracies:** The robot's URDF model had discrepancies, notably an inaccurate mass distribution (actual CoM was shifted rearward by ~60% compared to the model's geometric center). Correcting this CoM offset in the MPC significantly improved performance and reduced backward lean. Motor friction and rotor inertia were also identified as unmodeled dynamics contributing to sim-to-real gaps.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work represents a significant advancement in the field of legged loco-manipulation, particularly in bridging the gap between theoretical optimal control and real-world robot deployment. Its significance and potential impact are manifold:

1. **First Real-Time Whole-Body Inverse Dynamics MPC for Loco-Manipulation:** The paper delivers a crucial first demonstration of real-time, torque-level whole-body inverse dynamics MPC on a legged robot with a manipulator arm. This pushes the boundaries of what is computationally feasible for complex mobile manipulation systems, opening new possibilities for their autonomous operation.
2. **Unified and Simplified Control Architecture:** By directly optimizing joint torques through inverse dynamics within a single predictive layer, the framework eliminates the need for complex, hierarchical control stacks and separate low-level tracking controllers. This simplification makes the control pipeline more robust, easier to tune, and less prone to inconsistencies arising from model approximations or task prioritization conflicts between layers.
3. **Emergent, Physically Consistent Behaviors:** The direct whole-body torque optimization allows for the emergence of dynamically consistent behaviors that inherently account for the robot's full dynamics, physical constraints, and the strong coupling between locomotion and manipulation. This results in more natural, efficient, and robust interactions with the environment, as demonstrated by the robot's adaptive base pose and footstep adjustments during arm tasks and disturbance rejection.
4. **Enhanced Interaction Capabilities:** The ability to simultaneously optimize for end-effector force and motion targets at the torque level significantly enhances the robot's capacity for complex physical interactions. Tasks like pulling heavy loads (exceeding rated arm payload), pushing, and precise wiping showcase the framework's potential for industrial, logistics, and service applications where robots must manipulate objects with varying levels of force and dexterity.
5. **Leveraging State-of-the-Art Tools:** The implementation using open-source software (Pinocchio, CasADi) and a specialized solver (Fatrop) demonstrates a powerful toolkit for developing advanced robot controllers. The benchmarking results, particularly the computational efficiency achieved with inverse dynamics and Fatrop, provide valuable insights for future MPC development in robotics. The open-sourcing of the framework further lowers the barrier to entry for researchers in this domain.
6. **Insights into Sim-to-Real Challenges:** The detailed discussion of sim-to-real discrepancies, such as solve time variability, contact mode mismatches, and inaccuracies in mass distribution and motor modeling, provides critical empirical data. This informs future research directions, emphasizing the continued importance of advanced system identification, friction compensation, and potentially hybrid control strategies (e.g., integrating with reinforcement learning) to bridge the reality gap.
7. **Foundation for Future Research:** This work lays a strong foundation for several promising future directions, including explicit object dynamics modeling, collision avoidance within the MPC, exploration of alternative optimization solvers (like SQP), and integration with reinforcement learning to leverage its robustness against model inaccuracies while retaining model-based safety and planning capabilities.

In conclusion, this paper significantly advances the state of robotic control by demonstrating a practical and efficient real-time whole-body torque-level MPC for legged loco-manipulation. Its unified approach, robust performance, and open-source nature promise to accelerate the development of more autonomous, capable, and versatile mobile manipulator robots for a wide range of real-world applications.
