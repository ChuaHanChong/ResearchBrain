# MolmoSpaces: A Large-Scale Open Ecosystem for Robot Navigation and Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> MolmoSpaces introduces a large-scale, open ecosystem and benchmark suite for robot navigation and manipulation, providing over 230,000 diverse environments and 130,000 richly annotated objects compatible with multiple simulators. The framework demonstrated a strong sim-to-real correlation (e.g., Pearson R ~0.96 for the pick task) and effectively diagnosed policy sensitivities to factors like prompt phrasing and camera occlusion, facilitating the development of more robust robot policies.

## Problem

- Existing robot benchmarks and simulators often lack the scale, diversity, and physical realism needed to train and evaluate generalist, zero-shot robot policies against the 'long tail' of real-world variations.
- Physical robot experiments are resource-intensive, slow, and difficult to scale or reproduce, hindering systematic assessment of policy generalization.
- Current simulation platforms frequently fall short in simultaneously supporting high-fidelity physics-based manipulation within diverse, interactive, and multi-room environments with strong sim-to-real correlation.

## Method

- MolmoSpaces unifies and expands existing resources by providing over 230,000 diverse scenes (crafted, procedurally generated, LLM-generated) and over 130,000 annotated objects, all tuned for physical realism and multi-simulator compatibility (MuJoCo, ManiSkill, IsaacSim).
- The ecosystem includes over 42 million robust 6-DoF grasp annotations for a wide range of rigid and articulated objects, alongside diverse robot models with integrated kinematics and control.
- MolmoSpaces-Bench, a zero-shot benchmark suite, offers eight base tasks across navigation and manipulation, designed for large-scale, controlled evaluation, and supports LLM-based generation of complex, long-horizon tasks.

## Results

- MolmoSpaces-Bench showed a Pearson correlation coefficient (R) of approximately 0.96 and a Spearman rank correlation coefficient (ρ) of approximately 0.98 for the `pick` task, indicating strong sim-to-real correlation with real-world RoboArena data.
- Evaluated policies exhibited significant sensitivity to prompt phrasing, with minor instruction changes impacting success rates (e.g., DROID-frequent prompts boosting π0 performance) and heavy reliance on wrist-mounted cameras (occlusion reduced π0.5 success to 2%).
- π-models averaged 2.65 grasp-ungrasp transitions in successful episodes, demonstrating the benefit of longer task horizons, and showed distinct preferences for top-down grasps, while CAP policies favored side grasps.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Large-scale, diverse, and physically realistic simulation environments can serve as a reliable and predictive proxy for real-world robot performance, as evidenced by strong sim-to-real correlation.
> - Systematic distributional evaluations in simulation reveal critical policy sensitivities to factors like prompt phrasing, initial joint positions, and camera views, which are crucial for diagnosing and improving policy generalization.
> - The quality and type of grasp conditioning, along with the robot's end-effector and task horizon, significantly influence manipulation success rates and preferred grasping strategies across different policies.
