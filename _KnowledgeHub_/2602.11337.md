# MolmoSpaces: A Large-Scale Open Ecosystem for Robot Navigation and Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> MolmoSpaces introduces a large-scale, open ecosystem and benchmark suite for robot navigation and manipulation, providing over 230,000 diverse environments and 130,000 richly annotated objects compatible with multiple simulators. The framework demonstrated a strong sim-to-real correlation (e.g., Pearson R ~0.96 for the pick task) and effectively diagnosed policy sensitivities to factors like prompt phrasing and camera occlusion, facilitating the development of more robust robot policies.

## Problem

- Existing robot benchmarks and simulators often lack the scale, diversity, and physical realism needed to train and evaluate generalist, zero-shot robot policies against the 'long tail' of real-world variations.
- Physical robot experiments are resource-intensive, slow, and difficult to scale or reproduce, hindering systematic assessment of policy generalization.
- Current simulation platforms frequently fall short in simultaneously supporting high-fidelity physics-based manipulation within diverse, interactive, and multi-room environments with strong sim-to-real correlation.

## Method

- MolmoSpaces unifies and expands existing resources by providing over 230,000 diverse scenes (crafted, procedurally generated, LLM-generated) and over 130,000 annotated objects, all tuned for physical realism and multi-simulator compatibility (MuJoCo, ManiSkill, IsaacSim).
- The ecosystem includes over 42 million robust 6-DoF grasp annotations for a wide range of rigid and articulated objects, alongside diverse robot models with integrated kinematics and control.
- MolmoSpaces-Bench, a zero-shot benchmark suite, offers eight base tasks across navigation and manipulation, designed for large-scale, controlled evaluation, and supports LLM-based generation of complex, long-horizon tasks.

## Results

- MolmoSpaces-Bench showed a Pearson correlation coefficient (R) of approximately 0.96 and a Spearman rank correlation coefficient (ρ) of approximately 0.98 for the `pick` task, indicating strong sim-to-real correlation with real-world RoboArena data.
- Evaluated policies exhibited significant sensitivity to prompt phrasing, with minor instruction changes impacting success rates (e.g., DROID-frequent prompts boosting π0 performance) and heavy reliance on wrist-mounted cameras (occlusion reduced π0.5 success to 2%).
- π-models averaged 2.65 grasp-ungrasp transitions in successful episodes, demonstrating the benefit of longer task horizons, and showed distinct preferences for top-down grasps, while CAP policies favored side grasps.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Large-scale, diverse, and physically realistic simulation environments can serve as a reliable and predictive proxy for real-world robot performance, as evidenced by strong sim-to-real correlation.
> - Systematic distributional evaluations in simulation reveal critical policy sensitivities to factors like prompt phrasing, initial joint positions, and camera views, which are crucial for diagnosing and improving policy generalization.
> - The quality and type of grasp conditioning, along with the robot's end-effector and task horizon, significantly influence manipulation success rates and preferred grasping strategies across different policies.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotics research is increasingly focused on developing generalist, open-vocabulary policies capable of zero-shot deployment in complex, real-world environments. However, a significant challenge in this pursuit is the lack of adequate infrastructure for evaluation and training. Existing robot benchmarks frequently emphasize short-horizon skills in single scenes, which limits their ability to assess long-horizon, compositional tasks or a robot's robustness to the vast "long tail" of variations in scene layouts, object geometries, and task specifications encountered in everyday life. Furthermore, physical robot experiments are resource-intensive, slow, and difficult to scale or reproduce.

Simulation offers a scalable alternative, but current simulators and datasets often present limitations in terms of scene and object diversity, physical realism, support for articulated interactions, or the range of tasks they can facilitate. While initiatives like Open X-Embodiment address data scaling, and projects such as Objaverse, ProcTHOR, Holodeck, and InternScenes contribute to increasing 3D asset or scene diversity, they frequently fall short in simultaneously supporting high-fidelity physics-based manipulation within diverse, interactive environments. Similarly, large-scale grasp datasets exist but often require substantial integration effort into functional scenes. Benchmarks for vision-language-action (VLA) models (e.g., LIBERO, CALVIN, VLABench) and mobile manipulation (e.g., BEHAVIOR-1K, ManiSkill-HAB) have emerged, but they can be constrained by scenario realism or scale. Ensuring strong sim-to-real correlation remains a critical challenge, with efforts like SIMPLER and PolaRiS attempting to bridge this gap, often with limitations in task scope or generalization assessment.

MolmoSpaces positions itself as a comprehensive, open ecosystem that unifies and significantly expands upon these fragmented advancements. It addresses the need for large-scale, diverse, physically realistic, and multi-simulator compatible environments, objects, and grasp annotations, specifically designed to support the full spectrum of embodied tasks, including static and mobile manipulation, navigation, and long-horizon multiroom tasks. By emphasizing zero-shot evaluation and demonstrating strong sim-to-real correlation, MolmoSpaces aims to provide a reliable platform for evaluating the generalization capabilities of generalist robot policies, thereby advancing the development of more robust and adaptable robotic systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this work is to introduce MolmoSpaces, a fully open and comprehensive ecosystem, including a benchmark suite (MolmoSpaces-Bench), designed to facilitate the large-scale evaluation and training of robot policies. This ecosystem aims to address critical limitations in existing robotics research infrastructure.

The motivation for MolmoSpaces stems from several key challenges in advancing robot learning:

- **Addressing the "Long Tail" Problem:** Real-world environments are characterized by immense variations in scene layouts, object properties, and task instructions. Current robot benchmarks often fail to represent this vast "long tail" of everyday situations, leading to policies that lack robustness in diverse, unseen scenarios. A core motivation is to provide an infrastructure capable of exposing policies to a sufficiently broad distribution of environments, objects, and tasks to accurately measure generalization.
- **Overcoming Limitations of Physical Evaluation:** Conducting robot experiments in physical environments is inherently expensive, time-consuming, and difficult to reproduce consistently at scale. This hinders the rigorous and systematic assessment of policies across numerous controlled scenarios. The authors are motivated by the need for a scalable alternative that enables systematic stress testing and performance characterization across thousands of scenarios.
- **Enhancing Simulation Realism and Scope:** While simulation offers scalability, existing platforms often lack a combination of scene-scale diversity, physical realism (for contact-rich manipulation), articulated object interactions, and support for long-horizon compositional tasks in realistic indoor settings. A key motivation is to unify these elements into a single ecosystem that also demonstrates a strong correlation between simulated and real-world performance, making simulation results a reliable proxy.
- **Facilitating Zero-Shot Generalization Evaluation:** As robot learning progresses towards generalist, open-vocabulary policies, it becomes crucial to evaluate their performance in zero-shot settings, meaning without task-specific fine-tuning on benchmark data. Many existing benchmarks, by focusing on short-horizon skills or specific scene types, provide insufficient signal for models nearing saturated performance, failing to probe true generalization to novel situations.
- **Providing Open and Extensible Resources:** The research community benefits from open-source tools and datasets. The authors are motivated to provide a fully open ecosystem that researchers can leverage not only for benchmarking but also for synthesizing their own diverse datasets for training robust policies, thereby fostering community-driven progress.
- **Enabling Diagnostic Analysis of Policy Behavior:** Beyond aggregate performance metrics, it is vital to understand *why* policies succeed or fail. The scale of simulation allows for systematic perturbation of environmental and policy factors (e.g., prompt phrasing, initial robot poses, sensor inputs). This diagnostic capability is a strong motivation to identify specific failure modes and sensitivities, which can directly inform algorithm improvement.

In essence, MolmoSpaces is motivated by the need to create a rigorous, scalable, and open platform that can accurately assess and drive progress towards truly general-purpose robotic intelligence capable of operating reliably in the complexity of the real world.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The development of MolmoSpaces involved the creation and integration of several large-scale, high-fidelity components: scenes, objects, grasp data, robot models, and a modular experimental framework, culminating in a benchmark suite.

#### 3.1. MolmoSpaces Ecosystem Components

- **MolmoSpaces-Scenes (Over 230k environments):**
 - **Diversity:** Five distinct datasets are provided: `MSCrafted` (120 hand-crafted single-room scenes, e.g., kitchens, bedrooms); `MSProc` (12k procedurally generated multi-room residential scenes); `MSProcObja` (110k `MSProc` scenes augmented with Objaverse objects); `MSMultiType` (110k diverse scene types, e.g., art studies, cat cafes, generated via LLM-based procedural generation); and `MSTwin` (a high-fidelity digital twin of a real-world kitchen).
 - **Sourcing and Compatibility:** Scenes are sourced from AI2-THOR, ProcTHOR, and Holodeck. They are processed and tuned to be physically realistic and loadable across multiple popular simulators: MuJoCo, ManiSkill, and IsaacSim (via USD format).
 - **Generation (MSMultiType):** The Holodeck pipeline was extended to use a hierarchical selection of 546 room types and 101 scene types from the SUN database, suitable for THOR and Objaverse object taxonomies. LLM-based procedural generation is guided by scene specifications, optionally incorporating stylistic persona descriptions (filtered from PersonaHub via GPT-4.1-mini) to increase visual diversity. A "grid" layout constraint was introduced to the object placement optimizer for structured, uniform object arrangement.
 - **Quality Testing:** A systematic validation pipeline ensures physical realism and interaction feasibility. Four tests evaluate scene stability (no jitter), object intersections (no collisions between free objects), rigid object liftability (can be lifted by 5 cm), and articulation of movable objects (can actuate through 70% of range). Scenes are settled, and problematic objects are removed or adjusted. High pass rates (over 95% in MuJoCo, consistent in Isaac Sim) demonstrate robustness.

- **MolmoSpaces-Objects (Over 130k objects):**
 - **Sources:** Comprises 1.6k AI2-THOR objects (rigid and articulated) and 129k Objaverse objects.
 - **Annotation:** Objaverse objects are annotated with VLM-generated descriptions, mass estimates, canonical poses, pickable/receptacle properties, and WordNet synsets (GPT-4o). A complementary GPT-4.1 annotation addresses issues like multi-object presence, missing/excessive geometry, texture quality, and receptacle presence.
 - **Filtering:** A multi-stage filtering process ensures quality and compatibility, including criteria for metadata completeness, single object per file, statistical scale consistency, texture quality (score ≥ 4), cross-renderer fidelity (CLIP similarity ≥ 0.6), compact file size, watertight colliders, and robust synset coverage. An additional filter for `MSProcObja` maps synsets to placement-compatible THOR categories using heuristic similarities.
 - **Physical Properties:** Rigid objects' mass and density are validated against LLM-estimated values. Articulated objects' joint properties and part densities are manually tuned using a simulated Franka FR3 robot that was system-identified against real robot trajectories. Collider meshes are generated using COACD, with primitive colliders for receptacles and convex decomposition for manipulable objects (or primitives for very small/thin items for stability).
 - **Metadata and Compatibility:** All objects include extensive physical and semantic metadata, convex colliders, canonical coordinate definitions, and are provided in formats compatible with MuJoCo, IsaacSim, and ManiSkill.

- **MolmoSpaces-Grasp (Over 42M annotated grasps):**
 - **Scope:** Provides 6-DoF grasp poses for 48,111 rigid and articulable objects.
 - **Generation Pipeline:** Builds on existing methods (GraspGen, 6-DOF GraspNet, ACRONYM) with extensions for articulated objects. Antipodal contact pairs are sampled using a Robotiq 2F-85 gripper model. For articulated objects, sampling is restricted to functional interaction points (e.g., handles).
 - **Robustness and Diversity:** Up to 1,000 diverse grasps per object are selected, ensuring robustness through controlled linear and rotational perturbations for rigid objects and actuation feasibility (70% joint range motion) for articulated objects.
 - **In-Situ Validation:** Candidate grasps undergo collision checks at pre-grasp, grasp, and along the execution trajectory using a floating Robotiq 2F-85 gripper. Success rates are reported only for grasps that pass checks and complete the intended motion (lift for rigid, actuate for articulated).
 - **Refinement:** The pipeline was updated to bias contacts towards fingertip centers for stability and to prefer fingertip-edge contacts for small/thin objects.

- **Robots:**
 - **Diverse Embodiments:** Includes static (Franka FR3 with Franka Hand, Robotiq 2F-85, CAP grippers), mobile (Rainbow RB-Y1 holonomic mobile manipulator), and floating grippers (CAP, Robotiq). The Franka FR3 with Robotiq 2F-85 gripper is configured as a DROID system with specified camera intrinsics/extrinsics.
 - **Control:** Supports absolute/relative joint position commands for manipulators (gravity-compensated joint-space stiffness controller) and absolute/relative poses for mobile platforms.
 - **Kinematics:** Provides built-in forward and inverse kinematics solvers (JAX-based, GPU-accelerated Levenberg-Marquadt optimization) and a cuRobo wrapper for RB-Y1.

- **Modular Experiment Composition:** The framework enables flexible combination of any scene, object, robot, and camera configuration. It provides multi-view RGB, optional depth, proprioceptive signals (joint states, poses), task-state information, task annotations, planner signals, and action histories.

- **Data Collection:** Supports both manual data collection via the TeleDex iPhone app and scripted data generation using pre-computed grasps to control robots for defined tasks.

#### 3.2. MolmoSpaces-Bench Benchmark Suite

- **Task Definition:** Comprises eight base tasks across navigation, manipulation, and mobile manipulation: `navigate-to`, `pick`, `pick-and-place`, `pick-and-place-next-to`, `pick-and-place-color`, `open`, `close`, and `open-door`. Each task has well-defined success conditions and dense reward functions.
- **Evaluation Protocol:** Designed for zero-shot evaluation, meaning policies are assessed without task-specific fine-tuning on the benchmark data. Benchmarks require explicit diversity in scenes, object categories, and robot configurations, and all trials are verified for solvability.
- **Benchmark Creation:** A full set of benchmarks is generated for all combinations of environments and tasks (e.g., `MSCrafted`, `MSProc`, `MSProcObj`, `MSMultiType`), with 2,000 samples per task. Preferred evaluation configurations are suggested (e.g., `MSCrafted` for `open/close` tasks, `MSProcObj` for object diversity in manipulation).
- **Extensions:**
 - **Controlled Variants:** Facilitates hypothesis testing by creating adversarial benchmarks that perturb specific factors, such as initial robot configurations or environmental clutter.
 - **LLM-based Long-Horizon Task Generation:** An LLM-based system generates complex, abstract, long-horizon tasks (e.g., "Make a salad") by sequencing base tasks based on scene descriptions and predefined atomic checks.

#### 3.3. Evaluation Methods

- **Policy Selection:** Evaluated state-of-the-art policies include PI models (π0, π0-FAST, π0.5 - VLA models trained on real-world DROID data, using DROID setup), CAP models (task-specific, conditioned on 3D contact points from Gemini-Robotics-ER-1.5, using a custom gripper), RING (embodiment-agnostic, simulation-trained navigation policy), and DualVLN (dual-system VLN foundation model).
- **Sim-to-Real Correlation:** Performance in MolmoSpaces-Bench is compared to real-world data from RoboArena and CAP for pick, open, and close tasks using Pearson and Spearman rank correlation coefficients.
- **Distributional Evaluations:** Systematic perturbations are applied to scene parameters and sensor inputs, including prompt phrasing, initial joint positions, lighting intensity, camera occlusion, and point conditioning noise, to analyze policy robustness and identify failure modes.
- **Controlled Policy Comparison:** A unified Franka FR3 arm setup with three RGB-D cameras is used for fair manipulation task comparison. Analysis includes the impact of task horizon and the number of grasp-ungrasp transitions on policy success.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of MolmoSpaces-Bench using various state-of-the-art policies yielded several key findings regarding policy performance, robustness, and the utility of the simulation environment.

#### 4.1. Benchmark Utility and Policy Performance

MolmoSpaces-Bench effectively differentiated the performance of various robot policies, including VLA models (π-models) and classical baselines, across a range of diverse, unseen environments and objects in a zero-shot setting. A general trend of performance improvement was observed across successive generations of π-models (e.g., π0.5 outperforming π0), indicating steady progress in model development. For navigation, RING, trained on semantic navigation tasks, performed better than DualVLN, which is trained on detailed instructions, when evaluated on MolmoSpaces-Bench's semantic navigate-to task.

#### 4.2. Sim-to-Real Correlation

A strong correlation was observed between policy performance in MolmoSpaces-Bench and real-world outcomes. For the `pick` task, the Pearson correlation coefficient (R) was approximately 0.96 and the Spearman rank correlation coefficient (ρ) was approximately 0.98, based on comparisons with 752 real-world RoboArena pick tasks. This suggests that MolmoSpaces-Bench serves as a predictive and reliable proxy for real-world robot performance. For `open` and `close` tasks, consistent positive correlations were also observed, though with wider credible intervals due to fewer available real-world episodes.

#### 4.3. Distributional Evaluations and Policy Robustness

Systematic variations in environmental and policy factors revealed critical sensitivities and failure modes for the evaluated policies:

- **Prompt Sensitivity:** Minor changes in task instruction phrasing significantly impacted the success rates of π models, particularly earlier generations. For example, using prompt phrases more frequent in the DROID training dataset led to a performance boost for π0, bringing its success rate closer to that of π0.5. This indicates that language-conditioning components of earlier π models might be a source of generalization limitations.
- **Initial Joint Position Sensitivity:** Perturbing the initial joint positions of the π0.5 policy away from the default configuration used in DROID resulted in a decline in task success.
- **Lighting Robustness:** Varying lighting intensity had a minimal impact on policy performance, suggesting that visual distribution shifts are effectively mitigated by image-based data augmentation during policy training.
- **Camera Occlusion Dependence:** The π0.5 policy exhibited a strong reliance on wrist-mounted visual input. Occluding the wrist camera reduced its success rate to 2%, whereas occluding the third-person camera caused a smaller but still substantial drop to 20%.
- **Contact Conditioning Sensitivity (CAP):** The CAP policies demonstrated high sensitivity to the quality of the initial 3D "contact" conditioning point, with increased noise in these points leading to reduced success.
- **Grasping Approach Preferences:** Analysis of successful grasps showed that π0.5 policies tended to favor top-down grasps, leading to better performance on objects with top openings (e.g., mugs). In contrast, CAP policies favored side grasps, performing better on objects where side grasps are feasible (e.g., bottles). These distinct preferences explained variations in per-object category success rates between the policy families.
- **Task Horizon and Retries:** Policies, particularly π models, benefited from longer task horizons, allowing for multiple grasp-ungrasp transitions before achieving success. Oracle termination, which stops an episode upon the first successful state, showed higher success rates than fixed-horizon termination, indicating that policies sometimes achieve success but subsequently undo it if given more time. For instance, π0.5 averaged 2.65 grasp-ungrasp transitions in successful episodes, while π0 averaged 4.63.

#### 4.4. Controlled Policy Comparison

To enable fair comparisons, a unified Franka FR3 arm setup was used for manipulation benchmarks, with three RGB-D cameras. Task generation filtered out scenarios not physically achievable under the DROID setup. The choice of end-effector was noted as a factor in task difficulty.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

MolmoSpaces represents a notable contribution to the field of robot learning, with significant implications and potential impact:

- **Foundation for Scalable Robotics Research:** By providing a fully open ecosystem encompassing a vast number of diverse environments (over 230k), richly annotated object assets (130k+), and extensive grasp datasets (42M+), MolmoSpaces establishes a foundational infrastructure for scalable data generation, policy training, and benchmark creation. This openness is intended to lower barriers for researchers and accelerate the development cycle for robotic systems.
- **Rigorous Measurement of Generalization:** The ecosystem directly addresses the "long tail" problem in robotics by offering unprecedented scale and diversity in scenes and objects. This enables researchers to evaluate robot policies' generalization capabilities more rigorously than before, moving beyond evaluation on a few cherry-picked scenarios to comprehensive assessment across broad distributions of tasks, environments, and objects. This is crucial for advancing towards truly generalist robots.
- **Acceleration of Progress Towards General-Purpose Robotics:** By offering a community-driven and comprehensive platform for benchmarking and training, MolmoSpaces is expected to foster innovation and accelerate the pace of research towards robust, general-purpose robotic intelligence capable of operating effectively in complex, unstructured real-world settings.
- **Enhanced Policy Diagnostics and Improvement:** The detailed distributional evaluations, enabled by the scale and configurability of MolmoSpaces-Bench, provide critical diagnostic insights. The ability to systematically probe policy behavior under perturbations (e.g., prompt phrasing, initial poses, camera views) allows researchers to pinpoint specific failure modes and sensitivities. This diagnostic power can directly inform and guide the iterative improvement of algorithms and the design of more diverse and robust training data.
- **Validation of Simulation as a Real-World Proxy:** The demonstrated strong sim-to-real correlation, particularly for the pick task, validates MolmoSpaces-Bench as a reliable and predictive proxy for real-world robot performance. This finding is significant because it supports a shift from expensive and slow physical experiments to more scalable, repeatable, and cost-effective simulation-based evaluations, without sacrificing relevance to real-world deployment.
- **Bridging Gaps in Existing Research:** MolmoSpaces integrates and extends various aspects of prior work, combining large-scale scene generation, high-fidelity physics, object diversity, detailed grasp annotations, and multi-simulator compatibility into a unified, ready-to-use platform. This addresses the fragmentation often seen in existing resources, where individual projects might excel in one aspect but lack others.
- **Support for Future Research Directions:** The ecosystem is designed to support future endeavors such as scaling data generation for reinforcement learning, investigating the scaling behaviors of robot foundation models, and exploring more complex, long-horizon tasks through its LLM-assisted task generation capabilities. This positions MolmoSpaces as a valuable resource for tackling the next generation of robotics problems.
