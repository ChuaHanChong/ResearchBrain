# Beyond Noise Steering: Dual-Latent Space Reinforcement Learning for Generative Robot Policy

> [!summary] Summary
> DLSRL adapts frozen generative robot policies online by learning two latents: an initial-noise latent that steers behavior mode, and an action-representation latent mapped to adapter features residually injected into frozen Transformer action-token hidden states. Trained via action-space critic distilled into a latent critic, it speeds adaptation on RoboMimic and LIBERO, reaching about 99% success on Can versus 90% for noise-only steering.

## Problem

- Pretrained generative policies mismatch downstream distributions, yet full fine-tuning of billion-parameter models is costly and parameter-efficient methods still need offline demonstrations and gradient updates.
- Initial-noise steering only sets the generation starting point, so its influence on later intermediate action representations inside the frozen generator stays indirect and weak.
- Residual policies correct actions after decoding, never shaping internal representation evolution, which matters when tasks need precise contact locations, action magnitudes, or local trajectory fixes.

## Method

- ==Dual-latent actor== outputs an initial-noise latent steering behavior mode plus an action-representation latent, both conditioned on the observation and optimized purely from online task reward.
- Lightweight ==adapter feature mapper== converts the representation latent into action-token-shaped features, scaled by injection strength and residually added to selected frozen Transformer block outputs, no new layers.
- **DLSRL**'s dual-latent policy optimization trains an action-space ==TD critic==, distills its values into a ==latent critic== over noise and adapter features, then updates actor and mapper with ==entropy regularization==.

## Results

- On RoboMimic Can the method reaches roughly **99%** success versus about **90%** for noise-only steering, with faster early gains on Square and earlier saturation on Lift.
- Across six LIBERO tasks with a frozen flow-matching policy, average episode length drops **15.27%** to **25.40%** relative to noise-only steering, with far smaller variance.
- Ablation with noise steering disabled still lifts Can success from about **27%** to **52%**; injection strength **0.06** is best, **0.09** fluctuates, **0.03** improves slowly.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Frozen generators expose more than one usable control interface; combining boundary control with interior representation modulation buys sample efficiency without touching base weights.
> - Gains concentrate in adaptation speed and execution efficiency rather than higher final success, and precision-demanding tasks benefit most while easy tasks eventually equalize.
> - Modulation strength is a genuine tradeoff knob: too weak learns slowly, too strong perturbs pretrained representations and destabilizes late training.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of general-purpose robotic manipulation has seen advancements with the development of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. These models leverage large-scale vision-language and robotic datasets to learn expressive action priors, integrating visual perception, language understanding, and action prediction. Recent policy models have adopted generative action heads, such as Diffusion Policy and flow-matching techniques exemplified by RDT-1B and π0, to represent continuous and multimodal behavior distributions. These generative policies transform noise into action sequences, providing a foundation for complex manipulation tasks.

However, a key challenge arises when these VLA policies encounter discrepancies with their pretraining distribution, necessitating downstream adaptation. Full fine-tuning of large models, often with billions of parameters, is computationally intensive. Parameter-efficient methods exist, but they typically rely on offline demonstrations and gradient-based policy modifications. Consequently, online adaptation, particularly when the pretrained policy can remain frozen, is a valuable approach for scenarios with limited additional demonstrations.

Reinforcement Learning (RL) has emerged as a method to improve generative policies. Some approaches directly fine-tune diffusion policies using policy gradients, as seen in Diffusion Policy Policy Optimization (DPPO). A complementary strategy involves keeping the base policy frozen while learning an external guidance or correction mechanism. Examples include Diffusion Steering via Reinforcement Learning (DSRL), which learns an observation-conditioned initial-noise policy to steer a frozen generator without backpropagation through its parameters. Other methods involve external dynamics models to guide denoising trajectories (DynaGuide), learning residual actions on top of a fixed policy (Residual RL, Policy Decorator), or combining human corrections with noise-space optimization (UniSteer, LPS). A commonality among these methods is their ability to achieve task-specific adaptation without updating the pretrained generator.

Existing external steering interfaces predominantly operate at the boundaries of action generation. Initial-noise steering, while influencing the overall behavioral mode, has an indirect effect on later representations. Residual policies, conversely, apply corrections after action generation, thus not shaping the evolution of internal action representations directly. This limitation can be critical for tasks requiring precise adjustments to contact locations, action magnitudes, or local trajectories during the generation process itself. In controllable image generation, techniques like ControlNet and T2I-Adapter have demonstrated the effectiveness of injecting lightweight features into intermediate representations to modulate frozen generators. However, these methods are primarily designed for image generation and typically rely on predefined conditions with offline supervision, rather than addressing online robotic feedback.

This research addresses the identified gap by proposing an RL mechanism that integrates global behavior selection (via initial-noise steering) with direct modulation of intermediate action representations. It aims to extend policy guidance beyond the initial sampling phase to the intermediate feature modulation phase within the generative network, without requiring updates to the base policy itself.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this work stems from the limitations observed in existing reinforcement learning methods for adapting pretrained generative robot policies. While current approaches can steer the initial noisy space of these generative models, they typically fail to modulate intermediate action representations during the generation process. This limitation can lead to performance degradation and inefficiency, particularly in tasks requiring precise, fine-grained control or when dealing with significant distribution shifts from the pretraining data. The indirect nature of initial-noise steering and the "post-hoc" application of residual policies do not allow for direct shaping of the internal evolution of action representations.

The key objectives of this research are:
1. **Develop a novel reinforcement learning framework that enables dual-latent space control.** This involves simultaneously optimizing two distinct types of latent variables: an initial-noise latent variable for steering behavior generation and an action-representation latent variable for intermediate feature modulation. The goal is to extend policy guidance from the initial sampling initialization phase to the intermediate representation phase within a frozen generative model.
2. **Design a lightweight and non-invasive mechanism for intermediate feature modulation.** This mechanism should effectively map the action-representation latent variable to adapter features and inject them into the hidden states of intermediate action tokens. Crucially, this injection must be achieved via residual connections without requiring any updates to the parameters of the base generative policy. This ensures computational efficiency and preserves the pre-learned capabilities of the large generative model.
3. **Demonstrate the efficacy of the proposed framework in accelerating online robot policy adaptation.** The framework aims to improve the speed at which a robot policy can adapt to new tasks or environments through online interaction, while maintaining competitive final performance compared to existing state-of-the-art online adaptation methods.
4. **Evaluate the generalizability of the proposed approach across different generative policy architectures and robotic manipulation tasks.** The framework should be applicable to various generative models, including diffusion and flow-matching policies, and demonstrate consistent benefits in diverse manipulation scenarios.

By addressing these objectives, the research aims to provide a more direct and comprehensive control interface for adapting generative robot policies, potentially leading to more efficient and robust robotic agents in real-world applications.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, Dual-Latent Space Reinforcement Learning (DLSRL), introduces a framework for online adaptation of frozen generative robot policies by controlling both the initial noise and intermediate action representations.

**A. Problem Formulation**
Robotic manipulation tasks are framed as a Markov Decision Process (MDP). At each time step $t$, the policy receives an observation $o_t$, comprising visual input ($I_t$), robot proprioceptive state ($q_t$), and optionally a language instruction ($\ell$). The policy's output is a continuous action chunk $a_t \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times d_a}$, where $H$ is the action prediction horizon and $d_a$ is the action dimensionality. A pretrained generative base policy $G_\phi$, with frozen parameters $\phi$, generates this action chunk by transforming random noise $z_t \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I)$ conditioned on the observation: $a_t = G_\phi(o_t, z_t)$. This formulation applies to both diffusion and flow-matching policies. The objective is to learn a lightweight external control module through online interaction to maximize the expected discounted return $J = E[\sum_{t=0}^{T-1} \gamma^t r_t]$, without updating the base policy $G_\phi$.

**B. Dual-Latent Actor**
Unlike existing noise-space RL methods where an actor generates only an initial-noise latent variable $z_t \sim \pi_z^\theta(\cdot | o_t)$, DLSRL employs a dual-latent actor. This actor, parameterized by $\theta$, produces two distinct latent variables based on the current observation $o_t$:
$(z_t, u_t) \sim \pi_\theta(\cdot | o_t)$
where $z_t$ is the initial-noise latent variable and $u_t$ is the action-representation latent variable. The initial-noise latent variable $z_t$ primarily influences the overall behavioral mode selected by the base policy. The action-representation latent variable $u_t$ is mapped to adapter features $f_t$ via a lightweight adapter feature mapper $A_\omega$:
$f_t = A_\omega(u_t)$
The frozen base policy then generates the action chunk using both the observation, initial-noise, and adapter features:
$a_t = G_\phi(o_t, z_t, f_t)$
This design allows $u_t$ and $f_t$ to provide direct representation-level control during action generation, complementing the global behavior steering of $z_t$. Both latent variables are optimized based on online task rewards, while $\phi$ remains frozen and only $\theta$ and $\omega$ are updated.

**C. Action-Token Hidden-State Modulation**
The adapter feature $f_t$ modulates action generation by influencing the intermediate hidden states of the generative network, specifically within its Transformer blocks. DLSRL avoids introducing new trainable Transformer layers. As depicted in the methodology, $f_t$ is scaled by an injection strength $\lambda_{\text{inj}}$ and added residually to the action-token hidden states at the outputs of selected frozen Transformer blocks.
For the $k$-th generation update step and $l$-th Transformer block, the output action-token hidden states $H_{t,k}^{(l)}$ are modulated as follows:
$\tilde{H}_{t,k}^{(l)} = H_{t,k}^{(l)} + \delta_t$
where $\delta_t = \lambda_{\text{inj}} f_t$ is the adapter modulation. $H_{t,k}^{(l)} \in \mathbb{R}^{N_a \times d_h}$, where $N_a$ is the number of action tokens and $d_h$ is the Transformer hidden dimension. The adapter feature $f_t$ also has the shape $\mathbb{R}^{N_a \times d_h}$, ensuring direct alignment. The modulated hidden states $\tilde{H}_{t,k}^{(l)}$ are then passed to the subsequent Transformer block. The same adapter feature $f_t$ is shared across selected Transformer blocks and generation update steps. This residual injection only applies to action-token positions and does not modify other contextual tokens (e.g., visual, language, proprioceptive). This approach enables modification of intermediate action representations without altering the base-network architecture or its frozen parameters.

**D. Dual-Latent Policy Optimization**
The optimization strategy follows an action-to-latent value-distillation mechanism. It involves two critics and the dual-latent actor.
1. **Action-space critic $Q_{\text{act}}^\psi(o_t, a_t)$:** This critic is trained using a standard off-policy temporal-difference (TD) objective from online interaction data. It estimates the expected return of action chunks generated under dual-latent control.
2. **Latent space critic $Q_{\text{lat}}^\nu(o_t, z_t, f_t)$:** This critic is optimized by distilling value estimates from the action-space critic. Given an observation $o_t$ from the replay buffer, a standard Gaussian sample $\hat{z}_t$, and a sample $\hat{u}_t \sim \pi_u^\theta(\cdot | o_t)$, the corresponding adapter feature $\hat{f}_t$ and action chunk $\hat{a}_t$ are obtained. The objective is:
 $L_{\text{distill}} = E \left[ \left( Q_{\text{lat}}^\nu(o_t, \hat{z}_t, \hat{f}_t) - \text{sg}[Q_{\text{act}}^\psi(o_t, \hat{a}_t)] \right)^2 \right]$
 where $\text{sg}[\cdot]$ denotes the stop-gradient operation, ensuring gradients apply only to $\nu$. This allows $Q_{\text{lat}}^\nu$ to learn the joint effect of $z_t$ and $f_t$ on returns without backpropagating through the frozen generator.
3. **Dual-latent actor ($\pi_\theta$) and adapter feature mapper ($A_\omega$):** These are optimized by minimizing an entropy-regularized objective:
 $L_{\text{actor}} = E \left[ \alpha \log \pi_z^\theta(z_t | o_t) - Q_{\text{lat}}^\nu(o_t, z_t, f_t) \right]$
 Although entropy regularization is applied to the initial-noise branch, gradients of $Q_{\text{lat}}^\nu$ with respect to $z_t$ and $f_t$ propagate through both actor branches and the adapter feature mapper.

The training procedure involves alternate updates of the action-space critic, latent space critic, and the dual-latent actor/adapter feature mapper, while $G_\phi$ remains frozen. During deployment, only the dual-latent actor and adapter feature mapper are used.

**E. Experimental Setup**
Experiments were conducted on two sets of tasks:
- **RoboMimic:** Lift, Can, and Square tasks, using a pretrained Transformer-based Diffusion Policy. Comparisons included Base Policy, JSRL, DPPO, and DSRL.
- **LIBERO:** Six tasks (Stove-On, CreamCheese-to-Tray, Bowl-Drawer-to-Plate, WineBottle-to-Rack, Plate-to-StoveFront, Bowl-to-TopDrawer), utilizing a flow-matching VLA policy ($\pi_0$). DSRL was the primary comparison.
The success rate was evaluated over 100 episodes during training.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments evaluated DLSRL across different generative policy architectures and robotic manipulation tasks, focusing on online adaptation efficiency and performance.

**A. Online Adaptation Efficiency**
On the RoboMimic tasks (Lift, Can, Square), DLSRL demonstrated faster performance improvement during the early and intermediate stages of training compared to other methods.
- **Lift Task:** DLSRL achieved a near-saturated performance level earlier in training. While DSRL eventually narrowed the performance gap in later stages, DLSRL's initial acceleration was notable.
- **Can Task:** DLSRL reached approximately 99% success in the later training stages, surpassing DSRL's approximately 90%.
- **Square Task:** DLSRL exhibited faster and more stable performance gains throughout the training process.
The results indicated that DLSRL's primary advantage was faster adaptation rather than uniformly higher final performance, with this benefit being more pronounced in tasks requiring greater action precision (Can and Square). DPPO exhibited slower improvement, and JSRL did not consistently outperform the Base Policy under the given interaction budget.

**B. Transfer to Flow-Matching VLA Policies**
On the six LIBERO tasks, using the pretrained π0 flow-matching policy, DLSRL generally improved the success rate more rapidly and maintained higher performance over most of the interaction budget compared to DSRL.
- DLSRL typically reached saturated performance with fewer environment interactions.
- Quantitatively, DLSRL achieved shorter average episode lengths across all six tasks. The reduction in average episode length ranged from 15.27% (WineBottle-to-Rack) to 25.40% (Stove-On) compared to DSRL, indicating improved task execution efficiency.
These consistent improvements suggest that the advantages of dual-latent control are not restricted to diffusion policies and are observable in flow-matching-based VLA policies as well.

**C. Contribution of Representation Modulation (Ablation Study)**
To isolate the effect of representation-level modulation, an ablation study was conducted with DLSRL-Rep on the Can task. DLSRL-Rep retained only action-token hidden-state modulation, disabling learned initial-noise steering.
- DLSRL-Rep improved steadily from an initial success rate close to that of the frozen base policy (approximately 27%) to 52%.
This finding indicates that modifying intermediate action-token representations alone can enhance the base policy's performance, even without the concurrent learned initial-noise steering. This result supports the hypothesis that the combination of both control interfaces in the full DLSRL framework contributes to the observed acceleration in the adaptation process.

**D. Effect of Modulation Strength**
An analysis of the action-token hidden-state injection strength ($\lambda_{\text{inj}}$) was performed on the Can task, with values of $\lambda_{\text{inj}} \in \{0, 0.03, 0.06, 0.09\}$.
- $\lambda_{\text{inj}} = 0$ corresponds to no representation modulation, showing only the effect of noise steering (similar to DSRL).
- $\lambda_{\text{inj}} = 0.09$ resulted in the most rapid initial performance improvement but also exhibited larger fluctuations later in training.
- $\lambda_{\text{inj}} = 0.03$ produced a more gradual yet stable improvement path.
- $\lambda_{\text{inj}} = 0.06$ provided a balance between adaptation speed and stability, eventually achieving a 100% success rate.
These observations suggest that appropriate representation-level control requires sufficient modulation strength to be effective, without excessively perturbing the pretrained action representations, which could lead to instability.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces a Dual-Latent Space Reinforcement Learning (DLSRL) framework, which represents an advancement in the online adaptation of frozen generative robot policies. Its significance lies in addressing a previously identified limitation where existing RL methods for generative policies primarily steer the initial noisy space but do not modulate intermediate action representations.

The main contributions and potential impacts are:
1. **Enhanced Control Granularity:** DLSRL provides a novel control interface that simultaneously optimizes both initial-noise latent variables (for global behavior selection) and action-representation latent variables (for intermediate feature modulation). This dual-control mechanism allows for more fine-grained and direct adjustment of action generation, moving beyond merely influencing the starting point or applying post-hoc corrections.
2. **Improved Adaptation Efficiency:** The experimental results consistently demonstrate that DLSRL accelerates online adaptation speed across diverse robotic manipulation tasks and different generative policy architectures (diffusion and flow-matching). This efficiency gain is particularly valuable for real-world robotic applications where obtaining extensive online interaction data can be costly or time-consuming. Faster adaptation enables robots to more quickly adjust to novel environments or task variations.
3. **Computational Efficiency:** By maintaining the base generative policy frozen, DLSRL avoids the computationally expensive process of full fine-tuning large, high-parameter models. The lightweight residual modulation mechanism, which injects adapter features into hidden states without modifying the base network's architecture or parameters, ensures that the adaptation process remains parameter-efficient. This approach makes it feasible to adapt large foundation models for robotics in resource-constrained settings.
4. **Broader Applicability:** The effectiveness of DLSRL across both Diffusion Policy and π0 (flow-matching) architectures suggests its potential as a general method for adapting various types of generative robot policies. This broad applicability increases its relevance to the wider field of robotic learning.
5. **Robustness and Precision:** The ability to directly modulate intermediate action representations allows for more precise corrections to aspects like contact locations, action magnitudes, or local trajectories during the generation process. This could lead to more robust policy performance, especially in tasks demanding high dexterity or accuracy where minor errors can lead to task failure.

The proposed DLSRL framework opens avenues for future research into the intricate interplay of injection layers, generation steps, and modulation strengths, which could further optimize adaptation performance. Its immediate impact could be observed in robotic systems requiring rapid online learning and adaptation, such as those deployed in dynamic or unstructured environments where pretraining data may not fully capture all possible scenarios.
