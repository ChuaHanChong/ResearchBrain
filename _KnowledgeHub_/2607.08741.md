# ARDY: Autoregressive Diffusion with Hybrid Representation for Interactive Human Motion Generation

> [!summary] Summary
> ARDY introduces an autoregressive diffusion framework that generates 3D human motion in real-time, combining high controllability from online text prompts and diverse kinematic constraints. The system achieves an average generation latency of 33ms (4-step model) and demonstrates superior control accuracy and perceptual quality over existing autoregressive methods on the HumanML3D benchmark.

## Problem

- Offline 3D human motion generation models provide high controllability but are too slow for real-time interactive applications.
- Existing online motion generation methods offer real-time speeds but often lack comprehensive controllability for complex text semantics or long-horizon kinematic goals.
- A fundamental trade-off exists between real-time interactive performance and precise, high-level control in human motion generation.

## Method

- The **ARDY** framework employs a ==hybrid motion representation==, with an explicit global root and a learned latent embedding for the body, balancing control precision and computational efficiency.
- An ==autoregressive two-stage diffusion model== is developed to denoise hybrid motion tokens, conditioned on variable history, text prompts, and future spatial goals.
- A ==latency-aware replanning mechanism== is used at test-time to mask inference latency, ensuring smooth transitions and real-time responsiveness for user interactions.

## Results

- ARDY achieved an average generation latency of **33ms** for its 4-step diffusion model and **63ms** for its 10-step model on an RTX 4090 GPU, supporting interactive rates.
- On the HumanML3D dataset, ARDY surpassed prior autoregressive methods like DiP across all metrics, achieving higher R-precision (**0.690** vs. **0.609**) and significantly lower joint error (**2.48 cm** vs. **9.20 cm** for in-horizon goals).
- A human perceptual study consistently favored ARDY over DiP, with preferences for motion quality (**65.8%**), semantic alignment (**67.5%**), and goal accuracy (**64.6%**).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A hybrid motion representation effectively combines precise global control with efficient learning of complex body dynamics within a generative model.
> - Decomposing the denoising process into an interleaved two-stage architecture (root then body prediction) significantly improves both motion fidelity and adherence to spatial constraints.
> - Native conditioning on long-horizon kinematic goals within an autoregressive diffusion framework enables precise control without relying on external optimization or reinforcement learning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The generation of realistic 3D human motion is an area of active research with applications spanning character animation, virtual simulations, and humanoid robotics. The field broadly encompasses two categories of approaches: offline and online (or interactive) motion generation.

Offline motion generation models, such as those based on diffusion (e.g., Karunratanakul et al. 2023; Rempe et al. 2026; Tevet et al. 2023) or generative masked modeling (e.g., Guo et al. 2024; Pinyoanuntapong et al. 2025), have demonstrated capabilities in producing high-fidelity motions controllable via complex text prompts and various kinematic constraints, including pose keyframes and joint positions. However, these methods typically operate by generating an entire sequence of poses in parallel, leading to inference times that are often too slow for real-time interactive applications. While some advancements have focused on accelerating the denoising process, the fundamental design remains primarily suited for non-interactive scenarios.

In contrast, online or interactive motion generation methods are designed for real-time synthesis, commonly employing autoregressive models that generate motion sequentially. While these models are fast and can produce realistic animations, they have historically faced limitations in controllability. Many existing online approaches either support text conditioning without extensive kinematic control (e.g., Xiao et al. 2025) or enable kinematic constraints without accommodating text input (e.g., Chen et al. 2024; Shi et al. 2024). A subset of recent interactive methods attempts to integrate both text and kinematic constraints (e.g., Tevet et al. 2025; Zhao et al. 2025a), but their effectiveness can be limited by restricted context windows, which impede the understanding of global text semantics and the realization of long-horizon kinematic goals. Furthermore, some methods necessitate additional control mechanisms, such as expensive test-time optimization or reinforcement learning (RL) policies, to achieve precise kinematic control.

This work, ARDY, positions itself as a system designed to bridge the gap between these two paradigms. It aims to combine the high controllability offered by offline models (complex text prompts and flexible kinematic constraints) with the real-time, streaming generation capability characteristic of online methods. The paper distinguishes itself by offering native support for online text prompting and flexible, long-horizon kinematic constraints within an autoregressive diffusion framework, aiming to eliminate the need for external control modules.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a robust system for generating realistic 3D human motions in real-time within interactive applications. The authors identify a critical gap in existing motion generation techniques: while offline methods offer high controllability via text and kinematic constraints, they lack the inference speed necessary for interactive settings. Conversely, current online methods, despite their real-time capabilities, often compromise on controllability, struggling with complex text semantics or long-horizon kinematic goals due to limited context windows.

The key motivations and specific objectives driving the development of ARDY include:

- **Achieving Interactive Generation with High Controllability:** The central motivation is to overcome the trade-off between real-time performance and precise control. ARDY aims to enable streaming motion generation that is highly controllable through both online text prompts and flexible kinematic constraints.
- **Supporting Diverse Kinematic Constraints:** A detailed objective is to natively support a comprehensive suite of kinematic constraints relevant to interactive applications. This includes root waypoints and trajectories, full-body keyframes, and sparse joint positions and rotations, applicable over long temporal horizons.
- **Handling Complex Text Semantics and Long-Horizon Goals:** The model is designed to process and understand complex natural language descriptions for motion, as well as to achieve kinematic goals that extend beyond a short, immediate generation window. This requires a mechanism to manage variable history context and incorporate future goals effectively.
- **Ensuring Real-time Performance:** A core requirement is to achieve low-latency motion synthesis suitable for real-time user interaction. The system aims for responsive generation from real-time user inputs, such as mouse and keyboard commands.
- **Simplifying the Control Pipeline:** ARDY intends to learn controllable generation natively from its training data, directly conditioned on text labels and sampled kinematic constraints. This approach aims to eliminate the need for additional control modules, such as expensive test-time optimization or reinforcement learning-based control policies, which are often required by other methods.
- **Developing an Efficient Motion Representation:** The authors aim to devise a motion representation that balances the need for precise explicit control over certain motion aspects with the computational efficiency required for generative learning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The ARDY framework comprises two primary components: a motion tokenizer that learns a compact latent representation of body motion, and an autoregressive two-stage motion diffusion model that denoises hybrid motion tokens.

#### 3.1. Hybrid Motion Representation

To balance computational efficiency for generative learning with the need for direct, explicit control, ARDY employs a hybrid motion representation. Each motion frame `m` is decomposed into `(m_root, m_body)`.
- **Explicit Root Representation:** `m_root` captures global root features, including 3D global position (`p`) and 2D heading orientation (`cosψ, sinψ`). Representing the root explicitly in global coordinates aims to avoid compounding errors inherent in local velocity-based representations and facilitates direct overwriting for spatial constraints defined in scene space.
- **Latent Body Representation:** `m_body` (6D global joint rotations, non-root joint positions, global joint velocities, binary floor contact labels) is replaced by `x_body`, a compact latent embedding derived from a learned tokenizer.
- **Hybrid Token Formation:** For the diffusion model, the latent body motion `x_body` is concatenated with the patchified explicit root motion `m_root` to form hybrid tokens `x = [m_root; x_body]`. A patch size `P=4` frames is used, meaning each token encodes multiple frames of motion.

#### 3.2. Body Motion Tokenizer

A motion tokenization network is trained as an asymmetric conditional autoencoder to compress high-dimensional explicit body features into a compact latent space.
- **Encoder:** Embeds patchified explicit body motion into latent tokens `x_body`.
- **Decoder:** Reconstructs the explicit body motion from the hybrid tokens `[m_root; x_body]`. Crucially, within the decoder, the global root motion `m_root` is first transformed into a local representation (1D angular velocity of heading, x and z components of linear root velocity, y-component of root height). This local representation is found to mitigate foot skating during reconstruction.
- **Architecture:** Both encoder and decoder utilize 8-layer causal transformer layers with causal attention to maintain temporal causality.
- **Training:** The tokenizer is trained with a reconstruction loss and an additional foot-skating loss, which penalizes the velocities of foot joints predicted to be in contact with the ground. Finite Scalar Quantization (FSQ) is adopted for the latent space, providing 64 discrete levels per feature dimension, chosen for its training stability.

#### 3.3. Controllable Interactive Motion Generation Formulation

The problem is formulated as a conditional autoregressive generation task. The model `F` is trained to synthesize a short window of `C` future motion tokens, conditioned on:
- A variable-length history motion `x_(-H+1):0` (up to `H` previous tokens).
- A text prompt `s` describing motion semantics.
- Spatial goals `g_1:(C+F)` which can span `C` tokens within the current prediction window and `F` additional tokens beyond it.
This formulation allows ARDY to handle variable history context, crucial for complex non-cyclic motions, and natively incorporate long-horizon kinematic goals without additional control policies.

#### 3.4. Autoregressive Two-Stage Diffusion Model

The core of ARDY is a transformer-based diffusion denoiser that learns to predict clean hybrid motion tokens.
- **Diffusion Process:** At each denoising step `k`, the model takes noisy hybrid tokens `x_1:C_k` along with conditioning inputs, and predicts the clean hybrid tokens `x_1:C_0_hat`.
- **Conditioning:** The diffusion step and text conditioning are fed as separate tokens. Sinusoidal positional encodings are used for motion tokens, while learned embeddings are used for text and diffusion tokens.
- **Spatial Goal Conditioning:**
 - Spatial goals `g` are represented as a masked version of the explicit motion representation.
 - The root component of the input noisy tokens `m_1:C_root` is directly *overwritten* with the root constraint from `g`, allowing precise root trajectory control.
 - Explicit body goal features and a corresponding binary mask (`v`) are concatenated with the input tokens along the feature dimension to inform the model about body constraints.
 - Long-horizon constraints `g_(C+1):(C+F)` are fed as additional tokens to the transformer.
- **Interleaved Two-Stage Denoiser Architecture:** This design decomposes the prediction of root and body motion.
 1. **Root Transformer:** First predicts the explicit clean global root motion `m_1:C_root_hat`.
 2. **Body Transformer:** Then predicts the clean latent body tokens `x_1:C_body_hat`, *conditioned on the detached prediction from the root transformer*.
 These predictions are concatenated to form the full hybrid motion prediction. This interleaved process ensures continuous mutual influence between root and body throughout denoising.
- **Loss Function:** The denoiser is trained using the DDPM framework with a "simplified" loss incorporating:
 - `L_hybrid`: Smooth L1 loss between predicted and ground-truth hybrid tokens.
 - `L_dec`: Smooth L1 loss between decoded predicted explicit body motion and ground-truth explicit body motion.
 - `L_goal`: Smooth L1 loss specifically penalizing unfulfilled constrained components in the full explicit motion prediction.
 - `L_consist`: L2 regularization for consistency between directly predicted joint positions and those derived via forward kinematics from predicted joint rotations.
- **Training Details:** Trained on motion clips of varying lengths (1-10 seconds) with a fixed-size generation window of `G=40` frames, resulting in dynamically varying history and future context lengths. Data augmentation includes random rotations and random sampling of diverse spatial constraints. Classifier-free guidance is enabled by randomly dropping text prompts and spatial constraints during training. Default configuration uses 10 diffusion steps, with an efficient 4-step model also developed.

#### 3.5. Test-Time Operation

During inference, ARDY operates autoregressively, generating sequential windows of motion.
- **Sliding Window:** Generated frames from a previous window become history for the subsequent window. Both history and future contexts are managed with a truncated sliding window (up to 8 seconds max).
- **Root Alignment:** The root component of history poses is translated to the origin before being fed to the model, and generated motion is translated back into global coordinates.
- **Latency-Aware Replanning:** To maintain interactivity, a non-blocking replanning mechanism is employed. When new user input is detected, a buffer of `B` already-generated frames is played back while the new sequence is generated asynchronously, masking inference latency and ensuring smooth transitions.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research provides both qualitative and quantitative results from an interactive demo, ablation studies on a large-scale proprietary dataset, and benchmark evaluations on a public dataset.

#### 4.1. Interactive Demo Results

The interactive demonstration highlights ARDY's capabilities in real-time.
- **Latency:** The efficient 4-step diffusion model achieves an average generation latency of 33 ms, and the 10-step model achieves 63 ms on an RTX 4090 GPU, supporting interactive rates.
- **Controllability:** The system successfully processes complex text descriptions and adapts to dynamic changes in user-specified text prompts and interactive kinematic constraints. These constraints include dense root trajectories, sparse root waypoints, full-body keyframes, and sparse joint positions/rotations.
- **Locomotion Control:** Supports real-time mouse-based waypoint following and keyboard-based root velocity control.

#### 4.2. Ablation Study on Bones Rigplay Dataset

Evaluations on the large-scale Bones Rigplay dataset (700 hours of motion data) assessed key architectural choices:
- **Hybrid Motion Representation:** The proposed hybrid representation significantly outperforms a purely explicit motion representation across motion quality (lower FID) and control accuracy metrics. For example, joint position error is 0.025m for ARDY vs. 0.130m for explicit, and root trajectory error is 0.015m vs. 0.033m. This suggests the latent body embedding facilitates more efficient generative learning compared to high-dimensional explicit features.
- **Global-to-Local Root Conversion in Decoder:** Removing this conversion in the tokenizer decoder leads to a notable increase in foot skating (0.303 m/s vs. 0.264 m/s for ARDY), confirming the importance of local root representations for preserving physical plausibility and motion quality.
- **Two-Stage Denoiser Design:** The interleaved two-stage architecture yields superior performance compared to a one-stage baseline that jointly predicts root and body motion. It results in higher-fidelity text-conditioned motion (comparable R-prec/FID) and significantly lower spatial constraint errors (e.g., keyframe body error of 0.023m for ARDY vs. 0.079m for one-stage). This indicates that decomposing root and body prediction assists in simultaneously achieving high fidelity and precise control.

#### 4.3. Hyperparameter and Tokenizer Type Analysis

Further analysis on the Bones Rigplay dataset explored the impact of various settings:
- **Generation Horizon:** Longer horizons (e.g., 40 frames) generally improve motion fidelity (lower FID) and semantic alignment (higher R-precision) compared to shorter horizons (e.g., 4 or 8 frames). However, an 8-frame horizon showed faster learning of constraint adherence and quicker action transitions.
- **Diffusion Steps:** While extreme reductions (1 or 2 steps) degrade quality and constraint adherence, ARDY's few-step models (e.g., 4 steps) still achieved competitive performance, demonstrating the robustness of the learned representation. Increasing steps from 4 to 10 provided slight improvements across metrics.
- **Tokenizer Patch Size:** A patch size of 4 frames provided a balance between performance and stability. A single-frame patch led to training instability, while a larger 8-frame patch improved FID/R-precision but degraded skating and constraint accuracy due to increased information loss within patches.
- **Tokenizer Latent Space Capacity:** Performance was relatively similar across different FSQ configurations (e.g., 16-32, 64-32, 64-128, 64-256). A smaller capacity (FSQ 16-32) slightly improved FID/R-precision but degraded fine-grained constraint adherence.
- **Tokenizer Type:** VAE and FSQ tokenizers performed comparably to a vanilla autoencoder in terms of metrics. However, the FSQ tokenizer demonstrated superior training stability, especially with longer horizons, leading to its selection as the default.

#### 4.4. Benchmark Evaluation on HumanML3D Dataset

ARDY was compared against state-of-the-art offline and autoregressive models on the HumanML3D dataset.
- **Offline Model Comparison (vs. MaskControl):**
 - ARDY achieved competitive text-following (R-precision of 0.729 vs. MaskControl's 0.760 before optimization, 0.732 for dataset) and motion quality (FID of 0.044 vs. MaskControl's 0.050).
 - ARDY demonstrated a lower foot skating ratio (6.28% vs. MaskControl's 7.27%).
 - Crucially, ARDY yielded significantly lower spatial control errors before test-time optimization (4.15 cm vs. MaskControl's 46.18 cm), suggesting a stronger native generative prior. With test-time optimization, ARDY achieved 0.30 cm error at 9.25s latency, compared to MaskControl's 0.45 cm error at 68.65s latency.
- **Autoregressive Model Comparison (vs. DiP):**
 - ARDY surpassed DiP across all metrics for both "in-horizon" and "out-of-horizon" goal scenarios.
 - For "in-horizon" goals, ARDY achieved higher R-precision (0.690 vs. DiP's 0.609), lower FID (0.092 vs. 0.967), less skating (7.07% vs. 12.29%), and lower joint error (2.48 cm vs. 9.20 cm).
 - For "out-of-horizon" goals, DiP showed a sharp increase in joint error (17.64 cm), indicating limitations in long-term planning, while ARDY maintained high accuracy (2.92 cm).
 - **Perceptual Study:** A human perceptual study involving 240 pairwise comparisons consistently favored ARDY over DiP in motion quality (65.8% preference), semantic alignment (67.5% preference), and goal accuracy (64.6% preference), validating the quantitative gains.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of ARDY represents an advancement in the field of 3D human motion generation by addressing the long-standing challenge of combining real-time interactivity with precise, high-level control.

- **Bridging the Interactive-Controllability Gap:** ARDY provides a framework that effectively integrates online text prompting and a diverse suite of kinematic constraints, including those spanning long temporal horizons, into a real-time motion generation system. This addresses a limitation of prior interactive methods which often sacrificed control or struggled with long-term goals.
- **Enhanced Controllability Without Auxiliary Modules:** The method's ability to natively support various kinematic constraints and long-horizon goals without requiring additional test-time optimization or RL-based control policies simplifies the workflow for animators and developers, and potentially improves efficiency by reducing computational overhead during runtime.
- **Robustness and Quality:** The robust performance of ARDY, demonstrated through extensive evaluations on both large-scale proprietary and public datasets, indicates its capacity to generate high-quality, semantically aligned motions while adhering to complex constraints, even with a limited number of diffusion steps. The human perceptual study further validates that these quantitative improvements translate to perceived qualitative gains.
- **Architectural Contributions:** The introduction of a hybrid motion representation (explicit root combined with a latent body embedding) and an interleaved two-stage autoregressive diffusion denoiser provides a novel approach to optimize both precise root control and efficient body motion generation. The latency-aware replanning mechanism also offers a practical solution for deploying diffusion models in real-time interactive environments.
- **Broadening Application Scope:** The capabilities demonstrated by ARDY could have implications across several domains:
 - **Character Animation:** It can enable animators and game developers to create more dynamic and responsive characters that react fluidly to user inputs and environmental changes.
 - **Interactive Simulations:** The technology could be integrated into various simulation environments, allowing virtual agents to perform complex, context-aware actions in real time.
 - **Humanoid Robotics:** High-quality, controllable human motion generation can provide crucial data for training and real-time planning of humanoid robots, facilitating the development of more agile and versatile robotic systems.

While ARDY exhibits limitations, such as the potential inefficiency of explicit past frame usage for extremely long tasks and the absence of inherent physical dynamics, it establishes a foundation for future research in areas such as efficient memory structures, accelerated diffusion models, and the integration of physics-based modeling to achieve even more physically plausible and robust generated motions.
