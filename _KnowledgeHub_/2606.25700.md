# Memory-Efficient Policy Libraries with Low-Rank Adaptation in Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> This research investigates the application of Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) to online Reinforcement Learning (RL) in multi-task robotics, demonstrating that it creates memory-efficient specialist policy libraries. The method reduced trainable parameters by 92.8% to 99.4% and achieved storage savings up to 93% for a library of 50 policies, while maintaining task success rates comparable to full fine-tuning.

## Problem

- Multi-task Reinforcement Learning (RL) faces catastrophic forgetting, leading to the necessity of maintaining specialist policy libraries.
- Storing a library of full specialist policies incurs high memory and storage demands, hindering deployment on resource-constrained robotic hardware.
- The direct applicability of Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (PEFT) techniques like LoRA to online multi-task robotics RL, especially for creating policy libraries, was largely unexplored.

## Method

- A pre-trained base Reinforcement Learning (RL) policy, utilizing the PPO algorithm, was fine-tuned for various robotic manipulation tasks.
- ==Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA)== was implemented by injecting small, trainable low-rank matrices into the existing policy layers while freezing the original base policy weights.
- Performance, memory efficiency, and computational costs of LoRA fine-tuning were compared against full fine-tuning across six distinct Meta-World robotic manipulation tasks.

## Results

- LoRA fine-tuning reduced the number of trainable parameters by **92.8%** to **99.4%** compared to full fine-tuning, depending on the chosen rank.
- Storing a library of LoRA-adapted policies achieved up to **93%** memory savings for 50 specialist policies compared to storing 50 fully fine-tuned policies.
- LoRA maintained task success rates comparable to full fine-tuning across most tasks, with higher ranks (e.g., rank 8) enabling successful training on more complex tasks, and also reduced computational cost per PPO environment step by **23.5%** to **30.2%**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - LoRA successfully transfers from Large Language Models (LLMs) to the domain of online Reinforcement Learning for multi-task robotics.
> - Significant reductions in memory footprint for policy storage and computational cost during training can be achieved with LoRA without substantial degradation in task success rates.
> - The constrained adaptation provided by LoRA can sometimes offer regularization benefits, leading to improved performance or lower failure rates compared to full fine-tuning for certain tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in this paper is situated within the field of Reinforcement Learning (RL), specifically addressing challenges in multi-task robotics. Multi-task RL aims to develop agents capable of performing multiple distinct tasks, which is particularly relevant for robotic systems expected to operate in diverse environments. A significant hurdle in multi-task learning is catastrophic forgetting, where a policy trained on a new task loses its ability to perform previously learned tasks.

One approach to mitigate catastrophic forgetting is to maintain a library of specialist policies, each trained for a specific task. While this method bypasses the issue of forgetting, it introduces a new challenge: the high memory and storage demands associated with storing a separate, fully trained policy for each task. This becomes a critical limitation for deployment on resource-constrained robotic hardware, where memory access speed and storage capacity are often limited.

In recent years, Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (PEFT) techniques, such as Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA), have demonstrated success in Large Language Models (LLMs). LoRA, by decomposing weight updates into smaller, low-rank matrices and freezing the original model weights, significantly reduces the number of trainable parameters during fine-tuning. This approach has led to substantial reductions in memory usage and computational cost in the context of LLMs.

However, the application of LoRA within the domain of robotics and RL remains relatively unexplored. While some related work has investigated adapter-based fine-tuning for skill generalization across different robotic embodiments (e.g., Lu et al.), these studies typically focus on adapting a single skill to varying physical platforms or a limited set of related tasks. Other approaches, like Mixture-of-Experts (MOE) (e.g., Arora, Cheng et al.), aim for a single adaptive policy that can generalize to unseen tasks by combining simpler interactions. This contrasts with the expert policy library approach, which focuses on pre-training distinct specialists for known tasks.

This paper distinguishes itself by specifically investigating the direct transferability of LoRA to online RL in a multi-task robotics setting, focusing on the creation and storage efficiency of a library of specialist policies for a single robot tackling diverse manipulation tasks. The aim is to bridge the gap between PEFT advancements in LLMs and the practical memory constraints faced in robotic RL.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to investigate whether the Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) technique, which has proven effective in Large Language Models (LLMs) for minimizing memory usage and computation during fine-tuning, can be successfully transferred and applied to the domain of robotics and Reinforcement Learning (RL).

Specifically, the authors aim to:
1. **Evaluate Memory Efficiency:** Determine if LoRA can significantly reduce the memory footprint required for storing a library of specialist policies in a multi-task robotics setting, particularly when compared to full fine-tuning of all model layers.
2. **Assess Computational Performance:** Quantify the computational savings, in terms of Floating-Point Operations (FLOPs), achieved by using LoRA during the fine-tuning process for RL policies.
3. **Compare Policy Performance:** Ascertain whether LoRA fine-tuning maintains a comparable level of task success rate and learning efficiency (timesteps to success) relative to full fine-tuning, across a variety of robotic manipulation tasks.
4. **Demonstrate a Novel Use Case:** Introduce and validate the concept of using LoRA to efficiently create and deploy a library of specialist policies from a shared base policy, designed for seamless task switching in robotic applications.

The motivation behind these objectives stems from several practical challenges in multi-task robotics:
- **Catastrophic Forgetting:** Training a single policy for multiple tasks often leads to the agent forgetting previously learned skills. A library of specialist policies offers a robust solution to this, but is limited by storage capacity.
- **Resource Constraints in Robotics:** Robotic platforms, especially mobile robots, often have limited onboard memory and computational resources. Storing many large policy networks can quickly exhaust these resources, necessitating the use of slower swap memory or limiting the number of deployable tasks.
- **Deployment and Scalability:** Efficient storage is crucial for deploying robots capable of performing numerous tasks. A memory-efficient approach could enable robots to switch between a wider array of specialized skills without significant delays caused by loading policies from external storage.
- **Knowledge Transfer:** The ability to fine-tune a pre-trained base policy for new tasks leverages prior learning, potentially speeding up training and improving stability in the often noisy RL environment.

By addressing these points, the research seeks to provide a viable solution for building scalable and deployable multi-task robotic systems, where memory and computational efficiency are paramount.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors employed a methodology centered on fine-tuning a pre-trained base Reinforcement Learning (RL) policy using Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) and comparing its performance against full fine-tuning.

**A. Reinforcement Learning Algorithm:**
The study utilized the Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) algorithm, implemented from the Stable Baselines3 (SB3) library. PPO is an on-policy algorithm known for its stability and efficiency, operating within an actor-critic framework. The policy network (actor) and value network (critic) each consisted of three dense layers with 400 nodes. Observations and rewards were normalized, and four environments were run simultaneously using SB3’s VecEnv for parallel training.

**B. Environment:**
The experiments were conducted using the Gymnasium API integrated with the Meta-World environment. Meta-World provides 50 robotic manipulation tasks for a Sawyer-arm setup, involving actions such as reaching, pushing, and grasping. The tasks feature randomized object and goal positions to encourage generalization. The study specifically focused on the MT1 setting, where a single policy is trained for variations within one task, deviating from Meta-World's intended multi-task evaluation regimes.
- **Action Space:** A tuple representing changes in the end-effector's 3D position and gripper torque.
- **Observation Space:** 39 values, including the position and orientation of two objects, the goal position, and data from the previous observation.

**C. Training Pipeline:**
The training process involved two main stages:
1. **Base Policy Training:** A base policy was initially trained on the "pick-place" task from Meta-World for 2 million timesteps. This task was selected due to its requirement for 3-axis arm movement, two-stage operation, and randomized primary/secondary goals, along with its importance for learning gripper usage, which is fundamental to many Meta-World tasks.
2. **Fine-tuning:** The pre-trained base policy was then fine-tuned to a suite of six target tasks: "button-press-topdown-wall," "hammer," "drawer-open," "window-open," "basketball," and "peg-unplug-side." These tasks were chosen to represent a variety of manipulation challenges.

**D. Fine-tuning Configurations:**
Two fine-tuning approaches were compared:
- **Full Fine-tuning:** All layers of the base policy were updated during training for the target task.
- **LoRA Fine-tuning:** The original base policy weights were frozen. Low-rank trainable matrices (`LoRA_down` and `LoRA_up`) were injected into the existing layers. The update `∆W` was decomposed, and only these low-rank matrices were optimized. Experiments were conducted with five different LoRA ranks: 1, 2, 4, 8, and 12.

**E. Hyperparameters and Evaluation:**
- **Shared Hyperparameters:** For both base policy training and all fine-tuning versions, consistent hyperparameters were used: 2,000,000 timesteps (with early stopping if a ≥98% success rate was achieved in 50 evaluation runs), a rollout buffer of 8192 steps, a learning rate of 10^-4, a minibatch size of 256, a PPO value function coefficient of 0.4, and a PPO clip range of 0.15. Unspecified parameters utilized SB3 defaults.
- **Evaluation:** Each fine-tuning variation (full fine-tune and each LoRA rank for each task) was run 100 times to account for environmental randomness. Success was defined as achieving at least a 98% evaluation success rate. Deterministic action selection was used for evaluation.

**F. LoRA Architecture Implementation:**
The authors developed a custom `LoRaLinear-class` that inherited from SB3's `ActorCriticPolicy` class. This class wrapped the layers of the pre-trained model. During the forward pass, the output from the frozen base model was element-wise added to the output from the newly introduced low-rank matrices (`LoRA_up(LoRA_down(x))`), effectively allowing the small LoRA matrices to adapt the policy.

**G. Computation Calculation:**
Computational cost was quantified by tracking the number of Floating-Point Operations (FLOPs) per PPO update, which included both a forward and a backward pass. This `FLOP_update` was normalized by the number of environment steps (`n_steps * n_envs`) to derive `FLOP_step`. The total computation in MFLOPs was then calculated by multiplying `FLOP_step` by the total number of PPO environment steps.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The study performed 3600 runs, with 100 runs for each of the 6 tasks across 5 LoRA ranks (1, 2, 4, 8, 12) and full fine-tuning. The main findings are categorized into training performance, memory efficiency, and computational cost.

**A. Training Performance:**
- **Success Rates and Timesteps to Success:** For a majority of the tested tasks, including "button-press-topdown-wall," "hammer," "drawer-open," and "window-open," LoRA fine-tuning achieved success rates comparable to full fine-tuning of all layers. Even with the lowest rank (rank 1), these tasks consistently reached the 98% success threshold, though sometimes requiring slightly more training timesteps.
- **Challenging Tasks:** For more complex tasks such as "basketball" and "peg-unplug-side," lower LoRA ranks (e.g., rank 1) exhibited a higher rate of training failures or struggled to reach the target success rate. These tasks generally required higher LoRA ranks (ranging from 2 to 8) to achieve consistent success, performing at a level similar to full fine-tuning. For instance, rank 1 struggled significantly with "basketball," showing a high failure rate.
- **Anomalous Case - Drawer-Open:** The "drawer-open" task presented an exception, where LoRA policies, particularly at lower ranks, demonstrated higher performance (lower training failure rate) compared to the full fine-tuning baseline. This suggests a potential benefit of constrained, low-rank adaptation for certain tasks that benefit from knowledge transfer.

**B. Memory Efficiency:**
- **Reduction in Trainable Parameters:** LoRA significantly reduced the number of trainable parameters during fine-tuning. Compared to full fine-tuning, which updated 675,609 parameters, LoRA fine-tuning updated:
 - Rank 1: 4,082 parameters (99.4% savings)
 - Rank 2: 8,160 parameters (98.8% savings)
 - Rank 4: 16,316 parameters (97.6% savings)
 - Rank 8: 32,628 parameters (95.2% savings)
 - Rank 12: 48,940 parameters (92.8% savings)
 This represents a reduction factor ranging from approximately 14 to 160 times fewer trainable parameters.
- **Storage Savings for Policy Libraries:** When considering a library of multiple specialist policies sharing a single 2.7 MB base policy, the memory savings became substantial. For example, with rank 8 LoRA adapters (each adding approximately 135 KB):
 - For 10 policies: Total storage was 4.1 MB, representing an 85% saving compared to 27.1 MB for 10 fully fine-tuned policies.
 - For 50 policies: Total storage was 9.5 MB, representing a 93% saving compared to 135.5 MB for 50 fully fine-tuned policies. This implies a storage saving of 2.6 MB (48%) for 2 policies, and 10.1 MB (75%) for 5 policies.

**C. Computational Cost:**
- **FLOPs per PPO Environment Step:** LoRA reduced the computational cost per PPO environment step compared to full fine-tuning (17,503,460 FLOPs).
 - Rank 1: 12,223,888 FLOPs (30.2% savings)
 - Rank 2: 12,329,916 FLOPs (29.6% savings)
 - Rank 4: 12,541,972 FLOPs (28.3% savings)
 - Rank 8: 12,966,084 FLOPs (25.9% savings)
 - Rank 12: 13,390,196 FLOPs (23.5% savings)
- **Forward vs. Backward Pass:** The forward pass showed only a marginal overhead for LoRA (0.6% for rank 1 to 7.3% for rank 12). The primary computational savings originated from the backward pass, which was approximately 43-50% lighter with LoRA due to optimizing fewer parameters.
- **Overall Training Impact:** While FLOPs per step decreased, the authors noted that the most computationally intensive part of their training setup was environment simulation and data collection. Consequently, the observed gains in computational performance from LoRA had a negligible impact on overall training speed in their specific simulator environment.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The findings of this research demonstrate the effective transferability and utility of Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) within the context of online Reinforcement Learning (RL) for robotics. This work holds several significant implications and potential impacts:

1. **Enabling Memory-Efficient Policy Libraries:** The most substantial impact is the validation of LoRA as a method to create highly memory-efficient libraries of specialist policies for multi-task robotics. The observed reduction in trainable parameters by factors ranging from 20 to 160, and storage savings of up to 93% for a library of 50 policies, directly addresses a critical challenge in deploying complex robotic systems. This efficiency can be the decisive factor in whether an entire library of specialized skills can be stored in a robot's limited onboard memory, avoiding the performance penalties associated with using slower swap memory or continuously loading policies.

2. **Facilitating Seamless Task Switching in Robotics:** By allowing multiple specialist policies to reside in memory simultaneously, LoRA can enable robots to switch between different tasks more rapidly and seamlessly. This capability is crucial for applications where a robot needs to adapt quickly to changing operational demands or unexpected environmental conditions.

3. **Validation of LoRA in a New Domain:** The study successfully extends the application of LoRA beyond Large Language Models (LLMs) to a complex decision-making domain like RL in robotics. This broadens the scope of PEFT techniques and suggests their potential utility for a wider array of deep learning applications where fine-tuning large models is necessary.

4. **Maintaining Performance with Reduced Resources:** The research indicates that LoRA fine-tuning can achieve task success rates comparable to full fine-tuning across various manipulation tasks. This demonstrates that significant memory and computational savings do not necessarily come at the expense of performance, offering a practical trade-off for resource-constrained systems. For certain tasks, like "drawer-open," LoRA even exhibited a lower failure rate than full fine-tuning, suggesting potential regularization benefits.

5. **Improved Knowledge Transfer:** The effectiveness of LoRA fine-tuning from a pre-trained base policy implies efficient knowledge transfer from a general baseline task to specific target tasks. This can lead to more stable and potentially faster learning during the fine-tuning phase of new tasks.

6. **Future Research Directions:** The paper also outlines several avenues for future research, including exploring different baseline policies, testing LoRA on a larger and more diverse set of tasks (e.g., beyond manipulation, or with qualitatively different objectives), and evaluating its compatibility with other prominent RL algorithms such as Soft Actor-Critic (SAC), Deep Q-Networks (DQN), or Deep Deterministic Policy Gradient (DDPG). This suggests that the current work lays a foundation for further exploration of efficient fine-tuning in RL.

In summary, this research provides empirical evidence that LoRA is a viable and advantageous technique for developing scalable and memory-efficient multi-task robotic systems, offering practical benefits for deployment on hardware with limited resources.
