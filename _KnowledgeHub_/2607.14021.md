# Industrial Dexterity Benchmark: A Hardware-Software Benchmarking Platform for Industrial Dexterous Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Analog Devices, Inc. developed the Industrial Dexterity Benchmark (IDB), a hardware-software platform, and a multimodal diffusion-based policy (AG-iDP3) for complex industrial manipulation tasks like datacenter cable management. The system leverages RGB, 3D point clouds, and wrench data, achieving a 78% success rate for combined grasp and insert tasks with multi-view RGB sensing, outperforming a single-camera RGB baseline's 36% success rate.

## Problem

- Industrial dexterous manipulation tasks (e.g., cable routing, connector insertion) heavily rely on manual labor due to the brittleness and lack of scalability of traditional robotic methods.
- Existing learned robotic policies often focus on simulated environments or simple tabletop tasks with large tolerances, making their transferability to tight-clearance industrial assembly questionable.
- Current diffusion policies for visuomotor control lack systematic evaluation of fusing multiple image modalities and rarely incorporate force/torque sensing, which is crucial for contact-rich industrial tasks.

## Method

- Developed **Industrial Dexterity Benchmark (IDB)** boards, custom low-cost platforms mimicking real-world industrial manipulation challenges (e.g., datacenter cable management, automotive harnesses).
- Created **DAG-ROS**, a scalable ==ROS2==-based hardware-software infrastructure for data collection via ==teleoperation==, real-time robot control, and training/deployment of learned policies.
- Proposed **AG-iDP3**, a multimodal ==diffusion-based policy== framework that fuses diverse sensor inputs, including RGB images, 3D ==point clouds==, robot joint positions, and wrist-frame ==wrench== data, integrated within a hybrid ==behavior tree== architecture.

## Results

- The multi-view RGB configuration (wrist + scene cameras) achieved the highest performance with a **78%** combined grasp and insert task success rate on the datacenter cable-cleaning task.
- All multimodal policy configurations outperformed the single-camera RGB Diffusion Policy baseline, which achieved a **36%** total success rate.
- The policies demonstrated data efficiency, requiring approximately **100 teleoperated demonstrations** per task phase, making them practical for industrial deployment.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Fusing multiple sensor modalities (RGB, 3D point clouds, wrench) significantly improves policy robustness and performance for industrial dexterous manipulation compared to single-modality baselines.
> - End-to-end learned policies can achieve practical performance in complex industrial tasks with relatively low data requirements (approx. 100 teleoperated demonstrations per task phase), offering a more efficient alternative to classical pipelines.
> - A hybrid control architecture, combining learned policies for manipulation and classical evaluators for task-specific termination criteria, provides a robust and scalable solution for orchestrating multi-phase industrial tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in this paper addresses a persistent challenge in industrial automation: dexterous manipulation. Tasks such as cable routing, connector insertion, and precision assembly continue to rely significantly on manual labor despite advancements in robotics. This gap is particularly evident in high-stakes environments like datacenters, where uptime requirements exceed 99.99%, and dense layouts necessitate precise, contact-aware manipulation.

Historically, robotic solutions for these tasks have often employed classical, modular pipelines involving separate components for perception (e.g., computer vision), planning, and control. The authors' initial attempts to solve the National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST) Assembly Task Board #4 (ATB4) with such classical methods revealed their brittleness, sensitivity to environmental variations, and difficulty in scaling to minor task changes. This experience aligns with a broader trend in robotics research recognizing the limitations of purely classical approaches for complex, real-world tasks and motivating a shift toward end-to-end learned policies.

Within the landscape of learned policies, the paper positions itself specifically within the area of imitation learning, particularly leveraging diffusion models for visuomotor control. It builds upon existing diffusion policy frameworks like Diffusion Policy (DP), 3D Diffusion Policy (DP3), and improved Diffusion Policy (iDP3), as well as applications in dexterous manipulation like DexDiffuser. The authors identify specific gaps in the current research on diffusion policies:
- A lack of systematic evaluation of fusing multiple image modalities (e.g., RGB and point clouds) within a single diffusion policy.
- A predominant focus on simulated environments or tabletop pick-and-place tasks with large tolerances, questioning their transferability to tight-clearance industrial assembly.
- An absence of force/torque sensing as a direct input to diffusion policies, despite its criticality for contact-rich insertion tasks where visual occlusion is common.

By introducing novel benchmarking boards tailored to industrial challenges, developing a scalable imitation learning infrastructure, and proposing a multimodal diffusion-based policy framework that integrates diverse sensor inputs, this work aims to bridge the gap between advanced learned manipulation techniques and the stringent requirements for robustness, scalability, and performance in industrial automation. It seeks to demonstrate that end-to-end learned policies, when appropriately designed and implemented, can overcome the limitations of traditional robotic methods in these demanding scenarios.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to advance industrial automation by developing more robust, scalable, and efficient solutions for dexterous manipulation tasks that currently necessitate human intervention. This overarching goal is broken down into several specific objectives, driven by identified limitations in existing robotic capabilities:

- **Address Manual Labor Dependence:** The core motivation stems from the continued reliance on human labor for complex manipulation tasks like cable routing, connector insertion, and precision assembly in industrial settings. These tasks, particularly in environments like datacenters, require high precision, coordination, and contact-awareness, making them challenging for traditional automation.
- **Overcome Brittleness and Scalability Issues of Classical Robotics:** The authors' prior experience with classical computer vision and control techniques on the NIST ATB4 board revealed that while functional under controlled conditions, such solutions were brittle, sensitive to environmental factors (e.g., lighting), and labor-intensive to recalibrate or reprogram for minor task variations. This motivated a shift towards a more flexible and scalable paradigm.
- **Develop Industrial Dexterity Benchmarks (IDB):** To enable systematic evaluation and development of learned policies tailored to industrial requirements, a key objective was to create a set of custom benchmarking boards. These boards are designed to mimic real-world challenges more accurately than existing benchmarks, covering tasks such as datacenter cable management, automotive cable harnesses, and planetary gearbox assembly.
- **Establish a Scalable Imitation Learning Infrastructure:** A practical objective was to build a comprehensive hardware-software platform (DAG-ROS) capable of supporting rapid data collection, teleoperation, sensor integration, real-time robot control, and model training. This infrastructure is designed to be modular and scalable, facilitating the development and deployment of end-to-end learned policies.
- **Investigate Multimodal Diffusion Policies for Robust Manipulation:** A central technical objective was to develop and evaluate a multimodal diffusion-based policy framework (AG-iDP3). This framework aims to fuse diverse sensor inputs—specifically RGB images, 3D point clouds, joint positions, and wrist-frame wrench data—to enhance policy robustness and performance, particularly in contact-rich industrial scenarios where visual occlusion is common and proprioceptive feedback is critical.
- **Demonstrate Data Efficiency:** The authors sought to develop policies that could be trained with a relatively small number of teleoperated demonstrations (approximately 100 per task phase), making the approach practical for real-world industrial deployment where extensive data collection can be costly and time-consuming.
- **Validate End-to-End Learned Policies Against Baselines:** A critical objective was to experimentally compare the performance of their proposed multimodal learned policies against single-modality baselines (e.g., single-camera RGB) and, implicitly, against the limitations observed in classical pipelines, to quantify the benefits of the end-to-end learning approach.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research adopts a multi-faceted approach, combining hardware development, software infrastructure, and novel policy architecture to address industrial dexterous manipulation.

**A. Industrial Dexterity Benchmark (IDB) Boards**
The authors developed three custom benchmarking platforms, inspired by the NIST ATB boards, to simulate industrial manipulation challenges:
- **IDB Board #1 (Datacenter Cable Manipulation):** The primary focus of this paper, designed to replicate dense connector spacing, constrained cable routing, and limited workspace access in datacenters. It features RJ45, SC simplex, and LC duplex fiber connections, along with a circular pad for connector cleaning. Two tasks are defined: cable port swap and fiber cable maintenance (unplug, clean, re-insert).
- **IDB Board #2 (Automotive Cable Harness):** Designed to test manipulation of flexible cables in constrained, occluded spaces and precise connector insertion. It features multiple 6-pin and 2-pin connectors, routing clips, and LEDs for connection verification.
- **IDB Board #3 (Gearbox Assembly):** Focuses on fine manipulation skills for assembling and disassembling a planetary gearbox, involving picking parts, aligning gear meshes, and tightening bolts.
All boards are designed for low-cost fabrication using 3D printing, laser cutting, and off-the-shelf components.

**B. DAG-ROS Imitation Learning Infrastructure**
DAG-ROS is a ROS2-based, Dockerized framework supporting the entire imitation learning pipeline:
- **Data Collection Subsystem:** Employs a GELLO-style leader arm for teleoperation. A data aggregator produces time-aligned streams of RGB images, point clouds, joint positions, and wrist force/torque (F/T) data at 15 Hz. A GUI facilitates recording, and a validity checker ensures data quality before conversion to Zarr format for training.
- **Real-time Control Subsystem:** An Ubuntu 24.04 RT-kernel host runs ROS2 Control with URDF integration, driving a Franka FR3 robot arm equipped with a Robotiq 2F-85 gripper and a wrist-mounted Intel RealSense D405 camera. The CRISP impedance controller operates at 50 Hz, sending commands to the FR3 via the Franka Control Interface (FCI) at 1 kHz.
- **Inference and Deployment Subsystem:** A dedicated GPU node (NVIDIA RTX 5090) performs inference using the trained policies, subscribing to robot and camera topics and publishing action chunks at 15 Hz. All demonstrations and deployment runs are logged in MCAP format for offline analysis.

**C. AG-iDP3 Multimodal Diffusion Policy**
AG-iDP3 is a multimodal diffusion-based policy framework, extending existing 3D Diffusion Policy (DP3, iDP3) and diffusion-policy formulations:
- **Input Modalities:** The policy fuses data from multiple sensors:
 - **RGB Images:** From a wrist-mounted RealSense D405 and an optional scene RealSense D435i. Each is processed by a fine-tuned R3M backbone (ResNet18).
 - **Point Clouds:** From an optional scene RealSense D435i (stereo) or an Analog Devices EVAL-ADTF3175 direct Time-of-Flight (ToF) camera. These are downsampled and encoded by a lightweight multi-stage PointNet encoder.
 - **Proprioceptive States:** Robot joint positions and wrist-frame wrench data are passed unencoded.
- **Policy Architecture:** All encoded and unencoded inputs are concatenated into a single observation vector. This vector is fed into a diffusion U-Net, which predicts an action chunk (T=15 joint actions for this work).
- **Modality Gating:** The framework incorporates a modality-gating mechanism, allowing specific sensor inputs (e.g., wrist wrench) to be selectively masked during training or deployment for different task phases.

**D. Deployment Motion Control**
The policy's discrete action chunks are converted into smooth, compliant robot motion commands:
- **Action Chunking:** The first N=3 steps of each 15-step action chunk are queued for immediate execution. A background re-inference process is triggered adaptively to ensure a new chunk arrives before the queue empties.
- **Trajectory Smoothing:** A cubic spline interpolation is applied to the N committed waypoints, anchored at the last executed action. This spline is then sampled at 50 Hz to generate a dense stream of joint-space targets for the CRISP impedance controller, ensuring smooth motion and respecting robot velocity/acceleration limits while mitigating discontinuities at chunk boundaries.
- **Temporal Ensembling:** Overlapping predictions from successive inference chunks are blended using an exponentially weighted average (k=0.01), where more recent inferences receive higher weighting, to further smooth the trajectory.

**E. Behavior Tree Architecture**
A PyTrees-based behavior tree orchestrates the cable-cleaning task on IDB Board #1:
- The overall policy flow is defined by a SEQUENCE node, executing phases like "move to home," "grasp connector," "clean connector," and "insert connector."
- Key manipulation phases (Grasp, Clean, Insert) are implemented as PARALLEL nodes, running a learned policy concurrently with a classical evaluator node. The evaluator provides task-specific termination criteria (e.g., gripper closure for grasp, contact wrench for clean, gripper opening for insert) to stop the policy and transition to the next phase. This hybrid architecture combines the adaptability of learned policies with the reliability of classical control and evaluation.

**F. Experimental Evaluation**
The cable-cleaning task on IDB Board #1 was used for evaluation. This task consists of three phases: Grasp, Clean, and Insert. Six distinct sensor configurations (summarized in Table I of the paper) were evaluated, including a single-camera RGB baseline. Each configuration was tested with 48 trials (12 trials across four receptacle positions). Policies were trained using approximately 100 teleoperated demonstrations per task phase.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of six sensor configurations on the IDB Board #1 datacenter cable-cleaning task yielded several findings regarding policy performance:

- **Multimodal Policy Outperformance:** All four multimodal policy configurations (configurations 1-4 in Table I) demonstrated improved performance compared to the single-camera RGB Diffusion Policy (DP) baseline (configuration 6), which achieved a 36% total success rate. This indicates the benefit of incorporating additional sensor modalities.
- **Optimal Configuration:** The best performance was achieved by the multi-view RGB configuration (configuration 1: RGB Wrist D405 + RGB Scene D435i), which utilized an R3M encoder for each camera. This configuration attained a 78% combined grasp and insert task success rate.
- **Impact of 3D Context on Grasping:** The presence of 3D context, either through point cloud data or multiple RGB cameras providing different perspectives, correlated with higher grasp success rates. Five configurations that included 3D context achieved grasp success rates ranging from 88% to 98%. In contrast, the single-RGB configuration (Config 6), lacking 3D context, exhibited a substantially lower grasp success rate of 48%.
- **Insertion as Performance Differentiator:** For configurations 1-5 (excluding the single-RGB baseline), the insertion phase served as the primary factor differentiating overall task success. Insertion success rates varied by 48 percentage points across these configurations, while grasp success rates varied by only 10 percentage points. The cleaning phase consistently achieved a 100% success rate across all configurations and was therefore excluded from the total score calculation.
- **ToF Sensor Performance:** In a direct comparison between the two scene cameras, the EVAL-ADTF3175 Time-of-Flight (ToF) sensor (Config 2) surpassed the RealSense D435i (Config 4) by 7 percentage points in total success rate. This suggests the EVAL-ADTF3175 ToF may be more suitable for the specific conditions of industrial datacenters.
- **Egocentric Point Cloud vs. RGB:** The egocentric point-cloud-only iDP3 (configuration 5) achieved a 52% total success rate, outperforming the egocentric RGB-only DP baseline (configuration 6, 36% total success). This result aligns with previous findings on the advantages of egocentric point-cloud policies over egocentric RGB policies.
- **Data Efficiency:** The training of well-performing policies for all tested configurations required approximately 100 teleoperated demonstrations per task phase. This demonstration count is considered practical for on-site data collection and retraining in industrial environments.
- **Wrench Gating Effectiveness:** The per-phase wrench gating mechanism demonstrated that wrist wrench input was beneficial specifically during the contact-rich insertion phase, while providing no measurable benefit during the grasp and clean phases.
- **System Integration:** The hybrid behavior tree successfully orchestrated the end-to-end cable-cleaning task across all evaluated configurations, demonstrating that the parallel execution of learned policies and classical evaluators can scale beyond single-phase operations.
- **Comparison to Classical Pipelines:** The learned policy approach exhibited efficiency advantages over the classical pipeline used for the NIST board challenge, requiring approximately 100 demonstrations per task phase versus thousands of labeled images, extensive tuning, and reprogramming for each new task.
- **Robustness Observations:** The learned policy demonstrated brittleness to minor visual scene changes (e.g., removal of a background object, variation in cable routing along the robot arm). Workarounds, such as image cropping to focus on the task area and pulley-based cable management, were implemented to mitigate these issues, but the observation suggests the visual encoder may latch onto incidental scene features.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a multi-faceted contribution to the field of industrial robotics, with implications for both academic research and practical industrial applications.

**Advancing Industrial Automation:** The most direct impact of this work is its potential to accelerate the automation of dexterous manipulation tasks that currently constitute a significant bottleneck in industrial processes. By demonstrating a viable end-to-end learned approach for tasks like cable management and precision assembly, the research offers a pathway to reducing reliance on manual labor in critical, high-uptime environments such as datacenters.

**Establishing Reproducible Benchmarking Standards:** The introduction of the Industrial Dexterity Benchmark (IDB) boards (Datacenter Cable Manipulation, Automotive Cable Harness, Gearbox Assembly) is a significant contribution. These low-cost, reproducible platforms provide standardized environments for evaluating robotic systems on industrially relevant tasks. This can foster comparative research, enable consistent performance measurement, and accelerate the development and deployment of advanced manipulation solutions across the robotics community. The open-sourcing of these designs further amplifies their potential impact.

**Validating End-to-End Learned Policies:** The empirical results, demonstrating that multimodal diffusion policies trained with approximately 100 demonstrations per phase can significantly outperform single-modality baselines and classical approaches, validate the shift towards end-to-end learning for industrial dexterity. This provides evidence that learned policies can offer superior robustness, generalization, and deployment efficiency compared to traditional modular pipelines.

**Highlighting Multimodal Sensing Benefits:** The study quantitatively illustrates the advantages of fusing multiple sensor modalities (RGB images, 3D point clouds, joint positions, wrist wrench) for complex manipulation tasks, especially in contact-rich and constrained environments. The superior performance of multimodal configurations, particularly the multi-view RGB, and the beneficial role of force/torque sensing during insertion, guides future sensor integration strategies for industrial robots.

**Providing a Practical and Scalable Framework:** The DAG-ROS infrastructure, combined with the AG-iDP3 multimodal diffusion policy and the PyTrees behavior tree architecture, offers a comprehensive, modular, and data-efficient framework. Its design for rapid data collection, real-time control, and hybrid policy execution (combining learned and classical methods) provides a practical recipe for developing and deploying dexterous manipulation solutions in industrial settings, reducing the overhead associated with robot bring-up and task adaptation.

**Guiding Future Research Directions:** The paper identifies several clear avenues for future research, including algorithmic improvements (e.g., richer encoders, dynamic prediction horizons, Cartesian-space models), robustness enhancements (e.g., domain randomization, simulation-based training), advanced sensing modalities (e.g., ToF, event cameras, tactile sensing), and compute optimization (e.g., edge deployment on NPUs). These identified directions can help steer further innovation in industrial dexterous manipulation.

Overall, this work presents a self-contained system that integrates hardware, software, and policy architecture, demonstrating a practical and effective approach to addressing a long-standing challenge in industrial automation. Its contributions are significant for advancing the capabilities of robots in demanding, high-stakes industrial environments.
