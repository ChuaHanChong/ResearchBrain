# HiF-VLA: Hindsight, Insight and Foresight through Motion Representation for Vision-Language-Action Models

> [!summary] Summary
> HiF-VLA enhances Vision-Language-Action models by integrating hindsight, insight, and foresight through compact motion representations. This approach improves temporal coherence and causal consistency for long-horizon manipulation tasks, achieving higher success rates and greater efficiency compared to existing methods.

## Problem

- Existing VLA models often assume the Markov property, leading to "temporal myopia" and fragmented execution in long-horizon manipulation tasks.
- Prior methods for historical context (e.g., stacking raw frames) are computationally intensive, increase inference latency, and introduce pixel-level redundancy.
- Pixel-level future predictions for foresight are prone to local distortions, semantic drift, and lack effective continuous temporal modeling.

## Method

- **HiF-VLA** leverages compact motion vectors (MVs) derived from video codec standards to represent historical and future dynamics, reducing redundancy and computational overhead.
- A bidirectional temporal reasoning framework unifies hindsight (past dynamics), insight (current observation), and foresight (anticipated future MVs and actions).
- A Hindsight-Modulated Joint Expert adaptively integrates historical priors via AdaLN to guide foresight motion and action generation, ensuring temporal consistency without disrupting the VLM backbone.

## Results

- HiF-VLA achieved up to a **3.4%** absolute improvement in success rate on the LIBERO-Long benchmark (**94.4%** third-view, **96.4%** multi-view) and surpassed baselines in average task length on CALVIN ABC-D.
- The framework demonstrated superior efficiency, incurring negligible additional computational cost (**1.03x** peak GPU memory, **1.13x** latency) compared to pixel-level foresight, and maintained low overhead with increasing temporal context.
- In real-world experiments on an AgileX Piper robotic arm, HiF-VLA substantially improved success rates across long-horizon tasks, for example, achieving **57.9%** success on "Cover block and stack bowls" compared to the OpenVLA-OFT baseline's **33.3%**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Motion vectors provide a more efficient and precise representation of temporal context for VLA models, capturing dynamic changes while filtering static background noise.
> - Integrating both hindsight and foresight through motion-centric reasoning is crucial for robust, causally consistent decision-making in long-horizon manipulation, enabling a "think-while-acting" paradigm.
> - Conditioning hindsight within a dedicated expert module, rather than directly injecting it into the VLM, effectively preserves the VLM's pre-trained visual-language alignments while enhancing it with dynamic context.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models have emerged as a framework for robotic manipulation, integrating visual and linguistic cues to generate actions. A common characteristic of many existing VLA models, such as RT-2 and OpenVLA, is their implicit assumption of the Markov property, meaning they largely base action predictions solely on the current observation. This simplification can lead to temporal myopia, particularly in long-horizon manipulation tasks that require maintaining coherence and continuity over extended periods. Without explicit modeling of temporal dependencies, the consistency between successive actions may degrade, resulting in fragmented task execution.

Prior efforts to address this temporal myopia have typically involved incorporating historical context by stacking multiple past observation frames, as seen in methods like Octo and RoboVLMs. However, this approach presents several limitations: it is computationally intensive, increases inference latency, and introduces significant pixel-level redundancy. The high similarity between adjacent raw frames can obscure relevant dynamics, making it challenging for models to focus on meaningful changes.

Another line of work focuses on foresight, predicting future visual subgoals to guide action generation, exemplified by CoT-VLA and Seer. These methods often rely on pixel-level future predictions, which can be prone to local distortions, semantic drift, and may not capture continuous temporal evolution effectively.

This paper positions HiF-VLA as an advancement within this landscape by proposing a motion-centric approach to temporal modeling. Instead of raw pixel data, HiF-VLA leverages motion vectors as a compact and informative representation of temporal context and world dynamics. This approach aims to capture inter-state changes while mitigating static pixel-level noise and computational overhead. Furthermore, HiF-VLA introduces a bidirectional temporal reasoning framework that unifies hindsight (past dynamics), insight (current observation), and foresight (anticipated future dynamics), departing from models that primarily focus on one-sided temporal reasoning or dense pixel-level predictions. By doing so, HiF-VLA seeks to provide a more efficient, structured, and causally consistent mechanism for temporal understanding in VLA models.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for robust, temporally coherent, and causally consistent long-horizon manipulation tasks. The authors identify a significant limitation in most existing VLA models: their reliance on the Markov property, which confines decision-making to the current observation and leads to "temporal myopia." This myopia is particularly problematic for complex, multi-step tasks where actions are sequentially dependent and require an understanding of how past states led to the present, and how current actions will influence future states.

The motivation for HiF-VLA stems from several key observations:

1. **Inefficiency and Redundancy of Existing Temporal Modeling:** Current methods that incorporate historical context often rely on stacking raw visual frames. This approach is computationally expensive, increases inference latency, and introduces substantial redundancy due to high pixel-level similarity between consecutive frames. This redundancy can obscure salient, task-relevant dynamics, making it difficult for the model to discern meaningful changes from static background noise.
2. **Compactness and Informativeness of Motion:** The authors posit that motion provides a more precise and efficient representation of history and environmental dynamics than raw visual frames. Motion vectors directly capture the dynamics of interactions (e.g., object movement, drawer closure) while filtering out static information. This makes motion an ideal primitive for representing temporal memory in a way that is both expressive and computationally efficient.
3. **Necessity of Bidirectional Temporal Reasoning:** Robust decision-making, especially in long-horizon tasks, requires an agent to possess both hindsight and foresight. Hindsight allows the agent to interpret past dynamics and understand how its current state was reached, grounding decisions in verified outcomes. Foresight enables the anticipation of plausible future dynamics, facilitating proactive, goal-directed behavior rather than purely reactive responses. Integrating both aspects within a unified framework is motivated by the need for more intelligent, "think-while-acting" embodied agents.
4. **Limitations of Pixel-Level Foresight:** Existing approaches for foresight reasoning, which generate future visual subgoals, are often susceptible to local distortions, semantic drift, and lack continuous temporal modeling. This can undermine temporal consistency in complex environments. The authors propose using structured motion vectors for future anticipation to overcome these issues.
5. **Maintaining VLM Integrity:** Directly integrating historical visual information into the VLM backbone can interfere with the established alignments between visual and language modalities, potentially degrading performance. The design of HiF-VLA aims to inject historical context in a manner that guides the decision-making process without disrupting these core VLM functionalities.

Based on these motivations, the key objectives for HiF-VLA are:
- To propose a novel VLA framework that explicitly models temporal dependencies through compact motion representations.
- To develop distinct modules for acquiring hindsight priors (encoding past dynamics), performing foresight reasoning (anticipating future motions and actions), and integrating these through a hindsight-modulated joint expert.
- To demonstrate that leveraging motion for bidirectional temporal reasoning can significantly improve performance on long-horizon manipulation benchmarks.
- To show that this approach can reduce computational overhead and improve inference efficiency compared to frame-stacking or pixel-level prediction methods.
- To validate the effectiveness and robustness of the HiF-VLA framework in real-world robotic manipulation tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

HiF-VLA is a unified framework designed to imbue Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models with bidirectional temporal reasoning capabilities by leveraging motion as a compact representation of temporal context. The approach expands the temporal receptive field beyond instantaneous observations, incorporating both past dynamics (hindsight) and anticipated future dynamics (foresight) to inform action generation.

The core methodology revolves around three main components:

#### 3.1. Hindsight Prior Acquisition

- **Problem Addressed:** Relying solely on current observations can lead to unstable perception. Existing methods of stacking raw historical frames introduce redundancy, high computational cost, and may distract the model with static pixel information.
- **Approach:** HiF-VLA introduces Motion Vectors (MVs) as a compressed, structured representation of historical dynamics. MVs, derived from video codec standards like MPEG-4, capture the displacement of macroblocks between adjacent frames. This allows for near-lossless video reconstruction and high compression, effectively preserving task-relevant dynamics while discarding redundant static pixels.
- **Implementation Details:** The current observation $o_t$ acts as a keyframe, and a historical window of length $m$ (e.g., 4 or 8 frames) provides a Group of Pictures (GOP) unit composed of MVs (e.g., $MV_{t-m:t-m+1}$ to $MV_{t-1:t}$) and $o_t$. These MVs are represented as a tensor of size $h \times (H//16) \times (W//16) \times 2$, encoding historical motion trajectories. A lightweight, 4-layer ViT-based encoder, combined with shallow 3D convolutions, processes these MVs into compact hindsight tokens $M_h \in R^{K_h \times d}$. This process reduces temporal redundancy and preserves local motion continuity.

#### 3.2. Foresight Reasoning with Insight

- **Problem Addressed:** Pixel-level future subgoal predictions are prone to local distortions and semantic drift, obscuring task-relevant dynamics and lacking continuous temporal modeling.
- **Approach:** Instead of raw future pixels, HiF-VLA predicts more general and structured MVs as spatiotemporal targets for future action execution. This captures the trajectory evolution of the manipulator and scene entities, reducing pixel redundancy.
- **Implementation Details:** The model integrates $K_f$ learnable foresight query tokens ($q_f$) and $K_a$ empty action tokens ($q_a$) into the VLM embedding space. These tokens are concatenated with the task instruction $l$ and the current observation $o_t$ embeddings. This combined input is fed into a VLM backbone (Prismatic-7B initialized with OpenVLA weights), which performs parallel reasoning over continuous visual dynamics and action generation. This yields foresight motion tokens $M_f \in R^{K_f \times d}$ and action latent tokens $A_f \in R^{K_a \times d}$. The parallel reasoning aims to enrich the VLM's internal thought process.

#### 3.3. Hindsight-Modulated Joint Expert

- **Problem Addressed:** Predicting actions in isolation lacks foresight into future dynamics. Directly embedding historical motion into the VLM input might disrupt the VLM's pre-trained visual-language alignment.
- **Approach:** This module jointly models actions and motions as complementary streams within a shared temporal latent space. Historical information (hindsight) is introduced as an adaptive conditional prior to guide future inference and ensure temporal consistency.
- **Implementation Details:** Hindsight motion tokens $M_h$ are projected through a linear layer to obtain conditional vectors $h_c$. These vectors modulate both foresight motion tokens $M_f$ and action tokens $A_f$ within each joint expert module via Adaptive Layer Normalization (AdaLN). AdaLN applies scaling ($\gamma(h_c)$) and shifting ($\beta(h_c)$) parameters based on $h_c$ to the normalized representations. The joint expert consists of 6 Transformer layers. Within these layers, $M_f$ and $A_f$ form two parallel streams that interact via cross-stream non-causal joint attention, allowing bidirectional influence while maintaining separate Feed-Forward Networks (FFNs) for disentangled representations. Rotary Positional Embedding (RoPE) is used for positional information.
- **Output:** The fused motion and action representations are projected through their respective heads to generate the future motions $\tilde{m}_{t:t+n}$ and the actions $\tilde{a}_{t:t+n}$. This design aims to constrain future motion-action patterns, ensuring temporally consistent and physically plausible behaviors.

#### 3.4. Unified HiF-VLA Model and Training

- **Input Representation:** The current observation is processed by DINOv2 and SigLIP visual encoders for a hybrid visual embedding. Learnable foresight motion queries and empty action tokens are concatenated with the instruction embedding and current observation embedding. Historical motion sequences are compressed via spatio-temporal convolution and a ViT encoder to generate hindsight priors.
- **Parallel Decoding and Joint Expert:** The VLM backbone uses a non-causal attention mask for joint prediction of future motion and action latents. These three token streams (hindsight, foresight, action) are fused by the Hindsight-Modulated Joint Expert.
- **Training Objective:** The model is trained to jointly predict both actions and future motion. Two L1-loss objectives are used: $L_{MV}$ for motion vector reconstruction and $L_A$ for action prediction. The overall loss is $L_{all} = L_A + \lambda \cdot L_{MV}$, where $\lambda$ is a balancing factor (set to 0.01).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted for HiF-VLA focused on evaluating its performance on long-horizon benchmarks, its efficiency and scalability, the contribution of its individual components through ablation studies, and its applicability in real-world robotic tasks.

#### 4.1. Overall Performance (RQ1)

- **LIBERO-Long Benchmark:** HiF-VLA demonstrated improved performance on this benchmark, which involves ten multi-subgoal manipulation tasks.
 - In the third-view setup (using a single primary camera), HiF-VLA achieved a 94.4% average success rate, representing a 3.4% absolute improvement over the OpenVLA-OFT baseline (91.0%). This performance was comparable to or surpassed multi-view baselines.
 - In the multi-view setup (using primary and wrist cameras), HiF-VLA reached a 96.4% average success rate, outperforming other state-of-the-art VLA models.
- **CALVIN ABC-D Benchmark:** This benchmark evaluates generalization on unseen environments (D, after training on A-C). HiF-VLA surpassed the baseline by 0.25 in terms of average task length, exhibiting superior performance in both third-view and multi-view settings. For instance, in multi-view, HiF-VLA achieved an average task length of 4.35, compared to OpenVLA-OFT's 4.10 and Seer's 4.28.
These results indicate that HiF-VLA’s bidirectional temporal perception and reasoning architecture contribute to a more effective understanding of long-term action dependencies, leading to enhanced adaptability and robustness in complex long-horizon tasks.

#### 4.2. Efficiency and Redundancy Analysis (RQ2)

Experiments on LIBERO-Long evaluated efficiency, peak GPU memory usage, and inference latency.
- **Foresight vs. Subgoal:** Incorporating motion-based foresight (HiF-VLA) incurred negligible additional cost (1.03x peak GPU memory, 1.13x latency) compared to the baseline. In contrast, pixel-level subgoal prediction methods (e.g., + Subgoal variant) resulted in higher overhead (1.24x peak GPU memory, 1.59x latency).
- **Hindsight vs. History Frames:** Using compact motion representations for hindsight (HiF-VLA) introduced minimal overhead (1.02x peak GPU memory, 1.61x latency for a history length of 4). Directly stacking raw history frames (+ History frames variant) significantly increased computational burden (2.06x peak GPU memory, 3.15x latency) and sometimes degraded performance, suggesting that redundant pixel information can dilute relevant temporal cues.
These findings demonstrate HiF-VLA’s advantages in reducing redundancy and improving inference efficiency through its motion-centric temporal modeling.

#### 4.3. Inference Scalability (RQ3)

The inference efficiency was evaluated with increasing hindsight lengths.
- Multi-frame baselines exhibited an almost linear increase in latency as the length of history grew (e.g., over 4.5x higher latency for 8-frame history compared to the vanilla VLA).
- HiF-VLA maintained a consistently low computational overhead, with latency increasing only marginally with longer history lengths. This indicates superior scalability with respect to temporal context.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies (RQ4)

Ablation studies investigated the contribution of different components and hyper-parameters.
- **Hindsight Length:** Optimal performance was observed at a hindsight length of 8 frames for both third-view (94.4%) and multi-view (96.4%) settings, suggesting that moderate temporal dependencies are sufficient for most tasks in LIBERO-Long.
- **Hindsight Embedding Position:** Conditioning hindsight within the expert module via AdaLN achieved a higher success rate (94.4%) compared to direct injection into the VLM (92.8%). This suggests that motion-based hindsight may interfere with the VLM's pre-trained visual and language alignments if directly introduced, and that expert-conditioned modulation provides a more effective path for low-level dynamic information.
- **Motion vs. State:** Replacing motion vectors (MVs) with robot proprioceptive states (S) for both hindsight and foresight (S+S) resulted in a clear performance drop (92.0% SR vs. 94.4% SR for M+M). This indicates that MVs capture interaction-driven visual dynamics that are not fully encoded in robot states alone.
- **Bidirectional Interaction vs. Causal Separation:** The joint expert's bidirectional attention between foresight motion and action tokens (94.4% SR) outperformed a decoupled, causally separated variant (87.4% SR). This highlights the importance of synergistic interaction between these two streams for effective decision-making. The order of tokens in bidirectional attention had a negligible effect (<0.5%).
- **Different Motion Representations:** Both MVs and optical flow yielded similar success rates. However, MVs were significantly more computationally efficient (121.6 ms vs. 186.8 ms for 4-frame history), indicating that MVs provide a more practical motion representation.
- **"Think-while-Acting" Paradigm:** Training the motion prediction pathway alongside the action prediction branch led to faster convergence and more stable training for motion prediction, supporting the synergistic interaction between the two streams and the model's "think-while-acting" capability.

#### 4.5. Real-world Experiments (RQ5)

HiF-VLA was deployed on an AgileX Piper robotic arm for three long-horizon real-world tasks (Place blocks on plates, Cover block and stack bowls, Press buttons in order).
- Compared to the OpenVLA-OFT baseline, HiF-VLA achieved substantial improvements:
 - **Place blocks on plates:** 65% (HiF-VLA) vs. 62.5% (OpenVLA-OFT).
 - **Cover block and stack bowls:** 57.9% (HiF-VLA) vs. 33.3% (OpenVLA-OFT).
 - **Press buttons in order:** 34.2% (HiF-VLA) vs. 17.4% (OpenVLA-OFT).
The baseline often struggled with tasks requiring subtle state transitions (e.g., distinguishing pressed vs. unpressed buttons), while HiF-VLA's broad temporal receptive field enabled more reliable detection and execution. These results demonstrate the robustness and effectiveness of HiF-VLA in practical robotic settings.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of robotic manipulation by addressing a fundamental limitation of many Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models: temporal myopia. The introduction of HiF-VLA, a framework that integrates hindsight, insight, and foresight through motion representation, marks a notable step towards more capable and generalist robot policies.

The significance of this work can be understood through several points:
- **Novel Temporal Representation:** By utilizing low-dimensional, structured motion vectors (MVs) instead of dense raw pixel frames, HiF-VLA offers a more compact and efficient method for encoding temporal information. This approach effectively captures dynamic changes while filtering out static redundancy, leading to improved computational efficiency and reduced inference latency, which are critical for real-time robotic control.
- **Unified Bidirectional Temporal Reasoning:** HiF-VLA's framework systematically integrates historical context (hindsight), current observations (insight), and future predictions (foresight). This bidirectional temporal expansion of the receptive field allows robots to ground decisions in past outcomes and anticipate future consequences, fostering a more proactive and intelligent "think-while-acting" paradigm. This unified approach can lead to more robust and adaptive behaviors in complex, multi-step tasks.
- **Enhanced Performance and Generalization:** The demonstrated performance gains on challenging long-horizon benchmarks like LIBERO-Long and CALVIN ABC-D, along with superior results in real-world manipulation tasks, suggest that HiF-VLA improves temporal consistency, causal coherence, and overall task success rates. Its generalization capabilities on unseen environments in CALVIN ABC-D indicate a potential for broader applicability.
- **Scalability and Efficiency:** The architecture's ability to maintain low and marginally increasing latency with longer temporal contexts, in contrast to the linear scaling observed in frame-stacking methods, highlights its superior inference scalability. This efficiency could enable the deployment of more sophisticated temporal reasoning in resource-constrained robotic systems.
- **Improved Model Design:** The ablation studies provide insights into effective ways of integrating temporal information. Specifically, the finding that conditioning hindsight within the expert module (via AdaLN) is more effective than direct VLM injection suggests strategies for preserving the integrity of pre-trained VLMs while enhancing them with dynamic context. The importance of bidirectional interaction between motion and action streams further informs architectural choices for future embodied AI systems.

The potential impact of HiF-VLA extends to various areas:
- **Advancement of Generalist Robot Policies:** By improving a robot's ability to reason over long temporal horizons and handle complex sequential tasks, HiF-VLA contributes to the development of more generalist robot policies capable of executing diverse and intricate manipulation scenarios.
- **Practical Robotics Applications:** The demonstrated success in real-world experiments, particularly in tasks requiring subtle state detection and consistent sequential execution, suggests that HiF-VLA could be applied to a wider range of industrial and service robotics challenges where robustness and long-term planning are essential.
- **Foundational Research in Embodied AI:** The work highlights the critical role of compact, structured temporal representations in embodied agents, potentially influencing future research directions in world modeling, task planning, and human-robot interaction.

The identified limitation regarding the dependence of motion representation on estimation accuracy and sensitivity to noise in highly dynamic scenes suggests avenues for future work, such as exploring large-scale pre-training on internet videos to enhance motion understanding and generation capabilities.
