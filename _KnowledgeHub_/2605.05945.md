# MobileEgo Anywhere: Open Infrastructure for long horizon egocentric data on commodity hardware

> [!summary] Summary
> MobileEgo Anywhere introduces an open infrastructure for collecting long-duration, egocentric multimodal data using commodity LiDAR-enabled smartphones, alongside a comprehensive processing pipeline. The framework produces a 200-hour dataset featuring continuous 6 DoF camera poses, depth maps, 3D hand poses, and automatically generated hierarchical action labels, offering a scalable resource for Vision Language Action (VLA) model development.

## Problem

- Existing egocentric datasets for VLA models often contain short episode durations, hindering the learning of long-horizon temporal dependencies for complex robotic tasks.
- High hardware barriers, involving specialized and expensive equipment, limit the accessibility and scalability of high-fidelity egocentric data collection.
- Many datasets lack precise actionable modalities like continuous 6 DoF pose, depth, 3D hand pose, and sufficiently detailed action language labels required for VLA policies.

## Method

- A framework utilizing **MobileEgo Anywhere** — commodity ==LiDAR-enabled iOS devices== (iPhone Pro models) with an open-source mobile application for recording synchronized ==RGB-D streams==, IMU data, and ARKit-derived ==6 DoF camera poses==.
- An open-source Python processing pipeline that transforms raw mobile captures into training-ready formats, extracting robust ==3D hand trajectories==, atomic action labels, and hierarchical task instructions.
- Automated 3D hand pose estimation using ==WiLoR== and a ==VLM-based labeling system== for generating detailed, object-modified atomic action labels, further structured into hierarchical goals by a language model.

## Results

- Release of a **200-hour dataset** comprising **354 sessions** of diverse household activities, with continuous recordings up to **108 minutes**, including synchronized RGB-D, 6 DoF poses, 3D hand poses, and multi-level language annotations.
- ARKit's long-term pose tracking demonstrated minimal cumulative drift, remaining **below 1 cm** in most scenarios and **under 0.1%** of the total trajectory length.
- Validated 3D hand pose consistency with median bone length coefficient of variation **below 1%** for most bones and **over 99.99%** of joint angles falling within biomechanical limits; automated hierarchical instruction generation achieved distinct temporal scale separation with high cost-efficiency (**$1.29** for all sessions).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Commodity LiDAR-enabled smartphones provide sufficiently stable and accurate long-term 6 DoF pose tracking (e.g., <1.5 cm drift over hour-long whole-house activities) and depth maps for VLA applications.
> - Automated pipelines can generate more detailed and temporally consistent action labels and hierarchical task instructions than human annotators, at a low cost.
> - The proposed framework effectively bridges the gap between hardware accessibility and the demand for long-horizon, high-fidelity egocentric data by democratizing the collection process.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models has created a demand for large-scale egocentric datasets to train general-purpose robotic policies. Existing research indicates a scaling law between the volume of egocentric human data and VLA model performance, suggesting that further improvements necessitate significantly larger and more diverse datasets.

The current landscape of egocentric datasets for robotics presents several limitations that this work aims to address. Earlier datasets like Ego4D and EPIC-KITCHENS-100 provided extensive video footage primarily for action recognition and human-object interaction, but generally lacked the precise, continuous 6 DoF (Degrees of Freedom) pose tracking essential for robotic policy learning. More recent efforts, driven by the emergence of Foundation Models and VLA architectures, have begun to incorporate pose information. For example, EgoScale offers precise poses but is characterized by short episode durations. EgoExo4D provides rich modalities including pose, depth, and hand annotations, but relies on specialized and commercially unavailable hardware such as Meta’s Project Aria glasses and synchronized external cameras, limiting its accessibility and scalability. Other datasets like HOI4D, ARCTIC, and HOT3D offer precise hand tracking but are confined to short segments (seconds to minutes) and controlled laboratory environments. EgoDex provides a large volume of dexterous manipulation data captured on Apple Vision Pro, but with short demonstration durations (approximately 9 seconds per demo).

In the context of scalable data collection interfaces, methods such as teleoperation and kinesthetic teaching can yield high-quality samples but are resource-intensive, requiring operators and robots for each demonstration. Approaches like the Universal Manipulation Interface (UMI) reduce the hardware barrier by using handheld grippers, yet still necessitate specialized physical mounts and calibrated setups.

This work positions itself to overcome these two principal limitations: the brevity of episodes and the high hardware/infrastructure barriers associated with current egocentric data collection. By leveraging commodity smartphone sensors for long-term pose tracking, the authors propose an accessible and scalable framework for collecting long-horizon egocentric data in uncontrolled, everyday environments, directly contributing to the foundational data requirements for advancing VLA models and general-purpose robotics. The paper also acknowledges the challenge of maintaining stable state tracking over long sessions, a known difficulty for SLAM pipelines which often suffer from computational intractability or accumulated drift; it highlights how modern mobile AR frameworks like ARKit address this through sensor fusion.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop and release an open, accessible infrastructure for collecting large-scale, long-horizon egocentric data suitable for training Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models on commodity hardware. This overarching goal is driven by several key motivations:

1. **Addressing Data Scarcity and Scale for VLA Models:** VLA models are demonstrating potential for general-purpose robot policies, but their performance is significantly dependent on the volume and quality of egocentric human data. Existing scaling laws suggest that substantial further gains in model performance necessitate orders of magnitude more data. The current data landscape is insufficient in both quantity and diversity for these advanced models.
2. **Overcoming Limitations of Existing Egocentric Datasets:**
 - **Short Episode Durations:** Most existing datasets feature episodes lasting only a few minutes. This brevity is insufficient to capture the long-horizon temporal dependencies and sequential reasoning required for complex robotic task execution, which often involves multi-step processes over extended periods.
 - **High Hardware Barriers:** Traditional methods for collecting high-fidelity egocentric data often rely on specialized, expensive, or commercially unavailable hardware (e.g., motion capture systems, custom glasses, or calibrated lab setups). This creates significant barriers for researchers and potential data contributors, hindering widespread data collection and limiting diversity.
3. **Democratizing Data Collection:** The research aims to lower the entry barrier for creating VLA-ready datasets. By utilizing ubiquitous commodity hardware—specifically modern smartphones with advanced sensors like LiDAR and Visual-Inertial Odometry (VIO)—the goal is to enable data collection "anywhere" by a broad base of users, moving beyond controlled lab environments or proprietary hardware ecosystems.
4. **Enabling Persistent State Tracking:** Long-horizon tasks require continuous and accurate 6 DoF pose tracking to maintain spatial consistency throughout extended sessions. The motivation is to achieve this robust tracking on edge devices without accumulating significant drift, thereby providing reliable world-anchored trajectories crucial for imitation learning in robotics.
5. **Providing Rich, Structured Annotations:** Beyond raw video, VLA models require rich annotations. The objective is to develop automated and hierarchical labeling pipelines that provide detailed atomic action labels (object, action, location) and structure these into multi-level task instructions (session goal, sub-goals, episodes), offering multi-scale supervision for advanced VLA architectures.
6. **Validating Data Utility:** A critical motivation is to demonstrate that the data collected through this proposed infrastructure is not only extensive and rich in modalities but also serves as a usable and effective training signal for downstream VLA models, rather than just being a raw collection.

In summary, the motivation is to fill a critical gap in the VLA research landscape by providing an accessible, scalable, and high-fidelity egocentric data collection and processing infrastructure that facilitates the training of more generalizable and capable robotic policies for complex, long-horizon tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The MobileEgo Anywhere framework is structured around three core components: a mobile capture application, an open-source processing pipeline (STERA), and a newly collected dataset.

**A. Hardware Configuration and Capture Methodology**

The hardware setup utilizes a LiDAR-enabled iPhone Pro, which is secured to a head-worn rig to capture a first-person perspective of the participant's hands and workspace. This approach leverages the smartphone's built-in sensors for data acquisition.

1. **Sensor Utilization:** During recording, ARKit, Apple's augmented reality framework, performs real-time sensor fusion. This process combines high-frequency Inertial Measurement Unit (IMU) data with visual keyframes from the RGB-D stream to generate continuous 6 DoF camera poses and per-frame depth maps.
2. **Mobile Application:** A dedicated mobile application is employed to manage data collection. It exports raw sensor data, including RGB frames, depth maps, IMU readings, and camera intrinsics, in the MCAP format. The application ensures all modalities are registered to a common high-resolution timestamp, which is critical for downstream 3D reconstruction and action recognition.
3. **Hands-Free Operation:** Data collection is initiated and terminated using voice commands ("start"/"stop"), allowing contributors to perform activities hands-free, which promotes naturalistic interaction.

**B. STERA Processing Pipeline**

The STERA (Scalable TRajectory Extraction and Annotation) pipeline is an offline processing suite that transforms the raw MCAP logs into standardized, training-ready formats.

1. **3D Hand Trajectory Estimation:**
 - **Method:** The pipeline employs WiLoR, a system for end-to-end 3D hand localization and reconstruction, with MANO parameterization. WiLoR is selected for its ability to estimate hand joints under biomechanical constraints, providing robustness against partial occlusions common in first-person manipulation videos.
 - **Localization:** The relative 3D coordinates generated by WiLoR are then localized into a consistent global frame. This is achieved by sampling ARKit depth maps at the detected hand joint locations and applying the extrinsic camera transformation. The result is world-anchored 3D hand trajectories suitable for imitation learning.
2. **Atomic Action Labels:**
 - **Automated Annotation:** To generate fine-grained language labels for 200 hours of video, an automated annotation pipeline is utilized. This pipeline partitions raw video into contiguous temporal spans.
 - **VLM Processing:** Each span is processed by a Vision-Language Model (VLM), which generates a short imperative sentence describing the action. These sentences are constrained to include object modifiers (e.g., color, material, size) and spatial prepositions (e.g., "from," "to," "on") when supported by video evidence (e.g., "transfer dough from metal bowl to large plate").
 - **Validation:** Automated labels were validated against human annotations, showing greater descriptive detail (7.95 words vs. 2.94 words per label) and a reduction in temporal defects (zero defects vs. 9.9% in human annotations).
3. **Hierarchical Task Instructions:**
 - **Structure:** The atomic action labels are organized into a three-level instruction tree to capture the long-horizon structure of activities: a session-level goal, intermediate sub-goals, and individual episodes.
 - **Language Model Grouping:** A language model groups temporally contiguous atomic spans into episodes, clusters related episodes into sub-goals, and synthesizes a session-level goal grounded in the objects and actions across all spans.
 - **Invariants:** Three structural invariants are enforced during generation: unique assignment of each atomic span to exactly one episode, exact timestamp boundaries for episodes and sub-goals, and full session coverage without gaps. Seven different language models were evaluated for this task, with six producing fully valid outputs.

**C. Data Quality Validation**

The quality of the collected data was assessed through multiple methods:

1. **ARKit Pose Accuracy:**
 - **Motion Capture Comparison:** Absolute accuracy of ARKit pose estimates was quantified by comparing them against ground truth from a 30-camera Vicon motion capture system. Trajectories included diverse motion profiles such as naturalistic tasks, walks, closed-loop traversals, and spinning sequences. Metrics included Absolute Trajectory Error (ATE RMSE), relative ATE, and Relative Pose Error (RPE) for translation and rotation.
 - **Long-Term Drift Evaluation:** Since ARKit is a closed-source system, its long-term drift was assessed by placing an ArUco marker at the session start and revisiting it at the temporal midpoint and session end across six different environments.
2. **3D Hand Pose Consistency (Ground-Truth Free):** This was evaluated across 98 sessions using three indirect metrics:
 - **Bone Length Constancy:** Measured the median coefficient of variation (CV) for the 20 MANO bone lengths.
 - **Joint Angle Plausibility:** Verified that joint flexion angles fell within published biomechanical limits.
 - **Wrist Dynamics:** Analyzed the distributions of wrist velocity and acceleration to check for smooth, physically plausible motion.
3. **Hierarchical Instruction Quality:** Analyzed the generated instruction hierarchy for temporal scale separation, linear scaling of episode/sub-goal counts with session length, and episode granularity (spans per episode).

**D. Training Signal Validation**

To ascertain the utility of the collected data as a training signal, the VITRA vision-language-action framework was mid-trained. The VITRA-VLA-3B model was initialized, its vision encoder was frozen, and it was conditioned on hierarchical instructions. The training objective was to supervise MANO hand pose prediction for 10,000 steps. Evaluation was performed on a held-out split of 27 sessions, predicting directly in MANO hand-parameter space.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The MobileEgo Anywhere project yielded a new infrastructure and a corresponding dataset, along with validations of its components.

**A. Dataset Characteristics and Modalities**

- **Dataset Size and Duration:** The released dataset comprises 200 hours of egocentric data across 584 sessions from 20 contributors. Sessions average 20.5 minutes, with the longest continuous episode reaching 108 minutes.
- **Unique Modality Combination:** The dataset is described as the only one that combines consumer hardware with continuous 6 DoF pose tracking, LiDAR depth, MANO 3D hand annotations, and sessions exceeding one hour. This contrasts with existing datasets like EgoExo4D, which offer similar modalities but require commercially unavailable hardware.

**B. ARKit Pose Accuracy**

- **Motion Capture Ground Truth Comparison:**
 - Absolute Trajectory Error (ATE RMSE) and Relative ATE (RMSE) were evaluated against Vicon motion capture ground truth. Relative ATE remained below 1% for nine out of ten sequences, and rotational RPE was below 4 degrees. The exception was a short spinning sequence, where rapid rotation led to slightly elevated values.
 - Translational RPE consistently stayed below 5 cm across all sequences. These results indicate that ARKit Visual-Inertial Odometry (VIO) on a consumer iPhone Pro provides sufficient trajectory accuracy for tasks such as world-frame hand-pose anchoring.
- **Long-Term Drift Evaluation:**
 - Using ArUco markers revisited across six environments, end-of-session drift was below 1 cm in most cases (e.g., Kitchen activity: 0.7 cm, Living-space activity: 0.4 cm). The maximum drift observed was 1.5 cm for a whole-house traversal.
 - Drift remained below 0.1% of the total trajectory length in all evaluated scenarios, demonstrating the efficacy of ARKit tracking for long-term VLA applications.

**C. 3D Hand Pose Consistency**

Evaluated using ground-truth-free metrics across 98 sessions:

- **Bone Length Constancy:** The median coefficient of variation (CV) for the 20 MANO bone lengths was 1.27% for the left hand and 1.43% for the right hand. Excluding the pinky distal phalanx (which showed higher relative error due to its short physical length), the pooled median CV dropped below 1%. This indicates stability of bone length estimates.
- **Joint Angle Plausibility:** Over 99.99% of the 15 finger flexion angles (MCP, PIP, DIP) fell within published biomechanical limits across all sessions, with unimodal distributions consistent with diverse grasp types.
- **Wrist Dynamics:** Median wrist velocity was 0.34 m/s (left) and 0.27 m/s (right), with median acceleration of 2.7 m/s² and 1.5 m/s² respectively. These velocities are consistent with those reported for everyday activities, and the smooth, unimodal distributions suggest the absence of teleportation-scale discontinuities.

**D. Atomic Action Labels and Hierarchical Task Instructions**

- **Atomic Action Labels:** The automated annotation pipeline produced 75,857 atomic action labels. These labels were more descriptive (averaging 7.95 words vs. 2.94 words for human annotations) and contained zero temporal defects, unlike human annotations which had numerous issues (e.g., zero-duration segments, overlapping pairs).
- **Hierarchical Instructions:** The pipeline successfully decomposed all 584 sessions into 9,922 episodes and 2,212 sub-goals.
 - **Temporal Scale Separation:** Each level of the hierarchy occupied a distinct temporal band, with a consistent 4–8x scale separation between adjacent levels (5s atomic spans, 39s episodes, 3.9 min sub-goals, 16.8 min sessions). This structure emerged organically from the data.
 - **Scaling with Session Length:** Episode and sub-goal counts scaled approximately linearly with session length, indicating adaptation to session complexity.
 - **Episode Granularity:** 80% of episodes contained 10 or fewer atomic spans, with a median of 5 spans per episode, demonstrating a compact and manageable structure. The computational cost for this hierarchical structuring was negligible.

**E. Training Signal Validation**

- **Action-Prediction Error Reduction:** Mid-training the VITRA vision-language-action framework on the MobileEgo Anywhere egocentric trajectories for 10,000 steps resulted in a monotonic decrease in held-out action-prediction loss from 0.265 to 0.214, representing a 19% reduction. This reduction was observed on unseen sessions, indicating generalization rather than memorization.
- **Concentration in Finger Articulation:** The loss reduction was more pronounced in finger articulation (-24% per hand) compared to the wrist root (-14%), suggesting that the model effectively learned the fine, dexterity-relevant hand structure encoded in the labels.
- **Model Calibration:** The untrained VITRA-VLA-3B model exhibited mis-scaled predictions on the new data, with a best-of-10 wrist error of 554 mm. Mid-training calibrated the model to the data distribution, significantly reducing this error to 70 mm.

These results collectively establish that the MobileEgo Anywhere framework provides a robust method for collecting high-quality, long-horizon egocentric data that serves as a usable training signal for VLA models.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The MobileEgo Anywhere framework and its associated dataset represent a contribution to the field of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model development and robotic learning, with several potential impacts:

1. **Democratization of Egocentric Data Collection:** By leveraging commodity smartphones (specifically LiDAR-enabled iPhone Pros) and releasing a free mobile application alongside an open-source processing pipeline (STERA), the project significantly lowers the barriers to entry for collecting high-quality egocentric data. This accessibility enables a broader range of researchers and contributors worldwide to generate VLA-ready datasets without requiring specialized, expensive, or proprietary hardware, fostering greater participation and potentially leading to more diverse data.
2. **Enabling Long-Horizon Robotic Learning:** The capability to collect continuous, hour-plus egocentric trajectories with robust 6 DoF pose tracking directly addresses a critical limitation of existing datasets—their short episode durations. Long-horizon data is essential for training VLA models that can understand and execute complex, multi-step robotic tasks requiring sustained interaction and persistent state tracking, moving beyond short, isolated actions.
3. **Rich and Structured Data for Advanced VLA Models:** The dataset provides a comprehensive set of modalities crucial for VLA research, including synchronized RGB-D streams, continuous 6 DoF ARKit poses, LiDAR depth maps, MANO 3D hand trajectories in a consistent world frame, and multi-level hierarchical language annotations. This rich, structured data can support the development of more sophisticated VLA architectures that learn from human demonstrations at multiple temporal scales, from atomic actions to overall session goals.
4. **Validation of Data Utility:** The demonstrated ability of the MobileEgo Anywhere data to reduce held-out action-prediction error in a pre-existing VLA framework (VITRA) confirms its utility as an effective training signal. This validation, particularly the improvement in finger articulation and the calibration of the model to the data distribution, suggests that the data can contribute to developing more dexterous and generalizable robot policies.
5. **Foundation for Future Robotics Research:** The infrastructure and dataset can serve as a foundation for further research in several areas:
 - **Scaling VLA Models:** Providing an avenue for significantly increasing the volume and diversity of egocentric human data, which is hypothesized to be critical for scaling VLA models and achieving more general-purpose robot intelligence.
 - **Sim-to-Real Transfer:** While not directly addressed in this paper, high-fidelity human egocentric data with precise pose and hand information can inform the design of more realistic simulations and improve sim-to-real transfer capabilities for robotic tasks.
 - **Human-Robot Collaboration:** The detailed understanding of human manipulation and intent captured in the dataset can aid in developing robots that can better understand and assist humans in complex tasks.

In summary, MobileEgo Anywhere mitigates key technical and logistical challenges in egocentric data collection, potentially accelerating the development of more capable and generalizable robotic systems by providing accessible, large-scale, and high-fidelity human demonstration data.
