# Gait-Net-augmented Implicit Kino-dynamic MPC for Dynamic Variable-frequency Humanoid Locomotion over Discrete Terrains

> [!summary] Summary
> This paper presents a Gait-Net-augmented implicit kino-dynamic Model Predictive Control (MPC) framework enabling dynamic, variable-frequency humanoid locomotion over discrete terrains. The method concurrently optimizes foot contact force, location, and step duration by using a neural network to predict step duration and an implicit kinematic approach for computational efficiency, demonstrating robust performance on a small-size humanoid robot through varied terrain and disturbances.

## Problem

- Traditional model predictive control (MPC) methods for humanoid locomotion often rely on fixed-time discretizations or simplified models, limiting adaptability to disturbances and challenging terrains.
- Simultaneously optimizing step duration, step placement, and contact forces within a single, computationally efficient, real-time framework remains a challenge due to multi-linear coupling.
- Simplified dynamic models (e.g., SRBM, Centroidal Dynamics) often lack explicit kinematic coupling, requiring separate inverse kinematics (IK) or whole-body control, and struggle to generate kinematically feasible spatial momentum references.

## Method

- A **Gait-Net**-augmented implicit kino-dynamic ==MPC== framework is proposed, combining a lightweight neural network (Gait-Net) with a Sequential Convex MPC (SCMPC) algorithm.
- Gait-Net predicts the preferred step duration (MPC sampling time `dt`) for the upcoming stride, converting it from an optimization variable into a parameter, thereby simplifying the multi-linear coupling problem.
- The implicit kino-dynamic approach ensures kinematic feasibility by iteratively updating spatial momentum and pose reference trajectories using a fast analytical inverse kinematics solver, avoiding direct joint state optimization within the MPC.

## Results

- Gait-Net's PCA-reduced input (R^6) achieved a **26%** reduction in inference time with minimal RMSE increase and demonstrated robustness to sensor noise.
- The proposed Gait-Net-augmented Implicit KD-MPC significantly outperformed Whole-Body Dynamics (**12.05x** slower) and Explicit Kino-dynamic (**7.28x** slower) MPCs in computational efficiency.
- Hardware experiments on a small-size humanoid robot showed the controller's ability to adjust step durations dynamically for push-recovery, carrying unknown loads, navigating uneven terrain, and traversing discrete terrain (20cm gaps, 10cm obstacles) while maintaining robust locomotion.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating a lightweight neural network (Gait-Net) to predict preferred step duration simplifies the MPC problem by converting a complex multi-linear decision variable into an efficiently managed parameter.
> - Implicit kinematic assurance, achieved by iteratively updating spatial momentum and pose reference trajectories based on local solutions from a fast analytical IK, avoids the computational burden of explicit joint state optimization.
> - The inherent lack of strong correlation between gait frequency and foot location in simplified dynamics models can be effectively addressed by learning-augmented approaches like Gait-Net, leading to more natural and kinematically aware gaits.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Executive Summary

The paper addresses a critical challenge in humanoid robotics: achieving dynamic and robust locomotion with adaptive step duration and placement over challenging, discrete terrains. Traditional model-predictive control (MPC) methods, often relying on fixed-time discretizations or simplified models, struggle to simultaneously optimize these crucial gait parameters, leading to suboptimal performance in dynamic and variable conditions.

The authors propose a novel solution: a Gait-Net-augmented implicit kino-dynamic MPC. This framework uniquely combines a lightweight neural network, "Gait-Net," with a Sequential Convex MPC (SCMPC) algorithm. Gait-Net predicts the preferred step duration, effectively transforming it from a complex decision variable into an efficiently managed parameter within the MPC. The implicit kino-dynamic approach ensures kinematic feasibility by iteratively updating spatial momentum and pose reference trajectories based on local solutions, avoiding the computational burden of explicit joint state optimization. Validated extensively in high-fidelity simulations and on small-size humanoid hardware, the proposed method demonstrates superior capabilities in variable-frequency walking, navigating complex 3D discrete terrains, and robustness against unknown disturbances, showcasing a significant step towards more natural and adaptive humanoid locomotion.

### 2. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Humanoid locomotion control is a complex field, largely revolving around balancing stability, dynamics, and adaptability across diverse terrains. The paper effectively situates its contribution by reviewing the limitations of existing approaches:

- **Offline Contact Planning:** Historically, footstep planning (e.g.,) was decoupled from motion control. While efficient for trajectory generation, these open-loop methods often suffer from error accumulation and lack real-time adaptability to disturbances.
- **Contact-Implicit MPC (CI-MPC):** More recent advancements like CI-MPC unify contact planning and motion control. However, they involve highly complex, nonlinear problems with significant computational demands, often requiring costly pre-computation or suffering from slow online speeds.
- **Simplified Model-based MPC (CMPC, SRBM, CD-MPC, Kino-dynamic MPC):**
 - **Convex MPC (CMPC):** Widely used in legged locomotion (e.g., MIT Cheetah), CMPC linearizes dynamics (e.g., Single Rigid-Body Model - SRBM) for faster control. A major limitation is the requirement for predefined contact locations, typically determined by heuristics (Raibert), capture point methods, or separate optimizations (LIPM). These methods embed fixed-time step durations, making adaptation to challenging terrains difficult.
 - **Centroidal Dynamics (CD-MPC):** Offers a simplified yet effective representation of whole-body dynamics suitable for real-time planning. However, CD-MPC lacks explicit kinematic coupling, often necessitating lower-level inverse kinematics (IK) or whole-body control, and typically relies on predefined gait schedules.
 - **Kino-dynamic MPC:** An extension of CD-MPC that includes full kinematics, offering advantages in ensuring feasible whole-body motions. Explicit kino-dynamic MPCs, however, optimize joint states directly, leading to significant computational burden.
- **Variable Frequency Locomotion:** The ability to adapt gait frequency in real-time is crucial for robust locomotion but remains under-explored, often addressed at higher-level planning or with fixed frequencies. Optimizing sampling time introduces multi-linear coupling, increasing complexity.
- **Learning-augmented Optimization-based Control:** Recent trends combine learning (e.g., Reinforcement Learning) with model-based approaches to overcome limitations. Neural networks can approximate complex functions or improve model fidelity (e.g., predicting centroidal inertia). This paper contributes to this growing area by using a neural network (Gait-Net) to inform and simplify the optimization problem, addressing the weak correlation between gait frequency and foot location inherent in simplified dynamics models.

This work specifically tackles the gap of simultaneously optimizing step location, step duration (variable frequency), and contact forces within a single, computationally efficient, real-time framework. It bridges the gap between high-fidelity whole-body control and efficient simplified-model control by proposing an "implicit" kino-dynamic approach augmented by learning.

### 3. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to enable **natural, dynamic, and variable-frequency humanoid locomotion over discrete terrains** by simultaneously optimizing key gait parameters.

The primary motivations driving this work are:

- **Limitations of Fixed-Time Discretization:** Traditional reduced-order-model-based optimal control techniques for humanoid locomotion rely on fixed-time discretization. This reliance severely limits their responsiveness to various disturbances and results in suboptimal performance, especially in challenging conditions requiring adaptive gait.
- **Need for Simultaneous Optimization:** Existing methods typically handle step duration and placement separately or embed fixed durations, making it challenging to adapt efficiently. The authors aim for a unified framework that can concurrently optimize step location, step duration, and contact forces.
- **Addressing Multi-linear Coupling:** Integrating step duration into MPC as an optimization variable introduces complex multi-linear coupling, significantly increasing computational demands. A key motivation is to devise an efficient method to handle these complexities in real-time.
- **Enhancing Simplified Dynamic Models:** Simplified models like SRBM and Centroidal Dynamics lack a strong intrinsic correlation between gait frequency and foot location. The authors are motivated to bridge this gap by incorporating learning (Gait-Net) to inject whole-body dynamics awareness into a computationally lighter MPC framework.
- **Implicit Kinematic Assurance:** While full kino-dynamic MPC is computationally heavy due to explicit joint state optimization, CD-MPC often requires separate IK. The motivation is to achieve kinematic feasibility without directly optimizing joint states, by intelligently designing and updating reference trajectories.
- **Robustness to Unknown Perturbations:** The ultimate goal is to develop a controller capable of dynamic walking that is robust to external disturbances, unknown loads, and challenging terrain discontinuities, even with limited terrain preview.

In summary, the paper aims to develop a computationally efficient, robust, and adaptive humanoid locomotion controller that can intelligently adjust its gait frequency and foot placement in real-time for dynamic interaction with complex environments.

### 4. Methodology and Approach

The core of the proposed method is a **Gait-Net-augmented implicit kino-dynamic Model Predictive Control (MPC)** framework. This novel approach combines supervised learning with a sequential convex MPC (SCMPC) algorithm to efficiently solve multi-linearly constrained optimization problems.

#### 4.1. Gait-frequency Network (Gait-Net)

- **Purpose:** The Gait-Net is a lightweight neural network designed to predict the preferred step duration (in terms of MPC sampling time `dt`) for the upcoming stride. This crucial step simplifies the optimization problem by transforming `dt` from a decision variable into a parameter, thus handling the problematic multi-linear coupling with foot location and contact forces.
- **Data Collection:** To train Gait-Net, high-fidelity humanoid locomotion data is collected from a whole-body MPC controller in simulation. This baseline controller is chosen for its accurate representation of kinematics and dynamics and its robustness under disturbances, allowing for the generation of variable-frequency walking data. The data includes robot floating-base states (position, velocity, Euler angles, angular velocity) and world-frame foot locations, capturing 600 seconds of walking data across 15 different constant velocities, with randomized step durations (150-400 ms) and applied external impulses (10-100 N).
- **Latent Space Feature Reduction:** The initial input feature space (R^16) is reduced using Principal Component Analysis (PCA). PCA identifies the most significant and minimally correlated features across six principal axes. By selecting one feature with a high absolute loading from each of these top six axes, the input to Gait-Net is streamlined (R^6), enabling more efficient inference while maintaining prediction accuracy.
- **Output:** The Gait-Net outputs the preferred MPC sampling time `dt` based on the robot's current state and desired foot placement.

#### 4.2. Gait-Net-augmented Kino-dynamic MPC

The proposed MPC is "implicit" in its handling of kinematics, striking a balance between the high computational cost of full whole-body MPC and the kinematic limitations of pure Centroidal Dynamics (CD-MPC).

- **Motivation for Implicit Approach:** Explicit kino-dynamic MPCs optimize joint states directly, which is computationally burdensome. CD-MPC, while faster, needs subsequent inverse kinematics (IK) or whole-body control for execution, and generating spatial momentum references without kinematic awareness can be challenging. The authors leverage the assumption that if spatial momentum and pose evolution align with well-designed trajectories, kinematic assurance can be implicitly embedded in these reference trajectories, rather than as explicit constraints.
- **Optimization Variables:** The MPC optimizes the spatial momentum vector (`h`), its primitive (centroidal pose `H`), and the search directions (`δ`) for ground contact wrench (`δf`, `δτ`), foot location (`δpf,i`), and CoM position (`δpc`) over a finite horizon. The MPC sampling time (`dt`) is obtained from Gait-Net and treated as a parameter.
- **Sequential Convex MPC (SCMPC):** To solve the inherently nonlinear dynamics arising from bilinear and multi-linear terms (e.g., `(p_f,i - pc) × f_i × dt`), the problem is transformed into sequential Quadratic Programs (QPs). This is inspired by Sequential Quadratic Programming (SQP).
 - **Linearization:** The key assumption (Assumption 2) is that the product of search directions for bilinear variables (`δa * δb`) is minimal and negligible, especially as sequential iterations converge. This allows the linearization of the dynamics (equations 14-15) and other constraints into a convex form at each iteration.
 - **Objective Function:** The objective function (Eq. 18) minimizes the difference between current states/control inputs and their respective reference trajectories (spatial momentum, centroidal pose, foot location, CoM position) while minimizing ground reaction wrenches.
 - **Linearized Dynamics:** The centroidal dynamics are linearized into a state-space form (`xk+1 = Ak*xk + Bk*uk + Ck`), where `Ak`, `Bk` are state-space matrices and `Ck` contains constant terms from the previous iteration's solution.
 - **Constraints:** Standard locomotion constraints are applied:
 - **Linear Momentum and CoM Position:** CoM position dynamics are explicitly linked to linear momentum.
 - **Friction Pyramid:** A linearized conservative approximation of the friction cone.
 - **Contact-switching Constraints:** Binary contact schedule (`σ`) enables or disables ground reaction forces and moments for swing/stance legs, and enforces stationary foot locations during stance.
 - **Dynamic Range of Foot Location:** Foot location bounds are applied based on robot position and a one-step preview of terrain data.
- **Implicit Kinematics Assurance in Trajectory Reference (Remark 1):** This is a critical aspect. After obtaining local solutions for foot location and CoM position in each sequential iteration, a fast analytical inverse kinematics (IK) solver is used to compute corresponding joint trajectories. These joint trajectories then update the spatial momentum (`h_ref`) and centroidal pose (`H_ref`) reference trajectories for the next iteration. This iterative update ensures that the planned motions are kinematically feasible, effectively embedding kinematic consistency without explicitly optimizing joint angles in the MPC.

#### 4.3. Gait-Net-augmented Sequential MPC Algorithm (Algorithm 1)

The overall algorithm iterates as follows:
1. **Initialization:** Initialize states, control inputs, and a nominal MPC `dt`. Generate initial reference trajectories.
2. **Iterative Solving (While Loop):** The loop continues until a maximum number of iterations (`j_max`) or a desired tolerance for search direction (`η`) is met.
 - **Solve CMPC Subproblem:** A QP problem is solved to determine the search directions (`δu_j`) for contact wrenches, foot locations, and CoM positions, using the current `dt_j` from Gait-Net.
 - **Update Control Inputs:** The control inputs are updated (`u_j+1 = u_j + δu_j`).
 - **Update MPC Sampling Time (`dt`):** The Gait-Net (`N_GN`) is queried with the current robot state (q, q_dot) and the newly optimized foot location (`p_f^j`) to predict the updated MPC sampling time (`dt_j+1`).
 - **Update Reference Trajectories:** Based on the new foot locations and CoM positions from the current iteration, and the updated `dt`, the spatial momentum and centroidal pose reference trajectories are re-computed using the fast analytical IK. This ensures kinematic consistency.
3. **Output:** Once convergence is achieved, the optimized control inputs are returned.

Crucially, the full Gait-Net-augmented Kino-dynamic MPC runs only at the *beginning of each footstep* to determine the step duration. For the rest of that footstep, the determined `dt` is fixed, and the kino-dynamic MPC solves only for contact location and wrenches.

### 5. Main Findings and Results

The authors conducted extensive validation in both high-fidelity simulation and on custom small-size humanoid hardware.

#### 5.1. Gait-Net Performance

- **Efficiency:** The PCA-reduced feature space (R^6) for Gait-Net achieved a 26% reduction in inference time compared to the full feature space (R^16), with a small increase in RMSE (from 4.98e-3 to 6.79e-3). This demonstrates the effectiveness of feature reduction for real-time efficiency.
- **Robustness to Sensor Noise:** Gait-Net demonstrated strong robustness to sensor noise (IMU and joint encoder noise), with worst-case RMSE differing by only 0.39 ms from the noise-free case. This indicates its reliability in realistic hardware conditions.

#### 5.2. MPC Solving Method Comparison

A comparison of different MPC models and solving approaches (Whole-Body Dynamics (WBD), Explicit Kino-dynamic (KD), Centroidal Dynamics (CD), and the Proposed Implicit KD) was performed in 3D simulations:

- **Computational Efficiency:** The proposed Gait-Net-augmented Implicit KD-MPC significantly outperforms WBD-MPC (12.05x slower) and Explicit KD-MPC (7.28x slower) in terms of normalized solve time. It also exhibits a faster convergence rate (17.1 average QPs) compared to CD-MPC with SQP (19.5 QPs) and especially the Alternating Direction (AD) approach (27.5 QPs), which was 2.58 times slower than the baseline CD-MPC.
- **Variable Gait Frequency:** Qualitative 2D simulation comparisons highlighted the advantages of the proposed method:
 - **Fixed `dt`:** Failed to adapt, leading to suboptimal or failed locomotion on discrete terrains.
 - **NMPC (optimizing `dt` as a variable):** Resulted in inconsistent and unintuitive `dt` solutions, often leading to failures, as `dt` lacks a strong physical correlation with step location within simplified models.
 - **Proposed (Gait-Net-augmented):** Demonstrated natural and kinematically aware walking. Gait-Net ensured `dt` was correlated with stride length and robot state, enabling adaptive and consistent step durations, especially for preparing for or clearing terrain gaps.

#### 5.3. 3D Locomotion over Discrete Terrain (Simulation)

- The robot successfully navigated a 3D stepping-stone course with a commanded forward velocity of 0.75 m/s.
- The method ensured that the foot locations remained within the designated stepping stone patches, demonstrating effective satisfaction of foot location constraints with only a one-step preview of terrain data.
- Spatial momenta measurements closely tracked MPC predictions, validating the proposed Gait-Net-augmented sequential method's ability to accurately predict and control robot dynamics.

#### 5.4. Hardware Experiment Validation

Experiments on a 3D small-size humanoid robot ("HECTOR V2") demonstrated the practical applicability and robustness of the proposed framework.

- **Locomotion under Unknown Perturbations:**
 - **Push-Recovery (Fig. 1a):** The controller dynamically adjusted step durations to maintain balance under external pushes.
 - **Carrying Unknown Load (Fig. 1b):** The robot successfully adapted its gait while carrying a 0.75 kg unknown object.
 - **Uneven Terrain Locomotion (Fig. 1c):** The robot navigated uneven terrain by adjusting step durations.
 - Gait-Net-augmented MPC dynamically adjusted `dt` (within [0.045, 0.07] s bounds) in response to these disturbances, deviating from the nominal 0.045 s (0.225 s step duration) on flat terrain. This confirms Gait-Net's ability to adapt based on robot state and foot location.
- **Baseline Comparison in Loco-manipulation (Pushing Carts):**
 - When pushing a 35 kg unknown load (219% of robot's mass), the proposed method significantly outperformed an SRBM MPC with fixed gait frequency, which struggled to handle the large disturbance.
 - The proposed method allowed longer step durations for effective pushing (favoring near foot locations) and shorter steps for balance recovery (larger steps), demonstrating adaptive and effective loco-manipulation.
- **Discrete Terrain Locomotion (Gaps and Obstacles):**
 - **20-cm Gap (Fig. 1d):** The robot successfully traversed a 20-cm-wide virtual gap at 0.75 m/s, maintaining velocity tracking. Foot placement allowed for some toe/heel overlap with the taped area, facilitated by CWC constraint adaptation for line-foot contact.
 - **10-cm Obstacle + 15-cm Gap (Fig. 1e):** The robot navigated a more complex course with an unknown height obstacle and a virtual gap. The robot intelligently leaned to one side, using one foot on flat ground while the other took a longer step to clear the obstacle, showcasing advanced adaptive behavior.

- **NN-augmented SQP Sensitivity:** The integration of Gait-Net into SQP was generally robust. Only 2.8% of instances required a fallback to nominal `dt` and pure SQP, adding a minor (23%) average solve time for those cases, indicating good practical stability.
- **MPC Weight Parameter Sensitivity:** The controller showed reasonable robustness to variations in MPC weight parameters (L1 and L2 objectives), with a broad range of values (10% to 300% of original hand-tuned values) yielding stable performance in both simulation and hardware, indicating ease of tuning.

### 6. Significance and Potential Impact

- **Pioneering Variable-Frequency Locomotion:** This work makes a significant stride in enabling truly variable-frequency locomotion for humanoids, a crucial capability for natural and robust walking over diverse and dynamic terrains. By intelligently adapting step duration in real-time, the robot can react more effectively to disturbances and optimize its gait for specific terrain features.
- **Novel Learning-Augmented Control:** The Gait-Net-augmented sequential MPC framework is a novel contribution that effectively bridges the gap between high-fidelity whole-body dynamics and computationally efficient simplified models. It demonstrates a powerful paradigm for incorporating learning into optimization-based control to handle complex nonlinearities (like multi-linear coupling) and inject kinematic awareness implicitly.
- **Implicit Kinematic Assurance:** The method's approach to implicitly embedding kinematic consistency through iterative reference trajectory updates is an elegant solution, offering computational benefits over explicit joint state optimization while maintaining kinematic feasibility. This makes it more practical for real-time deployment.
- **Enhanced Robustness and Adaptability:** The comprehensive validation, especially on hardware, highlights the controller's superior perturbation resistance (push-recovery, unknown loads) and ability to navigate complex discrete terrains (gaps, obstacles) with only a one-step preview. This represents a significant advancement in real-world applicability for humanoid robots.
- **Practical Applicability:** The efficient and robust performance on a small-size humanoid robot at a dynamic speed (0.75 m/s) over challenging terrains demonstrates the practical viability of the proposed control architecture for real-world applications requiring agile and adaptive bipedal locomotion.

**Limitations:**

- **Limited Terrain Preview:** The current direct foot position constraint relies on real-time CoM position feedback and only uses a one-step preview of terrain data. This means that constraints for subsequent steps within the prediction horizon (beyond the immediate next step) must be deactivated. While practical for real-time perception, it limits the controller's ability to plan for longer-horizon complex terrains that might require multiple steps of foresight.
- **Gait-Net Training Data Dependence:** The performance of Gait-Net is dependent on the quality and diversity of the whole-body MPC data used for training. While the current dataset is shown to be sufficient, extending to more varied gaits, speeds, and disturbances would further enhance its generalization capabilities.
- **NN-SQP Convergence Guarantee:** Although the NN-augmented SQP generally exhibits good convergence, the paper acknowledges that integrating a neural network does not *guarantee* convergence, necessitating a fallback mechanism. While rare (2.8% instances), this indicates a theoretical limitation that future work could address.
- **Generalization of Analytical IK:** The analytical IK solution for the 5-DoF legs with line-foot, while efficient, is specific to this robot's kinematics. While this is standard practice, transferring this specific solution to other humanoid platforms would require re-derivation.

### 7. Conclusion

The paper successfully introduces a novel Gait-Net-augmented implicit kino-dynamic MPC framework that significantly advances dynamic, variable-frequency humanoid locomotion. By intelligently integrating a lightweight neural network (Gait-Net) to predict step durations and employing a sequential convex MPC for efficient optimization, the framework concurrently optimizes foot contact force, location, and step duration. The implicit kinematic assurance, achieved through iterative reference trajectory updates, provides a computationally efficient means to ensure feasible whole-body motions without directly optimizing joint states.

Extensive validation in high-fidelity simulations and on small-size humanoid hardware robustly demonstrates the controller's capabilities. It enables the robot to perform adaptive walking over discrete terrains, handle unknown disturbances (pushes, loads), and navigate complex obstacles and gaps, showcasing superior performance compared to baseline approaches. While the current method relies on a one-step terrain preview, the proposed framework represents a significant step towards more natural, robust, and agile humanoid locomotion in dynamic and unstructured environments, paving the way for future advancements in real-world robot deployment.
