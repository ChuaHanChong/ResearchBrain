# Generalized Linear Mode Connectivity for Transformers

> [!summary] Summary
> A new framework enables low- or zero-loss linear mode connectivity between independently trained Transformer models, including Vision Transformers and GPT-2, by leveraging generalized network symmetries beyond simple permutations. The method successfully connects models with different architectural widths, achieving near-zero interpolation barriers on various datasets.

## Problem

- Existing Linear Mode Connectivity (LMC) techniques, predominantly relying on discrete neuron permutations, failed to achieve low- or zero-loss paths for complex Transformer architectures like ViTs and GPT-2.
- A complete understanding of Transformer loss landscape geometry was hindered by the perceived isolation of independently trained models in parameter space, preventing effective model merging.
- Previous LMC methods could not effectively align or merge models with varying architectural widths, limiting their applicability in diverse scenarios.

## Method

- Introduced a generalized framework formalizing four hierarchical classes of ==function-preserving== network symmetries: permutation, semi-permutation, orthogonal, and invertible transformations.
- Developed a 'Learned Matching' strategy that optimizes alignment parameters using task loss, initialized by an unsupervised 'Weight Matching' approach for efficient and effective alignment.
- Applied the symmetry framework specifically to Transformer components, highlighting orthogonal symmetries along the residual path as crucial for enabling width-heterogeneous model alignment.

## Results

- Demonstrated low- and zero-loss linear interpolation paths between independently trained Vision Transformers and GPT-2 models for the first time, achieving near-zero loss barriers on most datasets (e.g., ViTs on CIFAR-10/100, GPT-2 on Tiny Shakespeare) using the Learned Matching method.
- Successfully extended the alignment to multi-model settings and models with different architectural widths (e.g., a **512** embedding dimension model with 1/16th size counterparts), maintaining near-zero interpolation barriers.
- The unsupervised Weight Matching variant provided substantial reductions in loss barriers, often outperforming permutation-only methods, highlighting its practical utility in data-free scenarios.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Transformers possess richer symmetry classes beyond simple neuron permutations, particularly orthogonal symmetries along the residual path, which are critical for achieving true LMC and aligning models of different widths.
> - Independently trained Transformer models, when reparameterized using these generalized functional symmetries, reside within a common, broadly connected low-loss basin in the loss landscape.
> - The primary performance gap between unsupervised Weight Matching and supervised Learned Matching lies in the fine-tuning of the global orthogonal map responsible for aligning the residual stream.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper "Generalized Linear Mode Connectivity for Transformers" significantly advances the understanding of neural network loss landscapes, particularly the phenomenon of Linear Mode Connectivity (LMC). LMC describes the unexpected finding that independently trained deep neural networks, even when converging to seemingly distinct minima in parameter space, can often be connected by low- or zero-loss paths. This phenomenon challenges the naive view of complex, high-dimensional loss landscapes as composed of many isolated, high-barrier basins.

Early work in mode connectivity (e.g., Freeman and Bruna, 2016; Garipov et al., 2018; Draxler et al., 2018) initially focused on non-linear paths, suggesting that SGD solutions lie on a connected manifold. Subsequent research, particularly on LMC (Frankle et al., 2020; Entezari et al., 2021), posited that this connectivity could be revealed through linear paths, provided that symmetries in the network's parameterization were adequately addressed. The key insight was that functionally equivalent models might appear distant in weight space due to symmetries like neuron permutations.

Prior state-of-the-art approaches, exemplified by "Git Re-Basin" (Ainsworth et al., 2022) and Optimal Transport (OT) fusion (Singh and Jaggi, 2020), have largely focused on leveraging discrete neuron permutations to align models. These methods successfully demonstrated LMC for simpler architectures such as shallow multilayer perceptrons (MLPs), VGG networks, and ResNets. These foundational works cemented the importance of symmetry-aware analysis in understanding loss landscapes and inspired "model re-basin" techniques designed to transform independently trained networks into a shared low-loss valley.

However, a significant gap remained: these permutation-based methods often failed to achieve true LMC (i.e., near-zero barriers) for more complex, modern architectures like Transformers. Existing attempts to merge Transformer models (e.g., Imfeld et al., 2023; Verma and Elbayad, 2024) achieved reduced loss barriers but often reported persistent non-zero barriers, indicating that permutation symmetries alone were insufficient to capture the full range of functional equivalences in these sophisticated models. This suggested that Transformers possess richer, previously unexploited symmetry classes.

This paper directly addresses this limitation by introducing a more generalized and comprehensive framework for understanding and leveraging these richer symmetries. By formalizing four classes of transformations—permutations, semi-permutations, orthogonal transformations, and general invertible maps—the authors significantly broaden the scope of symmetry-aware analysis. This generalized perspective allows them to, for the first time, demonstrate low- and zero-barrier linear interpolation paths between independently trained Vision Transformers and GPT-2 models. In doing so, this work not only unifies and extends prior re-basin techniques but also significantly advances the empirical evidence supporting the hypothesis that complex neural networks, when appropriately reparameterized, reside in surprisingly connected loss landscapes.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to achieve **low- or zero-loss linear mode connectivity (LMC) between independently trained Transformer models**, including both Vision Transformers (ViTs) and GPT-2, and extending to multi-model and width-heterogeneous settings.

The motivation for this objective stems from a critical observation and a recognized limitation in the existing literature:
1. **Limitations of existing LMC techniques for Transformers:** Prior work on LMC predominantly relied on discrete neuron permutation symmetries, which proved effective for simpler architectures like MLPs and CNNs. However, empirical findings (including those by Verma and Elbayad, 2024, cited here) showed that these permutation-based approaches often fail to reveal true LMC in Transformers, leaving persistent loss barriers. This suggests that Transformers possess a richer set of symmetries not captured by simple reordering of neurons.
2. **Uncovering deeper loss landscape structures:** The inability to find low-barrier paths in Transformers, despite their widespread use and theoretical importance, hindered a complete understanding of their loss landscape geometry. The authors were motivated to investigate if a broader definition of symmetry could resolve these perceived disconnections and reveal a more interconnected parameter space.
3. **Broadening applicability of model merging:** Successfully achieving LMC in Transformers has profound practical implications. If independently trained Transformer models can be brought into the same "basin" through function-preserving transformations, it opens new avenues for:
 - **Ensembling:** Merging multiple models into a single, high-performing one without sacrificing performance.
 - **Federated and Continual Learning:** Facilitating model aggregation and knowledge transfer in distributed or sequential learning settings.
 - **Transfer Learning:** Enabling more effective transfer of learned representations between models.
 - **Adversarial Robustness:** Potentially allowing the transfer of desirable properties like robustness across connected basins.
4. **Enabling heterogeneous model alignment:** Traditional LMC work largely focused on identically structured models. The motivation here also includes extending alignment capabilities to models with differing architectural widths, which has practical implications for model compression, expansion, and interoperability across different scales.

In essence, the paper is motivated by the desire to fill a theoretical gap in the understanding of Transformer loss landscapes and, in doing so, unlock significant practical benefits for deep learning applications. By introducing a unified and generalized framework for symmetry, the authors aim to overcome the limitations of previous LMC approaches and provide a more comprehensive toolset for analyzing and manipulating modern neural architectures.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core methodology of this work revolves around defining and leveraging a **generalized framework for network symmetries** that extends beyond simple permutations. This framework allows for the identification of function-preserving transformations that can align independently trained Transformer models, enabling low- or zero-barrier linear mode connectivity.

#### 3.1. Generalized Symmetry Framework

The paper formalizes four hierarchical classes of function-preserving transformations:
- **Permutation (S1):** Reorders inputs/neurons without affecting function. Characteristic of element-wise operations like GELU, sigmoid, softmax, tanh. Represented by permutation matrices.
- **Semi-permutation (S2):** Extends permutation by allowing sparse, weighted mixing of input dimensions. Arises in linearly decomposable, piecewise-linear functions like RELU, PRELU, and importantly, multi-head attention *inter-head* operations. Represented by sparse, stochastic matrices.
- **Orthogonal (S3):** Preserves vector norms and angles (rotations, reflections). Critical for components that normalize inputs, such as RMSNorm, and observed along the Transformer's residual path. Represented by orthogonal matrices, allowing for width expansion.
- **Invertible (S4):** The most general class, for layers preserving linearity. Includes layers whose transformations are unconstrained by sparsity or orthogonality. Relevant for attention mechanisms' QK and OV circuits (*intra-head*). Represented by full-rank invertible matrices.

#### 3.2. Symmetries in Transformers

The framework is specifically applied to Transformer components:
- **Feed-Forward Layer:** Exhibits permutation symmetry (for GELU) or semi-permutation symmetry (for RELU) across its hidden dimension.
- **Multi-Head Attention (MHA):**
 - *Intra-head:* QK and OV circuits (Elhage et al., 2021) possess invertible symmetries. These are handled as canonical representations for alignment.
 - *Inter-head:* The summation of heads exhibits semi-permutation symmetry, allowing reordering and linear decomposition across heads.
- **Residual Path:** The paper highlights an orthogonal symmetry along the residual stream, particularly when RMSNorm is used. LayerNorm can also be reformulated to exploit this orthogonal symmetry. This is crucial for enabling width-heterogeneous alignment, as orthogonal matrices can be rectangular (M ≥ N), allowing dimensionality expansion.

#### 3.3. Alignment Strategies

The paper proposes three strategies for estimating the function-preserving transformations (global orthogonal matrix `O` for residual stream, permutation matrices `P_FF` for FFN neurons, semi-permutation matrices `P_H` for attention heads):

- **Activation Matching:** (Baseline, based on Verma and Elbayad, 2024) Aligns layers by comparing intermediate activations on a shared dataset.
- **Weight Matching (Ours):** An unsupervised, data-free method adapted from Ainsworth et al. (2022).
 - For FFNs: Solves a layer-wise "sum of bilinear assignments problem" (SOBLAP) to maximize weight similarity under permutation constraints, often approximated via coordinate descent.
 - For Attention Heads: Constructs a cost matrix based on the Frobenius norm difference between QK and OV circuits, then solves a linear assignment problem for head-level semi-permutations.
 - For Residual Stream: Estimates a global orthogonal matrix `O` by solving the Procrustes problem, which has a closed-form solution via SVD.
- **Learned Matching (Ours):** A supervised approach that directly optimizes the alignment parameters using task loss.
 - Introduces unconstrained latent matrices (`Z_FF`, `Z_H`, `Z_O`) that are projected onto their respective symmetry classes (`PROJPERM`, `PROJORTH`) at each step.
 - Initialization: Latent matrices are initialized using the weight matching procedure.
 - Optimization: The interpolation `λ * θ_A + (1 - λ) * π(θ_B)` is formed (where `π(θ_B)` is the aligned model B), and the task loss is minimized. Gradients are backpropagated through the differentiable projections (straight-through estimator for permutations, SVD for orthogonal).

#### 3.4. Multi-Model Merging

The framework extends to merging multiple models:
- **Universe Matching:** Iteratively aligns multiple models `{θ_m}` to a shared "universe" reference `U(t)`, which is the average of the aligned models from the previous iteration.
- **Learned Refinement:** Further refines the multi-model alignments by minimizing a multi-model barrier objective using a Dirichlet-sampled interpolation coefficient `λ`.

#### 3.5. Soft Symmetries (Appendix D)

The paper also explores "soft permutations" as a continuous relaxation of hard permutations, where doubly stochastic matrices (from the Birkhoff polytope via Sinkhorn normalization) allow for more flexible many-to-one or one-to-many neuron mappings. While potentially improving merged model performance, this can sacrifice exact functional equivalence.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The study presents compelling empirical evidence demonstrating the efficacy of its generalized symmetry framework for uncovering Linear Mode Connectivity (LMC) in Transformers.

1. **First-Time LMC for Transformers:** The most significant finding is the successful demonstration of **low- and zero-loss linear interpolation paths between independently trained Vision Transformers (ViTs) and GPT-2 models**. This marks a crucial advancement, as prior permutation-only methods typically failed to achieve true LMC for these complex architectures, leaving persistent non-zero barriers.
2. **Superior Performance of Learned Matching:** The proposed **Learned Matching** method consistently outperforms all other alignment approaches. It achieves **zero or near-zero loss barriers** on most datasets for both ViTs (CIFAR-10/100, Tiny ImageNet) and GPT-2 (Tiny Shakespeare). The only exception is the BookCorpus dataset, where it achieves a significantly reduced but still positive barrier of 0.42.
3. **Strong Performance of Weight Matching:** Even the unsupervised and training-free **Weight Matching** variant yields substantial reductions in loss barriers, often surpassing permutation-only learned matching (similar to Git Rebasin). This highlights its practical utility, especially in scenarios where data or computational resources for fine-tuning are limited.
4. **Multi-Model Connectivity:** The framework successfully extends to **multi-model settings**, demonstrating that multiple independently trained Transformer models (e.g., three CIFAR-10 models) can be merged while maintaining a near-zero interpolation barrier. Visualizations show that both weight matching and learned matching effectively flatten the loss surface over the simplex spanned by multiple models, creating a shared low-loss manifold.
5. **Width-Heterogeneous Alignment:** A novel capability is the successful **alignment of models with different architectural widths**. The study shows that a larger model can be aligned with significantly smaller counterparts (e.g., 512 embedding dimension with 1/16th size models), achieving near-zero interpolation barriers. This is attributed to the exploitation of orthogonal symmetries along the residual path, which allows for dimensionality-expanding transformations while preserving functionality.
6. **Analysis of the Gap between Weight and Learned Matching:** An important insight is that the performance gap between weight matching and learned matching primarily stems from refinements in the **orthogonal map (`O`)** that aligns the residual stream. While weight matching effectively determines the broader permutations, learned matching makes small, targeted corrections to the orthogonal transformation. This suggests that enhancing data-free estimation of `O` could further close this performance gap.
7. **Ablation Study Insights:**
 - **Initialization:** Initializing learned matching with the transformations found by weight matching is crucial. It leads to significantly faster convergence and lower loss barriers compared to random initialization.
 - **Sampling Strategy:** For learned matching, a narrow uniform sampling range (e.g., `U(0.4, 0.6)`) for the interpolation coefficient `λ` yields more consistent and lower loss barriers compared to fixed `λ` or broader sampling ranges.
 - **Orthogonal vs. Permutation Matching:** Within the weight matching scheme, orthogonal matching consistently reduces loss barriers over iterations, converging around five iterations, whereas permutation-only matching shows negligible improvement. This reinforces the critical role of richer symmetries.
 - **Soft Permutations:** While soft permutations (using Sinkhorn normalization) can improve the test-time performance of the merged model, they typically do so at the cost of perfect functional equivalence at the aligned endpoint.

In summary, the paper empirically validates its generalized symmetry framework by achieving unprecedented LMC in Transformers, showcasing the power of leveraging richer symmetry classes for model alignment and merging.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "Generalized Linear Mode Connectivity for Transformers" paper has profound significance, both for fundamental understanding in deep learning and for practical applications.

#### 5.1. Fundamental Understanding of Loss Landscapes

- **Challenging the Notion of Isolated Minima:** By consistently achieving zero-barrier LMC in complex Transformer architectures, the work strongly supports the hypothesis that independently trained neural networks converge to solutions residing within a common, broad, and deeply connected basin in the loss landscape, once appropriate functional symmetries are considered. This challenges the intuition of high-dimensional parameter spaces being riddled with isolated, high-barrier local minima.
- **Extending LMC Theory:** The paper extends the empirical evidence for LMC from simpler architectures (MLPs, CNNs) to Transformers, which are the dominant models in modern AI. This is a crucial step towards a unified theory of deep learning optimization and generalization.
- **Insights into Transformer Geometry:** The findings reveal a surprising structural richness in Transformer parameter spaces, where symmetries (especially orthogonal ones) play a more critical role than previously acknowledged. This deepens our theoretical understanding of how these powerful models function and generalize.

#### 5.2. Advancements in Model Alignment and Merging

- **Unified Framework for Symmetries:** The formalization of a hierarchical framework encompassing permutation, semi-permutation, orthogonal, and invertible symmetries provides a powerful conceptual and practical tool for analyzing and aligning diverse network components. This framework unifies and generalizes previous ad-hoc alignment techniques.
- **First-Time Zero-Barrier LMC for Transformers:** The ability to achieve zero-barrier connections for Transformers is a major technical breakthrough, addressing a long-standing challenge in the field.
- **Enabling Heterogeneous Model Alignment:** The demonstration of LMC between models of different architectural widths is highly significant. This capability opens doors for merging or composing models with varying computational footprints or for progressive training strategies that involve changing model scales.

#### 5.3. Broad Practical Implications

The enhanced ability to align and merge complex models has wide-ranging potential impacts across various subfields of AI:
- **Model Ensembling:** Enables the creation of more robust and high-performing ensemble models by merging multiple independently trained models into a single, compact representation without significant performance degradation, simplifying deployment compared to traditional ensembling.
- **Federated Learning:** Facilitates more effective aggregation of models trained on diverse, non-IID client data. By aligning client weights before aggregation (e.g., in Federated Averaging), the variance of aggregated models can be reduced, and convergence improved.
- **Continual Learning:** Offers a parameter-efficient strategy for knowledge retention and integration. New tasks can be learned by aligning and merging the model state from the new task with previous model states, avoiding catastrophic forgetting without relying on large replay buffers or complex regularization.
- **Transfer Learning and Model Distillation:** Improved alignment techniques can facilitate the transfer of knowledge between models, potentially making distillation processes more efficient or enabling more effective fine-tuning strategies.
- **Adversarial Robustness:** As discussed in the paper, robust models are often associated with flatter minima. The existence of zero-barrier paths could allow for "basin hopping," where a high-accuracy but non-robust model is reparameterized and brought into the basin of a robust counterpart, potentially inheriting robustness properties while maintaining high clean accuracy. This opens a new research direction for improving model resilience.
- **Efficient Training and Pruning:** Insights into connected landscapes can inform better initialization strategies, more efficient optimization paths, and potentially guide structural pruning methods by identifying redundant or functionally equivalent components.

#### 5.4. Future Research Directions

The work also points to several promising avenues for future research, including:
- Improving data-free estimation of orthogonal transformations to further reduce the gap between weight and learned matching.
- Scaling the methodology to align larger, more contemporary language models (e.g., beyond the small GPT-2 variants used due to resource constraints).
- Extending alignment capabilities to models trained on partially or entirely different tasks.
- Further refining the use of soft symmetries for optimal trade-offs between functional equivalence and merged model performance.

In conclusion, this paper not only resolves a critical bottleneck in understanding Transformer loss landscapes but also provides a versatile, generalized framework with the potential to significantly impact the development and deployment of robust, efficient, and interconnected deep learning models across numerous applications.
