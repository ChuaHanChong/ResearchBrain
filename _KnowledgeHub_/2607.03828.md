# ObjRetarget: An Object-Aware Motion Retargeting Framework with Anthropomorphic Arm Constraints and Polyhedral Hand Modeling

> [!summary] Summary
> ObjRetarget, developed by researchers from Sun Yat-sen University, The Chinese University of Hong Kong, Shenzhen, and the University of Portsmouth, introduces an object-aware human-to-robot motion retargeting framework that decouples arm and hand motion. It integrates anthropomorphic arm constraints and polyhedral hand modeling for stable multi-finger contact, achieving an average success rate of 75.8% across six dexterous manipulation tasks on a RealMan robot.

## Problem

- Existing human-to-robot motion retargeting methods often overlook explicit hand-object contact modeling, leading to limited accuracy and poor generalization.
- Many current approaches treat the robot arm and hand as a unified system, disregarding their distinct functional roles and scales of motion, which can create conflicts between global motion smoothness and local contact stability.
- Interaction-aware methods, particularly those based on reinforcement learning, frequently suffer from limited generalization capability to variations in object geometry, initial poses, or task conditions, often requiring extensive training.

## Method

- The **ObjRetarget** framework explicitly decouples arm-level global motion generation from contact-sensitive hand manipulation, with each optimized separately.
- Arm trajectories are refined through an optimization framework guided by ==task-adaptive arm-plane regularization== for anthropomorphic motion and task-oriented end-effector tracking.
- Hand manipulation uses a ==contact-aware staged retargeting== mechanism that, during contact, employs ==polytope-cluster-based geometric consistency optimization==, preserving local hand-object geometry using edge-length and relative-pose invariants.

## Results

- **ObjRetarget** achieved an average task success rate of **75.8%** on a RealMan dual-arm robot across six dexterous manipulation tasks, outperforming baseline methods like OKAMI (**61.6%**) and ORION (**50.8%**).
- The framework demonstrated robust generalization, maintaining stable performance across different human demonstrators with varying hand sizes, motion habits, and manipulation styles.
- Ablation studies confirmed that both the hand-object geometric consistency module and the anthropomorphic arm constraints are critical, with the full framework exhibiting the lowest object slip distance (**0.012 m**) and best geometric consistency (**0.081**).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Separating the optimization of global arm movements from fine, contact-sensitive hand manipulation is crucial for generating both natural, anthropomorphic arm motions and stable hand-object interactions.
> - Integrating adaptive anthropomorphic constraints, such as arm-plane regularization based on wrist motion trends, can significantly improve the naturalness, consistency, and accuracy of retargeted robot arm trajectories.
> - Modeling multi-finger contacts using geometric invariants within polytope clusters provides a robust method for preserving local hand-object contact structures, which is essential for stable and semantically meaningful dexterous manipulation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of embodied intelligence and dexterous manipulation research is actively pursuing methods for robots to acquire complex manipulation skills from human demonstrations, particularly through observation of human videos. A central challenge in this area is reliably retargeting human intent into executable robot actions while ensuring stable hand-object contact throughout the manipulation process.

Existing motion retargeting approaches often focus on pose or joint mapping in free space, reproducing demonstrated kinematics without explicitly modeling object exchange, contact transitions, or sustained hand-object interaction. Methods that rely on direct hand mapping also exhibit limitations in these areas. Furthermore, many current techniques treat the robot arm and hand as a unified system, disregarding their distinct functional roles and scales of motion. The arm is primarily responsible for large, global movements for object approach and positioning, while the hand performs fine, contact-sensitive actions crucial for grasp stability. This unified treatment can lead to conflicts between global motion smoothness and local contact stability, hindering robustness and naturalness in tasks involving object interaction.

Interaction-aware approaches, particularly those utilizing reinforcement learning (RL) for end-to-end optimization, can implicitly learn contact behaviors. However, these methods frequently suffer from limited generalization capability, with performance degradation observed when object geometry, initial poses, or task conditions vary. They often require extensive training processes with large-scale interaction rollouts and careful reward design, and can be sensitive to variations in object characteristics or initial conditions.

More recent single-video retargeting methods, such as OKAMI and ORION, leverage human demonstrations to generate robot motions. While OKAMI aims to teach humanoid robots manipulation skills, it may struggle with multi-stage operations and continuous contact control due to a lack of explicit hand-object modeling. ORION uses open-world object graphs to infer object-centric actions for generalization across objects and layouts, but it does not model fine-grained hand-object contacts.

ObjRetarget positions itself within this landscape by addressing these identified limitations. It proposes a human-to-robot motion retargeting framework that explicitly models hand-arm motion differences and incorporates object interaction constraints directly into the retargeting process. By decoupling arm-level global motion from contact-sensitive hand manipulation and integrating structured hand-object geometric modeling with anthropomorphic arm constraints, ObjRetarget aims to achieve stable, natural, and generalizable dexterous manipulation without relying on extensive training procedures or highly precise object models. This approach seeks to provide a structured, human-informed formulation that enables stable interaction and improved generalization across diverse objects and task scenarios.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this work is to develop a human-to-robot motion retargeting framework, named ObjRetarget, that enables robots to learn dexterous manipulation skills from human manipulation videos. This framework aims to translate human intent into executable robot actions while ensuring stable hand-object contact throughout complex manipulation processes.

The motivation for this work stems from several challenges observed in existing approaches:
- **Lack of Explicit Contact Modeling**: Many current motion retargeting methods often overlook the explicit modeling of hand-object contact relationships. This omission can lead to limited accuracy and poor generalization, as successful manipulation fundamentally relies on preserving physically meaningful contact interactions.
- **Limitations of Reinforcement Learning**: While reinforcement learning-based approaches can implicitly learn contact behaviors, they frequently exhibit limitations in generalization. Their performance may degrade when confronted with variations in object geometry, initial poses, or task conditions, making them less robust for diverse real-world scenarios.
- **Disregarding Arm-Hand Specialization**: Most existing methods treat the robot arm and hand as a unified system, failing to account for their distinct functional roles. The arm is responsible for large-scale, global movements that determine object approach and spatial positioning, while the hand performs fine, contact-sensitive actions critical for grasp stability and successful manipulation. Ignoring this fundamental distinction often results in conflicts between maintaining global motion smoothness and achieving local contact stability.
- **Need for Natural and Accurate Robot Motions**: To truly imitate human manipulation, robots must not only reproduce fine-grained finger motions but also coordinate these with large-scale arm movements, ensuring that grasping, transport, and object operation appear both physically stable and behaviorally natural. This requires simultaneous reasoning about contact geometry, motion continuity, and task-dependent constraints.

To address these motivations, ObjRetarget sets forth the following specific objectives:
- To propose a human-to-robot retargeting framework that explicitly separates global arm motion from contact-sensitive hand manipulation.
- To refine retargeted arm trajectories through anthropomorphic motion constraints and redundancy-aware optimization, thereby generating natural and accurate arm movements that adapt to varying object initial poses while following human movement patterns.
- To model multi-finger contacts using polytope clusters and preserve contact structure through geometric invariants, enhancing grasp stability and interaction reliability in hand manipulation.
- To empirically demonstrate improved manipulation success rates and contact stability on real robots across various dexterous tasks.
- To validate the framework's generalization capability to different demonstrations, object poses, and task settings.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

ObjRetarget is designed as an object-interaction-oriented human-to-robot retargeting framework that learns robot manipulation from human operation videos. The framework consists of several integrated components to process human video input, generate and refine arm and hand motions, and coordinate their execution.

#### 3.1. Human Reference Plan Generation

The initial stage involves extracting a comprehensive human reference plan from an RGB-D manipulation video.
1. **Human Body Motion Reconstruction**: The SLAHMR pose estimator is employed to recover human motion sequences, providing articulated joint trajectories.
2. **Object Detection and Tracking**: A vision-language model identifies manipulated objects, and object pose tracking estimates their spatial trajectories over time.
3. **Synchronization and Contact Detection**: These components are integrated to produce synchronized sequences of human joint poses, object point-cloud poses, and human-object interaction distances. Contact events are detected by thresholding the interaction distance, allowing the system to differentiate between free motion and physical interaction phases.

#### 3.2. Hand-Arm Decoupled Motion Generation

ObjRetarget explicitly models the distinct motion characteristics of the arm and hand, generating their motions separately.

##### 3.2.1. Arm-level Motion Generation

Arm motion generation follows a two-stage strategy:
- **Motion Initialization via Motion Retargeting**: An initial robot trajectory is generated using a prior retargeting method, serving as a feasible and structurally consistent motion prior. This process encodes human skeleton graphs into a latent space and decodes them into robot joint configurations, minimizing a reference loss $L_{ref}$ that considers end-effector position error ($L_{pos}$), orientation error ($L_{ori}$), and fingertip consistency ($L_{tip}$) relative to the human demonstration. This provides a macro-level arm reference.
- **Trajectory Refinement via Optimization**: The initialized trajectory is refined through an optimization framework guided by anthropomorphic motion constraints and task objectives.
 - **Task-Adaptive Arm-Plane Regularization**: To prevent non-anthropomorphic elbow configurations (e.g., flipping, jitter), the arm plane formed by the robot's shoulder, elbow, and wrist is regularized. Instead of directly matching the human arm-plane normal, a reference bending direction $n_{ref}^{arm}(t)$ is adaptively constructed from the wrist's motion trend (the cross product of the vector from shoulder to wrist and the wrist velocity direction). A loss function $L_{plane}(t)$ penalizes deviations between the robot's arm-plane normal $n_{robot}^{arm}(t)$ and $n_{ref}^{arm}(t)$. An adaptive weighting $w(t)$ prioritizes this prior during large, fast arm motions and reduces its influence during fine contact manipulation.
 - **Task-Oriented End-Effector Tracking Loss**: To ensure task accuracy, a standard end-effector pose tracking objective is included. $L_{task}(t)$ minimizes the squared error in wrist position and orientation relative to a reference wrist pose, with orientation error mapped from SO(3) to its Lie algebra.
 - **Overall Arm Optimization Objective**: The robot joint configuration $q(t)$ is optimized by minimizing a sum of $L_{task}(t)$, $L_{plane}(t)$, and a temporal smoothness term (squared joint velocity, $||q(t) - q(t-1)||^2$). Weighting factors $\lambda_p$ and $\lambda_s$ control the anthropomorphic regularization and temporal smoothness contributions.

##### 3.2.2. Hand-level Motion Generation

Hand manipulation employs a contact-aware staged retargeting mechanism:
- **Non-Contact Phase**: When no contact is detected (hand-object interaction distance greater than a threshold $\delta$, set to 0.05 cm), the system follows initialized hand motion commands from the initial retargeting stage.
- **Contact Phase**: Once contact is detected, a polytope-cluster-based geometric consistency optimization is activated.
 - **Hand-Object Contact Representation**: Hand keypoints (five fingertips, palm center) and the object surface point cloud are extracted. Finger-wise contact detection identifies local contact regions $C_f$ on the object surface for each contacting finger $f$, and a representative contact point $c_f$ (centroid of $C_f$) is computed.
 - **Polytope Cluster Construction**: For each contacting finger $f$, a local tetrahedral unit $T_f$ is constructed using four vertices: the palm center, the fingertip keypoint of $f$, the fingertip of an adjacent finger, and the corresponding object contact point $c_f$. Multiple simultaneous contacts form a polytope cluster, representing local hand-object geometry.
 - **Geometric-Invariant Optimization**: Hand motion retargeting is formulated as a nonlinear optimization problem under geometric invariants. The overall geometric consistency loss $L$ is a weighted sum of individual tetrahedron losses $L(T_f)$, which is composed of:
 - **Edge-Length Loss ($L_{edge}$)**: Constrains the local tetrahedral geometry by minimizing the squared differences in edge lengths between the robot and human demonstration tetrahedra.
 - **Relative-Pose Invariant Loss ($L_{pose}$)**: Distinguishes cases where geometric structure is preserved but manipulation semantics differ. It constrains the relative position of the object contact point with respect to a local coordinate frame defined by hand keypoints, preserving contact direction and spatial distribution relative to the hand.
 - **Overall Hand Optimization Objective**: The robot joint configuration $q$ is optimized to minimize the weighted sum of $L(T_f)$, subject to robot joint limits $q_{min} \le q \le q_{max}$.

#### 3.3. Synchronized Arm–Hand Execution

A unified temporal scheduler coordinates the execution of both arms and hands. Arm motions are executed using the anthropomorphically retargeted commands. Hand motions switch dynamically: following initialized commands during non-contact phases and polytope-cluster-optimized commands during contact phases to preserve local contact structures and manipulation semantics. This cycle automatically repeats for multiple contact events.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy of ObjRetarget was evaluated through a series of experiments on a RealMan dual-arm robot platform performing various dexterous manipulation tasks, including grasping, placement, pouring control, and articulated-object interaction. Each task was repeated 20 times, with object poses randomly initialized within the robot's workspace to assess robustness under realistic perception uncertainty. The framework was compared against OKAMI and ORION.

#### 4.1. Comparative Experiments on Motion Retargeting

1. **Multi-method comparison**: ObjRetarget, OKAMI, and ORION were systematically evaluated across six tasks: Sequential Place, Soft Place, Drawer Close, Fruit Place, Pour Water, and Bimanual Place.
 - **Success Rates**: ObjRetarget achieved an average success rate of 75.8%, outperforming OKAMI (61.6%) and ORION (50.8%). The proposed framework demonstrated notable gains in contact-sensitive and multi-stage scenarios, such as Pour Water (16% vs. 12% for baselines), Drawer Close (13% vs. 10% and 8%), Sequential Place (14% vs. 12% and 9%), and Bimanual Place (15% vs. 13% and 8%).
 - **Implications**: These results indicate that the structured hand-object modeling in ObjRetarget contributes to improved contact stability, while the unified arm-motion constraints enhance temporal consistency and coordination. OKAMI performed comparably on simpler tasks but showed degradation in operations requiring sustained contact. ORION exhibited the lowest overall performance, suggesting difficulties in maintaining stable grasping and coordinated wrist behavior without explicit motion and contact modeling.

2. **Cross-demonstrator generalization**: ObjRetarget's robustness to individual human differences was assessed by testing demonstrations from three distinct individuals with varying hand sizes, motion habits, and manipulation styles on the Soft Place, Fruit Place, and Drawer Close tasks.
 - **Performance Stability**: ObjRetarget maintained stable performance across all demonstrators, with consistently high success rates. While minor variations in outcomes were observed due to differing manipulation styles, the performance ranges remained comparable across individuals.
 - **Implications**: This demonstrates that the system does not rely on a specific motion style for correct functionality, indicating robust generalization across diverse human demonstrations.

#### 4.2. Ablation Study

1. **Hand–object geometric consistency module**: The contribution of the structured contact modeling was evaluated by comparing full ObjRetarget with configurations where human-demonstration-based initialization was removed (W/o Init Retarget) and where both initialization and hand-object geometric consistency were removed (W/o Hand Geom.). Metrics included object slip distance, geometric consistency, and task success.
 - **Results**: Full ObjRetarget exhibited the lowest object slip distance (0.012 m), best geometric consistency (0.081), and highest task success (75.8%). "W/o Init Retarget" showed degraded performance (0.025 m slip, 0.183 geometric consistency, 65.0% success), indicating that initialization contributes to stability and convergence. "W/o Hand Geom." resulted in the poorest performance (0.045 m slip, 0.275 geometric consistency, 53.3% success), characterized by significant slippage, contact failures, and posture collapse.
 - **Implications**: This ablation confirms the critical role of both the initialization trajectory and the hand-object geometric consistency module in achieving stable contact and successful task execution.

2. **Ablation of Arm Initialization and Anthropomorphic Constraints**: The impact of initialization-based retargeting and task-adaptive arm-plane regularization was assessed by comparing full ObjRetarget with "W/o Init Retarget" and "W/o Arm-Plane Opt" (removing arm-plane regularization). Metrics included mean per-joint position error (MPJPE), end-effector quaternion distance (Quat), Fréchet distance for trajectory shape similarity, and task success rate.
 - **Results**: Full ObjRetarget achieved the best results across all metrics (0.088 m MPJPE, 0.192 rad Quat, 0.124 m Fréchet, 75.8% success), producing trajectories closely aligned with human demonstrations. "W/o Init Retarget" showed increased MPJPE (0.125 m) and Fréchet distance (0.313 m), suggesting that initialization reduces the search space and improves accuracy. "W/o Arm-Plane Opt" resulted in higher quaternion distance (0.337 rad) and Fréchet distance (0.251 m), indicating unnatural elbow motion and oscillations, which reduced trajectory consistency and overall shape similarity, despite a slightly lower MPJPE than "W/o Init Retarget."
 - **Implications**: This study demonstrates that both the initial retargeting prior and the anthropomorphic arm-plane regularization contribute to the accuracy, naturalness, and consistency of the generated arm trajectories.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

ObjRetarget presents a structured human-to-robot motion retargeting framework that addresses several key challenges in learning dexterous manipulation from human videos. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Addressing Core Challenges in Dexterous Manipulation**: The framework directly confronts the limitations of existing retargeting methods that often neglect explicit contact modeling or struggle with generalization. By decoupling arm and hand motions and integrating object-aware constraints, it provides a method for robots to achieve stable hand-object contact and accurate execution in complex manipulation tasks.
- **Enhanced Stability and Generalization**: The experiments demonstrate that ObjRetarget achieves higher manipulation success rates and improved contact stability across a diverse set of dexterous tasks compared to baseline methods. The framework also exhibits robustness to variations in object initial poses, task settings, and human demonstrator styles, indicating a notable improvement in generalization ability. This is particularly impactful for real-world robotic deployment where environments and tasks are often dynamic and varied.
- **Anthropomorphic and Natural Motion Generation**: The incorporation of anthropomorphic arm constraints, specifically the task-adaptive arm-plane regularization, allows the robot to generate global arm movements that are not only accurate but also consistent with natural human motion patterns. This contributes to robot behaviors that are both effective and intuitive, potentially improving human-robot collaboration and acceptance.
- **Robust Multi-Finger Contact Handling**: The novel polyhedral hand modeling method, which represents multi-finger contacts as local geometric units and preserves their structure through geometric invariants, offers a robust approach to managing complex hand-object interactions. This explicit modeling of contact semantics is crucial for fine-grained manipulation where precise control over interaction forces and geometry is required to prevent slippage or grasp failure.
- **Efficiency in Skill Acquisition**: By leveraging human manipulation priors from videos and employing a structured optimization approach, ObjRetarget avoids the extensive training processes often associated with reinforcement learning-based methods. This could lead to a more efficient and data-sparing paradigm for robot skill acquisition.

**Limitations and Future Work**:
Despite its advancements, the current framework has acknowledged limitations. It does not yet fully exploit dynamic feedback in complex hand-object interactions, potentially limiting its adaptability to highly dynamic tasks. Furthermore, the method is presently confined to upper-limb manipulation. Future research endeavors are planned to integrate adaptive force feedback mechanisms to enhance responsiveness and to extend ObjRetarget to whole-body robotic manipulation, aiming for more general and compliant dexterous manipulation systems.

**Overall Impact**:
ObjRetarget offers a step toward more capable and versatile robotic manipulation systems. Its ability to learn complex dexterous skills from human video demonstrations with improved stability, naturalness, and generalization could simplify the programming of robots for a wider array of applications, particularly in human-centric environments where mimicking human dexterity and understanding nuanced interactions are beneficial.
