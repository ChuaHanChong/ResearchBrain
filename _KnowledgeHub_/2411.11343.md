# Latent Knowledge-Guided Video Diffusion for Scientific Phenomena Generation from a Single Initial Frame

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Shanghai Jiao Tong University developed a framework enabling video diffusion models to generate scientifically plausible videos of complex phenomena like fluid dynamics and typhoons from a single initial frame. This approach integrates latent scientific knowledge using masked autoencoders and optical flow predictors, resulting in improved physical consistency and outperforming baseline models across various scientific metrics.

## Problem

- General video diffusion models struggle to generalize to scientific phenomena, exhibiting severe domain gaps and producing physically inconsistent results.
- Scientific datasets are often small, lack extensive descriptive language annotations, and cannot be augmented with standard techniques that might violate physical laws.
- Existing generative models often produce visually compelling but physically inaccurate or 'hallucinated' outputs when applied to processes governed by scientific laws.

## Method

- A two-stage framework extracts static (via Masked Autoencoder) and dynamic (via ==Optical Flow== Predictor) latent scientific knowledge from raw, unannotated training videos.
- This extracted knowledge is combined with initial frame features and projected through a novel quaternion network, incorporating both spatial and frequency domain information, to generate 'pseudo-language prompt embeddings'.
- These embeddings then guide a pre-trained video ==diffusion model==, efficiently fine-tuned using **LoRA**, to generate subsequent frames that adhere to scientific principles.

## Results

- The proposed method consistently generated more realistic, temporally coherent, and scientifically plausible videos for fluid dynamics simulations and real-world typhoon observations compared to several advanced baselines.
- It achieved substantial improvements, often by an order of magnitude, in physics-aware metrics (e.g., SFE, SE, QCE, VE), confirming enhanced adherence to underlying physical laws.
- Ablation studies demonstrated that each component, including static and dynamic knowledge, spatial and frequency domain modeling, and the quaternion network, contributes critically to improving scientific plausibility and accuracy.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Latent scientific knowledge, decomposed into static and dynamic components and extracted from unannotated data, can effectively guide video diffusion models for scientific generation.
> - A quaternion network-based projection, leveraging both spatial and frequency domain information, provides a robust method to fuse multimodal knowledge into pseudo-language prompts, overcoming the lack of explicit annotations.
> - Parameter-efficient fine-tuning (LoRA) enables the adaptation of large, pre-trained diffusion models to data-scarce scientific domains while maintaining scientific fidelity.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated at the intersection of generative AI, particularly video diffusion models, and scientific computing, addressing a critical gap in the application of state-of-the-art generative techniques to scientific domains.

**Evolution of Video Diffusion Models:** The paper begins by acknowledging the remarkable success of diffusion models, originally rooted in non-equilibrium thermodynamics, in various video generation tasks. It traces the lineage from early Video Diffusion Models (VDM) to more advanced architectures like Make-a-Video, Video LDM, Imagen Video, TAV, Stable Video Diffusion (SVD), and CogVideoX. These models have achieved impressive results in generating visually plausible natural scenes, demonstrating capabilities in text-to-video generation, temporal consistency, and high-resolution output. The authors rightly position their work as building upon these advancements, recognizing the power of these models as "general-purpose world simulators."

**The Scientific Domain Gap:** Despite their successes in natural scenes, the paper highlights a significant limitation: these models "struggle to generalize to scientific phenomena such as fluid simulations and meteorological processes, where underlying dynamics are governed by scientific laws." This struggle stems from several unique challenges inherent to scientific data:
1. **Severe Domain Gaps:** Scientific data often involves abstract representations, physical quantities (e.g., velocity fields, temperature, pressure), and distinct visual characteristics that differ greatly from the everyday images and videos typically used to train large diffusion models.
2. **Limited Training Data:** Unlike the vast, internet-scale datasets available for natural scene generation, scientific datasets are frequently small, curated, or generated through costly simulations or observations.
3. **Lack of Descriptive Language Annotations:** Scientific phenomena are often difficult to articulate precisely using natural language, making text-to-video generation—a common paradigm in modern diffusion models—infeasible or unreliable.
4. **Adherence to Scientific Laws:** The primary challenge is ensuring that generated videos not only look realistic but also faithfully adhere to the underlying physical laws and principles governing the phenomena. Generic diffusion models may produce visually appealing but physically inconsistent or impossible outcomes.

**Bridging the Gap: Knowledge-Guided Generation:** This paper directly addresses the aforementioned challenges by proposing a novel framework that moves "beyond traditional natural vision-based synthesis and toward latent knowledge-grounded scientific phenomena generation." It aims to imbue video diffusion models with "latent scientific phenomena knowledge" to overcome data scarcity, annotation limitations, and the critical need for scientific consistency. This approach represents a significant step towards developing AI models that can serve as reliable tools for scientific discovery, simulation, and prediction, rather than merely artistic or entertainment-focused generative tools. It pushes the boundary of generative AI from merely mimicking appearances to internalizing fundamental physical realities.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of this research is to enable video diffusion models to generate realistic, temporally consistent, and scientifically plausible videos of complex scientific phenomena, such as fluid dynamics simulations and meteorological processes, starting from just a single initial frame.

The motivations behind this objective are rooted in the inherent limitations of current video diffusion models when applied to scientific domains:

1. **Overcoming Domain Generalization Issues:** Modern video diffusion models, primarily trained on vast datasets of natural scenes, excel at creating visually compelling content within that domain. However, their performance degrades significantly when confronted with scientific phenomena, which possess distinct visual characteristics, underlying dynamics, and data distributions. The authors are motivated to bridge this "pronounced domain gap" to make these powerful generative models applicable to scientific research.
2. **Addressing Data Scarcity:** Scientific datasets are often inherently smaller and more challenging to acquire than common image/video datasets. Running extensive simulations or collecting real-world observational data can be resource-intensive and time-consuming. The ability to generate realistic scientific videos from a *single initial frame* is a direct response to this "data scarcity" challenge, enabling generation even when extensive sequential data for training is unavailable.
3. **Mitigating the Absence of Language Annotations:** Text-to-video generation is a dominant paradigm in diffusion models, relying on rich textual prompts for control. However, precisely describing complex scientific phenomena with natural language prompts is often "hard to articulate" or "infeasible." This paper is motivated to bypass the reliance on explicit language supervision by deriving "pseudo-language prompt embeddings" directly from visual data and latent scientific knowledge.
4. **Ensuring Scientific Consistency and Fidelity:** Perhaps the most critical motivation is to ensure that the generated videos adhere to the "underlying dynamics... governed by scientific laws." Unlike generating a natural scene where minor inconsistencies might be forgiven, scientific applications demand strict adherence to physical principles. The existing models often "struggle to capture generalizable scientific principles," leading to "unrealistic artifacts and hallucinations" that violate scientific plausibility. The authors aim to integrate "latent scientific phenomena knowledge" to guarantee that generated videos are not only visually convincing but also scientifically sound.
5. **Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning for Specialized Domains:** Given the goal of adapting large, pre-trained video diffusion models to niche scientific domains with limited data, the motivation also includes developing a "parameter-efficient fine-tuning" strategy. This approach minimizes the computational overhead and data requirements, making the adaptation process practical.

In essence, the paper seeks to transform video diffusion models from general-purpose visual synthesis tools into scientifically informed "world simulators" capable of understanding and extrapolating complex physical realities from minimal observational data.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework tackles the challenges of scientific video generation by integrating latent scientific knowledge into pre-trained video diffusion models via a two-stage pipeline: (1) Latent Knowledge Extraction and (2) Knowledge-Guided Generation.

#### 3.1. Latent Knowledge Extraction

Recognizing that scientific data constraints prohibit standard data augmentation techniques (which might violate physical laws), the authors decompose scientific knowledge into static and dynamic components, extracting them from raw, unannotated training videos:

- **Static Latent Scientific Phenomenon Knowledge:** This is extracted using a **Masked Autoencoder (MAE)**, specifically a Vision Transformer (ViT) architecture. Given an image, it's divided into non-overlapping patches. A subset of these patches (P_vis) is retained, while the rest (P_masked) are withheld. The MAE is trained to reconstruct the masked regions from the visible context. This forces the MAE to implicitly learn and encode the spatial relationships and governing scientific laws that dictate how phenomena look and behave in a static sense. The authors specifically choose MAE over augmentations like noise injection or rotation, which would be "inappropriate for scientific data" as they would "violate the underlying scientific laws."
- **Dynamic Latent Scientific Phenomenon Knowledge:** To capture temporal evolution and motion patterns, the framework utilizes an **Optical Flow Predictor (OFP)**, also based on a ViT backbone. This OFP is trained using pseudo ground-truth optical flows, which are computed via the Farnebäck algorithm. The OFP learns to estimate the flow vectors between consecutive frames. Once trained, both the MAE and OFP are frozen. For a given initial frame `f_0`, their decoder outputs are used to extract static (`E_M`) and dynamic (`E_O`) knowledge embeddings.

#### 3.2. Knowledge-Guided Generation

The extracted latent scientific knowledge is then used to guide a pre-trained video diffusion model (e.g., SVD or CogVideoX) to generate scientifically plausible videos.

- **Initial Frame Encoding:** The single initial frame `f_0` is first encoded by a pre-trained VAE (Variational Autoencoder) to obtain conditional features (`E_c`) and by a CLIP (Contrastive Language-Image Pre-training) image encoder to obtain visual features (`F_o`).
- **Pseudo-Language Prompt Generation:** This is a core novel contribution, designed to overcome the lack of descriptive language annotations for scientific phenomena. Instead of relying on a text encoder, the framework leverages CLIP’s inherent robust vision-language alignment to generate "pseudo-language prompt embeddings" directly from visual features and extracted scientific knowledge.
 - **Quaternion Network-based Projection:** Inspired by prior work showing the effectiveness of quaternion networks (QNNs) in modeling multimodal relationships within CLIP, the authors adopt QNNs for this projection.
 - First, linear layers project `F_o`, `E_M`, and `E_O` into compatible feature spaces (`ˆF_o`, `ˆE_M`, `ˆE_O`).
 - Learnable text embeddings (`T_L`) are initialized. These four components (text, vision, static knowledge, dynamic knowledge) are then organized along the orthogonal axes of a quaternion latent space to form `Q_l = T_L + ˆF_o i + ˆE_M j + ˆE_O k`. QNNs are used because they inherently model multi-dimensional correlations and preserve geometric structure, which is vital for spatially aligned multimodal fusion.
 - **Spatial and Frequency Domain Modeling:** Crucially, the authors recognize that "frequency-domain information is key in representing scientific phenomena." Therefore, this quaternion formulation is extended to the frequency domain by applying Fast Fourier Transform (FFT) to the visual and knowledge embeddings, creating `Q_FFT_l`.
 - Separate quaternion networks (`Q_t` and `Q_FFT_t`) process `Q_l` and `Q_FFT_l` to generate spatial (`E_S_L`) and frequency (`E_F_L`) pseudo-language prompt embeddings, respectively.
 - Finally, a fusion network combines `E_S_L` and `E_F_L` to form the ultimate pseudo-language prompt embedding (`E_L`).
- **Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning:** The generated `E_L` embeddings are injected into the cross-attention layers of the pre-trained UNet backbone of the video diffusion model. This integration is performed using **LoRA (Low-Rank Adaptation)**. LoRA introduces small, trainable low-rank matrices alongside the frozen pre-trained weights of the attention projection matrices (Q, K, V), significantly reducing the number of parameters to fine-tune. This makes the adaptation efficient and effective even with limited scientific training data.
- **Video Generation:** During training, all frames of a target video are encoded into latents, noised, and the UNet learns to denoise them, guided by `E_c` and `E_L`. At inference, `f_0` is used to derive `E_M`, `E_O`, and subsequently `E_L`, which then guides the DDIM (Denoising Diffusion Implicit Models) sampler to generate the subsequent frames of the scientifically plausible video.

This comprehensive approach allows the model to leverage rich visual features from existing pre-trained models while explicitly grounding its generation process in extracted scientific principles, both static and dynamic, and across different representational domains (spatial and frequency), all without relying on explicit language annotations.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The paper presents extensive experiments to validate its approach, utilizing both quantitative metrics and qualitative visual comparisons on diverse scientific phenomena.

#### 4.1. Experimental Setup

- **Baselines:** The method is compared against several advanced video generation baselines: the raw Stable Video Diffusion (SVD), CogVideoX, LoRA-fine-tuned versions of SVD and CogVideoX (LoRA(SVD), LoRA(CogX)), Tune-A-Video (TAV), and Simple Diffusion Adapter (SimDA).
- **Datasets:**
 - **Simulated Fluid Dynamics:** Four computational fluid dynamics (CFD) scenarios: Rayleigh-Bénard Convection (RBC), Cylinder flow, DamBreak, and DepthCharge. These represent diverse fluid behaviors governed by specific physical laws.
 - **Real-world Typhoon Observations:** Four distinct typhoon events from the Digital Typhoon dataset (202001, 202009, 202102, 202204), representing complex meteorological processes.
 - For each phenomenon, 9 videos were used for fine-tuning and 1 for testing, reflecting a data-constrained scenario.
- **Evaluation Metrics:** A comprehensive set of eight metrics was employed:
 - **Numerical Accuracy:** RMSE (Root Mean Squared Error) and SSIM (Structural Similarity Index Measure) measure pixel-level and structural similarity.
 - **Physics-aware/Scientific Fidelity:** SFE (Stream Function Error), SE (Smoothness Error), GS (Gradient Smoothness), CS (Continuity Score), QCE (Q-Criterion Error), and VE (Vorticity Error). These metrics assess adherence to fluid dynamics principles like streamline accuracy, temporal smoothness, spatial-temporal consistency, vortex preservation, and rotational dynamics. Notably, QCE and VE are specific to incompressible 2D flow fields found in simulation data. Lower values are better for all metrics except SSIM.

#### 4.2. Qualitative Evaluation

Visual comparisons vividly demonstrate the superiority of the proposed method:
- **Baseline Model Failures:** Standard diffusion models (like SVD) without explicit latent scientific knowledge, even after fine-tuning, often generated "unrealistic artifacts and hallucinations." For instance, in DamBreak simulations, they produced physically incorrect fluid behaviors (e.g., unnatural stillness, upward motion). For typhoons, they generated "implausible distortions," such as transforming typhoon clouds into textures resembling "snow-covered mountains or leather."
- **Our Method's Success:** In contrast, the authors' approach, "guided by latent scientific knowledge," consistently produced phenomena that "align better with underlying scientific principles." The generated videos were "temporally coherent sequences that reflect physically meaningful progression," showcasing a more accurate interpretation of the initial frame and adherence to scientific plausibility.

#### 4.3. Quantitative Evaluation

The quantitative results, summarized in Table 1, strongly support the qualitative observations:
- **Overall Outperformance:** The proposed method (Ours(SVD) and Ours(CogX)) achieved superior performance across most scenarios and metrics compared to all baselines.
- **Significant Improvement in Scientific Metrics:** Particularly noteworthy were the substantial improvements, often by "an order of magnitude," in scientific metrics like SFE, SE, QCE, and VE on the fluid simulation datasets. This is crucial as it directly confirms the model's enhanced ability to generate sequences that "better conform to fluid dynamics" and other physical laws.
- **Strong Performance on Real-world Data:** On the more complex real-world typhoon dataset, the model consistently delivered state-of-the-art results across nearly all metrics, validating its effectiveness beyond controlled simulations.
- **One Limitation (Continuity Score):** The only exception was a slightly weaker performance on the CS (Continuity Score) metric for typhoon data. The authors attribute this to "residual bias from pre-trained diffusion models toward nature-image coherence" that might not be fully corrected with limited scientific data fine-tuning, indicating a potential area for future research.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies

Ablation studies on the Cylinder fluid simulation dataset systematically evaluated the contribution of each component (Table 2):
- **Essential Components:** Removing static physical phenomenon knowledge (w/o SPK), dynamic physical phenomenon knowledge (w/o DPK), spatial domain modeling (w/o SDM), frequency domain modeling (w/o FDM), or the quaternion network (w/o QN) consistently degraded performance across all metrics. This confirms that each proposed component plays a "critical role" in enhancing scientific plausibility and accuracy.
- **Joint Modeling:** The results underscore that incorporating both static and dynamic priors, and modeling representations in both spatial and frequency domains, significantly improves the model's ability to generate sequences consistent with fundamental laws of motion.
- **Quaternion Network Efficacy:** Table 4 further highlights that the quaternion network-based projection yields superior performance compared to linear networks or cross-attention mechanisms for generating pseudo-language prompt embeddings, demonstrating its effectiveness in capturing complex cross-domain semantics.
- **LoRA Rank Sensitivity:** The study also showed that the LoRA rank is a sensitive hyperparameter, emphasizing the need for careful selection to achieve optimal results (Table 3).

In summary, the main findings clearly demonstrate that integrating latent scientific knowledge, extracted via MAE and optical flow, and channeled through a novel quaternion-based spatial-frequency projection into a video diffusion model (fine-tuned with LoRA), significantly enhances the generation of scientifically plausible and accurate videos across diverse simulated and real-world scientific phenomena.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research marks a substantial advancement in the field of generative AI, particularly in its application to scientific and engineering domains. Its significance and potential impact span several key areas:

#### 5.1. Scientific Contribution and Bridging the Gap

The primary significance lies in presenting the "first framework for teaching video diffusion models to generate more scientifically plausible phenomena" under realistic constraints (limited data, no language annotations). This work effectively bridges the gap between the visually compelling but often physically inconsistent outputs of general-purpose video diffusion models and the stringent requirements of scientific fidelity. By embedding "latent scientific phenomenon knowledge," the model moves beyond superficial realism to internalize and adhere to fundamental physical laws, a crucial step for AI to function as a reliable "world simulator." This represents a paradigm shift from purely data-driven visual synthesis to knowledge-guided, physics-informed generation.

#### 5.2. Enabling New Scientific Research and Discovery

The ability to generate scientifically accurate videos from minimal input (a single initial frame) has profound implications for various scientific fields:
- **Accelerated Simulation and Prediction:** Researchers can generate plausible evolutions of complex phenomena (e.g., fluid flows, weather patterns, material transformations) without needing to run computationally expensive simulations from scratch or wait for real-world observations. This can accelerate hypothesis testing and predictive modeling.
- **Exploration of "What-if" Scenarios:** The model could be used to explore various potential outcomes or behaviors of a system under different initial conditions, aiding in understanding complex dynamics that might be difficult to observe or simulate traditionally.
- **Data Augmentation:** In fields with scarce experimental or observational data, this method can generate synthetic yet scientifically consistent data, expanding training sets for other downstream AI models (e.g., for classification, forecasting, or anomaly detection).
- **Complex System Understanding:** By generating accurate temporal sequences, researchers can gain deeper insights into the progression and interactions within complex systems like atmospheric dynamics, ocean currents, or chemical reactions.

#### 5.3. Practical Applications in Engineering and Education

Beyond fundamental research, the framework holds practical value:
- **Engineering Design and Prototyping:** Engineers could quickly visualize and test design iterations for systems involving fluid dynamics (e.g., aerospace, automotive, civil engineering) or material science without extensive physical prototyping or simulation time.
- **Meteorology and Climate Modeling:** Generating more accurate short-term weather forecasts or simulating long-term climate scenarios could be enhanced. The improved fidelity in typhoon generation is a direct demonstration of this potential.
- **Education and Training:** Realistic, dynamic visualizations of scientific principles can significantly enhance learning experiences for students, allowing them to observe and interact with phenomena that are otherwise abstract or inaccessible.

#### 5.4. Advancing Trustworthy AI

By explicitly building in mechanisms for scientific consistency, this work contributes to the development of more trustworthy AI systems. In high-stakes applications where physical accuracy is paramount (e.g., medical imaging, autonomous systems, critical infrastructure monitoring), models that adhere to known physical laws are more reliable and dependable. This reduces the risk of AI-generated content leading to incorrect conclusions or hazardous decisions.

#### 5.5. Future Research Directions

The identified limitation regarding the Continuity Score on real-world typhoon data suggests fertile ground for future research. Further efforts could focus on:
- Developing more sophisticated techniques to reduce the "residual bias" from natural-image pre-training.
- Exploring adaptive or curriculum learning strategies to better integrate scientific priors.
- Extending the framework to a broader range of scientific phenomena, including those in 3D, multi-physics simulations, or with different governing equations.
- Investigating real-time or interactive generation capabilities for more dynamic exploration.

In conclusion, this paper presents a significant stride towards making generative AI a powerful and reliable tool for scientific inquiry. By thoughtfully integrating latent scientific knowledge into state-of-the-art video diffusion models, the authors have laid a foundation for AI systems that can not only generate visually compelling content but also adhere to the fundamental laws governing our physical world, thereby opening new avenues for research, innovation, and understanding.
