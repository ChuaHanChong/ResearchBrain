# BIFROST: Bridging Invariant Feature Representation for Observation-space Sim2Real Transfer

> [!summary] Summary
> BIFROST develops a framework for zero-shot sim2real policy transfer by learning a shared latent representation from raw observations, which aligns behavioral equivalence across simulation and reality. This approach enabled higher success rates and lower errors in visual navigation, contact-rich manipulation, and visual servoing tasks compared to baseline methods like domain adaptation and co-training.

## Problem

- The 'sim2real gap' hinders deploying simulation-trained policies on physical robots due to discrepancies in perception and dynamics.
- Existing methods often address perceptual and dynamics gaps in isolation, or require perfect simulator fidelity/true state access, limiting their robustness to compounded differences.
- Achieving effective zero-shot transfer from raw observations in partially observable settings remains a significant challenge.

## Method

- **BIFROST** trains a ==shared history encoder== (a ==GRU==) using paired offline data from simulation and reality, mapping observation-action sequences into a common latent space.
- The representation learning objective combines ==reward prediction==, ==latent dynamics prediction==, and a ==cross-domain alignment loss (Wasserstein-1 distance)==, derived from the ==Generalized Bisimulation Metric==, to ensure behavioral equivalence.
- A control policy is trained in the source domain (via ==Soft Actor-Critic==) using this frozen, shared latent space and then deployed directly in the target domain for ==zero-shot transfer==.

## Results

- BIFROST achieved a **0.50 ± 0.08** success rate in cross-simulator egocentric visual navigation, outperforming BDA (**0.34 ± 0.05**) under combined visual and dynamics gaps.
- In sim2real tabletop manipulation, BIFROST demonstrated a **0.50 ± 0.07** success rate for pushing and a **7.64 ± 0.33 degree** joint error for visual servoing, consistently surpassing baseline methods.
- Ablation studies confirmed the complementary roles of reward prediction and latent dynamics prediction in anchoring the latent space and providing dynamic structure for robust cross-domain alignment.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Cross-domain bisimulation alignment, applied in a learned latent space from observation histories, can effectively unify the treatment of both perceptual and dynamics gaps.
> - Supervising the latent representation with target-domain reward prediction is crucial to prevent overfitting to source-domain features and ensure task relevance.
> - Using the Wasserstein-1 dual for cross-domain alignment provides a more stable and effective alignment signal during training compared to other optimal transport metrics.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Modern robot learning frequently leverages large-scale training in simulated environments due to the inherent costs, time consumption, and operational risks associated with real-world data collection. However, a significant challenge in deploying simulation-trained policies on physical robots is the "sim2real gap," which arises from discrepancies between simulated and real-world conditions. This gap typically manifests in two primary forms: the perceptual gap, where rendered images in simulation differ from real camera images, and the dynamics gap, where physics engines imperfectly approximate real-world contact, friction, and actuation.

The existing research landscape for addressing the sim2real gap can be broadly categorized into several strategies:
- **Simulator Calibration/System Identification:** Methods aim to reduce the simulator-reality mismatch by calibrating physical parameters or modeling complex dynamics, often using real-world data.
- **Domain Randomization (DR):** This approach trains policies to be robust to variations by randomizing simulator parameters (dynamics, visuals, or both) during training, thereby tolerating rather than eliminating mismatch.
- **Privileged Information/Teacher-Student Frameworks:** These methods exploit the availability of ground-truth state information in simulation, which is unavailable in reality. A "teacher" policy trained with privileged information then distills its knowledge into a "student" policy that operates on partial, real-world-like observations.
- **Domain Adaptation:** Techniques that attempt to align the visual or dynamic characteristics of the simulator with reality, often through adversarial training or image translation.

While these strategies have advanced sim2real transfer, they frequently address the perceptual and dynamics gaps in isolation. When both gaps coexist, these methods often require composition or layering, which may not account for the interactions between gap sources.

A complementary perspective, which forms the theoretical foundation for this work, involves "behavioral equivalence" and "state abstraction." This line of research, rooted in concepts like probabilistic bisimulation and bisimulation metrics, seeks to identify control-relevant invariances that remain stable across domain differences. Such approaches abstract away irrelevant details, allowing for policy transfer without requiring perfect simulator fidelity. While single-MDP bisimulation metrics have been established, cross-domain formulations, such as the Generalized Bisimulation Metric (GBSM), are more recent but typically assume access to underlying true states. Concurrently, empirical and theoretical work on co-training shared representations across simulation and reality has shown promise in reducing real-world data requirements when underlying structure is shared.

BIFROST positions itself by integrating these latter two perspectives. It extends bisimulation-based behavioral equivalence from the unobservable state space to a learned latent space derived from observation histories. By leveraging co-training on paired cross-domain data, BIFROST aims to unify the treatment of both perceptual and dynamics gaps within a single representation learning framework, specifically designed for partially observable settings. This places it in contrast to methods that tackle each gap separately or require full state access.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation for this research stems from the observation that despite domain-specific differences in rendering and physics, a fundamental shared structure exists between a task performed in simulation and in reality. This shared structure implies that equivalent actions from equivalent configurations should ideally lead to equivalent long-term outcomes, regardless of the domain. The paper posits that identifying and exploiting this shared structure from raw observations is crucial for achieving effective sim2real transfer, particularly zero-shot transfer where policies trained in simulation are directly deployed in reality without further adaptation.

This understanding directly leads to the central question of the work: **"How can we learn a shared representation from raw observations that enables zero-shot policy transfer across domains?"**

To address this question, the authors are motivated by the concept of defining cross-domain similarity through behavioral equivalence. This principle suggests that two states, even from different domains, are similar if identical actions taken from them lead to equivalent long-term outcomes. While the Generalized Bisimulation Metric (GBSM) provides a theoretical foundation for quantifying such cross-domain state similarity by comparing reward equivalence and transition equivalence, it relies on direct access to the true states in both domains. Existing methods that adapt bisimulation for representation learning from observations (e.g., DBC, MICo) are generally restricted to single-MDP contexts.

The key insight driving BIFROST is that by performing cross-domain bisimulation alignment within a *learned latent space*, derived from observation histories, both perceptual and dynamics gaps can be unified into a single representation learning problem. This approach negates the need for true state access and avoids the complexity of decomposing and individually adapting to each gap source.

The specific objectives of this work are two-fold:
1. **Introduce cross-domain bisimulation alignment as a representation learning mechanism:** The goal is to develop a method that addresses both perceptual and dynamics gaps simultaneously, without requiring access to true states in either domain. This mechanism aims to map observation-action sequences from different domains, which lead to behaviorally equivalent outcomes, to nearby latent states.
2. **Validate the framework empirically:** The authors aim to provide empirical evidence that BIFROST achieves effective zero-shot transfer on various tasks, including sim2sim visual navigation and sim2real contact-rich manipulation and visual servoing. This validation is designed to demonstrate BIFROST's efficacy in scenarios where existing domain adaptation and co-training baselines are insufficient, particularly under compounded domain gaps.

In summary, the core motivation is to learn a universally applicable, behaviorally equivalent latent representation from raw observations that transcends domain differences, thereby enabling robust zero-shot policy transfer from simulation to reality.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

BIFROST (Bridging Invariant Feature Representation for Observation-space Sim2Real Transfer) operates through a two-phase pipeline designed for zero-shot sim2real transfer from raw observations in Partially Observable Markov Decision Processes (POMDPs).

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation

The sim2real setting is formalized using two POMDPs: a source domain (simulation, Msrc) and a target domain (real world, Mtgt). These share the same action space (A) and discount factor (γ), but differ in transition dynamics (Psrc ≠ Ptgt, dynamics gap) and sensing functions (ov,src ≠ ov,tgt for the same underlying state, perceptual gap). Since the true state (s) is not fully recoverable from a single observation (o), the agent must condition on an observation-action history (ht) which is compressed into a low-dimensional latent state (zt = ϕ(ht)) by a shared history encoder (ϕ).

#### 3.2. Phase 1: Cross-Domain Representation Learning

In the initial phase, a shared history encoder ϕ is trained using paired offline data collected from both the source and target domains. The objective is to map observation-action sequences from both domains into a common latent space such that histories yielding similar rewards and similar latent successor distributions are positioned nearby, irrespective of their domain origin. The encoder is implemented as a Gated Recurrent Unit (GRU) network that processes the history `h_t = (o_0, a_0, ..., o_t)` to output a latent state `z_t = ϕ(h_t)`.

This training is driven by three loss components, conceptually derived from the Generalized Bisimulation Metric (GBSM) but adapted for partially observable settings and learned latent spaces:

1. **Reward Prediction Loss (L_reward):** A reward predictor `b_R(Z × A → R)` is trained to estimate the *target-domain reward* (`r_tgt`) from latent states encoded from *both* source and target histories. This loss supervises both source and target latent representations to prioritize features predictive of real-world task outcomes, ensuring task relevance in the shared latent space.
 `L_reward = E[(b_R(ϕ(h_src), a) - r_tgt)^2 + (b_R(ϕ(h_tgt), a) - r_tgt)^2]`

2. **Latent Dynamics Prediction (L_LDP):** A latent forward model `b_P(Z × A → ∆(Z))` is parameterized as a conditional Gaussian `N(μ(z, a), σ^2(z, a)I)`. This loss trains `b_P` to predict the next latent state within each domain, thus enforcing dynamic coherence between successive latent states produced by the encoder.
 `L_LDP = E[||μ(z_i, a_i) - z_{i+1}||^2 / (2σ^2(z_i, a_i)) + (d_z / 2) ln σ^2(z_i, a_i)]`

3. **Cross-Domain Alignment Loss (L_align):** This loss minimizes the Wasserstein-1 (W1) distance between the predicted latent successor distributions of *paired* source and target histories. This is the core mechanism for cross-domain alignment. The W1 distance is approximated via the Kantorovich-Rubinstein duality using a learned 1-Lipschitz critic (`f_ψ`), ensuring that the distributions of subsequent latent states are aligned across domains.
 `L_align = E[W_1(b_P(· | ϕ(h_src), a), b_P(· | ϕ(h_tgt), a))]`

The total representation learning objective is a weighted sum of these three components: `L = L_reward + λ_LDP L_LDP + λ_align L_align`.

#### 3.3. Paired Trajectory Collection

Learning cross-domain representations necessitates correspondences between observations. This is achieved through a specific data collection procedure (Algorithm 1):
- A dataset `D_tgt` is first collected in the target domain.
- For each trajectory `τ` in `D_tgt`, `n_τ` segments of length `l` are sampled.
- For each segment, the simulator (`M_src`) is initialized with the *observable configuration* (`ξ_i`) extracted from the target observation (`o_tgt_i`) at the segment's start (e.g., joint angles, object poses).
- Unobservable state variables (e.g., friction coefficients) are set to default values.
- The exact action sequence recorded from the target trajectory is then replayed in the simulator.
- This generates a paired dataset `D_paired = {(o_src_k, o_tgt_k, a_k, r_tgt_k)}` where `k` indexes corresponding timesteps. This process ensures that any subsequent divergence in observations is attributable to domain-specific discrepancies (rendering, physics) rather than differences in initial task-relevant states or actions.

#### 3.4. Phase 2: Policy Learning and Deployment

Once the encoder ϕ and the reward predictor `b_R` are trained, their parameters are frozen. A control policy `π(Z → A)` is then trained via online reinforcement learning (specifically, Soft Actor-Critic, SAC) within the source domain `M_src`. During this phase, the policy receives `z_t = ϕ(h_t)` as its state input and uses `b_R(z_t, a_t)` as its reward signal. The policy is thus trained entirely within the shared latent space, without direct exposure to domain-specific raw observations or true rewards.

At deployment in the target domain (`M_tgt`), the same frozen encoder ϕ maps target-domain histories into this established latent space. The policy then acts based on these latent states, enabling zero-shot transfer without any further adaptation or fine-tuning on real-world data.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

BIFROST was evaluated against several baselines in both sim2sim and sim2real transfer scenarios, with an additional ablation study. The baselines included Direct Transfer, Target-Only, BDA (Bi-directional Domain Adaptation), Co-Training (Behavioral Cloning), and Co-Training (Offline RL with IQL). The evaluation focused on zero-shot performance in the target domain.

#### 4.1. A. Sim2Sim Transfer: Visual Navigation

Two visual navigation tasks in maze environments were used, each with a geodesic-distance-based dense reward and partial observability.
- **Cross-Fidelity Transfer under Top-Down Observation:**
 - **Setup:** Source: OpenCV 2D maze with direct velocity commands. Target: MuJoCo 3D maze with PD-controlled point mass and overhead camera. Both used 64x64 RGB crops and target goal RGB vector.
 - **Challenge:** Extreme visual gap (flat shapes vs. 3D shaded surfaces); small dynamics gap.
 - **Results (Table I):** BIFROST achieved a success rate of 0.68 ± 0.08, comparable to BDA (0.67 ± 0.05). This suggests that for a dominant visual gap and minimal dynamics gap, a strong visual adaptor (like BDA) can perform similarly. Co-Training (Offline RL) (0.63 ± 0.08) and (BC) (0.59 ± 0.08) improved over Target-Only (0.46 ± 0.09). Direct Transfer was 0.19 ± 0.04.
- **Cross-Simulator Transfer under Egocentric Observation:**
 - **Setup:** Source: SAPIEN environment. Target: MuJoCo environment. Both used a Hello Robot Stretch with an onboard forward-facing camera, providing 64x64 RGB-D image, target goal RGB, and body-frame velocity.
 - **Challenge:** Compound visual and dynamics gaps (different simulator backends, simplified kinematics vs. full rigid-body dynamics), and stronger partial observability requiring spatial memory.
 - **Results (Table I):** BIFROST demonstrated a substantial advantage with 0.50 ± 0.08 success, outperforming BDA (0.34 ± 0.05). This indicates that bisimulation-based alignment is more effective when both visual and dynamics gaps are significant and coupled. Co-Training (BC) (0.37 ± 0.07) showed improvement over Target-Only (0.16 ± 0.07), but Co-Training (Offline RL) (0.17 ± 0.14) became unstable, yielding results comparable to Target-Only and highlighting the difficulty of Q-function bootstrapping across mismatched domains. Direct Transfer was near zero (0.03 ± 0.02), confirming severe domain discrepancy.
- **Qualitative Visualization (Figure 3):** t-SNE embeddings showed that while ImageNet-pretrained ResNet18 features failed to bridge the cross-simulator gap (producing domain-separated clusters), BIFROST's encoder successfully collapsed this separation. The latent space organized by goal identity rather than domain origin, confirming the preservation of task-relevant structure.

#### 4.2. B. Sim2Real Transfer: Tabletop Manipulation

Two sim2real experiments used a SO-101 manipulator arm, with SAPIEN as the source and the physical robot with an overhead 128x128 RGB camera as the target.
- **Push T:**
 - **Setup:** Pushing a T-shaped block to a target pose. Observations: overhead RGB and robot joint angles. Actions: 2D end-effector position.
 - **Challenge:** Differences in friction, contact dynamics, and actuation noise of the low-cost arm.
 - **Results (Table II):** BIFROST achieved the highest success rate (0.50 ± 0.07), outperforming BDA (0.42 ± 0.09). Co-Training (BC) (0.33 ± 0.08) showed marginal improvement over Target-Only (0.25 ± 0.08). Co-Training (Offline RL) (0.28 ± 0.12) again degraded.
- **Visual Servoing:**
 - **Setup:** Moving the arm to a target configuration specified by a reference image. Observations: concatenated current and target overhead views (no proprioceptive). Actions: joint position command.
 - **Challenge:** Dominant dynamics gap from unmodeled friction, backlash, and compliance in physical servo actuators.
 - **Results (Table II):** BIFROST yielded the lowest joint error (7.64 ± 0.33 deg), reducing it by 0.55 degrees compared to BDA (8.19 ± 0.75 deg). Co-Training (Offline RL) produced the highest error (11.79 ± 1.40 deg), performing worse than Direct Transfer (10.41 ± 1.78).
- **Overall Sim2Real:** BIFROST consistently demonstrated the best performance with lowest variance across seeds on both tasks. The consistent advantage suggests the alignment mechanism generalizes across different types of domain gaps (contact dynamics, actuator modeling).

#### 4.3. C. Ablation Study (Egocentric Visual Navigation)

- **Component-wise Contribution (Table III):**
 - **w/o Reward (L_reward):** Removed shared target-reward prediction. Resulted in higher source return (0.5144 ± 0.2138) but lower target return (0.2136 ± 0.1420) and elevated variance. This suggested the encoder overfit to source-domain features without target-reward supervision.
 - **w/o Latent Dynamics Prediction (L_LDP):** Omitted the self-predictive loss. Showed graceful degradation (target return 0.2315 ± 0.1204). The reward predictor still grounded the latent space, but weakened dynamics prediction reduced the structure for `L_align`.
 - **Full BIFROST:** Achieved the highest target return (0.2511 ± 0.0951) with the lowest variance, indicating the complementary roles of `L_reward` (anchoring to target outcomes, preventing source overfitting) and `L_LDP` (providing dynamic structure for alignment).
- **Choice of Optimal Transport Metric for L_align (Table IV):**
 - **MSE-Alignment:** Used Mean Squared Error between mean vectors of successor distributions. Yielded highest source return but lowest target return (0.1263 ± 0.1099), characteristic of source-domain overfitting due to unconstrained variance parameters.
 - **W2-Closed-Form:** Used Wasserstein-2 distance between diagonal Gaussians, penalizing differences in both means and standard deviations. Improved target return to 0.2058 ± 0.1362.
 - **BIFROST (W1 Dual):** Using Wasserstein-1 distance. Produced the highest target return (0.2511 ± 0.0951) with lower cross-seed variance. The W1 dual formulation provided a more sustained alignment signal during training as its gradient does not diminish upon convergence of distributions.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The BIFROST framework introduces a method for sim2real transfer that addresses a central challenge in robot learning: the compounded nature of perceptual and dynamics gaps between simulation and reality. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Unified Gap Resolution:** BIFROST unifies the treatment of both perceptual and dynamics gaps within a single representation learning problem. Unlike many existing methods that require separate adaptation modules or layered approaches for each gap type, BIFROST's cross-domain bisimulation alignment mechanism operates on behavioral consequences in a learned latent space, making it potentially more robust to complex and interacting discrepancies.
2. **Zero-Shot Policy Transfer from Raw Observations:** The framework enables zero-shot transfer of policies trained exclusively in simulation to physical robots, using only raw observations. This bypasses the need for costly and time-consuming real-world data collection for fine-tuning policies, significantly enhancing the practical utility of simulation-based training.
3. **Leveraging Behavioral Equivalence in Latent Space:** By extending the theoretical foundation of bisimulation metrics to learned latent spaces derived from observation histories, BIFROST provides a principled approach to identifying and exploiting shared task-relevant structures across domains, even in partially observable settings where true state access is unavailable.
4. **Empirical Validation Across Diverse Tasks:** The empirical results on both sim2sim visual navigation (with extreme visual and compound gaps) and sim2real contact-rich manipulation and visual servoing tasks demonstrate the effectiveness and generality of BIFROST. The consistent outperformance of BIFROST over various baselines, especially under severe and uncharacterized domain gaps (such as unmodeled friction, backlash, and contact compliance in physical robots), suggests its practical applicability.
5. **Addressing Limitations of Co-Training and Domain Adaptation:** The findings indicate that simple co-training (especially with offline RL methods) can be unstable or ineffective when domain distributions diverge significantly, and even dedicated domain adaptation methods may struggle with compound gaps. BIFROST's explicit alignment objective addresses these limitations, providing more stable and effective transfer.

The potential impact of BIFROST lies in its capacity to accelerate the development and deployment of robot learning systems. By reducing the dependency on extensive real-world data and highly accurate simulators, it could facilitate broader application of reinforcement learning in robotics, particularly for tasks where collecting real-world data is prohibitive. Future work, particularly in improving the robustness of paired data collection to dynamics discontinuities, could further expand its applicability.
