# STRNet: Visual Navigation with Spatio-Temporal Representation through Dynamic Graph Aggregation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Sun Yat-sen University, Insta360 Research, and Wuhan University developed STRNet, a spatio-temporal representation framework for visual navigation that integrates dynamic graph aggregation for spatial understanding and a lightweight temporal fusion module. This method achieves high success rates and fewer collisions in simulated and real-world environments by providing richer visual encodings to the navigation policy.

## Problem

- Learning-based visual navigation systems often rely on generic visual encoders that lose fine-grained spatial and temporal structural information critical for robust robotic control.
- Conventional encoders struggle to capture irregular spatial layouts and subtle motion cues, leading to navigation failures like oscillations, stalls, or collisions.
- Existing policy-focused methods frequently use simplistic temporal pooling, resulting in impoverished representations that limit accurate action prediction and progress estimation.

## Method

- **STRNet** proposes a unified ==spatio-temporal representation== framework that enhances visual encoding by explicitly capturing spatial geometry and temporal dynamics from image sequences.
- A ==graph-based spatial aggregation module== dynamically constructs graphs from per-frame features, using ==soft edge weights== and ==multi-scale contrast enhancement== to capture irregular spatial layouts.
- A ==temporal fusion module== combines ==hybrid temporal shifting== with ==multi-resolution difference-aware convolution== to efficiently inject motion cues and integrate temporal dynamics into the representation.

## Results

- **STRNet** achieved **100%** success rates with zero collisions in basic and adaptation tasks across simulated indoor and outdoor environments (2D-3D-S, Citysim), surpassing baselines.
- It demonstrated superior robustness in long-range tasks (**98%** success rate, **0.02** collisions) and under noisy sub-target selection (**84%** success rate) compared to state-of-the-art methods.
- Ablation studies confirmed that both the spatial aggregation and temporal fusion modules are essential and complementary, with their combined removal causing severe performance collapse, and the system maintained real-time inference speed with fewer parameters than most baselines.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Effective visual navigation requires a foundational visual encoding that unifies reasoning about irregular spatial layouts and temporally causal events, rather than just advanced policy heads.
> - Dynamic graph aggregation can effectively model fine-grained spatial geometry and semantic topological relationships from visual observations, overcoming limitations of grid-based representations.
> - A lightweight combination of temporal shifting and difference-aware convolution can efficiently capture motion cues, preventing loss of crucial temporal context that differentiates navigation states.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Visual navigation, a fundamental capability for autonomous mobile robots, involves guiding a robot to a specified visual goal based on a sequence of first-person observations. Historically, this field has progressed from traditional modular pipelines, which separated tasks like visual mapping, localization, and path planning, to modern learning-based, end-to-end approaches. Traditional methods, while relying on geometric principles, often faced issues with cumulative error over long horizons.

Recent advancements in learning-based visual navigation have largely concentrated on improving decision-making modules, such as goal-conditioned policies, behavior cloning, or instruction-following frameworks. Examples include ViNT, NoMaD, and NaviBridger, which leverage techniques like diffusion policies and topological memory for action generation and long-range planning. These methods have demonstrated considerable progress in enabling robots to predict actions directly from visual input, bypassing the need for explicit mapping or localization.

However, a recurring observation in the literature is that these advanced policy heads frequently rely on visual encoders originally designed for generic computer vision or video understanding tasks. These encoders, typically ImageNet-pretrained Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) followed by simple temporal pooling (e.g., average pooling), are often inadequate for the precise, fine-grained control decisions demanded by robotic navigation. This inadequacy can result in the loss of critical geometric and motion cues, manifesting as navigation failures such as oscillations, stalls, or collisions in real-world deployments. The provided t-SNE visualization (Figure 1a) supports this, showing entangled feature embeddings from conventional temporal pooling.

The current work positions itself to address this identified gap by focusing on the quality and structure of encoded visual representations, rather than solely on downstream decision-making. It acknowledges that effective visual navigation requires simultaneous reasoning about irregular spatial layouts and temporally causal events, a challenge that simple pooling or permutation-invariant self-attention mechanisms often fail to capture. The paper highlights that current encoders struggle to unify scene geometry and motion encoding, leading to impoverished representations that limit accurate action prediction and progress estimation. By proposing a unified spatio-temporal representation framework, this research aims to enhance the foundational visual encoding, thereby providing richer, more discriminative features to improve the performance and robustness of goal-conditioned visual navigation systems. This approach aligns with the broader field's growing interest in more sophisticated spatio-temporal representation learning techniques, including graph-based methods and attention mechanisms, but applies them specifically to the context of robotic navigation to address its unique requirements.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from an identified limitation in current learning-based visual navigation systems. While significant progress has been made in developing advanced policy heads and decision-making strategies, these methods frequently rely on conventional visual encoders that employ simplistic feature extraction and temporal pooling mechanisms. The authors argue that such encoders, often borrowed from generic computer vision or video understanding tasks, are ill-suited for the demands of robotic navigation. Specifically, they tend to lose fine-grained spatial and temporal structural information, which is critical for accurate action prediction and reliable progress estimation in dynamic and complex environments.

The paper highlights that this loss of information manifests in several ways:
1. **Loss of Fine-grained Spatial Structure:** Traditional encoders, by treating visual data as regular grids or sequences, may fail to capture the irregular, semantically meaningful spatial layouts encountered in real-world environments (e.g., distinguishing doorways from walls, or recognizing the topology of corridors and obstacles). This can lead to suboptimal behaviors such as jittering, stalling, or collisions.
2. **Loss of Fine-grained Temporal Dynamics:** Simple temporal pooling or recurrent networks can smooth away subtle optical flow signals or motion cues that differentiate crucial navigation states (e.g., approaching a goal versus moving sideways). This can result in hesitation, erratic trajectories, or an inability to accurately assess progress towards a goal. The t-SNE visualization in Figure 1a, depicting entangled feature embeddings, illustrates this issue by showing that features from near- and far-to-goal states are indistinguishable, which indicates a failure to capture temporal context effectively.

To address these limitations, the key objectives of this work are:

1. **Develop a Unified Spatio-Temporal Representation Framework:** The central objective is to propose a novel framework, STRNet, that treats representation learning as a core component of visual navigation, rather than a secondary process. This framework aims to enhance visual encoding by explicitly capturing both spatial geometry and temporal dynamics from image sequences and goal observations.
2. **Enhance Spatial Understanding through Graph-Based Aggregation:** To overcome the limitations of grid-based representations for irregular spatial layouts, an objective is to design a graph-based spatial aggregation module. This module interprets per-frame features as graphs, where nodes represent image regions and edges encode learned similarity scores, enabling the capture of fine-grained spatial geometry and semantic topological relationships.
3. **Incorporate Lightweight Temporal Dynamics Modeling:** To inject motion cues efficiently without incurring high computational costs, an objective is to develop a temporal fusion module. This module is designed to combine hybrid temporal shifting with multi-resolution difference-aware convolution, producing a compact yet expressive spatio-temporal representation.
4. **Support Robust Goal-Conditioned Navigation and Progress Estimation:** The ultimate objective is for the enriched spatio-temporal representation to serve as a robust foundation for downstream decision-making. This includes driving a diffusion-based policy for synthesizing control actions and a regressor for estimating the temporal distance to the goal, thereby enabling reliable and efficient navigation in various environments.

By pursuing these objectives, the authors aim to improve the foundational visual representation, leading to more robust, accurate, and adaptable navigation policies for mobile robots.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed STRNet framework is designed to enhance visual encoding for goal-conditioned visual navigation by focusing on the spatio-temporal structure of visual inputs. The architecture processes a sequence of first-person visual observations and a goal image to predict control actions and estimate temporal distance to the goal. The framework comprises three main stages: visual encoding, spatio-temporal feature fusion, and dual-headed prediction.

#### 3.1. Problem Definition

Given a sequence of past observations $O = \{I_t\}_{t=T-p}^T$ and a goal image $I_g$, the task is to learn a policy $\pi$ that predicts a control action $a \in \mathbb{R}^d$ and a temporal distance estimate $\tau \in \mathbb{R}^+$. Each observation $I_t$ is encoded into a feature vector $\phi(I_t)$, and the sequence is fused into a context representation $c_T$ for subsequent action and distance prediction.

#### 3.2. Overview of Network Framework

STRNet begins by extracting features from observation and goal images using a shared CNN backbone. These extracted features then undergo spatio-temporal feature fusion, which involves a graph-based spatial aggregation module and a temporal fusion module. The resulting fused spatio-temporal features, $c_T$, are fed into two lightweight task heads: a diffusion-based action policy head and a temporal distance estimation head.

#### 3.3. Spatial Feature Aggregation

The spatial feature aggregation module addresses the limitations of standard CNNs and Transformers in capturing irregular spatial layouts and semantic relationships within individual frames. It models the environment as a graph where nodes represent image regions and edges encode contextual relationships.

1. **Dynamic Axial Graph Construction:** Inspired by Vision GNNs, this module constructs a dynamic axis-aligned graph. For an input feature map $X$, each location $(i, j)$ is treated as a node. Candidate neighbors are identified via circular shifts $R(x_{i,j}, s)$. A soft contrast $d_s(i, j) = \|x_{i,j} - R(X, s)_{i,j}\|_1$ is computed, and soft edge weights $w_s(i, j) = \exp(-d_s(i, j)/\tau)$ are derived, creating a content-adaptive graph without explicit k-NN search.
2. **Graph Feature Aggregation:** This process involves three hierarchical stages:
 - **Positional Encoding:** A depthwise convolution generates a spatial offset map $\phi(X)$, which is added to the node features ($ \tilde{X} = X + \phi(X)$) to embed spatial layout information.
 - **Multi-scale Contrast Enhancement:** To capture spatial reasoning across various receptive fields, a multi-scale directional convolution module is employed. Circular shifts over multiple strides $s \in \{K, 2K, \dots\}$ are applied horizontally and vertically. Contrastive residuals are computed between shifted and original features. The most salient residual, $\Delta = \max(\Delta_i, \Delta_j)$, is retained via a contrast-aware masking rule, highlighting structural contrasts across scales.
 - **Residual Transformation:** The aggregated features are passed through a $1 \times 1$ convolution with normalization and residual connections with the original feature $X$. This step aims to increase expressiveness and mitigate over-smoothing effects.
 The overall spatial aggregation is formulated as $\hat{X} = T(A(P(X)) + \tilde{X})$, where $P(\cdot)$ is positional encoding, $A(\cdot)$ is contrast-aware graph convolution, and $T(\cdot)$ is the residual transformation.

#### 3.4. Temporal Feature Fusion

This module models temporal dynamics across a sequence of spatially refined features $\hat{X}_t$ to integrate motion cues.

1. **Hybrid Temporal Shift Module:** This module injects short-range temporal context with low computational cost. The channel dimension of the per-frame feature vector $\bar{X}$ is split into four groups (forward, backward, bi-directional, residual). Integer circular shifts are applied along the time axis for the forward, backward, and bi-directional groups ($X_t^f = X_{t-1}^f$, $X_t^b = X_{t+1}^b$, $X_t^{bi} = \frac{1}{2}(X_{t-1}^{bi} + X_{t+1}^{bi})$). A depthwise $3 \times 1 \times 1$ Conv3D followed by a pointwise $1 \times 1 \times 1$ Conv3D fuses the channels, and the result is added back to $\bar{X}$, yielding motion-aware features $X_{tsm}$.
2. **Dynamic Multi-resolution Contrast:** This component exposes motion-related contrasts at different receptive fields. $X_{tsm}$ is processed over a pyramid of $K$ spatial scales. At each scale $k$, adaptive average-pooling creates a coarser tensor, which is then circularly rolled by half its height and width. This rolled tensor is up-sampled back to the original resolution and subtracted from the original input ($X_{tsm}$) to form a scale-specific residual $\Delta_k = \tilde{X}_k - X_{tsm}$. A cosine-similarity mask $m_k$ is applied to select spatial locations where rolled features are closer than the mean similarity, highlighting salient temporal changes. The masked residuals are re-weighted by learnable coefficients $\beta_k$ and summed to form $X_{diff}$.
3. **Contrast-aware Fusion:** Finally, the original $X_{tsm}$ and the difference tensor $X_{diff}$ are concatenated and fused by a $1 \times 1 \times 1$ Conv3D, producing the final spatio-temporal representation $X_{fused}$ for the policy and distance heads.

#### 3.5. Training Details

The entire network is jointly trained using supervised learning. The total loss function $L_{total} = \alpha \cdot L_{dist} + (1-\alpha) \cdot L_{diff}$ combines:
- **Action Generation Loss ($L_{diff}$):** A masked mean squared error loss derived from a diffusion policy framework, where the model iteratively denoises expert actions perturbed with Gaussian noise.
- **Temporal Distance Estimation Loss ($L_{dist}$):** A mean squared error between the predicted and ground-truth temporal distances.
The model uses the Adam optimizer with cosine annealing, a batch size of 256, and an initial learning rate of 0.0001, with $\alpha=0.0001$.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of STRNet was conducted across various simulated and real-world environments, demonstrating its performance in comparison to state-of-the-art baselines.

#### 4.1. Overall Quantitative Performance

STRNet consistently outperformed baseline methods—ViNT, NoMaD, and NaviBridger—across basic, adaptation, and long-range tasks in both indoor (2D-3D-S, GRScenes) and outdoor (Citysim) simulated environments.

- **Indoor (2D-3D-S):** In the basic task, STRNet achieved a 100% success rate with zero collisions and a path length of 56.02m $\pm$ 0.071m, outperforming all baselines. For the adaptation task, it also achieved a 100% success rate with only 0.02 collisions and a path length of 37.07m $\pm$ 0.110m, indicating strong generalization capabilities.
- **Outdoor (Citysim):** STRNet achieved a 100% success rate with zero collisions in the basic task (path length 50.92m $\pm$ 0.770m). In the adaptation task, it secured a 92% success rate with 0.08 collisions and a path length of 138.86m $\pm$ 57.681m, significantly outperforming baselines, which showed higher collision rates (e.g., NoMaD with 1.10 collisions) and lower success rates (e.g., NoMaD with 36%).
- **GRScenes:** On the GRScenes dataset, STRNet achieved the highest Success Rate (0.79), the lowest average collisions (0.51), and the highest Average SPL (0.80), indicating superior reliability, safety, and efficiency in cluttered indoor scenes compared to ViNT, NoMaD, and NaviBridger.

#### 4.2. Long-range Task Analysis

In the long-horizon task within the 2D-3D-S environment, STRNet achieved a 98% success rate with minimal collisions (0.02) and the shortest path length (145.63m $\pm$ 6.80m), demonstrating its robustness and efficiency over extended navigation tasks. Other methods showed significantly lower success rates (e.g., NoMaD at 30%, ViNT at 68%).

#### 4.3. Robustness of Sub-target Selection

When random noise was introduced into sub-target selection, STRNet exhibited superior robustness. It maintained an 84% success rate with 0.34 collisions, outperforming NaviBridger (76% success, 0.47 collisions) and significantly exceeding NoMaD (34% success, 0.90 collisions), which degraded substantially under noisy conditions.

#### 4.4. Qualitative Performance

- **Navigation Trajectories:** Qualitative trajectories (Figure 4) showed that STRNet produces stable and efficient navigation paths in both 2D-3D-S and Citysim environments, even under adaptation and long-range conditions.
- **Failure Cases of Baselines:** Figure 5 illustrated common failure modes of the NoMaD baseline, attributed to impoverished spatio-temporal representations. These included jittering/stalling due to poor spatial cues, hesitation/wandering from smoothed temporal features, over-steering from weak motion signals, and collisions due to overlooking obstacles.
- **Real-world Evaluation:** In real-world experiments using a Diablo robot (Figure 6), STRNet predicted smoother, goal-consistent trajectories that aligned well with drivable corridors and maintained safe offsets from boundaries. In contrast, NoMaD exhibited rigid or hesitant commands, indicating difficulty in maintaining stable and accurate motion signals.

#### 4.5. Ablation Studies

Ablation studies confirmed the complementary roles of the proposed spatial aggregation (SA) and temporal fusion (TF) modules (Table 5).
- When both SA and TF were enabled, the model achieved a 98% success rate with 0.02 collisions in the long-horizon task.
- Removing SA resulted in a significant performance drop to 88% success and 0.12 collisions, indicating the importance of structured spatial understanding.
- Removing TF caused a substantial degradation to 38% success and 0.78 collisions, highlighting the necessity of coherent temporal reasoning.
- Removing both modules led to a severe collapse in performance, achieving only 28% success, underscoring that both spatial and temporal reasoning are indispensable and address distinct, essential aspects of navigation.

#### 4.6. Computational Analysis

STRNet demonstrated a favorable balance between model complexity and inference efficiency (Table 6). It had the lowest number of parameters (11.83M) compared to ViNT (14.04M) and NoMaD (13.60M). While its FLOPs (3.56M) were slightly higher than NoMaD (1.70M), it maintained a comparable inference time (36.52ms vs. 36.06ms for NoMaD), suggesting real-time capability while providing a stronger spatio-temporal encoding.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents STRNet, a spatio-temporal representation framework that addresses a foundational aspect of visual navigation: the quality of visual feature encoding. By moving beyond simplistic feature encoders and temporal pooling strategies commonly used in existing learning-based approaches, the work demonstrates a method for enhancing the visual representations supplied to navigation policies.

The significance of this work lies in several areas:

1. **Improved Representation Learning for Navigation:** The paper identifies and addresses the limitation of generic visual encoders in capturing the fine-grained spatial geometry and temporal dynamics essential for robust robotic navigation. This focus on representation learning as a core component, rather than solely on policy design, offers a paradigm shift in visual navigation research.
2. **Robust and Efficient Navigation Performance:** Through comprehensive evaluations, STRNet consistently demonstrates superior performance across various metrics—higher success rates, fewer collisions, and shorter path lengths—in diverse indoor and outdoor environments, including challenging long-range and adaptation tasks. This indicates that richer visual encodings can directly translate to more reliable, safer, and more efficient robot navigation.
3. **Generalizable Visual Backbone:** The proposed framework provides a generalizable visual backbone that can enhance goal-conditioned control. Its ability to extract and fuse structured spatio-temporal features makes it a potentially adaptable component for a range of downstream navigation tasks and policy architectures.
4. **Enhanced Robustness to Uncertainty:** The demonstrated robustness to noisy sub-target selection suggests that STRNet's representations are more resilient to imperfections in higher-level planning or goal specification, which is crucial for real-world deployments where perfect information is rare.
5. **Practical Applicability:** The computational analysis indicates that STRNet achieves a balance between model complexity and inference speed, making it suitable for real-time operation on robotic platforms. The qualitative results from real-world robot experiments further underscore its practical utility by showing smoother, more consistent control actions compared to baseline methods.

The potential impact of STRNet extends beyond benchmark improvements. By offering a robust mechanism for encoding visual information, it could contribute to:
- **Advancements in Embodied AI:** Providing more informative sensory inputs to embodied agents could lead to more intelligent behaviors in complex environments, aiding tasks like exploration, object manipulation, and human-robot interaction.
- **Autonomous Systems:** The enhanced navigation capabilities could improve the performance of autonomous driving systems, service robots, and drones that rely on visual cues for perception and decision-making in dynamic and unstructured settings.
- **Foundational Research:** The principles of dynamic graph aggregation for spatial reasoning and hybrid temporal shift with multi-resolution contrast for temporal dynamics could inform future research in spatio-temporal representation learning across various computer vision and robotics domains.

The authors also outline future work, including the integration of additional sensory inputs (e.g., depth, LiDAR, language commands) and memory mechanisms, suggesting a pathway for expanding the framework's capabilities to tackle even more complex navigation challenges.
