# FRAME: Floor-aligned Representation for Avatar Motion from Egocentric Video

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Max Planck Institute for Informatics developed FRAME, a method leveraging head-mounted stereo video and 6D device pose tracking to estimate human motion from an egocentric perspective. The approach achieved a 28% reduction in MPJPE (to 47.53 mm) over previous state-of-the-art methods and eliminated body-floor penetration, providing real-time, physically plausible full-body tracking suitable for VR/AR applications.

## Problem

- Scarcity of large-scale, high-quality real-world datasets for egocentric motion capture, leading to reliance on synthetic data and domain gaps.
- Existing egocentric methods suffer from inaccurate lower limb estimation, resulting in motion artifacts like body-floor penetration and foot skating.
- Prior sensor configurations for egocentric motion capture are often impractical for consumer VR/AR devices, hindering real-world deployment.

## Method

- Developed "SELF," a large-scale real-world dataset collected with a lightweight VR-based setup using head-mounted stereo fisheye cameras and on-board 6D device pose tracking.
- Introduced **FRAME**, a neural network architecture that explicitly leverages device pose for a floor-aligned reference frame, integrating stereo vision and a temporal fusion module.
- Implemented a cross-training caching strategy to improve model generalization by exposing the temporal module to more realistic error distributions from the backbone.

## Results

- FRAME achieved a Mean Per Joint Positional Error (MPJPE) of **47.53** mm, representing a **28%** improvement over the previous state-of-the-art method.
- The method demonstrated superior motion quality by entirely eliminating body-floor penetration (**100%** Non-Penetration Percentage with **0.00** mm Mean Penetration Error) and significantly reducing foot sliding (**3.47** cm/s).
- The model operates at an inference speed of approximately 300 FPS (**2.68** ms) on an NVIDIA RTX 3090 GPU, making it suitable for real-time VR/AR applications.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly integrating on-device 6D pose tracking and establishing a floor-aligned reference frame significantly improves the accuracy and physical plausibility of egocentric pose estimation, especially for lower limbs.
> - A large-scale, diverse real-world dataset collected with a consumer-like VR setup can effectively reduce reliance on synthetic data and mitigate generalization issues.
> - Employing a cross-training caching strategy during multi-stage model training enhances generalization by preventing downstream modules from overfitting to perfectly accurate upstream predictions.
