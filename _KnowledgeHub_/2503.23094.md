# FRAME: Floor-aligned Representation for Avatar Motion from Egocentric Video

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Max Planck Institute for Informatics developed FRAME, a method leveraging head-mounted stereo video and 6D device pose tracking to estimate human motion from an egocentric perspective. The approach achieved a 28% reduction in MPJPE (to 47.53 mm) over previous state-of-the-art methods and eliminated body-floor penetration, providing real-time, physically plausible full-body tracking suitable for VR/AR applications.

## Problem

- Scarcity of large-scale, high-quality real-world datasets for egocentric motion capture, leading to reliance on synthetic data and domain gaps.
- Existing egocentric methods suffer from inaccurate lower limb estimation, resulting in motion artifacts like body-floor penetration and foot skating.
- Prior sensor configurations for egocentric motion capture are often impractical for consumer VR/AR devices, hindering real-world deployment.

## Method

- Developed "SELF," a large-scale real-world dataset collected with a lightweight VR-based setup using head-mounted stereo fisheye cameras and on-board 6D device pose tracking.
- Introduced **FRAME**, a neural network architecture that explicitly leverages device pose for a floor-aligned reference frame, integrating stereo vision and a temporal fusion module.
- Implemented a cross-training caching strategy to improve model generalization by exposing the temporal module to more realistic error distributions from the backbone.

## Results

- FRAME achieved a Mean Per Joint Positional Error (MPJPE) of **47.53** mm, representing a **28%** improvement over the previous state-of-the-art method.
- The method demonstrated superior motion quality by entirely eliminating body-floor penetration (**100%** Non-Penetration Percentage with **0.00** mm Mean Penetration Error) and significantly reducing foot sliding (**3.47** cm/s).
- The model operates at an inference speed of approximately 300 FPS (**2.68** ms) on an NVIDIA RTX 3090 GPU, making it suitable for real-time VR/AR applications.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly integrating on-device 6D pose tracking and establishing a floor-aligned reference frame significantly improves the accuracy and physical plausibility of egocentric pose estimation, especially for lower limbs.
> - A large-scale, diverse real-world dataset collected with a consumer-like VR setup can effectively reduce reliance on synthetic data and mitigate generalization issues.
> - Employing a cross-training caching strategy during multi-stage model training enhances generalization by preventing downstream modules from overfitting to perfectly accurate upstream predictions.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Egocentric motion capture, which involves estimating body pose from a head-mounted camera, is an area of growing importance for applications in virtual reality (VR), augmented reality (AR), remote collaboration, and robotic control. Traditional VR body pose estimation often relies on sparse inputs, such as head and hand poses. However, there is a push towards more accurate full-body tracking, leading to research in leveraging existing forward-facing cameras, dedicated body-facing cameras, inertial measurement units (IMUs), time-of-flight sensors, and pressure-sensing insoles.

Among these approaches, ego-facing head-mounted cameras offer specific advantages, including a top-down view of the user's body and surrounding environment, which can capture detailed visual cues like clothing and wrinkles. Previous work in this domain has explored both monocular and stereo camera setups. However, these methods frequently assume impractical sensor configurations, such as fisheye cameras mounted on protruding bases that are not suitable for consumer devices. Many existing solutions also rely heavily on synthetic datasets for pretraining due to the scarcity of large-scale, high-quality real-world data. This reliance often results in a significant domain gap, leading to challenges in generalization to real-world scenarios, temporal inconsistencies, and artifacts such as body-floor penetration and foot skating, particularly for lower limb estimation.

The current research positions itself to address these limitations by introducing a large-scale real-world dataset collected with a lightweight, realistic VR-based setup. It also proposes a methodology that explicitly integrates multi-modal input—specifically, camera feeds and on-board 6D device pose tracking—to improve accuracy and temporal coherence. By focusing on practical sensor configurations and principled integration of available device tracking information, this work aims to mitigate the generalization issues and common motion artifacts observed in prior egocentric motion capture systems. It seeks to provide a foundation for robust, real-time body tracking in consumer VR/AR environments, moving beyond the reliance on synthetic data and impractical hardware setups.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to advance the field of egocentric motion capture for VR and AR applications by addressing persistent challenges related to data scarcity, generalization to real-world environments, and motion quality. The authors are motivated by several specific problems and limitations observed in existing egocentric pose estimation methods:

- **Scarcity of Annotated Real-World Data:** A significant motivation is the limited availability of large-scale, high-quality real-world datasets for egocentric motion capture. Current real-world datasets are often small, restricting the ability of models to generalize effectively. This scarcity has led to a heavy reliance on synthetic datasets, which introduce a domain gap that models struggle to bridge when applied to real-world scenarios. The authors aim to overcome this by collecting a substantially larger and more diverse real-world dataset.

- **Challenges of Egocentric View:** The inherent characteristics of egocentric views, such as heavy occlusions and limited contextual information, make accurate 3D body pose prediction difficult. This is particularly problematic for lower limbs, where existing methods often produce inaccurate or unstable predictions, leading to artifacts like body-floor penetration and foot skating. The research seeks to improve the accuracy and stability of lower limb tracking.

- **Impractical Sensor Configurations:** Many prior works utilize head-mounted camera setups that are not practical for consumer VR/AR devices. These setups often involve large, protruding cameras or checkerboards for ground truth alignment, which restrict natural movement and limit recording duration. A key motivation is to develop a system that uses a lightweight, realistic sensor configuration that more closely mimics future consumer VR devices, enabling controller-less body tracking.

- **Ineffective Multi-Modal Data Integration:** Modern VR headsets provide valuable multi-modal information, including on-board 6D device pose tracking. However, existing egocentric motion capture methods frequently overlook this readily available input or attempt to learn its relationship to body pose implicitly. The authors are motivated to explicitly leverage the geometric properties and known relationships between camera feeds and device pose in a differentiable manner, integrating this information to enhance pose prediction.

- **Temporal Inconsistencies and Motion Artifacts:** Existing methods often suffer from temporal inconsistencies and specific artifacts, such as floating feet, ground penetration, and jitter in sequential predictions. A motivation is to develop an approach that yields high-quality, smooth, and physically plausible motion capture results by incorporating temporal coherence and geometrically sound constraints.

- **Computational Efficiency:** For real-time VR/AR applications, the motion capture system must operate at high frame rates on consumer hardware. The authors aim to develop an efficient architecture capable of real-time performance.

To address these motivations, the key objectives of the paper are:
1. To introduce a lightweight VR-based data collection setup with on-board, real-time 6D pose tracking.
2. To create the most extensive real-world dataset for ego-facing, ego-mounted cameras to date, characterized by its size and motion variability, thereby reducing the need for synthetic data pretraining.
3. To propose FRAME, a simple yet effective architecture that combines device pose and camera feeds through geometrically sound multimodal integration, achieving state-of-the-art body pose prediction.
4. To develop a novel training strategy to enhance the model’s generalization capabilities, specifically addressing overfitting and the domain gap between backbone and temporal refinement modules.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology presented in this paper involves the creation of a novel large-scale egocentric dataset and the development of a neural network architecture, FRAME, designed to leverage the unique characteristics of egocentric, VR-based motion capture.

#### 3.1. SELF Dataset Collection

To address the lack of real-world egocentric data, the authors developed the "SELF" dataset.
- **Capture System:** A custom-made, lightweight wearable rig was developed, centered around a Meta Quest 3 VR headset. This rig includes a downward-facing stereo fisheye camera (capturing 640x480 resolution at 30Hz) whose positioning is designed to mimic a realistic VR scenario. The Quest 3's internal SLAM algorithm provides on-device real-time 6D head pose tracking. For ground truth alignment during data collection, a 3D-printed ArUco board with six markers was mounted on the device.
- **Studio Environment:** Data was captured in a studio equipped with 120 synchronized and calibrated 4K RGB cameras. Markerless motion capture (Captury system) was used to acquire ground truth skeletal motion.
- **Alignment and Synchronization:** A comprehensive calibration process was employed to ensure spatial and temporal synchronization.
 - **Calibration:** Each session began with a one-minute sequence where participants performed basic motions. This established markerless capture rigging and calculated transformations between cameras, markers, and the headset using an external checkerboard visible to both the headset and studio cameras. Light toggling was used for clock synchronization.
 - **Headset Tracking Alignment:** The 6D pose of the ArUco board was estimated for each frame by solving a Perspective-n-Point problem using external camera detections, followed by a global optimization minimizing reprojection errors across all cameras.
 - **On-Device Pose Alignment:** The VR on-device tracking data, in its own coordinate system and clock, was aligned with the studio reference frame. This was achieved by solving a minimization problem to find a time offset, a transformation from VR to studio coordinates, and a fixed transformation between the ArUco board and VR frames. Levenberg-Marquardt optimization and iterative grid search were used.
- **Dataset Structure and Scale:** The dataset involved 14 participants, each performing two sessions with distinct clothing. Each session included 50 predefined actions (20 seconds each, with pauses) covering various sports and daily activities. The dataset comprises over 7 hours of video and approximately 1.6 million individual images. A separate test set, containing about an hour of data from two additional participants, was created for generalization testing. Each time-frame includes stereo fisheye images, the onboard device pose `T_D(t)`, and ground truth joint positions `J_t`.

#### 3.2. FRAME Model Architecture

The FRAME model takes stereo egocentric video and the onboard device pose as input to estimate human pose.
- **Fisheye-based Pose Estimation (Backbone):**
 - A ResNet50 backbone (modified to omit the final residual block for higher spatial resolution features) processes each stereo image `I`.
 - A convolution-based head predicts 2.5D keypoint representations for each camera view: 2D heatmaps `H_i` and depthmaps `D_i`.
 - Heatmaps are normalized using a softmax operation with a learnable temperature parameter `β_i`.
 - 2D pixel coordinates `(u_i, v_i)` are obtained via soft-argmax, and depth `d_i` is calculated as a weighted sum of the depthmap by the normalized heatmap.
 - These `(u_i, v_i, d_i)` values are unprojected into 3D space using the fisheye camera model, yielding local pose predictions `J_L` and `J_R` for the left and right cameras, respectively.
- **Coordinate Systems Alignment:**
 - The model explicitly leverages the onboard headset pose `T_D` and the fixed relative transformations `M_L`, `M_R` between the headset and cameras to compute global camera poses `T_L`, `T_R`.
 - A critical step is the definition of a new **floor-aligned reference frame F**. This frame's origin is at the ground level, its y-axis is aligned vertically with gravity, and its horizontal axes are determined by projecting the x-axis of the camera's average frame onto the horizontal plane. This provides a stable, environment-aligned reference.
 - The predicted 3D joints `J_L` and `J_R` are then rototranslated into this common floor-aligned frame, yielding `J_F_L` and `J_F_R`. This step accounts for device slipping by using the continuously tracked headset pose.
- **Stereo Temporal Fusion (STF):**
 - A Transformer encoder module takes a sequence of aligned pose pairs from the current and previous 19 frames (sampled at 15Hz) in the `F` frame.
 - The STF module (8 layers, 512 feedforward dimension, 32 attention heads) integrates these historical predictions, along with the stereo and floor-aligned information, to output a refined, temporally coherent motion sequence. During inference, only the most recent frame's refined pose is outputted.

#### 3.3. Cross-Training Caching

To improve model generalization and address the issue of downstream modules (like STF) overfitting to overly accurate backbone predictions, a Cross-Training Caching approach, inspired by k-fold cross-validation, was introduced.
- The training set is divided into `k` subsets.
- The backbone model is iteratively retrained `k` times, each time on `k-1` subsets, and its predictions are cached on the held-out subset.
- These cached predictions, which represent realistic error distributions on unseen data, are then exclusively used to train the STF module.
- The final inference model employs a backbone trained on the entire training set.

#### 3.4. Implementation Details

Input images are resized to 256x256. The AdamW optimizer is used with a batch size of 16. The backbone is trained for 6 epochs with an L2 loss on predictions in camera coordinates. The STF module is then trained for 2 epochs using the cached predictions from the cross-training strategy, with an L2 loss on predictions in global coordinates.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents quantitative and qualitative results, along with ablation studies, to demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed dataset and methodology.

#### 4.1. Dataset Contribution

The "SELF" dataset represents a significant contribution to egocentric motion capture. It is substantially larger and more diverse than previous real-world datasets, offering over 7 hours of video and approximately 1.6 million frames. This scale reduces the reliance on synthetic data and provides a robust benchmark for real-world generalization. The inclusion of high-quality, on-device head tracking directly from a VR device provides realistic input for pose estimation.

#### 4.2. Quantitative Performance Comparison

The FRAME method was compared against state-of-the-art egocentric motion capture methods (UnrealEgo, EgoGlass, EgoPoseFormer) retrained on the SELF dataset. The evaluation used standard metrics including Mean Per Joint Positional Error (MPJPE), Procrustes-aligned MPJPE (PA-MPJPE), 3D Percentage of Correct Keypoints (3D-PCK), Jitter, Non-Penetration Percentage (NPP), Mean Penetration Error (MPE), and Foot Sliding (FS).

- **Accuracy:** FRAME achieved an MPJPE of 47.53 mm, a 28% improvement over the previous best method, EgoPoseFormer (69.18 mm). Similarly, PA-MPJPE was 35.86 mm. The 3D-PCK reached 92.56%, indicating high accuracy in keypoint estimation.
- **Motion Quality:** FRAME demonstrated superior temporal stability and physical realism. Jitter was reduced to 4.96 mm, indicating smoother sequential predictions. Critically, FRAME achieved a 100.0% NPP with 0.00 mm MPE, effectively eliminating body-floor penetration artifacts. Foot sliding was also significantly reduced to 3.47 cm/s.
- **Efficiency:** The model achieved an inference speed of 2.68 ms (approximately 300 FPS) on an NVIDIA RTX 3090 GPU, which is considerably faster than compared methods, making it suitable for real-time applications.

These results indicate that the explicit leveraging of device pose tracking for floor alignment and the multi-modal integration approach contributed to improved accuracy, particularly for lower-body alignment and overall motion quality.

#### 4.3. Qualitative Results

Qualitative comparisons (Figure 6) confirmed that FRAME predicts more accurate body poses, showing improved handling of contacts with the floor and better lower limb estimation compared to prior state-of-the-art approaches. The predicted 3D poses were observed to be physically more plausible and free from common artifacts.

#### 4.4. Ablation Study

A systematic ablation study evaluated the contribution of each component of the FRAME architecture:
- **Stereo vs. Monocular:** Using only a single camera (monocular) resulted in the highest MPJPE (87.94 mm), confirming the benefit of stereo cues for depth estimation. Stereo fusion, whether by averaging or a learned MLP, consistently improved accuracy.
- **Shared Head & Learnable SoftArgmax:** Processing image features jointly (shared head) reduced MPJPE to 74.13 mm, and the learnable softargmax further decreased it to 71.31 mm, suggesting benefits from joint processing and adaptive 2D distribution conversion.
- **Frame Alignment (to F):** Explicitly leveraging the device pose to rototranslate predictions into the floor-aligned frame `F` was a critical factor, significantly reducing MPJPE to 59.53 mm. This improvement was particularly noticeable in lower limb accuracy (Figure 7), addressing issues like floating feet.
- **Cross-Training Caching (CT):** The proposed cross-training caching strategy reduced MPJPE to 54.08 mm, demonstrating its effectiveness in enhancing model generalization by exposing the Stereo Temporal Fusion (STF) module to more realistic error distributions during training.
- **Stereo Temporal Fusion (STF):** The incorporation of the STF module, which integrates information from previous frames, yielded the best overall performance, reducing MPJPE to 47.53 mm. This highlights the importance of temporal coherence in refining pose estimates and correcting transient errors.

#### 4.5. Generalizability of Floor-aligned Frame

To demonstrate the broader applicability of the floor-aligned frame design, a monocular method (EgoWholeBody) was retrained on the left camera view of the SELF dataset. When the initial predictions were rototranslated into the floor-aligned frame `F` before refinement, the MPJPE significantly reduced from 85.63 mm (baseline) to 76.43 mm. This confirmed that the floor-aligned reference frame is a generally applicable design choice that can improve other egocentric pose estimation approaches.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents several significant contributions with potential impacts on the fields of computer vision, virtual reality, and augmented reality.

- **Advancement in Egocentric Motion Capture:** The FRAME method establishes a new benchmark for egocentric 3D human pose estimation, significantly outperforming prior state-of-the-art approaches in terms of accuracy, temporal stability, and realism of motion, particularly for lower limbs. This advancement addresses long-standing challenges such as foot-ground penetration and sliding.
- **Rich, Real-World Dataset:** The introduction of the large-scale SELF dataset is a substantial contribution. By providing a diverse and extensive collection of real-world egocentric video with accurate ground truth and realistic VR device tracking, it reduces the dependence on synthetic data and enables more robust training and evaluation of models for real-world applications. This dataset is likely to become a valuable resource for future research in the domain.
- **Enhanced VR/AR Immersion:** The improved accuracy and temporal coherence of body pose tracking, especially for lower limbs, can directly enhance the sense of presence and realism in VR/AR experiences. By enabling high-quality full-body avatars without the need for cumbersome external trackers or controllers, this work contributes to more immersive and natural user interactions in virtual environments.
- **Practicality and Real-time Performance:** The lightweight sensing setup and the high inference speed (300 FPS) of the FRAME model demonstrate its practical applicability for deployment on consumer-grade VR/AR headsets. This efficiency is critical for real-time interactive applications, making advanced body tracking more accessible to a broader user base.
- **Principled Multi-Modal Integration:** The explicit leveraging of known geometric transformations and on-device 6D pose tracking represents a principled approach to integrating multi-modal data. This methodology, particularly the concept of a floor-aligned reference frame, offers a robust and generalizable design that can be adopted by other egocentric pose estimation methods, as demonstrated by the ablation study and application to a monocular baseline.
- **Improved Generalization:** The cross-training caching strategy provides a novel approach to addressing domain gaps and overfitting in multi-stage deep learning models. This training technique could find application in other areas where sequential or hierarchical model components struggle with error distributions from upstream predictions.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The work lays a foundation for future exploration. The authors suggest integrating additional modalities available in VR headsets, such as environment meshes, forward-facing cameras, eye-gaze tracking, and hand pose estimation. Furthermore, the combination of physics-based modeling with the current approach could address remaining challenges related to occlusions and self-contact, leading to even more robust and physically accurate motion capture. The method could also serve as a starting point for driving photorealistic avatars or estimating physical quantities from egocentric signals.
