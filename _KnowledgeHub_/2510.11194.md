# Aligning Deep Implicit Preferences by Learning to Reason Defensively

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Tencent and Peking University developed the Critique-Driven Reasoning Alignment (CDRA) framework to help large language models better understand deep implicit user preferences and perform defensive reasoning. This framework, leveraging a new critique-annotated dataset, achieved 93.0% Deep Alignment Accuracy and reduced misleading risks on a challenging benchmark.

## Problem

- Large Language Models often fail to infer deep implicit user preferences beyond explicit instructions, leading to superficial and less relevant responses.
- Existing alignment methods lack mechanisms for defensive reasoning, making models unable to proactively identify and mitigate risks or ambiguities in user queries.
- Current outcome-based feedback is too sparse to guide the complex, multi-step reasoning required for robust and truly aligned AI behavior, creating a 'cognitive gap'.

## Method

- The ==Critique-Driven Reasoning== Alignment (**CDRA**) framework comprises a novel dataset, a personalized reward model, and a policy alignment algorithm; the dataset, DeepPref, was built via a Tree of Thoughts (ToT) framework with diverse reasoning paths annotated with step-wise natural language critiques and scalar scores.
- A Personalized Generative Process Reward Model (Pers-GenPRM) was trained to generate these critique-score pairs for each reasoning step, providing dense, interpretable feedback to overcome limitations of scalar rewards.
- Critique-Driven Policy Alignment (CDPA), an online reinforcement learning algorithm, utilizes Pers-GenPRM's process-level rewards to optimize the policy model, guiding it towards defensible and preference-aligned reasoning.

## Results

- CDRA achieved a Deep Alignment Accuracy of **93.0%** on the DeepPref benchmark, significantly surpassing baseline methods in both deep mining (**85.7%**) and innovative expansion (**75.7%**) of implicit user preferences.
- The framework demonstrated superior defensive reasoning capabilities, obtaining the lowest Misleading Risk scores (**26.3%** on DeepPref and **22.0%** on PrefEval) by proactively identifying and mitigating potential issues.
- Ablation studies confirmed that the Personalized Generative Process Reward Model (Pers-GenPRM) is essential for bridging the preference gap, and Rejection Sampling Fine-Tuning (RFT) is critical for establishing a robust foundational policy.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Aligning Large Language Models with deep implicit preferences and defensive reasoning requires a shift from outcome-based scalar rewards to structured, process-level feedback, particularly natural language critiques.
> - Developing datasets that provide explicit, step-wise supervision on reasoning paths is crucial for teaching LLMs to understand latent user intent and proactively address potential risks.
> - A generative reward model that produces human-intelligible critiques for each reasoning step, combined with an online policy optimization algorithm, enables LLMs to effectively internalize and execute sophisticated reasoning processes.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research addresses a critical challenge in the field of Large Language Model (LLM) alignment, specifically the transition from LLMs as instruction-followers to collaborative partners. The prevailing paradigm for LLM alignment, often rooted in Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) or methods like Direct Preference Optimization (DPO), has largely focused on optimizing for surface-level appeal or adherence to explicit instructions. These methods typically rely on outcome-based supervision or scalar feedback, which the authors contend leads to superficial responses that mimic stated preferences rather than genuinely understanding and reasoning about a user's latent intent.

The paper identifies two specific limitations in current approaches: a "preference gap" and a "process gap." The preference gap refers to the inability of LLMs to infer deep, implicit user preferences, which include unstated goals, semantic context, and risk tolerances. The process gap describes the models' failure to perform defensive reasoning—proactively identifying and mitigating potential risks or ambiguities within a query. Existing work on personalized alignment, including tuning-free methods (e.g., retrieval-augmented generation, prompt engineering) and tuning-based methods (e.g., SFT, DPO), often struggles with performance consistency, inference overhead, or is limited to mimicking styles and handling single-turn interactions, thereby failing to capture deeper intent.

Furthermore, current supervision benchmarks predominantly rely on pairwise preferences and outcome-based evaluations. While these can assess explicit adherence, they do not provide the granular signal needed to evaluate or train the intermediate reasoning process. This contrasts with the need for robust reasoning, particularly in open-domain tasks where objective ground truth is often absent.

This work positions itself as a novel paradigm, Critique-Driven Reasoning Alignment (CDRA), which aims to bridge these identified gaps. It shifts the focus from supervising final outcomes to supervising the underlying reasoning process itself. By introducing a new data benchmark for process-level supervision, a reasoning-based reward model, and a process-aware reinforcement learning algorithm, the research seeks to move beyond superficial mimicry to a more fundamental cognitive alignment, enabling LLMs to act as more reliable and trustworthy collaborative agents.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core objective of this research is to enable Large Language Models (LLMs) to engage effectively in user-centric interactions by achieving personalized alignment that goes beyond superficial adherence to explicit instructions. The authors posit that current LLMs exhibit two significant limitations, which serve as the primary motivation for this work:

1. **The Preference Gap:** This refers to the LLMs' inability to infer a user's *deep implicit preferences*. These are the unstated goals, semantic contexts, and risk tolerances that genuinely drive a user's query but are not explicitly articulated. For instance, a user stating "I don't feel comfortable sharing my real-time location" but asking for a way to update their family, implies a deeper preference for privacy and control over personal data, beyond merely avoiding real-time location sharing. Current methods, by focusing on surface-level preferences, fail to grasp these latent intentions.

2. **The Process Gap:** This describes the LLMs' failure to perform *defensive reasoning*. This cognitive process involves proactively identifying and mitigating potential risks or ambiguities inherent in a user's query or a generated response. Using the same example, a model might correctly avoid real-time sharing but then suggest a service that logs aggregated location data. While technically compliant with the explicit constraint, this fails to account for the implicit privacy principle, creating a new privacy liability and thus demonstrating a lack of defensive reasoning. The reliance on sparse, outcome-based scalar feedback in existing reinforcement learning approaches contributes to this gap, leading to responses that are described as superficial, brittle, and short-sighted.

To address these dual challenges, the research proposes a novel paradigm called **Critique-Driven Reasoning Alignment (CDRA)**. The key objectives of CDRA are:

- **Reframing Alignment:** To transform alignment from a scalar reward-matching task into a structured reasoning process. This involves teaching the model not only to generate answers but also to critically evaluate how well those answers respect deeper preferences and manage latent risks.
- **Bridging the Preference Inference Gap:** To develop mechanisms that enable LLMs to infer and respond to users' deep implicit preferences. This is addressed by introducing the **DeepPref benchmark**, a dataset providing process-level supervision to deconstruct query semantics and reveal latent risks.
- **Instilling Defensive Reasoning:** To integrate a defensive reasoning capability into LLMs. This is achieved through the **Personalized Generative Process Reward Model (Pers-GenPRM)**, which frames reward modeling as a personalized reasoning task, generating explicit critique chains to evaluate response alignment and safety.
- **Guiding Policy Optimization:** To leverage this structured, interpretable reward signal for policy model training. This is implemented via **Critique-Driven Policy Alignment (CDPA)**, an online reinforcement learning algorithm that integrates both numerical and natural language feedback, thereby resolving the "zero-advantage" problem where multiple outcomes may be equally good but differ in reasoning quality.

In essence, the motivation is to move beyond mere compliance with explicit instructions to fostering genuine understanding and robust, safe reasoning in LLMs, transforming them into more capable and trustworthy collaborative partners.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The Critique-Driven Reasoning Alignment (CDRA) framework is designed to bridge the identified preference and process gaps by shifting LLM alignment from outcome-based supervision to a process-driven, structured reasoning approach. The methodology comprises three interconnected components: DeepPref dataset construction, Personalized Reward Modeling (Pers-GenPRM), and Critique-Driven Policy Alignment (CDPA).

#### 3.1. Overview

The overarching goal is to align a large language model's policy $\pi$ with a user's deep implicit preferences $P$, given a query $q$, to generate an optimally aligned response $y$. CDRA aims to address the difficulties in inferring implicit preferences and the limitations of sparse, uninterpretable scalar rewards.

#### 3.2. The DeepPref Dataset

To provide the necessary process-level supervision, the authors constructed **DeepPref**, a large-scale, critique-annotated dataset ($D_{DeepPref}$).
- **Content:** The dataset consists of 3000 unique scenarios across 20 diverse domains. Each scenario includes a tuple of a detailed preference $P$ (designed to include nuanced, sometimes conflicting values and unstated goals) and an open-ended, ambiguous query $q$.
- **Data Construction Process:**
 1. **Diverse Path Generation:** A Tree of Thoughts (ToT) framework is employed to generate reasoning paths for each $(P, q)$ pair. This is guided by a "multi-faceted cognitive council" of five distinct expert personas (The Sociologist, The Psychologist, The Pragmatist, The Pedagogue, The Contrarian). Each persona proposes different lines of reasoning, creating a diverse set of reasoning chains $\tau_i$, which are sequences of steps $(s^i_1, s^i_2, \ldots, s^i_{T_i})$. Heuristic pruning is applied to manage the search space and maintain focus on promising paths.
 2. **Step-wise Critique and Scoring:** A powerful LLM evaluator (e.g., GPT-4.1) annotates each individual reasoning step $s^i_j$ within a chain $\tau_i$. For every step, conditioned on the preceding context $(P, q, \tau^{\le j}_i)$, the evaluator generates:
 - A detailed textual critique $c^i_j$, assessing the step's alignment with preferences $P$ and its effectiveness in mitigating risks.
 - A corresponding scalar quality score $r^i_j \in \mathbb{R}$, quantifying the quality of that specific reasoning step.
- **Output:** The dataset comprises tuples of $(P, q, \tau_i, \{c^i_j, r^i_j\}_{j=1}^{T_i})$. A subset $D_{Rea}$ contains all reasoning chains and critiques for reward model training, and a subset of the highest-quality paths, $D_{RFT}$, is reserved for policy model fine-tuning.
- **Preference Forms:** The work explicitly distinguishes between "Explicit Preferences" (directly stated) and "Deep Implicit Preferences" (embedded within context, values, and unstated goals), with the latter being the core focus for bridging the preference gap.

#### 3.3. Personalized Reward Modeling (Pers-GenPRM)

This component addresses the subjectivity of user preferences and the limitations of simple scalar rewards by operationalizing reward modeling as a reasoning task.
- **Mechanism:** Pers-GenPRM acts as a step-wise critic model. For each reasoning step $s^i_j$ in a chain $\tau_i$, it takes the preceding context $(P, q, \tau^{\le j}_i)$ and is trained to generate a critique-score pair $(c^i_j, r^i_j)$.
- **Training:** Pers-GenPRM is trained via Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) on the $D_{DeepPref}$ dataset. The objective is to maximize the log-likelihood of generating the ground-truth critique-score pairs, decomposing the loss across generating the critique and then the score conditioned on the critique.
- **Output:** This training yields a dual-component reward for each step: an interpretable textual critique ($c^i_j$) and a quantitative scalar reward ($r^i_j$) grounded in that critique. This process provides a dense, granular, and transparent signal that directly supervises the model's cognitive process, resolving the "zero-advantage" problem by differentiating between reasoning paths of varying quality.

#### 3.4. Critique-Driven Policy Alignment (CDPA)

CDPA is an online reinforcement learning algorithm designed to fine-tune the policy model using the structured, critique-grounded rewards from Pers-GenPRM. It builds upon GRPO but introduces a fine-grained, token-level advantage signal.
- **Step 1: Policy Initialization:** The policy $\pi_{\theta}$ is initialized using Rejection Sampling Fine-Tuning (RFT) on the high-quality $D_{RFT}$ subset of DeepPref.
- **Step 2: Group Sampling:** For each input $(P, q)$, a group of $G$ responses $\{y_i\}_{i=1}^G$ is sampled from the current policy $\pi_{\theta}$. Each response $y_i$ consists of $T_i$ reasoning steps.
- **Step 3: Process-Level Reward Generation:** The trained Pers-GenPRM assigns a critique-grounded scalar reward $r^i_j$ to each reasoning step $s^i_j$ within every sampled response.
- **Step 4: Critique-Grounded Advantage Estimation:** For each token $t$ belonging to a reasoning step $s^i_j$ in response $y_i$, its token-level reward $r_{i,t}$ is set as the reward of that step, $r^i_j$. These token-level rewards are then normalized (zero mean, unit variance) across all $G$ responses in the group at the corresponding token position to compute the advantage $\hat{A}(t, y_i)$. This provides a granular signal contrasting each token's step quality.
- **Step 5: Policy Update:** The policy is updated using a PPO-style clipped objective function ($J_{CDPA}$), incorporating the per-token advantage and a KL divergence regularization term to prevent large policy shifts. This process-level feedback creates a rich gradient that guides the policy towards generating responses built on high-quality, defensible reasoning.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted to evaluate CDRA focused on three dimensions: Deep Preference Understanding, Defensive Reasoning, and Explicit Preference Adherence, utilizing an "LLM-as-a-judge" framework with DeepSeek-V3.2-Exp as the evaluator. Performance was assessed on the DeepPref and PrefEval datasets, and human evaluation was performed on the ALOE multi-turn dialogue benchmark.

**Quantitative Analysis:**
- **Deep Preference Understanding:** CDRA consistently demonstrated superior performance in understanding deep implicit preferences.
 - On the DeepPref dataset, CDRA achieved the highest Deep Alignment Accuracy (Acc$_{DA}$) at 76.3%, surpassing baselines such as SFT (75.0%) and GRPO (70.3%). This was supported by its leading scores in Deep Mining (m$_{dm}$) at 65.0% and, notably, Innovative Expansion (m$_{ie}$) at 42.7%, where it showed a substantial lead over the next-best method (SFT at 40.3%).
 - On the PrefEval dataset, CDRA maintained its lead in Acc$_{DA}$ at 62.5% compared to SFT (58.0%) and GRPO (51.8%), indicating its generalization capability across different benchmarks for implicit preferences.
- **Defensive Reasoning:** CDRA exhibited robust defensive reasoning capabilities, indicated by its low Misleading Risk (Acc$_{Mis}$) scores across both benchmarks. While the paper notes a managed trade-off against the Thoughtfulness metric (m$_{th}$), CDRA's overall performance balance in generating novel and deeply aligned ideas was considered effective in bridging both preference and process gaps.
- **Explicit Preference Adherence:** The advancements in complex reasoning did not compromise CDRA's ability to follow explicit preferences. CDRA achieved the highest Preference Following Accuracy (Acc$_{PF}$) on DeepPref (84.7%) and remained competitive on PrefEval (68.8%), showcasing its reliability in adhering to stated instructions.
- **Human Evaluation on Multi-Turn Dialogue:** On the ALOE dataset, CDRA attained the highest average alignment score (3.92 on a 1-5 scale) over multi-turn conversations, outperforming all baselines. Its alignment score demonstrated consistent improvement as conversations progressed, peaking at 4.6 in later turns. This suggests CDRA's superior ability to accumulate user context and maintain deep alignment over extended interactions.

**Qualitative and Mechanism Analysis:**
- **Latent Intent Reasoning:** Qualitative examples illustrated that CDRA moved beyond literal interpretations, which often led to "Preference Unaware Violations" in baseline models like TPO. CDRA's process involved grappling with underlying semantics and unstated risk tolerances, leading to more creative and defensively sound suggestions. An example provided involved game recommendations where CDRA recognized an implicit preference against fast-paced action, while TPO recommended a third-person shooter.
- **Attention Distribution Analysis:** Analysis of attention distribution (Figure 6) revealed that CDRA concentrated 35.7% of its attention mass on the "Preference Region" of the input prompt. This suggested that the Pers-GenPRM supervision effectively teaches the policy model where to focus, enabling it to anchor generation on user constraints and reduce preference unaware violations.

**Ablation Study:**
- An ablation study on reward modeling paradigms confirmed the necessity of CDRA's approach. CDRA, which combines process supervision and critique supervision, achieved the highest Deep Alignment Accuracy at 76.3%.
- It outperformed alternative reward paradigms (e.g., GRPO with standard RM, Generative RM, or Process RM alone) and simpler heuristics like Rubric-based models and Test-Time Scaling. The performance gap was particularly notable in Innovative Expansion, where CDRA (42.7%) established a substantial lead over these methods (e.g., 34.7% for Rubric-based RM), indicating that such heuristics could not replicate the nuanced reasoning required to uncover latent preferences. This validates that the critical reasoning process itself, rather than just final outcomes or intermediate steps, is essential for deep alignment.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research introduces Critique-Driven Reasoning Alignment (CDRA), which represents a conceptual shift in the approach to Large Language Model (LLM) alignment. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Formalizing and Addressing Fundamental Gaps:** The work formally defines and directly addresses the "preference gap" (inability to infer deep implicit preferences) and the "process gap" (lack of defensive reasoning). This formalization provides a structured framework for understanding and tackling key limitations in current LLM personalization. By focusing on these gaps, the research aims to move beyond superficial compliance towards a more profound understanding of user intent.
- **Novel Paradigm for Alignment:** CDRA redefines LLM alignment from a scalar reward-matching task to a structured, critical reasoning process. This is a departure from outcome-based supervision and offers a pathway for LLMs to not only generate responses but also to internalize a process of evaluating those responses against deeper user preferences and potential risks.
- **Technical Innovations with Broad Applicability:** The framework introduces three core technical innovations:
 1. **DeepPref Benchmark:** This is the first large-scale, critique-annotated dataset designed for process-level supervision. Its multi-faceted cognitive council simulation for generating reasoning chains and step-wise critiques provides a rich data foundation for teaching nuanced reasoning and risk mitigation. This dataset can serve as a valuable resource for future research in process-level alignment and interpretable AI.
 2. **Personalized Generative Process Reward Model (Pers-GenPRM):** By framing reward modeling as a reasoning task that generates explicit textual critique chains before deriving scores, Pers-GenPRM introduces a transparent and interpretable reward signal. This addresses the "zero-advantage" problem in RL and provides human-intelligible rationales for model evaluations, enhancing auditability and trust.
 3. **Critique-Driven Policy Alignment (CDPA):** This algorithm effectively integrates both numerical and natural language feedback from Pers-GenPRM into an online reinforcement learning process. By focusing on a fine-grained, token-level advantage signal derived from reasoning critiques, CDPA steers policy models towards fundamentally aligned and defensible reasoning paths.
- **Enhanced LLM Capabilities and Trustworthiness:** The experimental results demonstrate that CDRA achieves superior performance in both deep preference understanding and robust reasoning compared to existing methods. This indicates a tangible improvement in LLMs' ability to:
 - Infer latent user intent, leading to more personalized and satisfying interactions.
 - Proactively identify and mitigate risks, contributing to safer and more reliable AI systems.
 - Maintain alignment over long-form dialogues, which is crucial for complex collaborative tasks.
- **Path Towards Interpretable and Trustworthy AI:** By externalizing the cognitive evaluation process through critiques, CDRA contributes to the development of more interpretable AI. Users and developers can understand *why* a model made a certain decision, fostering greater trust in LLM outputs. This shift from simple mimicry to genuine cognitive synergy is a key step towards developing LLMs that can function as truly reliable collaborative agents in diverse real-world scenarios.
- **Impact on Future Research:** The concepts and methodologies introduced could influence future research in reward modeling, preference learning, and safety alignment for LLMs, encouraging a deeper focus on the reasoning process rather than just outcomes.
