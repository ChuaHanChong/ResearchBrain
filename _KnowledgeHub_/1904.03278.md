# AMASS: Archive of Motion Capture as Surface Shapes

> [!summary] Summary
> A new method, MoSh++, effectively converts diverse marker-based motion capture data into a consistent 3D mesh representation, forming AMASS, the largest publicly available archive of human motions with detailed surface shapes and soft-tissue dynamics. MoSh++ achieved an average shape reconstruction error of 7.4mm and a pose/soft-tissue error of 7.3mm, outperforming previous methods, and the AMASS dataset unifies over 42 hours of motion from 346 subjects into a standardized format.

## Problem

- Existing motion capture (mocap) datasets are fragmented and heterogeneous, making it difficult to combine them for large-scale data-driven research.
- Traditional mocap methods primarily focus on skeletal joint positions, often discarding rich surface information and soft-tissue dynamics crucial for realistic animation and detailed analysis.
- A scarcity of large, consistently formatted 3D human motion datasets limits the progress of deep learning models in human motion synthesis, pose estimation, and action recognition.

## Method

- The authors developed **MoSh++**, a robust method for recovering full 3D human shape and pose from standard optical marker-based motion capture data.
- **MoSh++** employs a ==two-stage optimization== process that fits the ==SMPL-H== and ==DMPL== body models to sparse marker data, first estimating a consistent body shape and then per-frame pose and soft-tissue dynamics.
- **MoSh++** was applied to 15 diverse mocap datasets to create **AMASS**, the largest publicly available, unified archive of human motions represented as detailed 3D meshes.

## Results

- MoSh++ achieved an average shape reconstruction error of **7.4mm** with **46 markers**, and a combined pose and soft-tissue error of **7.3mm**, outperforming its predecessor MoSh (**12.1mm** and **10.24mm** respectively).
- The AMASS dataset unifies **15** different optical marker-based mocap datasets, comprising over **42 hours** of motion from **346** unique subjects.
- Every frame in AMASS is represented in a consistent SMPL-based format, including **16-dimensional** shape, **8-dimensional** soft-tissue, and **90-dimensional** pose parameters (including hands), making it readily usable for graphics and vision tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The creation of the Synchronized Scans and Markers (SSM) dataset provides a crucial resource for quantitatively evaluating 3D human reconstruction methods against ground truth 4D scans.
> - MoSh++ effectively integrates SMPL-H for detailed hand articulation and DMPL for realistic soft-tissue dynamics, moving beyond skeletal representations to full 3D surface shapes.
> - An adaptive weighting factor for marker occlusion (`q`) in MoSh++ maintains plausible poses even with significant missing markers, enhancing robustness to real-world mocap issues.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of human motion capture and modeling is a cornerstone for applications ranging from computer animation and virtual reality to robotics, biomechanics, and human-computer interaction. Recent advances in deep learning have dramatically increased the demand for large, diverse, and consistently formatted datasets, a demand that traditional motion capture (mocap) data struggles to meet.

Historically, mocap datasets have been collected using various marker sets, hardware systems, and lab-specific procedures. This heterogeneity makes it exceedingly difficult to combine them into a single, comprehensive dataset. Furthermore, most traditional mocap approaches focus on extracting skeletal joint positions, often discarding rich surface information and soft-tissue dynamics, which are crucial for realistic animation and detailed analysis. While some efforts have been made to create "supersets" of mocap data (e.g., MocapClub, EKUT, Holden et al.), these often involve standardizing to fixed body proportions or retargeting to a common skeleton, which fundamentally alters the original data and discards vital information about individual body shapes and their correlation with motion. Such retargeting can also introduce artifacts and reduce the richness of the original capture.

"AMASS" directly addresses these critical limitations. It stands as a significant advancement by:

- **Unifying Fragmented Data:** It tackles the challenge of disparate mocap datasets by introducing a robust method, MoSh++, to convert them all into a single, unified, and consistent representation based on the **SMPL (Skinned Multi-Person Linear) body model**. SMPL has already become a de facto standard in human modeling due to its learnable, anatomically plausible, and widely compatible structure. This unification is paramount for leveraging the full potential of deep learning, which thrives on large, consistent data volumes.
- **Preserving and Enhancing Surface Detail:** Unlike traditional skeletal-focused approaches, AMASS (via MoSh++) recovers the *full 3D surface shape* of the body, including soft-tissue dynamics and hand articulations. This is a crucial distinction, moving beyond mere joint coordinates to provide dense, rigged 3D meshes. This detailed representation is essential for generating realistic animations, rendering, and for computer vision tasks that require understanding human shape in addition to pose. The integration of DMPL for soft-tissue dynamics and MANO for hands represents a significant leap from previous attempts like the original MoSh, which lacked accurate soft-tissue or hand modeling.
- **Addressing Data Scarcity for Deep Learning:** By aggregating 15 different mocap datasets into over 40 hours of motion data, AMASS provides an unprecedented scale for training deep learning models. This large corpus can enable breakthroughs in data-driven motion synthesis, pose estimation from monocular images/videos, action recognition, and human performance analysis, where prior datasets were simply too small and limited.
- **Bridging Graphics and Vision:** The output, being SMPL parameters, is directly usable in computer graphics pipelines for animation and rendering. Critically, it also serves as high-quality synthetic training data for computer vision tasks, helping to bridge the "reality gap" by providing ground-truth 3D meshes that can be rendered into images or videos with precise annotations.

In essence, AMASS represents a fundamental infrastructure contribution to the fields of computer vision, graphics, and robotics. It leverages state-of-the-art statistical body models (developed by some of the same authors) to transform a wealth of disparate, underutilized mocap data into a powerful, consistent, and publicly accessible resource, thereby laying the groundwork for the next generation of data-driven human-centric AI.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind the AMASS project stems from a fundamental bottleneck in deep learning research for human motion: the scarcity of large, varied, and consistently formatted training data. The authors explicitly state, "Large datasets are the cornerstone of recent advances in computer vision using deep learning. In contrast, existing human motion capture (mocap) datasets are small and the motions limited, hampering progress on learning models of human motion."

Against this backdrop, the paper sets forth two interrelated core objectives:

1. **To develop a robust and accurate method (MoSh++) for recovering full 3D human shape and pose from standard optical marker-based motion capture data.** This objective aims to overcome the limitations of previous methods, which either lost surface information, failed to capture realistic soft-tissue dynamics, or did not include detailed hand motion. The authors sought a method that could work with arbitrary marker sets and produce results compatible with modern graphics pipelines.
2. **To create the largest publicly available, unified database of human motions (AMASS) by applying the MoSh++ method to a vast collection of existing motion capture data.** The goal here is to consolidate a fragmented landscape of mocap datasets into a coherent, standardized format (SMPL parameters) that retains the richness of the original data, including full 3D body shapes and soft-tissue deformations, thereby enabling machine learning for diverse applications in animation and computer vision.

In summary, the authors were motivated by the critical need for "training data for deep learning" in the domain of human motion. They recognized that disparate data formats and the loss of crucial surface information in traditional mocap methods were significant barriers. Their solution involved developing a sophisticated conversion tool and then leveraging it to construct a foundational dataset designed to accelerate research in data-driven human modeling.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of the AMASS project lies in its novel method, **MoSh++**, which is a significant extension and improvement upon the authors' previous work, MoSh (Motion and Shape capture from sparse markers). MoSh++'s primary function is to convert raw marker-based motion capture data into detailed 3D human meshes parameterized by the SMPL body model.

#### 3.1. The Body Model: SMPL, SMPL-H, and DMPL

A critical decision was the choice of the underlying body model. MoSh++ eschews the less compatible BlendSCAPE model used in MoSh, opting for the widely adopted and graphics-friendly **SMPL (Skinned Multi-Person Linear Model)**. This choice is fundamental for distributing a usable dataset. The authors integrate two key extensions of SMPL:

- **SMPL-H:** This variant extends SMPL to include highly articulated hands, crucial for capturing realistic human motion. It uses a total of 52 joints (24 for the body, 28 for hands).
- **DMPL (Dynamic Mesh Parameterization Learning):** This component allows MoSh++ to model realistic soft-tissue deformations. Unlike MoSh, which approximated soft-tissue motion by changing identity parameters, DMPL leverages a dynamic shape space learned from thousands of 4D body scans, enabling more accurate and natural-looking skin dynamics from sparse markers.

The combined model, referred to simply as "SMPL" in the paper for brevity, allows for the representation of body shape (16 dimensions, β), pose (90 dimensions, θ, including hands), and dynamic soft-tissue deformations (8 dimensions, φ). This compact yet expressive parameterization is vital for efficient data storage and processing.

#### 3.2. Model Fitting: Two-Stage Optimization

MoSh++ employs a two-stage optimization process, similar in spirit to MoSh, but with crucial modifications and additions:

- **Stage I: Shape and Latent Marker Estimation (Per-Subject)**
 - **Objective:** To estimate a single, consistent body shape (β) for a subject and the latent, pose-invariant 3D positions of the markers (~M) relative to the body surface. This is done by fitting the model to a small number (F=12) of randomly chosen frames from the subject's mocap sequence.
 - **Objective Function (Equation 3):** This energy function minimizes the distance between simulated markers (from the SMPL model) and observed markers. It incorporates several regularization terms:
 - `ED`: Data term, measuring L2 distance between simulated and observed markers. Its weight is scaled (`b = 46/n`, where `n` is observed markers) to account for varying marker set sizes.
 - `Eβ`: Mahalanobis distance shape prior on SMPL shape components.
 - `EθB` & `EθH`: Regularizers for body and hand pose parameters, respectively. `EθH` projects the hand pose into MANO's low-dimensional space and uses a Mahalanobis distance.
 - `ER`: Encourages latent markers to remain a prescribed distance from the body surface.
 - `EI`: Penalizes deviations of latent markers from their initialized locations.
 - **Optimization Strategy:** Uses a Threshold Acceptance method as a fast annealing strategy over four stages of graduated optimization, incrementally increasing the data term weight while decreasing regularization.
 - **Hyperparameter Tuning:** The critical `λ` weights for the objective function terms are *not* hand-tuned. Instead, they are systematically determined using a line search on the newly introduced **Synchronized Scans and Markers (SSM)** dataset (described below).

- **Stage II: Pose and Soft-tissue Dynamics Estimation (Per-Frame)**
 - **Objective:** To optimize the pose (θ) and soft-tissue deformation coefficients (φ) for *each individual frame* of the mocap sequence, assuming the subject's shape and latent marker positions are fixed from Stage I.
 - **Objective Function (Equation 6):** Builds upon Stage I's data and pose terms, adding:
 - `Eu`: Temporal smoothness term for pose changes (reduces jitter).
 - `Eφ`: Mahalanobis prior on the 8 DMPL soft-tissue coefficients, using covariance from the DYNA dataset.
 - `Ev`: Temporal smoothness term for soft-tissue deformations.
 - **Initialization:** For the first frame, the model is initialized with a rigid transformation. Subsequent frames are initialized with the solution from the previous frame.
 - **Two-Step Per-Frame Optimization:** The optimization is refined in two steps per frame: first optimizing only pose (without dynamics terms to prevent them from explaining large pose changes), then adding dynamics terms for a final joint optimization of pose and soft-tissue.
 - **Robustness to Occlusion:** A novel adaptive weighting factor `q` is introduced for the pose prior (`λθ`). This factor increases the prior weight dynamically based on the number of missing markers in a frame, ensuring more plausible poses even under significant marker occlusion.
 - **Hyperparameter Tuning:** Similar to Stage I, the `λ` weights (including `q`) for this stage are optimized using the SSM dataset.

#### 3.3. Evaluation Dataset: Synchronized Scans and Markers (SSM)

To rigorously tune hyperparameters and quantitatively evaluate MoSh++, the authors collected a novel dataset called **Synchronized Scans and Markers (SSM)**. This dataset consists of subjects simultaneously recorded with:
- A high-resolution **4D scanning system** (capturing dense 3D meshes in motion) providing *ground-truth* body shapes and soft-tissue dynamics.
- A traditional **OptiTrack optical mocap system** (capturing sparse marker data).
This synchronized dataset allows for direct, frame-by-frame comparison of MoSh++'s reconstructed meshes against ground truth, enabling precise tuning and validation of the method's accuracy. SSM itself is a valuable contribution, with data from two subjects slated for public release.

#### 3.4. Optimization and Runtime

The optimization uses Powell's gradient-based dogleg minimization, implemented with the Chumpy auto-differentiation package. While not real-time (Stage I: ~25 min/sequence; Stage II: ~2 sec/frame with dynamics), the approach is designed for offline processing of large archives.

#### 3.5. Dataset Amalgamation

Finally, armed with the robust MoSh++ method, the authors systematically processed 15 different pre-existing mocap datasets (including CMU, MPI-HDM05, KIT, BioMotion Lab, etc.). This involved handling the varied marker sets and data formats inherent in these diverse sources. Manual inspection and correction were performed to address common mocap problems like swapped or mislabeled markers, ensuring high data quality in the final AMASS dataset.

This multi-faceted methodology, combining state-of-the-art body models, a refined optimization scheme, and a purpose-built validation dataset, culminates in the creation of AMASS: a large-scale, unified, and high-quality resource for human motion research.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The paper presents several key findings and results, demonstrating the superior performance of MoSh++ and detailing the scale and characteristics of the resulting AMASS dataset.

#### 4.1. MoSh++ Accuracy Evaluation on SSM Dataset

The novel Synchronized Scans and Markers (SSM) dataset proved instrumental in quantitatively evaluating MoSh++ against ground truth 4D scans.

- **Shape Estimation Accuracy:** MoSh++ significantly outperformed its predecessor, MoSh, in reconstructing accurate body shapes.
 - Using a standard 46-marker set, MoSh++ achieved an average shape reconstruction error of **7.4mm**, compared to MoSh's 12.1mm.
 - Remarkably, MoSh++ with only 46 markers achieved nearly the same accuracy as MoSh using a denser 67-marker set, highlighting its efficiency and robustness to sparse input.
 - The authors also empirically determined the optimal number of SMPL shape components to be 16, finding that more components led to overfitting on sparse markers despite potentially reducing marker error.

- **Pose Estimation Accuracy (without soft-tissue):** When evaluating pose reconstruction accuracy without considering dynamic soft-tissue deformations, MoSh++ again showed improvement.
 - It achieved an average pose estimation error of **8.1mm** with 46 markers, outperforming MoSh's 10.5mm.

- **Pose and Soft-tissue Estimation Accuracy (with DMPL):** This is where MoSh++ truly demonstrates its advantage, leveraging the DMPL model for dynamic soft-tissue.
 - MoSh++ achieved the most accurate results in this configuration, with an average error of **7.3mm**, significantly better than MoSh's 10.24mm.
 - Qualitative examples (Figure 7) visibly illustrate MoSh++'s ability to capture realistic soft-tissue motion in regions like the chest and stomach, which MoSh's approach of merely changing identity parameters could not achieve.
 - The optimal number of DMPL dynamic components was determined to be 8, again striking a balance between capturing detail and avoiding overfitting.

- **Hand Articulation:** While quantitative ground-truth data for hand articulation was not available for evaluation, qualitative results (Figure 8) demonstrate that MoSh++, when provided with hand markers, successfully fits the SMPL-H model to produce realistic and articulated hand poses, a feature largely absent in MoSh. The adaptive weighting for marker occlusion (the `q` factor) also proved effective in maintaining plausible poses even when foot markers were missing (Figure 4).

#### 4.2. AMASS Dataset Characteristics

The culmination of the MoSh++ method applied to a vast array of existing data resulted in the creation of AMASS, a resource of unprecedented scale and consistency.

- **Size and Diversity:** AMASS unifies **15 different optical marker-based mocap datasets**, spanning a wide range of motion types, subjects, and original marker sets (from 37 to 91 markers).
 - It contains over **42 hours** of motion data.
 - It includes data from **346 unique subjects**.
 - It comprises **11,451 individual motions**.
- **Unified Format:** Critically, every frame in AMASS is represented in a consistent format:
 - **16-dimensional SMPL 3D shape parameters (β)**.
 - **8-dimensional DMPL soft-tissue coefficients (φ)**.
 - **90-dimensional full SMPL pose parameters (θ)**, which include detailed hand articulations.
- **Accessibility:** The AMASS dataset is publicly available to the research community at http://amass.is.tue.mpg.de, making it a readily usable resource for a wide array of research. The authors commit to continuously expanding the dataset with new community contributions.
- **Richness of Data:** Unlike previous efforts that normalized bodies or focused solely on skeletons, AMASS retains the full 3D body shape and soft-tissue deformations, offering a much richer representation of human motion. Users can choose to use the full model or simplify it (e.g., by ignoring shape/dynamics if only joint locations are needed), providing flexibility.

In summary, the main findings highlight that MoSh++ is a significantly more accurate and comprehensive method for marker-based 3D human reconstruction than its predecessor, especially in capturing nuanced soft-tissue dynamics. This improved method enabled the creation of AMASS, a truly monumental dataset that unifies a diverse corpus of mocap data into a consistent, high-fidelity 3D mesh representation, addressing a critical data gap for deep learning in human motion research.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The AMASS dataset and the underlying MoSh++ method represent a highly significant contribution to the fields of computer vision, computer graphics, biomechanics, and machine learning, with the potential for broad and lasting impact.

#### 5.1. Enabling Data-Driven Approaches and Deep Learning

The most profound impact of AMASS lies in its ability to democratize and fuel data-driven research, particularly with deep learning. Deep learning models are notoriously data-hungry, and the lack of large, consistent, and well-annotated 3D human motion datasets has been a major bottleneck. AMASS directly addresses this by providing:

- **Unprecedented Scale:** Over 40 hours of motion data is a massive increase over previously fragmented collections, enabling the training of more robust and generalizable deep learning models.
- **Consistency:** By unifying 15 disparate datasets into the standardized SMPL format, AMASS removes the burden of data pre-processing and format conversion for researchers, allowing them to directly leverage the entire corpus. This consistency is crucial for effective deep learning.
- **Richness of Representation:** Providing full 3D meshes with shape and soft-tissue dynamics (not just skeletal joints) opens up new avenues for learning algorithms that can understand and generate more realistic and detailed human motion and appearance.

This will accelerate progress in areas such as:
- **Monocular 3D Human Pose and Shape Estimation:** Training models to reconstruct 3D human pose and shape from single images or videos, moving beyond sparse skeletons to full body surface estimation.
- **Human Motion Synthesis:** Generating novel, realistic human motions for animation, games, and virtual reality, including subtle soft-tissue movements.
- **Action Recognition and Understanding:** Developing more nuanced recognition systems that can interpret human activities based on full body dynamics.
- **Human-Robot Interaction:** Enabling robots to better understand and predict human movement.

#### 5.2. Bridging Computer Graphics and Vision

AMASS, with its SMPL-based output, inherently bridges the gap between computer graphics and computer vision:

- **High-Quality Animation and Visualization:** The rigged SMPL meshes are directly compatible with standard animation software and game engines, making AMASS immediately useful for content creation and realistic visualization.
- **Synthetic Data Generation:** The consistent 3D representation allows researchers to render diverse synthetic images and videos of humans in various poses and environments, which can be used to augment real-world datasets or even create entirely synthetic training data for computer vision tasks, addressing issues like data privacy and annotation costs. This aligns with the work of some of the authors (e.g., Varol et al.).

#### 5.3. Advancement of 3D Human Modeling Techniques

MoSh++ itself is a significant methodological advance:

- **Superior Marker-to-Mesh Conversion:** It demonstrates significantly improved accuracy in reconstructing body shape and pose from sparse markers, especially in capturing realistic soft-tissue dynamics and hand articulations, which were challenging for previous methods.
- **Robustness to Varying Inputs:** Its ability to handle diverse marker sets and adapt to marker occlusion makes it a robust tool for processing real-world mocap data.
- **Foundational Validation Dataset (SSM):** The introduction of the Synchronized Scans and Markers (SSM) dataset provides a crucial resource for future research. Having ground-truth 4D scans synchronized with mocap markers enables rigorous quantitative evaluation and hyperparameter tuning for similar methods, which was previously difficult.

#### 5.4. Long-Term Research and Commercial Trajectory

The involvement of Meshcapade GmbH and the explicit conflict of interest disclosure point to the strong commercialization potential of this research. The underlying SMPL, MANO, and FLAME models, now bolstered by the AMASS dataset, are foundational for creating digital humans for entertainment, fashion, virtual try-on, and other industries. The authors' commitment to continuously expanding AMASS underscores its vision as an evolving, community-supported infrastructure.

Furthermore, the authors outline clear directions for future work, indicating AMASS's role as a springboard for continued innovation: extending SSM to include articulated hands, integrating facial mocap with the FLAME model, improving runtime, and leveraging AMASS itself to train models for denoising mocap data or automatically labeling markers.

In conclusion, "AMASS: Archive of Motion Capture as Surface Shapes" is a landmark paper that addresses a critical data bottleneck in human motion research. By providing a large-scale, unified, and high-fidelity dataset of human motions as full 3D meshes, it serves as a powerful catalyst for advancements in deep learning for human understanding, synthesis, and interaction, while solidifying the position of SMPL-family models as the gold standard in the field.
