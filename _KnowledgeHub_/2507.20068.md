# PERRY: Policy Evaluation with Confidence Intervals using Auxiliary Data

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Stanford University developed PERRY, a framework comprising CP-Gen and DR-PPI, to provide statistically valid confidence intervals for off-policy evaluation when incorporating auxiliary data. This work offers reliable uncertainty estimates for both state-conditioned and average policy values, enhancing the trustworthiness of OPE in complex, high-dimensional environments.

## Problem

- Traditional Off-Policy Evaluation (OPE) methods often yield biased or high-variance estimates due to limited data coverage or challenges in high-dimensional state spaces.
- While augmented data from generative models can improve OPE accuracy, its naive use introduces biases from model inaccuracies without principled uncertainty quantification.
- The absence of reliable confidence intervals for OPE, especially with augmented data, hinders its application in high-stakes domains where robust policy comparison and deployment decisions are critical.

## Method

- **CP-Gen** (==Conformal Prediction with Generated data==) provides ==state-conditioned confidence intervals== for OPE using an ==epsilon-approximation== for importance weights, enabling its application in continuous, high-dimensional MDPs.
- **DR-PPI** (==Doubly Robust Prediction-Powered Inference==) combines doubly robust estimation with prediction-powered inference and ==cross-fitting== to compute asymptotically valid confidence intervals for the average policy value.
- Both methodologies explicitly account for potential biases introduced by generative models used for data augmentation and incorporate techniques like ==IPS ratio clipping== to manage variance.

## Results

- **CP-Gen** consistently produced valid and often tighter conformal prediction intervals for initial-state-conditioned policy values across diverse domains, outperforming or extending previous baselines to continuous, high-dimensional settings.
- **DR-PPI** generated valid confidence intervals for the average policy value, demonstrating robustness in challenging environments where naive augmented baselines failed to cover the true value or produced impractically large intervals.
- Empirical evaluations across Inventory Control, Sepsis, D4RL HalfCheetah, and MIMIC-IV datasets validated the theoretical guarantees, showing the practical applicability of both methods in real-world scenarios.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Simply augmenting data in OPE without principled uncertainty quantification can lead to biased estimates and invalid confidence intervals.
> - Conformal prediction can be extended to continuous, high-dimensional MDPs for state-conditioned OPE by approximating importance weights, enabling valid uncertainty estimates where prior methods struggled.
> - A combination of doubly robust estimation and prediction-powered inference, using cross-fitting, provides a robust and asymptotically valid method for overall policy value estimation even with imperfect generative models.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Off-policy evaluation (OPE) is a subfield of reinforcement learning (RL) focused on estimating the value of a new policy using data collected from a different, existing behavior policy, without the need for direct deployment. This is particularly crucial in high-stakes domains, such as healthcare, education, and robotics, where deploying untested policies could incur significant costs or risks. Traditional OPE methods include Importance Sampling (IS), Direct Method (DM), and Doubly Robust (DR) approaches. However, these methods often encounter challenges related to dataset coverage, where the target policy might propose actions or states that are underrepresented in the available historical data, leading to biased or high-variance value estimates.

Recent advancements in OPE have explored the use of auxiliary data, often synthesized by generative models, to enhance dataset coverage and improve the accuracy of OPE estimates. While these data augmentation techniques have demonstrated potential in improving performance, they introduce a new challenge: the augmented data may be biased or drawn from a distribution that differs from the offline behavior policy. A significant gap in the existing literature is the lack of principled uncertainty quantification for OPE estimates when using augmented datasets. Current methods for generating confidence intervals (CIs) for OPE are typically asymptotic or sample-dependent and do not specifically address scenarios involving augmented data. Furthermore, methods adapted for Markov Decision Processes (MDPs) can be computationally intractable or have high variance in continuous, high-dimensional state spaces.

This research addresses this gap by proposing methodologies to construct statistically valid confidence intervals for OPE estimates that leverage augmented data. The work aims to provide reliable uncertainty estimates, which are essential for comparing policy values and making informed decisions in real-world, high-stakes applications, thereby contributing to the development of more robust and trustworthy OPE tools.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop methods for constructing valid confidence intervals for off-policy evaluation (OPE) when incorporating augmented datasets. This goal is driven by several motivations:

1. **Addressing the Limitations of Augmented Data in OPE:** While auxiliary datasets, particularly those generated by generative models, have been shown to enhance the accuracy of OPE by improving data coverage, their use can introduce biases due to inaccuracies in the generative process. Existing OPE methods that use data augmentation lack principled uncertainty quantification, making it difficult to assess the reliability of the resulting policy value estimates. The authors aim to rectify this by developing methods that explicitly account for potential distributional shifts and biases introduced by augmented data.

2. **Ensuring Reliable Uncertainty Estimates in High-Stakes Domains:** In critical applications such as healthcare, reliable uncertainty estimates are paramount. Decision-makers need to understand the range of possible outcomes for a new policy, not just a point estimate, to make informed comparisons and deployment decisions. The current absence of valid confidence intervals for OPE with augmented data impedes the application of these powerful techniques in such sensitive contexts.

3. **Providing State-Conditioned Confidence Intervals (Motivation for CP-Gen):** In many human-centered applications, stakeholders are interested in the expected value of a policy conditioned on a specific initial state, Vπ(s0). For instance, a clinician might want to evaluate a treatment policy for patients at a particular disease stage. Data for such specific initial states is often sparse, making data augmentation particularly valuable. However, existing conformal prediction methods for OPE in MDPs struggle with high-dimensional and continuous state spaces, and none directly incorporate augmented data. The motivation for CP-Gen is to overcome these challenges by developing a conformal prediction method capable of providing valid, state-conditioned confidence intervals in such settings, leveraging augmented data to improve data richness.

4. **Estimating Confidence Intervals for Average Policy Performance (Motivation for DR-PPI):** While state-conditioned confidence intervals are important, a common task in OPE is to estimate the average policy performance over a distribution of initial states, Vπ = Es0[Vπ(s0)]. Relying solely on CP-Gen for this average value would necessitate a union bound over individual state-conditioned intervals, which would likely result in impractically wide and uninformative confidence intervals. Therefore, there is a distinct need for a method that can provide valid confidence intervals for the overall, unconditional policy value. DR-PPI is motivated by this need, aiming to provide a robust and asymptotically valid confidence interval for the average policy value by combining doubly robust estimation techniques with concepts from prediction-powered inference, specifically designed to correct for potential inaccuracies in generated trajectories.

In summary, the research is motivated by the critical need for statistically valid uncertainty quantification in off-policy evaluation when augmented data is used, addressing both state-conditioned and overall policy value estimation in complex, high-dimensional environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors propose two distinct methodologies, CP-Gen and DR-PPI, to construct valid confidence intervals for OPE in settings that leverage auxiliary (augmented) data. Both methods are designed to account for potential biases or distributional shifts introduced by generative models used for data augmentation.

#### 3.1. Confidence Intervals for OPE Value Estimation from a Particular State: CP-Gen

CP-Gen (Conformal Prediction with Generated data) addresses the estimation of the value of a target policy conditioned on a particular initial state, Vπe(s0), which is relevant for personalized decision-making.

- **Core Idea:** CP-Gen estimates Vπe(s0) by combining the value derived from generated trajectories with a correction term. This correction term quantifies the difference between the returns of observed offline trajectories and corresponding generated trajectories that originate from the same initial state. The goal is to construct a confidence interval for this return difference, which, when added to the average return of the generated trajectories, provides a valid interval for Vπe(s0).
- **Conformal Prediction Adaptation:** The method adapts conformal prediction for high-dimensional MDPs. Conformal prediction typically provides prediction regions for individual predictions. A key challenge in this OPE context is accounting for the distributional shift between the behavior and target policies, which necessitates weighting calibration scores.
- **ϵ-Approximation for Importance Weights:** Prior conformal OPE methods relied on estimating importance weights, w(s, δrr′), which are expectations of the Inverse Propensity Score (IPS) ratio over observations sharing the same initial state (s) and return difference (δrr′). This exact calculation is intractable for continuous or high-dimensional state and reward spaces due to data sparsity. CP-Gen introduces an **ϵ-approximation** for these weights, wϵ(s, δrr′). This approximation estimates the weight using trajectories that are within an ϵs-radius of the initial state s and an ϵr-radius of the return difference δrr′. This approach assumes that IPS ratios are similar for trajectories starting in similar states and having similar return differences.
- **Algorithm 1 Steps:**
 1. The offline behavior dataset (Dπb) is split into a training set (Dtr) and a calibration set (Dcal).
 2. A generative model (T) is fitted using Dtr.
 3. For each trajectory in Dtr, M synthetic trajectories are generated under the behavior policy (πb), starting from the same initial state. Pairs of (original, generated) trajectories are recorded.
 4. Similarly, for each trajectory in Dcal, N synthetic trajectories are generated under πb with the same initial state, forming (original, generated) pairs.
 5. For each pair in Dcal, the ϵ-approximated weight wϵ(s, δrr′) is calculated using the (original, generated) pairs from Dtr (Equation 5).
 6. Non-conformity scores (Vj,n) are calculated as the return difference R(τj) - R(ˆτj,n) for each pair in Dcal.
 7. These scores and weights are used to construct a weighted empirical cumulative distribution function, F(x,y)n (Equation 7), from which the confidence interval ˆCn,α(s) is derived (Equation 6).
 8. Finally, the expected return from the generative model under the target policy, starting from state s, is combined with this interval to provide the final confidence interval for Vπe(s).

#### 3.2. Estimation of Average Policy Performance: DR-PPI

DR-PPI (Doubly Robust Prediction-Powered Inference) aims to estimate the overall average policy value, Vπe = Es0[Vπe(s0)], and provide valid confidence intervals for it. This method addresses the limitation of CP-Gen where a union bound over many state-conditioned intervals would be too wide.

- **Core Idea:** DR-PPI combines doubly robust estimation with prediction-powered inference (PPI). It generates an initial estimate of the policy value using a generative model (analogous to the Direct Method) and then corrects this estimate using an importance sampling (IS)-based term derived from the original offline data. This correction accounts for the potential inaccuracy of the generative model.
- **Cross-fitting:** To improve efficiency and reduce bias, the method employs a cross-fitting strategy. The offline behavior dataset (Dπb) is split into two equal parts, D1 and D2.
- **Algorithm 2 Steps:**
 1. A generative model (f1) is trained on D1.
 2. Nf rollouts are generated from f1 under the target policy (πe) to provide an initial model-based estimate of the policy value.
 3. For each trajectory τj in D2, M synthetic trajectories {ˆτm,j}M_m=1 are generated from f1, starting from the same initial state as τj.
 4. An IS-based correction term is calculated using D2: it is the difference between the re-weighted return of each original trajectory τj (e.g., using IS, WIS, or PDIS) and the average return of the M generated trajectories starting from τj's initial state (Equation 9).
 5. The first cross-fitted estimate, ˆVDR-PPI:1, is obtained by summing the model-based estimate and the average correction term from D2.
 6. The process is mirrored: a generative model (f2) is trained on D2, and a second estimate, ˆVDR-PPI:2, is calculated using D1 for correction.
 7. The final policy value estimate, ˆVDR-PPI, is the average of ˆVDR-PPI:1 and ˆVDR-PPI:2 (Equation 10).
 8. The variance of ˆVDR-PPI is estimated by combining plug-in estimates of the variance of the model-based and IS-based terms from both splits (Equation 11).
 9. An approximate confidence interval ˆCα is then constructed using the estimated value, its variance, and a standard normal quantile (Equation 12).

#### 3.3. Practical Considerations

- **IPS Ratio Clipping:** To manage high variance from large importance sampling ratios, especially when policies differ significantly, the authors apply clipping to the largest IPS ratios. They set the clipping constant at n^1/2 (where n is the number of samples), which is known to balance bias and variance.
- **Cross-fitting with Limited Data:** In situations where the behavior dataset is too small to be effectively split for cross-fitting, the authors suggest performing the procedure without sub-dividing the dataset. This can still yield valid but potentially higher-variance confidence intervals.

#### 3.4. Theoretical Guarantees

The methods are supported by theoretical guarantees under standard assumptions in OPE literature (common support, bounded reward, bounded IPS weights).

- **CP-Gen (Theorem 1):** Under additional assumptions of Lipschitz continuity for policies and transition dynamics, score smoothness, and bounded density, CP-Gen is proven to produce valid conformal prediction intervals within a small margin of error. Critically, the ϵ-approximation is shown to introduce a controlled error bound, enabling its use in continuous, high-dimensional settings.
- **DR-PPI (Theorem 2):** DR-PPI is proven to achieve asymptotically valid confidence intervals, regardless of the specific importance-sampling correction technique used (IS, WIS, or PDIS).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The empirical evaluation of CP-Gen and DR-PPI was conducted across four distinct domains: Inventory Control (continuous state/reward), Sepsis (8-dimensional state, 8 actions), D4RL HalfCheetah (17-dimensional state, continuous actions), and MIMIC-IV (real-world electronic health records, 20-dimensional state, 5 actions). The methods were compared against several baselines, including Importance Sampling (IS), Augmented Importance Sampling (AugIS), Direct Method (DM), Doubly Robust (DR), and Augmented Doubly Robust (AugDR).

#### 4.1. CP-Gen Performance

- **Valid Conformal Prediction Intervals:** CP-Gen consistently produced valid conformal prediction intervals that covered the true initial-state-conditioned policy value, Vπe(s), across all tested domains (Inventory Control, Sepsis, D4RL HalfCheetah, and MIMIC-IV). This was observed despite the use of the ϵ-approximation strategy for calculating importance weights, which was introduced to handle high-dimensional and continuous state/action spaces.
- **Superiority over Baselines:**
 - **DM-style Baseline:** A direct method baseline, which averages returns of synthetic trajectories starting from the given initial state, sometimes yielded biased results, particularly when the generative model quality was low (e.g., in D4RL, MIMIC-IV).
 - **Comparison with Foffano et al.:** In the Inventory Control domain (discretized to allow comparison), CP-Gen produced confidence intervals that were 25.69% tighter than those generated by the prior method of Foffano et al., while still covering the ground truth value. Furthermore, CP-Gen uniquely extends the applicability of conformal prediction for OPE to settings with continuous and high-dimensional states and rewards, which was not possible with the prior method.
- **Interval Tightness:** CP-Gen often achieved tight confidence intervals, demonstrating its efficiency in uncertainty quantification.

#### 4.2. DR-PPI Performance

- **Valid Confidence Intervals for Average Policy Value:** DR-PPI consistently generated valid confidence intervals that covered the true average policy value, Vπe, across all evaluated domains (Inventory Control, Sepsis, D4RL HalfCheetah, and MIMIC-IV). This aligns with the theoretical guarantees of asymptotic validity.
- **Challenges with Naive Augmentation Baselines:** Most baseline approaches that incorporated generated trajectories naively (e.g., AugIS, AugDR) produced biased intervals that often failed to cover the ground truth policy value. This highlights a critical flaw in simply augmenting data without principled uncertainty quantification, underscoring the necessity of methods like DR-PPI.
- **Robustness against Poor Model Quality:** In environments where Q-learning functions were difficult to train accurately (e.g., D4RL and MIMIC-IV), standard DR and AugDR baselines often resulted in impractically large, "exponentially large" confidence intervals, indicating their instability. DR-PPI, in contrast, maintained valid coverage even in these challenging settings.
- **Factors Influencing Performance:**
 - DR-PPI demonstrated better performance, yielding tighter confidence intervals, in stochastic environments where the learned generative model was reasonably accurate.
 - In deterministic environments (e.g., D4RL HalfCheetah) or settings with poor-quality generative models, DR-PPI's performance was comparable to IS baselines, suggesting that the benefits of variance reduction from comparing observed and generated trajectories were diminished in such cases.

In summary, the empirical results support the theoretical claims, demonstrating that both CP-Gen and DR-PPI successfully address the challenge of constructing valid confidence intervals for OPE when leveraging augmented data, outperforming or extending the capabilities of existing baselines, especially in complex, high-dimensional settings.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces principled methods for uncertainty quantification in off-policy evaluation (OPE) when leveraging auxiliary data, addressing a notable gap in the existing reinforcement learning literature. The significance and potential impact of this work span several dimensions:

1. **Enhanced Reliability and Trustworthiness of OPE:** By providing valid confidence intervals, the proposed methods (CP-Gen and DR-PPI) enable a more robust assessment of policy values derived from augmented datasets. This is crucial for practitioners to gauge the reliability of OPE estimates, compare different candidate policies with statistical confidence, and make informed decisions, especially in critical applications.

2. **Broader Applicability to High-Stakes Domains:**
 - **CP-Gen:** The development of CP-Gen, which provides state-conditioned confidence intervals using an ϵ-approximation, significantly expands the applicability of OPE to complex, high-dimensional, and continuous state and action spaces. This is particularly impactful for "human-centered applications" such as personalized medicine, education, and adaptive user interfaces, where decisions often hinge on a patient's or user's specific initial state. The ability to provide an uncertainty estimate for a policy's value *from a given starting state* is directly relevant to these domains.
 - **DR-PPI:** DR-PPI provides a robust method for estimating confidence intervals for the average policy performance, a common objective in policy selection and deployment. This is vital for overall policy comparisons and for safely deploying new policies by quantifying the risk associated with their estimated value.

3. **Leveraging Augmented Data Effectively and Safely:** The work demonstrates that while augmented data can improve OPE accuracy, its naive incorporation can lead to biased estimates and invalid confidence intervals. CP-Gen and DR-PPI offer principled frameworks to harness the benefits of data augmentation (e.g., improved coverage) while explicitly accounting for and correcting potential biases or inaccuracies introduced by generative models. This allows for the more effective and safer use of synthesized data in OPE.

4. **Advancements in Conformal Prediction and Doubly Robust Estimation:**
 - CP-Gen introduces a novel approach to applying conformal prediction in high-dimensional MDPs through its ϵ-approximation strategy, overcoming previous tractability issues. This extends the utility of conformal prediction to more complex RL environments.
 - DR-PPI integrates doubly robust estimation with prediction-powered inference, offering a robust and asymptotically valid method for overall policy value estimation, which has implications for causal inference in RL settings.

5. **Practical Implications for RL System Design:** The empirical validation across diverse simulators (robotics, healthcare, inventory management) and a real-world healthcare dataset (MIMIC-IV) demonstrates the practical utility of these methods. For RL system designers, the ability to obtain reliable uncertainty estimates enables more cautious and data-driven deployments, potentially accelerating the adoption of RL in sensitive environments.

In conclusion, this research significantly contributes to the field of safe and robust reinforcement learning by providing statistically sound methodologies for uncertainty quantification in off-policy evaluation with augmented data. The findings offer practical tools and theoretical guarantees that are expected to enhance the trustworthiness and applicability of OPE in a wide array of real-world, high-stakes decision-making scenarios.
