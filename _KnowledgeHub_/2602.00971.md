# Unveiling the Cognitive Compass: Theory-of-Mind-Guided Multimodal Emotion Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers introduced HitEmotion, a Theory-of-Mind (ToM)-based hierarchical benchmark for multimodal emotion understanding, and TMPO, a ToM-guided preference optimization framework. The TMPO framework, utilizing Qwen2.5-Omni-7B, surpassed proprietary models on 16 of 24 cognitively demanding tasks, moving MLLMs towards deeper mental state simulation rather than just emotion recognition.

## Problem

- Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) struggle with deep emotional understanding, often focusing on surface-level recognition and neglecting the dynamic, context-dependent nature of emotions linked to mental states.
- Existing evaluation benchmarks lack a unified cognitive framework (Theory of Mind) for systematically diagnosing MLLM capabilities in emotional reasoning, failing to identify specific breakpoints across increasing cognitive depths.
- Generic Chain-of-Thought (CoT) approaches in MLLMs frequently generate unfaithful or inconsistent reasoning chains, substituting causal attribution with simple template matching and lacking explicit tracking of intermediate mental states.

## Method

- Developed **HitEmotion**, a ==Theory-of-Mind== (ToM)-based hierarchical benchmark, by curating and organizing 24 datasets into three cognitive levels of increasing complexity to diagnose MLLM emotional intelligence.
- Proposed a novel Theory-of-Mind reasoning chain preference optimization (TMPO) framework that utilizes structured reasoning templates and leverages intermediate mental states as supervisory signals and reward sources for reinforcement learning.
- Constructed gold-standard ground-truth reasoning chains through a four-step pipeline involving AI generation, automated filtering and correction, self-reflection normalization, and human verification.

## Results

- Baseline MLLMs consistently showed declining performance as tasks increased in cognitive complexity across the HitEmotion benchmark, with no task at the highest 'Emotion Cognition and Reasoning' level achieving an average score above 60.
- The TMPO framework significantly improved the backbone model's performance, with the fully-optimized model (Qwen2.5-Omni-**7B**) outperforming leading proprietary systems on 16 of the 24 cognitively demanding tasks.
- ToM prompting provided clear performance advantages for capable models, especially at higher cognitive levels, but showed inconsistent gains or even deterioration for models with insufficient baseline reasoning capacity.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicit Theory-of-Mind (ToM) guidance is critical for advancing MLLMs' emotional reasoning, enabling a shift from general emergent abilities to domain-acquired skills with improved auditability.
> - Hierarchical evaluation benchmarks structured by cognitive depth, aligning with ToM developmental levels, are essential for precisely pinpointing MLLM capability breakpoints and diagnosing reasoning deficits.
> - A multi-dimensional reward function, incorporating structural adherence, content correctness, ToM-specific process language, and logical consistency, effectively refines MLLMs' reasoning chains for factual grounding and robustness.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of artificial intelligence has seen substantial advancements in Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs), enabling them to process and integrate information from various modalities such as text, images, and video. Despite this progress, the paper identifies a persistent limitation in MLLMs' capacity for deep emotional understanding. Existing approaches primarily focus on surface-level emotion recognition, often neglecting the dynamic, context-dependent nature of emotions and their intricate relationships with underlying mental states like beliefs and intentions. This oversimplification restricts MLLMs' interpretability and performance in complex affective intelligence tasks.

Recent benchmarks, including EmoBench and EmotionHallucer, have empirically demonstrated that state-of-the-art (SOTA) MLLMs encounter difficulties with emotionally complex tasks requiring perspective-taking or reasoning over conflicting multimodal cues, frequently leading to emotional hallucinations. However, the paper argues that these existing evaluations, while exposing symptoms, suffer from fragmented task designs that hinder a deeper diagnosis of the root causes. There is an identified absence of a unified cognitive framework, termed a "Cognitive Compass," to guide the systematic evaluation of mental state reasoning. Specifically, previous benchmarks do not organize emotional reasoning tasks according to the developmental levels of Theory of Mind (ToM), which delineates cognitive depth from first-order belief inference to second-order recursive reasoning. This lack of structured evaluation prevents the precise identification of a model's reasoning capability breakpoints.

Furthermore, the paper notes that while generic Chain-of-Thought (CoT) approaches in MLLMs can exhibit reasoning abilities, these tend to emerge from general properties rather than from cognition-specific, supervised training. This often results in reasoning chains that appear coherent but lack faithfulness, exhibiting issues such as substituting causal attribution with simple template matching, sensitivity to minor prompt changes, limited capacity for updating in response to counterfactuals, and an inability to explicitly track or maintain consistency among intermediate mental states. Consequently, current systems often function as shallow emotional "fact retrievers" rather than deep "mental state simulators" capable of inferring the complex interplay between mind and emotion.

This research aims to address these identified gaps by introducing a ToM-grounded framework for both evaluation and model enhancement. It seeks to shift the paradigm in emotion understanding from superficial fact retrieval to a more profound mental simulation, thereby advancing MLLMs towards more genuinely empathetic and affectively intelligent AI systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this work stems from the observed limitations in the emotional understanding capabilities of current Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs). Despite their advancements in various multimodal tasks, MLLMs frequently demonstrate suboptimal performance in tasks requiring deep emotional comprehension. This is attributed to their tendency to focus on surface-level emotion recognition while largely overlooking the complex, dynamic, and context-dependent nature of human emotions, which are intrinsically linked to other cognitive mental states such as beliefs and intentions. The researchers argue that a genuine affective intelligence necessitates explicit modeling of Theory of Mind (ToM), which serves as the cognitive foundation from which emotions emerge.

Existing evaluation benchmarks are noted to be insufficient in diagnosing the specific breakpoints of MLLM capabilities in emotional reasoning. They often provide only a coarse overall score, failing to systematically categorize tasks by increasing cognitive depth, particularly in relation to ToM developmental levels. This lack of diagnostic precision impedes the identification of fundamental flaws in model reasoning processes, which often manifest as superficial, template-matching, or inconsistent reasoning chains, rather than robust, cognition-specific, and faithful mental state simulations.

To address these dual shortcomings in evaluation and reasoning, the study sets forth two core objectives:

1. **To introduce HitEmotion:** This objective involves developing a Theory-of-Mind (ToM)-based hierarchical benchmark for multimodal emotion understanding. HitEmotion is designed to systematically organize evaluation tasks into three cognitive levels of increasing depth: Emotion Perception and Recognition, Emotion Understanding and Analysis, and Emotion Cognition and Reasoning. The motivation behind this hierarchical structure is to precisely pinpoint and measure a model's capability breakpoints across different cognitive demands, thus serving as a "Cognitive Compass" for diagnosing MLLM emotional intelligence.
2. **To propose a novel framework for Theory-of-Mind reasoning chain preference optimization (TMPO):** This objective aims to enhance MLLMs' emotional reasoning capabilities. The motivation is to move beyond generic Chain-of-Thought approaches by designing structured reasoning templates based on ToM principles. TMPO pioneers the use of intermediate mental states within these reasoning chains as both supervisory signals and reward sources for reinforcement learning. The goal is to cultivate a "domain-acquired" skill for emotional reasoning, as opposed to a "general emergent" ability, thereby boosting model performance, robustness, and auditability in complex emotional scenarios.

Collectively, these objectives are motivated by the goal of advancing MLLMs from being mere "emotional fact retrievers" to becoming "deep mental state simulators," ultimately contributing to the development of more genuinely empathetic and human-centric AI systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed in this research addresses both the evaluation and enhancement of multimodal emotion reasoning in MLLMs. It involves the construction of a new benchmark, HitEmotion, and the development of a novel training framework, TMPO.

#### 3.1. HitEmotion Benchmark Construction

The HitEmotion benchmark was constructed to provide a hierarchical and ToM-grounded evaluation resource.
- **Data Curation:** Researchers aggregated and curated 24 publicly available datasets from the field of multimodal affective computing. These datasets span diverse affective domains, including emotion recognition, sentiment analysis, humor understanding, and causal reasoning.
- **Hierarchical Organization:** The datasets were systematically organized into a three-tiered hierarchy reflecting increasing cognitive complexity, aligned with ToM principles:
 - **Level 1: Emotion Perception and Recognition (EPR):** This foundational level evaluates the ability to perceive and classify explicit emotional states across modalities (e.g., FESD, ISA, MESA, MER, MSA, OSA, SIA, SOER, SPER, SCEA).
 - **Level 2: Emotion Understanding and Analysis (EUA):** This intermediate level requires contextual awareness and relational reasoning to interpret emotions' functions and intents in situational settings (e.g., DPTM, EBIA, HU, IAVE, MABSA, MQE, MSD, MDER).
 - **Level 3: Emotion Cognition and Reasoning (ECR):** This highest level involves causal and second-order reasoning, requiring models to explain emotion causes, track temporal dynamics, and interpret nuanced expressions (e.g., EER, EI, LR, MECPE, SD, SFA).
- **Standardization and Quality Assurance:** Original task names, data structures, and evaluation metrics were preserved, but all datasets were unified into a standardized closed-label QA format. A rigorous quality assurance protocol involved dual-annotator cross-review and arbitration for a stratified one-third sample of each dataset to validate the consistency of "prompt-answer-context" triplets. Official test splits were exclusively used to prevent data leakage.

#### 3.2. Theory-of-Mind Reasoning Chain Preference Optimization (TMPO)

TMPO is a framework designed to enhance emotional understanding in MLLMs by guiding their reasoning processes.
- **Task Definition:** The objective is for an MLLM to infer an emotion-related output (o) and its underlying cognitive reasoning chain (τ) from multimodal inputs (Text T, Audio A, Video V), formally represented as (T, A, V) → (τ, o).
- **Ground-Truth Reasoning Chain Construction:** Since explicit reasoning chains (τ) are typically unavailable in existing datasets, a gold-standard version was constructed via a four-step pipeline:
 1. **Generation:** Initial candidate reasoning pathways were generated by GPT-5 using multimodal inputs and a tailored prompt, augmented by an auxiliary report from Qwen2-Audio to incorporate salient audio cues. Three independent candidate chains were produced.
 2. **Filtering & Correction:** Candidate chains were automatically evaluated against ground-truth labels. Chains yielding an exact match were preserved. Completely incorrect chains were flagged for intensive correction, while partially incorrect ones received targeted revisions by Gemini-2.5-pro.
 3. **Self-Reflection Normalization:** Corrected and initially correct chains underwent a final self-reflection step with GPT-4o to standardize formatting, ensure logical clarity, and refine ToM concept articulation.
 4. **Human Verification:** The refined reasoning chains were manually reviewed by two computer science PhD students to validate reasoning against source multimodal information, correcting any factual inaccuracies, logical inconsistencies, or misinterpretations.
- **ToM-Style Prompting Mechanism:** Task-specific prompts (P) were designed to elicit the desired reasoning chain (τ). These prompts are structured across three levels of cognitive complexity, corresponding to the HitEmotion hierarchy, to guide the model towards increasingly sophisticated reasoning:
 - **Level 1 (First-Order Mental State Attribution):** Guides the model to map multimodal cues to immediate emotional states, considering task-specific modalities.
 - **Level 2 (Relational & Contextual Mind Modeling):** Requires reasoning about the relationship between an emotional state and its context, such as linking an emotion to a specific target.
 - **Level 3 (Causal Attribution & Second-Order Reasoning):** Elicits reasoning about emotion causes and social interpretation, involving causal inference and inferring others' beliefs about a subject's state.
- **Stage 1: ToM-Aligned Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT):**
 - **Objective:** To establish a foundational capability for structured reasoning, enabling the model to generate responses that produce correct task-specific outputs and articulate the underlying cognitive process.
 - **Method:** A multimodal backbone model (Qwen2.5-Omni-7B) is fine-tuned to generate complete structured strings `y = <think>τ</think><answer>o</answer>`, explicitly disentangling intermediate reasoning steps (`τ`) from the final output (`o`).
 - **Loss Function:** Standard negative log-likelihood loss `L_SFT(θ) = -E[log π_θ(y|P, T, A, V)]`.
- **Stage 2: ToM-Based Preference Optimization with Group-wise Reward Policy Optimization (GRPO):**
 - **Objective:** To refine the SFT model further, ensuring generated reasoning chains are factually grounded, logically consistent, and robust.
 - **Process:** For a given prompt, N candidate outputs `y_i` are sampled from the current policy. Each candidate is evaluated using a custom, multi-dimensional reward function `R(y)`. The policy is updated via the GRPO objective to maximize `E[ (π_θ(y_i) / π_old(y_i)) A_i ] - β D_KL(π_θ || π_ref)`, where `A_i` are normalized advantage scores derived from `R(y_i)`, and the KL-divergence term stabilizes optimization.
 - **Reward Assignment:** The comprehensive reward function `R(y) = μ_1 R_structure + μ_2 R_content + μ_3 R_process + μ_4 R_consistency` consists of four components:
 - `R_structure`: Rewards adherence to the correct sequence of reasoning steps and presence of required step headers, with a task-aware design.
 - `R_content`: Evaluates the correctness of the final output `o` against the ground truth using standard task-specific metrics.
 - `R_process`: Promotes the use of ToM-specific language (e.g., "belief," "intention") within the reasoning chain, calculated as a normalized count of unique keywords.
 - `R_consistency`: Penalizes logical fallacies by assessing internal contradictions within `τ` and external contradictions with the input context, utilizing a large language model judge. Weights (`μ_1=0.4, μ_2=1.0, μ_3=0.1, μ_4=1.0`) prioritize correctness and logical grounding.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted using the HitEmotion benchmark and the TMPO framework yielded several key findings regarding the emotional intelligence capabilities of MLLMs.

**Deficiencies in Multimodal Emotion Analysis:**
The performance of baseline models on the HitEmotion benchmark substantiated the critique that current MLLMs exhibit significant limitations in multimodal emotion analysis.
- **Performance Hierarchy:** Across the three hierarchical levels of tasks, model performance consistently declined as task complexity increased. At the foundational Level 1 (Emotion Perception and Recognition), only three out of ten tasks achieved average scores above 60, even for top-performing models. For instance, Gemini-2.5-Pro reached 78.39 on FESD, while many models remained around the 50-point range. As tasks moved to Level 2 (Emotion Understanding and Analysis), only two tasks surpassed the 60-point threshold. Critically, within the cognitively demanding Level 3 (Emotion Cognition and Reasoning), no task achieved an average score above 60. This performance gradient confirms the benchmark's ability to differentiate models across distinct levels of emotional reasoning.
- **Persistent Weaknesses:** The results indicate that current MLLMs possess rudimentary emotional intelligence and continue to struggle with higher-order emotional reasoning tasks that require implicit inference, causal attribution, structured extraction, or nuanced pragmatic understanding (e.g., dialogue dynamics, sarcasm, humor).

**Model Performance Characteristics:**
- **Closed-Source Models Lead:** Proprietary models, specifically the GPT and Gemini series, consistently outperformed open-source counterparts. This advantage is attributed to their larger parameter scales and extensive pretraining on diverse datasets. For example, Gemini-2.5-Pro achieved 78.39 on the FESD task and GPT-4.1 reached 71.46 in zero-shot settings, notably surpassing most open-source models. The Gemini series generally showed superior multimodal emotion recognition due to their native capacity for processing video and audio inputs.
- **Open-Source Potential with Fine-tuning:** While proprietary models maintained a lead, task-specific fine-tuning demonstrated the potential for open-source models to achieve competitive results. Models like Emotion-LLaMA-7B and Qwen2.5-VL-32B, despite being smaller, showed improvements that allowed them to approach the performance of some zero-shot proprietary models on specific tasks, suggesting that targeted optimization can help bridge the capability gap.

**Effects of ToM Prompting:**
- **Benefits for Capable Models:** The application of ToM prompting provided clear performance advantages for closed-source models (GPT-4.1, Gemini-2.5-Pro) and high-capacity open-source models (Qwen2.5-VL-32B, InternVL3-38B). These improvements were observed across most tasks and became more pronounced at higher cognitive levels, indicating that models with stronger baseline reasoning abilities are better equipped to leverage intermediate reasoning chains provided by ToM-guided prompts.
- **Inconsistent Gains for Weaker Models:** Conversely, models with insufficient representational and reasoning capacity did not exhibit consistent gains from ToM prompting and, in some instances, showed performance deterioration. This suggests that weaker models are more prone to generating hallucinations when attempting to exploit ToM principles, compromising the integrity of the reasoning chain.
- **Ablation Study on ToM Prompting:** An ablation study confirmed the effectiveness of ToM-style prompting. ToM-Init (establishing a cognitive reasoning path) consistently outperformed generic Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting. ToM-Full (integrating explicit task-relevant ToM keywords) further yielded substantial performance gains over ToM-Init, validating the importance of explicitly incorporating key ToM concepts for maximizing reasoning potential.

**TMPO Framework Effectiveness:**
- **Substantial Performance Uplift:** The TMPO framework demonstrated consistent and significant performance improvements for the backbone model across all task categories. Both the Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) stage and, particularly, the Group-wise Reward Policy Optimization (GRPO) stage contributed to this success.
- **Outperforming Proprietary Models:** On complex tasks requiring nuanced reasoning (Level 2 and 3), the fully-optimized TMPO model frequently surpassed the performance of top-tier proprietary models, emerging as the leading performer on 16 of the 24 tasks. This highlights TMPO's capability in teaching models to reason effectively.
- **Perceptual Limitations:** For some direct, perception-driven tasks (Level 1), the TMPO model still lagged behind some leading systems. This suggests that the reasoning-focused optimization of TMPO cannot fully overcome inherent limitations in the base model's raw multimodal perception capabilities.
- **Reward Components Ablation:** An ablation study on the reward function components confirmed their synergistic importance. `R_structure` was foundational, preventing "Format Collapse" by enforcing the required XML schema. `R_content` provided the most substantial gain by directly optimizing for correct final answers. `R_consistency` further boosted performance by eliminating logical fallacies. `R_process` offered complementary refinement by encouraging ToM-specific terminology.

In summary, the experiments establish HitEmotion as a robust diagnostic tool for MLLM emotional intelligence and TMPO as an effective method for enhancing deep emotional reasoning, particularly for cognitively demanding tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of artificial intelligence by addressing fundamental limitations in the emotional understanding capabilities of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs). The significance and potential impact of this work are multi-faceted:

- **Advancing Affective Intelligence:** By introducing an explicit modeling of Theory of Mind (ToM), the work provides a cognitive substrate for genuine affective intelligence in MLLMs. This approach aims to move MLLMs beyond superficial emotion recognition towards a deeper, more human-like mental state simulation, fostering the development of truly empathetic AI systems.
- **Comprehensive Evaluation Toolkit:** The HitEmotion benchmark offers the research community a practical and robust toolkit for systematically diagnosing MLLMs' capabilities in emotional understanding. Its hierarchical structure, grounded in developmental levels of ToM, allows for precise identification of capability breakpoints across increasing cognitive depths. This specificity is crucial for identifying underlying reasoning deficits, rather than merely observing performance symptoms.
- **Enhanced Reasoning Methodology:** The proposed TMPO framework, incorporating a ToM-guided reasoning chain and preference optimization with process-level supervision, provides a novel method for enhancing MLLMs' emotional reasoning. By training models to generate more faithful and coherent rationales through explicit mental state tracking, it shifts reasoning from a "general emergent" ability to a "domain-acquired" skill. This improvement in auditability and robustness is vital for deploying MLLMs in sensitive, human-centric applications.
- **Bridging Performance Gaps:** The demonstration that the TMPO-optimized model can surpass leading proprietary systems on many cognitively demanding tasks indicates a practical pathway for open-source models to achieve advanced emotional intelligence. This could democratize access to sophisticated emotional reasoning capabilities, reducing reliance on large, closed-source models.
- **Improved Interpretability and Trust:** By encouraging MLLMs to articulate their reasoning processes through structured, ToM-aligned chains, the framework inherently improves the interpretability of their emotional understanding. Models that can explain *why* an emotion is inferred, rather than just *what* the emotion is, are likely to be more trusted and effective in real-world human-AI interactions.
- **Future Research Directions:** The identified limitations, such as the dependence on base model perceptual capabilities and the scope of ToM applicability primarily to social reasoning, also outline clear directions for future research. This includes scaling TMPO to larger omni-modal models and exploring its utility in broader social intelligence domains beyond emotion.

In conclusion, this work provides both the diagnostic tools and the enhancement methodologies necessary to push MLLMs beyond rudimentary emotion recognition. By integrating psychological theory into computational models, it facilitates the development of AI systems capable of more nuanced, profound, and reliable emotional understanding, which is a critical step towards more human-centric and intelligent AI.
