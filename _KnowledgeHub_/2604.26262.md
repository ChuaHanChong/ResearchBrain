# Semantic Foam: Unifying Spatial and Semantic Scene Decomposition

> [!summary] Summary
> Semantic Foam introduces a 3D scene representation that unifies spatial and semantic decomposition by augmenting Radiant Foam's explicit Voronoi mesh with identity features and spatial regularization. This approach enables direct, post-processing-free interaction and editing of 3D scenes, achieving competitive mIoU in object-level segmentation and maintaining high reconstruction fidelity.

## Problem

- Modern 3D reconstruction methods (e.g., 3DGS, NeRFs) produce unstructured representations (millions of primitives) lacking inherent semantic understanding, hindering interactive applications.
- Challenges exist in consistently propagating 2D semantic labels into 3D, leading to issues like unlabeled object interiors, inconsistent signals, and imprecise boundaries in existing neural field and point-based methods.
- Current semantic segmentation techniques often struggle with non-convex objects or require complex post-processing heuristics, limiting direct manipulation of reconstructed scenes.

## Method

- **Semantic Foam** extends Radiant Foam's ==non-overlapping Voronoi mesh== by associating an ==Identity Encoding feature== with each 3D cell to represent semantic instances.
- Introduces a ==2D Identity Loss== (cross-entropy against DEVA masks) and a ==Total Variation Loss== for spatial smoothness.
- Integrates these losses with photometric and geometry regularization, enabling simultaneous learning of appearance, geometry, and semantics.

## Results

- **Semantic Foam** achieved **0.82 mIoU** on Mip-NeRF360, outperforming baseline Gaussian-based methods.
- Successfully enabled direct object extraction, removal, and insertion with robust handling of non-convex object geometries.
- Reconstruction fidelity (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS) remained nearly identical to the original Radiant Foam.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Augmenting an explicit volumetric mesh structure, like Radiant Foam's Voronoi diagram, with learnable identity features provides an intrinsic, differentiable framework for unified spatial and semantic scene decomposition.
> - An explicit spatial regularization, such as the Total Variation loss applied to identity features across adjacent Voronoi cells, is crucial for achieving clean, consistent semantic boundaries and complete object interiors in 3D.
> - This unified representation intrinsically supports direct 3D scene editing tasks like object extraction, removal, and insertion without requiring complex post-processing or being limited to convex object shapes.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of 3D scene reconstruction has advanced significantly, with modern techniques capable of generating detailed and photorealistic representations from multi-view images. Methods such as Neural Radiance Fields (NeRFs) and particularly 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) have enabled high-fidelity view synthesis, with 3DGS achieving real-time rendering speeds. However, a limitation of many high-performing reconstruction methods is their output format: millions of unstructured primitives (e.g., Gaussians) or continuous volumetric fields (e.g., NeRFs). These representations often lack inherent semantic understanding, making them challenging to use in downstream applications that require interaction, editing, or processing of individual objects.

Previous research has attempted to integrate semantic decomposition into these models. NeRF-based approaches have extended their continuous volumetric fields with dedicated probability or feature branches, often supervised by lifting 2D semantic features or labels from foundation models like SAM, DINO, or CLIP. While these methods enable semantic understanding, the continuous nature of NeRFs typically necessitates post-processing heuristics to decompose the scene into distinct physical regions, limiting their utility for precise object manipulation.

Similarly, 3DGS-based methods have associated semantic information with individual Gaussian primitives. Approaches like SA3D, LabelGS, GaussianCut, SAGA, iSegMan, FeatureSplatting, LangSplat, and Gaussian Grouping attach labels or feature vectors to Gaussians, often supervised by 2D masks. However, these point-based representations are not truly volumetric, leading to several challenges. These include unlabeled primitives in object interiors, artifacts caused by occlusion or inconsistent supervision across views, and semantic ambiguity from overlapping primitives. Furthermore, the unstructured spatial layout of Gaussians can make neighborhood-based regularization computationally expensive and result in imprecise object boundaries. For instance, Gaussian Grouping might use convex hulls for extraction, which can remove unrelated geometry and restrict extraction to convex objects.

This work introduces Semantic Foam, building upon the recently proposed Radiant Foam representation. Radiant Foam combines aspects of point-based methods (for efficiency) with the volumetric nature of NeRFs through a non-overlapping, differentiable Voronoi mesh. By extending Radiant Foam, this research aims to overcome the limitations of both NeRF and 3DGS semantic decomposition approaches by providing a representation that offers both explicit spatial decomposition and integrated, spatially consistent semantic understanding, thereby enabling direct and clean scene editing. This positions Semantic Foam as an advancement towards unifying spatial and semantic scene understanding in a structured, editable 3D representation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to develop a 3D scene representation that intrinsically supports both spatial and semantic decomposition, facilitating intuitive interaction and editing within reconstructed scenes. This goal is driven by several motivations rooted in the limitations of existing methods:

1. **Bridging the Gap between Photorealistic Reconstruction and Semantic Understanding:** Modern 3D reconstruction techniques, while achieving high visual fidelity, often yield unstructured representations (e.g., millions of Gaussian primitives or continuous neural fields). This lack of semantic labeling makes it difficult for applications to interact with, modify, or process individual objects within a reconstructed scene. The motivation is to move beyond mere visual realism to a representation that understands the logical and physical structure of objects.

2. **Addressing Challenges in 3D Semantic Labeling from 2D Data:** Ground truth 3D semantic volumetric labels are scarce. Consequently, most methods rely on lifting 2D semantic labels or features (often generated by large computer vision foundation models) into 3D. This process, however, is prone to inconsistencies due to varying viewpoints, occlusions, and the inherent 2D nature of the supervision signal. A key motivation is to develop a method that can derive consistent 3D semantic information from 2D supervision despite these challenges.

3. **Overcoming Limitations of Continuous Neural Field Representations:** NeRF-based models, while powerful, represent scenes as continuous volumetric fields. Decomposing these continuous fields into distinct, editable physical regions often requires complex post-processing heuristics, which limits their practicality for direct manipulation. The motivation is to enable a representation that inherently facilitates discrete object segmentation without additional processing.

4. **Mitigating Artifacts and Inconsistencies in Point-Based Representations (3DGS):** Gaussian Splatting methods, while fast, are not "truly" volumetric. This leads to issues such as unlabeled regions within object interiors, semantic ambiguities from overlapping Gaussians, and artifacts arising from a lack of semantic connectivity or inconsistent supervision across views. For example, methods like Gaussian Grouping may resort to convex hull approximations for object extraction, which can lead to the removal of unintended geometry or restrict editing to convex shapes. The motivation is to create a semantic representation that is truly volumetric, spatially consistent, and does not suffer from these point-based limitations.

5. **Leveraging Structured Spatial Decomposition for Semantic Regularization:** The Radiant Foam representation, which uses a Voronoi mesh, naturally provides an explicit, non-overlapping spatial partitioning of the scene. This inherent structure offers a unique opportunity for applying spatial regularization. The motivation is to exploit this explicit connectivity to enforce smoothness and consistency in learned semantic features, even in regions that lack direct image-space supervision due to occlusion.

To address these motivations, the specific objectives of Semantic Foam are:
- To extend Radiant Foam's volumetric mesh to incorporate and learn label-aware 3D segmentation.
- To introduce a novel volumetric total variation loss, leveraging the Voronoi adjacency graph, to enforce spatial smoothness and feature consistency across cells.
- To enable robust scene editing capabilities, including precise object extraction, removal, and insertion, without requiring post-processing steps or suffering from common artifacts.
- To demonstrate that this approach can achieve segmentation performance comparable to or superior to state-of-the-art Gaussian-based methods.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Semantic Foam extends the Radiant Foam representation to integrate semantic decomposition within its volumetric mesh structure. The methodology involves augmenting Radiant Foam’s primitives with semantic information and introducing a specialized loss function for training.

#### 3.1. Radiant Foam Preliminaries

The foundation of Semantic Foam is Radiant Foam, a 3D scene representation based on a non-overlapping, differentiable volumetric mesh derived from a Voronoi diagram. The scene space is partitioned into convex polyhedral cells ($C_i$), each associated with a primal vertex ($p_i$). Within each cell, the volumetric radiance field is parameterized by a constant density ($\sigma$) and view-dependent appearance (spherical harmonics coefficients $c$). Volume rendering integrates these properties along a ray to compute the final radiance. While Radiant Foam provides effective spatial partitioning, its original formulation focuses solely on appearance and geometry, lacking the semantic categorization required for scene editing.

#### 3.2. Semantic Foam Extension

To introduce semantic capabilities, Semantic Foam augments each Voronoi cell with a new attribute: an **Identity Encoding Feature** ($f$). This feature is a piecewise constant, view-independent vector assigned to each cell, designed to represent 3D semantic instances or object identities. The view-independent nature is chosen because semantic information is considered an intrinsic property of an object, irrespective of viewing direction.

During training, a **differentiable semantic identity rendering** ($I_r$) is computed along each ray, analogous to the standard appearance rendering. This involves integrating the Identity Encoding Features ($f_n$) along the ray using the volume rendering formulation:
$I_r = \sum_{n=1}^{N} T_n \cdot (1 - \exp(-\sigma_n \delta_n)) \cdot f_n$
where $T_n$ is the transmittance and $\delta_n$ is the segment width. The resulting $I_r$ vector, representing the 3D-consistent semantic projection into image space, is then mapped to $K$ semantic identity classes using a single linear layer followed by a softmax function:
$p_k = \text{softmax}_k(\text{LL}(I_r))$
where $p_k$ are the probabilities for class $k$, and LL(·) is the linear layer. This process is optimized jointly with Radiant Foam's native photometric loss.

#### 3.3. Loss Function

The identity features are trained using two primary losses:
- **2D Identity Loss ($L_{identity}$):** This is a cross-entropy loss that supervises the rendered identity features ($I_r$) against ground-truth 2D semantic segmentation masks. During training, the predicted class probabilities ($p_k$) are compared to the one-hot ground-truth labels ($y_k$) in image space:
 $L_{identity} = \sum_{k=1}^{K} -y_k \log(p_k)$
 Ground-truth masks are generated using the Decoupled Video Segmentation Approach (DEVA) in "everything" mode for all training views.
- **Total-Variation (TV) Loss ($L_{TV}$):** To enforce spatial smoothness and prevent noisy identity features, especially in unobserved regions (like object interiors), a total-variation loss is applied. This loss leverages the explicit mesh adjacency of the Voronoi diagram. It is defined as a weighted L1 loss between the identity feature $f_i$ of a cell and the identity features $f_j$ of its adjacent neighbors ($N(i)$):
 $L_{TV,i} = \sum_{j \in N(i)} ||f_i - f_j||_1 \cdot A_{ij}$
 where $A_{ij}$ is the surface area of the mutual face shared by cells $c_i$ and $c_j$. The total TV loss is the mean of $L_{TV,i}$ across all cells.

The overall training objective combines these losses with Radiant Foam's native rendering loss ($L_{rgba}$) and quantile loss ($L_{quantile}$):
$L = L_{rgba} + \lambda_1 L_{quantile} + \lambda_2 L_{identity} + \lambda_3 L_{TV}$

#### 3.4. Optimization

The optimization protocol largely follows Radiant Foam. Training begins with a sparse point cloud initialized by COLMAP. Throughout training (20k iterations), Radiant Foam's densification and pruning heuristics are applied: new Voronoi sites are introduced in under-represented areas, and unutilized cells are removed. When new cells are created, their identity encoding feature is cloned from the parent. Specific learning rates with cosine annealing schedules are used for all parameters (position, density, spherical harmonics, and identity encodings). The weight for $L_{TV}$ ($\lambda_3$) is exponentially decayed to reduce over-smoothing. Gradient propagation through the face area for $L_{TV}$ is halted, and the value is clamped to a minimum of 1. Density gradients are also stopped from flowing through $L_{identity}$ to prevent unwanted density variations from noisy segmentation masks.

#### 3.5. Semantic Foam for Scene Editing

Once trained, the per-cell identity encoding features are passed through the learned linear layer to determine the semantic class for each cell. This enables several scene editing capabilities:
- **Object Extraction:** For a target semantic class, high-density cells belonging to that class are selected. To obtain a complete object boundary (the "shell"), the 1-ring neighbors of these high-density cells, including adjacent empty cells sharing the same semantic identity, are also extracted. This method utilizes the explicit Voronoi diagram adjacency.
- **Object Removal:** Identified cells of a target semantic class are pruned from the representation. The Voronoi diagram is then re-triangulated to maintain a continuous, non-overlapping partition of the remaining space.
- **Object Insertion:** Any existing Voronoi cells in the insertion region are deleted. The primal vertices of the new object are introduced, and the mesh is re-triangulated to seamlessly integrate it into the global scene.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several findings regarding the performance and capabilities of Semantic Foam across various tasks and datasets.

#### 4.1. Quantitative Segmentation Performance

Semantic Foam was evaluated against state-of-the-art Gaussian-based segmentation methods (LabelGS, Gaussian Grouping, and SAGA) on 16 scenes from the Mip-NeRF360, LERF-Mask, and LLFF datasets.
- **mIoU (mean Intersection over Union):** Semantic Foam achieved competitive or superior mIoU scores. For example, on Mip-NeRF360, it showed 0.82 mIoU compared to SAGA's 0.81. Similarly, it performed well on LERF-Mask and LLFF, often matching or slightly surpassing baseline methods (Table 1). The paper notes that higher mIoU with comparable mAcc (mean Accuracy) indicates precise boundaries and avoidance of over-segmentation, a common issue in Gaussian-based methods.
- **mAcc (mean Accuracy):** Semantic Foam generally maintained mAcc scores on par with existing baselines.

#### 4.2. Reconstruction Fidelity

A comparison of reconstruction metrics (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS) between Semantic Foam and the original Radiant Foam (Table 3) indicated that the inclusion of semantic learning did not significantly degrade the model's ability to reconstruct scene appearance and geometry. Semantic Foam preserved nearly identical reconstruction fidelity.

#### 4.3. Qualitative Segmentation and Object Extraction

Qualitative comparisons (Figures 4 and 8) demonstrated that Semantic Foam produces clean, well-localized segmentation masks and high-fidelity object extractions. In contrast, prior Gaussian-based methods frequently generated dilated segmentations that incorporated background regions or exhibited over-/under-segmentation, resulting in less accurate object boundaries. Semantic Foam's approach yielded masks that more faithfully captured the true object structure.

#### 4.4. Ablation Study: Impact of Total Variation Loss

An ablation study (Table 2, Figure 7) confirmed the importance of the Total Variation (TV) loss.
- Without $L_{TV}$, the model showed a noticeable decline in segmentation quality, producing object masks that failed to capture complete objects and exhibiting noisy identity features.
- With $L_{TV}$ incorporated, the model yielded cleaner and more structured identity encodings, enhancing the fidelity of segmentation maps and leading to improved mIoU and mAcc scores.

#### 4.5. Scene Editing Capabilities

Semantic Foam demonstrated robust capabilities for scene editing tasks (Figures 5, 9, 10):
- **Object Extraction:** The method enabled seamless and native extraction of objects, including non-convex shapes, directly from the scene. This contrasted with Gaussian-based methods like Gaussian Grouping, which often rely on post-hoc heuristics (e.g., convex-hull construction) that can lead to imprecise extractions or the removal of unintended geometry.
- **Object Removal:** By pruning relevant Voronoi cells, Semantic Foam allowed for effective object deletion without introducing structural artifacts or requiring auxiliary cleanup steps.
- **Object Insertion:** The representation supported coherent insertion of novel assets by localizing the insertion region and re-triangulating the Voronoi mesh to accommodate new primal sites, preserving overall geometric consistency.

#### 4.6. Efficiency

Efficiency measurements (Table 7) showed that Semantic Foam largely preserved the training and inference efficiency of Radiant Foam. The inclusion of identity features resulted in an approximately 18% increase in model size, which is considered a reasonable trade-off for enabling semantic segmentation capabilities.

#### 4.7. Additional Comparisons

Further quantitative comparisons (Table 8) against InstanceGaussian and GARField on the LERF-Masked dataset indicated that Semantic Foam surpassed both methods in average mIoU metrics, demonstrating its competitive performance against other advanced segmentation approaches.

#### 4.8. Limitations

The authors acknowledge two primary limitations:
- **Reliance on 2D Supervision:** The pipeline's dependence on 2D image-based foundation models for supervision can lead to inconsistent signals across different images, as these models are not inherently 3D-aware.
- **Discrepancy in Evaluation vs. Supervision:** The 3D segmentation process is evaluated per-primitive, while training supervision occurs on features accumulated through volume rendering. This potential misalignment could be suboptimal.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

Semantic Foam represents a notable advancement in 3D scene representation, offering a unified framework for both spatial and semantic decomposition. Its significance and potential impact span several areas:

1. **Enabling Interactive 3D Graphics and Content Creation:** By providing a structured, semantically aware representation that allows for direct object extraction, removal, and insertion without complex post-processing, Semantic Foam addresses a major bottleneck in interactive graphics applications. This capability could significantly streamline workflows for 3D content creators, game developers, and AR/VR experiences, making it easier to manipulate reconstructed real-world scenes.

2. **Advancing 3D Scene Understanding:** The method contributes to the broader goal of 3D scene understanding by offering a representation that not only renders photorealistically but also intrinsically understands the semantic categories and spatial boundaries of objects. This deeper understanding is crucial for applications requiring intelligent interaction with environments, such as robotics, autonomous navigation, and intelligent virtual assistants.

3. **Improved Segmentation Quality and Consistency:** Unlike many existing point-based or continuous field methods, Semantic Foam's volumetric mesh and explicit spatial regularization (via the Total Variation loss) lead to cleaner, more precise, and spatially consistent semantic boundaries, particularly for non-convex objects and in regions with limited direct observation. This enhanced quality is vital for reliable downstream tasks.

4. **Foundation for Robust Scene Editing:** The ability to perform editing operations like object extraction, deletion, and insertion natively within the representation, without resorting to heuristic post-processing, provides a more robust and artifact-free editing experience. This fidelity in editing could foster new possibilities for creating mixed-reality environments or synthesizing novel scenes from captured data.

5. **Bridging the Gap between Reconstruction and Application:** Semantic Foam helps bridge the gap between advanced 3D reconstruction techniques, which often yield unstructured data, and practical applications that demand semantically meaningful and editable 3D models. This makes high-fidelity reconstruction more accessible and useful for a wider range of downstream tasks.

6. **Direction for Future Research:** The identified limitations, particularly the reliance on 2D supervision and the potential misalignment between per-primitive evaluation and accumulated feature supervision, highlight specific avenues for future research. This includes exploring 3D-aware foundation models for supervision signals and refining loss functions to ensure better alignment, which could further enhance the robustness and accuracy of semantic decomposition.
