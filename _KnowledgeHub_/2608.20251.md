# Video2DoorTraversal: Push Door Traversal via Simulated Door Twins

> [!summary] Summary
> Video2DoorTraversal turns one RGB video into a deployable door-traversal policy for wheel-legged robots: DoorTwin reconstructs a metric, articulated sim-ready door twin, a simulation-in-the-loop agent generates verified expert trajectories, and ArticuACT learns base-arm coordination from onboard depth, reaching 96.57% real-world success.

## Problem

- Prior articulated-object learners (DoorGym, UniDoorManip, ArticuBot) mostly cover door opening from large-scale simulation but not full doorway traversal.
- Legged/humanoid traversal systems rely on preconstructed or procedurally generated doors, manual task structure, or extensive reward design rather than instance-specific reconstruction.
- Real-to-sim-to-real methods (Human2Sim2Robot, X-SIM, SIMPACT) mainly target fixed-base rigid-object manipulation, need extra scene scans, and rarely use articulation as an explicit task representation.

## Method

- **DoorTwin**: single-video pipeline combining DAGE metric geometry estimation, SAM 3 segmentation, and Articraft-based articulation-aware generation with reference-view rendering and critique to build an instance-aligned door twin.
- ==Simulation-in-the-loop agentic expert generation==: converts recovered articulation into a parameterized skill program (BaseMoveTo, RotateHandle, PushDoor, Pass, etc.) and iteratively diagnoses and refines failed Isaac Gym rollouts into executable demonstrations.
- **ArticuACT**: an ==Action Chunking Transformer== variant with robot-centric Plucker ray-map conditioning across dual depth cameras and auxiliary future interaction-state prediction (contact, handle-rotation, door-opening progress) supervising base, arm, and gripper commands.

## Results

- Real-world evaluation across five doors: **96.57%** average traversal success versus **65.71%** for vanilla ACT, **36.58%** OA Replay, and **18.29%** direct replay.
- Simulation benchmark: **97.27%** traversal success versus **66.41%** DP3, **64.45%** Diffusion Policy, **50.78%** UniDoorManip, and **12.50%** DoorGym.
- Ablations: Plucker conditioning plus interaction-state prediction lift joint-command traversal success from **64.84%** to **97.27%**; full agentic trajectory generation beats rule-based baseline by **11.25** points and no-rollout variant by **37.50** points; zero-shot cross-door success reaches **80.95%**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Using articulation as the shared task representation lets one reconstruction pipeline jointly serve asset generation, expert-trajectory synthesis, and policy deployment, not just one stage.
> - Simulator rollout feedback is what makes agentic trajectory generation reliable; geometric alignment or VLM reasoning alone cannot produce physically executable demonstrations.
> - Explicit pixel-to-robot-frame geometric conditioning (Plucker rays) plus auxiliary contact/progress prediction generalizes as a recipe for contact-rich, camera-viewpoint-sensitive mobile manipulation beyond doors specifically.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Autonomous door opening and traversal represents a complex, long-horizon loco-manipulation challenge for mobile robots in human-centric environments. This task requires not only precise manipulation of a door handle but also coordinated control of the robot's base and arm to approach the door, unlock it, manage interaction with a moving panel, and finally pass through a narrow opening without collision.

Existing research has addressed various aspects of this problem, but typically in isolation or with specific limitations. Some approaches, such as DoorGym, UniDoorManip, and ArticuBot, focus on learning articulated-object manipulation, primarily door opening, from large-scale simulation. However, these often assume preconstructed or procedurally generated door assets and do not comprehensively tackle the full doorway traversal task. Other methods enhance real-world robustness through online adaptation or haptic feedback, or achieve traversal using reinforcement learning or teacher–student training on legged and humanoid platforms. These systems often depend on manually specified task structures, extensive reward engineering, external geometric inputs, or additional real-world adaptation for each new door instance.

The concept of "real-to-sim-to-real" learning aims to reduce the need for extensive real-world robot data by leveraging human videos or real observations to generate simulation environments and training data. Prior work in this area, like HUMAN2SIM2ROBOT, X-SIM, and Video2Sim2Real, has demonstrated success in transferring task information for embodiment-specific policy learning, but primarily for fixed-base rigid-object manipulation and often requiring additional scene or object scans. Other methods, such as DemoGen, SIMPACT, RoboSnap, and V-Dreamer, utilize reconstructed or generated simulations for demonstration generation, planning, or evaluation. However, many of these approaches treat manipulated objects as rigid bodies or reconstruct generic scene geometry without explicitly leveraging articulated kinematics as a task representation. Furthermore, simulation is often employed in only one stage of the pipeline rather than holistically supporting instance-specific reconstruction, executable demonstration generation, and closed-loop mobile deployment.

This work contributes to the landscape by addressing the central challenge of bridging instance-specific articulation and closed-loop execution for complete door traversal. It proposes a comprehensive real-to-sim-to-real pipeline that connects a digital twin reconstructed from a single RGB video with simulator-verified expert generation and onboard depth-based policy execution. Unlike previous methods, it does not rely on human-motion retargeting, online adaptation, or externally provided door poses, but instead derives the task parameters directly from a single video and executes the complete behavior using onboard perception for wheel-legged mobile manipulators.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enable autonomous door opening and traversal for wheel-legged mobile manipulators in real-world environments, specifically focusing on push doors. The authors aim to achieve this through a real-to-sim-to-real framework that minimizes human intervention and real-world data collection.

The motivation for this work stems from several challenges inherent in the door traversal task and limitations of existing approaches:

1. **Complexity of Door Traversal:** The task is a long-horizon, contact-rich loco-manipulation problem that demands precise handle interaction, coordinated base-arm control, and collision-free passage through a narrow doorway. This complexity makes it difficult for robots to execute reliably.
2. **Limitations of Prior Work:**
 - **Partial Solutions:** Many existing methods only address isolated components, such as handle manipulation or door pushing, rather than the complete approach-open-traverse sequence.
 - **Reliance on Pre-existing Assets/Knowledge:** Approaches often depend on preconstructed or procedurally generated door models, or require manual specification of task structures, extensive reward engineering, or external geometric inputs (e.g., 6D object poses or point clouds). This limits their applicability to new, unseen doors.
 - **Real-world Adaptation Burden:** Some systems require significant real-world data collection or per-door adaptation, which is time-consuming and resource-intensive.
3. **Bridging Instance-Specific Articulation and Closed-Loop Execution:** A significant challenge in real-to-sim-to-real pipelines is ensuring that the simulated environment is sufficiently accurate to train policies that transfer effectively to the real world. For articulated objects like doors, this means the reconstructed digital twin must be metrically and kinematically accurate to support contact-rich simulation, and the learned behavior must be robust to perception and dynamics discrepancies during real-world deployment.
4. **Single-Video Input for Generality:** The authors aim to reduce the data requirement for deploying robots to new doors by enabling the entire pipeline to be initiated from a single RGB video of a real door. This contrasts with methods that require multiple scans, human demonstrations, or detailed CAD models.
5. **Onboard Perception for Autonomy:** The system is designed to operate solely with onboard perception (dual-depth cameras) and proprioceptive states during deployment, eliminating the need for privileged information or off-board processing.

To address these motivations, the research formulates door traversal as an articulation-conditioned policy-learning problem. The recovered door twin serves as a common task representation that bridges scene reconstruction, expert demonstration generation, and policy execution, thereby enabling robust, closed-loop behavior from minimal real-world input.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The Video2DoorTraversal framework is a single-video real-to-sim-to-real pipeline designed for wheel-legged mobile manipulators to perform door opening and traversal. It comprises three main stages: DoorTwin for asset generation, an agentic expert for trajectory generation, and ArticuACT for policy learning and execution.

#### 3.1. A. DoorTwin: One-shot Sim-Ready Door Generation

The first stage, DoorTwin, reconstructs a metrically aligned, articulated, and simulation-ready door asset from a single RGB video. This process enhances existing articulated asset generation by integrating real-world geometric grounding and appearance transfer.

1. **Video Geometry Grounding:**
 - **Depth and Pose Estimation:** The input RGB video is first processed by DAGE to estimate metrically scaled scene geometry and camera poses for each frame. For areas with glass or reflection, LingBot-Depth refines the depth predictions.
 - **Door Segmentation and Aggregation:** SAM 3 segments the complete door assembly in every video frame. The masked metric geometry from each frame is transformed into a common reference frame using the estimated camera poses, creating a multi-view point cloud of the door.
 - **Global Coordinate Frame and Dimensions:** Principal component analysis (PCA) on the aggregated door point cloud establishes a global coordinate frame. The two largest in-plane directions define door width and height, and the shortest approximates the door surface normal. This aggregation provides stable estimation of global door dimensions.
 - **Handle Localization:** A Vision-Language Model (VLM) selects a reference frame with a complete door mask. Within this frame, the door panel and handle are segmented to recover their local geometry. The panel-relative handle location is then calculated from the centers of their 3D bounding boxes. These aggregated door dimensions and panel-relative handle location form the primary geometric constraints for asset generation.

2. **Articulation-Aware Program Generation:**
 - The recovered geometric constraints are converted into a structured specification for an Articraft-based agent. This specification includes metric dimensions, panel-relative handle location, and required articulation (fixed frame, revolute joint for panel, handle attached to panel).
 - Asset generation proceeds coarse-to-fine, constructing the global door assembly first, then refining the handle component and its attachment. This order helps manage scale differences.
 - Articraft's native procedural validators check for structural validity, mesh interpenetration, and valid joint definitions, ensuring the generated asset is physically loadable.

3. **Reference View Rendering and Critique:**
 - To ensure instance-level consistency with the input video, a reference view rendering and critique loop is introduced. The generated asset is rendered from the viewpoint of the selected reference frame.
 - A visual critic evaluates the rendered asset against the masked RGB reference image, assessing global silhouette, handle type, hinge side, relative proportions, and handle placement.
 - If a mismatch is detected, structured feedback is provided to the generation agent, which updates the construction program while keeping video-derived metric constraints fixed. This iterative process continues until the asset passes both procedural and visual similarity checks.

4. **Delighting and Appearance Transfer:**
 - Once the articulated geometry is finalized, the appearance of the observed door is transferred. A GPT-based image editing procedure removes environmental illumination and shadows from the reference RGB frame to obtain a cleaner appearance.
 - The untextured door model and the processed reference image are then used by Tripo 3D to synthesize texture and material maps.

The output DoorTwin is thus metrically grounded, articulated, validated for simulation, visually matched to the real door, and ready for policy training.

#### 3.2. B. Agentic Expert Trajectory Generation

Given the reconstructed DoorTwin (ΘD), expert demonstrations are generated using a simulation-in-the-loop agent.

1. **Parameterized Skill Program:** Instead of generating low-level control code, the agent instantiates an interpretable, parameterized skill program (ΠD). This program consists of a sequence of high-level skills (e.g., BASEMOVETO, ROTATE HANDLE, PUSH DOOR, PASS), each with compact task-level parameters (e.g., approach distance, grasp offset, handle rotation). Interaction targets are defined in a handle-local frame, and pushing directions are determined by hinge orientation.

2. **Simulation and Refinement Loop:**
 - The skill program is executed in parallel Isaac Gym environments. Each rollout records task-progress signals (handle rotation, door angle, unlocking state, body passage) and feasibility signals.
 - Upon failure, the agent receives a diagnostic summary and multi-view keyframes. It attributes the failure to a likely cause and proposes bounded modifications to the skill parameters.
 - A local simulator search evaluates neighboring parameter candidates, accepting only those that satisfy task, collision, and kinematic constraints.
 - This generate–execute–diagnose–refine loop continues until a successful rollout is achieved or a budget is exhausted.

3. **Data Collection:** Only successful rollouts are retained as demonstrations. To ensure robustness, these expert programs are replayed under randomized robot, scene, and sensor configurations (e.g., initial base/door poses, friction, damping, door resistance, camera extrinsics, and depth map augmentation). Only rollouts remaining successful under these perturbations are used for policy learning. Trajectories are recorded at 25 Hz.

#### 3.3. C. ArticuACT Policy

ArticuACT is an imitation learning policy built upon the Action Chunking Transformer (ACT), designed for coordinated base, arm, and gripper control.

1. **Inputs and Outputs:** At each time step, the policy takes dual-view depth images (front-view Df, wrist-view Dw) and a 9-D robot state (proprioception). It predicts an action chunk of length H=100, comprising base forward velocity, base yaw velocity, six arm joint commands, and one gripper command.

2. **Robot-Centric Plücker Conditioning:**
 - Following Jiang et al., this module explicitly conditions the visuomotor policy on camera geometry. For each image pixel, a Plücker ray is constructed using camera intrinsics and extrinsics relative to the robot base frame.
 - Separate Plücker maps for front and wrist cameras are processed by lightweight convolutional encoders and concatenated with corresponding ResNet-18 depth features before projection to the Transformer dimension. This provides explicit, pixel-aligned camera geometry in the robot's coordinate frame, reducing spatial ambiguity.

3. **Future Interaction-State Prediction:**
 - In addition to robot actions, each future decoder token predicts a three-dimensional interaction state: stable handle contact, handle-rotation progress, and door-opening progress. This results in an H × 3 interaction chunk temporally aligned with the action chunk.
 - These predictions serve as auxiliary supervision during training. They are not fed back into the action decoder but encourage the shared decoder features to encode crucial contact and task-progress information.

4. **Training Objective:** The complete training objective combines masked ℓ1 action loss (Lact), binary cross-entropy for contact (Lcontact), and Smooth ℓ1 losses for handle and door progress (Lhandle, Ldoor), along with a KL regularization term (LKL). Interaction losses are backpropagated through shared decoder and policy backbone, with labels required only during training.

During real-world deployment, ArticuACT relies solely on onboard depth observations and proprioceptive states, without requiring privileged information.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research provides extensive evaluations in simulation and on real robots, demonstrating the effectiveness of the Video2DoorTraversal framework.

#### 4.1. A. Asset Generation (DoorTwin)

DoorTwin was evaluated against Articraft, Articulate-Anything, and PhysX-Omni on 20 real-world door instances, using GPT-5.5 for Articraft and Articulate-Anything, and Qwen2.5-VL for PhysX-Omni.

- **Quantitative Metrics:** DoorTwin achieved the highest Scale Score (94.95%), indicating superior metric accuracy for door width, height, and handle position. It also had the highest Masked IoU (0.972), PSNR (18.53), and VLM Score (56.74), while demonstrating the lowest LPIPS (0.408), (although SSIM was slightly lower than PhysX-Omni). These results indicate DoorTwin's ability to preserve both metric geometry and instance-specific visual characteristics.
- **Qualitative Observations:** PhysX-Omni sometimes misidentified doors, leading to significant errors. Articulate-Anything often showed mismatched panel proportions and handle placement. Articraft, while producing structurally valid assets, exhibited errors in metric scale, local components, and appearance. DoorTwin demonstrated better consistency in geometry and appearance compared to these baselines.
- **Effect of Reference-View Critic:** The iterative critique loop was shown to progressively improve instance-level consistency, correcting missing components, size, local geometry, and placement of features like door markings and handles, complementing structural validation with visual similarity.

#### 4.2. B. Simulation Results

The complete system was evaluated on 20 door instances with 256 trials per method, comparing against various baselines including open-loop replay, object-aware replay, reinforcement learning (DoorGym), other visuomotor policies (UniDoorManip, Vanilla ACT, Diffusion Policy, DP3).

- **Success Rates:** Our method achieved a 98.44% door opening success rate and a 97.27% full traversal success rate, which were significantly higher than all baselines.
 - Replay and OA Replay performed poorly (14.06% and 53.13% traversal success, respectively), indicating sensitivity to handle-pose estimation errors.
 - DoorGym struggled (12.50% traversal success) due to the large exploration space and difficulty in reward design for the long-horizon task.
 - UniDoorManip showed reasonable door opening but degraded during traversal (50.78% traversal success), possibly due to its third-person point-cloud observations being less effective for precise base-arm coordination.
 - Imitation learning baselines (Vanilla ACT, Diffusion Policy, DP3) achieved 64.84%, 64.45%, and 66.41% traversal success respectively, demonstrating that ArticuACT's design elements provided improvements.

#### 4.3. C. Ablation Study

1. **Policy Components and Command-Space Ablation:**
 - Both Plücker conditioning and interaction-state prediction consistently improved traversal performance over vanilla ACT.
 - Their combination yielded the best results, improving traversal success rate by 17.58% with end-effector commands and 26.18% with joint commands compared to vanilla ACT.
 - Joint-command policies consistently outperformed end-effector-command counterparts, suggesting better alignment with low-level control.
2. **Agentic Trajectory Generation Ablation:**
 - Removing simulator feedback resulted in a substantial performance drop (48.13% success rate), indicating that geometric alignment or VLM reasoning alone cannot produce physically executable trajectories reliably.
 - The full method achieved 85.63% success, outperforming a rule-based baseline (74.38%) and a VLM fix-log variant (85.00%) with fewer refinement loops, confirming the importance of simulator verification and iterative agent refinement.
3. **Data Scaling Analysis:** Traversal success consistently improved with more demonstrations, rising from 59.38% with 50 trajectories to 97.27% with 200, with the largest gains observed within the first 100 demonstrations.

#### 4.4. D. Real-World Results

The complete pipeline was deployed on a Unitree A2-W wheel-legged mobile manipulator equipped with a Z1 arm and two Intel RealSense D435 cameras.

- **Average Success Rate:** The method achieved a 96.57% average success rate across five real door instances, significantly outperforming Replay (18.29%), OA Replay (36.58%), and Vanilla ACT (65.71%). This demonstrates robust generalization across different door geometries.
- **Execution Efficiency:** The mobile manipulator completed the full approach, opening, and traversal sequence in approximately 13 seconds on average, which is comparable to related systems.
- **Interaction State Alignment:** The predicted interaction states (contact, handle rotation, door opening progress) were temporally aligned with the physical task progression observed in real-world execution.
- **Zero-Shot Cross-Door Generalization:** The policy, trained on one reconstructed door, achieved an 80.95% average zero-shot success rate across three structurally similar unseen doors (25/35, 31/35, and 29/35 trials respectively).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Video2DoorTraversal framework presents a comprehensive approach for enabling autonomous door opening and traversal for mobile manipulators, addressing several limitations of prior research. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Reduced Data Requirement:** By utilizing a single RGB video as input for generating a simulation-ready digital twin and training expert demonstrations, the framework significantly reduces the need for extensive manual data collection or CAD model creation for new environments. This addresses a major bottleneck in robot deployment.
2. **Instance-Specific Articulation for Robust Simulation:** The DoorTwin component enables the reconstruction of metrically accurate and articulated digital twins that faithfully represent real-world door instances. This detailed representation is crucial for supporting contact-rich simulation and training policies that transfer effectively to physical robots.
3. **Automated Expert Trajectory Generation:** The simulation-in-the-loop agent automates the generation of physically executable expert demonstrations. This eliminates the need for human teleoperation or complex reward engineering, making the process more scalable and efficient for various door configurations.
4. **Robust Closed-Loop Control with Onboard Perception:** ArticuACT, with its robot-centric Plücker conditioning and interaction-aware auxiliary supervision, facilitates robust base-arm coordination using only onboard depth sensors and proprioception. This design enhances geometric grounding and contact-aware execution, which are critical for dynamic, interactive tasks like door traversal.
5. **High Real-World Performance and Efficiency:** The demonstrated 96.57% average success rate across diverse real doors, coupled with an average traversal time of approximately 13 seconds, indicates the system's practical applicability and efficiency in performing a complex loco-manipulation task.
6. **Meaningful Zero-Shot Generalization:** The observed 80.95% zero-shot success rate on structurally similar unseen doors suggests that the learned policy captures generalizable skills, allowing for deployment in new environments without requiring per-door retraining or specific trajectory generation.

The Video2DoorTraversal framework has the potential to impact various domains where mobile robots interact with articulated objects in unstructured environments. This includes applications in logistics, domestic assistance, security, and industrial automation, where robots need to navigate and operate within human-centered spaces. The approach of leveraging minimal real-world data to generate high-fidelity simulations for policy learning offers a pathway toward more autonomous and adaptable robotic systems. Future work, as indicated by the authors, will extend the framework to handle a broader range of door types and handle mechanisms, further enhancing its versatility.
