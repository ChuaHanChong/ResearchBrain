# DAgger Diffusion Navigation: DAgger Boosted Diffusion Policy for Vision-Language Navigation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers introduced DAgger Diffusion Navigation (DifNav), an end-to-end policy for Vision-and-Language Navigation in Continuous Environments (VLN-CE), utilizing a diffusion model to represent multi-modal action distributions and DAgger for robust training. DifNav achieved state-of-the-art performance on the R2R-CE benchmark, outperforming existing two-stage and end-to-end methods by 12-32 points in various success metrics.

## Problem

- Existing VLN-CE methods suffer from global sub-optimization and error propagation due to their two-stage waypoint-based frameworks.
- The performance of two-stage approaches is bottlenecked by the quality of their waypoint predictors, limiting flexibility in continuous action spaces.
- End-to-end policies trained with Behavioral Cloning struggle with compounding errors, long-horizon decision-making, and modeling multi-modal action distributions in continuous environments.

## Method

- Proposed **DAgger** Diffusion Navigation (DifNav), an end-to-end policy directly mapping agent state to actions in continuous navigation space.
- Integrated a conditional ==diffusion policy== to model multi-modal action distributions, allowing for diverse and executable action sampling.
- Employed DAgger-boosted online training to mitigate compounding errors and enhance policy robustness through interactive imitation learning.

## Results

- DifNav achieved state-of-the-art performance on the R2R-CE benchmark, outperforming the best two-stage method (BEVBert) by **13%** SR and **18%** SPL, and the best end-to-end method (WS-MGMap) by **32%** SR and **32%** SPL.
- Ablation studies confirmed that using three recent observations for history, 0.5m waypoint spacing for demonstrations, and DAgger-boosted training were crucial for optimal performance and robustness.
- The DAgger-trained policy learned to effectively recover from navigation errors, a capability absent in policies trained solely with Behavioral Cloning, significantly reducing collision rates from **238.2** to as low as **8.7**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Diffusion policies are effective for modeling complex, multi-modal action distributions in continuous VLN, enabling more flexible and robust navigation behaviors.
> - An end-to-end architecture, combined with DAgger, can surpass two-stage waypoint-based methods by avoiding error propagation and directly optimizing for navigation goals.
> - Temporal distance prediction for navigation completion is more effective than direct classification, leading to superior performance and more natural trajectories.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Vision-and-Language Navigation (VLN) is a field in embodied artificial intelligence where an agent must follow natural language instructions to navigate through an environment. Initially, much VLN research focused on discrete environments, where agents are constrained to predefined connectivity graphs, simplifying the action space. However, to better reflect real-world navigation challenges, the Vision-and-Language Navigation in Continuous Environments (VLN-CE) setting was introduced. VLN-CE requires agents to execute low-level, continuous actions (e.g., precise movements and rotations) in complex, partially observable 3D spaces, leading to increased difficulty and performance gaps compared to discrete VLN.

Previous approaches to VLN-CE have predominantly adopted a two-stage waypoint-based framework. This framework first employs a waypoint predictor to generate a set of navigable waypoints, effectively discretizing the continuous action space. Subsequently, a sub-goal planner selects an intermediate waypoint from this set based on the navigation instructions. While this modular decomposition has improved performance over purely low-level policies, it introduces several limitations. These include global sub-optimization due to separate proxy objectives for each stage (waypoint generation and sub-goal planning), and a performance bottleneck arising from the strong reliance on the quality of the predicted waypoints. If the waypoint predictor generates inaccessible or incorrect waypoints, the agent's ability to recover is severely limited, leading to error propagation.

Alternative end-to-end approaches, which directly map visual observations and language instructions to navigation actions, have been explored, often utilizing Behavioral Cloning (BC). However, these methods have faced challenges in continuous environments, particularly with long-horizon decision-making, spatial accessibility inference, and obstacle avoidance. A significant issue in BC is compounding error, where small, initial deviations from expert trajectories accumulate, leading to distributional shifts between training and testing data and subsequent policy failures. Efforts to address this, such as 'student-forcing' or incorporating reinforcement learning, have required extensive data collection and still lagged behind two-stage methods.

This work introduces diffusion policies, which have shown success in robotic manipulation tasks for modeling complex, multi-modal action distributions, into the VLN-CE domain. Diffusion models are capable of generating diverse outputs by iteratively denoising a noisy input, making them suitable for scenarios where multiple correct actions or paths may exist. The paper also integrates DAgger (Dataset Aggregation), an interactive imitation learning algorithm, to address the compounding error problem prevalent in BC by actively augmenting training data with expert corrections. By proposing an end-to-end diffusion policy enhanced with DAgger, this research aims to overcome the limitations of both two-stage frameworks and traditional end-to-end BC methods in VLN-CE.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives of this research are to address the identified limitations within existing VLN-CE methodologies by proposing an end-to-end navigation policy. The key motivations stem from the shortcomings of current approaches:

1. **Addressing Global Sub-optimization and Error Propagation in Two-Stage Frameworks:** Existing VLN-CE methods typically decompose the task into a waypoint generation stage and a sub-goal planning stage. This modularity leads to global sub-optimality because each stage is optimized for its own proxy objective rather than the overall navigation task. For instance, the waypoint predictor might be optimized for generating accessible waypoints based on environmental connectivity, while the sub-goal planner optimizes for the navigation goal. This decoupling can result in a mismatch, where accurately predicted waypoints may not lead to the instruction-following goal, or suboptimal waypoints constrain the planner's effectiveness. Furthermore, errors made in the waypoint prediction stage can propagate to the sub-goal planning stage, leading to unrecoverable navigation failures, especially when the generated waypoints are misleading or inaccessible. The motivation is to overcome this by unifying these stages into a single, end-to-end optimized policy.

2. **Mitigating the Performance Bottleneck Caused by Waypoint Predictors:** The performance of two-stage approaches is heavily dependent on the quality of the first-stage waypoint predictor. If the predictor fails to generate suitable waypoints that guide the agent towards the goal, the navigation task may fail regardless of the sub-goal planner's capability. This creates a bottleneck, as the agent's action space is severely restricted by the generated waypoints. The objective is to eliminate this reliance on an intermediate waypoint predictor, allowing the agent to operate directly in the continuous action space, thereby enabling more flexible and potentially optimal path planning.

3. **Enhancing Spatial Reasoning and Capturing Multi-Modal Action Distributions in Continuous Environments:** End-to-end policies trained with Behavioral Cloning (BC) in continuous environments often struggle with long-horizon decision-making, inferring spatial accessibility for obstacle avoidance, and dealing with the inherent ambiguity of navigation. Continuous environments present multiple plausible trajectories for a given instruction, due to scene layout variability and linguistic ambiguity. Traditional BC, which typically learns a deterministic mapping from states to single actions, struggles to capture this multi-modal nature of expert behavior. The motivation is to develop a policy capable of modeling these diverse, multi-modal action distributions more effectively, allowing the agent to explore various instruction-following behaviors and choose executable actions.

4. **Counteracting Compounding Errors in Imitation Learning:** Policies trained with BC suffer from compounding errors. When the agent deviates slightly from the expert's trajectory, it encounters states not present in the training data distribution. As the policy continues to make errors, these deviations accumulate, leading to a significant shift in the state distribution, from which the policy cannot recover. This problem is particularly acute in long-horizon navigation tasks in continuous spaces. The objective is to implement a mechanism that addresses this distributional shift and improves the policy's robustness, enabling it to recover from error states.

In summary, the core motivation is to develop a robust, end-to-end VLN-CE agent that can directly learn complex, multi-modal action distributions in continuous spaces, without the limitations of modular frameworks or naive imitation learning, ultimately improving navigation performance and robustness.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, DAgger Diffusion Navigation (DifNav), is an end-to-end VLN-CE policy that directly maps an agent's state to actions in a continuous navigation space. Its architecture integrates a cross-modal state encoder, a conditional diffusion policy, and a temporal distance predictor. To enhance robustness against compounding errors, DifNav employs a DAgger-boosted online training regime.

#### 3.1. Task Setup

DifNav operates within the VLN-CE setting, utilizing the Habitat Simulator based on Matterport3D scenes. The agent executes low-level actions such as FORWARD (0.25m), ROTATE LEFT/RIGHT (15°), and STOP, guided by natural language instructions. At each step, the agent receives panoramic RGB-D observations (12 RGB and 12 depth images) from discrete horizontal viewpoints.

#### 3.2. Approach Overview

DifNav's framework consists of:
- A **cross-modal state encoder** to integrate historical visual observations, navigation instructions, and current observations into a latent state representation.
- A **conditional diffusion policy** that models a multi-modal action distribution, conditioned on the encoded state, to sample executable actions.
- A **temporal distance predictor** to estimate the normalized distance to the navigation goal.

The policy is initially trained via Behavioral Cloning (BC) and subsequently refined through **DAgger-boosted online training** within the Habitat Simulator to augment the training data distribution.

#### 3.3. State Encoding

The state encoder processes three types of inputs: visual observations, navigation instructions, and historical trajectory information.

- **Vision Encoding:** Panoramic RGB-D observations `O_t = {I_rgb, I_d}` are processed. RGB features `V_rgb_t` are extracted using a pre-trained ViT/32, and depth features `V_depth_t` are obtained from a pre-trained depth encoder. Heading angles are encoded as `V_angle_t = (sinθ, cosθ)`. These are combined via linear layers and layer normalization, then passed through a panorama encoder (a multilayer transformer) to produce final observation embeddings `O'_t`.

- **Instruction Encoding:** Word embeddings for the natural language instruction `W` are enhanced with position and token type embeddings. These are then fed into a pre-trained multilayer transformer to generate word representations `_cW`.

- **History Trajectory Encoding:** The history is represented by average pooling of past visual representations `Avg(O'_t)`. For each historical waypoint, Euclidean distance `d_t`, trajectory distance `t_t`, relative heading `h_t`, and a navigation step embedding `u_t` are calculated and concatenated with `Avg(O'_t)` through linear layers, forming history trajectory embeddings `T_t`. The model encodes the three most recent observations as history.

- **Cross-Modal Reasoning:** A multilayer cross-modal transformer performs reasoning. Its cross-attention module uses `T_t` as query to attend to `_cW`. A subsequent self-attention layer processes `O'_t` and `T_t` as inputs. The output represents the agent's current state, `S_t`.

#### 3.4. Multi-Modal Action Distribution Modeling

Instead of directly predicting a single action, DifNav models a multi-modal action distribution `p(a_t|S_t)` using a state-conditioned diffusion policy. The action `a_t` represents the next navigation action.
- **Diffusion Process:** A sequence of future actions `a^K_t` is initialized with Gaussian noise. The policy iteratively denoises this sequence over `K` steps, `a^{k-1}_t = alpha * (a^K_t - gamma * epsilon_theta(S_t, a^k_t, k) + N(0, sigma^2I))`, ultimately yielding a noise-free action `a^0_t`.
- **Noise Prediction Network:** A 1D conditional U-Net with 15 convolutional layers (`epsilon_theta`) predicts the noise. It is conditioned on the current state `S_t`, the noisy action `a^k_t`, and the denoising step `k`.
- **Training:** The network is trained by adding noise `epsilon_k` to ground-truth actions `a^0` and optimizing `epsilon_theta` to predict this noise using a Mean Squared Error (MSE) loss: `L_WP = MSE(epsilon_k, (S_t, epsilon_theta(a^0 + epsilon_k, k)))`.

#### 3.5. Temporal Distance Predictor

A Multilayer Perceptron (MLP) `f_d(S_t)` predicts the normalized distance from the agent to the goal, `d(o_t, g_t)`. This predictor is trained jointly with the diffusion policy using an MSE loss: `L_Dist = lambda * MSE(d(o_t, g_t), f_d(S_t))`.

#### 3.6. DAgger-Boosted Online Learning

To mitigate compounding errors inherent in BC, DifNav employs DAgger. This interactive imitation learning algorithm refines the policy and augments the training data.
- **Procedure:** At each decision step, the agent executes its current policy `Rollout(S_t)` with a probability `p`, or queries an expert for a corrective action `label_t` with probability `(1-p)`. The `alpha` parameter in the formula `a_t = Rollout(S_t) p > alpha` and `a_t = label_t p <= alpha` defines the probability threshold for expert intervention. The expert action minimizes the geodesic distance to the expert trajectory within a predefined radius.
- **Data Augmentation:** After each episode, the generated trajectory (a mix of policy actions and expert interventions) is collected to augment the training dataset.
- **Iterative Refinement:** Multiple rounds of DAgger training are conducted to iteratively optimize the policy and expand the expert demonstrations. In the experiments, five rounds were performed.

#### 3.7. Training Details

- **Pretraining:** A default policy is initially trained using low-level expert demonstrations from the R2R-CE dataset.
- **Waypoint Spacing:** To expand the action generation space beyond the default 0.25m waypoint spacing, expert trajectories are sub-sampled at intervals of `n=2` (yielding 0.5m spacing) to create sparse training trajectories.
- **Action Sequence Length:** The diffusion policy is configured to predict only the next instruction-conditioned action (`length=1`).
- **Loss Function:** The overall loss function is `L_DifNav(phi, theta, f_d) = L_WP + lambda * L_Dist`, where `phi` are state encoder parameters, `theta` are diffusion policy parameters, and `lambda = 10^-4` is the weighting factor for the distance loss.
- **Hyperparameters:** ViTB/32 (CLIP) for RGB, pre-trained ResNet-50 for depth, LXBERT for text (all frozen). LXMERT pre-trained weights for initialization. AdamW optimizer (learning rate 10^-4), 100 epochs, batch size 256. Square Cosine Noise Scheduler with K=10 denoising steps.
- **Fine-tuning:** After DAgger learning, the policy is fine-tuned using the augmented demonstrations.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted on the R2R-CE benchmark dataset demonstrate the performance of DifNav, including comparisons with state-of-the-art methods and ablation studies.

#### 4.1. Comparison with State-Of-The-Art Methods

DifNav was compared against three categories of VLN-CE models: two-stage waypoint-based, end-to-end with discrete action spaces, and end-to-end with continuous action spaces. The evaluation metrics included Navigation Error (NE), Oracle Success Rate (OSR), Success Rate (SR), SR penalized by Path Length (SPL), and Collision Rate (CR).

- **Overall Performance:** DifNav consistently achieved higher navigation performance across all evaluated scenes (Open Area, Narrow Space, Stairs) compared to existing state-of-the-art methods.
 - **Versus End-to-End Waypoint Models:** DifNav substantially outperformed WS-MGMap, the second-best end-to-end waypoint model, by an average of 28 points in OSR, 32 in SR, and 32 in SPL. This is attributed to the diffusion policy's ability to model multi-modal action distributions and DAgger's mitigation of compounding errors.
 - **Versus Low-Level Action Space Models:** DifNav achieved significant improvements over CMA, the best-performing low-level action space method, with average gains of 47 in OSR, 59 in SR, and 59 in SPL. This indicates the benefit of direct action prediction in 3D space, which reduces the sequence length for modeling long-horizon behaviors.
 - **Versus Two-Stage Frameworks:** DifNav outperformed the state-of-the-art two-stage method, BEVBert, by an average of 12 OSR, 13 SR, and 18 SPL. This result is notable because DifNav achieves this without relying on a waypoint predictor, demonstrating the effectiveness of its end-to-end architecture in avoiding error propagation and directly optimizing for the navigation task.
- **Consistency across Environments:** DifNav exhibited consistent performance across different environment types, suggesting its robustness to environmental variations, unlike waypoint predictor-reliant models which can be sensitive to such changes.

#### 4.2. Ablation Study

Ablation studies were conducted to assess the impact of various design choices.

- **Representations of Historical Information:**
 - Using only the current observation resulted in the lowest Collision Rate (CR) of 7.1, but poorer overall navigation performance (49.5% SR).
 - Using the three most recent observations yielded the best navigation performance (1.7 NE, 94.4% OSR, 91.4% SR, 88.8% SPL, 8.7 CR).
 - Using five recent observations or a sparse history trajectory resulted in lower performance than three observations. The paper suggests that excessive historical information might dilute focus or introduce misleading data.

- **Waypoint Spacing in Demonstration Trajectories:**
 - Training with 0.5m waypoint spacing (n=2) resulted in the best performance (1.7 NE, 94.4% OSR, 91.4% SR, 88.8% SPL).
 - Smaller (0.25m) or larger (1.0m) spacings led to degraded navigation performance. Excessively small spacing increased the action horizon complexity, while large spacing made single-step prediction challenging due to sparse supervision. Larger spacings also led to longer navigation trajectories.

- **Navigation Completion Detection Network:**
 - Predicting normalized distance to the goal and stopping when below a threshold achieved superior performance (1.7 NE, 94.4% OSR, 91.4% SR, 88.8% SPL) compared to a direct classification for stopping.
 - Classification-based stopping, especially with weighted loss to address class imbalance, severely degraded navigation ability, resulting in abnormally short trajectories (4.6 TL) and low success rates (17.5% SR). This suggests that predicting progress via distance is more effective than a binary stop decision.

- **DAgger-Boosted Online Training:**
 - Without DAgger training, the policy exhibited very poor performance (5.2 NE, 21.0% SR, 15.9% SPL, 238.2 CR), highlighting severe compounding errors.
 - With DAgger, performance improved substantially across all metrics. A lower `alpha` (0.10, indicating less frequent expert intervention) generally led to better navigation performance (2.2 NE, 83.3% SR, 71.0% SPL) and lower collision rates (14.0 CR) compared to higher `alpha` values.
 - The improvement is attributed to the policy learning to recover from error states through augmented training data. Lower `alpha` allows the agent to explore more diverse situations requiring corrective actions, although it can result in slightly longer trajectories and thus lower SPL due to recovery actions.

#### 4.3. Qualitative Analysis

Qualitative observations further support the quantitative findings:

- **Addressing Compounding Errors:** Visualizations demonstrated that a policy trained solely on BC failed to complete navigation tasks even on training data, drifting off-distribution. DAgger-boosted training enabled the policy to complete tasks robustly.
- **Error Recovery:** The DAgger-trained policy learned to replan and recover from navigation errors (e.g., turning back when off-course), a capability absent in policies trained only on expert demonstrations.
- **Two-Stage vs. DifNav:** An example showed a two-stage method failing to navigate to stairs because its waypoint predictor did not generate suitable waypoints. DifNav, by directly modeling multi-modal actions in continuous space, successfully navigated to the goal.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents several significant contributions and has the potential for notable impact in the field of embodied artificial intelligence, particularly for vision-and-language navigation.

1. **Introduction of Diffusion Policies to VLN-CE:** DifNav represents the first application of a diffusion policy for modeling multi-modal action distributions in vision-and-language navigation. This is a novel architectural paradigm for VLN, moving beyond traditional discriminative or regression-based methods. The capability of diffusion models to generate diverse, plausible actions is particularly relevant for VLN-CE, where instructions can be ambiguous and multiple equivalent paths may exist. This could lead to more flexible and human-like navigation behaviors.

2. **Resolution of Key Limitations in VLN-CE:** The work directly addresses fundamental challenges that have constrained previous VLN-CE approaches:
 - **Global Sub-optimality:** By providing an end-to-end framework, DifNav bypasses the issues of global sub-optimality and error propagation inherent in two-stage waypoint-based systems. This simplifies the learning objective and allows for direct optimization towards the instruction-following goal.
 - **Performance Bottleneck:** Eliminating the reliance on a waypoint predictor removes a significant performance bottleneck and potential point of failure. The agent can directly operate in the continuous navigation space, offering greater freedom and potentially more optimal path planning.
 - **Compounding Errors:** The integration of DAgger effectively mitigates compounding errors, a long-standing challenge in imitation learning for long-horizon tasks. This dramatically improves the robustness of the policy, allowing the agent to recover from deviations and explore a wider range of states during deployment.

3. **Improved Navigation Performance and Robustness:** The experimental results demonstrate that DifNav achieves state-of-the-art navigation performance across various environmental conditions, outperforming existing two-stage and end-to-end methods. This higher performance, combined with improved robustness and error recovery capabilities learned through DAgger, suggests a more reliable and deployable navigation agent.

4. **Advancement in Learning Complex Action Distributions:** The success of DifNav in modeling multi-modal action distributions in a complex continuous environment highlights the power of diffusion policies for embodied AI tasks involving high-dimensional and diverse action spaces. This could inspire further research into using generative models for policy learning in other challenging robotics and embodied AI domains beyond manipulation.

5. **Potential for Broader Impact:** The proposed end-to-end, robust policy for continuous VLN brings the task closer to real-world applications. Agents capable of robustly following natural language instructions in free-form 3D spaces could be used in diverse settings, from personal robotics to assistive technologies and autonomous systems, where navigating complex and dynamic environments based on human commands is crucial. The work contributes to the development of more capable and intelligent embodied agents.
