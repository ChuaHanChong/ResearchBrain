# DAgger Diffusion Navigation: DAgger Boosted Diffusion Policy for Vision-Language Navigation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers introduced DAgger Diffusion Navigation (DifNav), an end-to-end policy for Vision-and-Language Navigation in Continuous Environments (VLN-CE), utilizing a diffusion model to represent multi-modal action distributions and DAgger for robust training. DifNav achieved state-of-the-art performance on the R2R-CE benchmark, outperforming existing two-stage and end-to-end methods by 12-32 points in various success metrics.

## Problem

- Existing VLN-CE methods suffer from global sub-optimization and error propagation due to their two-stage waypoint-based frameworks.
- The performance of two-stage approaches is bottlenecked by the quality of their waypoint predictors, limiting flexibility in continuous action spaces.
- End-to-end policies trained with Behavioral Cloning struggle with compounding errors, long-horizon decision-making, and modeling multi-modal action distributions in continuous environments.

## Method

- Proposed **DAgger** Diffusion Navigation (DifNav), an end-to-end policy directly mapping agent state to actions in continuous navigation space.
- Integrated a conditional ==diffusion policy== to model multi-modal action distributions, allowing for diverse and executable action sampling.
- Employed DAgger-boosted online training to mitigate compounding errors and enhance policy robustness through interactive imitation learning.

## Results

- DifNav achieved state-of-the-art performance on the R2R-CE benchmark, outperforming the best two-stage method (BEVBert) by **13%** SR and **18%** SPL, and the best end-to-end method (WS-MGMap) by **32%** SR and **32%** SPL.
- Ablation studies confirmed that using three recent observations for history, 0.5m waypoint spacing for demonstrations, and DAgger-boosted training were crucial for optimal performance and robustness.
- The DAgger-trained policy learned to effectively recover from navigation errors, a capability absent in policies trained solely with Behavioral Cloning, significantly reducing collision rates from **238.2** to as low as **8.7**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Diffusion policies are effective for modeling complex, multi-modal action distributions in continuous VLN, enabling more flexible and robust navigation behaviors.
> - An end-to-end architecture, combined with DAgger, can surpass two-stage waypoint-based methods by avoiding error propagation and directly optimizing for navigation goals.
> - Temporal distance prediction for navigation completion is more effective than direct classification, leading to superior performance and more natural trajectories.
