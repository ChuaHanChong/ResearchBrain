# Think Only When You Need with Large Hybrid-Reasoning Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Large Hybrid-Reasoning Models (LHRMs) from Microsoft Research and Peking University adaptively determine whether to engage in extended reasoning or provide a direct answer, addressing the "overthinking" issue in current large reasoning models. Trained with a two-stage pipeline, LHRMs reduce token consumption and latency while achieving strong reasoning capabilities, notably improving Hybrid Accuracy by over 90% compared to alternative methods at 7B scale.

## Problem

- Current Large Reasoning Models (LRMs) "overthink," generating lengthy reasoning traces even for simple queries.
- This overthinking leads to excessive token consumption, increased latency, and wasted computational resources.
- Existing models struggle to balance robust reasoning for complex tasks with efficient, direct responses for simple queries.

## Method

- Introduces ==Large Hybrid-Reasoning Models (LHRMs)== capable of adaptively selecting between "Thinking" and "No-Thinking" modes.
- Employs a ==two-stage training pipeline==: Hybrid Fine-Tuning (HFT) to establish distinct modes and ==Hybrid Group Policy Optimization (HGPO)== for adaptive mode selection.
- HGPO uses an online reinforcement learning approach with inter-group and intra-group rewards to teach optimal mode choice for diverse queries.

## Results

- LHRMs consistently outperformed baselines, achieving average performance gains of **9.2%** (1.5B) and **7.1%** (7B) over HFT-DPO on various benchmarks.
- Demonstrated superior adaptive behavior, with LHRM-7B achieving over **90%** higher Hybrid Accuracy than HFT-DPO, correctly selecting reasoning modes.
- Showed strong reasoning capabilities, improving **10.3%** (1.5B) and **13.6%** (7B) on AIME24 over HFT-DPO, while reducing thinking ratios for simpler queries.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Adaptive mode selection, mimicking human System 1/System 2 thinking, significantly improves efficiency and user experience in large language models.
> - A specialized reinforcement learning algorithm like Hybrid Group Policy Optimization (HGPO) is effective in training models to dynamically choose optimal reasoning paths.
> - The new Hybrid Accuracy (H_Acc) metric is crucial for evaluating a model's ability to correctly select the appropriate reasoning mode for varied queries.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of Large Language Models (LLMs) has marked a significant advancement in AI, demonstrating impressive capabilities in understanding and generating human-like text. However, a major paradigm shift has been the emergence of **Large Reasoning Models (LRMs)**, which aim to enhance LLMs' problem-solving abilities by incorporating explicit "thinking processes" or reasoning traces before generating a final answer. Techniques like Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting or fine-tuning models to generate such traces (e.g., DeepSeek-R1, OpenAI's o1/o3 series) have shown substantial improvements in complex tasks like mathematics, programming, and commonsense reasoning.

Despite their enhanced reasoning prowess, current LRMs face a critical challenge: **overthinking**. This refers to the phenomenon where LRMs expend excessive computational resources and generate lengthy thinking traces even for simple queries, leading to increased token consumption, higher latency, and a degraded user experience. For instance, a model might "think step by step" to answer a trivial question like "Hello." This inefficiency limits their practical applicability in real-world scenarios where responsiveness and resource optimization are crucial.

This paper directly addresses this gap by introducing **Large Hybrid-Reasoning Models (LHRMs)**, which represent a novel approach to overcome the overthinking problem. Instead of forcing all queries through a laborious thinking process or relying solely on a direct answer, LHRMs are designed to adaptively determine *when* to engage in extended thinking and when to provide a direct, concise response. This work pioneers the concept of a model that mimics human cognitive processes, where simple tasks are handled intuitively (System 1 thinking) and complex problems demand deliberate thought (System 2 thinking). By striking an optimal balance between the "System 1" ability (fast, intuitive responses) and "System 2" ability (slow, deliberate reasoning), LHRMs aim to significantly improve both the efficiency and applicability of reasoning-capable models, setting a new direction in the evolution of adaptable AI systems. This contrasts with existing efforts that either focus solely on improving reasoning performance (often at the cost of efficiency) or on making LLMs more general-purpose without a specific mechanism for adaptive reasoning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a novel class of language models, Large Hybrid-Reasoning Models (LHRMs), that can **adaptively decide whether to engage in extended thinking processes** based on the nature and complexity of the user query. This adaptive capability is motivated by several critical limitations of existing Large Reasoning Models (LRMs):

- **Addressing Overthinking:** Current LRMs generate explicit thinking traces for *all* queries, regardless of difficulty. This leads to substantial overhead in terms of token consumption, increased latency, and wasted computational resources for simple, straightforward questions. The authors aim to mitigate this "overthinking" problem, as exemplified by the LRM's lengthy internal monologue for a simple "Can you help me please?" query in Figure 1.
- **Balancing Reasoning and Efficiency:** The goal is to achieve an optimal trade-off between strong reasoning capabilities (essential for complex problems) and high efficiency (crucial for real-time applications and simpler tasks). Traditional LLMs lack robust reasoning, while LRMs are inefficient. LHRMs seek to combine the strengths of both.
- **Enhancing Real-World Applicability and User Experience:** By adapting their thinking mode, LHRMs aim to better align with human cognitive processes and expectations. Users prefer quick, direct answers for simple queries and detailed reasoning for complex ones. This adaptive behavior promises a more natural, responsive, and satisfying user experience, making these models more viable for diverse real-world deployments.
- **Developing a Robust Training and Evaluation Framework:** To achieve adaptive thinking, the authors are motivated to develop a new two-stage training pipeline that can teach models this nuanced behavior. Furthermore, they recognize the need for a dedicated metric beyond conventional task performance to quantitatively assess a model's "hybrid thinking" capability, leading to the introduction of Hybrid Accuracy.

In essence, the paper seeks to build a more intelligent and efficient reasoning system that avoids unnecessary computation while maintaining high performance on tasks that truly demand elaborate thought, thereby optimizing resource utilization and improving practical utility.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of the proposed solution is the **Large Hybrid-Reasoning Model (LHRM)**, trained using a novel **two-stage training pipeline**:
1. **Stage I: Hybrid Fine-Tuning (HFT)** for cold start and initial capability.
2. **Stage II: Hybrid Group Policy Optimization (HGPO)** for adaptive mode selection.

#### 3.1. Stage I: Hybrid Fine-Tuning (HFT)

HFT serves as an effective "cold start," enabling the model to initially learn to support two distinct thinking modes: **Thinking** (⊢) and **No-Thinking** (⊬). This stage prevents mode collapse and establishes a robust foundation for the subsequent reinforcement learning phase.

- **Data Construction:** A hybrid-formatted supervised fine-tuning dataset is created:
 - **Think-style subset:** Comprises high-quality math, code, and science questions from existing datasets (e.g., MATH500, LiveCodeBench, DeepSeek-R1 data). Answers are generated by powerful LRMs (like DeepSeek-R1) and verified. Each instance is explicitly annotated with `<think>` and `</think>` tags around the intermediate reasoning steps.
 - **No-Think-style subset:** Consists of simple, factual, and conversational queries primarily from WildChat-1M. A classifier is used to filter out complex reasoning tasks. These examples are wrapped with `<no_think>` and `</no_think>` tags, indicating that direct answers are sufficient.
 - The combined dataset, after deduplication, totals 1.7 million examples, providing a format-consistent foundation for training the model to recognize and generate responses in both styles.
- **Optimization Objective:** The model is trained using a standard next-token prediction objective (cross-entropy loss), aiming to predict the next token given the prior context, thereby learning the specific formatting and content associated with each thinking mode.

#### 3.2. Stage II: Hybrid Group Policy Optimization (HGPO)

Following HFT, HGPO is introduced as an online reinforcement learning (RL) algorithm. Its purpose is to teach the LHRM to **adaptively select the most appropriate reasoning mode** (Thinking or No-Thinking) for a given query, while simultaneously improving the overall quality (helpfulness and harmlessness) of the responses.

- **Sampling Strategy:** For each query `q` from a task prompt set, `N` candidate responses are sampled from the current policy model (`π_θ_HFT`) under *both* Thinking and No-Thinking modes. This generates two groups of outputs.
- **Reward Scoring and Assignment:**
 - A reward model (`R_ϕ`) assigns a score to each sampled response. For queries with definitive answers (e.g., math problems), rule-based rewards are used; otherwise, a trained parametric reward model (Llama-3.1-Tulu-3-8B-RM) is applied.
 - To capture both the relative quality *across* different reasoning modes and the answer quality *within* each mode, two types of binary rewards are computed for each response:
 - **Inter-group reward (`r_inter`):** Assigned '1' if the response's mode (Thinking or No-Thinking) had the *higher average reward* for that query across all samples, relative to the other mode (with a margin `δ` to control bias). This explicitly encourages the model to choose the better-performing mode.
 - **Intra-group reward (`r_intra`):** Assigned '1' if the response was the *highest-scoring within its own mode* (e.g., the best Thinking response among all Thinking samples for that query). This promotes high-quality generation within the chosen mode.
- **Advantage Estimation:** The paper uses GRPO (Generalized Policy Optimization) as the default advantage estimator to combine `r_inter` and `r_intra` into a per-token advantage (`A_t_i`). This advantage guides the policy updates, ensuring that tokens contributing to higher-reward responses (both within-mode and overall-best-mode) are reinforced. A coefficient `α` controls the weight of inter-group advantages, and the indicator function `1{o_t_i ∈ Φ}` ensures `r_inter` only influences the policy for the special tokens (`<think>`, `<no_think>`) that determine mode selection.
- **Optimization Objective:** HGPO optimizes the policy model `π_θ` by maximizing an objective function similar to Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) or its variants (e.g., REINFORCE++). This objective leverages the estimated advantages to update the policy while maintaining closeness to the reference policy (`π_ref`) using a KL divergence penalty to ensure training stability and prevent drastic policy shifts.

#### 3.3. Evaluating Hybrid Thinking Capability (Hybrid Accuracy, H_Acc)

To quantitatively assess the model's ability to correctly select the appropriate reasoning pattern, the authors propose a new metric called **Hybrid Accuracy (H_Acc)**.

- **Mechanism:** For each query, LHRM samples responses in both Thinking and No-Thinking modes. A reward model scores these responses. The mode with the higher average score (or the shorter response if scores are equal or very close) is designated as the "ground-truth preferred reasoning mode" (`m_gt`).
- **Calculation:** LHRM then autonomously selects a reasoning mode (`m_p`) for the query. H_Acc is the proportion of queries for which LHRM's selected mode (`m_p`) matches the ground-truth preferred mode (`m_gt`). This metric directly measures how well the model learns to choose the optimal reasoning approach for different query types.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental results validate the effectiveness of LHRMs and the proposed two-stage training pipeline across various benchmarks and model scales (1.5B and 7B parameters, based on Qwen-2.5-math-base).

#### 4.1. Overall Performance

- **Superior Performance:** LHRMs consistently outperform all comparable baselines, including traditional LLMs (Qwen-2.5 Math/Instruct series), LRMs (DeepSeek-R1-Distill series), and alternative hybrid training baselines (HFT, HFT-DPO, HFT-RFT).
- **Significant Gains:** LHRMs achieved average improvements of 9.2% (1.5B) and 7.1% (7B) over HFT-DPO, and even larger gains over HFT-RFT (18.3% at 1.5B, 11.5% at 7B).
- **Reasoning and General Capabilities:** LHRMs demonstrated strong performance on challenging reasoning benchmarks (e.g., 10.3% and 13.6% improvement on AIME24 for 1.5B and 7B respectively over HFT-DPO) and general capabilities (e.g., 50.2% on Alpaca and 93.4% on Arena-Hard for 7B over HFT-DPO), confirming its ability to excel in both complex problem-solving and general conversational tasks.
- **Hybrid Thinking Capability:** LHRMs achieved the best Hybrid Accuracy (H_Acc), significantly outperforming HFT-DPO by 93.8% and HFT-RFT by 44.7% at the 7B scale. This is a direct testament to HGPO's effectiveness in teaching the model proper mode selection.

#### 4.2. Effect of Training Stages

- **Stage I (HFT) Effectiveness:** HFT alone significantly improved reasoning and general capabilities compared to base Qwen2.5 models and DeepSeek-R1-Distill models, demonstrating its success in providing a robust cold start and preventing mode collapse during hybrid reasoning.
- **Stage II (HGPO) Effectiveness:** Comparing LHRMs to HFT, HGPO substantially further improved reasoning and general capabilities, alongside dramatic improvements in H_Acc (over 31.4% at 1.5B and 110.2% at 7B), confirming its role in enabling adaptive thinking.
- **HGPO vs. DPO/RFT:** HGPO showed superior downstream performance and mode selection accuracy compared to DPO and RFT applied to the HFT checkpoint, highlighting the unique advantages of the group policy optimization approach.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies and Analysis

- **Advantage Estimators:** HGPO proved robust to the choice of advantage estimator (GRPO, RLOO, REINFORCE++), with all yielding competitive performance.
- **Margin (δ) Impact:** The `δ` hyperparameter in reward assignment acts as a control knob for balancing thinking modes. A larger `δ` encourages No-Thinking, useful when responsiveness is prioritized, while a smaller `δ` favors Thinking for higher reasoning quality.
- **Thinking Ratio within Domain (MATH500):** LHRMs adaptively adjusted its thinking ratio based on query difficulty. Unlike HFT (which maintained a high thinking ratio), LHRMs-7B showed a decreasing thinking ratio for simpler queries while maintaining high accuracy, effectively reducing reasoning overhead. For challenging queries, it consistently engaged in full thinking.
- **Thinking Ratio across Domain:** HGPO exhibited strong cross-domain generalization. Even when RL training was solely on math and general data, the thinking ratio for the unseen code domain gradually increased, leading to improved code performance. This suggests that the learned hybrid thinking patterns are transferable.
- **Thinking Ratio across Model Size:** Model capacity influenced adaptive behavior. Larger models (7B) shifted towards No-Thinking for solvable problems to boost efficiency, while smaller models (1.5B) increased their use of Thinking to compensate for limited inherent capabilities.
- **Case Analysis:** Examples (Figure 1, 7, 9, 10) visually confirmed LHRMs' adaptive behavior: using No-Thinking for simple math or conversational queries for fast, accurate responses, and switching to Thinking mode for complex reasoning problems to produce precise solutions.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of Large Hybrid-Reasoning Models (LHRMs) marks a significant step forward in the development of intelligent and efficient large language models, addressing a critical limitation of current Large Reasoning Models (LRMs). Its significance and potential impact are multifaceted:

- **Mitigation of Overthinking and Improved Efficiency:** The most immediate and practical impact is the effective solution to the "overthinking" problem. By adaptively choosing between extended thinking and direct responses, LHRMs can significantly reduce token consumption, lower inference latency, and minimize computational costs. This makes advanced reasoning capabilities more economically viable and practical for real-world deployment, especially in high-throughput or latency-sensitive applications.
- **Enhanced User Experience:** LHRMs mimic human cognitive processes, where intuitive responses are given for simple queries, and deliberate thought is applied to complex ones. This leads to a more natural, responsive, and satisfying user experience, as models provide concise answers when appropriate and detailed reasoning when needed.
- **New Paradigm for Model Design and Training:** The paper proposes a novel two-stage training pipeline (Hybrid Fine-Tuning followed by Hybrid Group Policy Optimization) specifically designed to instill adaptive thinking. This pipeline and the accompanying HGPO algorithm provide a robust framework for building future hybrid AI systems that can dynamically adjust their computational effort.
- **Novel Evaluation Metric:** The introduction of Hybrid Accuracy (H_Acc) is a crucial contribution. It provides a quantitative and correlated measure of a model's ability to correctly select the appropriate reasoning mode, offering a new dimension for evaluating the intelligence and efficiency of adaptive AI systems beyond traditional task-specific metrics.
- **Cross-Domain Generalization:** The observed ability of LHRMs to transfer learned hybrid thinking patterns from one domain (e.g., math) to another unseen domain (e.g., code) demonstrates strong generalization capabilities. This suggests that the model learns a fundamental "decision-making policy" about reasoning effort rather than just memorizing domain-specific rules, enhancing its versatility.
- **Optimizing Model Capacity Utilization:** The analysis of thinking ratio across model sizes provides valuable insights: larger models learn to be more efficient by using No-Thinking for solvable problems, while smaller models leverage Thinking more frequently to compensate for inherent capacity limitations. This understanding can guide future model architecture and training strategies tailored to specific resource constraints and performance targets.

In conclusion, this work champions a reconsideration of how extended thinking processes are employed in large models. By providing a solid starting point for building truly adaptive hybrid thinking systems, LHRMs pave the way for more efficient, intelligent, and human-aligned AI agents capable of navigating diverse tasks with optimal cognitive effort.
