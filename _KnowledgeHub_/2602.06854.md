# SEMA: Simple yet Effective Learning for Multi-Turn Jailbreak Attacks

> [!summary] Summary
> SEMA, developed by researchers from the University of Rochester and Microsoft Research, presents a framework for learning multi-turn jailbreak attacks against large language models. It achieves up to 99.7% ASR@20 on HarmBench against GPT-4.1-mini by employing response-agnostic generation and an intent-drift-aware reward, outperforming previous state-of-the-art methods by 33.9% on AdvBench.

## Problem

- Existing single-turn jailbreak attacks are insufficient to model realistic, interactive multi-turn threats against Large Language Models (LLMs).
- Multi-turn attacks face high exploration complexity in their search space and suffer from 'intent drift,' where the attacker's malicious objective can be lost over dialogue turns.
- Current multi-turn jailbreak methods are often rigid (manual scripts, templates), opaque (closed-source APIs), or costly due to repeated victim interactions.

## Method

- A two-stage training process, combining ==Prefilling Self-Tuning== and online Reinforcement Learning (RL) with Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO).
- Response-agnostic open-loop generation, where the attacker generates an entire sequence of multi-turn prompts in one shot, decoupled from victim responses.
- An Intent-Drift-Aware Reward function, explicitly designed to anchor and score the harmful objective's persistence across all dialogue turns.

## Results

- Achieved state-of-the-art Attack Success Rates (ASR@1) across various open and closed-source victim models, outperforming prior methods by an average of **33.9%** on AdvBench.
- Demonstrated high scalability with attempt budget, reaching **96.8%** ASR@5 and **99.7%** ASR@20 against GPT-4.1-mini on HarmBench.
- Exhibited strong transferability with Transfer Attack Success Rates (TASR@1) up to **92.6%** when prompts successful against one model were applied to a different target victim LLM.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decoupling prompt generation from victim responses (open-loop) significantly reduces the exploration complexity and interaction costs of multi-turn adversarial attacks.
> - An explicit reward mechanism for maintaining malicious intent is crucial to counter 'intent drift' and ensure generated multi-turn attacks remain harmful and aligned with the original objective.
> - Simple self-tuning using minimal structural cues can enable stable generation of diverse, parseable multi-turn adversarial plans without external data or predefined strategies.
