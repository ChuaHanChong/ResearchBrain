# SEMA: Simple yet Effective Learning for Multi-Turn Jailbreak Attacks

> [!summary] Summary
> SEMA, developed by researchers from the University of Rochester and Microsoft Research, presents a framework for learning multi-turn jailbreak attacks against large language models. It achieves up to 99.7% ASR@20 on HarmBench against GPT-4.1-mini by employing response-agnostic generation and an intent-drift-aware reward, outperforming previous state-of-the-art methods by 33.9% on AdvBench.

## Problem

- Existing single-turn jailbreak attacks are insufficient to model realistic, interactive multi-turn threats against Large Language Models (LLMs).
- Multi-turn attacks face high exploration complexity in their search space and suffer from 'intent drift,' where the attacker's malicious objective can be lost over dialogue turns.
- Current multi-turn jailbreak methods are often rigid (manual scripts, templates), opaque (closed-source APIs), or costly due to repeated victim interactions.

## Method

- A two-stage training process, combining ==Prefilling Self-Tuning== and online Reinforcement Learning (RL) with Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO).
- Response-agnostic open-loop generation, where the attacker generates an entire sequence of multi-turn prompts in one shot, decoupled from victim responses.
- An Intent-Drift-Aware Reward function, explicitly designed to anchor and score the harmful objective's persistence across all dialogue turns.

## Results

- Achieved state-of-the-art Attack Success Rates (ASR@1) across various open and closed-source victim models, outperforming prior methods by an average of **33.9%** on AdvBench.
- Demonstrated high scalability with attempt budget, reaching **96.8%** ASR@5 and **99.7%** ASR@20 against GPT-4.1-mini on HarmBench.
- Exhibited strong transferability with Transfer Attack Success Rates (TASR@1) up to **92.6%** when prompts successful against one model were applied to a different target victim LLM.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decoupling prompt generation from victim responses (open-loop) significantly reduces the exploration complexity and interaction costs of multi-turn adversarial attacks.
> - An explicit reward mechanism for maintaining malicious intent is crucial to counter 'intent drift' and ensure generated multi-turn attacks remain harmful and aligned with the original objective.
> - Simple self-tuning using minimal structural cues can enable stable generation of diverse, parseable multi-turn adversarial plans without external data or predefined strategies.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper positions itself centrally within the rapidly evolving field of **Large Language Model (LLM) safety and security**, specifically focusing on **jailbreak attacks**. Jailbreaking refers to crafting inputs that bypass an LLM's safety alignments, prompting it to generate harmful, unethical, or disallowed content.

Historically, much of the research in this area has concentrated on **single-turn jailbreaks**, where a single, carefully crafted prompt aims to elicit a harmful response. However, the authors argue that this model is insufficient for capturing the true threat landscape of real-world chatbots. Chatbots operate in **interactive, multi-turn settings**, where users (including malicious attackers) can engage in prolonged dialogues. This paper correctly identifies single-turn attacks as merely a "special case" of the broader multi-turn threat.

The transition to multi-turn attacks introduces significant new challenges, primarily **exploration complexity** and **intent drift**.
- **Exploration Complexity**: Each turn in a dialogue exponentially expands the search space for effective adversarial prompts, making it combinatorially harder to find successful attack sequences.
- **Intent Drift**: As a dialogue progresses, the attacker's original malicious intent can gradually shift towards benign or irrelevant topics, inadvertently disarming the attack even if the model doesn't explicitly refuse. This is particularly problematic for methods that condition future turns on victim responses, as minor safe deflections can derail the entire attack.

Existing multi-turn jailbreak approaches are categorized into two main families, each with its limitations:
1. **Manually designed staging paradigms**: These involve expert-crafted, scripted dialogues that incrementally lead to harmful content (e.g., Jigsaw Puzzle, RED QUEEN). While effective in specific scenarios, they are labor-intensive, lack diversity, and are brittle to changes in LLM policies or platforms.
2. **Template-driven pipelines**: These methods automate prompt generation, often using other LLMs (sometimes closed-source APIs) to instantiate strategy templates and synthesize the next turn based on the victim's previous reply (e.g., Crescendo, GOAT, CoA, FITD). These approaches suffer from template rigidity, opacity due to reliance on closed-source APIs, and high cost due to repeated interactions with the victim model, leading to a brittle and expensive search.

A second line of related work involves **search and training-based jailbreaks**, which use optimization or learning (e.g., GCG, Autodan, ADV-LLM, PAP, MRJ, Jailbreak-R1). While showing promise, many are suffix-centric (limited to adding adversarial tokens), depend on gradient access (white-box), or are anchored to fixed, predefined strategies or external synthetic data. Importantly, methods like Jailbreak-R1 are restricted to single-turn attacks, limiting their applicability to the realistic multi-turn threat model.

SEMA distinguishes itself by addressing the core limitations of existing work. It aims to develop a multi-turn attacker that:
- Operates in an **open-loop, response-agnostic** manner, decoupling prompt generation from victim responses to reduce exploration complexity and interaction costs.
- Explores the multi-turn space **freely** without predefined strategies, external data, or hand-authored scripts.
- Effectively counters **intent drift** by explicitly anchoring the harmful objective across turns through a novel reward mechanism.
- Is **simple, scalable, reproducible**, and built on **open-source attacker LLMs**, making it a more accessible and robust tool for red-teaming.

By focusing on these aspects, SEMA pushes the boundaries of automated red-teaming, offering a more realistic, robust, and cost-effective method for stress-testing LLM safety alignments in a manner that strictly subsumes and extends prior single-turn efforts.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of the SEMA research is to develop a **simple yet effective framework for learning multi-turn jailbreak attacks** that accurately reflects real-world adversarial interactions with chatbots. The authors explicitly aim to train a multi-turn attacker without relying on existing strategies, external data, or costly victim feedback mechanisms.

The key motivations driving this research stem from the identified shortcomings of current LLM jailbreaking techniques:

1. **Realistic Threat Model**: The primary motivation is to address the gap between academic jailbreak research and the real-world threat model. Single-turn attacks, while important, are deemed insufficient because real chatbots operate interactively. Multi-turn interactions allow attackers to "stage context, obfuscate harmful intent, and incrementally bypass defenses," making them inherently harder to detect and mitigate. Therefore, studying multi-turn jailbreaks is crucial for understanding and bolstering LLM safety in practice.
2. **Overcoming Exploration Complexity**: Existing multi-turn methods face a combinatorial explosion in the search space due to the branching factor of possible prompts and victim responses. This complexity limits their scalability and effectiveness. A core motivation for SEMA is to devise an approach that efficiently explores this vast space.
3. **Combating Intent Drift**: A significant challenge in multi-turn interactions is that the harmful objective can gradually "drift" into benign or irrelevant topics. This phenomenon reduces the attack's effectiveness, even if the LLM doesn't explicitly refuse. SEMA aims to explicitly address and prevent this drift, ensuring the attacker maintains its malicious objective throughout the dialogue.
4. **Reducing Reliance on Rigid, Costly Approaches**: Current multi-turn methods often depend on:
 - **Manually designed scripts**: These are labor-intensive, lack diversity, and are not easily scalable.
 - **Template-driven pipelines**: These impose rigidity, limit creativity, and frequently rely on expensive, opaque closed-source APIs and repeated interactions with the victim model (closed-loop attack), making them costly and brittle.
 - **External corpora or predefined strategies**: Many learning-based methods rely on pre-collected adversarial data or fixed strategies, which can limit the diversity and adaptability of the learned attacks.
 SEMA is motivated by the desire to move beyond these limitations, offering a data-free, strategy-agnostic, and cost-efficient solution.
5. **Enabling Automatic Red-Teaming**: Ultimately, the goal is to provide a "stronger and more realistic stress test for LLM safety" and to "enable automatic redteaming to expose and localize failure modes." By developing a highly effective, scalable, and automated multi-turn jailbreak framework, SEMA aims to empower developers and researchers to systematically identify and patch vulnerabilities in LLM safety alignments before they are exploited in the wild.

In essence, SEMA seeks to unify the realism of multi-turn attacks with the efficiency and scalability needed for robust LLM safety evaluation, addressing the critical weaknesses of current adversarial testing paradigms.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

SEMA, a "simple yet effective" framework, tackles multi-turn jailbreak attacks through a novel two-stage training process, critically incorporating **response-agnostic open-loop generation** and an **intent-drift-aware reward** mechanism.

#### 3.1. Preliminaries: Response-Agnostic Open-Loop Generation

A foundational design choice in SEMA is to decouple the attacker's prompt generation from the victim's responses. Instead of the conventional closed-loop approach where the attacker generates the next prompt conditioned on the dialogue history and the victim's last reply, SEMA's attacker outputs a **complete sequence of *T* adversarial prompts in one shot** ($Q_{adv} = \{q_{adv,t}\}_{t=1}^T$), conditioned only on a system prompt and the original harmful query.

This **open-loop generation** offers several benefits:
- **Reduced Exploration Complexity**: It shrinks the search space from a combinatorial product of prompts and responses to the prompt space alone, significantly cutting the branching factor.
- **Lower Interaction Cost**: It avoids repeated, costly interactions with the victim model during the attacker's planning phase.
- **Batched Sampling**: It enables efficient parallel processing of attack sequences.
- **Unification**: It naturally unifies single-turn ($T=1$) and multi-turn ($T>1$) jailbreaks under a single framework.

The attacker model is trained using **online reinforcement learning (RL)**, specifically an adapted version of Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) (Shao et al., 2024), which is designed for stable and efficient exploration.

#### 3.2. Stage 1: Prefilling Self-Tuning

The first stage addresses the practical challenge of generating usable, well-formatted open-loop multi-turn adversarial prompts, especially when starting with a safety-aligned LLM that might refuse such requests or fail to follow structural instructions.

- **Prefilling Rollout**: To overcome initial refusals and ensure proper formatting, SEMA introduces a "prefilling rollout." This involves injecting a minimal, non-refusal structural cue (e.g., the list marker "1.") at the start of the attacker's output. This simple prefix encourages the model to continue generating a structured list of turns ("2.", "3.", etc.), bypassing initial safety filters and guiding it toward producing a parseable multi-turn sequence.
- **Self-Tuning**: Batches of these non-refusal, correctly formatted rollouts (generated under the prefix anchor) are then collected. These self-generated sequences are used for **Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT)** of the attacker model. Critically, no external data or manual filtering/revision is applied. The attacker learns from its own diverse, well-structured, non-refusal outputs.

This stage has a dual effect: it operationalizes open-loop multi-turn attacks by stabilizing parseable rollouts, and it does so without imposing predefined strategies or external data, thus preserving the capacity for open-ended exploration in the subsequent RL stage.

#### 3.3. Stage 2: Reinforcement Learning with Intent-Drift-Aware Reward

After prefilling self-tuning, the attacker is capable of generating structured, non-refusal multi-turn plans. The second stage uses RL to train this attacker to generate plans that not only bypass victim defenses but also **persistently maintain the harmful objective** across all turns, countering intent drift.

- **System Instruction ("Ask the Same Thing Differently")**: The attacker is provided with a system prompt that explicitly instructs it to generate a multi-turn adversarial plan (up to $T_{max}$ turns) such that "the final turn, when read in the context of preceding turns and plausible victim replies, should yield the same canonical answer as the original harmful query." This instruction inherently anchors the harmful intent. The "different way" encourages exploration of circuitous or obfuscated paths to the objective.
- **Reward Design**: The core innovation here is the **Intent-Drift-Aware Reward** ($R_{IDA}$), which is computed via a simulated jailbreak attack. For each generated multi-turn plan ($Q_{adv}$), it is executed against a training-time victim model. An evaluation model (e.g., GPT-4.1-mini) then scores the *final-turn response* ($r_T$) based on three components:
 1. **Alignment($r_T, q$)**: Measures how well the final response aligns with the original harmful intent, penalizing intent drift.
 2. **Risk($r_T$)**: Scores the inherent compliance risk or harmfulness of the final response.
 3. **Detail($r_T$)**: Rewards concrete, actionable, and specific answers.
 The combined reward is $R_{IDA}(r_T, q) = \frac{1}{2} \text{Alignment}(r_T, q) \cdot (\text{Risk}(r_T) + \text{Detail}(r_T))$.
 An additional **format reward ($R_{format}$)** is included to ensure the attacker continues to produce parseable multi-turn outputs.
- **GRPO Optimization**: The collected rewards are then used within the GRPO objective to update the attacker's policy. GRPO's group sampling and relative advantage mechanism facilitate stable updates and exploration of high-reward outputs.

By combining these stages, SEMA effectively learns to generate diverse, multi-turn adversarial prompts that consistently bypass LLM safety alignments while rigorously preserving the attacker's malicious intent.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

SEMA demonstrates significant advancements in multi-turn jailbreak attacks, achieving state-of-the-art performance across a wide range of evaluation settings.

#### 4.1. State-of-the-Art Attack Success Rates (ASR)

- **Overall Dominance**: SEMA consistently achieves the highest ASR@1 (Attack Success Rate with one attempt) across multiple datasets (AdvBench, HarmBench), various open-source (Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct, Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, GPT-oss-20B) and closed-source (GPT-4.1-mini, GPT-4o) victim models, and diverse jailbreak judges (LLM classifier, HarmBench classifier, No Refusal Phrase Indicator, Qwen3Guard).
- **Quantitative Edge**: For instance, on AdvBench, SEMA achieves an average ASR@1 of 80.1% across three closed-source and open-source victim models, outperforming the previous state-of-the-art by 33.9%. On HarmBench, similar strong results were observed (e.g., 75.0% average ASR@1).
- **Robustness to Judges**: SEMA not only achieves high success rates by lenient judges (e.g., No Refusal Phrase Indicator) but also maintains its lead under stricter, intent-aware classifiers (LLM Classifier, HarmBench Classifier, Qwen3Guard), confirming its ability to produce truly harmful, intent-aligned content.
- **Outperformance of Baselines**: SEMA significantly outperforms all categories of baselines, including single-turn attacks (FlipAttack, ADV-LLM, Jailbreak-R1), manually-scripted multi-turn attacks (Jigsaw Puzzle), template-driven interactive multi-turn attacks (Crescendo, GOAT, CoA, FITD, ActorAttack, X-Teaming), and even its own Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) and Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) variants. This underscores the effectiveness of its unique open-loop, data-free, and intent-aware RL approach.

#### 4.2. Scalability with Attempt Budget (ASR@N)

- **Efficient Success Conversion**: SEMA efficiently converts additional attempt budgets into higher attack success rates. Against GPT-4.1-mini on HarmBench, SEMA achieved 96.8% ASR@5 and reached 99.7% ASR@20, indicating near-total jailbreak capability with a reasonable number of trials. This performance significantly outpaces baselines like Jailbreak-R1 and Augmentation, which require many more attempts to reach comparable (though still lower) success rates.
- **Secure Model Performance**: Even against the highly secure GPT-oss-20B, SEMA's ASR rose from 37.6% at N=1 to 80.8% at N=20 on AdvBench, demonstrating its capacity to effectively challenge frontier models when given multiple attempts.

#### 4.3. Strong Transferability (TASR)

- **Cross-Model Generalization**: SEMA exhibits excellent transferability, maintaining high TASR@1 (Transfer Attack Success Rate) when adversarial prompts successful against a source victim are applied to a target victim.
- **Quantitative Transfer**: On AdvBench, SEMA achieved TASR@1 rates of 85.1% (Qwen2.5-3B → Llama-3.1-8B), 92.6% (Qwen2.5-3B → GPT-4.1-mini), and 91.1% (Llama-3.1-8B → GPT-4.1-mini). Similar high rates were observed on HarmBench (e.g., 88.6% from Qwen2.5-3B → GPT-4.1-mini). This indicates that the learned multi-turn strategies are not overly specific to the training-time victim but generalize well across different LLM architectures and safety alignments.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies

- **Intent-Drift-Aware Reward is Crucial**: Removing the intent alignment component from the reward function significantly degrades ASR (e.g., 67.5% to 50.4% on AdvBench by LLM Classifier), while increasing refusal rates. Directly using a simple "No-Refusal Reward" leads to benign outputs and zero success by stricter judges, confirming that explicitly rewarding intent alignment is vital for generating truly harmful attacks.
- **Prefilling Self-Tuning is Essential**: Without the prefilling self-tuning stage, the attacker model consistently refused to generate adversarial prompts, preventing stable learning and convergence in the RL stage. This highlights its role in enabling usable rollouts and stabilizing training.
- **Optimal Number of Turns**: Increasing the maximum number of turns ($T_{max}$) during training generally enhances attack capability up to an optimum (e.g., $T_{max}$=7). However, further increasing turns (e.g., to 10 for smaller attackers) can impair performance, possibly due to model capacity limitations in generating high-quality, longer adversarial sequences.

#### 4.5. Case Analysis

- **Diverse Attack Strategies**: Case studies showcase SEMA's ability to generate distinct multi-turn attack schemes that avoid intent drift. Examples include gradually escalating requests (e.g., from historical overview to bomb assembly steps) or framing harmful requests within fictional scenarios (e.g., a "thriller novel" for blackmail instructions). These diverse tactics successfully jailbreak even highly secure models like GPT-oss-20B and Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct where other baselines fail.

In summary, SEMA not only achieves superior attack success rates across a broad spectrum of evaluations but also does so with a reproducible, cost-effective, and robust methodology that overcomes fundamental challenges of multi-turn adversarial interactions.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The SEMA framework represents a significant step forward in the field of LLM safety and red-teaming, with several profound implications and potential impacts:

1. **Enhanced LLM Safety Evaluation and Red-Teaming**: SEMA provides a "stronger and more realistic stress test for LLM safety" than prior methods. By demonstrating state-of-the-art performance in multi-turn jailbreaks that preserve harmful intent, it offers a more sophisticated and effective tool for automated red-teaming. This enables developers and safety researchers to proactively identify and localize complex failure modes and vulnerabilities in safety-aligned chatbots, which might be missed by single-turn or less robust multi-turn attacks. The ability to automatically generate diverse, intent-stable multi-turn adversarial plans can significantly improve the robustness of future LLMs.

2. **Addressing Key Challenges in Adversarial AI**: SEMA directly addresses two critical challenges in multi-turn adversarial interactions: **exploration complexity** and **intent drift**. Its open-loop, response-agnostic generation dramatically reduces the search space and interaction costs compared to closed-loop methods. The intent-drift-aware reward mechanism ensures that generated attacks remain focused on the malicious objective, a vital component for effective adversarial testing in interactive settings. This methodological innovation could inspire new approaches in other areas of AI safety where maintaining a specific objective through complex interactions is crucial.

3. **Simplicity, Reproducibility, and Accessibility**: Despite its effectiveness, SEMA is described as a "compact, reproducible framework." Its reliance on self-generated data (data-free), lack of dependence on external corpora or hand-crafted strategies, and utilization of open-source attacker LLMs makes it highly accessible to the broader research community. This contrasts sharply with many template-driven methods that often rely on opaque, costly closed-source APIs. The availability of code further promotes transparency and accelerates follow-up research.

4. **Cost-Effectiveness and Efficiency**: By employing an open-loop generation strategy and leveraging smaller, open-source attacker models, SEMA achieves high attack success rates with significantly lower inference costs and faster runtimes compared to interactive, API-dependent baselines. This efficiency makes continuous, large-scale red-teaming more economically feasible for organizations.

5. **Unifying Attack Paradigms**: SEMA's design unifies single-turn and multi-turn jailbreaks by treating adversarial prompts as sequences, thereby simplifying the conceptual framework for analyzing adversarial interactions. This unified perspective can foster more holistic research into LLM vulnerabilities.

6. **Future Research Directions**: The paper explicitly points towards future work, including co-evolving defenses, expanding to multimodal settings (vision, audio), and developing turn-efficient closed-loop attackers. SEMA lays a strong foundation for these next steps, providing a benchmark and a robust methodological starting point for pushing the boundaries of LLM safety research into more complex and dynamic environments.

In essence, SEMA not only demonstrates a powerful new capability for jailbreaking LLMs but, more importantly, provides a principled, efficient, and scalable framework that will be instrumental in the ongoing arms race between LLM capabilities and their safety alignments. Its impact will primarily be in strengthening the defensive posture of LLMs against increasingly sophisticated real-world threats.
