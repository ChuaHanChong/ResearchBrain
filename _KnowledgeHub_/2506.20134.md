# From 2D to 3D Cognition: A Brief Survey of General World Models

> [!summary] Summary
> This survey provides a structured and forward-looking review of general world models, emphasizing their transition from primarily 2D visual perception to comprehensive 3D spatial cognition. It highlights how advancements in 3D representations and the integration of physical and learned world knowledge are enabling models to achieve 3D physical scene generation, spatial reasoning, and interaction, outlining critical challenges and future research directions.

## Problem

- Prior world models primarily focused on 2D visual perception, which limited their ability to accurately simulate motion, physical interactions, and causal reasoning in true 3D environments.
- A lack of systematic analysis existed to categorize emerging techniques and clarify their roles in advancing 3D cognitive world models, despite rapid progress in 3D-aware generative models.
- Existing surveys often focused on rendering realism or abstract structural modeling without a unified perspective on the transition to comprehensive 3D cognition and active interaction.

## Method

- The survey introduces a ==conceptual framework== to systematically review world models as they evolve from ==2D perception== to ==3D cognition==, organizing various components and capabilities.
- It dissects core cognitive capabilities -- ==3D Physical Scene Generation==, ==3D Spatial Reasoning==, and ==3D Spatial Interaction== -- analyzing how models achieve these by leveraging ==3D representations== and ==world knowledge==.
- The paper examines real-world applications and identifies open challenges and promising future directions across data, modeling, and deployment, providing a roadmap for research.

## Results

- The survey established that the field of world models is undergoing a paradigm shift towards 3D cognitive systems, recognizing that understanding and interacting with a geometrically consistent 3D world is fundamental for general intelligence.
- It synthesized that advanced 3D representations and the integration of physical and learned world knowledge are the two fundamental pillars enabling this transition.
- The work structured advancements around three core cognitive capabilities: 3D physical scene generation, 3D spatial reasoning, and 3D spatial interaction, demonstrating their broad applicability across embodied AI, autonomous driving, digital twins, and gaming/VR.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The transition to 3D cognition in world models is driven by advancements in 3D representations (e.g., NeRF, 3DGS) and the integration of diverse world knowledge (physical laws via differentiable simulations and learned commonsense from large pretrained models like LLMs/VLMs).
> - Three interdependent cognitive capabilities underpin 3D world modeling: 3D physical scene generation (creating physically plausible environments), 3D spatial reasoning (interpreting and analyzing 3D dynamics), and 3D spatial interaction (enabling goal-directed physical engagement).
> - Identifying persistent challenges in data heterogeneity, model scalability, and real-time performance is crucial, alongside proposing breakthrough paths like standardized data formats and advanced fusion techniques.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This survey paper is strategically positioned at a critical juncture in the development of Artificial General Intelligence (AGI), specifically within the domain of "World Models." World models are computational frameworks designed to learn representations of the external world and predict future states, serving as a core component in the pursuit of more intelligent and autonomous AI systems.

Historically, early world models primarily focused on 2D visual perception and simulation. The authors highlight significant strides made by leading AI organizations, such as Meta's self-supervised Joint Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA) and OpenAI's Sora video generation model. While these models demonstrate impressive capabilities in abstract representation learning and 2D video generation with implicit physical dynamics, they face inherent limitations when addressing motion simulation, physical interaction, and causal reasoning within truly 3D environments. JEPA's hierarchical encoders may discard low-level geometric details, leading to degraded performance in dynamic occlusion, while Sora's pixel-level correlations lack explicit 3D structure, limiting its ability to simulate complex physics or spatial causality.

The current landscape is witnessing a paradigm shift towards **3D-aware generative world models**. Recent examples like World Lab’s Large World Models (LWMs), Google DeepMind’s Genie 2, and Odyssey’s Explorer signify this transition. These models leverage advancements in neural scene representations (e.g., Neural Radiance Fields, 3D Gaussian Splatting) to synthesize geometrically consistent, interactive 3D environments. This collective progress indicates a move from mere 2D simulations to 3D cognitive systems capable of perceiving, reasoning, and interacting with complex 3D environments.

This survey paper addresses a crucial gap in this rapidly accelerating field: the **lack of systematic analysis** to categorize emerging techniques and clarify their roles in advancing 3D cognitive world models. While prior surveys have touched upon related areas like multimodal learning, physical video synthesis, or dimensional scaling in generative models, they often focus on rendering realism or abstract structural modeling without a unified perspective on the transition to 3D cognition and active interaction. The authors explicitly distinguish their work from others by providing a comprehensive and structured view that unifies 3D representations, world knowledge (physical priors, learned commonsense), and spatial cognitive capabilities (generation, reasoning, interaction). By doing so, the survey aims to provide a much-needed conceptual framework, guide future research, and foster a deeper understanding of the core technical pathways and challenges in building robust and generalizable 3D world models.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this survey stems from the recognition of a significant shift in the field of world models: the evolution from 2D perceptual simulation towards comprehensive 3D spatial cognition. Despite rapid advancements in 3D-aware generative models, the authors identify a critical gap in the existing literature—a lack of systematic analysis and categorization of the diverse techniques emerging in this domain. As research approaches an "inflection point," there is an urgent need to understand and clarify the core technical pathways, capabilities, and challenges that define the development of 3D cognitive world models.

Based on this motivation, the key objectives of this survey are:

1. **Introduce a Conceptual Framework:** To provide a structured and forward-looking review of world models as they transition from 2D perception to 3D cognition. This framework is designed to organize the various components and capabilities.
2. **Highlight Key Technological Drivers:** To emphasize two fundamental pillars driving this transition:
 - **Advances in 3D Representations:** Understanding how various 3D data formats (e.g., NeRF, 3DGS, point clouds, meshes) enable more accurate and detailed world modeling.
 - **Incorporation of World Knowledge:** Exploring the integration of both explicit physical laws (from differentiable simulations) and learned knowledge (spatial commonsense, semantic priors from large pretrained models like LLMs and VLMs).
3. **Dissect Core Cognitive Capabilities:** To analyze three interdependent capabilities that underpin 3D world modeling:
 - **3D Physical Scene Generation:** The ability to reconstruct and synthesize volumetric environments that adhere to physical plausibility, ensuring geometric fidelity and dynamic realism.
 - **3D Spatial Reasoning:** The capacity to infer spatial relations, object semantics/functions, and environmental dynamics by integrating geometric computation with semantic and commonsense priors.
 - **3D Spatial Interaction:** Equipping models with the capacity for goal-directed, physically consistent interaction, ranging from embodied agent behaviors to user-driven editing.
4. **Examine Real-World Applications:** To illustrate how these burgeoning capabilities are being deployed in practical domains such as embodied AI, autonomous driving, digital twins, and gaming/VR.
5. **Identify Challenges and Future Directions:** To delineate the persistent obstacles across data, modeling, and deployment aspects, and to outline promising avenues for advancing more robust and generalizable 3D world models.

In essence, the survey aims to serve as a roadmap for researchers and practitioners, providing a unified perspective on the foundational elements, emerging capabilities, and future trajectory of 3D cognitive world models.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

As a survey paper, the authors' methodology involves a comprehensive and structured review of existing literature, organized around a novel conceptual framework designed to clarify the transition from 2D to 3D cognition in world models.

The core of their approach is built upon a **conceptual framework** (illustrated in Fig. 1 of the paper) that posits two foundational components essential for enabling spatial cognitive capabilities:
1. **3D Representations:** The survey systematically reviews various 3D data formats and neural representations, including point clouds, meshes, 3D occupancy grids, Signed Distance Functions (SDF), Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF), and 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS). For each, they provide a brief description of its characteristics and relevance for 3D world modeling.
2. **World Knowledge:** The authors categorize world knowledge into two primary sources:
 - **Physical Knowledge from Physics Simulation:** This includes an overview of classic simulation methods (Material Point Method (MPM), Position-Based Dynamics (PBD), Finite Element Method (FEM)) and a discussion of modern differentiable physics engines and large-scale 3D simulation platforms (e.g., Taichi, Nvidia Warp, FluidLab, Genesis, AI2-THOR, Habitat, ThreeDWorld, MetaUrban). The emphasis is on how these tools encode physical laws and provide ground truth for dynamics.
 - **Learned Knowledge via Large Pretrained Models:** This section explores how Large Language Models (LLMs) and Vision-Language Models (VLMs) contribute to world knowledge, specifically focusing on spatial commonsense (e.g., from LLaMA, PaLM, GPT-4V, and prompting strategies) and semantic priors (e.g., from CLIP, BLIP, LLaVA, Grounding DINO, SAM, and 2D diffusion models like Stable Diffusion).

Building upon these foundational pillars, the survey then dissects three core cognitive capabilities, mirroring the "perceive-think-act" loop in intelligent systems:
- **3D Physical Scene Generation:** This section is divided into static scene generation (layout-guided and physics-informed methods) and dynamic scene generation (physics-regularized and physics simulation-based methods), providing tables summarizing representative works, their 3D representations, and incorporated priors.
- **3D Spatial Reasoning:** This capability is broken down into static spatial understanding (point-language alignment for point cloud understanding, and language-guided reasoning over NeRF/3DGS) and dynamic spatial analysis (occupancy-based and neural rendering-based approaches for forecasting scene evolution). Again, tables summarize key methods, their use of LLMs/VLMs, and reasoning tasks.
- **3D Spatial Interaction:** This section differentiates between egocentric embodied interaction (human-scene and agent-scene interaction) and exocentric scene manipulation (static and dynamic scene manipulation), detailing how world models facilitate goal-directed physical interaction.

Finally, the survey examines the **deployment of these capabilities in real-world applications**, including embodied AI, autonomous driving, digital twins, and gaming/VR, showcasing how specialized models combine these core capabilities to address domain-specific challenges. The report concludes by identifying **open challenges and promising future directions** across data, modeling, and deployment, providing a roadmap for future research.

This structured approach, characterized by a clear conceptual framework, systematic categorization of existing work, and identification of technological drivers and cognitive capabilities, allows the survey to provide a unified and comprehensive understanding of the field's trajectory.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

As a survey paper, the "findings and results" are not experimental outcomes but rather the synthesis, classification, and identification of key trends and challenges within the field. The main findings presented by this survey are:

1. **Paradigm Shift Towards 3D Cognition:** The field of world models is undergoing a fundamental transition from primarily focusing on 2D visual perception and simulation to developing 3D cognitive systems. This shift is driven by the realization that true general intelligence necessitates an understanding of and interaction with the geometrically consistent and physically coherent 3D world.
2. **Two Fundamental Pillars for 3D World Models:** The authors identify and elaborate on two crucial technological drivers enabling this transition:
 - **Advanced 3D Representations:** The evolution from traditional 3D data formats (point clouds, meshes, occupancy grids, SDFs) to powerful neural implicit representations like Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) and particularly 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) is pivotal. These representations facilitate photorealistic view synthesis, efficient rendering, and the encoding of complex scene geometry and appearance, making them ideal substrates for trainable world models.
 - **Integration of World Knowledge:** The survey demonstrates that modern world models increasingly rely on two types of knowledge:
 - **Physical Knowledge:** Achieved by incorporating explicit physical laws through differentiable simulation methods (MPM, PBD, FEM) and robust physics engines, ensuring physically plausible scene generation and interaction.
 - **Learned Knowledge:** Derived from large pretrained models (LLMs, VLMs) which implicitly encode spatial commonsense (e.g., typical object relationships) and semantic priors (object categories, attributes), enabling higher-level reasoning and instruction following.
3. **Emergence of Three Core Cognitive Capabilities:** The paper structures the advancements around a triad of interdependent cognitive capabilities:
 - **3D Physical Scene Generation:** Techniques are evolving to create not just visually appealing but also physically plausible 3D environments, both static (layout-guided, physics-informed) and dynamic (physics-regularized, simulation-based, or learning physical properties). This ensures geometric fidelity, material plausibility, and coherence over time.
 - **3D Spatial Reasoning:** World models are gaining the ability to interpret and analyze 3D environments. This includes static spatial understanding (aligning point clouds, NeRF, or 3DGS with language for captioning, QA, grounding, and segmentation) and dynamic spatial analysis (forecasting scene evolution and object trajectories, often through occupancy grids enhanced by semantic priors).
 - **3D Spatial Interaction:** Models are moving from passive observation to active participation. This involves egocentric embodied interaction (synthesizing human/agent motions and object manipulations that are physically and semantically plausible) and exocentric scene manipulation (user-driven editing of 3D scenes via text or visual inputs, maintaining spatiotemporal and physical consistency).
4. **Broad Applicability Across Diverse Domains:** These cognitive capabilities are demonstrated to be foundational for significant advancements in:
 - **Embodied AI:** Enabling agents to perceive, reason, plan, and act in complex 3D environments.
 - **Autonomous Driving:** Facilitating real-time perception, prediction, and planning in dynamic traffic scenarios.
 - **Digital Twins:** Creating scalable, controllable, and physically accurate digital replicas of real-world entities and urban environments.
 - **Gaming/VR:** Developing immersive, physically plausible, and interactive virtual worlds with automated content generation.
5. **Identification of Key Challenges and Future Directions:** The survey systematically outlines the persistent obstacles in data (heterogeneity, quality, annotation limitations), modeling (scalability, integration of complex physics, real-time multimodal pipelines), and deployment (inference latency, scalability in multi-agent systems). It also suggests breakthrough paths, such as standardized data formats, advanced fusion techniques, model compression, federated learning, and causal reasoning frameworks, to guide future research toward robust and generalizable 3D world models.

In essence, the survey's main result is a comprehensive, structured understanding of the current state and future trajectory of 3D cognitive world models, providing a conceptual map for navigating this complex and rapidly advancing field.
