# When Do We Not Need Larger Vision Models?

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from UC Berkeley and Microsoft Research developed 'Scaling on Scales (S²)', a parameter-free method that enables smaller, pre-trained vision models to achieve performance comparable to or exceeding much larger models. This approach processes images across multiple scales, resulting in significantly fewer parameters and lower computational costs for similar performance, notably achieving state-of-the-art visual detail understanding with a LLaVA-1.5 model on the V* benchmark.

## Problem

- The prevailing 'scaling law' paradigm in AI relies on continuously increasing model size (parameters, depth, width) to achieve better performance, leading to high computational costs, energy consumption, and accessibility barriers.
- Training and deploying large vision models demand immense computational power, resulting in substantial carbon footprints and financial barriers that limit research and application access.
- Existing multi-scale methods in computer vision often require specialized architectures, extensive re-training, or are primarily used as test-time augmentations, not as general parameter-free scaling approaches.

## Method

- The paper introduces =='Scaling on Scales (S²)'==, a ==parameter-free scaling approach== that allows any pre-trained and frozen smaller vision model to process images at multiple scales.
- S² employs an =='S²-Wrapper'== that interpolates input images to various scales, splits larger scales into sub-images, and feeds each individually into the same frozen model.
- Features extracted from different scales are then merged and concatenated to create a ==unified multi-scale representation== with a consistent output token count, suitable for downstream tasks like classification or integration with large language models.

## Results

- S²-enhanced smaller vision models (e.g., ViT-B) achieved comparable or superior performance to significantly larger models (e.g., ViT-H, ViT-G) across diverse perception tasks using remarkably fewer parameters (**0.28x to 0.07x**).
- Scaling an OpenCLIP vision backbone with S² enabled LLaVA-1.5 to achieve state-of-the-art visual detail understanding on the V* benchmark, surpassing commercial models like Gemini Pro and GPT-4V.
- When pre-trained with S², smaller models matched or exceeded the performance of larger models, demonstrating that their generalization capabilities are not inherently limited but benefit from multi-scale training.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The features learned by larger vision models can be effectively approximated by those of multi-scale smaller models through a simple linear transform, suggesting comparable representational capacity.
> - Smaller models' generalizability limitations are often not inherent but stem from single-scale pre-training; multi-scale pre-training can close performance gaps with larger models.
> - Splitting large images into sub-images and processing them independently is crucial for efficiency and performance in multi-scale processing, preventing quadratic complexity issues.
