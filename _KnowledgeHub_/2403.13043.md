# When Do We Not Need Larger Vision Models?

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from UC Berkeley and Microsoft Research developed 'Scaling on Scales (S²)', a parameter-free method that enables smaller, pre-trained vision models to achieve performance comparable to or exceeding much larger models. This approach processes images across multiple scales, resulting in significantly fewer parameters and lower computational costs for similar performance, notably achieving state-of-the-art visual detail understanding with a LLaVA-1.5 model on the V* benchmark.

## Problem

- The prevailing 'scaling law' paradigm in AI relies on continuously increasing model size (parameters, depth, width) to achieve better performance, leading to high computational costs, energy consumption, and accessibility barriers.
- Training and deploying large vision models demand immense computational power, resulting in substantial carbon footprints and financial barriers that limit research and application access.
- Existing multi-scale methods in computer vision often require specialized architectures, extensive re-training, or are primarily used as test-time augmentations, not as general parameter-free scaling approaches.

## Method

- The paper introduces =='Scaling on Scales (S²)'==, a ==parameter-free scaling approach== that allows any pre-trained and frozen smaller vision model to process images at multiple scales.
- S² employs an =='S²-Wrapper'== that interpolates input images to various scales, splits larger scales into sub-images, and feeds each individually into the same frozen model.
- Features extracted from different scales are then merged and concatenated to create a ==unified multi-scale representation== with a consistent output token count, suitable for downstream tasks like classification or integration with large language models.

## Results

- S²-enhanced smaller vision models (e.g., ViT-B) achieved comparable or superior performance to significantly larger models (e.g., ViT-H, ViT-G) across diverse perception tasks using remarkably fewer parameters (**0.**28x** to 0.07x**).
- Scaling an OpenCLIP vision backbone with S² enabled LLaVA-1.5 to achieve state-of-the-art visual detail understanding on the V* benchmark, surpassing commercial models like Gemini Pro and GPT-4V.
- When pre-trained with S², smaller models matched or exceeded the performance of larger models, demonstrating that their generalization capabilities are not inherently limited but benefit from multi-scale training.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The features learned by larger vision models can be effectively approximated by those of multi-scale smaller models through a simple linear transform, suggesting comparable representational capacity.
> - Smaller models' generalizability limitations are often not inherent but stem from single-scale pre-training; multi-scale pre-training can close performance gaps with larger models.
> - Splitting large images into sub-images and processing them independently is crucial for efficiency and performance in multi-scale processing, preventing quadratic complexity issues.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The current landscape of artificial intelligence, particularly in areas like large language models (LLMs) and visual understanding, is heavily dominated by the "scaling law" paradigm. The prevailing belief is that simply scaling up model size—increasing parameters, depth, and width—leads to consistently better performance, provided sufficient data and computational resources. This has driven the development of models with billions, and even tens of billions, of parameters, like GPT-4, LLaMA, and various large vision transformers (e.g., ViT-G, DINOv2-Giant, OpenCLIP-Giant). This "bigger is better" strategy has indeed yielded remarkable advancements across various tasks, from text generation to image classification, segmentation, and multimodal understanding.

However, this relentless pursuit of larger models comes at a significant cost. Training and deploying these colossal models demand enormous computational power, leading to immense energy consumption, substantial carbon footprints, and high financial barriers. This creates an accessibility gap, limiting cutting-edge research and application to only a few well-resourced organizations.

This paper directly challenges this prevailing paradigm. While multi-scale representation is not a new concept in computer vision—techniques like SIFT, HOG, Feature Pyramid Networks (FPNs), and U-Nets have long utilized features at different scales for robust object recognition and dense prediction tasks—these methods typically involve designing specialized architectures, extensive re-training, or are primarily employed as test-time augmentations with limited scaling. Existing research on scaling vision models has largely focused on optimizing the scaling of *model size* or increasing the *input resolution during pre-training*, but usually not exceeding resolutions like 512x512 due to computational demands.

The unique contribution of this work is its proposition of "Scaling on Scales (S²)" as a *general, parameter-free scaling approach* that can be applied to *any pre-trained and frozen smaller vision model*. By demonstrating that S² can match or even exceed the performance of much larger models while using significantly fewer parameters and comparable GFLOPs, the paper argues for a paradigm shift. It suggests that scaling *input resolution and processing it intelligently across scales* can be a more efficient and effective alternative to merely increasing model size, thus offering a path toward more resource-efficient and accessible high-performance vision models. This work directly addresses the computational and environmental sustainability concerns associated with the current scaling trend.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to critically re-evaluate the necessity of continuously scaling up vision model size for achieving superior visual understanding. Instead of accepting larger models as the default solution, the authors aim to explore and validate an alternative scaling dimension: the scaling of image scales.

The key motivations driving this research are:

1. **Challenging the "Bigger is Better" Assumption:** To question whether the performance gains observed with larger models are solely attributable to their increased parameter count and capacity, or if these gains can be achieved through other, potentially more efficient, means.
2. **Developing a Resource-Efficient Scaling Strategy:** To introduce and evaluate "Scaling on Scales (S²)" as a method that allows pre-trained, smaller vision models to achieve performance comparable to, or even surpassing, much larger models, but with significantly fewer parameters and potentially more efficient computation. This addresses the high computational cost, energy consumption, and environmental impact associated with current model scaling trends.
3. **Enhancing Accessibility to High-Performance Models:** By demonstrating that smaller models can be made highly effective through S², the research aims to democratize access to state-of-the-art visual understanding capabilities, making them viable for researchers and practitioners with limited computational resources.
4. **Investigating the Learning Capacity of Smaller Models:** To delve deeper into the representational power of smaller models and understand if they inherently lack the capacity to learn what larger models learn, or if their perceived limitations stem from their pre-training conditions (e.g., single-scale pre-training).
5. **Informing Future Model Design and Pre-training Strategies:** To provide insights that could lead to new architectural designs or pre-training methodologies that prioritize efficient multi-scale processing over brute-force parameter scaling, particularly by exploring the impact of multi-scale pre-training.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of the paper's methodology revolves around the concept of **Scaling on Scales (S²)**, implemented via a novel, parameter-free mechanism called **S²-Wrapper**.

**S²-Wrapper Mechanism:**
The S²-Wrapper is designed to extend any pre-trained vision model to process images at multiple scales without introducing any additional trainable parameters to the model itself. The process involves several key steps and design choices:

1. **Multi-Scale Interpolation:** An input image is first interpolated to multiple different scales (e.g., 224x224, 448x448, 672x672). Importantly, for fair comparison, the paper notes that these larger images are often *interpolated* from the original lower-resolution image, ensuring no additional high-resolution information is introduced that wasn't present in the original input. For practical applications, direct use of high-resolution images is recommended.
2. **Sub-Image Splitting:** For each interpolated larger scale (e.g., 448x448), the image is split into smaller sub-images (e.g., four 224x224 sub-images for a 448x448 image). This is a crucial design choice.
3. **Individual Sub-Image Processing:** Each of these sub-images (including the original scale image) is fed *separately* into the *same pre-trained, frozen vision model*.
4. **Feature Map Merging and Pooling:** The features extracted from the multiple sub-images corresponding to a single larger scale are then merged back into a larger feature map (e.g., 4x (16x16) feature maps merge into a 32x32 feature map). This merged feature map is then average-pooled to the *original spatial size* of the feature map (e.g., 16x16), ensuring a consistent output token count regardless of the input scale.
5. **Multi-Scale Feature Concatenation:** Finally, the average-pooled feature maps from all processed scales are concatenated along the channel dimension. This results in a final multi-scale feature representation that has the same spatial dimensions as the single-scale feature but a higher channel dimension.

**Key Design Rationales:**
- **Splitting Large Images:** This avoids the quadratic computational complexity (in image resolution) characteristic of self-attention mechanisms in models like ViT when processing very large images directly. It also prevents performance degradation caused by incorrect position embedding interpolation, a known issue when directly feeding large images into models pre-trained on smaller resolutions.
- **Individual Sub-Image Processing:** This allows the S²-Wrapper to be truly parameter-free and model-agnostic. It works with *any* pre-trained vision model without requiring architectural modifications or re-training specific components (e.g., relative position embeddings).
- **Constant Output Token Count:** By pooling feature maps to the original spatial size, the number of output tokens remains constant. This is vital for downstream tasks, especially for multimodal LLMs (MLLMs), as it prevents an increase in the LLM's context length, thereby avoiding computational overhead.

**Evaluation Approach:**
The authors conducted extensive experiments across a wide range of vision tasks and model architectures to compare S² scaling against traditional model size scaling:

1. **Diverse Downstream Tasks:**
 - **Perception Tasks:** Image Classification (ImageNet), Semantic Segmentation (ADE20k), Depth Estimation (NYUv2). These represent fundamental capabilities of vision models.
 - **Multimodal LLMs (MLLMs):** Evaluated on a LLaVA-style model with an OpenCLIP vision backbone, across visual detail understanding (V*), VQA benchmarks (VQAv2, TextVQA, VizWiz), and general MLLM benchmarks (MMMU, MathVista, MMBench, SEED-Bench, MM-Vet).
 - **Robotic Manipulation:** A cube picking task using an MVP vision encoder.
2. **Varied Vision Models:** ViT, DINOv2, OpenCLIP (transformer-based models), and ConvNeXt (convolutional network, in appendix) were used, spanning different pre-training datasets (ImageNet-21k, LVD-142M, LAION-2B) and objectives (supervised, unsupervised, weakly-supervised).
3. **Fair Comparison:** For all tasks, the vision backbone was frozen, and only a lightweight task-specific head (linear layer, Mask2Former, VPD depth decoder, LoRA for LLM) was trained. Comparisons were made at comparable GFLOPs to ensure similar computational budgets.
4. **In-depth Analysis of Larger Model Advantages:**
 - **Qualitative Analysis:** Investigated specific examples where larger models showed superior generalization (e.g., rare or ambiguous image instances) over S²-scaled smaller models.
 - **Feature Reconstruction:** Quantified how much of a larger model's feature representation could be linearly reconstructed from a multi-scale smaller model's features, using mutual information as a metric.
 - **Model Capacity:** Assessed the memorization capability and training loss of smaller S²-scaled models versus larger models to compare their effective learning capacity.
 - **Multi-Scale Pre-training:** Conducted experiments to see if pre-training smaller models *with* S² scaling could further boost their generalization capabilities, addressing the observed performance gaps.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded several significant findings that challenge the conventional wisdom of vision model scaling:

1. **S² Outperforms or Matches Larger Models in Diverse Tasks:**
 - On a wide range of downstream tasks, including image classification, semantic segmentation, depth estimation, and robotic manipulation, a pre-trained and frozen *smaller* vision model (e.g., ViT-B or ViT-L) enhanced with S² often achieved comparable or *superior* performance to significantly larger models (e.g., ViT-H or ViT-G). This was achieved with remarkably fewer parameters (0.28x to 0.07x) and comparable GFLOPs.
 - S² demonstrated particular advantages in **dense prediction tasks** (segmentation, depth estimation), which naturally benefit from multi-scale features for detailed understanding.
 - In **Multimodal LLMs**, S² scaling on a large-size OpenCLIP model consistently improved performance across all three benchmark types (visual detail, VQA, MLLM benchmarks). Notably, by scaling image resolution to 1008x1008, LLaVA-1.5 with S² achieved state-of-the-art performance in visual detail understanding on the V* benchmark, surpassing even commercial models like Gemini Pro and GPT-4V. This highlights the ability of S² to unlock fine-grained visual comprehension.
 - For **robotic manipulation** (cube picking), S² on a base-size MVP model led to a ~20% increase in success rate, significantly outperforming the ~6% gain achieved by simply scaling up to a larger MVP model.

2. **Nuance in Scaling Strategy:** While S² generally excels, the "sweet spot" between model size scaling and S² scaling varies depending on the specific pre-trained model. For some models (e.g., ViT, OpenCLIP), S² from a base model eventually performs similarly to S² from a large model under high computational budgets. For others (e.g., DINOv2), S² from a large model provides a clearer advantage, suggesting that a sufficiently strong base model is beneficial for S² to leverage its full potential.

3. **Larger Models' Generalization on Hard Examples:** Qualitative analysis showed that while S²-scaled smaller models perform strongly, larger models (without S²) sometimes maintain an advantage in generalizing to rare or ambiguous examples (e.g., sculptures of common objects, images with multiple correct labels due to dataset flaws). This suggests a benefit of larger models in memorizing more diverse or "outlier" instances during extensive pre-training.

4. **Comparable Learning Capacity of Multi-scale Smaller Models:**
 - **Feature Approximation:** A crucial finding is that the features learned by larger models can be remarkably well approximated by those of multi-scale smaller models through a simple linear transform. The reconstruction ratio (mutual information) of a multi-scale base model's features to a large model's features was often close to 100% of the information reconstructed by an even larger (e.g., huge or giant) model. This suggests that multi-scale smaller models have the *capacity* to learn most, if not all, of the representations found in their larger counterparts.
 - **Model Capacity Metrics:** Multi-scale smaller models showed similar memorization capabilities and comparable or even lower training losses on classification tasks compared to larger models, further supporting the idea that their learning capacity is similar.

5. **Importance of Multi-scale Pre-training:** The paper demonstrates that the generalizability gap observed in some cases (where S²-scaled smaller models were still slightly behind larger models when pre-trained on single scale) can be closed by **pre-training smaller models with S² scaling as well**. When pre-trained with S², smaller models were able to match or even exceed the performance of larger models, confirming that their weaker generalization was not an inherent capacity issue but rather a consequence of their single-scale pre-training.

6. **Ablation Study Insights:** The ablation studies confirmed the effectiveness of key S²-Wrapper design choices: splitting large images into sub-images is critical for performance and efficiency (preventing OOM errors and leveraging parallel processing), and concatenating features from different scales is superior to simply adding them.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The findings presented in "When Do We Not Need Larger Vision Models?" carry profound significance and potential impact across several dimensions of AI research and deployment:

1. **Shift in Scaling Paradigm:** This work provides compelling evidence that scaling model size is not the *only* or always the *most efficient* path to better visual understanding. It introduces a viable alternative in "Scaling on Scales" (S²), urging a re-evaluation of current scaling strategies. This could lead to a broader exploration of other scaling dimensions beyond just model parameters, such as data diversity, training time, or architectural innovations.

2. **Resource Efficiency and Sustainability:** By demonstrating that comparable or superior performance can be achieved with significantly fewer parameters, S² offers a direct pathway to more resource-efficient AI. This translates to:
 - **Reduced Computational Cost:** Less powerful hardware or shorter training/inference times required.
 - **Lower Energy Consumption:** Decreased carbon footprint associated with AI development and deployment.
 - **Democratization of AI:** Making state-of-the-art vision capabilities more accessible to researchers, smaller institutions, and developers with limited access to large-scale computing infrastructure. This fosters broader participation and innovation in the field.

3. **Enhanced Model Capabilities for Fine-grained Understanding:** The remarkable achievement of S²-enhanced MLLMs in visual detail understanding (surpassing GPT-4V on V* benchmark) highlights its practical utility. This capability is crucial for applications requiring minute visual inspection, such as medical imaging, quality control in manufacturing, detailed scene understanding for autonomous systems, and advanced human-computer interaction.

4. **Rethinking Pre-training Strategies:** The finding that multi-scale pre-training significantly boosts the generalization of smaller models is a critical insight. It suggests that future pre-training efforts should not only focus on data volume or model size but also explicitly incorporate multi-scale information during the learning process. This could lead to the development of more robust and generalizable foundation models that are inherently capable of multi-scale understanding from the outset.

5. **Inspiration for New Architectural Designs:** The S²-Wrapper's success, particularly its mechanism of splitting images and processing sub-images independently, could inspire new neural network architectures that intrinsically handle multi-scale inputs more efficiently. The discussion on "scale-selective processing" mimicking human visual attention points towards future research into dynamic and adaptive scaling mechanisms, where models selectively process image regions at optimal scales.

6. **Improved Inference Latency for Single Images:** The inherent parallelism of S² (processing multiple sub-images independently) means that for applications where single-image latency is critical (e.g., real-time robotics, autonomous driving), S² could enable faster processing of high-resolution inputs by leveraging parallel computation, even with the same total GFLOPs.

7. **Practical Tool Release:** The release of a Python package (bfshi/scaling_on_scales) that allows for one-line application of S² makes this research immediately usable by the community, fostering rapid adoption and further experimentation.

In summary, this paper represents a significant contribution by challenging a fundamental assumption in deep learning scaling. It provides a practical and effective alternative that is both performant and resource-efficient, opening new avenues for research into more sustainable, accessible, and capable vision AI systems.
