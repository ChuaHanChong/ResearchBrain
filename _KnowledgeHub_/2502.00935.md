# Generalizing Safety Beyond Collision-Avoidance via Latent-Space Reachability Analysis

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Carnegie Mellon University and Delft University of Technology developed Latent Safety Filters, a method that extends robot safety beyond collision avoidance to hard-to-model failures like spilling. The approach uses learned latent representations from high-dimensional sensor data to perform reachability analysis in a robot's 'imagination,' demonstrating a 16% reduction in constraint violations in simulation and successfully preventing spills on a physical robotic arm.

## Problem

- Traditional robot safety methods (e.g., Hamilton-Jacobi reachability, CBFs) are limited to low-dimensional state spaces and require explicit, hand-engineered mathematical definitions of 'unsafe' states, primarily for collision avoidance.
- Generalizing robot safety to 'hard-to-model' failures, such as spilling, toppling, or breaking objects, is challenging because these nuanced hazards are difficult to define mathematically but are intuitively identifiable from high-dimensional sensor data (e.g., images).
- Synthesizing safe robot controllers often requires extensive data collection that can involve unsafe real-world interactions, making the process risky and inefficient.

## Method

- A generative world model learns a compact, lower-dimensional latent representation from high-dimensional observations (e.g., RGB images) and models the system's transition dynamics within this latent space.
- Safety is specified by training a classifier in the learned latent space to identify 'failure states' from human-labeled safe/unsafe observations, transforming complex safety constraints into a tractable data labeling task.
- Hamilton-Jacobi reachability analysis is performed within the world model's 'imagination' using reinforcement learning to compute a latent value function, from which a safety-preserving policy and an identifiable unsafe set are derived.
- At deployment, a policy-agnostic safety filter monitors any base task policy, overriding its actions with the learned safety policy only when the world model predicts the robot will enter an unsafe region.

## Results

- In a Dubins car collision-avoidance task, the learned latent safety monitor achieved an F1-score of 0.984 for classifying safe/unsafe states, closely matching a privileged ground-truth baseline.
- For a visual manipulation task, the method reduced constraint violations by 16% (from 36% to 20%) compared to an un-shielded base policy, while maintaining a high task success rate of 80% and zero incompletion.
- On real hardware, the safety filter successfully prevented a robotic arm from spilling Skittles, shielding both human teleoperators and various imitation-learned policies, and demonstrated generalization to out-of-distribution bag colors and backgrounds.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Complex, 'hard-to-model' safety constraints, such as preventing spilling or toppling, can be effectively specified and learned as classification problems directly in the latent space of a generative world model.
> - Hamilton-Jacobi (HJ) reachability analysis can be tractably performed within the 'imagination' of a learned world model, enabling the derivation of safety policies and unsafe sets without requiring extensive, potentially dangerous, real-world interactions.
> - A policy-agnostic safety filter, operating on latent representations, can robustly shield diverse base policies (e.g., human teleoperation, imitation learning) by overriding actions only when necessary, balancing safety with minimal interference to task performance.
