# Generalizing Safety Beyond Collision-Avoidance via Latent-Space Reachability Analysis

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Carnegie Mellon University and Delft University of Technology developed Latent Safety Filters, a method that extends robot safety beyond collision avoidance to hard-to-model failures like spilling. The approach uses learned latent representations from high-dimensional sensor data to perform reachability analysis in a robot's 'imagination,' demonstrating a 16% reduction in constraint violations in simulation and successfully preventing spills on a physical robotic arm.

## Problem

- Traditional robot safety methods (e.g., Hamilton-Jacobi reachability, CBFs) are limited to low-dimensional state spaces and require explicit, hand-engineered mathematical definitions of 'unsafe' states, primarily for collision avoidance.
- Generalizing robot safety to 'hard-to-model' failures, such as spilling, toppling, or breaking objects, is challenging because these nuanced hazards are difficult to define mathematically but are intuitively identifiable from high-dimensional sensor data (e.g., images).
- Synthesizing safe robot controllers often requires extensive data collection that can involve unsafe real-world interactions, making the process risky and inefficient.

## Method

- A generative world model learns a compact, lower-dimensional latent representation from high-dimensional observations (e.g., RGB images) and models the system's transition dynamics within this latent space.
- Safety is specified by training a latent-space classifier for 'failure states' from human-labeled data; Hamilton-Jacobi reachability then runs in the world model's 'imagination' via RL to derive a safety policy and unsafe set.
- At deployment, a policy-agnostic safety filter monitors any base task policy, overriding its actions with the learned safety policy only when the world model predicts the robot will enter an unsafe region.

## Results

- In a Dubins car collision-avoidance task, the learned latent safety monitor achieved an F1-score of 0.984 for classifying safe/unsafe states, closely matching a privileged ground-truth baseline.
- For a visual manipulation task, the method reduced constraint violations by 16% (from 36% to 20%) compared to an un-shielded base policy, while maintaining a high task success rate of 80% and zero incompletion.
- On real hardware, the safety filter successfully prevented a robotic arm from spilling Skittles, shielding both human teleoperators and various imitation-learned policies, and demonstrated generalization to out-of-distribution bag colors and backgrounds.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Complex, 'hard-to-model' safety constraints, such as preventing spilling or toppling, can be effectively specified and learned as classification problems directly in the latent space of a generative world model.
> - Hamilton-Jacobi (HJ) reachability analysis can be tractably performed within the 'imagination' of a learned world model, enabling the derivation of safety policies and unsafe sets without requiring extensive, potentially dangerous, real-world interactions.
> - A policy-agnostic safety filter, operating on latent representations, can robustly shield diverse base policies (e.g., human teleoperation, imitation learning) by overriding actions only when necessary, balancing safety with minimal interference to task performance.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotics is rapidly advancing, with a growing demand for autonomous systems to operate safely in complex, unstructured, and human-centric environments. Historically, robot safety has largely been equated with "collision avoidance," a problem typically tackled using formal control-theoretic frameworks like Hamilton-Jacobi (HJ) reachability or Control Barrier Functions (CBFs). While these methods offer strong theoretical guarantees, their practical application has been limited by a fundamental constraint: they require accurate, low-dimensional mathematical models of the robot's dynamics and explicit, hand-engineered definitions of "unsafe" states (e.g., distances to obstacles).

This paper addresses a critical gap in this landscape: **generalizing robot safety beyond simple collision avoidance to "hard-to-model" failures.** These failures—such as spilling a drink, toppling cluttered objects, or breaking fragile items—are difficult, if not impossible, to describe with precise mathematical equations or low-dimensional state variables. However, they are intuitively and visibly identifiable from high-dimensional sensor data, such as RGB images.

The broader research landscape is characterized by several converging trends:
- **The rise of deep learning and world models:** Generative world models have shown remarkable success in learning compact, predictive latent representations from high-dimensional perceptual data, enabling planning and control directly from pixels. This work leverages this capability.
- **Data-driven safety:** There's a growing interest in learning safety properties and controllers directly from data, rather than relying solely on first-principles models. This includes inverse reinforcement learning for constraint inference or learning barrier functions from demonstrations.
- **Safety filtering/shielding:** This paradigm involves a "safety filter" that monitors a nominal task policy and overrides it with a safe action only when necessary, ensuring minimal invasiveness while guaranteeing safety. This decoupling allows task performance and safety to be developed somewhat independently.

This paper distinguishes itself by uniquely combining these trends. Unlike prior data-driven safety methods that might infer constraints in a privileged state space or require recovery demonstrations, this work:
- **Operates directly on raw, high-dimensional observations:** Bypassing the need for hand-crafted state estimation or feature extraction.
- **Learns safety constraints as a classification problem in latent space:** Making constraint specification intuitive (labeling images as safe/unsafe) rather than requiring mathematical formulation.
- **Leverages the "imagination" of a learned world model:** This allows for tractable, offline safety analysis and controller synthesis without the need for extensive, potentially unsafe, real-world interactions. The world model's ability to predict future latent states under different actions becomes the backbone for reachability analysis.
- **Provides a policy-agnostic safety filter:** Capable of safeguarding any arbitrary base policy, from human teleoperation to complex imitation-learned behaviors.

By addressing the challenge of nuanced safety in high-dimensional, perception-rich environments, this research significantly pushes the frontier of practical, deployable robotic autonomy. It represents a crucial step towards making robots truly robust and trustworthy for operation in complex, unstructured settings where unforeseen hazards abound.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to **generalize safe robot controllers beyond traditional collision-avoidance to prevent failures that are difficult to model mathematically but are intuitively identifiable from high-dimensional observations.** The paper clearly articulates this motivation by posing the everyday scenario of a robot in a home, highlighting that safety extends far beyond merely avoiding physical contact, encompassing nuanced behaviors like "spilling coffee too fast" or "causing other dishes to fall."

The core motivations driving this work are:
- **Limitations of Traditional Safety Frameworks:** While Hamilton-Jacobi (HJ) reachability is a rigorous mathematical framework for safety, its practical application has been confined to scenarios with hand-engineered collision-avoidance constraints and low-dimensional state-space representations. This is due to the computational intractability of HJ reachability in high dimensions and the difficulty of mathematically encoding complex, intuitive safety constraints.
- **Need for Nuanced Safety:** Real-world robotic deployment, especially in unstructured environments like homes, necessitates a more sophisticated understanding of "safety." Robots need to avoid outcomes like liquid overflowing, objects breaking, or items spilling—hazards that are easily recognized by humans from visual cues but are extremely challenging to define algorithmically using explicit state variables or first-principles dynamics models.
- **Bridging Perception and Control:** High-dimensional observations (e.g., RGB images) contain rich information about the state of the world, including subtle cues that indicate impending failure. The challenge lies in connecting this raw perceptual data to robust control actions that ensure safety.
- **Reducing Unsafe Online Interactions:** Synthesizing safe controllers often requires extensive data collection, potentially involving interactions that lead to unsafe outcomes. The authors aim to minimize this by leveraging the "imagination" of a learned world model for offline safety analysis.
- **Policy Agnosticism:** The goal is to develop a safety mechanism that can safeguard *any* underlying task policy, whether it's an imitation-learned controller, a reinforcement learning policy, or even direct human teleoperation, without requiring specific re-training or modification of the task policy itself.

To achieve these motivations, the key objectives articulated in the paper are:
1. **Formulate a Hamilton-Jacobi reachability problem in the latent space of a generative world model.** This involves defining the "failure set" as a classification problem on the learned latent embeddings of observations.
2. **Develop a tractable computational method for Latent Safety Filters.** This involves approximating the latent HJ value function and safety policy using reinforcement learning techniques within the world model's "imagination," thereby avoiding costly real-world unsafe interactions.
3. **Validate the approach on vision-based safe-control tasks in both simulation and hardware.** This includes demonstrating its performance in:
 - A benchmark collision-avoidance task (Dubins car) to compare against privileged state solutions.
 - A contact-rich manipulation task in simulation where objects can topple.
 - Real-world hardware experiments involving preventing a robotic arm from spilling the contents of an open bag of Skittles, showcasing its ability to handle truly hard-to-model failures and its generalization capabilities.
4. **Demonstrate the policy-agnostic nature of the safety filter,** showing its effectiveness in shielding diverse base policies, including human teleoperation and various imitation-learned policies.

By achieving these objectives, the research aims to provide a practical and robust solution for deploying safe robots in complex, uncertain environments, moving beyond the traditional limitations of explicit safety definitions.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core methodology revolves around extending the rigorous Hamilton-Jacobi (HJ) reachability framework into the high-dimensional, learned latent space of a generative world model. This allows the system to operate directly on raw sensor data (e.g., RGB images) and reason about safety without requiring hand-engineered state representations or explicit dynamics models.

The approach can be broken down into several interconnected components:

1. **World Model Learning:**
 - **Purpose:** To learn a compact, lower-dimensional latent state `z` from high-dimensional observations `o` and to model the system's dynamics (how `z` evolves given an action `a`).
 - **Architecture:** The paper uses two types of world models:
 - **Recurrent State Space Model (RSSM):** Used in the Dubins car and initial simulation manipulation tasks. This model learns a deterministic recurrent state and a stochastic component, providing a latent state `z = {h_t, x_t}`.
 - **DINO-WM:** Used for the hardware experiments. This model leverages a powerful pre-trained vision transformer (DINOv2) as a frozen encoder to map raw images into dense patch tokens (the latent state). A separate vision transformer then learns the transition dynamics, predicting future patch tokens conditioned on past tokens and actions.
 - **Training:** World models are trained via self-supervised learning objectives (e.g., observation reconstruction for RSSM, teacher-forcing for DINO-WM) on diverse offline datasets. Crucially, these datasets can include *successes, random exploration, and even unsafe demonstrations*, providing comprehensive coverage of the robot's interaction space without requiring explicit expert safety demonstrations.

2. **Safety Specification as Latent-Space Classification:**
 - **Challenge:** Traditional HJ reachability requires a "margin function" `ℓ(s)` that mathematically defines the failure set `F` (where `ℓ(s) < 0`). For complex failures like "spilling," this is intractable.
 - **Solution:** The failure set `F_latent` is learned directly in the latent space as a classifier `ℓ_μ(z)`.
 - **Training:** This classifier is trained on datasets of observations manually labeled as safe or unsafe (`D_safe`, `D_unsafe`). This transforms the challenging problem of formal constraint definition into a more accessible data labeling task (identifying visually apparent failures). The classifier aims to output positive values for safe states and negative values for unsafe states.

3. **Latent-Space Reachability Analysis (in Imagination):**
 - **Concept:** Instead of performing reachability analysis in the real world or a hand-engineered simulator, the analysis is conducted within the "imagination" of the learned world model. This means predicting future latent states using the world model's transition function.
 - **Equation:** The core of HJ reachability is solving a Bellman-like equation (Eq. 4), which computes a value function `V_latent(z)`. This function quantifies how "doomed" the robot is to enter the failure set `F_latent` from a given latent state `z`, despite its best efforts.
 - **Tractability:** Since exact solutions to HJ equations in high-dimensional spaces are intractable, the authors approximate `V_latent(z)` using **Reinforcement Learning (RL)**. They introduce a time-discounting factor (Eq. 5) to induce a contraction mapping, ensuring convergence. Specifically, DDQN is used for discrete action spaces (Dubins car), and DDPG is used for continuous action spaces (manipulation tasks).

4. **Derivation of Safety Policy and Unsafe Set:**
 - **Safety Policy (π_safety):** From the learned value function `V_latent(z)`, a safety-preserving policy `π_safety(z)` is derived. This policy dictates the action that maximizes the value function (i.e., keeps the robot safest).
 - **Unsafe Set (U_latent):** The set of states from which the robot is *doomed* to fail is identified as the zero-sublevel set of `V_latent(z)`. Importantly, `U_latent` is expected to be a superset of `F_latent` (the immediate failure states), allowing for pre-emptive action.

5. **Safety Filtering at Deployment:**
 - **Mechanism:** At runtime, the method acts as a "safety filter." It monitors the actions proposed by any base task policy `π_task` (e.g., a human teleoperator, an imitation learning policy).
 - **Decision:** The proposed action is fed into the world model to predict the next latent state (`z_predicted`). If the predicted value `V(z_predicted)` falls below a safety threshold `ϵ` (indicating the robot is entering an unsafe region), the safety filter overrides `π_task` and executes the safety-preserving action from `π_safety`. Otherwise, `π_task` is allowed to execute. This ensures minimal interference with the task.

**Experimental Setup and Validation:**
The methodology is rigorously evaluated across three increasingly complex scenarios:
- **Dubins Car (Simulation):** A 3D collision-avoidance task, allowing comparison against a known ground-truth HJ solution to assess the quality of the learned latent representations and controllers.
- **Visual Manipulation (Simulation):** A contact-rich task of lifting a block amidst clutter, where the safety constraint is "not topple other blocks." This tests handling more nuanced, vision-based failures. It compares against soft constraints and a constrained MDP baseline (SQRL).
- **Franka Manipulator (Hardware):** The challenging "prevent spilling Skittles from an open bag" task. This is the ultimate test for truly hard-to-model failures. Experiments include shielding human teleoperators, strong/weak imitation learning policies, and stress-testing generalization to out-of-distribution bag colors and backgrounds.

This comprehensive approach demonstrates a powerful integration of deep learning's perceptual capabilities with control theory's safety guarantees, offering a principled way to achieve nuanced safety in complex robotic tasks.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents compelling findings across both simulation and hardware experiments, demonstrating the efficacy and generalizability of Latent Safety Filters for complex, hard-to-model failures.

**A. Simulation Results (Dubins Car: How Close to Privileged Safety?)**
- **Runtime Monitor Quality:** In the Dubins car collision-avoidance task, where a privileged (ground-truth) safety controller can be computed, LatentSafe's runtime monitor (V_latent) achieved an F1-score of 0.984 for classifying safe/unsafe states. This is remarkably close to the privileged baseline (0.987 F1-score), indicating that the learned latent representations and value functions accurately capture the underlying safety properties despite operating purely from images.
- **Safety Policy Quality:** The LatentSafe policy (π_latent) successfully maintained safety in 96% of test states, comparable to the privileged baseline's 98.4%. This quantitatively confirms that the latent-space approach yields highly performant safety controllers.
- **Effect of Incomplete Knowledge:** An ablation study showed that a world model trained with biased (incomplete) action coverage led to a more pessimistic (overly conservative) unsafe set. This highlights the importance of sufficiently diverse data for world model training, as the safety filter can only protect against what the world model can predict.

**B. Simulation Results (Visual Manipulation: Scaling to Complex Failures)**
- **Non-Trivial Unsafe Set:** Qualitatively, the learned latent failure classifier (F_latent) correctly identified states *where blocks had already fallen*. However, the latent unsafe set (U_latent), derived from HJ reachability, *pre-emptively* identified states where the blocks were merely *wobbling and doomed to fall*, even before visible failure. This crucial distinction validates the method's ability to compute truly predictive unsafe sets for nuanced, high-dimensional failures.
- **Quantitative Performance:**
 - LatentSafe significantly reduced constraint violations (20% violation rate) compared to the un-shielded base policy (Dreamer, 36% violation rate) and a Constrained MDP baseline (SQRL, 28% for ϵ_risk=0.1 and 22% for ϵ_risk=0.05).
 - Crucially, LatentSafe achieved this safety improvement while maintaining a high task success rate (80%) and zero incompletion rate. In contrast, SQRL, particularly at a more conservative safety setting (ϵ_risk=0.05), drastically increased incompletion (70% incompletion), demonstrating LatentSafe's superior ability to balance safety and task performance without excessive conservatism or hyperparameter sensitivity.

**C. Hardware Results (Preventing Hard-to-Model Robot Failures - Skittles Spilling)**
- **Shielding Human Teleoperators:** LatentSafe successfully overridden human teleoperation actions that would have led to spilling the Skittles (e.g., sharply pulling up the bag from the bottom). It also provided nuanced corrections, slowing down side-to-side motions with an unsafe grasp, demonstrating a subtle understanding of the dynamics. Importantly, it allowed safe teleoperation (e.g., grasping the open end) without interference, showcasing its minimal invasiveness.
- **Shielding Autonomous Imitation-Learned (IL) Policies:**
 - **Weak Policy (DiffusionAdv):** LatentSafe led to a remarkable 63.6% decrease in constraint violations for a suboptimal IL policy trained on potentially unsafe demonstrations. Furthermore, for the failures that did occur, LatentSafe significantly reduced their severity (mostly single Skittles spilled, compared to large spills by the unshielded policy), highlighting its effectiveness in mitigating the impact of errors.
 - **Strong Policy (DiffusionOpt):** When shielding a highly performant IL policy, LatentSafe exhibited minimal interference. Both the shielded and unshielded strong policies achieved successful grasps with only one minor (single-Skittle) spill in 15 trials. This confirms that the filter is *not overly conservative* and does not impede well-performing policies.
- **Out-of-Distribution (OOD) Generalization:**
 - **Positive Generalization:** The same Latent Safety Filter successfully generalized to *different colors of Skittles bags* and *changes in the background* (black cloth). The filtering behavior remained consistent, preventing spills across these visual variations. The authors attribute this to the powerful DINOv2 encoder, which maps semantically similar objects to similar latent embeddings.
 - **Limitations in Generalization:** The method struggled with OOD *candy types* (M&Ms), which had significantly different physical properties and dynamics (stiffer bags, different weights). Despite visual similarity (as indicated by DINOv2 embeddings), the world model's learned dynamics did not generalize, leading to spills. This highlights that while visual generalization is strong, the safety filter's effectiveness is tied to the world model's ability to predict dynamics, which can be challenging for unseen physical interactions in low-data regimes.

In summary, the main findings robustly demonstrate that Latent Safety Filters can effectively detect, predict, and mitigate complex, hard-to-model failures directly from high-dimensional observations. They offer a practical, policy-agnostic solution that significantly enhances safety without crippling task performance, marking a substantial step towards more reliable autonomous robots in the real world.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research on Latent Safety Filters carries significant implications for the future of robotics and artificial intelligence, offering a robust bridge between theoretical safety guarantees and practical, real-world robotic deployment.

**1. Generalizing Robot Safety for Real-World Deployment:** The most profound impact of this work is its ability to move robot safety beyond simple collision avoidance. By tackling "hard-to-model" failures like spilling, toppling, or breaking, the paper addresses a critical bottleneck for deploying robots in unstructured, human-centric environments (e.g., homes, offices, service industries). These nuanced safety constraints are intuitive for humans but have historically been intractable for robots due to the difficulty of mathematical formulation. Latent Safety Filters provide a practical pathway for robots to "understand" and prevent such complex hazards.

**2. Bridging Perception and Control with Rigorous Guarantees:** The integration of deep learning (generative world models, vision transformers) with control theory (Hamilton-Jacobi reachability) is a major conceptual leap. It enables robots to reason about safety directly from high-dimensional raw sensor data (like RGB images) without requiring explicit, hand-engineered state representations or first-principles dynamics models. This is crucial for real-world scenarios where precise physical models are unavailable or too complex. While acknowledging that formal assurances for learned components are an area for future work, the grounding in HJ reachability provides a strong theoretical foundation.

**3. Data-Efficient Safety Synthesis:** By conducting safety analysis within the "imagination" of a learned world model, the method significantly reduces the need for extensive, and potentially dangerous, real-world interactions to synthesize safety controllers. The world model can be trained on diverse data, including successes, random exploration, and even some unsafe demonstrations, without requiring specific "recovery demonstrations" for the safety policy itself. This makes the approach more practical and safer for learning.

**4. Policy-Agnostic and Non-Invasive Safety:** The safety filter paradigm proposed is inherently policy-agnostic. It can be seamlessly integrated with and safeguard any underlying base policy, whether it's a human teleoperator, an imitation-learned policy, or a reinforcement learning agent. This modularity is highly significant because it allows task-specific policy development to proceed independently of safety considerations, with the safety filter acting as a universal guardian. Its "minimally invasive" nature ensures that the base policy is only overridden when strictly necessary, preserving task performance as much as possible.

**5. Advancing the Capabilities of World Models:** The work highlights the utility of generative world models not just for planning or task performance, but also as a powerful substrate for formal safety analysis. It demonstrates that the predictive capabilities learned by world models can be directly leveraged for rigorous control-theoretic applications, opening new avenues for research in both world models and safe AI.

**6. Accelerating Robot Adoption and Trust:** As robots become more capable and ubiquitous, public trust and acceptance will hinge on their ability to operate safely and reliably. By offering a robust method to prevent complex and intuitive failures, this research contributes directly to building safer robots. This increased safety could accelerate the adoption of autonomous systems in sensitive environments, from assistive robotics in homes to complex manipulation tasks in factories.

**Limitations and Future Work:** The authors openly acknowledge limitations, particularly concerning the world model's ability to predict all possible unsafe outcomes and generalize to vastly different dynamics (as seen with M&Ms). This points to exciting future work on quantifying uncertainties in learned models, improving generalization, and integrating formal assurances more deeply into the learning pipeline.

In conclusion, "Generalizing Safety Beyond Collision-Avoidance via Latent-Space Reachability Analysis" is a highly significant contribution that bridges fundamental gaps in robot autonomy. It offers a principled, practical, and scalable approach to enable robots to operate safely and intelligently in complex, perception-rich real-world environments, paving the way for a new generation of truly trustworthy autonomous systems.
