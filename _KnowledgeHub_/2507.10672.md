# Vision Language Action Models in Robotic Manipulation: A Systematic Review

> [!summary] Summary
> A comprehensive systematic review synthesizes Vision Language Action (VLA) models for robotic manipulation, categorizing architectural trends, benchmarking datasets, and evaluating simulation platforms. Researchers from Khalifa University and Universitat Politecnica de Catalunya identify dominant architectural components, reveal critical gaps in multimodal, high-complexity datasets, and outline challenges in simulation for robotic manipulation.

## Problem

- The rapidly expanding and fragmented research landscape in Vision Language Action (VLA) models for robotics lacks a unified synthesis.
- A clear, structured understanding of diverse VLA architectural foundations, benchmark datasets, and evaluation protocols is missing.
- Difficulty in contextualizing current advancements and identifying key limitations or promising future directions within the VLA field.

## Method

- Conducted a rigorous ==systematic review== of 102 **VLA** models, 26 datasets, and 12 simulation platforms published between 2022 and 2025, using both traditional database searches and LLM augmentation.
- Developed a structured taxonomy for **VLA** model architectures and introduced a novel ==quantitative benchmarking framework== for datasets based on task complexity and modality richness.
- Provided a critical evaluation of simulation platforms and identified persistent challenges across architectural design, dataset availability, and simulation capabilities, outlining a strategic roadmap for future research.

## Results

- Categorized 102 VLA models into architectural paradigms, distinguishing between large generalist architectures (e.g., RT-2, Octo) and modular, task-specialized systems (e.g., DexVLA, CLIPort).
- Benchmarked 26 foundational datasets using a novel quantitative framework, visually demonstrating the scarcity of datasets providing both high task complexity and rich multimodal information, with Kaiwu being an outlier.
- Reviewed 12 simulation platforms, highlighting their diverse capabilities in data generation and sim-to-real facilitation while pointing out common challenges in physics accuracy, visual fidelity, and API integration.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Dominant architectural choices for VLA models include CLIP/SigLIP-based Vision Transformers, LLaMA family language encoders, and diffusion-based Transformers for action decoding.
> - There is a significant and underexplored gap in VLA datasets that combine very high task complexity with comprehensive multimodal integration.
> - Simulation platforms exhibit trade-offs between visual realism and computational throughput, often lacking native language grounding APIs, which complicates data generation and sim-to-real transfer.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The integration of vision, language, and action into a unified framework represents a **paradigm shift** in robotics and embodied artificial intelligence. Traditionally, robotic systems have relied on task-specific programming, limiting their adaptability to dynamic and unstructured environments. Vision Language Action (VLA) models address this limitation by leveraging the generalization capabilities of large-scale foundation models (like GPT, BERT, ViT, CLIP) to enable robots to comprehend natural language instructions, perceive their surroundings, and autonomously execute complex tasks. This evolution is crucial for achieving truly generalist robotic agents capable of human-like interaction and adaptability.

The field of VLA models is characterized by rapid advancements, with a continuous influx of new architectures, datasets, and frameworks (as illustrated in Figure 1 of the paper, showing a significant increase in VLA models and datasets from 2022 to 2025). Despite this growth, the authors identify a critical **gap in the literature**: a comprehensive and systematic synthesis that organizes and categorizes the architectural foundations, benchmark datasets, simulation platforms, and evaluation protocols. This review precisely fills that void, providing a much-needed **contextualization of the state-of-the-art** and identifying emerging patterns, limitations, and future opportunities.

By systematically analyzing a large body of work (102 VLA models, 26 datasets, and 12 simulation platforms), this paper consolidates fragmented knowledge, offering a unified perspective on the current landscape. It moves beyond individual model descriptions to identify overarching trends, persistent challenges, and strategic directions, thereby serving as a foundational reference for researchers, practitioners, and newcomers to the field. This systematic review is instrumental in charting the course for the next generation of embodied foundation models and generalist robotic intelligence.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind this systematic review stems from the rapid proliferation of Vision Language Action (VLA) models in robotics and the subsequent need for a consolidated understanding of the field. The authors note that despite the fast pace of development, there is a **critical lack of a comprehensive and systematic synthesis** that structures the diverse architectural foundations, benchmark datasets, simulation platforms, and evaluation protocols that define the VLA landscape. Without such a synthesis, it becomes challenging to contextualize current advancements, identify key limitations, and pinpoint promising future directions.

Based on this motivation, the **key objectives** of this research are:

1. **Structured Taxonomy of VLA Architectures:** To present a detailed classification of VLA model architectures, categorizing them based on their distinct methods for integrating visual perception, natural language understanding, and embodied control. This includes analyzing the roles of vision encoders, language encoders, and action decoders.
2. **Novel Quantitative Benchmarking for VLA Datasets:** To introduce and apply a new quantitative framework for systematically benchmarking VLA datasets. This framework uses empirically adjusted metrics for task complexity and modality richness to map current datasets, revealing gaps in the data landscape, especially concerning datasets that combine high task complexity with comprehensive multimodality.
3. **In-Depth Review of Simulation Platforms:** To critically evaluate the most crucial simulation platforms used in VLA research, assessing their sensory modalities, application domains, and their effectiveness in generating large-scale, reproducible VLA data and facilitating sim-to-real transfer.
4. **Identification of Challenges and Future Roadmap:** To recognize persistent challenges in VLA model development across architectural design, dataset availability, and simulation capabilities, and subsequently articulate a clear roadmap for future research directions. This includes emphasizing areas like architectural modularity, scalable data generation strategies, dynamic simulation, and unified language-grounding APIs for robust real-world deployment.

Ultimately, the paper aims to serve as both a **technical reference and a conceptual roadmap** to accelerate research in embodied foundation models and generalist robotic intelligence, providing insights from dataset generation to the real-world deployment of robotic agents.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors employed a rigorous systematic review methodology to synthesize the current state of Vision Language Action (VLA) models. Their approach can be broken down into several key steps:

1. **Literature Search and Selection Criteria:**
 - **Databases:** An extensive search was conducted across major academic databases including IEEE Xplore, Elsevier, Springer Nature, MDPI, Wiley, and arXiv. The inclusion of arXiv preprints was crucial given the rapid evolution of the VLA field, ensuring the capture of the latest breakthroughs.
 - **Keywords:** Comprehensive sets of keywords were developed for each thematic area:
 - **VLA Models:** "vision language action," VLA, "Vision Language Models for Robotic manipulation," "Multimodal Robotic Control," "Vision-Language Grounding," "Visuomotor Transformer," "End-to-End Robot Learning."
 - **Training Datasets:** "VLA dataset," "manipulation dataset," "embodied AI dataset for manipulation."
 - **Simulation Tools:** "simulator for robotic manipulation," "embodied AI data-generation simulator," "robotic manipulation simulator," "realistic dynamic simulation for robotic manipulation."
 - **LLM Augmentation:** To ensure comprehensive coverage, traditional database searches were complemented by conversational queries using a large language model (e.g., GPT). Specific prompts were designed for each thematic area to identify relevant VLA models, datasets, and simulators published between 2022 and 2025.
 - **Filtering:** The merged lists from database searches and LLM queries were deduplicated, and a manual validation process was performed to select original research that proposed or evaluated novel VLA models, datasets, or simulators for robotic manipulation or data generation.

2. **Categorization and Analysis of VLA Models:**
 - **Architectural Paradigms:** 102 VLA models were systematically analyzed and categorized into key architectural paradigms. This involved dissecting each model into its core components: vision encoder, language encoder, and action decoder.
 - **Contribution Analysis:** Models were evaluated based on their "end-to-end" nature versus "component-focused" contributions, highlighting their main technical innovations and the datasets they utilized (Table 1).
 - **Architectural Trends:** A mind map (Figure 8) was created to visualize prevailing architectural trends, focusing on encoder and decoder classes used by at least three different models, such as CLIP/SigLIP-based ViTs, LLaMA/Vicuna for language, and diffusion/autoregressive transformers for action decoding.

3. **Benchmarking and Analysis of VLA Datasets:**
 - **Unified Data Schema:** The review first established a structured overview of the general dataset format commonly used in VLA training pipelines, integrating visual, language, and action/control streams into episode-level directories and standardized storage formats (Figure 9).
 - **Novel Quantitative Framework:** A two-dimensional characterization framework was introduced to benchmark 26 foundational datasets. This framework quantifies:
 - **Task Complexity (C_task):** Integrating factors like the average number of low-level actions per episode, number of distinct high-level skills, degree of sequential task dependency, and linguistic abstraction level (Equation 4).
 - **Modality Richness (C_mod):** Incorporating the number of distinct modalities, mean quality of modalities, fidelity of temporal alignment, and presence of reasoning-critical modalities (Equation 5).
 - **Visualization:** The results were visualized on a two-dimensional plot (Figure 10), where bubble size represented dataset scale, effectively highlighting underexplored regions in the data landscape.

4. **Evaluation of Simulation Platforms:**
 - 12 simulation platforms were reviewed for their effectiveness in generating large-scale data and facilitating sim-to-real transfer.
 - **Criteria:** Each simulator was assessed based on its supported sensory modalities, primary use cases, core technical capabilities, and the datasets that rely on them (Table 3).

5. **Application Domains and Model Evaluation:**
 - VLA models were categorized into six application domains: Manipulation and Task Generalization, Autonomous Mobility, Human Assistance and Interaction, Robotic Platforms, Virtual Environments, and Edge and Low-Power Deployment (Table 4).
 - Ten representative VLA models were selected for a detailed comparative evaluation based on success rate, zero-shot generalization, and real-robot validation (Table 5).

6. **Identification of Challenges and Future Directions:**
 - The review concluded by synthesizing the identified limitations into architectural, dataset, and simulation challenges.
 - A strategic roadmap for future research directions was outlined, emphasizing solutions for each challenge.

The comprehensive and multi-faceted methodology ensures a holistic understanding of the VLA field, providing both a descriptive overview and a critical analysis of its foundational components.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The systematic review provides a comprehensive overview of the VLA landscape, revealing key findings across VLA models, datasets, and simulation platforms.

**VLA Models and Architectural Trends:**
The review analyzed 102 VLA models, identifying dominant architectural trends (Figure 8, Table 1).
- **Component Dominance:** For **vision encoders**, CLIP and SigLIP-based Vision Transformers (ViT) are widely favored for their strong visual-text alignment capabilities, alongside other ViT variants (DINOv2, Qwen2 VIT) and CNN-based encoders (ResNet, EfficientNet).
- **Language Encoders:** The **LLaMA family** (including LLaMA-2) and Vicuna-based models are the most common choices for natural language understanding and zero-shot reasoning, while T5-style, GPT-based, and Qwen-based encoders also feature prominently.
- **Action Decoders:** **Diffusion-based Transformers** emerged as the leading choice, offering fine-grained, temporally smooth control through iterative denoising. Autoregressive Transformer heads, MLP/token predictor heads, and specialized planning heads (e.g., Model Predictive Control) are also widely used.
- **Generalist vs. Specialized:** A key finding is the emergence of two distinct trajectories (Table 5):
 - **Large Generalist Architectures:** Models like RT-2, Octo, Gato, and OpenVLA leverage massive transformer backbones and diffusion decoders trained on millions of diverse trajectories, excelling in broad zero-shot generalization across many tasks and robots.
 - **Modular and Task-Specialized Systems:** Models such as DexVLA, CLIPort, TLA, and RoboAgent employ targeted modules (e.g., object-centric ViTs, tactile encoders, LoRA adapters) to enhance robustness and data efficiency for specific manipulation skills, often bridging sim-to-real gaps effectively.
- **Applications:** Manipulation and task generalization remain the dominant application domain, covering a vast array of models, though VLA applications also extend to autonomous mobility, human assistance, robotic platforms (humanoids, quadruped), virtual environments, and edge deployment (Table 4).

**VLA Training Datasets:**
The review comprehensively analyzed 26 foundational datasets, introducing a novel quantitative benchmarking framework based on task complexity (C_task) and modality richness (C_mod).
- **Data Landscape Gaps:** The benchmarking visualization (Figure 10) revealed a significant **underexplored region**: a lack of datasets combining very high task complexity with comprehensive multimodal integration. Most datasets are concentrated in lower-to-moderate complexity and modality richness.
- **Evolution of Datasets:**
 - **Early Datasets (e.g., EmbodiedQA, R2R):** Characterized by very low complexity and minimal modality, focusing on discrete decision-making.
 - **Mid-Generation Datasets (e.g., ALFRED, RLBench, CALVIN):** Introduce longer-horizon tasks and richer sensory streams (RGB, depth, proprioception, language), moving into medium complexity and rich modality.
 - **Recent Large-Scale Datasets (e.g., Open X-Embodiment, DROID):** Push towards high task complexity and rich modality, often featuring synchronized RGB-D, language, and multi-skill trajectories across various robot embodiments.
- **Kaiwu's Position:** The Kaiwu dataset stands out as the only one positioned at the extreme of both high task complexity and comprehensive modality richness, integrating a wide array of sensors (vision, depth, language, proprioception, haptics, etc.). This highlights its role as a potential benchmark for future generalist agents.

**Simulation Tools:**
12 simulation platforms were reviewed, underscoring their critical role in scalable, repeatable, and extensively annotated data generation (Table 3).
- **Diverse Capabilities:** Simulators like AI2-THOR and Habitat excel in photorealistic indoor navigation, while NVIDIA Isaac Sim and Gazebo offer advanced physics, LiDAR, and multi-robot support. PyBullet, MuJoCo, and SAPIEN provide precise contact physics for dexterous manipulation.
- **Trade-offs:** There is a constant trade-off between visual realism and computational throughput. High-fidelity platforms often have lower frame rates, while lightweight renderers, though fast, suffer from domain gaps, impacting sim-to-real transfer.
- **Integration Challenges:** Many simulators lack native support for language grounding APIs, necessitating custom annotation pipelines that increase development overhead and lead to fragmented data formats.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This systematic review significantly impacts the Vision Language Action (VLA) field by providing a much-needed **technical reference and conceptual roadmap**. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Consolidating Knowledge and Contextualizing Research:** By systematically analyzing over a hundred VLA models, dozens of datasets, and a dozen simulation platforms, the review consolidates a vast and rapidly expanding body of fragmented research. This provides researchers with a clear, organized overview of the state-of-the-art, allowing them to quickly grasp the foundational principles, prevailing architectural trends, and common methodologies. This consolidation is invaluable for newcomers and seasoned experts alike, saving significant time and effort in literature review.

2. **Identifying Gaps and Guiding Future Research:** The novel quantitative benchmarking framework for VLA datasets (Figure 10) is a crucial contribution. By visually highlighting the "underexplored regions" – particularly the lack of datasets combining very high task complexity with comprehensive multimodal integration – the review precisely **points to critical research gaps**. This serves as a strategic direction for future data collection and generation efforts, encouraging the creation of more holistic and challenging benchmarks that are essential for developing truly generalist robotic agents.

3. **Informing Architectural Design and Development:** The detailed taxonomy of VLA architectures, including the breakdown of vision encoders, language encoders, and action decoders, offers practical insights for model design. Understanding the strengths and weaknesses of different component choices (e.g., diffusion vs. autoregressive action decoders, LLaMA vs. T5 for language) helps researchers make informed decisions, potentially leading to more efficient, robust, and generalizable VLA models. The identified challenges in tokenization, modality fusion, and generalization across embodiments provide a clear agenda for architectural improvements.

4. **Enhancing Simulation and Real-World Deployment:** The in-depth review of simulation platforms and the challenges associated with them (e.g., physics accuracy, visual realism, lack of language grounding APIs) emphasizes the need for better simulation tools. By outlining these limitations, the review advocates for advancements in simulation technology that can more reliably bridge the "sim-to-real" gap. This will accelerate the development and safe deployment of VLA-driven robots in complex, unstructured real-world environments.

5. **Accelerating Progress Towards Generalist Robotic Intelligence:** The overarching goal of VLA research is to enable robots to operate autonomously and intelligently in diverse, open-ended settings. This review contributes directly to that goal by providing a structured framework for understanding current capabilities and limitations. By identifying key challenges and outlining a roadmap for future research directions (e.g., scalable pretraining protocols, modular designs, robust multimodal alignment), it serves as a catalyst for innovation, pushing the field closer to realizing general-purpose embodied intelligence.

In essence, this systematic review transcends a mere summary of existing work; it acts as a **critical compass** for the VLA research community. By synthesizing core principles, evaluating methodologies, and articulating open problems, it provides the necessary foundation for shaping the development of the next generation of vision-language-grounded robotic intelligence, ultimately bridging foundational AI models with practical, real-world robotic applications.
