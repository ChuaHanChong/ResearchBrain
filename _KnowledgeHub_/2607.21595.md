# 3D-Aware VLMs with Implicit and Explicit Geometries

> [!summary] Summary
> A framework called VLM-IE3D enhances Vision-Language Models with comprehensive 3D spatial awareness by integrating both high-level implicit and fine-grained explicit 3D geometric representations derived solely from RGB video inputs. This approach improves performance by up to 22.4% in 3D dense captioning (C@0.5) and 9.2% in 3D visual grounding (Acc@0.25) compared to baselines, while maintaining efficiency.

## Problem

- Existing 2D-based Vision-Language Models (VLMs) lack fine-grained 3D spatial understanding for tasks like accurate localization and detailed object relationships.
- Prior RGB-only 3D VLM methods rely on coarse implicit 3D representations, which are insufficient for precise quantitative geometric reasoning.
- Explicit 3D data provides fine-grained geometry but necessitates specialized 3D sensors or extensive reconstruction, limiting real-world accessibility.

## Method

- The **VLM-IE3D** framework integrates implicit and explicit 3D geometric representations into VLMs using only RGB video sequences.
- It employs a ==3D geometry encoder (AnySplat)== to extract high-level ==Implicit Geometry Tokens (IGTs)== and reconstruct explicit 3D attributes like depth maps.
- A lightweight ==3D explicit embedding module== converts reconstructed explicit 3D attributes into detailed ==Explicit Geometry Tokens (EGTs)==, which are then fused with IGTs and 2D visual tokens by a ==3D-aware adapter== before being passed to a VLM backbone.

## Results

- Achieved a C@0.5 score of **80.4%** in 3D dense captioning, a **22.4%** improvement over the Qwen2.5-VL-3B baseline and the highest among all compared methods.
- Demonstrated **43.2%** Acc@0.25 in 3D visual grounding, outperforming all other RGB-only methods with a **9.2%** increase over the Qwen2.5-VL-3B baseline.
- Obtained an F1_25 score of **42.8%** in 3D video detection, marking an **11.9%** gain over the Qwen2.5-VL-3B baseline and a **4.6%** gain over VG LLM.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Comprehensive 3D spatial understanding in VLMs benefits significantly from combining both abstract implicit 3D priors and detailed explicit 3D geometric information derived from RGB video.
> - A lightweight 3D explicit embedding module can effectively encode quantitative geometric details without requiring heavy 3D backbones, preserving computational efficiency.
> - An Implicit-Explicit Attention (IEA) module is effective for synergistically fusing complementary implicit and explicit 3D representations, enhancing overall 3D awareness.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Vision-Language Models (VLMs) have demonstrated advancements in integrating visual and linguistic information, particularly in tasks involving 2D images. These models often achieve proficiency in tasks such as object recognition, captioning, and visual question answering within a 2D context. However, a limitation of many existing VLMs arises when addressing tasks that require a fine-grained understanding of 3D space, such as accurate spatial localization, reasoning about object relationships in three dimensions, or navigating complex environments. This limitation stems from their primary reliance on 2D visual inputs and the inherent challenges in inferring precise 3D geometry from such data.

The broader research landscape in 3D Vision-Language Models can be categorized into two main streams. One stream involves methods that leverage explicit 3D data, such as point clouds, depth maps, or multi-view 2D features aggregated into 3D representations. Models like 3D-LLM, PointLLM, and Video-3D LLM fall into this category, demonstrating strong 3D capabilities by directly incorporating structured 3D information. While effective, these approaches typically necessitate specialized 3D sensors or extensive 3D reconstruction processes, which can be costly, difficult to deploy, and less accessible in many real-world scenarios where only 2D RGB data is readily available.

The second, more recent, stream focuses on enhancing VLMs with 3D spatial awareness using only 2D inputs, primarily RGB videos. This approach aims to circumvent the dependency on specialized 3D data. Existing methods in this category often employ 3D geometry encoders to extract "implicit" 3D representations from video sequences. These implicit representations, akin to a "3D cognitive map," are typically high-level latent vectors that capture coarse, global spatial layouts and general structural relationships within a scene. While they provide an abstract understanding of 3D space, their compressed and latent nature can render them insufficient for tasks demanding fine-grained geometric details, precise localization, or quantitative spatial reasoning. For instance, such representations might identify a "room layout" but struggle to pinpoint the exact 3D coordinates of a specific object or its precise size.

This paper positions itself within the second stream but identifies a critical gap: the inadequacy of solely implicit 3D representations for fine-grained 3D tasks. It draws an analogy to human cognition, where both abstract spatial awareness and detailed geometric understanding are utilized. The work aims to bridge this gap by integrating explicit 3D geometric information, reconstructed from RGB videos, alongside the implicit representations. This approach seeks to equip VLMs with both the holistic spatial priors and the fine-grained structural details necessary for comprehensive 3D understanding and reasoning, all while maintaining the accessibility of RGB-only inputs.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to enhance the 3D spatial awareness and reasoning capabilities of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) by equipping them with a more comprehensive understanding of 3D geometry. This is achieved through the integration of both implicit and explicit 3D geometric representations, derived exclusively from RGB video inputs.

The motivation for this work arises from a recognized limitation in the current generation of VLMs, particularly those designed for 3D tasks using only 2D visual observations. While these models have made progress in inferring 3D information from RGB videos, they predominantly rely on "implicit" 3D representations. These implicit representations, often learned through 3D geometry encoders, tend to capture coarse, high-level spatial priors and global layouts of a scene. They provide a general "3D cognitive map," indicating, for example, the overall arrangement of furniture in a room or the typical structure of a bedroom. However, their compressed, latent nature makes it challenging for language models to interpret quantitative geometric properties, such as precise object dimensions, exact spatial coordinates, or detailed structural nuances. This deficiency limits their performance in tasks requiring fine-grained spatial understanding, accurate localization, and precise reasoning.

The paper highlights this limitation by drawing parallels with cognitive science and robotics. In human cognition, individuals form both abstract "3D cognitive maps" for overall scene awareness and more detailed, often geometrically precise, "3D reconstruction maps" for specific tasks. Similarly, robotic systems typically leverage explicit 3D representations (e.g., depth maps, point clouds) that provide structured, interpretable geometric data for fine-grained spatial tasks like navigation and localization. The current RGB-only VLM frameworks, by largely relying on implicit representations, are described as lacking the "3D reconstruction map" component, thereby leaving fine-grained geometric information inaccessible for precise reasoning.

Therefore, the key motivation is to bridge this interpretability gap. The researchers aim to develop a unified framework that can extract and leverage both:
1. **Implicit Geometry Tokens (IGTs):** To capture high-level, global geometric priors and abstract spatial understanding, akin to a "3D cognitive map."
2. **Explicit Geometry Tokens (EGTs):** To encode detailed, quantitative geometric structures from reconstructed 3D attributes (such as depth maps or point clouds), providing a "3D reconstruction map" with fine-grained local details.

By integrating these two complementary types of geometric representations, along with 2D visual cues, the objective is to intrinsically inject strong 3D inductive biases into VLMs. This is intended to enable VLMs to perform comprehensive spatial understanding and reasoning at both holistic and fine-grained levels, without the requirement for any additional explicit 3D data inputs.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The VLM-IE3D framework is designed to integrate implicit and explicit 3D geometric representations from RGB video sequences into Vision-Language Models (VLMs). The overall architecture comprises three main streams for token extraction and a 3D-aware adapter for their fusion, followed by a pre-trained VLM backbone.

#### 3.1. Overall Framework

Given an RGB video sequence (I_i) and a natural language query (Q), the framework processes the input through:
1. **2D Visual Encoder:** Extracts standard 2D visual tokens (T_2D) from each frame.
2. **3D Geometry Encoder:** Processes the video sequence to produce Implicit Geometry Tokens (IGTs) (T_I) and reconstructs explicit 3D attributes.
3. **3D Explicit Embedding Module:** Converts the reconstructed explicit 3D attributes into Explicit Geometry Tokens (EGTs) (T_E).

These three types of tokens are then fed into a **3D-aware adapter**, which fuses them into enhanced 3D-aware visual tokens (T_3D). Finally, a pre-trained VLM backbone (Qwen2.5-VL-3B) utilizes T_3D and Q to generate the final response.

#### 3.2. Implicit Geometry Tokens (IGTs)

IGTs are designed to capture high-level, global 3D spatial priors. The framework employs **AnySplat** as its 3D geometry encoder. This encoder, pre-trained on various 3D geometric tasks (e.g., depth estimation, point reconstruction), features an image encoder, a fusion decoder with cross-frame interaction via self-attention, and task-specific prediction heads for 3D attributes.

The implicit 3D representations derived from the outputs of AnySplat's fusion decoder are used as IGTs (T_I). These tokens are intended to encode abstract latent vectors that represent the overall layout of a scene (e.g., typical room structure) and global spatial relationships between objects (e.g., "a TV is mounted on the wall, and a sofa is placed in front of the TV"). This approach aims to provide strong generalization for 3D structural modeling without explicit 3D inputs.

#### 3.3. Explicit Geometry Tokens (EGTs)

EGTs aim to encode detailed, quantitative 3D structural information. These tokens are generated from explicit 3D attributes reconstructed by the same 3D geometry encoder (AnySplat). Specifically, the prediction heads of the 3D geometry encoder provide depth maps, camera poses, and 3D Gaussian splats. Point maps are subsequently derived by back-projecting the reconstructed depth maps into 3D coordinates using the reconstructed camera poses.

These explicit 3D attributes (X_E) are then transformed into EGTs (T_E) by a lightweight **3D explicit embedding module**. To maintain efficiency and scalability, this module is designed to be simple, consisting of a one-layer patch embedding followed by a two-layer Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP). This avoids the computational overhead of heavy 3D backbones. For the default implementation, depth maps are selected as the explicit 3D attribute due to their accessibility and parameter efficiency. EGTs function as latent representations of these explicit 3D data, providing specific position information and fine-grained structural details that complement the broader priors of IGTs.

#### 3.4. 3D-Aware Adapter

The 3D-aware adapter is responsible for effectively integrating the 2D visual tokens (T_2D), IGTs (T_I), and EGTs (T_E).
1. **Token Compression:** Initially, spatially adjacent 2x2 features within each type of token are concatenated and processed by a two-layer MLP. This compression step reduces the spatial resolution of the tokens.
2. **Implicit-Explicit Attention (IEA) Module:** To align and fuse the complementary implicit and explicit 3D representations, an IEA module is introduced. This module employs a multi-head cross-attention (MCA) layer. For each frame, the compressed IGTs serve as queries, while the compressed EGTs act as both keys and values. A residual connection is applied after the MCA operation, producing integrated 3D tokens (T_3D_fused).
3. **Fusion with 2D Visual Tokens:** The integrated 3D tokens (T_3D_fused) are then fused with the compressed 2D visual tokens (T_2D) via element-wise addition, resulting in the final unified 3D-aware visual representation (T_3D).

#### 3.5. Implementation Details

- **VLM Backbone:** Qwen2.5-VL-3B is adopted.
- **3D Geometry Encoder:** AnySplat is utilized.
- **Training:** The 2D visual encoder and 3D geometry encoder are frozen during training. The VLM backbone and 3D explicit embedding module remain trainable. The Adam optimizer is used with a specific learning rate schedule.
- **Input Processing:** Images are rescaled and cropped to 392 × 518, with a patch size of 14, channel dimension of 2048, and maximum sequence length of 32 frames.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The VLM-IE3D framework was evaluated across a range of 3D understanding and spatial reasoning tasks, demonstrating its performance relative to existing methods.

#### 4.1. Results on 3D Scene Understanding Tasks

VLM-IE3D consistently demonstrated performance improvements over other RGB-only methods and achieved competitive results with methods that utilize explicit 3D inputs.

- **3D Dense Captioning (Scan2Cap):** VLM-IE3D achieved a C@0.5 score of 80.4%, which was the highest among all compared methods, including those leveraging 3D scene inputs. It also obtained an M@0.5 score of 28.8%, leading among 2D-visual-input methods. This represents a substantial improvement of 22.4% in C@0.5 over the Qwen2.5-VL-3B baseline and 1.8% over VG LLM, indicating enhanced 3D spatial understanding for generating object descriptions.

- **3D Visual Grounding (ScanRefer):** The model achieved 43.2% accuracy at an IoU threshold of 0.25 and 16.9% at 0.50, outperforming all other methods that rely solely on 2D inputs. Specific improvements include a 9.2% increase in Acc@0.25 over Qwen2.5-VL-3B and 6.8% over VG LLM. With proposal refinement, VLM-IE3D reached 55.4% Acc@0.25 and 48.9% Acc@0.50, narrowing the performance gap with methods that use direct 3D scene inputs.

- **3D Video Detection:** VLM-IE3D obtained P25, R25, and F1_25 scores of 44.2%, 41.9%, and 42.8% respectively. These figures represent improvements of 12.1% (P25), 11.8% (R25), and 11.9% (F1_25) over the Qwen2.5-VL-3B baseline. Compared to VG LLM, VLM-IE3D showed gains of 2.5% (P25), 6.2% (R25), and 4.6% (F1_25). These results suggest an enhanced ability to detect and localize objects within 3D video scenes.

#### 4.2. Efficiency and Complexity Analysis

VLM-IE3D was designed to be lightweight. It introduced approximately 0.10 billion additional trainable parameters compared to the VG LLM baseline, representing a 3.2% increase. The inference speed was marginally impacted, with a decrease of 1 FPS (from 7 FPS for VG LLM to 6 FPS for VLM-IE3D on a single H100 GPU). This indicates that the added geometric capabilities do not introduce substantial computational overhead.

#### 4.3. Qualitative Results Analysis

Qualitative comparisons demonstrated that VLM-IE3D provided more accurate temporal grounding and produced tighter, more precise 3D bounding boxes for visual grounding tasks compared to baselines like Qwen2.5-VL and VG LLM. In 3D video detection, VLM-IE3D mitigated common failure cases such as scale mismatch and floating predictions, aligning more accurately with object geometry.

#### 4.4. Results on Spatial Reasoning (VSI-Bench)

On the VSI-Bench spatial reasoning benchmark, VLM-IE3D achieved an average performance of 47.6% with 4B parameters. This score surpassed several larger open-source models (e.g., LLaVA-NeXT-Video-72B at 40.9%) and proprietary models (e.g., Gemini-1.5-Pro at 45.4%). The model performed notably well in object counting (67.5%), exceeding VG LLM by 1.5%. For multiple-choice tasks requiring geometric perception, VLM-IE3D achieved a 3.1% gain over VG LLM in Relative Distance (47.7% vs. 44.6%). These results suggest that explicit representations aid in resolving complex spatial ambiguities.

#### 4.5. Ablation Studies

- **Implicit and Explicit Geometry Tokens:** Experiments demonstrated that both IGTs and EGTs individually improved performance over the baseline. IGTs showed a larger individual improvement (F1_25 from 30.9% to 40.5%), while EGTs also provided a gain (F1_25 from 30.9% to 34.7%). Their combined integration yielded further synergistic gains, reaching an F1_25 of 42.8%, validating their complementary nature.
- **Fusion Strategy:** The proposed Implicit-Explicit Attention (IEA) module for fusing IGTs and EGTs achieved the highest performance (F1_25 of 42.8%) compared to concatenation, element-wise addition, or weighted addition strategies. Simple element-wise addition was found sufficient for fusing 2D and 3D tokens.
- **Explicit 3D Attribute:** Depth maps, point maps, and 3D Gaussian splats as explicit attributes all resulted in comparable performance improvements (F1_25 ranging from 42.5% to 42.8%), indicating robustness to the specific choice of explicit representation. Depth maps were selected for the default setting due to accessibility and efficiency.
- **3D Explicit Embedding Design:** The lightweight design (one-layer patch embedding followed by a two-layer MLP) for the 3D explicit embedding module was found to be effective, achieving significant performance gains with minimal parameters. A deep encoder, surprisingly, yielded suboptimal performance, hypothesized to over-abstract and create redundancy with IGTs.
- **3D Geometry Encoder:** The framework demonstrated generalization capabilities across different 3D geometry encoders (VGGT, AnySplat, π3), with all tested encoders improving performance over the baseline. The ability to use depth maps from an external source like DepthAnything V2 also showed strong performance, highlighting the framework's flexibility.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The VLM-IE3D framework represents a development in the field of 3D-aware Vision-Language Models, particularly for scenarios where direct 3D inputs are not available.

One significant aspect of this work is its capability to **enhance fine-grained 3D spatial understanding and reasoning from purely RGB video sequences**. By integrating both implicit and explicit 3D geometric representations, the model addresses a limitation of prior RGB-only methods that primarily relied on coarse implicit representations. This dual approach allows VLMs to grasp both the holistic layout of a scene and intricate local geometric details, which is relevant for tasks requiring precise localization and quantitative spatial reasoning.

The methodology contributes to **reducing the dependency on specialized 3D sensors and explicit 3D data**. This makes 3D-aware VLM capabilities more accessible and deployable in diverse real-world environments where only conventional 2D video data can be readily acquired. This accessibility has implications for broader adoption in applications such as consumer robotics, augmented reality, and mobile computing.

The demonstrated performance improvements across various 3D tasks—including 3D video object detection, 3D visual grounding, 3D dense captioning, and spatial reasoning—suggest a more robust and capable VLM for complex spatial interactions. The framework's ability to achieve competitive results with methods that use explicit 3D inputs, while operating solely on RGB data, indicates a step toward narrowing the performance gap between these two categories of 3D VLMs.

Furthermore, the **lightweight design and modularity** of VLM-IE3D are notable. The minimal increase in trainable parameters and acceptable inference speed, coupled with the proven robustness across different choices of explicit 3D attributes and geometry encoders, suggests that the framework is practical and can be readily integrated with future advancements in 3D geometry reconstruction techniques. This flexibility could facilitate future research into more sophisticated 3D-aware VLMs without incurring prohibitive computational costs.

The potential impact extends to various domains that require comprehensive spatial intelligence. These include embodied AI for autonomous agents, advanced robotics for navigation and manipulation, and immersive technologies that demand an accurate understanding of 3D environments. By providing VLMs with a richer, more interpretable understanding of 3D space, this work contributes to the development of more intelligent and spatially aware artificial systems.
