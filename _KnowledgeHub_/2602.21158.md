# SELAUR: Self Evolving LLM Agent via Uncertainty-aware Rewards

> [!summary] Summary
> SELAUR introduces a reinforcement learning framework that integrates multi-type intrinsic uncertainty signals into reward shaping for large language model (LLM) agents, achieving superior task success rates on interactive benchmarks like ALFWorld and WebShop. This approach enables agents to learn more effectively from both successful and failed reasoning trajectories by providing dense, confidence-aligned supervision.

## Problem

- Existing reinforcement learning approaches for LLM agents often rely on sparse, outcome-based rewards, offering limited guidance for complex, multi-step reasoning processes.
- Traditional reward signals typically indicate correctness but do not account for the LLM's confidence, overlooking valuable intrinsic uncertainty signals.
- Effectively extracting learning signals from failed trajectories is challenging, as conventional binary rewards often discard valuable gradient information when an agent fails to achieve a goal.

## Method

- A ==multi-metric uncertainty estimation module== quantifies token-level uncertainty using a weighted combination of entropy, least confidence, and margin.
- ==Hierarchical aggregation mechanisms== transform token-level uncertainties into step-level and exponentially discounted trajectory-level uncertainty scores.
- A novel ==failure-aware reward shaping mechanism== augments step rewards and replaces trajectory rewards with uncertainty signals in failed cases, converting failures into positive learning opportunities.

## Results

- SELAUR consistently outperformed strong reinforcement learning baselines (PPO, RLOO, GRPO, GiGPO) on ALFWorld and WebShop benchmarks, achieving up to **0.8906** success rate on ALFWorld (Qwen2.5-1.5B) and **0.8935** task score on WebShop (Qwen2.5-7B).
- Ablation studies confirmed that the full integration of all three uncertainty measures and the proposed failure-aware reward shaping mechanism were crucial for achieving optimal performance.
- Qualitative case studies demonstrated SELAUR's ability to avoid repetitive, suboptimal loops and adapt exploration dynamically based on confidence levels, leading to more coherent and efficient decision trajectories.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Intrinsic uncertainty, derived directly from an LLM's probability distributions, serves as a dense and valuable learning signal for reinforcement learning agents.
> - By explicitly learning from failed trajectories through uncertainty-aware rewards, agents can achieve more robust policy improvements and overcome limitations of sparse reward systems.
> - Combining diverse uncertainty metrics (entropy, least confidence, margin) provides a more comprehensive characterization of model confidence, leading to more stable and effective exploration strategies.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of large language models (LLMs) has progressed from processing single-turn queries to enabling multi-step reasoning and decision-making within interactive environments, where LLMs function as agents. This transition necessitates effective mechanisms for guiding the agents' learning process, particularly through reward design in reinforcement learning (RL) paradigms.

Traditional RL approaches for LLMs often rely on sparse rewards, typically assigned only upon the final outcome of a task. While effective in driving agents toward success, these methods provide limited feedback for intermediate steps, potentially hindering the learning of complex multi-step reasoning. Recent advancements have addressed this by introducing step-level credit assignment, which evaluates individual decisions within a trajectory, thereby offering more granular learning signals and improving policy optimization stability.

Despite these advancements, a critical aspect of LLM behavior—intrinsic uncertainty—has largely remained unaddressed in reward design for agent training. Uncertainty quantification (UQ) in LLMs has been explored for various applications, including answer verification, hallucination detection, and self-improvement, demonstrating its utility as an indicator of model reliability. Similarly, in the broader RL field, uncertainty has been leveraged to drive exploration through intrinsic motivation. However, the explicit integration of these uncertainty signals into the reward function for training LLM agents has not been extensively investigated.

This work, SELAUR, aims to bridge this gap by incorporating LLM's intrinsic uncertainty directly into the reward design for reinforcement learning agents. It extends existing step-level reward mechanisms by providing confidence-aligned supervision, enabling agents to use their own uncertainty as an internal learning signal. This approach differentiates itself from methods that rely on explicit external or self-generated evaluations (e.g., in RLHF, ReAct, Voyager, Reflexion) by deriving feedback from the model's internal confidence estimates. By doing so, SELAUR contributes to the development of more robust and efficient LLM agents capable of navigating complex interactive environments and learning from experiences that might otherwise offer limited supervisory value.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the training of multi-step decision-making LLM agents by explicitly integrating their intrinsic uncertainty into the reinforcement learning reward mechanism. This objective is driven by two main motivations:

First, the authors propose that LLM uncertainty serves as a direct measure of model confidence, providing valuable insights for guiding the learning process. In multi-step tasks, an agent's confidence can vary at each decision point. High uncertainty at a particular step may indicate an exploratory state where the agent is unsure, suggesting that alternative strategies should be encouraged. Conversely, low uncertainty implies a confident decision, which should be reinforced to promote policy convergence. Current reward mechanisms often overlook this confidence signal, potentially leading to inefficient exploration or the reinforcement of low-confidence "correct" actions that are not robust. By incorporating uncertainty, the reward signal can more accurately reflect not only the correctness of a decision but also the model's confidence in that decision, leading to more nuanced and effective guidance.

Second, the research is motivated by the potential to extract meaningful learning signals from failed trajectories, a common challenge in traditional RL settings. In many complex tasks, agents frequently encounter failures. When rewards are based solely on the final outcome, failed trajectories typically provide a binary negative signal or no signal at all, leading to the discarding of potentially valuable intermediate information. The authors argue that even when a trajectory fails, step-level uncertainty can reveal which decisions were particularly uncertain or, conversely, which partial decisions showed promise. This fine-grained feedback, derived from the model's internal state of uncertainty, can transform otherwise uninformative failures into structured learning opportunities, enabling the agent to refine its policy more effectively. This mechanism is intended to improve learning stability and exploration efficiency by leveraging all generated experiences, not just successful ones.

In summary, the key objectives are to:
1. Develop a unified framework for quantifying token-level uncertainty in LLMs.
2. Aggregate these token-level uncertainties into meaningful step- and trajectory-level confidence estimations.
3. Integrate these uncertainty-aware signals into both step-level and trajectory-level rewards through a failure-aware reshaping mechanism.
The overarching motivation is to enable LLM agents to leverage intrinsic uncertainty to balance exploration and exploitation more effectively, extract dense learning signals from failures, and ultimately achieve more stable and robust policy learning in interactive environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

SELAUR (Self Evolving LLM Agent via Uncertainty-aware Rewards) is a reinforcement learning framework designed for LLM agents that systematically incorporates uncertainty into its reward design. The framework consists of three primary modules: Uncertainty Estimation, Step and Trajectory Aggregation, and Failure-aware Reward Shaping.

#### 3.1. Uncertainty Estimation

This module quantifies the uncertainty of the LLM at each token decoding step. The authors combine three distinct metrics to capture various aspects of predictive reliability:

- **Entropy ($u_{ent}$):** This metric measures the dispersion of the probability distribution over the entire vocabulary. A higher entropy value indicates that the model's probability mass is spread across many tokens, suggesting lower confidence. The formula is:
 $u_{ent_{t,j}} = - \frac{\sum_c p_{t,c} \log p_{t,c}}{\log |V|}$, where $p_{t,c}$ is the predicted probability of token $c$ at step $t$, and $|V|$ is the vocabulary size. The normalization by $\log |V|$ ensures values are within $$.
- **Least Confidence ($u_{lc}$):** This measure focuses on the probability of the most likely (selected) token. A lower probability for the selected token indicates higher uncertainty. The formula is:
 $u_{lc_{t,j}} = 1 - p_{t,(1)}$, where $p_{t,(1)}$ is the probability of the chosen token at step $t$.
- **Margin ($u_{mar}$):** Margin-based uncertainty assesses the difference between the probabilities of the top two most likely tokens. A smaller margin implies greater ambiguity between the best and second-best choices. The formula is:
 $u_{mar_{t,j}} = \sigma \left( \frac{1 - (p_{t,(1)} - p_{t,(2)})}{s} \right)$, where $p_{t,(1)}$ and $p_{t,(2)}$ are the top-2 token probabilities, $s$ is a scaling factor, and $\sigma(\cdot)$ is the sigmoid function for normalization.

These three metrics are then combined into a single aggregated token-level uncertainty measure using tunable weights:
$u_{t,j} = w_{ent} u_{ent_{t,j}} + w_{lc} u_{lc_{t,j}} + w_{mar} u_{mar_{t,j}}$
This aggregation aims to provide a comprehensive uncertainty estimate by considering global distribution spread, local decision confidence, and distinctiveness between top choices.

#### 3.2. Step and Trajectory Aggregation

Token-level uncertainties are too granular for direct use in RL. Therefore, SELAUR aggregates them hierarchically:

- **Step-level aggregation ($u_{step_t}$):** For each step $t$ in a trajectory, the token-level uncertainties are averaged:
 $u_{step_t} = \frac{1}{J} \sum_j u_{t,j}$, where $J$ is the number of tokens in the current step.
- **Trajectory-level aggregation ($U(\tau)$):** To obtain an overall uncertainty for an entire trajectory $\tau$, the step-level uncertainties are combined using an exponentially discounted aggregation. This assigns greater importance to later steps, which often have a stronger influence on the final outcome:
 $U(\tau) = \frac{\sum_{t=1}^T \lambda^{T-t} u_{step_t}}{\sum_{t=1}^T \lambda^{T-t}}$, where $T$ is the total number of steps in the trajectory, and $\lambda \in (0, 1]$ is a discount factor.

#### 3.3. Failure-aware Reward Shaping

This module is central to SELAUR, modifying standard RL rewards to leverage uncertainty, particularly in failed trajectories.

- **Step-wise shaping ($\tilde{r}_{step_t}$):** When a trajectory fails, the reward for each individual step is augmented by its normalized uncertainty. This means that instead of receiving a zero or negative reward, failed steps contribute a positive value proportional to their uncertainty, encouraging the agent to learn from its less confident decisions. For successful trajectories, the standard step reward $r_t$ is used.
 $\tilde{r}_{step_t} = \begin{cases} 1[\text{fail}] \cdot w_t \hat{u}_{step_t}, & \text{if fail} \\ r_t, & \text{otherwise} \end{cases}$
 Here, $w_t$ is a step-dependent weight (set to 0.95 in experiments to ensure failure rewards are smaller than success rewards), and $\hat{u}_{step_t}$ is the normalized step uncertainty.

- **Trajectory-level shaping ($\tilde{r}_{traj}$):** Similarly, for a failed trajectory, the overall trajectory reward is replaced by its aggregated uncertainty $U(\tau)$. This ensures that even failed trajectories provide a dense, informative signal for learning, rather than a sparse penalty. For successful trajectories, the standard trajectory reward $r$ is used.
 $\tilde{r}_{traj} = \begin{cases} U(\tau), & \text{if fail} \\ r, & \text{otherwise} \end{cases}$

This reward shaping mechanism transforms failed experiences into positive learning signals, prompting the agent to revisit uncertain reasoning paths and refine its policy. By aligning reward dynamics with model uncertainty, SELAUR aims to bridge the gap between sparse outcome-based rewards and dense, internal confidence-based signals.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy of SELAUR was evaluated through experiments on two interactive benchmarks and compared against several baseline methods.

#### 4.1. Experimental Setup

- **Benchmarks:**
 - **ALFWorld:** Emphasizes generalization and instruction following in a simulated household environment.
 - **WebShop:** Focuses on goal-oriented decision-making in a realistic e-commerce setting.
- **Base Models:** Qwen2.5-1.5B and Qwen2.5-7B, representing different model sizes.
- **Baselines:**
 - **Prompting:** A simple instruction-following approach without RL adaptation.
 - **PPO (Proximal Policy Optimization):** A standard reinforcement learning algorithm.
 - **RLOO (Reinforce-style Optimization):** A method for learning from human feedback.
 - **GRPO (Group Policy Optimization):** An RL algorithm.
 - **GiGPO (Group-in-Group Policy Optimization):** A baseline that introduces step-level reward modeling.
- **Metrics:**
 - **Task Success Rate:** Primary measure for both ALFWorld and WebShop, indicating successful task completion.
 - **Task Score:** For WebShop, quantifies the alignment between user intent and the agent’s final product selection.
- **Training Parameters:** Learning rate of $1 \times 10^{-6}$, 150 total training steps, rollout size of 8. Maximum interaction steps were 50 for ALFWorld and 15 for WebShop.

#### 4.2. Main Results

As presented in Table 1, the prompted agent consistently exhibited poor performance across both benchmarks and model sizes, highlighting the necessity of reinforcement-based adaptation for complex interactive tasks. Among the learning-based methods:

- **PPO** demonstrated competitive performance, particularly with the smaller 1.5B model, but was noted to be less efficient due to its additional critic network.
- **RLOO** and **GRPO** achieved balanced results with improved computational efficiency.
- **GiGPO**, by incorporating step-level reward modeling, generally surpassed the performance of PPO, RLOO, and GRPO, indicating the benefit of finer-grained reward signals throughout a trajectory.

**SELAUR consistently achieved the strongest performance across all benchmarks and both model sizes.** For the Qwen2.5-1.5B model, SELAUR recorded an overall average success rate of 0.8906 on ALFWorld and a task score of 0.8812 with a success rate of 0.7656 on WebShop, outperforming GiGPO which achieved 0.8828, 0.8017, and 0.6757, respectively. With the larger Qwen2.5-7B model, SELAUR maintained its lead, achieving an overall average success rate of 0.9687 on ALFWorld and a task score of 0.8935 with a success rate of 0.7968 on WebShop, again surpassing GiGPO's 0.9453, 0.8867, and 0.7929. These results suggest that the integration of diverse uncertainty measures allows SELAUR to better identify and explore uncertain states, leading to more comprehensive policy improvement.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

**Effect of Different Uncertainty Measures (Table 2):**
An ablation study on the WebShop dataset examined the contribution of individual and combined uncertainty metrics.
- Using only one type of uncertainty (entropy, least confidence, or margin) or pairwise combinations yielded partial improvements compared to the baseline (GiGPO without uncertainty) but did not match the performance of the full SELAUR model.
- Entropy alone, while capturing overall policy distribution sharpness, was observed to overlook nuanced ambiguities. Least confidence and margin metrics provided more granular perspectives by focusing on the competitive relationship between top actions.
- The superior performance of the unified model (Entropy + Least Confidence + Margin) indicates that these measures are complementary, collectively identifying diverse "zones of uncertainty" that a single metric might miss.
- Visualization of entropy during training (Fig. 2) showed that SELAUR maintains higher entropy in later training stages compared to variants, suggesting sustained and broader exploration.

**Effect of Different Reward Functions (Table 3):**
The study also explored alternative ways of incorporating uncertainty into the reward function:
- **Negative variant:** Replaced failure rewards with negative uncertainty values, aiming to penalize uncertain actions. This variant underperformed SELAUR, achieving 0.8516 average success on AlfWorld and 0.7539 on WebShop.
- **Exponential variant:** Attenuated rewards for successful trajectories based on their uncertainty (encouraging more confident successes). This also underperformed SELAUR, with 0.8750 on AlfWorld and 0.7031 on WebShop. The excessive discounting of rewards was hypothesized to weaken the reinforcement of successful behaviors.
- SELAUR's proposed formulation (augmenting failed step rewards with normalized uncertainty and replacing failed trajectory rewards with aggregated uncertainty) achieved the best overall performance (0.8906 on AlfWorld and 0.7656 on WebShop), indicating an effective balance between exploration and exploitation.

#### 4.4. Case Studies

Qualitative analysis in the WebShop environment (Fig. 3) compared SELAUR with GiGPO.
- **GiGPO** frequently exhibited repetitive intermediate actions and became trapped in local exploration loops, indicating "uncertainty collapse" where it might stick to suboptimal confident behaviors.
- **SELAUR**, leveraging its uncertainty-aware guidance, dynamically adjusted its exploration based on confidence levels. This mechanism allowed the agent to recognize and escape unproductive cycles after a few repetitions, ultimately leading it toward the correct solution more efficiently. This suggests that SELAUR's approach promotes more coherent and adaptive decision trajectories.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The SELAUR framework introduces a novel paradigm for training Large Language Model (LLM) agents by explicitly integrating their intrinsic uncertainty into the reinforcement learning (RL) reward mechanism. This represents a significant contribution to the field of LLM agents and RL, addressing limitations prevalent in existing methods.

### 6. Significance

- **Dense and Informative Supervision:** SELAUR moves beyond sparse, outcome-based rewards by generating dense, confidence-aligned supervision at the token, step, and trajectory levels. This provides more granular and actionable feedback to the agent, which can accelerate learning and improve policy stability in complex multi-step tasks.
- **Effective Exploitation of Failures:** A key aspect of SELAUR is its failure-aware reward reshaping mechanism. By transforming failed experiences into meaningful learning signals through uncertainty quantification, the framework mitigates the problem of discarding valuable gradient information from unsuccessful trajectories. This allows agents to learn not just from what worked, but also from where they were uncertain or made mistakes.
- **Enhanced Exploration and Robustness:** The integration of multiple uncertainty metrics (entropy, least confidence, margin) enables a more comprehensive characterization of model confidence. This multi-faceted uncertainty estimation fosters a more balanced exploration strategy, allowing the agent to dynamically adjust its behavior between exploiting confident actions and exploring uncertain but potentially valuable paths. Empirical results demonstrate that SELAUR maintains higher entropy during training, indicating sustained exploration and improved adaptability.
- **Addressing Limitations of Existing RL for LLMs:** SELAUR addresses the common challenge of sparse rewards in LLM agent training and extends prior work on step-level credit assignment by incorporating an internal, intrinsic signal derived from the LLM's own confidence. This offers an alternative to methods that rely heavily on external feedback or explicit self-reflection heuristics, making the learning process potentially more autonomous and efficient.

**Potential Impact:**

- **More Capable and Autonomous LLM Agents:** The ability to learn effectively from uncertainty and failures can lead to the development of more robust, adaptive, and efficient LLM agents. These agents could operate more reliably in dynamic and complex environments where complete information or perfect reward signals are unavailable.
- **Improved Performance in Interactive Tasks:** The consistent performance improvements demonstrated across benchmarks like ALFWorld and WebShop suggest that SELAUR's approach can significantly boost success rates and task alignment in various interactive decision-making scenarios, from instruction following to e-commerce navigation.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** This work lays a foundation for future investigations into leveraging intrinsic LLM properties for agent training. The authors suggest extensions to vision-centric and multimodal tasks, where uncertainty can be even more complex and critical for robust decision-making. It also opens avenues for exploring uncertainty quantification in high-dimensional observations and more diverse forms of uncertainty beyond token probabilities.
- **Reduced Need for Human Supervision:** By enabling agents to derive more self-sufficient learning signals from their own uncertainties, SELAUR could potentially reduce the extensive human feedback or curated demonstrations often required to train powerful LLM agents, making the development process more scalable.

In conclusion, SELAUR's novel approach to integrating uncertainty-aware rewards into RL for LLM agents offers a robust mechanism for improving learning dynamics, exploration efficiency, and overall task performance, with broad implications for the advancement of autonomous AI systems.
