# Bridging the Gap Between Learning in Discrete and Continuous Environments for Vision-and-Language Navigation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from The Australian National University and University of Adelaide developed a Candidate Waypoints Predictor, enabling Vision-and-Language Navigation agents trained in discrete environments to effectively operate in continuous settings. This approach achieved new state-of-the-art results on R2R-CE and RxR-CE datasets, demonstrating over 10% higher success rates on RxR-CE while using significantly less computational resources.

## Problem

- A significant performance gap exists between Vision-and-Language Navigation (VLN) agents trained in simplified discrete environments and their performance in more realistic continuous environments.
- Advanced VLN techniques developed for discrete settings are challenging to directly apply to continuous spaces due to fundamental differences in action definition and spatial understanding.
- Previous continuous VLN approaches often require high computational resources (e.g., reinforcement learning) or may predict navigation paths that are not physically traversable.

## Method

- Introduces a **Candidate Waypoints Predictor (CWP)**, a ==Transformer-based== module that infers a set of accessible ==waypoints== around the agent from ==RGBD panoramic observations==.
- Developed a refined process to generate high-quality, physically traversable ==connectivity graphs== from Matterport3D for Habitat-Matterport3D environments, serving as ground truth for CWP training.
- Enables existing discrete VLN agents (like ==CMA== and ==VLN-BERT==) to utilize these predicted ==high-level waypoints== for navigation in continuous environments, decomposing chosen waypoints into low-level actions in the simulator, and employs ==waypoint augmentation== during training.

## Results

- The Candidate Waypoints Predictor effectively reduced collision rates and outperformed baseline models by accurately identifying accessible waypoints in continuous environments.
- The method significantly reduced the absolute discrete-to-continuous performance gap for agents by **11.76% SPL** for CMA and **18.24% SPL** for VLN-BERT, making their performance comparable to or surpassing agents with ground-truth graphs.
- New state-of-the-art results were achieved on R2R-CE and RxR-CE test sets, with **over 10% higher SR** and **6% higher nDTW** on RxR-CE, while dramatically reducing computational cost compared to prior methods.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Identifying accessible directions (view selection) is the primary bottleneck in learning to navigate in continuous environments, more so than fine-grained low-level control.
> - Sophisticated models developed for discrete Vision-and-Language Navigation (VLN) can be effectively transferred to continuous environments if a mechanism for high-level, discrete-like action selection is provided.
> - Augmenting training data by sampling diverse waypoint positions within predicted navigable regions improves an agent's generalization and robustness in continuous navigation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This paper operates within the rapidly evolving field of **Embodied Artificial Intelligence**, specifically focusing on **Vision-and-Language Navigation (VLN)**. VLN requires an AI agent to interpret natural language instructions and navigate through a 3D environment based on visual observations. The field has largely bifurcated into two distinct research paradigms:

- **Discrete Environments:** These typically rely on pre-defined connectivity graphs (like in Matterport3D, used by datasets such as R2R and RxR). Agents in these environments perform "high-level" actions, effectively teleporting between interconnected nodes. This simplifies the navigation problem by abstracting away low-level controls and providing explicit "navigable directions" through panoramic views. Many significant advancements in VLN, including sophisticated cross-modal matching techniques, data augmentation strategies, and transformer-based pre-training models, have emerged from this discrete setup.
- **Continuous Environments:** These simulate more realistic physical spaces where agents operate without a pre-defined graph and must infer their movements using "low-level" actions (e.g., "move forward 0.25 meters," "turn left 15 degrees"). Datasets like R2R-CE and RxR-CE, built on simulators like Habitat, represent this challenging scenario.

The core problem this paper addresses is the **significant "domain gap"** between these two paradigms. Agents trained in discrete environments, despite their advanced capabilities, often perform poorly when directly applied to continuous environments. The paper quantifies this gap, noting a substantial 20% difference in success rate for agents with the same architecture. This performance disparity stems from the fundamental difference in how "action" is defined and how spatial accessibility is understood. Discrete environments provide this information intrinsically via the graph, while continuous environments demand the agent to learn it from raw sensory input.

Prior work in continuous VLN has explored various solutions, such as semantic map representations and language-aligned waypoint supervision. Other related areas, such as Hierarchical Visual Navigation, also consider the use of sub-goals. The authors specifically differentiate their work from **Sim2Real-VLN** and **Waypoint Models**. While Sim2Real-VLN also predicted sub-goals, it was trained on connectivity graphs that contained invalid edges (i.e., through obstacles), which are not suitable for realistic navigation. Waypoint Models, although effective, relied on computationally intensive reinforcement learning (DDPPO) and did not decouple waypoint prediction from agent decision-making.

This paper's contribution lies in bridging this gap by enabling the transfer of powerful discrete-VLN learning techniques to continuous environments. By providing a mechanism for "high-level actions" in continuous spaces, it allows the sophisticated architectures developed for discrete VLN to be leveraged, thereby pushing the field closer to real-world applicability for embodied agents and robots.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The motivation for this research stems directly from the observed performance disparity and practical limitations in current VLN systems:

- **Bridging the Discrete-to-Continuous Gap:** The most prominent motivation is the substantial performance gap between agents trained in discrete environments (which simplify navigation via connectivity graphs) and those in continuous environments (which are more realistic but significantly harder to master). The paper explicitly states that advanced techniques from discrete VLN are challenging to apply directly to continuous VLN.
- **Leveraging Discrete VLN Advances:** Many sophisticated methods have been developed for discrete VLN, including pre-trained visiolinguistic transformers, advanced scene memory, and effective data augmentation. The authors are motivated to find a way to make these powerful techniques transferable and beneficial for navigation in continuous spaces, rather than having separate, less performant approaches.
- **Real-World Relevance:** Navigation in continuous spaces is inherently closer to real-world robotic applications. The ultimate goal of embodied AI is to deploy agents in physical environments. Therefore, enabling efficient learning and robust navigation in continuous simulations is a crucial step towards practical deployment.
- **Understanding the Value of High-Level Actions:** The authors highlight that discrete navigation's efficiency comes from "panoramic high-level actions" (view selection and waypoint teleportation), which transform a complex control problem into a simpler text-to-image grounding task. A key motivation is to quantitatively evaluate and demonstrate the critical value of these high-level actions, especially the ability to know accessible waypoints, in continuous settings.

Based on these motivations, the paper sets forth several key objectives:

- **Develop a Waypoints Predictor:** To create a module that can generate a set of accessible "candidate waypoints" in continuous environments at each timestep, effectively constructing a local navigability graph on the fly.
- **Refine Connectivity Graphs:** To adapt and refine existing discrete connectivity graphs (from Matterport3D) to accurately represent navigable spaces in continuous Habitat-Matterport3D environments, serving as ground truth for training the predictor.
- **Enable High-Level Actions in Continuous VLN:** To demonstrate that by providing predicted waypoints, agents originally designed for high-level actions in discrete VLN can be effectively transferred to and trained in continuous environments.
- **Enhance Generalization:** To propose and validate a method (waypoint augmentation) that diversifies training observations and paths, thereby improving the agent's generalization ability in continuous spaces.
- **Achieve State-of-the-Art Performance:** To surpass existing methods in continuous VLN on benchmark datasets like R2R-CE and RxR-CE, proving the efficacy of their proposed approach.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of the paper's methodology revolves around introducing a **Candidate Waypoints Predictor (CWP)** to bridge the discrete-to-continuous gap in VLN. This predictor allows agents to leverage high-level, discrete-like actions in continuous environments, thus adapting powerful discrete VLN models.

#### 3.1. Candidate Waypoints Predictor (CWP)

The CWP is designed to infer navigable locations (waypoints) around the agent in a continuous space.
- **Input:** RGB and Depth panoramas, composed of 12 single-view images at 30-degree separations.
- **Visual Encoders:** Two frozen ResNet-50 models are used: one pre-trained on ImageNet for RGB, and another pre-trained for point-goal navigation on Gibson for depth. This leverages pre-existing visual understanding.
- **Spatial Modeling:** A multi-layer Transformer (two layers, 12 self-attention heads) processes the combined RGBD features. Crucially, self-attention is constrained to local neighbors (i-1, i+1) to model spatial relationships between views within the panorama.
- **Prediction Head:** A multi-layer perceptron (MLP) classifier maps the Transformer outputs to a heatmap. This heatmap represents a discretized 2D circle around the agent (120 angles * 12 distances), where each cell indicates the probability of an accessible waypoint. Angles are 3 degrees apart, and distances range from 0.25m to 3.00m (0.25m separation).
- **Waypoint Extraction:** Non-Maximum Suppression (NMS) is applied to the heatmap to extract a set of K (max 5) distinct neighboring waypoints.

#### 3.2. Connectivity Graphs in Habitat-Matterport3D (Ground Truth for CWP)

To train the CWP, robust ground-truth connectivity graphs for continuous environments were essential. The authors refined the existing Matterport3D (MP3D) graphs to fit the Habitat-Matterport3D (Habitat-MP3D) spaces.
- **Refinement Process:** This involved several steps:
 - **Projection:** Initial MP3D graph nodes were projected to corresponding positions in Habitat.
 - **Criteria:** Four strict criteria were established to ensure graph quality: nodes not adhering to obstacles, minimal new node additions, maintained edge straightness, and merging of short edges (<0.25m).
 - **Sampling & Adjustment:** For invalid nodes, 264 candidate new positions were sampled within a 0.35m radius. These candidates were scored based on distance to obstacles, new node count, and edge straightness.
 - **Adding Nodes:** Detours were introduced by adding new nodes if edges traversed obstacles, using Habitat's `shortest_path_follower` to generate valid paths.
 - **Merging:** Nodes within 0.5m were merged for conciseness, with the entire adjustment process iterated until the graph was fully navigable.
 - **Fitting to R2R-CE Trajectories:** The refined graph was further adjusted to ensure starting and ending points of R2R-CE trajectories aligned with graph nodes, with new nodes added if necessary. This also involved filtering out original R2R-CE samples that did not have a well-matched discrete path.

#### 3.3. CWP Training and Evaluation

- **Dataset:** For each node on the refined Habitat-MP3D graph, a local sub-graph of adjacent waypoints was converted into a 120x12 heatmap, serving as ground truth. Waypoints were represented as Gaussian distributions to allow for prediction tolerance.
- **Training Objective:** Mean Squared Error (MSE) between predicted and ground-truth heatmaps.
- **Comparison:** The CWP was compared against a baseline (no Transformer) and a U-Net-based model (similar to Sim2Real). Evaluation metrics included `|∆|` (difference in waypoint count), `%Open` (ratio of predicted waypoints in open space), and `dC`/`dH` (Chamfer/Hausdorff distances for point clouds).

#### 3.4. Navigation Agent Training and Evaluation

- **Navigators:** Two widely used VLN agents were chosen:
 - **Cross-Modal Matching Agent (CMA):** A simpler sequence-to-sequence network with visual and language attentions. Trained from scratch.
 - **Recurrent VLN-BERT (VLN-BERT):** A transformer-based network pre-trained for discrete VLN, leveraging pre-trained V&L transformers.
- **Learning Objective:** Simple imitation learning with a cross-entropy loss between predicted action probabilities and oracle actions.
- **Oracle Action Definition:**
 - For R2R-CE: The waypoint with the shortest geodesic distance to the target.
 - For RxR-CE (multi-room paths): The waypoint leading to the closest "sub-goal" on the ground-truth path, which is defined as the intersection of a 3-meter ring around the agent and the ground-truth path.
- **Action Execution:** Once a high-level waypoint is chosen by the agent, it is decomposed into a sequence of low-level controls in the Habitat simulator (instead of direct teleportation).
- **Training Strategy:** Schedule sampling was used to gradually transition from teacher forcing to student forcing.
- **Waypoint Augmentation (Key Novelty):** During agent training, a new waypoint is sampled from the heatmap patch corresponding to the agent's selected view. This introduces variability in observations and step lengths, forcing the agent to learn more robust behaviors and improving generalization.
- **Habitat Simulator Configurations:** Standard R2R-CE and RxR-CE configurations were used, with adjustments (e.g., turning angles, allowing/disallowing sliding) to facilitate CWP usage and fair comparisons.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments presented in the paper convincingly demonstrate the efficacy of the proposed candidate waypoints predictor and the overall framework.

#### 4.1. Value of High-Level Actions (Proof of Concept)

Initial experiments confirmed the fundamental advantage of high-level actions.
- **View Selection is Crucial:** Agents capable of "view selection" (knowing navigable directions, even if only moving 0.25m) achieved success rates close to those navigating on ground-truth graphs. This suggests that identifying accessible directions is the primary bottleneck in learning to navigate in continuous spaces, rather than fine-grained low-level control.
- **Teleportation Improves Efficiency:** While view selection is key for learning, "waypoint teleportation" (moving directly to a distant waypoint) significantly reduces execution time and slightly improves success rate, highlighting efficiency benefits.

#### 4.2. Candidate Waypoints Predictor (CWP) Performance

- The Transformer-based CWP outperformed a U-Net-based baseline (similar to Sim2Real) in terms of accuracy metrics (Chamfer and Hausdorff distances), while maintaining a high percentage of predicted waypoints in open, accessible space. This is critical as the U-Net baseline often predicted waypoints blocked by obstacles.
- The CWP demonstrated generalizability to unseen environments, proving its robustness.

#### 4.3. Bridging the Discrete-to-Continuous Gap

- **Significant Improvement:** Agents using the predicted waypoints showed a substantial performance increase compared to agents using only low-level actions. For the Cross-Modal Matching Agent (CMA), the CWP reduced the absolute discrete-to-continuous gap by 11.76% SPL. For VLN-BERT, this reduction was even more significant, at 18.24% SPL.
- **Comparable to Ground Truth:** When agents were trained on *predicted* waypoints, their performance (e.g., SPL) became comparable to, or even surpassed, agents navigating on the refined ground-truth graphs. For VLN-BERT, the gap in Success Rate (SR) and SPL was reduced by 77% and 90% respectively compared to low-level action agents.
- **Reduced Collisions:** The introduction of the waypoints predictor significantly reduced the collision rate during navigation (from 15% to 7% in an example), directly indicating its effectiveness in guiding agents along navigable paths.

#### 4.4. Effectiveness of Waypoint Augmentation

- Training agents with augmented waypoints (sampling new positions within the predicted heatmap) further improved generalization ability and overall performance. In some cases (e.g., CMA in unseen environments), this even led to agents outperforming those trained solely on ground-truth graphs. This augmentation effectively diversifies the agent's experience with different step lengths and visual observations.

#### 4.5. State-of-the-Art (SoTA) Results

- The proposed method achieved new state-of-the-art results on the R2R-CE and RxR-CE test sets.
- On R2R-CE, the CMA agent improved significantly over previous methods (e.g., 6% SR and 3% SPL over Waypoint Models).
- Crucially, these SoTA results were achieved with **significantly lower computational cost**. The authors' approach, based on imitation learning with a single GPU (3.5 days), dramatically reduced training resources compared to reinforcement learning methods like DDPPO (which used 64 GPUs for 5 days in Waypoint Models).
- On the multilingual RxR-CE dataset, both CMA and VLN-BERT achieved over 10% higher SR and 6% higher nDTW than previous best models.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research marks a significant advancement in the field of Vision-and-Language Navigation, particularly in bridging the gap between simplified research settings and real-world applicability.

- **Enabling Transfer Learning for VLN:** The most critical impact is demonstrating a robust and effective method to transfer the rich advancements made in discrete VLN (e.g., sophisticated language-vision models, pre-training techniques) to the more challenging and realistic continuous environments. This means that future innovations in discrete VLN can potentially be directly beneficial for continuous VLN without fundamental architectural redesigns.
- **Paradigm Shift for Continuous VLN Training:** By providing a "high-level action" interface via predicted waypoints, the paper effectively transforms the complex low-level control problem in continuous VLN into a more manageable waypoint selection problem. This simplifies the learning objective, making training more efficient and effective, as evidenced by the superior performance achieved with imitation learning and fewer computational resources.
- **Increased Efficiency and Accessibility:** Achieving state-of-the-art performance with significantly less computational cost (single GPU vs. multiple GPUs for previous methods) democratizes research in this area. It allows more researchers and institutions to experiment with and build upon advanced VLN models.
- **Paving the Way for Real-World Embodied AI:** The ability for VLN agents to reliably navigate in continuous, photo-realistic environments (like Habitat-Matterport3D) is a crucial step towards deploying intelligent robots that can follow human instructions in real-world indoor spaces. The concepts developed here, such as dynamically predicting navigable areas, are highly relevant for real-world robotics where pre-built maps might be unavailable or imperfect.
- **Broad Applicability within Embodied AI:** The paper explicitly highlights the potential for the "candidate waypoints predictor" and "navigable-view selection" concepts to be applied to a wider range of embodied AI tasks beyond VLN, including PointNav (navigating to a specific point), ObjectNav (navigating to an object), Audio-Visual Navigation, and general embodied task-completion problems. This suggests the proposed methodology could serve as a foundational component for various intelligent agent behaviors.

**Limitations and Future Work (as identified by the authors):**
The authors transparently discuss several limitations, offering clear directions for future research:
- **Fixed Number of Candidates:** The current CWP predicts a fixed maximum number of waypoints (5). A dynamic number of waypoints might be more appropriate for varying spatial structures.
- **Performance on Rare Structures:** The predictor struggles with rare environmental structures like stairs, due to insufficient training data for these specific scenarios. Addressing this by identifying and oversampling such data, or improving loss functions, is a future step.
- **Lack of Online Adjustment:** The agent fully trusts the predictor's output. Integrating online adjustment mechanisms, allowing the agent to adapt waypoints based on local observations or unexpected control outcomes, could enhance robustness.
- **Transferability to Out-of-Domain Scenes:** While shown generalizable to unseen MP3D environments, the CWP's transferability to entirely different (out-of-domain) scenes (e.g., new building styles, outdoor environments) remains an open question.
- **State-Conditioned Prediction:** Incorporating agent state information (e.g., navigation progress, landmarks from instructions) into the waypoint prediction process could allow the CWP to generate more goal-directed waypoints.

Overall, this paper presents a compelling solution that not only pushes the state-of-the-art in continuous VLN but also offers a pragmatic and efficient approach to bridging a critical domain gap, making embodied AI research more accessible and relevant to real-world applications.
