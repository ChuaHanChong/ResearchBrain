# Grasp What You Want: Embodied Dexterous Grasping System Driven by Your Voice

> [!summary] Summary
> An Embodied Dexterous Grasping System (EDGS) was developed to enable robots to interpret human voice commands and visual data for performing object grasping in cluttered environments. This system integrates voice-driven semantic enrichment with a human-inspired grasp planning and optimization pipeline, achieving a 96.1% overall success rate across diverse real-world application scenarios.

## Problem

- Robots struggle to accurately interpret human intentions from imprecise voice commands for complex dexterous grasping tasks.
- Existing data-driven grasping methods suffer from high computational costs and significant sim-to-real transfer challenges, while analytical methods lack generalization for cluttered scenes and novel objects.
- Traditional robotic grippers often lack the advanced manipulation capabilities and adaptability required for natural human interaction and diverse tasks in unstructured environments.

## Method

- ==The Embodied Dexterous Grasping System (EDGS)== integrates multimodal instruction processing (voice and vision) with a human-inspired grasp planning and optimization framework for dexterous hands.
- A Referring Expression Representation Enrichment (RERE) method fuses voice commands with visual features (e.g., instance category, color, shape, material, position) to refine object descriptions and enhance semantic-object alignment in cluttered scenes.
- Dexterous Grasp Candidates Generation (DGCG) extracts object features (skeleton line, characteristic centroid) and combines them with a hand workspace model to propose grasp poses, which are then refined by Dexterous Grasp Refinement (DGR) using force closure, grasp wrench space analysis, and cost-based movement selection (STOMP).

## Results

- The RERE method improved segmentation performance, with Grounded SAM showing a **19.5** IoU point increase (from **44.9** to **64.4**) when all enriched dimensions were incorporated.
- EDGS achieved a **100%** success rate on several diverse objects in single-object grasping and an **87.5%** success rate on challenging objects like Wooden Block and Tape, outperforming some state-of-the-art methods.
- The system demonstrated an overall **96.1%** success rate across 360 attempts in realistic cluttered environments for various object categories (fruits, households, vegetables), with a **95.5%** success rate in object-by-object grasping tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Fusing imprecise voice commands with detailed visual information via cross-modal alignment significantly improves object segmentation and disambiguates human intentions, particularly in complex or cluttered environments.
> - Deriving object feature vectors from a characteristic centroid and skeleton line, combined with human-inspired thumb-finger coordination and multi-finger wrapping principles, leads to robust and adaptable dexterous grasp candidates.
> - Evaluating grasp quality through force closure and grasp wrench space metrics, alongside optimizing movement paths to minimize effort, effectively filters and selects the most efficient and stable grasp actions for dexterous hands.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in this paper is situated within the broader context of human-robot collaboration (HRC) and Embodied Artificial Intelligence (AI), specifically addressing challenges in robotic dexterous grasping and natural language interaction. As robotics technology advances, the demand for more intuitive and effective collaboration between humans and robots has increased. This includes enabling robots to interpret complex human intentions conveyed through voice commands and to perform intricate manipulation tasks in unstructured, cluttered environments.

Current approaches to robotic grasping generally fall into two categories: data-driven methods and analytical approaches. Data-driven methods leverage large datasets to learn grasping strategies, offering generalization across various objects and scenes. However, these methods often face limitations such as high computational costs, scarcity of suitable datasets for dexterous hands, and difficulties in transferring policies from simulation to real-world scenarios (sim-to-real gap). Analytical methods, while precise in controlled settings, frequently struggle with vague human instructions and complex, cluttered scenes, and some require pre-existing CAD models, which restricts their applicability to novel objects. Traditional robotic grippers, such as parallel-jaw and suction systems, often lack the advanced manipulation capabilities and adaptability required for natural human interaction and diverse tasks in non-standardized environments.

This research addresses these gaps by proposing an integrated system that combines multimodal perception—specifically, voice commands fused with visual information via Vision-Language Models (VLMs)—with a human-inspired dexterous grasping strategy. By focusing on enhancing semantic-object alignment from imprecise natural language and developing robust grasping policies for multi-fingered hands, the paper contributes to the development of more capable and adaptive robotic systems for real-world deployment. The objective is to move beyond the limitations of existing methods, providing a system that can understand nuanced human instructions and execute complex dexterous grasps in dynamic and cluttered settings, thereby advancing the state of the art in embodied dexterous grasping and human-robot interaction.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an Embodied Dexterous Grasping System (EDGS) capable of interpreting human voice commands to perform object grasping in cluttered environments. The motivation stems from several limitations observed in current robotic systems designed for human-robot collaboration and dexterous manipulation.

#### 2.1. Key Objectives

1. **Enhance Semantic-Object Alignment from Voice Commands and Visual Data:** A central objective is to improve the robot's ability to accurately understand human intentions conveyed through imprecise natural language instructions. This involves fusing voice commands with visual information using Vision-Language Models (VLMs) to create a rich, multi-dimensional representation of target objects, thereby reducing ambiguity in complex and cluttered scenes. This specific objective is addressed through the development of the Referring Expression Representation Enrichment (RERE) method.
2. **Develop a Robust and Efficient Dexterous Grasping Strategy:** The research aims to design a grasping strategy that is both precise and efficient, inspired by human hand-object interactions. This involves incorporating principles such as thumb-object axis coordination, multi-finger wrapping, and fingertip interaction with object contact mechanics to achieve stable and adaptable grasps. This objective is fulfilled by the Dexterous Grasp Candidates Generation (DGCG) and Dexterous Grasp Refinement (DGR) modules.
3. **Achieve High Grasp Success Rates in Cluttered Environments:** The system is intended to demonstrate stable and high success rates when executing complex grasping tasks in real-world, unstructured, and cluttered environments, moving beyond the limitations of controlled settings or reliance on pre-defined object models.
4. **Overcome Limitations of Current Grasping Solutions:** The motivation includes addressing the computational costs and sim-to-real transfer challenges of data-driven methods, as well as the inability of analytical methods to accommodate vague human instructions and generalize to objects without CAD models. The goal is to provide a more practical and deployable solution for dexterous grasping.

#### 2.2. Motivation

- **Natural Human-Robot Interaction:** Current robots often struggle to interpret human intentions from voice commands alone, leading to unnatural and inefficient interactions. The research aims to bridge this gap by enabling more direct and intuitive communication.
- **Dexterity in Unstructured Environments:** Traditional grippers frequently lack the adaptability and manipulation capabilities required for diverse tasks in cluttered and unstructured settings. Human-inspired dexterous grasping is sought to enhance the robot's ability to interact with varied objects.
- **Ambiguity in Language:** Natural language instructions can be vague or incomplete. The research is motivated by the human ability to use visual cues to resolve such ambiguities, applying this concept to robotic perception to refine object descriptions.
- **Computational Efficiency and Real-world Deployment:** Many existing data-driven dexterous grasping methods are computationally intensive or suffer from a significant sim-to-real gap. The paper aims to develop a system that is robust, efficient, and suitable for real-world deployment without extensive pre-training on specific datasets for dexterous hands or reliance on perfect simulation fidelity.
- **Adaptive Grasping:** Humans instinctively adapt their grasp based on object properties and context. The paper is motivated by replicating this adaptability, leveraging object features and a robust grasp policy to ensure stable and precise grasps.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The Embodied Dexterous Grasping System (EDGS) is structured as a modular framework integrating multimodal instruction processing with a grasp planning and optimization pipeline, designed for dexterous robotic hands. The system is comprised of three primary modules: Enriched Representation Guided Segmentation (ERGS), Dexterous Grasp Candidates Generation (DGCG), and Dexterous Grasp Refinement (DGR).

#### 3.1. Enriched Representation Guided Segmentation (ERGS)

The ERGS module aims to enhance object recognition in complex environments by integrating textual descriptions with visual information. It features two main components:

- **Voice-Driven Referring Expression Acquisition:** The process begins with acquiring an initial description of the target object via speech commands. To mitigate noise and linguistic ambiguity inherent in speech, an adaptive audio capture method is employed. This method dynamically sets a signal energy threshold `E(t)`, triggering capture when the energy exceeds the threshold and ceasing when it falls below for an extended period. The transcribed initial text, `T_orig`, often vague, then undergoes further refinement.
- **Referring Expression Representation Enrichment (RERE):** RERE refines `T_orig` by fusing it with visual input `I` (RGB-D data) through a cross-modal alignment mechanism. If alignment is deemed insufficient, the system prompts for clarification. A rule-based system semantically enriches the text based on visual features across key dimensions:
 - **Instance Category:** Identifies the object type from visual cues to ensure consistency with the scene, particularly in multi-object environments.
 - **Color and Shape:** Incorporates extracted color and geometric features for a more accurate visual description.
 - **Material and Texture:** Identifies unique surface characteristics (e.g., metal, glass) for object differentiation.
 - **Position:** Integrates spatial location (e.g., "on the left," "at the center") to further specify the object's whereabouts.
 The final enriched text description, `T_final`, is generated by a mapping function `f(·)` that combines `T_orig`, cross-modal alignment `A(T_orig, I)`, visual features `V_feat(I)` (color, shape, material, position), contextual cues `C(T_orig, I)`, and weight parameters `w_C`, `w_S`, `w_M`, `w_P`. For vague inputs, RERE leverages contextual information to optimize descriptions. This process emphasizes dynamic multimodal information fusion, adjusting feature weights based on scene requirements to select salient features.

#### 3.2. Dexterous Grasp Candidates Generation (DGCG)

Given a segmented point cloud, DGCG generates a set of potential grasp actions. This module comprises:

- **Object Feature Extraction:** Inspired by human grasping behavior, the system processes RGB-D data to determine a characteristic centroid and feature vector. Unlike methods relying solely on Principal Component Analysis (PCA) axes which may be limited for unsymmetrical objects, the approach extracts the object skeleton line using a thinning algorithm. The characteristic centroid `C*` is defined as the point on the skeleton closest to the pixel centroid `C`. The object feature vector `V` is then derived from the tangential line at `C*` in the X-Y plane and the eigenvector `v_1` corresponding to the largest eigenvalue of the PCA, assigned at `C*`.
- **Grasp Affordance and Candidates Generation:** This step evaluates grasp feasibility by integrating the object feature vector with the hand configuration. Each finger's workspace is modeled as a truncated spline, while the thumb's workspace is a planar space. The total hand workspace `W_total` is the union of these. A fixed pinching axis `D` within the hand's workspace, inspired by human thumb-finger coordination, is aligned with the object feature vector `V`. The hand's workspace is projected to compute intersections `S_i` with the object surface `O`.
 A grasp affordance function `A(S_i, V, μ)` quantifies the probability of a successful grasp, where `μ` is the frictional coefficient provided by a VLM. The affordance score `K_o` defines the range of a Gaussian distribution `G(m)` used to sample grasp poses `m ∈ R^6`, capturing both spatial and rotational aspects. Inverse kinematics is then applied to determine the hand joint space configuration `h_i ∈ R^6` for each sampled grasp, forming the set of action candidates `{A_i}` by concatenating `{M_i}` and `{H_i}`.

#### 3.3. Dexterous Grasp Refinement (DGR)

The DGR module filters and optimizes the generated grasp candidates `{A_i}` to determine the best action `A_best`.

- **Grasp Quality Evaluation:** Grasp candidates are evaluated based on Force Closure and Grasp Wrench Space (GWS). The frictional coefficient `μ_i` is obtained from VLM-informed texture classification and a priori tables. Force Closure determines if a grasp pose can resist external forces through stable contact points. Grasps satisfying force closure proceed to GWS analysis, which quantitatively assesses grasp quality by measuring resistance to external forces and moments. GWS is defined as the convex hull of frictional forces and pressures. The radius `q` of the smallest 6D sphere enclosing the GWS quantifies the grasp’s disturbance resistance. The top three grasps `{A_i}` with the highest `q` values are selected.
- **Cost-based Movement Selection:** Inspired by the human tendency to minimize effort, the Stochastic Trajectory Optimization for Motion Planning (STOMP) method is employed to optimize paths. Path constraints are incorporated, including pose constraints (end-effector alignment with target), joint constraints (permissible joint limits), and dynamic obstacle avoidance (using MoveIt). The optimal grasp `A_best` is selected by minimizing the sum of squared 6D joint differences `Σ(||ΔJ_i,k||^2)` calculated from inverse kinematics, representing the shortest and most efficient path.

#### 3.4. Experimental Setup

The experimental setup consisted of a single UR5 robotic arm equipped with an Inspire dexterous hand. An RGB-D camera mounted on the upper frame captures visual and depth data. The system operates without pre-provided CAD models, focusing on grasping diverse, unseen household items in real-world scenarios.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents experimental evaluations of the EDGS system across three main areas: instruction semantic enrichment, grasp success rates, and real-world application scenarios.

#### 4.1. Instruction Semantic Enrichment Experiment (RERE)

The effectiveness of the RERE method was evaluated using the GraspNet-1Billion dataset with three segmentation models: Grounded SAM, SEEM, and Florence-2. The results demonstrated an improvement in segmentation performance, measured by mean Intersection over Union (mIoU), when RERE was applied.

- **Overall Performance:** Improvements in IoU ranged from 5.0 to 25.7 points across the tested models. Grounded SAM exhibited the most substantial enhancement, with its IoU score increasing from 44.9 to 64.4, an improvement of 19.5 points. SEEM and Florence-2 showed improvements of 12.2 and 7.3 points, respectively.
- **Ablation Study:** An ablation study on RERE dimensions (Instance Type, Texture, Shape, Material, Relative Position) confirmed that the inclusion of additional dimensions systematically improved segmentation accuracy. The highest IoU (64.4) was achieved when all enriched dimensions were incorporated, suggesting that a comprehensive set of features is most effective.
- **Challenges and Errors:** Identified challenges included cases where original referring expressions were unconventional or outside the model's knowledge base, leading to difficult modality alignment. In scenarios with occluded objects, RERE sometimes favored identifying similar, complete objects, resulting in misclassification or incomplete segmentation. Common segmentation errors observed were class confusion, boundary inaccuracies, object merging, and false negatives.

#### 4.2. Grasp Success Rate Experiment

The grasping capabilities of EDGS were quantitatively analyzed in both single-object and object-by-object grasping tasks.

- **Single Object Grasping:** EDGS was evaluated on eleven diverse objects. The system demonstrated competitive performance, achieving a 100% success rate on several objects, including Pringles, Cleaner, Mustard Bottle, Cup, and Pitcher. For challenging objects like Wooden Block and Tape, EDGS achieved an 87.5% success rate, outperforming some other methods such as DexDiffuser, ISAGrasp, and Matak. These results indicate the system's robustness across a variety of objects and poses.
- **Object-by-Object Grasping:** In scenarios where objects were sequentially removed from a cluttered scene, EDGS achieved a 95.5% success rate. This performance exceeded that of other state-of-the-art approaches, including DexGraspNet 2.0 (90.7%), ISAGrasp (54.8%), HGCNet (16.4%), and GraspTTA (6.0%). This suggests EDGS's efficiency and adaptability in multi-object, cluttered environments.

#### 4.3. Application Scenario Experiments

EDGS was tested in realistic, cluttered environments using objects from three categories: fruits, households, and vegetables.

- **Performance in Clutter:** The system processed voice commands and integrated auditory and visual cues to make grasping decisions. It maintained high success rates across diverse object types and cluttered scenarios. The highest success rate was observed in the fruits category (98.3%), followed by vegetables (95.8%) and household items (94.2%). The overall success rate across 360 attempts was 96.1%.
- **Error Analysis:** Primary causes of grasping failures included: 1) **Object Shape Complexity:** Irregularly shaped objects, particularly in the household category, led to higher failure rates. 2) **Environmental Clutter:** Dense object arrangements sometimes hindered accurate identification and segmentation. 3) **Dynamic Interaction:** The system occasionally struggled with objects requiring delicate handling, resulting in slips during the transfer phase.

#### 4.4. Limitations and Discussion

The paper acknowledges several limitations of the current EDGS system:
- **Sensory Integration:** The absence of human-level multimodal sensory integration limits the system's ability to accurately identify and segment densely cluttered and overlapping objects.
- **Semantic Mapping:** Current segmentation methods delineate object boundaries but lack structural-to-functional semantic mappings, hindering nuanced manipulation.
- **Haptic Sensing and Dexterity:** The lack of fine-grained haptic sensing components restricts dynamic adaptability to complex scenarios. Furthermore, the limited degrees of freedom of the hand may constrain the solution space for high-quality grasp poses and in-hand re-orientation capabilities.
- **Single-Hand/Arm Limitations:** The single-hand, single-arm configuration prevents achieving force closure for larger objects that would typically require coordinated dual-hand or dual-arm actions.

Despite these limitations, the system is presented as a cohesively integrated hand-eye-brain embodied humanoid system, enabling modular deployment across various robotic platforms and diverse application scenarios.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Embodied Dexterous Grasping System (EDGS) contributes to the field of robotics by addressing challenges in human-robot collaboration, particularly in the context of dexterous manipulation driven by natural language commands.

**Key areas of significance and potential impact include:**

1. **Enhanced Human-Robot Interaction:** By enabling robots to interpret human intentions from voice commands and fuse them with visual information, EDGS facilitates more intuitive and direct communication. This could make robotic systems more accessible and user-friendly for non-expert operators, fostering more seamless collaboration in various settings.
2. **Advancement in Embodied AI and Dexterous Grasping:** The system integrates multimodal perception (VLM-based RERE) with human-inspired grasping strategies (DGCG and DGR) in a cohesive framework. This integration represents an advancement in embodied AI by demonstrating a robot's ability to perceive, reason, and act in complex physical environments, specifically focusing on dexterous manipulation. The robust grasping policy, particularly its performance in cluttered scenes and against other state-of-the-art methods, indicates progress in this domain.
3. **Improved Performance in Unstructured Environments:** EDGS's demonstrated high success rates in complex, cluttered, and unstructured real-world scenarios address a significant challenge for robotic systems. The ability to handle diverse objects without pre-provided CAD models and adapt to varying arrangements makes it more practical for real-world deployment compared to systems limited to controlled environments or requiring extensive pre-training.
4. **Practical Applications and Automation:** The system's performance in application scenarios with fruits, household items, and vegetables suggests its potential for various industries. This includes automation in manufacturing, logistics, and service robotics where dexterous manipulation and intelligent interaction with a wide range of objects are required. It could also function as a versatile robotic assistant in domestic or specialized industrial tasks.
5. **Foundation for Future Research:** The paper identifies and discusses current limitations, such as the need for human-level multimodal sensory integration, structural-to-functional semantic mappings, fine-grained haptic sensing, and multi-hand/arm coordination. These insights provide clear directions for future research, motivating further development in areas critical for bridging the gap between current robotic capabilities and human dexterity.

Overall, this work proposes a modular and effective framework that combines advanced perception with sophisticated grasping, potentially increasing the versatility and deployment potential of intelligent robotic systems in everyday applications.
