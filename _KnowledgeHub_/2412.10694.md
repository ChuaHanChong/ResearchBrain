# Grasp What You Want: Embodied Dexterous Grasping System Driven by Your Voice

> [!summary] Summary
> An Embodied Dexterous Grasping System (EDGS) was developed to enable robots to interpret human voice commands and visual data for performing object grasping in cluttered environments. This system integrates voice-driven semantic enrichment with a human-inspired grasp planning and optimization pipeline, achieving a 96.1% overall success rate across diverse real-world application scenarios.

## Problem

- Robots struggle to accurately interpret human intentions from imprecise voice commands for complex dexterous grasping tasks.
- Existing data-driven grasping methods suffer from high computational costs and significant sim-to-real transfer challenges, while analytical methods lack generalization for cluttered scenes and novel objects.
- Traditional robotic grippers often lack the advanced manipulation capabilities and adaptability required for natural human interaction and diverse tasks in unstructured environments.

## Method

- ==The Embodied Dexterous Grasping System (EDGS)== integrates multimodal instruction processing (voice and vision) with a human-inspired grasp planning and optimization framework for dexterous hands.
- A Referring Expression Representation Enrichment (RERE) method fuses voice commands with visual features (e.g., instance category, color, shape, material, position) to refine object descriptions and enhance semantic-object alignment in cluttered scenes.
- Dexterous Grasp Candidates Generation (DGCG) extracts object features (skeleton line, characteristic centroid) and combines them with a hand workspace model to propose grasp poses, which are then refined by Dexterous Grasp Refinement (DGR) using force closure, grasp wrench space analysis, and cost-based movement selection (STOMP).

## Results

- The RERE method improved segmentation performance, with Grounded SAM showing a **19.5** IoU point increase (from **44.9** to **64.4**) when all enriched dimensions were incorporated.
- EDGS achieved a **100%** success rate on several diverse objects in single-object grasping and an **87.5%** success rate on challenging objects like Wooden Block and Tape, outperforming some state-of-the-art methods.
- The system demonstrated an overall **96.1%** success rate across 360 attempts in realistic cluttered environments for various object categories (fruits, households, vegetables), with a **95.5%** success rate in object-by-object grasping tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Fusing imprecise voice commands with detailed visual information via cross-modal alignment significantly improves object segmentation and disambiguates human intentions, particularly in complex or cluttered environments.
> - Deriving object feature vectors from a characteristic centroid and skeleton line, combined with human-inspired thumb-finger coordination and multi-finger wrapping principles, leads to robust and adaptable dexterous grasp candidates.
> - Evaluating grasp quality through force closure and grasp wrench space metrics, alongside optimizing movement paths to minimize effort, effectively filters and selects the most efficient and stable grasp actions for dexterous hands.
