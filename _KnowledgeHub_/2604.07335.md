# TAMEn: Tactile-Aware Manipulation Engine for Closed-Loop Data Collection in Contact-Rich Tasks

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers developed TAMEn, a tactile-aware manipulation engine that enhances closed-loop visuo-tactile data collection and policy learning for contact-rich bimanual tasks. The system demonstrated an average 75% task success rate by integrating tactile feedback, online feasibility validation, and an AR-based recovery mechanism.

## Problem

- Existing data collection systems often lack fine-grained tactile information critical for subtle contact events in bimanual manipulation.
- Current handheld interfaces for data collection face limitations in hardware adaptability across different grippers and struggle with the trade-off between tracking precision and portability.
- Demonstrations collected via handheld systems frequently contain unexecutable trajectories, leading to poor replayability and inefficient data acquisition without online feasibility checks.
- Lack of efficient methods to collect interactive recovery data with authentic tactile feedback during robot execution for refining policies in near-failure states.

## Method

- A ==modular wearable interface== adaptive to heterogeneous gripper morphologies, supporting dual-mode acquisition (sub-millimeter motion capture and VR-based portable tracking).
- A ==feasibility-aware data acquisition pipeline== with online validation checks for robot executability, plus an ==AR-based teleoperation system (tAmeR)== with tactile feedback for recovery data collection.
- A ==pyramid-structured data regime== for staged policy learning: large-scale tactile pretraining, task-specific bimanual imitation, and closed-loop recovery-based refinement.

## Results

- TAMEn's object-based tracking achieved **100%** success rate for both novice and experienced users, outperforming marker-only tracking (**32–78%**).
- Online feasibility validation enabled **100%** replay success of collected demonstrations, a substantial improvement over **12–39%** without validation.
- The full **TAMEn** system with tactile pretraining and online recovery achieved an average **75%** task success rate across four contact-rich bimanual tasks, demonstrating improved generalization to unseen objects and robustness under visual disturbances.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Tactile sensing substantially improves manipulation policy performance in contact-rich tasks, with tactile pretraining further enhancing the handling of stage-dependent tactile variations.
> - Implementing online feasibility validation during data collection significantly improves the replayability of demonstrations, ensuring all collected data is executable on the robot.
> - Integrating closed-loop online recovery data collected from policy-induced failures proves to be a data-efficient method for policy refinement and robustness compared to simply adding more nominal demonstrations.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Robotic manipulation, particularly in contact-rich and bimanual tasks, necessitates advanced data collection methods. Current approaches often face limitations in acquiring high-quality, large-scale datasets that are crucial for training robust policies. Teleoperation systems, while offering precise control, typically rely on visual feedback, thereby lacking fine-grained tactile information critical for tasks involving subtle contact events such as force changes or incipient slip. This limitation is particularly evident in bimanual manipulation, where operators may struggle to interpret contact states, leading to reduced data collection efficiency and ambiguous supervision for policy learning.

Handheld paradigms, such as UMI-style systems, have emerged as a more efficient and intuitive alternative for collecting large-scale demonstrations. While some recent handheld interfaces have integrated tactile sensors, they often present their own set of challenges. These include hardware designs that are typically specific to particular end-effectors, impeding transferability to grippers with differing kinematics or geometries. Furthermore, existing systems frequently encounter a trade-off between tracking precision, which is often reliant on external motion capture systems, and portability, which favors more flexible, in-the-wild deployment. Another significant limitation is the lack of online feasibility checking during demonstration, which can result in collected trajectories that are not reliably executable on a robot, necessitating extensive offline filtering. Crucially, these setups generally do not facilitate the collection of interactive recovery data during actual robot execution, meaning they miss authentic tactile information vital for refining policies in near-failure states.

This research introduces TAMEn as a system designed to address these gaps. It aims to advance the capabilities of handheld interfaces by offering solutions for hardware adaptability, resolving the precision-portability trade-off, enabling online feasibility validation, and facilitating closed-loop collection of recovery data with tactile feedback. By doing so, it contributes to the development of more robust, generalizable, and efficient methods for visuo-tactile data acquisition and policy learning in complex bimanual manipulation scenarios.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The motivation for TAMEn arises from several identified deficiencies in current robot manipulation data collection methodologies, particularly for contact-rich bimanual tasks:

- **Challenge of Contact-Rich Bimanual Manipulation:** Physical interaction, often involving two end-effectors coordinating through shared objects under dynamic force and deformation, is fundamental to many tasks. Subtle contact events (onset, excessive loading, incipient slip) are crucial for success but are difficult to infer from vision alone. Existing data collection systems often do not adequately capture or leverage this fine-grained tactile information.
- **Hardware Adaptability and Precision-Portability Trade-off:** Many existing hardware designs for data collection are gripper-specific, limiting their applicability across heterogeneous robot grippers. Additionally, there is a persistent trade-off between high-precision tracking (e.g., motion capture) which lacks portability, and portable tracking (e.g., VR-based) which may compromise accuracy for contact-rich tasks. This restricts the quality and diversity of collected data.
- **Poor Replayability of Demonstrations:** Handheld demonstrations, especially for complex bimanual motions, often contain trajectories that violate robot-specific constraints (e.g., inverse kinematics, joint limits, workspace). The absence of online feasibility checking during the collection process means these infeasible demonstrations are only identified and filtered post-hoc, leading to inefficient data acquisition and increased manual effort.
- **Lack of Recovery Data Collection with Authentic Tactile Information:** Successful nominal demonstrations alone are insufficient for robustly handling near-failure states, which are common in real-world contact-rich manipulation. Existing handheld setups struggle to collect interactive recovery data during robot execution, and typically lack the authentic tactile feedback necessary for human operators to provide effective corrective actions that reflect realistic failure scenarios.

To address these motivations, the research establishes the following key objectives:

1. **Develop a Visuo-Tactile Data Engine:** To create a closed-loop system for efficient visuo-tactile data collection in bimanual contact-rich tasks, encompassing hardware, acquisition strategy, and policy learning.
2. **Design an Adaptive Human-Machine Interface:** To engineer a wearable interface that supports a dual-mode acquisition pipeline (sub-millimeter motion capture and VR-based in-the-wild acquisition) and can rapidly adapt to heterogeneous gripper morphologies. This aims to balance data quality, environmental diversity, and hardware versatility.
3. **Propose a Feasibility-Aware Data Collection Recipe:** To integrate real-time validation during data collection to ensure demonstrations are executable on the target robot. Additionally, to organize heterogeneous multimodal data into a pyramid-structured regime for staged policy learning, improving data efficacy.
4. **Establish a Closed-Loop Data Flywheel:** To leverage an AR-based teleoperation system with tactile feedback (tAmeR) for collecting recovery data during robot execution. This aims to refine policies using corrective data gathered from realistic failure states, enabling continuous policy improvement.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The TAMEn system integrates a human-machine interface, a data acquisition pipeline, and a policy learning framework to facilitate closed-loop visuo-tactile learning in contact-rich bimanual manipulation.

**System Overview**
The system is designed to perform three functions: efficient multimodal data collection balancing precision and portability, ensuring data executability on a robot and organizing it for staged learning, and supporting closed-loop policy refinement through AR-based teleoperation with tactile feedback. It operates in two data collection modes—a precision mode and a portable mode—recording synchronized visual, tactile, and motion data. Collected motions undergo online feasibility checks for robot executability. For recovery, an AR application, tAmeR, provides real-time visual and tactile feedback for teleoperation during policy execution. The diverse data sources are structured into a pyramid for representation pretraining, task-specific bimanual learning, and recovery-oriented refinement.

**Hardware Design**
The core of TAMEn is a modular wearable interface built around a rigid–soft structure that couples the operator's thumb and index finger to enable natural manipulation. An inverted crank-slider mechanism translates finger motion into synchronized gripper actuation while maintaining direct contact at the fingertip. This design supports modular fingertip sensing modules, allowing various visuo-tactile sensors (e.g., GelSight, Xense, DW-Tac, PaXini, and the authors' custom sensor) to be integrated with minimal modification.

The system incorporates interchangeable tracking attachments for dual-mode acquisition:
- **Motion-capture mode:** Utilizes a NOKOV system for sub-millimeter pose tracking. Markers are arranged in a structured layout to enhance visibility and track gripper opening.
- **Portable mode:** Replaces the marker assembly with a quick-detachable VR handle, facilitating in-the-wild data collection and recovery-oriented teleoperation. This mode offers a lower-cost solution for rapid deployment.

The interface is designed for adaptation across different gripper morphologies (e.g., flexion-extension and parallel-jaw grippers) by adjusting a small set of geometric parameters. This standardization aims to simplify deployment across heterogeneous robot grippers without requiring instance-specific redesign. Kinematic equations are provided for flexion-extension grippers to show how jaw opening width and fingertip displacement are parameterized by slider motion, and for parallel-jaw grippers, by crank and linkage lengths.

**Feasibility-Aware Data Acquisition Pipeline**
To enhance data quality and robot executability, TAMEn implements:
- **Structured motion capture tracking:** Each handheld interface is represented as a structured marker object with predefined geometric topology. This allows the tracking system to exploit structural consistency for robust pose estimation and marker recovery even under partial occlusion or noisy conditions, improving stability over marker-only tracking.
- **Feasibility validation for robot execution:** During data collection, recorded gripper poses are simultaneously mapped to target robot poses and checked online for executability. This validation encompasses inverse-kinematics solutions, workspace and joint soft limits, and motion constraints like joint-speed and TCP-speed bounds. Infeasible motions trigger real-time feedback to the operator, enabling immediate correction and improving data validity.
- **AR-based recovery mode (tAmeR):** An AR application, implemented on a Pico 4 Ultra headset, provides operators with real-time visual (wrist-view RGB fisheye) and tactile feedback. This immersive teleoperation system enables the collection of recovery-oriented data by human intervention during policy execution, focusing on realistic failure states.

**Pyramid-Structured Data Regime**
TAMEn organizes data into a three-layered pyramid for staged policy training:
- **Base Layer:** Consists of large-scale single-arm visuo-tactile data (e.g., FreeTacMan dataset). This data is used to pretrain tactile representations, providing broad priors over contact dynamics.
- **Middle Layer:** Contains task-specific bimanual demonstrations. These demonstrations build upon the tactile priors to learn coordinated manipulation behaviors.
- **Top Layer:** Comprises recovery data collected from policy-induced failure cases. This layer is designed to improve policy robustness by addressing specific failure modes.

**Closed-Loop Policy Learning**
Policy learning is formulated as a closed-loop visuo-tactile problem, trained in three coupled stages corresponding to the data pyramid layers:
1. **Tactile Representation Pretraining:** The tactile branch is initialized by pretraining tactile representations on the large-scale single-arm dataset using a contrastive objective (Equation 5), defining positive visual sets from aligned current and next-timestep visual embeddings.
2. **Task-Specific Bimanual Imitation Learning:** A downstream ACT-style transformer policy, taking dual wrist-mounted fisheye RGB and tactile observations, is trained on task-specific bimanual demonstrations using a supervised action loss (Equation 6). The policy predicts a 16-dimensional action vector for dual-arm joint commands and gripper actions.
3. **Recovery-Based Refinement:** After initial training, the policy is deployed, and human corrections collected via tAmeR during failure-prone states are added to a recovery dataset. These corrected trajectories are integrated into subsequent policy updates using a DAgger-style loop, allowing the policy to adapt to its own execution's state distribution.

The policy architecture employs ResNet-18 backbones for visual and tactile encoding, projecting features into a shared latent space before fusion and transformer-based action prediction (4-layer encoder, 7-layer decoder). Training uses a learning rate of 1x10^-5 and a KL weight of 10, performed on trajectory sequences.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments evaluated TAMEn across four contact-rich bimanual tasks: herbal transfer, cable mounting, binder clip removal, and dish washing, using a dual-arm JAKA K1 robot platform.

**Data Acquisition System Efficiency and Quality (Q1)**
- **Tracking Robustness:** Comparative tests on the dish washing task demonstrated that object-based tracking, utilized by TAMEn, achieved a 100% success rate for both novice and experienced users. This contrasts with marker-only tracking, which yielded success rates of 32% for novice users and 78% for experienced users, indicating its susceptibility to occlusions and disturbances.
- **Tracking Accuracy:** Trajectory error analysis, with motion capture as a reference, showed that VR-based tracking maintained errors within 1 cm. This indicates an improved balance between portability and stability compared to SLAM-based methods, which exhibited larger errors in low-feature environments.
- **Data Validity:** Online feasibility validation significantly improved the replay success rate of collected demonstrations. Without this validation, only 39% of herbal transfer and 12% of cable mounting demonstrations were replayable. With online validation, 100% replay success was achieved for both tasks, demonstrating the system's ability to filter infeasible motions during collection.

**Impact of Tactile Sensing (Q2)**
- Incorporating tactile sensing into the policy (Ours + Tactile w/o Pretrain) improved the average task success rate from 34% (Vision-Only ACT) to 55% across the tested tasks. This improvement was particularly noted in tasks such as cable mounting, where tactile feedback assisted in detecting cable slippage during grasping and confirming successful seating, and in dish washing, for maintaining stable contact between the sponge and dish.

**Impact of Pretraining and Recovery Data (Q3)**
- **Tactile Pretraining:** Pretraining the tactile encoder on a large-scale single-arm dataset (Ours + Pretrain) further increased the average success rate from 55% to 65%. This gain was observed in tasks like herbal transfer, where it aided in stable support of deformable objects, and binder clip removal, where it contributed to maintaining a stable grasp on a spring-loaded clip. These results suggest that tactile pretraining enhanced the policy's ability to manage stage-dependent tactile variations.
- **Recovery Data:** The integration of online recovery data through a DAgger-style update loop (Ours + Pretrain + 10% Online Recovery) elevated the average success rate from 65% to 75%. This effect was shown to be more efficient than simply adding 50% more nominal demonstrations (which achieved 70% success) and more effective than using 10% offline recovery data (which yielded 56% success). Online recovery, addressing policy-induced failures directly, proved to be a data-efficient method for policy refinement.
- **Generalization to Unseen Objects:** When evaluated on objects with altered appearances, TAMEn (+ Pretrain + DAgger) demonstrated improved generalization. For instance, in herbal transfer, success increased from 0% (vision-only) to 60%, and in binder clip removal, from 30% to 60%. This indicates that the visuo-tactile cues and pretraining aided in reliance on underlying task structure rather than overfitting to specific visual textures.
- **Robustness under External Disturbances:** TAMEn also showed improved robustness under lighting disturbances. In the "post-grasp disturbance" setting (where visual perturbation occurred after grasping), TAMEn (+ Pretrain + DAgger) achieved a 70% success rate in herbal transfer and 40% in cable mounting, while the vision-only policy largely failed. This suggests that tactile pretraining and recovery data contribute to the policy's ability to compensate for degraded visual perception during contact-rich execution.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The TAMEn system addresses several critical limitations in data collection and policy learning for robotic manipulation, particularly in contact-rich bimanual tasks. Its contributions hold potential significance across various dimensions:

- **Enhanced Data Quality and Efficiency:** By integrating online feasibility validation, TAMEn significantly reduces the collection of unexecutable demonstrations, thereby improving data quality and collection efficiency. This streamlines the process of generating datasets for robot learning, reducing the manual effort associated with post-hoc filtering.
- **Versatile Hardware and Data Acquisition:** The modular hardware design allows for rapid adaptation across heterogeneous grippers and compatibility with various visuo-tactile sensors. The dual-mode acquisition pipeline, balancing sub-millimeter precision with portable, VR-based tracking, broadens the environments in which high-quality data can be collected. This versatility has the potential to make robot data collection more accessible and less resource-intensive.
- **Robustness through Multimodal Learning and Closed-Loop Refinement:** The pyramid-structured data regime systematically leverages large-scale tactile priors, task-specific bimanual demonstrations, and critical recovery data. This staged learning approach, combined with the closed-loop recovery mechanism (tAmeR) that incorporates human feedback from real-world policy failures, contributes to the development of more robust and resilient manipulation policies. The demonstrated improvements in generalization to unseen objects and robustness under visual disturbances indicate a step towards more deployable robotic systems.
- **Advancement in Contact-Rich Manipulation:** By emphasizing visuo-tactile sensing and its integration into learning frameworks, TAMEn specifically targets the challenges of contact-rich manipulation. The findings highlight the critical role of tactile information, especially when visual cues are ambiguous or compromised. This work can inform future research and development of robots capable of intricate physical interactions required in fields such as manufacturing, assembly, and domestic assistance.
- **Open-Source Contribution:** The commitment to open-sourcing the hardware and dataset has the potential to foster reproducibility and accelerate research in the visuo-tactile manipulation community. This can lower the barrier for other researchers to build upon and extend the presented work.

Future work includes extending the framework to dexterous hands for finer-grained manipulation and evaluating cross-sensor generalization, which could further broaden the applicability and impact of such tactile-aware systems.
