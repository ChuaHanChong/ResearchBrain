# TAMEn: Tactile-Aware Manipulation Engine for Closed-Loop Data Collection in Contact-Rich Tasks

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers developed TAMEn, a tactile-aware manipulation engine that enhances closed-loop visuo-tactile data collection and policy learning for contact-rich bimanual tasks. The system demonstrated an average 75% task success rate by integrating tactile feedback, online feasibility validation, and an AR-based recovery mechanism.

## Problem

- Existing data collection systems often lack fine-grained tactile information critical for subtle contact events in bimanual manipulation.
- Current handheld interfaces for data collection face limitations in hardware adaptability across different grippers and struggle with the trade-off between tracking precision and portability.
- Demonstrations collected via handheld systems frequently contain unexecutable trajectories, leading to poor replayability and inefficient data acquisition without online feasibility checks.
- Lack of efficient methods to collect interactive recovery data with authentic tactile feedback during robot execution for refining policies in near-failure states.

## Method

- A ==modular wearable interface== adaptive to heterogeneous gripper morphologies, supporting dual-mode acquisition (sub-millimeter motion capture and VR-based portable tracking).
- A ==feasibility-aware data acquisition pipeline== with online validation checks for robot executability, plus an ==AR-based teleoperation system (tAmeR)== with tactile feedback for recovery data collection.
- A ==pyramid-structured data regime== for staged policy learning: large-scale tactile pretraining, task-specific bimanual imitation, and closed-loop recovery-based refinement.

## Results

- TAMEn's object-based tracking achieved **100%** success rate for both novice and experienced users, outperforming marker-only tracking (**32–78%**).
- Online feasibility validation enabled **100%** replay success of collected demonstrations, a substantial improvement over **12–39%** without validation.
- The full **TAMEn** system with tactile pretraining and online recovery achieved an average **75%** task success rate across four contact-rich bimanual tasks, demonstrating improved generalization to unseen objects and robustness under visual disturbances.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Tactile sensing substantially improves manipulation policy performance in contact-rich tasks, with tactile pretraining further enhancing the handling of stage-dependent tactile variations.
> - Implementing online feasibility validation during data collection significantly improves the replayability of demonstrations, ensuring all collected data is executable on the robot.
> - Integrating closed-loop online recovery data collected from policy-induced failures proves to be a data-efficient method for policy refinement and robustness compared to simply adding more nominal demonstrations.
