# Does Reinforcement Learning Really Incentivize Reasoning Capacity in LLMs Beyond the Base Model?

> [!summary] Summary
> Research from Tsinghua University reveals that current Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) primarily enhances the sampling efficiency of Large Language Models for already existing reasoning patterns. This optimization often narrows the models' overall reasoning capacity at higher pass@k values, contrasting with distillation, which effectively expands their problem-solving scope.

## Problem

- The prevailing belief that RLVR enables LLMs to acquire novel reasoning abilities, similar to how traditional RL discovers new strategies.
- A lack of rigorous investigation into whether current RLVR truly expands the *scope* of problems an LLM can solve, or merely optimizes the sampling of existing solutions.
- Traditional evaluation metrics often fail to accurately capture an LLM's full reasoning potential or its 'reasoning capability boundary'.

## Method

- Introduced `==pass@k==` as a robust evaluation metric to define an LLM's 'reasoning capability boundary' by measuring the proportion of problems solvable within *k* trials.
- Systematically evaluated reasoning capabilities across diverse LLM families, sizes, multiple RL algorithms (e.g., PPO, GRPO), and various task domains (mathematics, code, visual reasoning).
- Employed deep analytical techniques, including perplexity analysis and solvable-problem coverage, to determine if RLVR-generated reasoning paths are novel or already within the base model's distribution.

## Results

- RLVR-trained models consistently show improved `pass@1` but decreased `pass@k` for larger `k` (e.g., `k=**128**`, `k=**256**`) compared to their base counterparts across all tested benchmarks.
- Perplexity analysis confirmed that reasoning paths generated by RLVR models are largely 'in-distribution' for the base models, suggesting no fundamental discovery of novel reasoning.
- The 'Sampling Efficiency Gap' was found to be consistently large and similar across various RL algorithms, indicating that current RLVR methods are far from optimally leveraging the base model's full potential.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Current RLVR primarily improves the *sampling efficiency* of existing reasoning patterns within an LLM, leading to higher `pass@1` accuracy.
> - RLVR often *narrows* the overall reasoning coverage of an LLMs, reducing its potential to solve problems when given multiple attempts (`pass@k` for larger `k`).
> - Distillation, unlike current RLVR, can genuinely expand a model's reasoning boundary by transferring and introducing new reasoning patterns from a stronger teacher model.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This paper directly addresses a fundamental and increasingly critical question within the rapidly evolving field of Large Language Models (LLMs) and their reasoning capabilities. Recent advancements have seen the emergence of highly capable reasoning-centric LLMs, such as OpenAI-o1, DeepSeek-R1, and Kimi-1.5, which excel in complex logical tasks, particularly in mathematics and programming. A key innovation driving these models forward is **Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR)**. This paradigm involves fine-tuning pretrained LLMs using automatically computable rewards (e.g., correct mathematical answers, passing unit tests for code), a method lauded for its scalability and efficiency in overcoming the limitations of human-curated instruction tuning.

Inspired by the success of traditional Reinforcement Learning (RL) in enabling agents (like AlphaGo or those playing Atari games) to discover novel strategies and surpass human performance through continuous self-improvement, it has become a widely held belief that RLVR similarly empowers LLMs to autonomously develop *new and emergent reasoning patterns*. This optimistic view positions RLVR as a promising pathway toward continuously self-evolving LLMs and, consequently, more powerful artificial general intelligence.

This paper, however, takes a critical stance against this prevailing narrative. It systematically probes whether current RLVR truly leads to the acquisition of *novel* reasoning abilities or merely optimizes the sampling efficiency of reasoning patterns *already inherent* in the base model. This investigation is crucial because it challenges a core assumption about RLVR's transformative potential. By using robust metrics like `pass@k` (which measures a model's *potential* to solve a problem with multiple attempts, rather than just its average performance), the authors aim to rigorously define the "reasoning capability boundary" of LLMs before and after RLVR training.

The work also contrasts RLVR with **distillation**, another method for enhancing model capabilities, arguing that distillation can genuinely introduce new reasoning patterns from a stronger teacher model, thereby expanding the student model's reasoning scope. This comparison further clarifies the specific mechanisms through which LLMs acquire and improve reasoning abilities. By systematically evaluating across diverse models, RL algorithms, and benchmarks, this study aims to provide a comprehensive and nuanced understanding of RLVR's actual impact, distinguishing between improved efficiency and genuine capability expansion. It builds upon earlier, more limited analyses that also questioned aspects of RLVR, pushing for a more rigorous and comprehensive examination of its fundamental effects.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to rigorously investigate a fundamental, yet underexamined, question regarding the effectiveness of current Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) in enhancing Large Language Models (LLMs): **Does current RLVR genuinely enable LLMs to acquire novel reasoning abilities, similar to how traditional RL discovers new strategies through exploration, or does it simply utilize reasoning patterns already present in the base model?**

This core question is motivated by several factors:

- **Prevailing Optimism vs. Empirical Scrutiny:** Despite the significant empirical success and widespread adoption of RLVR in improving LLM performance on complex tasks like mathematics and programming, the underlying mechanisms and true scope of its impact on *reasoning capacity* remain largely unscrutinized. There is a strong, yet potentially unchecked, belief that RLVR allows LLMs to "continuously self-improve" and develop "novel reasoning abilities."
- **Defining "Reasoning Capability Boundary":** Traditional evaluation metrics (e.g., greedy decoding, average nucleus sampling accuracy) primarily reflect average-case performance. These metrics risk underestimating a model's true potential if it can solve difficult problems with more attempts, even if it fails on the first few. To address this, the authors introduce a more robust evaluation metric, `pass@k`, which defines a model's "reasoning capability boundary" as the proportion of problems it can solve within *k* trials. This metric is essential for a rigorous assessment of whether RLVR *truly expands* the set of problems an LLM can solve.
- **Understanding the "Discovery" Aspect of RL:** In traditional RL, agents often autonomously discover entirely new strategies. The authors are motivated to determine if RLVR endows LLMs with this same capacity for *discovery*—generating reasoning paths that were previously non-existent or statistically improbable in the base model's output distribution. Without such exploration and discovery, RLVR's role might be limited to optimizing existing knowledge, which has different implications for AI development.
- **Guiding Future LLM Development:** A clear understanding of RLVR's true effects is critical for guiding future research and development of LLMs. If current RLVR methods are not achieving their purported goal of generating novel reasoning, then new paradigms and algorithmic improvements will be necessary to unlock the full potential of RL for LLM reasoning.

In essence, the research aims to move beyond anecdotal evidence and average performance metrics to provide a comprehensive, systematic, and quantitative analysis that clarifies whether RLVR genuinely broadens an LLM's intrinsic problem-solving capabilities or merely makes existing capabilities more accessible and efficient.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors adopted a comprehensive and systematic approach to investigate the reasoning capacity boundaries of LLMs before and after RLVR training.

#### 3.1. Evaluation Metric: Pass@k

The cornerstone of their methodology is the **`pass@k` metric**, extended from code generation tasks to all tasks with verifiable rewards.
- **Definition:** For a given problem, `pass@k` is 1 if at least one of *k* sampled outputs is correct; otherwise, it is 0. The average `pass@k` over a dataset reflects the proportion of problems a model can *potentially* solve within *k* trials, providing a robust measure of its reasoning capacity *coverage* or *boundary*.
- **Rationale:** Unlike traditional metrics (greedy decoding, average nucleus sampling) that reflect average-case performance, `pass@k` reveals whether a model possesses the *potential* to solve a problem, even if it requires multiple attempts. This is crucial for determining if RLVR introduces new capabilities.
- **Estimation:** An unbiased, low-variance estimator is used, where `n` samples (n ≥ k) are generated per problem, and `pass@k` is calculated based on the count of correct samples.
- **Validity for Math:** To address the "random guessing" issue in mathematical problems (where an incorrect Chain-of-Thought (CoT) might yield a correct final answer), the authors manually inspected CoTs for a subset of challenging problems, confirming that correct answers largely resulted from genuinely valid reasoning paths.

#### 3.2. Experimental Scope and Setup

The study systematically probed reasoning capabilities across a wide array of dimensions:
- **LLM Families & Sizes:** Qwen2.5 (7B/14B/32B base variants), LLaMA-3.1-8B, and preliminary experiments with the frontier Magistral-Medium-2506 API.
- **RL Algorithms:** PPO, GRPO, Reinforce++, RLOO, ReMax, and DAPO. These were often implemented using open-source RLVR frameworks like SimpleRLZoo, Oat-Zero, DAPO, Code-R1, EasyR1, and VeRL, allowing for comparison across different algorithmic approaches.
- **Task Domains & Benchmarks:**
 - **Mathematical Reasoning:** GSM8K, MATH500, Minerva, Olympiad, AIME24, AMC23.
 - **Code Generation:** LiveCodeBench, HumanEval+, MBPP+.
 - **Visual Reasoning:** MathVista-TestMini, MathVision-TestMini (focusing on math within visual contexts).
- **Training and Evaluation Protocol:**
 - **Zero-RL Setting:** For math tasks, RL was applied directly to pretrained base models to isolate the effect of RLVR without supervised fine-tuning (SFT). For coding and visual reasoning, instruction-tuned models were used as starting points, following common practice, to assess RLVR's effect on already fine-tuned models.
 - **Consistent Prompting:** The same zero-shot prompts (or default benchmark prompts) were used for both base and RLVR-trained models during evaluation, avoiding few-shot prompting for base models to ensure a fair comparison and eliminate potential confounding effects.
 - **Sampling Parameters:** A temperature of 0.6 and a top-p value of 0.95 were generally used, with a maximum generation of 16,384 tokens, allowing for diverse sampling.

#### 3.3. Deep Analysis Techniques

Beyond `pass@k` curves, the authors performed several deeper analyses:
- **Accuracy Distribution Analysis:** Histograms were used to visualize how the frequency of problems solved at different accuracy levels changed after RLVR training, revealing whether improvements stemmed from solving *new* problems or optimizing *existing* ones.
- **Solvable-Problem Coverage Analysis:** Direct comparison of the specific set of problems solved by the base model versus the RLVR model, identifying overlaps and unique successes/failures.
- **Perplexity Analysis:** Computed the perplexity of RLVR-generated responses under the base model's distribution. Lower perplexity indicates that the base model assigns a higher likelihood to these responses, suggesting they are already "in-distribution" for the base model, rather than truly novel. This was also tracked over training steps.
- **Comparison with Distillation:** A comparative study was conducted with a distilled model (DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B) to contrast its effect on reasoning boundaries with that of RLVR.
- **Ablation Studies:** Investigated the impact of various RL training components:
 - **Different RL Algorithms:** Quantified the "Sampling Efficiency Gap" (∆_SE_ = pass@1 of RL model - pass@k of base model, where k=256), to compare how effectively algorithms leveraged the base model's potential.
 - **Training Steps:** Analyzed the evolution of `pass@1` and `pass@256` over the course of RL training.
 - **Number of Rollouts (n):** Examined the effect of sampling more responses per prompt during training.
 - **KL Loss:** Evaluated the impact of the KL divergence penalty term, which constrains model deviation from the original policy.
 - **Entropy:** Assessed if reduced output entropy in RLVR models contributed to narrower reasoning boundaries by matching the RLVR model's entropy to the base model's.

This comprehensive methodological framework allowed the authors to not only observe trends but also delve into the underlying reasons for RLVR's observed behavior, providing a multi-faceted understanding of its actual impact on LLM reasoning.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The systematic investigation yielded several surprising and critical findings that challenge conventional wisdom about RLVR's role in LLM reasoning:

#### 4.1. RLVR Improves Sampling Efficiency but Narrows Reasoning Coverage

- **Initial Boost, Subsequent Decline:** While RLVR-trained models consistently outperform their base counterparts at small `k` (e.g., `pass@1`), indicating improved sampling efficiency for correct responses, this advantage reverses as `k` increases. At larger `k` values (e.g., `k=128`, `k=256`), base models consistently achieve higher `pass@k` scores across all benchmarks (math, coding, visual reasoning) and LLM families.
- **Reduced Scope:** This suggests that current RLVR training does not expand the scope of solvable problems; instead, it often *reduces* the overall reasoning coverage, making the model less likely to find a correct path given a sufficiently large number of samples, even if one existed in the base model's distribution.
- **Manual Validation:** Manual inspection of Chain-of-Thought (CoT) responses confirmed that base models could produce correct CoTs for many problems initially thought to be exclusive to RLVR models.

#### 4.2. Reasoning Paths Generated by RLVR Models Already Exist in Base Models

- **No New Problem Solving:** Accuracy distribution analysis revealed that RLVR increases the frequency of high accuracies (near 1.0) but also the frequency of zero accuracies. This indicates that performance gains at `pass@1` stem from more efficiently sampling solutions for *already solvable* problems, not from enabling the model to solve *new* problems.
- **Subset Relationship:** Solvable-problem coverage analysis showed that the set of problems solvable by RLVR models is almost entirely a subset of those solvable by the base models. Very few instances (e.g., ~1% in MATH500) were found where an RLVR model succeeded where the base model completely failed.
- **In-Distribution Responses:** Perplexity analysis demonstrated that the reasoning paths generated by RLVR models have low perplexity under the base model's distribution. This strongly suggests that these paths are already "in-distribution" for the base model, meaning RLVR mainly sharpens the existing probability distribution of correct responses rather than generating fundamentally novel ones. Perplexity of RL-generated responses under the base model also decreased as training progressed, reinforcing this observation.

#### 4.3. Current RLVR Algorithms Perform Similarly and Remain Far from Optimal

- **Consistent Sampling Efficiency Gap:** The "Sampling Efficiency Gap" (∆_SE_), defined as the difference between an RL model’s `pass@1` and the base model’s `pass@256` (as a proxy for its upper bound), showed only minor variations across six popular RL algorithms (PPO, GRPO, Reinforce++, RLOO, ReMax, DAPO).
- **Significant Room for Improvement:** This gap remained consistently large (e.g., above 40 points), highlighting that current RLVR methods are far from optimally leveraging the full potential of the base model's inherent capabilities.

#### 4.4. RLVR Training Progressively Narrows Reasoning Boundary

- As RLVR training advanced, `pass@1` on the training set improved, but `pass@256` (reasoning coverage) progressively decreased. This suggests that continuous RLVR training, while making the model more decisive on known good paths, comes at the cost of pruning less frequent, but potentially correct, reasoning branches.

#### 4.5. Distillation Genuinely Expands the Reasoning Boundary

- In stark contrast to RLVR, distillation from a stronger teacher model (e.g., DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B) consistently and significantly elevated the `pass@k` curve above that of the base model. This demonstrates that distillation *can* introduce new reasoning patterns and genuinely expand a model's reasoning scope beyond its initial capacity.

#### 4.6. Ablation Studies and Scaling

- Increasing the number of rollouts (`n`) during training slightly improved `pass@k` at higher `k` values, but did not alter the fundamental trend of the base model eventually outperforming the RLVR model.
- Including a KL divergence term reduced `pass@k` performance, suggesting that constraining deviation too much can be detrimental to exploration.
- While a decrease in output entropy contributes to the narrowing of the reasoning boundary, matching the RLVR model's entropy to the base model's did not reverse the observed trend, implying other factors are at play.
- Preliminary experiments with a large, frontier model (Magistral-Medium) showed similar `pass@k` trends, suggesting these conclusions may hold across scaling levels.

In summary, the paper provides strong evidence that current RLVR methods primarily optimize the *efficiency* of sampling existing reasoning patterns within the base model's distribution, rather than *discovering* or *generating* fundamentally new reasoning capabilities.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research paper carries significant implications for the understanding, development, and future direction of Large Language Models, particularly in the domain of reasoning.

#### 5.1. Reassessment of RLVR's Fundamental Role

The most profound impact of this work is its critical re-evaluation of Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR). By rigorously demonstrating that current RLVR primarily enhances the *sampling efficiency* of existing reasoning patterns rather than generating *novel* capabilities, the paper challenges a prevalent and optimistic belief within the AI community. This shifts the perception of RLVR from a mechanism for continuous self-evolution and discovery to one of distribution shaping and optimization within a predefined latent space. This distinction is crucial for understanding the true progress in LLM reasoning.

#### 5.2. Guiding Future Research in LLM Reasoning

By identifying a critical gap between the ideal goals of RL (discovery of new strategies) and the observed outcomes of current RLVR for LLMs, the paper provides a clear mandate for future research. It highlights that current RLVR methods have not yet fully realized RL's potential to elicit genuinely novel reasoning. This insight can prevent misdirected efforts and encourage a pivot towards more fundamental advancements.

#### 5.3. Call for New RL Paradigms for LLMs

The paper effectively underscores the limitations of current RL approaches when applied to the vast and complex action space of language generation. It implicitly argues that direct application of traditional RL methodologies to LLMs, without significant adaptation, may be inherently constrained by the pretrained prior. This leads to a strong call for new RL paradigms specifically tailored for LLMs, suggesting several promising directions:
- **Effective Exploration Mechanisms:** Beyond token-level sampling, exploration in high-level abstraction spaces (e.g., program-level evolution) might be necessary to discover truly out-of-distribution reasoning patterns.
- **Deliberate and Large-Scale Data Curation with Curriculum Learning:** A structured curriculum, starting with easier subproblems, could gradually expand the model's capabilities and provide more meaningful reward signals for complex tasks.
- **Fine-Grained Process Signals:** Moving beyond binary outcome rewards to incorporate intermediate process-level signals (e.g., correctness of individual reasoning steps) could significantly improve exploration efficiency and guide the model toward better solution trajectories.
- **Multi-Turn Agent Interaction (Agentic RL):** Shifting from single-turn responses to iterative refinement with environmental feedback, tool use, and active information gathering could enable LLMs to generate novel experiences and learn in a more dynamic, human-like manner. This aligns with the vision of an "era of experience" in AI.

#### 5.4. Reaffirming Distillation's Role

By contrasting RLVR's limitations with distillation's ability to genuinely expand reasoning scope, the paper implicitly reaffirms the value of knowledge transfer from stronger models. This provides a clear alternative or complementary strategy for improving smaller models' capabilities, especially if the goal is to introduce *new* reasoning abilities rather than just optimizing existing ones.

#### 5.5. Impact on Model Evaluation and Interpretation

The extensive use of `pass@k` and deep analytical techniques like perplexity and accuracy distribution analysis sets a higher standard for evaluating LLM reasoning. Researchers will need to consider not just average performance but also the *breadth* and *origin* of a model's capabilities when making claims about advancements. This work helps disentangle improvements in "how easily" a model solves problems from "what problems" it can fundamentally solve.

In conclusion, this paper serves as a significant academic contribution, providing a sober and data-driven perspective on the current state of RLVR for LLMs. It acts as a crucial checkpoint in the ongoing quest for advanced AI, guiding the community towards more impactful and scientifically grounded avenues for unlocking the true reasoning potential of large language models. While acknowledging the rapid evolution of the field and the limitations of access to proprietary models, its findings offer a robust foundation for re-thinking the role of reinforcement learning in shaping the intelligence of future LLM generations.
