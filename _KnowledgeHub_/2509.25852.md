# Reinforced Embodied Planning with Verifiable Reward for Real-World Robotic Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> The REVER framework enables robots to execute complex, multi-step manipulation tasks from natural language instructions in real-world settings through a novel verifiable reward and data synthesis pipeline. Its VLM, RoboFarseer, demonstrates competitive planning performance and achieves high success rates in real-world robotic tasks.

## Problem

- Scarcity of large-scale, sequential manipulation data for long-horizon tasks in real-world environments.
- Absence of dense, interpretable rewards for Vision-Language Model (VLM) planning, hindering effective fine-tuning for adaptive behavior.
- Existing VLM applications often hallucinate unreachable states or violate physical constraints, struggling with dynamic real-world environments.

## Method

- Developed **REVER** (Reinforced Embodied Planning with Verifiable Reward), a framework integrating ==data synthesis==, ==VLM fine-tuning==, and ==hierarchical execution==.
- Created a systematic data collection and synthesis pipeline to generate the ==LEAP dataset==, converting raw kinesthetic demonstrations into structured ==Vision-Instruction-Plan triplets==.
- Designed a novel ==verifiable reward function== based on plan format and semantic content similarity, used with the ==GRPO algorithm== to fine-tune the **RoboFarseer** VLM planner.

## Results

- **RoboFarseer** (**7B** parameters) outperformed larger open-source and proprietary models on long-horizon planning benchmarks like LEAP-L(MCQ), scoring **59.3%**, and achieved **76%** on open-ended planning, doubling the score of Gemini-2.5-Pro.
- The framework achieved overall success rates up to **90%** in real-world manipulation tasks such as 'Bring food & drinks', providing an approximately **+60%** boost compared to relying solely on low-level control.
- Failure analysis indicated that downstream control failures were the dominant bottleneck (**43.6%**) rather than high-level planning or reasoning errors, highlighting the need for more robust visuo-motor control.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A grammar-aware, verifiable reward function effectively fine-tunes VLMs for generating executable, long-horizon plans, overcoming limitations of traditional imitation learning.
> - Automated synthesis of compositional task data from elementary skill demonstrations addresses the critical scarcity of sequentially annotated real-world manipulation datasets.
> - Integrating a VLM as both a high-level planner and an execution monitor within a hierarchical framework significantly boosts real-world task success, demonstrating the necessity of explicit planning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses a fundamental challenge in embodied AI: enabling robots to execute long-horizon manipulation tasks based on free-form natural language instructions. While Vision-Language Models (VLMs) have emerged as potential high-level planners, their real-world deployment is hindered by several limitations. Previous VLM applications often exhibit behaviors such as hallucinating unreachable states, violating physical constraints, or failing to adapt to unexpected observations, indicating a gap between static reasoning and the dynamic, feedback-rich nature of real-world task execution.

A significant impediment lies in the learning paradigm. Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) trains VLMs to imitate expert sequences but offers limited mechanisms for recovery or novel recombination of actions. Reinforcement Learning (RL) presents an alternative for adaptive behavior; however, its application to embodied VLMs is nascent, particularly for open-ended planning where dense, interpretable rewards are difficult to formulate. Existing reward approaches, such as LLM-as-a-Judge methods, can be noisy and unreliable, while hand-crafted heuristics often fail with increasing scene complexity.

Data scarcity further compounds this problem. Current real-world datasets, like the Universal Manipulation Interface (UMI), primarily focus on short, atomic skills and lack the compositional breadth and sequential annotation necessary for training and evaluating long-horizon planners. Consequently, many existing VLM planners are confined to synthetic benchmarks or experience rapid performance degradation when faced with the visual diversity and action variability of physical environments.

This research aims to bridge these identified gaps by introducing a framework that enables VLMs to generate and validate multi-step manipulation plans in real-world settings, supported by a novel data generation pipeline and a verifiable reward mechanism.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enable robots to reliably execute long-horizon manipulation tasks from free-form natural language instructions in real-world environments. The authors identify two principal challenges motivating their work:

1. **Scarcity of Large-Scale, Sequential Manipulation Data:** There is a lack of comprehensive datasets that couple natural language instructions with corresponding multi-step action plans, specifically for long-horizon manipulation tasks in real-world settings. Existing datasets, such as those derived from the Universal Manipulation Interface (UMI) framework, primarily provide demonstrations of atomic skills. While valuable for low-level control, these datasets often lack the compositional annotations and sequential structuring required to train VLMs for complex, multi-stage planning. This data gap restricts the ability of VLMs to learn hierarchical reasoning and temporal coherence necessary for extended tasks.

2. **Absence of Dense, Interpretable Rewards for VLM Planning:** Fine-tuning VLMs for planning objectives is challenging due to the difficulty in defining effective reward signals. Traditional supervised fine-tuning (SFT) relies on imitating expert trajectories, which limits generalization and recovery capabilities. While reinforcement learning (RL) offers potential for adaptive behavior, implementing it for embodied VLMs, especially for open-ended planning, is hindered by the lack of interpretable and computationally inexpensive reward functions. Prior attempts, such as using LLMs as judges, often yield noisy and unverifiable scalar signals that fluctuate with prompts, while hand-crafted heuristics are not robust to increasing scene complexity. This makes it difficult to provide VLMs with clear, consistent feedback for improving their planning abilities.

To address these motivations, the authors propose the REVER framework with the following specific objectives:

- To develop a VLM (RoboFarseer) capable of generating "chain-of-thought" reasoning that performs temporal and spatial reasoning to ensure physically plausible and logically coherent long-horizon plans.
- To create a novel, automated data collection and synthesis pipeline that converts raw, real-world kinesthetic demonstrations of atomic skills into structured Vision-Instruction-Plan triplets suitable for training VLMs for compositional tasks. This involves extending the UMI framework.
- To design a verifiable and interpretable reward function that can score generated plans based on their syntactic validity and semantic coverage relative to ground-truth skill sequences. This reward aims to overcome the limitations of existing reward mechanisms, enabling effective reinforcement fine-tuning without reliance on human labels or simulator queries.
- To implement a hierarchical execution framework where the fine-tuned VLM functions as both a high-level planner and an execution monitor, verifying step-wise task completion to enable a closed-loop, autonomous system for real-world robotic manipulation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The REVER (Reinforced Embodied planning with Verifiable Reward) framework integrates three core components: a systematic data collection and synthesis pipeline, a reinforcement fine-tuning method using a verifiable reward, and a hierarchical execution framework.

**A. Elementary-Skills-based Real-world Data Collection and Synthesis**

This component focuses on generating a structured, multi-purpose training corpus from raw, in-the-wild video demonstrations.

1. **Skill Acquisition and Compositional Task Synthesis:**
 - A set of *N* atomic manipulation skills is acquired through kinesthetic demonstrations using the UMI gripper. Each skill is demonstrated multiple times, resulting in raw video trajectories.
 - Long-horizon tasks are programmatically synthesized by composing ordered sequences of *K* atomic skills from this library. This allows for creating diverse and complex tasks from a finite set of basic operations.

2. **Automated Annotation Generation:**
 - For each synthesized task, the pipeline processes video segments to generate annotations for two learning objectives: planning and completion verification.
 - Keyframes are extracted for each sub-task: an initial observation (*o*init) before the sub-task and a final observation (*o*final) after completion.
 - **Plan Annotation (y_plan):** Given the initial observation of the first sub-task (*o*(1)init) and a high-level user instruction (*I*user), the ground-truth is the full sequence of sub-tasks (e.g., *(s*(1)..., *s*(K))).
 - **Completion Annotation (y_comp):** This is a binary classification (True/False) indicating whether a specific sub-task (*s*(k)) was successfully completed, based on the pair of observations (*o*(k)init, *o*(k)final) and the sub-task instruction. Negative samples are generated by pairing initial observations with frames from mid-execution.
 - **LEAP Dataset:** The annotations are assembled into a structured format where each data point is a triplet (*q, O, y*), with *O* being observations, *q* a dynamically generated prompt, and *y* the ground-truth. The dataset contains 12,000 tasks, split 9:1 for training and testing, and includes two subsets: LEAP-L (long-horizon sequential planning) and LEAP-U (instruction-aligned planning).

**B. VLM Planner Fine-tuning with Verifiable Reward**

The fine-tuning of the VLM planner, named RoboFarseer (based on Qwen2.5-VL-7B), leverages a novel verifiable reward function using the GRPO optimization algorithm.

1. **The REVER Verifiable Reward Function:** The reward *R(P_g, P_gt)* for a generated plan *P_g* and ground-truth plan *P_gt* is a weighted sum of a format score and a content score: *R = w_f * R_format + w_c * R_content*.
 - **Format Score (R_format):** This component ensures the model outputs structured reasoning traces. It assigns a score of 1 if *P_g* adheres to a predefined tag template (e.g., `<think>...</think><answer>...</answer>`) and 0 otherwise.
 - **Content Score (R_content):** This measures semantic similarity between *P_g* and *P_gt* using a maximum weight bipartite matching problem. A bipartite graph is constructed where vertices represent steps from *P_g* and *P_gt*. The edge weight *Sim(p_i, p'_j)* is a weighted sum of action similarity (*Sim_act*, 0.3 weight) and object similarity (*Sim_obj*, 0.7 weight). *Sim_act* is 1 if action verbs match; *Sim_obj* is 1 if object/location arguments are similar (exact match, substring, or within the same semantic category). The maximum matching weight, normalized by the length of the longer plan, gives *R_bm*. A length penalty *w_l* * |M − N|* is subtracted, where *M* and *N* are plan lengths and *w_l* = 0.1. For completion data, *R_content* is 1 for an exact match and 0 otherwise.

2. **Planner Fine-tuning with GRPO:** The GRPO (Group Reinforcement Policy Optimization) algorithm is employed for fine-tuning. It computes a relative advantage (ˆ*A*i) for each response by normalizing its reward against the group's statistics. GRPO optimizes a clipped surrogate objective function, inspired by PPO, to maximize advantage while controlling policy updates. A KL-divergence term is included to prevent large deviations from the reference policy, ensuring stable learning.

**C. Hierarchical Execution Framework via Planning and Control**

The system integrates the VLM planner with a low-level control policy for real-world deployment.

1. **System Components:**
 - **Planner (RoboFarseer):** Generates a multi-step sub-task list from a high-level user request and initial visual observation.
 - **Control Policy:** A low-level policy (diffusion policy with a frozen language encoder and FiLM conditioning) executes single, concrete sub-task instructions. It is trained on UMI human demonstrations for end-effector pose trajectories.
 - **Execution Monitor (RoboFarseer):** Operating in a verification mode, the VLM continuously assesses (at 5Hz) whether the current sub-task has been completed by comparing the current visual observation against the initial state for that sub-task.

2. **Execution Flow (Algorithm 1):**
 - **Initial Planning:** The VLM Planner generates an initial sub-task list *P*.
 - **Monitored Execution Loop:** The system iterates through the sub-tasks. For each sub-task, the Execution Monitor passes it to the control policy for asynchronous execution. While the control policy is active, the VLM Monitor repeatedly checks for task completion.
 - **Adaptation:** If the monitor signals completion, the system advances to the next sub-task. If execution fails (e.g., timeout), the monitor feeds the current world state back to the VLM Planner, which generates an updated sub-task list to adapt to the new situation. This cycle continues until all sub-tasks are completed.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of REVER and RoboFarseer involved both planning benchmarks and real-world robotic deployments.

**A. Planning Evaluation**

1. **Multiple-Choice Question (MCQ) Benchmarks:**
 - RoboFarseer (7B parameters) demonstrated competitive performance on LEAP-L(MCQ), scoring 59.3%, which surpassed all open-source competitors (e.g., Qwen2.5-VL-72B at 50.1%, RoboBrain2-32B at 36.8%) by at least 9 percentage points, despite being significantly smaller.
 - On LEAP-U(MCQ), RoboVQA, and ShareRobot-Planning, RoboFarseer achieved the highest accuracy among all evaluated models, including larger proprietary models like Gemini-2.5-Pro (66.9% on LEAP-L) and Claude-Sonnet-4 (64.1% on LEAP-L).
 - On EgoPlan2, RoboFarseer secured the second position (56.4%), closely trailing RoboBrain2-32B (57.2%), indicating that a 7B model trained with REVER can rival larger embodied specialists.

2. **Open-Ended Planning with Bipartite Matching Score:**
 - When models were required to generate complete plans (rather than selecting from options), RoboFarseer exhibited a substantial performance margin. On LEAP-L, it achieved 76%, which was more than double the score of Gemini-2.5-Pro (24%) and triple that of RoboBrain2-32B (26%).
 - Proprietary models like Claude-Sonnet-4 yielded near-zero scores due to deviations from the specified skill grammar. This outcome indicates that the verifiable-reward fine-tuning within REVER effectively teaches the VLM to produce long, executable, and grammar-compliant sequences, a capability not consistently observed in models primarily fine-tuned on general dialogue or short-horizon data.

3. **Impact of In-Context Examples:**
 - The provision of two in-context examples in the prompt generally improved the planning performance of baselines on open-ended planning. However, RoboFarseer maintained a 92% exact-match rate, confirming that its verifiable-reward training enforces format fidelity and grammar compliance beyond what simple few-shot prompting can achieve.

**B. Real-World Evaluation**

Experiments were conducted on a Dobot Nova5 6-DoF robot arm in three household scenarios: "Tidy up Desktop," "Brew Tea," and "Bring Food & Drinks," using ten abstract user instructions, each evaluated over ten episodes.

1. **Overall Success Rates:**
 - The "Bring food & drinks" family of tasks achieved the highest average overall success rate (90%), with step-level accuracies exceeding 95%. This suggests that tasks with unambiguous semantic anchors and shorter action chains are relatively easier for the planner.
 - In the "Tidy up Desktop" scenario, shape-based grouping tasks (e.g., "put all round objects into the basket") reached 90% overall success, while finer-grained stationery arrangement tasks (e.g., "organize all stationery") dropped to 60%. This reduction was attributed to difficulties in detecting and grasping thin, low-contrast objects, highlighting challenges related to both geometric and material properties.
 - "Brew Tea" tasks proved to be the most challenging, with overall success rates dropping to 50-70%. Challenges included the millimetre-level precision required for pouring, difficulties in localizing liquid surfaces from a single RGB view, and stable grasping of small, slippery objects. These factors contributed to cascading errors due to early perception or manipulation failures in longer-horizon tasks.

2. **Ablation Study (Without VLM Planner):**
 - An ablation study that removed the VLM planner and relied solely on the low-level control policy showed a significantly lower average overall success rate of 14%, and 0% success on the "Brew tea" tasks. This finding corroborates the conclusion that explicit high-level planning is essential for successful compositional, long-horizon manipulation in real-world environments.

3. **Failure Cause Analysis:**
 - Analysis of unsuccessful cases revealed the distribution of failure causes:
 - **Perception errors:** 26.9% (mis-classification of object state or pose).
 - **Reasoning errors:** 16.2% (incorrect decomposition of high-level instructions into elementary skills).
 - **Downstream control failure:** 43.6% (the dominant source, where the low-level policy could not achieve the required gripper pose, insertion angle, or pouring motion, despite a correct generated plan).
 - **Miscellaneous issues:** 3.3% (e.g., malformed output syntax).
 - These statistics suggest that the primary bottleneck in the current system is related to execution (low-level control) rather than high-level planning, indicating an area for future research focus on robust visuo-motor control.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces REVER, a framework that addresses critical limitations in enabling robots to perform long-horizon manipulation tasks from natural language instructions in real-world environments. Its significance and potential impact are multifold:

- **Advancement in Embodied Planning:** REVER provides a novel approach to reinforcement learning for VLMs, specifically designed for embodied planning. By leveraging a verifiable, grammar-aware reward, it enables effective fine-tuning for complex, multi-step tasks, which has been a persistent challenge due to data scarcity and difficulties in reward engineering.
- **Development of RoboFarseer:** The resulting model, RoboFarseer, demonstrates competitive or superior performance compared to proprietary models that are significantly larger in scale. Its ability to generate interpretable chain-of-thought plans and function as both a planner and a progress monitor represents a step towards more autonomous and robust robotic systems.
- **Contribution of LEAP Dataset:** The creation and release of the LEAP dataset, derived from large-scale kinesthetic demonstrations via UMI, provides a valuable resource for the research community. This dataset, comprising Vision-Instruction-Plan triplets for long-horizon tasks, directly addresses the scarcity of sequentially annotated real-world manipulation data.
- **Enhanced Real-World Task Success:** The comprehensive system, integrating high-level planning with low-level control, achieves a substantial improvement in real-world task success rates (approximately 60% boost) compared to relying solely on a low-level controller. This demonstrates the critical role of explicit high-level planning in compositional robotic manipulation.
- **Identification of Bottlenecks:** The detailed failure analysis, indicating that downstream control failures constitute the dominant source of errors (43.6%), provides a clear direction for future research. This insight suggests that efforts should prioritize robust visuo-motor control and real-time reactive refinement of low-level actions to unlock further capabilities.
- **Facilitating Reproducibility and Future Research:** The commitment to open-sourcing all model weights, datasets, and source code will facilitate reproducibility and accelerate future research in embodied AI and robotic manipulation. This open approach can foster further development in areas such as enlarging skill vocabularies, incorporating reactive replanning mechanisms, and extending the framework to more complex scenarios like bimanual and loco-manipulation.

In summary, this work contributes to the field by offering a principled framework for teaching VLMs to reason and plan for complex robotic tasks, supported by novel data and a robust evaluation methodology, while also highlighting key areas for future investigation.
