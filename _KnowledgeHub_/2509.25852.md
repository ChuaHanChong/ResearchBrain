# Reinforced Embodied Planning with Verifiable Reward for Real-World Robotic Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> The REVER framework enables robots to execute complex, multi-step manipulation tasks from natural language instructions in real-world settings through a novel verifiable reward and data synthesis pipeline. Its VLM, RoboFarseer, demonstrates competitive planning performance and achieves high success rates in real-world robotic tasks.

## Problem

- Scarcity of large-scale, sequential manipulation data for long-horizon tasks in real-world environments.
- Absence of dense, interpretable rewards for Vision-Language Model (VLM) planning, hindering effective fine-tuning for adaptive behavior.
- Existing VLM applications often hallucinate unreachable states or violate physical constraints, struggling with dynamic real-world environments.

## Method

- Developed **REVER** (Reinforced Embodied Planning with Verifiable Reward), a framework integrating ==data synthesis==, ==VLM fine-tuning==, and ==hierarchical execution==.
- Created a systematic data collection and synthesis pipeline to generate the ==LEAP dataset==, converting raw kinesthetic demonstrations into structured ==Vision-Instruction-Plan triplets==.
- Designed a novel ==verifiable reward function== based on plan format and semantic content similarity, used with the ==GRPO algorithm== to fine-tune the **RoboFarseer** VLM planner.

## Results

- **RoboFarseer** (**7B** parameters) outperformed larger open-source and proprietary models on long-horizon planning benchmarks like LEAP-L(MCQ), scoring **59.3%**, and achieved **76%** on open-ended planning, doubling the score of Gemini-2.5-Pro.
- The framework achieved overall success rates up to **90%** in real-world manipulation tasks such as 'Bring food & drinks', providing an approximately **+60%** boost compared to relying solely on low-level control.
- Failure analysis indicated that downstream control failures were the dominant bottleneck (**43.6%**) rather than high-level planning or reasoning errors, highlighting the need for more robust visuo-motor control.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A grammar-aware, verifiable reward function effectively fine-tunes VLMs for generating executable, long-horizon plans, overcoming limitations of traditional imitation learning.
> - Automated synthesis of compositional task data from elementary skill demonstrations addresses the critical scarcity of sequentially annotated real-world manipulation datasets.
> - Integrating a VLM as both a high-level planner and an execution monitor within a hierarchical framework significantly boosts real-world task success, demonstrating the necessity of explicit planning.
