# AMPED: Adaptive Multi-objective Projection for balancing Exploration and skill Diversification

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Gwangju Institute of Science and Technology and Seoul National University developed AMPED, an adaptive multi-objective projection method that balances exploration and skill diversification in unsupervised reinforcement learning. The system achieved superior performance on 12 downstream tasks from the URLB benchmark, surpassing state-of-the-art baselines by up to 35.01% in interquartile mean (IQM) scores.

## Problem

- Reinforcement learning often faces challenges with inefficient exploration and the need for extensive, domain-specific reward engineering in environments with sparse or delayed rewards.
- Skill-Based Reinforcement Learning (SBRL) struggles to simultaneously optimize conflicting objectives: maximizing mutual information for skill diversity and maximizing state entropy for broad exploration.
- Prior integrated approaches for skill discovery lacked robust theoretical foundations or practical effectiveness in resolving inherent gradient conflicts between exploration and diversity objectives.

## Method

- **AMPED** introduces an adaptive ==multi-objective projection== method for pretraining diverse and exploitable skills using intrinsic rewards that combine entropy-based exploration, Random Network Distillation (RND) novelty, and Anisotropic InfoNCE (AnInfoNCE) for skill separation.
- It employs PCGrad (Projecting Conflicting Gradients) as a gradient surgery mechanism during pretraining to explicitly mitigate conflicts between the exploration and diversity objective gradients.
- The method integrates a Soft Actor-Critic (SAC)-based adaptive skill selector during fine-tuning, allowing the agent to intelligently sample and utilize the most suitable pretrained skills for specific downstream tasks.

## Results

- AMPED achieved the highest median, interquartile mean (IQM), and mean scores, along with the smallest optimality gap, across 12 URLB benchmark tasks, outperforming methods like BeCL by **17.96%** (IQM) and ComSD by **35.01%**.
- Qualitative experiments in maze environments demonstrated AMPED's ability to learn well-separated, distinct skills while maintaining comprehensive state coverage, overcoming the trade-offs observed in baseline methods.
- Ablation studies confirmed that each component of AMPED—RND, AnInfoNCE loss, gradient surgery, and the adaptive skill selector—contributes positively to overall performance, with gradient surgery notably impacting results across domains.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly resolving gradient conflicts between exploration and skill diversity objectives through gradient surgery leads to more effective and stable skill learning, addressing a fundamental challenge in multi-objective reinforcement learning.
> - Combining diverse intrinsic reward components (entropy, RND, AnInfoNCE) enables the acquisition of a skill repertoire that simultaneously achieves broad state space exploration and clear skill distinguishability.
> - Increased skill diversity can theoretically reduce the sample complexity required for fine-tuning by simplifying the task of identifying the most suitable skill for a given policy.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of reinforcement learning (RL) continues to face challenges related to efficient exploration, particularly in environments characterized by sparse or delayed rewards. Traditional RL approaches often necessitate extensive, domain-specific reward engineering, which can limit scalability and generalizability. Skill-Based Reinforcement Learning (SBRL) offers an alternative by pretraining a policy capable of executing various skills through unsupervised skill discovery. This pretraining phase aims to equip agents with a repertoire of reusable behaviors that can be quickly adapted to new tasks.

Within SBRL, a common strategy involves using Unsupervised Reinforcement Learning (URL) objectives during pretraining to discover diverse and useful skills. These objectives typically involve two main components: maximizing mutual information (MI) between skills and state trajectories to promote skill diversity, and maximizing state entropy to encourage broad exploration of the environment. However, these two objectives can be conflicting. MI-driven objectives, while promoting distinct skills, may lead to premature specialization and curtailed exploration. Conversely, maximizing state entropy to ensure broad coverage can sometimes result in skills that are not sufficiently distinguishable, thereby limiting their utility for downstream tasks.

Previous studies, such as Constrained Ensemble Exploration for Unsupervised Skill Discovery (CeSD) and Balancing Behavioral Quality and Diversity in Unsupervised Skill Discovery (ComSD), have attempted to integrate these objectives. However, these methods have been noted to possess limitations, including a lack of robust theoretical foundations or practical shortcomings in simultaneously achieving both objectives without significant trade-offs.

This research positions itself within this landscape by recognizing the inherent gradient conflicts that arise when attempting to optimize exploration and diversity objectives concurrently. It proposes a principled framework rooted in multi-objective reinforcement learning to explicitly address these conflicts, aiming to bridge the two URL paradigms and overcome the limitations of prior integrated approaches. The work seeks to develop a method that effectively harmonizes exploration and skill diversification, leading to more robust and generalizable skill learning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an adaptive multi-objective projection method named AMPED (Adaptive Multi-objective Projection for balancing Exploration and skill Diversification) that effectively pretrains a diverse and exploitable set of skills for rapid adaptation in sparse-reward environments. This overarching goal is motivated by and supported through several specific objectives:

1. **Jointly Maximize Exploration and Skill Diversity:** A central motivation stems from the observation that existing SBRL methods struggle to simultaneously optimize both exploration (to cover the state space) and skill diversity (to ensure distinct, useful behaviors). This often leads to trade-offs where gains in one objective come at the expense of the other. The research aims to achieve both without compromising either.
2. **Mitigate Gradient Conflicts between Objectives:** The paper identifies that optimizing exploration and diversity objectives concurrently with shared network parameters can lead to conflicting gradients. These conflicts can result in inefficient updates, where improvements in one objective negatively impact the other, hindering stable learning. A key objective is to explicitly resolve these gradient conflicts to enable more effective and stable optimization.
3. **Enhance Skill Utility through Adaptive Selection:** Many prior SBRL approaches select skills uniformly at random during fine-tuning, potentially underutilizing the diversity learned during pretraining. The motivation here is to introduce an adaptive skill selection mechanism that can intelligently leverage the pretrained skill repertoire, choosing skills best suited for specific downstream tasks.
4. **Provide Theoretical and Empirical Justification:** The work is motivated by the need for both theoretical and empirical evidence to support its claims. Specifically, it aims to theoretically demonstrate that increased skill diversity can reduce the sample complexity required for fine-tuning when coupled with an effective skill selector. Empirically, it seeks to demonstrate that the proposed method surpasses existing SBRL baselines across various benchmarks, with each component contributing meaningfully to overall performance.

By addressing these objectives, the research aims to contribute to more efficient skill acquisition in RL, reducing reliance on handcrafted rewards and enabling agents to generalize more effectively across diverse tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, AMPED, integrates multiple techniques to balance exploration and skill diversification during pretraining and utilizes an adaptive skill selection mechanism during fine-tuning. The overall training process is divided into two main phases: skill pretraining and fine-tuning.

#### 3.1. Skill Pretraining Phase

During pretraining, the agent is conditioned on randomly sampled skills and optimized using a combination of intrinsic rewards designed to promote both exploration and diversity. A Deep Deterministic Policy Gradient (DDPG) agent is employed for this optimization. Crucially, the gradients derived from these intrinsic rewards are subjected to a gradient surgery mechanism.

- **Intrinsic Rewards:** The intrinsic reward function is a linear combination of exploration and diversity terms: `r_exploration + r_diversity`.
 - **Exploration Reward (`r_exploration`):** This reward combines two components:
 1. **Entropy-based term:** Derived from the discounted state occupancy measure `H(S_tot)`. This term encourages uniform visitation of the state space. It is approximated using a particle-based method based on k-nearest neighbors in an embedded state space. An encoder is trained using a contrastive loss (`L_CIC`) to generate meaningful state embeddings for this purpose. The computational complexity of `O(n log n)` for kNN estimation is mitigated by truncating the replay buffer to the most recent 5,000 states.
 2. **Random Network Distillation (RND) term:** This term (`r_rnd(s) = ||f_θ(s) - f_target(s)||^2`) rewards the agent for encountering novel states by measuring the prediction error of a randomly initialized target network. RND offers `O(n)` complexity and complements the entropy-based estimator, providing robustness, especially in high-dimensional spaces or when data is scarce.
 The final exploration reward is `r_exploration(s) = α * r_entropy(s) + β * r_rnd(s)`, where `α` and `β` are positive scaling coefficients.
 - **Diversity Reward (`r_diversity`):** To enforce distinct skill behaviors, the method adopts the Anisotropic InfoNCE (AnInfoNCE) objective, adapted from Behavior Contrastive Learning (BeCL). This objective (`L_AINCE`) maximizes the mutual information between states sampled from trajectories induced by the same skill. It explicitly encourages states generated by the same skill to cluster together while pushing apart states generated by different skills. This is achieved by minimizing a loss function that contrasts positive sample pairs (from the same skill) against negative samples (from different skills) using a learnable diagonal matrix `Λ`. AnInfoNCE is motivated as a superior alternative to objectives that saturate when skill distributions no longer overlap, providing a stronger gradient signal for separation.

- **Gradient Surgery:** To address the conflicting nature of exploration and diversity objectives, AMPED integrates PCGrad (Projecting Conflicting Gradients) as a gradient surgery method.
 - **Mechanism:** During optimization, if the inner product of the exploration gradient (`g_expl`) and the diversity gradient (`g_div`) is negative (indicating conflict), one gradient is projected onto the orthogonal complement of the other. The decision of which gradient to project is randomized with a probability `p`. This process ensures that the updated gradient does not interfere negatively with the descent direction of the other objective. The adjusted gradients are then summed to update the critic network parameters.

#### 3.2. Fine-tuning Phase

Following pretraining, the agent enters the fine-tuning phase where it adapts to specific downstream tasks using extrinsic rewards.

- **Adaptive Skill Selection:** Unlike methods that randomly sample skills or fix them, AMPED introduces a Soft Actor-Critic (SAC)-based skill selector `p(z|s)`.
 - **Mechanism:** This selector is trained jointly with the policy during fine-tuning. At each time step, the selector samples a skill `z` based on the current state `s`. The policy then takes an action conditioned on this selected skill, and both the policy and the skill selector are updated using the extrinsic reward from the downstream task.
 - **Exploration-Exploitation:** An `ϵ-greedy` strategy is employed for skill selection, with `ϵ` decaying exponentially over time to balance exploration of new skills with exploitation of high-performing ones.
 - **Evaluation:** During evaluation, the skill selector operates deterministically, employing a greedy strategy to maximize task performance.

#### 3.3. Theoretical Analysis

The paper provides a theoretical result demonstrating the benefit of skill diversity. It states that for a finite state space and finite horizon, if skills are sufficiently diversified (i.e., the total variation distance `δ` between different skill occupancy measures is large), then the probability of a greedy skill selector choosing the wrong skill decreases exponentially with the number of samples (`n`). Specifically, `Pr[bz ≠ z*] ≤ 2^S * H * exp(-nΔ^2/2)`, where `Δ = δ - 2ε` and `ε` is the distance between the target policy's occupancy and the closest skill's occupancy. This formalizes the intuition that a more diverse skill set simplifies the task of identifying the most suitable skill for a given policy, thereby reducing fine-tuning sample complexity.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents both qualitative and quantitative findings across various environments to evaluate AMPED's performance and the contribution of its components.

#### 4.1. Qualitative Assessment on Maze Environments

Experiments on the Tree Maze and Square Maze environments provided visual insights into the skill discovery capabilities of AMPED compared to baselines (DIAYN, BeCL, CIC, CeSD, ComSD).
- **Skill Distinguishability and State Coverage (Figure 7, Figure 13):** AMPED demonstrated the ability to learn well-separated skills while simultaneously achieving comprehensive state coverage in both Tree and Square Mazes. Baselines like DIAYN and BeCL often exhibited distinct skills but left parts of the environment unexplored. Conversely, CIC and ComSD achieved good state coverage but frequently showed entangled or less distinguishable skill trajectories.
- **Effect of Skill Count (Figure 12):** When increasing the number of skills from 5 to 25 in the Tree Maze, AMPED consistently maintained clear separation and full coverage, whereas CIC and ComSD showed increasing overlap.
- **Entropy and Mutual Information (Figure 14):** In the Square Maze, AMPED successfully maximized both state entropy (indicating broad exploration, comparable to exploration-focused methods like CIC) and mutual information between skills and states (indicating skill diversity, comparable to diversity-focused methods like BeCL). This suggests AMPED effectively balances both objectives.
- **Skill Evolution (Figure 15):** Visualizations of skill trajectories over training epochs in the Tree Maze showed an initial phase of rapid exploration, expanding state coverage, followed by refinement where skills carve out distinct, non-overlapping regions.

#### 4.2. Quantitative Assessment on URLB Benchmark

AMPED was quantitatively evaluated on 12 downstream tasks across the Walker, Quadruped, and Jaco domains from the URLB benchmark. Methods were pretrained for 2 million steps and fine-tuned for 100,000 steps.
- **Overall Performance (Figure 8, Table 13):** AMPED achieved the highest median, interquartile mean (IQM), and mean scores, alongside the smallest optimality gap, outperforming strong baselines including DIAYN, APT, BeCL, CIC, RND, CeSD, and ComSD. Notably, AMPED surpassed BeCL by 17.96% (IQM), CIC by 15.02%, and APT by 9.73%. It also demonstrated significant improvements over the recent diversity-exploration hybrids CeSD (20.91%) and ComSD (35.01%). These results indicate that explicitly addressing exploration-diversity gradient conflicts leads to substantial performance gains.
- **Time-Series Performance (Figures 18, 19, 20):** Plots of normalized returns over fine-tuning steps for Walker, Quadruped, and Jaco tasks showed AMPED consistently outperforming CIC and BeCL, particularly in Walker and Quadruped.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

Comprehensive ablation studies were conducted to determine the contribution of each component of AMPED.
- **Component Contribution (Table 1):** Removing any single component (RND, AnInfoNCE loss, gradient surgery, or the skill selector) generally resulted in degraded overall returns.
 - Excluding RND led to a 21.1% drop in Walker and 16.2% in Quadruped, affirming its role in exploration.
 - Removing AnInfoNCE reduced returns by 5.7% (Walker), 2.4% (Quadruped), and 16.5% (Jaco), underscoring the necessity for skill separation.
 - Disabling gradient surgery caused drops of 4.3% (Walker), 9.3% (Quadruped), and 26.5% (Jaco), highlighting the value of conflict resolution.
 - Omitting the skill selector led to a 6.2% decrease in Quadruped and 4.5% in Jaco, suggesting its importance despite a minor gain in Walker.
- **Gradient Projection Ratio (Figure 9, Table 14):** Comparing different projection strategies (projecting exploration onto diversity, diversity onto exploration, and environment-specific ratios), AMPED's balanced projection ratio yielded the highest aggregate returns across domains.
- **Number of Skills (Figure 10, Table 15):** The default configuration of 16 skills showed the strongest performance in Quadruped and Jaco and competitive results in Walker, indicating the importance of selecting an appropriate skill dimension rather than simply increasing it.
- **Reward Scaling (Table 2, Table 3):** Ablations on the scaling coefficients `α` (entropy) and `β` (RND) demonstrated that the default balanced weights achieved the best aggregate performance, though further fine-tuning could potentially yield additional improvements.
- **Skill Selection Analysis (Table 4, Table 5):** While the SAC-based skill selector underperformed an "Oracle Best Skill" scenario (where the best single skill is manually selected), it consistently concentrated on a small subset of skills during fine-tuning (Table 5), suggesting a stable and reusable skill space. The performance gap indicates potential bottlenecks in the selector's learning, possibly due to sparse rewards.

#### 4.4. Training Time Comparison (Table 10, Table 11)

AMPED's pretraining time (approximately 13-14 hours) was competitive with other robust methods like CeSD and BeCL, although longer than purely entropy-based methods like CIC. Fine-tuning time for AMPED incurred a modest overhead (0.06-0.11 hours) due to the SAC-based skill selector.

#### 4.5. Comparison with METRA (Table 12)

Discrete and Continuous METRA variants underperformed AMPED across all URLB tasks, suggesting that while METRA focuses on metric-aware abstraction for high-dimensional settings, AMPED's explicit exploration-diversity balancing is more effective for the URLB control suite.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of reinforcement learning by proposing AMPED, a method that explicitly addresses the challenge of balancing exploration and skill diversification in unsupervised skill discovery. The work's significance stems from several key aspects:

- **Principled Resolution of Objective Conflicts:** A primary contribution is the demonstration that explicitly recognizing and mitigating gradient conflicts between exploration and diversity objectives, using a technique like gradient surgery (PCGrad), leads to substantial improvements in skill learning. This provides a principled approach beyond heuristic weighting schemes often employed in multi-objective settings.
- **Enhanced Skill Repertoire Quality:** By effectively combining entropy-based exploration, RND-driven novelty, and AnInfoNCE-based skill separation, AMPED enables the acquisition of a skill repertoire that is both broadly exploratory and highly diverse. This translates to policies that are more adaptable and efficient during fine-tuning for downstream tasks, as evidenced by superior performance on the URLB benchmark.
- **Theoretical Grounding for Diversity:** The theoretical analysis demonstrating that greater skill diversity reduces fine-tuning sample complexity provides formal support for the importance of generating a well-separated skill set. This strengthens the motivation for diversity-promoting objectives in SBRL.
- **Adaptive Skill Utilization:** The introduction of an adaptive skill selector during fine-tuning allows the agent to intelligently leverage the learned skill diversity, moving beyond random skill selection. While the current selector shows areas for improvement, its conceptual foundation for flexible skill deployment is significant.
- **Broader Applicability:** The core insight regarding the resolution of gradient conflicts between multiple learning signals is not limited to SBRL. It holds potential for application in other multi-objective or multi-task reinforcement learning scenarios where competing objectives may hinder overall learning efficiency.

The potential impact of AMPED is in advancing the capability of agents to learn general-purpose behaviors in complex, sparse-reward environments with less human supervision. This can accelerate the development of more autonomous and adaptable AI systems in domains such as robotics, game playing, and complex control tasks, by reducing the intensive effort typically required for reward function design. Future research directions, such as refining conflict-resolution techniques, improving reward estimators, and dynamically adjusting skill dimensions, are identified, indicating avenues for further development in skill-based RL.
