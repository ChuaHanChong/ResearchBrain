# Percept-WAM: Perception-Enhanced World-Awareness-Action Model for Robust End-to-End Autonomous Driving

> [!summary] Summary
> Percept-WAM, developed by researchers from Yinwang Intelligent Technology Co. Ltd. and Fudan University, integrates explicit 2D and 3D scene understanding into a single Vision-Language Model backbone for robust end-to-end autonomous driving. The model achieves strong performance on perception tasks, with 51.7 mAP on COCO 2D detection and 0.589 mAP on nuScenes BEV 3D detection, and enhances trajectory planning, yielding 0.36m L2 error on nuScenes and 90.2 PMDS on NAVSIM with efficient streaming inference.

## Problem

- Current Vision-Language Models (VLMs) and Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models lack precise metric spatial grounding and struggle with 3D localization due to indirect QA-style supervision.
- End-to-end autonomous driving models, particularly diffusion-based ones, often sacrifice explicit spatial understanding and the rich reasoning capabilities of Large Language Models (LLMs).
- Autonomous driving systems are susceptible to accumulated geometric errors and make brittle decisions in challenging long-tail scenarios, stemming from weak spatial awareness and unreliable perception.

## Method

- Introduces a ==World-Awareness-Action== framework that embeds detailed 2D (World-PV) and 3D (World-BEV) world states, including metric coordinates and calibrated confidence, directly within a single pretrained VLM backbone.
- Employs ==grid-conditioned dense perception== with an ==IoU-aware scoring mechanism== and parallel ==autoregressive decoding== to enable robust and efficient multi-object detection and segmentation across various scales and conditions.
- Integrates trajectory generation using dedicated ==World-Action tokens== and a ==query-based decoding== approach, enhanced by a ==streaming inference== strategy to achieve real-time performance for continuous video inputs.

## Results

- Achieves 51.7 mAP on COCO 2D detection, 33.0 mAP on nuScenes mono 3D detection, and 41.7 mAP on nuImages 2D instance segmentation, outperforming several baselines including Mask R-CNN and FCOS3D.
- Attains 0.589 mAP and 0.645 NDS for nuScenes BEV 3D object detection, alongside superior BEV map segmentation with 87.0 IoU on drivable areas and 70.9 IoU on pedestrian crossings.
- Demonstrates an average trajectory L2 error of 0.36m on nuScenes open-loop planning and scores 90.2 PMDS on NAVSIM closed-loop evaluation, surpassing DriveVLM and DiffusionDrive, while achieving a **40%** inference speedup (707 ms frame latency) with streaming inference.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Implicitly integrating explicit 2D/3D scene understanding within a unified VLM effectively bridges the gap between high-level reasoning and precise geometric competence, addressing a key limitation in prior VLM/VLA systems.
> - Encoding metric coordinates and calibrated confidence directly into 'world tokens' provides a more reliable, persistent, and interpretable representation of the environment, crucial for enhancing robustness in complex and long-tail autonomous driving scenarios.
> - A perception-enhanced two-stage training strategy demonstrates that improvements in the unified perception model directly translate to superior performance in downstream end-to-end trajectory planning tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Autonomous driving (AD) research has seen a significant evolution, with increasing reliance on advanced perception and reasoning capabilities. Early systems often decoupled perception, prediction, and planning into distinct modules. More recently, the field has moved towards end-to-end (E2E) approaches, aiming to directly map sensor inputs to control outputs, thereby reducing error propagation and improving holistic performance. Within this E2E paradigm, Vision-Language Models (VLMs) and Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models have gained traction due promising human-like reasoning abilities.

However, the authors identify critical limitations in the current landscape:
- **Weak Spatial Grounding in VLMs/VLAs:** Many existing VLA frameworks leverage VLM backbones, but general-purpose VLMs often struggle with precise metric geometry, 3D localization, temporal consistency, and reliable confidence estimation. Methods that frame spatial understanding as "QA-style supervision" (e.g., asking "What is the distance to the moving object ahead?") provide only indirect localization signals, leading to imprecise, non-persistent, and poorly calibrated world states, especially in crowded or complex scenes.
- **Lack of Reasoning in Diffusion-based E2E Models:** Some recent E2E methods, like DiffusionDrive, adopt encoder-diffusion-decoder pipelines for trajectory prediction, abandoning LLM-based architectures. While offering generative control, they often lack the explicit spatial task learning and rich reasoning capacity of Large Language Models (LLMs) crucial for handling complex, unscripted driving scenarios.
- **Brittle Decisions from Minor Errors:** In real-world AD, even small geometric errors (e.g., detection bias, yaw drift, BEV/occupancy inaccuracies) can accumulate into safety-critical failures, particularly in "long-tail scenarios" (e.g., night, rain, small or rare objects) that are difficult to anticipate and train for exhaustively.

Percept-WAM positions itself as a solution to bridge this gap. It aims to integrate robust, explicit 2D/3D scene understanding directly into a single VLM, thereby combining the reasoning capabilities of LLMs with precise spatial perception, an approach conceptually aligned with "planning-oriented frameworks such as UniAD" but implemented within a VLM context. By unifying perception and action within a single model, Percept-WAM seeks to enhance robustness and stability, addressing the fundamental challenge of ensuring reliable spatial awareness for safe and effective autonomous driving.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core motivation behind Percept-WAM stems from the critical need for accurate and robust spatial perception in autonomous driving, particularly when dealing with the complexities of real-world "long-tail scenarios" and intricate interactions. The authors pinpoint several key issues with existing approaches:

1. **Addressing Inaccuracies and Instabilities:** Current AD systems frequently fail due to inaccuracies and instabilities in spatial perception. This is exacerbated in challenging conditions like adverse weather, low light, or the presence of small/rare objects, where traditional models or even nascent VLM-based systems fall short.
2. **Improving Spatial Grounding in VLMs:** While Vision-Language Models offer promising avenues for reasoning in complex scenarios, they are inherently weak at metric spatial grounding and understanding. The prevalent "QA-style spatial reasoning" in many VLA systems provides insufficient, indirect localization signals, leading to limited perception and localization capabilities. The objective is to move beyond indirect reasoning to explicit, persistent, and localizable world states.
3. **Integrating Reasoning with Robust Perception for E2E Driving:** Methods that purely focus on end-to-end trajectory prediction (e.g., diffusion-based models) often sacrifice explicit spatial understanding and the high-level reasoning capabilities of LLMs. Conversely, VLMs excel at reasoning but lack precise geometric competence. Percept-WAM aims to create a unified framework that *implicitly integrates 2D/3D scene understanding abilities within a single VLM* to leverage the best of both worlds: robust perception and rich logical reasoning, directly facilitating appropriate control decisions.
4. **Enhancing Robustness in Challenging Scenarios:** A primary objective is to develop a model that performs reliably in situations where current systems falter, such as detecting small objects, handling far-range perception, or maintaining stability under various long-tail conditions. This requires a more explicit and confident representation of spatial information.
5. **Achieving High Efficiency and Real-time Performance:** Given the demanding nature of autonomous driving applications, the model must not only be accurate but also efficient. Percept-WAM aims for rapid inference through mechanisms like parallel decoding and streaming inference, without sacrificing performance.

In summary, the overarching objective is to develop a "perception-enhanced World-Awareness-Action Model" that can precisely perceive, understand, reason about, and act upon the environment, thereby enabling more robust and reliable end-to-end autonomous driving.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Percept-WAM introduces a novel **World-Awareness-Action framework** that implicitly embeds detailed 2D and 3D world states within a single, pretrained Vision-Language Model (VLM) backbone (specifically, InternVL2-8B). This unification is achieved through specialized "world tokens" and a sophisticated decoding mechanism.

The methodology can be broken down into three main components:

#### 3.1. Perception-Enhanced World Tokens

The core innovation lies in defining two families of tokens for spatial perception:
- **World-PV (Perspective-View) Tokens:** These tokens represent image-plane understanding, crucial for 2D perception tasks. They are derived from the VLM's image features, patchified into a grid, where each grid location acts as a localized query for single-object perception. This allows for high-resolution input processing by dynamically tiling images and fusing features.
- **World-BEV (Bird's-Eye View) Tokens:** These learnable query tokens form the foundation for 3D spatial understanding in the BEV representation. They implicitly model the mapping from PV features to BEV space via cross-attention. World-BEV tokens can be initialized randomly for camera-only input or from pretrained LiDAR encoder features (e.g., PointPillars) for multi-modal fusion, injecting metrically grounded 3D priors.

Crucially, both World-PV and World-BEV tokens encode **metric coordinates along with calibrated confidence scores**. This provides localized, reusable evidence for downstream reasoning and planning, moving beyond indirect QA-style supervision.

#### 3.2. Grid-Conditioned Dense Perception

To address challenges in detecting objects across varying distances and scales, Percept-WAM employs:
- **Grid-Conditioned Prediction Head:** World-PV tokens (interpolated from image features) and World-BEV tokens (from BEV grid cells) act as fine-grained features tied to local coordinates. Each grid token predicts bounding boxes or segmentation masks aligned with its location.
- **IoU-aware Scoring:** Recognizing that VLMs can be overconfident, Percept-WAM introduces an explicit IoU-based confidence token for each predicted box, conditioned on its attributes. This improves confidence calibration, especially in crowded scenes, by providing a more interpretable and localization-sensitive reliability measure. A novel "confidence-tuning dataset" is generated from intermediate model predictions, reflecting realistic IoU distributions, and a loss-mask scheme ensures precise learning of both boxes and confidence.
- **Parallel Autoregressive Decoding:** To maintain high throughput without sacrificing stability, the model decodes predictions in parallel across grids. This significantly enhances inference efficiency for dense multi-object inference, especially vital for real-time autonomous driving.
- **Task Formulation:** Perception tasks (2D/3D detection, instance/semantic segmentation in PV, 3D detection/map segmentation in BEV) are unified by serializing predictions into natural-language-like token sequences. Continuous labels are normalized and discretized into integer bins, supervised with cross-entropy loss, enabling open-vocabulary detection. Segmentation is cast as a feature retrieval task using `<mask>` tokens.

#### 3.3. From Perception to Action

Building upon robust 2D/3D perception, Percept-WAM integrates trajectory generation:
- **World-Action Tokens:** These tokens are introduced to align and fuse perceptual information for future trajectory prediction.
- **Query-Based Trajectory Decoding:** A lightweight MLP decoder generates trajectories from world tokens. To enhance alignment across modalities, four sets of point-level queries (Q_pv, Q_bev, Q_ego, Q_full) are used. Q_pv, Q_bev, and Q_ego interact only with their respective modality features (World-PV, World-BEV, and ego-state data) via attention masks, while Q_full has access to all features to decode the final trajectory. This parallel decoding ensures alignment and efficiency.
- **Streaming Inference:** To handle continuous video inputs efficiently for real-world applications, a streaming KV cache strategy is adopted. This involves reusing KV cache from previous frames and a dual-recomputation mechanism to mitigate distribution drift and stabilize computations over long sequences, leading to significant inference speedups.

In essence, Percept-WAM establishes a unified backbone where the model can output both perception results (2D/3D bounding boxes/masks) and trajectory control decisions, demonstrating a holistic approach to end-to-end autonomous driving.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

Percept-WAM demonstrates strong performance across a diverse set of perception and planning benchmarks, often matching or surpassing specialized models, and highlighting the benefits of its unified approach.

#### 4.1. PV Perception Results

- **2D Detection:** Percept-WAM achieves **51.7 mAP on COCO 2D detection** and **49.9 mAP on nuImages 2D detection**. This significantly surpasses baselines like Mask R-CNN (47.8 mAP) and other multimodal LLMs like LMM-Det (47.5 mAP), especially when trained on both AD and general-purpose datasets. A notable synergy was observed between 2D and 3D PV detection, with 2D performance improving by 3.2 mAP due to unified modeling.
- **Monocular 3D Detection:** The model achieves **33.0 mAP on nuScenes mono 3D detection**, outperforming FCOS3D (32.1 mAP).
- **2D Instance Segmentation:** Percept-WAM reaches **41.7 mAP on nuImages 2D instance segmentation**, surpassing Mask R-CNN (38.6 mAP).
- **2D Semantic Segmentation:** It obtains **62.8 mIoU on nuImages, 54.3 mIoU on ADE20K, and 50.3 mIoU on COCOstuff**.
- **Qualitative Results:** Visualizations demonstrate strong capability in detecting and segmenting multiple objects in crowded, long-range, and small-object scenarios. It also exhibits robust open-vocabulary and long-tail generalization (e.g., detecting birds or shopping carts).
- **IoU-based Confidence Prediction:** Ablation studies show that the proposed IoU-based confidence prediction, when trained on a dataset reflecting realistic model prediction distributions, significantly improves performance (e.g., +1.5 AP and +2.3 AP75 on nuImages 2D detection) compared to class-score-only baselines or other confidence-tuning methods. This leads to more accurate and interpretable bounding box scores.

#### 4.2. BEV Perception Results

- **BEV 3D Object Detection:** Percept-WAM achieves **0.589 mAP and 0.645 NDS on nuScenes BEV 3D detection**. This performance is competitive with classical detectors like PointPillars (0.523 mAP) and SECOND (0.526 mAP), even without using sequential information and with relatively low image resolution.
- **BEV Map Segmentation:** It achieves superior performance in map segmentation, with **87.0 IoU on drivable area and 70.9 IoU on pedestrian crossings**, outperforming methods like BEVFusion.
- **Ablation Studies:** Initializing World-BEV tokens with LiDAR encoder features (for C+L mode) significantly boosts mAP (by 8.2%), and data augmentation further improves it (by 8.1%). Increasing grid resolution from 20x20 to 40x40 adds another 9.1% mAP.

#### 4.3. E2E Trajectory Planning Results

- **nuScenes Open-Loop Metrics:** Percept-WAM achieves an average trajectory L2 error of **0.36m**, outperforming many existing BEV-based methods like UniAD (0.46m) and VLM-based methods like DriveVLM (0.4m).
- **NAVSIM Closed-Loop Metrics:** On the more challenging NAVSIM benchmark, Percept-WAM scores **90.2 PMDS**, surpassing DiffusionDrive (88.1 PMDS) by 2.1 points.
- **Two-Stage Training:** A perception-enhanced two-stage training strategy (Percept-WAM*) further improves planning performance, demonstrating that stronger perception directly leads to better downstream E2E trajectory planning.
- **Inference Efficiency:** Through streaming inference, the model achieves a short frame latency of **707 ms** for query-based decoding, representing a 40% reduction compared to non-streaming inference, with minimal impact on accuracy.
- **Qualitative Results:** Visualizations show robust trajectory planning even in complex and long-tail scenarios, such as navigating construction zones, yielding to pedestrians in rain, or accounting for occluded cyclists at night.
- **Ablation on Trajectory Decoding:** Query-based trajectory scoring and selection outperforms AR-based and simple query-based trajectory generation methods in closed-loop NAVSIM evaluations (90.2 PMDS), highlighting the effectiveness of aligning imitation learning with closed-loop metrics.

In essence, Percept-WAM successfully demonstrates that explicitly integrating strong 2D and 3D perception capabilities within a unified VLM backbone not only yields state-of-the-art perception but also significantly enhances end-to-end trajectory planning performance, all while maintaining practical inference efficiency.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

Percept-WAM represents a significant step forward in the field of autonomous driving, particularly in addressing the critical challenge of robust perception and reasoning in complex, real-world scenarios. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Bridging the Perception-Reasoning Gap:** The most impactful contribution is the successful implicit integration of comprehensive 2D/3D scene understanding directly within a single Vision-Language Model. This approach effectively combines the high-level reasoning capabilities of LLMs with the precise geometric competence traditionally lacking in VLMs, overcoming a key limitation of prior VLM/VLA systems that relied on indirect, QA-style spatial grounding. This unification promises more intelligent and context-aware driving decisions.

2. **Enhanced Robustness and Safety:** By explicitly encoding metric coordinates and calibrated confidence in "world tokens," Percept-WAM provides a more reliable and persistent representation of the environment. Innovations like IoU-aware scoring and grid-conditioned dense perception directly contribute to improved accuracy and stability in detecting long-tail, far-range, and small objects—scenarios where current AD systems often struggle. This enhanced robustness is crucial for increasing the safety and reliability of autonomous vehicles in diverse and unpredictable real-world conditions.

3. **Improved End-to-End Performance:** The strong performance on both perception benchmarks (e.g., COCO, nuScenes) and closed-loop trajectory planning simulations (e.g., NAVSIM) validates the hypothesis that a stronger, unified perception model translates directly to better driving actions. Outperforming specialized detectors/segmenters and competitive planning models demonstrates the synergistic benefits of joint optimization within a single backbone.

4. **Efficiency for Real-world Deployment:** The integration of parallel autoregressive decoding and streaming inference techniques addresses the practical demands of real-time autonomous driving. Achieving low frame latency (e.g., 707 ms) while maintaining high accuracy makes Percept-WAM a more viable candidate for real-world deployment than models with high computational overhead.

5. **Foundation for Future AD Systems:** Percept-WAM's framework, with its perception-enhanced world tokens and unified architecture, lays a strong foundation for future research. It demonstrates a promising paradigm for developing truly intelligent AD agents that can not only perceive but also deeply understand and reason about their surroundings. Future work could explore more advanced architectures (e.g., Mixture-of-Experts) and reinforcement learning frameworks that tightly couple perception accuracy with trajectory planning, pushing the boundaries of autonomous intelligence.

In conclusion, Percept-WAM offers a comprehensive and robust solution to fundamental challenges in autonomous driving. By unifying explicit spatial perception with VLM-based reasoning and action, it paves the way for a new generation of more capable, reliable, and safe autonomous driving systems.
