# C$^3$ache: Accelerating World Action Models with Cross Inference Chunk Cache

> [!summary] Summary
> C3ache accelerates World Action Models (WAMs) by introducing a training-free caching method that exploits computational redundancy across consecutive inference chunks. The approach achieves up to 2.5x wall-clock inference speedup on the LIBERO benchmark and 1.84x on RoboTwin, largely maintaining task success rates.

## Problem

- World Action Models (WAMs) provide strong generalization capabilities for robot control but suffer from high computational costs due to their iterative 'imagine-then-execute' inference paradigm.
- The multi-step denoising process within each WAM inference chunk, involving a Diffusion Transformer, results in significant latency, impeding real-time closed-loop robotic applications.
- Existing diffusion model acceleration techniques primarily focus on redundancy within a single denoising trajectory, overlooking potential computational consistency across successive inference chunks.

## Method

- **C3ache** introduces a training-free mechanism to cache and reuse the 'residual' (accumulated output of DiT blocks) from a previously computed inference chunk.
- For subsequent chunks, the cached residual is retrieved to directly reconstruct the final block output for initial denoising steps, bypassing the expensive DiT block computations.
- The method employs a cache refresh interval (τ) to update stored residuals and selectively applies caching only to earlier denoising steps where intermediate computations exhibit higher cross-chunk correlation.

## Results

- C3ache achieved up to a 2.5× wall-clock inference speedup on the LIBERO benchmark with negligible (e.g., +**0.20%**) change in average task success rate.
- On the RoboTwin benchmark, the method demonstrated up to a 1.84× wall-clock inference speedup, with one configuration yielding a **1.05%** drop in success rate and another showing a +**0.45%** increase at **1.56x** speedup.
- Extending caching to later denoising steps (e.g., [0, 8] or [0, 9]) led to sharp drops in success rate (e.g., **15.55%** on RoboTwin with τ=0), confirming that residuals are less stable and less suitable for caching at these stages.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Empirical analysis shows high cosine similarity between intermediate residuals of successive WAM inference chunks, indicating significant cross-chunk computational redundancy.
> - Caching the residual (R = h_L - h_0) allows new observations to influence the current output (h_L = h_0 + ˜R), providing a balance between computational savings and adaptability.
> - The stability of residuals across chunks diminishes in later denoising steps; therefore, selective caching of only the initial steps and an appropriate cache refresh interval are crucial for maintaining performance.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in this paper is situated within the broader landscape of generalist robot policies, specifically addressing computational efficiency challenges associated with World Action Models (WAMs).

Historically, Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models such as RT-2, OpenVLA, and π0 have been prominent in robot control. These models leverage semantic priors from large-scale image-text data. However, their generated behaviors are often constrained by the specific demonstrations they were trained on, limiting their generalization capabilities to novel motions or unseen environments.

World Action Models (WAMs) offer an alternative paradigm. By pairing action prediction with a video-modeling objective, WAMs learn to predict the evolution of a scene. This objective allows them to learn from vast quantities of unlabeled video data, which is more abundant than action-labeled robot demonstrations. Consequently, WAMs have demonstrated enhanced generalization capabilities to novel motions and unseen environments compared to VLAs of similar scale.

Despite their generalization benefits, WAMs face a significant computational challenge. Their "imagine-then-execute" inference paradigm involves synthesizing future frames through a video-diffusion process, which is computationally intensive. This process requires running multiple inference chunks, each involving a costly multi-step denoising process typically performed by a Diffusion Transformer (DiT). Such a high computational load can conflict with the latency requirements of closed-loop control systems.

Previous research efforts to accelerate diffusion models, and by extension WAMs, have primarily focused on exploiting redundancy *within a single inference chunk's denoising trajectory*. These methods vary in granularity:
- **Block-level caching:** DeepCache and Block Caching reuse entire network blocks across denoising steps.
- **Layer-level caching:** Learning-to-Cache identifies and skips specific layers.
- **Token-level caching:** ToCa caches only stable tokens.
- **Adaptive caching:** Methods like PAB adapt what is reused and when, or TeaCache and AdaCache tailor schedules to inputs.
- **Distillation:** Techniques like SnapFlow reduce the number of denoising steps required.
- **Autoregressive video diffusion:** Methods like Ca2-VDM leverage key-value caching across successive segments in long video generation.

The current work identifies and addresses a previously under-exploited source of redundancy: *redundancy across inference chunks*. While existing methods treat each inference chunk as an independent computational unit, this paper empirically demonstrates that intermediate computations, specifically residuals within the denoising process, exhibit strong correlations between successive chunks when a robot executes smooth, temporally coherent behaviors. This gap in existing acceleration strategies motivates the proposed C3ache method, which aims to leverage this cross-chunk redundancy without requiring model retraining. The concurrent work X Cache also identifies cross-chunk redundancy, but targets few-step distilled driving world models where inter-step redundancy is already minimized, distinguishing it from C3ache's focus on the standard multi-step denoising regime.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations for this research stem from the observed trade-off between the generalization capabilities of World Action Models (WAMs) and their significant computational cost during inference.

**Core Problem:**
WAMs exhibit superior generalization to novel motions and environments due to their video-modeling objective, which allows learning from abundant unlabeled video data. However, their inference process is computationally expensive. To complete a task, a WAM operates over multiple "inference chunks," and each chunk necessitates a multi-step denoising process implemented via a Diffusion Transformer (DiT). This iterative denoising, involving numerous forward passes of the action expert for each chunk, contributes to high latency, which can be prohibitive for real-time, closed-loop robot control.

**Motivation for Investigating Cross-Chunk Redundancy:**
Existing acceleration techniques for diffusion models and WAMs predominantly focus on exploiting redundancy *within* a single inference chunk's denoising trajectory. These methods target inefficiencies across denoising steps, layers, or tokens within one chunk. The authors observe that this localized focus overlooks a potentially substantial source of redundancy: consistency *across* successive inference chunks. When a robot executes a smooth, continuous behavior, the observed scene changes gradually, and the predicted actions evolve smoothly. This temporal coherence suggests that the internal computations of the WAM might also exhibit continuity from one chunk to the next.

**Specific Research Questions and Objectives:**
The paper explicitly poses two key questions that define its objectives:
1. **Does any redundancy exist in WAM across chunks?** The first objective was to empirically analyze the internal states and computations of a WAM to determine if significant correlations or similarities exist between consecutive inference chunks at corresponding denoising steps.
2. **Can we exploit this redundancy to accelerate WAM's inference?** The second objective was to design and implement a method that leverages any identified cross-chunk redundancy to reduce the computational cost of WAM inference, aiming for speedup without substantially degrading task performance.

**Motivation for Caching Residuals:**
Through empirical analysis (detailed in Section 3.2 of the paper), the authors specifically observe that the "residuals" (defined as the output of the Diffusion Transformer block stack minus its input embedding, R = h_L - h_0) computed at a given denoising step are strongly correlated from one inference chunk to the next. This observation is crucial because the computation of R constitutes the majority of the cost of a DiT forward pass. If an accurate estimate of R could be reused from a previous chunk, the expensive DiT blocks could be bypassed for the current chunk. Choosing to cache R over the final output (h_L) or velocity prediction (v) is motivated by the desire to allow the new observation from the current chunk (h_0) to still influence the final output, thereby maintaining some adaptivity while reducing computation.

**Overall Goal:**
The overarching goal of this work is to introduce C3ache, a training-free method designed to cache and reuse these cross-chunk residuals. This aims to significantly accelerate WAM inference, thereby making these generalized robot policies more practical and deployable in latency-sensitive applications, while preserving their task success rates.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The C3ache method is a training-free approach designed to accelerate World Action Models (WAMs) by exploiting redundancy across consecutive inference chunks. The methodology builds upon the principles of flow matching for action generation and the architecture of Diffusion Transformers (DiTs).

#### 3.1. Preliminaries of WAMs and Action Generation

- **Multi-chunk Action Generation:** WAMs generate actions in discrete chunks. At each control cycle *c*, an action expert predicts a chunk *x_c* of *H* future actions. The robot executes a portion of *x_c*, the environment updates, and a new observation forms the context (*ctx_c*) for generating the next chunk *x_c+1*. An entire episode comprises a sequence of *C* such inference chunks.
- **Flow Matching for Action Generation:** Each action chunk *x_c* is generated iteratively through *K* denoising steps. The action expert, *v_θ*, is a flow-matching model that learns to predict the velocity field connecting a noisy sample (ϵ_c ~ N(0, I)) to the clean action chunk *x_c* along a straight path. During inference, this ODE is integrated discretely. Starting from noise *x_c(0) = ϵ_c*, the model iteratively refines the estimate: *x_c(k) = x_c(k-1) + (t_k - t_k-1) v_c_k*, where *v_c_k* is the velocity prediction at step *k* of chunk *c*. The total cost for an episode is proportional to *C* × *K*, as each of the *C* chunks requires *K* full forward passes of the action expert, with no reuse of computation across chunks.
- **Action Expert as a Diffusion Transformer (DiT):** The action expert is structured as a DiT. The noisy action chunk is embedded into action tokens (*h_0*). Denoising time (*t*) is incorporated via adaptive normalization. These tokens then pass through *L* DiT blocks. Each block typically consists of an attention layer and a feedforward network, wrapped in residual connections. The attention mechanism is a joint attention (e.g., MoT), allowing action tokens to attend to both other action tokens and video tokens (from *ctx_c*). The velocity prediction is derived from the output of the final block, *h_L*, by a linear head.
- **Definition of Residual (R):** Due to the residual connections within DiT blocks, the output of the final block can be expressed as *h_L = h_0 + R*, where *R* represents the accumulated contribution of all *L* blocks (*R = h_L - h_0*). The authors term *R* the "residual of the action expert." The critical insight is that computing *R* constitutes the majority of the forward pass cost (running all *L* blocks). If *R* could be obtained without running the blocks, a denoising step could be performed with significantly reduced cost. This paper denotes the residual at denoising step *k* of chunk *c* as *R_c_k*.

#### 3.2. Key Empirical Observation

To motivate C3ache, an empirical analysis was conducted using Fast-WAM on the LIBERO benchmark, spanning 2,000 episodes and 29,924 inference chunks. The analysis focused on the cosine similarity between corresponding tensors (output of the final block *h_L*, the residual *R*, and the velocity prediction *v*) from *adjacent inference chunks* at the *same denoising step*.
- **High Cross-Chunk Correlation:** The results indicated high mean cosine similarity for *h_L*, *R*, and *v* across successive inference chunks at the same denoising step. This suggested that computations (particularly the residual *R*) from a previous chunk could be largely relevant for the current one, revealing a substantial source of redundancy.
- **Variability Across Denoising Steps:** The standard deviation of cosine similarity was observed to increase as the denoising process approached its end (i.e., at later denoising steps). This implies greater variability across chunks in the final stages of denoising, suggesting that caching might be less effective or even detrimental for these later steps.

#### 3.3. C3ache Mechanism

Based on these observations, C3ache was designed with the following components:
- **Choice of Cached Quantity:** The residual *R* was selected for caching, rather than *h_L* or *v*. This choice is strategic because caching *R* allows the current chunk's input embedding (*h_c',k'_0*) to still contribute to the final block output (*h_c',k'_L = h_c',k'_0 + ˜R_c,k*). This permits the new observation to influence the current chunk's computation while reusing the core transformation (*˜R_c,k*) from a previous chunk, offering a balance between efficiency and adaptability.
- **Caching Operation:** When a chunk *c* is designated as a "full-computation chunk" (i.e., when the cache needs to be refreshed), the action expert performs a complete forward pass for all *L* DiT blocks at each denoising step *k*. The residual, *∆R = h_c,k_L - h_c,k_0*, is computed and stored in the cache as *˜R_c,k*.
- **Reusing Operation:** For subsequent "cached chunks" (*c'*) within a defined refresh interval, the model skips the computationally expensive *L* DiT blocks for specific denoising steps *k'*. Instead, it retrieves the cached residual *˜R_c,k* (from the last full-computation chunk *c*) and directly reconstructs the final block output: *h_c',k'_L = h_c',k'_0 + ˜R_c,k*. This bypasses the most expensive part of the computation.
- **Cache Refresh Interval (τ):** A critical parameter is the refresh interval *τ*. Chunks are processed in groups of *τ*. Every *τ*-th chunk performs a full computation to update *˜R_c,k*, ensuring the cached residuals remain relevant to the evolving scene. For the *τ-1* chunks in between, the cached *˜R_c,k* is reused. The very first chunk (c=0) always performs a full computation to initialize the cache. If *τ=0*, the cache would never be refreshed.
- **Selective Caching of Denoising Steps:** In line with the empirical observation about increased variability at later denoising steps, C3ache only applies caching to a subset of the initial denoising steps (e.g., steps or), avoiding the tail steps where residuals are less stable across chunks.

This methodology provides a mechanism for significant computational savings by reusing prior computations, while the refresh schedule and selective caching aim to mitigate performance degradation by ensuring the cached information remains sufficiently up-to-date and reliable.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments evaluated C3ache on two benchmarks, LIBERO and RoboTwin, using a Fast-WAM backbone with 10 denoising steps and an action horizon of 32. The primary metrics were total wall-clock inference speedup and task success rate.

#### 4.1. Overall Performance and Speedup

- **LIBERO Benchmark:** C3ache achieved up to a **2.5× wall-clock inference speedup** compared to the Fast-WAM baseline. Notably, this speedup was attained with **negligible degradation in task success rate**, with some configurations showing no drop or even a slight increase in average success rate (e.g., +0.20% for cache steps with τ=0, achieving 2.51× speedup).
- **RoboTwin Benchmark:** C3ache demonstrated up to a **1.84× wall-clock inference speedup**. For this benchmark, a configuration achieving 1.84× speedup resulted in a 1.05% drop in average success rate. Another configuration, with a 1.56× speedup, showed a slight increase in success rate (+0.45%).

#### 4.2. Effect of Cache Steps and Refresh Interval

The experiments explored the impact of the range of denoising steps for which caching is applied ("cache steps") and the cache refresh interval (*τ*).

- **Cache Step Range:**
 - Initial experiments utilized cache step ranges (7 steps cached) and (8 steps cached). These ranges generally yielded positive speedups with acceptable success rates.
 - Ablation studies extending the cache step range to and resulted in sharp drops in success rate on both LIBERO and RoboTwin, irrespective of the refresh interval. This observation is consistent with the initial empirical analysis (Section 3.2), which showed increased variability in residual cosine similarity across chunks at later denoising steps. Caching these less stable later steps proved detrimental to performance.
- **Refresh Interval (τ):**
 - The refresh interval *τ* influenced the balance between speedup and success rate. A *τ* of 0 implies the cache is never refreshed after the first chunk, while larger *τ* values mean less frequent refreshes.
 - On LIBERO, using 7 cached steps, *τ*=0 provided the highest speedup (2.10×) with a slight success rate increase (+0.55%). For 8 cached steps, *τ*=0 yielded the highest speedup (2.51×) with a small success rate increase (+0.20%).
 - On RoboTwin, more frequent cache refreshes (smaller *τ*) were sometimes beneficial, particularly for the cache range. For instance, with 8 cached steps:
 - *τ*=0 led to a significant 15.55% drop in success rate, despite offering 1.68× speedup.
 - *τ*=4 significantly mitigated this drop to 2.50% while still providing 1.55× speedup.
 - This suggests that the optimal *τ* can be task-dependent.
 - **Impact of Task Length on Refresh Interval:** A more detailed analysis on RoboTwin revealed a monotonic trend:
 - For the shortest tasks (3–5 chunks per episode), a *τ*=0 (no refresh) maintained success rates.
 - For moderate-length tasks (6–11 chunks), *τ*=0 led to an average 10.81% drop.
 - For long tasks (12+ chunks), *τ*=0 caused a substantial 34% degradation. This indicates that longer-horizon episodes, where cached information can become stale over more steps, necessitate more frequent cache refreshes (smaller *τ* values).

#### 4.3. Implementation Details

- All reported speedups cover the full inference pipeline, including components not subject to caching (VAE encoding, video prefill for KV cache, action token embedding, projection to velocity predictions, and scheduler steps), providing a realistic measure of real-world efficiency gains.
- Experiments were conducted on a single NVIDIA A100 80GB GPU.

In summary, C3ache effectively exploits cross-chunk redundancy in WAMs, achieving substantial wall-clock inference speedups while largely preserving task success rates. The method's effectiveness is contingent on judicious selection of the cache step range (avoiding later, more variable steps) and tuning the refresh interval *τ* based on task characteristics, particularly task length.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The C3ache method contributes to the field of robot learning and generative modeling by addressing a critical computational bottleneck in World Action Models (WAMs), with several significant implications:

- **Novel Redundancy Exploitation:** The primary significance lies in the identification and effective exploitation of *cross-inference chunk redundancy*. Previous acceleration techniques for diffusion models and WAMs predominantly focused on redundancy *within* a single chunk's denoising process. C3ache reveals and leverages the temporal coherence in intermediate residual computations across successive chunks, representing a new avenue for efficiency gains that complements existing within-chunk methods.
- **Enhanced WAM Practicality:** WAMs offer strong generalization capabilities to novel motions and environments, a crucial feature for developing generalist robot policies. However, their high computational cost during inference has been a barrier to real-time deployment. By achieving significant wall-clock speedups (up to 2.5× on LIBERO and 1.8× on RoboTwin) with negligible degradation in task success rates, C3ache directly enhances the practicality and deployability of WAMs in latency-sensitive applications like closed-loop robotic control. This makes it more feasible to integrate highly generalized robot policies into real-world systems.
- **Training-Free Acceleration:** C3ache is a training-free method. It does not require modifications to the WAM's architecture or its training procedure. This characteristic makes it straightforward to integrate with existing and future WAM implementations, reducing development overhead and facilitating immediate application.
- **Applicability to Iterative Generative Models:** While specifically demonstrated for WAMs using Diffusion Transformers and flow matching, the underlying principle of caching and reusing residuals across sequential, iterative inference steps could potentially extend to other autoregressive or iterative generative models in domains beyond robotics, such as long-horizon video generation or other sequential decision-making tasks where temporal smoothness leads to cross-step computational redundancy.
- **Foundation for Future Optimization:** The paper highlights a limitation regarding the manual tuning of the refresh interval (τ). This observation points to a clear and important direction for future research: developing adaptive or dynamic refresh schedules. Such schedules could automatically adjust τ based on real-time factors like task difficulty, scene complexity, or the observed rate of change in the physical situation. This would further optimize the trade-off between speedup and success rate, moving towards more robust and universally applicable acceleration techniques.
- **Contribution to Sustainable AI/ML:** By significantly reducing the computational resources required for WAM inference, C3ache contributes to more energy-efficient and sustainable practices in the deployment of large AI models, particularly in resource-intensive fields like robotics.

In essence, C3ache marks a step towards making advanced, generalized robot learning models more efficient and viable for practical applications, broadening their potential impact on autonomous systems.
