# Stochastic Multi-Objective Kinodynamic Planning Against Adversaries

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at the University of Michigan developed a framework for multi-objective kinodynamic planning that balances execution cost and safety risk in stochastic, adversarial environments. Their Stochastic Multi-Objective Stable Sparse RRT (SMO-SST) algorithm consistently generated superior Pareto fronts, achieving lower minimum risk and cost while accurately accounting for reactive adversaries through closed-loop policy evaluation.

## Problem

- Existing motion planning methods for stochastic, adversarial environments are either computationally intractable for high-dimensional kinodynamic systems or lack formal probabilistic safety guarantees.
- Traditional chance-constrained planners rely on open-loop trajectory evaluations, leading to overly conservative risk estimates when dealing with reactive adversaries.
- Accurate risk evaluation is challenging due to complex, non-Gaussian interactions between an ego agent and stochastic hybrid adversaries, which analytical propagation methods struggle to model.

## Method

- The planning problem is formulated over sequences of ==closed-loop policies== and their execution durations, allowing the ego agent to react to state-dependent adversarial behavior.
- Empirical risk evaluation is integrated directly into the tree expansion via ==Monte Carlo particle rollouts==, simulating coupled continuous and discrete dynamics.
- **Stochastic Multi-Objective Stable Sparse RRT (SMO-SST)** is introduced, utilizing ==Pareto-aware pruning== and ==witness nodes== to construct an approximate Pareto front of policy sequences, optimizing for both execution cost and failure probability.

## Results

- SMO-SST significantly outperformed the baseline SMO-RRT, achieving a Hypervolume of **51.72±24.79** compared to **17.91±20.48**, indicating a substantially superior Pareto front of solutions.
- SMO-SST found policy sequences with lower minimum empirical failure probability (**0.14±0.15**) and lower minimum expected stage cost (**112.59±15.62**) than SMO-RRT.
- The framework provides finite-sample probabilistic correctness certificates for plans, ensuring that the estimated failure risk is a reliable upper bound on the true risk.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Planning in the space of closed-loop policies, rather than open-loop trajectories, enables more accurate and less conservative risk assessment against reactive adversaries.
> - Directly integrating sample-based Monte Carlo rollouts for risk evaluation is effective for capturing complex, non-Gaussian interactions with stochastic hybrid adversaries.
> - Sparse search tree structures with multi-objective dominance-based pruning (SMO-SST) can significantly improve the quality and diversity of the discovered Pareto front in multi-objective planning problems.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses the problem of provably safe motion planning in stochastic, adversarial environments, a domain with direct relevance to urban air mobility, self-driving cars, and social navigation. This field generally grapples with ensuring robustness against worst-case adversary behavior while maintaining computational tractability for high-dimensional kinodynamic systems.

Existing approaches to this problem exhibit specific limitations:
- **Formal methods (e.g., Hamilton-Jacobi reachability analysis):** These methods offer exact closed-loop policies and rigorous safety guarantees. However, they are computationally intensive due to the "curse of dimensionality," making them intractable for complex, high-dimensional kinodynamic systems.
- **Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL):** DRL can synthesize closed-loop policies efficiently at runtime in reactive, multi-agent environments. However, DRL-based approaches typically lack formal probabilistic safety guarantees. They also tend to be sample-inefficient during training and struggle with generalization to out-of-distribution adversary configurations, leading to potential failures in safety-critical applications.
- **Model-based continuous planning (sampling-based or optimization-based):** These methods offer scalability and aim for rigorous guarantees. Prior kinodynamic planners often manage uncertainty by assuming known Gaussian distributions, employing Kalman filtering, or modeling obstacles explicitly for Linear Time-Invariant (LTI) systems. Some approaches relax exact noise assumptions by bounding risk across ambiguity sets for systems with bounded disturbances. Multi-objective Monte Carlo methods evaluate chance constraints over control trajectories to construct Pareto-optimal plans when safety and performance are distinct objectives.

A critical limitation highlighted by the authors in existing chance-constrained kinodynamic planners is their reliance on evaluating risk by analytically propagating uncertainty over *open-loop nominal trajectories*. This paradigm is considered insufficient for environments with *reactive adversaries* because it fails to capture the coupled interaction between the ego agent's closed-loop tracking policy and the adversary's state-dependent responses. This oversight can lead to an artificially inflated estimation of constraint violation probability, resulting in overly conservative and potentially suboptimal plans.

This paper positions itself as a development aimed at bridging this gap by shifting the planning space to sequences of *closed-loop policies* and integrating sample-based risk evaluation directly into the tree construction process via Monte Carlo particle rollouts. This approach seeks to provide probabilistic guarantees for chance-constraint satisfaction while addressing the limitations of prior methods in handling reactive adversaries and multi-objective optimization.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop methods for multi-objective kinodynamic planning in stochastic, adversarial environments where adversaries probabilistically transition between modes based on the ego agent's state. Specifically, the goal is to construct the Pareto-front of paths that balance two conflicting objectives: execution cost and the probability of safety constraint violation (risk).

The core motivations for this work stem from the limitations of current planning methodologies:
- **Addressing Overly Conservative Planning:** Traditional chance-constrained planners evaluate risk over open-loop trajectories. This open-loop evaluation cannot account for the ego agent's real-time reactive capabilities in response to dynamic adversaries, leading to an overestimation of risk and consequently, overly conservative plans. The motivation is to overcome this by planning in a space that explicitly considers closed-loop policies.
- **Accurate Risk Evaluation in Dynamic Environments:** In environments with stochastic hybrid adversaries, the interactions are complex, state-dependent, and often non-Gaussian. Existing analytical propagation methods for uncertainty struggle to accurately model these coupled interactions. The authors are motivated to integrate empirical, sample-based risk evaluation through Monte Carlo particle rollouts directly into the planning process to capture these complex dynamics more faithfully.
- **Achieving Multi-Objective Optimization with Guarantees:** The problem inherently involves a trade-off between performance (cost) and safety (risk). The objective is to find a set of Pareto-optimal solutions that represent these trade-offs, providing the user with a spectrum of choices rather than a single solution. This needs to be done while providing formal probabilistic safety guarantees, a feature often missing in learning-based approaches.

To achieve these objectives, the authors articulate three main contributions:
1. **Closed-Loop Policy Space Formulation:** The planning problem is formulated over sequences of closed-loop policies and their execution durations, which is intended to mitigate the conservative behavior associated with open-loop trajectory evaluations.
2. **Empirical Risk Evaluation Integration:** Closed-loop particle rollouts are directly integrated into the tree expansion phase of the planning algorithms. This simulation of coupled, non-Gaussian interactions between the ego controller and adversaries is designed to enable finite-sample probabilistic correctness certificates for the plans.
3. **Multi-Objective Pareto Optimization:** The development of Stochastic Multi-Objective Stable Sparse RRT (SMO-SST) aims to construct a sparse search tree that explicitly maintains an approximate Pareto front of non-dominated policy sequences, thereby trading off execution cost and the probability of constraint violation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology involves defining a comprehensive system model, formulating the multi-objective planning problem, and introducing two sampling-based algorithms: Stochastic Multi-Objective RRT (SMO-RRT) and Stochastic Multi-Objective Stable Sparse RRT (SMO-SST).

**A. System Model**
- **Ego Agent Dynamics:** The ego agent is modeled as a discrete-time nonlinear system with affine disturbances: $x_{t+1} = \bar{f}_e(x_t, u_t) + G(x_t)w_t$. The state $x_t \in X \subset \mathbb{R}^n$, control $u_t \in U \subset \mathbb{R}^m$, and process noise $w_t \in W \subset \mathbb{R}^w$. Assumptions include compact $X$ and $U$, locally Lipschitz $f_e$, i.i.d. process noise $w_t$ with a bounded, locally Lipschitz PDF, and a full-row-rank noise injection matrix $G(x)$.
- **Adversary Model:** A team of $N$ adversaries is modeled as stochastic hybrid systems. Each adversary $i$ has a hybrid state $\xi_{i,t} = [m_{i,t}, z_{i,t}^\top, c_{i,t}]^\top$, comprising a discrete mode ($m_{i,t} \in \{IDLE, ACTIVE, TERMINAL\}$), a continuous physical state ($z_{i,t}$), and an accumulated cost ($c_{i,t}$). Continuous dynamics are mode-dependent. Mode transitions are governed by independent Bernoulli random variables $\nu_{i,t}$, with activation probability $\rho_{act}(x_t, \xi_{i,t})$ (from IDLE to ACTIVE) and termination probability $\rho_{term}(c_{i,t})$ (from ACTIVE to TERMINAL). The safe set $S(\xi_t)$ is defined as $X \setminus \bigcup S_{unsafe,i}(\xi_{i,t})$, where $S_{unsafe,i}(\xi_{i,t})$ is a closed region with a Lebesgue measure zero boundary.
- **Full System State:** The combined system state $y_t = [x_t^\top, \xi_{1,t}^\top, \ldots, \xi_{N,t}^\top]^\top$ evolves as $y_{t+1} = F(y_t, u_t, w_t, \nu_t)$. A metric $d_Y$ is defined on this state space, including a penalty for mode mismatch.

**B. Problem Formulation**
- **Policy Space:** Planning occurs over sequences of $K$ feedback controllers $\pi = \{\pi^{(i)}\}_{i=1}^K$, where each $\pi^{(i)}: Y \to U$, and corresponding execution durations $T = \{\tau^{(i)}\}_{i=1}^K$. The space of policies $\Pi$ is assumed to be a separable metric space with a probability measure with full support.
- **Multi-Objective Cost Function:** The objective is to minimize a vector-valued cost function $J(\pi, T) = [J_\eta(\pi, T), J_c(\pi, T)]^\top$:
 - **Failure Probability ($J_\eta$):** $1 - P(\bigwedge_{t=0}^T (x_t \in S(\xi_t)) \land (x_T \in X_g))$. This is the probability of violating a safety constraint or failing to reach the goal set $X_g$.
 - **Expected Execution Cost ($J_c$):** $E_{w,\nu}[\sum_{t=0}^{T-1} \ell(x_t, \pi^{(k_t)}(y_t)) + \ell_T(x_T)]$, where $\ell$ is the stage cost and $\ell_T$ is the terminal cost.
- **Optimization Problem:** The problem is formulated as minimizing $J(\pi, T)$ subject to $y_0 = y_{init}$ and a maximum tolerance on failure probability, $J_\eta(\pi, T) \leq \eta_{max}$.

**C. Algorithms**
The paper introduces two sampling-based algorithms:
1. **Stochastic Multi-Objective RRT (SMO-RRT):**
 - **Node Representation:** Each node $v$ in the rapidly-exploring random tree maintains a particle set $P_v = \{y_v^{(i)}\}_{i=1}^{N_{parts}}$ (representing the uncertainty in system state) and an expected physical state $x = E[h_x(P)]$.
 - **Tree Expansion:** Instead of sampling control inputs, the algorithm samples a feedback policy $\pi \in \Pi$ and an execution duration $\tau$.
 - **`Simulate` Subroutine:** For a candidate policy sequence, this subroutine performs a closed-loop Monte Carlo rollout. Each particle in the set $P_v$ is simulated independently. For each particle, the sampled policy $\pi$ determines the control input, and the joint continuous and discrete dynamics are propagated. Particles that violate environment safety constraints are pruned.
 - **Risk Evaluation and Pruning:** The empirical failure rate from the rollouts is used to construct a probabilistic upper bound $\bar{q}$ on the true failure risk $q$ (Theorem 3 provides a correctness certificate: $P(q \le \bar{q}) \ge 1-\delta_c$). If $\bar{q} > \eta_{max}$, the candidate policy is deemed unsafe and discarded.
 - **Goal:** The algorithm constructs a tree of valid policy sequences.

2. **Stochastic Multi-Objective Stable Sparse RRT (SMO-SST):**
 - SMO-SST extends SMO-RRT by incorporating mechanisms from Stable-Sparse Trees (SST) to improve performance, particularly by managing graph sparsity and maintaining a diverse Pareto front.
 - **Witness Nodes:** Unlike SMO-RRT, SMO-SST uses "Witness" nodes. Each witness node represents the best node found within a specific spatial radius $\delta_v$. In this multi-objective context, a witness maintains an additive $\delta_J$-approximate Pareto front of nodes within a spatial radius $\delta_s$.
 - **$\delta_J$-Dominance:** A new dominance relation, $J_A \prec_{\delta_J} J_B$, is introduced, where $J_A$ is considered to dominate $J_B$ if $J_A[k] \le J_B[k] - \delta_J[k]$ for all objectives $k$, and strictly less for at least one objective. This allows for a relaxed Pareto front, controlled by the parameter $\delta_J$.
 - **Pareto-Aware Pruning:** When a new node is generated, it is compared against existing representative nodes within its witness's influence. If the new node significantly improves the Pareto front (based on $\delta_J$-dominance), it becomes a representative, and previously dominated nodes are pruned (moved to $V_{inactive}$). This selective pruning aims to continually cull suboptimal branches.
 - **`ParetoBestNear` Function:** The selection of the parent node for expansion is modified to return the Pareto front of nodes within radius $\delta_v$ of a sample, from which a node is chosen uniformly at random to encourage diverse exploration.
 - **Completeness Trade-off:** The pruning mechanism in SMO-SST, which compares nodes based on expected ego state, may incorrectly compare nodes representing distinct stochastic realities, leading to a sacrifice of the probabilistic completeness guaranteed by SMO-RRT.

**D. Analysis**
The paper presents theoretical analysis to support the algorithms:
- **Continuity of Objective Function (Theorem 1):** Through a series of lemmas (Lemmas 1-4), the authors prove that the multi-objective cost mapping $J(\pi, T)$ is continuous with respect to the policy sequence metric $D$. This is crucial for applying sampling-based approximations.
 - Lemma 1 establishes bounded conditional trajectory divergence, showing that small differences in policies lead to bounded state divergence.
 - Lemma 2 proves the continuity of mode sequence probabilities.
 - Lemma 3 demonstrates the continuity of the expected cost functional.
 - Lemma 4 establishes the continuity of the chance constraint functional.
- **Probabilistic Completeness of SMO-RRT (Theorem 2):** Under strict feasibility assumptions, SMO-RRT is shown to be probabilistically complete, meaning that the probability of finding a strictly feasible policy sequence approaches 1 as the number of iterations $N$ tends to infinity. This relies on the existence of a feasible neighborhood (Corollary 1) and the property that the probability of extending a valid prefix sequence guarantees an infinite sum (Lemma 5).
- **Finite-Sample Policy Correctness Certificate (Theorem 3):** Based on prior work, a finite-sample bound $\bar{q}$ is derived for the probability of failure $q$ of a fixed policy sequence (based on $k$ failures in $n$ Monte Carlo rollouts). This certificate states that $P(q \le \bar{q}) \ge 1-\delta$, providing a formal justification for integrating empirical risk evaluation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation was conducted through simulations in an adversarial environment, with SMO-RRT used as a baseline to demonstrate the improvements of SMO-SST, as direct comparison with other chance-constrained planners was deemed inconsistent due to differing model assumptions (LTI systems, Gaussian noise vs. reactive, closed-loop adversaries).

**A. Experimental Setup**
- **Simulation Environment:** C++ simulations on an Apple M2 processor. The ego agent is a 2D discrete-time unicycle with state $[p_x, p_y, \theta, v]^\top$ and control $[a, \omega]^\top$. Process noise $w_t$ affects position and velocity.
- **Policy Space:** Model Predictive Path Integral (MPPI) controllers are used, parameterized by a reference target $x_{ref}$ in a $64 \times 64$ spatial domain. The MPPI controller employs 64 rollouts, a horizon of 20, control perturbation standard deviations $\sigma_a = 2.0$, $\sigma_\omega = 1.0$, and a temperature $\lambda = 1.0$. The stage cost penalizes deviation from $x_{ref}$ and proximity to active threats.
- **Adversary Model:** Eight static adversary installations are uniformly distributed. An IDLE adversary fires a homing projectile (ACTIVE mode) with a probability $p_{fire}(d)$ that depends on the ego agent's distance $d$. Projectiles pursue the ego at a constant speed until their lifetime budget is exceeded or they "capture" the ego (spatial distance $\le 0.25$). This model is inspired by a Basic Engagement Zone (BEZ) concept.
- **Parameters:** $N_{parts} = 256$ rollout particles for risk estimation, a planning budget of $N_{iter} = 200,000$ iterations, a maximum chance-constraint threshold $\eta_{max} = 0.5$, and a correctness margin $\delta_c = 0.05$. SMO-SST parameters: $\delta_v = 0.5$, $\delta_s = 0.25$, and $\delta_J = [0.01, 1.0]^\top$. SMO-RRT exploration bias: $\epsilon = 0.01$. Final trajectories were validated with $N_{eval} = 1000$ out-of-sample Monte Carlo simulations.

**B. Simulation Results**
The algorithms were evaluated using standard multi-objective optimization metrics over 10 independent Monte Carlo trials, with results presented as mean $\pm$ standard deviation in Table I.
- **Hypervolume (HV):** Quantifies the volume of the objective space strictly dominated by the estimated Pareto front (higher is better).
 - SMO-RRT: $17.91 \pm 20.48$
 - SMO-SST: $51.72 \pm 24.79$
- **Minimum Risk ($J_{\eta,min}$):** Minimum achieved empirical failure probability (lower is better).
 - SMO-RRT: $0.32 \pm 0.15$
 - SMO-SST: $0.14 \pm 0.15$
- **Minimum Cost ($J_{c,min}$):** Minimum expected stage cost (lower is better).
 - SMO-RRT: $171.04 \pm 29.21$
 - SMO-SST: $112.59 \pm 15.62$
- **Iterations to Solution ($N_{iters,sol}$):** Required planning iterations to discover an initial feasible solution (lower is better).
 - SMO-RRT: $6610 \pm 4042$
 - SMO-SST: $8040 \pm 4481$

**C. Interpretation**
Figure 1 graphically illustrates the findings, showing a representative spatial trajectory and the Pareto fronts generated by both planners.
- **Pareto Front Quality:** SMO-SST consistently discovered a Pareto front of policy sequences that dominated those found by SMO-RRT. This is quantitatively supported by the significantly higher Hypervolume value for SMO-SST, indicating that it covers a larger and more desirable region in the objective space.
- **Objective Minimization:** SMO-SST achieved lower minimum empirical risk ($J_{\eta,min}$) and lower minimum expected stage cost ($J_{c,min}$) compared to SMO-RRT. This indicates that SMO-SST was able to find policies that were both safer (lower failure probability) and more efficient (lower cost).
- **Computational Performance:** SMO-SST required a slightly higher number of iterations to find an initial feasible solution ($N_{iters,sol}$) compared to SMO-RRT. The authors note that this increase is an expected characteristic of SST-based implementations, as the algorithm dedicates computational resources to populate witness sets and perform aggressive pruning. However, this increased initial computational investment yielded demonstrably superior solutions.

The results collectively indicate that the Pareto-selection and witness-pruning mechanisms implemented in SMO-SST effectively improved multi-objective optimization performance, leading to a more diverse and globally dominant set of risk-cost trade-offs.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a framework for multi-objective kinodynamic planning that is designed to be applicable to complex, safety-critical autonomous systems operating in environments with sophisticated, reactive adversaries. The significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Improved Safety and Performance in Adversarial Environments:** By shifting the planning paradigm from open-loop trajectories to sequences of closed-loop policies, the proposed methods (SMO-RRT and SMO-SST) offer a more accurate and less conservative approach to risk assessment. This allows autonomous agents to react to state-dependent adversarial behavior, which is crucial in dynamic scenarios such as urban air mobility, self-driving cars, and social navigation. The ability to find a Pareto front of solutions offers designers the flexibility to choose control policies that balance safety and performance based on mission requirements.
- **Enhanced Realism of Risk Evaluation:** The integration of Monte Carlo particle rollouts directly into the tree expansion process enables the planner to account for non-Gaussian uncertainties and coupled interactions between the ego agent and stochastic hybrid adversaries. This empirical risk evaluation provides more realistic probabilistic safety guarantees, addressing a critical gap left by analytical methods that rely on simplifying assumptions.
- **Theoretical Foundations for Probabilistic Guarantees:** The establishment of continuity for the multi-objective mapping, probabilistic completeness for SMO-RRT, and finite-sample correctness certificates for empirical risk evaluation provides a formal theoretical basis for the proposed algorithms. These guarantees are important for deploying autonomous systems in real-world safety-critical applications.
- **Advancements in Sampling-Based Planning:** The development of SMO-SST, which incorporates Pareto-aware pruning and witness nodes, represents an advancement in sampling-based motion planning for multi-objective problems. Despite a potential trade-off in probabilistic completeness for the pruned graph (which is noted as an area for future work), SMO-SST demonstrably improves the quality and diversity of the discovered Pareto front, yielding superior solutions compared to a baseline RRT approach.
- **Broad Applicability:** The system and adversary models are general enough to encompass a wide range of adversarial scenarios, including those found in multi-agent systems, social navigation (e.g., pedestrian interactions), and autonomous driving (e.g., interactions with other vehicles or unexpected obstacles).

Future work, as noted by the authors, will focus on addressing the probabilistic completeness of the pruned SMO-SST graph by developing distribution-aware spatial metrics (e.g., Wasserstein metric) for node comparison and pruning. This could further solidify the theoretical guarantees while retaining the performance benefits of sparse tree structures.
