# Unsupervised Discovery of Failure Taxonomies from Deployment Logs

> [!summary] Summary
> Framework that discovers failure taxonomies from raw robot deployment logs without labels: VLM infers structured failure explanations from downsampled failure trajectories, then an LLM-optimizer clusters explanations via ensemble-and-refine aggregation into named, coherent failure modes. Beats BERTopic baselines and improves runtime monitoring and targeted data collection across manipulation, driving, and navigation.

## Problem

- Manual failure-log analysis in deployed robotic systems (autonomous vehicles, household robots) is time-consuming and does not scale to large volumes of real-world data.
- Prior language-based failure reasoning in robotics operates only at the individual-episode level, explaining single failures without discovering corpus-level structure.
- Simulation-based falsification methods find failures under predefined parameterizations but still require manual inspection to identify underlying causes, and generic text/image clustering ignores temporal, causal failure semantics.

## Method

- ==Semantic Observation Downsampling==: bidirectional CLIP-embedding change-point selection anchored at the failure frame, retaining only semantically distinct frames to compress trajectories within VLM context limits.
- **Failure Reasoning**: a VLM (Gemini 2.5 Pro) with chain-of-thought prompting infers a structured, grounded failure explanation per trajectory from perception, task context, and action plan.
- ==Taxonomy Aggregation==: an LLM-as-optimizer ensemble-and-refine procedure generates diverse clustering rephrasings then reconciles candidate taxonomies into one consolidated, semantically coherent set of named failure clusters.

## Results

- On RoboFail, taxonomy aggregation reaches Cluster Precision **0.920**, Taxonomy Coverage **1.000**, and Semantic Alignment Score **0.958**, beating BERTopic (**0.696**) and BERTopic-LLM (**0.875**).
- Trajectory-to-cluster assignment achieves **85.53%** weighted F1, far above an embedding-similarity baseline at **32.41%** F1; failure reasoning scores LLM-Judge **0.76** (sim) and **0.567** (real-world) with Gemini 2.5 Pro.
- Taxonomy-augmented runtime monitoring lifts OOD F1 to **77.9%** (driving) and **50.0%** (navigation) versus supervised classifiers and LLM-AD baselines; failure-guided data collection cuts navigation failure rate from **46%** to **18%** versus 34% for uniform collection.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Clustering in a semantic reasoning space (inferred explanations) rather than raw perceptual or lexical space recovers root-cause failure structure that keyword-based topic models like BERTopic miss.
> - General-purpose VLM reasoning generalizes better to unseen failure distributions than manipulation-specific fine-tuned models, which hallucinate and overfit to training viewpoints.
> - Injecting discovered failure-mode context into monitors and data-collection pipelines is a cheap, model-agnostic lever for closed-loop safety improvement without new detection or training architectures.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The integration of robotic systems into complex, unstructured real-world environments necessitates robust mechanisms for handling unexpected scenarios and failures. Traditional validation methods, primarily reliant on simulation or controlled testing, often fall short in capturing the full spectrum of real-world deployment challenges, leading to undetected failure modes. Learning from failures observed during deployment presents a promising avenue for improving system robustness.

Existing research in this domain can be categorized into several areas. **Semantic clustering of images** leverages vision-language models (VLMs) for grouping static image collections, either based on human-specified criteria or automatically discovered ones. However, these methods typically focus on perceptual similarity and do not directly address the temporal and causal nature of failure modes in robotic trajectories. **Text clustering and topic modeling** approaches, including extensions of classical probabilistic models and embedding-based methods, aim to discover abstract topics within text corpora. This work differentiates itself by operating on multimodal trajectories rather than standalone text and focusing on semantically coherent failure modes grounded in deployment logs.

**Failure mining in autonomous systems** largely involves falsification techniques, where systems are tested in simulated environments to provoke failures. These methods often require predefined parameterizations and subsequent manual inspection to ascertain root causes. The current work aims to automate the entire process, moving beyond predefined parameters and manual analysis by leveraging large-scale deployment data for unsupervised failure reasoning and categorization.

**Language-based failure reasoning in robotics** has seen recent advancements with the integration of Large Language Models (LLMs) to generate natural language explanations for individual failures in robotic tasks. These efforts typically focus on episode-level diagnostics and correction. In contrast, this research addresses the distinct problem of identifying and organizing corpus-level failure structures, specifically discovering and organizing *recurring failure modes* across entire datasets of deployment logs in an unsupervised manner, rather than just explaining isolated incidents.

This work contributes to the landscape by introducing an unsupervised framework that bridges these areas, enabling the automatic discovery of semantically coherent and actionable failure taxonomies directly from multimodal, failure-centered trajectories. This approach aims to overcome the scalability limitations of manual analysis and the specificity of supervised or simulation-based methods, providing a generalized solution for understanding and mitigating diverse failure types in autonomous systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an unsupervised method for discovering failure taxonomies from large volumes of raw, multimodal deployment logs collected from robotic systems. This involves identifying semantically coherent and actionable failure modes directly from perceptual trajectories without requiring predefined labels or extensive human annotation.

The motivation for this work stems from several challenges inherent in the deployment of autonomous systems:
- **Scalability of Failure Analysis:** As robotic systems are increasingly deployed in complex, unstructured real-world environments (e.g., autonomous vehicles, household robots), they inevitably encounter a diverse array of failures. Analyzing the vast quantities of perceptual data generated by these failures manually is time-consuming, expensive, and unscalable for human experts.
- **Limitations of Traditional Validation:** Existing validation pipelines, predominantly simulation-based or controlled testing, often fail to capture the long-tail edge cases and real-world complexities that lead to failures during actual deployment. This necessitates learning from real-world failure data.
- **Need for Actionable Insights:** While individual failure incidents provide data, the goal is to extract recurring patterns and underlying causes that can inform systematic improvements to system robustness and safety. Isolated, episode-level descriptions are less useful for guiding broad system enhancements.
- **Absence of Predefined Taxonomies:** In many real-world deployment scenarios, a comprehensive, expert-defined taxonomy of failure modes does not exist or is incomplete. The ability to automatically derive such a taxonomy from raw data is therefore valuable.
- **Enhancing Downstream Safety Workflows:** Discovered failure taxonomies are intended to provide structured knowledge that can be directly applied to improve system safety. Specific motivations include guiding targeted data collection for policy refinement, enabling more efficient and focused training efforts on critical failure scenarios, and enhancing runtime failure monitoring systems by providing context-aware early-warning signals.

In summary, the research aims to transform unstructured failure logs into organized, interpretable knowledge structures (taxonomies) that enable systematic and scalable learning from failures, thereby facilitating closed-loop safety improvements in autonomous systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework for unsupervised failure taxonomy discovery from multimodal deployment logs operates in three main stages: inferring structured failure explanations from perceptual sequences, aggregating these explanations into semantically coherent failure modes, and assigning individual trajectories to these discovered modes.

#### 3.1. Semantic Observation Downsampling

To efficiently process long failure trajectories while retaining critical causal context, the method employs a semantic observation downsampling technique. For each failure sequence, a temporal window of frames around the failure event is considered. This window includes both pre-failure buildup and post-event consequences.
- The process uses CLIP embedding function, $\phi(\cdot)$, to represent each frame as a vector in $\mathbb{R}^d$. Cosine similarity, $\text{sim}(i, j)$, is calculated between these embeddings.
- Downsampling is performed by anchoring at the failure frame and then bidirectionally selecting frames. A frame is retained only if its semantic similarity to the most recently selected frame in that direction falls below a threshold, $\tau_o$. This ensures that semantically redundant observations are removed, resulting in a compressed subsequence ($\tilde{o}^n_{1:M}$) that captures salient transitions and events, which is crucial for efficient VLM processing.

#### 3.2. Failure Reasoning

Each downsampled sequence is then provided to a Vision-Language Model (VLM) with a structured prompt. The prompt directs the VLM to summarize the scene and agent behavior throughout the trajectory and to infer a plausible cause for the failure, explicitly grounding its reasoning in the observed perceptual evidence. A Chain-of-Thought prompting strategy is adopted to encourage explicit intermediate reasoning steps, leading to a set of natural language failure reasons, $R = \{r_n\}^N_{n=1}$, for all $N$ failure instances. Gemini 2.5 Pro was selected for this stage due to its empirical reasoning performance.

#### 3.3. Failure Taxonomy Discovery via Semantic Aggregation

The core of taxonomy discovery involves clustering these failure explanations ($R$) into a set of $L$ distinct, semantically coherent failure modes. The objectives for this clustering are: (i) strong intra-cluster semantic coherence, (ii) minimal conceptual overlap between clusters, and (iii) comprehensive coverage of the entire set of explanations.
- An LLM is employed as an optimizer for the clustering task, generating clusters represented by a natural language name ($s_l$), a short textual description ($d_l$), and representative keywords ($\kappa_l$). The optimal number of clusters, $L$, is implicitly determined by the LLM based on the coherence criteria.
- To enhance robustness and handle large datasets, an "ensemble-and-refine" strategy is used. An initial clustering prompt is perturbed to generate multiple diverse rephrasings. Each rephrasing independently clusters the failure explanations into a candidate taxonomy. Subsequently, the LLM is prompted to reconcile these multiple candidate taxonomies into a single, consolidated aggregate taxonomy ($\{C\}^{\text{Aggregate}}$). This reconciliation process resolves inconsistencies, merges overlapping categories, and unifies semantic labels, leading to a more comprehensive and internally consistent output. OpenAI o4-mini was used for this stage.

#### 3.4. Assigning Trajectories to Failure Modes

Once the consolidated taxonomy is established, each individual failure trajectory is assigned to one or more of the discovered failure modes.
- Given a failure explanation ($r_n$) and the details of the aggregate taxonomy (cluster names $s_l$, descriptions $d_l$, and keywords $\kappa_l$), an LLM is prompted to map $r_n$ to the most appropriate cluster(s).
- Trajectories whose explanations do not align well with any existing cluster are flagged as outliers. These outliers may indicate rare, ambiguous, or novel failure types, providing opportunities for future taxonomy refinement. OpenAI o4-mini was also used for this assignment task.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The framework was evaluated across three robotic domains: robot manipulation, real-world car crash videos, and vision-based indoor robot navigation, assessing interpretability, consistency, and practical utility for downstream tasks.

#### 4.1. Case Study 1: Robot Manipulation (RoboFail Dataset)

This study used the RoboFail dataset, which includes expert-defined failure taxonomies, allowing for quantitative validation.

- **Validation of Failure Reasoning:**
 - The VLM (Gemini 2.5 Pro) was provided with perception inputs, task context, and robot action plans. It demonstrated strong semantic alignment with expert annotations, achieving a Cosine Similarity (CS) of 0.60, ROUGE_L of 0.2589, and an LLM-Judge (LLM-J) score of 0.76 on simulation data, indicating reliable failure explanation.
 - **Ablation on Reasoning Models:** Gemini 2.5 Pro generally outperformed other VLMs (LLaVA-NeXT, Qwen2.5-VL-7B, OpenAI o4-mini, Cosmos-Reason1-7B) in semantic alignment, especially with LLM-J scoring.
 - **Ablation on Downsampling Methods:** The proposed semantic observation downsampling consistently outperformed fixed-frame-rate sampling baselines across CS, ROUGE_L, and LLM-J metrics. This suggested that embedding-based change-point selection effectively preserves semantically informative transitions.
 - **General-Purpose vs. Fine-Tuned Models:** The general-purpose Gemini 2.5 Pro framework outperformed specialized, instruction-fine-tuned VLMs (AHA-13B, RoboFAC-7B) on unseen real-world RoboFail data, demonstrating better generalization and reduced hallucinated explanations.

- **Validation of Taxonomy Recovery:**
 - The method produced semantically coherent clusters corresponding to distinct root causes (e.g., "Manipulation & Control Failures," "Planning Parameter & Resource Selection Errors," "Perception & State Awareness Failures"). These clusters captured fine-grained distinctions and offered an interpretable organization of the dataset.
 - **Comparison with Baselines:** When compared to BERTopic and BERTopic-LLM, the proposed method yielded a sharper, diagonally dominant similarity matrix (Fig. 4), indicating a near one-to-one alignment with the expert-defined taxonomy. BERTopic variants produced broader, overlapping clusters.
 - **Quantitative Metrics:** The aggregated taxonomy achieved a Cluster Precision (CP) of 0.920, Taxonomy Coverage (TC) of 1.000, and a Semantic Alignment Score (SAS) of 0.958, outperforming single runs and both BERTopic baselines. This demonstrated high interpretability, consistency with expert knowledge, and distinct categories.

- **Validation of Trajectory-to-Cluster Assignment:**
 - The framework achieved a weighted F1 score of 85.53% for assigning individual failure episodes to the obtained taxonomy. This significantly outperformed an embedding-similarity baseline (32.41% F1), confirming the taxonomy's discriminative power and practical utility for consistent categorization.

#### 4.2. Case Study 2: Real-World Car Crash Videos (Nexar Dataset)

Applying the framework to the Nexar car crash dataset, which lacks expert annotations, yielded a semantically coherent taxonomy of recurring driving failure modes (e.g., "Rear End Collisions," "Intersection Right of Way Violations," "Unsafe Cut In / Lane Change Intrusions"). Qualitatively, these clusters aligned with established pre-crash typologies from traffic safety research (e.g., U.S. DoT Volpe Center), demonstrating the framework's ability to recover semantically grounded failure categories directly from raw deployment video logs without predefined labels.

#### 4.3. Case Study 3: Vision-Based Indoor Robot Navigation

For a vision-based ground robot navigating indoor office environments, the method discovered interpretable collision failure modes such as "Thin Protruding Objects," "Uniform/Featureless Surfaces," and "Narrow Gap Misjudgments." Several of these aligned with failure types previously identified manually (e.g., protruding corners, glass doors, featureless walls), indicating the framework can rediscover known causes and organize new patterns into a coherent taxonomy.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a framework for the unsupervised discovery of failure taxonomies from multimodal deployment logs, addressing the critical challenge of scalability in analyzing complex robotic system failures. Its significance and potential impact are multifaceted:

- **Scalability and Automation:** The primary contribution is automating the identification and organization of recurring failure patterns. By transforming raw, unstructured failure trajectories into structured, semantically coherent explanations and taxonomies, the framework mitigates the limitations of manual failure analysis, which is time-consuming and unscalable for large-scale deployments.
- **Interpretability and Actionability:** The discovered failure modes are presented in natural language with descriptions and keywords, making them inherently interpretable by human developers and engineers. This interpretability facilitates a deeper understanding of underlying failure causes, enabling more targeted and effective interventions.
- **Foundation for Closed-Loop Safety Improvements:** The work demonstrates how these taxonomies provide actionable structure for downstream safety workflows:
 - **Runtime Failure Monitoring:** Augmenting VLM-based runtime monitors with taxonomy-level context improved failure detection (higher F1 scores on both in-distribution and out-of-distribution samples) and enabled earlier anticipation of failures. This capability can serve as an early-warning signal, potentially triggering safeguard policies to prevent accidents, as demonstrated in the glass door scenario.
 - **Targeted Data Collection and Policy Refinement:** The discovered failure modes can guide focused data collection in high-risk environmental regions (e.g., areas causing "Featureless Walls" collisions). This targeted approach leads to more efficient policy refinement, significantly reducing failure rates compared to uniform data collection, thereby accelerating robustness improvements.
- **Generalizability Across Domains:** The successful application across diverse domains—robot manipulation, autonomous driving, and indoor navigation—suggests the framework's generalizability and applicability to a wide range of autonomous systems operating in varied environments.
- **Unsupervised Nature:** The unsupervised nature of the approach is crucial, as it eliminates the need for costly and time-consuming human annotation of failure data or pre-definition of failure categories, making it practical for real-world deployment where labeled failure data is scarce.

Despite these advancements, the authors acknowledge limitations, such as the potential for variations in taxonomies based on different reasoning strategies and the possibility of plausible but incorrect VLM explanations. Future work plans to incorporate causal validation and hierarchical clustering architectures to address these. Overall, this research offers a practical and generalizable method to systematically learn from real-world failures, contributing directly to enhancing the robustness, reliability, and safety of autonomous systems.
