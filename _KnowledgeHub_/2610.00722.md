# JEPA-TTT: Persistent Test-Time Training of Latent World Models for Planning under Dynamics Shifts

> [!summary] Summary
> JEPA-TTT adapts a pretrained action-conditioned JEPA world model to changed transition dynamics during deployment. It updates only the latent dynamics predictor with a self-supervised latent prediction loss and keeps the encoder and offline-trained reward head frozen. Dense replay over a buffer that persists across episodes drives the updates, and CEM planning needs no goal image or online reward. Across eight shifts, planning improves 153% and latent prediction error drops 83%.

## Problem

- Conventional world models assume the same transition dynamics at training and test time, so changes in actuators, contacts or object motion cause systematic prediction errors that corrupt planning.
- Prior action-conditioned JEPA world models such as V-JEPA 2-AC, DINO-WM, PLDM and LeWorldModel study offline training and planning, not persistent adaptation to a new dynamics regime during deployment.
- Concurrent AdaJEPA resets its adapted model and buffer every episode, needs a goal image and learns only from short within-episode history, so adaptation never accumulates.

## Method

- ==Predictor-only test-time training==: a ==stop-gradient latent MSE loss== updates the action encoder and autoregressive predictor from executed transitions, while the visual encoder and reward head stay frozen.
- ==Offline-trained reward head==: it regresses task score from frozen encoder features, so ==CEM== ==model predictive control== can rank candidate action sequences by discounted predicted return without goal images or online rewards.
- ==Dense replay with persistence==: prediction windows start at every environment step, about K times more than block-boundary windows, and predictor, optimizer state and buffer all carry across episodes.

## Results

- Across eight shifts in PushT, Two-Room, Reacher and OGBench-Cube, mean best held-out score rises from **0.267** (frozen) to **0.678**, beating PPO-TTT at **0.307** and AdaJEPA at **0.297**.
- After 500 test-time episodes, five-block autoregressive latent MSE falls **83%** on average (**45-99%** per shift), and best score nears a 3,000-episode offline reference on six of eight shifts.
- Ablations: dense windows lift mean AUC from **0.495** (compute-matched sparse) to **0.548** and replay lifts it to **0.571**; persistence raises best score from **0.289** to **0.729** at equal updates.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Freezing the encoder and reward head while adapting only dynamics confines test-time learning to predicting consequences, which gives a stable target and avoids representation drift during online updates.
> - Persistence matters more than clever per-episode adaptation: carrying predictor and optimizer state across episodes more than doubled planning performance for the same number of updates.
> - How online data is organized, through dense temporal offsets plus replay, matters beyond extra gradient steps; forgetting and simultaneous visual or reward shifts remain untested.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Overview

When an action has different consequences at deployment than during training, a world model can mispredict what will happen and lead its planner toward the wrong action. In a representative PushT example, a 90° action rotation changes what a command does. The frozen model's predicted ranking favors plan A, but the ground-truth comparison favors plan B. After the model learns from what the agent did and observed, and retains that learning across episodes, it predicts the better ordering. This is an illustrative example. It does not show that adaptation fixes every plan or task.

Selected qualitative examples of test-time training correcting the ranking of candidate plans. Plan A was optimized with Frozen JEPA and plan B with JEPA-TTT, both from the same initial state. The frozen model ranks A above B, although ground truth ranks B above A. JEPA-TTT recovers the correct ordering. Both planners use the same frozen reward head, and a pretrained decoder is used only to visualize predictions.

### 2. Learn from the transition

The agent predicts and acts, observes what happened, and uses that transition to improve its predictions for future planning. The method is called JEPA-TTT. Its world model is an action-conditioned JEPA (Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture). The model predicts future *latent representations*, which are compact encoded states, rather than reconstructing pixels.

A model-predictive-control planner uses the current predictor and a fixed head to score candidate action sequences. It executes the first action block, observes the result, and then replans. JEPA-TTT updates only its dynamics predictor. Its visual encoder and offline-trained reward head stay frozen. The observed transition provides a self-supervised target: the predictor is trained to match the frozen encoder's representation of the next observation.

Online environment rewards and goal images are not inputs to planning or test-time training. The scoring head is trained from task labels collected offline. Predictor parameters, optimizer state, and the replay buffer persist across episodes.

### 3. Persistence, not just more gradient steps

One concern is that persistent adaptation might only reflect taking more gradient steps. The paper's matched persistence control holds the number of predictor updates fixed. In that control, carrying predictor and optimizer state across episodes, rather than resetting them, raises mean best score from **0.289 to 0.729** and mean AUC from **0.273 to 0.637**. This is a separate control, not the main dense-replay protocol. The result supports the value of persistence under the tested setup. It does not establish that persistence alone explains all main-result gains.

### 4. Planning recovers across shifts

The aggregate planning evidence covers **eight shifts in four continuous-control environments**, after deployments of 500 test-time episodes. Two measures are reported:

- **Best held-out score** is the maximum of the three-run mean across evaluations at episodes 50 through 500.
- **Normalized AUC** is the per-run area under held-out score from episode 0 to 500, divided by 500 and averaged over runs.

JEPA-TTT's mean best score is **0.678 versus 0.267** for frozen JEPA. Its mean AUC is **0.571 versus 0.267**. It improves both metrics over the frozen model on every shift and outperforms the reported baselines on every shift for both metrics. One of those baselines, PPO-TTT, adapts with oracle online environment rewards, unlike JEPA-TTT.

Prediction improves alongside planning. On fixed trajectories under the shifted dynamics, both models receive the same observed context and future actions. Each predicts five latent states without intermediate observations. After 500 episodes, five-block autoregressive latent-prediction MSE falls by **83% on average**. This is consistent with improved dynamics predictions supporting better planning. It does not prove that prediction error is the only cause of the planning gains.

Planning performance across eight shifts: (a) best held-out score, (b) normalized score AUC. Bars and error bars are means and standard deviations over three deployment runs. PPO-TTT uses oracle online environment rewards.

### 5. A model that keeps learning

JEPA-TTT updates only the latent predictor and allows adaptation to accumulate over time. The experiments cover one dynamics change that remains fixed during test time. Simultaneous visual or reward shifts, sequences of dynamics changes, returning to earlier dynamics, and catastrophic forgetting are untested.
