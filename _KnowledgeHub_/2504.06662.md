# RAMBO: RL-Augmented Model-Based Whole-Body Control for Loco-Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> The RAMBO framework combines model-based whole-body control with reinforcement learning to enable robust and precise loco-manipulation for legged robots. This hybrid approach achieved significantly lower end-effector position errors, reaching 0.039m in quadruped tasks, and successfully performed dynamic tasks like bipedal cart pushing on a Unitree Go2 robot.

## Problem

- Legged robots face significant challenges in 'loco-manipulation' tasks, which demand both coordinated locomotion and precise physical interaction with objects.
- Model-based controllers, while precise, are fragile to model inaccuracies and unmodeled disturbances in real-world environments.
- Purely learning-based (RL) methods, despite their robustness, often struggle with fine-grained end-effector precision and can be sample-inefficient for complex tasks.

## Method

- **RAMBO** integrates a computationally efficient model-based ==Whole-Body Controller== (WBC), formulated as a ==Quadratic Program== (QP), to generate precise feedforward joint torques and optimize contact forces.
- A learned Reinforcement Learning (RL) policy provides adaptive feedback corrections, specifically modifying base acceleration targets for the QP and supplying additive corrections to desired joint positions.
- The final joint torque command combines feedforward torques, gravity compensation, and a PD control term based on the RL-corrected desired joint positions.

## Results

- RAMBO achieved significantly lower end-effector position errors in simulation (e.g., **0.039m** for quadruped, **0.036m** for biped) compared to various purely RL or model-based baselines.
- Successfully demonstrated dynamic loco-manipulation tasks on a Unitree Go2 quadruped robot in real-world scenarios, including three-legged walking while holding objects and bipedal walking while pushing a shopping cart.
- The framework exhibited robustness against external disturbances, uneven terrains, and enabled compliant physical interactions, such as an interactive "handshake," highlighting its adaptability and potential for safe human-robot collaboration.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Combining the analytical precision of model-based optimization with the adaptability and resilience of reinforcement learning effectively addresses the trade-offs in loco-manipulation.
> - The learned feedback policy significantly enhances robustness against model inaccuracies and unmodeled disturbances, which is crucial for real-world deployment.
> - Providing corrective feedback at the joint position level, rather than solely at the base acceleration level, proved more effective in improving end-effector tracking precision for manipulation tasks.
