# RAMBO: RL-Augmented Model-Based Whole-Body Control for Loco-Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> The RAMBO framework combines model-based whole-body control with reinforcement learning to enable robust and precise loco-manipulation for legged robots. This hybrid approach achieved significantly lower end-effector position errors, reaching 0.039m in quadruped tasks, and successfully performed dynamic tasks like bipedal cart pushing on a Unitree Go2 robot.

## Problem

- Legged robots face significant challenges in 'loco-manipulation' tasks, which demand both coordinated locomotion and precise physical interaction with objects.
- Model-based controllers, while precise, are fragile to model inaccuracies and unmodeled disturbances in real-world environments.
- Purely learning-based (RL) methods, despite their robustness, often struggle with fine-grained end-effector precision and can be sample-inefficient for complex tasks.

## Method

- **RAMBO** integrates a computationally efficient model-based ==Whole-Body Controller== (WBC), formulated as a ==Quadratic Program== (QP), to generate precise feedforward joint torques and optimize contact forces.
- A learned Reinforcement Learning (RL) policy provides adaptive feedback corrections, specifically modifying base acceleration targets for the QP and supplying additive corrections to desired joint positions.
- The final joint torque command combines feedforward torques, gravity compensation, and a PD control term based on the RL-corrected desired joint positions.

## Results

- RAMBO achieved significantly lower end-effector position errors in simulation (e.g., **0.039m** for quadruped, **0.036m** for biped) compared to various purely RL or model-based baselines.
- Successfully demonstrated dynamic loco-manipulation tasks on a Unitree Go2 quadruped robot in real-world scenarios, including three-legged walking while holding objects and bipedal walking while pushing a shopping cart.
- The framework exhibited robustness against external disturbances, uneven terrains, and enabled compliant physical interactions, such as an interactive "handshake," highlighting its adaptability and potential for safe human-robot collaboration.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Combining the analytical precision of model-based optimization with the adaptability and resilience of reinforcement learning effectively addresses the trade-offs in loco-manipulation.
> - The learned feedback policy significantly enhances robustness against model inaccuracies and unmodeled disturbances, which is crucial for real-world deployment.
> - Providing corrective feedback at the joint position level, rather than solely at the base acceleration level, proved more effective in improving end-effector tracking precision for manipulation tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of legged robotics has seen remarkable advancements in mobility over diverse and challenging terrains. However, extending these capabilities to "loco-manipulation"—tasks requiring coordinated locomotion and active physical interaction with objects—remains a significant hurdle. This challenge arises because loco-manipulation demands both precise end-effector (EE) control and inherent robustness against unmodeled dynamics and disturbances, often presenting conflicting control objectives.

Currently, two dominant paradigms address robotic control: model-based methods and learning-based methods. Model-based controllers, such as Model Predictive Control (MPC) and Whole-Body Control (WBC), leverage explicit physical models to achieve precise planning and optimal torque-level commands. They excel at accounting for contact forces and generating stable, predictable motions. However, their performance heavily relies on the accuracy of the system dynamics model and careful parameter identification. They struggle with uncertainties, unmodeled environmental interactions, and dynamic disturbances, where model inaccuracies can lead to degraded performance or instability. Simpler models like the Single Rigid Body (SRB) can be computationally efficient but sacrifice accuracy, while more complex models increase computational burden.

In contrast, learning-based approaches, particularly those using Reinforcement Learning (RL), have demonstrated impressive robustness in various locomotion and manipulation tasks. By training policies in physics-based simulators with techniques like domain randomization, RL-based controllers can learn adaptive behaviors that are resilient to unknown dynamics and unexpected interactions. However, pure RL approaches face their own set of limitations in loco-manipulation. They often operate in joint position space, leading to policies that produce excessively large target positions to generate sufficient interaction forces, thereby prioritizing robustness at the expense of precision in end-effector force and position control. Training these policies can also be sample-inefficient due to the vast exploration space, often requiring complex reward shaping or motion priors. Direct torque-based RL policies exist but are notoriously difficult to train and require high-frequency updates.

RAMBO critically addresses this existing gap by proposing a hybrid framework that synergistically combines the strengths of both paradigms while mitigating their individual weaknesses. It builds upon prior work that has explored integrating model-based components (e.g., centroidal models) with learned policies but specifically focuses on using a computationally efficient Whole-Body Controller (WBC) for feedforward torque generation and an RL policy for corrective feedback. This approach stands out by explicitly generating torque-level commands through optimization, ensuring precision, while a learned feedback policy enhances robustness without requiring the policy to directly manage all complex dynamics. This framework represents a significant step towards enabling legged robots to perform robust, precise, and efficient loco-manipulation in real-world, unstructured environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The ultimate objective of this research is to empower legged robot controllers with the ability to perform robust, precise, and efficient whole-body loco-manipulation. This entails enabling robots to simultaneously execute complex locomotion patterns while precisely interacting with and manipulating their surroundings.

The core motivation stems from the inherent trade-offs and limitations of purely model-based and purely learning-based control approaches for loco-manipulation tasks:
- **Addressing Model-Based Limitations:** Model-based controllers, while precise, are highly susceptible to model inaccuracies, unmodeled disturbances, and environmental variations. Real-world scenarios are inherently complex and difficult to model perfectly. The authors aim to overcome this fragility by introducing a robust feedback mechanism.
- **Enhancing Learning-Based Precision:** Purely learning-based methods, particularly those using RL, often excel in robustness but struggle with the fine-grained control of interaction forces and precise end-effector positioning, which are critical for many manipulation tasks. They tend to achieve robustness by aggressive, high-magnitude actions that can compromise precision. The motivation is to imbue learning-based systems with the precision typically associated with model-based methods.
- **Synergistic Integration:** The primary motivation is to combine the best features of both worlds: leveraging the analytical rigor and precise torque control capabilities of model-based optimization, while simultaneously harnessing the adaptability and resilience to unmodeled effects offered by reinforcement learning.

To achieve this, the paper's key objective is to introduce RAMBO, a hybrid control framework designed to:
1. **Generate precise feedforward torques:** By employing a model-based Whole-Body Controller (WBC) formulated as a Quadratic Program (QP), which explicitly optimizes end-effector contact forces. This ensures precise, physically consistent torque commands.
2. **Provide robust feedback corrections:** By training a reinforcement learning (RL) policy that generates corrective terms. These corrections compensate for any discrepancies between the simplified model and real-world dynamics, unmodeled disturbances, and errors in state estimation, thereby enhancing the overall robustness of the system.
3. **Validate on diverse real-world tasks:** To demonstrate the framework's effectiveness across a variety of challenging loco-manipulation tasks on a physical quadruped robot, encompassing both quadrupedal and bipedal locomotion, and involving object pushing, balancing, and holding.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

RAMBO (RL-Augmented Model-Based WhOle-body Control) is structured as a hybrid control framework comprising three primary components: motion reference generation, feedforward torque calculation via Whole-Body Control (WBC), and feedback correction by a learned Reinforcement Learning (RL) policy.

**A. Motion Reference Generation:**
The framework begins by generating a kinematic motion reference for both the robot's base and its joints. This reference is primarily derived from user commands, including desired base velocities (forward, side, turning) expressed in the robot's projected frame {P}, and desired end-effector (EE) positions for manipulation limbs. For locomotion, predefined gait patterns dictate contact schedules and swing foot trajectories. These trajectories are generated using a heuristic strategy based on cubic Bézier curves connecting keyframe positions (lift, mid-air, land) for each leg. Inverse kinematics (IK) is then used to translate these desired base and EE positions into desired joint positions ($\hat{q}_j$). Unspecified references default to current states or zeros, providing a flexible interface for varied tasks.

**B. Generating Feedforward Torque via Whole-Body Control (WBC):**
To ensure precise control and account for the dynamic interactions, RAMBO utilizes a computationally lightweight Whole-Body Controller based on a Single Rigid Body (SRB) model. The SRB model simplifies the robot's dynamics by assuming most mass is concentrated in the base and limbs are massless, prioritizing computational efficiency over full dynamic fidelity.
1. **Base Acceleration Target:** Desired linear and angular accelerations for the robot's base ($\hat{a}$) are computed using proportional-derivative (PD) controllers that track the commanded base position, orientation, linear velocity, and angular velocity references.
2. **Quadratic Program (QP) Formulation:** A QP is formulated to optimize the contact reaction forces ($\mathbf{B}u_i$) at all end-effectors. The objective function minimizes the tracking error of the desired base acceleration and desired EE forces (for manipulation limbs), while also regularizing the forces exerted by locomotion limbs. Constraints are crucial:
 - **Dynamic Consistency:** The sum of all contact forces and torques must generate the desired base acceleration (Eq. 10b).
 - **Contact State:** Swing feet are constrained to have zero vertical force, while stance feet must satisfy friction cone constraints (Eq. 10d, 10e) and force limits (Eq. 10f).
3. **Feedforward Joint Torques:** The optimal reaction forces ($\mathbf{B}u_i$) obtained from the QP are then transformed into feedforward joint torques ($\tau_{FF_j}$) using the Jacobian transpose of each end-effector (Eq. 11). An additional term ($\tau_{GC_j}$) is calculated to compensate for gravity and limb inertia effects for each joint.

**C. Learned Policy (Reinforcement Learning):**
The purely model-based WBC, despite its precision, can be limited by the SRB model inaccuracies and unmodeled real-world dynamics. To address this, RAMBO augments the WBC with a learned feedback policy trained using reinforcement learning.
1. **Markov Decision Process (MDP):** The problem is framed as an MDP. The observation space (O) is comprehensive, including proprioceptive information (base height, velocities, joint states), gait phase, desired joint positions, user velocity commands, and EE position/force commands. A 6-step history of observations is stacked to provide temporal context.
2. **Action Space (A):** Critically, the RL policy's action space is split into two separate heads:
 - **Base Acceleration Correction ($\Delta \mathbf{P}\hat{a}$):** An additive correction to the base acceleration target fed into the QP.
 - **Joint Position Correction ($\Delta\hat{q}_j$):** An additive correction to the desired joint positions, which are then used in the final PD control loop.
3. **Final Torque Command:** The final joint torque command sent to the robot is a sum of the feedforward torques from WBC and gravity compensation, plus a PD control term based on the *corrected* desired joint positions: $\tau_j = \tau_{FF_j} + \tau_{GC_j} + k_p(\tilde{q}_j - q_j) + k_d(\dot{\hat{q}}_j - \dot{q}_j)$, where $\tilde{q}_j = \hat{q}_j + \Delta\hat{q}_j$.
4. **Reward Function:** The reward function is designed as a product of task-related sub-rewards (tracking base height, orientation, linear/angular velocity, EE position) and regularization terms (contact mismatch, joint acceleration/torque, action rate/scale). This encourages precise task execution while promoting smooth, stable, and energetically efficient movements.
5. **Training and Deployment:** The policy is trained using Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) within Isaac Lab, a massively parallel GPU simulation framework. Differentiable QP solvers (qpth) are used during training, while a faster solver (OSQP) is employed for real-time deployment on hardware at 100 Hz. Robustness and sim-to-real transfer are ensured through extensive domain randomization (e.g., friction, mass, CoM offset, actuator delays, state noise, external pushes) and the application of virtual external forces during training to simulate manipulation loads.

This hybrid approach allows the model-based WBC to handle the primary dynamics and force optimization, while the RL policy learns to provide adaptive, robust corrections for unforeseen complexities, effectively combining precision with resilience.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

RAMBO demonstrates significant advancements in whole-body loco-manipulation capabilities, validated through extensive simulation and real-world experiments on a Unitree Go2 quadruped robot. The results highlight its ability to achieve both precise end-effector control and robust dynamic locomotion across diverse tasks and locomotion modes.

**Demonstrated Capabilities:**
RAMBO was successfully applied to various challenging loco-manipulation tasks:
- **Quadrupedal Tasks:** The robot performed three-legged walking while using its front-left leg for manipulation tasks such as holding soft objects and balancing a plate.
- **Bipedal Tasks:** The robot walked solely on its hind legs in an upright posture, utilizing both front legs for manipulation, including pushing a shopping cart and holding dice. This bipedal demonstration showcases the framework's potential for humanoid robots.

**Quantitative Evaluation (Simulation):**
A rigorous comparison was conducted in simulation against several baselines, focusing on tracking errors for base linear velocity, base angular velocity, and end-effector (EE) position. Baselines included:
- **Vanilla:** End-to-end RL policies trained to track commands.
- **Imitation:** RL policies tracking kinematic references.
- **Residual:** RL policies producing joint position residuals without WBC.
- **RAMBO-base:** WBC with RL policy providing only base acceleration correction.
- **RAMBO-joint:** WBC with RL policy providing only joint position correction.
- **RAMBO-ff:** WBC only (no RL).

The results, summarized in Table V, consistently showed that **RAMBO achieved comparable or superior performance across all metrics**, particularly exhibiting a **clear and significant advantage in tracking target end-effector positions**. For example, in quadruped tasks, RAMBO achieved an EE position error of 0.039m, substantially lower than Vanilla (0.254m), Residual (0.077m), RAMBO-base (0.168m), and RAMBO-ff (0.286m). Similar improvements were observed in bipedal tasks (RAMBO: 0.036m vs. Vanilla: 0.431m). This indicates RAMBO's superior precision in manipulation while maintaining robust locomotion.

Key insights from the ablations and comparisons:
- **Importance of Learned Feedback:** RAMBO-ff (WBC only) showed significantly higher tracking errors, especially for EE position, underscoring the necessity of the learned feedback policy to compensate for model inaccuracies and enhance robustness.
- **Effectiveness of Joint-Level Correction:** RAMBO-joint outperformed RAMBO-base, highlighting that providing corrective feedback at the joint position level is more effective for reducing EE tracking error than only modifying base acceleration targets.
- **Baseline Limitations:** Vanilla and Imitation policies struggled to balance precision and robustness, often sacrificing EE tracking for stability. Residual policies, while better than vanilla, failed to generate sufficient contact forces for stable bipedal walking without the WBC component.

**Real-world Experiments:**
The efficacy of RAMBO translated successfully to hardware demonstrations. The robot reliably executed dynamic loco-manipulation tasks such as pushing a shopping cart and holding a sponge while walking (Fig. 5). Furthermore, RAMBO demonstrated remarkable **robustness against external disturbances and uneven terrains** in both quadrupedal and bipedal modes (Fig. 6), indicating successful sim-to-real transfer and the adaptability of the learned policy.

**Compliance:**
A notable feature enabled by RAMBO is its ability to allow for **compliance**. By lowering the PD gains at manipulation joints, the robot can maintain its end-effector position and stability while walking, even when subject to external physical pushes. This was demonstrated through an interactive "handshake" (Fig. 8), showcasing RAMBO's capacity for safe and adaptive physical interaction, a crucial aspect for human-robot collaboration. This compliance is achieved by decoupling the feedforward torque generated by the WBC from the PD feedback, allowing for a flexible trade-off between tracking accuracy and compliant behavior.

In summary, RAMBO successfully integrates the precision of model-based control with the robustness of reinforcement learning, enabling legged robots to perform complex, dynamic loco-manipulation tasks with high accuracy and adaptability in real-world environments.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The RAMBO framework represents a significant advancement in the field of legged robotics and whole-body control, with broad implications for future research and practical applications. Its core significance lies in effectively bridging the long-standing gap between the precision offered by model-based control and the robustness provided by learning-based methods, a crucial step for deploying legged robots in dynamic and unstructured real-world environments.

**Enhanced Capabilities for Legged Robots:** By enabling dynamic locomotion concurrently with precise object manipulation, RAMBO expands the operational capabilities of legged robots far beyond simple mobility. This means robots can now not only navigate complex terrains but also actively interact with and manipulate their surroundings, opening doors for tasks that were previously challenging or impossible. Examples like pushing a shopping cart or balancing a plate while walking demonstrate real-world utility in domestic, industrial, or service robotics.

**Improved Precision and Robustness:** The quantitative results clearly show RAMBO's superior performance in end-effector tracking precision compared to purely learning-based or model-based baselines, while simultaneously maintaining robustness against disturbances. This is vital for tasks requiring delicate interaction or specific force application, where previous RL-based methods often sacrificed precision for stability. The hybrid architecture ensures that torque-level commands are optimally generated based on dynamics, while the RL policy provides the necessary adaptive corrections for unmodeled effects, leading to more reliable and accurate task execution.

**Generalizable and Flexible Framework:** The demonstration of RAMBO across both quadrupedal and bipedal locomotion modes on the same hardware platform (Unitree Go2), using a single overarching framework, underscores its generalizability. This versatility suggests that the underlying principles could be adapted to a wide range of legged systems, including future humanoids, without requiring a complete redesign of the control architecture for each new configuration or task. The user-commandable interface also provides a flexible tool for researchers and practitioners.

**Enabling Safe and Adaptive Interaction:** The ability of RAMBO to enable compliant behaviors is a critical contribution. For robots operating in human environments or interacting with unknown or delicate objects, compliance is paramount for safety and adaptability. By allowing a flexible trade-off between tracking accuracy and compliance, RAMBO paves the way for more natural and safer physical human-robot interaction, such as handshakes or collaborative manipulation tasks.

**Future Research Directions and Impact:**
The paper outlines several promising avenues for future research, highlighting the long-term impact of RAMBO:
- **State Estimation and Sensor Fusion:** Addressing the current reliance solely on proprioceptive information, which can lead to state estimation drift, by integrating additional sensing modalities (e.g., vision, force-torque sensors) will significantly enhance accuracy and robustness.
- **Advanced Dynamics Models:** Integrating full-order Whole-Body Control (WBC) dynamics models, rather than the simplified SRB model, could further improve precision and handle more complex manipulation dynamics, especially for higher-inertia objects or more aggressive maneuvers.
- **Application to Humanoids:** The successful bipedal loco-manipulation tasks are a strong indicator of RAMBO's potential for humanoid robots, a field with immense challenges in coordinated whole-body control.
- **Online Model Adaptation:** Extending the framework with online model adaptation techniques could allow the robot to continually refine its internal models, further improving generalization and precision in novel or dynamically changing environments.

In conclusion, RAMBO represents a significant step towards developing truly versatile and capable legged robots. By offering a robust, precise, and adaptable control solution for complex loco-manipulation tasks, it moves the field closer to realizing intelligent robots that can seamlessly navigate and interact with the physical world, impacting areas from logistics and manufacturing to exploration and service.
