# Cognitive Science-Inspired Evaluation of Core Capabilities for Object Understanding in AI

> [!summary] Summary
> A study synthesizes diverse cognitive science frameworks to define comprehensive object understanding and then evaluates current AI systems, revealing that while AI excels at isolated capabilities like segmentation or physical prediction, it crucially lacks functional integration across perceptual grouping, behavioral grouping, and affordance identification. This fragmentation, the research posits, prevents the development of holistic object understanding and robust world models in AI.

## Problem

- Biological agents possess a robust sense of objecthood, enabling adaptive behavior and accurate predictions, while current AI systems often struggle with flexible and integrated object understanding in dynamic environments.
- Cognitive science lacks a singular, unified theoretical account of objecthood, leading to fragmented understanding and evaluation of this fundamental capability.
- Existing AI benchmarks typically assess isolated aspects of object understanding, such as segmentation or physical prediction, rather than a comprehensive, integrated sense of objecthood.

## Method

- Synthesized diverse cognitive science perspectives (==Gestalt psychology==, developmental psychology, ==enactive cognition==) to consolidate a comprehensive, multi-layered theoretical framework for objecthood.
- Identified three core, interconnected capabilities for **object understanding** in biological agents: perceptual grouping/segmentation, behavioral grouping, and affordance identification.
- Evaluated current AI paradigms by categorizing them based on their ==knowledge of and interaction with the world==, analyzing how they conceptualize, implement, and evaluate objecthood capabilities against the cognitively inspired framework.

## Results

- AI models demonstrate competence in **individual object understanding capabilities**, such as visual segmentation for perceptual grouping, learning object dynamics for behavioral grouping, and leveraging pre-trained knowledge for affordance recognition.
- A critical finding across most AI paradigms is the **pervasive lack of functional integration** among perceptual grouping, behavioral grouping, and affordance identification, leading to specialized rather than holistic object understanding.
- Emerging integrated approaches, like those combining **large language models with embodied interaction** in 3D simulated environments, show promise but currently face limitations such as sparse updates and embodiment being a 'posterior' addition rather than intrinsic to the foundational design.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - In biological systems, core object understanding capabilities (perceptual grouping, behavioral grouping, affordance identification) are inherently intertwined and functionally integrated.
> - Current AI systems primarily develop object understanding capabilities in isolation, with specialized models excelling in individual areas without robust functional integration.
> - The fragmented approach in AI often requires human modelers to implicitly perform "cognitive work" (e.g., pre-segmenting inputs), thereby hindering the development of truly general and integrated object understanding in AI agents.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research paper positions itself at the intersection of cognitive science and artificial intelligence (AI), addressing the fundamental concept of "object understanding" or "objecthood." In cognitive science, understanding objects, space, and causality—often referred to as intuitive physics—is recognized as a core component of biological agents' world models, enabling effective prediction, planning, and navigation in complex environments. Despite its recognized importance, the paper notes that there is no singular, unified theoretical account of objecthood, with various frameworks (e.g., Gestalt psychology, developmental psychology, enactive cognition) providing disparate insights.

Within the AI landscape, the development of robust world models is a significant area of research. However, AI systems frequently encounter challenges in exhibiting flexible, context-sensitive reasoning about objects, a capability that biological agents demonstrate seamlessly. Existing AI benchmarks often assess isolated aspects of object understanding, such as segmentation or physical prediction, rather than a comprehensive, integrated sense of objecthood.

This work aims to bridge these disciplinary gaps. By synthesizing diverse cognitive science perspectives on objecthood, the paper provides a consolidated theoretical foundation for AI researchers. Subsequently, it evaluates current AI paradigms against this cognitively informed framework, identifying limitations—particularly a lack of functional integration across object understanding capabilities—and proposing directions for developing more robust and biologically plausible AI systems. The paper thus contributes to the ongoing effort to imbue AI with a more general and integrated understanding of the physical world.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The paper identifies "intuitive physics" and, specifically, a robust "sense of objecthood" as a foundational capability for both human and animal intelligence, underpinning adaptive behaviors, predictions, and interactions within complex environments. The core motivation for this research stems from the observation that while biological agents excel at these tasks, current AI systems often struggle, displaying a lack of flexibility and integrated understanding when faced with dynamic, open-ended scenarios. The authors suggest that this discrepancy is partly due to the absence of a comprehensive theoretical account of objecthood in cognitive science and a fragmented approach to its evaluation and implementation in AI.

Against this background, the paper outlines two primary objectives:

**First Aim: Comprehensive Overview of Objecthood in Cognitive Science**
The initial objective is to provide a comprehensive overview of core theoretical frameworks that address objecthood in cognitive science. This aim specifically draws upon Gestalt psychology, enactive cognition, and developmental psychology. Within each framework, the researchers intend to:
- **Identify core capabilities:** Pinpoint the specific abilities that each framework attributes to object understanding.
- **Explore functional roles:** Determine how these identified capabilities contribute to the construction of world models in biological agents.
The motivation for this aim is that objecthood has traditionally been studied in isolation, focusing on individual capabilities like segmentation. A consolidated view is necessary to define a holistic sense of objecthood that transcends these individual aspects. By bringing these disparate frameworks together, the authors aim to reveal the multi-layered nature of objecthood, which is crucial for building AI systems with robust world models that can interpret dynamic environments, make reliable predictions, and respond flexibly.

**Second Aim: Evaluation of Object Understanding in AI**
The second objective is to evaluate how current AI paradigms approach and test objecthood capabilities in comparison to the insights gleaned from cognitive science. This evaluation involves:
- **Defining AI paradigms:** Conceptualizing an AI paradigm as a combination of how objecthood is understood, the methods used to study it, the data utilized, and the evaluation techniques employed.
- **Comparative analysis:** Analyzing how the core objecthood capabilities identified in the first part of the paper are measured and evaluated in artificial systems.
The motivation here is to assess whether the numerous existing AI benchmarks related to objecthood adequately capture the complexity observed in biological agents. By comparing AI approaches with cognitive science perspectives, the paper aims to identify limitations in current AI methodologies.

**Overarching Contribution and Motivation:**
The overarching contribution, derived from these two aims, is the identification of a significant limitation in current AI approaches: the lack of functional integration across core objecthood capabilities. The authors argue that this fragmented focus results in specialized AI systems that address only isolated aspects of object understanding, rather than solving the challenge holistically. Capabilities such as segmentation, behavioral grouping, and affordance recognition are inherently intertwined in biological systems. By treating them separately, AI modellers "do the hard (cognitive) work" for their agents, preventing the development of general, integrated object understanding. Consequently, the paper seeks to explore novel evaluation approaches that align with an integrated vision of objecthood, proposing methods that could advance AI from isolated capabilities towards general-purpose intelligence with genuine object understanding in real-world contexts.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research employs a two-part methodology: a comprehensive literature review and synthesis within cognitive science, followed by an analytical review and classification of current AI paradigms for object understanding.

**Part 1: Cognitive Science-Inspired Framework for Object Understanding**

The first part of the methodology involves a theoretical investigation into object understanding as conceptualized across different branches of cognitive science. This is achieved through a literature review focused on identifying core capabilities and their functional roles. The paper draws predominantly on three theoretical frameworks:

1. **Gestalt Psychology (Perceptual Grouping and Segmentation):**
 - **Focus:** This framework investigates how unstructured sensory stimuli are organized into coherent perceptual experiences of discrete objects.
 - **Cues:** The authors detail both 2D static cues (e.g., Wertheimer's Gestalt principles like proximity, symmetry, similarity, closure, continuity, and parallelism) and 3D static and motion cues (e.g., monocular depth cues such as occlusion, relative size, texture gradient, and motion parallax).
 - **Function:** Emphasized as enabling coding efficiency, reducing the complexity of visual inputs into manageable units for further processing.
 - **Developmental Insights:** The discussion includes observations on infant perception, noting a developmental progression from reliance on motion cues to the integration of Gestalt principles, suggesting a shift from coarse-grained to detailed object modeling.

2. **Developmental Psychology (Behavioural Grouping):**
 - **Focus:** This framework examines how agents understand object behavior and how this understanding facilitates object identification and prediction.
 - **Principles:** It covers early-emerging principles of object behavior in infants, such as rigidity, cohesion, boundedness, no action at a distance, persistence, continuity, solidity, inertia, support, containment, and collision.
 - **Function:** Positioned as enabling predictions about how objects will behave, thus intrinsically linking perception with reasoning about objects. The paper also discusses the "perception-conception" relationship, where perceiving objects is related to reasoning about their behavior.

3. **Ecological Psychology and Enactive Philosophy (Identification of Affordances):**
 - **Focus:** This framework emphasizes that perception is fundamentally for, and intertwined with, action.
 - **Concept of Affordances:** Affordances are defined as action possibilities arising from the relationship between an agent and its environment, integrating exteroception (external world perception) and proprioception (body movement perception).
 - **Empirical Evidence:** The authors cite empirical studies (e.g., Tucker & Ellis, 1998, 2001; Boroditsky et al., 2010) demonstrating that motor or affordance representations are automatically generated and can causally influence object perception, even in the absence of explicit intent to act.

Throughout this part, the paper acknowledges that while these capabilities are often studied independently in cognitive science, they are inherently intertwined and functionally integrated in biological agents, setting the stage for the critique of AI systems.

**Part 2: Evaluation of Object Understanding in AI**

The second part of the methodology involves evaluating current AI paradigms against the cognitively inspired framework. The authors introduce a conceptual framework categorized along two dimensions: "Knowledge about the world" (low/high) and "Interaction with the world" (no/high), forming four quadrants. For each identified AI paradigm, the analysis specifies:
- **Method:** The algorithms and techniques employed (e.g., CNNs, VAEs, Transformers, DRL).
- **Objecthood Conceptualization:** How the paradigm implicitly or explicitly defines objecthood (e.g., visual pattern detection, contextual relationships, dynamic interaction).
- **Data:** The types of data used (e.g., still images, 3D models, video frames, text, simulation states).
- **Evaluation:** The metrics and benchmarks used to assess performance (e.g., IoU, mAP, FG-ARI, success rate, reward maximization).

The paper categorizes and analyzes specific AI benchmarks and approaches:
- **No Interaction, Low Knowledge (Bottom-left):** Focuses on segmentation-based approaches in computer vision (e.g., MONet, PartNet, SOLV).
- **No Interaction, High Knowledge (Top-left):** Examines generative AI and prompting methodologies (e.g., Segment Anything Model (SAM), Flamingo, SceneLLM, ShapeLLM, VLM/LLM combinations for robotics).
- **High Interaction, No Knowledge (Bottom-right):** Covers Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL) and simulation-based approaches using game engines (e.g., OGRE, PHYRE, Neural Physics Engine (NPE), Physion, Animal-AI Environment).
- **Combining Knowledge and Interaction (Top-right):** This quadrant is identified as under-explored, but the paper discusses emerging work that attempts to bridge this gap, such as the LLM-AAI framework and Embodied Agent Interface (EAI) framework.

The analysis in this part aims to identify the strengths and, crucially, the limitations of each AI paradigm in achieving a comprehensive and integrated understanding of objecthood, particularly in comparison to the multi-faceted nature observed in biological systems.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yields several key findings, first from the cognitive science synthesis and then from the evaluation of AI paradigms.

**From Cognitive Science Synthesis:**
The paper identifies three core capabilities that contribute to object understanding in biological agents, emphasizing their inherent interconnectedness:
1. **Perceptual Grouping and Segmentation (c1):** This capability involves structuring raw sensory input into discrete objects based on visual cues. It utilizes both 2D static cues (e.g., Gestalt principles like proximity, similarity, closure) and 3D static and motion cues (e.g., occlusion, motion parallax). Its functional role is primarily coding efficiency, making the visual environment manageable.
2. **Behavioural Grouping (c2):** This capability relates to understanding objects based on their predictable physical behaviors (e.g., rigidity, cohesion, causality, support, containment). It is crucial for predicting future events and reasoning about object interactions. The paper highlights developmental evidence suggesting that infants' initial reliance on motion cues for object segmentation eventually integrates with an understanding of these behavioral principles.
3. **Identification of Affordances (c3):** This capability describes how agents perceive objects in terms of action possibilities, which are fundamentally linked to the agent's body and its situated interaction with the environment. Empirical studies demonstrate that motor representations and affordances can causally influence object perception.

A central finding from this synthesis is that, in biological systems, these three capabilities are not independent but rather intertwined, with each often providing inputs or context for the others.

**From AI Evaluation:**
The analysis of current AI paradigms against this cognitive science-inspired framework reveals a significant pattern:

- **Progress in Isolated Capabilities:** AI systems have made progress in individual aspects of object understanding.
 - **Perceptual Grouping (c1):** Models in the "No Interaction, Low Knowledge" quadrant (e.g., MONet, PartNet, SOLV) demonstrate proficiency in visual segmentation, identifying objects or parts based on geometric features in static images or videos.
 - **Behavioural Grouping (c2):** "High Interaction, No Knowledge" models (e.g., Neural Physics Engine, Physion, OGRE, PHYRE) excel at learning object dynamics and physical interactions within simulated environments, developing robust physical reasoning.
 - **Affordance Identification (c3):** "No Interaction, High Knowledge" models (e.g., Segment Anything, Flamingo, SceneLLM) leverage large-scale pre-trained knowledge to understand contextual, relational, and functional aspects of objects, enabling zero- or few-shot affordance recognition. Embodied AI models in the "High Interaction, No Knowledge" quadrant also learn affordances through direct interaction.

- **Core Limitation: Lack of Functional Integration:** The most prominent finding is that, despite progress in isolated capabilities, most AI paradigms exhibit a critical lack of functional integration across c1, c2, and c3.
 - Segmentation models (c1) often operate without understanding dynamic behaviors (c2) or affordances (c3).
 - Physical reasoning models (c2) frequently rely on pre-segmented inputs, thus bypassing the need for autonomous perceptual grouping (c1), and may lack broader semantic/affordance understanding (c3).
 - Generative AI models focused on affordances (c3) often process structured inputs and do not autonomously perform segmentation (c1) or engage in dynamic behavioral prediction (c2).
 This fragmentation implies that AI systems are highly specialized, where modellers implicitly "do the cognitive work" by providing inputs that would typically be the outputs of other objecthood capabilities in biological systems. This approach hinders the development of a holistic and flexible object understanding.

- **Promising Direction: Integrated Approaches:** The "Combining Knowledge of the World with Interaction with the World" quadrant (Top-right) is identified as a crucial area for future development. Emerging frameworks like LLM-AAI and EAI represent initial attempts to integrate multiple modalities (textual, visual, interactive) with real-time agent interaction in 3D simulated environments. These approaches require agents to simultaneously apply perceptual grouping, behavioral prediction, and affordance understanding, indicating a move towards functional interdependence. However, current limitations in these foundation models include sparse updates (via fine-tuning) and embodiment being "posterior" to initial training, which prevents full commitment to an integrated approach.

In summary, while AI has achieved competence in various facets of object understanding, a holistic, functionally integrated sense of objecthood, akin to that in biological agents, remains largely unachieved due to the fragmented nature of current AI research and evaluation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research holds several significances and potential impacts across both cognitive science and artificial intelligence:

- **Redefining AI's Approach to Object Understanding:** The primary significance of this paper is its identification of the critical limitation in current AI: the lack of functional integration across core object understanding capabilities. By explicitly highlighting this "piecemeal approach," the research challenges the prevailing paradigm of developing specialized AI systems and advocates for a shift towards more holistic and integrated architectures. This redirection can significantly influence future AI research agendas and resource allocation, moving beyond incremental improvements in isolated tasks.

- **Bridging Cognitive Science and AI:** The paper serves as a valuable interdisciplinary bridge. By synthesizing disparate theoretical frameworks from cognitive science (Gestalt, developmental, enactive) into a coherent overview of objecthood, it provides AI researchers with a robust, biologically inspired conceptual foundation. This synthesis can inform the design of AI systems that more closely mimic human and animal cognitive processes, potentially leading to more effective and generalizable AI. The authors explicitly hope to foster dialogue between these communities, which could lead to a more unifying framework for studying object understanding in both fields.

- **Advancing Robust World Models:** A profound understanding of objecthood is presented as foundational to building robust "world models" in AI. World models enable agents to predict events, plan actions, and adapt to novel circumstances. By pursuing the functional integration of perceptual grouping, behavioral grouping, and affordance identification, AI systems can develop more nuanced, adaptive, and context-sensitive world models. This advancement can significantly enhance AI's ability to operate effectively in dynamic, unstructured, and open-ended real-world environments.

- **Towards General-Purpose AI:** The current specialization of AI systems in object understanding limits their ability to generalize and adapt across diverse tasks and domains. By promoting an integrated approach, the paper outlines a pathway towards more general-purpose AI. An AI system that autonomously combines segmentation with dynamic physical reasoning and actionable affordance understanding would be less reliant on pre-computed inputs and human-defined task structures, moving closer to the flexibility observed in biological intelligence.

- **Informing New Evaluation Paradigms:** The critique of existing AI benchmarks, which often assess isolated capabilities, implicitly calls for the development of new evaluation metrics and environments. These future benchmarks would need to test the *integration* and *interdependence* of objecthood capabilities, rather than just individual performance. The discussion of nascent frameworks like LLM-AAI and EAI represents early steps in this direction, suggesting a shift towards embodied, interactive evaluations that demand a more comprehensive form of object understanding.

- **Impact on Downstream AI Applications:** Improvements in integrated object understanding would have broad implications for various AI applications. This includes, but is not limited to, robotics (e.g., more skillful manipulation, safer human-robot interaction, robust navigation), autonomous systems (e.g., self-driving cars with better predictive capabilities), and agents capable of complex problem-solving and planning in physical domains.

In conclusion, this research is significant for its diagnostic clarity regarding AI's current limitations in object understanding and its prescriptive guidance for future development. By advocating for a cognitively inspired, integrated approach, it lays foundational groundwork for more capable, adaptable, and genuinely intelligent AI systems.
