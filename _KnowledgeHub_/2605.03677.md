# Uni-OPD: Unifying On-Policy Distillation with a Dual-Perspective Recipe

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Zhejiang University and Tencent introduce Uni-OPD, a unified on-policy distillation framework that enhances student exploration and teacher supervision reliability in LLMs and MLLMs. The framework consistently improves performance across multi-teacher, strong-to-weak, and cross-modal distillation settings, achieving average gains up to 3.4% in code generation for LLMs and 3.1% in math reasoning for MLLMs over standard methods, while demonstrating faster convergence.

## Problem

- Existing post-training methods for large language models (LLMs) and multimodal LLMs (MLLMs), like supervised fine-tuning (SFT) and reinforcement learning (RL), suffer from exposure bias or limited training stability.
- On-policy distillation (OPD) offers an alternative but is hampered by insufficient student exploration of diverse, informative states.
- Teacher supervision in OPD is often unreliable, as token-level guidance does not consistently align with trajectory-level correctness, leading to misleading learning signals.

## Method

- **Uni-OPD** employs a ==dual-perspective optimization== strategy focusing on student exploration and teacher supervision reliability.
- From the student's perspective, a joint offline and online data balancing strategy is used to ensure exploration of diverse, informative states by adjusting for instance difficulty and maintaining a balanced ratio of correct/incorrect trajectories.
- From the teacher's perspective, an ==outcome-guided margin calibration== mechanism rectifies unreliable token-level supervision by enforcing "order-consistency" between trajectory-level distillation returns and global outcomes, primarily through a "==Margin Shift==" approach.

## Results

- Uni-OPD consistently surpassed standard on-policy distillation (OPD) across 5 domains and 16 benchmarks, including LLMs and MLLMs, in various distillation settings (single/multi-teacher, strong-to-weak, cross-modal).
- For multi-teacher LLM distillation, Uni-OPD achieved average gains of **1.5%** in math reasoning and **3.4%** in code generation; for MLLMs, it improved math reasoning by **3.1%**, logic reasoning by **2.5%**, and document understanding by **0.5%**.
- The framework effectively enabled strong-to-weak distillation from a 30B teacher to 1.7B and 4B students, and successfully integrated capabilities in cross-modal distillation (e.g., text-only code into multimodal models).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Skewed difficulty distributions in training data and degenerate groups of student-generated rollouts limit the effectiveness of on-policy learning by providing suboptimal exploration signals.
> - Teacher supervision in distillation can suffer from "order-consistency" violations, where token-level guidance does not accurately reflect trajectory-level correctness due to factors like out-of-distribution student rollouts.
> - Explicitly balancing data by difficulty and dynamically managing correct/incorrect trajectory ratios in batches, alongside outcome-guided calibration of teacher returns, significantly enhances the robustness and reliability of distillation.
