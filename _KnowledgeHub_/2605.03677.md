# Uni-OPD: Unifying On-Policy Distillation with a Dual-Perspective Recipe

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Zhejiang University and Tencent introduce Uni-OPD, a unified on-policy distillation framework that enhances student exploration and teacher supervision reliability in LLMs and MLLMs. The framework consistently improves performance across multi-teacher, strong-to-weak, and cross-modal distillation settings, achieving average gains up to 3.4% in code generation for LLMs and 3.1% in math reasoning for MLLMs over standard methods, while demonstrating faster convergence.

## Problem

- Existing post-training methods for large language models (LLMs) and multimodal LLMs (MLLMs), like supervised fine-tuning (SFT) and reinforcement learning (RL), suffer from exposure bias or limited training stability.
- On-policy distillation (OPD) offers an alternative but is hampered by insufficient student exploration of diverse, informative states.
- Teacher supervision in OPD is often unreliable, as token-level guidance does not consistently align with trajectory-level correctness, leading to misleading learning signals.

## Method

- **Uni-OPD** employs a ==dual-perspective optimization== strategy focusing on student exploration and teacher supervision reliability.
- From the student's perspective, a joint offline and online data balancing strategy is used to ensure exploration of diverse, informative states by adjusting for instance difficulty and maintaining a balanced ratio of correct/incorrect trajectories.
- From the teacher's perspective, an ==outcome-guided margin calibration== mechanism rectifies unreliable token-level supervision by enforcing "order-consistency" between trajectory-level distillation returns and global outcomes, primarily through a "==Margin Shift==" approach.

## Results

- Uni-OPD consistently surpassed standard on-policy distillation (OPD) across 5 domains and 16 benchmarks, including LLMs and MLLMs, in various distillation settings (single/multi-teacher, strong-to-weak, cross-modal).
- For multi-teacher LLM distillation, Uni-OPD achieved average gains of **1.5%** in math reasoning and **3.4%** in code generation; for MLLMs, it improved math reasoning by **3.1%**, logic reasoning by **2.5%**, and document understanding by **0.5%**.
- The framework effectively enabled strong-to-weak distillation from a 30B teacher to 1.7B and 4B students, and successfully integrated capabilities in cross-modal distillation (e.g., text-only code into multimodal models).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Skewed difficulty distributions in training data and degenerate groups of student-generated rollouts limit the effectiveness of on-policy learning by providing suboptimal exploration signals.
> - Teacher supervision in distillation can suffer from "order-consistency" violations, where token-level guidance does not accurately reflect trajectory-level correctness due to factors like out-of-distribution student rollouts.
> - Explicitly balancing data by difficulty and dynamically managing correct/incorrect trajectory ratios in batches, alongside outcome-guided calibration of teacher returns, significantly enhances the robustness and reliability of distillation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of sophisticated reasoning abilities, domain-specific knowledge, and the alignment with human preferences in large language models (LLMs) and multimodal large language models (MLLMs) remains a significant challenge during the post-training phase. Historically, this has involved a two-stage process: supervised fine-tuning (SFT) followed by reinforcement learning (RL). SFT, being inherently off-policy, is prone to "exposure bias," where the model encounters out-of-distribution erroneous states during inference, leading to compounding errors. Conversely, on-policy RL methods, such as GRPO, mitigate distribution shift through online sampling but often rely on sequence-level or terminal rewards, complicating fine-grained credit assignment and limiting training stability over long durations.

In response to these limitations, on-policy distillation (OPD) has emerged as an alternative post-training paradigm. OPD combines the strengths of RL (on-policy sampling) and SFT (token-level supervision) by training a student model on its own generated trajectories while leveraging token-level feedback from a teacher model under a reverse Kullback-Leibler (KL) objective. This approach has demonstrated empirical success in transferring knowledge and capabilities from expert models to a single student model.

However, existing OPD research has primarily focused on LLM distillation, with extensions to MLLMs being limited to specific tasks within single modalities (e.g., video or speech). A fundamental gap in the research landscape concerns a comprehensive understanding of the conditions under which OPD reliably improves model performance and how to generalize it across diverse model architectures and modalities. This paper positions itself within this landscape by identifying core bottlenecks that hinder effective OPD and proposing a unified framework to address these limitations, thereby enhancing its reliability and applicability across both LLMs and MLLMs, including scenarios like strong-to-weak and cross-modal distillation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop Uni-OPD, a unified on-policy distillation (OPD) framework that is broadly applicable to both Large Language Models (LLMs) and Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs). This framework aims to efficiently consolidate the diverse capabilities of specialized expert models into a single student model during the post-training stage.

The motivation for this work stems from two fundamental bottlenecks identified in current OPD methodologies that limit their consistent and reliable improvement:

1. **Insufficient Exploration of Informative States:** For effective learning, the student model must generate and explore a sufficiently diverse range of trajectories that are also appropriately challenging. If the student primarily generates overly easy or overly difficult states, or if the generated states lack variety, the learning signal becomes suboptimal. This insufficient exploration can hinder the student's ability to develop robust reasoning paths and generalize across different problem complexities. Existing approaches, such as filtering out overly easy or hard samples, can inadvertently reduce data diversity, further limiting exploration. Therefore, a key motivation is to devise strategies that promote the student's exposure to a balanced spectrum of informative, self-generated states.

2. **Unreliable Teacher Supervision for Student Rollouts:** OPD relies on the teacher model to provide fine-grained, token-level supervision for the student's self-generated trajectories. However, the reliability of this supervision is not consistently guaranteed. Several factors can lead to unreliable signals:
 - **Out-of-Distribution (OOD) Degradation:** When student rollouts venture into regions of the state space that are sparse or out-of-distribution relative to the teacher's training, the teacher's log-likelihood predictions can become noisy.
 - **Overestimation of Incorrect Trajectories:** Incorrect student trajectories might receive spuriously high aggregated distillation returns if their local token patterns align with high-confidence regions of the teacher, even if the overall trajectory is wrong.
 - **Underestimation of Correct Trajectories:** Conversely, correct student trajectories might receive abnormally low aggregated returns if their generation paths deviate from the teacher's dominant or most likely sequences, potentially suppressing valid but alternative reasoning strategies.
 A crucial aspect of reliable supervision is "order-consistency," meaning that the aggregated token-level guidance (distillation return) for a correct trajectory should consistently be higher than for an incorrect trajectory. When this consistency is violated, the student receives misleading signals, which can destabilize training and lead to suboptimal learning outcomes. The motivation is thus to calibrate teacher supervision to ensure that the global outcome reward provides a stable anchor for fine-grained token-level feedback.

By addressing these two core bottlenecks, the research aims to establish OPD as a more robust, versatile, and effective optimization paradigm for large models.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Uni-OPD, the proposed unified framework, is designed to enhance On-Policy Distillation (OPD) by implementing a dual-perspective optimization strategy that targets both student exploration and teacher supervision reliability. The framework utilizes a generalized objective function J_Uni-OPD(θ) = ∑ w_i D_KL(π_θ || π_Ti), which allows for the aggregation of supervision from multiple specialized teacher models, making it applicable to both single-teacher and multi-teacher distillation scenarios.

**Student's Perspective: Joint Offline and Online Data Balancing Strategy**

The methodology from the student’s perspective focuses on ensuring that the student explores informative states by generating diverse trajectories with appropriate difficulty levels.

1. **Offline Difficulty-Aware Data Balancing:**
 - **Procedure:** Before the main training, a one-time offline rollout pass is performed where the student model generates multiple (N=8) independent responses for each training instance. The empirical pass rate (k/N, where k is the number of correct responses) serves as a proxy for the instance's difficulty.
 - **Motivation:** The research empirically observes that training data often exhibits skewed difficulty distributions (e.g., U-shaped or mirrored J-shaped), with many overly easy or hard samples. Unlike conventional approaches that might filter out these extremes (which can reduce data diversity and hinder performance), Uni-OPD aims to preserve the full spectrum of difficulty.
 - **Strategy:** For U-shaped distributions, instances of intermediate difficulty (e.g., 1-7 correct out of 8) are upsampled. For mirrored J-shaped distributions, all non-trivial instances (1-8 correct) are upsampled. This rebalancing reshapes the data distribution to be more uniform, ensuring exposure to a rich variety of learning signals.

2. **Online Correctness-Aware Data Balancing:**
 - **Procedure:** During active training, this strategy dynamically maintains a balanced composition of correct and incorrect trajectories within each batch of rollouts. A target correct-to-total ratio (γ* ≈ 0.5) is set for the batch. If the current batch ratio deviates significantly from γ*, the over-represented side (correct or incorrect trajectories) is down-weighted by subsampling within groups.
 - **Motivation:** As training progresses, student performance varies, leading to degenerate rollout groups (e.g., all correct or all incorrect trajectories) where outcome-level contrast is lost, making outcome-guided calibration ineffective.
 - **Strategy:** By enforcing a balanced ratio, the student consistently receives meaningful contrastive signals from both successful and unsuccessful attempts, which stabilizes on-policy learning and prevents convergence to local optima.

**Teacher's Perspective: Outcome-Guided Margin Calibration for Reliable Supervision**

This part of the methodology addresses the unreliability of teacher supervision by ensuring that token-level guidance aligns with trajectory-level correctness.

1. **Trajectory-level Distillation Return (G_OPD):**
 - **Definition:** G_OPD(q, τ) = (1/|τ|) ∑ log (π_T(o_t | q, o_<t) / π_θ(o_t | q, o_<t)). This metric quantifies the teacher's average log-likelihood preference over the student for a given trajectory.
 - **Order Consistency:** The core principle is that G_OPD for correct trajectories (τ+) should be greater than or equal to G_OPD for incorrect trajectories (τ-), mirroring the behavior of outcome-based RL rewards R(q,τ).
 - **Motivation:** In practice, this order consistency is not guaranteed due to issues like OOD degradation or misestimation by the teacher.

2. **Outcome-Guided Margin Calibration:**
 - **Prompt-level Margin (m(q)):** Defined as min(G_OPD(q, τ+)) - max(G_OPD(q, τ-)), this metric directly assesses whether the ordering is violated in the most adversarial case for a given prompt. A positive margin m(q) ≥ δ (where δ > 0 is a safety margin) indicates strict order consistency.
 - **Calibration Strategies (applied when m(q) < δ):**
 - **Margin Mask:** This strategy selectively discards unreliable prompt groups. A "Greedy Margin Mask" variant removes the single most adversarial trajectory iteratively until the margin condition is met or a minimum retention ratio is maintained. This ensures training proceeds only with reliable supervision.
 - **Margin Shift:** Instead of discarding, this method applies a minimal additive correction to the trajectory-level returns to restore the margin. If λ(q) = δ - m(q) is the deficit, it can be applied in three directions:
 - *Lift:* Add λ(q) to all positive trajectories.
 - *Suppress:* Subtract λ(q) from all negative trajectories.
 - *Spread:* Add λ(q)/2 to positive and subtract λ(q)/2 from negative trajectories.
 The "Spread" direction with group-level mean normalization is adopted for main experiments, as it distributes the correction symmetrically, preserving relative ordering while ensuring the calibrated margin equals δ. This uses the outcome signal as a global anchor to adjust the teacher’s trajectory-level scores.

The framework integrates these components by sampling prompt batches (offline balancing), performing rollouts and calculating distillation returns, applying online balancing, calibrating returns based on outcome-guided margins, and finally updating the student policy using the calibrated token-level advantages. The implementation leverages existing training infrastructure like Miles, Megatron-LM, and SGLang.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents extensive experimental validation of Uni-OPD across 5 domains and 16 benchmarks, covering various distillation settings for both LLMs and MLLMs.

**Overall Performance and Convergence:**
Figure 1 illustrates that Uni-OPD consistently outperforms standard OPD across multi-teacher, strong-to-weak, and cross-modal distillation settings on math reasoning and code generation tasks. Furthermore, Uni-OPD demonstrates faster convergence compared to traditional RL methods, highlighting its efficiency.

**Single-Teacher and Multi-Teacher Distillation on LLMs and MLLMs:**
- **LLMs (Qwen3-4B student, Table 1):** In single-teacher distillation, Uni-OPD achieved 48.7% on math reasoning and 63.2% on code generation, outperforming OPD (44.6% math, 59.0% code) and ExOPD (48.0% math, 62.1% code). For multi-teacher distillation, Uni-OPD yielded average gains of 1.5% in math reasoning (48.5%) and 3.4% in code generation (63.6%) over standard OPD (47.0% math, 60.2% code).
- **MLLMs (Qwen3-VL-4B-Instruct student, Table 2):** Uni-OPD consistently delivered the best average performance across all three domains: 63.9% for math reasoning, 51.7% for logic reasoning, and 83.7% for document understanding in single-teacher settings. In multi-teacher scenarios, Uni-OPD improved average scores from 57.9% to 61.0% on math reasoning, 50.0% to 52.5% on logic reasoning, and 83.4% to 83.9% on document understanding over OPD. Consistent gains were also observed in smaller student models (Qwen3-1.7B LLM and Qwen3-VL-2B MLLM, Appendix D.1).

**Strong-to-Weak Distillation:**
Table 3 demonstrates Uni-OPD's effectiveness in transferring capabilities from a large teacher (Qwen3-30B) to significantly smaller students (Qwen3-4B and Qwen3-1.7B). For the 4B student, Uni-OPD achieved 42.9% in math reasoning (+1.7 points over OPD) and 60.8% in code generation (+1.8 points over OPD). For the 1.7B student, performance increased to 25.0% on math reasoning (+1.2 points) and 52.7% on code generation (+3.6 points). These results indicate Uni-OPD's ability to bridge capacity gaps effectively.

**Cross-Modal Distillation:**
In cross-modal settings (Qwen3-VL-4B-Instruct student distilling from textual code and multimodal math teachers, Table 4), Uni-OPD improved average scores from 64.1% to 65.6% on code generation and from 62.7% to 63.9% on multimodal math reasoning, compared to standard OPD. This suggests that Uni-OPD can effectively integrate capabilities from text-only and multimodal domains within a unified student model. Further experiments (Appendix D.3, Table D.3) confirmed similar gains for cross-modal distillation involving code generation and logic reasoning.

**Ablation Study:**
The ablation study (Table 5) confirms the individual contribution of each proposed strategy. Removing offline data balancing, online data balancing, or margin calibration individually led to performance degradation. Specifically, the average gains over vanilla OPD reached +1.5/+3.4 points on math/code for the Qwen3-4B-RL teacher, and +1.7/+1.8 points for the Qwen3-30B-A3B-Instruct teacher. Margin shift generally yielded slightly better results than margin mask (Table 6), with the "Group + Mean + Spread" configuration proving most robust and effective (Appendix D.3, Table D.5). The study also showed that a rollout number of n=16 provided a good balance between return estimation quality, training stability, and computational efficiency (Appendix D.3, Table D.6).

**Qualitative Evaluation:**
Figure 6 visually demonstrates how outcome-guided margin calibration rectifies unreliable teacher supervision. Heatmaps show that the calibration uniformly shifts per-token rewards to restore order consistency between correct (low distillation return becoming high) and incorrect (high distillation return becoming low) trajectories, leading to more reliable and higher-quality model outputs.

**Downstream Task Evaluation:**
Evaluations on 8 general benchmarks (Appendix D.2, Table D.4), including MMLU, ARC, and GSM8K, showed that Uni-OPD maintains strong performance across general-purpose capabilities. This indicates that the specialized training does not degrade the model's broader abilities.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Uni-OPD framework represents an advancement in the field of on-policy distillation by addressing fundamental limitations related to student exploration and the reliability of teacher supervision. Its significance and potential impact are multi-faceted:

- **Enhanced Reliability and Effectiveness of OPD:** By systematically identifying and mitigating issues such as insufficient exploration and inconsistent teacher feedback, Uni-OPD establishes a more robust and effective paradigm for knowledge distillation. This improves the stability of the training process and leads to more consistent performance gains.
- **Broadened Applicability Across Models and Modalities:** The unified nature of Uni-OPD, demonstrated through its effectiveness in both LLMs and MLLMs, across single-teacher, multi-teacher, strong-to-weak, and cross-modal distillation settings, significantly expands the scope of OPD. This versatility makes it a practical tool for diverse model development scenarios, enabling the consolidation of specialized capabilities into a single, general-purpose model.
- **Practical Insights into Reliable Distillation:** The detailed analysis of data balancing strategies and outcome-guided margin calibration mechanisms offers concrete, empirically validated techniques. These insights can guide future research and development in optimizing distillation processes, particularly concerning how to effectively manage data difficulty and ensure high-quality supervisory signals.
- **Efficient Capability Transfer:** The results demonstrate that Uni-OPD achieves strong reasoning capabilities with substantially fewer optimization steps compared to traditional reinforcement learning, suggesting a more efficient pathway for post-training large models. The ability for students to effectively absorb new capabilities from domain-specific teachers, and in some cases even surpass them, highlights the potential for continuous model improvement.
- **Support for Modality-Agnostic Reasoning:** The success of cross-modal distillation, where capabilities originating from text-only domains are integrated into multimodal models, suggests that reasoning patterns can be treated as largely modality-agnostic. This opens avenues for developing truly unified models capable of processing and reasoning across heterogeneous data types within a single training stage.
- **Scalable Merging of Specialized Expertise:** The framework’s ability to cleanly merge distinct capabilities from multiple teachers into a single student model provides a scalable strategy for building versatile AI systems. This is particularly relevant for constructing general reasoners that can combine expertise from numerous domain specialists, with potential synergistic effects among related capabilities.

Looking forward, the identified directions for future work—including scaling Uni-OPD to larger teacher models, applying it to broader capability merging (e.g., agentic planning, tool use), and uncovering the mechanistic principles of OPD—suggest that this research lays a foundational groundwork for continued advancements in scalable and reliable knowledge distillation. The commitment to open-sourcing materials further supports its potential impact by fostering broader adoption and accelerating subsequent research.
