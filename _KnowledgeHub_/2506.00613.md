# WorldGym: World Model as An Environment for Policy Evaluation

> [!summary] Summary
> WorldGym introduces an action-conditioned video generation model that functions as a high-fidelity environment for evaluating robot control policies. It achieves a strong Pearson correlation (r=0.78) with real-world policy success rates, maintains relative policy rankings, and efficiently diagnoses generalization issues with out-of-distribution inputs, thereby reducing the need for costly physical testing.

## Problem

- Evaluating robot control policies in the real world is costly, time-consuming, and carries significant safety risks, limiting experimental scale.
- Traditional handcrafted simulators (e.g., MuJoCo) require extensive manual effort to build and maintain, struggle with realism for complex dynamics, and often lead to a 'sim-to-real gap'.
- Existing model-based reinforcement learning often struggles with compounding errors over long rollouts, while model-free offline policy evaluation relies on assumptions rarely met in real-world robotics.

## Method

![[2506.00613-method.png]]
*WPE inference scheme: latent video diffusion with window size w plus autoregressive transformer predicting future tokens conditioned on actions via sliding window of context c and horizon h.*

- Developed **WorldGym**, an autoregressive, ==action-conditioned video generation== model using a ==latent Diffusion Transformer== (**DiT**) trained on diverse robot interaction datasets.
- Integrated a flexible prediction horizon that dynamically adjusts to a policy's action chunk size, enabling efficient and parallelized policy rollouts.
- Utilized a ==Vision-Language Model== (VLM), specifically **GPT-4o**, to automatically assess task success and compute rewards from generated video sequences, eliminating the need for hand-coded reward functions.

## Results

- Policy success rates within WorldGym showed a strong Pearson correlation (r = 0.78, p < 0.001) with real-world performance across 17 challenging Bridge evaluation tasks, with mean success rates differing by only **3.3%**.
- WorldGym consistently preserved the relative performance rankings among different state-of-the-art VLA models (RT-1-X, Octo, OpenVLA), different model versions/sizes, and various training checkpoints.
- Out-of-distribution evaluations revealed specific weaknesses in current policies, such as struggles with shape distinction over color cues, susceptibility to 2D visual distractors (e.g., OpenVLA misinterpreting a laptop image in **15%** of trials), and near-chance performance on complex classification tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A single, learned video-based world model can effectively serve as a high-fidelity, interactive environment for evaluating diverse robot policies across various tasks and morphologies.
> - Leveraging large Vision-Language Models for reward computation provides a generalizable and accurate method for assessing task success directly from generated videos and language instructions.
> - Generative world models enable efficient and scalable testing of policy generalization on out-of-distribution inputs (e.g., novel objects, environments, language instructions), offering diagnostic insights into policy limitations.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The evaluation of robot control policies is a critical bottleneck in the development and deployment of autonomous systems. Traditionally, this has relied on two primary methods: real-world testing and handcrafted software simulators. Both approaches, however, present significant limitations that WorldGym aims to address.

Real-world testing is inherently costly, time-consuming, and carries risks of physical damage or safety hazards. This severely limits the scale and frequency of policy evaluation, hindering rapid iteration and development. Handcrafted simulators, such as MuJoCo or Bullet, offer a safer and more controlled environment. However, they demand immense manual effort to construct and maintain, especially when modeling complex physical dynamics, diverse object interactions (e.g., soft objects), or intricate environments. This manual effort often results in a "sim-to-real gap," where policies trained or evaluated in simulation fail to perform robustly in the real world due to discrepancies in physics, perception, or object properties.

Within the machine learning context, policy evaluation has been explored extensively in reinforcement learning (RL). Model-based RL involves learning a dynamics model from past experiences to simulate future states for policy rollouts. However, much of this prior work focuses on single-task settings, where learning an accurate dynamics model can be as challenging, if not more so, than learning the policy itself, often leading to compounding errors during long rollouts. Conversely, model-free offline policy evaluation (OPE) methods aim to estimate policy values directly from logged data, but often rely on assumptions about full observability or access to ground-truth states that are rarely met in real-world robotics with image-based observations.

WorldGym strategically positions itself within this landscape by leveraging the recent advancements in large-scale generative models, specifically action-conditioned video generation. Recent works have demonstrated the capability of generative models to emulate physical world interactions, conditioned on various inputs like text or keyboard strokes. WorldGym extends this idea to robotic control, proposing that a video-based world model can serve as a highly realistic and generalizable environment for evaluating robot policies. The core insight underpinning this work is that "While there can be many tasks and policies, there is only one physical world in which we live that is governed by the same set of physical laws." This observation motivates the development of a *single, universal world model* capable of evaluating diverse policies across numerous tasks and environments, thus overcoming the single-task limitations of traditional model-based RL.

Compared to other emerging solutions, WorldGym differentiates itself by providing flexible, efficient evaluation for various policy types and by enabling out-of-distribution (OOD) testing with minimal human effort. For example, unlike some prior diffusion world models for robotics (e.g., NVIDIA's Cosmos), WorldGym's design allows for flexible prediction horizon lengths, adapting to different policy action chunk sizes. It also contrasts with methods like SIMPLER (Li et al., 2024), which construct software-based simulators from images but require significant hand-engineering for OOD scenarios. WorldGym's approach offers a more scalable and automated way to probe policy generalization, making it a significant contribution towards bridging the sim-to-real gap and accelerating robust robot learning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind WorldGym stems from the inherent difficulties and limitations associated with evaluating robot control policies in real-world settings and conventional simulators. The authors articulate several key challenges:
- **Cost and Risk of Real-World Testing:** Deploying and testing robots in the real world is expensive, time-consuming, and carries safety risks, making large-scale experimentation impractical.
- **Limitations of Handcrafted Simulators:** Existing simulators struggle with realism and generality, especially for complex dynamics and varied object interactions, leading to the notorious "sim-to-real gap." They also require substantial manual effort to create and maintain.
- **Need for Generalization:** As robot learning progresses, there is an increasing need to evaluate policies' ability to generalize to novel tasks, objects, and environments without incurring prohibitive real-world testing costs.

Against this backdrop, the authors set out to achieve several key objectives with WorldGym:
1. **Develop a World-Model-Based Policy Evaluation Environment:** The core objective is to create an autoregressive, action-conditioned video generation model (WorldGym) that can serve as a high-fidelity, interactive proxy for real-world robotic environments.
2. **Establish High Correlation with Real-World Performance:** A crucial goal is to demonstrate that policy success rates measured within WorldGym are strongly correlated with their actual performance in real-world scenarios, thereby validating its utility as a reliable evaluation tool.
3. **Preserve Relative Policy Rankings:** The framework should be robust enough to consistently maintain the relative performance rankings between different policies, including various versions, sizes, and training checkpoints of the same model, mimicking real-world outcomes.
4. **Enable Efficient and Flexible Policy Rollouts:** WorldGym aims to support efficient evaluation of diverse policies by flexibly aligning its video generation horizon with policies' varying action chunk sizes, optimizing computational resources.
5. **Facilitate Out-of-Distribution (OOD) Generalization Testing:** A significant objective is to allow for straightforward and efficient testing of robot policies' generalization capabilities on novel tasks and environments by manipulating initial frames (OOD images) and language instructions (OOD language) within the learned world model.
6. **Uncover Policy Strengths and Weaknesses:** By enabling OOD testing, the authors aim to identify specific failure modes, biases, and limitations of modern vision-language-action (VLA) based robot policies, guiding future research and development towards more robust AI.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

WorldGym’s methodology revolves around constructing a robust action-conditioned video generation model that acts as a generative simulator, coupled with a vision-language model (VLM) for reward computation.

#### 3.1. Building the World Model

- **Architecture and Training:** The core of WorldGym is a latent Diffusion Transformer (DiT), similar to models used for large-scale image and video generation. It is trained on sequences of video frames paired with corresponding robot actions. The model employs Diffusion Forcing (Chen et al., 2024) to enable autoregressive generation of future frames. Robot action vectors (7-dimensional, including end-effector position and gripper state) are linearly projected and used to condition the diffusion process via AdaLN-Zero modulation, ensuring the generated video closely follows the robot's intended movements. To enhance adherence to actions, the model is trained with random action dropout and classifier-free guidance. Conditioning on past frames is managed through causal temporal attention blocks, allowing the model to learn temporal dependencies.
- **Data Sources:** The world model is trained on a diverse collection of robot datasets from the Open-X Embodiment initiative, including Bridge V2 (Walke et al., 2023) and RT-1 (Brohan et al., 2022). Action spaces across these different robots are unified by normalizing their 10th- to 90th-percentile values to match the RT-1 dataset, facilitating generalization across varied robot morphologies. The model uses a context length of 20 frames, employing a sliding window for longer rollouts.

#### 3.2. Rolling Out a Policy in the World Model

- **Iterative Evaluation Loop:** Policy evaluation in WorldGym follows an iterative loop. An initial observation frame (`o_0`) is provided to a robot policy, which then predicts a chunk of actions (`a_pred`). This action chunk, along with preceding frames and actions, is fed into the world model.
- **Flexible Horizon Length:** A key innovation for efficiency is WorldGym's ability to dynamically adjust its prediction horizon (`h`) to match the policy's action chunk size (`|a_pred|`). Unlike models with fixed context lengths, this flexible approach allows the world model to denoise multiple latent frames in parallel for each action chunk, significantly speeding up rollouts for policies with varying output frequencies and optimizing GPU utilization. The latest generated frame then becomes the next observation for the policy, continuing the loop for hundreds of interactive steps.

#### 3.3. VLM as Reward

- **GPT-4o for Success Evaluation:** Instead of hand-coding reward functions, WorldGym leverages a Vision-Language Model (VLM), specifically GPT-4o, to determine task success. The VLM receives the sequence of generated frames from a policy rollout and the original language instruction for the task.
- **Prompting and Partial Credit:** A carefully designed prompt is used to query GPT-4o, including a rubric for success (1), failure (0), and optional partial credit (0.5) for tasks where intermediate progress is meaningful. This approach allows for nuanced evaluation.
- **Validation of VLM Reward:** The authors validated GPT-4o's accuracy as a reward model by comparing its predictions on real RT-1 dataset videos against ground-truth success labels. The results showed high true positive and true negative rates, with a particularly low false positive rate, ensuring that the VLM does not over-estimate policy performance.

#### 3.4. Evaluating the World Model (Prior to Policy Evaluation)

Before using WorldGym for policy evaluation, its fidelity and responsiveness were rigorously tested:
- **Agreement with Validation Split:** The world model's ability to generate realistic videos was assessed by conditioning it on action sequences from real-world validation data. The generated videos were compared qualitatively to actual robot footage, demonstrating close emulation of real-robot movements across various morphologies.
- **End-Effector Control Sweeps:** To verify the world model's control capabilities beyond training data, hard-coded policies were used to execute single-axis movements (e.g., move left, close gripper). The generated videos faithfully followed these arbitrary control inputs, confirming the model's understanding of basic robot dynamics.

#### 3.5. Evaluating Policies in WorldGym

With confidence established in the world model, policies were evaluated across three main axes:
- **Correlation with Real-World Performance:** Three leading VLA policies (RT-1-X, Octo, OpenVLA) were evaluated on the OpenVLA Bridge evaluation suite. For each trial, WorldGym was initialized with a real-world initial frame, and the policy's rollout was simulated and scored by the VLM reward.
- **Preservation of Policy Rankings:** This involved comparing:
 - The relative rankings of different VLA models (RT-1-X, Octo, OpenVLA).
 - Different versions and sizes of the same policies (e.g., Octo-Small vs. Octo-Base, OpenVLA v0.1 vs. 7B).
 - The performance of policies (UniPi, DexVLA) at different stages of their training (across various checkpoints).
- **Out-of-Distribution (OOD) Input Evaluation:**
 - **OOD Image Input:** Modern image generation models (e.g., Nano Banana) were used to modify initial frames with unseen objects, distractors, or for specific classification tasks. Policies were then evaluated on these synthetically altered environments.
 - **OOD Language Instruction:** Existing initial frames were paired with modified language instructions (e.g., changing target objects or goals) to test policies' generalization to novel tasks.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of WorldGym yielded several significant findings across its fidelity, correlation with real-world performance, policy ranking capabilities, and insights into policy behaviors with out-of-distribution inputs.

#### 4.1. World Model Fidelity and Reliability

- **Realistic Emulation:** Qualitative assessment demonstrated that the WorldGym's generated video rollouts closely mirrored real-robot dynamics across various initial observations and different robot morphologies (e.g., Bridge, RT-1, VIOLA, Berkeley UR5 robots). This fidelity was observed when conditioning the model on real-world action sequences.
- **Action Adherence:** End-effector control sweeps, where the robot was commanded to move along single action dimensions (e.g., open/close gripper, move left/right), showed that the world model faithfully emulated these arbitrary action sequences, even when they were not explicitly present in the training data.
- **VLM as Reward:** The use of GPT-4o as a reward function proved highly effective. When validated against real RT-1 dataset videos, GPT-4o achieved high true positive (81%) and true negative (97%) rates, with a notably low false positive rate (3%). This indicates its reliability in accurately assessing task success without overestimating performance.

#### 4.2. Correlation with Real-World Policy Performance

- **Strong Correlation:** A pivotal finding was the strong correlation between policy success rates within WorldGym and their real-world counterparts. Across a suite of 17 challenging Bridge evaluation tasks, the Pearson correlation coefficient between WorldGym and real-world success rates was **r = 0.78 (p < 0.001)**.
- **Close Mean Success Rates:** The mean success rates for policies (RT-1-X, Octo, OpenVLA) in WorldGym differed by an average of only 3.3% from their real-world values. For instance, OpenVLA achieved 70.6% in the real world versus 67.4% in WorldGym, demonstrating WorldGym's quantitative accuracy.

#### 4.3. Preservation of Policy Rankings

- **Consistent VLA Rankings:** WorldGym successfully preserved the relative performance rankings among different state-of-the-art VLAs (RT-1-X, Octo, OpenVLA), consistent with published real-world findings. OpenVLA consistently emerged as the top performer.
- **Model Versions and Sizes:** Evaluations of different versions and sizes of Octo (Small vs. Base) and OpenVLA (v0.1 7B vs. 7B) within WorldGym showed that larger and more recent models achieved higher success rates, again aligning with known real-world performance improvements.
- **Training Progress:** When evaluating robot policies (video-based UniPi and diffusion-based DexVLA) at various training checkpoints, WorldGym consistently assigned higher success rates to policies with more training steps. This demonstrates its utility as a reliable signal for monitoring policy learning and selecting optimal checkpoints.

#### 4.4. Insights from Out-of-Distribution (OOD) Evaluations

- **Object Generalization Challenges:** When presented with OOD initial images, policies struggled to distinguish objects solely by shape. For example, OpenVLA often picked the closer object when a carrot and an orange were present, only reliably picking the orange when the carrot's color was edited to red, suggesting a reliance on color cues over shape.
- **Distraction by 2D Facades:** Modern policies showed susceptibility to adversarial visual distractors. OpenVLA mistakenly attempted to interact with an image of a carrot displayed on a laptop screen in 15% of trials, indicating a challenge in distinguishing between 2D representations and 3D objects. The addition of random OOD distractors significantly degraded the performance of all tested VLAs, with OpenVLA being the most robust but still suffering a 41.5% drop.
- **Classification Limitations:** While OpenVLA performed perfectly on simple OOD color classification tasks (e.g., "pick red" paper), all policies scored near chance on more complex classification tasks involving shapes, celebrity faces, or animals.
- **OOD Language Generalization:** WorldGym facilitated evaluation of policies on unseen language instructions (e.g., "Move the pot to the counter"). OpenVLA generally exhibited stronger generalization capabilities in these scenarios, likely due to its more powerful VLM backbone and richer pre-training data.
These OOD findings provide valuable diagnostic information, highlighting specific weaknesses in current robot policies that can inform future research directions without requiring costly real-world experiments.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

WorldGym represents a significant step forward in robot policy evaluation, offering a practical and powerful framework with far-reaching implications for the field of robotics and AI.

- **Reduced Cost and Accelerated Development:** The most immediate impact of WorldGym is its ability to provide a safe, reproducible, and highly efficient environment for policy evaluation. By largely replacing expensive and time-consuming real-world testing, it democratizes access to robot development, enabling faster iteration cycles, rapid prototyping, and more extensive experimentation. Researchers can quickly test new policy architectures, hyperparameters, or training regimes without needing physical robots or complex simulator setup, thereby accelerating the pace of robot learning research.

- **Enhanced Reliability and Generalization Testing:** WorldGym's capacity to facilitate Out-of-Distribution (OOD) evaluations is transformative. The ease with which researchers can introduce novel objects, modify environments, or alter task instructions allows for systematic probing of policy robustness and generalization capabilities. The insights gained from OOD tests – such as policies' struggles with shape distinction, susceptibility to 2D distractors, or limitations in complex classification – are invaluable. These findings pinpoint specific vulnerabilities in current VLA models, guiding future research to develop more robust and truly generalizable robot intelligence. This proactive identification of failure modes before real-world deployment is critical for building trustworthy AI systems.

- **Standardized Benchmarking:** By offering a consistent, controllable, and high-fidelity evaluation environment, WorldGym can serve as a standardized platform for benchmarking different robot policies. Its ability to preserve relative policy rankings across various models, sizes, and training stages ensures that comparisons are meaningful and reliable, fostering healthy competition and clear progress in the field.

- **Bridging the Sim-to-Real Gap:** WorldGym directly addresses the long-standing sim-to-real gap by learning environmental dynamics from real-world video data, rather than relying on hand-coded physics engines. While the authors acknowledge that fully realistic object interaction remains a challenge for current generative models, WorldGym provides a highly practical starting point for pre-deployment sanity checks, reducing the effort required to transfer policies from simulation to reality.

- **Future Research Directions:** This work opens up several exciting avenues for future research. It motivates further improvements in world model fidelity, especially for complex object interactions and long-horizon tasks. It also encourages the development of robot policies that are specifically designed to be robust to the OOD challenges identified by WorldGym, such as better 3D understanding, improved object recognition across variations, and more sophisticated reasoning from language instructions.

In conclusion, WorldGym is not merely another simulator; it is a novel paradigm for robot policy evaluation that harnesses the power of large-scale generative AI. By offering unprecedented efficiency, scalability, and diagnostic capabilities, it lays a crucial foundation for the development and deployment of safer, more reliable, and ultimately more capable autonomous robots.
