# Ensuring Force Safety in Vision-Guided Robotic Manipulation via Implicit Tactile Calibration

> [!summary] Summary
> A diffusion-based framework, SafeDiff, enables vision-guided robots to perform constrained manipulation tasks like opening doors while ensuring force safety by implicitly calibrating vision-based plans with real-time tactile feedback. This system achieved high success rates and substantially reduced harmful forces across diverse door types and under disturbances in both simulation and real-world experiments, benefiting from few-shot sim-to-real transfer.

## Problem

- Vision-guided robotic manipulation in constrained environments can generate unintended, harmful forces due to discrepancies between planned and actual object motion paths.
- Traditional force control methods (e.g., impedance control) often require explicit modeling, limiting adaptability, while current deep learning methods prioritize task success over precise force safety.
- There is a lack of robust methods to integrate real-time tactile feedback for dynamic adjustment of vision-guided plans, especially in unstructured environments.

## Method

- Developed **SafeDiff**, a novel ==diffusion-based framework== that generates a sequence of safe robot states by integrating real-time force feedback to implicitly calibrate vision-guided predictions.
- Utilizes a ==Vision-Guided Mapping Module (VMM)== as an encoder to create an initial state representation and a ==Tactile-Guided Calibration Module (TCM)== as a decoder to refine states using force feedback via ==cross-attention==.
- Introduced **SafeDoorManip50k**, a large-scale multimodal simulation dataset with force feedback, along with novel evaluation metrics for force safety.

## Results

- SafeDiff (V+T) outperformed baselines in simulation for both seen and unseen doors, achieving **80.07%** Success Rate (SuR) for seen and **81.03%** SuR for unseen doors, while significantly reducing Average Harmful Force (around **5.0 N**) and increasing Safety Rate (e.g., SaR-80@10N of **78.73%** for seen doors).
- Tactile calibration proved crucial, with SafeDiff (V+T) maintaining significantly lower harmful forces and higher safety rates compared to its vision-only variant (SafeDiff V), especially under environmental disturbances.
- Demonstrated effective few-shot sim-to-real transfer, achieving **100%** SuR and high safety on real-world doors with only **20%** of real data for fine-tuning, and exhibiting robust generalization to unseen real doors and manual disturbances.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Implicit tactile calibration, mirroring human sensory integration, effectively corrects vision-guided state trajectories to prevent harmful forces during constrained manipulation.
> - Diffusion models can be adapted to plan safe robot states, dynamically adjusting to environmental constraints and disturbances through sensory feedback.
> - A comprehensive multimodal dataset including tactile information is crucial for training models that can achieve both task success and robust force safety.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Robotic manipulation in complex, unstructured environments frequently involves objects with constrained motion trajectories, such as opening doors or drawers. In such scenarios, discrepancies can arise between the robot's vision-guided end-effector trajectory and the object's actual physical motion path. These discrepancies can generate unintended forces, which, if sufficiently large, may lead to task failure and potential damage to the manipulated object or the robotic system itself. Ensuring precise force regulation is thus a critical aspect of both safe and efficient robotic manipulation.

Traditionally, force safety in robotics has been addressed through methods like impedance control. These methods involve regulating the stiffness and damping characteristics of the robot's end-effector to achieve compliant motion and adapt to external forces. However, impedance control often requires explicit modeling of interaction dynamics, limiting its applicability primarily to structured environments where system parameters can be precisely defined.

With the increasing demand for robotic deployment in diverse and unstructured settings, end-to-end deep learning approaches have emerged as an alternative. These methods offer enhanced adaptability and data-driven force regulation without the necessity of explicit system modeling. Despite advancements in deep learning for robotics, much of the existing research has predominantly focused on improving task success rates, often without specific attention to the critical aspect of precise force control and the mitigation of harmful forces.

This work addresses this identified gap by introducing a novel deep learning-based approach aimed at enhancing force safety in robotic manipulation. It departs from traditional force control methods by employing pure position control, which can reduce hardware requirements while maintaining adaptability. The paper defines "force safety" as the ability to keep harmful forces during manipulation below a predefined threshold, identifying "safe states" as those that adhere to the object's physical motion constraints.

The research draws inspiration from bionic principles, observing that humans typically estimate future object states using visual perception and then refine these estimates in real-time based on tactile feedback. This biomimetic approach forms the foundation for integrating real-time tactile feedback to dynamically adjust vision-guided robot states. The authors also highlight a gap in existing datasets for door manipulation, noting that while many offer visual data, they often overlook tactile information crucial for safe planning. To address this, a new large-scale simulation dataset, SafeDoorManip50k, is introduced, which incorporates multimodal data, including force feedback.

In summary, this work positions itself at the intersection of vision-based robotic manipulation and multimodal sensing, specifically targeting the often-neglected challenge of force safety by integrating tactile feedback into a state planning framework within unstructured environments. It aims to overcome limitations of traditional control methods and current deep learning approaches by explicitly prioritizing safety alongside task success.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to ensure force safety in vision-guided robotic manipulation, particularly in tasks where the robot's motion trajectory is constrained by the physical properties of the manipulated objects. This objective stems from the observation that discrepancies between vision-guided trajectories and object motion paths can generate unintended and harmful forces, potentially leading to task failure or damage to the robot and the object.

Specific objectives and motivations driving this work include:

1. **Mitigating Harmful Forces:** The central motivation is to prevent or significantly reduce the occurrence of harmful forces during manipulation. These forces arise when the robot's end-effector trajectory deviates from the object's constrained motion path. The authors define "force safety" as maintaining these harmful forces within a specified safe threshold.
2. **Addressing Limitations of Existing Methods:**
 - **Traditional Force Control (e.g., Impedance Control):** While effective in structured environments, these methods require explicit modeling of interaction dynamics, making them less suitable for the adaptability needed in unstructured settings.
 - **End-to-End Deep Learning Approaches:** Current deep learning methods primarily focus on improving task success rates and often do not explicitly consider or optimize for precise force control and safety. This work aims to bridge this gap by incorporating force safety as a primary concern.
3. **Developing a Novel State Planning Approach:** The paper aims to propose a new deep learning-based state planning method that uses pure position control, thereby potentially reducing hardware requirements compared to complex force control systems. The motivation here is to plan "safe states" that strictly adhere to the physical properties and constrained trajectories of manipulated objects.
4. **Integrating Multimodal Sensory Feedback:** Inspired by human manipulation, a key motivation is to dynamically integrate real-time tactile feedback to refine vision-guided state plans. Humans estimate future states visually and adjust based on touch. Replicating this capability in robots is challenging due to the complex, non-linear dynamics between current force feedback and future state refinements. The proposed diffusion model, SafeDiff, is designed to approximate these dynamics.
5. **Enhancing Robustness and Generalization:** The goal is to develop a system that is robust against external disturbances and can generalize effectively to unseen objects and environments. Tactile feedback is hypothesized to be crucial for this robustness by enabling implicit calibration.
6. **Establishing a Force Safety Benchmark:** Recognizing the lack of comprehensive benchmarks for force safety in robotic manipulation, the authors are motivated to introduce novel evaluation metrics that precisely assess force-wise and scenario-wise safety.
7. **Creating a Large-Scale Multimodal Dataset:** Existing datasets for tasks like door opening often focus on visual data, lacking crucial tactile information needed for safe manipulation planning. The creation of SafeDoorManip50k aims to provide a large-scale, multimodal resource that collects force feedback, supporting the training and evaluation of methods focused on force safety.
8. **Facilitating Few-shot Sim-to-Real Transfer:** A practical motivation is to reduce the extensive data requirements and risks associated with real-world robot training by enabling effective transfer of learned policies from simulation to the real world with minimal real-world fine-tuning.

Overall, the core motivation is to enhance the safety and reliability of robotic manipulation in constrained and unstructured environments by developing an intelligent system that can dynamically adjust its plans based on real-time force feedback, inspired by biological principles, and supported by dedicated datasets and evaluation metrics.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology centers on SafeDiff, a novel diffusion-based framework designed to ensure force safety in vision-guided robotic manipulation tasks involving constrained object motion. The core idea is to generate a prospective robot state sequence by integrating real-time force feedback as a calibration signal for vision-guided predictions.

**Diffusion Model Foundation:**
The approach leverages diffusion models, which are generative models that learn to synthesize data by reversing a Markovian process where Gaussian noise is incrementally added to data samples. The forward process systematically corrupts data, while the reverse process involves training a neural network to estimate the reverse conditional distribution, effectively undoing the noise to recover the original data. Inspired by Decision Diffuser, SafeDiff adapts this generative capability to plan safe robot states.

**Overall Framework (SafeDiff):**
SafeDiff aims to generate a consistent robot state sequence $S = \{S_k\}_{k=1}^L$ of length $L$ that ensures force safety, conditioned on visual-tactile information observed during manipulation. The model employs an encoder-decoder architecture:
- **Input:** The model receives a set of Gaussian noise $N \in \mathbb{R}^{L \times 7}$ (where 7 represents the end-effector pose), visual representation $O$ of the current scene context (derived from an image $I \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times W \times 3}$), and the current robot state $R \in \mathbb{R}^{7+6+7+7}$ (encompassing end-effector pose, velocity, and joint position, velocity). Real-time force feedback $F$ is also provided for calibration.
- **Output:** After $T$ denoising iterations, the model produces the sequence of $L$ consecutive robot states $S \in \mathbb{R}^{L \times 7}$. The use of robot states (end-effector pose) instead of direct actions is motivated by the observation that door opening trajectories are predictable, and robot states are more directly correlated with potential harmful force magnitudes, simplifying the integration of tactile feedback during training.

**Network Architecture Design:**
The network follows a multi-scale U-Net inspired architecture, facilitating hierarchical feature extraction and reconstruction.

1. **Vision-Guided Mapping Module (VMM) - Encoder:**
 - The VMM constitutes the encoder, composed of a series of modules at different temporal scales (downsampling the sequence length).
 - **Initial State Representation:** It generates an initial robot state representation using visual information ($I$) and the current robot state ($\hat{S}$).
 - **Scene Context Extraction:** A Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) followed by a Resnet block extracts the current scene context (e.g., door size, relative position) from $I$ and $\hat{S}$.
 - **FiLM Conditioning:** Following FiLM, this extracted scene context is used as affine coefficients ($\alpha, \beta$) to condition the input Gaussian noise ($N$), mapping it into an initial state representation ($S^*$). Specifically, $S^* = \text{Res}(\alpha \cdot N + \beta)$.
 - **Temporal Coherence:** A self-attention (Sttn) layer and another Resnet block are applied to enhance the temporal coherence of these initial state representations.

2. **Tactile-Guided Calibration Module (TCM) - Decoder:**
 - The TCM forms the decoder, consisting of a series of modules for progressive upsampling and refinement.
 - **Temporal Coherence Enhancement:** Before calibration, the state representation $S^*$ from the encoder is further processed through a combination of two Resnet blocks and one self-attention block to enhance temporal coherence.
 - **Safety Context Extraction:** An MLP extracts "safety context" from the real-time force feedback $F$. The authors posit that harmful forces and state errors are two physical forms of force insecurity in different spaces (force space vs. state space).
 - **Implicit State Residual Calibration:** A cross-attention block (Cttn) maps this extracted safety context into implicit state residuals. These residuals are then added to calibrate the initial state trajectory generated by the encoder: $S^* = S^* + \text{Cttn}(S^*, \text{MLP}(F))$. This process refines the vision-guided states to progressively satisfy the constraints imposed by the door's properties, thereby ensuring force safety.
 - **Hierarchical Processing:** The encoder's VMM performs progressive downsampling (e.g., sequence length $L \to L/16$, feature dimensions $3 \to 256$), while the decoder's TCM performs progressive upsampling, integrating tactile features to refine the trajectory. Shortcut connections between corresponding encoder and decoder layers are used to ensure effective gradient propagation and preserve fine-grained details.

**Training and Data:**
- **Offline Learning:** The model is trained using offline demonstrations collected from a simulator. This allows the model to learn the complex dynamics of door opening and embed this knowledge into the state representation.
- **Online Calibration:** During inference, real-time tactile feedback is utilized for online implicit calibration of the vision-guided states.

**Dataset (SafeDoorManip50k):**
To support this methodology, a new large-scale simulation dataset, SafeDoorManip50k, was created. It includes a diverse collection of 57 doors with unique designs and textures. Modifications were made to the door handle collision mesh in Isaac Gym to ensure accurate readings of x-axis harmful forces. The dataset includes 47,727 training demonstrations and extensive test scenarios for both seen and unseen doors, with randomized parameters like door type, size, position, hinge properties, robot stiffness, and lighting conditions. Ground truth labels for door opening angle and handle pose are derived analytically from the simulation engine. The data collection strategy includes introducing temporally decaying random positional noise to target coordinates during execution to facilitate the model's implicit tactile calibration capabilities.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents quantitative and qualitative results from both simulated and real-world experiments, evaluating the effectiveness of the SafeDiff framework in ensuring force safety during robotic manipulation.

**Evaluation Metrics:**
The authors introduced several metrics specifically for force safety assessment:
- **Success Rate (SuR):** Percentage of tasks completed without exceeding a peak force of 20 N.
- **Average Harmful Force (AHF):** Mean magnitude of harmful force ($\|F_{\text{harmful}}\|$).
- **Maximum Harmful Force (MHF):** Peak magnitude of harmful force ($\|F_{\text{harmful}}\|$).
- **Safety Rate (SaR-95 and SaR-80):** Percentage of successfully manipulated scenarios where $\ge 95\%$ (or $\ge 80\%$) of the generated states maintain $\|F_{\text{harmful}}\|_k \le f$ (a defined force threshold).

**Simulation Experiments:**
Experiments were conducted using the SafeDoorManip50k dataset, comparing SafeDiff (with visual input only, "Ours (V)", and with visual + tactile inputs, "Ours (V+T)") against three baselines: Li et al., Haptic-ACT, and UniDoorManip. The task involved opening a door to an angle greater than 30 degrees.

- **Overall Performance (Table 1):**
 - SafeDiff (V+T) consistently outperformed all baselines across almost all metrics for both seen and unseen door scenarios.
 - For **seen doors**, SafeDiff (V+T) achieved a SuR of 80.07%, AHF of 5.07 N, and MHF of 15.03 N. Critically, its SaR values were substantially higher, e.g., SaR-80@10N was 78.73% compared to the next best at 43.12%.
 - For **unseen doors**, SafeDiff (V+T) maintained strong performance with SuR of 81.03%, AHF of 5.08 N, and MHF of 14.59 N. Its generalization capabilities for safety were evident with a SaR-80@10N of 79.33%, significantly outperforming other methods.
- **Ablation Study (Q1: Tactile Calibration Impact):**
 - Comparing SafeDiff (V+T) with SafeDiff (V) (without tactile feedback) highlighted the importance of tactile calibration. While SafeDiff (V) showed a reasonable SuR (78.89% seen, 51.49% unseen), its AHF, MHF, and particularly SaR values were much worse. For instance, SaR-80@10N dropped from 78.73% to 57.65% for seen doors and from 79.33% to 21.35% for unseen doors. This indicates that tactile feedback is crucial for ensuring force safety and for robust generalization, as vision-only planning struggles with complex dynamics in dynamic, unstructured environments.
- **Disturbance Experiment (Q2: Environmental Disturbances):**
 - When a periodic impulsive disturbance was applied (0.03m positional deviation at 1.5Hz), SafeDiff (V+T) demonstrated effective counteraction. It maintained harmful forces within a relatively small range and successfully completed the door-opening task. In contrast, SafeDiff (V) and other baselines failed to overcome the disturbance effectively, leading to higher harmful forces or task failure.

**Real-world Experiments:**
Real-world experiments were conducted using a KUKA iiwa14 robot, Intel RealSense D435i camera, and internal force sensors. Three doors (one seen, two unseen) were used. Due to hardware constraints, experiments were on a smaller scale, but multiple repetitions and randomizations were performed.

- **Q1: Few-shot Sim-to-Real Transfer:**
 - A model initially trained on 700 simulation demonstrations and then fine-tuned with only 20% (22 demonstrations) of 110 real-world demonstrations (Sim(100%)+Real(20%)) achieved 100% SuR on the seen real door. It also exhibited low AHF (3.763 N), MHF (18.581 N), and high SaR values (100% for SaR-95/80@10N and SaR-95/80@15N), performing comparably or even better in terms of safety metrics than a model trained on 100% real data. This indicates the effectiveness of few-shot fine-tuning and the value of the large-scale simulation dataset.
- **Q2: Real-world Generalization:**
 - The few-shot fine-tuned model showed robust generalization to unseen real-world doors, maintaining 100% SuR and relatively low harmful forces. The authors noted that the model was more sensitive to changes in door size than appearance, but implicit tactile calibration successfully corrected the motion trajectory, aligning with the bionic inspiration.
- **Q3: Anti-disturbance in Real-world:**
 - Under manual external disturbances during door opening, SafeDiff (V+T) effectively calibrated these disturbances online, keeping harmful forces at a low level. Compared to the baseline (Li et al.), which showed significantly higher harmful forces and failed to meet safety thresholds under disturbance (0% SaR values), SafeDiff (V+T) demonstrated strong adaptability.

In summary, the main findings indicate that SafeDiff, particularly with its implicit tactile calibration mechanism, significantly enhances force safety in robotic manipulation. It achieves high success rates while substantially reducing harmful forces, generalizes well to unseen scenarios, and exhibits robustness against environmental disturbances, both in simulation and real-world settings, with the added benefit of few-shot sim-to-real transfer.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents several significant contributions and has potential impacts on the field of robotic manipulation:

1. **Advancement in Force Safety:** The work directly addresses a critical and often underserved aspect of robotic manipulation: force safety. By explicitly defining, measuring, and optimizing for the reduction of harmful forces, it moves beyond mere task success rates to ensure the physical integrity of both robots and manipulated objects. This is a crucial step for the reliable deployment of robots in real-world scenarios, especially when interacting with delicate or human-proximate environments.

2. **Novel Deep Learning Architecture for Force Control:** The introduction of SafeDiff, a diffusion-based model integrating real-time tactile feedback for implicit state calibration, offers a novel paradigm for force regulation. Unlike traditional impedance control, it operates with pure position control, potentially simplifying hardware requirements and increasing adaptability to unstructured environments without needing explicit dynamic modeling.

3. **Enhanced Robustness and Generalization:** The demonstrated ability of SafeDiff to maintain force safety and task success under various disturbances (both simulated and real-world) and generalize to unseen objects is highly significant. This robustness is critical for real-world applications where environmental conditions and object properties can vary unpredictably. The strong performance on unseen doors and under disturbances highlights the adaptability endowed by the tactile calibration mechanism.

4. **Efficient Sim-to-Real Transfer:** The success of few-shot fine-tuning from simulation to real-world environments is a major practical impact. This capability significantly reduces the need for extensive and costly real-world data collection, which is often a bottleneck in robot learning. It also minimizes the risk of damaging real robots or objects during the initial stages of learning, thereby accelerating research and development.

5. **Creation of a New Benchmark and Dataset:** The introduction of the SafeDoorManip50k dataset and a set of comprehensive, physically grounded evaluation metrics (SuR, AHF, MHF, SaR-95, SaR-80) fills a notable gap in the research landscape. This benchmark provides a standardized platform for future research in force-safe robotic manipulation, enabling robust comparison and fostering innovation in the field. The multimodal nature of the dataset, particularly its inclusion of force feedback, is invaluable.

6. **Bionic Inspiration for Robot Design:** The approach, inspired by how humans use vision for estimation and touch for real-time adjustment, suggests a promising direction for developing more intelligent and dexterous robotic systems. This biomimetic integration of sensory modalities could lead to more intuitive and adaptable robot behaviors.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Industrial Applications:** Robots could perform assembly, handling, or quality control tasks with delicate components, reducing damage rates and improving product quality.
- **Service Robotics:** Enhances safety in applications like elderly care, domestic assistance, or logistics, where robots interact with fragile items or in environments with humans.
- **Exploration and Disaster Response:** Robots operating in unknown or hazardous environments could adapt more safely to physical interactions.
- **Reduced Development Costs:** The few-shot sim-to-real capability can significantly lower the barrier for developing new robotic manipulation skills, making advanced robotics more accessible.

While the current work focuses on door-opening tasks and gripper manipulation, the foundational principles and the benchmark established could inspire broader research into other complex manipulation tasks, dexterous manipulation, and the integration of diverse multimodal sensors for ensuring force safety in a wider range of robotic applications. The limitations acknowledged by the authors themselves also serve as clear avenues for future impactful research building on this work.
