# AeroVerse: UAV-Agent Benchmark Suite for Simulating, Pre-training, Finetuning, and Evaluating Aerospace Embodied World Models

> [!summary] Summary
> AeroVerse, a comprehensive benchmark suite, is introduced to advance UAV-agent research by providing tools for simulating, pre-training, fine-tuning, and evaluating aerospace embodied world models. The developed SkyAgentX model, integrating specialized architectures and training, achieved an average 8.52% performance improvement over baseline visual-language models across four core aerospace embodied tasks.

## Problem

- Absence of a standardized benchmark suite, including specific tasks and datasets, for developing autonomous UAV intelligence in complex outdoor 3D urban environments.
- Existing embodied world models primarily focus on ground-level agents, limiting their direct applicability and performance for UAVs in aerospace-specific tasks.
- Significant challenges and high costs associated with acquiring and annotating large-scale 3D data from UAVs in real-world, dynamic outdoor settings.

## Method

- Developed **AeroVerse**, a comprehensive benchmark suite featuring ==AeroSimulator== for high-fidelity UAV flight simulation in realistic urban scenes.
- Constructed extensive datasets, including ==AerialAgent-Ego15k== (real-world) and ==CyberAgent-Ego500k== (virtual image-text-pose), along with five expert-annotated downstream task datasets.
- Introduced **SkyAgentX**, a UAV-agent large model that integrates aerospace embodied ==Chain-of-Thought reasoning== and ==Multitask Curriculum Learning== for perception, reasoning, navigation, and planning.

## Results

- SkyAgentX consistently outperformed existing 2D and 3D visual-language models, achieving an average **8.52%** improvement across scene awareness, spatial reasoning, navigational exploration, and task planning.
- Baseline models, including the GPT-4 series, showed marked performance degradation on more complex aerospace tasks and frequently exhibited hallucinations, especially 3D-LLM when generalizing from indoor to outdoor environments.
- Qualitative analysis and human evaluations confirmed SkyAgent and SkyAgentX produced more accurate, detailed, and coherent outputs for complex tasks like spatial reasoning and planning, closely aligning with ground truth compared to baselines.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Existing 2D and 3D visual-language models show significant limitations and often hallucinate when applied to complex aerospace embodied tasks, highlighting the need for domain-specific solutions.
> - Domain-specific data, specialized architectures like Chain-of-Thought, and progressive training strategies (multitask curriculum learning) are crucial for developing robust and adaptable UAV autonomy.
> - Automated, GPT-4-based evaluation methods can effectively align with human preferences for complex, open-ended tasks in embodied intelligence, providing flexible and objective assessment.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of autonomous systems, particularly unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs), represents a significant area of research in the aerospace sector. Current UAV applications, such as inspection, monitoring, and rescue operations, frequently rely on manual remote control, which is resource-intensive and prone to human error and fatigue. The broader research landscape is shifting towards equipping these platforms with autonomous perception, cognition, and action capabilities, termed "aerospace embodied intelligence."

Recent advancements in visual-language models (VLMs) and embodied world models have significantly enhanced the capabilities of intelligent agents, primarily in ground-level and indoor scenarios. These models enable agents to perceive environments and plan tasks effectively. However, the application of such models to UAV intelligent agents in complex outdoor, three-dimensional (3D) urban environments remains largely unexplored. This gap is attributed to the lack of systematic and standardized benchmark suites tailored for aerospace embodied intelligence, including definitions for UAV-specific tasks, methods for 3D data acquisition, and high costs associated with data collection in dynamic, large-scale outdoor settings.

This work positions itself to address this identified research gap by constructing AeroVerse, a comprehensive benchmark suite. AeroVerse is designed to provide the necessary tools—a simulation platform, large-scale datasets, standardized tasks, and evaluation metrics—to facilitate the simulation, pre-training, fine-tuning, and evaluation of aerospace embodied world models for UAVs. By focusing on UAV-specific challenges, this research aims to bridge the current disconnect between general embodied intelligence research and its specialized application in the aerospace domain.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this study is to construct a comprehensive benchmark suite, AeroVerse, to advance the simulation, pre-training, fine-tuning, and evaluation of aerospace embodied world models for UAVs. This overarching goal is driven by several key motivations stemming from the challenges in developing autonomous UAV intelligence:

- **Necessity for Autonomous UAVs:** Current UAV applications are largely manually controlled, leading to high operational costs, efficiency limitations, and potential human errors (e.g., in photovoltaic inspections or traffic monitoring). There is an urgent need to empower UAVs with autonomous intelligence to reduce these dependencies and enhance operational capabilities.
- **Gap in Embodied World Models for UAVs:** Existing embodied world models predominantly focus on ground-level agents in indoor scenarios or unmanned vehicles in outdoor environments. Research on UAV intelligent agents, particularly concerning aerospace embodied world models, has been limited, lacking systematic benchmarks and datasets.
- **Undefined UAV Embodied Tasks:** Unlike ground-based agents with established tasks like indoor/outdoor navigation or command following, UAV agents operate in a four-dimensional (space-time) environment with unique complexities such as scene randomization and local observability. The specific downstream tasks for aerial-embodied agents, encompassing awareness, cognition, planning, and decision-making, have not been clearly defined or standardized.
- **Difficulty and High Cost of UAV 3D Data Acquisition:** Obtaining 3D data for outdoor environments, especially for UAVs, requires specialized equipment and skilled professionals, posing a higher barrier compared to indoor 3D data collection. The extensive operational range and complex, obstacle-rich environments (e.g., buildings, trees) of UAVs lead to significantly high costs and training requirements for data annotators.

To address these motivations, the study defines five specific downstream tasks for UAV-embodied agents:
- **Aerospace Embodied Scene Awareness:** Enabling UAVs to perceive and describe the surrounding 3D environment from a first-person perspective based on their location.
- **Aerospace Embodied Spatial Reasoning:** Empowering UAVs to model spatial relationships between objects in a 3D scene and reason about these relationships in response to specific questions.
- **Aerospace Embodied Navigational Exploration:** Requiring UAVs to comprehend long-range navigation commands, explore urban environments, and answer questions about object characteristics at destinations.
- **Aerospace Embodied Task Planning:** Enabling UAVs to generate detailed, landmark-level, multi-stage path plans from an initial position to a target endpoint in large urban scenes.
- **Aerospace Embodied Motion Decision:** Facilitating real-time, iterative decision-making for UAVs to adjust action strategies based on environmental interactions and historical actions to reach a target endpoint, completing an end-to-end closed loop of perception, reasoning, planning, and action.

By developing AeroVerse, the authors aim to provide the foundational resources to overcome these challenges and accelerate research in aerospace embodied intelligence.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology centers on constructing AeroVerse, a benchmark suite composed of a simulation platform, specialized datasets, evaluation metrics, and a novel UAV-agent model.

#### 3.1. Simulation Platform: AeroSimulator

AeroSimulator is developed using Unreal Engine 4 for environment loading and Microsoft AirSim for drone model construction, enabling realistic drone flight scenarios.
- **Scenes:** It incorporates four realistic urban scenes (Shanghai, Shenzhen, School, Residence) derived from the UrbanScene3D dataset, which are 3D reconstructions of actual physical locations. These scenes vary in size and object density, ranging from 30 hectares to 3,700 hectares. The simulator supports various lighting (day, evening, night), seasonal (spring, summer, autumn, winter), and climatic conditions (sunny, cloudy, light snow) to enhance sim-to-real transferability.
- **Observations:** The simulated drone is equipped with five cameras (forward, backward, left, right, overhead) operating in three modes:
 - **RGB Camera:** Captures 1920x1080 resolution images.
 - **Depth Camera:** Produces depth maps at 1920x1080 resolution, with depth encoding up to 500 meters.
 - **Object Segmentation Camera:** Generates segmentation maps (e.g., gray for buildings, green for trees, red for vehicles) at 1920x1080 resolution.
- **Actions:** The simulator supports continuous changes in position, direction, and speed. For training, eight low-level actions are defined: forward (5m), left turn (15 degrees), right turn (15 degrees), ascend (1m), descend (1m), left shift (1m), right shift (1m), and stop.

#### 3.2. Dataset Suite

Ten trained experts spent eight months developing a comprehensive dataset suite comprising two pre-training datasets and five downstream task instruction datasets.
- **AerialAgent-Ego15k (Real-world Pre-training):**
 - **Image Acquisition:** 15,094 high-resolution (e.g., 6000x4000) first-person view city images captured by drones from real urban areas (Lihu, Longhua, Yingrenshi, Yuehai) derived from the UrbanBIS dataset. 10,000 images were randomly selected for the dataset.
 - **Text Generation:** LLaVA-1.5-13B is used to generate fine-grained multi-attribute text descriptions (quantity, appearance, shape, spatial relationships of buildings, roads, trees, etc.) from a first-person perspective, excluding sky descriptions.
 - **Statistics:** Contains 158,379 sentences and 2,167,455 words (21,489 unique), with average description length of 144 words (max 440).
- **CyberAgent-Ego500k (Virtual Pre-training):**
 - **Image Acquisition:** 500,000 first-person perspective images (512x512 pixels) generated from AeroSimulator across the four virtual cityscapes, with dense sampling in object-rich areas. Drone poses are recorded randomly to avoid obstacles.
 - **Image-Text-Pose Generation:** Includes drone first-person images, multi-attribute text descriptions (appearance, quantity, shape, absolute/relative position of objects), and image-text-pose alignment (drone's position and orientation) to enhance self-centered scene understanding.
 - **Statistics:** Contains 4,725,682 sentences and 63,539,302 words (94,823 unique), with average description length of 127 words (max 865).
- **Downstream Task Instruction Datasets (SkyAgent-Scene3k, SkyAgent-Reason3k, SkyAgent-Nav3k, SkyAgent-Plan3k, SkyAgent-Act3k):**
 - These datasets are constructed through expert annotation using AeroSimulator, involving drone navigation and data collection in 3D urban environments.
 - **SkyAgent-Scene3k:** 3,000 instances requiring multi-directional (front, back, left, right) and multi-attribute descriptions (quantifier, color, shape, object) of urban elements from the drone's perspective.
 - **SkyAgent-Reason3k:** 3,000 instances of question-and-answer pairs designed to enhance spatial reasoning capabilities across six modes: Color, Count, Shape, Object, Predictive, and Counterfactual Reasoning.
 - **SkyAgent-Nav3k:** 3,000 instances providing long-range, multi-stage navigation instructions with refined object attribute descriptions and embedded questions for scene exploration (e.g., describe objects at destination).
 - **SkyAgent-Plan3k:** 3,000 instances for task planning, where the UAV agent generates detailed, step-by-step path plans between specified start and end points, identifying landmark-level objects.
 - **SkyAgent-Act3k:** Dense motion sequences and orientations for 3,000 flight paths, focusing on real-time motion decisions from start to end points beyond visual range, avoiding collisions and unnecessary detours.
 - **Image Enhancement:** Data augmentation simulates drone body jitter (camera tilts) and motion blur (directional motion blur kernels) to reflect real-world challenges.

#### 3.3. Evaluation Metrics: SkyAgent-Eval

Automated evaluation methods are developed based on GPT-4, aligning with human preferences, complementing traditional metrics (BLEU-N, CIDEr, SPICE).
- **LLM-Judge-Scene:** Evaluates scene descriptions based on granularity and directional accuracy.
- **LLM-Judge-Reason&Nav:** Analyzes correlation and utility between AI responses and reference answers for reasoning and navigation tasks.
- **LLM-Judge-Plan:** Focuses on alignment of key action sequences and accuracy of landmark descriptions in path planning.
- **Human Evaluation:** A randomly sampled subset of 100 instances per task is scored by experts (0-1 scale) for accuracy, completeness, logical correctness, and feasibility, to validate automated findings.

#### 3.4. SkyAgentX Model Architecture

SkyAgentX is presented as the first UAV-agent large model integrating "perception-reasoning-navigating-planning."
- **Base Model:** Built upon a pre-trained general vision-language model, Internvl-2.5-8B.
- **Aerospace Embodied Chain-of-Thought:** A mechanism that mimics human step-by-step reasoning, breaking down complex problems into intermediate steps, making the decision-making process transparent. Training data for this is automatically expanded through large models (e.g., GPT-4) using prompt engineering.
- **Aerospace Embodied Multitask Curriculum Learning:** A phased, incremental training strategy. The model is progressively trained through the five downstream tasks from simple (Scene Awareness) to complex (Motion Decision), allowing it to adapt to dynamic scenarios. This sequential training builds foundational environmental understanding, then spatial reasoning, followed by navigation, and finally task planning.
- **Integration:** SkyAgent (the model with chain-of-thought and fine-tuning on four tasks) is further enhanced with curriculum learning to form SkyAgentX, unifying perception, reasoning, navigating, and planning into a single framework.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments benchmarked ten mainstream 2D and 3D visual-language models (VLMs) against SkyAgent and SkyAgentX on the AeroVerse suite, revealing their capabilities and limitations in aerospace embodied tasks.

#### 4.1. Overall Performance of Baseline Models

- **Limitations of Existing VLMs:** The results demonstrate significant limitations of existing 2D/3D VLMs, including the GPT-4 series, in complex aerospace embodied tasks.
- **Task-Specific Performance:** Existing models achieved relatively higher scores on **SkyAgent-Scene3k** (scene awareness), but their performance declined markedly on more complex tasks like spatial reasoning, navigational exploration, and task planning.
- **3D-LLM Specifics:** 3D-LLM, despite being a 3D VLM, showed subpar performance, often exhibiting hallucinations due to its primary training on indoor 3D data, which did not generalize effectively to outdoor urban scenes.
- **2D VLM Performance:** 2D VLMs generally outperformed 3D-LLM, likely due to larger training image-text pairs enhancing their generalization. However, they still produced instances of hallucinations or failed to incorporate full 3D context. GPT-4o and GPT-4-vision-preview consistently demonstrated stronger performance among the baselines.

#### 4.2. Performance of SkyAgent and SkyAgentX

- **Superior Performance:** SkyAgentX consistently outperformed existing methods, achieving an average improvement of 8.52% across four core tasks (scene perception, spatial reasoning, navigation exploration, and task planning).
- **SkyAgent-Scene3k (Scene Awareness):** SkyAgent showed substantial improvements in BLEU (e.g., 0.4302 in Shanghai vs. Qwen's 0.2305) and SPICE (0.3083 vs. GPT-4o's 0.1114), indicating enhanced lexical and semantic expression. Human evaluation confirmed a strong preference for SkyAgent's detailed and accurate descriptions (0.87 in Shanghai vs. GPT-4o's 0.45).
- **SkyAgent-Reason3k (Spatial Reasoning):** SkyAgent achieved high BLEU/SPICE scores (0.4598/0.3846 in Shanghai), outperforming baselines. SkyAgentX, with curriculum learning, further improved the LLM-Judge-Reason score and significantly boosted BLEU in complex scenarios (e.g., 23% increase in Residence). Human evaluation showed SkyAgentX's reasoning was decisively preferred (0.88 in Shanghai vs. GPT-4o's 0.40).
- **SkyAgent-Nav3k (Navigational Exploration):** While GPT-4o was strong in this task, SkyAgent achieved a competitive LLM-Judge-Nav score of 0.4903 in Shanghai. Human evaluation for SkyAgentX (0.85 in Shanghai vs. GPT-4o's 0.70) indicated clearer and more useful navigational instructions.
- **SkyAgent-Plan3k (Task Planning):** Many baseline models performed poorly, often failing to generate coherent long-distance plans. SkyAgent achieved a high LLM-Judge-Plan score (0.6175 in Shanghai), significantly surpassing GPT-4o. SkyAgentX further improved planning capabilities, demonstrating enhanced stability and alignment with human preferences (0.90 in Shanghai from human judges vs. GPT-4o's 0.35).
- **Qualitative Analysis:**
 - SkyAgent's outputs aligned closely with ground truth, accurately describing multi-directional scene elements without hallucinations, unlike 3D-LLM's indoor-like descriptions or 2D VLMs' minor inaccuracies.
 - For spatial reasoning, both SkyAgent and SkyAgentX correctly deduced answers requiring 3D context, effectively suppressing irrelevant visual distractions and demonstrating robust constraint propagation.
 - In navigational exploration and task planning, SkyAgent and SkyAgentX showed strong instruction-following and landmark-anchored path generation, outperforming other models in consistency and accuracy, though further refinement in geometric constraints and redundancy reduction was noted.

#### 4.3. Generalization Capabilities

- **Scene Generalization:** All models struggled more in the dense "Campus" scene and performed better in the smaller "Residence" area. SkyAgent and SkyAgentX consistently demonstrated the best performance across all four scenarios.
- **Task Generalization:** SkyAgent and SkyAgentX achieved state-of-the-art performance across all defined tasks (scene awareness, spatial reasoning, navigation, task planning), demonstrating strong captioning abilities in Task 1 and superior reasoning/planning in Tasks 2, 3, and 4.
- **Scaling Law Impact:** Increasing model parameters from 7 billion to 13 billion did not consistently correlate with improved performance, suggesting that architectural innovations and domain-specific data/training strategies (like those in SkyAgentX) are more critical than raw model size for these embodied tasks.

#### 4.4. Technology Ethics

The study acknowledges risks associated with autonomous drone technology, including privacy leakage from visual sensors (necessitating data desensitization) and airspace safety issues from malicious exploitation of flight algorithms. AeroSimulator includes no-fly zone settings (30m to 50m altitude limits) as a safety measure.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The AeroVerse benchmark suite addresses a critical gap in the field of embodied intelligence by providing the first comprehensive platform specifically tailored for unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs) in complex outdoor environments. Its significance and potential impact are multifaceted:

- **Standardized Research Platform:** AeroVerse establishes a systematic and standardized benchmark suite, providing a common ground for researchers to simulate, pre-train, fine-tune, and evaluate aerospace embodied world models. This standardization is crucial for fair comparison of new methods, accelerating research and development in UAV autonomy.
- **Comprehensive Task Definition:** The clear definition of five aerospace embodied downstream tasks (scene awareness, spatial reasoning, navigational exploration, task planning, and motion decision) offers a structured framework. This framework guides future research directions, moving beyond generic embodied intelligence to specific, complex challenges faced by UAV agents.
- **Pioneering Datasets:** The introduction of the first large-scale real-world image-text pre-training dataset (AerialAgent-Ego15k) and a virtual image-text-pose alignment dataset (CyberAgent-Ego500k), along with five expert-annotated instruction datasets, addresses the severe data scarcity for UAV-agent training. These datasets are foundational for developing robust aerospace world models capable of adapting to both simulated and real-world environments.
- **Advanced Evaluation System:** The development of GPT-4-based evaluation metrics (SkyAgent-Eval) provides a flexible, objective, and comprehensive assessment system that aligns closely with human preferences, enhancing the credibility of evaluation outcomes for complex, open-ended tasks.
- **State-of-the-Art Model (SkyAgentX):** The proposed SkyAgentX model, which integrates an aerospace embodied chain-of-thought mechanism and multitask curriculum learning, demonstrates superior performance. It serves as a strong baseline and a generalized solution for autonomous drone intelligence systems, unifying perception, reasoning, navigating, and planning into an end-to-end framework.
- **Revealing Limitations of Existing Models:** Extensive benchmarking of mainstream 2D/3D visual-language models systematically reveals their significant limitations and common failure modes in aerospace embodied tasks. This provides crucial insights for the community, highlighting the necessity for specialized approaches and guiding future model design.
- **Bridging Sim-to-Real Gap:** By integrating real-world datasets with high-fidelity simulated environments and conducting preliminary real-world deployments, AeroVerse aims to facilitate the transfer of models from simulation to practical applications. This is critical for moving research outcomes from theoretical concepts to deployable autonomous UAV systems.
- **Enabling Real-World Applications:** The advancements fostered by AeroVerse have the potential to unlock a wide range of autonomous UAV applications, including automated inspection (e.g., power lines, infrastructure), environmental monitoring (e.g., river garbage detection, forest fire rescue), logistics delivery, and urban surveillance, thereby reducing costs and increasing efficiency in various sectors.

In conclusion, AeroVerse significantly contributes to the field by providing an essential, integrated research infrastructure that will accelerate the development of more capable, robust, and safer intelligent agents for the aerospace domain, pushing the boundaries of autonomous UAV technology.
