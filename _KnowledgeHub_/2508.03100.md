# AVATAR: Reinforcement Learning to See, Hear, and Reason Over Video

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Arizona State University developed AVATAR, an off-policy reinforcement learning framework that enhances Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) for multimodal reasoning over video. The framework achieves superior performance across diverse audio-visual benchmarks while demonstrating a 5x improvement in sample efficiency compared to baseline GRPO.

## Problem

- Current multimodal large language models (MLLMs) struggle with precise spatiotemporal fusion and alignment across video, audio, and language for complex, long-horizon reasoning tasks.
- Existing Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) methods are data-inefficient due to their on-policy nature, particularly problematic for costly video data, and suffer from a 'vanishing advantage problem' where uniform rewards stall learning.
- Standard GRPO employs uniform credit assignment, failing to emphasize critical reasoning steps (e.g., planning, grounding, synthesis) crucial for complex multimodal responses.

## Method

- AVATAR implements an ==off-policy training architecture== with a ==stratified replay buffer==, dynamically categorizing experiences into Easy, Medium, and Hard tiers based on reward, along with a 'hinting mechanism' for persistently challenging prompts.
- It introduces ==Temporal Advantage Shaping (TAS)==, a critic-free modification to GRPO that uses a U-shaped parabolic weighting function to assign higher learning signals to tokens at the beginning (planning) and end (synthesis) of reasoning sequences.
- The framework employs a combination of reward functions (format, final answer accuracy, self-rewarding via majority vote, and a stepwise reasoning judge) and uses a ==Video-Context Reference Score (VCRS)== as a stable baseline for off-policy advantage calculation.

## Results

- AVATAR improved performance by **+4.9** on OmniBench, **+3.0** on DailyOmni, **+2.3** on AV-Odyssey (Qwen2.5-Omni baseline), and **+5.3** on MVBench (Ola-7B baseline) across various audio-visual reasoning benchmarks.
- The framework achieved a **5x** improvement in sample efficiency, reaching **0.80** accuracy within **400** training iterations, requiring **80%** fewer generated completions compared to baseline GRPO.
- Ablation studies confirmed that both the stratified replay buffer and Temporal Advantage Shaping (TAS) each contributed substantial performance gains, validating their individual effectiveness in addressing data inefficiency and credit assignment challenges.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Off-policy learning, augmented by a stratified replay buffer and curriculum-based difficulty, significantly enhances sample efficiency and stabilizes the learning process in reinforcement learning for multimodal MLLMs.
> - Nuanced credit assignment, specifically by amplifying learning signals for initial planning and final synthesis steps, is crucial for effectively training MLLMs on long-horizon and complex multimodal reasoning tasks.
> - Stabilizing the advantage signal through a moving average reference (VCRS) is essential to overcome the vanishing advantage problem, ensuring continuous learning even when group rewards are uniform.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Multimodal large language models (MLLMs) are increasingly applied to tasks involving reasoning over long-horizon video, which necessitates precise spatiotemporal fusion and alignment across modalities such as video, audio, and language. This domain requires MLLMs to balance dense temporal coverage for narrative understanding with high spatial resolution for visual grounding. Reinforcement learning (RL), particularly Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), has emerged as a method for enhancing such reasoning capabilities in MLLMs. While GRPO has demonstrated effectiveness in verifiable domains with dense, deterministic rewards (e.g., mathematics), its application to open-ended video domains presents several limitations.

Existing MLLMs for audio-video understanding have focused on architectural enhancements, including dedicated audio branches, video connectors, clue aggregators, and token interleaving. These methods often excel at direct video question answering but typically do not explicitly rationalize their thoughts through reasoning traces, which can be a limitation for complex audio-visual tasks requiring multi-step reasoning and fine-grained cross-modal alignment, such as localizing sound sources.

The current work identifies three key limitations of GRPO in the context of multimodal video reasoning:
1. **Data inefficiency**: As an on-policy method, standard GRPO discards experiences after a single policy update, leading to high data requirements. This is particularly problematic for video data, which is often expensive and resource-intensive to annotate and process. While some prior methods (e.g., RePO, veRL) attempt to reuse past data, they often treat all experiences equally, failing to prioritize difficult or informative samples.
2. **Vanishing advantage problem**: GRPO's advantage estimation can collapse when rewards within a group of generated responses are identical or nearly identical. This phenomenon eliminates the learning signal, preventing policy updates and hindering learning, especially for consistently easy or difficult queries. Previous attempts to mitigate this, such as modifying sampling or prioritizing individual samples, have not fully resolved the issue of insufficient reward variation within training groups.
3. **Uniform credit assignment**: GRPO assigns a single scalar reward uniformly to all tokens within a generated reasoning chain, irrespective of their varying contributions to the final response. This approach overlooks the differential importance of tokens in Transformer architectures, such as the attention sink effect where initial tokens are critical for planning and final tokens for synthesizing the answer. For long-horizon video reasoning, this uniform treatment can dilute the gradient signal across the sequence, failing to adequately reinforce critical planning and synthesis phases.

This research introduces AVATAR to address these identified limitations, aiming to develop a more robust, data-efficient, and effective RL framework for multimodal reasoning, particularly for audio-video tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to improve multimodal reasoning capabilities in MLLMs, specifically focusing on video and audio-visual tasks, by overcoming the noted limitations of existing Reinforcement Learning (RL) approaches like Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO). The motivation behind AVATAR is rooted in the specific challenges GRPO encounters when applied to complex, open-ended video domains.

The detailed objectives and motivations are:

1. **To enhance data efficiency and resolve the vanishing advantage problem in GRPO**:
 - **Motivation**: Standard GRPO is an on-policy method that discards valuable past experiences, leading to significant data inefficiency. This is particularly costly in the video domain, which relies on large volumes of expensively annotated data. Furthermore, GRPO suffers from the "vanishing advantage problem," where a lack of reward variance within a group of generated responses (e.g., all uniformly correct or incorrect) results in zero-valued advantages, thus eliminating the learning signal and preventing policy updates. Existing replay mechanisms often fail to address this issue adequately as they do not explicitly ensure sufficient reward diversity within training groups.
 - **Objective**: Introduce an off-policy training architecture that reuses past experiences effectively. This involves implementing a stratified replay buffer designed to retain both successful and failed trajectories based on their historical performance. By ensuring a diverse set of experiences within each training group, the framework aims to induce reward variation, guarantee non-zero advantages, and enable consistent gradient updates, even for challenging queries where reward variance would otherwise collapse.

2. **To address the uniform credit assignment problem in GRPO**:
 - **Motivation**: GRPO assigns a uniform scalar advantage to every token in a generated reasoning sequence. This simplistic credit assignment mechanism fails to account for the differential importance of various reasoning phases. For instance, initial tokens are often crucial for planning and establishing context (e.g., attention sink effect in Transformers), while final tokens are critical for synthesizing the ultimate answer. In long-horizon video reasoning, this uniform treatment dilutes the learning signal, particularly for structurally critical boundary tokens, and makes it difficult to reinforce key planning and synthesis steps.
 - **Objective**: Develop a novel credit assignment strategy, Temporal Advantage Shaping (TAS), that modulates advantages based on a token's position in the reasoning chain. TAS aims to assign greater weight to tokens at the beginning (planning phase) and end (synthesis phase) of the sequence. This is hypothesized to provide a more targeted learning signal, reinforce critical reasoning steps, and mitigate attention drift, especially beneficial for audio-video tasks requiring accurate early grounding (e.g., locating a speaker) and final synthesis (e.g., combining speech and visual cues).

3. **To improve structured multimodal reasoning capabilities, especially for audio-visual tasks**:
 - **Motivation**: While many MLLMs show proficiency in direct video question answering, they often struggle with complex audio-visual tasks that demand multi-step reasoning and fine-grained cross-modal alignment, such as localizing sound sources. The limitations of GRPO (data inefficiency, vanishing advantages, uniform credit assignment) hinder the development of robust reasoning chains in these challenging scenarios.
 - **Objective**: Integrate the proposed off-policy architecture and TAS within a structured, multi-stage RL training pipeline. This curriculum is designed to progressively build reasoning skills, starting from general visual reasoning, advancing to audio-visual alignment, and concluding with fine-grained audio-based object localization. The framework aims to enhance the model's ability to rationalize its thoughts through explicit reasoning traces and perform accurate cross-modal grounding.

By addressing these specific shortcomings, AVATAR seeks to enable MLLMs to learn from a richer diversity of experiences, focus their learning on the most critical reasoning steps, and ultimately achieve stronger, more stable, and more data-efficient performance in multimodal video and audio-visual reasoning tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

AVATAR (Audio-Video Agent for Alignment and Reasoning) is an off-policy Reinforcement Learning (RL) framework built upon Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), designed to address the challenges of data inefficiency, vanishing advantages, and uniform credit assignment in multimodal video reasoning.

#### 3.1. Foundation: Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)

GRPO fine-tunes MLLMs by comparing multiple candidate responses generated by a behavior policy $\pi_{\theta_{old}}$ for a given prompt $q$. Each candidate output $o_i$ typically includes intermediate reasoning steps and a final answer. Scalar rewards $R(o_i)$ are assigned to each response using predefined reward functions. These rewards are then normalized within the group to obtain relative advantages $A_i = \frac{R(o_i) - \mu_R}{\sigma_R + \epsilon_{adv}}$, where $\mu_R$ and $\sigma_R$ are the mean and standard deviation of group rewards, and $\epsilon_{adv}$ is for numerical stability. GRPO maximizes a clipped surrogate objective with KL regularization to update the target policy $\pi_\theta$, encouraging higher probability for better responses while regularizing against a reference policy $\pi_{ref}$ (the pretrained model).

#### 3.2. AVATAR's Core Components

1. **Off-Policy Architecture**: This component is designed to improve sample efficiency and mitigate the vanishing advantage problem.
 - **Stratified Replay Buffer**: A buffer of size 10,000 is employed, divided into three fixed-capacity tiers based on difficulty: Easy (25%), Medium (35%), and Hard (40%). New experiences (q, o, $\pi_{\theta_{off}}$) are stored, with tier assignment determined by a prompt's moving average reward, $\bar{R}(q)$, which serves as a dynamic difficulty metric. Dynamic quantile thresholds based on the $\bar{R}(q)$ score distribution assign experiences to tiers (bottom 40% to 'Hard', next 35% to 'Medium', top 25% to 'Easy'). This stratification ensures that difficult samples are retained in the 'Hard' tier for repeated exposure, allowing the model to learn from failures and preventing successful trajectories on difficult prompts from being overwritten, thereby preserving reward variance.
 - **Hinting Mechanism**: To address situations where the policy struggles with consistently hard prompts (low $\bar{R}(q)$) and stops exploring (low KL divergence), a pre-computed hint is triggered. Hints, generated by a Qwen2.5-VL-72B model using the full problem context (video, audio caption, query, ground truth), provide strategic guidance (e.g., "first locate the object making the sound, then count") without revealing the final answer. This mechanism promotes targeted exploration and helps the agent escape local optima.
 - **Hybrid Training with Selective Replay**: At each training step, a batch combines newly generated on-policy samples and off-policy samples retrieved from the replay buffer. Importance sampling ratios $r_{off_i}(\theta) = \frac{\pi_\theta(o_i|q)}{\pi_{\theta_{off}}(o_i|q)}$ are applied to off-policy data to correct for policy drift. The total objective $J_{AVATAR}(\theta)$ combines the standard on-policy GRPO loss with a corrected off-policy term, weighted by $\alpha$.
 - **Video-Context Reference Score (VCRS)**: To stabilize off-policy updates, which can suffer from a noisy group mean ($\mu_R$) for stale buffer samples, AVATAR introduces VCRS, denoted as $\bar{R}(q)$. This is a stable moving average of rewards for a prompt, computed over its last 20 on-policy instances. $\bar{R}(q)$ replaces the noisy $\mu_R$ in the off-policy advantage calculation, $A_{i,off} = \frac{R(o_i) - \bar{R}(q)}{\sigma_{R,off}}$, providing a more reliable advantage signal.

2. **Temporal Advantage Shaping (TAS)**: This component addresses the uniform credit assignment problem.
 - TAS introduces a U-shaped parabolic weighting function $w_t$ applied to the sequence-level advantage $A_i$, yielding a token-specific shaped advantage $A_{TAS_{i,t}} = w_{i,t} \cdot A_i$.
 - For a sequence of length $L$, each token's position $t$ is normalized to $\tilde{t} = \frac{t}{L-1}$. The weight $w_t = 1.0 + \lambda_{TAS} \cdot (2\tilde{t} - 1)^2$ is computed, where $\lambda_{TAS}$ controls the amplitude of weight variation. This function assigns minimal weight (1.0) to middle tokens ($\tilde{t}=0.5$) and higher weights (1.0 + $\lambda_{TAS}$) to tokens near the beginning ($\tilde{t}=0$) and end ($\tilde{t}=1$), corresponding to the planning and synthesis phases. This position-dependent weighting focuses learning on critical reasoning phases.

#### 3.3. Reward Functions

AVATAR uses a combination of reward functions:
- **Format Reward ($R_{format}$)**: A binary reward checking adherence to the predefined reasoning format (`<think>...</think><answer>...</answer>`).
- **Final Answer Accuracy ($R_{acc}$)**: A black-box evaluation of the final answer compared to ground truth. For numerical tasks, relative Mean Absolute Error (rMAE) provides a dense signal.
- **Self-Rewarding ($R_{self}$)**: Determines a "pseudo-correct" answer by majority vote among K generated answers in a group (ties broken by model confidence). Individual answers receive a binary reward based on agreement with this consensus.
- **Stepwise Reasoning Judge ($R_{judge}$)**: A white-box reward from a frozen VLM judge (InternVL3-2B) that provides a detailed score ($s_{judge} \in$) for reasoning quality within the `<think>` block, evaluating logical consistency and use of grounding clues.

#### 3.4. Training Pipeline

AVATAR is evaluated within a three-stage RL training pipeline of increasing complexity:
- **Stage 0: Cold Start SFT**: Supervised Fine-Tuning using Video-R1-CoT and TPO datasets.
- **Stage 1: RL Visual Reasoning**: Uses Video-R1 dataset with $0.5 \times R_{format} + 0.5 \times R_{acc}$ rewards.
- **Stage 2: RL Audio-Visual Reasoning**: Uses AVQA and AVE datasets with $0.2 \times R_{format} + 0.4 \times R_{acc} + 0.4 \times R_{self}$ rewards.
- **Stage 3: RL Audio-Object Localization**: Uses AVSBench dataset with $0.2 \times R_{format} + 0.4 \times R_{acc} + 0.4 \times R_{judge}$ rewards.

#### 3.5. Implementation Details

The framework is implemented using the Qwen2.5-Omni-7B and Ola-7B MLLMs, with LoRA adapters attached to the Vision Encoder, Audio Encoder, MLP, and LLM. Key hyperparameters include $\lambda_{TAS}=0.3$, $\alpha=0.6$, and $\beta=0.1$.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of AVATAR involved comparisons against state-of-the-art multimodal understanding models and standard GRPO, across various audio-visual and video-only benchmarks. Ablation studies were conducted to assess the contribution of individual components.

#### 4.1. Performance on Audio-Visual and Video Reasoning Benchmarks

- **Audio-Visual Reasoning (Table 2)**: AVATAR consistently outperformed both the baseline models (Qwen2.5-Omni-7B and Ola-7B) and standard GRPO. For Qwen2.5-Omni, AVATAR achieved improvements of +4.9 on OmniBench (compared to GRPO's +1.2), +3.0 on DailyOmni (vs. +0.8), and +2.3 on AV-Odyssey (vs. +1.5). These gains were approximately four times those of GRPO on OmniBench. Similar trends were observed with Ola-7B, where AVATAR generally doubled GRPO's improvements (e.g., +3.4 vs. +1.8 on DailyOmni, +3.2 vs. +1.4 on AV-Odyssey). These results suggest that the stratified replay buffer and TAS effectively address vanishing advantages and cross-modal attention drift.
- **General Video Understanding and Reasoning (Table 3)**: AVATAR demonstrated improved stability and performance on reasoning-intensive benchmarks. On Video-Holmes, AVATAR achieved a +4.5 gain, nearly doubling GRPO's +2.6. For temporal ordering on TOMATO, where standard GRPO showed no statistically significant improvements, AVATAR achieved a +1.8 gain. This indicates the role of TAS in focusing optimization on critical planning and synthesis phases, especially for long-horizon reasoning.

#### 4.2. Resolution of Vanishing Advantages and Sample Efficiency

- **Vanishing Advantages (Figure 4)**: Analysis of advantage distributions showed that GRPO's advantages concentrated around zero, indicating a significant vanishing advantage problem. In contrast, AVATAR's advantage distribution exhibited a bimodal shape, reflecting maintained reward diversity through its stratified replay buffer. This indicates that AVATAR effectively reconstructs group-wise reward variance, enabling consistent learning signals.
- **Sample Efficiency (Figure 5)**: AVATAR achieved 0.80 accuracy within 400 iterations (1,600 unique completions), demonstrating a 5x greater sample efficiency compared to GRPO, which failed to reach this performance even after 1,000 iterations (8,000 unique completions). GRPO exhibited oscillatory and unstable accuracy with frequent reward collapses, while AVATAR maintained a smoother, more consistent learning trajectory, never dropping below 0.3 accuracy.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

- **Component-wise Ablation (Table 4)**:
 - The stratified replay buffer was a primary contributor for audio-visual tasks, surpassing GRPO by +2.4 on OmniBench, suggesting its effectiveness in addressing sparse reward signals in cross-modal alignment.
 - TAS was particularly effective for long-horizon video reasoning tasks, outperforming the buffer on VSI-Bench (+1.1 vs. +0.7 over baseline) and Video-TT (+2.0 vs. +1.5), indicating the benefit of position-dependent weighting for planning and synthesis.
 - Combining both components consistently yielded the strongest performance (e.g., +2.6 on Video-MMMU), highlighting their complementary roles.
- **Training Curriculum and Advantage Shaping (Table 5)**: The staged training curriculum (SFT + Stage 1 RL + Stage 2 RL + Stage 3 RL) demonstrated consistent, progressive improvements in reasoning ability. TAS (parabolic weighting) significantly outperformed alternative shaping strategies (linear decay, linear incline, uniform, inverse parabolic weights), validating that emphasizing early planning and late synthesis phases is most effective.
- **Impact of TAS Across Reasoning Lengths (Figure 6)**: TAS improvements amplified with longer reasoning sequences. Gains were more pronounced for sequences exceeding 400 tokens, particularly on benchmarks like MMVU and Video-Holmes. This suggests TAS is effective in preserving credit assignment and stabilizing optimization in long-horizon reasoning where uniform credit assignment often dilutes reward signals.

#### 4.4. Additional Findings (from Supplementary Material)

- **Empirical Focus of TAS (Figure 7)**: Visual analysis confirmed that TAS successfully induced a parabolic-shaped loss profile, empirically focusing the learning signal on critical planning and synthesis phases, unlike GRPO's flat loss distribution.
- **Task-specific Component Impact (Table 6)**: The off-policy architecture primarily drove gains in hallucination tasks (WorldSense), while TAS was more impactful on temporal logic tasks (Temporal Prediction, Audio Change), requiring precise initial grounding and final synthesis.
- **Optimal On-policy/Off-policy Ratio (Figure 8)**: Optimal performance was achieved with a balanced 4-4 split (4 on-policy, 4 off-policy samples) within a group of eight, demonstrating that balanced mixing prevents policy drift from excessive off-policy data and sample inefficiency from pure on-policy training.
- **Effect of SFT (Table 7)**: Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) before RL training was crucial for establishing cross-modal grounding, improving convergence, and enhancing generalization. Removing SFT led to significant performance degradation for both GRPO and AVATAR.
- **Generalization Beyond Video (Table 8)**: AVATAR showed strong generalization capabilities to multimodal math reasoning benchmarks, outperforming other reasoning models and achieving significant gains. In this domain, hinting and TAS emerged as primary contributors.
- **Hinting Mechanism Efficacy (Figure 9)**: The hinting mechanism effectively unlocked the "hard tail" of the problem distribution, increasing solvability from ~68% (with replay buffer only) to over 80%, converting previously unsolvable problems into informative learning signals.
- **Scalability to Smaller Models (Tables 11, 12)**: Experiments with a 3B parameter Qwen2.5-Omni model confirmed that AVATAR consistently outperformed both the baseline 3B model and GRPO 3B, demonstrating broad applicability across different model scales.

In summary, the results indicate that AVATAR effectively addresses GRPO's fundamental limitations, leading to superior performance, increased data efficiency, and enhanced learning stability across diverse multimodal video reasoning tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of AVATAR carries several significant implications for the field of multimodal reasoning and reinforcement learning, with potential impacts on the development and application of advanced AI systems.

Firstly, AVATAR directly addresses three critical limitations of Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)—data inefficiency, the vanishing advantage problem, and uniform credit assignment—which are particularly salient in complex, resource-intensive domains like long-horizon video reasoning. By offering systematic solutions through its off-policy architecture, stratified replay buffer, hinting mechanism, and Temporal Advantage Shaping (TAS), AVATAR enhances the fundamental robustness and efficacy of RL-based MLLM training. This contribution is important for overcoming training instabilities and poor generalization often observed in current RL approaches for multimodal tasks.

Secondly, the observed performance gains across a wide range of audio-visual and video reasoning benchmarks demonstrate AVATAR's ability to significantly improve model accuracy and reasoning quality. For instance, the substantial improvements on benchmarks like OmniBench, Video-Holmes, and MMVU suggest that AVATAR enables MLLMs to perform more accurate cross-modal integration, detect subtle temporal cues, and engage in more structured, multi-step reasoning. The qualitative analysis further supports this, showing AVATAR's capacity for deeper contextual understanding and more precise synthesis of multimodal information compared to baseline GRPO.

Thirdly, the framework's superior sample efficiency, demonstrated by requiring 80% fewer generated completions to reach target performance, has substantial practical implications. Training MLLMs, especially on video data, is computationally expensive and time-consuming. Reducing the data requirement by a factor of five can lead to significant savings in computational resources and time, making the development and iteration of advanced multimodal models more accessible and sustainable.

Fourthly, AVATAR's architectural components, such as the stratified replay buffer that dynamically manages sample difficulty and the TAS that adaptively shapes learning signals, are designed with a degree of task-agnosticism. The empirical evidence of AVATAR generalizing effectively to multimodal math reasoning, where different components (hinting and TAS) become primary drivers of performance, suggests a broader applicability beyond video. This indicates that the principles of managing diverse experiences and targeting credit assignment are transferable, potentially impacting other complex reasoning domains that benefit from structured learning from sequential data.

Finally, the explicit focus on addressing phenomena like the attention sink effect and differentiating between planning and synthesis phases in reasoning chains provides insights into how MLLMs process and learn from sequential information. This fine-grained control over the learning signal could inspire future research into more neurally plausible or architecturally aligned RL training strategies for large neural networks.

Looking forward, the research suggests extending AVATAR to streaming audio-visual reasoning, indicating a potential impact on real-time applications such as autonomous systems, surveillance, and interactive AI agents that require continuous, dynamic multimodal understanding. Overall, AVATAR presents a notable step towards developing more intelligent, efficient, and robust MLLMs capable of complex reasoning over diverse real-world multimodal data.
