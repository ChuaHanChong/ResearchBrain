# AVATAR: Reinforcement Learning to See, Hear, and Reason Over Video

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Arizona State University developed AVATAR, an off-policy reinforcement learning framework that enhances Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) for multimodal reasoning over video. The framework achieves superior performance across diverse audio-visual benchmarks while demonstrating a 5x improvement in sample efficiency compared to baseline GRPO.

## Problem

- Current multimodal large language models (MLLMs) struggle with precise spatiotemporal fusion and alignment across video, audio, and language for complex, long-horizon reasoning tasks.
- Existing Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) methods are data-inefficient due to their on-policy nature, particularly problematic for costly video data, and suffer from a 'vanishing advantage problem' where uniform rewards stall learning.
- Standard GRPO employs uniform credit assignment, failing to emphasize critical reasoning steps (e.g., planning, grounding, synthesis) crucial for complex multimodal responses.

## Method

- AVATAR implements an ==off-policy training architecture== with a ==stratified replay buffer==, dynamically categorizing experiences into Easy, Medium, and Hard tiers based on reward, along with a 'hinting mechanism' for persistently challenging prompts.
- It introduces ==Temporal Advantage Shaping (TAS)==, a critic-free modification to GRPO that uses a U-shaped parabolic weighting function to assign higher learning signals to tokens at the beginning (planning) and end (synthesis) of reasoning sequences.
- The framework employs a combination of reward functions (format, final answer accuracy, self-rewarding via majority vote, and a stepwise reasoning judge) and uses a ==Video-Context Reference Score (VCRS)== as a stable baseline for off-policy advantage calculation.

## Results

- AVATAR improved performance by **+4.9** on OmniBench, **+3.0** on DailyOmni, **+2.3** on AV-Odyssey (Qwen2.5-Omni baseline), and **+5.3** on MVBench (Ola-7B baseline) across various audio-visual reasoning benchmarks.
- The framework achieved a **5x** improvement in sample efficiency, reaching **0.80** accuracy within **400** training iterations, requiring **80%** fewer generated completions compared to baseline GRPO.
- Ablation studies confirmed that both the stratified replay buffer and Temporal Advantage Shaping (TAS) each contributed substantial performance gains, validating their individual effectiveness in addressing data inefficiency and credit assignment challenges.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Off-policy learning, augmented by a stratified replay buffer and curriculum-based difficulty, significantly enhances sample efficiency and stabilizes the learning process in reinforcement learning for multimodal MLLMs.
> - Nuanced credit assignment, specifically by amplifying learning signals for initial planning and final synthesis steps, is crucial for effectively training MLLMs on long-horizon and complex multimodal reasoning tasks.
> - Stabilizing the advantage signal through a moving average reference (VCRS) is essential to overcome the vanishing advantage problem, ensuring continuous learning even when group rewards are uniform.
