# Evolutionary Machine Learning meets Self-Supervised Learning: a comprehensive survey

> [!summary] Summary
> This survey paper systematically reviews the growing body of literature combining Evolutionary Machine Learning (EML) and Self-Supervised Learning (SSL), formally proposing 'Evolutionary Self-Supervised Learning (E-SSL)' as a distinct sub-area. It introduces a novel taxonomy for E-SSL based on 72 identified works, highlighting the prevalence of autoencoder models in applications where EC enhances SSL and vice-versa.

## Problem

- A growing number of studies combine Evolutionary Machine Learning (EML) and Self-Supervised Learning (SSL), but a comprehensive review and systematization of this intersection was absent.
- The complementary strengths of EML (automating design, robustness) and SSL (learning from unlabeled data) were not fully explored or formalized in a unified framework.
- The lack of a structured taxonomy made it difficult to understand the diverse ways these two fields interact and to guide future research.

## Method

- Conducted a systematic literature review across major academic databases, using combined keywords for Evolutionary Computation and Self-Supervised Learning to identify relevant papers.
- Formalized ==Evolutionary Self-Supervised Learning (E-SSL)== as a new research area to provide a cohesive identity to the combined efforts of EML and SSL.
- Developed a novel, structured ==taxonomy for E-SSL==, categorizing existing works based on how evolutionary algorithms and self-supervised learning interact and the specific components they target.

## Results

- Identified **72** relevant papers published since 2014, demonstrating the increasing interest in combining EML and SSL.
- Proposed a novel taxonomy for Evolutionary Self-Supervised Learning (E-SSL), categorizing works into two main branches: "EC for SSL" (e.g., optimizing pretext tasks, topology, learning) and "SSL for EC" (e.g., enhancing evolutionary representation, operators, fitness evaluation).
- Revealed the predominance of autoencoder models in both EC for SSL (e.g., evolving AE/VAE topologies) and SSL for EC (e.g., using DAEs for evolutionary representation or mutation operators) applications.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Evolutionary Self-Supervised Learning (E-SSL) emerges as a distinct and rapidly growing interdisciplinary field, providing a structured approach to combining two powerful AI paradigms.
> - The combination offers mutual benefits: EML can automate and enhance SSL processes, while SSL can provide robust representations to improve evolutionary algorithms.
> - A key challenge lies in automating pretext task design and developing standardized, efficient fitness metrics that balance speed and fidelity in E-SSL evaluations.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work operates at the intersection of two prominent fields within Artificial Intelligence: Evolutionary Machine Learning (EML) and Self-Supervised Learning (SSL). EML involves the application of evolutionary computation techniques to design, optimize, or enhance machine learning algorithms. It is recognized for its ability to automate the design of machine learning models, potentially leading to robust and unexpected solutions without relying on human-designed heuristics. SSL, on the other hand, addresses the challenge of limited labelled data by enabling models to learn useful representations from unlabelled data through automatically generated "pseudo-labels" or pretext tasks. This approach has gained significant traction, especially with advancements in deep learning models across various data modalities.

The authors identify a gap in the existing literature: while extensive surveys exist for EML and SSL individually, and some cover related intersections (e.g., EML with Supervised Learning, or Evolutionary Computation in Generative Adversarial Networks), none provide a comprehensive overview specifically detailing how EML and SSL can be combined. This gap is particularly salient given the increasing research interest in leveraging the strengths of both paradigms. The combination holds the promise of improving the robustness of SSL-learned representations and automating the evolutionary processes for designing deep neural networks, especially in scenarios where labelled data is scarce.

This survey aims to formalize this emerging research area, which the authors term "Evolutionary Self-Supervised Learning (E-SSL)," by consolidating existing knowledge, proposing a definition, and establishing a taxonomy. By doing so, it positions itself as a foundational resource for researchers seeking to understand the current state and future directions of E-SSL.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this survey stems from the observed increase in research combining Evolutionary Machine Learning (EML) and Self-Supervised Learning (SSL). Both fields offer distinct advantages: EML can automate algorithm design and produce robust, often novel, machine learning solutions, while SSL excels at learning valuable feature representations from large volumes of unlabelled data, addressing the bottleneck of costly and time-consuming data labelling. The synergistic potential of these two fields—where EML could refine SSL processes and automate the design of deep neural networks, simultaneously reducing reliance on labelled data—motivates a consolidated review.

The authors identified a notable absence of comprehensive surveys that specifically address the combined application of EML and SSL. Existing EML surveys touch upon the use of unlabelled data but do not detail representation learning efforts, while SSL surveys lack depth regarding the automation of pretext task design or architecture search using evolutionary methods. This informational void prompted the current work.

Based on this motivation, the key objectives of the survey are explicitly stated:
1. **Consolidate Current Knowledge:** To gather and synthesize existing research that integrates EML and SSL, providing a structured overview of the diverse approaches taken to combine these two fields.
2. **Formalize Evolutionary Self-Supervised Learning (E-SSL):** To propose a formal definition for this emerging research area, establishing a clear scope and nomenclature for future studies.
3. **Introduce a Taxonomy:** To develop a systematic classification framework for E-SSL, derived from the analyzed studies, which categorizes works based on how EML and SSL interact and which components they target.
4. **Identify Challenges and Future Research Directions:** To discuss the current limitations and open problems within E-SSL and suggest promising avenues for future research, aiming to guide the field's growth and maturity.

These objectives collectively aim to provide a foundational document that structures the nascent E-SSL field, making it more accessible for researchers and fostering further innovation at this interdisciplinary frontier.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

This paper presents a comprehensive literature review and survey rather than original experimental research. The authors systematically identified and analyzed studies at the intersection of Evolutionary Machine Learning (EML) and Self-Supervised Learning (SSL).

**Literature Search Strategy:**
The identification of relevant papers involved a keyword-based search across several scientific databases. The search strategy combined terms related to Evolutionary Computation (EC) with terms related to SSL using the "AND" operator.
- **EC-related terms:** "evolutionary," "evolutionary neural architecture search," "genetic programming," "evolutionary strategies," "cma-es."
- **SSL-related terms:** "self-supervised," "pretext task," "autoencoder," "contrastive," "siamese networks," "unsupervised pretraining."
The searches were conducted on Google Scholar, IEEExplore, ACM Digital Library, and SpringerLink. This systematic approach yielded a total of 72 publications that fell within the defined scope of the work. The authors explicitly excluded studies focusing on Evolutionary Computation in Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) as a dedicated survey already exists for that specific intersection.

**Defining Evolutionary Self-Supervised Learning (E-SSL):**
A core methodological step was the formalization of "Evolutionary Self-Supervised Learning (E-SSL)" as the encompassing research area for works combining EML and SSL. This definition is presented as a subset of EML, specifically concerning the application of EC to ML models that learn through SSL.

**Proposed Taxonomy:**
The authors developed a two-tiered taxonomy to classify the identified E-SSL works, reflecting the primary direction of interaction between EC and SSL:
1. **EC for SSL:** This category encompasses studies where Evolutionary Computation algorithms are applied to enhance or optimize various aspects of Self-Supervised Learning. This category is further subdivided based on:
 - **SSL Stage Targeted:** Whether EC is applied during the pretext task or the downstream task.
 - **Component Optimized:** Within the pretext task stage, EC can target the dataset (e.g., pseudo-labels, augmentation policies), the model topology (e.g., network architecture, layer configurations), or the learning process (e.g., weights, loss function hyperparameters). Some works address multiple components simultaneously.
 - **Characterization:** For each paper in this category, a detailed table (Table 1) characterizes the work by "What is evolved," "SSL goal type," "SSL customized by EC," "How," "When is EC applied," "Fitness metric," and whether a "Label scarcity study" was conducted.

2. **SSL for EC:** This category includes studies where Self-Supervised Learning models or principles are utilized to improve components or processes within Evolutionary Computation algorithms. This category is further subdivided based on the specific EC component targeted:
 - **Evolutionary Representation:** Using SSL features to encode problem spaces or learn genotype-phenotype mappings.
 - **Variation Operators:** Employing SSL models to design or inform crossover and mutation operators.
 - **Fitness Evaluation:** Utilizing SSL models to calculate fitness or as surrogate models to accelerate evaluation.
 - **Characterization:** For each paper in this category, a table (Table 2) describes "What is evolved," if an "Off-the-shelf model" is used, "SSL goal type," "What is used for evolution," and the "EC component targeted."

Beyond classification, the methodology involved analyzing trends in the number of publications over time (Figure 1) to support the claim of growing interest. The relationships between E-SSL and other fields like ML, EC, and EML are conceptually illustrated (Figure 3). Finally, the comprehensive review of individual studies within the taxonomy leads to the identification of overarching challenges and the formulation of future research directions, structured into specific areas such as pretext task design automation, experiment design under label scarcity, fitness metric exploration, fitness evaluation speed-up, and exploration of other meta-architectures.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The survey identified 72 relevant publications, with a consistent increase in the number of papers since 2014, indicating growing research interest in the field of Evolutionary Self-Supervised Learning (E-SSL). The findings are structured according to the proposed taxonomy, highlighting the various ways EML and SSL interact.

**Overall Trends:**
- A majority of the surveyed works (approximately 80%) focus on applying Evolutionary Computation (EC) for Self-Supervised Learning (SSL), suggesting a greater emphasis on using EC to enhance SSL processes rather than the reverse.
- Autoencoders (AEs), particularly those relying on input reconstruction objectives, are the most frequently adopted meta-architecture across both categories.
- Despite the motivation for SSL being label scarcity, a limited number of studies include comprehensive analyses of performance under varying degrees of labelled data availability.

**EC for SSL (EC applied to enhance SSL):**
This category focuses on optimizing different components of the SSL pipeline using EC algorithms.

- **Pretext task – Dataset:** A limited number of studies optimize elements related to the training data. Approaches include evolving pseudo-labels for representation learning (e.g., GenNAS by Li et al.) or optimizing data augmentation policies (e.g., Barrett et al.) to improve downstream task performance.
- **Pretext task – Topology:** This is a significant area of research, with numerous studies evolving the architecture of neural networks, predominantly autoencoders.
 - **Autoencoder Evolution:** Works have explored evolving dense AEs (e.g., Tabak et al., Assunção et al., Hajewski and Oliveira), convolutional AEs (e.g., Suganuma et al., Han et al., Sun et al., Kanwal et al., Dimanov et al.), and variational AEs (e.g., Hajewski and Oliveira, Chen et al. (EvoVAE), Shang et al. (AOC-VAE)). EC algorithms like Genetic Algorithms (GA), Evolution Strategies (ES), and Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO) are commonly employed. These efforts often focus on minimizing reconstruction loss or a composite objective that includes downstream performance metrics.
 - **Other Topologies:** Beyond AEs, EC has been used to evolve deep neural network topologies for tasks like random label prediction (Zhang et al.), contrastive SSL (Xue et al., MaskTAS for Vision Transformers), and rotation prediction (Garcia et al. (CGP-NASV2)). Genetic Programming has also been applied to evolve tree-based autoencoding structures (Rodriguez-Coayahuitl et al.).
- **Pretext task – Learning:** This sub-area involves evolving aspects of the learning process itself.
 - **Weight Evolution:** Early approaches directly evolved AE weights (David and Greental, Cai et al.), reducing the need for differentiable architectures.
 - **Hyperparameter Evolution:** More commonly, EC optimizes hyperparameters of loss functions, such as sparsity penalty coefficients (Cheng et al.), KL divergence coefficients in VAEs (Wu et al. (eVAE), Emm and Zhang (SA-eInfoVAE)), or combinations of sub-losses in contrastive learning (Sors et al., Jin et al. (AutoSSL), Piergiovanni et al. (ELo), Liu et al.).
 - **Quantization:** EC has been used for post-training quantization, evolving scale parameters and bit widths for model compression while maintaining performance (Frumkin et al. (EvolQ), Ramachandran et al. (CLAMP-ViT, LPQ)).
 - **Pretext Task Discovery:** Some works use EC to find optimal combinations of existing pretext tasks (e.g., AutoSSL for graph NNs, ELo for video representation learning) rather than designing new ones.
- **Pretext task – Multiple components:** Several studies evolve combinations of dataset, topology, and learning components simultaneously (e.g., Lander and Shang (EvoAE), Charte et al., Li et al., Preen et al., Silhan et al., Sun et al. (EuDNN), Vinhas et al. (EvoDeNSS)). This offers a more holistic optimization but increases the search space complexity.
- **Downstream task:** EC is applied to models already pre-trained with SSL. Examples include optimizing parameters of audio effects chains (Steinmetz et al.), generating alternative text inputs for text matching (Zhou et al.), fine-tuning pretrained language models by evolving learning parameters or prompts (Bu et al. (AutoFT), Sun et al.), pruning self-supervised models (Ludwig and Claes), or evolving classifier layers on top of learned representations (Hu et al., Ha and Gao). Rodrigues et al. even explored GP for tabular data downstream tasks.

**SSL for EC (SSL applied to enhance EC algorithms):**
This category explores how SSL principles can improve the internal mechanisms of evolutionary algorithms.

- **Evolutionary Representation:** SSL-learned representations are used to encode individuals or problem spaces, often through autoencoders. This reduces dimensionality, makes the search space more navigable, and can automate genotype-phenotype mapping. Examples include using denoising AEs for large-scale multi-objective optimization (Tian et al., Cui et al., Hu et al.) and variational AEs to encode neural network architectures in evolutionary Neural Architecture Search (NAS) (Yuan et al. (EAEPSO), Gong et al., Xiao et al. (HEP-ENAS), Li et al. (ENAO)).
- **Variation Operators:** SSL models are leveraged to inform or replace traditional genetic operators. Denoising AEs have been used as generative models to sample new individuals for mutation or crossover (Wittenberg et al. (DAE-GP), Probst, Thakkar et al.). Shem-Tov et al. proposed a mutation operator inspired by masked language modeling.
- **Fitness Evaluation:** SSL models assist in fitness assignment, either by directly providing fitness values or acting as surrogate models.
 - **Off-the-shelf Models:** Pre-trained SSL models like CLIP (Contrastive Language-Image Pre-training) are used to compute fitness based on semantic similarity between evolved artifacts and textual prompts (Machado et al., Sacadura et al., Freiberger et al., Yu et al.).
 - **Surrogate Models:** SSL can train predictors that estimate the performance of candidate solutions, thereby speeding up fitness evaluation in NAS. Wei et al. proposed self-supervised neural predictors (regression-based and contrastive-based) to guide evolutionary NAS, while Dang et al. used AEs to reduce the dimensionality of architecture representations fed to surrogate models.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This survey represents a significant contribution to the evolving landscape of Artificial Intelligence by formally defining and systematically mapping the emerging field of Evolutionary Self-Supervised Learning (E-SSL). Its significance and potential impact can be understood across several dimensions:

**Formalization and Structuring of a Nascent Field:**
By proposing a definition and a comprehensive taxonomy for E-SSL, the survey provides a structured framework for an area that has been growing organically. This formalization offers a common vocabulary and conceptual organization, which is crucial for interdisciplinary research. It allows researchers from both Evolutionary Machine Learning (EML) and Self-Supervised Learning (SSL) to better understand the contributions and challenges at their intersection, facilitating more focused and cumulative research efforts.

**Highlighting Synergistic Potential:**
The detailed review underscores the inherent benefits of combining EML and SSL. EML's capacity for automated design and optimization, coupled with SSL's ability to learn from unlabelled data, presents a powerful paradigm for developing more robust, adaptable, and data-efficient machine learning models. This synergy is particularly impactful in domains where labelled data acquisition is expensive, time-consuming, or scarce.

**Identification of Research Gaps and Opportunities:**
The survey's thorough analysis reveals several key challenges and underexplored areas. For instance, the prevalence of autoencoder models with reconstruction objectives suggests an opportunity to explore other SSL meta-architectures and objective functions within an evolutionary context. The identified lack of standardized experimental designs for label scarcity scenarios highlights a critical need for rigorous evaluation methodologies to truly demonstrate the label-efficiency advantages of E-SSL. Furthermore, the discussion on the trade-off between fitness evaluation speed and fidelity, and the relatively fewer works applying SSL to enhance EC components (beyond representation learning), points to specific avenues for future innovation.

**Guiding Future Research:**
By articulating these challenges and suggesting concrete future research directions—such as automating pretext task design, developing high-fidelity yet computationally efficient fitness metrics, and exploring diverse meta-architectures for both EC for SSL and SSL for EC—the survey serves as a roadmap for researchers. This guidance can accelerate the development of novel algorithms and applications, preventing redundant efforts and channeling resources towards impactful problems.

**Catalyst for Interdisciplinary Collaboration:**
The survey bridges two distinct yet complementary fields, encouraging collaboration between researchers in evolutionary computation and self-supervised learning. This interdisciplinary dialogue can foster a deeper understanding of complex adaptive systems and intelligent agents, leading to breakthroughs that might not emerge from isolated disciplinary efforts.

**Practical Implications:**
The long-term impact of E-SSL research, as outlined by this survey, includes the potential to:
- **Automate AI Development:** Reduce human effort in designing and tuning machine learning models and their components.
- **Enhance Model Robustness:** Evolve models that are more resilient to noise and variations in data.
- **Improve Data Efficiency:** Develop powerful models with significantly less reliance on costly labelled datasets, democratizing access to advanced AI for data-poor domains.
- **Discover Novel Solutions:** Leverage the stochastic and exploratory nature of evolutionary algorithms to uncover unexpected and superior machine learning architectures or learning strategies.

In conclusion, this survey is significant for consolidating, categorizing, and critically assessing the nascent field of E-SSL. It not only maps the current state of research but also provides a clear vision for its future, positioning itself as a foundational reference for advancing both theoretical understanding and practical applications at the frontier of evolutionary computation and self-supervised learning.
