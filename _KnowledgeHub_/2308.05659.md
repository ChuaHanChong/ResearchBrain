# AD-CLIP: Adapting Domains in Prompt Space Using CLIP

> [!summary] Summary
> AD-CLIP proposes a data-driven prompt learning framework within CLIP's prompt space, addressing unsupervised domain adaptation by learning domain-invariant and class-generalizable prompts. The method establishes new performance benchmarks on Office-Home, VisDA-2017, and Mini-DomainNet, with average accuracy improvements up to 2.2% over previous state-of-the-art.

## Problem

- Deep learning models struggle with the "domain-shift problem" where training and test data distributions diverge, limiting generalization in real-world applications.
- Traditional Unsupervised Domain Adaptation (UDA) methods, often relying on CNNs, lack "semantic richness" to capture deeper conceptual relationships for robust generalization across visually diverse domains.
- Prior attempts to apply CLIP to UDA used ad-hoc prompting or neglected intrinsic visual distributions, leading to suboptimal performance or overfitting.

## Method

- Utilizes ==frozen CLIP vision and text backbones==, integrating only a minimal set of learnable projector networks for style and content features.
- Develops a novel ==prompt learning scheme== that dynamically combines a ==Domain-Agnostic Token (DAT)== and image-specific context tokens derived from multi-scale visual features.
- Introduces a ==Target-to-Source Style Mapping Network== to approximate source domain style characteristics from target data, enabling robust inference without explicit source access.

## Results

- Achieved state-of-the-art average accuracy across UDA benchmarks: **90.5%** on Office-Home, **92.7%** on VisDA-2017, and **91.4%** on Mini-DomainNet (with ViT-L/14 backbone).
- Outperformed previous state-of-the-art methods by an average of **1.8%** on Office-Home, **2.2%** on VisDA-2017, and **1.6%** on Mini-DomainNet.
- t-SNE visualizations confirm that AD-CLIP successfully achieves both domain invariance and class discriminativeness in the learned text embeddings, validating its core objective.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Leveraging multi-scale visual features for content extraction consistently improves performance, indicating the importance of nuanced visual information for prompts.
> - The Domain-Agnostic Token is critical for learning domain-invariant representations, while the Target-to-Source Style Mapping Network prevents overfitting and maintains performance during target-only inference.
> - Both Entropy Minimization and KL Divergence Loss terms are indispensable for promoting discriminative alignment within the target domain and ensuring distributional similarity between source and target prompt embeddings.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The remarkable advancements in deep learning have primarily been demonstrated in supervised learning environments where training and test data adhere to the same statistical distribution. However, a pervasive challenge known as the "domain-shift problem" frequently arises in practical applications when these distributions diverge, severely compromising the generalization ability of deep learning models.

**Unsupervised Domain Adaptation (UDA)** is a crucial subfield of transfer learning designed to mitigate this issue. UDA methods aim to learn a domain-agnostic representation space by utilizing both labeled source data and unlabeled target data, enabling models trained on one domain to perform effectively on another. Traditional UDA techniques span various methodologies, including adversarial learning (e.g., DANN, CyCADA), entropy minimization, statistical distance minimization (e.g., MMD), and pseudo-labeling. While these methods have achieved notable successes, they predominantly rely on convolutional neural network (CNN) backbones that are trained exclusively on visual data. A critical limitation of these CNN-based approaches is their inherent lack of "semantic richness," meaning the extracted features may struggle to capture deeper, conceptual relationships essential for robust generalization across domains with significant visual discrepancies. This often results in suboptimal performance in complex or visually diverse scenarios.

In parallel, the landscape of artificial intelligence has been revolutionized by the emergence of **large-scale Vision-Language Models (VLMs)**, such as CLIP and VisualBERT. These models, pre-trained on vast collections of image-text pairs, learn a joint embedding space imbued with rich semantics. This semantic richness grants VLMs exceptional generalization and zero-shot classification capabilities. Consequently, there is an increasing interest in adapting these powerful foundation models for various downstream tasks, often through **prompt learning**. Prompt learning involves designing or learning textual prompts to guide the VLM towards specific task objectives. Existing prompt learning techniques, like CoOp and CoCoOp, primarily focus on improving generalization or fine-tuning models for specific tasks by learning context tokens or conditional prompts. Other notable works, such as AutoPrompt and MaPLe, explore automated prompt generation and multi-modal prompt learning, respectively.

However, despite the immense potential of VLMs, particularly CLIP, their application alongside sophisticated prompt learning strategies to the specific, challenging problem of **Unsupervised Domain Adaptation** has remained largely underexplored. Initial efforts in this burgeoning area, such as DAPL, represent early attempts to apply CLIP to UDA. Yet, these methods often suffer from limitations, including reliance on ad-hoc or manually specified domain information (which is often impractical in real-world scenarios) and a neglect of the intrinsic visual distributions of classes, potentially leading to overfitting.

AD-CLIP addresses this significant gap by proposing a novel, data-driven prompt learning strategy specifically tailored for UDA within the CLIP framework. It aims to overcome the semantic limitations of traditional CNN-based DA methods and the deficiencies of earlier CLIP-based DA approaches. By actively conditioning prompt learning on both image style and content information, AD-CLIP positions itself as a critical advancement in making VLMs more effective and reliable tools for tackling the domain shift problem, thereby expanding their utility beyond conventional zero-shot and few-shot learning paradigms. This work contributes fundamentally to the broader goal of developing more generalizable and adaptable AI systems by seamlessly integrating powerful foundation models with the complex realities of real-world data variability.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The foundational motivation underpinning the AD-CLIP framework arises from the inherent fragility of deep learning models when confronted with domain shifts—scenarios where the statistical characteristics of training and testing data diverge. While Unsupervised Domain Adaptation (UDA) offers a critical mechanism to bridge this gap, existing UDA techniques, predominantly built upon traditional convolutional neural networks (CNNs), often exhibit limitations due to their restricted "semantic richness." These vision-only backbones, despite their impressive performance in many supervised settings, frequently struggle to extract features that are conceptually robust and readily transferable across domains exhibiting significant visual differences.

The emergence of large-scale Vision-Language Models (VLMs) like CLIP has presented a transformative opportunity. These models, trained on vast quantities of image-text pairs, possess a joint embedding space that is profoundly rich in semantics, endowing them with exceptional generalization and zero-shot classification capabilities. However, the full potential of these powerful foundation models for directly addressing the unsupervised domain adaptation problem, particularly through robust prompt learning, has not been thoroughly explored. Existing attempts, such as DAPL, often employ simplistic or hand-crafted prompting mechanisms, which can be suboptimal or impractical in diverse DA scenarios and may overlook crucial visual distribution information, potentially leading to overfitting.

Against this backdrop, the key objectives and motivations driving the development of AD-CLIP are:

1. **To fully exploit CLIP's semantic richness for UDA:** The primary goal is to harness the deep semantic representations offered by CLIP to formulate more robust and effective domain adaptation solutions, thereby transcending the limitations imposed by vision-only feature extractors.
2. **To introduce a novel, data-driven, and domain-agnostic prompt learning strategy:** The core innovation involves adapting domains by learning prompts directly within CLIP's flexible prompt space. This entails designing a mechanism to learn prompt tokens that are intrinsically unbiased towards any specific domain, thereby ensuring strong domain-invariance.
3. **To develop prompts that are simultaneously domain-invariant and class-generalizable:** The learned prompts must adeptly capture the distinctive visual characteristics of individual classes while maintaining their effectiveness across varying domains. This is achieved by conditioning prompt learning on both image "style" (representing domain-specific attributes) and "content" (representing class-specific visual features).
4. **To provide a robust solution for target-only inference:** A practical challenge in UDA arises when only target domain samples are available during the inference stage, making it difficult to generate domain-specific prompts without access to source knowledge. AD-CLIP aims to overcome this by proposing a novel cross-domain style mapping network capable of hallucinating source domain characteristics from target data.
5. **To achieve effective domain alignment in CLIP's embedding space:** The framework seeks to align the source and target domains not just visually, but also semantically within CLIP's joint embedding space. This is accomplished by enforcing discriminative feature learning within classes and promoting distributional similarity between domain-specific prompt embeddings.
6. **To attain state-of-the-art performance with minimal additional parameters:** The objective is to introduce only a small set of learnable projector networks, keeping the powerful CLIP backbone frozen. This strategy maintains the efficiency and superior generalization benefits of the pre-trained foundation model while facilitating effective domain adaptation.

In essence, AD-CLIP is motivated by the critical need for more semantically rich, adaptable, and practically deployable domain adaptation techniques. It aims to fully capitalize on the transformative advancements in large-scale vision-language foundation models to significantly improve generalization performance in challenging, real-world cross-domain visual recognition tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

AD-CLIP introduces a novel framework for Unsupervised Domain Adaptation (UDA) by reformulating the problem within the adaptable prompt space of pre-trained Vision-Language Models (VLMs), specifically CLIP. The methodology is meticulously designed to learn domain-invariant and class-generalizable prompts by leveraging multi-scale visual features extracted from CLIP's frozen vision encoder.

The core of AD-CLIP's approach is structured around several interconnected components:

#### 3.1. Frozen CLIP Backbones and Learnable Projectors

The framework strategically utilizes the powerful, pre-trained, and frozen vision backbone (`f_v`) and text backbone (`f_t`) of CLIP. To integrate domain- and image-specific information into the prompt space without requiring extensive fine-tuning of the large foundation model, AD-CLIP introduces a minimal set of learnable networks:
- **Style Projector (`P_v`):** This is a compact encoder-decoder network specifically designed to process and project image style information.
- **Content Projector (`C_v`):** This network consists of a dense encoder followed by `L` dense decoder layers. Its role is to extract and project image content features, generating `L` distinct prompt tokens.
- **Target-to-Source Style Mapping Network (`f_smn`):** Another encoder-decoder network, `f_smn` is critical for approximating source style characteristics from target domain samples during the inference stage.

#### 3.2. Proposed Prompt Learning Scheme

AD-CLIP's innovative prompt learning scheme generates flexible prompts by dynamically combining domain-specific and image-specific visual cues:

1. **Domain Token Learning:** To embed crucial domain knowledge and ensure the resulting prompts are domain-agnostic, a special domain token, `[D_s; D_t]`, is learned.
 - **Style Information Extraction:** Multi-scale style information is extracted from `M` encoder layers of `f_v`. This information is characterized by the first-order (mean, `μ`) and second-order (standard deviation, `σ`) batch-wise feature statistics from each layer. These statistics are concatenated to form a comprehensive style vector: `F_bar(x) = [μ_1; σ_1; ...; μ_M; σ_M]`.
 - **Style Projection:** The `P_v` network then maps `F_bar_s` (derived from source images) to `D_s` and `F_bar_t` (from target images) to `D_t`. This dynamic conditioning ensures that prompts are informed by the specific style of each domain.

2. **Image-Specific Token (IST) Learning:** To capture fine-grained visual distributions and enhance class generalizability, `L` image-specific context tokens (`{V_l}^L_{l=1}`) are learned.
 - **Content Information Extraction:** Multi-scale image content features (`F_hat(x) = [f^1_v(x); ...; f^M_v(x)]`) are obtained from various layers of `f_v`, allowing for the capture of visual details at multiple levels of abstraction.
 - **Content Projection:** The `C_v` network processes `F_hat(x)` to produce the `L` image-specific context tokens `V_l`.

3. **Constructing the Final Prompt:** For any given image `x` and its corresponding class `y`, the complete prompt is dynamically assembled as:
 `Prompt_y(x) = [[D_t; D_s]; V_1; ...; V_L; [CLS_y]]`. This structure intelligently integrates domain context, image-specific visual details, and the class label, facilitating a robust and adaptive representation.

#### 3.3. Target-to-Source Style Mapping Network for Inference

A critical practical challenge in UDA is inference, where often only target domain data is accessible, making direct acquisition of source domain style features (`D_s`) impossible. AD-CLIP addresses this by introducing the `f_smn` network.
- **Functionality:** `f_smn` takes the target domain style features (`F_bar_t`) as input and learns to predict or "hallucinate" the corresponding source domain style (`D_s`).
- **Training:** `f_smn` is trained using an `ℓ_2` loss, minimizing the squared difference between the predicted `D_s` and the actual `D_s`: `L_smn = ||D_s - f_smn(F_bar_t)||^2_2`. This mechanism ensures that even without explicit source data during inference, a relevant `D_s` can be approximated, maintaining prompt integrity.

#### 3.4. Loss Functions for Domain Alignment and Classification

AD-CLIP employs a comprehensive loss function meticulously crafted to achieve both effective classification in the source domain and robust domain alignment:

1. **Supervised Contrastive Loss (`L_ce`):** This is a standard cross-entropy loss applied to drive the image-text mapping for labeled source data. It computes the prediction probability `p(y|x)` of an image `x` belonging to class `y` based on the cosine similarity between its visual embedding `f_v(x)` and the text embedding of its dynamically generated prompt `f_t(Prompt_y(x))`. This loss term ensures discriminative feature learning within the source domain.

2. **Alignment Loss (`L_Align`):** This novel objective is pivotal for cross-domain alignment and consists of two synergistic sub-components:
 - **Entropy Minimization (`L_em`):** Applied to the similarity distributions of target samples with all class prompts. By minimizing the entropy of these distributions, this term encourages each target sample to align strongly with a single class prototype (prompt) while being pushed away from others. This effectively promotes discriminative clustering of target features in the semantic space.
 - **KL Divergence Loss (`L_KL`):** Measures and minimizes the distributional divergence between the prompt embeddings generated for the target domain (`Prompt_t`) and those for the source domain (`Prompt_s`). This ensures that the dynamically generated prompts for both domains yield similar semantic embedding distributions, thereby fostering robust cross-domain transferability.

#### 3.5. Total Loss and Inference

The overarching objective function for training AD-CLIP is a weighted sum of these carefully designed losses: `L_total = [L_ce + L_smn + L_Align]`.

During inference, given an unlabeled target image, its visual features `f_v(x)` are compared to the text embeddings of all possible class prompts. These prompts are generated using the `f_smn` network to approximate `D_s` from `F_bar_t`. The class that maximizes the similarity score `p(y|x)` is then selected as the final prediction.

This meticulous methodology, by systematically dissecting and leveraging multi-scale visual features for domain-aware prompt engineering and combining them with robust alignment losses, positions AD-CLIP as a sophisticated and highly effective solution to the unsupervised domain adaptation problem within the powerful context of vision-language models.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The effectiveness of AD-CLIP was thoroughly evaluated through extensive experiments conducted on three widely recognized benchmark datasets for unsupervised domain adaptation (UDA): Office-Home, VisDA-2017, and Mini-DomainNet. Performance was quantified using the target domain top-1 accuracy (mean ± standard deviation) over three runs, and compared against a diverse range of state-of-the-art UDA methods, encompassing traditional CNN-based approaches, Transformer-based methods, and other vision-language foundation models.

#### 4.1. Comparison to State-of-the-Art Methods

AD-CLIP consistently demonstrated superior performance across all evaluated datasets and various CLIP vision backbones, including ResNet-50/101, ViT-B/16, and ViT-L/14.

- **Overall Performance Benchmark:** AD-CLIP successfully established a new performance benchmark for UDA tasks, significantly outperforming prior best methods:
 - **Office-Home:** Achieved an average improvement of 1.8% over the previous state-of-the-art. For instance, utilizing a ViT-L/14 backbone, AD-CLIP attained 90.5% average accuracy, surpassing DAPL (88.7%) and baseline CLIP (87.0%).
 - **VisDA-2017:** Demonstrated a 2.2% increase in average accuracy compared to the best existing methods. With a ViT-L/14 backbone, AD-CLIP achieved an impressive 92.7% average accuracy, notably higher than DAPL (90.5%) and baseline CLIP (89.1%).
 - **Mini-DomainNet:** Showcased a 1.6% improvement in average accuracy. Using a ViT-L/14 backbone, AD-CLIP obtained 91.4% average accuracy, outperforming DAPL (89.8%) and baseline CLIP (88.3%).

- **Comparison with DAPL:** A crucial observation was AD-CLIP's significant outperformance of DAPL, which is the most directly comparable prompt learning method for DA. On VisDA-2017, for example, AD-CLIP surpassed DAPL by 0.8% (with ResNet-101), 1.2% (with ViT-B/16), and 2.2% (with ViT-L/14) on average. This highlights the superior efficacy of AD-CLIP's sophisticated and data-driven prompt learning strategy over more simplistic, hand-crafted approaches.

- **Specific Case (VisDA-2017 with ResNet-101):** While AD-CLIP demonstrated overall superiority, it was noted that with a ResNet-101 backbone on VisDA-2017, traditional Transformer-based methods (SSRT and CDTrans) achieved better results on 7 out of 12 classes (e.e., car, knife, person, plant). Nevertheless, AD-CLIP's *average* performance across all classes remained superior, indicating its robust generalizability even if not strictly leading in every single category for a particular, less powerful backbone.

#### 4.2. Ablation Analysis and Insights

Extensive ablation studies were conducted to provide crucial insights into the architectural design and the individual contributions of AD-CLIP's various components:

- **t-SNE Visualization of Text Embeddings:** Visualizations on the Office-Home dataset vividly demonstrated that AD-CLIP successfully achieves both **domain invariance** (evidenced by overlapping regions between text embeddings from different domains like art and clipart) and **discriminativeness** (indicated by clear separation between different class embeddings). This confirms the framework's core objective of learning generalized and differentiated prompts.

- **Sensitivity to Multi-Scale Features:** The performance of AD-CLIP consistently improved as more layers from the CLIP vision backbone were incorporated for content feature extraction. This finding underscores the benefit of leveraging multi-scale visual information to capture more nuanced and discriminative features, thereby validating the design choice of `C_v` to process features from multiple layers.

- **Sensitivity on Prompt Behaviour:**
 - Omitting the **Domain-Agnostic Token (DAT)** resulted in a noticeable decrease in performance, highlighting its critical role in guiding the model towards learning domain-invariant representations.
 - Utilizing **manual Image-Specific Tokens (IST)** yielded only minor improvements compared to the dynamically learned ones, emphasizing the superior effectiveness of data-driven prompt generation.
 - Employing a **constant Source-Domain Style Token (SST)** (an average style derived from all source domains) led to decreased performance and even overfitting, underscoring the necessity of the `f_smn` network for dynamic and contextual style approximation during inference.
 - The full AD-CLIP model, integrating the `f_smn` mechanism, consistently delivered the best performance, validating its holistic design for robust domain adaptation, especially in scenarios where source data might be unavailable during inference.

- **Sensitivity on Loss Terms:**
 - Removing either the **Entropy Minimization (`L_em`)** term or the **KL Divergence Loss (`L_KL`)** term individually led to a significant drop in performance. This confirmed their indispensable roles in promoting discriminative alignment within the target domain and ensuring distributional similarity between source and target prompt embeddings, respectively.
 - The study also indicated that there was no significant performance difference between using multi-scale features versus only the final layer features when considering the overall impact on the loss functions. This suggests robustness to this specific design choice for style and content features in the context of the combined loss, even though multi-scale *content* features generally improved results as observed in other analyses.

- **Image Token Length:** Experiments identified an optimal length of `L=4` for the image-specific context tokens, indicating that a moderate number of tokens is most effective for capturing relevant visual information without introducing unnecessary complexity.

- **Model Complexity:** AD-CLIP demonstrated a relatively small increase in computational resources (GFLOPs) compared to baseline methods (e.g., only 0.18% more than DAPL). This marginal overhead is well-justified by its substantial performance gains, positioning it as an efficient and effective solution for UDA.

In summary, the main findings decisively affirm that AD-CLIP's novel prompt learning strategy, which adeptly disentangles and leverages multi-scale visual style and content, coupled with robust alignment losses and an innovative inference mechanism, significantly advances the state-of-the-art in unsupervised domain adaptation by effectively harnessing the profound semantic power of CLIP.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The AD-CLIP framework represents a substantial advancement in the field of unsupervised domain adaptation (UDA) by ingeniously integrating the capabilities of large-scale Vision-Language Models (VLMs) with a sophisticated, data-driven prompt learning paradigm. Its contributions extend beyond merely achieving superior performance, offering profound theoretical and practical implications for developing truly robust and adaptable artificial intelligence systems.

#### 5.1. Novelty and Methodological Advancement

The most salient significance of AD-CLIP lies in its pioneering approach to solving the UDA problem by operating directly within the prompt space of CLIP. Unlike conventional methods that often rely on visual backbones with limited semantic depth or ad-hoc prompting strategies, AD-CLIP introduces a principled, data-driven mechanism to learn prompts that are inherently domain-invariant and class-generalizable. By meticulously disentangling and leveraging multi-scale visual style and content features, it dynamically generates prompts that are not only contextually rich but also robust to various domain shifts. The innovation of the target-to-source style mapping network (`f_smn`) to "hallucinate" source domain characteristics during target-only inference is a particularly clever and practical solution to a prevalent challenge in real-world UDA scenarios.

#### 5.2. Enhancing CLIP's Utility for Domain Adaptation

AD-CLIP convincingly demonstrates that VLMs like CLIP possess immense potential not only for zero-shot or few-shot learning but also as powerful foundational models for domain adaptation, provided they are guided by carefully designed prompt learning strategies. This work moves beyond merely using CLIP as a frozen feature extractor by developing an adaptive "prompting" layer that effectively unlocks and channels its capabilities for challenging cross-domain tasks. This opens significant new avenues for research into how pre-trained foundation models can be efficiently adapted with minimal learnable parameters for complex transfer learning scenarios, maximizing their applicability across diverse tasks.

#### 5.3. Improved Robustness and Generalizability

The consistent achievement of state-of-the-art performance across diverse and challenging benchmark datasets (Office-Home, VisDA-2017, Mini-DomainNet) unequivocally underscores AD-CLIP's ability to construct a more discriminative and domain-aligned semantic embedding space. The comprehensive ablation studies further corroborate the critical role played by each component—including the domain-agnostic token, image-specific tokens, multi-scale feature utilization, entropy minimization, and KL divergence loss—in achieving this enhanced robustness. This improved generalization capability directly translates into the potential for deploying AI systems more reliably in real-world environments where variations in data distribution are an unavoidable reality.

#### 5.4. Practical Implications

From a practical perspective, AD-CLIP offers several compelling advantages:
- **Reduced Data Dependency:** By relying on the robust pre-trained CLIP model and integrating only a small set of learnable projector networks, the framework minimizes the need for extensive labeled data in the target domain, which is a core tenet and benefit of UDA.
- **Computational Efficiency:** Despite its methodological sophistication, AD-CLIP exhibits a relatively minor increase in computational overhead (GFLOPs) compared to existing baselines. This efficiency makes it a highly viable option for practical deployment without incurring prohibitive computational costs associated with fine-tuning entire large models.
- **Versatile Application:** The framework's demonstrated ability to effectively handle domain shifts ranging from synthetic-to-real images to natural image variations suggests its broad applicability across a wide spectrum of computer vision tasks.

#### 5.5. Future Research Directions

The authors have explicitly outlined their plans to extend AD-CLIP to address specific, high-impact applications such as person re-identification and medical imaging. This commitment highlights the framework's potential as a foundational method that can be tailored to critical real-world problems where domain shifts are prevalent and robust, reliable performance is paramount. In fields like medical imaging, for instance, models frequently struggle when trained on data from one clinical setting and applied to another due to variations in imaging equipment, protocols, or patient demographics. AD-CLIP's capacity to learn domain-invariant representations could prove transformative in these sensitive and life-critical applications.

In conclusion, AD-CLIP represents a significant leap forward in making deep learning models more adaptable, robust, and reliable. By ingeniously leveraging the profound semantic power of CLIP through a novel and principled prompt learning paradigm, it not only advances the theoretical understanding of domain adaptation but also provides a powerful, practical tool for developing advanced AI solutions capable of thriving in a world characterized by continuously evolving data distributions.
