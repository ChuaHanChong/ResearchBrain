# StructRL: Structured Action-Space Exploration for Flow-Based VLAs

> [!summary] Summary
> StructRL is an action-space RL framework for flow-based VLA policies that keeps the ODE decoder deterministic and injects AR(1)-correlated, action-group-scaled noise after denoising, updated via last-step replay; it beats in-chain exploration baselines on LIBERO, ManiSkill, and two real-robot tasks.

## Problem

- Existing RL methods for flow-based VLAs inject isotropic or temporally independent noise inside the denoising chain, ignoring temporal smoothness and per-group scale needed for safe robot exploration.
- Structured noise injected at intermediate flow timesteps gets diluted by remaining denoising steps, which pull perturbed samples back toward the SFT action manifold before execution.
- Prior likelihood-based RL objectives attach probabilities to intermediate denoising-chain transitions, decoupling the training signal from the actually executed action.

## Method

- **StructRL** runs the pretrained flow decoder as a ==deterministic reverse ODE== to produce a clean action, then adds structured noise directly at the action-space output rather than mid-chain.
- ==Last-step replay== recomputes the terminal Euler jump from a stored late denoising state during policy updates, giving a tractable action-level log-probability without full-chain rollouts.
- A learned ==AR(1)-correlated, group-aware noise distribution== scales position, rotation, and gripper dimensions separately, regularized with entropy, budget, and smoothness penalties.

## Results

- On LIBERO with GR00T N1.5, StructRL reaches **99.0%** average success versus 96.5% for Flow-SDE+PPO and 52.5% for the few-shot SFT baseline.
- On ManiSkill, moving injection from in-chain to action-space endpoint alone lifts OOD average success from 39.3% to **67.3%** on pi_0, isolating injection location as the dominant driver of OOD gains.
- Real-world Franka trials show StructRL reaching **84%** success (21/25) on pick-banana after 60 minutes versus 56% (14/25) for Gaussian action-space exploration, plus faster convergence on plug-charger-in.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Where stochasticity is injected relative to a generative decoder's remaining computation can matter more than the shape of the noise distribution itself.
> - Perturbations added mid-pipeline can be silently absorbed by downstream deterministic processing, a general risk for RL atop any multi-step generative policy.
> - Matching the noise's structure to the action representation's semantics (temporal order, unit heterogeneity) mainly boosts exploration efficiency, not asymptotic performance.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this work fits into the broader research landscape

This work operates within the field of robotic manipulation, specifically focusing on Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for continuous control. VLA models typically integrate pre-trained Vision-Language Model (VLM) backbones with continuous action heads, trained on diverse robot datasets. Recent advancements in VLA policies have seen the adoption of flow matching for generating continuous action chunks, exemplified by models such as $\pi_0$, $\pi_{0.5}$, and GR00T. These models are predominantly trained using large-scale behavior cloning (imitation learning).

While imitation learning provides a foundation, its performance is inherently limited by the quality and coverage of the offline demonstration data. To overcome these limitations and improve policy performance beyond the initial dataset, online reinforcement learning (RL) is increasingly recognized as a crucial technique for adapting pre-trained VLA models to new, potentially out-of-distribution (OOD) tasks.

Existing RL methods for flow-based VLAs, such as $\pi_{RL}$, Flow-CPS, and $\pi$-StepNFT, generally integrate stochasticity into the denoising chain of the flow model. This typically involves injecting isotropic or temporally independent noise. However, the authors argue that effective exploration for continuous robot control requires a more structured form of noise. This structured noise should ideally exhibit temporal smoothness within action chunks and possess different scaling factors across distinct action groups (e.g., position, rotation, gripper actions) to align with the physical constraints and safety requirements of robotic operation.

The paper identifies a gap: current in-chain noise injection methods do not explicitly control temporal correlation or action-group scaling at the action level. Furthermore, it introduces the concept of "Structured Noise Dilution," a phenomenon where noise injected at intermediate flow steps is weakened by subsequent denoising, thereby losing its intended structure before action execution. StructRL positions itself as an alternative approach that addresses these limitations by relocating policy stochasticity directly to the action space and employing a novel training mechanism.

### 2. Key objectives and motivation

The primary objectives and motivations for developing StructRL are:

1. **Address the limitations of imitation learning in VLA models:** Pre-trained flow-based VLA models, while powerful, are bounded by their offline training data. They may struggle with tasks requiring actions outside the distribution of observed demonstrations or when adapting to new environments. Reinforcement learning offers a pathway to improve these policies through interaction with the environment.
2. **Enhance exploration efficiency and effectiveness in continuous robotic control:** For robot manipulation, exploratory actions should be structured to be physically meaningful and safe. This implies:
 - **Temporal smoothness:** Jittery or abrupt actions within a continuous action chunk can destabilize robot execution or lead to unsafe behaviors. Exploration should ideally generate temporally coherent movements.
 - **Action-group-aware scaling:** Different components of a robot action (e.g., end-effector position, rotation, gripper state) have varying ranges, units, and safety sensitivities. Applying uniform noise levels across all action dimensions can be inefficient or detrimental. Exploration noise should be scaled appropriately for each action group.
3. **Overcome "Structured Noise Dilution":** The authors observe that when structured noise is injected during an intermediate step of the flow model's denoising process, the subsequent denoising steps tend to pull the perturbed state back towards the manifold of the Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) action distribution. This process can dilute or overwrite the intended temporal correlation and action-group scaling of the injected noise, preventing it from manifesting effectively in the executed action. The motivation is to design an exploration mechanism that preserves the intended structure of the noise.
4. **Develop a tractable RL framework for flow-based VLAs that supports action-space exploration:** Traditional flow-based RL methods tie policy likelihoods to intermediate denoising states, making it difficult to define and evaluate structured noise directly in the final action space. A key motivation is to devise a training signal that can update the flow decoder while the policy's stochasticity is applied directly to the final action output.

In summary, the motivation stems from the need for more intelligent and effective exploration strategies for adapting flow-based VLA models to complex robotic tasks, particularly in scenarios requiring generalization and efficient online learning, while addressing fundamental issues related to how noise propagates through generative models.

### 3. Methodology and approach

StructRL proposes a novel action-space reinforcement learning framework for flow-based VLA models to facilitate structured exploration. The methodology can be broken down into several key components:

#### 3.1. Identification of Structured Noise Dilution

The authors first empirically demonstrate "Structured Noise Dilution." They use a GR00T N1.5 SFT model and inject structured perturbations (with controlled temporal correlation and per-group scales) at two locations:
- **In-chain:** Before the remaining denoising steps.
- **Action-space:** After denoising completes.
They measure the actual structure of the executed action. Results indicate that in-chain injected noise largely loses its intended structure, with the output correlation and group scales staying close to the SFT model's inherent characteristics. In contrast, action-space injection effectively translates the desired structure to the executed action. This observation motivates the relocation of exploration stochasticity.

#### 3.2. Action-Space RL Framework with Last-Step Replay

To avoid noise dilution, StructRL implements the following framework:
- **Deterministic ODE Decoder:** The pre-trained flow decoder (velocity field $v_\theta$) is run deterministically to predict a "clean" action chunk $x^{\text{clean}}_0 = \mathcal{F}_{\theta}^{T \to 0}(x_1)$, starting from an initial Gaussian noise $x_1 \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I)$.
- **Action-Space Noise Injection:** Exploration noise $\Delta_0$ is added directly to this clean action output: $a = x^{\text{clean}}_0 + \Delta_0$. Since $\Delta_0$ is applied after the entire denoising process, it is not diluted by subsequent flow steps.
- **Last-Step Replay for Policy Likelihood:** To provide a tractable training signal for the flow decoder, a mechanism called "last-step replay" is introduced.
 - During rollout, the executed action $a$ and a "late" denoising state $x_{t_n}$ (typically the state just before the final denoising step, $n=1$) are stored.
 - During policy updates, the deterministic jump from the stored $x_{t_n}$ to the clean action $\hat{x}^{\text{clean}}_0 = \mathcal{F}_{\theta}^{n \to 0}(x_{t_n})$ is replayed.
 - The "replayed" action-space perturbation $\Delta^\theta_0 = a - \hat{x}^{\text{clean}}_0$ is then computed.
 - The policy log-probability $\log \pi_\theta(a | o)$ is evaluated based on the likelihood of this $\Delta^\theta_0$ under the chosen noise distribution. This ensures that the likelihood is tied to the executed action rather than an intermediate denoising state.
- **RL Algorithm Integration:** This action-level log-probability can be integrated into standard likelihood-based RL objectives. For simulation, PPO (Proximal Policy Optimization) is used due to parallel rollout capabilities. For real-world interaction with sequential data collection, AWAC (Advantage-Weighted Actor-Critic) with an expectile value function is adopted.

#### 3.3. Structured Noise Instantiation

The Gaussian baseline noise distribution is replaced with a learnable structured distribution $p_\phi(\Delta_0 | h_\theta)$, where $h_\theta$ is the decoder's final hidden representation. This distribution incorporates two key properties:
- **Action-Group-aware Scaling:** Robot actions are typically composed of groups (position, rotation, gripper). Each group has distinct characteristics. StructRL partitions the action dimensions into these groups. A global base noise level $\sigma_{\text{base}}$ sets the overall magnitude, while a lightweight MLP head predicts element-wise multipliers $\sigma_{c,d}$ for each action dimension, bounded by group-specific ranges $[\alpha_{g(d)}, \beta_{g(d)}]$.
- **AR(1) Temporal Correlation:** To ensure smooth exploration within an action chunk, an AR(1) (autoregressive model of order 1) noise sequence is sampled. For each action dimension $d$, $\epsilon_{c+1,d} = \rho \epsilon_{c,d} + \sqrt{1 - \rho^2} \zeta_{c,d}$, where $\rho \in [0, 1)$ controls temporal correlation and $\zeta_{c,d} \sim \mathcal{N}(0, 1)$.
- **Combined Perturbation:** The final structured action-space perturbation is $\Delta_{0,c,d} = \sigma_{c,d} \epsilon_{c,d}$.
- **Structured Log-Probability:** The log-probability for this structured noise involves the AR(1) density for the corresponding normalized noise coordinates ($\epsilon^\theta_{c,d} = \Delta^\theta_{0,c,d} / \sigma_{c,d}$) and a change-of-variables term for the scale factors.
- **Regularization:** To maintain useful exploration, the learned scale $\sigma$ is regularized using three terms: a dimension-entropy term (discourages scale collapse), a budget term (keeps overall noise magnitude near target), and a temporal smoothness term (avoids abrupt scale changes along the chunk).

### 4. Main findings and results

The experiments were conducted on three simulation benchmarks (LIBERO, ManiSkill, CALVIN) and two real-world tasks, using $\pi_0$, $\pi_{0.5}$, and GR00T N1.5 as base VLA models.

#### 4.1. Simulation Results

- **LIBERO Benchmark (Table 2):**
 - StructRL achieved an average success rate of 99.0% on GR00T N1.5, outperforming the $\pi_{RL}$ (Flow-SDE+PPO) baseline by 2.5 percentage points.
 - On $\pi_0$ and $\pi_{0.5}$, StructRL consistently improved performance over existing in-chain baselines, though the gains were modest as most RL methods approached saturation on this benchmark. The benchmark primarily served as an in-distribution sanity check.
- **ManiSkill Benchmark (Table 3):** This benchmark tested out-of-distribution (OOD) performance.
 - StructRL achieved the highest in-distribution success rate of 87.8% on $\pi_0$ and matched the best on $\pi_{0.5}$.
 - For OOD evaluation, the action-space RL framework (both Gaussian Baseline and StructRL) demonstrated a clear advantage over Flow-SDE's in-chain noise injection.
 - Moving the injection location from Flow-SDE to the endpoint Gaussian Baseline improved OOD average success by 28.0 points on $\pi_0$ (39.3% to 67.3%) and 16.6 points on $\pi_{0.5}$ (49.3% to 65.9%). This indicates that placing exploration in the action space is a significant factor in OOD performance.
 - StructRL further refined the endpoint covariance and demonstrated benefits in learning efficiency, often achieving similar final performance to the Gaussian Baseline but more rapidly.
- **CALVIN Benchmark (Table F1):** For long-horizon chaining, StructRL achieved the highest average sequence length and maintained the strongest performance at longer sequence lengths (e.g., above 0.93 at Len4). The performance gap over time-independent in-chain baselines increased with sequence length, supporting the utility of action-space temporal correlation for complex multi-stage tasks.

#### 4.2. Ablation Studies (Fig. 3)

Conducted on LIBERO-Long with GR00T N1.5, these studies provided insight into StructRL's design choices:

- **Sensitivity to Noise Level ($\sigma_{\text{base}}$):** Moderate action-space noise levels (e.g., $\sigma_{\text{base}}=0.1$) provided stable online improvement, while very small levels under-explored and very large levels destabilized training.
- **Noise Injection Scheme:**
 - StructRL reached 90% success in approximately 40 training steps, whereas the endpoint Gaussian Baseline required about 80 steps. This highlights that structured action-space noise improves exploration efficiency.
 - The final performance after saturation was comparable between StructRL and the Gaussian Baseline, indicating that structure primarily offers efficiency gains rather than a universal final performance advantage.
 - In-chain structured noise (Flow-SDE-structured) performed similarly to isotropic in-chain noise (Flow-SDE), reinforcing the "Structured Noise Dilution" hypothesis.
- **Train–Inference Consistency:** Configurations where the training replay depth matched the inference-time ODE step showed substantially better performance. A larger matched replay jump (e.g., 2nd-last step) further improved exploration efficiency. This supports the importance of consistency in the last-step replay mechanism.
- **Robustness to Denoising Steps (T):** Varying the number of denoising steps $T \in \{2, 3, 4, 5\}$ showed that the last-step replay mechanism provided an effective optimization signal for the full flow field, achieving comparable convergence speeds and final performance across different $T$ values.

#### 4.3. Real-World Experiment (Fig. 4)

StructRL was evaluated on a Franka Research 3 robot for "pick-banana" (PnP) and "plug-charger-in" (fine manipulation) tasks using an asynchronous AWAC learner.

- Both tasks were initialized with SFT on 10 demonstrations, resulting in 0% success before online RL.
- For "pick-banana," StructRL achieved an 84% success rate (21/25 trials) after 60 minutes of online RL, compared to 56% (14/25 trials) for the Gaussian baseline.
- For "plug-charger-in," StructRL reached a stable and fully successful regime in approximately 30 minutes, whereas the Gaussian baseline required about 35 minutes.
- These results suggest that structured exploration enhances exploration efficiency in real-world online RL settings, leading to faster adaptation and higher success rates with sparse rewards.

### 5. Significance and potential impact

The StructRL framework presents several significant contributions and potential impacts on the field of robotic control with VLA models:

1. **Addressing a Fundamental Problem in Generative RL:** The explicit identification and empirical verification of "Structured Noise Dilution" highlights a fundamental challenge when combining generative models (like flow-based VLAs) with reinforcement learning. This insight provides a clearer understanding of why naive noise injection within denoising chains may be suboptimal for robotics.
2. **Improved Exploration for Robotics:** By relocating stochasticity directly to the action space and incorporating both temporal correlation (AR(1) noise) and action-group-aware scaling, StructRL offers an exploration strategy that is more aligned with the physical realities and safety requirements of robotic manipulation. This leads to more physically meaningful and less jittery exploratory actions.
3. **Enhanced Learning Efficiency and OOD Performance:** The simulation results, particularly on ManiSkill, demonstrate that moving exploration to the action space significantly improves out-of-distribution (OOD) performance. Furthermore, structuring this action-space noise improves learning efficiency, allowing policies to reach high performance levels with fewer training steps. This is critical for practical applications where data collection and training time can be costly.
4. **Tractable RL Framework for Flow-Based VLAs:** The "last-step replay" mechanism is a notable methodological contribution. It enables the policy gradient updates to effectively propagate back to the flow decoder, even when the policy's likelihood is evaluated solely on the final action-space perturbation. This bypasses the complexity of defining likelihoods over intermediate denoising states, simplifying the RL objective for this class of generative models.
5. **Real-World Applicability:** The successful deployment and improved performance of StructRL on real-world robotic tasks (pick-banana, plug-charger-in) validate its practical utility. Faster online adaptation from sparse binary rewards, even with limited initial demonstrations, indicates that StructRL can accelerate the deployment and fine-tuning of VLA models in dynamic physical environments.
6. **Broader Impact on Generalist Robots:** As VLA models become more generalist, the ability to efficiently adapt them to novel tasks through online RL is paramount. StructRL's approach to structured action-space exploration contributes to this goal by enabling more robust and efficient learning, which could be particularly beneficial for generalist robots operating in diverse and unstructured settings.

In summary, StructRL offers a more principled and effective way to integrate reinforcement learning with flow-based VLA models, particularly by making exploration noise more robot-centric. This advancement has the potential to make robot adaptation more efficient, robust, and capable of handling a wider range of manipulation tasks.
