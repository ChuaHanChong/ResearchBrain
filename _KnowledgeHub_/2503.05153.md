# Generative Trajectory Stitching through Diffusion Composition

> [!summary] Summary
> CompDiffuser is a diffusion planner that generates long-horizon plans without long-horizon training data. It splits a trajectory into overlapping chunks and trains one bidirectional denoiser to generate each chunk conditioned on neighbors' noisy samples, so information propagates during denoising. Autoregressive compositional sampling stitches short segments into feasible goal-directed plans, reaching 65-68% success on OGBench Giant mazes where baselines score near zero.

## Problem

- Monolithic diffusion planners model whole-plan distributions, so they need long-horizon data covering every start-goal pair and fail beyond training horizons.
- Reinforcement-learning trajectory stitching depends on locating overlap points between chunks, which breaks down when connecting regions are tiny or data is noisy exploration.
- Prior compositional generative planners sample only under conjunctions of given conditions or fixed task skeletons, so they cannot extend to longer sequences or new goals.

## Method

- ==Compositional factorization== splits a trajectory into K overlapping chunks and factors the plan distribution into neighbor-conditioned terms, with start and goal states conditioning the two end chunks.
- A ==noisy-sample conditioning== objective trains one denoiser to predict a chunk given neighboring chunks corrupted at the same noise level, giving bidirectional information propagation instead of blocked Gibbs sampling.
- Two compositional samplers: parallel denoising on previous-timestep neighbors, and autoregressive denoising conditioned on the already-denoised previous chunk; overlapping regions merge via ==exponential trajectory blending==.

## Results

- On OGBench Giant stitch tasks, success reaches **68%** PointMaze, **65%** AntMaze, **67%** HumanoidMaze, versus 29%, 20%, 5% for the compositional diffusion baseline and at most 21% for HIQL.
- Ablations confirm both design choices: autoregressive sampling beats parallel on AntMaze Giant (**65% vs 48%**), and replanning lifts AntMaze Giant from **27% to 65%**.
- Scaling planner state to 29D keeps **97%** on AntMaze Medium but drops Giant to 28%; on AntSoccer the 17D planner reaches **69%** arena and 17% medium, beating all baselines.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Conditioning generative modules on each other's partially denoised samples is a cheap way to coordinate separate models, replacing slow iterative Gibbs-style composition.
> - Causal conditioning on less-noisy neighbors buys plan coherence at the cost of parallelism, and the gap widens as more chunks are composed.
> - Compositional generation extrapolates horizon but not indefinitely: error accumulates along long conditioning chains, and the optimal chunk count remains task-dependent.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research directly addresses a critical challenge in robotic decision-making: long-horizon planning, particularly when training data for such complex tasks is scarce. The paper positions itself at the intersection of three rapidly evolving areas: generative models for planning, trajectory stitching, and compositional generative models.

**Generative Models for Planning:** Recent advancements have seen generative models, especially diffusion models (e.g., Diffuser, Decision Diffuser), emerge as powerful tools for robot planning. These models learn distributions over entire plan sequences, effectively transforming planning into a sampling problem. This amortizes the computational cost of traditional search and optimization methods. However, the authors highlight a significant limitation: these models are typically constrained to solve tasks similar to their training data. Exhaustively modeling joint distributions over long-horizon plan sequences for all possible start and goal states is extremely sample-inefficient, demanding vast amounts of costly long-horizon data. CompDiffuser aims to overcome this data limitation by enabling generalization to *new* long-horizon tasks from *short-horizon* training data.

**Trajectory Stitching:** The concept of trajectory stitching, originating from the Reinforcement Learning (RL) literature, offers a pathway to compositional generalization. It involves combining chunks of different, often high-reward, trajectories to create novel, longer policies. The primary challenge in this approach lies in identifying appropriate stitching points that maintain dynamic consistency and physical feasibility across combined segments. Prior work has explored data augmentation, model-based methods, and sequence modeling for stitching. CompDiffuser brings the generative power of diffusion models to this problem, allowing for the generation of entirely new, dynamically consistent composite trajectories rather than merely assembling existing ones.

**Compositional Generative Models:** The paper also situates itself within the broader field of compositional generative models. While many existing works in this domain focus on sampling under the conjunction of several given conditions (e.g., visual content, human motion generation) or exploring sequential compositional models that operate within a predefined skeleton, CompDiffuser distinguishes itself. It aims to achieve compositional planning that scales to generating much longer sequences and completing new tasks *without relying on predefined, task-dependent skeletons*. Instead, it learns to directly stitch short trajectory segments in a flexible, data-driven manner.

In summary, CompDiffuser represents a novel synthesis, leveraging the expressive power of diffusion models to generate diverse and high-quality plans, while simultaneously addressing the data inefficiency of monolithic generative planners and the rigidness of prior compositional methods by enabling flexible, dynamically consistent trajectory stitching from short, readily available data. This work pushes the boundaries of compositional generalization in robotic planning, paving the way for more adaptable and efficient learning systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to enable generative planners, specifically those based on diffusion models, to effectively solve long-horizon robotic tasks without the prohibitive requirement of extensive long-horizon training data. The authors aim to achieve this while ensuring that the generated plans remain physically feasible and goal-directed.

The key motivations driving this work stem from the limitations of current state-of-the-art generative planning approaches:
- **Data Scarcity for Long-Horizon Tasks:** Collecting comprehensive, long-horizon plan data that covers all possible start and goal state combinations for complex robotic tasks is exceptionally sample-inefficient and costly. This significantly restricts the applicability of current generative planners to real-world scenarios.
- **Generalization Gap:** Monolithic generative planners, trained on specific task distributions, struggle to generalize to new tasks that require significantly longer horizons or involve unseen sequences of sub-tasks. They tend to collapse when faced with out-of-distribution (O.O.D.) start-goal conditions.
- **Maintaining Physical Consistency:** While trajectory stitching offers a solution for compositional generalization, ensuring that stitched segments connect smoothly and maintain dynamic consistency and physical feasibility is a non-trivial challenge. Traditional stitching methods often struggle with this, particularly in complex, high-dimensional environments.

To address these motivations, the authors propose **CompDiffuser**, aiming to:
- **Learn Compositional Trajectory Distributions:** Instead of learning a single, monolithic distribution for entire long trajectories, the objective is to model the distribution compositionally by breaking it down into overlapping chunks and learning their conditional relationships.
- **Enable Effective Trajectory Stitching:** Develop a mechanism that allows generative models to stitch these shorter trajectory chunks together, forming coherent, long-horizon plans for new, unseen tasks.
- **Leverage Bidirectional Information Propagation:** Introduce a novel approach where information flows bidirectionally between trajectory segments during the generation process, ensuring physical consistency and smooth transitions at stitching points.
- **Achieve Goal-Conditioned Causal Generation:** The final plans must be conditioned on specific start and goal states, allowing for purposeful and directed behavior.

Ultimately, the overarching motivation is to unlock the full potential of generative models for complex robotic planning by making them more data-efficient, generalizable, and robust to novel tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

CompDiffuser introduces a novel generative framework for trajectory stitching, primarily leveraging the progressive denoising capabilities of diffusion models and a compositional modeling approach.

#### 3.1. Compositional Trajectory Modeling

The core idea is to transform a long-horizon planning problem, defined by a start state ($q_s$) and a goal state ($q_g$), into sampling a trajectory $\tau$ by composition. Instead of directly modeling the entire trajectory $\tau$, CompDiffuser subdivides it into $K$ overlapping sub-chunks, $\tau_1, \ldots, \tau_K$. The probability distribution $p_{\theta}(\tau | q_s, q_g)$ is then represented compositionally as a product of conditional probabilities:
$p_{\theta}(\tau | q_s, q_g) \propto p_1(\tau_1|q_s, \tau_2) \prod_{k=2}^{K-1} p_k(\tau_k|\tau_{k-1}, \tau_{k+1}) p_K(\tau_K|\tau_{K-1}, q_g)$.
This formulation posits that each trajectory chunk $\tau_k$ depends only on its immediate neighbors ($\tau_{k-1}$ and $\tau_{k+1}$), and the end chunks depend on the start/goal states and their single neighbor. This modularity allows for the generation of trajectories significantly different from those seen in training, as long as the intermediate chunks themselves are plausible.

#### 3.2. Training Compositional Trajectory Models

A key challenge in composing trajectories is ensuring dynamically consistent and feasible transitions between overlapping chunks. CompDiffuser addresses this through a clever mechanism called **noisy-sample conditioning**.
- **Single Diffusion Model:** Rather than training separate models for each conditional distribution, a single denoising network $\epsilon_{\theta}$ is trained. This network learns to predict the noise (or the clean sample $\tau^0_k$) given a noisy trajectory chunk $\tau^t_k$, the current noise level $t$, and conditional information from its neighbors.
- **Bidirectional Conditioning:** The crucial insight is to condition the denoising of a segment $\tau^t_k$ on *noisy versions* of its neighboring segments ($\tau^t_{k-1}$ and $\tau^t_{k+1}$) at the *same noise level $t$*. This allows information to propagate bidirectionally during the reverse diffusion process, ensuring that as one segment takes shape, it influences its neighbors to adopt compatible configurations. The training objective for this is $L_{nbr} = E_{\tau \in D,t,k} [\|\epsilon - \epsilon_{\theta}(\tau^t_k, t | \tau^t_{k-1}, \tau^t_{k+1})\|^2]$.
- **Start/Goal Conditioning:** The same denoising network is also trained to incorporate start state ($q_s$) and goal state ($q_g$) conditioning for the first ($\tau_1$) and last ($\tau_K$) chunks, respectively. Objectives $L_{start}$ and $L_{end}$ are defined analogously, replacing neighbor chunks with $q_s$ or $q_g$.
- **Efficient Conditioning:** In practice, conditioning is applied only on the small overlapping regions between consecutive trajectories, which is sufficient to maintain consistency efficiently.

#### 3.3. Compositional Trajectory Planning (Inference)

At inference time, CompDiffuser generates a long-horizon plan by iteratively denoising the $K$ initialized noisy trajectory chunks. Two sampling schemes are proposed:
- **Parallel Sampling:** All trajectory chunks $\tau^t_{1:K}$ are denoised concurrently. Each chunk $\tau^t_k$ is conditioned on the noisy adjacent chunks ($\tau^t_{k-1}$, $\tau^t_{k+1}$) from the *previous* denoising timestep $t$. This is computationally efficient as it allows for batch processing but limits instantaneous information propagation between chunks.
- **Autoregressive Sampling:** To achieve stronger coordination, chunks are denoised sequentially (e.g., from $\tau_1$ to $\tau_K$). Each $\tau^t_k$ is conditioned on the *already denoised (less noisy) version of the preceding chunk $\tau^{t-1}_{k-1}$* and the *noisy version of the succeeding chunk $\tau^t_{k+1}$* from the previous timestep. This causal information flow leads to more coherent plans but is computationally less efficient due to its sequential nature. This method is predominantly used in the experiments due to its superior performance.

After the full denoising process, the clean trajectory chunks ($\tau^0_{1:K}$) are merged into a final composite trajectory $\tau_{comp}$ using **exponential trajectory blending** in their overlapping regions.

#### 3.4. Inverse Dynamics Model and Replanning

To execute the generated plans, an MLP-based inverse dynamics model is trained to predict actions given the current observation and a subgoal from the plan. CompDiffuser also supports **flexible replanning** during execution. If the agent deviates from the planned trajectory beyond a threshold, a new plan is synthesized, starting from the agent's current state and potentially using a receding horizon scheme for $K$ to encourage faster convergence as the agent approaches the goal.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

CompDiffuser consistently demonstrates significant improvements over existing methods across a wide range of challenging trajectory stitching benchmarks, validating its ability to generate coherent long-horizon plans from short-horizon training data.

#### 4.1. PointMaze Environments

- **Ghugare et al. Datasets:** CompDiffuser achieved 100% success rates across U-Maze, Medium, and Large mazes, drastically outperforming baselines (e.g., RvS, DT) which struggled (e.g., RvS at 3% for Large) due to their inability to autonomously identify small overlapping regions for stitching. This highlights CompDiffuser's effectiveness in learning subtle connection points.
- **OGBench PointMaze Datasets:** For tasks requiring significantly longer horizons than seen in training, CompDiffuser achieved 100% success on Medium and Large mazes, and a remarkable 68% on the Giant maze. This substantially outperformed all offline RL baselines and even other diffusion-based compositional planners like Generative Skill Chaining (GSC), which achieved only 29% on the Giant maze, often collapsing or generating disconnected/out-of-distribution (O.O.D.) trajectories (Figure 4).

#### 4.2. High-Dimension Tasks (AntMaze, HumanoidMaze, AntSoccer)

- **AntMaze and HumanoidMaze (Stitch Datasets):** CompDiffuser maintained high success rates even as planning horizon and state-space complexity increased (up to 50D for humanoid agents). For example, in AntMaze Giant, CompDiffuser achieved 65% success, significantly surpassing HIQL (21%) and GSC (20%). Similar trends were observed for HumanoidMaze, where CompDiffuser achieved 67% on Giant tasks, while the best baseline, HIQL, only managed 12%.
- **AntMaze with Low-Quality Data (Explore Datasets):** Even with highly noisy, clustered, and low-quality training data, CompDiffuser demonstrated robust performance. In AntMaze Giant Explore, it achieved 27% success, outperforming GSC (21%) and significantly all other offline RL baselines (which were 0%). This suggests resilience to imperfect data.
- **AntSoccer:** For multi-skill tasks like guiding an ant to a ball and then dribbling it to a goal, CompDiffuser successfully stitched these two distinct "skills" (which were only observed in isolation in training data). Both 4D (ant/ball x-y) and 17D (ant/ball x-y + ant joint positions) planners excelled, with the 17D variant showing slightly higher success (e.g., 69% vs 65% in Arena), indicating the benefit of more detailed state information.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

- **Planning in High Dimension Space:** CompDiffuser performed consistently across 2D, 15D, and 29D state spaces for AntMaze Medium. While success rates decreased for 15D and 29D in AntMaze Large and Giant due to increased complexity, it remained competitive, showcasing its capability to model complex dynamics.
- **Number of Composed Trajectories (K):** Experiments on PointMaze-Giant-Stitch revealed stable performance for $K$ between 7 and 12, with optimal performance around $K=9-10$. This suggests flexibility in handling varying composition lengths, as the model adapted by either extending or consuming redundant states.
- **Replanning Capability:** Implementing replanning significantly boosted performance in more complex "Giant" mazes (e.g., AntMaze Giant: 65% with replan vs 27% without), allowing the agent to recover from execution errors or suboptimal inverse dynamics actions.
- **Parallel vs. Autoregressive Sampling:** Autoregressive sampling consistently outperformed parallel sampling in terms of plan quality, especially for longer and more complex tasks. This empirically validates that the causal information flow, where chunks condition on less noisy versions of preceding chunks, leads to more coherent and physically consistent transitions.

Overall, the findings strongly support CompDiffuser's ability to generalize to long-horizon, novel tasks by compositionally stitching short trajectory segments. Its robustness to data quality, scalability to high-dimensional state spaces, and the effectiveness of its bidirectional conditioning and autoregressive sampling mechanisms are key differentiators.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

CompDiffuser represents a significant step forward in the field of robotic planning and generative models, with profound implications and potential impact across several dimensions:

- **Addressing the Data Bottleneck in Long-Horizon Planning:** The most critical impact of CompDiffuser is its ability to enable generative planners to solve long-horizon tasks *without requiring long-horizon training data*. This directly addresses a major bottleneck in real-world robotics, where collecting exhaustive demonstrations for complex, multi-stage tasks is often impractical, costly, and time-consuming. By learning from easily obtainable short trajectory segments, CompDiffuser makes data collection significantly more efficient and accessible.

- **Enhancing Generative Compositional Generalization:** This work moves beyond simply generating variations of seen trajectories. It demonstrates true compositional generalization, where distinct short skills or trajectory chunks, learned independently, can be flexibly combined to synthesize entirely novel, long-horizon plans for unseen tasks. This capability is fundamental for developing more intelligent and autonomous robotic systems that can adapt to a wider range of situations.

- **Robustness Across Diverse Environments and Tasks:** The extensive experimental results showcase CompDiffuser's robustness across varying environment sizes, agent state dimensions (from 2D point agents to 50D humanoid robots), trajectory types (maze navigation, ball dribbling), and even with low-quality, noisy training data. This versatility suggests broad applicability in different robotic domains, from mobile navigation to manipulation.

- **Architectural Innovation for Consistency:** The introduction of bidirectional noisy-sample conditioning and autoregressive sampling is a key technical contribution. This mechanism ensures physical consistency and smooth transitions between stitched trajectory chunks, which has historically been a major challenge in compositional planning. It highlights a powerful way to propagate information and enforce coherence within complex generative processes.

- **Increased Flexibility and Adaptability:**
 - **Multi-modality:** The inherent multi-modal nature of diffusion models is preserved, allowing CompDiffuser to generate diverse trajectory morphologies (multiple valid paths to a goal), which can be further leveraged for preference-based planning or robustness.
 - **Replanning Capability:** The ability to flexibly replan during execution enables robots to recover from deviations or unexpected events, a crucial feature for deployment in dynamic and uncertain real-world environments.

- **Potential for Broader Impact:** While demonstrated in robotic planning, the core idea of compositional sequence generation through diffusion composition could extend to other domains requiring long-sequence generation from short segments, such as long-form content generation in natural language processing or music composition, where maintaining global coherence while generating locally consistent parts is critical.

- **Future Research Directions:** The authors openly acknowledge limitations, such as potential error accumulation in very long chains of composition and the need for manual specification of $K$ (number of chunks). These limitations pave the way for exciting future research in areas like adaptive $K$ selection, advanced rejection sampling techniques, and incorporating more sophisticated MCMC methods to further enhance robustness and autonomy.

In conclusion, CompDiffuser significantly advances the state-of-the-art in generative robotic planning by offering a data-efficient and robust solution for compositional trajectory stitching. Its innovations hold promise for accelerating the development of truly intelligent and adaptable robotic systems capable of tackling complex, long-horizon tasks in diverse and unpredictable environments.
