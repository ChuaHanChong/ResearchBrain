# LLaVA-NeXT-Interleave: Tackling Multi-image, Video, and 3D in Large Multimodal Models

> [!summary] Summary
> LLaVA-NeXT-Interleave introduces a unified framework for Large Multimodal Models (LMMs) to handle multi-image, video, and 3D data by leveraging an interleaved image-text format. The model achieves state-of-the-art results on new comprehensive benchmarks for these diverse visual scenarios while maintaining strong performance on single-image tasks.

## Problem

- Existing open-source Large Multimodal Models (LMMs) primarily focus on single-image understanding.
- Handling diverse visual data like multi-image, video, and 3D scenes typically requires fragmented approaches with specialized models.
- This fragmentation leads to inefficient development, lack of generalizability, and limited knowledge transfer across different visual contexts.

## Method

- Develops **LLaVA-NeXT-Interleave**, a single LMM that unifies multi-image, multi-frame (video), multi-view (3D), and multi-patch (single-image) scenarios under a generalized ==interleaved image-text format==.
- Curates **M4-Instruct**, a large-scale ==instruction-following dataset== (1.1M samples) encompassing tasks from all "M4" domains, converted to the interleaved format.
- Employs ==instruction-tuning==, initializing from a pre-trained LLaVA-NeXT-Image checkpoint, and trains with mixed interleaved data formats and combined data scenarios.

## Results

- Achieves an average score of **62.3%** on the new LLaVA-Interleave Bench, outperforming prior open-source models like Mantis (**39.6%**) and demonstrating robust generalization to out-domain multi-image tasks.
- Delivers superior performance on multiple video benchmarks (e.g., NExT-QA **79.1%**, MVBench **54.9%**) compared to other video-specific LMMs, with Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) further boosting results to surpass larger models.
- Obtains leading results in 3D perception (average **59.2%** on five in-domain benchmarks) solely from multi-view images, without requiring additional 3D inputs like point clouds, while maintaining strong single-image capabilities.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The interleaved image-text format can serve as a universal data template, effectively unifying diverse visual input types (multi-image, video, 3D, high-resolution single images) into a single architectural paradigm.
> - Training a single model on a diverse range of visual data formats simultaneously fosters "emergent capabilities" and improves performance across individual tasks through synergistic knowledge transfer.
> - Leveraging a strong pre-trained single-image Large Multimodal Model (LMM) as a starting point is crucial for efficient and high-performing development of more complex interleaved multimodal models.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Large Multimodal Models (LMMs) has witnessed explosive growth, building upon the success of Large Language Models (LLMs) by integrating visual capabilities. Prominent examples like OpenAI's GPT-4V and Google's Gemini have showcased impressive abilities in understanding and interacting with single images, driven by extensive vision-language pre-training and instruction tuning. In the open-source community, models such as LLaVA, BLIP, OpenFlamingo, and IDEFICS have democratized access to powerful LMMs, enabling a wide array of image and language understanding tasks.

However, despite these advancements, the existing landscape of open-source LMMs has largely concentrated on optimizing performance for *single-image* scenarios. This focus, while valuable for foundational capabilities, leaves a significant gap in addressing the complexity and diversity of real-world visual data. Many practical applications, ranging from surveillance and robotics to scientific analysis and content creation, inherently involve sequences of images (videos), multiple distinct images (e.g., photo albums, comparative analysis), or multi-view representations of 3D environments.

Traditionally, researchers have approached these multi-modal challenges by developing specialized models for each distinct scenario:
- **Multi-image understanding:** Models like Mantis and VPG-C have emerged to handle tasks involving multiple static images.
- **Video understanding:** Video-specific LMMs such as VideoChatGPT, Video-LLaVA, and LLaMA-VID are designed to process temporal information.
- **3D understanding:** Approaches like Point-Bind & LLM and 3D-LLM often incorporate point clouds or multiple views to grasp spatial relationships.

This fragmented approach leads to several inefficiencies: it requires labor-intensive development of separate architectures, training pipelines, and datasets for each modality or scenario. This results in a collection of task-specific models that lack generalizability across different visual contexts and cannot leverage knowledge transfer between them.

LLaVA-NeXT-Interleave directly addresses this fragmentation by proposing a unified framework. The core insight is to leverage the "interleaved image-text format" not just for in-context learning or basic multi-image tasks, but as a universal data template capable of representing single images, multiple images, video frames, and 3D views. This conceptual leap aims to consolidate diverse visual input types under a single architectural paradigm. By doing so, this work pushes the frontier of LMMs towards true versatility, moving beyond specialized capabilities to a more holistic understanding of the visual world, mirroring the versatility of human perception which seamlessly integrates information from various visual sources. It builds upon the established success of the LLaVA series, known for its instruction-tuning methodology, and seeks to extend that paradigm to a broader array of visual data.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of LLaVA-NeXT-Interleave is to develop a single, comprehensive Large Multimodal Model (LMM) that can effectively and simultaneously handle a diverse range of real-world visual scenarios. The authors identify a critical limitation in current open-source LMMs, which predominantly focus on single-image understanding, leading to fragmented approaches for more complex visual data.

The key motivations driving this research are:

- **Unifying Diverse Visual Scenarios:** The most significant motivation is to overcome the current fragmentation in LMM research. Instead of developing separate models for multi-image analysis, video understanding, and 3D scene comprehension, the authors aim to create a general framework. They propose that the "image-text interleaved format" (where images and text are interspersed) can serve as a universal template to represent various visual inputs, including:
 - **Multi-image:** Standard scenarios involving multiple distinct images.
 - **Multi-frame (Video):** Treating video as a sequence of frames.
 - **Multi-view (3D):** Representing 3D environments through multiple camera perspectives.
 - **Multi-patch (Single-image):** Even high-resolution single images can be processed as multiple patches, fitting the interleaved paradigm.
 They collectively refer to these as "M4" scenarios.

- **Maintaining Single-Image Performance:** A crucial practical motivation is to extend capabilities without compromising existing strengths. The new model must not only excel in multi-image, video, and 3D tasks but also maintain or even improve the state-of-the-art performance achieved by previous LLaVA models on conventional single-image tasks. This ensures backward compatibility and practical utility.

- **Enabling Emerging Capabilities through Cross-Scenario Training:** By training a single model on such a diverse range of visual data formats and tasks, the authors hypothesize that the model can learn to compose knowledge across domains. This could lead to "emerging capabilities," where the model can perform novel tasks that were not explicitly trained, by combining understanding from different visual settings (e.g., applying "spot the difference" logic from static images to video frames). This represents a step towards more generalizable AI.

- **Developing Comprehensive Training and Evaluation Resources:** To support the development of such an ambitious unified LMM, there is a clear need for high-quality, diverse training data and robust evaluation benchmarks. Therefore, a key objective is to:
 - **Curate M4-Instruct:** A large-scale, meticulously compiled instruction-following dataset encompassing tasks from all four M4 domains.
 - **Curate LLaVA-Interleave Bench:** A dedicated benchmark to comprehensively evaluate the multi-image performance of LMMs, including both in-domain (seen during training) and out-domain (novel) tasks to assess generalization.

In essence, LLaVA-NeXT-Interleave seeks to build a more robust, versatile, and efficient LMM capable of handling the true complexity of real-world visual data, thereby advancing the pursuit of more general artificial intelligence.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology of LLaVA-NeXT-Interleave revolves around the central idea of **unifying diverse visual inputs under a general interleaved image-text format** and then employing advanced instruction-tuning techniques. The approach can be broken down into architectural choices, data compilation, and training strategies.

#### 3.1. Architecture and Base Model

The model architecture follows the established framework of LLaVA-NeXT, comprising:
- **Vision Encoder:** SigLIP-400M with 384x384 resolution, responsible for extracting visual features from input images.
- **Intermediate Projector:** A two-layer Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) that maps the visual features from the encoder into the embedding space of the language model.
- **Large Language Model (LLM):** Qwen 1.5, available in 0.5B, 7B, and 14B parameter sizes, serving as the core reasoning and generation component.

A critical aspect of the approach is **Technique 1: Continue training from single-image models.** Instead of training from scratch, LLaVA-NeXT-Interleave initializes its training from an off-the-shelf LLaVA-NeXT-Image checkpoint. This base model has already undergone a two-stage training process (image-caption pre-training and single-image fine-tuning), effectively leveraging its pre-existing proficiency in single-image understanding and visual-language alignment. This warm start significantly boosts performance and training efficiency for the more complex interleaved scenarios.

#### 3.2. M4-Instruct Dataset Curation

To enable multi-modal capabilities across the "M4" scenarios (Multi-image, Multi-frame, Multi-view, Multi-patch), the authors meticulously compiled **M4-Instruct**, a comprehensive instruction-following dataset containing 1,177.6k samples. The key is converting all these diverse visual inputs into the unified interleaved format.

- **Multi-image Scenarios:** This domain covers 12 real-world tasks, including "Spot the Difference," "Visual Story Telling," "Image Edit Instruction Generation," "Interleaved Multi-image Dialogue," "Multi-image Puzzle," and "Low-level Multi-image Assessment." Data is sourced from existing public datasets (e.g., NLVR2, Coinstruct) and rigorously converted. Additionally, new high-quality tasks like "Real-world Difference," "Synthetic Difference," and "Twitter Post" were annotated using GPT-4V to expand diversity.
- **Multi-frame (Video) Scenarios:** Video data is transformed into sequences of multiple frames. Two primary tasks are included: "Video Detailed Captioning" and "Video VQA." Datasets like LLaVA-Hound, NExT-QA, and STAR contribute to this section.
- **Multi-view (3D) Scenarios:** 3D environments are represented by multi-view images captured from different perspectives, allowing the model to infer spatial information. Tasks include "Embodied VQA" (dialogue and planning) and "3D Scene VQA" (captioning and grounding). Training data is gathered from nuScenes QA, ALFRED, ScanQA, and 3D-LLM.
- **Multi-patch (Single-image) Scenarios:** To preserve and enhance single-image capabilities, 40% of the stage-2 fine-tuning data from LLaVA-NeXT (which utilizes an "any resolution" training approach by dividing high-resolution images into multiple low-resolution patches) is randomly sampled and included. This implicitly fits the multi-image interleaved format.

#### 3.3. LLaVA-Interleave Bench

To comprehensively evaluate multi-image performance, the authors curated the **LLaVA-Interleave Bench**, consisting of 17K challenging instances across 13 tasks. It is divided into:
- **In-domain Evaluation:** Tasks seen during training (e.g., Q-Bench, NLVR2, newly curated multi-image tasks) to verify proficiency in familiar scenarios.
- **Out-domain Evaluation:** Tasks that do not overlap with training scenarios (e.g., MathVerse-mv, SciVerse-mv, Mantis-Eval, BLINK, MMMU-mv) to assess the model's generalization capacity to novel multi-image contexts.

#### 3.4. Training Techniques

Beyond the dataset and base model, two additional techniques are applied during the instruction-tuning phase:

- **Technique 2: Mixed Interleaved Data Formats during Training.** The model is trained to be robust to different input formats for image tokens. It supports:
 - "In-the-front format": All image tokens are placed at the beginning of the prompt, with `<image>` placeholders within the text.
 - "Interleaved format": Image tokens are inserted exactly where their corresponding `<image>` placeholders appear in the text.
 Training with both formats ensures flexibility and robustness during inference.

- **Technique 3: Combining Different Data Scenarios Improves Individual Task Performance.** This is a core hypothesis of the paper. Unlike previous works that often fine-tune with only one type of data (e.g., only multi-image or only video), LLaVA-NeXT-Interleave simultaneously instruction-tunes with all four M4 tasks. The rationale is that distinct visual scenarios can provide complementary semantics and instruction-following capabilities, leading to performance improvements across individual tasks and fostering emergent cross-domain abilities. Post-training, the model can also undergo Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) for further performance refinement, particularly noted for video tasks.

This comprehensive methodology, integrating a strong base model, a unified and diverse dataset, a robust evaluation benchmark, and carefully designed training techniques, underpins LLaVA-NeXT-Interleave's ability to tackle a broad spectrum of visual understanding tasks.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

LLaVA-NeXT-Interleave demonstrates compelling performance across all four "M4" interleaved scenarios, establishing new state-of-the-art results for open-source models while showcasing remarkable emerging capabilities.

#### 4.1. Quantitative Performance

- **Multi-image Results (LLaVA-Interleave Bench):**
 - LLaVA-NeXT-Interleave significantly surpasses previous open-source models like LLaVA-NeXT-Image, VPG-C, and Mantis on the LLaVA-Interleave Bench.
 - For the 14B parameter model, it achieves an average score of 62.3%, outperforming Mantis (7B) at 39.6%.
 - Crucially, it shows strong performance in both **in-domain** (e.g., "Spot the Difference," "Visual Story Telling") and **out-domain** evaluation. In out-domain tasks like Mantis-Eval and BLINK, its performance is comparable to or even better than GPT-4V in some aspects, demonstrating robust generalization to unseen scenarios. This indicates that the diverse M4-Instruct dataset effectively transfers knowledge.

- **Multi-frame (Video) Results:**
 - Despite not being specifically designed as a video-centric model, LLaVA-NeXT-Interleave achieves superior results on multiple video benchmarks (NExT-QA, MVBench, ActivityNet-QA, VDD, VideoChat-GPT) compared to other video-based LMMs of similar sizes.
 - The 14B model achieves an NExT-QA score of 79.1% and an MVBench score of 54.9%.
 - A significant finding is that applying **Direct Preference Optimization (DPO)** after M4-Instruct tuning further boosts video performance. The 7B LLaVA-NeXT-Interleave (DPO) model even surpasses the larger LLaVA-NeXT-Video (34B) on VDD and VideoChat-GPT benchmarks, highlighting effective temporal understanding and reasoning from sequential frames within the unified framework.

- **Multi-view (3D) Results:**
 - For 3D perception, LLaVA-NeXT-Interleave obtains leading results across five in-domain benchmarks, covering both indoor and outdoor scenarios (e.g., ScanQA, 3D-assisted Dialogue, Task Decomposition from 3D-LLM, nuScenes VQA, ALFRED).
 - The 14B model achieves an impressive average score of 59.2%.
 - Notably, it significantly outperforms models like 3D-LLM and Point-Bind & LLM, which often require additional 3D inputs like point clouds. LLaVA-NeXT-Interleave demonstrates strong 3D understanding solely from multi-view images, validating the efficacy of the interleaved format for spatial reasoning.

- **Multi-patch (Single-image) Results:**
 - A key success is the model's ability to **maintain strong single-image performance** while expanding to multi-modal capabilities. As shown in Table 4, LLaVA-NeXT-Interleave (both 7B and 14B versions) performs comparably to, and in some cases slightly better than, the dedicated LLaVA-NeXT-Image model across standard single-image benchmarks like AI2D, ChartQA, DocVQA, MME, SciQA, and POPE. This indicates that integrating single-image data as "multi-patch" into the interleaved training doesn't degrade its original capacity but rather reinforces instruction-following abilities.

#### 4.2. Ablation Studies of Proposed Techniques

The paper includes ablation studies confirming the effectiveness of its core techniques:
- **Continuing Training from Single-image Models:** Initializing from a pre-trained LLaVA-NeXT-Image checkpoint consistently yields better interleaved multi-image performance compared to training directly from a Stage-1 pre-trained model. This validates the benefit of leveraging existing, high-quality single-image proficiency.
- **Mixed Interleaved Data Formats:** Training with both "in-the-front" and "interleaved" image token placements enhances robustness and benefits results across different input formats, making the model more flexible.
- **Combining Different Data Scenarios:** Incrementally adding single-image and multi-image data to video-only training significantly improves video task performance. This supports the hypothesis that cross-scenario training provides complementary semantics and instruction-following capabilities, leading to synergistic improvements.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

LLaVA-NeXT-Interleave represents a significant stride in the development of Large Multimodal Models, moving beyond the current paradigm of single-image focus to a truly unified and versatile architecture. Its significance and potential impact are multifaceted:

#### 5.1. Towards General-Purpose LMMs

The most profound impact of this work is its contribution to the realization of truly general-purpose LMMs. By demonstrating that a single model can effectively handle multi-image, video, 3D, and single-image data within a unified framework, LLaVA-NeXT-Interleave challenges the prevailing fragmented approach. This unification reduces the need for developing and maintaining separate task-specific models, streamlining research and development efforts in multimodal AI. It brings us closer to an AI that perceives and reasons about the visual world in a more holistic, human-like manner.

#### 5.2. Efficiency and Scalability

Consolidating diverse visual tasks into one model offers immense practical benefits in terms of efficiency. Instead of deploying multiple specialized models, applications can rely on a single, powerful LMM, reducing computational overhead and simplifying system design. This efficiency makes LMMs more scalable for real-world deployments across various industries, from autonomous systems and medical imaging to content creation and surveillance.

#### 5.3. Novel Capabilities and Generalization

The emergence of cross-task and cross-modal transfer capabilities is a groundbreaking aspect of this research. The ability for LLaVA-NeXT-Interleave to generalize "spot the difference" from static images to videos, or to create Twitter posts from videos, without explicit training for these compositions, signifies a leap in reasoning and adaptability. This hints at a more profound understanding of visual concepts and their underlying semantic relationships, potentially leading to unforeseen applications and more robust AI systems that can adapt to novel situations with minimal fine-tuning.

#### 5.4. Public Resources and Democratization of Research

The release of the comprehensive M4-Instruct dataset and the LLaVA-Interleave Bench is a crucial contribution to the open-source community. These high-quality, diverse resources will serve as foundational tools for future research in unified LMMs, accelerating progress by providing standardized benchmarks and rich training data. This democratization of advanced multimodal capabilities will enable a broader range of researchers and developers to build upon this work, fostering innovation and pushing the boundaries of what LMMs can achieve.

#### 5.5. Competitive Performance against Closed-Source Models

Achieving competitive, and in some cases superior, performance against large closed-source models like GPT-4V, especially with smaller parameter counts (e.g., 7B model surpassing 34B counterparts after DPO), underscores the effectiveness of LLaVA-NeXT-Interleave's methodology. This demonstrates that innovative architectural and training strategies can enable open-source models to rival proprietary solutions, promoting transparency and accessibility in AI development.

#### 5.6. Broader Real-World Applications

The enhanced versatility of LLaVA-NeXT-Interleave opens doors for complex real-world applications that demand integrated multi-modal understanding. Examples showcased, such as multi-image painting style recognition, PPT summarization and Q&A, and multi-document VQA, highlight its potential for intelligent assistants, advanced document processing, educational tools, and creative applications that interact with the visual world in nuanced ways.

In conclusion, LLaVA-NeXT-Interleave not only pushes the state-of-the-art in multimodal understanding but also lays a critical cornerstone for the development of truly unified, efficient, and generalizable LMMs, promising to transform how AI interacts with and comprehends the visual world.
