# Chart-FR1: Visual Focus-Driven Fine-Grained Reasoning on Dense Charts

> [!summary] Summary
> A new framework, Chart-FR1, enhances Multimodal Large Language Models' (MLLMs) ability to understand and reason over high information density (HID) charts through focused reasoning mechanisms. The framework achieves state-of-the-art performance, outperforming GPT-4o on the new HID-Chart benchmark and other existing MLLMs across various chart understanding tasks.

## Problem

- Existing MLLMs struggle with fine-grained perception on High Information Density (HID) charts, often omitting critical visual cues.
- Current MLLMs are susceptible to redundant or noisy visual information when reasoning on dense charts, degrading accuracy and efficiency.
- Reinforcement learning algorithms used for MLLM reasoning lack adaptive mechanisms, with fixed KL penalties over-restricting exploration for complex reasoning chains.

## Method

- Introduced ==Focus-CoT== (Focusing Chain-of-Thought), a mechanism that explicitly links reasoning steps to fine-grained visual evidence (OCR text, bounding boxes) through supervised fine-tuning.
- Developed Focus-GRPO (Focus-Driven Reinforcement Learning Algorithm), which refines visual focusing efficiency via an information-efficiency reward and an adaptive KL penalty for flexible reasoning depth control.
- Constructed HID-Chart, a novel benchmark with an information-density metric and human annotation, specifically designed to rigorously evaluate MLLMs on high information density charts.

## Results

- Chart-FR1 achieved a mean accuracy of **67.1%** across five popular chart benchmarks, surpassing the base Qwen2.5-VL-**7B** by **6.1%** and closed-source GPT-4o (**64.0%**).
- On the newly constructed HID-Chart benchmark, Chart-FR1-**7B** achieved **53.0%** accuracy, outperforming Qwen2.5-VL-**7B** by **10.0%**, Qwen2.5-VL-**72B** by **1.5%**, and GPT-4o (**51.2%**) by **1.8%**.
- Ablation studies demonstrated that the adaptive KL penalty and information-efficiency reward in Focus-GRPO each contributed **1.3%** to the average accuracy, confirming their individual effectiveness in improving reasoning.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly associating reasoning steps with localized visual cues (OCR text, image regions) significantly enhances fine-grained perception and overall reasoning accuracy.
> - Optimizing visual focusing efficiency by penalizing redundant information and adaptively controlling reasoning depth using a variable KL penalty is crucial for robust performance on complex, dense charts.
> - A two-stage training approach combining supervised fine-tuning for initial visual focus activation and reinforcement learning for refined, adaptive reasoning is effective for developing advanced chart understanding capabilities.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Chart understanding and reasoning are critical tasks in various domains, involving the interpretation of quantitative information encoded through a combination of text, geometric shapes, colors, and layouts. Recent advancements in Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs), including general-purpose models like GPT-4o and Qwen2.5-VL, as well as chart-specific MLLMs such as ChartReasoner and EvoChart, have demonstrated progress in these areas. Additionally, reasoning MLLMs, including R1-VL and Vision-R1, leverage reinforcement learning (RL) to enhance multimodal deep reasoning capabilities.

Despite these advancements, existing MLLMs encounter challenges when processing charts with high information density (HID). These challenges include difficulty in fine-grained perception, susceptibility to redundant or noisy visual information, and a lack of adaptive deep reasoning mechanisms. Current models often rely on global visual embeddings, leading to the omission of crucial visual cues and struggles in performing sufficiently deep reasoning with captured elements. Furthermore, the use of fixed KL penalties in RL algorithms can over-restrict models when longer reasoning chains and extensive visual cues are required.

This work addresses these identified limitations by proposing a novel framework specifically designed to improve MLLM performance on HID charts. It aims to enhance perception, focusing efficiency, and adaptive deep reasoning, while also introducing a new benchmark to facilitate further research in this specialized area. This positions the research within the efforts to improve the robustness, interpretability, and performance of MLLMs for complex visual-linguistic tasks, particularly in scenarios where data visualization is dense and intricate.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the capabilities of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) for understanding and reasoning over high information density (HID) charts. The authors identify three major challenges that motivate this work:

1. **Limited Fine-Grained Perception:** Existing MLLMs often process global visual embeddings, which can lead to the omission of critical visual cues embedded within dense chart layouts. This limitation prevents models from accurately extracting specific, localized information necessary for precise reasoning. The motivation is to enable models to selectively attend to and perceive fine-grained details within complex charts.

2. **Redundant or Noisy Visual Information:** When MLLMs attempt to perform deep reasoning on HID charts, the presence of redundant or irrelevant visual information can degrade reasoning accuracy and efficiency. Models struggle to filter out noise and focus on high-value cues. The motivation here is to develop mechanisms that promote efficient focusing by penalizing redundant visual content, thereby improving the signal-to-noise ratio for reasoning.

3. **Lack of Adaptive Deep Reasoning:** Current reinforcement learning (RL) algorithms used for enhancing MLLM reasoning often employ a fixed KL penalty, which can overly constrain the model's exploration space. This becomes problematic when HID charts necessitate longer and more complex reasoning chains, requiring the discovery of numerous visual cues. The motivation is to introduce an adaptive mechanism that allows for flexible control over reasoning depth, adjusting the constraint based on the richness of visual information encountered.

To address these motivations, the paper sets forth specific objectives:
- To propose **Focus-CoT (Focusing Chain-of-Thought)**, a mechanism that explicitly links reasoning steps to fine-grained visual evidence, such as region-level image patches and OCR information, thereby enhancing perception.
- To introduce **Focus-GRPO (Focus-Driven Reinforcement Learning Algorithm)**, which optimizes visual focusing efficiency through an information-efficiency reward to compress redundant visual information, and an adaptive KL penalty to enable flexible control over reasoning depth.
- To construct **HID-Chart**, a novel benchmark designed with an information-density metric to rigorously evaluate MLLMs on HID charts, thereby addressing a gap in existing chart benchmarks regarding chart diversity, domain coverage, and information density.
- To develop **Chart-FR1**, a model trained with a two-stage focused reasoning paradigm, integrating Focus-CoT and Focus-GRPO, to achieve state-of-the-art performance in chart understanding and reasoning, particularly for HID charts.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology involves a two-stage focused reasoning training framework designed to enhance the fine-grained reasoning capability of MLLMs on HID charts. The base model utilized is Qwen2.5-VL-7B.

#### 3.1. Cold-Start for Visual Focused Reasoning (Stage 1)

The first stage focuses on supervised fine-tuning to imbue the model with preliminary visual focused reasoning abilities using a mechanism called **Focus-CoT**.

- **Focus-CoT Mechanism:** Focus-CoT extends the traditional Chain-of-Thought (CoT) by explicitly embedding visual focusing actions within reasoning steps. A `<focus>` tag is introduced to anchor reasoning steps to critical visual elements. Each focusing action comprises two sub-actions: OCR text extraction and local image region localization using bounding boxes. Subsequent reasoning steps are then guided by this focused information, establishing a tight coupling between perception and reasoning.

- **Automated Focus-CoT Generation Pipeline:** To create a high-quality dataset for supervised fine-tuning, an automated pipeline is developed:
 1. **Data Collection and CoT Generation:** Training samples are selected from existing chart datasets based on difficulty, quality, diversity, and authenticity. For each sample, the base model (Qwen2.5-VL) is prompted to generate eight initial CoT reasoning paths.
 2. **Format Filtering and Correctness Evaluation:** Reasoning chains are filtered for adherence to the CoT format. An LLM evaluates the correctness of candidate paths, categorizing samples as easy, medium, or hard based on a `pass@k` metric.
 3. **Conditional CoT Reconstruction:** A powerful teacher model (GPT-5) is used to associate the initial CoT with crucial visual evidence. If an original reasoning path is incorrect, the teacher model identifies the error, inserts a `<focus>` tag to gather correct visual information (OCR text and bounding boxes), and revises subsequent reasoning and the answer. If the original path is correct, `<focus>` tags are inserted at key points to verify reasoning steps with visual evidence, without altering the logic.
 4. **Quality Filtering:** Final rule-based and LLM-based filters ensure answer correctness, format adherence, and removal of redundant visual information.
- **Supervised Fine-Tuning:** The model parameters are updated by minimizing a cross-entropy loss over the generated Focus-CoT dataset, aiming to preserve the base model's general capabilities while introducing visual focused reasoning knowledge.

#### 3.2. Visual Focus-Driven Reinforcement Learning (Focus-GRPO) (Stage 2)

The second stage refines the model's visual focusing and reasoning through a novel reinforcement learning algorithm, **Focus-GRPO**, which builds upon standard GRPO.

- **Multi-dimensional Reward Function:** Focus-GRPO introduces several reward components to guide policy optimization:
 1. **Relaxed-Accuracy Reward ($R_{relaxed\,acc}$):** This reward addresses numerical fluctuation issues in chart QA by defining correctness with a tolerance margin (e.g., within 5% for numerical answers, exact match otherwise). It provides a more stable learning signal than strict accuracy.
 2. **Format Reward ($R_{format}$):** Encourages the generation of structured Focus-CoT outputs by assigning scores based on adherence to the specified format (Focus-CoT, CoT, or other).
 3. **Information-Efficiency Reward ($R_{efficiency}$):** This exponential decay function penalizes redundant visual information within the `<focus>` tags, encouraging the model to extract high-value cues efficiently. Redundancy is calculated across three sub-components: OCR-OCR text similarity, Box-Box Intersection over Union (IoU), and OCR-Box text similarity (between OCR text and bounding box labels). A similarity threshold ($\tau$) is used to avoid unnecessary penalties.
- **Adaptive KL Penalty:** Unlike fixed KL penalties in standard GRPO, Focus-GRPO dynamically adjusts the penalty coefficient ($\beta'$) based on the amount of focused information ($N_{info} = (N_{ocr} + N_{box})/2$). This mechanism relaxes the KL constraint when rich cues are focused (encouraging deeper exploration) and tightens it when cues are limited (ensuring policy stability).
- **Policy Optimization:** The total reward, combining relaxed-accuracy, format, and information-efficiency rewards, is used to compute advantages via a group-relative method. The policy is then optimized to maximize an objective function that includes the adaptive KL penalty term, using the PPO clipping hyperparameter ($\epsilon$).

#### 3.3. HID-Chart Benchmark Construction

To facilitate evaluation on HID charts, the authors constructed **HID-Chart** with a new information-density metric.

- **Chart-ID Metric:** This metric quantifies chart complexity by scoring charts along four dimensions (Information richness, efficiency, clarity, and interactivity) on a five-point scale, evaluated by GPT-5. The final Chart-ID is a weighted sum of these scores.
- **Human-in-the-Loop Pipeline:**
 1. **Data Curation:** Approximately 2500 charts from diverse sources (scientific publications, websites, data visualization libraries) are collected.
 2. **Chart Filtering:** Charts with high Chart-ID scores are retained to form a high-quality candidate pool.
 3. **Question Generation:** GPT-5 generates multiple candidate questions emphasizing diversity, complexity, and realism, specifically targeting fine-grained reasoning.
 4. **Human Annotation and Rewriting:** A team of graduate students removes simple/ambiguous questions, upgrades single-step questions to multi-step reasoning ones, performs initial answer annotations, and conducts cross-annotation for verification.
The final HID-Chart dataset comprises 1561 high-quality QA pairs across 734 charts, covering 10 chart types and 8 domains, with an average information density of 3.94.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents experimental results demonstrating the effectiveness of Chart-FR1 across multiple benchmarks and through ablation studies.

#### 4.1. Overall Performance on Chart Understanding and Reasoning Benchmarks

Chart-FR1, based on Qwen2.5-VL-7B, achieved a mean accuracy of 67.1% across five popular chart benchmarks: ChartQA, CharXiv, EvoChart, ChartBench, and PlotQA. This performance surpassed the base Qwen2.5-VL-7B model by an average of 6.1%. Chart-FR1 also outperformed other MLLM categories, including several closed-source models like GPT-4o (64.0% average accuracy), general MLLMs, chart-specific MLLMs, and general reasoning MLLMs, indicating the efficacy of the two-stage focused reasoning training framework.

#### 4.2. Performance on HID-Chart Benchmark

On the newly constructed HID-Chart benchmark, Chart-FR1-7B achieved an average accuracy of 53.0%. This result was 10.0% higher than the base Qwen2.5-VL-7B (43.0%) and surpassed larger open-source models like Qwen2.5-VL-72B (51.5%) by 1.5%. It also exceeded the performance of the closed-source GPT-4o (51.2%) by 1.8%. The evaluation revealed a general degradation in performance for existing MLLMs as information density increased, while Chart-FR1 demonstrated more robust performance across different information density intervals and subject categories.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies on Focus-GRPO Components

An ablation study on the components of Focus-GRPO, comparing it to a standard GRPO implementation, yielded the following:
- Focus-GRPO improved average performance by 3% over standard GRPO (67.1% vs. 64.1%).
- Removing the adaptive KL penalty led to a 1.3% drop in average accuracy (to 65.8%), suggesting that a fixed KL penalty excessively constrains exploration in deep reasoning scenarios.
- Excluding the information-efficiency reward also resulted in a 1.3% decline (to 65.8%), indicating that penalizing redundant visual information is beneficial for reasoning accuracy.
- Even with both adaptive KL penalty and information-efficiency reward removed, the remaining Focus-GRPO components (e.g., relaxed-accuracy reward) still outperformed standard GRPO by 1.4%, highlighting the benefit of a more stable reward environment.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies on Two-Stage Training and Focused Cues

- **Effectiveness of Two-Stage Training:** Removing the cold-start stage (supervised fine-tuning with Focus-CoT) led to a 2.4% decrease in average accuracy (from 67.1% to 64.7%), demonstrating its role in initially activating visual focused reasoning. Removing the Focus-GRPO stage entirely resulted in a 4.4% drop (to 62.7%), confirming the critical role of reinforcement learning in enhancing focusing efficiency and adaptive deep reasoning.
- **Impact of Focus-CoT Cues:** Removing OCR text from focusing cues caused a 2.6% performance drop (to 64.5%), while removing bounding box localization led to a 1.9% decrease (to 65.2%). This indicates that both OCR text and local image regions are essential visual cues for fine-grained reasoning.

#### 4.5. Generalization and Teacher Model Impact

- **Generalization:** The method demonstrated generalization by showing improved performance when applied to different base models, specifically Qwen2.5-VL-3B and Qwen3-VL-8B.
- **Teacher Model:** The performance remained stable when GPT-5 was replaced by other open-source MLLMs as the teacher model for Focus-CoT generation, suggesting that the method's effectiveness relies on the focus-driven CoT mechanism itself rather than a specific teacher model's superior intelligence.

#### 4.6. Hyperparameter Analysis

The similarity threshold ($\tau$) for the information-efficiency reward was found to impact performance, with $\tau=0.9$ yielding the highest average accuracy across benchmarks. This supports the concept that penalizing highly similar visual information effectively reduces redundancy. The hyperparameter $\alpha$ for controlling the decay rate of the information-efficiency reward was optimized at $\alpha=2$. The adaptive KL penalty was observed to lead to longer reasoning chains when a greater number of visual cues were focused, confirming its role in promoting adaptive deep reasoning.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces a novel framework, Chart-FR1, that addresses several critical limitations of current Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) in processing High Information Density (HID) charts. The significance and potential impact of this work are multifaceted:

1. **Advancement in Chart Understanding and Reasoning:** By introducing Focus-CoT and Focus-GRPO, the research provides concrete mechanisms to enhance fine-grained perception, optimize visual focusing efficiency, and enable adaptive deep reasoning. This directly contributes to improving MLLM capabilities in a challenging domain where existing models often struggle due to visual clutter and complex information encoding. The demonstrated state-of-the-art performance against advanced MLLMs across multiple benchmarks validates the effectiveness of the proposed approaches.

2. **Improved Robustness for Complex Visual-Linguistic Tasks:** The ability of Chart-FR1 to maintain performance with increasing information density, as evidenced on the HID-Chart benchmark, indicates a step towards more robust and reliable MLLMs for real-world applications. Scientific papers, industry reports, and data journalism frequently employ HID charts, and a model capable of accurately interpreting them can accelerate research, decision-making, and knowledge dissemination.

3. **Enhanced Interpretability of MLLM Reasoning:** The Focus-CoT mechanism, by explicitly linking reasoning steps to localized visual cues (OCR text and bounding boxes), provides greater transparency into the model's decision-making process. This improved interpretability can build user trust and facilitate debugging, which is crucial for deploying MLLMs in sensitive or high-stakes applications.

4. **New Benchmark for Challenging Scenarios:** The creation of HID-Chart, accompanied by a quantifiable information-density metric (Chart-ID), fills a significant gap in existing evaluation benchmarks. This new resource provides a standardized and challenging environment for researchers to rigorously test and compare MLLM performance on complex charts, thereby promoting further progress in the field of fine-grained chart reasoning. Its diverse chart types and broad domains also contribute to a more comprehensive evaluation landscape.

5. **Generalizability and Adaptability of Reinforcement Learning in MLLMs:** The Focus-GRPO algorithm, with its relaxed-accuracy reward, information-efficiency reward, and adaptive KL penalty, offers a refined approach to applying reinforcement learning for multimodal reasoning. These innovations address issues like sparse rewards and over-penalization, making RL more effective for complex tasks requiring extensive visual exploration. The demonstrated generalization across different base models further underscores the potential for wider adoption and impact.

In summary, this work pushes the boundaries of MLLM capabilities in chart understanding by addressing key challenges in fine-grained perception and adaptive reasoning. The proposed methods and the new benchmark are expected to significantly influence future research and development in multimodal AI, leading to more capable and trustworthy systems for interpreting complex visual information.
