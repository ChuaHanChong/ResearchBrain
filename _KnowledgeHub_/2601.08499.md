# EfficientFSL: Enhancing Few-Shot Classification via Query-Only Tuning in Vision Transformers

> [!summary] Summary
> EfficientFSL, developed by researchers from Fudan, Tsinghua, and East China University of Science and Technology, presents a framework for enhancing few-shot classification in Vision Transformers using a query-only tuning paradigm. It achieves state-of-the-art performance across diverse benchmarks and cross-domain tasks with only 1.25M trainable parameters for a ViT-S backbone.

## Problem

- Utilizing large Vision Transformers (ViTs) for few-shot learning (FSL) is challenging due to their high computational and memory demands for full fine-tuning.
- Existing Parameter-Efficient Transfer Learning (PETL) methods often modify the ViT backbone, risking alteration of pre-trained knowledge or overfitting in data-scarce FSL settings.
- Few-shot classification frequently struggles with distribution shifts between the limited support set (for prototypes) and the query set, leading to degraded generalization.

## Method

- EfficientFSL employs a =='query-only' tuning paradigm==, completely freezing the pre-trained ViT backbone and training only lightweight, external modules to extract task-relevant features.
- It integrates a ==Forward Block== for each ViT layer, where a trainable ==Active Block== generates task-specific queries that interact with the frozen ViT's intermediate features.
- A ==Combine Block== adaptively fuses multi-layer, multi-type features using conditional weighting, and a ==Support-Query Attention Block== refines class prototypes by aligning them with query sample distributions.

## Results

- Achieved state-of-the-art performance on **four** in-domain few-shot benchmarks (miniImageNet, tieredImageNet, CIFAR, FC100) and **six** cross-domain datasets, surpassing full fine-tuning and other SOTA FSL methods.
- Demonstrated superior parameter efficiency, utilizing only **1.25M** trainable parameters for ViT-S and **2.48M** for ViT-B, a significant reduction compared to full fine-tuning (e.g., **21.7M** for ViT-S).
- Ablation studies confirmed the critical roles of the Forward Block's trainable components, the adaptive feature fusion in the Combine Block, and the prototype refinement by the Support-Query Attention Block.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Freezing the entire pre-trained Vision Transformer while tuning external querying modules effectively preserves the model's general knowledge and prevents overfitting in data-limited FSL scenarios.
> - Adaptive, conditional aggregation of hierarchical features from various ViT layers is crucial for forming robust and generalized representations, especially in few-shot settings.
> - Dynamically adjusting class prototypes using query-aware attention effectively mitigates distribution shifts between support and query sets, thereby enhancing classification accuracy and generalization.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper "EfficientFSL" sits at the critical intersection of two rapidly evolving fields in artificial intelligence: **Few-Shot Learning (FSL)** and **Parameter-Efficient Transfer Learning (PETL)**, specifically applied to **Vision Transformers (ViTs)**.

**Few-Shot Learning (FSL)** addresses the challenge of training models that can generalize to new categories with very limited labeled data, mimicking human-like learning capabilities. Traditional deep learning models typically require vast amounts of data, making them impractical for many real-world applications where data annotation is costly or scarce. Early FSL methods primarily focused on meta-learning (learning to learn), data augmentation, or metric-based approaches. Metric-based methods, like Prototypical Networks, are particularly popular for their simplicity and effectiveness in learning embedding spaces where similar samples are close.

The emergence of **Vision Transformers (ViTs)** has revolutionized computer vision, demonstrating superior performance over traditional CNNs on various tasks, especially with large pre-training datasets. Their powerful representational capacity makes them highly attractive for FSL, where strong feature extraction is paramount. However, this comes with a significant drawback: ViTs are typically very large models, and **fully fine-tuning** all their parameters for new tasks demands immense computational resources (GPU memory, training time) and storage. This makes them impractical for the "low-resource scenarios" that FSL aims to address.

This is where **Parameter-Efficient Transfer Learning (PETL)** comes into play. PETL methods aim to adapt large pre-trained models to downstream tasks by modifying and training only a small fraction of their parameters, keeping the vast majority of the backbone model frozen. Common PETL techniques include adding lightweight adapter modules, using low-rank matrix decomposition (e.g., LoRA), or employing soft prompts. While effective, many existing PETL methods for vision still involve inserting modules *into* the backbone, thereby altering the original feature flow and risking overfitting or reduced generalization in data-scarce FSL settings.

**EfficientFSL directly addresses this gap.** It leverages the power of pre-trained ViTs for FSL while explicitly mitigating the computational and memory overhead through a novel "query-only" fine-tuning paradigm. Unlike prior PETL methods that modify the backbone, EfficientFSL freezes the entire pre-trained ViT backbone and instead introduces a lightweight, *external* querying module. This approach attempts to fully preserve the rich, generalized knowledge within the frozen ViT while learning task-specific features efficiently. The work contributes to the broader landscape by offering a more robust and efficient way to deploy large models in FSL, especially in cross-domain settings where distribution shifts are common. It seeks to combine the best of both worlds: the representational power of ViTs and the efficiency of PETL, optimized for the unique challenges of few-shot classification.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objectives and motivations driving the development of EfficientFSL are clearly articulated in the paper:

1. **Bridge the Gap between Powerful ViTs and Resource Constraints in FSL:** The primary motivation is to enable the use of large Vision Transformers (ViTs), known for their superior representational capacity, for Few-Shot Classification (FSL). While ViTs achieve remarkable performance, their immense parameter count makes full fine-tuning computationally expensive, memory-intensive, and time-consuming, rendering them impractical for the very "low-resource scenarios" FSL is designed for. EfficientFSL aims to unlock the potential of ViTs in these constrained environments.

2. **Achieve Competitive FSL Performance with Significant Computational Reduction:** The goal is to develop a fine-tuning framework that can match or even surpass the performance of existing FSL methods, including those that use full fine-tuning or less efficient PETL techniques, while drastically cutting down the number of trainable parameters, GPU memory requirements, and training time. This focuses on practical utility and scalability.

3. **Propose a Novel "Query-Only" Tuning Paradigm:** The authors aim to introduce a new approach that fundamentally differs from existing Parameter-Efficient Transfer Learning (PETL) methods. Instead of inserting modules *into* the frozen backbone, which can alter feature flow and risk overfitting in data-limited FSL, EfficientFSL seeks to freeze the entire pre-trained model and only tune a lightweight "querying module" that *extracts* task-relevant information from the backbone's intermediate representations. This "decoupling" design is intended to preserve the pre-trained knowledge more effectively.

4. **Enhance Robustness to Distribution Shift in FSL:** Few-shot classification often suffers from distribution shifts between the small support set (used for class prototypes) and the query set (to be classified). A key objective is to explicitly address this by proposing a mechanism that refines prototypes to better align with the query set distribution, thereby improving generalization and accuracy.

In essence, EfficientFSL is motivated by the desire to make state-of-the-art vision models (ViTs) genuinely accessible and effective for few-shot learning tasks, particularly in scenarios where computational resources are limited, without compromising classification accuracy or generalization ability.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

EfficientFSL proposes a novel parameter-efficient fine-tuning framework for few-shot classification with Vision Transformers (ViTs), built upon a "query-only" tuning paradigm. The architecture comprises three main components: the Forward Block, the Combine Block, and the Support-Query Attention Block (SQ Attention Block), all integrated with a Prototypical Network (PN) classifier.

**Overview:**
The core idea is to completely freeze the pre-trained ViT backbone. EfficientFSL takes the intermediate representations from *each layer* of this frozen ViT as input. A lightweight, trainable querying module then synthesizes task-specific queries to extract relevant features without modifying the backbone itself.

**1. Forward Block:**
This block is designed to integrate task-specific knowledge with the general knowledge from the frozen ViT. Each Forward Block (one per ViT layer) consists of two sub-blocks:

- **Active Block:** This is the lightweight, *trainable* component. It takes the output from the previous layer of the EfficientFSL pipeline (H_{i-1}) and adds a trainable prompt (P_i). This combined input then passes through a bottleneck-structured projection layer (`Proj(·)`), a self-attention layer, and a Multilayer Perceptron (MLP). Critically, all projection layers within the Active Block are bottleneck-structured to ensure parameter efficiency. The output of the Active Block, F_i, captures task-specific knowledge.
- **Frozen Block:** This component is entirely *frozen* and reuses the pre-trained parameters of the ViT. It uses F_i (from the Active Block) as the **query** and the intermediate features (X_i) from the corresponding layer of the *frozen pre-trained ViT* as the **key** and **value**. This "query-only" mechanism allows the Active Block to selectively extract information from the powerful pre-trained representations without altering them. The output features from this block are F_att_i (from attention), F_mlp_i (from MLP), and H_i (the final output).

**2. Combine Block:**
The Forward Blocks produce hierarchical features (F_att_i, F_mlp_i, H_i) from each ViT layer. The Combine Block is responsible for adaptively fusing these multi-layer, multi-type features into a unified, rich representation (F_agg).

- **Feature Alignment:** All hierarchical features are first aligned to a common dimensionality using a shared, bottleneck-structured `MLP_shared_Combine` layer. This enhances parameter efficiency by reusing projection layers across features.
- **Adaptive Feature Aggregation:** Instead of simple averaging, the Combine Block employs an adaptive weighting mechanism. It uses a `Weight MLP` to extract dynamic weights (w_att_i, w_mlp_i, w_H_i) from the final-layer hidden state (H_n). These weights are then applied to the aligned features through a weighted sum, yielding the aggregated feature representation (F_agg). This conditional weighting allows the model to prioritize more relevant features from different layers and types.

**3. Support-Query Attention Block (SQ Attention Block):**
This component addresses the common issue of distribution shift between the support set (used to form class prototypes) and the query set in FSL.

- **Prototype Refinement:** After extracting aggregated features (F_agg) for both support and query samples, initial class prototypes (s) are computed by averaging support features (following Prototypical Network principles). The SQ Attention Block then refines these prototypes.
- **Query-Aware Adjustment:** For each prototype 's' and query sample 'q', a learnable projection (`Proj(·)`) is applied to 'q' to make the alignment class-aware. The updated prototype (s_att) is then calculated as a weighted sum of the original prototype 's' and 'q' (after projection), where the weight 'α' is derived from the correlation between 's' and the projected 'q'. This effectively shifts the prototype closer to the center of its corresponding query distribution, mitigating bias.

**Classification Head:**
Finally, the updated prototypes (s_att) are used with a **Prototypical Network (PN) classifier**. Classification is performed by computing the cosine similarity between each query sample's aggregated feature (F_agg) and the refined prototypes (s_att) of all classes, predicting the label based on the nearest prototype.

**Parameter Efficiency:**
The entire framework prioritizes parameter efficiency. Bottleneck-structured fully connected layers are extensively used throughout the Active and Combine Blocks. The pre-trained ViT backbone is completely frozen, and only the lightweight modules (Active Blocks, Combine Block's MLPs, SQ Attention Block's projection, and trainable prompts P_i) are trained. The paper specifies minimal trainable parameters (e.g., 1.25M for ViT-S, 2.48M for ViT-B).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

EfficientFSL demonstrates significant advancements in few-shot classification, particularly in its ability to leverage large Vision Transformers (ViTs) with remarkable parameter efficiency. The main findings can be summarized as follows:

1. **State-of-the-Art (SOTA) Performance on In-Domain FSL Benchmarks:**
 - EfficientFSL consistently outperforms recent SOTA methods and even full fine-tuning baselines across four common in-domain few-shot datasets: miniImageNet, tieredImageNet, CIFAR, and FC100, for both 5-way 1-shot and 5-way 5-shot settings.
 - For instance, with a ViT-S backbone, EfficientFSL achieves 97.40% (1-shot) and 99.05% (5-shot) on miniImageNet, significantly higher than full fine-tuning (89.41%/95.59%) and other SOTA methods like FewVS (86.80%/90.32%).
 - When using a ViT-B backbone pre-trained on ImageNet-21K, performance is further boosted, reaching 98.34% (1-shot) and 99.12% (5-shot) on miniImageNet, and substantially improving on more challenging datasets like FC100 (80.13%/88.81%).

2. **Superior Parameter Efficiency:**
 - A key strength of EfficientFSL is its extremely low parameter count compared to competitors. For ViT-S, it uses only 1.25M trainable parameters, which is dramatically less than the 21.7M parameters of full fine-tuning or other ViT-based approaches. For ViT-B, it uses 2.48M parameters.
 - This efficiency translates directly into practical benefits, as shown in comparisons with mainstream Parameter-Efficient Transfer Learning (PETL) approaches (Adapter, AdaptFormer, LoRA) on FC100. EfficientFSL not only achieves higher accuracy (e.g., 80.13% with ViT-B vs. LoRA's 74.19%) but also boasts faster training times (e.g., 59.62 s/epoch vs. LoRA's 76.35 s/epoch for ViT-B) and competitive peak memory usage and inference speed.

3. **Strong Generalization in Cross-Domain FSL:**
 - Evaluations on six cross-domain few-shot datasets (CUB, Stanford Cars, Places, Plantae, EuroSAT, CropDiseases) demonstrate EfficientFSL's superior generalization capabilities.
 - Outperforming existing cross-domain SOTA methods like LDPNet, FLoR, StyleAdv, and AMT-FT across all benchmarks in both 1-shot and 5-shot scenarios validates its effectiveness in real-world applications with domain shifts.

4. **Effectiveness of Individual Components (Ablation Studies):**
 - **Forward Block (Active Block):** Ablation studies confirmed the critical roles of its components. Removing the projection layer (Proj.) led to a sharp performance drop, highlighting its importance for high-quality feature representations. Removing the attention and MLP modules (Att & MLP) significantly reduced parameters but also dropped performance, indicating their necessity for capturing task-specific information. The trainable prompts (P_i) were also crucial.
 - **Combine Block:** While removing Combine Block modules had a smaller impact on parameter reduction, the accuracy drop highlighted its role in efficient feature fusion. Adaptive, conditional weighting (using H_n to derive weights) achieved the best performance compared to simple averaging or fixed weights, demonstrating that dynamic aggregation of multi-layer features is key for robust generalization, especially in low-data settings.
 - **Support-Query Attention Block (SQ Attention Block):** Removing the SQ Attention Block consistently decreased accuracy across all datasets and shots. The t-SNE visualizations clearly illustrated that the SQ Attention Block effectively shifts prototypes closer to the distribution center of the corresponding query samples, thereby mitigating the support-query distribution bias and enhancing classification accuracy. Removing the learnable projection on 'q' also resulted in a slight but consistent performance drop, confirming its role in class-aware alignment.

5. **Progressive Feature Space Refinement:**
 - t-SNE visualizations illustrated the progressive refinement of the feature space as each component of EfficientFSL was incrementally added. Starting from a highly entangled space with only a pre-trained ViT, the introduction of prompts, projection layers, Active Block, Combine Block, and finally the SQ Attention Block led to increasingly tighter, more compact, and clearly separated class clusters, qualitatively demonstrating the effectiveness of the modular design in improving representation quality for FSL.

In summary, EfficientFSL not only sets new SOTA records in few-shot classification across diverse benchmarks but also achieves this with unparalleled parameter efficiency, making it a highly practical and scalable solution for leveraging powerful large models in resource-constrained FSL scenarios.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

EfficientFSL presents a significant advancement in the field of few-shot learning and parameter-efficient transfer learning, with substantial potential impact across several dimensions:

1. **Democratizing Access to Large Models for FSL:** The most immediate impact of EfficientFSL is making powerful Vision Transformers (ViTs) practically accessible for few-shot learning tasks, especially in "real-world low-resource scenarios." Previously, the computational and memory demands of fine-tuning large ViTs limited their adoption. By drastically reducing trainable parameters, GPU memory usage, and training time, EfficientFSL enables researchers and practitioners with modest computational resources to leverage the representational power of SOTA models for tasks with limited data. This democratizes high-performance FSL.

2. **Advancement in Parameter-Efficient Transfer Learning (PETL):** EfficientFSL introduces a novel "query-only" fine-tuning paradigm that represents a distinct evolution in PETL. Unlike methods that insert modules *into* the backbone, EfficientFSL freezes the entire pre-trained model and extracts information via external trainable queries. This approach may offer superior preservation of the pre-trained model's general knowledge and prevent detrimental alterations to its feature flow, which is particularly beneficial in data-scarce FSL settings where overfitting is a major concern. This could inspire new directions in PETL research for other domains as well.

3. **Enhanced Robustness to Distribution Shift:** The introduction of the Support-Query Attention Block (SQ Attention Block) is a crucial contribution. Distribution shift between support and query sets is a pervasive problem in FSL that often degrades generalization. By dynamically adjusting prototypes to align with the query set distribution, EfficientFSL provides a robust mechanism to mitigate this bias, leading to more accurate and reliable classification, especially in challenging cross-domain FSL scenarios. This component's efficacy could be adapted to other metric-based FSL approaches.

4. **Improved Generalization Capabilities:** The consistent SOTA performance on both in-domain and cross-domain benchmarks, coupled with its parameter efficiency, highlights EfficientFSL's strong generalization. This is vital for real-world applications where new tasks or domains might exhibit variations from the training data. Its ability to adapt effectively to unseen categories with minimal examples makes it suitable for diverse applications like medical image analysis, specialized industrial inspection, or novel object detection where large annotated datasets are unavailable.

5. **Foundation for Future Research:** EfficientFSL's modular design (Forward Block, Combine Block, SQ Attention Block) offers a clear framework for further innovation. Future work could explore more advanced query generation mechanisms, alternative adaptive feature fusion strategies, or integrate this "query-only" paradigm with other FSL approaches beyond Prototypical Networks. The principle of interacting with a frozen backbone purely through query generation could also be extended to other vision tasks (e.g., few-shot segmentation or object detection) or even other modalities.

In conclusion, EfficientFSL provides an elegant and highly effective solution to a critical problem at the forefront of AI research. By demonstrating that powerful large models can be leveraged for few-shot learning tasks with exceptional efficiency and accuracy, it paves the way for wider adoption of advanced AI in data-constrained and resource-limited environments, potentially accelerating progress in numerous application domains.
