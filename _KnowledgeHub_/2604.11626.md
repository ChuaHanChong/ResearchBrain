# RationalRewards: Reasoning Rewards Scale Visual Generation Both Training and Test Time

> [!summary] Summary
> RationalRewards introduces a reasoning-based reward model for visual generation that produces structured, multi-dimensional critiques to guide image generation. This framework achieves state-of-the-art preference prediction among open-source models with less training data and consistently improves text-to-image and image-editing generator performance through both reinforcement learning and efficient test-time prompt refinement.

## Problem

- Traditional scalar reward models for visual generation compress complex human judgments into a single score, losing critical nuance and making it difficult to understand generator failures.
- The lack of explicit reasoning in scalar reward models can lead to 'reward hacking' during reinforcement learning, where models inflate scores without genuine quality improvement.
- Acquiring large-scale, human-annotated rationales (detailed critiques) needed to train more sophisticated reasoning-based reward models is prohibitively expensive.

## Method

- The authors developed **RationalRewards**, a ==reward model== that generates multi-dimensional natural language critiques (e.g., Text Faithfulness, Visual Quality) and associated scores for generated images.
- They propose ==Preference-Anchored Rationalization== (**PARROT**), a ==variational framework== that trains **RationalRewards** to generate these critiques by ==distilling knowledge== from a larger 'Teacher' VLM using existing pairwise preference data, without requiring direct rationale annotations.
- **RationalRewards** enables optimization in two ways: providing dense, semantically decomposed rewards for generator ==fine-tuning== (parameter space) and directly guiding ==prompt revisions== for post-generation improvement (prompt space).

## Results

- RationalRewards (**8B** parameters) achieved state-of-the-art preference prediction accuracy among open-source models on MMRB2 (e.g., **64.2** on T2I), GenAI-Bench, and EditReward-Bench, utilizing **10-20x** less training data than comparable baselines.
- As an RL reward signal, RationalRewards consistently improved generator performance, for instance, boosting FLUX.1-dev on UniGenBench++ by **+9.37 points** (from **60.97** to **70.34**) and outperforming scalar reward models across text-to-image and image-editing tasks.
- The test-time 'Generate-Critique-Refine' loop significantly improved image quality, often matching or exceeding RL-tuned performance (e.g., increasing Flux model score from **3.84** to **4.01** on ImgEdit-Bench) with minimal additional computational overhead.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicit, multi-dimensional reasoning provided by a reward model can stabilize reinforcement learning, reduce reward hacking, and consistently enhance visual generator performance more effectively than scalar rewards or generic VLM judges.
> - The PARROT framework efficiently distills pre-trained knowledge from a larger VLM, allowing a smaller reward model to achieve state-of-the-art preference prediction accuracy with significantly less training data.
> - A 'Generate–Critique–Refine' loop, leveraging RationalRewards' critiques for test-time prompt optimization, can unlock latent capabilities in existing generators and yield improvements comparable to or exceeding costly RL fine-tuning without altering model parameters.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of visual generation, particularly text-to-image and image-editing models, has advanced significantly, leading to increasingly photorealistic and instruction-following outputs. A critical component in further improving these generators is the reward model, which evaluates the quality and alignment of generated content with human preferences. Historically, most reward models have functioned as scalar evaluators, compressing complex human judgments into a single, unexplained numerical score. This approach, while providing a clear objective for reinforcement learning (RL) or supervised fine-tuning (SFT), has several limitations. It discards the underlying reasoning behind human preferences, can lead to generators exploiting superficial correlations rather than learning principled evaluation criteria, and is susceptible to "reward hacking" where models achieve high reward scores without genuine quality improvement.

Recent efforts have begun to explore generative reward models and the use of multimodal Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning for improving visual synthesis. However, these often require costly human rationale annotations or lack the explicit calibration to human preferences for effective optimization.

This work addresses these limitations by introducing a reasoning-based reward model that generates structured, multi-dimensional critiques. It positions itself as a transformation of reward models from passive evaluators into active optimization tools, offering both fine-grained feedback for parameter-space optimization (RL) and direct guidance for prompt-space optimization (test-time refinement). The proposed framework for training such a model, Preference-Anchored Rationalization (PARROT), also addresses the challenge of acquiring large-scale rationale annotations by leveraging existing pairwise preference data. This positions RationalRewards as an advancement toward more interpretable, robust, and efficient methods for guiding visual generative models.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a reward model for visual generation that transcends the limitations of traditional scalar reward models by integrating explicit, multi-dimensional reasoning. The authors aim to demonstrate that by teaching reward models to produce detailed critiques before assigning scores, they can be transformed into more effective optimization tools for visual generators.

The motivation behind this objective stems from several identified deficiencies in the current paradigm:
- **Loss of Human Judgment Nuance:** Conventional reward models condense rich human judgments (e.g., perceptual quality, instruction faithfulness, physical plausibility, text rendering) into a single scalar, thereby discarding the nuanced reasoning that underlies preference. This loss of information hinders generators from understanding *why* an output is good or bad.
- **Susceptibility to Reward Hacking:** Without explicit reasoning, scalar reward models can inadvertently encourage generators to exploit superficial cues or biases to inflate their scores, leading to a degradation of actual output quality (as illustrated in Figure 3). This instability limits the reliability of RL-based fine-tuning.
- **Lack of Interpretability and Actionability:** The "black box" nature of scalar reward models offers little insight into specific areas for improvement. This makes debugging generator failures and guiding development challenging.
- **High Cost of Rationale Annotation:** While reasoning-based feedback is desirable, obtaining human-annotated rationales at the scale required for training large models is prohibitively expensive. This creates a practical barrier to developing more sophisticated reward models.

To address these motivations, the authors propose two complementary avenues through which a reasoning-based reward model can improve visual generators:
1. **Parameter Space Optimization:** By providing structured, multi-dimensional critiques, the reward model can offer dense, semantically grounded feedback for reinforcement learning, replacing opaque scalar gradients with interpretable explanations of deficiencies. This is expected to foster more stable and principled learning, mitigating reward hacking.
2. **Prompt Space Optimization:** The explicit critiques can be directly converted into targeted prompt revisions. This "Generate–Critique–Refine" loop allows for post-generation optimization, improving outputs without requiring costly parameter updates to the generator. This approach aims to unlock latent capabilities in existing generators that might be under-elicited by suboptimal initial prompts.

The overarching goal is to achieve state-of-the-art preference prediction and generator improvement while leveraging readily available preference data more efficiently than existing methods.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of the methodology revolves around Preference-Anchored Rationalization (PARROT), a variational training framework designed to teach reward models to produce explicit, multi-dimensional rationales from existing pairwise preference data without requiring direct rationale annotations.

#### 3.1. Variational Framework: The Hindsight-Foresight Decomposition (PARROT)

The authors frame the problem by introducing a latent natural language rationale `z` that explains a human preference `y` for a given input `x` (comprising two images and a user request). The objective is to learn an evaluator reward model, termed the "Student" (`Pθ(z, y|x)`), that can generate both the rationale `z` and predict the preference `y`. This is achieved by maximizing an Evidence Lower Bound (ELBO) on the observed preferences, which naturally decomposes into three phases (Figure 4):

**Phase 1: Rationale Generation (Hindsight - Constructing `qϕ(z|x,y)`)**
- A "Teacher" Vision-Language Model (VLM), specifically Qwen3-VL-32B-Instruct, is used to generate candidate rationales.
- Instead of unguided generation, the Teacher VLM is prompted with "preference anchoring," where the known ground-truth human preference label `y` is provided as a hint. This focuses the Teacher on generating rationales consistent with the observed label, thus inferring the posterior `qϕ(z|x,y)`.
- The rationales are structured as multi-dimensional critiques across four aspects: Text Faithfulness, Image Faithfulness, Physical and Visual Quality, and Text Rendering (Figure 5). Each aspect includes a justification and a float-valued score (1-4 scale).

**Phase 2: Predictive Consistency Filtering (Maximizing `E[log Pθ(y|x,z)]`)**
- To ensure the generated rationales are predictively sufficient and not merely linguistically plausible, a "consistency check" is applied.
- The Teacher VLM is re-queried with the input `x` and the generated rationale `z`, but *without* the original preference label `y`.
- A rationale `z` is retained only if the Teacher can independently recover the original preference `y` based solely on `x` and `z`. This filtering process approximates maximizing the first term of the ELBO, discarding hallucinatory or uninformative rationales. Approximately 72% of rationales survive this check.

**Phase 3: Foresight Learning (Minimizing `DKL(qϕ(z|x,y) || Pθ(z|x))`)**
- A smaller "Student" VLM (Qwen3-VL-Instruct-8B) is trained via supervised fine-tuning (SFT) on the filtered, high-quality rationales.
- The Student learns to generate rationales `Pθ(z|x)` and predict preferences *without* access to the ground-truth preference label `y`, effectively learning to reason proactively.

#### 3.2. Bridging Pairwise Training and Pointwise Deployment

- While PARROT trains on pairwise preference data, downstream applications (RL, prompt refinement) require pointwise feedback.
- A "Pointwise Projection Strategy" is introduced: the Teacher VLM is prompted to assess each image in isolation, using the validated pairwise rationale `z_pair` as a reference hint.
- The Teacher articulates absolute scores (1-4 float scale) for each of the four dimensions, creating a pointwise dataset `D_point`.
- The Student model is jointly trained on both `D_pair` and `D_point` to enable both pairwise and pointwise assessments.

#### 3.3. From Evaluator to Optimizer: Dual-Space Optimization

RationalRewards facilitates optimization in two complementary spaces:

- **Parameter Space (SFT/RL Fine-Tuning):**
 - The multi-dimensional scores provide dense, semantically decomposed reward signals for RL fine-tuning of image generators.
 - The structured rationales offer natural-language explanations for reward assignments, which aids interpretability and is hypothesized to reduce reward hacking (Figure 3, Takeaway 1).
 - For RL, the per-dimension scores are typically aggregated into a scalar reward (e.g., through equal-weight averaging of applicable dimensions) (Appendix E.1).

- **Prompt Space (Test-Time Refinement):**
 - The natural-language rationales identify specific deficiencies in generated images.
 - A "Generate–Critique–Refine" loop (Figure 7) is implemented: RationalRewards critiques an initial generation, identifies concrete issues, and translates them into targeted prompt revisions.
 - If any dimension score falls below a set threshold (e.g., 3.0), the refined prompt is used for re-generation. This approach trades test-time compute for improved fidelity without altering generator parameters (Takeaway 3).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The study presents several key findings across preference modeling and dual-space optimization for visual generation:

#### 4.1. Accuracy in Preference Modeling (Evaluator Performance)

- **State-of-the-art Open-Source Performance:** RationalRewards (8B parameters) achieved state-of-the-art preference prediction accuracy among open-source reward models across three benchmarks: Multimodal Reward Bench 2 (MMRB2), GenAI-Bench, and EditReward-Bench (Table 1).
 - For example, on MMRB2 (T2I), RationalRewards (Qwen3-VL-8B) scored 64.2, surpassing other open-source VLMs like Qwen3-VL-32B (64.1) and specialized reward models like UnifiedReward-7B (59.8).
- **Competitive with Commercial Models:** The 8B RationalRewards model demonstrated performance competitive with commercial models, outperforming Gemini-2.5-Flash and approaching the performance of Gemini-2.5-Pro on preference prediction (e.g., 70.3 on MMRB2 (Edit) vs. Gemini-2.5-Pro at 71.3).
- **Data Efficiency:** The model achieved this performance using significantly less training data (10–20× less) than comparable scalar reward baselines, attributing this efficiency to PARROT's distillation of pre-trained knowledge from the teacher model through structured rationales.
- **PARROT vs. Direct Distillation Ablation:** An ablation study showed that direct SFT distillation from Qwen3-VL-32B-Instruct to the 8B backbone (without PARROT's preference-anchored rationalization) underperformed RationalRewards across all benchmarks (Table 1, "Qwen3-VL-32B-Instruct Distillation"). This indicates that the structured rationalization process is critical to the performance gains, not merely the larger teacher model's knowledge.

#### 4.2. Optimization in Dual Spaces (Generator Improvement)

**Parameter Space Tuning (RL)**
- **Consistent Improvements over Baselines:** When used as an RL reward signal (aggregating multi-dimensional scores into a scalar), RationalRewards consistently improved both text-to-image and image-editing generators beyond scalar reward models and generic VLM judges (Tables 2 and 3).
 - **Text-to-Image (UniGenBench++):** RationalRewards-guided RL lifted FLUX.1-dev from a baseline of 60.97 to 70.34 overall (+9.37 points), significantly outperforming MultiReward (60.12) and the direct Qwen3-VL-32B judge (66.53) (Table 2). Similar improvements were observed for SD-3.5-Medium and Qwen-Image.
 - **Image Editing (ImgEdit-Bench, GEdit-Bench-EN, PICA-Bench):** RationalRewards-guided RL improved Flux.1 Kontext from 3.52 to 3.84 on ImgEdit-Bench, surpassing EditReward-guided RL (3.66) (Table 3).
- **Reduced Reward Hacking and Stable Training:** The multi-dimensional reasoning provided by RationalRewards was observed to mitigate reward hacking, leading to more stable reward growth and sustained generation quality throughout training, in contrast to scalar rewards that could increase while visual quality degraded (Figure 3, 9, 10, 11, 12). The 8B RationalRewards also outperformed the 32B Qwen3-VL-32B model used directly as a judge, reinforcing PARROT's value.

**Prompt Space Tuning (Test-Time Refinement)**
- **Matching or Exceeding RL Performance:** A significant finding was that the Generate–Critique–Refine loop, a purely inference-time intervention requiring no parameter updates, frequently yielded improvements comparable to or exceeding computationally expensive RL fine-tuning (Table 3).
 - On ImgEdit-Bench, prompt tuning with RationalRewards boosted the RL-tuned Flux model from 3.84 to 4.01 overall. For Qwen-Image-Edit, prompt tuning on top of RL achieved the best overall score of 4.43, demonstrating complementarity.
 - On GEdit-Bench-EN Overall, prompt tuning (8.33) slightly surpassed RL alone (8.29) for Qwen-Image-Edit.
- **Efficiency:** This test-time loop added approximately 0.4 seconds of VLM inference overhead per image, a considerably lower computational cost compared to the hundreds of GPU-hours required for RL fine-tuning.
- **Latent Capabilities Hypothesis:** These results suggest that existing generators possess latent capabilities that are often under-elicited by suboptimal prompts, and RationalRewards' critiques can effectively unlock this capacity by providing preference-aware, targeted corrections (Figure 6, Takeaway 3).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The RationalRewards framework presents several significant contributions and potential impacts on the field of visual generation:

- **Paradigm Shift in Reward Modeling:** The work advocates for a shift from opaque scalar reward models to transparent, reasoning-based evaluators. This change moves reward models from passive discriminators to active optimization interfaces, providing not just a score but an explanation of *why* an output is good or bad.
- **Efficient Training of Reasoning Models:** The PARROT framework offers a principled and scalable method for training high-quality reasoning-based reward models from readily available pairwise preference data. This significantly reduces the reliance on costly, hand-annotated rationales, democratizing access to advanced reward modeling techniques.
- **Enhanced Reinforcement Learning:** By providing multi-dimensional, semantically grounded rewards, RationalRewards stabilizes RL-based fine-tuning, mitigates reward hacking, and consistently improves generator performance beyond traditional scalar or generic VLM judges. This can lead to more robust and reliable methods for training visual generative models.
- **Novel Test-Time Optimization:** The "Generate–Critique–Refine" loop introduces an effective post-generation prompt optimization strategy that enhances output quality without requiring any parameter updates to the generator. This provides a flexible and computationally efficient alternative or complement to traditional fine-tuning, applicable to any frozen generator.
- **Unlocking Latent Generator Capabilities:** The finding that test-time prompt tuning can match or exceed RL-based fine-tuning suggests that current generative models may possess unexpressed capabilities that can be unlocked through precise, preference-calibrated linguistic guidance. This opens new avenues for research into understanding and eliciting the full potential of large generative models.
- **Improved Interpretability and Diagnostics:** The explicit critiques from RationalRewards provide valuable diagnostic feedback to both users and developers. This interpretability can facilitate better understanding of model failures, accelerate debugging, and guide the development of more robust and aligned generative systems. As shown in Figure 8 and 13, this can also enable explainable quality control, regional flaw grounding, and dense visual rewards.
- **Broader Applications:** Beyond direct generator optimization, the capability of RationalRewards to produce structured critiques and pinpoint deficiencies can be valuable in various applications, including data curation (by identifying problematic generations for filtering), diagnostic feedback for complex tasks, and potentially for facilitating human-AI collaboration in creative endeavors.
