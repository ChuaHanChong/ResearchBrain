# VAIC: Vision-Guided Humanoid Agile Object Interaction Control via Decoupled Commands

> [!summary] Summary
> The VAIC framework enables humanoid robots to perform agile object interactions using onboard depth vision and proprioception, guided by decoupled high-level commands. It infers unobservable object dynamics and adapts to severe visual occlusion, achieving up to 91.7% success rates in diverse simulated interaction tasks and demonstrating generalization on physical hardware.

## Problem

- Current humanoid controllers often rely on dense, joint-level reference trajectories, which are impractical for real-world autonomy or high-level teleoperation.
- Existing methods assume perfect state observability of objects and environment, which is unattainable in physical deployments due to visual occlusion, sensor noise, and blind spots.
- A "deployment gap" persists, making it challenging to transfer simulated humanoid capabilities to physical robots for complex object interaction tasks.

## Method

- Developed **VAIC**, a two-stage distillation framework that trains a privileged teacher and then a vision-guided student policy.
- Introduced a decoupled command interface (velocity targets and binary interaction phase indicators) to provide high-level user intent without requiring dense reference motions.
- Implemented a recurrent Object Adaptation module that implicitly infers dynamic object states from onboard depth streams, proprioception, and decoupled commands, even under severe visual occlusion.

## Results

- VAIC achieved success rates of **66.7%** for box carrying, **91.7%** for cart interaction, and **83.3%** for skateboard interaction in simulation, consistently outperforming existing baselines.
- The framework demonstrated robustness to variations in object sizes, interaction distances, and initializations, and successfully generalized to unseen dynamics and out-of-distribution commands on real hardware.
- Mechanism analysis revealed the Object Adaptation module accurately tracks object states under severe occlusion, with the binary interaction phase being crucial for preventing temporal drift during dynamic transitions.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decoupling high-level user intent from low-level interaction execution via simple commands effectively bridges the gap between ideal simulation and robust physical deployment.
> - Implicitly inferring unobservable object dynamics and environmental states from noisy onboard perception and proprioception is critical for adaptive, whole-body interaction.
> - A two-stage distillation approach with an asymmetrically conditioned student policy allows learning complex interaction skills without relying on dense reference trajectories or perfect state knowledge during deployment.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Humanoid robots are being developed with the expectation of operating in complex, unstructured human environments, where they must perform tasks involving interactions with objects. These tasks, such as carrying bulky items or manipulating carts, necessitate intricate whole-body coordination, where the dynamics of the interacting object directly influence the robot's balance and contact mechanics.

Current research in humanoid control, particularly advancements leveraging deep reinforcement learning and motion imitation, has expanded the capabilities of humanoid-object interaction. Methods like DeepMimic and AMP have laid groundwork for acquiring robust motor skills through reference-conditioned training and adversarial motion priors, primarily focusing on free-body motion. More recent efforts have extended these to general motion tracking and retargeting for robustness and deployability.

However, a significant "deployment gap" persists when transitioning these simulated capabilities to physical robots. Existing controllers frequently rely on two restrictive assumptions:
1. **Dense Reference Trajectories:** They often require dense, joint-level reference trajectories, which are impractical for real-world autonomy or high-level human teleoperation where only abstract intent (e.g., velocity commands) is available.
2. **Perfect State Observability:** They assume access to ground truth 6D poses of objects and precise terrain geometry. In physical deployments, a robot's onboard vision can be severely occluded by objects it is manipulating, and real-time state estimation is susceptible to sensor noise and blind spots.

Previous works in humanoid-object interaction (e.g., HAIC, PhysHSI, VisualMimic) have demonstrated agile interactions by modeling coupled dynamics or using vision for tasks like locomotion, but often still assume object states are readily available or use perception primarily for static environment navigation. VAIC directly addresses these limitations by introducing a framework that operates on onboard depth vision and proprioception, without requiring dense reference trajectories or perfect state knowledge during deployment. This work positions itself as an advancement in perceptive humanoid control, aiming to infer latent object dynamics for adaptive, whole-body interaction based on user-friendly commands.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a unified control framework that enables humanoid robots to execute diverse and agile interactions with objects in unstructured environments. The authors identify and explicitly aim to overcome two fundamental limitations that hinder the practical deployment of current humanoid controllers:

1. **Eliminating Reliance on Dense Reference Trajectories:** Current state-of-the-art controllers often require dense, joint-level reference motions to guide the robot's actions. This "kinematic hand-holding" is impractical for real-world applications where human operators typically provide high-level, sparse commands (e.g., desired velocity or task phases). The motivation is to shift from low-level kinematic tracking to a high-level, intent-driven command interface that is more intuitive for human operators and suitable for autonomous task execution.
2. **Addressing Imperfect State Observability:** Existing methods frequently assume perfect knowledge of object states, such as precise 6D poses and environmental geometry. In real-world scenarios, onboard sensors face challenges like visual occlusion (e.g., an object being carried blocking the robot's view), sensor noise, and blind spots, making perfect state estimation unattainable. The motivation is to enable the robot to implicitly infer or reconstruct critical unobservable object dynamics and environmental states solely from its onboard perception (depth streams) and proprioceptive history.

To achieve these objectives, the authors propose a core insight: to decouple high-level user intent from low-level interaction execution. This decoupling is intended to bridge the gap between oracle-level simulation performance and robust, perceptive physical deployment. The research is specifically motivated by the need for a single, generalized policy that can adapt to radically different dynamic interaction profiles (e.g., carrying, pushing, balancing) without requiring task-specific retraining. The goal is to advance autonomous humanoid deployment by developing a control system that is robust to typical real-world complexities such as visual occlusion and variations in object properties or environmental conditions.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The Vision Guided Agile Interaction Control (VAIC) framework employs a two-stage distillation paradigm designed to enable vision-guided control using onboard depth and a decoupled user command interface.

#### 3.1. Decoupled Command Interface

The framework introduces a user-friendly command interface, `u_t = (v_t, c_t)`, which distills high-level task intent into two components:
- `v_t ∈ R^3`: A multi-axis velocity target `[v_x, v_y, v_yaw]` representing the robot's navigational intent.
- `c_t ∈ {0, 1}`: A per-frame binary indicator specifying the manipulation phase (0 for navigation/approach, 1 for active contact/interaction).
These commands are extracted from a dataset of human-object interaction demonstrations, which are retargeted to the humanoid robot and refined in a physics-based simulation.

#### 3.2. Stage 1: Teacher Training and Student Warm-up

This initial stage focuses on training a privileged teacher policy and concurrently warming up the student modules through distillation.
- **Teacher Policy Training:** A privileged teacher policy is trained using Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) in an oracle simulation setting. The teacher has access to precise ground-truth information: exact object kinematics (`s_obj_t`), terrain geometry (`s_env_t`), dense reference motions (`s_ref_t`), and noise-free proprioceptive states (`s_prop_t`). A Privilege Encoder `f_enc_priv` compresses this multimodal context into a dense privileged latent `z_priv_t`, which, along with proprioception, informs the Teacher Actor to output optimal joint actions `a_tea_t`. The depth camera is intentionally disabled during this stage, with visual input zero-injected to ensure the teacher focuses purely on mastering physical dynamics and to maintain dimensional consistency for the student.
- **Concurrent Distillation:** During the teacher's PPO exploration, its rollout data is used to warm up the deployable student modules via supervised learning:
 - **Object Adapter:** Trained with `L_obj = ||s_hat_obj_t - s_obj_t||^2` to implicitly infer object dynamics.
 - **Privilege Adapter:** Trained with `L_priv = ||z_hat_priv_t - z_priv_t||^2` to align latent representations.
 - **Student Actor:** Undergoes behavior cloning (`L_actor = ||a_stu_t - a_tea_t||^2`).
Crucially, the student actor is conditioned only on the decoupled commands `(v_t, c_t)`, not the dense reference kinematics, establishing an asymmetric input design.

#### 3.3. Stage 2: Vision-Guided Student Exploration

In this stage, the student policy actively explores the environment, and the reliance on dense reference motion and ground-truth object states is removed.
- **Student Actor Control:** The Student Actor takes full control using PPO, optimizing for task-completion and command-tracking rewards.
- **Online Adaptation of Modules:** The Object Adapter and Privilege Adapter continue to be updated online via supervised learning using the exact states available from simulation rollouts generated by the student's exploration. This coupling helps prevent representation drift.
- **Object Adapter Functionality:** A CNN-GRU Depth Encoder `f_enc_depth` processes the noisy onboard depth stream `d_t` to extract temporal features `z_depth_t`. The Object Adapter `f_obj_adapt` then implicitly infers the dynamic object state `s_hat_obj_t` from `z_depth_t`, proprioception `s_prop_t`, and the decoupled commands `(v_t, c_t)`. The binary indicator `c_t` is critical for mode-switching, helping the recurrent network anticipate changes in coupled dynamics during interaction transitions.
- **Privilege Adapter Functionality:** Using the inferred `s_hat_obj_t` and a nominal object template `P`, the Privilege Adapter `f_priv_adapt` projects spatial occupancy priors to reconstruct the privileged latent `z_hat_priv_t`, conditioned on `s_prop_t`, the transformed geometric feature `s_hat_geo_t = T(s_hat_obj_t, P)`, and the decoupled commands `(v_t, c_t)`.

#### 3.4. Experimental Setup

- **Hardware:** Humanoid robot equipped with an Intel RealSense D435i camera.
- **Control Loop:** Policy at 50 Hz, low-level joint controller at 200 Hz, depth observations at 60 Hz.
- **Data:** Human demonstrations motion-captured, retargeted to the robot, and refined in a physics-based simulation.
- **Training Environment:** Isaac Sim, NVIDIA RTX 4090 GPU. Stage 1 (Teacher): 4096 parallel environments, ~5 hours. Stage 2 (Student): 512 environments with real-time camera rendering, ~15 hours.
- **Domain Randomization:** Extensive randomization of robot dynamics, actuation, object interaction surfaces, object masses/scales, and external perturbations are applied in simulation to enhance sim-to-real transfer.
- **Depth Processing:** A rigorous pipeline includes extrinsic/intrinsic camera noise, random frame delay, measurement noise, episodic bias drift, pixel dropout, and depth quantization to mimic real-world sensor imperfections.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents quantitative and qualitative evaluations of the VAIC framework across diverse and challenging object interaction tasks, demonstrating its effectiveness and robustness in both simulation and real-world deployment.

#### 4.1. Quantitative Evaluation in Simulation

The framework was evaluated on three categories of whole-body interaction: Box Carrying, Cart Interaction, and Skateboard Interaction, against baselines including PPO, AMP, PhysHSI, and VisualMimic. Metrics included Success Rate (SR), Root State Metrics (position, orientation, linear/angular velocity, linear acceleration errors), and Object State Metrics (corresponding errors for the interactive object).

- **Box Interaction (Table 1):** VAIC achieved a success rate of 66.7%, significantly outperforming AMP (50.0%) and PPO (0.0%). It demonstrated lower root state errors (e.g., E_rpe of 0.59 compared to AMP's 0.78) and object state errors (e.g., E_ope of 0.62 compared to AMP's 1.01). This performance is attributed to VAIC's depth-based state prediction capabilities, which effectively mitigate severe visual occlusion caused by the carried box.
- **Cart Interaction (Table 2):** VAIC achieved a success rate of 91.7%, substantially higher than AMP (58.3%) and PPO (41.7%). It exhibited reduced root and object state errors across most metrics (e.g., E_rpe of 0.45 compared to AMP's 0.61; E_ope of 0.61 compared to AMP's 0.76). The system's ability to anticipate the physical feedback of the underactuated cart contributed to its superior performance.
- **Skateboard Interaction (Table 3):** VAIC achieved an 83.3% success rate, a considerable improvement over AMP (12.5%) and PPO (0.0%). It maintained lower tracking errors for both root and object states (e.g., E_rpe of 0.62 compared to AMP's 1.30; E_ope of 0.62 compared to AMP's 1.20). This demonstrates VAIC's capacity to handle impulsive contact forces and high-speed dynamics.

#### 4.2. Robustness to Spatial Initialization and Object Variations

VAIC's robustness was further assessed on the Box Carry task under varying initial conditions (object sizes and interaction distances).
- **Box Carry with Variations (Table 4):** VAIC consistently achieved high success rates (10/10 for Small-Near, Small-Far, Large-Near scenarios, and 7/10 for Large-Far). This generally surpassed PhysHSI and VisualMimic, which struggled with certain modified initializations (e.g., PhysHSI 8/10 for Large-Near). This indicates that VAIC implicitly develops an understanding of coupled dynamics.

#### 4.3. Generalization to Unseen Dynamics in Real-World Deployment

A significant finding is VAIC's generalization capability in real-world hardware deployments (Figure 3).
- The deployed VAIC policy successfully adapted to out-of-distribution velocity commands, unmodeled payload weights, varying interaction sides, and different box sizes.
- The Object Adaptation module implicitly inferred these altered physical relationships from historical data, enabling the humanoid robot to dynamically adjust its center of gravity and grasping postures to maintain stable interactions on the physical hardware.

#### 4.4. Mechanism Analysis

An analysis of the Object Adaptation module (Figure 4) revealed that its predicted object states (`s_hat_obj_t`) closely tracked the MuJoCo ground truth. This occurred even during conditions of severe self-occlusion (e.g., box carrying over varied terrains) and dynamic phases (e.g., cart pulling, skateboarding). The study highlights that conditioning the estimator on the binary interaction phase `c_t` was crucial in preventing temporal drift in representation, particularly during high-speed dynamic transitions.

#### 4.5. Ablation Study (Appendix E)

An ablation study on the Skateboard Interaction task demonstrated the importance of VAIC's components:
- **`w/o Depth`:** Removing depth input led to a catastrophic success rate drop (8.3%), indicating the necessity of exteroceptive perception for anticipating contact geometry.
- **`w/o Object Adapter`:** Disabling this module resulted in 0.0% success rate and significant divergence in object tracking, confirming its role in implicit state inference.
- **`w/o GRU`:** Removing the temporal recurrent unit reduced success rate (33.3%) and increased root position error, highlighting the importance of temporal context for maintaining a consistent belief of object dynamics.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The VAIC framework represents a notable advancement in the field of humanoid robotics, particularly in the domain of agile object interaction within unstructured environments. Its significance and potential impact stem from several key contributions:

- **Bridging the Deployment Gap:** The work directly addresses critical limitations that have historically hindered the real-world deployment of humanoid robots: the reliance on dense reference trajectories and perfect state observability. By eliminating these restrictive requirements, VAIC offers a more practical and scalable approach to robot control.
- **Intuitive and Decoupled Control:** The introduction of a high-level, decoupled command interface (velocity targets and interaction phase indicators) simplifies robot control. This allows for more intuitive teleoperation and facilitates autonomous navigation based on high-level intent rather than laborious, joint-level programming or tracking, which can reduce the operational complexity of humanoids.
- **Enhanced Robustness through Vision-Guided Adaptation:** The recurrent Object Adaptation module, operating on onboard depth data and proprioceptive history, enables the robot to implicitly infer unobservable object dynamics and adapt to severe visual occlusion. This capability enhances the robot's robustness to real-world uncertainties, such as variations in object attributes (size, weight), unmodeled dynamics, and changing environmental conditions.
- **Unified and Generalizable Policy:** The framework demonstrates that a single VAIC policy can successfully generalize across diverse, dynamic interaction tasks—including object carrying, cart manipulation, and skateboarding—without requiring task-specific retraining. This unified approach reduces development overhead and paves the way for more versatile humanoid capabilities.
- **Advancement in Perceptive Humanoid Control:** VAIC advances the integration of perception into whole-body control by using vision not merely for static environment understanding or obstacle avoidance, but for continuously inferring the dynamic state and physical properties of interacting objects. This is a crucial step towards more intelligent and adaptive physical interactions.
- **Practical Paradigm for Autonomous Deployment:** The extensive quantitative evaluations and successful real-world hardware deployments provide empirical evidence for the framework's robustness and adaptability. This research contributes a practical paradigm for the autonomous deployment of humanoids in complex, human-centric environments, moving closer to the vision of general-purpose humanoid assistants.

Despite its advancements, the authors acknowledge limitations that point to future research directions, such as susceptibility to sensor noise with transparent or reflective objects, vulnerability to extreme unmodeled dynamic variations, and the need for richer command spaces and tactile feedback to achieve fine-grained in-hand manipulation. Addressing these could further expand the impact of such vision-guided control systems.
