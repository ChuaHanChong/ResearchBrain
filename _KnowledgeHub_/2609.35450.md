# Uni-VLaT: Whole-Body Tactile Adaptation of VLA Policies for Humanoid Loco-Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> Uni-VLaT adapts pretrained VLA policies (Isaac-GR00T, π0.5) to whole-body tactile sensing on a Unitree G1 humanoid. A spatial-temporal tactile encoder feeds tokens into the action DiT, and the contextualized tactile tokens are trained to predict future tactile, proprioceptive, and visual latents. Across five real-robot loco-manipulation tasks it reaches 75% average success, 43 points above no-tactile and 7 above tactile input without prediction.

## Problem

- Pretrained VLA and humanoid loco-manipulation policies perceive interaction only through vision and proprioception, so occluded or sustained contact is regulated implicitly, risking insufficient or excessive contact.
- Prior whole-body tactile humanoid systems such as TACT, WT-UMI and HTD rely on dedicated architectures, leaving unclear how distributed touch can augment pretrained generalist policies and their priors.
- Where predictive supervision exists, it targets only future tactile or force signals, ignoring how contact co-evolves with body state and the visual scene.

## Method

- Spatial-temporal tactile encoder: per-region MLPs over eight body regions, eight learned-query ==cross-attention== tokens, and a ==temporal Transformer== over a four-frame history, gated into the action expert's ==DiT== trunk.
- Tactile-anchored multimodal prediction: mean-pooled post-DiT tactile tokens feed three MLP predictors forecasting absolute future tactile, proprioceptive, and visual latents four steps ahead against ==EMA== or frozen target encoders.
- Joint objective adds cosine-plus-SmoothL1 predictive losses to ==flow-matching== action loss; the VLM stays frozen, predictive heads are dropped at deployment, and actions are 64-d SONIC latent motion tokens.

## Results

- On Isaac-GR00T, average success rises from **32%** (no tactile) to **68%** (tactile input), **69%** (tactile prediction only), and **75%** (Uni-VLaT); Basket Loading improves **65%** to **80%**.
- Transfers across backbones: Table Sweeping gains **30 points** on both GR00T and π0.5, Back-Tap Walking goes **0%** to **85-90%**, Composed Cleanup stage score 43.3 to 70.0 and 26.7 to 46.7.
- Ablations on GR00T: predicting from pre-DiT tactile drops average success to **55%**, multimodal-input prediction to **60%**, and delta future targets to **35%**, versus **80%** for the full model.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Auxiliary future prediction should read from a modality's contextualized tokens after fusion, not raw inputs; direct multimodal prediction invites modality-specific shortcuts and sharply hurt sweeping success.
> - Predict absolute future latents, not deltas: small latent differences drown in noise, while absolute targets preserve persistent contact state, a caution for any JEPA-style auxiliary loss.
> - Touch-as-instruction tasks need only tactile input; predictive supervision pays off when contact must be interpreted alongside evolving body state and scene, such as load-adaptive holding.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Overview

A humanoid robot sweeping objects across a table depends on contact that a camera may not fully reveal. Vision can miss occluded contact. Without direct contact feedback, a controller may regulate interaction only indirectly, which makes sustained contact hard to stabilize. Uni-VLaT gives a pretrained policy whole-body touch. During training, it asks the policy to connect that touch with what its body and the scene will look like next. Across five real-robot contact tasks, the full approach does better on average than a policy denied tactile input.

Table Sweeping: contact-guided object sweeping.

### 2. Give touch a job

A VLA policy is a pretrained vision-language-action model with reusable visual, language, and action priors. Uni-VLaT adapts that policy rather than training a separate controller from scratch.

The method works in four steps:

- Tactile measurements are distributed over eight body regions.
- A causal four-frame history is encoded, so the representation can reflect contact onset, continuation, or release.
- Tactile tokens enter the pretrained policy with body-state and action representations, while visual and language features supply scene and task context.
- The resulting contextualized tactile features are pooled into a *tactile-anchored context*.

During training, modality-specific prediction heads use this context to predict four future latent representations in each of three streams: tactile, proprioceptive (the robot's body state), and visual. These are latent predictions, not reconstructions of raw observations. They supplement action learning, and the predictive heads and target encoders are omitted at deployment.

The change does not reduce generated tokens, attempts, elapsed time, or measured computation. It is an additional training signal for learning how physical interaction relates to body response and scene change.

Overview of Uni-VLaT (paper Fig. 2). (a) Deployment: for the instruction "sweep the desk from right to left," vision and language enter a pretrained VLM, tactile input is encoded, and state and action tokens enter the action expert, whose action tokens go through an action decoder to the robot. (b) Training only: a pooled tactile-anchored context feeds separate state, tactile, and vision predictors. Their outputs are compared with future representations from corresponding target encoders over four steps.

### 3. Touch with and without prediction

The evaluation uses five Unitree G1 tasks: Back-Tap Walking, Table Sweeping, Basket Loading, Human–Robot Hugging, and Composed Cleanup. Task success rate (SR) is the share of evaluated rollouts that meet that task's success criteria. The average SR is the mean of the five task-level rates. Main-table Isaac-GR00T configurations use 20 rollouts per task.

Three variants are compared, and all retain the same action-learning objective:

- **No Tactile** receives vision, language, and proprioception, but no tactile input or auxiliary prediction.
- **Tactile w/o Pred.** has the same tactile pathway but uses action-only training.
- **Uni-VLaT** adds joint future tactile, proprioceptive, and visual prediction.

Average SR is 75% for Uni-VLaT, 68% for Tactile w/o Pred., and 32% for No Tactile. The 7-point gain supports an added benefit from the full predictive-training package beyond tactile input alone. It does not establish which predictive target is solely responsible.

On Table Sweeping, Uni-VLaT raises SR from 60% to 75%. Success there requires sweeping all five objects across the tabletop center line within 60 seconds, and a dropped object counts as failure.

Back-Tap Walking shows a different pattern. A person's back contact signals "walk," and release signals "stop." No Tactile gets 0%, while tactile-enabled variants get 85–90%. The tactile-prediction-only variant gets 90%, versus 85% for Uni-VLaT. This supports the authors' distinction between touch used as an instruction and contact that must be interpreted as it evolves. It is not evidence that predictive supervision improves every task.

### 4. What the ablation isolates

The ablations test choices about the prediction context and target. The full model predicts all three future modalities from contextualized, post-DiT tactile features. The alternatives each change one choice:

- **Multimodal-Input Prediction** predicts from visual, tactile, and proprioceptive inputs.
- **Pre-DiT Tactile Prediction** uses pre-DiT tactile features as the source.
- **Delta Prediction Targets** predicts future-minus-current latent differences.

On Table Sweeping and Back-Tap Walking, the averages are 80%, 60%, 55%, and 35%, respectively. These results come from different rollout counts: full Uni-VLaT reuses 20-rollout results, while the other variants use 10. The pattern therefore supports the design, but it is not a definitive causal decomposition. The authors interpret absolute targets as preserving persistent contact and body-state information.

Ablation results with Isaac-GR00T (paper Table II): success rates in percent on Sweeping and Back-Tap, with averages, for Full Uni-VLaT and three variants.

### 5. Touch as context

In this design, training asks the policy to relate contact to future body-state and visual representations. At deployment, the resulting tactile-conditioned policy acts without the prediction heads. The evidence comes from five representative tasks on the Unitree G1. It does not show broad transfer to other robots or a measured safety improvement. The Diffusion Policy baseline did not enter physical rollout evaluation, so its SR is N/A, not zero.
