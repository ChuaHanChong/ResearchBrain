# Principled Fast and Meta Knowledge Learners for Continual Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from the University of Alberta and collaborators developed FAME, a dual-learner framework for Continual Reinforcement Learning, which introduces formal definitions for MDP distance and catastrophic forgetting. Inspired by human hippocampal-cortical memory, FAME employs a fast learner for rapid adaptation and a meta learner for knowledge integration, outperforming baselines in average performance, forward transfer, and forgetting across pixel-based and continuous control tasks.

## Problem

- Traditional deep reinforcement learning algorithms suffer from catastrophic forgetting when learning new tasks sequentially in continually evolving environments.
- Existing Continual Reinforcement Learning (CRL) methods often lack a unified theoretical foundation, relying on heuristics without clear optimization objectives for knowledge transfer and mitigating forgetting.
- Challenges include negative transfer, where prior knowledge hinders new task learning, and scalability issues with model expansion methods storing ever-growing past models.

## Method

- The study establishes new theoretical foundations for CRL, defining ==quantitative measures== for MDP difference (environment similarity) and catastrophic forgetting (performance degradation on past tasks).
- It proposes FAME (Fast And Meta Knowledge Learners), a dual-learner framework inspired by human hippocampal-cortical interactions, featuring a fast learner for rapid task acquisition and a meta learner for robust knowledge integration.
- The meta learner minimizes catastrophic forgetting through incremental updates (e.g., policy distillation using KL divergence or Wasserstein distance), while the fast learner employs an adaptive meta warm-up strategy to prevent negative transfer.

## Results

- FAME consistently achieved superior average performance, higher forward transfer, and lower forgetting compared to various baseline methods across pixel-based (MinAtar, Atari) and continuous control (Meta-World) benchmarks.
- The adaptive meta warm-up mechanism demonstrated its effectiveness by selecting the meta-learner with **95.1%** probability for revisited MinAtar environments and random initialization with **70.2%** for novel tasks, preventing negative transfer.
- FAME-WD (using Wasserstein distance) sometimes slightly outperformed FAME-KL (using KL divergence) in average performance and forgetting on Meta-World, suggesting the potential benefit of capturing richer distributional information for knowledge integration.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A principled approach to Continual RL benefits from formal definitions of MDP similarity and catastrophic forgetting, enabling clearer optimization targets for algorithmic design and analysis.
> - A dual-learner architecture, inspired by the human brain's memory system, effectively addresses the trade-off between plasticity (rapid adaptation to new tasks) and stability (retention of old knowledge).
> - Adaptive knowledge transfer mechanisms, such as intelligently selecting initial policies (e.g., from a meta-learner or randomly), are crucial to leverage prior knowledge effectively and avoid negative transfer in sequential learning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Traditional deep reinforcement learning (DRL) algorithms are typically designed for single tasks within stationary environments. This contrasts with human learning, which is characterized by the continuous acquisition of new skills across diverse and evolving scenarios. The field of Continual Reinforcement Learning (CRL) aims to bridge this gap, enabling agents to sequentially learn new tasks without catastrophically forgetting previously acquired knowledge. This necessitates balancing *plasticity* (rapid adaptation to new tasks) and *stability* (retention of old knowledge).

The existing landscape of CRL research comprises various strategies, including replay-based methods, regularization techniques, and model expansion approaches. However, the paper identifies a fragmentation within these efforts, noting that many algorithms are motivated by heuristics or developed from distinct perspectives, lacking a unified theoretical foundation. This deficiency hinders a principled understanding of when and why knowledge transfer is beneficial, how to effectively mitigate forgetting, and how to define explicit optimization objectives. Issues such as "negative transfer," where prior knowledge interferes with learning new tasks, and the scalability challenges of storing and utilizing an ever-growing pool of past models in model expansion methods, further underscore the need for more robust and theoretically grounded solutions.

This work positions itself to address these challenges by providing a new theoretical foundation for CRL through quantitative measures of environment similarity and catastrophic forgetting. It proposes a dual-learner framework inspired by the human learning and memory system, specifically the interplay between the hippocampus and cerebral cortex, to integrate complementary objectives of knowledge transfer and integration. By doing so, it aims to offer a more principled approach to understanding and developing adaptive continual learning agents, establishing deeper connections with transfer learning and multi-task learning paradigms.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a principled and effective framework for Continual Reinforcement Learning that enables agents to continually acquire new knowledge efficiently while mitigating catastrophic forgetting of previously learned skills. This overarching goal is broken down into several specific objectives, driven by identified limitations in the current CRL landscape:

1. **Establish New Foundations for Continual RL:** A core motivation is the observed lack of a theoretical foundation in much of existing CRL research. The authors aim to introduce formal definitions that underpin algorithmic innovations. Specifically, this involves:
 - **Quantifying MDP Difference:** To formally assess environment similarity, which is crucial for determining the potential benefits or harms of knowledge transfer, and the degree to which new tasks might interfere with old ones.
 - **Quantifying Catastrophic Forgetting:** To provide a measurable metric for knowledge retention across sequential tasks, applicable to both value- and policy-based RL, thereby offering a clearer optimization objective for mitigating forgetting.

2. **Propose a Dual-Learner Framework:** Inspired by the human hippocampal-cortical memory system, the study aims to design an architectural solution that decomposes the complex problem of continual RL into two complementary functions:
 - **Knowledge Transfer (Fast Learner):** To enable rapid adaptation to new environments by leveraging relevant prior knowledge. The motivation here is to overcome issues like "negative transfer" (where inappropriate prior knowledge hinders learning) and inefficient "finetuning" or "resetting" strategies.
 - **Knowledge Integration (Meta Learner):** To consolidate new experiences into a robust, generalizable, and stable knowledge base, ensuring the retention of past skills. This is motivated by the need to explicitly minimize catastrophic forgetting, which current multi-task RL approaches often fail to address directly.

3. **Validate the Framework Empirically:** The research aims to demonstrate the practical efficacy of the proposed dual-learner system across a variety of reinforcement learning tasks, encompassing different environment types (pixel-based, continuous control) and algorithmic paradigms (value-based, policy-based RL). This empirical validation is motivated by the need to show that the principled approach translates into superior performance in terms of learning efficiency, knowledge retention, and adaptation capabilities compared to existing baselines.

In essence, the motivation stems from the recognition that current DRL struggles with sequential learning, and existing CRL methods, while showing progress, lack a unified, principled approach to simultaneously addressing plasticity and stability. By drawing inspiration from cognitive neuroscience and providing formal definitions, the authors seek to develop a more robust, scalable, and biologically plausible continual learning agent.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology, termed FAME (Fast And Meta Knowledge Learners), is a dual-learner framework designed to address continual reinforcement learning by focusing on knowledge transfer and knowledge integration, inspired by the hippocampal-cortical interactions in the human brain.

#### 3.1. New Continual RL Foundations

The study first establishes two fundamental concepts for principled CRL:

- **Foundation 1: MDP Distance:** This defines a quantitative measure of similarity between two Markov Decision Processes (MDPs). It utilizes the distance between their optimal Q-functions ($Q^*$) or optimal policies ($\pi^*$), denoted as $d_Q(Q^*_1, Q^*_2)$ and $d_\pi(\pi^*_1, \pi^*_2)$, respectively. This distance can be computed using divergences or distances such as the $\ell_2$ loss or KL divergence.
- **Foundation 2: Catastrophic Forgetting (CF):** Grounded in the MDP distance, CF is defined to measure the degradation of performance on a previously learned task after learning a new one. For Q-functions, $CF(Q_{k-1}, Q_k) = \sum_{s,a} \mu^{Q_{k-1}}_{k-1}(s)\pi^{Q_{k-1}}(a|s)d_Q(Q_{k-1}(s,a), Q_k(s,a))$, and similarly for policies. Crucially, the weighting is based on the state-action visitation distribution of the *preceding* policy ($\pi_{k-1}$ or $\pi^{Q_{k-1}}$), ensuring that changes in previously important state-action pairs are captured, even if the new policy no longer visits them.

#### 3.2. FAME Dual-Learner Framework

The FAME framework comprises two components: a fast learner and a meta learner, each with distinct but complementary roles:

- **Fast Learner (Analogous to Hippocampus):** This component is responsible for rapidly acquiring knowledge from a new task. It leverages prior knowledge from the meta learner to facilitate quick adaptation.
- **Meta Learner (Analogous to Neocortex):** This component ensures knowledge integration by incrementally consolidating new experiences into the existing knowledge pool. Its primary objective is to minimize catastrophic forgetting.

The two learners are coupled through a continuous interplay of knowledge transfer and knowledge integration.

#### 3.3. Knowledge Integration (Meta Learner)

The meta learner's role is to incrementally integrate new experiences while explicitly minimizing catastrophic forgetting.

- **Q-Value-based Continual RL (Discrete Action Spaces):**
 - The objective is to find an optimal meta Q-function $Q^M_k$ that minimizes the sum of squared differences between $Q^M_k$ and all past fast learners $Q_i$, weighted by their respective state-action visitation distributions.
 - To overcome the limitations of Q-value scales across varied environments, a policy-based CF minimization is preferred. Q-values are converted into Softmax (Boltzmann) policies.
 - **Incremental Softmax Meta Learner Update (Proposition 1):** By employing KL divergence for the policy distance, the meta learner update for $Q^M_k$ simplifies to a Maximum Likelihood Estimator (MLE) problem. It seeks to fit $Q^M_k$ to a mixture of state-action distributions across all encountered environments, in principle updating incrementally based on $Q^M_{k-1}$ and $Q_k$.
 - **Meta Buffer (M):** To make this scalable, a small subset of state-action pairs (e.g., last N steps) from the fast learner's training in each environment is stored in a fixed-size meta buffer to approximate the state-action distributions $w_Q_i$.

- **Policy-based Continual RL (Continuous Action Spaces):**
 - The meta learner directly minimizes the policy-based catastrophic forgetting defined in Foundation 2.
 - **FAME-KL (Policy Distillation under Forward KL Divergence):** Analogous to the value-based case, minimizing CF under KL divergence leads to an MLE-like objective (Eq. 7), fitting the meta policy to a mixture of past policies.
 - **FAME-WD (Wasserstein Distance-based Knowledge Integration):** When policy functions are represented as (multivariate) Gaussian distributions, minimizing CF under the squared 2-Wasserstein distance yields a closed-form incremental update rule (Proposition 2). This allows for efficient integration by updating the mean and variance parameters of the meta policy, potentially capturing more complex distributional discrepancies than KL divergence.

#### 3.4. Knowledge Transfer (Fast Learner)

To facilitate rapid adaptation and circumvent negative transfer, an adaptive meta warm-up mechanism is introduced.

- **Adaptive Meta Warm-Up via One-vs-all Hypothesis Test:** When a new task $k$ arrives, the fast learner $Q_k$ (or $\pi_k$) is initialized by selecting the most effective warm-up strategy from three candidates: the preceding fast learner ($Q_{k-1}$ or $\pi_{k-1}$), the meta learner ($Q^M_{k-1}$ or $\pi^M_{k-1}$), or a random learner ($Q_0$ or $\pi_0$). This selection is performed via a one-vs-all hypothesis test based on policy evaluation (empirical returns) during an early interaction phase (L steps) with the new environment (Eq. 6).
- **Implementation Strategies:**
 - For **value-based RL:** If the meta learner is selected, directly initializing $Q_k$ from a policy $\pi^M_{k-1}$ is challenging. Instead, a Behavior Cloning (BC) regularization term is added to the fast learner's loss function in the early training phase, where $\pi^M_{k-1}$ serves as the expert.
 - For **policy-based RL:** If the meta learner or preceding fast learner is selected, direct parameter initialization of the fast policy $\pi_k$ is performed.

#### 3.5. Algorithmic Flow

The overall FAME algorithm (e.g., Algorithm 1 for value-based RL) proceeds sequentially for each task $k$:

1. **Knowledge Transfer:** The fast learner is initialized using the adaptive meta warm-up strategy based on policy evaluations during the initial L steps.
2. **Fast Learner Training:** The fast learner trains on the current task's environment, collecting experiences in its fast buffer (F). During the last N steps, a subset of state-action pairs (or states for FAME-WD) is also stored in the meta buffer (M).
3. **Knowledge Integration:** After the fast learner completes training, its fast buffer is reset. The meta learner is then updated using the data accumulated in the meta buffer from all previous tasks, according to the catastrophic forgetting minimization principle (Eq. 5, 7, or 8).

This cyclical interaction ensures that the fast learner rapidly adapts to new tasks while the meta learner robustly consolidates cumulative knowledge, balancing plasticity and stability.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The study conducted experiments across pixel-based (MinAtar, Atari) and continuous control (Meta-World) benchmarks to evaluate FAME's performance using standard metrics: Average Performance, Forward Transfer (plasticity), and Forgetting (stability).

#### 4.1. Pixel-based Environments (MinAtar and Atari Games - Discrete Actions)

- **MinAtar:** FAME consistently outperformed baseline methods (DQN-Reset, Finetune, MultiHead, LargeBuffer, PT-DQN) across all evaluation metrics.
 - **Average Performance:** FAME achieved the highest average performance and exhibited notable stability with minimal variations.
 - **Forward Transfer:** FAME demonstrated superior forward transfer, indicating effective knowledge utilization for faster learning in new tasks. LargeBuffer also showed comparable forward transfer, likely due to its extensive replay mechanism.
 - **Forgetting:** FAME exhibited lower forgetting, demonstrating better knowledge retention.
 - **Adaptive Meta Warm-Up Effectiveness:** Analysis of warm-up selection ratios revealed that for previously encountered environments, the meta warm-up was selected with a high probability (95.1%). When facing novel tasks, random initialization was more frequently chosen (70.2%), indicating the adaptiveness of the mechanism in avoiding negative transfer.

- **Atari Games (SpaceInvaders-v5 and Freeway-v5):** FAME (using PPO) also demonstrated superior average performance and forward transfer compared to baselines (PPO-Reset, Finetune, ProgressiveNet, PackNet). Finetune showed competitive forward transfer in SpaceInvaders, which the authors attributed to the underlying environmental dynamics and objectives being similar across the distinct playing modes.

#### 4.2. Robotic Manipulation Tasks (Meta-World - Continuous Actions)

Experiments on Meta-World, utilizing SAC as the base RL algorithm, compared FAME-KL and FAME-WD against Reset, Finetune, Average, PackNet, ProgressiveNet, and CompoNet.

- **Overall Performance:** Both FAME-KL and FAME-WD significantly outperformed most baselines in terms of average performance and forward transfer.
 - **Average Performance and Forgetting:** FAME-KL and FAME-WD achieved the highest average performance and near-minimal forgetting among methods not relying on task-specific model expansion (like PackNet, ProgressiveNet, CompoNet which store past model parameters and have zero forgetting by design). This indicates the meta learner's effectiveness in knowledge consolidation.
 - **Forward Transfer:** The superior forward transfer of FAME variants indicated that the adaptive meta warm-up successfully guided the fast learner to adapt to new environments using prior meta-learner knowledge.
 - **FAME-WD vs. FAME-KL:** While both FAME variants performed well, FAME-WD (Wasserstein distance) sometimes showed a slight edge in average performance and forgetting, while FAME-KL (KL divergence) occasionally exhibited higher forward transfer. The paper suggests that Wasserstein distance's ability to capture underlying data geometry might be beneficial in environments with high distributional shift.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies (MinAtar)

Ablation studies on MinAtar explored the impact of key hyperparameters:

- **Regularization Hyperparameter ($\lambda$) for Behavior Cloning:** Identified a balanced range for $\lambda$ (e.g., 1.0 or 5.0) that yielded favorable trade-offs between forward transfer and forgetting.
- **Warm-Up Step (L) for BC Regularization:** Showed that an appropriate duration for BC regularization (e.g., $5 \times 10^4$ steps) improved FT and average performance on some games without unduly worsening overall forgetting, while prolonged regularization could hinder adaptation.
- **Weight Estimation Step (N) for Meta Buffer:** Demonstrated that increasing N (i.e., collecting more data for meta learner integration) generally improved performance across all metrics, suggesting a trade-off with computational and memory constraints.
- **Policy Evaluation Step (n) for Adaptive Meta Warm-Up:** Indicated that a sufficient evaluation step (e.g., 600 steps) was necessary to effectively select the best warm-up strategy and maintain favorable forward transfer.

In summary, the empirical results consistently support the efficacy of the FAME dual-learner approach, demonstrating its ability to balance plasticity and stability more effectively than various baseline methods across diverse and challenging continual reinforcement learning scenarios.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of Continual Reinforcement Learning (CRL) through both theoretical advancements and a practical algorithmic framework, with several potential impacts:

1. **Principled Foundation for CRL:** The formal definitions of MDP difference and catastrophic forgetting provide a theoretical underpinning that has been largely absent in much of the existing CRL literature. This offers a principled basis for analyzing, comparing, and developing future CRL algorithms, moving beyond heuristic approaches. This could lead to more systematic advancements and a deeper understanding of the mechanisms behind effective continual learning.

2. **Novel Dual-Learner Architecture:** The FAME framework, with its distinct yet complementary fast and meta learners, offers a new architectural paradigm for CRL. The biological inspiration from hippocampal-cortical interactions provides a plausible model for addressing the trade-off between plasticity and stability, suggesting new avenues for designing intelligent agents.

3. **Enhanced Performance in Diverse Settings:** The consistent superior performance of FAME across various benchmarks—ranging from pixel-based tasks in MinAtar and Atari to continuous control problems in Meta-World, and applicable to both value- and policy-based RL—demonstrates its robustness and generalizability. This indicates a significant step towards building more capable continual learning agents for real-world applications.

4. **Effective Mitigation of Key Challenges:**
 - **Catastrophic Forgetting:** The meta learner's explicit objective of minimizing catastrophic forgetting, framed as an incremental update, offers a robust mechanism for knowledge retention.
 - **Negative Transfer:** The adaptive meta warm-up mechanism, which intelligently selects the most suitable prior knowledge (or no prior knowledge) for a new task, directly addresses the critical issue of negative transfer, improving the efficiency and success rate of adapting to novel environments.

5. **Scalability and Efficiency:** By employing fixed model sizes for both fast and meta learners and utilizing a moderate-sized meta buffer for knowledge integration, FAME addresses scalability concerns often associated with model expansion or extensive replay methods in CRL. The meta learner's updates, performed via efficient supervised learning, also minimize additional computational overhead.

6. **Unifying Connections:** The work elucidates deeper connections between continual, multi-task, and transfer reinforcement learning by integrating their core ideas within a single framework. This cross-pollination of concepts can inspire future research that draws upon the strengths of these related fields.

7. **Future Research Directions:** The study opens several avenues for future work, including exploring effective latent representations that can distill knowledge and guide reasoning, investigating more advanced knowledge transfer techniques (e.g., context embedding), and extending the framework to full continual RL settings where task boundaries are unknown or state/action spaces vary. These directions highlight the potential for FAME to serve as a foundational platform for further innovation in lifelong learning.

In conclusion, FAME represents a significant contribution by marrying theoretical rigor with a biologically inspired, empirically validated algorithmic solution. Its impact lies in advancing the fundamental understanding of continual reinforcement learning and providing a practical, high-performing framework for building more adaptive and robust artificial intelligence systems.
