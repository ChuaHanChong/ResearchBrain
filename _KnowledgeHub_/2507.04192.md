# JAX-MPM: A Learning-Augmented Differentiable Meshfree Framework for GPU-Accelerated Lagrangian Simulation and Geophysical Inverse Modeling

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at the University of Minnesota developed JAX-MPM, a differentiable Material Point Method framework for GPU-accelerated simulation of large-deformation geophysical flows. This framework achieved over 100x speedup compared to CPU-based solvers and accurately recovered complex spatially varying parameters, like initial velocity fields and friction coefficients, from sparse observational data.

## Problem

- Traditional numerical methods (FEM, FVM) struggle with large deformations, material fragmentation, and evolving topologies in geomechanical simulations due to mesh distortion.
- Estimating unobservable physical parameters (e.g., initial velocities, internal stresses, spatially varying friction) in geomechanical models is computationally intensive and complex with conventional inverse modeling techniques.
- Existing differentiable physics solvers often lack flexibility in automatic differentiation, multi-GPU support, or seamless integration with general machine learning frameworks for complex solid and geophysical systems.

## Method

- Developed **JAX-MPM**, a fully differentiable, GPU-accelerated ==Material Point Method (MPM)== solver built on the ==JAX framework==, enabling high-performance forward simulations of large-deformation geomechanical systems.
- Introduced a ==unified inverse modeling framework== that supports both ==Lagrangian (particle-based)== and ==Eulerian (region-based)== data supervision for flexible and scalable parameter inference.
- Integrated ==neural network parameterization== for spatially or temporally varying unknown fields, facilitating end-to-end differentiable pipelines for hybrid physics-machine learning workflows.

## Results

- Forward simulations accurately captured complex geophysical flow behaviors (dam-breaks, granular collapses) in 2D and 3D, showing close agreement with analytical solutions and experimental observations.
- **JAX-MPM** demonstrated significant computational speedups, achieving over **7x** compared to CPU-based C++ solvers and up to **>140x** over baseline CPU for large-scale 3D granular collapse simulations.
- Successfully recovered unknown scalar and neural network-parameterized spatially varying initial velocity fields, and spatially varying friction coefficients from sparse particle trajectory data, with accuracy improving with observation density.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Leveraging JAX's automatic differentiation (AD), JIT compilation, and GPU/TPU acceleration significantly enhances the efficiency and scalability of MPM for both forward and gradient-based inverse modeling.
> - Memory optimization techniques like checkpointing and segmented simulation loops are crucial for enabling reverse-mode AD over long simulation trajectories in high-fidelity physics solvers.
> - The unified output mapping for both Lagrangian and Eulerian supervision allows flexible data assimilation, effectively guiding the inverse estimation of parameters from diverse observational data types.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in this paper addresses challenges within the field of geomechanics and geophysical hazard modeling, specifically focusing on phenomena involving large deformations, complex material behavior, and often unobservable system conditions. Traditional numerical methods, such as the Finite Element Method (FEM) and Finite Volume Method (FVM), face limitations in scenarios characterized by extreme deformation, material fragmentation, and evolving topologies due to mesh distortion and entanglement. This has led to the development of meshfree methods, like Smoothed Particle Hydrodynamics (SPH), Discrete Element Method (DEM), and the Material Point Method (MPM), which represent materials as collections of particles, thereby enhancing robustness in large-deformation problems.

A parallel challenge in geomechanical modeling is the estimation of unknown or unmeasurable physical parameters, such as initial velocity fields, internal stress states, and spatially varying friction. Conventional inverse modeling approaches, including Bayesian inference and Markov chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) methods, are computationally intensive, especially for high-dimensional, nonlinear systems. Gradient-based alternatives, like adjoint solvers, improve efficiency but are complex to implement for systems with contact, friction, and path-dependent materials.

In recent years, differentiable programming (DP) has emerged as a paradigm in scientific computing, enabling automatic differentiation (AD) through simulation pipelines. DP-based methods can be broadly categorized into continuous formulations (e.g., Physics-Informed Neural Networks or neural operators) and discrete approaches that make established numerical solvers differentiable. While neural network-based methods offer flexibility and data-driven learning, they may lack explicit physical enforcement and often generalize poorly outside training distributions. Differentiable physics solvers, on the other hand, provide a physically consistent and scalable foundation for inverse modeling by enabling direct gradient computations through the entire simulation time-stepping process.

This paper positions JAX-MPM within the landscape of differentiable physics solvers. It seeks to combine the strengths of the Material Point Method, known for its robustness in large deformation problems, with the capabilities of modern automatic differentiation frameworks like JAX. While previous efforts have explored differentiable MPM solvers using domain-specific languages like Taichi, these have been noted for limitations in AD flexibility, multi-GPU support, and seamless integration with general machine learning frameworks. JAX-MPM aims to address these limitations by offering a unified, scalable, and fully differentiable meshfree platform for high-fidelity physical simulation and data assimilation in complex solid and geophysical systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind the development of JAX-MPM stems from the inherent complexities and limitations associated with modeling and predicting geophysical hazards such as landslides, dam-break flows, debris flows, and soil liquefaction. These phenomena are characterized by highly nonlinear, path-dependent material behavior, large-scale material failure, severe deformations, and rapidly evolving boundary conditions. These characteristics pose significant challenges for traditional mesh-based numerical methods, which often become computationally impractical or numerically unstable due due to mesh distortion and tangling.

Beyond accurate forward prediction, a critical need in geomechanical modeling is the ability to infer unobservable system conditions and material properties from limited data. Quantities like initial velocity fields, internal stress states, or spatially varying friction are often unknown or cannot be directly measured in real-world scenarios. Conventional inverse modeling techniques, while capable of parameter estimation, typically involve numerous repeated forward simulations within optimization loops. These methods are often computationally expensive, difficult to scale to high-dimensional problems, and challenging to implement for systems involving complex physics like contact, friction, and inelasticity. The development of adjoint solvers, while more efficient, typically requires extensive manual derivation for each specific problem.

Given these challenges, the overarching objective of this work is to develop a novel simulation framework that fundamentally integrates high-fidelity physical models, capable of capturing complex deformation and material inelasticity, with an efficient and scalable inverse modeling capability.

Specifically, the key objectives for JAX-MPM are to:

1. **Develop a fully differentiable, GPU-accelerated meshfree solver based on the Material Point Method (MPM):** This involves leveraging the JAX framework for its just-in-time (JIT) compilation, automatic differentiation (AD), and GPU/TPU acceleration to enable high-performance forward simulations of large-deformation geomechanical systems. The goal is to provide a robust platform for simulating phenomena like landslides and dam breaks.
2. **Introduce a unified inverse modeling framework supporting both Lagrangian (particle-based) and Eulerian (region-based) data:** The framework aims to allow flexible and scalable inference of physical parameters from diverse observational sources, accommodating both tracked particle trajectories and fixed-location sensor data, without modifying the core simulation pipeline.
3. **Demonstrate the potential for hybrid physics–machine learning workflows:** By seamlessly integrating with neural network representations, JAX-MPM intends to facilitate end-to-end differentiable pipelines for tasks such as parameter estimation and field reconstruction, where unknown spatially or temporally varying fields can be parameterized by neural networks. This aims to bridge traditional physics-based modeling with data-driven machine learning.

The motivation is thus to provide a versatile and efficient tool that can advance both the predictive capabilities of geomechanical simulations and the ability to extract critical, unobservable information from real-world data, thereby supporting improved risk assessment, mitigation strategies, and design in the context of natural hazards.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

JAX-MPM is designed as a general-purpose, differentiable meshfree solver built on the Material Point Method (MPM) and implemented within the JAX architecture. The methodology encompasses the physical modeling foundations of MPM, its numerical implementation details, and the integration of differentiable programming techniques for both forward and inverse modeling.

#### 3.1. Physical Modeling and Numerical Formulation

- **Governing Equations:** The framework solves the balance of linear momentum in the Lagrangian (material) frame, expressed through its weak form. Material points, representing the continuum, carry properties like mass, volume, velocity, and stress.
- **Discretization:** The continuum domain is discretized into Lagrangian material points that move over a fixed Eulerian background grid. Nodal shape functions, specifically tensor products of one-dimensional B-splines (quadratic in this study), are used for interpolation between particles and grid nodes. A lumped mass matrix approach is adopted for computational efficiency, and an explicit forward Euler time integration scheme is employed.
- **Constitutive Models:** JAX-MPM supports constitutive models for large-deformation geophysical flows.
 - **Newtonian Fluid:** Modeled as a weakly compressible fluid, with pressure computed using an artificial equation of state (EOS) and dynamic viscosity.
 - **Elastoplastic Solid:** Modeled using a non-associated Drucker–Prager (D-P) plasticity model with a tension cutoff. The Jaumann objective stress rate is used to maintain objectivity under large deformation, and a return mapping algorithm enforces the yield criterion.

#### 3.2. Implementation of Material Point Method (MPM)

Each time step in the MPM procedure consists of three stages:

- **Particle-to-Grid (P2G) Transfer:** Particle information (mass, momentum) is projected onto the background grid using interpolation kernels to compute nodal masses, grid velocities, and internal/external forces.
- **Grid-based Updates:** Nodal accelerations are calculated, and grid velocities are updated using explicit time integration. Dirichlet boundary conditions are imposed by overwriting boundary-node velocities, and ghost cells are used to maintain partition of unity with higher-order kernels. A frictionless contact model with a predictor–corrector scheme is implemented for interactions with rigid obstacles.
- **Grid-to-Particle (G2P) Transfer:** Updated grid information is transferred back to particles. JAX-MPM supports several transfer schemes, including FLIP, PIC, APIC, and TPIC. Particle positions and velocity gradients are updated based on interpolated grid velocities. The Update Stress Last (USL) scheme is adopted for stress evaluation.

#### 3.3. JAX-MPM: Differentiable Meshfree Solver for Forward and Inverse Modeling

The core of JAX-MPM's differentiable capability lies in its implementation within the JAX framework, which provides just-in-time (JIT) compilation, automatic differentiation (AD), and GPU/TPU acceleration.

- **Gradient Computation by Automatic Differentiation:** The physical simulation is formulated as a composition of differentiable operators, where the state update `S_t+1 = Φ(S_t; α)` is a differentiable mapping. JAX's reverse-mode AD (`jax.grad`) automatically computes gradients of any scalar quantity with respect to model parameters `α` (e.g., material properties, initial conditions) by traversing the computation graph backward.
- **PDE-constrained Optimization for Inverse Modeling:** Inverse problems are formulated as PDE-constrained optimization, minimizing the discrepancy between simulated and observed data.
 - **Differentiable Output Mapping:** A unified operator `Q` extracts simulated quantities corresponding to observations. This operator can handle both:
 - **Lagrangian supervision:** Tracking individual particle states (e.g., position `x_l^t`).
 - **Eulerian supervision:** Averaging particle states within fixed spatial regions (e.g., mean velocity `v_p^t` in `Ω_l`).
 - **Parameterization of Fields:** For spatially or temporally varying unknown parameters, a lightweight neural network `N_θ(x, t)` is used to approximate the field, with the network's weights `θ` becoming the trainable parameters.
- **Software Implementation Details for Performance and Memory:**
 - **Computational Speedup:** JAX-MPM leverages `jax.jit` for kernel fusion and efficient execution of MPM stages (P2G, grid updates, G2P) on accelerators. `jax.vmap` and vectorized array primitives are used for particle and grid parallelism, eliminating explicit Python loops.
 - **Memory Optimization:** To address the memory demands of reverse-mode AD for long simulation trajectories, JAX-MPM employs:
 - **Checkpointing (`jax.checkpoint` / `jax.remat`):** Intermediate grid states are discarded during the forward pass and recomputed during the backward pass, saving memory by only storing particle-level outputs.
 - **Segmented Simulation Loop (`jax.scan`):** The simulation loop is divided into segments, each checkpointed, reducing peak memory usage from `O(N_t)` to `O(N_t/n)`, where `N_t` is the total time steps and `n` is the number of segments.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research provides validation and performance results for JAX-MPM in both forward simulation and inverse modeling tasks.

#### 4.1. Numerical Results of Predictive Simulation (Forward Modeling)

- **2D Dam-Break Problem:**
 - JAX-MPM simulations of a shallow dam-break, using FLIP, TPIC, and APIC transfer schemes, produced results in close agreement with analytical shallow-water solutions. Minor discrepancies were observed early due to finite-depth effects.
- **Quasi-2D Granular Collapse (Aluminum-bar assemblage):**
 - The Drucker–Prager elastoplastic model accurately captured shear localization, with peak equivalent plastic strain concentrated near the base.
 - Predicted free surface and failure surface, defined by a plastic strain threshold, showed agreement with experimental observations from literature.
- **3D Dam-Break Problem with Rigid Obstacles:**
 - JAX-MPM captured complex three-dimensional fluid behaviors, including upward splashing, recirculation zones, and lateral flow separation downstream of obstacles.
 - Computational efficiency: A simulation with over 1.9 million particles on a 200 × 100 × 80 grid completed 1000 time steps in approximately 8.2 seconds (single precision) on a single GPU.
- **3D Granular Column Collapse (Influence of Aspect Ratio):**
 - Simulations for varying aspect ratios (H0/L0) demonstrated that higher aspect ratios led to greater vertical collapse, reduced static basal zones, and enhanced lateral spreading.
 - Normalized runout distance increased with aspect ratio, transitioning from linear to sublinear scaling, consistent with existing experimental observations.
 - **Computational Scalability:** Benchmarking against an open-source C++ solver (CB-Geo) on CPU, JAX-MPM on GPU achieved over a 7x speedup at the highest resolution (900,000 particles), completing 1000 steps in approximately 9 seconds (double precision).
- **3D Granular Cylinder Collapse (Influence of Friction Angle):**
 - Varying internal friction angles (20° to 40°) showed that higher friction resulted in steeper, more confined deposits and shorter runout distances, indicating greater resistance to shear deformation, consistent with theoretical and experimental studies.
 - **Computational Performance:** A simulation with over 2.7 million particles on a 400 × 400 × 100 grid completed 1000 steps in approximately 22 seconds (single precision) and 98 seconds (double precision). This represents a ~7x speedup over an optimized Fortran-based MPM solver with dynamic load balancing on 288 CPU cores and >140x over the baseline CPU version without load balancing.

#### 4.2. Inverse Modeling of Geophysical Flows using JAX-MPM

- **Inverse Estimation of Initial Velocity Constant:**
 - A scalar coefficient `α` defining the initial horizontal velocity field was successfully recovered using three observation strategies: full particle position, sparse particle position, and sparse velocity-monitor supervision (Eulerian).
 - All strategies converged to the ground truth value of `α = 2.0`. Velocity-monitor supervision showed the fastest and most stable convergence, despite using fewer observations, indicating a strong alignment between the supervised quantity and the parameter. Sparse particle supervision also performed effectively, sometimes faster than full particle supervision.
 - The optimized initial velocity led to flow fields closely matching reference simulations.
- **Inverse Estimation of Spatially Varying Initial Velocity Field:**
 - A continuous, spatially varying initial horizontal velocity field, parameterized by a three-layer feedforward neural network, was reconstructed from sparse particle position data.
 - Observation density was critical: 100 observation particles failed to recover meaningful spatial structure, while 500 particles achieved 6.4% relative L2 error, and 1000 particles achieved 3.7% error.
 - The reconstructed profiles for higher observation densities accurately captured the overall field, though deviations were observed near the lower boundary due to complex particle dynamics and contact interactions.
- **Inverse Estimation of Spatially Varying Frictional Coefficient:**
 - JAX-MPM successfully recovered four spatially varying Coulomb friction coefficients along the segmented bottom boundary of a 2D dam-break scenario using sparse particle trajectory data (100 and 500 particles).
 - Upstream friction coefficients were accurately identified across both sparsity levels. The accuracy of the downstream coefficient `μ3` was influenced by observation density: 14.5% error for 100 particles, reducing to 1.0% error for 500 particles.
 - The convergence behavior indicated that parameters influencing a larger number of particle trajectories (upstream) generated stronger gradient signals and converged more rapidly.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This study presents JAX-MPM as a significant advancement in the field of computational mechanics, particularly for geomechanical applications. Its primary significance lies in the development of a fully differentiable, GPU-accelerated Material Point Method (MPM) framework. This framework unifies high-fidelity predictive simulation with scalable gradient-based optimization within a single, end-to-end differentiable pipeline, leveraging the JAX ecosystem's capabilities for automatic differentiation, JIT compilation, and vectorized GPU execution.

The computational efficiency demonstrated by JAX-MPM is notable, achieving substantial speedups (up to 100x) compared to conventional CPU-based implementations. This performance is critical for large-scale simulations inherent in geophysical hazard analysis, enabling researchers to explore complex phenomena with high resolution and greater throughput. The framework’s ability to accurately capture large deformations, free-surface evolution, rigid-body contact, and diverse material behaviors, as validated through various 2D and 3D benchmarks, confirms its reliability for realistic geophysical flow simulation.

Beyond forward modeling, JAX-MPM's unique inverse modeling capabilities are a key contribution. It provides a unified formulation that accommodates both Lagrangian (particle-based) and Eulerian (region-based) supervision, allowing for flexible integration of diverse observational data. The successful recovery of unknown initial velocity fields (both parametric and non-parametric using neural networks) and spatially varying friction coefficients from sparse data represents a notable achievement. This is particularly significant as it marks a demonstration of a differentiable physics-based solver applied to large-scale Lagrangian geophysical flows for inverse frictional parameter estimation, a task often intractable or cumbersome with conventional non-differentiable methods.

The integration of neural networks within JAX-MPM to parameterize and reconstruct complex, spatially varying fields highlights the framework's potential for hybrid physics–machine learning (ML) workflows. This capability extends beyond basic parameter estimation, opening avenues for learning complex functional relationships and leveraging universal approximation properties while maintaining physical consistency.

The potential impact of JAX-MPM is broad, offering a flexible and efficient foundation for future research in several areas:

- **Inverse Modeling and Data Assimilation:** Improving the ability to infer unobservable system parameters and initial conditions from limited, sparse field observations in real-world geohazard scenarios. This can lead to more accurate risk assessments and calibrated predictive models.
- **Learning-Enabled Simulation:** Facilitating the development of more sophisticated, data-driven physical models through joint training of physical solvers with deep learning architectures.
- **Digital Twins for Geohazards:** Enabling the creation of high-fidelity computational replicas of real-world geotechnical systems, which can be continuously updated with observational data for real-time monitoring, forecasting, and scenario planning.
- **Intelligent Design of Geotechnical Systems:** Supporting the optimization and design of structures and mitigation strategies by allowing gradients to be computed with respect to design parameters through complex physical interactions.

In conclusion, JAX-MPM advances the integration of high-fidelity continuum mechanics simulation with modern differentiable programming, providing a robust, scalable, and versatile platform for addressing complex challenges in geohazards and beyond.
