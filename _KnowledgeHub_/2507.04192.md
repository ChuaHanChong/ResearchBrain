# JAX-MPM: A Learning-Augmented Differentiable Meshfree Framework for GPU-Accelerated Lagrangian Simulation and Geophysical Inverse Modeling

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at the University of Minnesota developed JAX-MPM, a differentiable Material Point Method framework for GPU-accelerated simulation of large-deformation geophysical flows. This framework achieved over 100x speedup compared to CPU-based solvers and accurately recovered complex spatially varying parameters, like initial velocity fields and friction coefficients, from sparse observational data.

## Problem

- Traditional numerical methods (FEM, FVM) struggle with large deformations, material fragmentation, and evolving topologies in geomechanical simulations due to mesh distortion.
- Estimating unobservable physical parameters (e.g., initial velocities, internal stresses, spatially varying friction) in geomechanical models is computationally intensive and complex with conventional inverse modeling techniques.
- Existing differentiable physics solvers often lack flexibility in automatic differentiation, multi-GPU support, or seamless integration with general machine learning frameworks for complex solid and geophysical systems.

## Method

- Developed **JAX-MPM**, a fully differentiable, GPU-accelerated ==Material Point Method (MPM)== solver built on the ==JAX framework==, enabling high-performance forward simulations of large-deformation geomechanical systems.
- Introduced a ==unified inverse modeling framework== that supports both ==Lagrangian (particle-based)== and ==Eulerian (region-based)== data supervision for flexible and scalable parameter inference.
- Integrated ==neural network parameterization== for spatially or temporally varying unknown fields, facilitating end-to-end differentiable pipelines for hybrid physics-machine learning workflows.

## Results

- Forward simulations accurately captured complex geophysical flow behaviors (dam-breaks, granular collapses) in 2D and 3D, showing close agreement with analytical solutions and experimental observations.
- **JAX-MPM** demonstrated significant computational speedups, achieving over **7x** compared to CPU-based C++ solvers and up to **>140x** over baseline CPU for large-scale 3D granular collapse simulations.
- Successfully recovered unknown scalar and neural network-parameterized spatially varying initial velocity fields, and spatially varying friction coefficients from sparse particle trajectory data, with accuracy improving with observation density.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Leveraging JAX's automatic differentiation (AD), JIT compilation, and GPU/TPU acceleration significantly enhances the efficiency and scalability of MPM for both forward and gradient-based inverse modeling.
> - Memory optimization techniques like checkpointing and segmented simulation loops are crucial for enabling reverse-mode AD over long simulation trajectories in high-fidelity physics solvers.
> - The unified output mapping for both Lagrangian and Eulerian supervision allows flexible data assimilation, effectively guiding the inverse estimation of parameters from diverse observational data types.
