# Raw2Drive: Reinforcement Learning with Aligned World Models for End-to-End Autonomous Driving (in CARLA v2)

> [!summary] Summary
> Raw2Drive introduces the first model-based reinforcement learning framework for end-to-end autonomous driving that operates directly from raw multi-view images and IMU data. This system surpasses existing imitation learning methods on challenging CARLA benchmarks, achieving a driving score of 71.36 on Bench2Drive's closed-loop evaluation while improving training efficiency.

## Problem

- Imitation Learning (IL) for end-to-end autonomous driving struggles with causal confusion and distribution shift, leading to poor generalization in complex scenarios.
- Applying Reinforcement Learning (RL) to end-to-end autonomous driving with raw sensor data has been an open problem due to high-dimensional observation spaces, sparse rewards, and significant computational expense.
- Existing Model-based Reinforcement Learning (MBRL) applications in autonomous driving predominantly rely on privileged or simplified information, not directly processing noisy raw sensor inputs.

## Method

- **Raw2Drive** proposes a novel ==dual-stream Model-based== Reinforcement Learning framework, comprising a privileged world model and a raw sensor world model.
- A sophisticated Guidance Mechanism is introduced to align the raw sensor stream with the more stable privileged stream through spatial-temporal and abstract-state alignment losses during training.
- The raw sensor world model strategically reconstructs BEV semantic masks without direct reward and continue heads, instead leveraging these crucial signals from the privileged world model for policy training (Head Guidance).

## Results

- Raw2Drive achieves state-of-the-art performance among raw sensor end-to-end autonomous driving methods on CARLA Leaderboard 2.0 (Driving Score 4.12) and Bench2Drive (Driving Score 71.36, Success Rate **50.24%**).
- The system demonstrates strong performance across various driving abilities on Bench2Drive, including Merging (**43.35%**), Overtaking (**51.11%**), and Emergency Brake (**60.00%**).
- Ablation studies validate the necessity of the dual-stream architecture, the specific configuration of world model heads, and the intricate guidance mechanisms for stable and high-performance learning from raw sensor data.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Training a world model directly from high-dimensional raw sensor data is more stable and effective when only focused on reconstructing observations, rather than directly predicting scalar rewards and continuation flags.
> - Aligning the encoded features and latent states (deterministic and stochastic) between a raw sensor world model and a privileged world model is critical for robust and consistent learning.
> - Employing 'Head Guidance' from a more stable privileged world model provides accurate and consistent reward and termination signals, which are essential for effective policy optimization in the raw sensor domain.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

End-to-End Autonomous Driving (E2E-AD) aims to directly map raw sensor inputs (e.g., camera, LiDAR) to control outputs (steering, throttle, brake), simplifying the traditional modular pipeline (perception, prediction, planning, control). Historically, **Imitation Learning (IL)** has been the dominant paradigm in E2E-AD, training models to mimic expert demonstrations. While popular, IL suffers from fundamental limitations:
1. **Causal Confusion:** The model might incorrectly associate actions with the wrong causes, especially in complex, dynamic scenarios.
2. **Distribution Shift (or Covariate Shift):** When the agent encounters states not present in the expert demonstration data, its performance degrades significantly. This leads to poor generalization to unseen situations.

**Reinforcement Learning (RL)** offers a promising alternative by optimizing policies through reward-driven interactions with the environment, allowing agents to learn beyond expert demonstrations and adapt to novel situations. Despite its theoretical advantages, applying RL to E2E-AD has faced significant practical challenges, including:
1. **Training Difficulty:** High-dimensional observation spaces (raw sensor data) and sparse rewards make RL training extremely difficult and computationally expensive, often leading to slow convergence or outright failure. Early RL attempts like MaRLn (2020) required enormous training steps (50M steps / 57 days) and still underperformed IL methods.
2. **Lack of Robustness:** Model-free RL methods, which directly learn policies without an explicit environmental model, can be inefficient and struggle with long-term planning.

Recently, **Model-based Reinforcement Learning (MBRL)** has shown success by learning a "world model" that predicts future states and rewards. This allows the agent to "imagine" consequences of actions, significantly improving sample efficiency and enabling better planning. However, existing successful MBRL applications in AD, such as Think2Drive (2024), have primarily relied on **privileged information** (e.g., ground-truth states, bounding boxes, HD maps) rather than raw sensor data. This is because privileged information is compact and structured, making world model training easier. The challenge of training an MBRL system directly from high-dimensional, noisy, and redundant raw sensor data remained largely unsolved in E2E-AD.

Raw2Drive critically fills this gap. It is presented as the **first MBRL framework for E2E-AD that operates directly from raw sensor input (multi-view images and IMU)**. It combines the benefits of MBRL (efficiency, planning) with the real-world applicability of raw sensor inputs, while directly addressing the limitations of IL and prior model-free RL methods in complex, interactive urban driving scenarios (specifically CARLA Leaderboard 2.0 and Bench2Drive, which feature many corner cases). By demonstrating state-of-the-art performance, Raw2Drive advocates for RL as a powerful paradigm shift beyond IL for autonomous driving.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind the Raw2Drive project are multifaceted:

1. **Mitigate Limitations of Imitation Learning (IL) in E2E-AD:** The authors explicitly aim to overcome the inherent problems of causal confusion and distribution shift that plague IL-based autonomous driving systems, which struggle to generalize to novel or complex scenarios not seen during training.
2. **Enable Reinforcement Learning (RL) for E2E-AD from Raw Sensor Data:** Despite RL's theoretical advantages in complex decision-making, its application to E2E-AD using raw sensor inputs has been an "open problem" due to training difficulties, high computational costs, and convergence issues. Raw2Drive seeks to make this a feasible and effective approach.
3. **Bridge the Gap for Model-Based Reinforcement Learning (MBRL):** While MBRL has shown promise in neural planning for AD (e.g., Think2Drive), it previously relied on simplified, privileged information. A key objective for Raw2Drive is to extend the success of MBRL to the more challenging and realistic setting of raw sensor data input.
4. **Achieve State-of-the-Art Performance on Challenging Benchmarks:** The paper aims to demonstrate that RL-based E2E-AD can not only work with raw sensor data but also surpass the performance of existing IL methods on rigorous benchmarks like CARLA Leaderboard 2.0 and Bench2Drive, which include many real-world corner cases.
5. **Improve Training Efficiency for RL-based E2E-AD:** Previous RL attempts were prohibitively expensive. Raw2Drive endeavors to achieve competitive performance with significantly less computational resources compared to prior RL methods and even some IL approaches, making RL a more practical option for AD research and development.
6. **Provide an Orthogonal Reference for Industry Applications:** By showcasing an efficient and high-performing RL framework, the authors hope to offer a valuable alternative paradigm for autonomous driving development beyond the prevalent IL-based approaches in the industry.

In essence, Raw2Drive is motivated by the desire to leverage the power of MBRL to build more robust, generalizable, and efficient end-to-end autonomous driving systems that learn directly from raw sensor observations, thereby addressing critical shortcomings of current paradigms.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Raw2Drive introduces a novel **dual-stream Model-based Reinforcement Learning (MBRL)** approach, designed to learn effectively from high-dimensional raw sensor data by leveraging an auxiliary privileged stream. The methodology unfolds in two main stages and relies on a carefully crafted "Guidance Mechanism."

**Overall Rationale:** Directly training a world model from complex, high-dimensional raw sensor data (like multi-view videos) is extremely difficult. The dual-stream design addresses this by first training a "privileged" stream with simpler, low-dimensional data, and then using this trained stream to guide the learning of the "raw sensor" stream.

**A. Stage 1: Privileged World Model and Policy Training**
This stage efficiently establishes a robust baseline model using structured, ground-truth information.
- **Privileged Input:** The model receives time-sequenced Bird's Eye View (BEV) semantic masks ($o_t$), derived from ground-truth bounding boxes and HD-Maps from the CARLA simulator. This input is compact and directly relevant for planning.
- **Privileged World Model (WM):** Similar to DreamerV3, this WM comprises an Encoder, a Recurrent State-Space Model (RSSM), and three heads: a Reward head, a Decoder head (to reconstruct BEV masks), and a Continue head (predicting if the episode continues). The WM is trained to predict future states, rewards, and continuations based on actions and current latent states.
- **Privileged Policy ($\pi$):** An actor-critic network is trained using Reinforcement Learning through rollouts within the privileged world model. The rewards and continuous flags for training are generated by the WM's heads.

**B. Stage 2: Raw Sensor World Model and Policy Training**
This stage focuses on learning from raw sensor data, guided by the knowledge acquired in Stage 1.
- **Raw Sensor Input:** Multi-view RGB images and IMU data ($\hat{o}_t$) are used, mirroring real-world driving conditions.
- **Raw Sensor World Model ($\hat{\text{WM}}$):**
 - **Encoder ($\hat{\text{Enc}}$):** BEVFormer is adopted to process multi-view images and output grid-shaped BEV features.
 - **RSSM:** The RSSM parameters are initialized from the privileged WM, providing a strong starting point.
 - **Heads:** Crucially, the raw sensor WM **only uses a Decoder head** to reconstruct BEV semantic masks. The Reward and Continue heads are omitted. This design choice is motivated by an empirical observation (and confirmed in ablation studies) that direct learning of scalar rewards and continuous flags from highly similar raw video frames, but with potentially abrupt changes in value, leads to unstable convergence.
- **Raw Sensor Policy ($\hat{\pi}$):** This policy is also an actor-critic network, trained by RL within the dual-stream world model, specifically using the raw sensor world model's dynamics.

**C. The Guidance Mechanism (Key Innovation)**
This mechanism is central to enabling stable and effective learning for the raw sensor stream by aligning it with the privileged stream. It addresses cumulative errors and train-validation gaps.

1. **Rollout Guidance:** This component ensures consistency between the two world models during rollouts, which is vital for policy learning in MBRL.
 - **Spatial-Temporal Alignment Loss:** Aligns the encoded states ($\hat{e}_t$ from raw sensor encoder with $e_t$ from privileged encoder) using Mean Squared Error (MSE). This ensures the raw sensor stream learns to extract spatially consistent, decision-relevant features.
 - **Abstract-State Alignment Loss:** Enforces consistency between the latent states of the two WMs:
 - **Deterministic State Alignment:** L2 loss is used to align the deterministic states ($\hat{h}_t$ and $h_t$). The deterministic state primarily models the ego vehicle's state.
 - **Stochastic State Alignment:** KL divergence is used to align the distributions of the stochastic states ($\hat{s}_t$ and $s_t$), which are sampled from independent one-hot distributions. The stochastic state focuses on anticipating other traffic participants' behaviors.
 - **Single-Sampling for Stochastic State:** To prevent cumulative errors from randomness during rollout, the stochastic state is sampled *only once* from the raw sensor stream's distribution. This sampled state is then fed into *both* the raw sensor and privileged world models for subsequent state deduction, ensuring aligned and stable supervision.
 - The combined rollout loss is $L_{Rollout} = \beta_e \sum_{t} \sum_{i=0}^{\text{grid num}} \text{MSE}(e_i^t, \hat{e}_i^t) + \sum_{t} (\beta_s \text{KL}(s_t, \hat{s}_t) + \beta_h \text{MSE}(h_t, \hat{h}_t))$.

2. **Head Guidance:** Since the raw sensor WM lacks reward and continue heads, these crucial signals for policy training are "borrowed" from the more stable privileged WM.
 - During raw sensor policy training, the raw sensor WM executes an action, generating a next latent state. Simultaneously, the privileged WM also rolls out with the same action.
 - Due to the consistency enforced by Rollout Guidance, the reward ($r_t$) and continuation flag ($c_t$) predicted by the privileged WM's heads are considered accurate and stable, and are thus used to supervise the raw sensor policy.
 - Additionally, the trained privileged policy is used to collect data for the replay buffer and to distill action distributions into the raw sensor policy, further enhancing learning.

**Training Pipeline:**
The overall training pipeline (Algorithm 1) alternates between collecting data via interaction with the simulator using the current policy (either privileged or raw sensor) and then updating the world models and policies. For the raw sensor stream, the world model training incorporates the $L_{Rollout}$ and the policy benefits from Head Guidance.

This dual-stream, guidance-driven approach allows Raw2Drive to tackle the complexity of raw sensor data while benefiting from the efficiency and stability of MBRL, a significant advancement in E2E-AD.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

Raw2Drive presents compelling evidence of its effectiveness, achieving state-of-the-art performance on challenging autonomous driving benchmarks and validating its design choices through comprehensive ablation studies.

**A. Performance on CARLA Leaderboard 2.0:**
- Raw2Drive significantly outperforms all existing Imitation Learning (IL) based methods for raw sensor end-to-end autonomous driving on CARLA Leaderboard 2.0 (Validation and Devtest benchmarks).
- For example, on the validation set, Raw2Drive achieves a Driving Score (DS) of 4.12, compared to the next best IL method (DriveAdapter*) at 0.92. Similarly, its Route Completion (RC) is 9.32% versus 1.52%, and Infraction Score (IS) is 0.43 versus 0.42.
- However, it's important to note that Raw2Drive's performance (DS 4.12) is still considerably lower than Think2Drive (DS 43.8), which uses *privileged information*. This highlights the inherent difficulty of learning from raw sensor data compared to ground-truth states, even with Raw2Drive's advancements. The authors emphasize that long-route evaluations in CARLA 2.0 can be misleading due to cumulative penalty mechanisms, hence focusing more on Bench2Drive.

**B. Performance on Bench2Drive:**
Bench2Drive is a more comprehensive benchmark designed with short routes and specific corner cases to evaluate various driving abilities.
- **Multi-Ability Benchmark:** Raw2Drive achieves a mean ability score of 53.34%, outperforming all IL-based raw sensor methods (e.g., DriveAdapter* at 42.08%, DriveTrans at 38.60%). It shows particularly strong performance in Merging (43.35%), Overtaking (51.11%), Emergency Brake (60.00%), and Traffic Sign (62.26%).
- **Closed-Loop Benchmark:** Raw2Drive achieves state-of-the-art performance among raw sensor end-to-end methods with a Driving Score (DS) of 71.36 and a Success Rate (SR) of 50.24%. This significantly surpasses IL baselines (e.g., DriveAdapter* with DS 64.22, SR 33.08%).
- Again, Think2Drive (privileged RL) sets a much higher benchmark (DS 91.85, SR 85.41%), reiterating the difference between privileged and raw sensor inputs. Nevertheless, Raw2Drive represents a significant leap for raw sensor-based methods.

**C. Computational Efficiency:**
- Raw2Drive achieves its final planner using approximately **64 H800 GPU days**. This cost can be reduced to 40 GPU days if the pre-trained privileged model from Think2Drive is reused.
- This is presented as significantly more efficient than imitation learning approaches used in industry for solving comparable numbers of corner cases in CARLA v2 (e.g., UniAD costs about 30 GPU days but only solves 3-4 corner cases). This highlights RL's potential for efficient learning when properly structured.

**D. Ablation Studies (on Dev10, a subset of Bench2Drive):**
The ablation studies provide critical insights into the design choices:

1. **Choice of Heads in Raw Sensor World Model:**
 - Using *only the decoder head* in the raw sensor WM resulted in the best performance (DS 83.5, SR 7.5/10).
 - Including reward and/or continuous heads significantly degraded performance (DS 46.6/34.5), confirming that these scalar signals are difficult to learn stably from raw sensor inputs due to perception confusion (similar frames, abrupt value changes).

2. **Abstract-State Alignment in Rollout Guidance:**
 - All components (encoder, deterministic, stochastic state alignment) are essential.
 - Removing any part (e.g., only encoder alignment: DS 36.4; encoder + deterministic: DS 38.3) severely harms performance, leading to failure in complex tasks. This underscores the necessity of consistent latent state representations between the two world models.

3. **Spatial-Temporal Alignment in Rollout Guidance:**
 - Both spatial and temporal alignment components are crucial.
 - Removing either (e.g., spatial only: DS 13.6; temporal only: DS 9.24) leads to poor performance, indicating that consistency across both space (BEV representation) and time (future predictions) is vital for stable world model rollouts.

4. **Head Guidance:**
 - Head Guidance (using privileged WM's reward/continue heads) improves policy training even when the raw sensor WM is configured with all three heads (DS 34.5 with HG vs. 26.4 without HG). This confirms that privileged supervision is more stable and accurate.

5. **Shared Parameter:**
 - Sharing parameters between the RSSM and the decoder head improved performance (DS 83.5 vs. 53.2 without shared RSSM, and 65.6 without shared head), suggesting more efficient representation learning and better consistency.

6. **Policy Finetuning:**
 - Finetuning the privileged policy with the raw sensor world model led to significant performance gains (DS 83.5) compared to directly using the privileged policy (DS 58.4). This highlights the importance of adapting the policy to the raw sensor world model's learned dynamics.

In summary, Raw2Drive not only achieves SOTA for raw sensor E2E-AD but also provides strong empirical justification for its dual-stream architecture, the judicious selection of world model heads, and the intricate guidance mechanisms.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

Raw2Drive represents a significant milestone in the field of end-to-end autonomous driving and reinforcement learning, with several important implications and potential impacts:

1. **Pioneering E2E MBRL with Raw Sensor Input:** The most significant contribution is being the *first* successful Model-based Reinforcement Learning framework for end-to-end autonomous driving that operates directly on raw sensor data (images and IMU). This addresses a long-standing challenge in the field, moving beyond methods that rely on privileged or simplified inputs.
2. **Validation of RL for Robust AD:** By achieving state-of-the-art performance on challenging benchmarks like CARLA Leaderboard 2.0 and Bench2Drive (which feature numerous complex "corner cases"), Raw2Drive provides strong empirical evidence that Reinforcement Learning can indeed overcome the limitations of Imitation Learning (such as causal confusion and distribution shift) and lead to more robust and generalizable driving policies. This shifts the perception of RL from a theoretically promising but practically difficult approach to a viable and superior alternative for AD.
3. **Novel Dual-Stream Architecture and Guidance Mechanism:** The proposed dual-stream design, effectively leveraging privileged information to guide the learning from raw sensors, is a novel and highly effective paradigm. The specific Guidance Mechanism, with its Spatial-Temporal, Abstract-State Alignment, and Head Guidance components, offers a blueprint for how to bridge the gap between abstract, clean representations and messy, high-dimensional sensor data in complex domains. The single-sampling trick for stochastic states is a subtle but crucial detail for stability.
4. **Improved Training Efficiency:** Demonstrating that a high-performing RL-based E2E-AD system can be trained with reasonable computational resources (64 H800 GPU days) is critical for practical adoption. This efficiency, especially when compared to the efforts required by some IL methods to solve fewer complex scenarios, makes RL a more attractive research direction.
5. **Foundation for Future Research:** Raw2Drive serves as a "stepping stone" for further exploration of RL in E2E-AD. It opens avenues for research into more sophisticated world models, improved guidance mechanisms, and applications to more diverse and realistic scenarios.
6. **Potential for Industry Impact:** By providing an "orthogonal reference" to the industry's prevalent IL-based approaches, Raw2Drive offers a fundamentally different and potentially safer and more reliable pathway for developing autonomous driving systems. Its ability to learn more robustly from interactions could lead to AD systems that generalize better to unforeseen circumstances, which is crucial for real-world deployment.

**Limitations and Future Directions (as acknowledged by authors):**
- **Reliance on Privileged Input During Training:** While the *inference* uses raw sensors, the *training* of Raw2Drive still heavily relies on privileged information for the auxiliary world model and the guidance mechanism. In real-world applications, ground-truth bounding boxes and HD-Maps might come from human annotation or advanced perception algorithms, which are not true "raw" inputs. Future work could aim to reduce or eliminate this reliance on privileged information during training.
- **Simulator Dependence:** The current work is confined to CARLA, which, while advanced, is still a simulator. Transferring these learnings to the real world remains a significant technical challenge. The authors correctly point to emerging technologies like 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) or diffusion-based simulators as potential future avenues for more realistic and scalable RL training environments.
- **Scope:** The work focuses on policy learning and the world model design. Integrating more advanced perception backbones or control interfaces might be explored in future iterations.

In conclusion, Raw2Drive is a groundbreaking contribution that pushes the boundaries of autonomous driving research by successfully implementing an end-to-end model-based reinforcement learning framework directly from raw sensor data. Its demonstrated performance and innovative methodology pave the way for more robust, generalizable, and efficient autonomous driving systems, profoundly impacting both academic research and future industry applications.
