# Hy-Embodied-VLM-1.0: Efficient Physical-World Agents

> [!summary] Summary
> Tencent Robotics X introduced Hy-Embodied-VLM-1.0, an embodied foundation model designed for physical-world agents, which achieves competitive performance across 38 diagnostic benchmarks and in closed-loop navigation. Utilizing a Mixture-of-Experts architecture, it processes information with only 3 billion active parameters, improving average scores by 4.4 percentage points over the strongest comparable baseline while maintaining high efficiency.

## Problem

- Translating advanced agentic capabilities from digital environments to physical-world agents presents challenges in action execution, environmental comprehension, and continuous adaptation.
- Physical agents need models capable of reasoning about actions, understanding consequences, and adapting to dynamic conditions, which goes beyond standard VLM tasks like visual question-answering.
- Existing general-purpose Vision-Language Models (VLMs) often lack the specific action-relevant understanding required for effective interaction in physical settings.

## Method

- Introduced **Hy-Embodied-VLM-1.0**, an ==embodied foundation model== tailored for efficient physical-world interaction.
- Established an ==action-centric capability taxonomy== to systematically develop and assess agentic capabilities, spanning perception, action reasoning, and adaptive planning.
- Designed with a ==Mixture-of-Experts (MoE)== architecture for the language backbone (30B total, 3B active params) and a ==native-resolution Vision Transformer==, enabling high capacity with efficient inference.

## Results

- Hy-Embodied-VLM-1.0 achieved an overall average score of **65.6** across **38 diagnostic benchmarks**, a **4.4 percentage point** improvement over the strongest comparable baseline, Qwen3.6-A3B.
- The model maintained computational efficiency, activating only **3 billion** parameters during inference, while achieving performance on par with a previous **32 billion** activated parameter model.
- It demonstrated strong capabilities in closed-loop Vision-Language Navigation, including a **57.9%** Success Rate in R2R-CE instruction following and a **38.3%** Success Rate in zero-shot object-goal navigation, outperforming baselines.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A structured action-centric capability taxonomy provides an effective framework for systematically developing and evaluating diverse embodied intelligence requirements.
> - Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) architectures can balance high model capacity with efficient inference, making large language models practical for latency-sensitive physical-world deployments.
> - A self-evolving, multi-stage training loop incorporating pre-training, supervised fine-tuning with 'thinking traces,' and reinforcement learning with rejection-sampling fine-tuning can cultivate robust embodied agentic reasoning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of advanced embodied agents for the physical world is a significant area within artificial intelligence research. Recent progress in digital-world agents has demonstrated capabilities such as multi-step interaction, tool use, and long-horizon reasoning. However, extending these agentic capabilities to physical environments introduces additional challenges. These include the necessity for models to reason about and execute actions, comprehend how the environment changes in response to those actions, and continuously adapt behavior to dynamic physical conditions. General-purpose Vision-Language Models (VLMs) typically focus on visual question-answering or image content description, which differs from the action-relevant understanding required for embodied agents.

This work addresses these challenges by contributing to the "Hy-Embodied" series, which aims to transfer intelligence developed in the digital domain to agents that interact with the physical world. It adheres to the Vision-Language Model paradigm, leveraging the reasoning and knowledge capabilities of large language models while maintaining multimodal perception. Hy-Embodied-VLM-1.0 represents an evolution from previous models in this series, specifically Hy-Embodied-0.5, through improvements in both data and model design. The introduction of an action-centric capability taxonomy is central to this effort, guiding the systematic development of embodied intelligence.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research was to construct generalizable and high-performing agents capable of operating effectively in the physical world. This involved developing an embodied foundation model, Hy-Embodied-VLM-1.0, explicitly designed for physical-world interaction.

The motivation for this work stems from the inherent difficulties in translating the advancements observed in digital agents to physical counterparts. Physical-world agents require more than fundamental multimodal perception and understanding; they must possess agentic capabilities for reasoning about actions, adapting to evolving situations, and interacting with the physical environment. Addressing these requirements formed the core motivation.

To systematically cultivate these capabilities, the researchers defined an action-centric capability taxonomy comprising three progressive dimensions:
- **Action-Relevant State Understanding:** This dimension focuses on the agent's ability to accurately and robustly comprehend its own state and that of its environment. This includes understanding fundamental physical properties, three-dimensional spatial structures, and attributes pertinent to the agent's embodiment.
- **Action–Transition Reasoning:** This dimension involves the agent's capacity to understand actions and agent–environment interactions, plan elementary actions, and reason about their consequences and the underlying physical principles governing state transitions.
- **Sequential and Adaptive Reasoning:** This dimension extends the agent's capabilities to handle complex, long-horizon action sequences. It necessitates reasoning over past actions and future goals, and performing reflection, repair, and recovery actions in response to deviations or failures during complex interactions.

A further key objective was to develop a model that not only exhibits strong physical-world understanding and interaction capabilities but also supports latency-sensitive deployment. This efficiency goal motivated specific architectural choices, such as the use of a Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) design, to combine high model capacity with efficient inference. The comprehensive evaluation suite, expanded to 38 benchmarks, was motivated by the need to thoroughly assess the diverse capabilities required by physical-world agents, moving beyond isolated task performance to a more integrated understanding of agent behavior.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed in developing Hy-Embodied-VLM-1.0 involved a structured approach guided by an action-centric capability taxonomy, a systematic data pipeline, a specialized model architecture, and a multi-stage training regimen.

#### 3.1. Capability Taxonomy

A three-level capability taxonomy was introduced to systematically characterize the requirements for embodied foundation models:
- **Action-Relevant State Understanding:**
 - *Physical and Semantic Perception:* Focused on object/part recognition, counting, pointing, attribute/state understanding, and depth/metric estimation.
 - *Spatial Reasoning:* Covered 3D geometry and relations, metric distance and orientation, perspective taking, pose reasoning, multi-view correspondence, and placement reasoning.
 - *Robotics-Centric Understanding:* Emphasized robot embodiment and viewpoint, task-relevant objects, functional parts, affordances, manipulation regions, and feasible placement regions for robotic action.
- **Action-Transition Reasoning:**
 - *Interaction Understanding:* Encompassed recognition and interpretation of human-robot-object interactions, identifying participants, targets, interaction semantics, and temporal localization.
 - *Action Decision and Grounding:* Concerned immediate action-oriented reasoning, including next-action/skill selection, grounding to target objects/parts, action point prediction, trajectory generation, and task-conditioned grounding.
 - *Physical Feasibility and Local Effects:* Focused on reasoning about spatial preconditions, physical executability, action mechanisms, postconditions, and immediate state changes induced by actions.
- **Sequential and Adaptive Reasoning:**
 - *Long-Horizon Composition and Planning:* Required organizing multiple actions or subgoals, goal decomposition, causal action ordering, progress-aware continuation, and multi-step skill composition.
 - *Vision-Language Navigation (VLN):* Involved following language goals through visual observations, spatial memory, egomotion, history, map reasoning, multi-step navigation, and wrong-turn recovery.
 - *Reflection, Repair, and Recovery:* Focused on adaptation to unexpected outcomes, including failure detection, causal analysis, counterfactual reasoning, plan repair, recovery, and replanning.

#### 3.2. Data Pipeline

A systematic data pipeline was developed, featuring data mixtures for pre-training, supervised fine-tuning (SFT), and reinforcement learning (RL), all structured according to the taxonomy.
- **Pre-training Data:** Hy-Embodied-VLM-1.0 utilized the pre-training data mixture from Hy-Embodied-0.5 without modification. This corpus provided foundational knowledge in visual perception, spatial reasoning, embodied-centric supervision, and general vision-language understanding, including object grounding, detection, depth estimation, segmentation, pointing, counting, spatial correspondence, geometry, affordance, trajectory understanding, and planning-oriented reasoning.
- **Supervised Fine-tuning Data:** This stage built upon the inherited SFT data from Hy-Embodied-0.5 and introduced additional capability-oriented data:
 - *Depth Reasoning:* Derived from datasets like Argoverse 2, Matterport3D, nuScenes, ScanNet++, and Taskonomy, including metric depth and chain-of-thought examples.
 - *Task-conditioned Grounding and Affordance Localization:* Focused on localizing task-relevant objects, functional parts, and feasible interaction/placement regions based on natural language instructions.
 - *Social Interaction:* Integrated egocentric videos, AVA v2.2, and HICO-DET, with annotations for interactant identification, gaze, pointing gestures, body pose, and human-object activities.
 - *Object-centric and Robot-centric Trajectory Supervision:* Provided visual observations and task/motion descriptions, supervising the model with ordered point sequences representing object, end-effector, or human-demonstrated manipulation traces.
 - *Causal Reasoning:* Constructed from egocentric and robotic manipulation videos, decomposing procedures into ordered steps with preconditions, effects, and dependencies.
 - *Failure-aware Robotic Reasoning:* Included data from robot execution videos and perturbed task plans to encourage reasoning about execution status, plan verification, error identification, and recovery suggestions.
 - *Vision-Language Navigation:* A two-stage dataset for instruction following and closed-loop decision-making in Habitat, utilizing a shortest-path oracle and Dataset Aggregation (DAgger).
- **Reinforcement Learning Data:** This stage focused on verifiable embodied reasoning tasks where outputs could be evaluated with rule-based rewards. It included data for spatial and metric state reasoning, pointing, grounding, affordance prediction, object-centric and robot-centric trajectory reasoning, and sequential process and navigation reasoning.

#### 3.3. Model Architecture

The model architecture was based on the Hy-series, employing a Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) architecture for the language backbone and a native-resolution Vision Transformer (ViT) for the vision encoder.
- **Language Backbone:** The Hy3-A3B large language model was used, featuring approximately 30 billion total parameters but activating only about 3 billion parameters per token during inference. This MoE design aimed to balance model capacity with inference efficiency.
- **Vision Encoder:** The Hy-ViT2 vision encoder was utilized, capable of processing images with arbitrary resolutions and aspect ratios to preserve fine-grained visual information without distortion.
- **Connector:** A lightweight connector linked the vision encoder and language model, projecting visual features into the language model's representation space.

#### 3.4. Training Regimen

The training of Hy-Embodied-VLM-1.0 was organized into a self-evolving post-training loop designed to cultivate embodied agentic reasoning.
- **Pre-Training:** The process began with initialization from a pre-trained VLM, followed by embodied continued pre-training on a large multimodal corpus. This stage established fine-grained visual grounding and general embodied understanding.
- **Mid-Training & SFT:** This continuous stage transformed the pre-trained backbone into an instruction follower covering the capability taxonomy. It combined extensive general embodied supervision with a small set of high-quality "thinking traces" (Chain-of-Thought demonstrations) to introduce explicit reasoning.
- **Reinforcement Learning (RL):** Policy optimization was performed using GRPO (Group-Relative Policy Optimization), which estimates advantages from a group of sampled responses without a separate value network. Task-aware embodied rewards were employed, using deterministic, structure-aware functions for geometric grounding, trajectories, discrete outputs, and continuous estimations, with an LLM-based judge as a fallback for open-ended reasoning.
 - *Stage I: Reasoning-Elicitation RL:* This stage refined the SFT policy, rewarding verifiable action-relevant conclusions. It produced a policy (θ_rl) used as a generator for rejection sampling.
 - *Stage II: Reward-Specialized RL:* Following rejection-sampling fine-tuning, this stage trained two specialists from a consolidated checkpoint (θ_cons): one for continuous-reward tasks (geometric and metric precision) and another for discrete-reward tasks (decision, planning, reflection accuracy). The parameters from these specialists were then fused to form the final deployed model (θ_final).
- **Rejection-Sampling Fine-Tuning (RFT):** This stage bridged the two RL stages. It involved synthesizing high-quality long-CoT data from the θ_rl policy by drawing candidate reasoning trajectories and filtering them based on answer correctness, thinking quality (using an LLM critic), and self-consistency. This approximately 1 million example corpus (D_cot) was then used to re-train a fresh model (θ_cons) from the pre-training checkpoint (θ_pt), internalizing stable embodied reasoning.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

Hy-Embodied-VLM-1.0 A3B was evaluated on a comprehensive suite of 38 diagnostic benchmarks and deployed in two closed-loop vision-language navigation (VLN) settings. The evaluations compared the model with others of comparable active-parameter scale (Hy-Embodied-0.5 MoT-2B, Qwen3.6-A3B, Embodied-R1.5, and Cosmos3-Nano).

#### 4.1. Overall Performance

Hy-Embodied-VLM-1.0 A3B demonstrated competitive performance across the evaluation suite.
- It achieved the highest average score across all three diagnostic capability categories.
- The model ranked first on 19 out of 38 benchmarks and second on an additional 11 benchmarks, placing it among the top two on 30 benchmarks.
- Its overall average score across all 38 benchmarks was 65.6.
- This score represented a 4.4 percentage point improvement over Qwen3.6-A3B (65.6 vs. 61.2), which was the strongest comparable baseline.
- Compared to the previous-generation Hy-Embodied-0.5 MoT-2B, Hy-Embodied-VLM-1.0 A3B improved average performance by 8.4 percentage points (65.6 vs. 57.2).
- Notably, despite activating only 3 billion parameters, the model achieved performance comparable to that of a previous-generation model with 32 billion activated parameters (Hy-Embodied-0.5 A30B).

#### 4.2. Action-Relevant State Understanding

On 23 benchmarks assessing action-relevant state understanding (covering physical and semantic perception, spatial understanding, and robotics-centric understanding), Hy-Embodied-VLM-1.0 A3B achieved an average score of 68.6.
- This performance exceeded Qwen3.6-A3B by 4.9 percentage points (68.6 vs. 63.7).
- The model secured the first rank on 11 of these 23 benchmarks and the second rank on 9 others, demonstrating capabilities across fine-grained perception, spatial reasoning, and robotics-centric grounding.
- Qualitative examples included accurate counting of objects, precise pointing to specified items, grounding objects based on compositional attributes, recognizing number types, inferring 3D spatial relations and object orientation, performing multi-view spatial reasoning, identifying task-specific affordances (e.g., where a bowl can be filled, where a mug should be grasped), and decomposing manipulation videos into subtasks.

#### 4.3. Action-Transition Reasoning

For the eight benchmarks focused on action-transition reasoning (including interaction understanding, action decision and grounding, and local robotic task reasoning), Hy-Embodied-VLM-1.0 A3B attained an average score of 64.1.
- This represented a 5.3 percentage point lead over Embodied-R1.5 (64.1 vs. 58.8), the strongest parameter-comparable baseline in this category.
- The model ranked first on five of these eight benchmarks (FineBench, CrossHOI-Bench, PIO, ShareRobot-Bench-Trajectory, and RoboBench-MCQ) and second on VABench-Point.
- These results indicated proficiency in interaction recognition, action grounding, trajectory prediction, and local robotic decision-making.
- Qualitative observations included identifying social dynamics and attended objects from gaze/pose, selecting appropriate next actions in sequences (e.g., placing a lid on a container), inferring required directional motions for object insertion, and reasoning about physical feasibility and viewpoint-dependent effects of actions.

#### 4.4. Sequential and Adaptive Reasoning

On seven benchmarks evaluating sequential and adaptive reasoning (encompassing history- and progress-aware reasoning, long-horizon planning and composition, and reflection, repair, and recovery), Hy-Embodied-VLM-1.0 A3B achieved an average of 57.4.
- This outscored Qwen3.6-A3B by 1.4 percentage points (57.4 vs. 55.9).
- The model achieved the highest performance on VLABench, RoboBench-Planning, and RoboFAC, and the second-highest on SITE-Bench-Video.
- These findings suggested competitive capabilities in temporal understanding, multi-step task composition, self-reflection, and recovery from failures.
- Qualitative examples demonstrated long-horizon planning (e.g., completing a dough-shaping task), progress-aware reasoning (e.g., selecting the next subtask based on package contents, ordering shuffled video frames), and reflection (e.g., diagnosing a failed carrot-cutting attempt due to lack of contact, proposing a recovery plan for failed cylinder insertion).

#### 4.5. Vision-Language Navigation (Closed-Loop Deployment)

- **Instruction-Following Navigation on R2R-CE (val-unseen, RGB-only, map-free):**
 - Hy-Embodied-VLM-1.0 A3B achieved the highest Success Rate (SR) of 57.9%, Success weighted by Path Length (SPL) of 54.2%, and the lowest Navigation Error (NE) of 4.5 meters. It ranked second in Oracle Success (OS) at 66.4%.
 - Compared to the strongest baseline for each metric, it improved SR by 0.4 and SPL by 2.1 percentage points, and reduced NE by 0.3 meters. The higher SR and lower NE, despite a slightly lower OS, indicated more reliable terminal-state estimation and path efficiency.
- **Zero-Shot Object-Goal Navigation on Matterport3D (MP3D, RGB-D):**
 - The model achieved an SR of 38.3% and an SPL of 11.2%.
 - This performance surpassed the Qwen3.6-35B-A3B baseline by 2.2 percentage points in SR and 1.4 points in SPL, providing evidence of effective category-conditioned planning, persistent search, and efficient termination in novel environments without specific parameter adaptation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of Hy-Embodied-VLM-1.0 represents an advancement in the field of embodied artificial intelligence, particularly concerning the development of agents for physical-world interaction.

A key significance of this work is its demonstration of **computational efficiency alongside strong capabilities**. By utilizing a Mixture-of-Experts architecture that activates only approximately 3 billion parameters during inference, the model maintains the representational capacity typically associated with much larger models while incurring significantly lower computational costs. This efficiency is important for the practical deployment of embodied agents in latency-sensitive real-world scenarios.

The systematic approach, guided by an **action-centric capability taxonomy**, provides a structured framework for developing and evaluating embodied intelligence. The model's consistent performance across this taxonomy—spanning action-relevant state understanding, action-transition reasoning, and sequential and adaptive reasoning—suggests a robust and broad competence in physical-world interaction. This includes fine-grained perception, spatial reasoning, interaction understanding, grounded action selection, and complex temporal planning with reflection and recovery mechanisms.

The **demonstrated performance in closed-loop vision-language navigation tasks**, including instruction-following and zero-shot object-goal navigation in unseen environments, indicates the model's ability to integrate perception, action reasoning, and adaptive planning into persistent agent behavior. This is crucial for real-world applications where agents must operate autonomously over extended periods and adapt to dynamic conditions.

Overall, Hy-Embodied-VLM-1.0 contributes an **efficient and potentially practical foundation model for future physical-world agents**. Its advancements in both capability and efficiency could facilitate the development of more versatile and deployable robotic systems and embodied AI applications that require sophisticated understanding and interaction with the physical environment.
