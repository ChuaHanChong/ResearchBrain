# Low-Rank Continual Personalization of Diffusion Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Warsaw University of Technology and IDEAS NCBR investigated methods for continual personalization of diffusion models using Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) without storing past task adapters. Their analysis revealed that reinitializing LoRA weights for each new task and merging them into the base model offers the best balance for acquiring new concepts while retaining previously learned knowledge.

## Problem

- Personalizing diffusion models sequentially using techniques like LoRA leads to catastrophic forgetting, where the model loses the ability to generate previously learned concepts.
- The storage of multiple task-specific LoRA adapters for merging or regularization becomes impractical due to memory constraints as the number of personalized concepts grows.
- Existing continual learning solutions for generative models often rely on retaining past data or adapters, which is not feasible in strict continual learning environments.

## Method

- The study evaluates four distinct strategies for managing ==LoRA== adapters in a strict continual learning regime: naive fine-tuning, standard reinitialization and merging, orthogonal reinitialization and merging, and magnitude-based selection of weights (MAGMAX adaptation).
- These strategies were applied to customize a Stable ==Diffusion== XL model for 10 sequential object and 10 sequential style personalization tasks.
- The approaches focus on adapter initialization and merging without relying on prior preservation regularization or access to past task data/adapters.

## Results

- Naïve continual fine-tuning exhibited the highest catastrophic forgetting, with initial task performance degrading significantly towards the base model's capabilities.
- The 'Merge & Initialization' method achieved the highest average performance scores across both object (0.675 CLIP-I, 0.457 DINO) and style (0.385 CSD, 0.285 DINO) personalization tasks, while maintaining low forgetting.
- Magnitude-based merging achieved the lowest average forgetting for both objects (0.018 CLIP-I, 0.036 DINO) and styles (0.093 CSD, 0.052 DINO), highlighting its strong stability, albeit with generally lower average scores.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Reinitializing LoRA weights for each new task and merging them into the continually updated base model (Merge & Initialization) provides an effective balance between learning new concepts (plasticity) and retaining old ones (stability).
> - Magnitude-based selection of LoRA weights effectively mitigates forgetting but can limit the model's capacity to adapt to new, precise generative traits.
> - Orthogonal reinitialization, while aimed at reducing conflicts, resulted in high forgetting for style personalization, suggesting that simple reinitialization might be more effective in certain adaptation contexts.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of image generation has experienced advancements with the introduction of diffusion models (DMs), which exhibit capabilities in synthesizing detailed objects and integrating complex styles. Personalization techniques, such as Dreambooth and Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA), have enabled the customization of these pre-trained models to generate novel concepts from limited data by fine-tuning a subset of parameters.

However, a challenge in applying these techniques sequentially across multiple personalization tasks (e.g., introducing new objects or styles) is catastrophic forgetting. This phenomenon describes the degradation of previously acquired knowledge when a model is trained on new tasks, often due to interference between task-specific adapters. Prior research has addressed this by carefully merging trained adapters, as seen in methods like ZipLoRA (Shah et al., 2023) and B-LoRA (Frenkel et al., 2024), which may involve optimization or targeting specific attention layers. These approaches often necessitate the availability of all past adapters during the merging process.

This work distinguishes itself by operating under a "strict continual learning regime." In this regime, the system does not retain previous task adapters; only the updated model (or model with a single adapter) is carried forward to the next task. This constraint addresses the practical limitation that storing numerous adapters, even in a low-rank form, can become memory-intensive as the number of personalized concepts grows. For instance, eight LoRA weight matrices of rank 64 can approximate the size of all adapted parameters in a Stable Diffusion XL model.

While other continual learning studies for diffusion models focus on additional regularization techniques (e.g., C-LoRA, STAMINA, CIDM, Jha et al., 2024) to consolidate knowledge, this paper investigates the fundamental impact of adapter initialization and merging strategies. It also adapts concepts from discriminative modeling, such as magnitude-based parameter selection (MAGMAX), to the generative domain of diffusion models, exploring their efficacy in mitigating forgetting without reliance on extensive memory resources for past task data or adapters.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the limitations of current personalization methods for diffusion models when applied in a sequential, continual learning scenario. While techniques like Dreambooth combined with LoRA allow for the efficient customization of generative models, their application across a series of tasks to integrate new objects or styles often leads to catastrophic forgetting. This issue arises from mutual interference among the adapters as the model adapts to new concepts, causing it to lose the ability to generate previously learned concepts.

A critical challenge highlighted by the authors is the impracticality of storing all task-specific adapters when dealing with numerous personalization tasks. Although LoRA significantly reduces the number of parameters to be adapted, accumulating adapters for many objects or styles still demands substantial memory. Existing solutions for mitigating forgetting often rely on having access to all previously trained adapters to perform effective merging or regularization. This requirement makes them unsuitable for scenarios where memory constraints or privacy concerns dictate that past task data or adapter weights cannot be retained.

Given these challenges, the key objectives of this study are:

- **Investigate Continual Personalization under a Strict Regime:** To evaluate the performance of personalization techniques for diffusion models within a strict continual learning framework. This regime mandates that, at any given time, only the current model or the model coupled with its most recent adapter is available, precluding access to adapters from prior tasks for subsequent merging operations.
- **Evaluate Naïve Continual Fine-tuning:** To establish a baseline by assessing the efficacy of a straightforward, sequential fine-tuning approach where LoRA adapter weights from a previous task are directly updated with new task data without reinitialization.
- **Compare Novel Initialization and Merging Strategies:** To analyze and compare the performance of three alternative methods designed to mitigate forgetting, relative to the naïve approach. These methods involve distinct strategies for initializing and merging LoRA adapters:
 - **Sequential Merging with Standard Reinitialization:** Creating a new LoRA adapter with standard initialization for each task and merging it with the base model before proceeding to the next task.
 - **Sequential Merging with Orthogonal Reinitialization:** Similar to the above, but initializing new LoRA adapters with orthogonal weights to minimize conflict with previously learned adaptations.
 - **Magnitude-based Selection of Weights:** Adapting a method (MAGMAX) that selects parameters with the highest magnitude from current and accumulated adapters to consolidate knowledge.
- **Characterize Plasticity and Stability:** To analyze the trade-offs exhibited by each evaluated technique in terms of plasticity (the ability to learn new tasks) and stability (the ability to retain knowledge of past tasks) within the continually adapted model.

By systematically evaluating these fundamental aspects of adapter management, the researchers aim to identify effective strategies for continual personalization of diffusion models that are viable under memory-constrained conditions, contributing to the development of more robust and scalable generative AI systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The study investigates continual personalization of diffusion models using LoRA under a strict continual learning paradigm. The core methodology involves training a base text-to-image diffusion model (Stable Diffusion XL) on a sequence of personalization tasks for objects and styles, employing various strategies for LoRA adapter initialization and merging.

**Base Model and Adaptation:**
The experiments utilize the Stable Diffusion XL model. Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) is applied to the W_Q, W_K, W_V, and W_O matrices within both the self-attention and cross-attention layers of the U-Net architecture. The LoRA rank (r) is consistently set to 64 across all experiments.

**Continual Learning Setup:**
The experiments are conducted in a class-incremental setup, where the model is fine-tuned on 10 consecutive tasks. Performance is averaged over 4 random orderings of these tasks, with 2 random seeds per ordering, totaling 8 experimental runs for each method.

**Datasets and Tasks:**
- **Object Personalization:** The DreamBooth dataset (Ruiz et al., 2023) is used, comprising images of 30 objects across 15 classes. For the experiments, 10 distinct object classes are selected, each represented by a unique object and associated with a unique DreamBooth token for fine-tuning.
- **Style Personalization:** The UnlearnCanvas collection (Zhang et al., 2024) is employed, featuring 20 objects in 60 different styles. For the experiments, 10 distinct styles are chosen, each paired with one object and five corresponding images. Unlike object personalization, real style names are used in prompts rather than tokens. A preliminary study on prompt templates (Appendix C) indicated that using full style names like "{name} image of {}" yielded better results than token-based prompts such as "image of {} in {token} style."

**Training Details:**
For each task, the model is trained with a learning rate of 1e-5, for 850 training steps, and a batch size of 1. Crucially, the prior preservation regularization technique (as used in DreamBooth) is not leveraged during fine-tuning.

**Evaluated Continual Personalization Methods:**
The study evaluates four distinct approaches to manage LoRA adapters in a continual learning context:

1. **Naïve Continual Fine-Tuning of LoRA Adapters:** In this baseline approach, the LoRA adapter weights from the previous task {A_{t-1}, B_{t-1}} are directly fine-tuned on the data from the current task t. There is no reinitialization of the adapter parameters. The final, continually updated adapter is merged into the base model only after all T tasks are completed.
2. **Continual Adapters Merging and Standard LoRA Reinitialization (Merge & Initialization):** For each new task t, a fresh LoRA adapter {A_t, B_t} is created with standard initialization (A initialized from a normal distribution N(0, I), B initialized to zeros). This new adapter is then fine-tuned on the current task's data. At the completion of task t, this newly trained adapter is merged with the *base model* (which incorporates all previous merged adaptations). The resulting merged model then serves as the starting point for the next task t+1, where a new LoRA adapter is again initialized from scratch.
3. **Continual Adapters Merging and Orthogonalized Reinitialization (Merge & Orthogonal Initialization):** This method extends the "Merge & Initialization" approach. For task t, B_t weights are initialized to zeros. The A_t weights are initialized such that their columns are orthogonal to the columns of the *sum* of all previously merged A matrices (A_{1...t-1}). This orthogonalization is achieved using Singular Value Decomposition (SVD), aiming to direct the fine-tuning process toward non-conflicting parameter spaces.
4. **Magnitude-based Selection of LoRA Weights (Magnitude-based Merging):** This approach adapts the MAGMAX method. After each task t, it consolidates knowledge by selecting the parameters with the highest magnitude from the already accumulated adapter {A_{1...t-1}, B_{1...t-1}} and the newly trained adapter {A_t, B_t}. The resulting merged adapter {A_{1...t}, B_{1...t}} is then carried forward. For a new task, the existing selected adapters are temporarily merged into the frozen base model, a new adapter is initialized standardly, optimized, and then subjected to the magnitude-based selection procedure.

**Evaluation Metrics:**
The performance of the fine-tuned models is assessed using image alignment metrics based on cosine similarity between feature embeddings of generated images and reference images.
- **Object Alignment:** Measured using features extracted from DINO (Caron et al., 2021) and CLIP (Radford et al., 2021) models.
- **Style Similarity:** Measured using DINO and CSD (Somepalli et al., 2024a) feature extractors. CSD is used instead of CLIP for style due to CLIP's noted bias towards image content.

For continual learning performance, two aggregate metrics are reported for the final model after all 10 tasks:
- **Average Score (S_T):** The mean performance (e.g., CLIP-I, DINO, CSD score) across all T tasks, with S_i^j representing the performance on task j of a model trained on i consecutive tasks. S_T = (1/T) * Σ_{j=1 to T} S_T^j.
- **Average Forgetting (F_T):** Measures the average drop in performance on past tasks, calculated as the average of the maximum observed performance minus the final performance for each past task. F_T = (1/(T-1)) * Σ_{j=1 to T-1} max_{1≤k≤T} (S_k^j - S_T^j).

N=8 images are generated for each of P=5 prompts, resulting in 40 samples per task, with 50 denoising diffusion steps.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental analysis consistently demonstrated that naïve continual fine-tuning of LoRA adapters leads to a significant degradation of previously acquired knowledge, whereas the proposed techniques generally mitigate this catastrophic forgetting.

**Overall Performance on Object and Style Personalization:**

- **Naïve Continual Fine-Tuning:** This approach showed the highest forgetting in both object and style personalization (Table 1 and 2). For object personalization, its DINO average score (0.402) was notably lower than Merge & Initialization, and its forgetting metrics were high (0.063 CLIP-I, 0.144 DINO). For style, it also exhibited high forgetting (0.184 CSD, 0.136 DINO), with its performance degrading towards the base model's capabilities over time (Figure 2).
- **Magnitude-based Merging:** This method achieved the lowest average forgetting for both object (0.018 CLIP-I, 0.036 DINO) and style (0.093 CSD, 0.052 DINO) tasks. This indicates high stability and strong knowledge retention. However, this came at the expense of lower average scores in most metrics, particularly for CLIP-I (0.643 for object) and CSD (0.289 for style). The authors suggest this indicates limited adaptation capabilities for new generative traits when a constrained number of weights are selected.
- **Merge & Orthogonal Initialization:** This approach generally achieved high plasticity, demonstrating good performance on newly trained tasks immediately after adaptation (Figures 9, 14, 19, 24 in Appendix B). For object personalization, its average scores were competitive with Naïve (0.673 CLIP-I, 0.403 DINO). However, it exhibited the highest average forgetting in style personalization (0.204 CSD, 0.141 DINO) and comparable high forgetting for objects (0.072 CLIP-I, 0.162 DINO), suggesting that while it readily adapts, it struggles to retain past knowledge when task vectors become more aligned, as hypothesized for style tasks.
- **Merge & Initialization (Standard Reinitialization):** This method demonstrated a balanced performance, achieving the highest average scores in both object (0.675 CLIP-I, 0.457 DINO) and style (0.385 CSD, 0.285 DINO) personalization. It also showed the second-lowest average forgetting for both tasks (object: 0.026 CLIP-I, 0.056 DINO; style: 0.131 CSD, 0.085 DINO). This indicates a suitable balance between plasticity (learning new tasks) and stability (retaining past knowledge). The researchers hypothesize that the reinitialization of LoRA weights in each task allows for sparse adaptation of the base model, contributing to this effect.

**Specific Observations:**

- **Plasticity and Stability Trade-offs:** The results highlight a trade-off. Merge & Orthogonal Initialization prioritizes plasticity, adapting well to new tasks but often forgetting older ones. Magnitude-based Merging prioritizes stability, preserving past knowledge effectively but with a reduced capacity for learning new, precise generative traits. Merge & Initialization appears to strike an effective balance.
- **Performance on First Task Over Time (Figure 2):** For both object and style personalization, the naive approach caused the performance on the first learned task to degrade significantly over subsequent tasks, approaching the base model's performance. The other methods maintained a higher DINO score for the initial task, indicating better knowledge retention.
- **Impact of Prompting for Style (Appendix C):** An investigation into prompt engineering for style personalization revealed that using full style names (e.g., "{name} image of {}") resulted in superior average scores and lower average forgetting compared to using generic tokens (e.g., "image of {} in {token} style"). This suggests that prompt formulation plays a role in the effectiveness of style adaptation.
- **Backward Transfer:** In some instances, particularly with the "Merge & Initialization" method for object personalization (e.g., tasks 1 and 2 in Figure 9), a phenomenon of backward transfer was observed, where performance on a previous task improved after adapting to a new task.

In summary, the study's findings indicate that while naïvely continually fine-tuning LoRA adapters leads to substantial forgetting, strategies involving reinitialization and careful merging can mitigate this issue. The "Merge & Initialization" method, which involves reinitializing LoRA weights for each new task and merging them into the continually updated base model, emerged as the most effective overall approach for balancing the acquisition of new knowledge with the retention of old knowledge in continual personalization of diffusion models.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the understanding and development of continual learning methods for generative models, specifically in the context of diffusion models. By focusing on the fundamental properties of LoRA adapter initialization and merging under a strict continual learning regime, the study addresses a practical challenge where the storage of all previous task adapters is infeasible.

The significance and potential impact of this work include:

- **Advancement in Continual Generative Learning:** The study systematically evaluates basic, yet critical, components of continual personalization (initialization and merging) for diffusion models. This provides a foundational understanding for building more sophisticated continual learning systems in the generative domain, moving beyond reliance on complex regularization or full model storage.
- **Practical Implications for Personalization:** Identifying "Merge & Initialization" as a robust and balanced approach offers a direct and implementable strategy for practitioners. This method provides a way to continually update personalized diffusion models for new objects or styles without accruing an ever-growing set of adapter weights, making it more scalable for real-world applications with memory constraints.
- **Insight into Plasticity-Stability Dilemma:** The clear characterization of the trade-offs between plasticity and stability across different merging and initialization strategies is valuable. The finding that orthogonal initialization, while promoting plasticity, can lead to higher forgetting, and that magnitude-based merging, while ensuring stability, might limit new concept acquisition, informs future research directions for optimizing this balance.
- **Guidance for Future Research:** The paper's hypothesis regarding the positive impact of reinitialization due to sparse adaptation opens avenues for further investigation into the underlying mechanisms of LoRA's behavior in continual learning. Understanding why standard reinitialization outperforms orthogonal approaches in style tasks, for instance, could lead to more nuanced adapter design.
- **Influence on Prompt Engineering for Generative Models:** The observation that prompt formulation significantly impacts style personalization performance provides a practical guideline for users and developers of personalized diffusion models. This highlights the interplay between model architecture, training methodology, and user input in achieving desired generative outcomes.

Overall, this research provides practical insights and a validated baseline strategy for continually customizing diffusion models in memory-constrained environments. It contributes to making generative AI systems more adaptive and scalable, enabling them to learn new concepts over time without extensive forgetting or prohibitive resource demands.
