# Low-Rank Continual Personalization of Diffusion Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Warsaw University of Technology and IDEAS NCBR investigated methods for continual personalization of diffusion models using Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) without storing past task adapters. Their analysis revealed that reinitializing LoRA weights for each new task and merging them into the base model offers the best balance for acquiring new concepts while retaining previously learned knowledge.

## Problem

- Personalizing diffusion models sequentially using techniques like LoRA leads to catastrophic forgetting, where the model loses the ability to generate previously learned concepts.
- The storage of multiple task-specific LoRA adapters for merging or regularization becomes impractical due to memory constraints as the number of personalized concepts grows.
- Existing continual learning solutions for generative models often rely on retaining past data or adapters, which is not feasible in strict continual learning environments.

## Method

- The study evaluates four distinct strategies for managing ==LoRA== adapters in a strict continual learning regime: naive fine-tuning, standard reinitialization and merging, orthogonal reinitialization and merging, and magnitude-based selection of weights (MAGMAX adaptation).
- These strategies were applied to customize a Stable ==Diffusion== XL model for 10 sequential object and 10 sequential style personalization tasks.
- The approaches focus on adapter initialization and merging without relying on prior preservation regularization or access to past task data/adapters.

## Results

- Naïve continual fine-tuning exhibited the highest catastrophic forgetting, with initial task performance degrading significantly towards the base model's capabilities.
- The 'Merge & Initialization' method achieved the highest average performance scores across both object (0.675 CLIP-I, 0.457 DINO) and style (0.385 CSD, 0.285 DINO) personalization tasks, while maintaining low forgetting.
- Magnitude-based merging achieved the lowest average forgetting for both objects (0.018 CLIP-I, 0.036 DINO) and styles (0.093 CSD, 0.052 DINO), highlighting its strong stability, albeit with generally lower average scores.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Reinitializing LoRA weights for each new task and merging them into the continually updated base model (Merge & Initialization) provides an effective balance between learning new concepts (plasticity) and retaining old ones (stability).
> - Magnitude-based selection of LoRA weights effectively mitigates forgetting but can limit the model's capacity to adapt to new, precise generative traits.
> - Orthogonal reinitialization, while aimed at reducing conflicts, resulted in high forgetting for style personalization, suggesting that simple reinitialization might be more effective in certain adaptation contexts.
