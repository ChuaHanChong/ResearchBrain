# Recursive Vision Language Models for General Symbolic Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Concordia University and Mila introduced R-Qwen, a framework that fine-tunes a pretrained Qwen LLM using a recursive refinement loop for symbolic reasoning puzzles. It demonstrated state-of-the-art accuracy across diverse benchmarks, including 88.5% on Sudoku and 82.1% on ARC-1, outperforming larger LLMs and prior recursive models, while employing Hierarchical Supervision Weighting to enhance training efficiency and stability.

## Problem

- Large Language Models (LLMs) struggle with complex symbolic reasoning tasks requiring systematic search, iterative refinement, backtracking, and precise structured outputs (e.g., Sudoku, mazes, ARC).
- LLMs' fixed-depth autoregressive reasoning leads to error propagation and limits their ability to perform extensive iterative procedures.
- Existing recursive models are often task-specific, operate in latent space, or do not effectively leverage the general knowledge and reasoning priors embedded in large-scale pretrained LLMs.

## Method

- **R-Qwen** integrates a pretrained Qwen LLM backbone with a ==recursive candidate refinement== loop, adapted parameter-efficiently using ==Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA)==.
- It casts various symbolic reasoning tasks as a unified iterative refinement problem, where the model generates and a task-specific function projects explicit candidate solutions at each step.
- A ==Hierarchical Supervision Weighting (HSW)== mechanism is introduced to stabilize and accelerate training by assigning higher weights to earlier refinement steps, reducing gradient variance, and improving convergence.

## Results

- R-Qwen achieved **88.5%** accuracy on Sudoku and **93.1%** on Maze, outperforming Grok-4-thinking by 26.7% and 19.6% respectively, and TRM by 13.8% and 7.8%.
- On ARC-1 and ARC-2, R-Qwen reached **82.1%** and **33.5%** accuracy, substantially exceeding Grok-4-thinking (66.7% and 16.0%) and TRM (44.6% and 7.8%).
- The Hierarchical Supervision Weighting (HSW) reduced training time for the Qwen backbone by approximately **2.04x** (from 45.8h to 22.5h) while improving accuracy by **3.4%**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Combining structured iterative computation (recursive reasoning) with broad pretrained knowledge (LLM priors) yields superior performance on complex symbolic reasoning tasks.
> - Refining explicit, interpretable candidate solutions through a deterministic constraint projection function enables effective task-specific supervision and robust state transitions.
> - Hierarchical Supervision Weighting (HSW) is crucial for improving training efficiency and stability in deep recursive models, significantly reducing training time while maintaining or improving accuracy.
> - The learned recursive refinement policy exhibits robustness at inference time, maintaining stability and improving performance even with additional steps beyond the training recursion horizon.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses a persistent challenge in the field of large language models (LLMs): their performance on hard symbolic reasoning tasks. While LLMs have demonstrated capabilities in knowledge- and language-intensive domains, tasks requiring systematic search, iterative refinement, backtracking, and exact structured outputs (such as Sudoku, maze pathfinding, and the Abstraction and Reasoning Corpus (ARC-AGI)) remain difficult for them. This difficulty is attributed to their fixed-depth autoregressive reasoning, which can propagate early errors and limits the execution of extensive iterative procedures.

Existing approaches to enhance LLM reasoning include Chain-of-Thought prompting and increased test-time computation, but these methods can be brittle on search-heavy problems or require substantial supervision. Recursive models, such as Hierarchical Reasoning Model (HRM) and Tiny Recursive Model (TRM), represent a complementary approach. These models enhance effective computational depth through iterative latent-state refinement without a proportional increase in parameter count. However, prior recursive models are often task-specific, operate in latent space, require specialized architectures, or do not leverage the linguistic and reasoning priors embedded in large-scale pretrained language models. Other related work explores looped computation, fixed-point refinement, or recursive self-calling, but these often involve architectural modifications or recursion over prompts rather than explicit candidate solutions.

This research aims to bridge the gap by integrating the strengths of both paradigms: the iterative, structured problem-solving capability of recursive models and the rich general knowledge and reasoning priors of pretrained LLMs. It positions itself as a task-general framework that allows a pretrained autoregressive model to learn a reusable recursive refinement policy without requiring backbone architectural modifications, while also complementing approaches that focus on visual structure and modality-aware correction for tasks like ARC.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a task-general recursive reasoning framework that can effectively solve challenging symbolic reasoning puzzles by repeatedly refining candidate solutions. The authors are motivated by the limitations of current LLMs in these domains and the untapped potential of combining recursive mechanisms with pretrained language priors.

Specifically, the key objectives and motivations include:
- **Addressing LLM limitations in symbolic reasoning:** Standard autoregressive LLMs struggle with tasks requiring systematic search, backtracking, and precise structured outputs due to their fixed computational depth and the propensity for early errors to propagate. The authors aim to overcome this by introducing an iterative refinement process.
- **Leveraging pretrained language model priors:** While small recursive networks like HRM and TRM have shown promise, they are typically trained from scratch and do not benefit from the extensive knowledge and reasoning capabilities acquired by large pretrained LLMs. The research seeks to integrate recursive reasoning with a pretrained LLM backbone to harness these priors.
- **Developing a task-general framework:** Current recursive models are often task-specific. A core motivation is to create a unified recursive search-and-refine framework applicable across diverse symbolic reasoning tasks (e.g., Sudoku, Maze, ARC, crosswords) without requiring unique architectural modifications for each.
- **Explicit candidate refinement:** Unlike methods that recurse over latent states or prompts, the authors aim for recursion over explicit, interpretable, and structured candidate solutions. This approach allows for direct supervision and the application of task-specific constraints between refinement steps.
- **Improving training efficiency and stability:** Recursive models, especially when deeply supervised, can face issues with gradient variance, particularly from later, less informative steps. The authors are motivated to introduce an efficient training strategy that stabilizes optimization, reduces training time and cost, and focuses learning on the most impactful refinement steps.
- **Demonstrating the complementarity of approaches:** The research seeks to provide evidence that recursive reasoning mechanisms and pretrained language model priors are complementary, leading to improved performance on complex symbolic puzzle-solving beyond what either approach achieves in isolation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework, named R-Qwen, casts various symbolic reasoning puzzles as a unified iterative refinement problem. It fine-tunes a single autoregressive LLM as a shared refinement operator.

**Core Framework Principles:**
- **Unified Task Representation:** Every task instance is represented as a triple (x, c, y\*), where 'x' is the serialized initial state (a string over a task alphabet Σ with ∅ for unfilled positions), 'c' is optional auxiliary context, and 'y\*' is the serialized target solution. Crucially, the set of clamped positions G(x) defines values that must be preserved.
- **Pretrained Backbone with Parameter-Efficient Adaptation:** R-Qwen uses a pretrained Qwen backbone, specifically Qwen3.5-27B. To adapt this backbone parameter-efficiently, Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) is employed. Only the low-rank adapters (∆θ) are trained, keeping the original backbone weights frozen. This allows for efficient training with a small fraction of trainable parameters (12M trainable parameters on a 27B backbone).
- **Recursive Candidate Refinement Loop:**
 1. **Input Construction:** At each step *t*, the model receives a chat-formatted prompt that includes the original problem (x), any context (c), the current serialized candidate solution (ˆy(t-1)), and the current refinement index (*t*) out of *N* total steps.
 2. **Generation:** The LoRA-adapted Qwen model generates an improved candidate (˜y(t)) via greedy decoding.
 3. **Constraint Projection:** The raw generated string ˜y(t) is passed through a deterministic, task-specific constraint projection function Πx(·). This function enforces hard structural constraints (e.g., preserving clamped initial values, ensuring outputs are within the alphabet, handling malformed outputs by reverting to the previous prediction). This guarantees a well-formed and feasible state for the next recursion step.
 4. **Feedback:** The projected candidate (ˆy(t)) then becomes the input for the next refinement step.

**Training Strategy:**
- **Deep Supervision:** A supervised loss is attached to every refinement step, not just the final output. The model is teacher-forced to emit the full target solution y\* at each step, and a token-level cross-entropy loss is computed only over the target completion (prompt tokens are masked). Gradients flow only through the current step, with previous candidate states detached.
- **Hierarchical Supervision Weighting (HSW):** This mechanism replaces uniform per-step weighting in standard deep supervision with an exponential decay (w_t = λ^(t-1) / Z_λ, where λ ∈ (0, 1]). Earlier refinement steps receive larger supervision weights than later ones. The motivation is to reduce gradient variance by emphasizing early, high-information corrections, thereby improving the signal-to-noise ratio and accelerating convergence.
- **Training Efficiency Mechanisms:**
 - **Supervision-Depth Curriculum:** The maximum number of supervised refinement steps (N_sup(e)) is gradually increased during training epochs. This stabilizes early training and exposes the model to longer trajectories later.
 - **Amortized Candidate Generation:** Autoregressive decoding, a costly operation, is performed only on a subset of steps (e.g., every *m*-th step and the final step). On intermediate steps, the candidate is carried forward, reducing computational cost while still applying deep supervision.
 - **Per-Instance Early Stopping:** For instances that reach their target solution, loss computation and decoding are stopped, focusing computational resources on unsolved examples.

**Inference Strategy:**
- At test time, the learned refinement operator is applied for a fixed horizon of *K* steps. The process starts with the original problem (ˆy(0) = x) and iteratively applies the generation-projection update from training. The same constraint projection Πx is used, ensuring all intermediate states are feasible.
- Metrics include position-level accuracy (fraction of correct cells/tokens) and exact-match accuracy (whole-solution correctness), evaluated at the final step and across all *t* steps to analyze convergence.

The same training algorithm, hyperparameters, and loss function are used for all tasks, with only task-specific serialization and constraint projection changing.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The R-Qwen framework was evaluated across eight challenging benchmarks spanning constraint satisfaction, path planning, abstract visual transformation, and language-grounded grid completion: Sudoku, Maze, ARC-AGI-1, ARC-AGI-2, ARC-AGI, BARC-100, Re-ARC, and CrossWordBench.

**Comparative Performance:**
- **Sudoku, Maze, ARC-1, and ARC-2:** R-Qwen consistently outperformed prior recursive reasoning models (HRM, TRM, LoopFormer) and substantially larger LLMs (e.g., GPT-5, Grok-4-thinking, Deepseek R1, Claude 3.7).
 - On Sudoku, R-Qwen achieved 88.5% accuracy, surpassing Grok-4-thinking by 26.7% and TRM by 13.8%.
 - On Maze, it reached 93.1% accuracy, outperforming Grok-4-thinking by 19.6% and TRM by 7.8%.
 - For ARC-1, R-Qwen achieved 82.1% accuracy, significantly higher than Grok-4-thinking (66.7%), VARC (60.4%), and TRM (44.6%).
 - On ARC-2, R-Qwen achieved 33.5% accuracy, a substantial improvement over Grok-4-thinking (16.0%) and TRM (7.8%).
- **Visual-Language Puzzles (ARC-AGI, BARC-100, Re-ARC):** R-Qwen demonstrated superior performance across these abstract reasoning benchmarks.
 - It achieved 47.8% on ARC-AGI, 66.0% on BARC-100, and 41.0% on Re-ARC.
 - These results represent improvements of 5.6%, 7.0%, and 5.0% respectively over the strong LLM baseline o4-mini, and even larger gains (25.8%, 14.0%, 20.0%) over the VLSR baseline.
- **Crossword Puzzle Task (CrossWordBench):** R-Qwen achieved the strongest overall accuracy on Word Coverage Rate (WCR) and Letter Coverage Rate (LCR) across both 7x7 and 14x14 grid sizes, improving upon previous best results.
 - On 7x7 grids, R-Qwen achieved 65.7% WCR and 72.8% LCR.
 - On 14x14 grids, it obtained 51.3% WCR and 55.0% LCR.
 - While competitive on Intersection Consistency Rate (ICR), o3-mini-high showed stronger performance in this specific metric.

**Ablation Studies:**
- **Number of Recursive Steps:** Performance consistently improved with more recursion steps, with the largest gains observed early on. Accuracy nearly saturated after approximately 8 steps. R-Qwen benefited more effectively from recursive refinement compared to LoopFormer, TRM, and HRM.
- **Training Efficiency (HSW):** Hierarchical Supervision Weighting (HSW) was shown to improve convergence speed while maintaining or improving accuracy.
 - For TRM, HSW reduced training time by approximately 2.09x (from 23.4h to 11.2h) while slightly improving accuracy by 0.6%.
 - For Qwen, HSW reduced training time by approximately 2.04x (from 45.8h to 22.5h) while improving accuracy by 3.4%.
 - HSW also outperformed the Halting Act (HA) method in both accuracy and convergence speed.
- **Evaluation Recursion Steps:** R-Qwen maintained stability and continued to improve with additional refinement steps during inference, even beyond its training recursion horizon. In contrast, LoopFormer, TRM, and HRM exhibited performance fluctuations or slight drops after certain recursion depths (around 10-16 steps).
- **Training–Evaluation Trade-off:**
 - Simply applying recursive refinement to a pretrained VLM at inference time without recursion-aware training did not improve performance, and sometimes even degraded it, consistent with observations that LLMs struggle with unguided self-revision.
 - Recursion-aware training enabled the model to learn an effective iterative refinement policy.
 - Longer training horizons did not monotonically improve performance, with an "overthinking" effect observed where accuracy increased, peaked, and then declined with excessive recursion (e.g., 20 steps).
 - Training with eight recursion steps yielded the best overall performance, demonstrating a balance between training depth and stability.
 - Applying a few additional recursion steps at inference time consistently yielded small improvements, indicating generalization of the learned refinement policy to slightly longer trajectories.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of R-Qwen marks a significant step in enhancing the symbolic reasoning capabilities of large language models. The key significance lies in successfully combining the structured, iterative problem-solving approach of recursive models with the rich, general-purpose priors of pretrained LLMs. This integration addresses a long-standing limitation of LLMs in tasks requiring systematic search, exact outputs, and iterative refinement.

**Key Impacts:**
- **Improved Performance on Hard Symbolic Tasks:** R-Qwen consistently achieves state-of-the-art results across a diverse set of challenging benchmarks (Sudoku, Maze, ARC-AGI, CrossWordBench). This demonstrates that recursive refinement, when properly integrated with pretrained models, can substantially outperform both prior recursive models and significantly larger, general-purpose LLMs and VLMs.
- **Task-Generality with Architectural Simplicity:** The framework is task-general, applying a unified recursive search-and-refine mechanism across heterogeneous symbolic and spatial tasks without requiring modifications to the LLM backbone architecture. This makes it a more versatile and scalable solution compared to task-specific or architecturally specialized recursive models.
- **Efficient and Stable Training:** The adaptation of Hierarchical Supervision Weighting (HSW) and the implementation of training efficiency mechanisms (supervision-depth curriculum, amortized generation, per-instance early stopping) lead to faster convergence, reduced training costs, and improved optimization stability. This makes the approach more practical for real-world application.
- **Robustness to Deeper Inference:** R-Qwen's ability to remain stable and even improve with additional refinement steps during inference, beyond its training horizon, suggests that it learns a more robust and generalizable refinement policy compared to some prior recursive models that exhibit performance degradation with excessive recursion.
- **Complementarity of Paradigms:** The findings reinforce the concept that structured iterative computation (recursive reasoning) and broad pre-trained knowledge (LLM priors) are complementary, offering a pathway to developing more capable and robust AI systems for complex reasoning tasks.

**Potential Future Directions:**
- The approach suggests potential for further gains by integrating explicit vision-language synergy mechanisms into the R-Qwen framework, as the current method is orthogonal to such synergy.
- Further investigation into the precise mechanisms that contribute to R-Qwen's inference-time robustness and the optimal balance between training and inference recursion horizons could lead to more refined models.
- The generalizability of this framework to other domains requiring iterative refinement and constraint satisfaction, beyond the evaluated benchmarks, could be explored.

In summary, R-Qwen represents a robust and effective framework for enhancing the symbolic reasoning capabilities of pretrained language models, offering a path toward more general and efficient problem-solving AI.
