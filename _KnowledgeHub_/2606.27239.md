# HumanoidUMI: Bridging Robot-Free Demonstrations and Humanoid Whole-Body Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from the Beijing Academy of Artificial Intelligence introduce HumanoidUMI, a portable and robot-free framework for collecting and learning humanoid whole-body manipulation skills from human demonstrations. This system successfully enables complex visuomotor behaviors on a Unitree G1 robot and significantly enhances data collection efficiency compared to traditional teleoperation, reducing reliance on operator expertise.

## Problem

- Traditional humanoid skill learning relies on resource-intensive robot-in-the-loop teleoperation, which is inefficient for large-scale data collection and requires expert operators and specialized hardware.
- Extending robot-free demonstration paradigms to full humanoid whole-body control, encompassing dynamic balance, posture adjustment, locomotion, and coordinated manipulation, presents complex challenges.
- Effectively translating human whole-body motion intent to humanoid robots requires addressing significant morphological and kinematic differences, as direct mapping often proves insufficient.

## Method

- A ==robot-free data collection== system that uses a PICO 4 VR setup for whole-body motion capture and custom UMI-inspired grippers with integrated fisheye cameras for wrist-view visuals and gripper state.
- A hierarchical visuomotor control pipeline comprising a high-level Diffusion Policy that predicts sparse keypoint motions, a Spatial Keypoint Retargeting (SKR) module, and a low-level learned whole-body controller.
- The SKR module converts sparse human keypoint trajectories into morphology-aware, kinematically feasible robot whole-body references using anisotropic adjustments and constrained inverse kinematics.

## Results

- HumanoidUMI successfully executed complex visuomotor tasks on a Unitree G1 humanoid robot, including pick-and-place, bimanual manipulation, dynamic ball throwing, and coupled loco-manipulation like under-table waste disposal and walking coffee delivery.
- Ablation studies confirmed the criticality of the Spatial Keypoint Retargeting (SKR) module and latency matching, with the inclusion of knee keypoints significantly improving performance for lower-body intensive tasks.
- The framework demonstrated **1.8x** to **12.4x** higher valid demonstration throughput compared to state-of-the-art teleoperation (TWIST2), especially for loco-manipulation tasks, and showed reduced dependence on operator skill.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Sparse spatial keypoints (pelvis, TCPs, feet, and optionally knees) can effectively represent human whole-body manipulation intent, enabling morphology-aware retargeting for humanoid robots.
> - A hierarchical control architecture that decouples high-level visuomotor reasoning from low-level robot-specific execution enhances interpretability, robustness, and transferability of learned skills.
> - Utilizing portable VR devices for robot-free data collection substantially improves the efficiency and accessibility of acquiring diverse, high-quality demonstration data for complex humanoid tasks, including loco-manipulation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of humanoid robotics is progressing towards more capable and autonomous systems, often relying on high-quality demonstration data for skill learning. This work is positioned within the broader research landscape addressing the significant challenge of efficiently acquiring such data, particularly for complex whole-body behaviors that necessitate coordinated perception, locomotion, and manipulation.

Historically, humanoid robot skill learning has largely depended on robot-in-the-loop teleoperation. Methods like TWIST2, CLONE, and Touch Dreaming exemplify this paradigm, where human operators directly control physical robots to generate training trajectories. While effective in providing embodiment-consistent data, these teleoperation approaches are constrained by several factors: they require direct access to expensive and often specialized robot hardware, demand operators with expert knowledge and skills, necessitate extensive safety supervision, and are inherently inefficient for large-scale data collection due to the time and effort involved in repeated hardware operation. This leads to high costs and limits the scalability of data acquisition.

In response to these limitations, robot-free or human-centric demonstration collection has emerged as a promising alternative. The Universal Manipulation Interface (UMI) series of works have demonstrated that portable handheld devices, coupled with wrist-centric visual observations, can effectively replace conventional teleoperation for robot-arm manipulation. This approach facilitates more efficient and "in-the-wild" data collection. Similar UMI-style principles have been extended to other robot types, including quadruped and aerial robots, and to dual-arm mobile manipulation with systems like HoMMI.

However, extending the UMI paradigm to full humanoid whole-body manipulation presents unique challenges. Humanoids must maintain balance, adjust posture, perform stepping, and execute morphology-dependent whole-body motions in conjunction with manipulation tasks. Previous work, such as HuMI, explored UMI-style data collection for humanoids using body tracking and grippers. While demonstrating feasibility, HuMI's reliance on Vive-based tracking introduced additional hardware costs and calibration complexity, and its retargeting procedure was tightly coupled with the downstream whole-body controller, potentially limiting its generality and interpretability.

HumanoidUMI directly addresses this gap by proposing a portable, low-cost, and robot-free framework specifically designed for humanoid whole-body manipulation. It seeks to provide a more accessible and efficient data collection mechanism than teleoperation and to offer a more explicit and decoupled approach to human-to-humanoid motion transfer compared to prior robot-free methods. By focusing on sparse spatial keypoints and a hierarchical control architecture, HumanoidUMI aims to bridge the gap between natural human demonstrations and deployable, coordinated whole-body behaviors on humanoid robots, thereby contributing to the scalability of humanoid skill learning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop and validate a portable, robot-free framework for the collection and learning of humanoid whole-body manipulation skills from human demonstrations. This overarching goal is driven by several key motivations and specific aims:

1. **Address Limitations of Existing Data Collection Methods:** The most significant motivation stems from the inherent drawbacks of traditional robot-in-the-loop teleoperation for humanoid data collection. Such methods are resource-intensive, requiring expensive robot hardware, skilled operators, stringent safety protocols, and being generally inefficient for generating large-scale, diverse datasets. The authors aim to overcome these limitations by introducing a system that can collect high-quality, task-relevant demonstrations without the physical robot needing to be present.

2. **Extend Robot-Free Paradigms to Whole-Body Humanoid Control:** While robot-free demonstration methods (e.g., UMI) have proven effective for robot-arm manipulation and some mobile systems, their extension to the complexities of humanoid whole-body control—which involves dynamic balance, posture adjustment, locomotion, and coordinated manipulation—remains underexplored. The research seeks to establish a viable and practical pipeline for capturing the intricate intent of human whole-body motion for humanoid deployment.

3. **Develop a Compact and Transferable Representation for Humanoid Motion:** Humanoid morphology and kinematics differ significantly from humans. Directly mapping human joint motions is often ineffective. A key objective is to identify and utilize a sparse yet expressive representation of human intent that captures task-relevant spatial structures. This representation should be amenable to morphology-aware retargeting, ensuring that the transferred motions are kinematically feasible and physically plausible for the target humanoid robot. The authors specifically propose a keypoint-based representation (pelvis, gripper TCPs, feet, and potentially knees) to achieve this.

4. **Design a Hierarchical Policy Framework for Robust Deployment:** To effectively bridge the embodiment gap and enable deployable behaviors, the research aims to establish a hierarchical control architecture. This architecture separates high-level task-space reasoning (predicting desired keypoint motions and gripper actions from visual input) from low-level, robot-specific execution (converting keypoints into whole-body references and tracking them stably). This decomposition is intended to improve interpretability, reduce policy dependence on specific robot morphology or controller designs, and enhance robustness.

5. **Enable Efficient and Accessible Data Collection:** A practical motivation is to lower the barrier to entry for collecting humanoid manipulation data. By utilizing lightweight, portable VR devices and UMI-inspired grippers, the framework aims to make data collection more accessible, allowing non-expert operators to generate demonstrations efficiently and at a lower cost, thereby accelerating research and development in humanoid skill learning.

In summary, the core objective of HumanoidUMI is to provide a comprehensive, portable, and efficient solution for learning complex humanoid whole-body skills by transforming natural, robot-free human demonstrations into executable robot behaviors through a structured, hierarchical control framework and a morphology-aware spatial keypoint retargeting mechanism.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

HumanoidUMI proposes a portable and robot-free framework for collecting human whole-body demonstrations and transforming them into deployable skills for humanoid robots. The methodology is structured into a data collection system and a hierarchical visuomotor control pipeline.

**A. Robot-free Data Collection System**
The system is designed to acquire synchronized human whole-body motion, local wrist-view visual observations, and gripper state information without the physical robot being present.

1. **Hardware Components:**
 - **VR-based Motion Capture:** Utilizes a PICO 4 VR system, comprising a headset, handheld controllers, and three lower-body trackers (two foot-mounted, one waist-mounted). This setup provides real-time 6-DoF body motion estimates in an SMPL-format body representation by fusing inertial, geomagnetic, and active infrared sensing.
 - **Handheld Manipulation Interfaces:** Two self-developed UMI-inspired grippers, each integrating a fisheye camera and a motor-driven rack-and-pinion mechanism. Each gripper has a dedicated slot for a PICO VR controller, ensuring stable grasping while capturing wrist-view visuals and gripper width.

2. **Recorded Data Modalities:**
 - **Whole-Body Keypoint Poses:** 6-DoF poses of two handheld controllers (gripper TCPs), pelvis, left foot, and right foot are extracted from the PICO SDK and SMPL representation. These five keypoints form a sparse representation. For lower-body-intensive tasks, left and right knee poses are optionally added, forming a seven-keypoint set. These human keypoint poses are coordinate-transformed to align with robot keypoint frames.
 - **Robot-Native Lower-Body Proprioception:** During data collection, the Spatial Keypoint Retargeting (SKR) module runs online to convert human keypoint trajectories into robot-native motion references, specifically recording the lower-body degrees-of-freedom (DoF) positions ($q_{low_t}$). These serve as proprioceptive conditioning for the high-level policy, allowing it to predict future keypoint motions based on the robot's support configuration.
 - **Wrist-View Images:** Synchronized images are captured by the fisheye cameras on the handheld grippers.
 - **Gripper Widths:** Measured by magnetic encoders on the gripper motors.
 - **Latency Matching:** A procedure similar to UMI is employed to calibrate and compensate for relative delays among sensing, gripper state, policy inference, and robot control, particularly critical for timing-sensitive tasks.

**B. Hierarchical Visuomotor Control**
The framework decomposes whole-body visuomotor learning into three sequential stages: a high-level policy, a spatial keypoint retargeting module, and a low-level whole-body controller.

1. **High-Level: Diffusion Policy:**
 - **Architecture:** An extension of Diffusion Policy, adapted for whole-body control in a sparse task space rather than joint space. It is a conditional diffusion model (Fig. 4).
 - **Action Space:** The policy predicts a receding-horizon action chunk ($a_{t+1:t+H}$) over a horizon $H=48$. The action for each timestep $\tau$ consists of the 6-DoF poses (3-D translation, 6-D continuous rotation) of the selected keypoints (pelvis, left/right TCPs, left/right feet; optionally left/right knees) and two scalar gripper widths. This results in a 47-dimensional (5-keypoint) or 65-dimensional (7-keypoint) action vector.
 - **Action Chunk Preparation:** Predicted keypoint poses are relative to their query-time pelvis frame, maintaining a consistent reference. Translations and gripper widths are min-max normalized.
 - **Observation:** The policy is conditioned on synchronized 224x224 left/right wrist-view RGB images and a three-frame history of a 15-D lower-body proprioceptive vector (12 leg joints, 3 waist joints). Upper-body joints are omitted as their configurations are resolved downstream.
 - **Inference:** At runtime, the policy denoises and inverse-normalizes a relative action chunk. The relative keypoint motions are decoded into pelvis-referenced target poses using the current robot state via forward kinematics and then passed to the SKR module.

2. **Bridge: Spatial Keypoint Retargeting (SKR) System:**
 - SKR is a morphology-aware pipeline designed to convert sparse human keypoint trajectories into kinematically feasible robot whole-body references. It addresses the body proportion, joint limit, and workspace differences between humans and humanoids.
 - **Keypoint Layouts:** Supports default 5-keypoint ($K_5 = \{\text{pelvis, L/R foot, L/R TCP}\}$}) and augmented 7-keypoint ($K_7 = K_5 \cup \{\text{L/R knee}\}$}) configurations.
 - **Morphology-Aware Adjustment:** After transforming human keypoints to the robot coordinate system and aligning them, SKR applies a pelvis-local anisotropic adjustment specifically to leg-related keypoints. The vertical displacement ($d_{i,z}$) from the pelvis to foot/knee keypoints is scaled by a factor $\lambda_{leg}$ (e.g., 0.75 for Unitree G1) to compensate for leg-length differences, while other coordinates remain unchanged.
 - **Constrained Inverse Kinematics (IK):** The adjusted keypoints are associated with semantic robot frames (pelvis, toe links, TCPs, knee links). Fixed offsets are applied. A constrained weighted IK problem is then solved to find the robot's root pose and joint configurations ($q_t^*$). The optimization minimizes pose errors to desired targets, regularizes joint motion, and respects joint limits, initialized from the previous solution for temporal consistency. A two-stage IK procedure prioritizes foot support/orientation, then refines pelvis/TCP positions (and knee positions for 7-keypoint). The resulting robot root pose and joint references are resampled and passed to the low-level controller.

3. **Low-Level: Whole-Body Controller:**
 - A learned motion-tracking controller, trained in MJLab, is used to execute the robot-native full-body references generated by SKR on the physical Unitree G1 robot.
 - **Reference Input:** SKR provides a short-horizon reference motion chunk including root position, root orientation, and joint configurations ($M_{ref}$).
 - **Control Output:** The controller runs at 50 Hz, receives current proprioceptive state, and a resampled reference window. Its 29-dimensional output is interpreted as a residual joint-position action. After clipping and scaling, the desired joint positions are computed and sent to the robot via joint-space PD control.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments were conducted on a Unitree G1 humanoid robot across five real-world tasks to evaluate three central questions regarding HumanoidUMI.

**A. Effectiveness of the Robot-Free Data Collection and Training Framework**
This evaluation focused on whether robot-free demonstrations could be successfully transformed into deployable visuomotor policies. Three tasks were used:
1. **Cluttered Tabletop Pick-and-Place:** Robot grasps bread among distractors and places it on a target plate.
2. **Bimanual Vegetable Collection:** Robot sequentially uses right hand for an eggplant and left hand for a cucumber.
3. **Dynamic Ball Throwing:** Robot performs a wind-up, releases a ball, and hits a target, requiring precise timing.

- **Qualitative Results:** HumanoidUMI demonstrated successful execution across all three tasks. The robot performed visual localization, grasping, transfer, and placement; coordinated two arms for sequential bimanual tasks; and generated a synchronized wind-up and release for dynamic throwing. This suggests the learned policy extends beyond basic end-effector reaching.
- **Ablation Analysis:**
 - **SKR vs. GMR (Generic Motion Retargeting):** For pick-and-place and bimanual collection, replacing Spatial Keypoint Retargeting (SKR) with GMR substantially reduced success rates. This indicates that SKR's morphology-aware scale adaptation, while preserving task-relevant spatial structure, is crucial for stable and consistent humanoid deployment.
 - **Latency Matching:** For dynamic ball throwing, removing latency matching also led to degraded performance. This highlights the importance of precise synchronization between sensing, gripper state, and robot actuation for timing-sensitive tasks.
- **Conclusion:** The complete HumanoidUMI pipeline consistently outperformed ablated variants, confirming the necessity of its core components (high-level policy, SKR, latency matching, low-level controller) for transforming robot-free demonstrations into robust, deployable humanoid skills.

**B. Whole-Body Manipulation Capability of HumanoidUMI**
This section evaluated the framework's ability to coordinate whole-body behaviors, involving lower body participation in manipulation, beyond just upper-body actions. Two challenging tasks were used, for which an augmented seven-keypoint variant (including knee keypoints) was also tested:
1. **Under-Table Waste Disposal:** Robot grasps an object, steps backward, bends knees and torso to reach a bin under a table, and releases the object.
2. **Walking Coffee Delivery:** Robot walks forward, stops stably, and extends an arm to deliver coffee.

- **Qualitative Results:** HumanoidUMI successfully completed both tasks on the Unitree G1. In waste disposal, the robot demonstrated complex posture changes, including foot repositioning and body lowering, to reach an out-of-reach target. In coffee delivery, locomotion and manipulation emerged as a coupled behavior, with the robot stably walking, stopping, and reaching. These results indicate that the framework supports complex whole-body coordination, where lower-body motion directly contributes to task completion.
- **Ablation Analysis (Knee Keypoints):** For these lower-body-intensive tasks, the augmented seven-keypoint variant significantly improved success rates compared to the default five-keypoint configuration. This suggests that including knee keypoints is critical for recovering correct lower-body motion, especially for knee flexion and body lowering. Without them, the lower-body posture is underconstrained, leading to less reliable whole-body execution.
- **Conclusion:** HumanoidUMI can generate physically executable humanoid skills involving object interaction, stepping, torso bending, knee flexion, and stable reaching, indicating strong whole-body coordination.

**C. Efficiency of Robot-Free Data Collection**
The efficiency of data collection was compared against TWIST2, a state-of-the-art teleoperation-based method, over 10 minutes for various tasks with both novice and experienced operators.

- **Valid Demonstration Throughput:**
 - **Bimanual and Throw Trash Tasks:** HumanoidUMI achieved approximately 1.8x to 2.5x higher valid demonstration throughput compared to TWIST2 for both novice and experienced operators.
 - **Walking Coffee Delivery:** The efficiency gain was substantially larger, especially for the novice operator, where HumanoidUMI collected 61 valid demonstrations while TWIST2 collected only 1. For the experienced user, HumanoidUMI still achieved a 12.4x speedup (62 vs 5 demonstrations).
- **Operator Skill Dependency:** HumanoidUMI demonstrated a lower operation barrier. Novice operators achieved throughput rates close to experienced users across all tasks. In contrast, TWIST2 showed a much larger expertise gap, particularly for loco-manipulation tasks like walking coffee delivery, which are cumbersome for direct teleoperation.
- **Conclusion:** HumanoidUMI consistently collects valid demonstrations more efficiently than teleoperation, with the most significant advantages observed in loco-manipulation tasks. It also reduces dependence on operator skill and extensive teleoperation training, making data collection more accessible and scalable.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

HumanoidUMI represents a step towards making advanced humanoid robot skill learning more accessible, efficient, and scalable. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Enabling Scalable Data Collection:** By offering a portable, low-cost, and robot-free data collection framework, HumanoidUMI addresses a fundamental bottleneck in humanoid robotics: the scarcity of high-quality, diverse demonstration data. The ability to collect data without direct robot teleoperation significantly reduces hardware requirements, operational costs, safety concerns, and the need for highly specialized operators. This approach can enable the creation of larger and more varied datasets, which are crucial for training robust and generalizable visuomotor policies using modern machine learning techniques.

2. **Bridging the Human-to-Humanoid Embodiment Gap:** The proposed Spatial Keypoint Retargeting (SKR) module is a key innovation in explicitly bridging the morphological and kinematic differences between human demonstrators and humanoid robots. By using sparse, task-relevant spatial keypoints and applying morphology-aware adjustments, SKR allows human intent to be translated into kinematically feasible and stable robot motions, rather than relying on direct, often problematic, joint-to-joint mapping. This contributes to more natural and effective transfer of complex whole-body skills.

3. **Advancing Whole-Body Control for Humanoids:** The hierarchical control framework, which decomposes high-level task-space planning from low-level execution, offers a structured approach to learning and deploying complex humanoid behaviors. This design separates visuomotor reasoning from embodiment-specific control, potentially improving policy interpretability, robustness to minor morphology changes, and the ability to handle tasks requiring coordinated manipulation, balance, and locomotion. The demonstrated capabilities in tasks involving bimanual coordination, dynamic throwing, and coupled loco-manipulation underscore the framework's potential to enable more versatile and agile humanoid robots.

4. **Lowering the Barrier to Entry for Robotics Research:** The use of readily available VR technology for data collection democratizes access to humanoid robotics research. Researchers and practitioners without access to advanced humanoid teleoperation systems or extensive training can still contribute to skill learning, fostering broader participation and accelerating innovation in the field.

5. **Impact on Real-World Applications:** The framework's ability to learn skills like under-table waste disposal and walking coffee delivery suggests its potential for applications in domestic, industrial, and service environments where humanoids could perform complex tasks requiring interaction with diverse environments and objects. By enabling robots to learn from human demonstrations in a more intuitive and efficient manner, HumanoidUMI could accelerate the deployment of humanoids in real-world scenarios.

In conclusion, HumanoidUMI offers a comprehensive and practical solution for generating humanoid whole-body skills from human demonstrations. Its contributions to efficient data collection, morphology-aware motion transfer, and hierarchical control design have the potential to significantly impact the scalability, accessibility, and capabilities of humanoid robot learning.
