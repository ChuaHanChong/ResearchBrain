# Quantifying the Gap between Understanding and Generation within Unified Multimodal Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from the University of Maryland, University of Waterloo, and MBZUAI developed GAPEVAL, a bidirectional benchmark, and a Multidimensional Item Response Theory-based "Gap Score" to quantify the disparity between understanding and generation capabilities in Unified Multimodal Models. Their findings indicate that current UMMs achieve functional coupling rather than deep cognitive unification, showing persistent performance gaps and disjoint knowledge representations.

## Problem

- It is unclear if Unified Multimodal Models (UMMs) truly integrate understanding and generation capabilities or if they merely coexist within a single architecture.
- Existing evaluation frameworks assess understanding and generation in isolation, failing to quantify the disparity or cognitive coherence between a model's bidirectional inference.
- A lack of principled metrics to diagnose whether UMMs achieve "surface-level unification" versus "deep cognitive convergence."

## Method

- Introduced **GAPEVAL**, a novel ==bidirectional benchmark== with ==646 high-quality questions== designed for symmetric evaluation in both text and image modalities.
- Developed the =="Gap Score"==, a principled metric grounded in ==Multidimensional Item Response Theory (MIRT)==, to quantitatively measure the disparity between a model's estimated understanding and generation abilities.
- Conducted comprehensive evaluations across ==nine state-of-the-art UMMs== and performed empirical studies on ==knowledge manipulation== (injection and editing) to probe underlying mechanisms.

## Results

- A persistent and significant disparity exists between understanding and generation performance across all evaluated UMMs, with many questions only answerable correctly in one modality.
- Models with higher overall accuracy often displayed wider modality gaps (higher Gap Scores), while some lower-performing models showed better integration between their capabilities.
- Empirical studies demonstrated that knowledge within UMMs is largely decoupled; knowledge injection or editing in one modality often failed to transfer or update consistently in the other.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Current UMMs exhibit "functional coupling" rather than true "cognitive unification," meaning their capabilities are architecturally linked but lack deep integration or shared knowledge.
> - Achieving high overall performance in UMMs does not automatically lead to better integration between understanding and generation capabilities; sometimes, higher performance correlates with wider modality gaps.
> - Knowledge within UMMs remains largely disjoint across modalities, as fine-tuning on one modality often has minimal impact on the other, indicating a failure of synergistic knowledge transfer and updating.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of artificial intelligence has seen tremendous advancements, particularly with the advent of Large Language Models (LLMs). Building upon this success, multimodal intelligence has emerged as a critical frontier, aiming to integrate reasoning across different data modalities, primarily text and images. Unified Multimodal Models (UMMs) represent the cutting edge of this endeavor, designed to perform both understanding tasks (e.g., visual question answering, image captioning, where text is generated from multimodal input) and generation tasks (e.g., text-to-image generation, where images are produced from textual prompts).

While UMMs have demonstrated impressive capabilities in both understanding and generation, a fundamental question remains largely unanswered: do these models genuinely *integrate* and *align* these two distinct capabilities at a cognitive level, or do they merely *coexist* as separate, albeit unified, components within a single architecture? Previous evaluation frameworks, as highlighted by the authors (e.g., MMMU, MMBench, GenEval), often assess understanding and generation in isolation or through task-bound synergies (e.g., UniEval, RealUnify, GIR-Bench). These benchmarks are insufficient to probe the *depth* of unification or quantify any *disparity* between a model's ability to understand and its ability to generate using the *same underlying knowledge*.

This paper addresses this critical gap by introducing **GAPEVAL**, a novel, bidirectional benchmark specifically designed to quantify the performance disparity between understanding and generation within UMMs. By formulating each question to be answerable in *both* image and text modalities, GAPEVAL moves beyond evaluating mere functionality towards assessing cognitive coherence and cross-modal consistency. The work explicitly aims to diagnose whether UMMs achieve "surface-level unification" (engineering-level coupling) or "deep cognitive convergence" (true integration of knowledge). This positions the paper as a crucial contribution to the foundational understanding of multimodal AI, pushing the research agenda beyond performance metrics to a more profound analysis of how these complex models truly operate internally.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation behind this research stems from a fundamental question about the nature of "unification" in modern multimodal models. Despite remarkable progress in UMMs, which integrate both understanding and generation capabilities into a single architecture, it remains unclear whether these two abilities are truly aligned and integrated, or if they simply coexist without deep synergy. The authors articulate this as a distinction between "engineering-level achievement" and "cognitive convergence."

Against this backdrop, the key objectives of the paper are:

1. **Introduce GAPEVAL:** To develop a novel, high-quality, bidirectional benchmark specifically designed to evaluate and quantify the intrinsic gap between understanding and generation capabilities within UMMs. This benchmark aims to provide a symmetric testbed where identical semantic intent can be probed for both textual and visual responses.
2. **Quantify the Gap:** To develop a principled metric, the "Gap Score," grounded in Multidimensional Item Response Theory (MIRT), to quantitatively measure the disparity and cognitive coherence between a model's bidirectional inference capabilities.
3. **Comprehensive Evaluation:** To conduct extensive experiments across a wide range of state-of-the-art UMMs (including both open-source and closed-source models with diverse architectures) to reveal the prevalence and characteristics of this understanding-generation gap.
4. **Empirical Analysis of Knowledge Manipulation:** To perform an in-depth empirical study from the perspective of knowledge manipulation (injection and editing). This aims to explore the underlying mechanisms contributing to the observed gap, specifically investigating whether knowledge within UMMs is truly shared and transferable across modalities or remains disjoint.
5. **Identify Limitations and Inform Future Research:** To highlight the limitations of current UMM architectures and training paradigms in achieving true cognitive unification, thereby paving the way for future research directions that focus on building more deeply integrated and coherent multimodal AI systems.

In essence, the research is driven by a desire to look beyond superficial performance metrics to understand the foundational capabilities of UMMs, asking if their impressive multimodal functionalities are a result of genuine internal coherence or merely sophisticated engineering that masks deeper disconnections.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research employs a multi-faceted methodology comprising the design of a novel benchmark, a sophisticated metric, comprehensive model evaluations, and an in-depth empirical study on knowledge manipulation.

#### 3.1. GAPEVAL Benchmark Design

- **Bidirectional Formulation:** The core innovation of GAPEVAL is its bidirectional nature. Each question is designed to be answered in *both* image and text modalities. This is achieved by creating pairs of prompts (e.g., "What is this activity?" for understanding, and "Generate a photo of this activity." for generation) that share the same semantic intent but require different output modalities. This ensures a fair and symmetric evaluation of a model's ability to process and generate information consistently across modalities.
- **Taxonomy:** GAPEVAL covers four critical categories of multimodal tasks:
 - **Instruction Following:** Evaluates a model's ability to interpret and execute complex instructions consistently across modalities, including explicit and implicit image edits.
 - **Numerical Perception:** Assesses the model's capacity to perceive and manipulate quantitative information (e.g., counting, swapping object quantities) in both text and image formats, preventing simple copying.
 - **World Knowledge:** Tests a model's ability to recognize, reason with, and apply broad factual and conceptual knowledge grounded in visual inputs, requiring semantic interpretation beyond surface-level patterns. It spans six subdomains.
 - **Reasoning:** Measures a model's capacity for complex logical and physical reasoning, incorporating subcategories like Image Selection, Knowledge Selection (rendering MMLU/MMMU problems visually), Real-world Reasoning, and Logical Reasoning, all requiring both textual descriptions and visual renderings.
- **Data Collection and Annotation:** The benchmark comprises 646 high-quality questions. Data collection involved a systematic, multi-stage process combining human curation and automated generation, with rigorous peer review to ensure consistency, clarity, and reliability.

#### 3.2. Metric Design: The Gap Score

To quantify the understanding-generation gap, a two-stage evaluation methodology is introduced:

- **First Stage: Capability Measurement:** For each question, model outputs are judged for correctness (binary: 1 or 0). **GPT-5-mini** (a large multimodal language model) is used as an "MLLM-as-a-Judge" for subjective evaluation, validated against human annotations (92% agreement rate on a held-out set).
- **Second Stage: Capability Gap Measurement:** To address limitations of simple averaging (which overlooks item difficulty), the authors adopt a **Multidimensional Item Response Theory (MIRT)** framework. MIRT models latent variables: a model's ability (θ) and an item's difficulty (β). It embeds both into a continuous latent space, where success probability is a smooth sigmoid function of their difference. This allows the model to differentiate between failures due to low ability versus high item difficulty. A Bayesian maximum a posteriori (MAP) optimization is used to jointly fit abilities and difficulties. The final "Gap Score" is derived from the difference between a model's estimated understanding ability (θ_text) and generation ability (θ_image), normalized between 0 and 100, with a penalty term for inconsistent failures and a reward for co-successes.

#### 3.3. Comprehensive Model Evaluation

- **Models Evaluated:** The study evaluates nine representative UMMs (7 open-source like Bagel, OneCAT, UniWorld-V1; 2 closed-source like Gemini2.5-Flash-Image, GPT-Image-1), covering diverse architectures and parameter scales. Additionally, four understanding-only models (e.g., GPT5, Gemini2.5 Flash, Qwen3-VL) and two generation-only models (FLUX.1-dev, Qwen-Image) are included as baselines.

#### 3.4. Empirical Analysis on Unified Knowledge (Knowledge Manipulation)

- **Motivation:** To investigate *how* knowledge evolves and is shared (or not shared) across modalities during training.
- **Formulation:** Knowledge entities are defined as (subject, relation, object) tuples, where subjects and objects can be text or image. The study focuses on vision-language relations, using images and text pointing to the same object as (subject, object) pairs.
- **Experiment Setup:** Three representative UMMs (Show-o, OmniGen2, Bagel) are selected for fine-tuning experiments.
 - **Knowledge Injection:** New, previously "unknown" knowledge instances are introduced to models.
 - **Knowledge Editing:** Existing, grounded knowledge is intentionally modified (e.g., swapping labels between conceptually related objects).
 - **Training Strategy:** Models are fine-tuned through *single-sided manipulation* (either on understanding or generation) to see its effect on *both* sides. Bagel, however, used a joint training strategy due to observed complete capability loss with single-sided fine-tuning.
- **Metric for Empirical Study:** GPT5-mini as judge for understanding tasks (injection & editing) and generation tasks (editing). **CLIP score** is used for generation tasks in knowledge injection (to measure similarity between generated and reference images for novel objects).

This rigorous methodological framework allows for a deep probe into the internal workings and consistency of UMMs, moving beyond simple task performance to analyze the cognitive alignment of their understanding and generation capabilities.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The study yielded several crucial findings that shed light on the true nature of "unification" in current multimodal models:

1. **Finding 1: Unifying Modalities Does Not Guarantee Balanced Cross-Modal Performance (Persistent Performance Gap).**
 - A significant disparity exists between understanding and generation within UMMs. A large proportion of questions could only be answered correctly in one modality, highlighting a lack of cross-modal consistency.
 - Remarkably, some UMMs (e.g., OmniGen2) performed *worse* on generation tasks than their standalone generation backbones (e.g., FLUX.1-dev). This suggests that architectural unification can sometimes dilute task-specific strengths, creating an inherent trade-off.
 - Understanding-only models consistently outperformed UMMs on comprehension-oriented tasks, while closed-source UMMs sometimes surpassed generation-only baselines on generative tasks. This implies a partial transfer of reasoning ability from understanding to generation, but often at the cost of overall balance.

2. **Finding 2: Good Performance Does Not Necessarily Correspond to High Unification.**
 - Counter-intuitively, models achieving higher overall accuracy often exhibited wider modality gaps (higher Gap Scores). For example, UniWorld-V1 showed high overall accuracy but also a noticeable gap. Conversely, a model like OneCAT, despite lower overall performance, displayed a smaller gap score, indicating better integration between its capabilities.
 - This suggests that achieving state-of-the-art performance in both understanding and generation does not automatically lead to deeper cognitive unification; rather, effective unification appears to stem from intentional architectural design or training strategies aimed at integration.

3. **Finding 3: A Performance-Lagging Effect Exists Across Models.**
 - The study observed a distinct trend: the modality gap initially *increases* for lower-performing models but then *decreases* once the overall capability reaches a relatively high level.
 - Lower-capability models show small gaps because both modalities fail simultaneously. As models improve, understanding capabilities mature faster and more substantially than generation, leading to an *asymmetric maturation* and a temporary widening of the gap.
 - Only high-performance models eventually develop robust joint reasoning across modalities, which gradually reduces the gap. This indicates a non-linear relationship between overall performance and unification.

4. **Finding 4: The Knowledge Within UMMs Remains Disjoint Across Modalities.**
 - Empirical studies using knowledge manipulation (injection and editing) revealed a pronounced misalignment of knowledge. Fine-tuning a model on one modality (e.g., understanding) often had little to no effect on the other modality (generation).
 - In knowledge editing experiments, outdated knowledge tended to persist in the non-trained modality, indicating a failure of synergistic updating.
 - Knowledge injection frequently introduced new inconsistencies, as models struggled to apply newly acquired knowledge consistently across modalities. These results strongly suggest that, despite architectural unification, the underlying knowledge representations in current UMMs are largely decoupled.

5. **Finding 5: Unbalanced, Slower Convergence of Different Modalities Explains the Performance Lag.**
 - Analysis of learning trajectories during knowledge editing showed that understanding accuracy rises sharply and saturates quickly with a few thousand training steps. In contrast, generation capability improves much more slowly, requiring a substantially larger training budget for effective knowledge adaptation.
 - This asymmetric convergence dynamic further confirms that the two capabilities rely on distinct update mechanisms, with generative pathways demanding more extensive optimization to internalize and express new knowledge. This finding provides a mechanistic explanation for the "performance-lagging effect" observed in Finding 3.

In summary, the findings consistently point to a "functional coupling" rather than "cognitive unification" in current UMMs, where capabilities are brought together architecturally but their underlying knowledge bases remain largely independent and unsynchronized in their learning dynamics.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research holds significant implications for the development and understanding of multimodal AI:

1. **Novel and Rigorous Evaluation Paradigm:** GAPEVAL fills a crucial void in multimodal model evaluation. By introducing a bidirectional benchmark and a principled "Gap Score" based on MIRT, the paper provides a more nuanced and psychologically informed way to assess the true integration of understanding and generation capabilities. This moves beyond merely checking if a model can perform tasks in both directions, to quantifying *how coherently* it does so. This new benchmark will be invaluable for researchers and developers to rigorously test and compare future UMMs.

2. **Deepened Understanding of UMM Limitations:** The findings critically challenge the notion that current UMMs achieve deep cognitive convergence. By demonstrating a persistent, significant gap between understanding and generation, the study reveals that existing models often achieve only "surface-level unification." This re-framing of the problem is essential, as it shifts focus from simply improving task performance to addressing the fundamental architectural and learning shortcomings that prevent true integration.

3. **Guidance for Future Architectural Design:** The empirical analysis on knowledge manipulation and unbalanced convergence provides concrete evidence that knowledge representations within UMMs often remain disjoint. This highlights the urgent need for new architectural designs that foster genuine *shared knowledge representations* and *synergistic learning mechanisms* across modalities. Future models should prioritize internal coherence and transferability of knowledge, rather than merely concatenating modality-specific components.

4. **Informed Training Strategies:** The observation of a "performance-lagging effect" and asymmetric convergence rates (understanding faster than generation) suggests that current training paradigms might not be optimal for achieving balanced multimodal capabilities. This work motivates the exploration of more sophisticated training strategies, such as dynamic weighting of understanding and generation losses, curriculum learning, or regularization techniques that explicitly encourage cross-modal knowledge alignment and synchronized learning.

5. **Paving the Way for Next-Level Unification:** By diagnosing the problem and identifying its underlying mechanisms, the paper lays a clear foundation for what the authors term "the next-level unification for UMMs." Achieving this will involve designing models that can seamlessly transfer and adapt knowledge between modalities, ensuring that improvements in one capability genuinely reinforce and strengthen the other. This could lead to more robust, efficient, and genuinely intelligent multimodal AI systems that truly mimic human-like cognitive coherence.

In essence, this research provides both a diagnostic tool and a theoretical framework for advancing multimodal AI. It serves as a call to action for the research community to move beyond functional performance gains and strive for a deeper, more fundamental integration of understanding and generation, thereby building truly unified and coherent artificial general intelligence.
