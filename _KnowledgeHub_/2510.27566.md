# Interact-RAG: Reason and Interact with the Corpus, Beyond Black-Box Retrieval

> [!summary] Summary
> INTERACT-RAG introduces a framework that gives LLM agents fine-grained control over information retrieval, moving beyond traditional black-box RAG systems. This approach led to a 9.7 Exact Match point improvement (22.5% relative gain) over leading baselines on RAG benchmarks, particularly in complex multi-hop question-answering tasks.

## Problem

- Existing agentic RAG frameworks treat information retrieval as a black box, limiting LLM agents to passively receiving text chunks.
- This passive interaction prevents agents from exercising fine-grained control over retrieval, such as adapting search strategies or dynamically shaping search context.
- Agents are often confined to inefficient query reformulation when encountering complex information-seeking tasks or retrieval failures.

## Method

- Developed **Interact-RAG**, which equips LLM agents with a ==Corpus Interaction Engine== offering fine-grained "Interaction Primitives" like multi-faceted retrieval, anchored matching, and context shaping.
- Implemented a reasoning-enhanced workflow with a Global-Planner, Adaptive-Reasoner, and Executor to guide hierarchical planning and iterative action execution.
- Trained autonomous, end-to-end agents using a two-stage process: Supervised Fine-Tuning on synthesized trajectories, followed by policy refinement with Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO).

## Results

- Interact-RAG achieved the highest performance across six RAG benchmarks, demonstrating an average of 9.7 Exact Match (EM) point improvement (**22.5%** relative gain) over the second-best method.
- The framework showed robust generalization, improving performance on both in-distribution and out-of-distribution datasets, with a **36.4%** relative EM gain on multi-hop Musique.
- Ablation studies confirmed that the fine-grained interaction primitives, Supervised Fine-Tuning, and Reinforcement Learning stages are all essential for the observed performance gains.
- The approach led to higher EM scores with fewer action iterations, indicating more efficient information retrieval compared to traditional query-only RAG methods.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Empowering LLM agents with fine-grained, interactive control over the retrieval process significantly enhances RAG system performance beyond black-box query reformulation.
> - A structured, reasoning-enhanced workflow enables LLMs to decompose, plan, and execute complex multi-step retrieval strategies effectively.
> - Combining Supervised Fine-Tuning for foundational mechanics with Reinforcement Learning for strategic policy optimization is crucial for developing autonomous, end-to-end interactive RAG agents.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Large Language Models (LLMs) often face limitations related to their static and potentially outdated training data, as well as a lack of domain-specific knowledge. Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) has emerged as a method to address these limitations by enabling LLMs to access and incorporate external, up-to-date information from a corpus.

The development of RAG systems has progressed through three phases. Initially, Static RAG involved a single retrieval step to provide relevant documents to an LLM. This evolved into Iterative RAG, which employs multi-step retrieval pipelines to progressively gather information for more complex tasks. The current advancement is Agentic RAG, where an LLM-centric agent autonomously orchestrates the entire RAG workflow, determining when and what to retrieve, and how to analyze the information. This includes both prompt-driven multi-agent systems and end-to-end trained agents utilizing techniques such as Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) and Reinforcement Learning (RL).

Despite these developments, a common limitation across existing agentic RAG frameworks is their treatment of the retrieval process as a black box. Agents typically issue a query and passively receive text chunks, often from embedding-based semantic retrievers. This passive interaction restricts the agent's ability to inspect or finely control the retrieval mechanism. Consequently, agent actions are largely confined to query reformulation, leading to inefficient trial-and-error loops when faced with complex information-seeking tasks or retrieval failures. This work by Hui et al. introduces a paradigm that aims to move beyond this black-box retrieval, providing the agent with explicit and fine-grained control over the information-seeking process.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to overcome the limitations of current agentic RAG frameworks, specifically their reliance on black-box retrieval. The authors contend that confining LLM agents to merely issuing queries and passively receiving results hinders their ability to effectively tackle complex information-seeking tasks. This passive role prevents agents from exercising fine-grained control over the retrieval process, such as adapting search strategies, focusing on specific entities, or dynamically managing the search context. When a retrieval fails due to nuanced phrasing or irrelevant distractions, existing agents are often limited to repetitive query paraphrasing, which can be an inefficient and unsuccessful strategy.

To address this, the key motivations for developing Interact-RAG are:
1. **Dismantle the Black-Box Retrieval:** To transform the LLM agent from a passive query issuer into an active manipulator of the retrieval process. This involves providing transparency and fine-grained control over how information is retrieved from the corpus.
2. **Enhance Information-Seeking Efficacy:** To enable agents to perform more sophisticated and adaptive information exploration. By having fine-grained control, the agent can employ diverse retrieval strategies, anchor searches on specific entities, and dynamically shape the information context, thereby improving the breadth, depth, and overall success rate of information retrieval.
3. **Empower Strategic Reasoning:** To equip the LLM agent with the capability to strategically reason about and execute complex multi-step retrieval plans. This involves not only providing retrieval tools but also enabling the agent to understand when and how to best use them, adapting its strategy based on feedback from the Corpus Interaction Engine.
4. **Develop a Robust Training Methodology:** To enable autonomous, end-to-end agents capable of internalizing these strategic retrieval policies. This requires a training pipeline that can effectively teach LLMs to leverage the interactive primitives, initially through structured reasoning and data synthesis, and subsequently through reinforcement learning for policy refinement.
5. **Demonstrate Generalizability and Robustness:** To validate that the proposed interactive reasoning paradigm and associated training methods lead to superior performance across various RAG benchmarks, including both single-hop and multi-hop question-answering tasks, and exhibit strong generalization capabilities to out-of-distribution datasets.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Interact-RAG introduces a novel paradigm to empower LLM agents with fine-grained control over the information retrieval process. The methodology comprises three core components: a Corpus Interaction Engine, a reasoning-enhanced workflow, and a two-stage training pipeline.

#### 3.1. Corpus Interaction Engine and Paradigm

The Corpus Interaction Engine disassembles the black-box retrieval by providing agents with a set of "Interaction Primitives." These primitives allow for diverse and fine-grained control over information retrieval:

- **Multi-Faceted Retrieval:**
 - `semantic search(query_s)`: Executes a dense retrieval based on embedding similarity for semantically related documents.
 - `exact search(keywords_e)`: Performs sparse retrieval using keyword ranking, suitable for precise terms or phrases.
 - `weighted fusion(w_s, w_e)`: Combines results from semantic and exact searches with adaptable weights specified by the agent.
- **Anchored Matching:**
 - `entity match(entity)`: Focuses the search on information segments strongly associated with a specified entity, mitigating distractions.
- **Context Shaping:**
 - `include docs(doc_ids)`: Ensures specified documents are retained in subsequent retrieval steps.
 - `exclude docs(doc_ids)`: Filters out irrelevant documents from future searches.
 - `adjust scale(n)`: Modifies the number of retrieved text chunks to match the complexity of the sub-problem.

Within the Interact-RAG pipeline, the LLM agent generates a structured output at each step, including a reasoning thought and a suite of concurrent actions from these primitives (formatted as parameterized function calls). The Corpus Interaction Engine executes these actions, returning a consolidated response (retrieved content, metadata) to the LLM, enabling iterative refinement. The engine is designed for computational efficiency, leveraging Full-Text Search (FTS) modules for exact and entity matching, and simple filters for context shaping.

#### 3.2. Reasoning-Enhanced Workflow

To enable LLMs to master the interactive pipeline, the authors developed a reasoning-enhanced workflow that decomposes the agent's actions into a hierarchical and iterative structure. This workflow, illustrated in Figure 2 of the paper, consists of three collaborative modules:

- **Global-Planner:** Receives the user query, analyzes it, and decomposes it into a primary step-by-step execution plan, providing a high-level strategic roadmap.
- **Adaptive-Reasoner:** Serves as the cognitive core. At each step, it analyzes the current state (previous actions, gathered information, planning objective) and issues one of two directives:
 - *Proceed:* If the sub-task is progressing well, it instructs the Executor to continue or conclude the response.
 - *Reflect & Refine:* If an obstacle (e.g., insufficient information) is encountered, it diagnoses the issue and refines the interaction strategy for the next action, potentially adjusting the primary plan.
- **Executor:** Translates the reasoner's directives into concrete, structured actions (function calls for interaction primitives). It generates the final answer once all sub-tasks are complete.

This modular design decouples planning, reasoning, and execution, which is intended to elicit more reliable output from general-purpose LLMs. It also serves as a data synthesis engine, generating high-quality agent trajectories for subsequent training.

#### 3.3. End-to-End Agent Training

To achieve an autonomous, end-to-end LLM agent, a two-stage training process is employed:

- **Trace Sampling and Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT):** The SFT stage teaches the LLM the fundamental mechanics of planning, reasoning, and interaction. High-quality trajectories are synthesized using the reasoning-enhanced workflow, filtering for successful paths where the agent's final answer matches the ground truth. The LLM is fine-tuned on these trajectories to predict sequences of thoughts and actions auto-regressively. Tokens of retrieved information are masked during loss calculation to prevent distraction.
- **Policy Refinement with Reinforcement Learning (RL):** Following SFT, RL is used to enable the agent to develop superior strategies through active exploration. The Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) algorithm is applied to refine the agent's policy.
 - **RL Objective:** The objective function encourages updates towards high-reward trajectories while stabilizing training through importance sampling and KL-divergence regularization. Retrieved information tokens are also masked during policy updates.
 - **Reward Function:** An outcome-based reward `R(τ)` guides the agent, factoring in both syntactic validity and answer accuracy. A trajectory incurs an initial penalty of -1, neutralized by a +1 reward for syntactic validity (`I{τ_valid}`). An additional +1 reward is given for task success (`I{y_ans}`), provided the trajectory is valid. The initial -1 penalty mathematically cancels out during GRPO's group-based normalization and does not influence training directly but provides intuitive logic for validity.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments were conducted across six RAG benchmarks: Natural Questions (NQ), PopQA (single-hop), HotpotQA, 2WikiMultiHopQA (2Wiki), MuSiQue, and Bamboogle (multi-hop). An additional evaluation on the MultiHop-RAG benchmark (non-wiki domain) was performed. Baselines included non-RAG, static RAG, iterative RAG, prompt-driven multi-agent, and end-to-end trained agents. The Qwen3-8B and Qwen2.5-7B models were used as backbones, with a 2018 Wikipedia dump as the retrieval corpus. Interact-RAG was trained on a combined 12K QA data from NQ, HotpotQA, and MuSiQue training splits.

#### 4.1. Overall Performance

Interact-RAG consistently achieved the highest performance across all six benchmarks.
- On average, Interact-RAG (8B version) demonstrated a 9.7 Exact Match (EM) point improvement (22.5% relative gain) over Search-R1, the second-best method. The 7B version showed a 10.1% relative improvement.
- Performance gains were more substantial on complex multi-hop QA tasks, with a 36.4% relative EM improvement on Musique. It also maintained strong performance on single-hop benchmarks like NQ (11.0% relative EM improvement) and PopQA (12.3% relative EM improvement).
- The trained agent exhibited strong generalization, showing consistent improvements on both in-distribution (HotpotQA, Musique, NQ) and out-of-distribution (PopQA, 2Wiki, Bamboogle) benchmarks.
- On the MultiHop-RAG non-wiki domain benchmark, Interact-RAG also significantly outperformed baselines (e.g., 81.4 EM for 8B Interact-RAG vs 66.3 for MA-RAG-8B and 73.0 for Search-R1-7B).
- Retrieval quality, measured by supporting fact coverage, also showed Interact-RAG outperforming baselines across multi-hop datasets.

#### 4.2. Ablation Study

- **Efficacy of Interaction Paradigm:** The "w/o Interaction" variant, which restricts the agent to black-box query-search, showed a marked performance drop across all evaluated datasets (e.g., -8.9% EM on 2Wiki, -10.9% on Musique, -10.4% on PopQA). This indicates that fine-grained control over retrieval is integral to Interact-RAG's performance.
- **Impact of Training Strategy:** Removing SFT led to significant performance degradation (e.g., -21.9% EM on Musique), highlighting its role in establishing fundamental reasoning and interaction mechanics. Omitting RL also caused declines (e.g., -18.6% EM on PopQA), indicating its necessity for strategic policy optimization.
- **Ablation on Interaction Primitives:** Fine-grained ablation revealed that removing individual primitives (Multi-Faceted Retrieval, Anchored Matching, Context Shaping) degraded performance, with the complete absence of interaction resulting in the most significant drop. Different primitives contributed varying degrees of improvement depending on the dataset characteristics, suggesting a synergistic effect.

#### 4.3. Training-Free Scenarios

The "Interact-RAG-Workflow" (training-free approach) consistently outperformed MA-RAG, demonstrating the intrinsic effectiveness of the reasoning-interaction paradigm even without model training. Ablations on this workflow showed significant performance drops when interaction capabilities were removed or when the structured workflow itself was omitted, indicating their effectiveness in orchestrating the RAG process.

#### 4.4. Detailed Analysis

- **Efficiency of Information Retrieval:** Interact-RAG achieved higher EM scores with fewer action iterations compared to query-only methods (Ours-Search and Search-R1), especially on complex multi-hop datasets. This suggests that fine-grained control enables more efficient navigation of the information space, reducing trial-and-error loops.
- **Training Dynamics in RL:** The SFT+RL approach showed consistent improvement after an initial warm-up, converging at a high-performance level. In contrast, an RL-only agent struggled to surpass the SFT-only baseline, indicating that SFT provides crucial foundational capabilities and strategic solution paths that are difficult to learn from scratch with RL alone.
- **Interaction Patterns Across Training Stages:**
 - *Non-Trained:* Limited engagement with interaction (average 1.82 turns, minimal interactive actions).
 - *SFT Stage:* Increased interaction turns (3.61 turns), reflecting learned reasoning strategies and engagement with the engine.
 - *RL Stage:* While interaction turns slightly decreased (3.16 turns), EM scores improved, indicating a transition to a more strategic, efficient, and accurate policy. The frequency of `Entity-Match` sharply increased, while `Doc-Shaping` decreased, suggesting a learned prioritization of precise searches and reduced need for noise filtering.
- **Cost-Performance Trade-off:** Interact-RAG incurred higher latency and token consumption compared to standard baselines due to explicit reasoning. However, control experiments with Search-R1 showed that significantly increased computational cost (3x) via sampling only yielded modest performance gains, still falling short of Interact-RAG.
- **Engine Overhead:** The Corpus Interaction Engine demonstrated negligible memory footprint (0.3GB for additional components on a 2.3GB vector database) and minimal latency increase (1.82s per iteration vs 1.76s for standard vector retrieval).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces Interact-RAG, a novel paradigm that addresses a fundamental limitation in existing agentic Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) systems: the treatment of information retrieval as a black box. By empowering LLM agents with fine-grained control over the retrieval process, Interact-RAG moves beyond passive query issuing, enabling more active and strategic information seeking.

The development of a Corpus Interaction Engine, equipped with primitives for multi-faceted retrieval, anchored matching, and context shaping, represents a significant step towards enhancing the transparency and manipulability of RAG. The reasoning-enhanced workflow provides a structured approach for LLMs to plan, adapt, and execute complex retrieval tasks, serving both as a robust training-free solution and a mechanism for generating high-quality training data. Furthermore, the two-stage training pipeline (Supervised Fine-Tuning followed by Reinforcement Learning) demonstrates an effective method for developing autonomous, end-to-end agents that can internalize and strategically apply these interactive capabilities.

The experimental results indicate that Interact-RAG significantly outperforms other advanced RAG approaches across diverse benchmarks, particularly on complex multi-hop question-answering tasks. The observed improvements in Exact Match scores, coupled with increased efficiency (fewer action iterations) and strong generalization to out-of-distribution datasets, suggest that the reasoning-interaction paradigm is effective and robust. The ablation studies further validate the critical contributions of both the interactive primitives and the multi-stage training strategy.

The potential impact of this work extends to enhancing the reliability and adaptability of LLM-based systems in knowledge-intensive domains. By allowing agents to dynamically refine their search based on intermediate results and context, Interact-RAG could lead to more accurate, robust, and explainable AI applications that depend on external knowledge. This paradigm offers a promising direction for future research in building sophisticated RAG systems, encouraging further exploration into more advanced RL designs and optimizations for cost-effectiveness in explicit reasoning trajectories.
