# Tactile-Conditioned Diffusion Policy for Force-Aware Robotic Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at the Technical University of Darmstadt developed the Force-Aware Robotic Manipulation (FARM) framework, which integrates high-dimensional tactile force distributions into a diffusion policy's action space to enable explicit force control in robotic manipulation. This system achieved a 100% success rate on dynamic screw-tightening tasks and superior performance in grape picking and plant insertion compared to baselines.

## Problem

- Robots often fail in contact-rich manipulation due to a lack of precise force control, leading to object damage or slippage.
- Current imitation learning methods primarily use visuotactile feedback as observations, not for explicit control of gripper forces.
- Replicating human-like dexterity in regulating grasp forces based on touch remains a core challenge in robotics.

## Method

- Introduced the FARM framework, a diffusion policy that explicitly predicts robot pose, grip width, and target grip force, using high-dimensional tactile force distributions as observations.
- Utilizes a robot-free data collection process with a human-held UMI gripper, and deploys policies on a geometrically identical, open-source Actuated UMI gripper.
- Employs a dual-mode gripper control system that intelligently switches between position control for free-space movements and closed-loop force control during object contact.

## Results

- FARM achieved a 100% success rate in the dynamically challenging screw-tightening task, significantly surpassing all baselines.
- The framework demonstrated high success (95%) in static force tasks such as Plant Insertion and Grape Picking, comparable to or exceeding force-aware baselines.
- Quantitative evaluation using Wasserstein-1 distance showed FARM's superior force matching to human demonstrations, particularly in screw tightening, with a W1 distance of 0.7538 N.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly incorporating high-dimensional tactile force distributions as both observations and actions enables robots to actively regulate and mimic human-like grasp forces.
> - Robot-free data collection with a geometrically matched demonstration and deployment gripper simplifies policy transfer and accelerates the development of force-aware skills.
> - A dual-mode gripper control strategy, switching between position and force control, enhances robustness and adaptability during various phases of manipulation.
