# SMILE: Smooth Motion for Improved Long-Horizon VLA Execution

> [!summary] Summary
> SMILE is an architecture-preserving action interface for vision-language-action models that replaces raw per-step action prediction with B-spline coefficient prediction for translation, rotation, and gripper. A fixed basis decodes coefficients into temporally coupled smooth controls, letting policies execute longer fixed horizons per model call. Applied to four action experts, it raises success while cutting amortized latency, reaching 98.0% on LIBERO.

## Problem

- Action chunking amortizes expensive policy calls, but raw chunks carry jitter, direction reversals, and outliers that compound over longer executed prefixes.
- Consequently, raw-action policies lose accuracy as execution horizon grows, forcing a trade between amortized latency and task success rather than improving both.
- Prior remedies alter chunk selection, execution length, or asynchronous scheduling instead of fixing the learned action representation that produces temporally inconsistent controls.

## Method

- ==B-spline== coefficient action representation: the expert predicts control-point coefficients per modality, decoded through a fixed basis into cumulative translation, ==SO(3)==-relative rotation, and clipped gripper commands.
- Architecture-preserving integration replaces only action-side input projection and output heads with coefficient heads, keeping each baseline's conditioner, predictive-visual or pixel-motion pathway, and ==denoising backbone==.
- Coefficient-target construction via ==least-squares projection== of expert trajectories onto the basis, with dataset-level normalization, boundary validity masks, and equally weighted per-modality ==denoising losses==.

## Results

- On LIBERO, SMILE-Evo1 lifts overall success **92.7%** to **98.0%** at horizon 16; SMILE-SmolVLA rises **74.0%** to **82.6%** with a **10.1x** per-action speedup.
- On CALVIN ABC-D, average sequence length improves **4.33** to **4.42** for VPP and **4.10** to **4.18** for DAWN, with latency dropping to **13.0** and **22.7 ms**.
- Real xArm cluttered success rises **8.3%** to **56.7%** (Evo1) and **70.8%** to **88.3%** (VPP); drops fall to zero, non-boundary acceleration drops **78.6%**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Smoothness imposed by the action representation, not by bigger backbones or planners, can break the accuracy-versus-horizon trade-off in chunked policies.
> - Gains concentrate within chunks rather than at refresh boundaries, indicating intra-chunk jitter, not replanning discontinuity, limits long-horizon open-loop execution.
> - A modest low-order basis suffices: cubic splines with eight control points beat higher degrees, since extra spline capacity erodes the useful smoothness bias.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work is situated within the field of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for robot control, specifically addressing challenges related to efficient and reliable long-horizon task execution. VLA models aim to enable robots to perform complex tasks by interpreting natural language instructions and visual observations to generate sequences of actions. A common strategy to make these models computationally practical is "action chunking," where a policy predicts a short sequence of low-level controls (an "action chunk"), and the robot executes multiple actions from this chunk before refreshing its observation and querying the model again. The number of actions executed per refresh is termed the "execution horizon" (H).

Increasing the execution horizon directly amortizes the computational cost of a single model call over more robot actions, thereby improving inference efficiency. However, a prevalent challenge in the VLA landscape is that raw action chunks often contain inconsistencies such as high-frequency oscillations, temporally erratic directions, or isolated outliers. While these errors might be tolerable for very short execution horizons, their accumulation over longer prefixes significantly degrades control quality and task success. This creates a fundamental trade-off: larger execution horizons enhance efficiency but tend to reduce task accuracy. Prior efforts have explored methods such as modifying chunk selection, adapting the execution horizon, or developing compact action representations (e.g., frequency domain, fixed-length B-spline tokens). However, these approaches either modify chunk selection/execution without altering the learned action representation, or introduce new general-purpose tokenizers without specifically focusing on the fixed-horizon accuracy–efficiency frontier for heterogeneous VLA models.

SMILE positions itself by directly targeting the intrinsic quality of the generated action chunks. It operates as an architecture-preserving interface, meaning it integrates into existing VLA models without requiring changes to their core visual-language conditioning pathways or main denoising backbones. By reparameterizing the action-side denoising target from raw per-step actions to compact B-spline coefficients, SMILE aims to generate inherently smoother and more temporally consistent action sequences. This approach seeks to enable VLA models to leverage longer fixed execution horizons reliably, thereby improving both task accuracy and amortized inference efficiency simultaneously, without increasing the model scale. This work contributes to the development of more robust and efficient robot control policies, particularly for temporally extended manipulation tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this work stems from the observed limitations of existing VLA models when attempting long-horizon robot control using action chunking:

1. **Degradation of Control Quality with Increased Execution Horizon:** VLA models commonly employ action chunking to reduce inference costs by executing multiple actions per model call. While this strategy improves computational efficiency, increasing the execution horizon (H) frequently leads to a decrease in control quality and task success rates. Raw action chunks generated by these models often exhibit high-frequency oscillations, temporal inconsistencies, and outliers. These errors, when executed over a longer sequence without immediate replanning, accumulate and destabilize robot behavior. For instance, empirical observations with baselines like SmolVLA and Evo1 demonstrate a clear drop in task success as the execution horizon increases.

2. **Trade-off Between Accuracy and Amortized Inference Efficiency:** The aforementioned degradation in control quality creates an inherent trade-off. Researchers must either accept lower accuracy to benefit from higher efficiency (longer horizons) or sacrifice efficiency for higher accuracy (shorter horizons). The goal is to overcome this limitation, enabling VLA models to achieve both higher accuracy and improved amortized inference efficiency concurrently.

3. **Need for Temporally Consistent Action Sequences:** The core issue is the lack of temporal coherence within the predicted action chunks. If individual actions within a chunk are not smoothly coupled, they can lead to jerky movements, contacts with unintended objects, or object drops, especially in complex, cluttered environments. Therefore, a method to enforce temporal smoothness and consistency in the generated action sequences is necessary.

Against this background, the key objectives of the SMILE project are:

1. **To introduce a B-spline coefficient action interface:** The primary objective is to develop a novel action representation that generates intrinsically smooth action sequences. This interface should enable VLA models to execute longer fixed horizons with enhanced task accuracy and reduced amortized inference latency per action. This is particularly targeted at improving performance in temporally extended manipulation tasks.

2. **To provide an architecture-preserving integration:** The method aims to integrate seamlessly into existing VLA model architectures. This means retaining each baseline’s original conditioning pathways (e.g., visual-language, predictive-visual, pixel-motion) and main denoising backbone, while maintaining a model size comparable to the corresponding baseline. The objective is to demonstrate that improvements can be achieved by solely modifying the action representation, rather than requiring extensive changes to the core perception or planning components of diverse policy designs.

3. **To demonstrate consistent performance gains and verify smoother execution:** The research aims to empirically validate the proposed method across a range of heterogeneous VLA action experts (VLM-centered, predictive-visual, explicit pixel-motion-conditioned) and benchmark environments (LIBERO, CALVIN, real-world xArm manipulation). A crucial objective is to directly verify that any observed improvements in task accuracy and efficiency correlate with substantially smoother fixed-horizon action execution, quantified by metrics such as reduced intra-chunk acceleration and fewer oscillatory action changes.

In summary, the motivation is to resolve the accuracy-efficiency dilemma in VLA models by improving the temporal quality of action chunks, thereby allowing for reliable, longer fixed-horizon execution without increasing model complexity.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

SMILE proposes a smooth hierarchical action interface designed to denoise VLA action experts by reparameterizing the action space.

**A. Overview**
Traditional action experts predict a raw action chunk $A_j = [a_{j,1}, \dots, a_{j,F}] \in R^{F \times d}$ for a generated chunk length $F$. During inference, the robot executes the first $H \le F$ actions. SMILE replaces this raw chunk with a compact macro-action $M_j = [\Theta_{tr}^j, \Theta_{rot}^j, \Theta_{grip}^j]$, which consists of B-spline coefficients for translation, rotation, and gripper control. A deterministic decoder then maps $M_j$ to an $F$-step smooth action sequence, from which the first $H$ actions are executed. The smoothness is inherent in the representation, as actions are jointly determined by a compact set of spline coefficients.

Crucially, SMILE is designed to be an **architecture-preserving replacement**. It retains the computationally dominant components of the baseline VLA model, including its VLA backbone, conditioning pathway, and main denoising backbone. The method only modifies the action-side input projection, allowing the denoiser to operate on noisy coefficient tokens, and replaces the raw-action output projection with coefficient heads. This design ensures compatibility across various VLA architectures (VLM-centered, predictive-visual, pixel-motion) without increasing the reported model scale.

**B. B-spline Action Representation and Decoding**
For a generated chunk of length $F$, a spline degree $P$, and $K$ control points, a fixed B-spline basis $\Phi^{(P,K)}_F \in R^{F \times K}$ is evaluated at $F$ uniformly spaced action times. For each chunk, the action expert predicts coefficient tensors:
- $\Theta_{tr}^s \in R^{K \times 3}$ for translation.
- $\Theta_{rot}^s \in R^{K \times 3}$ for rotation.
- $\Theta_{grip}^s \in R^{K \times 1}$ for gripper control.

These coefficients are decoded into continuous trajectories for cumulative translation ($P_s$), cumulative command-space rotation ($R_s$), and continuous gripper commands ($G_s$):
- $P_s = \Phi^{(P,K)}_F \Theta_{tr}^s$
- $R_s = \Phi^{(P,K)}_F \Theta_{rot}^s$
- $G_s = \text{clip}(\Phi^{(P,K)}_F \Theta_{grip}^s, g_{min}, g_{max})$

The native controller actions $a_{s,\tau}$ are then derived from these decoded trajectories:
- **Translation actions** ($a_{tr}^{s,\tau}$) are computed as finite differences of the cumulative path: $P_s(\tau) - P_s(\tau-1)$.
- **Rotation actions** ($a_{rot}^{s,\tau}$) involve mapping cumulative rotation vectors to SO(3) and taking adjacent relative rotations, then converting back to the controller's rotation-vector convention.
- **Gripper commands** ($a_{grip}^{s,\tau}$) are directly $G_s(\tau)$.

This process ensures that the decoded actions are compatible with the original robot controller. For all SMILE variants, $K=8$ control points and cubic splines ($P=3$) were selected based on ablation studies.

**C. Architecture-Preserving Denoising Objective**
SMILE modifies the target variable for denoising from raw action sequences to normalized spline coefficients. Let $\Theta_{m, \star}^{s, \text{norm}}$ be the target coefficient tensor for modality $m \in \{tr, rot, grip\}$, and $c_s$ be the baseline’s multimodal conditioning. The denoiser predicts a target (e.g., velocity field for flow-matching or noise for diffusion experts) $y_t^m$. The denoising objective (equation 8) for each modality is:
$L_{denoise}^m = \|f_\theta^m(\Theta_{s,t}, t, c_s) - y_t^m\|_F^2$
The total objective (equation 9) is a weighted sum:
$L_{total} = \lambda_{tr} L_{denoise}^{tr} + \lambda_{rot} L_{denoise}^{rot} + \lambda_{grip} L_{denoise}^{grip}$
Equal weights ($\lambda_{tr} = \lambda_{rot} = \lambda_{grip} = 1$) are used across all SMILE variants.

**D. Coefficient-Target Construction**
Target coefficients are constructed from the same expert action trajectories used by the baselines. For each eligible start step, the next $F$ native actions are collected. Translation and rotation actions are converted into cumulative command-space trajectories, and gripper commands are retained in their absolute form. The target coefficients $\Theta_{m, \star}^s$ for each modality are then obtained by least-squares projection onto the fixed B-spline basis (equation 10):
$\Theta_{m, \star}^s = \arg \min_\Theta \|\Phi^{(P,K)}_F \Theta - Y_s^m\|_F^2$
where $Y_s^m$ is the corresponding cumulative or absolute target trajectory. Dataset-level coefficient statistics are computed for normalization, and validity masks are applied near episode boundaries.

In essence, SMILE introduces a layer of abstraction where the VLA model learns to predict parameters of a smooth curve rather than individual discrete actions, thereby intrinsically improving the temporal consistency and quality of generated motion without altering the fundamental VLA architecture.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrate consistent accuracy and efficiency gains across various VLA models and benchmarks.

**A. LIBERO Benchmark**
Evaluated on the LIBERO benchmark (40 language-conditioned manipulation tasks across Spatial, Object, Goal, and Long suites), SMILE was applied to SmolVLA and Evo1.

- **SMILE-Evo1:** Increased overall success from 92.7% to 98.0%, while extending the execution horizon from 14 to 16. This simultaneously reduced amortized latency from 8.5 ms to 8.0 ms per action. This established an improved accuracy-efficiency operating point.
- **SMILE-SmolVLA:** Increased success from 74.0% to 82.6%, extending the horizon from 1 to 10. This resulted in a significant reduction in latency from 243.9 ms to 24.2 ms per action, representing a 10.1x speedup.
- **Suite-Level Gains:** SMILE-SmolVLA showed improvements across all four suites, with the largest gain on LIBERO-Long (from 42.8% to 63.6%), a suite emphasizing temporally extended tasks. SMILE-Evo1 also improved Spatial, Object, and Long suites, reaching 98.2% on LIBERO-Long.
- These improvements were achieved at comparable model-size scales, isolating the effect of the B-spline action representation.

**B. CALVIN Benchmark**
SMILE was evaluated with predictive-visual (VPP) and explicit pixel-motion-conditioned (DAWN) action experts on the CALVIN benchmark, which involves long-horizon language-conditioned tasks composed of five subtasks.

- **SMILE-DAWN:** Increased the execution horizon from 10 to 15, while improving the average completed sequence length from 4.10 to 4.18. Amortized latency was reduced from 32.0 ms to 22.7 ms per action.
- **SMILE-VPP:** Increased the execution horizon from 10 to 15, and improved average completed sequence length from 4.33 to 4.42. Amortized latency was reduced from 19.1 ms to 13.0 ms per action.
- **Later Subtask Gains:** Most improvements were observed in later subtasks, indicating SMILE's benefit in scenarios where errors can accumulate.
- SMILE-VPP achieved an average sequence length of 4.42, which is comparable to DreamVLA (4.44) but with a significantly lower amortized inference latency (13.0 ms vs. 193.2 ms), representing a 14.9x speedup. These results confirmed SMILE's generalization beyond VLM-centered models.

**C. Real-world Experiment (xArm)**
Experiments were conducted using a 7-DoF xArm7 with two RGB cameras on 12 lift-and-place tasks with six object categories under plain and cluttered arrangements. Evo1 and VPP were compared with their SMILE variants, fine-tuned on a collected dataset.

- **Overall Success:** SMILE variants improved success for every object category in both settings.
 - **Plain setting:** Evo1's average success increased from 26.7% to 78.3%; VPP's increased from 82.5% to 98.3%.
 - **Cluttered setting:** Evo1's average success increased from 8.3% to 56.7%; VPP's increased from 70.8% to 88.3%.
- **Reduced Errors in Clutter:** In cluttered environments, SMILE-Evo1 reduced the hit rate (distractor contact) by 22.5 percentage points (pp) and wrong-object pickup rate by 23.4 pp. SMILE-VPP reduced hit rate by 13.3 pp and wrong-object pickup rate by 0.8 pp.
- **Elimination of Object Drops:** Qualitative observations indicated more stable gripper behavior. The aggregate drop rate for baselines was eliminated by both SMILE variants in the plain setting (from 10.0% and 5.8% to 0.0%).
- **Increased Horizons and Speedups:** SMILE increased the executed horizon for Evo1 from 6 to 16 (2.7x speedup, 21.3 ms to 8.0 ms per action) and for VPP from 10 to 15 (1.5x speedup, 19.1 ms to 13.0 ms per action).

**D. Action Smoothness Analysis**
An analysis comparing SmolVLA and SMILE-SmolVLA at a matched execution horizon (H=10) on LIBERO quantified the mechanism behind the observed gains. Metrics included finite-difference acceleration (global, non-boundary, boundary) and velocity sign-change rate (Vel. ZCR).

- SMILE-SmolVLA reduced non-boundary acceleration by 78.6% and Vel. ZCR by 42.3%.
- Global and boundary acceleration were reduced by 18.6% and 20.5%, respectively.
- The substantially larger reduction within chunks suggests that coefficient-space generation primarily suppresses intra-chunk jitter. Qualitative traces showed SMILE maintaining a lower acceleration baseline and suppressing high-frequency fluctuations.

**E. Ablation Study**
Ablations on LIBERO confirmed that SMILE consistently outperformed raw-action baselines across multiple long execution horizons, demonstrating robustness. Hyperparameter tuning indicated that $K=8$ control points and a cubic spline degree ($P=3$) provided optimal performance, suggesting that a modest low-order basis is sufficient and that increased spline complexity does not necessarily improve success.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of SMILE represents a significant contribution to the field of robot control, particularly for long-horizon Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. Its primary significance lies in demonstrating a viable and broadly applicable method to overcome the inherent trade-off between inference efficiency and control accuracy in action-chunking policies.

The core impact stems from SMILE's ability to generate inherently smooth and temporally consistent action sequences through B-spline coefficient prediction. This enables VLA models to reliably utilize longer fixed execution horizons, leading to simultaneous improvements in:

1. **Task Accuracy:** Demonstrated by increased success rates across diverse benchmarks (LIBERO, CALVIN, real-world xArm) and varied VLA architectures. This improved accuracy is particularly critical for complex, temporally extended tasks where errors can compound over time.
2. **Amortized Inference Efficiency:** By supporting longer execution horizons, SMILE effectively reduces the number of model calls required per task, thereby significantly lowering the amortized inference latency per action. This translates to faster task completion and reduced computational overhead, making VLA models more practical for real-world deployment.

A key aspect of SMILE's impact is its **architecture-preserving nature**. By modifying only the action-side interface and retaining the original VLA backbone and conditioning pathways, SMILE offers a general solution that can be integrated into heterogeneous policy designs—including VLM-centered, predictive-visual, and explicit pixel-motion-conditioned action experts—without requiring substantial architectural re-engineering or increasing model scale. This broad applicability suggests that the principle of smooth coefficient-space generation is a fundamental mechanism for enhancing VLA performance.

The detailed action smoothness analysis provides empirical evidence directly linking the performance gains to the reduced jitter and oscillatory motions within action chunks. This scientific verification strengthens the understanding of why smoother representations contribute to more robust robot control. In real-world robotic applications, the observed reductions in object drops, contacts with distractors, and wrong-object pickups underscore the practical benefits of SMILE, particularly in cluttered and constrained environments.

In conclusion, SMILE establishes smooth coefficient-space generation as a general and effective strategy for improving the accuracy-efficiency operating point of compact VLA models. Its potential impact includes facilitating the development of more reliable, robust, and computationally efficient robotic systems capable of performing intricate, long-horizon tasks in diverse and challenging environments.
