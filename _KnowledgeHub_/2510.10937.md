# Neutral Agent-based Adversarial Policy Learning against Deep Reinforcement Learning in Multi-party Open Systems

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at the Chinese Academy of Sciences developed a neutral agent-based adversarial policy learning approach to attack Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL) agents in multi-party open systems. This method enables adversarial agents to learn policies to induce task failures in DRL victims without direct interaction or full access to environmental states, reducing victim winning rates to as low as 0% in various scenarios.

## Problem

- Existing adversarial attacks against DRL agents typically assume either full environmental control or direct interaction between attacker and victim, which is impractical in complex multi-party open systems.
- Attackers in multi-party open systems face challenges like inaccessible victim reward functions and operating under partial observability, hindering effective adversarial reward design and computation.
- The complexity of multi-party open systems, with varying numbers of agents and party affiliations, limits the applicability of current attack methods.

## Method

- A neutral agent-based adversarial policy learning framework is introduced, allowing bystander agents to learn adversarial policies that indirectly influence victim DRL agents within a Decentralized Partially Observable Markov Decision Process (Dec-POMDP).
- A novel adversary reward function is designed based on observable 'failure paths' (e.g., victim damage, task delay) and a weighted sum of their impacts, circumventing the need for private victim reward access.
- An LSTM-based estimation model is proposed to calculate step-wise adversarial rewards from partial observations, trained by matching episode-end total ground truth rewards, addressing the challenge of partial observability.

## Results

- The method reduced victim DRL agent winning rates from 95-100% (no attack) to 0-10% in Starcraft II scenarios and from 72-100% to 12-28% in Highway-env tasks, demonstrating broad effectiveness.
- The estimation-based reward model outperformed traditional models in attack effectiveness and exhibited significantly faster training convergence, for example, requiring 2 million episodes versus 18 million for the traditional model on the '8m' map.
- The proposed attack proved robust against simple retraining of victim agents and was largely unaffected by existing defense mechanisms like environment-manipulation based (CAMP) and game-theoretic (PATROL) defenses.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Neutral agents, even without direct interaction or full environmental control, can effectively learn and deploy adversarial policies to degrade the performance of DRL victim agents in complex multi-party environments.
> - Adversarial rewards can be effectively constructed from observable 'failure paths,' removing the dependency on private victim reward functions and making attacks feasible in black-box scenarios.
> - Partial observability for reward computation can be effectively managed using an estimation-based model, allowing adversarial agents to learn robust policies in realistic, complex systems.
