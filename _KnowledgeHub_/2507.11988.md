# Aime: Towards Fully-Autonomous Multi-Agent Framework

> [!summary] Summary
> ByteDance researchers developed Aime, a multi-agent framework that overcomes limitations of traditional static planning by enabling dynamic, reactive adaptation and on-demand agent specialization. It achieves superior performance across general reasoning, software engineering, and web navigation benchmarks, consistently outperforming state-of-the-art specialized agents.

## Problem

- Existing multi-agent systems (MAS) based on 'plan-and-execute' frameworks suffer from rigid plan execution, making them unable to adapt to real-time feedback or unexpected events.
- Current MAS often use agents with static, predefined roles and fixed toolkits, limiting their extensibility and ability to handle unforeseen or novel tasks.
- Inefficient communication and lack of a centralized state management system lead to context loss, redundant work, and coordination failures between agents.

## Method

- Aime introduces a ==Dynamic Planner== that continuously assesses the global task list and execution history to produce both strategic updates and tactical next actions, allowing for real-time re-planning.
- The ==Actor Factory== implements ==Dynamic Actor Instantiation==, constructing specialized agents on-demand by selecting relevant tool bundles and generating customized prompts based on specific subtask requirements.
- A centralized ==Progress Management Module== serves as a shared memory, maintaining a hierarchical 'progress list' for real-time status tracking and facilitating structured, explicit communication between components.

## Results

- Aime achieved a **77.6%** success rate on the GAIA benchmark, outperforming leading general-purpose agent frameworks like Langfun (**71.5%**) and Trase (**70.3%**).
- It successfully resolved **66.4%** of issues on SWE-bench Verified, surpassing specialized code agents such as OpenHands (**65.8%**) and SWE-agent (**62.4%**).
- Aime demonstrated a **92.3%** success rate on WebVoyager, exceeding strong baselines like Browser use (**89.1%**) and Operator (**87%**) in live web environments.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Continuous re-planning and dynamic task dispatch are essential for adaptability in multi-agent systems, moving beyond rigid, static plans.
> - On-demand agent specialization, enabled by dynamically selecting tools and generating prompts, significantly enhances flexibility and robustness to unforeseen tasks.
> - A centralized progress management module acts as a single source of truth, improving communication coherence and reducing context loss among agents.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of LLM Agents and Multi-Agent Systems (MAS) represents a critical frontier in artificial intelligence, building upon the transformative capabilities of Large Language Models (LLMs) in natural language understanding, reasoning, and generation. Initially, research focused on individual LLM agents, which leverage an LLM as a cognitive engine augmented with external tools (e.g., code interpreters, web search) to perform multi-step tasks. This has enabled autonomous problem-solving beyond the LLM's pre-trained knowledge.

Building on the success of single agents, the field has naturally progressed to Multi-Agent Systems, where teams of LLM agents collaborate. This paradigm is crucial for tackling problems that are too complex or broad in scope for a single agent, allowing for problem decomposition and the synergistic leverage of specialized agents. A dominant architectural approach in current LLM-based MAS is the "plan-and-execute" framework. In this model, a dedicated planner agent first decomposes a user's request into a static sequence of subtasks, which are then assigned to executor agents with predefined roles and toolsets. Examples of this paradigm include frameworks that simulate human organizational structures, such as MetaGPT and ChatDev, where agents assume roles like "product manager" or "engineer" and follow structured procedures.

However, the "plan-and-execute" model, despite its widespread adoption, suffers from fundamental limitations, particularly in dynamic or unpredictable environments. The rigid separation of planning and execution, static agent capabilities, and inefficient communication bottlenecks its effectiveness. This paper directly addresses these inherent weaknesses. Aime positions itself as a significant advancement by proposing a framework that moves beyond the static nature of traditional MAS. It aims to enable dynamic, reactive planning and on-demand agent specialization, fundamentally improving adaptability and robustness. This work is part of a broader trend seeking to develop more resilient and truly autonomous AI systems capable of operating effectively in the complex, ever-changing real world, contrasting with prior work that often produces static collaboration plans or focuses solely on optimizing individual agent design.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of Aime is to overcome the critical limitations prevalent in the conventional "plan-and-execute" framework for LLM-powered Multi-Agent Systems, thereby enabling more dynamic, reactive, and effective collaboration. The authors identify three fundamental challenges that motivate the development of Aime:

1. **Rigid Plan Execution:** In existing systems, plans are typically generated once and executed rigidly. The planner often remains idle during execution, rendering the system unable to adapt to real-time feedback, unexpected outcomes, or deviations by executor agents. This brittleness severely compromises system performance in dynamic environments. Aime aims to replace this static workflow with continuous adaptation.
2. **Static Agent Capabilities:** Current MAS often rely on agents with predefined, fixed roles and toolkits. This rigidity limits the system's extensibility and robustness, as it struggles to handle unforeseen tasks that demand novel skills, specialized knowledge, or different tools not initially assigned to an agent. The goal for Aime is to allow for flexible, on-demand agent specialization.
3. **Inefficient Communication:** Task handoffs between agents frequently lead to context loss, and the absence of a centralized state management system means agents operate with an incomplete view of overall progress. This can result in redundant work, coordination failures, and significant delays. Aime seeks to establish a single source of truth for system-wide state awareness to streamline communication.

In essence, Aime's core motivation is to develop a multi-agent framework that transforms the traditional rigid, top-down workflow into a fluid and adaptive architecture, thereby enhancing the adaptability, robustness, and overall task success rate of LLM-based MAS in complex, evolving scenarios.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Aime introduces a novel multi-agent framework designed for dynamic, reactive planning and execution, characterized by its continuous adaptation. It replaces the fixed planner-executor dichotomy with a more fluid architecture comprising four core components that work in concert: the Dynamic Planner, the Actor Factory, the Dynamic Actor, and the Progress Management Module.

#### 3.1. Dynamic Planner

The Dynamic Planner serves as the central orchestrator, addressing the rigidity of traditional static planners. It integrates high-level strategic overview with adaptive, incremental execution. At each step, it assesses the current task list and execution history to produce two outputs: an updated global task list (strategic output) and a specific, executable next action (tactical output). This dual focus allows it to modify the global plan and dispatch new tasks or contingencies in a single iteration, ensuring seamless re-planning based on real-time feedback without waiting for full subtask completion. Its interaction with the system state is strictly mediated through the Progress Management Module, ensuring consistency.

#### 3.2. Actor Factory (Dynamic Actor Instantiation)

To overcome the limitations of static agent capabilities, Aime introduces the Actor Factory, which implements Dynamic Actor Instantiation. Upon receiving a subtask from the Dynamic Planner, the factory analyzes its specifications and constructs a new, specialized Dynamic Actor on-demand. This involves:
- **Toolkit Selection:** Rather than providing all available tools, the factory selects appropriate *pre-packaged bundles* of tools (e.g., 'WebSearch', 'FileSystem') tailored to the specific subtask. This ensures functional completeness while reducing the LLM's cognitive load.
- **Prompt Generation:** A customized system prompt is dynamically assembled. This prompt includes a persona aligned with the subtask, concise descriptions of the selected tools, relevant knowledge dynamically retrieved from a knowledge base, global environmental context, and specifications for the required output format (e.g., JSON schema). This on-the-fly tailoring equips each actor with the precise capabilities and context needed for its task.

#### 3.3. Dynamic Actor

Once instantiated by the Actor Factory, a Dynamic Actor autonomously executes its assigned subtask using the ReAct (Reasoning and Acting) paradigm. In an iterative cycle, the actor’s LLM analyzes its objective and local history to generate a thought and a subsequent action (typically invoking a tool). The output from the tool becomes an observation for the next reasoning step. A crucial innovation is the actor's ability to proactively communicate progress. The actor’s toolkit is augmented with a special `Update Progress(status, message)` tool, which the LLM autonomously invokes at appropriate moments (e.g., milestones, obstacles). This provides the Dynamic Planner with near real-time visibility into ongoing activities. Upon subtask completion, the actor generates a structured report summarizing the outcome, status, and any relevant artifacts for subsequent tasks.

#### 3.4. Progress Management Module

This module serves as the centralized state manager and shared memory for the entire framework, establishing a single source of truth. Its core is a globally accessible, hierarchical "progress list" (e.g., a Markdown task list) that represents the complete task decomposition.
- **Real-time Status Tracking:** Each item in the list is marked with its current status (e.g., completed ‘[x]’, pending ‘[ ]’).
- **Embedded Context and Dependencies:** The hierarchical structure implicitly encodes dependencies, and each item can link to explicit completion criteria.

Coordination is achieved through two protocols:
- **Real-time Synchronization:** Dynamic Actors use the `Update Progress` tool to push incremental updates to the progress list, providing high-fidelity visibility to the Dynamic Planner.
- **Structured Task Conclusion:** Upon completing a subtask, the actor sends a formal, structured report (`o_t`) to the Dynamic Planner, which includes a status update, a narrative summary, and reference pointers to produced artifacts. This ensures context is explicitly maintained and accurately transferred.

#### 3.5. Workflow

The Aime framework operates through an iterative workflow:
1. **Task Decomposition:** The Dynamic Planner receives a user request, decomposes it into subtasks, and initializes the progress list in the Progress Management Module.
2. **(Sub)Task Dispatch:** The Dynamic Planner identifies the next executable subtask and dispatches it to the Actor Factory.
3. **Actor Instantiation:** The Actor Factory instantiates a specialized Dynamic Actor tailored to the subtask.
4. **ReAct Execution:** The Dynamic Actor executes its subtask in a ReAct loop, using its configured tools.
5. **Progress Update:** The actor continuously reports status updates to the Progress Management Module.
6. **Evaluation and Iteration:** Upon subtask completion, the actor reports the outcome to the Dynamic Planner, which evaluates it, updates the global plan, and dispatches the next subtask. This cycle continues until the overall user request is fulfilled. This integrated workflow ensures dynamic adaptation, efficient communication, and flexible role definition.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

To evaluate Aime's effectiveness, the authors conducted comprehensive experiments across three diverse and challenging benchmarks, comparing Aime against state-of-the-art specialized agents in their respective domains.

#### 4.1. Experimental Setup

- **Datasets:**
 - **GAIA (General AI Assistants):** A benchmark for general AI assistants requiring multi-step reasoning, tool use, and multi-modal content comprehension. Evaluation used the official exact string matching metric.
 - **SWE-bench Verified (Software Engineering):** A curated subset of SWE-bench for assessing an agent's ability to resolve real-world software engineering problems. Success is rigorously evaluated via unit tests.
 - **WebVoyager (Web Navigation):** An end-to-end benchmark for web agents interacting with live websites, measured by task success rate.
- **Baselines:**
 - **GAIA:** Langfun, Trase, OWL (leading general-purpose agent frameworks).
 - **SWE-bench Verified:** SWE-agent, OpenHands (top-performing code agents).
 - **WebVoyager:** Browser use, Operator, Skyvern (prominent web agents).
- **Consistency:** All agents, including Aime, utilized the same underlying LLM for a fair comparison, although the specific LLM model is not explicitly named in the abstract or introduction.

#### 4.2. Experimental Results

The experimental results, summarized in Table 1 of the paper, demonstrate Aime's consistent outperformance over highly specialized baselines, establishing its strong generalization capabilities and a new state-of-the-art across these diverse domains.

- **On GAIA:** Aime achieved a remarkable 77.6% success rate, significantly outperforming Langfun (71.5%), Trase (70.3%), and OWL (69.1%). This superior performance is attributed to the Dynamic Planner's ability to flexibly adapt its strategy when initial reasoning paths prove ineffective, which is crucial for GAIA's complex, multi-step problems.
- **On SWE-bench Verified:** Aime successfully resolved 66.4% of the issues, surpassing specialized agents like OpenHands (65.8%) and SWE-agent (62.4%). The authors attribute this advantage to the Actor Factory's capability to instantiate different types of agents on-the-fly (e.g., a "code-reader" for context understanding followed by a "debugger" for fault isolation), leading to a more robust problem-solving process in software engineering.
- **On WebVoyager:** Aime demonstrated superior robustness in live web environments, achieving an impressive 92.3% success rate. This exceeded strong baselines such as Browser use (89.1%), Operator (87%), and Skyvern (85.6%). Aime's tight feedback loop between Dynamic Actors and the Dynamic Planner enabled immediate re-planning and error recovery in response to unexpected website changes, resulting in a higher task completion rate.

In summary, Aime not only competes with but consistently outperforms specialized state-of-the-art agents in their respective domains, showcasing its adaptability, efficiency, and robustness across general reasoning, software engineering, and web navigation tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

Aime represents a significant stride towards building truly autonomous and resilient multi-agent systems powered by Large Language Models. Its primary significance lies in its direct and effective addressal of the fundamental limitations of the prevailing "plan-and-execute" framework in multi-agent collaboration: rigid planning, static agent capabilities, and inefficient communication.

The framework's core innovations — the **Dynamic Planner**, **Actor Factory** with dynamic actor instantiation, and a centralized **Progress Management Module** — collectively enable a paradigm shift from static, brittle execution to dynamic, reactive adaptation. This leads to several impactful implications:

1. **Enhanced Adaptability and Robustness:** By allowing continuous refinement of strategy based on real-time feedback and on-demand specialization of agents, Aime can better navigate unpredictable and complex environments. This adaptability makes it significantly more robust to unexpected outcomes or changes compared to systems reliant on predefined, static plans.
2. **Improved Efficiency and Coherence:** The centralized Progress Management Module acts as a single source of truth, drastically reducing context loss during task handoffs and mitigating coordination failures. This ensures that all components operate with a coherent, system-wide understanding of progress, leading to more efficient collaboration and less redundant work.
3. **Broad Generalizability:** The empirical results across diverse and challenging benchmarks (general reasoning, software engineering, web navigation) demonstrate that Aime's architectural innovations lead to superior performance not just in a niche area, but across a wide spectrum of complex problems. This generalizability is a crucial step towards creating more versatile and broadly applicable AI systems.
4. **Foundation for Advanced Autonomous Systems:** By establishing a more resilient and effective foundation for multi-agent collaboration, Aime paves the way for building more sophisticated autonomous agents capable of tackling increasingly complex, open-ended tasks in real-world scenarios. This could impact fields ranging from advanced virtual assistants and automated software development to complex scientific discovery, real-time operational management, and even embodied AI.

The authors' future work focuses on enhancing scalability for larger agent teams and empowering agents to autonomously acquire new capabilities, reducing their reliance on pre-curated tools. These directions underscore the ambition to move towards even more self-sufficient and capable multi-agent intelligence. Aime's contribution lies in its practical and effective framework for dynamic adaptation, offering a compelling alternative to static designs and pushing the boundaries of what LLM-powered multi-agent systems can achieve.
