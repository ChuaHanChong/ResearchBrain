# Enhancing Generalization in Vision-Language-Action Models by Preserving Pretrained Representations

> [!summary] Summary
> A framework by Grover et al. improves Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models' generalization and robustness for robotic manipulation by preserving pretrained Vision-Language Model (VLM) representations during fine-tuning. This method, which integrates a partially-frozen dual encoder, a string-based action tokenizer, and strategic co-training, leads to higher success rates in diverse visual and linguistic conditions, demonstrating improved zero-shot generalization and robustness to distractors in both simulation and real-world tasks.

## Problem

- Directly fine-tuning Vision-Language Models (VLMs) for robotic control often leads to "representation degradation," losing valuable semantic and spatial understanding acquired during large-scale pretraining.
- This degradation limits VLA models' generalization to novel visual environments, robustness to varied linguistic instructions, and ability to perform complex reasoning.
- Existing solutions like naive co-training or simple data augmentation struggle to effectively mitigate representation degradation, sometimes introducing conflicting training objectives.

## Method

- A ==partially-frozen dual encoder== architecture maintains robust, high-level features through a frozen encoder while a trainable encoder specializes visual processing for robotic tasks.
- A ==string-based action tokenizer== unifies the continuous robot action space with the language domain, allowing the pretrained Large Language Model backbone to effectively reuse its existing language representations for action prediction.
- Feature regularization is achieved through strategic co-training on a balanced mixture of robot demonstration data and diverse vision-language datasets, reinforcing semantic and spatial understanding.

## Results

- The full proposed recipe significantly improved average success rates in simulation (e.g., OpenVLA at **78.46%**, a **40%** improvement over baseline OpenVLA's **35.03%**), and demonstrated substantial gains in out-of-distribution visual robustness (e.g., **76.6%** for OpenVLA on Visual Variant Aggregation vs. **52.4%** for baseline OpenVLA).
- Learned representations showed higher quality, evidenced by improved linear-probe classification performance (**83%** for OpenVLA vs. **67%** for OpenVLA) and more clearly separated clusters in t-SNE visualizations.
- Models exhibited enhanced language robustness to paraphrased instructions (e.g., **84.52%** for OpenVLA with paraphrased instructions vs. **38.89%** for baseline OpenVLA) and superior performance on VQA benchmarks and real-world tasks, including improved resilience to distractors.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly preserving pretrained VLM representations is crucial for enhancing generalization and robustness in VLA models, preventing the loss of broad knowledge during task-specific adaptation.
> - Unifying action and language spaces via string-based tokenization allows for maximal reuse of pretrained language capabilities, enabling more coherent learning for both reasoning and precise action generation.
> - A carefully designed co-training strategy with a dual-encoder architecture effectively regularizes VLM features, maintaining strong vision-language reasoning abilities while improving robotic task performance.
