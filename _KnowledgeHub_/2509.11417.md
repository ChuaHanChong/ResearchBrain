# Enhancing Generalization in Vision-Language-Action Models by Preserving Pretrained Representations

> [!summary] Summary
> A framework by Grover et al. improves Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models' generalization and robustness for robotic manipulation by preserving pretrained Vision-Language Model (VLM) representations during fine-tuning. This method, which integrates a partially-frozen dual encoder, a string-based action tokenizer, and strategic co-training, leads to higher success rates in diverse visual and linguistic conditions, demonstrating improved zero-shot generalization and robustness to distractors in both simulation and real-world tasks.

## Problem

- Directly fine-tuning Vision-Language Models (VLMs) for robotic control often leads to "representation degradation," losing valuable semantic and spatial understanding acquired during large-scale pretraining.
- This degradation limits VLA models' generalization to novel visual environments, robustness to varied linguistic instructions, and ability to perform complex reasoning.
- Existing solutions like naive co-training or simple data augmentation struggle to effectively mitigate representation degradation, sometimes introducing conflicting training objectives.

## Method

- A ==partially-frozen dual encoder== architecture maintains robust, high-level features through a frozen encoder while a trainable encoder specializes visual processing for robotic tasks.
- A ==string-based action tokenizer== unifies the continuous robot action space with the language domain, allowing the pretrained Large Language Model backbone to effectively reuse its existing language representations for action prediction.
- Feature regularization is achieved through strategic co-training on a balanced mixture of robot demonstration data and diverse vision-language datasets, reinforcing semantic and spatial understanding.

## Results

- The full proposed recipe significantly improved average success rates in simulation (e.g., OpenVLA at **78.46%**, a **40%** improvement over baseline OpenVLA's **35.03%**), and demonstrated substantial gains in out-of-distribution visual robustness (e.g., **76.6%** for OpenVLA on Visual Variant Aggregation vs. **52.4%** for baseline OpenVLA).
- Learned representations showed higher quality, evidenced by improved linear-probe classification performance (**83%** for OpenVLA vs. **67%** for OpenVLA) and more clearly separated clusters in t-SNE visualizations.
- Models exhibited enhanced language robustness to paraphrased instructions (e.g., **84.52%** for OpenVLA with paraphrased instructions vs. **38.89%** for baseline OpenVLA) and superior performance on VQA benchmarks and real-world tasks, including improved resilience to distractors.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly preserving pretrained VLM representations is crucial for enhancing generalization and robustness in VLA models, preventing the loss of broad knowledge during task-specific adaptation.
> - Unifying action and language spaces via string-based tokenization allows for maximal reuse of pretrained language capabilities, enabling more coherent learning for both reasoning and precise action generation.
> - A carefully designed co-training strategy with a dual-encoder architecture effectively regularizes VLM features, maintaining strong vision-language reasoning abilities while improving robotic task performance.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This paper operates at the intersection of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) and robotic control, specifically addressing the challenges of building **Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models** for generalist robotic manipulation. The broader research landscape is currently dominated by the success of foundation models in vision and language, which leverage internet-scale data to achieve impressive generalization capabilities in passive perception and reasoning tasks. The ultimate goal in robotics is to achieve similar levels of generalization for embodied agents, enabling robots to perform diverse tasks across varied environments with natural language instructions.

Historically, robotics has faced a significant "data gap" – the difficulty and cost of collecting large-scale, action-labeled robot data comparable to the vast text and image datasets available for VLMs. This limitation has historically hindered the development of truly general-purpose robots. The emergence of VLAs aims to bridge this gap by fine-tuning powerful, pretrained VLMs on smaller robotic datasets, thereby inheriting their rich representations and generalization potential.

However, the authors identify a critical problem: directly fine-tuning these pretrained VLMs on robot data often leads to "representation degradation." This means the fine-tuning process, optimized for specific robotic actions, disrupts the valuable semantic and spatial understanding that the VLMs acquired during pretraining on web-scale data. This degradation manifests as reduced robustness to visual perturbations (e.g., background changes, distractors) and linguistic variations (e.g., instruction paraphrases), ultimately limiting the robot's generalization capabilities.

Previous attempts to mitigate this issue include input augmentation, spatially enriched encoders, or co-training on both vision-language and robotic data. However, the authors note that naive co-training can lead to conflicting objectives and performance degradation in both domains. This paper differentiates itself by proposing a novel "training recipe" that *explicitly aims to preserve* these valuable pretrained representations during adaptation, rather than simply augmenting data or naively combining training objectives. It builds upon and critically evaluates existing VLA architectures like OpenVLA and π0, which serve as strong baselines in the field. By addressing the fundamental problem of representation preservation, this work aims to unlock the full potential of VLMs for robust and generalizable robotic control, thereby advancing the field closer to the long-standing goal of general-purpose robots.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to **enhance the generalization and robustness of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models** for robotic manipulation by **better preserving the powerful pretrained representations** derived from Vision-Language Models (VLMs).

The motivation stems from a critical observation: while VLMs demonstrate impressive generalization capabilities on passive perception and reasoning tasks, their direct fine-tuning for active robotic control often leads to a degradation of these very representations. The authors’ preliminary experiments vividly illustrate this issue (Figure 1), showing significant performance drops in existing VLA models (OpenVLA and π0) when faced with minor visual changes (background variations, masking) or slight rephrasing of instructions. This suggests that the fine-tuning process for robotic actions, typically on smaller and more specialized datasets, causes a "catastrophic forgetting" or distortion of the rich, general knowledge embedded within the VLM's visual and linguistic encoders.

The paper argues that this representation degradation limits a VLA model's ability to:
1. **Generalize to novel visual environments:** Robots need to operate in diverse, unstructured settings, not just those seen in training data.
2. **Robustly respond to varied instructions:** Natural language commands can be phrased in many ways, and a generalist robot should understand synonyms and paraphrases.
3. **Perform complex reasoning:** The inherent reasoning capabilities of VLMs are crucial for understanding spatial relationships, affordances, and multi-step tasks.

Therefore, the key motivation is to find an effective "training recipe" that allows VLA models to adapt to the specific demands of robotic action prediction without sacrificing the broad generalization and robustness gained during VLM pretraining. The authors hypothesize that by strategically designing components that encourage the retention of these pretrained features, they can achieve significantly more capable and generalist robotic policies.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors propose a novel framework comprising three interconnected design choices to better preserve pretrained VLM representations during VLA fine-tuning:

1. **Partially-Frozen Dual Encoder Architecture:**
 - **Problem Addressed:** Direct fine-tuning of a single vision encoder often leads to representation degradation. Completely freezing it, however, limits adaptation to robotic tasks.
 - **Approach:** Instead of a single encoder, the model uses two "siamese" visual encoders:
 - **Frozen Encoder (Anchor):** This encoder's weights are kept frozen throughout training. It acts as an anchor, retaining the robust, semantically rich features learned during large-scale VLM pretraining.
 - **Trainable Encoder:** This encoder's weights are fine-tuned on the robotic action data. It provides the necessary flexibility for the model to specialize its visual processing for the nuances of robot manipulation.
 - **Mechanism:** The latent representations from both encoders are concatenated (`z_t = [ϕ_frozen(o_t) || ϕ_train(o_t)]`) and then passed to the downstream language model for action generation. This ensures that even as one encoder adapts, the other continuously provides stable, high-level features.

2. **String-Based Action Tokenizer:**
 - **Problem Addressed:** Traditional VLA models often remap rare language tokens to discretized action bins, discarding their pretrained semantics and breaking alignment with the VLM's original language representations.
 - **Approach:** Continuous robot actions (e.g., `(∆x, ∆y, ∆z, ϕ, θ, ψ, g)`) are cast into character sequences (strings). For instance, `∆x = 0.0312` becomes the token sequence `0 . 0 3 1 2`.
 - **Mechanism:** By representing numerical actions as strings, the model's output space is unified with its language processing domain. This allows the pretrained Large Language Model (LLM) backbone to maximally reuse its existing language representations for action prediction. It also enables a single output head to handle both action prediction (for robot data) and non-action prediction (for vision-language datasets during co-training). Autoregressive string generation also allows for step-by-step refinement of actions, potentially leading to more precise control.

3. **Feature Regularization via Co-training:**
 - **Problem Addressed:** Training solely on limited robot datasets often leads to overfitting and limits generalization. Naive co-training with general vision-language data can introduce conflicting objectives.
 - **Approach:** The model is co-trained on a carefully selected mixture of robot data and diverse vision-language (VL) datasets.
 - **Dataset Composition:**
 - **Robot Data:** OXE (Open X-Embodiment) dataset provides real-world robot demonstrations.
 - **Vision-Language Data:** Emphasizes spatial reasoning and affordances, including LLaVA Visual Instruct, CC3M (image captioning), VQASynth-Spatial (spatial reasoning), LLaVA OneVision (OCR, chart VQA), and RoboPoint (spatial grounding).
 - **Strategy:** A balanced sampling approach (50% robot data, 50% VL data per batch) ensures that both objectives contribute.
 - **Mechanism:** Leveraging the unified string-based output space (from the action tokenizer), the model learns both to predict actions and to perform vision-language reasoning tasks. This joint training prevents catastrophic forgetting of VLM capabilities and reinforces the semantic and spatial understanding crucial for generalization in robotics.

The proposed framework is generalizable and applied to two representative VLA architectures: **OpenVLA** and **π0**, initialized from their respective pretrained VLMs (DINO + SigLIP + Llama for OpenVLA; PaliGemma for π0). The models are fine-tuned primarily on the RT-1 dataset.

**Evaluation:**
- **Simulation:** Utilizes SimplerEnv, a platform known to correlate well with real-world performance.
 - **Visual Matching:** In-distribution evaluation with visuals similar to training data.
 - **Visual Variant Aggregation:** Out-of-distribution (OOD) evaluation with randomized backgrounds, textures, lighting, distractors, and camera poses to assess generalization and robustness.
- **Real-world:** Deployment on a ViperX 300s robot, fine-tuned on the Bridge dataset, to assess performance on novel instructions and distractors.
- **Metrics:** Task success rates, linear-probe classification performance (on CIFAR-10) for representation quality, t-SNE visualizations, language robustness (paraphrased instructions), and performance on standard VQA benchmarks.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments consistently demonstrate that the proposed framework significantly enhances the generalization, robustness, and overall performance of VLA models.

**A. Analysis of Different Design Choices (Q1: Effectiveness)**
- **Overall Performance:** In the SimplerEnv Visual Matching setup (in-distribution), the models trained with the full recipe (OpenVLA$^+$ and π0$^+$) substantially outperform their respective baselines. OpenVLA$^+$ achieves an average success rate of 78.46%, a nearly 40% improvement over baseline OpenVLA (35.03%). Similarly, π0$^+$ reaches 69.19%, a 7% improvement over baseline π0 (62.64%).
- **Individual Component Impact:**
 - **Partially-Frozen Dual Encoder (D):** Alone, it improved OpenVLA from 35.03% to 55.55%. Its full benefits, particularly for robustness, become evident in OOD evaluations.
 - **String Tokenizer (S):** Improved OpenVLA to 50.25% on its own. Critically, its synergy with co-training was strong (OpenVLA+SC at 78.17% vs. OpenVLA+C at 51.05%), indicating its role in unifying the output space for effective knowledge transfer.
 - **Co-training (C):** Combined with the string tokenizer (OpenVLA+SC), it yielded very strong performance, demonstrating the effectiveness of feature regularization.
- **Co-training Data Composition:** An ablation study showed that combining diverse vision-language datasets emphasizing spatial reasoning and affordances consistently improved robotic task performance, confirming the benefit of the chosen data mix.

**B. Analysis of Representation Generalization and Robustness (Q2: Preservation & Generalization)**
- **Visual Robustness (OOD Generalization):**
 - Both OpenVLA$^+$ and π0$^+$ showed substantial improvements in OOD settings.
 - On "Masked Background," OpenVLA$^+$ achieved 76.4% average success, compared to 55.5% for OpenVLA.
 - On "Visual Variant Aggregation" (randomized visuals), OpenVLA$^+$ reached 76.6% average success, significantly higher than OpenVLA's 52.4%. These results highlight improved robustness to visual perturbations.
- **Visualizing Learned Visual Representations (t-SNE):**
 - t-SNE plots of features extracted from the visual encoders on CIFAR-10 revealed a clear distinction. While baseline VLA models showed unclear boundaries between classes, features from models trained with the proposed method exhibited much tighter, more well-separated class clusters. This translates to higher linear-probe classification performance (e.g., 83% for OpenVLA$^+$ vs. 67% for OpenVLA), directly indicating better preservation of the semantic structures from pretrained VLMs.
- **Language Robustness:**
 - When evaluated on paraphrased instructions (unseen during training), our models significantly outperformed baselines, even those trained with instruction augmentation. For "PickCan," OpenVLA$^+$ achieved 84.52% with paraphrased instructions, whereas baseline OpenVLA with augmentation only reached 38.89%. This demonstrates that the string-based action tokenizer, by better leveraging pretrained language representations, leads to stronger zero-shot generalization to linguistic variations.
- **Reasoning Abilities (VQA Benchmarks):**
 - OpenVLA$^+$ and π0$^+$ achieved substantially higher accuracy across five standard VQA benchmarks (Text-VQA, POPE, GQA, VizWiz, VSR) compared to their baselines. This indicates that the proposed training recipe effectively preserves the general reasoning capabilities of the VLM backbones, which are critical for understanding complex robotic tasks.

**C. Real-World Evaluation (Q3: Real-World Transfer)**
- **Consistent Outperformance:** On the ViperX 300s robot, our models consistently outperformed baseline VLAs across all tested manipulation tasks.
- **Robustness to Distractors:** A key finding was the improved robustness to distractors. Baseline models often misinterpreted tasks, reaching for the closest object rather than the intended target when distractors were present. In contrast, our approach demonstrated a more robust understanding of goal-conditioned actions, successfully completing tasks even with semantically or spatially similar distractors. For example, in "PickCarrot" with a knife distractor, OpenVLA$^+$ scored 48.0% vs. OpenVLA's 20.0%.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research offers significant contributions to the field of robotic learning and the development of general-purpose intelligent agents. Its potential impact is multifaceted:

1. **Overcoming a Critical Bottleneck in VLA Development:** The paper directly addresses the long-standing problem of "representation degradation" when adapting powerful Vision-Language Models for robotic control. By providing a practical and effective framework to preserve these pretrained representations, it unlocks the full potential of VLMs for embodied AI, moving beyond passive perception to active, robust manipulation.

2. **A Generalizable "Training Recipe" for Robotics:** The proposed methods—dual-encoder design, string-based action tokenizer, and strategic co-training—are modular and can be integrated into existing and future VLA architectures. This provides valuable "practical insights" for researchers and engineers developing robotic policies, making the process of fine-tuning VLMs for real-world tasks more efficient and successful.

3. **Enhanced Generalization and Robustness:** The demonstrated improvements in generalization to out-of-distribution visual environments, robustness to diverse language instructions (including paraphrases), and resilience to distractors are crucial steps towards truly versatile robots. This means robots can operate more reliably in unstructured, dynamic, and unpredictable real-world settings, which is a significant leap from specialized, brittle robotic systems.

4. **Bridging the Gap between Language, Vision, and Action:** The string-based action tokenizer is particularly impactful as it elegantly unifies the action space with the language domain. This allows for maximal reuse of pretrained language representations, effectively grounding abstract language understanding into precise physical actions. This unification simplifies the model architecture and allows for more coherent joint training objectives.

5. **Accelerating the Path to Generalist Robots:** By enabling VLA models to retain and leverage the vast knowledge acquired from internet-scale data, this work brings the robotics community closer to achieving generalist robot manipulation policies. Such robots would be capable of learning quickly, adapting to novel situations, and performing a wide array of tasks with minimal human intervention, thereby transforming industries from manufacturing and logistics to healthcare and service robotics.

6. **Setting a New Standard for VLA Evaluation:** The comprehensive evaluation in both simulation (including OOD tests) and real-world scenarios, alongside VQA benchmarks and representation analysis (t-SNE), provides a robust methodology for assessing VLA performance. This sets a higher bar for future research, emphasizing not just in-distribution success but also true generalization and underlying representation quality.

In conclusion, this paper provides a robust framework that significantly improves the generalization and robustness of VLA models. By offering a principled approach to preserving pretrained VLM representations, it addresses a core challenge in robot learning and represents a substantial step forward in the quest for capable, general-purpose robotic agents.
