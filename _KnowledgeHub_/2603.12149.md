# Linking Perception, Confidence and Accuracy in MLLMs

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Zhejiang University, Alibaba Group, City University of Hong Kong, and University of Michigan developed a framework that links visual perception, confidence, and accuracy in Multi-modal Large Language Models. Their Confidence-Driven Reinforcement Learning (CDRL) training and Confidence-Aware Test-Time Scaling (CA-TTS) inference framework achieved new state-of-the-art results, improving accuracy on benchmarks such as Math-Vista by 5.4% to 79.5% and MMMU by 10.7% to 66.3% compared to previous methods like VL-Rethinker.

## Problem

- Multi-modal Large Language Models (MLLMs) primarily focus on enhancing visual perception and accuracy but largely overlook the model's ability to assess its own uncertainty and 'know when they do not know'.
- MLLMs exhibit systemic confidence miscalibration, particularly under conditions of visual degradation, where high confidence persists despite a sharp decline in actual accuracy.
- Existing calibration methods, often adapted from token-level approaches for LLMs, are misaligned with the holistic nature of MLLM visual perception and response generation.

## Method

- Developed ==Confidence-Driven== Reinforcement Learning (CDRL), a training methodology that uses perturbed image pairs and a novel confidence-based calibration reward to sensitize MLLMs to visual degradation and align confidence with visual understanding.
- Introduced Confidence-Aware Test-Time Scaling (CA-TTS), an adaptive multi-module inference framework consisting of Self-Consistency, Self-Reflection, and Self-Check modules, dynamically coordinated by an Expert Model to leverage calibrated confidence for enhanced performance.
- Defined MLLM confidence for an entire response as the arithmetic mean of the Negative Mean Log-Probability (NMLP) across all output tokens, providing a holistic measure suitable for visual perception.

## Results

- The integrated CDRL+CA-TTS framework achieved new state-of-the-art accuracy on four visual reasoning benchmarks: Math-Vista (**79.5%**), Math-Vision (**42.4%**), MMStar (**71.3%**), and MMMU (**66.3%**), consistently outperforming prior baselines.
- CDRL training significantly enhanced MLLM perceptual sensitivity, leading to 4-8 times larger confidence drops under visual uncertainty and improving calibration metrics (ECE) and uncertainty quantification (AUC).
- CA-TTS demonstrated superior test-time scaling, with a scaling slope 2.2-3.1 times steeper than baselines, achieving an **8.4%** increase in average accuracy with only **0.91** times more inference time compared to standard Majority Voting.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - MLLMs suffer from "perceptual bluntness," reasoning without adequately assessing the quality of their visual input, which is a root cause of miscalibration and unreliable outputs like hallucinations.
> - Explicitly linking visual perception to confidence during training through reinforcement learning can effectively calibrate MLLMs, making their confidence accurately reflect their understanding of visual input.
> - Properly calibrated confidence can serve as a "free lunch" for improving test-time performance by providing a reliable signal to dynamically allocate additional reasoning effort and coordinate diverse inference strategies.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses identified limitations within the current trajectory of Multi-modal Large Language Models (MLLMs). Existing research in MLLMs has primarily concentrated on enhancing visual perception to improve overall accuracy. Efforts have focused on optimizing visual data distributions, refining visual position encodings, and improving visual instruction tuning. While these advancements have contributed to higher accuracy, the paper identifies an under-explored aspect: the MLLMs' ability to assess their own uncertainty, specifically, whether models "know when they do not know."

This research situates itself at the intersection of MLLM visual perception studies, confidence calibration, and test-time scaling strategies.
- **Visual Perception Studies:** Previous MLLM developments, such as CLIP, LLaVA, and Qwen-VL, advanced visual-textual alignment. Research has also explored factors influencing perception, like token over-focus, difficulties with fine-grained visual cues, and challenges in positional encoding. Reinforcement learning, including RLHF and DPO, has been applied to enhance visual performance and mitigate hallucinations. This paper extends this area by explicitly integrating confidence calibration with visual perception.
- **Calibration for MLLMs:** While confidence calibration is a relatively mature field in Large Language Models (LLMs), its application to MLLMs is described as nascent. MLLMs exhibit systemic miscalibration, often characterized by overconfidence that diverges from actual accuracy. Existing MLLM calibration efforts have largely been extensions of LLM methods, focusing on multi-round interrogation, RL-prompting, or training-free techniques like visual contrastive decoding to address issues like hallucination. This work distinguishes itself by tackling the fundamental impact of visual components on calibration, recognizing a granularity mismatch between token-level LLM calibration and holistic MLLM visual perception.
- **Test-Time Scaling (TTS) Strategies:** With a shift from pre-training compute scaling, TTS has emerged to achieve performance improvements during inference by adaptively increasing computational expenditure. This includes parallel scaling (e.g., Self-Consistency), sequential scaling (e.g., Self-Refine), and hybrid approaches (e.g., Tree-of-Thoughts). Recent work has explored TTS in multimodal domains, often involving expert model confidence for reranking. This paper proposes a novel multimodal TTS framework that explicitly leverages calibrated confidence as a reliability indicator, coordinating multiple reasoning modules dynamically. The paper highlights a gap in robust multimodal TTS that fundamentally addresses the role of visual components and framework robustness.

In summary, this work contributes to the MLLM landscape by bridging the gap between visual perception enhancement and confidence calibration, demonstrating that a calibrated understanding of visual input is crucial for developing more reliable and effective MLLMs, particularly when integrated with adaptive test-time scaling.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to investigate and address the problem of confidence miscalibration in MLLMs, specifically how it relates to visual perception and its impact on accuracy. The authors are motivated by the observation that despite advances in MLLM visual perception, a critical question remains unanswered: "Does the model know when they do not know?" This question, inspired by Charles Darwin's observation on ignorance and confidence, highlights a fundamental disconnect in current MLLMs.

The key motivations and objectives are detailed as follows:

- **Identify and Quantify Confidence Miscalibration in MLLMs:** The authors hypothesize that MLLMs suffer from confidence miscalibration, particularly under conditions of visual degradation. To test this, they conduct a probing experiment where noise is progressively added to critical visual evidence in input images. They observe that model confidence remains stable despite a sharp decline in model accuracy under such perception degradation (illustrated in Figure 1). This discrepancy reveals that MLLMs maintain high confidence even when their visual understanding is compromised, indicating a severe miscalibration problem.
- **Address Granularity Mismatch in Confidence Calibration:** Previous work on LLM calibration often relies on token-level uncertainty or logits. However, the authors argue that this approach is misaligned with MLLMs' visual perception, which manifests holistically across an entire response rather than at individual token granularity. A key objective is to define and calculate confidence for an entire MLLM response (as the mean negative log-probability across all output tokens) to accurately reflect visual understanding.
- **Develop a Training Method for Perception-Confidence Alignment:** A core objective is to propose a novel training approach that explicitly links visual perception with confidence. The goal is to make MLLMs' confidence accurately reflect their visual understanding, thereby "bridging the gap between what they see and what they claim to know." This involves designing a reinforcement learning framework that rewards models for exhibiting calibrated confidence, especially in response to visual input changes.
- **Leverage Calibrated Confidence for Enhanced Test-Time Performance:** The authors hypothesize that properly calibrated confidence can serve as a "free lunch" for improving test-time performance. This means that once a model is calibrated, its self-reported confidence can act as a reliable indicator to identify uncertain predictions. The objective is to design a framework that can dynamically use these confidence signals to allocate additional reasoning effort and coordinate various inference strategies more effectively.
- **Propose an Adaptive Multi-Module Test-Time Scaling Framework:** Building on the calibrated confidence, a further objective is to develop a sophisticated Test-Time Scaling (TTS) system. This system aims to integrate and adaptively orchestrate different reasoning modules (e.g., self-consistency, self-reflection, visual self-check) based on confidence levels, guided by an "Expert Model." The intention is to achieve substantial accuracy gains while maintaining computational efficiency.

In essence, the research aims to move beyond simply improving MLLM accuracy by focusing on the often-overlooked aspect of self-awareness regarding visual understanding, thereby developing more robust and trustworthy multimodal AI systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework, designed to address perceptual bluntness and confidence miscalibration in MLLMs, consists of two main components: Confidence-Driven Reinforcement Learning from Vision (CDRL) for training, and Confidence-Aware Test-Time Scaling (CA-TTS) for inference.

#### 3.1. Confidence-Driven RL from Vision (CDRL)

CDRL is a training methodology aimed at enhancing perceptual sensitivity and calibrating the MLLM's confidence.

- **Preliminaries:**
 - **High-Quality Data Filtering:** A training set `D_RL` is constructed by aggregating data from six public benchmarks (three for mathematical reasoning, three for general VQA). An LLM-based pipeline is used to filter this pool based on quality, difficulty, and diversity, resulting in `D_Filtered` (1936 data points).
 - **Noised Image Generation:** To sensitize the model to visual perturbations, each original image `i` in `D_Filtered` is augmented with a perturbed counterpart `i'`. This `i'` is generated by applying a noise function `G_noise` guided by CLIP attention maps, which identify core semantic regions. This creates paired tuples `((i, i'), q, a)` for the final `D_RL`.
 - **Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO):** This algorithm optimizes a policy model `π_θ` against an initial reference model `π_ref`. For each sample `(i, i', q)`, `k` candidate output pairs `(o_j, o'_j)` are generated. The GRPO objective maximizes a reward-weighted average of these outputs while penalizing divergence from `π_ref` using KL-divergence.
 - **Reward Components:** The total reward `r_j` for an output `o_j` consists of three parts:
 - `R_Output,j`: Binary accuracy (1 if `a_j` matches ground truth `GT`, 0 otherwise).
 - `R_Format,j`: Reward for structural correctness of the output.
 - `R_Conf,j`: The novel Confidence-based Calibration Reward.

- **Confidence-based Calibration Reward (`R_Conf,j`):** This reward is designed to improve both perceptual sensitivity and confidence calibration using the original-noise image pair (`i, i'`) and their respective outputs (`o_j, o'_j`).
 - **Confidence Calculation (C):** The model's output confidence `C` for a sequence is defined as the arithmetic mean of the Negative Mean Log-Probability (NMLP) across all tokens. For each token, NMLP is calculated from the top `k` highest log-probability values. A lower NMLP indicates higher certainty.
 - **Reward Formulation:** `R_Conf,j` comprises two terms:
 - **Perception Term:** `α tanh(β * ∆C)`, where `∆C = C_j - C'_j` (confidence difference between original and noised image outputs). This term rewards a large confidence drop when the input image is noised, encouraging sensitivity to visual degradation.
 - **Calibration Term:** `(2 * R_Output,j - 1) * C_norm,j`. This term simplifies to `+C_norm,j` if the output is correct and `-C_norm,j` if incorrect. It incentivizes the model to be confident when accurate and unconfident when wrong.
 - **Gradient Update:** The calibration term's gradient updates are solely based on the original image's output `o_j` and its confidence `C_j` to avoid contaminating the training trajectory with perturbed information.

#### 3.2. Confidence-Aware Test-Time Scaling (CA-TTS)

CA-TTS is an adaptive multi-module inference framework that leverages the calibrated confidence from CDRL training. It consists of three core modules, dynamically coordinated by an Expert Model.

- **Expert Model Roles:** The Expert Model (Gemini-2.5-Pro in implementation) operates as:
 - **Planner:** Schedules the execution order of the three core modules.
 - **Voter:** Participates in confidence-weighted voting.
 - **Critic:** Generates critiques for self-reflection.

- **Core Modules:**
 - **Self-Consistency:** This module moves beyond simple majority voting by employing a confidence-driven, Expert Model-guided approach.
 - For each input (`Image i`, `Question q`), `n` samples of `(CoT, Answer, Confidence)` are collected.
 - **Internal Calibration:** Confidence-weighted majority voting aggregates confidence scores for each candidate answer `k` (`V_internal[k] = Σ C_i * I(A_i = k)`).
 - **External Calibration:** An Expert Model (Voter role) is provided with the image, question, and candidate list. It verbally outputs normalized confidence for each option (`C_expert`). This external vote, weighted by `τ1`, is added to the normalized internal vote to yield `V_final`.
 - **Self-Reflection:** The Expert Model acts as a Critic.
 - It generates a critique (`Crit`) based on the original image `i` and question `q` using a specific prompt (`P_critique`).
 - The base model then uses this `Crit` to reconsider its reasoning and generate a new `CoT_reflect` and `A_reflect`.
 - `A_reflect` is added to `V_final` with a weight `τ2`.
 - **Self-Check:** This module performs visual-level self-examination.
 - An original-noise image pair (`i, i'`) is generated for the test image.
 - Visual Contrastive Decoding (VCD) is applied to adjust the output, contrasting the log probabilities of generating an answer `y` from `i` and `i'`.
 - The answer `A_check` obtained from VCD is added to `V_final` with a weight `τ3`. This module does not directly involve the Expert Model.

- **Expert Planning:** The Expert Model, as a Planner, analyzes the input `(i, q)` and outputs a scheduling order `π` for the three modules. The modules are designed to be fully decoupled and order-insensitive, contributing to a shared voting dictionary `V_final`.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted demonstrate the effectiveness and robustness of the proposed framework (CDRL + CA-TTS) across several visual reasoning benchmarks.

- **Superior Performance over Baselines:**
 - The integrated framework achieved new state-of-the-art results, with consistent gains across four visual reasoning benchmarks: Math-Vista, Math-Vision, MMStar, and MMMU.
 - On Math-Vista, the proposed method achieved an ALL score of 79.5%, compared to 74.1% for VL-Rethinker.
 - On Math-Vision, it scored 42.4% (ALL), significantly higher than the 30.7% of VL-Rethinker.
 - For MMStar, it reached 71.3% (ALL), surpassing 63.4% of VL-Rethinker.
 - On MMMU, it achieved 66.3% (ALL), compared to 55.6% of VL-Rethinker.
 - The framework consistently outperformed both training-free baselines (Pass@1, Majority Voting, Deepconf) and other training-based methods (DreamPRM, R1-Onevision, VL-Rethinker, We-Think), using Qwen2.5-VL-7B as the base model.

- **Validation of Calibrated Confidence as a "Free Lunch":**
 - The results indicated that the calibrated confidence achieved during the CDRL training phase directly translated into substantial performance improvements during inference via the CA-TTS framework. The performance gains over training-free baselines, such as Pass@1 and Majority Voting, underscored this benefit.

- **Ablation Studies:**
 - **Independent and Synergistic Contributions:** Both CDRL and CA-TTS were found to contribute to performance independently, with their combination yielding the best results. CDRL alone provided a moderate boost (e.g., 48.8% to 52.2% on MMMU), while CA-TTS alone provided a significant leap (e.g., 64.7% to 77.8% on Math-Vista). The full CDRL+CA-TTS achieved the highest performance (e.g., 42.4% on Math-Vision), indicating that CDRL provides a superior model state for CA-TTS.
 - **Generalizability to Different Expert Models:** The framework's generalizability was demonstrated by testing it with various expert models (Qwen-2.5-VL-7B, Qwen-2.5-VL-72B, Qwen-VL-Max, GPT-5, Gemini-2.5-Pro). Even using Qwen-2.5-VL-7B itself as an expert model showed performance improvements over a majority voting baseline. Gemini-2.5-Pro achieved the highest overall average performance (64.85), followed by GPT-5 (64.14). This indicated that the method is robust and not overly dependent on a single expert model's performance.
 - **Enhanced Test-Time Scaling with Calibrated Confidence:** CA-TTS exhibited superior scaling properties compared to Majority Voting and DeepConf baselines. Its scaling slope (β1 = 3.65 on Math-Vision) was 2.2-3.1 times steeper than baselines, demonstrating more effective leverage of additional samples. Performance gaps widened as sample counts increased, confirming that calibrated confidence enables more efficient test-time computation.
 - **CDRL Enables Knowing When Visual Evidence is Insufficient:** CDRL training significantly enhanced the model's perceptual sensitivity. The base model showed minimal confidence drops (CD) under visual uncertainty (e.g., Occlusion, Viewpoint, Mosaic). After CDRL, the model exhibited substantially larger confidence drops (4-8x enhancement in sensitivity) across all perturbation types. CDRL also improved calibration metrics (ECE) and uncertainty quantification (AUC), indicating the model learned to assess perception quality and reduce confidence when visual evidence was degraded.
 - **Compatibility with Existing Frameworks:** When integrated as a plug-in with state-of-the-art baselines, CA-TTS, using Gemini-2.5-Pro as the expert, consistently enhanced their performance, achieving the best overall average (64.9%), surpassing We-Think by 3.9%. This suggested its universal applicability.
 - **Ablation Study and Inference Cost:** Removing any single module from CA-TTS resulted in a performance drop, validating the coordinated design. CA-TTS consumed only 0.91x more time than standard Majority Voting but delivered an 8.4% increase in average accuracy, demonstrating efficiency within the test-time scaling paradigm.

- **Case Study: CA-TTS vs. Tree-of-Thoughts (ToT):**
 - A case study illustrated CA-TTS's robust, decoupled reasoning process, where initial errors from one module (Self-Consistency) could be corrected by subsequent modules (Self-Reflection, Self-Check), guided by the Expert Planner and Critic.
 - In contrast, tree-based methods like ToT, despite their exploratory nature, were shown to be vulnerable to a single point of failure in their final evaluation model, potentially converging on incorrect answers despite extensive search. CA-TTS's multi-stage validation was presented as a more resilient approach.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of MLLMs by identifying and addressing a fundamental limitation: the disconnect between MLLM visual perception, confidence, and accuracy. The work posits that "perceptual bluntness"—where a model reasons without a reliable assessment of its visual input's quality—is a root cause of unreliable outputs, including hallucinations.

The significance and potential impact of this work include:

- **Paradigm Shift in MLLM Research:** The findings advocate for a shift from solely enhancing visual perception or text-level preference tuning to a "Perceive-then-Reason" approach. This emphasizes the co-optimization of visual grounding with confidence calibration. Future MLLM development may prioritize building systems that are not only capable but also self-aware of their visual understanding.
- **Development of More Trustworthy AI Systems:** By enabling MLLMs to "know when they do not know," the framework contributes to the development of more reliable and trustworthy AI. In critical applications where MLLMs might be deployed, such as medical diagnostics or autonomous driving, understanding and quantifying model uncertainty is paramount. This work provides a mechanism for models to identify and flag uncertain predictions, potentially reducing the risk of erroneous decisions.
- **Enhanced Test-Time Efficiency and Performance:** The concept of "free lunch" through calibrated confidence significantly impacts test-time scaling strategies. By allowing models to dynamically allocate computational resources based on their self-assessed certainty, the framework offers a more efficient way to improve performance during inference. This can lead to better accuracy with optimized compute, making advanced MLLM capabilities more practical.
- **Foundational for Addressing Hallucinations:** The paper frames hallucinations as an extreme form of calibration failure. By improving visual perception-aware confidence calibration, the proposed methods offer a fundamental approach to mitigate such issues, moving beyond reactive fixes to address the underlying cause.
- **Generalizability and Modularity:** The framework's modular design (CDRL for training, CA-TTS with its three decoupled modules) and its demonstrated compatibility with various expert models suggest its broad applicability. It can potentially serve as a universal enhancement plug-in for existing and future MLLMs, allowing them to leverage calibrated confidence for improved performance regardless of their base architecture.
- **Future Research Directions:** The authors identify several promising avenues for future work, including extending CA-TTS principles to data synthesis frameworks, reinforcement fine-tuning, agent-evolving systems, and self-play mechanisms. This indicates a potential to close the loop between inference-time scaling and post-training improvements, fostering autonomous model refinement.

In conclusion, this research presents a method for endowing MLLMs with a more nuanced self-awareness of their visual comprehension. By linking perception, confidence, and accuracy, it proposes a pathway toward more robust, interpretable, and ultimately, more reliable multimodal artificial intelligence.
