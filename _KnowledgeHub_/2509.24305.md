# Asynchronous Policy Gradient Aggregation for Efficient Distributed Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from AXXX and the Applied AI Institute introduced Rennala NIGT and Malenia NIGT, new asynchronous policy gradient aggregation algorithms designed for efficient distributed reinforcement learning. These algorithms improve upon existing methods by achieving better computational and communication time complexities, such as an O(κε⁻²) communication complexity which improves on AFedPG's O(κε⁻³), and robustly handle heterogeneous environments and computational resources.

## Problem

- Distributed reinforcement learning faces significant challenges from communication bottlenecks, system heterogeneity among agents, and the presence of stragglers.
- Distributed Policy Gradient methods are less explored, especially in scenarios with heterogeneous asynchronous computations and communication constraints.
- Previous asynchronous distributed RL algorithms like AFedPG lacked support for heterogeneous settings, exhibited suboptimal communication time complexity, and scaled unfavorably with the number of agents.

## Method

- Developed two novel algorithms: ==Rennala NIGT== for homogeneous settings and Malenia NIGT for heterogeneous distributed environments, building on Normalized Iterated Gradient (NIGT) techniques and asynchronous optimization.
- The algorithms employ an asynchronous gradient aggregation strategy where a central server waits for any agent to complete computation, allowing faster agents to contribute more and mitigating straggler effects.
- Implemented efficient communication mechanisms, including support for collective operations like AllReduce, to minimize communication overhead between agents and the central server.

## Results

- Rennala NIGT achieved a communication time complexity of O(κε⁻²), a notable improvement over AFedPG's O(κε⁻³) and demonstrated superior scalability with an increasing number of agents (`n`).
- Malenia NIGT established state-of-the-art time complexity for RL problems in heterogeneous, distributed, and asynchronous settings, addressing challenges like diverse reward functions and policy parameterizations.
- Empirical evaluations on MuJoCo tasks showed Rennala NIGT consistently achieved faster convergence and better performance than AFedPG and a Synchronized NIGT baseline under various heterogeneous computation and communication conditions, while Malenia NIGT achieved significantly higher rewards in truly heterogeneous environments.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Asynchronous aggregation schemes that prioritize contributions from faster agents can significantly improve computational efficiency and robustness in heterogeneous distributed RL settings.
> - Integrating momentum and normalization techniques into asynchronous distributed policy gradient methods can yield superior theoretical guarantees for convergence rates and time complexities.
> - Developing distinct but related algorithms, like Rennala NIGT and Malenia NIGT, allows for optimized performance and broader applicability across both homogeneous and heterogeneous distributed learning environments, including federated RL.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Reinforcement Learning (RL) has emerged as a fundamental tool in modern deep learning, with applications spanning robotics, control, recommender systems, game playing, and the fine-tuning of large language models through Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF). The training of contemporary machine learning and RL models frequently employs a distributed paradigm, where multiple agents, workers, or GPUs perform computations in parallel and communicate periodically. This distributed approach facilitates training at scale by leveraging extensive computational resources. However, distributed training introduces several challenges, including communication bottlenecks, system heterogeneity among agents, and the presence of stragglers or fault tolerance issues. These challenges have motivated the development of communication-efficient methods, asynchronous and decentralized optimization algorithms, and federated learning frameworks.

Policy Gradient (PG) methods constitute a class of RL algorithms that have demonstrated significant empirical performance. While non-distributed PG methods are theoretically well-understood, their distributed counterparts remain less explored, particularly in scenarios characterized by heterogeneous asynchronous computations and communication constraints.

Previous research in non-distributed PG methods has established sample complexities for finding stationary points, with advancements such as normalized PG methods achieving rates of O(ε⁻⁷ᐟ²) under weaker assumptions than earlier variants. In the domain of parallel and asynchronous optimization, studies have addressed both homogeneous and heterogeneous settings. For general stochastic optimization, methods like Rennala SGD and Malenia SGD (Tyurin & Richt´arik, 2023) have achieved optimal time complexities under specific computation models.

In distributed RL, prior work has explored federated reinforcement learning and decentralized policy optimization. The most relevant and recent work, AFedPG (Lan et al., 2025), proposed an asynchronous PG method building on Normalized Iterated Gradient (NIGT) principles and insights from asynchronous SGD. While AFedPG represented a step in addressing asynchronous distributed RL, it exhibited several limitations, including a lack of support for heterogeneous settings, suboptimal communication time complexity without AllReduce operation support, and a computational time complexity that could scale unfavorably with the number of agents. This current work aims to advance the state-of-the-art in distributed RL by addressing these specific limitations within asynchronous policy gradient aggregation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop and analyze efficient distributed reinforcement learning algorithms based on policy gradient methods, specifically addressing the challenges posed by asynchronous and parallel computations and communications. The work aims to improve upon existing methods, particularly the AFedPG algorithm (Lan et al., 2025), by achieving enhanced theoretical guarantees and practical performance.

The key motivations driving this research are:

1. **Addressing Computational and Communication Challenges in Distributed RL**: The authors aim to tackle fundamental hurdles in distributed RL, such as communication bottlenecks, heterogeneous computational capabilities among agents, and the need for algorithms robust to asynchronous operations.
2. **Extending Support to Heterogeneous Environments**: A significant motivation is to develop algorithms that can operate effectively not only in homogeneous settings (where all agents access the same environment and data distribution) but also in heterogeneous environments. The latter is particularly relevant for federated learning applications, where agents may have diverse reward functions, policy parameterizations, or local data distributions, and privacy constraints often prevent environment sharing.
3. **Improving Theoretical Efficiency**: The research seeks to establish new state-of-the-art theoretical guarantees for computational and communication time complexities. Specifically, the authors aim to:
 - **Achieve better communication complexity**: Improve the dependence on the error tolerance (ε) and support collective communication operations like AllReduce, which are crucial for practical distributed systems. The current state-of-the-art (AFedPG) was noted to have suboptimal communication complexity and lacked AllReduce support.
 - **Enhance computational complexity**: Develop algorithms whose computational time complexity scales more favorably with the number of agents (`n`), particularly in scenarios with a large number of distributed workers. AFedPG's complexity could increase with `n`, which is undesirable in large-scale settings.
 - **Provide stronger guarantees without additional assumptions**: The goal is to deliver these improvements under the same set of assumptions as previous works, avoiding new restrictive conditions.
4. **Leveraging Momentum and Normalization Techniques**: The authors are motivated to integrate momentum and normalization techniques, previously shown to improve convergence rates in non-distributed optimization, into asynchronous distributed RL frameworks, combined with a mild second-order smoothness assumption.
5. **Establishing a New Lower Bound**: To benchmark the performance of their proposed algorithms and quantify the remaining gap to optimal theoretical performance, the authors are motivated to establish a new lower bound for the considered problem setting. This provides a clearer understanding of the fundamental limits of achieving an ε-stationary point.

In summary, the motivation is to advance the fundamental understanding and practical efficiency of distributed reinforcement learning by developing robust, theoretically superior, and widely applicable asynchronous policy gradient aggregation methods.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed in this research involves defining the problem within both homogeneous and heterogeneous distributed settings, formulating assumptions for theoretical analysis, and developing two novel algorithms, Rennala NIGT and Malenia NIGT, inspired by existing optimization techniques.

**Problem Formulation:**
The core problem is to find optimal policy parameters `θ` ∈ ℝᵈ that maximize the expected return `J(θ)` of a discrete-time discounted Markov Decision Process (MDP). For practical purposes, the infinite trajectory is approximated by a finite trajectory of length `H`, leading to a truncated expected return `J_H(θ)`. The algorithms aim to find an `ε`-stationary point, where `E[||∇J(θ)||] ≤ ε`. The stochastic policy gradient `g_H(τ, θ)` is used as an unbiased estimator of `∇J_H(θ)`.

**Assumptions:**
The theoretical analysis relies on standard assumptions from the RL literature:
- **Policy smoothness (Assumption 2.1)**: The policy function `θ → π_θ(a, s)` is positive, twice continuously differentiable, with bounded gradients (`||∇logπ_θ(a|s)|| ≤ M_g`) and Hessians (`∇²logπ_θ(a|s) ≤ M_h`) of the log-policy.
- **Second-order smoothness (Assumption 2.2)**: The second derivative of the log-policy is Lipschitz continuous (`∇²logπ_θ(a|s) - ∇²logπ_θ'(a|s) ≤ l_2 ||θ - θ'||`).
These assumptions lead to properties for `J(θ)` such as L_g-Lipschitz gradients and L_h-Lipschitz Hessians, as well as bounds on the approximation error between `J_H(θ)` and `J(θ)`, and a bounded variance (`σ²`) for the stochastic gradients `g_H`.

**Homogeneous and Heterogeneous Setups:**
- **Homogeneous Setup**: All `n` agents share access to the same environment, data distribution, reward function, and policy `π_θ`.
- **Heterogeneous Setup**: Agents operate with arbitrary heterogeneous distributions, reward functions `r_i`, and policies `π_i,θ`. The objective becomes maximizing the average expected return `J(θ) = 1/n Σ J_i(θ)`. The definitions of truncated expected return and stochastic gradients are adapted for individual agents (`J_i,H(θ)` and `g_i,H(τ_i, θ)`).

**Computation and Communication Model (Assumption 2.5):**
For analyzing time complexities, the authors employ a model where:
- Computing one stochastic policy gradient `g_H` on agent `i` takes `h_i = ˙h_i × H` seconds (where `˙h_i` is the per-step simulation time and `H` is trajectory length). `h_i` are ordered from fastest to slowest.
- Transmitting a vector between an agent and a server, or between agents via AllReduce, takes `κ` seconds.
This model helps compare methods under realistic distributed constraints, though the core convergence proofs do not strictly require these static time bounds.

**New Methods: Rennala NIGT and Malenia NIGT:**
The proposed algorithms, Rennala NIGT (for homogeneous settings) and Malenia NIGT (for heterogeneous settings), are inspired by Normalized Iterated Gradient (NIGT) techniques (Cutkosky & Mehta, 2020) and asynchronous optimization frameworks (Tyurin & Richt´arik, 2023).

- **Algorithm 1 (Core Iteration)**: This algorithm outlines the main update steps, which are largely based on NIGT with momentum. It involves:
 1. Initialization of a momentum term `d₀` using an aggregated gradient.
 2. An initial policy update `θ₁`.
 3. In each iteration `t`:
 - An extrapolation step `e_θ_t` (a lookahead point).
 - Aggregation of stochastic gradients `g_t` at `e_θ_t` using either `AggregateRennala` or `AggregateMalenia`.
 - Momentum update for `d_t`.
 - Policy update `θ_t+1` in the direction of `d_t`, normalized by its L2 norm.

- **Algorithm 2 (AggregateRennala - Homogeneous)**: This procedure is used within Algorithm 1 for homogeneous settings:
 1. Broadcasts the current policy `θ` to all `n` agents.
 2. Each agent `i` independently starts sampling a trajectory `τ_i,1` and computing `g_H(τ_i,1, θ)`.
 3. The central server waits for any agent `j` to complete its computation.
 4. Upon receiving `g_H` from agent `j`, it adds this to a global sum `¯g`. Agent `j` is immediately instructed to start computing the next stochastic gradient (`τ_j,k+1`).
 5. This process continues until a total of `M` stochastic gradients have been collected from all agents.
 6. All calculations are stopped, and the aggregated `¯g` (typically `1/M * Σ g_H`) is returned, possibly via an AllReduce operation for communication efficiency.

- **Algorithm 3 (AggregateMalenia - Heterogeneous)**: This procedure is used within Algorithm 1 for heterogeneous settings:
 1. Similar to `AggregateRennala`, `θ` is broadcast.
 2. Each agent `i` samples from its *own* environment `p_i` and computes `g_i,H(τ_i,1, θ)`.
 3. The aggregation logic differs: it tracks `¯g_j` and `M_j` for each agent `j` (sum of gradients and count of gradients from agent `j`).
 4. The loop terminates when `(1/n Σ (1/M_i))⁻¹ ≥ M/n`, ensuring a balanced contribution from agents despite heterogeneity.
 5. The aggregated vector `1/n Σ (¯g_i / M_i)` is returned.

**Key Advantages of the Approach:**
- **Asynchronous and Straggler-Resilient**: The "wait for any agent" mechanism allows faster agents to contribute more, preventing slower agents (stragglers) from delaying the entire process.
- **Communication Efficiency**: Gradients are aggregated locally at agents before a single, collective communication (e.g., AllReduce) to update the central model, minimizing communication overhead.
- **Generalizability**: The framework supports both homogeneous and heterogeneous settings, a critical feature for real-world distributed and federated RL.
- **Theoretical Rigor**: The approach is backed by detailed convergence analysis and time complexity bounds that demonstrate improvements over prior methods.

**Theoretical Analysis:**
The theoretical analysis involves deriving bounds on the expected value of the norm of the gradient (`E[||∇J(¯θ_T)||]`) after `T` iterations. This is achieved by combining a standard descent lemma with a careful analysis of the momentum-based error term (`E[||d_t - ∇J_H(θ_t)||]`), which accounts for stochasticity, approximation errors due to finite horizon, and the asynchronous aggregation scheme. Optimal choices of learning rates, momentum parameters, trajectory length, and batch sizes (`M`, `M_init`) are derived to achieve the stated complexities. A lower bound analysis, drawing from existing work in non-convex stochastic optimization, is also performed to benchmark the new methods' optimality.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yields significant theoretical and empirical findings regarding the efficiency of asynchronous policy gradient aggregation in distributed reinforcement learning.

**Theoretical Findings:**

1. **Homogeneous Setup (Rennala NIGT):**
 - **Iteration Complexity (Theorem 4.1)**: Rennala NIGT is shown to find an ε-stationary point after `T = O(L_g∆/ε² + √L_h∆/ε^(3/2) + σ/√M_init ε + σ³/M√M_init ε³ + σ²√L_h∆/M ε^(7/2))` global iterations. This bound is independent of specific computation and communication times.
 - **Computational Time Complexity (Theorem 4.2)**: When considering computation times (`˙h_i`), Rennala NIGT achieves a complexity of `˜O(1/(1-γ) min_{m∈[n]} [(1/m Σ_{i=1}^m 1/˙h_i)⁻¹ (L_g∆/ε² + √L_h∆/ε^(3/2) + σ²/(mε²) + σ²√L_h∆/(mε^(7/2)))])`. This exhibits a harmonic-like dependence on agent speeds (`˙h_i`) and improves upon prior state-of-the-art methods like AFedPG. Specifically, for large `n`, AFedPG's complexity could grow with `n`, whereas Rennala NIGT's complexity decreases with `n`, demonstrating superior scalability and robustness to stragglers.
 - **Communication Time Complexity (Theorem 4.4)**: Including communication time `κ`, Rennala NIGT's total time complexity includes an additional term `˜O(κ (L_g∆/ε² + √L_h∆/ε^(3/2)))`. This translates to a communication complexity of `O(κε⁻²)`, which is a notable improvement over AFedPG's `O(κε⁻³)` (or `O(κε⁻^(7/2))` in worst-case scenarios for small ε). Rennala NIGT also supports the AllReduce operation, which is critical for practical distributed implementations.
 - **Generalization to Arbitrary Computation Dynamics (Theorems 4.3, 7.2)**: The theoretical framework is extended to support non-static and arbitrary computation time patterns, providing a more flexible analysis for diverse distributed environments.

2. **Heterogeneous Setup (Malenia NIGT):**
 - **Time Complexity (Theorem 5.1)**: Malenia NIGT achieves a time complexity of `˜O(κ (L_g∆/ε² + √L_h∆/ε^(3/2)) + 1/(1-γ) [˙h_n (L_g∆/ε² + √L_h∆/ε^(3/2)) + (1/n Σ ˙h_i) (σ²/(nε²) + σ²√L_h∆/(nε^(7/2)))])`. This is presented as the current state-of-the-art for RL problems in heterogeneous, distributed, and asynchronous settings. The computational term's dependence on `˙h_i` is "mean-like," reflecting the inherent challenges of heterogeneity. The communication complexity term remains consistent with Rennala NIGT.
 - **Generalization to Arbitrary Computation Dynamics (Theorem 7.3)**: Similar to Rennala NIGT, Malenia NIGT's analysis is extended to arbitrary computation power functions.

3. **Global Convergence (Theorems A.1, A.2, A.3)**: Under additional standard assumptions (e.g., related to the Fisher information matrix and relaxed weak gradient domination), both Rennala NIGT and Malenia NIGT are shown to converge globally to an `ε`-solution up to a small neighborhood of the global optimum. The time complexities for global convergence mirror those for finding an ε-stationary point.

4. **Lower Bound (Theorem G.1)**: A new lower bound is established for the time required by first-order zero-respecting algorithms that access unbiased stochastic gradients of `(L_g, L_h)`-twice smooth functions with `σ`-bounded variance. This lower bound indicates that a gap remains, for instance, between the achieved communication complexity of `κε⁻²` and the theoretical lower bound of `κε⁻^(12/7)`.

**Empirical Results:**

Experiments were conducted on various MuJoCo tasks (Humanoid-v4, Reacher-v4, Walker2d-v4, Hopper-v4) comparing Rennala NIGT with AFedPG and a Synchronized NIGT baseline, across different scenarios of computation and communication heterogeneity, and with increasing numbers of agents (`n=10`, `n=100`).

- **Homogeneous Settings**:
 - When computation times and communication delays were uniform (`h_i=1, κ_i=0`), all methods exhibited similar performance, as expected.
 - In scenarios with heterogeneous computation times (`h_i=√i`) and either no or moderate communication delays (`κ_i=0` or `κ_i=√i`), Rennala NIGT consistently demonstrated faster convergence compared to Synchronized NIGT and AFedPG.
 - The performance gap favoring Rennala NIGT became more pronounced in settings with increased communication times (`κ_i=√i × d^(1/4)`), highlighting its communication efficiency and robustness. This effect was observed for both `n=10` and `n=100` agents.
 - Experiments with decreasing computation times (`h_i=i^(1/4)`) also showed Rennala NIGT as the fastest method.

- **Heterogeneous Environment Settings**:
 - A specific experiment was conducted using Malenia NIGT against AFedPG in a truly heterogeneous environment setup (two agents, one with a standard Humanoid-v4 environment, the other with inverted states). Malenia NIGT achieved a significantly higher reward compared to AFedPG, which is consistent with Malenia NIGT's theoretical ability to handle both heterogeneous computations and environments, a feature AFedPG lacks.

In summary, the main findings include the development of theoretically superior asynchronous policy gradient aggregation methods, Rennala NIGT and Malenia NIGT, which are shown to be more efficient in terms of computational and communication complexities and more robust to heterogeneity and stragglers than previous state-of-the-art approaches. These theoretical advancements are supported by empirical results across multiple MuJoCo environments.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The contributions of this research carry significance for the advancement of distributed reinforcement learning and its practical applications.

1. **Advancement of Distributed RL Efficiency**: The introduction of Rennala NIGT and Malenia NIGT establishes new state-of-the-art benchmarks for computational and communication time complexities in asynchronous and parallel distributed RL. This directly addresses the critical need for more efficient algorithms to train increasingly complex RL models.

2. **Robustness to Real-World Heterogeneity**: The methods, particularly Malenia NIGT, explicitly account for and demonstrate resilience to heterogeneous computations, asynchronous operations, and diverse environmental dynamics across agents. This is a crucial step towards deploying distributed RL in realistic, non-uniform settings prevalent in large-scale systems and federated learning architectures.

3. **Enabling Scalable RL Training**: By offering provably better time complexities and improved handling of communication bottlenecks and stragglers, the proposed algorithms facilitate more effective scaling of RL training to massive computational resources. This can accelerate research and development in areas requiring extensive exploration, such as advanced robotics, autonomous systems, and large language model fine-tuning.

4. **Impact on Federated Reinforcement Learning**: Malenia NIGT's theoretical support and empirical validation for heterogeneous environments are particularly impactful for Federated Reinforcement Learning (FRL). FRL scenarios inherently involve agents with distinct local environments, data distributions, and possibly reward functions due to privacy concerns or logistical constraints. Malenia NIGT provides a robust algorithmic foundation for such settings, where prior methods like AFedPG were limited.

5. **Theoretical Framework and Lower Bounds**: The comprehensive theoretical analysis, including the establishment of new lower bounds, contributes to a deeper understanding of the fundamental limits and performance achievable in asynchronous distributed RL. This provides a clear reference point for future research to close existing optimality gaps. The demonstration of provable improvements without introducing additional assumptions also strengthens the theoretical underpinnings of the field.

6. **Practical Deployment and Engineering**: The support for AllReduce operations in Rennala NIGT and Malenia NIGT is a practical advantage. AllReduce is a widely adopted and optimized collective communication primitive in distributed computing frameworks, making the integration of these algorithms into existing infrastructure more straightforward and efficient.

Overall, this work is poised to significantly influence the design and deployment of distributed reinforcement learning systems, offering more efficient, robust, and scalable solutions that are better equipped to handle the complexities of modern computational environments.
