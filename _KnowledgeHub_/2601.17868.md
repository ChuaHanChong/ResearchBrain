# VidLaDA: Bidirectional Diffusion Large Language Models for Efficient Video Understanding

> [!summary] Summary
> VidLaDA introduces a diffusion language model architecture for video understanding, utilizing bidirectional attention to overcome limitations of autoregressive models in processing spatiotemporal video data. The framework, developed by researchers from Shanghai Jiao Tong University and Fudan University, integrates an efficient MARS-Cache strategy, achieving up to 12x inference speedup and competitive reasoning performance compared to state-of-the-art AR-based Video LLMs.

## Problem

- Existing Autoregressive (AR) Video Large Language Models (LLMs) face a "dual efficiency bottleneck" in both understanding and generation due to their unidirectional processing.
- AR models' causal attention mechanism imposes an asymmetric receptive field, leading to positional bias and suboptimal information capacity for comprehensive spatiotemporal video understanding.
- The serial decoding nature of AR models causes generation latency to scale linearly with output length, hindering efficiency for long-form video reasoning tasks.

## Method

- **VidLaDA** leverages a ==Diffusion Language Model (DLM)== backbone with full ==bidirectional attention== to enable deep spatiotemporal coupling and ==parallel token prediction==.
- It incorporates a novel inference framework called ==MARS-Cache (Multi-modal Asynchronous Refreshing Strategy)== to prune computational redundancy in DLM-based video decoding.
- ==MARS-Cache== employs ==multi-modal asynchronous refreshing==, ==frame-wise chunk attention==, and ==adaptive anchor token searching== to achieve significant speedups without compromising accuracy.

## Results

- VidLaDA consistently outperforms existing DLM baselines and achieves competitive performance with leading open-sourced AR-based Video LLMs, surpassing them on complex benchmarks like LongVideoBench and MLVU.
- The model exhibits enhanced spatiotemporal robustness, maintaining performance regardless of the position or sparsity of key visual events, unlike AR models which show significant positional and temporal biases.
- MARS-Cache delivers an **8-12x** inference speedup for DLM-based Video LLMs, enabling throughput approximately **1.3x** higher than AR baselines, with negligible impact on reasoning accuracy.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Bidirectional attention in DLMs inherently provides a more uniform receptive field and higher information capacity for aggregating distributed visual evidence, improving spatiotemporal understanding over causal AR models.
> - Computational redundancy in DLM inference for video can be substantially reduced by exploiting modality-dependent state stability, depth-dependent volatility, and temporal locality of visual features.
> - The MARS-Cache strategy demonstrates that a tailored caching and attention mechanism can enable DLM-based Video LLMs to achieve superior inference throughput compared to AR models while preserving or enhancing reasoning accuracy.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Video Large Language Models (Video LLMs) has rapidly progressed, aiming to integrate visual perception from videos with language reasoning for tasks such as video captioning, spatiotemporal question answering, and long-horizon decision making. The predominant architectural paradigm in existing Video LLMs involves coupling a pre-trained vision encoder (e.g., ViT) with an Autoregressive (AR) Large Language Model (LLM). This setup processes video frames into visual embeddings, which are then projected into the language embedding space, and responses are generated via next-token prediction using a causal attention mask.

However, the authors identify a fundamental mismatch between this AR paradigm and the inherent spatiotemporal nature of video data. Unlike text, visual semantics in video are distributed across space and time without an intrinsic unidirectional order. The causal attention mechanism, designed for sequential text, imposes a strictly unidirectional dependency. This limitation hinders global spatiotemporal aggregation and can lead to inefficient understanding and serial generation.

The broader landscape of language models has seen the emergence of Diffusion Language Models (DLMs) as an alternative to AR models for text generation. DLMs reframe generation as an iterative denoising process in a discrete space, characterized by full bidirectional attention and the ability to predict multiple tokens in parallel. This work positions itself by exploring the application of DLMs to video understanding, aiming to leverage their bidirectional nature to overcome the limitations of AR-based Video LLMs. It differentiates itself from existing DLM-based multimodal models by providing a formalized theoretical analysis of AR limitations in video contexts and by introducing an efficiency-focused inference framework tailored for long-form video.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an efficient Video LLM that overcomes the limitations of existing autoregressive (AR) models, particularly concerning video understanding and generation efficiency. The authors identify a "dual efficiency bottleneck" in current AR-based Video LLMs, which motivates their proposed solution:

**Motivation for Addressing Understanding Inefficiency:**
AR models, by imposing a causal attention mask, enforce a strictly unidirectional dependency. When video tokens are flattened into a 1D sequence, this causal masking leads to an asymmetric receptive field. Early tokens are visible to many subsequent positions, while later tokens receive less attention due to structural constraints. This results in:
- **Positional Bias and Non-uniform Utilization of Visual Evidence:** The model may over-rely on early visual tokens, failing to fully exploit bidirectionally available visual information. This is formalized in **Proposition 3.1**, which states that AR possesses an asymmetric receptive field precluding uniform spatiotemporal processing.
- **Suboptimal Information Capacity:** The restricted access to future visual tokens in AR decoding limits the usable information that can be extracted from the bidirectional vision encoder. **Proposition 3.2** formally states that the AR paradigm inherently limits cumulative pointwise information capacity, resulting in a strictly lower theoretical upper bound of spatiotemporal understanding compared to a bidirectional decoding paradigm.
- **Empirical Manifestations:** The paper demonstrates these biases through experiments showing brittle intra-frame performance when high-information patches are relocated within a frame, and degraded temporal robustness in causal video question answering when key evidence is sparse or located in the middle/late parts of a video. This suggests that AR models struggle to aggregate global temporal relationships, which is critical for complex spatiotemporal reasoning in longer videos.

**Motivation for Addressing Generation Efficiency:**
Beyond understanding, the AR paradigm suffers from inherent serial decoding, where generation latency scales linearly with the number of generated tokens. This restricts generation efficiency, particularly for long-form reasoning tasks often required in complex video understanding.

**Overall Objectives:**
To address these motivations, the authors propose VidLaDA, with the following key objectives:
1. **Develop a Video LLM based on Diffusion Language Models (DLMs):** Utilize the full bidirectional attention of DLMs to enable comprehensive spatiotemporal modeling, thereby mitigating the asymmetric receptive field issue and improving the theoretical upper bound of understanding.
2. **Enable Parallel Token Decoding:** Leverage the iterative denoising nature of DLMs to predict multiple tokens simultaneously, enhancing generation efficiency.
3. **Mitigate DLM Computational Overhead for Video:** Address the challenge that vanilla DLMs recompute attention over the entire multimodal sequence at every decoding step, which becomes prohibitive with massive video token sequences.
4. **Introduce an Acceleration Strategy (MARS-Cache):** Develop a multimodal asynchronous refreshing strategy to prune computational redundancy specific to video-language diffusion decoding, aiming to achieve significant speedups without compromising reasoning accuracy.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

VidLaDA integrates a robust vision encoder with a Diffusion Language Model (DLM) backbone and introduces a novel acceleration strategy called MARS-Cache.

#### 3.1. Model Architecture (VidLaDA)

- **Vision Encoder and Spatial Pooling:** The model utilizes SigLip2-SO400M as the vision encoder to extract features from input video frames. To manage the extensive token sequence, a 2x2 bilinear interpolation spatial pooling strategy is applied. This reduces the visual sequence length by a factor of 4 after projection into language embedding space, balancing computational efficiency with spatial structure preservation.
- **Bidirectional Diffusion Language Model:** The core of VidLaDA is a DLM with a full bidirectional attention mechanism. Unlike AR models that use causal masking, this architecture allows every token (visual or textual) to attend to the entire holistic sequence simultaneously. This design aims to ensure deep coupling between the vision encoder and language model, preserving dynamic video information integrity and facilitating unconstrained global interactions across video tokens, prompts, and partially denoised responses. It also supports parallel prediction of multiple tokens within a single step, addressing AR's serial generation bottleneck.
- **Training Pipeline:**
 - **Data Composition:** To address the scarcity of minute-scale, long-duration video understanding data, the authors curate a new dataset from FineVideo, focusing on 2-30 minute videos. This involves temporal stratification, instruction synthesis via LLM (Deepseek-V3.1), text-only bias filtering, and MLLM (Qwen3-VL-235B-A22B) consistency voting. This dataset is combined with LLaVA-Video-178K (for short-to-medium videos) and MAmmoTH-VL (for image-based instruction following).
 - **Multi-Stage Training:** A three-stage curriculum learning strategy is adopted, starting from a LLaDA-V checkpoint:
 1. **Short-Clip Temporal Pre-Training:** Equips the MLLM with temporal perception using 1.8M short videos (10s-3min, 32 frames, 8K tokens), fine-tuning all components (ViT, MLP, LLM).
 2. **Temporal Scaling Warm-up:** Transitions to higher temporal resolutions (64 frames, 16K tokens) using 500K samples from Stage 1, continuing full-parameter tuning.
 3. **Long-Form Video Expansion:** Focuses on long videos (2-30+ minutes) with 500K samples from the curated dataset, freezing the ViT and fine-tuning only the MLP and LLM with a reduced learning rate, and incorporating 10% text-only instructions.

#### 3.2. Efficient Video Reasoning via MARS-Cache

MARS-Cache (Multi-modal Asynchronous Refreshing Strategy) is an inference framework designed to prune redundancy in DLM-based Video LLM decoding, motivated by four empirical observations during state evolution:
1. **Chunk-wise Locality with Global Anchor Tokens:** Inter-frame attention primarily exhibits local dependencies, but a subset of "anchor tokens" consistently attracts high global attention and remains stable across decoding steps and layers. Deeper layers' anchor tokens are a subset of shallow layers'.
2. **Modality-Dependent Drift:** Visual tokens exhibit higher temporal stability (low drift) compared to textual tokens during decoding.
3. **Depth-Dependent Stability Variance:** Shallow network layers show high hidden state stability, while deeper layers exhibit significant volatility.
4. **Progressive Attention Sparsity:** Attention distribution evolves from diffuse in shallow layers to highly peaked and sparse in deep layers.

Based on these observations, MARS-Cache implements three key components:

- **Multi-modal Asynchronous Refreshing:** Leverages modality- and depth-dependent drift disparities. Context hidden states (visual tokens, prompts, decoded text) are cached and updated based on grouped refresh intervals (τ).
 - **Modality-wise:** Visual caches (τ_v) are refreshed less frequently than textual caches (τ_t) because visual states drift less (e.g., τ_v > τ_t).
 - **Layer-wise:** Smaller refresh intervals are assigned to deeper, more volatile layer groups (i.e., τ_g-1 > τ_g). A synchronization constraint (τ_g-1 = k ⋅ τ_g) ensures consistency.
- **Frame-wise Chunk Attention:** To reduce the computational overhead when visual caches *do* require refreshing, this strategy exploits temporal locality (Obs. 1). Attention for tokens within frame *n* is computed primarily with tokens from its local temporal neighborhood (frames *n-1, n, n+1*), reducing complexity from O(|E_V|^2) to O(|N(F_n)| × |E_V|). Full attention is still performed at the first decoding step for global context initialization.
- **Adaptive Anchor Token Searching:** Addresses the issue of chunking compromising long-range information flow.
 - **Adaptive Proxy Scoring:** Instead of computing a full attention map, an equidistant subsampling of query tokens (e.g., 128 queries) is used to compute a low-rank proxy attention matrix, identifying salient anchor tokens.
 - **Temporal Reuse:** Anchor token indices are identified only at the first decoding step and reused for subsequent sparse attention steps.
 - **Group-wise Allocation:** Anchor token search is performed once per layer group, with a decreasing budget (k_g) for deeper groups (Obs. 1). These anchor tokens, aggregated per frame, restore global connectivity by being visible to all tokens and attending to all other tokens.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments benchmark VidLaDA against state-of-the-art (SOTA) Autoregressive (AR) and Diffusion Language Model (DLM) baselines across various video understanding tasks.

- **Overall Performance (Table 1):**
 - VidLaDA consistently outperforms existing DLM-based baselines (LLaDA-V, SDAR-VL, Dream-VL) across all evaluated benchmarks (Video-MMMU, LongVideoBench, LVBench, EgoSchema, MVBench, MLVU dev/test, Video-MME).
 - VidLaDA demonstrates competitiveness with top-tier open-sourced AR-based Video LLMs (e.g., Qwen2.5-VL, LLaVA-OneVision, LLaVA-Video). Notably, it surpasses LLaVA-Video and LLaVA-OneVision on LongVideoBench and outperforms Qwen2.5-VL on MLVU (both Dev and Test splits), indicating strengths in complex spatiotemporal understanding where unidirectional modeling may constrain evidence aggregation.
- **Spatiotemporal Robustness (Figure 2):**
 - **Intra-Frame (Spatial Ordering):** AR baselines show performance degradation when high-information tokens are shifted from the start of the visual sequence, confirming positional bias. The bidirectional DLM baseline (LLaDA-V) maintains near-constant performance, demonstrating invariance to causal position.
 - **Inter-Frame (Temporal Context/Sparsity):** AR baselines exhibit a U-shaped sensitivity to event position, with mid-sequence events being particularly fragile. LLaVA-Video shows severe instability. In contrast, the DLM baseline maintains a near-flat accuracy profile across event positions and remains accurate even with sparse event frames, attributing this to bidirectional decoding's ability to aggregate global context.
- **MARS-Cache Acceleration (Table 2):**
 - Under Chain-of-Thought (CoT) inference settings, MARS-Cache delivers consistent and substantial throughput gains (Tokens Per Second, TPS) for both LLaDA-V and VidLaDA.
 - It achieves approximately 8-12× speedup over vanilla DLM decoding.
 - With MARS-Cache, DLMs can surpass the throughput of AR baselines (e.g., ~1.3× TPS compared to LLaVA-OV).
 - MARS-Cache consistently outperforms Dual-Cache (a previous DLM acceleration method), providing an additional ~1.3-1.5× TPS improvement by pruning redundancy through modal-wise cache refreshing and frame-wise chunk attention.
 - Despite aggressive state reuse, MARS-Cache largely preserves reasoning accuracy, with negligible fluctuations and even performance improvements in some settings.
- **Ablation Studies (Appendix F.5):**
 - **Architectural Advantage:** Controlled fine-tuning experiments (Appendix F.4) suggest that DLM architecture inherently utilizes spatiotemporal evidence more effectively than AR.
 - **Necessity of Anchor Tokens:** Removing anchor tokens significantly degrades performance. A tapered strategy (more tokens in shallow layers, fewer in deep) or full attention in shallow layers with aggressive pruning in deep layers achieves an optimal balance, confirming their importance for global connectivity.
 - **Asynchronous Refreshing:** Setting the visual refresh interval larger than the textual interval (optimally R_v/t ≈ 2) significantly boosts throughput without compromising accuracy. A pyramid-style layer-wise schedule (increasing update frequency for deeper, more volatile layers) outperforms uniform schedules and is complementary to modality-wise refreshing.
 - **Search Overhead:** A query subset size of 128 for adaptive anchor searching provides a robust trade-off, identifying high-quality global information hubs with negligible computational overhead, avoiding OOM issues associated with full-sequence calculations.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work introduces VidLaDA, a Diffusion Language Model-based framework for video understanding, which represents a structural departure from the prevalent Autoregressive Video LLMs. Its significance and potential impact stem from several key contributions:

1. **Addressing Foundational Limitations of AR Models:** VidLaDA offers a solution to the "dual efficiency bottleneck" of AR-based Video LLMs by leveraging bidirectional attention. This addresses the issues of asymmetric receptive fields and limited information capacity that compromise spatiotemporal understanding in AR models, as formally analyzed by the authors. The empirical evidence on spatiotemporal robustness supports the claim that bidirectional decoding allows for more effective aggregation of distributed visual evidence.
2. **Advancing Non-Autoregressive Video Understanding:** VidLaDA establishes a new baseline for non-autoregressive video understanding, demonstrating that DLM-based approaches can be competitive with, and in some complex spatiotemporal reasoning tasks, outperform state-of-the-art AR-based Video LLMs. This suggests a promising alternative paradigm for the field.
3. **Enabling Efficient DLM Inference for Video:** The introduction of MARS-Cache is a practical innovation that makes DLM-based Video LLMs computationally feasible for long-form video contexts. By intelligently pruning redundant computations based on multimodal and depth-dependent state dynamics, MARS-Cache achieves substantial throughput improvements (up to 12x speedup) without compromising accuracy, and even surpasses the inference speed of AR models in some configurations. This mitigates a major challenge of directly applying DLMs to computationally intensive video data.
4. **Implications for Complex Video Reasoning:** The ability of VidLaDA to model global spatiotemporal dependencies and its robustness to varying event positions and sparse evidence suggest improved capabilities for tasks requiring complex reasoning, such as long-horizon decision making or causal inference in videos. The qualitative examples of Chain-of-Thought inference further illustrate its potential in generating structured, logical reasoning trajectories.

The research's potential impact lies in paving the way for more efficient and robust Video LLMs that can better handle the complex and dynamic nature of video data. By open-sourcing the code and checkpoints, the authors facilitate further research and development in non-autoregressive multimodal language models, potentially leading to advancements in various video-centric AI applications.
