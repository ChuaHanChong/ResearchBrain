# Contrastive Learning of Medical Visual Representations from Paired Images and Text

> [!summary] Summary
> Stanford University researchers introduced ConVIRT, a contrastive learning framework that effectively learns medical visual representations by aligning paired images with their descriptive text. This unsupervised method achieved superior performance and data efficiency on medical image classification and retrieval tasks, often matching or exceeding ImageNet pretraining results with only 1% of the labeled data.

## Problem

- Medical imaging datasets are limited by the scarcity and high cost of expert human annotations.
- Transfer learning from ImageNet is often suboptimal for medical tasks due to significant domain differences and the need for fine-grained feature recognition.
- Rule-based label extraction from radiology reports suffers from inaccuracies and poor generalization across diverse text styles and medical domains.

## Method

- Develops **ConVIRT**, an unsupervised framework that learns medical visual representations by maximizing agreement between true image-text pairs.
- Employs a ==ResNet50== image encoder and a ==ClinicalBERT==-based text encoder, processing inputs through non-linear projection heads into a shared latent space.
- Utilizes a bidirectional ==InfoNCE==-like contrastive loss to align representations across image and text modalities.

## Results

- ConVIRT achieved the best overall performance on **four** medical image classification tasks, consistently outperforming ImageNet pretraining and other in-domain baselines.
- The framework demonstrated superior data efficiency, with ConVIRT pretrained models using only **1%** of labeled data often matching or surpassing ImageNet-initialized models trained on **100%** of labeled data.
- It yielded significantly higher precision in zero-shot image-image retrieval (e.g., **Prec@10 of 42.9**) and text-image retrieval (e.g., **Prec@10 of 57.5**) compared to baselines.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Leveraging naturally paired descriptive text with medical images is more effective for learning discriminative visual representations than image-only contrastive methods.
> - The cross-modal contrastive objective drives the model to learn features that are more semantically aligned and clinically meaningful, as evidenced by saliency maps focusing on relevant anatomical regions.
> - The pretraining method yields highly data-efficient representations, significantly reducing the amount of labeled data required for downstream tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Medical image understanding is a critical area with the potential to significantly transform healthcare, witnessing substantial advancements with deep learning. However, progress is often constrained by the scarcity of high-quality, expert human annotations. Existing approaches in this domain exhibit several limitations that this work addresses.

One common approach involves using annotations created by medical experts. While these annotations are of high quality, their creation is costly, resulting in medical image datasets that are significantly smaller than natural image datasets like ImageNet. To mitigate this data scarcity, models are frequently initialized with weights pretrained on ImageNet. However, this transfer learning strategy is often suboptimal because medical images (e.g., X-rays) possess characteristics and require fine-grained feature understanding that differ substantially from the object recognition tasks prevalent in natural images. Previous research, such as Raghu et al. (2019), has indicated that ImageNet pretraining may offer minimal or no benefit compared to random initialization for medical imaging tasks, suggesting a domain shift issue.

A second prevalent strategy involves extracting labels from the accompanying textual radiology reports using expert-crafted rules. This method allows for the creation of larger datasets, as these reports are a natural byproduct of clinical workflows. Nonetheless, rule-based label extraction is prone to inaccuracies, limited to a few specific categories, and often fails to fully leverage the rich information within the text. Furthermore, such rules are typically domain-specific and sensitive to variations in text style, hindering their generalization across different medical domains or institutions.

In parallel, advancements in unsupervised contrastive learning have shown promising results in learning representations from natural images. These methods typically rely on generating multiple augmented views of the same image and maximizing agreement between them. However, the authors observe that applying these image view-based contrastive methods directly to medical images yields only marginal benefits compared to ImageNet pretraining. This is attributed to the inherent high inter-class similarity of medical images, where subtle visual distinctions differentiate pathologies, making view-based augmentation less effective for learning discriminative features.

This research introduces ConVIRT, an alternative unsupervised strategy designed to overcome these limitations by exploiting the naturally occurring paired descriptive text associated with medical images. By establishing a bidirectional contrastive objective between image and text modalities, ConVIRT aims to learn higher-quality, domain-specific visual representations without requiring additional expert annotations or relying on rule-based label extraction. The work positions itself as a foundational effort in cross-modal representation learning for medical images.

The impact of ConVIRT extends beyond the medical domain, as noted by the authors. Subsequent general visual recognition studies, such as CLIP (Radford et al., 2021) and ALIGN (Jia et al., 2021), have adopted and scaled direct adaptations of the ConVIRT-style pretraining approach, demonstrating its broader applicability in learning transferable visual models from natural language supervision. Within the medical domain, ConVIRT has inspired numerous follow-up studies focusing on multimodal contrastive learning for medical imaging, including work on mixed data inputs (Wang et al., 2021), localized medical imaging (Müller et al., 2021), regional-word contrasting (Huang et al., 2021), mutual information maximization (Liao et al., 2021), medical visual question answering (Eslami et al., 2021), clinical risk prediction (Zang and Wang, 2021), and the incorporation of multiomics data or patient metadata (Han et al., 2021; Vu et al., 2021). This situates ConVIRT as a pioneering effort that established a paradigm for leveraging multimodal data to enhance medical AI applications.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to improve the learning of visual representations for medical images by addressing the challenges posed by annotation scarcity and the limitations of existing pretraining strategies.

The motivation for this work stems from several critical observations in the field of medical image understanding:

- **Scarcity of Expert Annotations:** Deep learning models typically require large volumes of labeled data for effective training. In medical imaging, obtaining high-quality annotations requires the expertise of medical professionals, which is both time-consuming and expensive. This results in smaller, more constrained datasets compared to those available for natural images, hindering the full potential of deep learning in healthcare.
- **Suboptimality of ImageNet Pretraining:** A common workaround for data scarcity is to transfer weights from models pretrained on large natural image datasets like ImageNet. However, medical images, such as X-rays, often necessitate the recognition of subtle, fine-grained visual features that are distinct from the coarse object recognition tasks typical of ImageNet. This domain discrepancy leads to suboptimal performance when directly applying ImageNet-pretrained models, as the learned features may not be relevant to medical diagnoses. Previous studies have sometimes found ImageNet pretraining to offer little advantage over random initialization for certain medical tasks, indicating a fundamental mismatch in feature extraction requirements.
- **Inaccuracies and Lack of Generalization in Rule-Based Label Extraction:** An alternative strategy is to automatically extract labels from the textual radiology reports that accompany medical images. These reports are abundant and naturally generated in clinical practice. However, rule-based systems for label extraction are often imperfect, yielding inaccurate or incomplete labels. Furthermore, these rules are typically narrowly defined for specific conditions or text styles, making them difficult to generalize across different medical institutions or diverse textual reporting formats. This limits the efficient utilization of the rich information present in the textual reports.
- **Limited Efficacy of Image-Only Contrastive Learning:** Recent advancements in unsupervised contrastive learning have demonstrated success in learning representations from natural images by contrasting different augmented views of the same image. However, when applied to medical images, where inter-class similarity is high (e.g., distinguishing between different lung pathologies), these methods provide only marginal benefits. The subtle visual cues critical for medical diagnosis may not be effectively captured by augmentations that are primarily designed for distinguishing vastly different natural objects.

Motivated by these limitations, the authors identified an opportunity in the naturally occurring paired medical image and descriptive text data. They hypothesized that leveraging this intrinsic pairing could facilitate the learning of more semantically meaningful and domain-specific visual representations without requiring additional expert labeling.

The key objectives of this work are therefore:
1. To develop a novel unsupervised strategy, named ConVIRT, for learning high-quality visual representations from medical images.
2. To specifically exploit the readily available paired descriptive text data (e.g., radiology reports) by establishing a learning objective that aligns representations across these two modalities.
3. To design a method that is domain-agnostic, meaning it can be applied across different medical specialties and institutions without requiring custom expert input or rule-based systems.
4. To empirically demonstrate that the representations learned by ConVIRT significantly outperform those obtained from existing pretraining methods, including ImageNet transfer and other in-domain initialization techniques, on various downstream medical image understanding tasks.
5. To show that ConVIRT leads to superior data efficiency, enabling comparable or better performance with substantially less labeled training data for downstream tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, Contrastive VIsual Representation Learning from Text (ConVIRT), is an unsupervised framework designed to learn medical visual representations by exploiting naturally occurring paired medical images and descriptive text. The core approach involves maximizing the agreement between true image-text pairs while simultaneously pushing apart representations of random, mismatched pairs via a bidirectional contrastive objective.

#### 3.1. Task Definition

The representation learning setting assumes paired inputs $(x_v, x_u)$, where $x_v$ is an image or group of images, and $x_u$ is a corresponding textual description. The objective is to learn a parameterized image encoder function $f_v$ that maps an image to a fixed-dimensional vector, which can then be transferred to downstream tasks like classification or retrieval. $f_v$ is modeled as a convolutional neural network (CNN).

#### 3.2. Contrastive Visual Representation Learning from Text (ConVIRT Framework)

The ConVIRT framework processes images and text through distinct but parallel encoding pipelines:
- **Image Encoding Pipeline:**
 - For an input image $x_v$, a random view $\tilde{x}_v$ is generated by applying a stochastic image transformation function $t_v$ sampled from a family of transformations $T$.
 - The transformed image $\tilde{x}_v$ is then passed through the image encoder $f_v$ to produce a fixed-dimensional representation $h_v$.
 - Finally, a non-linear projection head $g_v$ transforms $h_v$ into a $d$-dimensional vector $v = g_v(f_v(\tilde{x}_v))$.
- **Text Encoding Pipeline:**
 - For an input text $x_u$, a textual span $\tilde{x}_u$ is obtained by applying a sampling function $t_u$.
 - The text span $\tilde{x}_u$ is processed by a text encoder $f_u$ to yield a representation $h_u$.
 - A non-linear projection head $g_u$ then transforms $h_u$ into a $d$-dimensional vector $u = g_u(f_u(\tilde{x}_u))$.

The projection functions $g_v$ and $g_u$ ensure that representations from both modalities exist in the same $d$-dimensional space for contrastive learning.

#### 3.3. Training Objective

During training, a minibatch of $N$ image-text pairs $(x_v, x_u)$ is sampled, and their corresponding representations $(v, u)$ are computed. The training objective consists of two asymmetric InfoNCE-like loss functions:
- **Image-to-Text Contrastive Loss (`_ (v→u)`):** For each image representation $v_i$ in the minibatch, this loss maximizes its cosine similarity with its true paired text representation $u_i$, while minimizing similarity with all other $N-1$ text representations $u_k$ (acting as negative samples).
 `_ (v→u)_i = - log [exp(⟨v_i, u_i⟩/τ) / Σ_k=1^N exp(⟨v_i, u_k⟩/τ)]`
- **Text-to-Image Contrastive Loss (`_ (u→v)`):** Symmetrically, for each text representation $u_i$, this loss maximizes its cosine similarity with its true paired image representation $v_i$, and minimizes similarity with all other $N-1$ image representations $v_k$.
 `_ (u→v)_i = - log [exp(⟨u_i, v_i⟩/τ) / Σ_k=1^N exp(⟨u_i, v_k⟩/τ)]`
The final training loss $L$ is a weighted combination of these two losses, averaged over all positive image-text pairs in the minibatch:
 `L = (1/N) Σ_i=1^N (λ`_ (v→u)_i + (1 - λ)`_ (u→v)_i)`
where $\lambda \in$ is a scalar weight and $\tau \in \mathbb{R}^+$ is a temperature parameter that scales the logits.

#### 3.4. Realization of Components

- **Image Encoder ($f_v$):** ResNet50 architecture (He et al., 2016) is used.
- **Text Encoder ($f_u$):** A BERT encoder (Devlin et al., 2019) followed by a max-pooling layer. It is initialized with ClinicalBERT weights (Alsentzer et al., 2019) pretrained on MIMIC clinical notes. During pretraining, the embeddings and first 6 transformer layers are frozen, and the last 6 layers are fine-tuned.
- **Projection Functions ($g_v, g_u$):** Modeled as separate learnable single-hidden-layer neural networks with a ReLU non-linearity.
- **Image Transformations ($t_v$):** Sequential application of random cropping, horizontal flipping, affine transformation (rotation, translation, scaling), color jittering (brightness and contrast adjustments only, due to monochrome images), and Gaussian blur.
- **Text Transformations ($t_u$):** A simple uniform sampling of a single sentence from the input document $x_u$. This approach is chosen to preserve semantic meaning.

#### 3.5. Data for Pretraining

ConVIRT is pretrained using two distinct paired image-text datasets:
- **Chest Image Encoder:** MIMIC-CXR database v2 (Johnson et al., 2019), comprising approximately 217k chest radiograph image-text pairs.
- **Bone Image Encoder:** A proprietary collection of approximately 48k musculoskeletal (bone) image-text pairs from the Rhode Island Hospital system.

#### 3.6. Evaluation Tasks & Data

Pretrained image encoders are evaluated on three types of tasks:
- **Image Classification:** Four tasks are used: RSNA Pneumonia Detection, CheXpert image classification (multi-label), COVIDx (multi-class), and MURA bony abnormality detection. Models are evaluated in two settings:
 - **Linear Classification:** Frozen CNN weights, only a linear classification head is trained.
 - **Fine-tuning:** Both CNN weights and the linear head are fine-tuned.
 Evaluations are conducted with 1%, 10%, and 100% of labeled training data. Metrics: AUC (for most tasks), Accuracy (for COVIDx).
- **Zero-shot Image-image Retrieval:** Images are retrieved based on similarity to a query image. A custom CheXpert 8x200 Retrieval Dataset (8 abnormality categories, 10 expert-annotated queries, 200 candidate images per category) is used. Metric: Precision@k.
- **Zero-shot Text-image Retrieval:** Images are retrieved based on similarity to textual queries. Uses the same candidate images, but with 5 radiologist-written textual queries per abnormality category. Metric: Precision@k.

#### 3.7. Baseline Methods

ConVIRT is compared against several standard and competitive initialization methods:
- **Random Init:** Default random initialization for ResNet50.
- **ImageNet Init:** ResNet50 weights pretrained on ImageNet.
- **Caption-LSTM:** ImageNet-initialized CNN weights further pretrained using an image captioning task with a CNN-LSTM model.
- **Caption-Transformer:** ImageNet-initialized CNN weights further pretrained using a CNN-Transformer-based captioning model.
- **Contrastive-Binary-Loss:** Differs from ConVIRT by using a binary classification head to predict whether an image-text pair is real or fake, trained with binary cross-entropy.
For all baselines requiring paired image-text data, the same pretraining datasets as ConVIRT are utilized. Additionally, for analysis, comparisons are made against image-only contrastive learning methods SimCLR and MoCo v2.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of ConVIRT across multiple medical image understanding tasks and comparison with various baselines yielded several key findings:

#### 4.1. Classification Tasks

- **Linear Classification:**
 - ConVIRT consistently achieved the best overall results across all four classification tasks (RSNA Pneumonia, CheXpert, COVIDx, MURA) and across all data settings (1%, 10%, all training data).
 - Notably, for RSNA, CheXpert, and MURA tasks, ConVIRT pretrained with only 1% of the labeled training data surpassed or achieved comparable classification results to ImageNet initialization utilizing 100% of the labeled training data. This highlights ConVIRT's superior data efficiency and the quality of its learned representations.
 - In-domain initialization methods (Caption-Transformer, Caption-LSTM, Contrastive-Binary-Loss, ConVIRT) generally outperformed ImageNet initialization, which in turn outperformed random initialization.
- **Fine-tuning:**
 - In the fine-tuning setting, where both CNN weights and the classification head are optimized, ConVIRT again demonstrated superior performance, achieving the best results in 10 out of 11 settings. The exception was the CheXpert dataset with all training data, where ConVIRT's performance was comparable to Caption-Transformer.
 - Similar to the linear setting, ConVIRT exhibited significant data efficiency. Across all datasets, using only 10% of the labeled training data, ConVIRT achieved classification results that were better than or close to ImageNet initialization with 100% of the labeled training data.
 - The study found ImageNet initialization to be generally superior to random initialization, a finding that differed from some prior work (Raghu et al., 2019), which the authors attributed to potentially better optimization of their ImageNet baselines.

#### 4.2. Retrieval Tasks (Zero-shot)

- **Zero-shot Image-image Retrieval:**
 - ConVIRT achieved the highest precision (e.g., Prec@10 of 42.9) on the CheXpert 8x200 Retrieval Dataset, significantly outperforming all baselines.
 - ImageNet initialization showed only marginal improvement over random guess (Prec@10 of 14.4 vs. 12.5).
 - In-domain methods (Caption-Transformer, Caption-LSTM, Contrastive-Binary-Loss) substantially improved over ImageNet, with Contrastive-Binary-Loss showing the best performance among these baselines.
- **Zero-shot Text-image Retrieval:**
 - ConVIRT achieved markedly superior performance (Prec@10 of 57.5) compared to the Contrastive-Binary-Loss baseline (Prec@10 of 14.5).
 - The significant discrepancy between Contrastive-Binary-Loss's performance in image-image vs. text-image retrieval suggested that its binary classification objective, while possibly helpful for general image features, did not effectively align image and text representations in a common semantic space, unlike ConVIRT's explicit similarity-based contrastive loss.

#### 4.3. Analysis and Discussion

- **Comparison to Image-only Contrastive Learning:**
 - ConVIRT substantially outperformed popular image-only contrastive learning methods (SimCLR and MoCo v2) on representative classification and retrieval tasks. For instance, on RSNA linear classification (1% data), ConVIRT achieved an AUC of 90.7, compared to SimCLR's 86.3 and MoCo v2's 86.6. This indicates that leveraging paired text data is critical and highly effective for learning high-quality medical visual representations, especially given the high inter-class similarity in medical images.
- **Saliency Maps:**
 - Saliency map visualizations revealed that models pretrained with ConVIRT focused on more relevant anatomical regions indicative of abnormalities, compared to models pretrained with ImageNet, SimCLR, or MoCo v2. For example, for atelectasis, ConVIRT correctly highlighted the bottom lung regions, whereas other methods showed scattered or incorrect focus. This suggests that ConVIRT leads to learning more semantically aligned and clinically meaningful features.
- **Correlation between Contrastive Loss and End Task Performance:**
 - A clear positive correlation was observed between lower pretraining validation loss (indicating better performance on the contrastive pretraining task) and improved performance on downstream tasks (RSNA classification, image-image retrieval, text-image retrieval). This supports the notion that optimizing the ConVIRT objective directly contributes to learning better generalizable representations.
- **Hyperparameter Analysis:**
 - The performance of ConVIRT was found to be most sensitive to the temperature parameter ($\tau$), consistent with findings in other contrastive learning studies. Suboptimal $\tau$ values significantly degraded retrieval performance.
 - Changes in batch size had a less substantial impact on classification results but showed some effect on retrieval tasks.
 - Using non-linear projection heads for $g_v$ and $g_u$ generally resulted in better retrieval performance compared to linear layers, suggesting the benefit of increased representational capacity.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The ConVIRT framework represents a significant contribution to medical image understanding, particularly by addressing the pervasive challenge of data scarcity in healthcare. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Addressing Annotation Scarcity:** ConVIRT provides an effective unsupervised methodology for learning robust medical visual representations by leveraging naturally occurring paired descriptive text (radiology reports). This obviates the need for costly and time-consuming expert human annotations, which are a major bottleneck in developing deep learning models for medical applications.
- **Improved Representation Quality and Downstream Performance:** The method consistently demonstrated superior performance on multiple medical image classification and retrieval tasks compared to strong baselines, including ImageNet pretraining, captioning-based methods, and image-only contrastive learning. This indicates that ConVIRT learns higher-quality, domain-specific visual features that are more relevant for clinical diagnosis.
- **Enhanced Data Efficiency:** A crucial finding is ConVIRT's remarkable data efficiency. It achieved comparable or superior classification accuracy with as little as 10% of the labeled training data, relative to an ImageNet-initialized counterpart using 100% of the data. This substantial reduction in labeled data requirement is particularly impactful in healthcare, where labeling is expensive and data availability can be limited.
- **Domain-Agnostic and Generalizable Framework:** ConVIRT's approach is designed to be agnostic to specific medical specialties, making it broadly applicable across different types of medical images (e.g., chest X-rays, musculoskeletal images) and potentially adaptable to various clinical contexts. It does not rely on domain-specific rules, enhancing its generalizability.
- **Foundational Work for Cross-Modal Learning:** The principles underlying ConVIRT, particularly the bidirectional contrastive objective between images and text, have served as foundational inspiration for subsequent prominent multimodal models beyond the medical domain, such as CLIP and ALIGN. This demonstrates the broad applicability and innovative nature of the proposed framework for learning visual representations from natural language supervision.
- **Catalyst for Future Multimodal AI in Healthcare:** ConVIRT opens avenues for more sophisticated and cost-effective machine learning applications in healthcare. The authors suggest that the core idea is extensible to incorporate other multimodal healthcare data, such as multiomics data or patient metadata, further enhancing the robustness and efficiency of medical AI systems. This could lead to advancements in areas like medical visual question answering, image regeneration, and clinical risk prediction.
- **Facilitation of Research:** By publicly releasing the model and collected retrieval datasets, the authors facilitate further research and development in multimodal medical image understanding, encouraging replication, extension, and new innovations building upon their work.

In conclusion, ConVIRT offers a robust and data-efficient solution for learning high-quality medical visual representations, addressing critical challenges in the field and providing a foundational framework for future multimodal AI applications in healthcare.
