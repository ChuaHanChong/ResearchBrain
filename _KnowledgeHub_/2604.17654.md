# Poly-EPO: Training Exploratory Reasoning Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Stanford University researchers developed Polychromic Exploratory Policy Optimization (Poly-EPO), a Set Reinforcement Learning framework, to train language models that sustain generation diversity and improve generalization by inherently balancing exploration and exploitation. The approach led to higher pass@k coverage on mathematical reasoning tasks and enabled the discovery of a greater number of distinct successful strategies in synthetic problem environments.

## Problem

- Standard RL fine-tuning of Large Language Models (LLMs) leads to a rapid collapse in generation diversity, limiting their ability to generalize and discover varied solutions.
- Existing methods, like reward shaping with exploration bonuses, treat exploitation and exploration as separate objectives, failing to inherently balance them without careful hyperparameter tuning.
- Previous Set Reinforcement Learning (Set RL) frameworks lacked scalability for practical application in large-scale LLM post-training.

## Method

- Introduces **Poly-EPO** (==Polychromic Exploratory Policy Optimization==), a scalable ==Set Reinforcement Learning== framework that assigns rewards to sets of generated responses rather than individual ones.
- Employs a ==polychromic objective== that multiplies the average reward of a set by the diversity of its reasoning strategies, inherently encouraging a balance between exploitation and optimistic exploration.
- Utilizes an ==LM-judge== to cluster generations based on their high-level reasoning strategies, providing a diversity score integrated into a ==marginal set advantage== for policy gradient updates.

## Results

- Achieved up to **+20% higher pass@k coverage** on mathematical reasoning benchmarks (e.g., BeyondAIME, AIME), indicating improved generalization over standard baselines.
- Consistently increased the diversity of successful reasoning strategies throughout training, preventing the diversity collapse observed in **GRPO**.
- Discovered over **5x more distinct successful strategies** in synthetic domains and maintained greater useful diversity at inference time, leading to stronger majority-vote accuracy.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A multiplicative set objective, combining average reward and reasoning diversity, can intrinsically promote a synergistic balance between exploration and exploitation.
> - Optimistic exploration is fostered because individual unsuccessful generations can still receive positive learning signals if they contribute to the diversity of successful sets.
> - The marginal set advantage explicitly contains a covariance term, allowing the model to directly optimize for generations that simultaneously enhance both reward and diversity.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research addresses a fundamental challenge in the application of reinforcement learning (RL) to fine-tune large language models (LMs) for reasoning tasks: the decline in generational diversity. While RL has significantly enhanced LM reasoning capabilities, standard RL fine-tuning algorithms, such as Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) or Generalized Policy Optimization (GRPO), tend to converge on a narrow set of high-reward behaviors. This "diversity collapse" reduces the model's ability to explore alternative solutions, generalize to novel problems, and effectively leverage test-time compute methods that rely on diverse generations (e.g., verification, aggregation, search).

Prior work has attempted to mitigate this issue primarily through reward shaping, where an exploration bonus (e.g., entropy, UCB, or semantic diversity bonus) is added to the task reward. However, these methods often treat exploration and exploitation as separate objectives, requiring careful tuning of weighting hyperparameters (λ) and not explicitly optimizing for a synergistic balance between the two. Furthermore, some multi-sample frameworks, like those optimizing for pass@k, often use leave-one-out estimators, which implicitly reduce the shared learning signal to individual credit assignment, a paradigm distinct from the fundamental tenets of Set Reinforcement Learning.

This paper builds upon the recently introduced Set Reinforcement Learning (Set RL) framework, which assigns rewards to sets of actions or generations rather than individual ones, coupling samples under a shared learning signal. While previous Set RL work demonstrated its ability to encourage exploration, its scalability to large-scale LM post-training has been a challenge due to sample and computational complexities. This work aims to develop a principled and scalable Set RL approach that explicitly fosters optimistic exploration and balances exploration and exploitation synergistically, thus advancing the field's ability to train more robust and adaptable reasoning LMs.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation for this work stems from the observation that effective exploration is crucial for LMs to discover solutions to complex problems, generalize to novel scenarios, and scale performance with test-time compute. The authors identify three desiderata for an algorithm that effectively addresses the problem of diversity collapse during LM fine-tuning:

1. **Encourage Optimism under Uncertainty:** The algorithm should assign a positive learning signal to trajectories that employ novel reasoning strategies, even if these strategies have not yet yielded high rewards. This is intended to broaden the policy's capabilities by allowing it to refine promising but initially unsuccessful approaches, rather than immediately abandoning them for more familiar, high-reward ones. For example, an agent attempting an algorithmic problem should continue refining a dynamic programming approach even if initial attempts fail, instead of reverting to a simpler but less scalable exhaustive search.

2. **Encourage Synergy between Exploration and Exploitation:** The learning signal for each generation should depend on its contribution to the model's joint achievement of high task performance and diverse strategy exploration. This aims to allow the model to inherently learn a balance between these two goals through optimization, rather than relying on external, manually tuned weighting coefficients. The objective should integrate these two aspects so that neither is prioritized to the detriment of the other.

3. **Scalability for Large-Scale Language Model Post-Training:** The algorithm must be computationally and sample-efficient, ensuring that its overhead does not impede its application to the extensive resources typically required for fine-tuning large language models. This means avoiding methods that introduce significant additional computational burdens or necessitate an impractical number of samples.

The authors argue that standard RL algorithms do not explicitly incentivize optimistic exploration, leading to a collapse of diversity. Existing reward shaping methods, while attempting to encourage exploration, treat it as an additive objective to exploitation, which may lead to suboptimal balancing and reliance on sensitive hyperparameter tuning. The limitations of prior Set RL formulations regarding scalability motivated the development of a more practical approach for LM fine-tuning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of this work is the development of a scalable Set RL framework for language models and its instantiation in Polychromic Exploratory Policy Optimization (Poly-EPO).

#### 3.1. Set Reinforcement Learning for Language Models

The paper adapts the Set RL framework to the single-turn generation problem common in LM training. Instead of assigning a reward to individual generations, a set-level objective function `f(x, y_1:n)` is defined over a set of `n` generations (`y_1..., y_n`) sampled independently from the policy `π_θ(·|x)` for a given prompt `x`. This objective couples all generations within the set, providing a shared learning signal. A critical distinction from standard RL is that this set objective cannot be trivially reduced to a policy-independent per-generation reward.

The primary practical challenge of Set RL, particularly in LM training, is the statistical and computational cost of naive gradient estimation, which would require repeatedly sampling full sets. To address this, the authors propose a general recipe:

1. **Generation Sampling:** For a fixed prompt `x`, `N` independent generations (`y_1..., y_N`) are sampled from the current policy `π_θ(·|x)`. `N` must be greater than the set size `n`.
2. **Set Construction:** From these `N` generations, `K` sets (`G_1..., G_K`) of size `n` are constructed combinatorially (all `C(N, n)` subsets) or by uniform sampling without replacement.
3. **Set-Level Scoring:** For each constructed set `G_j`, the set-level score `f(x, G_j)` is computed using the defined objective function.
4. **Variance Reduction Baseline:** A set-level baseline `f_hat(x)` is computed as the average of the scores of all `K` constructed sets.
5. **Set Advantage Function:** A set advantage `A_set(x, G_j; f) = f(x, G_j) - f_hat(x)` is calculated for each set.
6. **Marginal Set Advantage:** The key innovation for scalability is the definition of a *marginal set advantage* for each individual generation `y_i`. This is computed as `A_marg_set(x, y_i; f) = (1/|G(y_i)|) * sum_{G in G(y_i)} A_set(x, G; f)`, where `G(y_i)` is the collection of all constructed sets containing `y_i`. This effectively distributes the shared set-level credit to individual generations.
7. **Standard RL Integration:** This marginal set advantage `A_marg_set(x, y_i; f)` can then replace the standard advantage function in existing RL algorithms (e.g., GRPO, PPO). The authors demonstrate that this estimator is an unbiased estimator of the true Set RL policy gradient (up to a scaling factor).

#### 3.2. Polychromic Exploratory Policy Optimization (Poly-EPO)

Poly-EPO instantiates this general Set RL framework with a specific objective designed to synergistically balance exploration and exploitation.

1. **Polychromic Objective:** The objective function used is `f_poly(x, y_1:n) = (1/n * sum_{i=1 to n} r(x, y_i)) * d(x, y_1:n)`. This objective is a product of the average reward of the generations in the set and the diversity of the reasoning strategies within that set. By using a product, the objective encourages the set to achieve both high reward and high diversity simultaneously, as neither factor can be zero if the overall score is to be maximized.
2. **Diversity Function:** To measure diversity, the method leverages a Language Model Judge (LM-judge). For a given prompt `x`, all `N` sampled generations are fed to an LM-judge, which clusters them based on the semantic similarity of their underlying reasoning strategies (both macro- and micro-strategies). The clustering explicitly disregards superficial differences like phrasing or arithmetic errors and is independent of the final correctness. A special "cluster 100" is reserved for degenerate or reward-hacking responses (e.g., gibberish, random guesses). The diversity `d(x, y_1:n)` of a set of `n` generations is then defined as `(|distinct clusters represented in y_1:n|) / n`.
3. **Algorithm Integration:** The Poly-EPO algorithm computes the marginal set-RL advantage for each generation using the polychromic objective and the LM-judge-based diversity measure. This advantage is then plugged into a standard RL update rule, specifically using a GRPO-like objective formulation where the advantage is applied uniformly across tokens of a generation.

#### 3.3. Theoretical Analysis

The paper includes an analysis comparing the logit shifts in standard RL versus Set RL. It shows that in Set RL, the log likelihood of a generation `y` changes according to its *marginal set advantage*, defined as `E_{y_2:n} [f(x, y, y_2:n)] - E_{y_1:n} [f(x, y_1:n)]`. This means the learning signal for `y` depends on its contribution to sets containing it, rather than just its individual reward.

For the polychromic objective, the marginal set advantage can be decomposed into two main terms:
- **Term 1 (Optimistic Exploration):** Reflects the expected contribution of `y` to the product of reward and diversity in sets containing it. This term can be positive even if `y` itself is incorrect or lacks diversity, provided other generations in the set contribute. This supports the optimistic exploration desideratum.
- **Term 2 (Exploration-Exploitation Synergy):** Captures how `y` influences the covariance between reward and diversity across sets containing `y`. This term favors generations that help sets simultaneously achieve high reward and high diversity, directly addressing the synergy desideratum.

This analysis highlights that Poly-EPO inherently incentivizes these behaviors, unlike reward-shaped standard RL, where the balance is determined by an external weighting hyperparameter and the exploration incentive can diminish as model accuracy improves.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy of Poly-EPO was evaluated on both large-scale mathematical reasoning benchmarks and synthetic domains designed to test diverse strategy discovery.

#### 4.1. Mathematical Reasoning Benchmarks

Experiments were conducted using Qwen-3-4B-Base on the POLARIS-53k dataset, evaluating on held-out benchmarks like BeyondAIME, AIME, HMMT, and Minerva. Poly-EPO was compared against GRPO and GRPO+DIV (GRPO with an LM-judge-based diversity bonus).

- **Pass@k Evaluations:** Poly-EPO demonstrated improved generalization, with its pass@k performance increasing more significantly as `k` (number of attempts) increased, yielding gains of up to 20% on test sets. This indicates that Poly-EPO produces generations with higher effective diversity compared to GRPO and GRPO+DIV, which exhibited substantial pass@k degradation, with the base model sometimes outperforming them at higher `k`.
- **Training Dynamics:**
 - **Diversity of Successful Strategies:** Poly-EPO steadily increased the average number of unique reasoning-strategy clusters among *correct* generations during training, reaching substantially higher levels than GRPO (which showed a decline) and GRPO+DIV (which remained flat). This suggests more effective exploration and discovery of a broader range of successful reasoning approaches.
 - **Coverage of Training Problems:** Poly-EPO and GRPO+DIV achieved a higher fraction of training prompts for which at least one correct generation was sampled, outperforming GRPO. This indicates that promoting exploration not only preserves diversity but also enhances the probability of finding successful solutions during training.
- **Branching in Generations:** Analysis of the branching structure of sampled generations on test sets revealed that Poly-EPO models branch substantially earlier in the generation process compared to GRPO. While GRPO models eventually reach a similar number of branches, their generations remain coupled for longer. This indicates that Poly-EPO encourages broader strategic exploration earlier in the reasoning trajectory at test time.
- **Majority Voting Evaluations:** Poly-EPO-trained models exhibited a consistently lower majority vote share compared to GRPO and GRPO+DIV. This signifies higher entropy over final answers and greater diversity in candidate responses. Despite this, Poly-EPO achieved equal or stronger majority-vote accuracy as `k` increased, often yielding the largest performance gains from additional samples. This suggests the diversity induced by Poly-EPO is useful, contributing to improved calibration and generalization.

#### 4.2. Synthetic Domains with Infinitely Many Valid Strategies

Poly-EPO was tested on "Polynomial Solving" and "Multi-digit Multiplication" tasks, both of which permit infinitely many distinct solution strategies.

- **Discovery of Diverse Strategies:** GRPO quickly converged to a single successful strategy. In contrast, Poly-EPO discovered over five times as many distinct successful strategies.
- **Diversity of Incorrect Generations:** Poly-EPO maintained higher diversity even among incorrect generations, indicating a broader coverage of strategies being attempted during optimistic exploration. These results underscore Poly-EPO's effectiveness in maintaining exploration and uncovering novel high-reward strategies in open-ended problem domains.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work makes contributions to the field of large language model training and reinforcement learning, particularly regarding exploration and diversity.

- **Addressing Diversity Collapse:** Poly-EPO provides a principled and scalable solution to the pervasive problem of diversity collapse observed during RL fine-tuning of LMs. By explicitly encouraging optimistic exploration and balancing it synergistically with exploitation, it helps LMs maintain and expand their repertoire of reasoning strategies.
- **Improved Generalization and Scalability:** The enhanced strategic diversity fostered by Poly-EPO translates into improved generalization to unseen problems and more effective utilization of test-time compute. This suggests that models trained with Poly-EPO may be more robust and adaptable, especially in applications where multiple attempts or diverse approaches are beneficial (e.g., automated theorem proving, complex code generation).
- **Theoretical Framework for Set RL:** The paper introduces a general recipe for optimizing LMs with Set RL and provides a theoretical bridge between Set RL and standard RL via the marginal set advantage. This framework clarifies how a shared set-level signal can be translated into per-generation learning signals, making Set RL more practical for large-scale LM applications.
- **Synergistic Exploration-Exploitation:** The polychromic objective and its inherent property of encouraging covariance between reward and diversity offer a distinct advantage over traditional reward shaping methods. This enables LMs to intrinsically learn to balance these competing objectives, reducing the need for heuristic tuning.
- **Discovery of Novel Strategies:** The results on synthetic domains highlight Poly-EPO's capacity to discover a broader range of successful strategies, which is critical for complex problems with many valid solution paths. This could lead to LMs that are more creative and innovative in their problem-solving approaches.

**Limitations** acknowledged by the authors include potential susceptibility to reward hacking when using LM-judges for clustering, the computational demands of clustering long generations, and a lack of systematic understanding regarding the scaling laws of the general Set RL recipe (e.g., optimal set size, number of rollouts). Future work could explore alternative diversity measures not reliant on LM-judges and compare Poly-EPO to other exploration-inducing techniques like synthetic data generation.
