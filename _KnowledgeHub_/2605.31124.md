# QVGGT: Post-Training Quantized Visual Geometry Grounded Transformer

> [!summary] Summary
> QVGGT introduces a post-training quantization framework that enables the deployment of the large Visual Geometry Grounded Transformer (VGGT) on resource-constrained platforms. It achieves near-lossless 4-bit quantization, reducing model memory by up to 4.9x and accelerating inference by up to 2.8x on hardware while maintaining high performance across 3D perception tasks.

## Problem

- The Visual Geometry Grounded Transformer (VGGT), a 1.2 billion parameter model, incurs substantial memory and computational costs during 3D attribute estimation.
- VGGT's large size limits its practical deployment on resource-constrained edge devices like UAVs and mobile AR systems requiring real-time performance.
- Generic post-training quantization (PTQ) methods or other compression techniques often fail to maintain performance for complex 3D geometry transformers.

## Method

- Implemented a selective ==mixed-precision quantization== strategy, keeping sensitive transformer blocks or layers at higher precision (FP16) while quantizing others to INT4.
- Introduced Camera Information Compensation (CIC) to filter outlier activations from camera/register tokens during scale calibration and reintroduce lost geometric information via a synthesized token during inference.
- Developed a task-aware scale searching mechanism that optimizes quantization scales using a combined objective incorporating multi-head prediction losses and a novel geometry consistency loss.

## Results

- QVGGT (W4A16) achieved near-lossless performance comparable to the FP32 baseline, with an AUC@30 of 89.4 on CO3Dv2 for camera pose estimation, versus 89.5 for FP32.
- Reduced model memory footprint by up to **4.9x** and achieved inference speedups of up to **2.8x** on real hardware (relative to FP32), making VGGT deployable on edge devices.
- Significantly outperformed generic PTQ methods (e.g., GPTQ at 76.9 AUC@30 on CO3Dv2) and showed competitive performance against concurrent specialized work (QuantVGGT at 89.2 AUC@30).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Different transformer blocks within VGGT exhibit heterogeneous sensitivity to quantization, necessitating a non-uniform mixed-precision approach.
> - High-magnitude, high-variance activations from specialized camera and register tokens can bias quantization scale estimation, degrading performance without specific handling.
> - Optimizing quantization scales based solely on layer-wise reconstruction error is insufficient for multi-head 3D geometry models; task-level objectives, including geometric consistency, are crucial.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The estimation of 3D attributes from visual inputs is a fundamental challenge in computer vision, with applications spanning robotics, augmented reality, and digital content creation. Historically, this task has been addressed by traditional geometry-based methods such as Structure-from-Motion (SfM) and Multi-View Stereo (MVS). While these methods, often relying on explicit geometric constraints and iterative optimization techniques like Bundle Adjustment, can achieve high accuracy, they are characterized by significant computational costs.

Recent advancements in deep learning have introduced learning-based approaches, which aim to directly predict 3D attributes from image data using feed-forward neural networks. Models like DUSt3R and MASt3R represent progress in this direction, simplifying the 3D reconstruction pipeline. The Visual Geometry Grounded Transformer (VGGT) represents a notable development in this line of research, unifying the prediction of multiple key 3D attributes—camera parameters, depth maps, and point clouds—within a single transformer-based architecture in a single forward pass.

Despite its capabilities, VGGT possesses a large parameter count (1.2 billion parameters), which results in substantial memory footprint and computational demands during inference. This characteristic limits its deployment on resource-constrained platforms such as Unmanned Aerial Vehicles (UAVs) and mobile Augmented Reality (AR) devices, where real-time performance and efficiency are critical.

To address the limitations imposed by large model sizes, various model compression techniques have been developed. Model pruning and knowledge distillation aim to reduce model complexity but often offer limited acceleration benefits on contemporary hardware. In contrast, quantization, which involves reducing the numerical precision of model parameters, can simultaneously decrease memory requirements and improve inference speed. While post-training quantization (PTQ) has been effectively applied to large language models (LLMs) and vision transformers (ViTs), its application to large transformer-based 3D reconstruction networks, such as VGGT, remains relatively underexplored.

This research, introducing QVGGT, addresses this specific gap by developing a tailored PTQ framework for 3D geometry transformers. It contributes to the broader effort of making large, high-performing deep learning models deployable in edge and real-time environments, specifically targeting the complexities and unique requirements of 3D vision tasks. The work is also presented in the context of concurrent research, such as QuantVGGT, indicating an emerging focus within the community on efficient deployment of 3D transformers.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an effective post-training quantization (PTQ) framework, named QVGGT, specifically designed to compress the Visual Geometry Grounded Transformer (VGGT) while preserving its performance in 3D attribute estimation. This objective is motivated by the significant limitations of VGGT's large model size and computational demands, which currently hinder its practical deployment on resource-constrained platforms.

The specific motivations driving the design of QVGGT stem from several observations regarding the behavior of VGGT under quantization:

1. **Heterogeneous Quantization Sensitivity:** The researchers observed that different transformer blocks within the VGGT architecture exhibit varying degrees of sensitivity to quantization. Applying a uniform low-bit quantization scheme across all blocks leads to uneven and often severe accuracy degradation. This motivated the development of a selective mixed-precision strategy, where more critical blocks or specific layers within them receive higher precision to mitigate performance loss, while less sensitive parts are quantized to a lower bit-width (e.g., 4 bits) for maximum compression.

2. **Outlier Activations from Camera and Register Tokens:** Analysis revealed that camera tokens and register tokens within VGGT's transformer blocks produce high-magnitude, high-variance activations. These outliers can disproportionately influence the estimation of quantization scales during activation calibration. When per-channel or per-group quantization scales are optimized to minimize reconstruction error, these extreme values can dominate the dynamic range, leading to biased scale estimations. This bias results in a suboptimal quantization resolution—coarse quantization for the majority of activations (image tokens) and overly fine-grained resolution for the outliers—thereby amplifying overall quantization error, particularly impacting the critical camera pose estimation head. This motivated the introduction of a token filtering mechanism to exclude these outliers during calibration, coupled with a method to compensate for the lost geometric information.

3. **Mismatch Between Layer-wise Reconstruction and Task-level Geometric Performance:** Standard PTQ methods typically optimize quantization scales based on minimizing layer-wise reconstruction errors. However, for a multi-head model like VGGT, which predicts interdependent 3D attributes (camera pose, depth, and point clouds) with strict geometric relationships, preserving numerical fidelity at the layer level does not automatically guarantee optimal performance on the downstream 3D prediction tasks. The researchers identified that the intrinsic geometric consistency among the prediction heads (e.g., a point map derived from camera pose and depth should match the directly predicted point map) is not explicitly considered in standard scale search. This motivated the design of a task-aware scale search mechanism that incorporates multi-head supervision and geometric consistency objectives, aligning the quantization process more directly with the actual 3D reconstruction quality.

In summary, the core objective is to make VGGT deployable on edge devices by significantly compressing it through PTQ. This is addressed by tackling the specific challenges of heterogeneous block sensitivity, problematic outlier tokens, and the need for task-specific optimization during quantization scale selection, all tailored to the unique characteristics of 3D geometry transformers.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

QVGGT is a three-stage post-training quantization framework designed to compress the Visual Geometry Grounded Transformer (VGGT). The methodology addresses specific challenges identified in quantizing this large, multi-head 3D perception model.

#### 3.1. Preliminary

VGGT is a feed-forward transformer architecture that takes a sequence of $N$ RGB images of a static 3D scene and outputs corresponding camera parameters ($g_i$), depth maps ($D_i$), and point maps ($P_i$) for each image. Inputs are processed as patched image tokens, augmented with a camera token ($t_{C_i}$) and four register tokens ($t_{R_i}$) for each frame. The model backbone consists of alternating frame blocks (intra-frame features) and global blocks (cross-frame features). Task-specific prediction heads then generate the 3D attributes.

The research focuses on weight-only Post-Training Quantization (PTQ), where floating-point weights ($w$) are mapped to low-bit integers ($q$) using symmetric uniform quantization. The quantization function is defined as $Q(w) = \Delta \cdot \text{Round}\left(\frac{w}{\Delta}\right)$, where $\Delta = \frac{\max(|w|)}{2^{N-1}}$ is the quantization step size for $N$ bits. The dequantized weight $\hat{w}$ is $\Delta \cdot q$.

#### 3.2. Selective Mixed-Precision Quantization

The first stage of QVGGT addresses the observation that different VGGT blocks exhibit heterogeneous sensitivity to quantization.
The researchers conducted a systematic sensitivity analysis by individually quantizing each transformer block and measuring the impact on the camera pose prediction (AUC@30 on CO3Dv2). This analysis revealed that the camera head's performance is particularly sensitive to the precision of certain blocks. Specifically, the 14th, 17th, and 23rd frame blocks, and the 23rd global block, were identified as sensitive.

Based on this analysis, QVGGT adopts a **selective mixed-precision quantization strategy**. For the identified sensitive blocks, only the attention projection layer is quantized to INT4, while the weights of their other layers are kept at FP16 precision. For all other blocks, which exhibited higher quantization robustness, full INT4 quantization is applied to their weights. This approach aims to maximize model compression while preserving the performance of critical components.

#### 3.3. Camera Information Compensation

The second stage tackles the issue of outlier activations originating from camera and register tokens. These tokens exhibit significantly larger magnitudes and higher variance compared to image tokens, particularly in several quantization-sensitive layers. When present during activation statistics collection for scale estimation, these outliers inflate the dynamic range of corresponding activation channels, biasing the quantization scales. This leads to an uneven quantization resolution, amplifying overall quantization error and negatively affecting camera pose estimation.

To mitigate this, QVGGT employs a two-pronged strategy:

1. **Camera-Token-Filtered Quantization:** During the calibration phase, activation statistics and scale search are performed using only the image tokens. This ensures that the optimized quantization scales ($s^*$) primarily reflect the dynamic range of the majority of tokens, thereby reducing bias from high-magnitude camera and register tokens.
2. **Camera Information Compensation (CIC):** Excluding camera tokens during scale search, while stabilizing quantization, removes essential global cues for the camera head. To compensate for this information loss, a **Camera Information Compensation Token (CICT)** is synthesized.
 - The CICT is derived from the distribution of camera-token activations collected from the calibration dataset.
 - Specifically, Principal Component Analysis (PCA) is applied to the centered calibration camera-token matrix ($X_c$).
 - The top-K principal directions ($U_K$) are retained. Each camera token sample is projected onto these directions, and the mean projection ($\bar{z}$) is computed.
 - A compensation token ($\tilde{x}_{\text{CICT}}$) is reconstructed using the mean projection and the principal components.
 - This token is then normalized to match the mean L2 norm of representative patch tokens from the calibration set ($x_{\text{CICT}} = \tilde{x}_{\text{CICT}} \cdot \frac{\|x_p\|_2}{\|\tilde{x}_{\text{CICT}}\|_2}$).
 - During inference, this synthesized $x_{\text{CICT}}$ is appended to the per-frame token sequence before it is passed to the camera head, reintroducing essential global geometric priors.

#### 3.4. Task-aware Scale Searching Mechanism

The third stage addresses the limitation that standard layer-wise reconstruction objectives for scale optimization do not fully capture the impact on downstream 3D geometric performance. VGGT's camera, depth, and point predictions are geometrically interdependent.

QVGGT proposes a **task-aware scale search mechanism** that guides scale selection using both multi-head prediction losses and geometric consistency constraints.
The objective function for scale selection, $L_{\text{task}}(s)$, combines several terms:

1. **Layer Reconstruction Loss ($L_{\text{recon}}$):** The standard layer-wise reconstruction error term is retained.
2. **Multi-head Losses:** Task-specific supervision from VGGT's three prediction heads is incorporated:
 - $L_{\text{camera}}$: Huber loss between predicted and ground truth camera parameters.
 - $L_{\text{depth}}$: Weighted L2 loss for depth maps, using predicted uncertainty.
 - $L_{\text{point}}$: Weighted L2 loss for point maps, using predicted uncertainty.
3. **Geometry Consistency Loss ($L_{\text{geom}}$):** This novel term enforces consistency between geometrically related outputs. It calculates the L2 distance between a point map reconstructed from the predicted depth map and camera parameters (using unprojection, $W_{\text{proj}}$) and the point map directly output by the point head ($W_{\text{direct}}$). This loss penalizes discrepancies between these two representations, ensuring that the selected quantization scales preserve the intrinsic 3D structure.

The final task-aware objective is $L_{\text{task}}(s) = L_{\text{recon}} + L_{\text{camera}} + \alpha L_{\text{depth}} + \beta L_{\text{point}} + L_{\text{geom}}$. The coefficients $\alpha$ and $\beta$ are set to 1, balancing the importance of depth and point-map supervision, while camera-head supervision and geometry-consistency loss are also weighted equally (weight=1.0). Quantization scales are determined by performing a grid search that minimizes this combined task-aware objective.

This geometry-aware design, including explicit compensation for camera tokens and task-level scale optimization, distinguishes QVGGT from concurrent work like QuantVGGT, which primarily focuses on general numerical stabilization techniques.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents QVGGT as a Post-Training Quantization (PTQ) framework that achieves near-lossless 4-bit quantization for VGGT.

**Overall Performance:**
QVGGT (W4A16) consistently achieves performance comparable to the full-precision (FP32) VGGT baseline across various 3D perception benchmarks, while significantly reducing model size and improving efficiency.

**Camera Pose Estimation (Table 1):**
- On the CO3Dv2 and RealEstate10K datasets, QVGGT (W4A16) achieved an AUC@30 of 89.4 and 85.0, respectively. This performance is nearly identical to the FP32 baseline (89.5 on CO3Dv2, 85.3 on RealEstate10K).
- In terms of latency, QVGGT demonstrated a 0.23s inference time on both datasets, which is faster than the FP32 baseline (0.38s and 0.37s).
- Generic PTQ methods (GPTQ and AWQ) showed significant performance degradation under W4A16 (e.g., GPTQ: 76.9 on CO3Dv2; AWQ: 54.6 on CO3Dv2), highlighting their inadequacy for VGGT.
- QVGGT also outperformed a concurrent work, QuantVGGT (W4A16), which achieved 89.2 on CO3Dv2 and 84.4 on RealEstate10K (latency not reported for W4A16).
- The method demonstrated cross-dataset generalization, as RealEstate10K was not used in the calibration process.

**Point Map Estimation (Table 2):**
- On the 7-Scenes and NRGBD datasets, QVGGT (W4A16) exhibited performance very close to the FP32 baseline across accuracy (Acc), completeness (Comp), and normal consistency (NC) metrics. For instance, on 7-Scenes, QVGGT achieved an accuracy mean of 0.031 (FP16: 0.030) and NC mean of 0.849 (FP16: 0.847). Similar trends were observed for NRGBD.
- QVGGT significantly outperformed generic quantization methods (SmoothQuant, GPTQ, AWQ) in these 3D reconstruction tasks.

**Ablation Study (Table 3):**
- **Naive Quantization (Q):** Quantizing all blocks without specific strategies resulted in severe performance degradation (54.57 AUC@30 on CO3Dv2, 0.122 accuracy mean on NRGBD).
- **Selective Mixed-Precision (S):** Enabling this component significantly improved performance (80.76 AUC@30 on CO3Dv2, 0.057 accuracy mean on NRGBD), confirming the heterogeneous sensitivity of VGGT blocks.
- **Token Filtering with Camera Information Compensation (D):** Adding this component further enhanced performance (85.91 AUC@30 on CO3Dv2, 0.054 accuracy mean on NRGBD). An additional ablation (Table A2) specifically for CIC showed that compensation improved AUC@30 from 84.51 to 89.39 on CO3Dv2, demonstrating its role in retrieving geometric information.
- **Task-aware Scale Search (T):** Incorporating this mechanism yielded the highest overall performance (89.39 AUC@30 on CO3Dv2, 0.029 accuracy mean on NRGBD), validating the benefit of task-level objectives.

**Robustness to Calibration Dataset Size (Table 4):**
- QVGGT exhibited high stability with varying calibration set sizes (16, 32, or 128 images), showing only minor performance variations (e.g., AUC@30 ranged from 87.86 to 89.39). This suggests that QVGGT does not require a large calibration dataset for robust performance.

**Efficiency Analysis:**
- **Memory Efficiency (Figure 4, Table 5):** QVGGT significantly reduced peak memory consumption. For a single input frame, it achieved a 4.9x memory reduction compared to FP32 VGGT. This reduction was maintained for increasing numbers of input frames (e.g., 3.7x reduction for 30 frames), effectively mitigating activation memory overhead.
- **Latency Efficiency (Table 5):** With two input frames, QVGGT achieved a 2.11x memory reduction and a 1.93x latency speedup relative to the FP16 baseline. The abstract also reports up to 2.8x real hardware speedup over FP32.

**Visualization Results (Figure 1, Figure A2):**
- Visual comparisons of 3D reconstruction outputs demonstrated that QVGGT (W4A16) produces results that are qualitatively comparable to those of full-precision VGGT across various scene categories and inference scenarios.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents QVGGT, a post-training quantization framework that effectively addresses the challenge of deploying large 3D geometry transformers, specifically VGGT, on resource-constrained platforms. The significance and potential impact of this work are multi-faceted:

1. **Enabling On-Device Deployment:** The primary impact is making high-fidelity 3D perception models, such as VGGT, feasible for deployment on edge devices like UAVs, mobile Augmented Reality (AR) devices, and embedded systems. By achieving near-lossless W4A16 quantization with significant memory reduction (up to 4.9x) and inference speedup (up to 2.8x), QVGGT removes a critical barrier for real-world applications requiring real-time 3D understanding.

2. **Advancement in Quantization for 3D Vision:** The work makes a specific contribution to the field of model quantization by developing techniques tailored to the unique characteristics of 3D geometry transformers. Unlike general-purpose PTQ methods that perform poorly on VGGT, QVGGT's geometry-aware design demonstrates that domain-specific considerations are crucial for successful quantization in complex tasks like 3D reconstruction.

3. **Novel Technical Contributions:** QVGGT introduces several methodological innovations:
 - **Selective Mixed-Precision Quantization:** Demonstrates the importance of identifying and protecting sensitive layers within complex transformer architectures to maintain performance during low-bit quantization.
 - **Token Filtering with Camera Information Compensation (CIC):** Addresses the issue of outlier tokens in vision transformers, a problem that could be generalized to other transformer-based models with specialized tokens. The CIC mechanism provides a novel way to stabilize calibration without sacrificing critical task-specific information.
 - **Task-aware Scale Search:** Highlights the necessity of aligning quantization objectives with downstream task performance, especially in multi-output, geometrically constrained models. This approach could inspire similar task-aware quantization strategies in other domains where layer-wise fidelity does not guarantee end-to-end task accuracy.

4. **Practical Implications for Real-World Systems:** The robustness of QVGGT to small calibration datasets, combined with its substantial efficiency gains, makes it a practical solution for developers. It reduces the overhead typically associated with preparing models for deployment, accelerating the integration of advanced 3D perception capabilities into diverse applications.

5. **Foundation for Future Research:** This work establishes a strong baseline and methodology for further research into efficient 3D geometry transformers. It opens avenues for exploring even lower bit-widths, different quantization schemes, or combining PTQ with other compression techniques (e.g., pruning) while maintaining high geometric accuracy and consistency.

In conclusion, QVGGT represents a significant step forward in making advanced 3D perception models more accessible and practical for a wider range of applications, contributing both a robust solution and novel insights into the specific challenges of quantizing complex 3D vision architectures.
