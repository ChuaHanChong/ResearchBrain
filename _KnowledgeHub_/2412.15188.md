# LMFusion: Adapting Pretrained Language Models for Multimodal Generation

> [!summary] Summary
> LMFusion introduces a framework for adapting pretrained text-only Large Language Models (LLMs) to perform multimodal generation by incorporating modality-specific modules and a decoupled training strategy. This approach maintains the LLM's original language performance, achieving identical scores to Llama-3 on text benchmarks, while also improving image understanding by 20% in CIDEr scores and image generation by 3.6% over baseline, all with 50% fewer computational resources.

## Problem

- The immense computational cost and resource demands of training state-of-the-art multimodal generative models from scratch.
- The issue of catastrophic forgetting, where fine-tuning pretrained text-only LLMs for multimodal tasks degrades their carefully learned language abilities.
- The challenge of efficiently leveraging existing computational investments in large text-only language models for multimodal development.

## Method

- A framework called ==LMFusion==, which modifies the Transfusion recipe by integrating ==modality-specific parameters== into a shared Transformer architecture.
- ==Modality-specific attention mechanisms== (QKV and output projections) and Feed-Forward Networks (FFNs) are introduced for text and image processing.
- A ==decoupled training strategy== initializes all text parameters from a pretrained Llama-3 8B model and freezes them, exclusively training the new image-specific modules and U-Net components.

## Results

- LMFusion preserves the text-only performance of the pretrained Llama-3 8B model, achieving identical scores on language benchmarks, unlike other adaptation methods.
- The model shows superior multimodal capabilities, with a **20%** improvement in CIDEr scores for image captioning and a **3.6%** improvement in image generation metrics on MS-COCO compared to Transfusion.
- LMFusion achieves these performance gains with significantly reduced computational cost, using only **50%** of Transfusion's FLOPs while outperforming it across tasks.
- The framework successfully generalizes to enhance existing Vision-Language Models, demonstrating its versatility by creating LLaVAFusion with strong understanding and generative abilities.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Freezing the pretrained text modules, when combined with a deeply separated multimodal architecture, effectively prevents catastrophic forgetting of language capabilities.
> - Deep modality separation, including modality-specific attention and FFNs, is crucial for achieving superior image understanding and generation in multimodal adaptation.
> - Decoupling training by focusing compute only on new image-specific modules, while leveraging pretrained text weights, yields substantial computational efficiency without sacrificing performance.
