# LMFusion: Adapting Pretrained Language Models for Multimodal Generation

> [!summary] Summary
> LMFusion introduces a framework for adapting pretrained text-only Large Language Models (LLMs) to perform multimodal generation by incorporating modality-specific modules and a decoupled training strategy. This approach maintains the LLM's original language performance, achieving identical scores to Llama-3 on text benchmarks, while also improving image understanding by 20% in CIDEr scores and image generation by 3.6% over baseline, all with 50% fewer computational resources.

## Problem

- The immense computational cost and resource demands of training state-of-the-art multimodal generative models from scratch.
- The issue of catastrophic forgetting, where fine-tuning pretrained text-only LLMs for multimodal tasks degrades their carefully learned language abilities.
- The challenge of efficiently leveraging existing computational investments in large text-only language models for multimodal development.

## Method

- A framework called ==LMFusion==, which modifies the Transfusion recipe by integrating ==modality-specific parameters== into a shared Transformer architecture.
- ==Modality-specific attention mechanisms== (QKV and output projections) and Feed-Forward Networks (FFNs) are introduced for text and image processing.
- A ==decoupled training strategy== initializes all text parameters from a pretrained Llama-3 8B model and freezes them, exclusively training the new image-specific modules and U-Net components.

## Results

- LMFusion preserves the text-only performance of the pretrained Llama-3 8B model, achieving identical scores on language benchmarks, unlike other adaptation methods.
- The model shows superior multimodal capabilities, with a **20%** improvement in CIDEr scores for image captioning and a **3.6%** improvement in image generation on MS-COCO compared to Transfusion, using only **50%** of Transfusion's FLOPs.
- The framework successfully generalizes to enhance existing Vision-Language Models, demonstrating its versatility by creating LLaVAFusion with strong understanding and generative abilities.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Freezing the pretrained text modules, when combined with a deeply separated multimodal architecture, effectively prevents catastrophic forgetting of language capabilities.
> - Deep modality separation, including modality-specific attention and FFNs, is crucial for achieving superior image understanding and generation in multimodal adaptation.
> - Decoupling training by focusing compute only on new image-specific modules, while leveraging pretrained text weights, yields substantial computational efficiency without sacrificing performance.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of multimodal generative models, capable of understanding and generating both text and images in arbitrary sequences, represents a frontier in artificial intelligence. Recent years have seen significant progress with models like Transfusion (Zhou et al., 2024), Chameleon (Team, 2024b), and Unified-IO (Lu et al., 2022, 2024), which demonstrate the power of unified architectures in handling interleaved text and image data. However, a major bottleneck in this landscape is the **exorbitant computational cost** associated with training such models from scratch. Training state-of-the-art text-only LLMs, such as Llama-3, already requires processing trillions of tokens, and extending this to multimodal data further amplifies the resource demands.

LMFusion directly addresses this challenge by proposing an alternative paradigm: **reusing and adapting existing pretrained LLMs** rather than building multimodal models from the ground up. This approach seeks to leverage the massive computational investment already made in training powerful text-only LLMs. The paper highlights a critical problem with naive fine-tuning of these LLMs for multimodal tasks: **catastrophic forgetting**, where the model's original language capabilities degrade significantly when exposed to multimodal data.

This work builds upon the architectural blueprint of Transfusion but differentiates itself by focusing on the *adaptation* of existing LLMs. While other approaches also initialize from pretrained LLMs (e.g., Ge et al., 2023; Sun et al., 2023; Wu et al., 2024b), LMFusion's distinct contribution lies in its specific strategy to *preserve* text-only performance while simultaneously developing strong visual understanding and generation abilities. It situates itself within the broader trend of **model sparsity** and **Mixture of Experts (MoE)** architectures, where modality-specific experts (like FFNs) are used to handle different data types. LMFusion extends this concept by introducing deeper separation, including modality-specific attention mechanisms, to effectively manage the distinct processing needs of text and images within a shared transformer framework.

The paper is thus a significant step towards more efficient and sustainable development of large-scale multimodal AI, acknowledging the practical limitations of "train-from-scratch" approaches for every new capability.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind LMFusion stems from two critical challenges in developing advanced multimodal generative models:

1. **High Computational Cost of Training from Scratch:** Existing powerful multimodal models often require immense computational resources and vast datasets to train from scratch, incurring significant financial and environmental costs. The paper explicitly references the 15 trillion tokens required to train Llama-3, illustrating the scale of this investment for even a single modality.
2. **Degradation of Pretrained Language Capabilities:** When attempting to extend pretrained text-only LLMs to multimodal tasks through naive fine-tuning, their meticulously learned language abilities often suffer from "catastrophic forgetting," leading to a significant drop in performance on text-only benchmarks.

Given these challenges, the **key objectives** of the LMFusion project are:

- **To develop a framework that effectively adapts pretrained text-only Large Language Models (LLMs) with multimodal generative capabilities.** This includes enabling them to understand and generate both text and images in arbitrary sequences.
- **To achieve this adaptation while completely preserving the LLMs' strong original language processing capabilities.** This is crucial for retaining the value of the initial computational investment and ensuring robust performance across all modalities.
- **To significantly reduce the computational demands** associated with developing multimodal generative models by leveraging existing pretrained weights and avoiding the need to retrain on text-only data.
- **To demonstrate a scalable and efficient approach** for multimodal model development that allows for parallel development of language and vision capabilities.
- **To generalize the framework** to enhance existing Vision-Language Models (VLMs) with generative capabilities, further showcasing its versatility.

The overarching goal is to make the development of highly capable multimodal AI more accessible and resource-efficient, capitalizing on the "computational investments" already made in text-only LLMs.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

LMFusion proposes a novel framework that builds upon the Transfusion model, but critically modifies its architecture and training strategy to adapt pretrained text-only LLMs for multimodal generation. The core of its methodology revolves around **modality-specific parameterization within a shared Transformer architecture** and a **decoupled training approach**.

**Foundation: Transfusion Recipe**
LMFusion starts with the Transfusion recipe, which is a unified multimodal model capable of text generation, image understanding, and image generation. It learns to jointly predict next tokens for language and diffuse image representations. It employs a standard Transformer architecture augmented with U-Net structures for image processing. The training objective combines a language modeling loss (cross-entropy) and a Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Models (DDPM) loss, weighted by a hyperparameter λ.

**LMFusion's Architectural Innovations:**
LMFusion is a decoder-only model, like Llama-3, consisting of *N* Transformer layers. Its key architectural differentiator is the introduction of **modality-specific modules** within these layers:

1. **Modality-Specific Input Projection:** Text tokens are projected to text hidden states via `Proj_text`, while noisy image representations are projected to image hidden states via a U-Net downsampler (`UNet-Down_img`).
2. **Modality-Specific Self-Attention (QKV and Output Projections):** Instead of shared QKV (Query, Key, Value) and O (Output) matrices, LMFusion uses separate sets for each modality: `QKV_text`, `O_text` for text, and `QKV_img`, `O_img` for images. Crucially, while the QKV projections are modality-specific, **cross-modal attention is enabled** by concatenating the queries, keys, and values from both text and image modalities before computing attention weights. This allows representations from one modality to attend to contexts from the other, facilitating cross-modal interaction.
3. **Modality-Specific Feed-Forward Networks (FFNs):** Similarly, separate FFNs (`FFN_text` and `FFN_img`) process the outputs of the attention layer for text and image data independently.
4. **Modality-Specific Output Projection:** Text hidden states are projected to logits via `LM-Head_text` for language generation, and image hidden states are projected to predicted noise via a U-Net upsampler (`UNet-Up_img`) for image diffusion.

**Training Strategy: Decoupled Learning**
The most critical aspect of LMFusion's approach is its training methodology, designed to prevent catastrophic forgetting:

1. **Initialization:** All parameters in the text modules (Proj_text, QKV_text, O_text, FFN_text, LM-Head_text) and the Transformer-based image modules (QKV_img, O_img, FFN_img) are initialized from a pretrained Llama-3 8B model. The U-Net components (downsampler and upsampler) are trained from scratch.
2. **Freezing Text Modules:** For the main experiments, the learning rate for the text modules (η_text) is set to 0, effectively **freezing them**. This ensures that Llama-3's pretrained language capabilities remain untouched.
3. **Training Image Modules:** Only the image-specific modules (including the U-Nets and the image-specific QKV, O, and FFN components) are trained with a standard AdamW optimizer and a dedicated learning rate (η_image).
4. **Data:** The model is trained on a collection of Shutterstock image-caption data, similar to Transfusion, emphasizing image generation conditioned on text, and vice versa. By freezing text modules, LMFusion eliminates the need to include text-only data during its continued training, saving computational resources.

**Experimental Setup and Evaluation:**
LMFusion is evaluated against Transfusion in controlled settings, including FLOPs-matched comparisons. Evaluation covers:
- **Language-only tasks:** Using standard benchmarks like HellaSwag, PIQA, SIQA, and WinoGrande to assess text performance.
- **Image Understanding:** Measuring CIDEr scores on MS-COCO for image captioning.
- **Image Generation:** Assessing FID and CLIP scores on MS-COCO for text-to-image generation.
Ablation studies are conducted to analyze the importance of modality separation (no, shallow, and deep separation) and learning rate decoupling.

**Extension to Vision-Language Models (LLaVAFusion):**
The paper further demonstrates the generalizability of the LMFusion recipe by applying it to a pretrained Vision-Language Model (VLM), LLaVA-NeXT. This variant, called LLaVAFusion, freezes the VLM's original transformer parameters and integrates a dedicated, image-specific transformer for generation, showing that the approach can enhance existing VLMs with generative abilities while preserving their understanding capabilities.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted by the authors convincingly demonstrate the effectiveness and efficiency of the LMFusion framework:

- **Preservation of Language Capabilities:** LMFusion successfully preserves the strong text-only performance of the pretrained Llama-3 8B model. On benchmarks like HellaSwag, SIQA, and WinoGrande, LMFusion achieves identical scores to the original Llama-3, significantly outperforming Transfusion, which showed a performance drop (e.g., 11.6% lower on text benchmarks). This confirms that freezing text modules effectively mitigates catastrophic forgetting.

- **Superior Image Understanding and Generation:**
 - **Image Understanding:** LMFusion substantially surpasses Transfusion in image understanding tasks, showing a remarkable **20% improvement** in CIDEr scores for image captioning on MS-COCO.
 - **Image Generation:** For image generation, LMFusion demonstrates superior quality, achieving better FID and CLIP scores compared to Transfusion, with a **3.6% improvement** in overall generation metrics.

- **Computational Efficiency:** LMFusion achieves these performance gains with significantly reduced computational cost. In a controlled comparison, LMFusion, using **only 50% of the total FLOPs** of Transfusion (by only training image modules and not processing text data), still outperforms Transfusion across all tasks. This highlights the efficiency gained by leveraging existing LLM weights and focusing training only on the new modality.

- **Importance of Deep Modality Separation:**
 - **Naive Finetuning (no separation):** Ablation studies reveal that directly fine-tuning a dense Llama-3 model for multimodal generation leads to a substantial degradation of its text-only performance (e.g., HellaSwag dropping by 15% initially), which never fully recovers.
 - **Shallow vs. Deep Separation:** The experiments demonstrate that both shallow separation (modality-specific FFNs only) and deep separation (modality-specific FFNs and attention mechanisms, as in LMFusion) significantly outperform the no-separation approach on image benchmarks. Critically, **deep separation proves more effective than shallow separation**, especially in image generation tasks, indicating the importance of separating attention mechanisms for different modalities.
 - **Freezing Text Modules in Deep Separation:** Unlike dense models where freezing text modules impairs image learning, in the deep modality separation setting, freezing text modules is crucial. It not only maintains original text performance but also facilitates strong performance in image understanding and generation.

- **Qualitative Image Editing Capabilities:** When fine-tuned on an image editing dataset, LMFusion qualitatively demonstrates effective performance in understanding both input images and textual prompts to execute desired edits, showcasing its versatility for interleaved multimodal tasks.

- **Generalizability to Vision-Language Models (LLaVAFusion):** The framework successfully extends to a pretrained VLM, LLaVA-NeXT, to create LLaVAFusion. This model achieves strong performance in both image understanding (on benchmarks like MMMU, ChartQA, RealWorldQA, MME-P) and image generation (FID), often outperforming other state-of-the-art multimodal models, demonstrating the framework's broad applicability beyond text-only LLMs.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

LMFusion represents a significant advancement in the field of multimodal AI, with several profound implications and potential impacts:

1. **Democratization of Multimodal AI Development:** By providing an efficient method to adapt existing powerful text-only LLMs for multimodal generation, LMFusion lowers the barrier to entry for developing highly capable multimodal AI. It significantly reduces the massive computational and data requirements typically associated with training such models from scratch, making advanced multimodal capabilities more accessible to a wider range of researchers and practitioners.

2. **Preservation of Foundational Capabilities:** The framework's ability to completely preserve the original language capabilities of pretrained LLMs is a critical breakthrough. It solves the long-standing problem of catastrophic forgetting in multimodal adaptation, ensuring that the vast knowledge and reasoning abilities embedded in these LLMs are fully retained while new visual competencies are acquired. This maximizes the return on prior computational investments.

3. **Modular and Parallel Development:** The modality-specific design of LMFusion promotes modularity in model development. It allows language and vision components to be developed and optimized in parallel, or for new modalities to be integrated without retraining the entire model. This de-risks the complexities and interdependencies often found in large-scale, joint-modality pretraining efforts, potentially accelerating research and development cycles.

4. **Enhanced Model Utility and Versatility:** LMFusion creates truly versatile AI agents capable of seamless interaction across text and image modalities. Models built with this framework can understand complex instructions involving both modalities, generate creative content (text and images), and perform intricate tasks like image editing, thereby unlocking new applications in creative industries, content generation, and human-computer interaction.

5. **Generalizability and Scalability:** The successful application of the LMFusion recipe to a Vision-Language Model (LLaVA-NeXT, creating LLaVAFusion) demonstrates its broad generalizability. This suggests that the framework can be applied to enhance a wide array of existing single-modality or dual-modality models with generative capabilities, further solidifying its potential as a scalable approach for building comprehensive multimodal foundation models.

6. **Sustainable AI Development:** In an era of increasing concerns about the energy consumption and environmental impact of large AI models, LMFusion offers a more sustainable path by promoting compute reuse. Leveraging and adapting existing models, rather than constantly training new ones from scratch, contributes to more resource-efficient AI development.

In conclusion, LMFusion offers a practical, efficient, and robust solution for integrating multimodal generative capabilities into pretrained LLMs. Its innovative architecture and training methodology provide a promising blueprint for the future development of powerful, versatile, and environmentally conscious multimodal AI systems.
