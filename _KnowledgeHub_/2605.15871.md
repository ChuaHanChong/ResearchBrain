# Agentic Discovery of Neural Architectures: AIRA-Compose and AIRA-Design

> [!summary] Summary
> AIRA-Compose and AIRA-Design are paired agentic frameworks in which LLM research agents autonomously design foundation-model architectures. AIRA-Compose has ensembles of agents arrange Attention, MLP and Mamba primitives into 16-layer proxy models, then aggregates and scales the winners; AIRA-Design has agents write novel sub-quadratic attention mechanisms and training scripts from scratch. Agent-found architectures beat Llama 3.2 and Bayesian-optimization NAS baselines at 1B scale.

## Problem

- Hybrid post-Transformer models outperform pure Transformers, but a 16-layer three-primitive space holds roughly 43M arrangements that prior work explores only by hand.
- Hand design leans on human intuition and expertise, so non-obvious primitive arrangements and entirely new computational primitives are routinely overlooked.
- Prior NAS frameworks use rigid Bayesian optimization and deterministic incremental search, which cannot form context-aware hypotheses about why an arrangement should work.

## Method

- **AIRA-Compose** recasts the ==Composer== search and proxy-evaluation steps as ==AIRS-Bench== tasks: agents emit 16-primitive strings over Attention, MLP and Mamba2, then ==aggregation== and ==extrapolation== stack or stretch winners to 350M-3B.
- **AIRA-Design** poses open-ended code generation: agents write an executable JAX/Flax ==CustomEncoder== implementing sub-quadratic attention for Long Range Arena, or rewrite a nanochat training script for Autoresearch.
- The **AIRA-dojo** harness drives search with ==Draft==, ==Debug==, ==Improve== and ==Analyze== operators under greedy tree search or one-shot scaffolds, plus a With-Literature variant supplying curated paper analyses and code repositories.

## Results

- At 1B scale with **37.5B** tokens, AIRAformer-D cuts validation loss to **2.734** from Llama 3.2's **2.815**, and AIRAformer-D and AIRAhybrid-D gain **2.4%** and **3.8%** downstream accuracy.
- IsoFLOP fits show AIRAformer-C scaling **54%** and **71%** faster than Llama 3.2 and the best Composer Transformer, with AIRAhybrid-D advancing the 1B latency-loss Pareto frontier.
- On Long Range Arena agents land within **2.3** and **2.6** points of human state of the art, yet peak task-average normalized score is only **0.26**; Autoresearch reached **0.968** bits-per-byte.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Iterative refinement is essential: every one-shot agent failed to submit a valid mechanistic design, while greedy tree search with debugging produced working code.
> - Attention-heavy arrangements lose at fixed compute but scale with steeper slopes, so isoFLOP and isoToken protocols rank architectures differently and both should be reported.
> - Agents excel at engineering-level recombination of known primitives rather than genuine algorithmic novelty, and literature access shifts strategies without uniformly improving results.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The research positions itself within the evolving landscape of artificial intelligence, specifically at the intersection of large language model (LLM) agents, neural architecture search (NAS), and the design of next-generation foundation models. Current LLMs predominantly rely on the Transformer architecture, which faces limitations such as quadratic complexity of attention and high memory costs for key-value caching during inference, particularly for long-context processing. This has motivated a shift towards post-Transformer and hybrid architectures that combine diverse computational primitives (e.g., Attention, Multi-Layer Perceptrons (MLPs), State Space Models (SSMs) like Mamba) to improve efficiency and performance.

Historically, model design has been driven by human expertise and intuition. However, as the design space for hybrid models becomes combinatorially vast, manual exploration is increasingly inefficient, potentially overlooking novel and performant configurations. Neural Architecture Search (NAS) methods have emerged to automate this process, though traditional NAS approaches are often computationally prohibitive for large models. Recent work in NAS has focused on approximations, supernet approaches, zero-cost performance proxies, or mechanistic design principles to allocate layers.

Simultaneously, LLM agents have demonstrated increasing capabilities in various scientific research tasks, including hypothesis formulation, code execution, result evaluation, and iterative refinement. A compelling frontier for agentic research is Recursive Self-Improvement (RSI), where agents autonomously discover and optimize the neural architectures that power them. This work contributes to this frontier by developing agent-driven frameworks for neural architecture discovery, aiming to move beyond human-designed baselines and traditional NAS methods. It leverages existing agent evaluation infrastructures like AIRS-Bench, which evaluate agents' ability to conduct independent scientific research within structured task environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to investigate the capability of LLM agents to autonomously design foundation models, including architectures beyond the standard Transformer paradigm. This aligns with the broader goal of Recursive Self-Improvement (RSI), enabling AI systems to enhance their own underlying structures.

The motivation for this work stems from several key challenges and opportunities:
- **Limitations of Transformer Architectures:** The quadratic complexity of attention and memory requirements of Transformers necessitate exploring alternative or hybrid architectures to improve efficiency, especially for long-context applications.
- **Combinatorial Vastness of Hybrid Model Design Space:** The increasing complexity of hybrid models, which interleave various computational primitives (e.g., Attention, MLP, Mamba), creates a combinatorial design space that is too extensive for manual human exploration. This risks overlooking highly performant or non-obvious architectural configurations.
- **Advancements in LLM Agents:** The demonstrated capabilities of LLM agents in automating aspects of scientific research, from hypothesis generation to code implementation and evaluation, suggest their potential for accelerating and expanding neural architecture discovery.
- **Need for Automated and Systematic Exploration:** The authors argue that NAS is an ideal application for AI research agents, as they can systematically explore the design space by pairing context-aware hypotheses with robust automated search and iterative refinement, thereby proposing fundamentally new architectures.

To achieve this, the research introduces a dual-framework approach with two specific objectives:
1. **AIRA-Compose (High-level Architecture Search):** To enable agents to discover new model architectures by combining predefined computational primitives (Attention, MLP, Mamba). The agents are tasked with searching and evaluating architectural candidates at a small scale, with only top-performing designs being scaled up for large-scale evaluation. This recasts the small-scale architectural exploration of the Composer framework into an agentic task.
2. **AIRA-Design (Low-level Mechanistic Implementation):** To empower agents to directly implement novel computational primitives (e.g., attention mechanisms for long-range dependencies) and optimize high-performing training scripts. This involves open-ended code generation evaluated on benchmarks like Long Range Arena (LRA) and Autoresearch.

These complementary objectives aim to demonstrate that AI research agents can autonomously create hybrid architectures and algorithmic optimizations that compete with or exceed human-designed baselines, thereby establishing a flexible paradigm for future foundation model discovery and advancing recursive self-improvement.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research employs a dual-framework approach, AIRA-Compose and AIRA-Design, built upon the AIRS-Bench evaluation framework and the AIRA-dojo harness.

#### 3.1. Core Infrastructure (AIRS-Bench and AIRA-dojo)

- **Agent Definition:** An agent is defined as an LLM combined with a scaffold, which dictates the algorithmic search policy and operators for solution space exploration.
- **AIRA-dojo Harness:** This system manages the agent's environment, execution, and tool access, guiding agents through tree-based code solution evolution. It uses four operators: `Draft` (generates initial solutions), `Debug` (corrects errors), `Improve` (refines solutions for metrics), and `Analyze` (reads solutions).
- **Scaffolds:**
 - **One-shot:** Calls `Draft` once, producing a single solution per run.
 - **Greedy:** Explores multiple solutions via tree-based search, starting with 5 initial `Draft` solutions. The `Improve` operator is applied to the best-performing solution based on validation fitness. `Debug` is triggered if a solution is deemed buggy.
- **AIRS-Bench Tasks:** Tasks are defined by a {problem, dataset, metric} triplet. For this work, `submission.csv` specifies an architecture or training loop (instead of model predictions), and `evaluate.py` encapsulates full training pipelines, ensuring consistent evaluation.

#### 3.2. AIRA-Compose (High-level Architecture Search)

- **Recasting Composer:** Steps 1 (Search Engine) and 2 (Evaluator) of the Composer framework are recast as AIRS-Bench tasks. Agents propose, evaluate, and refine 16-layer architectures. Steps 3 (Aggregator) and 4 (Extrapolator) are then used to scale agent-discovered architectures.
- **Primitives:**
 - **2-primitive search space (M, mA):** MLPs (M) and multi-head Attention (mA).
 - **3-primitive search space (M, mA, Mb):** MLPs (M), multi-head Attention (mA), and Mamba SSM (Mb).
 - Architectures are 16 layers at small scale, with specific configurations for 350M, 1B, and 3B parameter scales provided in Appendix D.
- **Datasets:** Three proxy datasets are used for small-scale evaluation, chosen for their correlation with at-scale performance:
 - **MAD (Poli et al., 2024):** Six synthetic token-manipulation tasks; uses average accuracy.
 - **BabiStories (Zhang et al., 2025):** Synthetic children’s stories; uses cross-entropy loss.
 - **DCLM (Li et al., 2024a):** Subset of a diverse language model corpus; uses cross-entropy loss.
- **Evaluation:** Agents validate hypotheses using a 70:30 train/validation split. Submitted architectures are trained on the full training set and evaluated on a withheld test set.
- **Compute Budget:** Fixed 24-hour compute budget for 2-primitive search, 60 hours for 3-primitive search on larger datasets (BabiStories, DCLM).
- **Scaling:** Top-performing small-scale designs are extrapolated to 350M, 1B, and 3B parameters using stretching (proportional expansion) or stacking (sequential repetition) methods.
- **Metrics for scaled-up models:** Cross-entropy validation loss, 0-shot downstream accuracy and normalized accuracy on 6 tasks (HellaSwag, WinoGrande, ARC-Easy/Challenge, PIQA, SciQ), and DCLM Core score (unweighted average across 14 tasks). IsoFLOP analysis is conducted by training models under varying FLOPs budgets to assess compute-optimal scaling.

#### 3.3. AIRA-Design (Low-level Mechanistic Design)

- **Open-ended Code Generation:** Agents implement novel neural network architectures from scratch (LRA tasks) or optimize training loops (Autoresearch tasks).
- **Long Range Arena (LRA) Tasks:**
 - **Objective:** Design memory-efficient, sub-quadratic attention mechanisms for long-range dependencies.
 - **Output:** A complete `model.py` file defining a `CustomEncoder` (or `CustomDualEncoder`) compatible with JAX/Flax.
 - **Task Versions:**
 - **Configurable:** Agents can specify key global variables (hyperparameters) within `model.py`.
 - **Non-Configurable:** Enforces default LRA parameters, restricting agents to architectural design.
 - **Datasets:** IMDB Sentiment Classification (Text), ListOps (hierarchical mathematical expressions), ACL Anthology Network (AAN) (Retrieval for document similarity). Accuracy is the evaluation metric.
- **Autoresearch Task:**
 - **Objective:** Autonomously improve a GPT language model training script to achieve the lowest possible validation bits-per-byte (BPB) within a 5-minute wall-clock training budget on a single GPU.
 - **Starting Point:** A baseline `train.py` with a nanochat model.
 - **Modifiable Aspects:** Model architecture (depth, width, attention patterns, positional encodings, activations), optimizer and learning rate schedules, training configuration (batch size, gradient accumulation). Data pipeline, tokenizer, and evaluation harness are read-only.
 - **Task Versions:**
 - **Default:** Standard setup.
 - **With Literature:** Provides agents with a curated selection of 41 research papers (structured analyses and 14 code repositories) to inform optimization strategies.
 - **Dataset:** Pre-tokenized web text from the ClimbMix corpus.
 - **Metric:** Validation bits-per-byte (BPB), where lower is better.

#### 3.4. Experimental Setup

- **Runs:** 20 seeds for one-shot agents, 10 seeds for greedy agents (unless specified). Each run lasts max 24 hours (or 60 hours for larger Compose tasks) or 500 steps.
- **Hardware:** One H200 GPU per agent for drafting/validation. Large-scale Compose experiments use 8 H200 (350M models) or 16 H200 (1B and 3B models) GPUs.
- **AIRA-Design Metrics:**
 - **Raw Score:** Direct accuracy/BPB for comparison with SOTA.
 - **Valid Submission Rate (VSR):** Proportion of runs yielding working, validated solutions.
 - **Normalized Score (NS):** Normalized between 0 (worst observed) and 1 (SOTA), using the "march of 9s" transformation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

#### 4.1. AIRA-Compose (High-level Architecture Search)

##### 4.1.1. Primitives (M, mA)

- **Architectures Discovered:** 2,307 unique architectures were explored. This search yielded 6 novel architectures, categorized into "AIRAformers" (A, B, C, D). These converged on distinct Attention-to-MLP (A:M) ratios: 7:9 for AIRAformers A and B, and 11:5 for C and D.
- **IsoToken Analysis (1B scale, fixed token budget):** Agent-discovered AIRAformers consistently outperformed Llama 3.2 and Composer-found alternatives. AIRAformer-D (Stretched) achieved the lowest validation loss (2.734), highest average 0-shot accuracy (59.7% raw, 60.8% normalized), and highest DCLM Core score (48.9%). The low variance across seeds indicated robust improvements.
- **IsoFLOP Analysis (350M, 1B, 3B scales, varying FLOPs budgets):** While attention-heavy AIRAformers (C and D) showed strong performance in the fixed-token-budget setting, architectures with balanced A:M ratios proved more compute-efficient in the isoFLOP regime. AIRAformer-C (Stacked) and AIRAformer-D (Stacked) achieved the steepest scaling slopes (more negative ∆q) compared to Llama 3.2, indicating faster improvement with increased compute.

##### 4.1.2. Primitives (M, mA, Mb)

- **Architectures Discovered:** The search yielded 2,248 unique architectures, probing a small fraction of the vast 3-primitive design space. This resulted in 8 hybrid architectures, called "AIRAhybrids" (A, B, C, D, E).
- **IsoToken Analysis (1B scale, fixed token budget):** AIRAhybrids generally outperformed established baselines including Mamba and approximated Nemotron-2, as well as Composer-found alternatives across linguistic and reasoning benchmarks. AIRAhybrid-D (Stretched) achieved the lowest validation loss (2.719) and highest 0-shot accuracy (60.5% raw, 62.2% normalized).
- **IsoFLOP Analysis (350M, 1B, 3B scales, varying FLOPs budgets):** Architectures with balanced Mamba-to-attention ratios (e.g., Nemotron-2, AIRAhybrid-B/D Stretched) achieved lower validation losses. Attention-heavy AIRAhybrid-C (Stretched) exhibited the steepest scaling slope (∆q = -0.14) relative to Nemotron-2, suggesting superior performance potential at larger scales, despite higher initial loss. The Composer baseline showed a flatter slope.
- **Latency-Validation Loss Pareto Frontiers:** At the 1B-parameter scale, AIRAhybrid-D (Stacked and Stretched) advanced the Pareto frontier, achieving lower validation loss at lower latencies compared to a Composer-found counterpart.

#### 4.2. AIRA-Design (Low-level Mechanistic Design)

##### 4.2.1. Long Range Arena (LRA)

- **Agent Performance:** Across 1,680 runs, Greedy Gemini 3 Pro and Greedy Opus 4.6 achieved the best accuracies, with Gemini 3 Pro reaching within 2.3 percentage points (pp) of human SOTA on document matching (82%) and 2.6pp on text classification (91%).
- **Architecture Nature:** Agent-designed architectures largely recombined and adapted existing efficient attention mechanisms (e.g., linear attention with ELU+1 kernels, hierarchical pooling, blockwise local attention with recurrent memory) rather than introducing fundamentally novel theoretical insights. This suggests proficiency in engineering-level synthesis but not yet algorithmic innovation.
- **Valid Submission Rate (VSR):** VSR varied significantly across agents, with Greedy Opus 4.6 consistently submitting valid solutions (VSR of 1.0) while weaker models fell below 10%. One-shot agents failed to produce any valid submissions, indicating the necessity of iterative refinement.
- **Normalized Score (NS):** All LRA tasks were classified as "hard" (0.10 ≤ NS < 0.20) on AIRS-Bench. The configurable setup generally yielded lower NS values, suggesting agents struggled to effectively leverage hyperparameter optimization.
- **Generalization Gap:** The gap between the best observed accuracy during exploration and the accuracy of the final submitted model was generally small, indicating agents were competent at identifying promising solutions.

##### 4.2.2. Autoresearch

- **Best BPB:** Greedy Opus 4.5 +Literature achieved the lowest validation BPB of 0.968, surpassing the published Autoresearch reference minimum (0.9775) by a delta of 0.0438 BPB (relative to the RADv1 baseline of 1.0121). Opus 4.6 and Opus 4.5 families showed more reliable convergence compared to other agents.
- **Impact of Literature Access:** Providing curated literature and code repositories influenced agents' optimization strategies. For instance, Opus 4.5 +Literature introduced focal loss, leading to a substantial single-step improvement. However, literature access did not uniformly improve all agents.
- **Optimization Strategies:** Agents employed diverse strategies, including model widening/deepening, adjusting learning rates, modifying value embeddings (e.g., sparsifying), and incorporating new loss functions. The full-file regeneration paradigm of the greedy scaffold often led to multiple simultaneous changes, complicating causal attribution of improvements.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research demonstrates the feasibility and competitive potential of using LLM agents for autonomous neural architecture search and design. The introduction of AIRA-Compose and AIRA-Design frameworks, built on AIRS-Bench, represents a step towards recursive self-improvement in AI systems.

The significance and potential impact include:
- **Advancing Foundation Model Design:** The discovery of 14 novel architectures (AIRAformers and AIRAhybrids) that surpass human-designed baselines (Llama 3.2, Nemotron-2) and traditional NAS alternatives (Composer-found models) at 1B-parameter scale suggests a new paradigm for developing more performant and efficient foundation models. The identification of architectures with favorable scaling properties and improved latency–validation loss trade-offs could lead to more optimized model deployment.
- **Acceleration of AI Research:** By automating the hypothesis formulation, experimentation, and iterative refinement cycles, agent-driven frameworks could accelerate the pace of AI research, allowing for exploration of design spaces that are too vast for human intuition or traditional optimization methods.
- **Enhanced Algorithmic Optimization:** The agents' ability to engineer high-performing attention mechanisms (approaching human SOTA on LRA) and optimize training loops (surpassing Autoresearch benchmarks) indicates their capacity to contribute to algorithmic advancements and training efficiency.
- **Step Towards Recursive Self-Improvement (RSI):** This work provides empirical evidence that AI research agents can autonomously discover and implement architectural and algorithmic improvements, a foundational step for AI systems to recursively enhance their own capabilities.
- **Modular and Scalable Frameworks:** The design of AIRA-Compose and AIRA-Design as modular and LLM-agnostic frameworks means they can be extended and adapted with different agent harnesses, reasoning models, and search spaces, facilitating future research in this area.

**Limitations and Future Work (as identified by the authors):**
- **Proxy-Target Gap in AIRA-Compose:** Small-scale proxy evaluations do not always perfectly predict large-scale performance. Future work aims to use advanced harnesses with multiple GPUs per agent to reduce this gap and explore larger models.
- **Non-Agentic Aggregation/Extrapolation:** The aggregation and extrapolation steps in AIRA-Compose are currently manual. Future research will focus on making these steps agentic.
- **Limited Search Space:** Current AIRA-Compose tasks focus only on primitive arrangements. Expanding the search space to include tunable hyperparameters or normalization choices could further leverage LLM potential.
- **Lack of Novel Mechanistic Design in AIRA-Design:** Agents primarily recombine existing ideas rather than generating fundamentally new theoretical insights for attention mechanisms.
- **Challenges in Low-Level Code Generation:** One-shot agents' failure and weaker agents' struggles in configurable LRA tasks highlight the need for improved iterative refinement, debugging capabilities, and better handling of expanded design spaces. The observed PyTorch bias and frequent OOM errors also point to environmental and framework-specific challenges.
- **Causal Attribution in Autoresearch:** The full-file regeneration in the greedy scaffold hinders understanding which specific modifications contribute to improvements. Future scaffolds might enable more incremental and interpretable code changes.
- **Inconsistent Literature Integration:** Literature access did not uniformly help all agents in Autoresearch, indicating challenges in effectively integrating prior knowledge into optimization strategies.

These limitations also highlight areas for future development that could further amplify the impact of agentic AI research.
