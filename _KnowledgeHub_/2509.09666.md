# Unified Multimodal Models as Auto-Encoders

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Peking University and Baidu developed Unified Multimodal Models as Auto-Encoders (UAE), a framework that unifies image-to-text understanding and text-to-image generation through a reconstructive reinforcement learning approach where text serves as the latent representation. This method enhanced fine-grained visual perception by up to 60.0% and achieved state-of-the-art text-to-image generation performance, alongside improved overall unification scores.

## Problem

- Existing Unified Multimodal Models (UMMs) often optimize image-to-text (I2T) understanding and text-to-image (T2I) generation in isolation, missing opportunities for mutual enhancement.
- Directly integrating I2T and T2I tasks can lead to sub-optimal performance, where optimizing one objective (e.g., diffusion-based generation) degrades the other (e.g., understanding capabilities).
- This often results in brittle joint training processes or necessitates decoupling modules, preventing synergistic benefits from their intrinsic connection.

## Method

- The paper proposes unifying I2T understanding and T2I generation under an ==Auto-Encoder== (AE) perspective, where text acts as the intermediate latent representation for image reconstruction.
- A reconstructive reinforcement learning (RL) method, Unified-GRPO, is introduced to maximize the semantic similarity between an original input image and its text-reconstructed version using CLIP embeddings as the reward function.
- Unified-GRPO is applied as a post-training fine-tuning step to both hybrid autoregressive-diffusion and fully autoregressive UMM architectures, updating only the language model component while keeping visual encoders/decoders frozen.

## Results

- The Unified Auto-Encoder (UAE) achieved the highest overall unified score (86.09) on the new Unified-Bench benchmark, outperforming GPT-4o-Image (85.95) and showing consistent improvements across various vision backbones.
- UAE demonstrated state-of-the-art text-to-image generation on GenEval with an overall score of **0.89** and led on GenEval++ for compositional understanding, indicating improved complex instruction following.
- The model's understanding capabilities showed significant gains in fine-grained visual recognition, with a +**24.4%** increase in the fine-grained overall MMT-Bench score, including a +**40.0%** boost in Small Object Detection and +**60.0%** in Person Re-ID.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Treating text as an intermediate latent representation enables a principled auto-encoding framework for cross-modal understanding and generation, linking I2T and T2I conceptually.
> - Optimizing for high-fidelity image reconstruction from generated text fosters a self-evolving, mutually reinforcing feedback loop that enhances both fine-grained visual understanding and faithful image generation simultaneously.
> - Reinforcement learning, guided by semantic similarity rewards, can effectively bridge the modality gap by encouraging the language model to extract comprehensive visual semantics for robust reconstruction.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of multimodal artificial intelligence has seen increasing interest in Unified Multimodal Models (UMMs) capable of both understanding and generation across different modalities, particularly images and text. Traditionally, image-to-text (I2T) understanding (e.g., image captioning, visual question answering) and text-to-image (T2I) generation have been treated as distinct tasks, often optimized independently. While attempts have been made to combine these capabilities within UMMs, existing approaches frequently encounter challenges. Directly integrating understanding and generation models can lead to sub-optimal performance, where optimizing one objective (e.g., diffusion-based generation) may degrade the other (e.g., understanding capabilities or learned representations), resulting in brittle joint training. Consequently, some works decouple these tasks, training modules separately and thereby potentially missing opportunities for mutual benefits from their intrinsic connection.

The research paper addresses this perceived decoupling and sub-optimality by proposing a unified framework. It positions itself within the landscape of UMMs by arguing for a more principled conceptual linkage between I2T and T2I. Current UMM architectures broadly fall into two categories: (1) AR-based approaches, which tokenize and process all modalities sequentially using an autoregressive transformer, and (2) Hybrid AR-Diffusion architectures, which combine autoregressive language models with diffusion-based image synthesis. This work seeks to enhance both architectural families by introducing a novel training paradigm that leverages the reconstructive nature of an auto-encoder, aiming for a synergistic improvement that previous independent or weakly coupled methods have not fully achieved. It builds upon advancements in reinforcement learning (RL) for large language models (LLMs) and their application to generative tasks, extending these to a cross-modal, reconstructive objective.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to unify image-to-text (I2T) understanding and text-to-image (T2I) generation under a shared Auto-Encoder (AE) perspective. The authors posit that despite their fundamental connection, these two multimodal tasks have largely been optimized in isolation, leading to missed opportunities for mutual enhancement.

The core motivation stems from an observation that existing unified multimodal models often face difficulties. Specifically, optimizing generative objectives (e.g., diffusion models) can negatively impact understanding capabilities and learned representations, and vice versa. This can lead to a "brittle" joint training process or necessitate decoupling understanding and generation modules, which prevents potential cross-task mutual benefits. The authors argue that this lack of demonstrable mutual gains weakens the rationale for truly "unified" systems, reducing them to mere parallel components.

To address these issues, the paper introduces a conceptual framework where text serves as an intermediate latent representation, bridging the two directions: encoding images into textual semantics (I2T) and decoding text back into images (T2I). The key insight is that for a true auto-encoder, if the encoder genuinely "understands" an image, it should capture all essential visual structure. Conversely, if the decoder genuinely "understands" the text, it should faithfully recover that structure back into an image. Therefore, high-quality reconstruction of the input image from its textual description becomes a direct proxy for enhancing both the understanding (I2T) and generation (T2I) tasks simultaneously.

This reconstructive objective motivates the development of a training methodology that encourages the encoder to extract comprehensive and accurate semantic information from the input image to maximize reconstruction quality. Concurrently, the decoder is optimized to generate images that accurately reflect the textual semantics provided by the encoder. This establishes a self-evolving, mutually reinforcing cross-modal feedback loop, aiming for a synergy where improvements in one module drive improvements in the other, thereby fostering richer semantic representations and more faithful visual generation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology unifies I2T understanding and T2I generation within an auto-encoding paradigm where text functions as the intermediate latent representation. Given an input image *x*, a Unified Multimodal Model (UMM) first produces a semantic description *y* (I2T), and then reconstructs an image *ˆx* from *y* (T2I). The central approach involves using reconstructive reinforcement learning (RL) to maximize the semantic similarity between the original input image *x* and the reconstructed image *ˆx*.

#### 3.1. Unified-GRPO

The core of the methodology is **Unified-GRPO**, a reconstructive reinforcement learning method. This method trains the model to maximize reconstruction fidelity. The reward function for this RL process is defined as the cosine similarity between the CLIP embeddings of the original image *x* and the reconstructed image *ˆx*: *R(x, ˆx) = cos(f<sub>CLIP</sub>(x), f<sub>CLIP</sub>(ˆx))*. This encourages the model to preserve semantic content through the text-based latent space.

Unified-GRPO is designed to be applicable to two prevalent UMM architectural families:

- **UMM-1 (Hybrid AR-Diffusion):** In this architecture (e.g., UniWorld, MetaQuery), an autoregressive language model (LLM), denoted as $\pi_\phi$, is responsible for multimodal understanding and provides language priors. A diffusion transformer (MM-DiT), denoted as $p_\theta$, is used for image generation. For Unified-GRPO, the LLM $\pi_\phi$ is fine-tuned using reconstructive RL, while the diffusion transformer $p_\theta$ is kept frozen and functions as part of the reward environment (alongside a frozen CLIP encoder).
 - **Process:** An input image *x* is processed by the LLM to sample *G* caption sequences, $\{y^{(i)}\}_{i=1}^G$, from the current policy $\pi_{\phi_{old}}$. For each caption $y^{(i)}$, its last hidden state $h_T^{(i)}$ is extracted and projected into a diffusion condition $c^{(i)} = g(h_T^{(i)})$. This condition $c^{(i)}$ is then used by the frozen diffusion transformer $p_\theta$ to synthesize a reconstructed image $\tilde{x}^{(i)}$. The LLM is updated using the GRPO objective, with the probability ratio $r_t^{(i)}(\phi) = \frac{\pi_\phi(y_t^{(i)} | x, y_{<t}^{(i)})}{\pi_{\phi_{old}}(y_t^{(i)} | x, y_{<t}^{(i)})}$. This process guides the LLM to generate hidden representations that maximize the diffusion model's reconstruction quality.
- **UMM-2 (Fully AR-based):** In this architecture (e.g., Janus-Pro, X-Omni), the same autoregressive model performs both I2T and T2I operations. Unified-GRPO is applied similarly, but the decoder $D_\phi$ is also autoregressive rather than diffusion-based. The RL pipeline is $x \xrightarrow{\pi_\phi} y \xrightarrow{\pi_\phi} \tilde{x}$, with the same reconstruction reward. This allows the fully autoregressive model to co-evolve its understanding and generation abilities within a unified token space.

**Training Details (from Supplementary Material):**
During the RL phase, only the LLM module is fine-tuned, while visual encoders/decoders (e.g., Diffusion Transformer, CLIP Visual Encoder) are kept frozen. This decision was based on empirical observations that updating the visual encoder during RL training could lead to instability and degradation in image quality. A pre-trained CLIP model acts as the reward model, providing semantic similarity scores. The training parameters include a learning rate of $1 \times 10^{-6}$, a batch size of 1, 4 sampled images per prompt for policy gradient estimation, a KL regularization coefficient of $1 \times 10^{-6}$, and an LLM temperature of 1.0. The LLM is not explicitly trained to generate descriptive captions, but this emerges as a result of optimizing for reconstruction quality.

#### 3.2. Unified-Bench: A Benchmark for Unified Models

To evaluate the unification achieved, the authors introduce **Unified-Bench**, a reconstruction-based benchmark.

- **Motivation:** Traditional evaluation metrics for image realism or caption fidelity alone do not fully assess whether a system is truly unified. Unified-Bench aims to directly test if the semantics extracted during understanding are sufficient for faithful regeneration, and if regeneration validates the completeness of understanding.
- **Protocol-1: Evaluation of Unified Score from Reconstruction Similarity:**
 - A set of 100 diverse source images is used.
 - The UMM generates a caption from an input image (I2T).
 - The same UMM then synthesizes an image from its own generated caption (T2I).
 - A "Unified-Score" is computed based on the semantic similarity between the original source image and the reconstructed image. This similarity is measured using four distinct vision backbones: CLIP, LongCLIP, DINO-v2, and DINO-v3, and an overall summary score is reported.
- **Protocol-2: Quality Evaluation of Model's Output Caption for Reconstruction:**
 - This protocol evaluates the "friendliness" of the generated captions for image generation.
 - Captions produced by the understanding model are compared pairwise against various baselines.
 - Four commercial LLM judges (Claude-4.1, GPT-4o, Grok-4, and o4-mini) are employed to assess caption quality.
 - The primary metric is the pairwise winning rate (%), indicating the percentage of times the model's caption is preferred over baselines.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrate that the proposed Unified-GRPO method, built upon the auto-encoder perspective, yields improvements across various aspects of multimodal understanding, generation, and their unification.

#### 4.1. Ablation Study on Unified-GRPO

Applying Unified-GRPO to two representative UMM architectures, UniWorld and Janus-Pro, consistently improved performance (Table 1). The most substantial gains were observed in generation and unification metrics, with improvements of 4-5% on generation benchmarks (GenEval, DPGBench) and over 6% on the Unified-Score. Understanding tasks showed modest gains overall, though specific fine-grained perceptual abilities were notably enhanced. The UniWorld-based model was selected as the primary backbone for subsequent experiments due to its stronger baseline performance. The supplementary material indicates that keeping the visual encoder frozen during fine-tuning was crucial for stability, as unfreezing it led to image quality degradation (Figure 9).

#### 4.2. Unification Evaluation

On the proposed Unified-Bench, the Unified Auto-Encoder (UAE) achieved the highest Overall unified score (86.09), outperforming GPT-4o-Image (85.95) (Table 2). Specifically, UAE showed top results on CLIP (90.50), DINO-v2 (81.98), and DINO-v3 (77.54), and comparable performance on LongCLIP (94.35 vs. 94.37). These consistent improvements across both contrastive (CLIP-family) and self-supervised (DINO-family) features suggest that UAE preserves layout- and texture-level semantics, leading to more faithful reconstructions. An extended evaluation on 2000 images confirmed the effectiveness of the method, maintaining a lead over baselines (Table 10).

#### 4.3. Text-to-Image Generation Evaluation

UAE demonstrated strong capabilities on standard and challenging T2I benchmarks:

- **GenEval (Table 4):** Without LLM rewriting, UAE achieved the best Overall score among unified models (0.86). It led in Counting (0.84) and Color attribution (0.79), co-led in Colors (0.90), and was competitive in other categories. With LLM rewriting, UAE achieved an overall score of 0.89, representing state-of-the-art performance in image generation. Qualitative results (Figure 4, 5, 6) illustrate the ability to recover detailed semantics and adhere to complex instructions.
- **GenEval++ (Table 5):** This benchmark tests compositional understanding with three or more objects and complex attributes. UAE achieved the best Overall score (0.475), leading on Color/Count (0.550) and Pos/Count (0.450), and showing runner-up performance on Color/Pos (0.550) and Multi-Count (0.400). This indicates significant improvement in complex instruction following and multi-attribute binding.
- **DPG-Bench (Table 9):** UAE secured top scores on Entity (91.43), Attribute (91.49), and Relation (92.07), and ranked second overall (84.74). This suggests advantages in faithful entity grounding and relational handling in generation.

#### 4.4. Image-to-Text Understanding Evaluation

Post-training, the encoder module exhibited improved image-to-text capabilities:

- **Caption Quality (Table 6):** Evaluated by commercial LLM judges (e.g., GPT-4o, Claude-4.1), UAE's understanding model (Qwen-2.5-VL-3B baseline) achieved high average win rates against various opponents (e.g., 94.7% vs. Show-o, 71.4% vs. OmniGen2), and remained competitive with GPT-4o (47.2%). This indicates improvements in completeness, attribute binding, and relational/spatial fidelity in captions.
- **Caption Quality for Reconstruction (Table 3):** Under the Unified-Bench "caption $\to$ generate $\to$ compare" protocol, captions produced by UAE resulted in the highest reconstruction similarity across all four backbones (Overall 86.09). This demonstrates that UAE's generated captions are more suitable for high-fidelity image generation.
- **MMT-Bench (Table 7 & 8):** On high-level meta-tasks (Table 7), the overall understanding score showed only marginal improvement (+0.2%). However, focusing on fine-grained visual recognition sub-tasks (Table 8) revealed substantial benefits: large absolute gains in Small Object Detection (+40.0%) and Person Re-ID (+60.0%), leading to a +24.4% increase in the fine-grained overall score. These results indicate that generation-augmented training can notably enhance fine-grained visual perception without harming overall understanding.
 - **Case Studies (Figure 7):** Qualitative examples confirmed improved ability to spot subtle differences and perform accurate visual object grounding, demonstrating richer and more precise semantic extraction.
- **OCR/DU Performance:** An initial decrease in OCR/DU performance was observed (Table 7). However, further analysis (Table 11 in Supplementary) attributed this to a decoder-side text-rendering bottleneck in the diffusion model (SD3). When SD3 was replaced with a diffusion model known for stronger text rendering (Qwen-Image), the performance drop disappeared, indicating that the limitation was in the generation component's ability to faithfully render text, rather than the encoder's understanding.

#### 4.5. Training Dynamics

The training dynamics of the reconstruction-oriented RL stage (Figure 8) showed that the reward score steadily increased as the policy learned to generate captions that more faithfully preserved visual information. Concurrently, the caption length gradually grew, indicating that the model produced richer and more detailed textual descriptions. These trends suggest that the RL optimization encouraged the model to encode progressively more complete image information into text, providing maximally informative representations for the downstream decoder.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work proposes a significant conceptual and methodological advancement in the development of unified multimodal models. By formalizing image-to-text (I2T) understanding and text-to-image (T2I) generation as an auto-encoding process, with text serving as a shared intermediate latent representation, the paper offers a principled framework for connecting these two traditionally isolated tasks.

The introduction of Unified-GRPO, a reinforcement-learning-based post-training method, is a key contribution. It demonstrates that by optimizing for reconstructive rewards – maximizing the semantic consistency between input and reconstructed images – a synergistic feedback loop can be established. This mechanism enables mutual enhancement, where improved semantic encoding benefits generation, and more faithful generation strengthens fine-grained visual perception.

The empirical results highlight several areas of impact:

- **Enhanced Fine-Grained Perception:** The most notable impact on understanding is the significant improvement in fine-grained visual perception tasks, such as small object detection and person re-identification. This suggests that the reconstructive objective forces the encoder to extract more precise and comprehensive visual semantics, which is critical for tasks requiring subtle differentiation and visual grounding.
- **Improved Generation Fidelity and Instruction Following:** On the generation side, the method consistently leads to higher-fidelity images and notably enhanced capabilities in complex instruction following, particularly in multi-attribute and compositional scenarios. This indicates that richer, more accurate textual descriptions from the understanding module empower the generation module to produce more precise and controllable outputs.
- **Broader Applicability:** Unified-GRPO is shown to be applicable to different UMM architectures (e.g., hybrid AR-Diffusion and fully AR-based systems), suggesting its potential as a general post-training strategy for various encoder-decoder multimodal systems.
- **Unified Evaluation Paradigm:** The proposed Unified-Bench provides a novel and more direct way to evaluate the "unification" of models, moving beyond isolated performance metrics to assess the coherence of information flow between understanding and generation.

The findings challenge the notion that multimodal tasks must be optimized independently or that joint training inevitably leads to performance degradation. Instead, the research suggests that a carefully designed reconstructive objective can foster a mutually reinforcing relationship. This paradigm could pave the way for more coherent, robust, and truly synergistic multimodal learning systems, potentially impacting areas such as advanced image editing, multimodal dialogue systems, and even extending to other modalities like audio and video where similar reconstructive principles could apply. While limitations regarding text rendering in generation models are acknowledged, they also open avenues for future research to further refine the generative components within this unified framework.
