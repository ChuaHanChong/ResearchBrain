# Learning Rate Transfer in Normalized Transformers

> [!summary] Summary
> A new parametrization named "νGPT" for Normalized Transformers (nGPT) introduces robust learning rate transfer capabilities across model width, depth, and token horizon. This modification to nGPT's scaling rules, informed by empirical alignment exponent measurements, significantly reduces the computational burden of hyperparameter tuning for large-scale models.

## Problem

- Training large neural networks requires extensive and costly hyperparameter (HP) search, which is impractical for modern LLMs.
- The existing Normalized Transformer (nGPT) architecture, despite its inherent stability, did not exhibit learning rate transfer across model width and token horizon.
- Debates persist regarding optimal scaling laws for learning rates across different model dimensions (e.g., depth) and the validity of assumptions in HP transfer theories like Maximal Update Parametrization (μP).

## Method

- **νGPT** modifies ==nGPT scaling rules== based on two desiderata: ==stability at initialization== and ==stable non-trivial feature learning== during training.
- Leverages empirical measurements of ==alignment exponents (α, ω, ν)== within nGPT to inform learning rate and initialization scaling.
- Key modifications: global LR scales as ==(iteration count)^(-1/3)==, hidden/unembedding LRs as ==(width)^(-3/4)==, with specific depth/width initialization values.

## Results

- **νGPT** achieves consistent learning rate transfer across a **5x range** of model widths (8 to 40 heads), outperforming nGPT.
- Demonstrates good LR transfer over a **16x range** of model depths (**8 to 128 layers**) with improved stability at larger depths.
- Optimal LR empirically decreases proportionally to **(iteration count)^(-1/3)** across model sizes and compute budgets.

## Takeaways

> - Empirical measurements revealed that ωhidden=ωoutput=1/2\omega_{hidden} = \omega_{output} = 1/2ωhidden=ωoutput=1/2 in nGPT, which contradicts the ωoutput=1\omega_{output} = 1ωoutput=1 assumption often used in classical μP derivations.
> - A 'mid alignment' assumption with max⁡{α,ν}=3/4\max\{α, ν\} = 3/4max{α,ν}=3/4 empirically guided the optimal learning rate scaling for width transfer in nGPT.
> - Aggressive depth-scaling of hidden layer learning rates, as proposed in some Depth-μP variants, was found to break transfer in nGPT, suggesting its trainable scale parameters inherently handle depth dynamics differently.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of large neural networks, particularly Transformer models, is heavily reliant on effective hyperparameter optimization. As models scale in size (width, depth, token horizon), the computational cost of finding optimal hyperparameters (HPs) through direct search becomes prohibitive. This has led to the emergence of "hyperparameter transfer" techniques, which aim to extrapolate optimal HPs from smaller, more tractable models to larger ones.

A significant body of work in this area originated from the Tensor Programs framework, which led to the Maximal Update Parametrization (μP) (Yang and Hu, 2021). μP proposed specific scaling rules for learning rates and initialization variances that allowed for HP transfer across model width, largely by ensuring that all preactivations and their updates remained stable (order-1) regardless of model size. Subsequent work has explored extensions to depth (Depth-μP, CompleteP) and debated underlying theoretical assumptions, such as alignment between weight changes and hidden states (Everett et al., 2024; Kosson et al., 2026).

This paper focuses on the Normalized Transformer, or nGPT (Loshchilov et al., 2025), a recently introduced architecture designed to improve training speed and stability by extensively using normalization. A key feature of nGPT is its ability to train without weight decay or learning rate warmup, which can simplify optimization. Despite having some hyperparameters explicitly scaled with model size, the authors observe that the original nGPT implementation does not inherently exhibit learning rate transfer across model width and token horizon.

This research aims to bridge the gap between the benefits of the nGPT architecture and the practical necessity of HP transfer. By revisiting and modifying the μP approach, informed by empirical observations of "alignment exponents" in nGPT, the authors propose a new parameterization, νGPT. This work positions itself as an advancement in making highly normalized and efficient Transformer architectures more amenable to large-scale deployment by providing reliable HP scaling rules. It contributes to the ongoing discussion about the theoretical underpinnings and practical implementation of HP transfer in diverse neural network architectures.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a novel parameterization for Normalized Transformers (nGPT) that facilitates effective learning rate transfer across various model scales. The authors introduce this new parameterization, termed νGPT.

The motivation for this work stems from several factors:

1. **Practical Challenge of Scaling Transformers:** As neural networks, particularly Transformers, grow in size to achieve higher performance, the process of tuning optimization hyperparameters (e.g., learning rate, batch size) becomes increasingly complex and computationally expensive. Direct exhaustive searches for optimal hyperparameters at large scales are often impractical due to the vast search space and high resource demands.
2. **Need for Hyperparameter Transfer:** To mitigate the computational burden, techniques for hyperparameter transfer are crucial. These techniques enable practitioners to derive optimal hyperparameters for large models by extrapolating from sweeps conducted on smaller models and limited token horizons. This significantly reduces the time and resources required for training large-scale models.
3. **Limitations of Existing nGPT:** The Normalized Transformer (nGPT) (Loshchilov et al., 2025) offers notable advantages, including impressive training speedups and the elimination of the need for weight decay or learning rate warmup. These characteristics make nGPT an appealing architecture for large language model development. However, despite possessing some hyperparameters that are designed to scale with model size, the authors empirically observe that the baseline nGPT architecture does not inherently exhibit consistent learning rate transfer across critical scaling dimensions such as model width (dimension) and token horizon. This absence of transferability hinders its practical scalability.
4. **Refining μP for Normalized Architectures:** The Maximal Update Parametrization (μP) approach (Yang and Hu, 2021) has provided a foundational framework for hyperparameter transfer in un-normalized neural networks. The authors hypothesize that while μP offers valuable insights, its underlying assumptions might not perfectly hold in the highly normalized nGPT setting. By leveraging recent theoretical advancements like alignment exponents (Everett et al., 2024) and combining them with extensive numerical experimentation, the researchers aim to adapt and refine the μP methodology specifically for nGPT. This allows for a more principled and empirically grounded derivation of scaling rules tailored to the nGPT architecture.
5. **Achieving Comprehensive Transfer:** The ultimate motivation is to derive a parameterization (νGPT) that exhibits robust learning rate transfer across all three key scaling axes: model width (number of heads/model dimension), depth (number of layers), and token horizon (total training steps/tokens processed). This comprehensive transferability is critical for efficiently and effectively scaling nGPT models to state-of-the-art sizes.

In summary, the detailed objective is to rectify the observed lack of learning rate transfer in nGPT by applying a modified μP approach, informed by empirical measurement of alignment exponents, to create νGPT—a parameterization that enables stable and performant learning rate transfer across width, depth, and token horizon without sacrificing baseline performance.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed in this research combines theoretical derivations, empirically driven heuristics, and extensive experimental validation. The core approach involves reparameterizing the Normalized Transformer (nGPT) to enable learning rate transfer, resulting in the proposed νGPT.

#### 3.1. Normalized Transformer (nGPT) Definition

The foundation of this work is the nGPT architecture (Loshchilov et al., 2025). Key distinguishing features of nGPT, as described in the paper, include:
- **ℓ2-Normalization (Norm(·)):** Standard RMSNorm or LayerNorm is replaced with a simple ℓ2-normalization (Norm(y) := y/∥y∥), which has no trainable parameters.
- **Residual-Post-Normalization:** Normalization is applied at the output of Attention and MLP blocks, rather than at the input.
- **Linear Interpolation Function (LERP) for Residuals:** Residual connections use trainable vectors α_A and α_M (constrained to be non-negative) for component-wise rescaling, instead of simple additions. These α parameters have initial values (α_A,init, α_M,init) and scales (α_A,scale, α_M,scale) that affect their learning rates.
- **Internal Normalization:** Keys and queries within the self-attention block are also ℓ2-normalized, with trainable scaling factors (s_qk). Similarly, weights in the MLP (W_ν, W_u) also have trainable scaling factors (s_ν, s_u).
- **Weight Normalization:** Columns of embedding/unembedding matrices and rows/columns of linear transformation matrices are normalized before each training step.
- **No Weight Decay or Warmup:** nGPT is designed to perform well without these common optimization techniques.

#### 3.2. Reparameterization for νGPT

The authors define νGPT by modifying specific hyperparameters and learning rate scalings based on two desiderata for HP transfer:
1. **Stability at Initialization:** Preactivations and logits should neither explode nor become trivial (i.e., ∥hℓn∥ = Θ(1) and ∥zn∥ = O(1)). This is largely handled by nGPT's inherent normalization.
2. **Stable Non-trivial Feature Learning:** Logits and preactivations should evolve non-trivially without blowing up (i.e., ∥∆hℓn∥ = Θ(1) and ∥∆zn∥ = Θ(1)), with learning rates set to the maximal possible scale without violating this.

#### 3.2.1. Width Corrections (m_width)

This is a primary focus, leveraging the concept of "alignment exponents" (Definition 3.1) from Everett et al. (2024). These exponents (α, ω, ν) quantify how changes in weights (∆W), activations (h), and their product (∆Wh, W∆h, ∆W∆h) scale with model dimension (d_model).
- **Empirical Alignment Assumption:** The authors empirically measure these exponents in nGPT. They observe that ω_hidden = ω_output = 1/2, contradicting the ω_output = 1 assumption of classical μP. Based on empirical analysis, particularly weighting exponents by loss decrease (Figure 5), they adopt a "mid alignment assumption" where max{α_hidden, ν_hidden} = max{α_output, ν_output} = 3/4.
- **"SignGD-like" Scaling:** Adam updates are assumed to behave like signGD, meaning each weight component's change is approximately ±η.
- **Derivations:**
 - **Embedding Learning Rate (η_input):** To ensure ∥∆E_input x_n∥ = Θ(1), η_input is scaled as m_width^-1/2 (proportional to d_model^-1/2).
 - **Hidden Layer Learning Rate (η_hidden):** To maintain ∥∆(Wh_n)∥ = Θ(1) in MLP and Attention blocks, η_hidden is scaled as m_width^-3/4 (proportional to d_model^-3/4), derived from the mid-alignment assumption.
 - **Unembedding Learning Rate (η_output):** Similarly, η_output is scaled as m_width^-3/4.
 - **Output Scaler (s_z,init):** To ensure logits are Θ(1) at initialization (consistent with ω_output = 1/2), s_z,init is scaled as m_width^1/2 (proportional to d_model^1/2).
 - **Fixed Scalers (α_A,scale, α_M,scale, s_qk,scale, s_z,scale):** These are kept constant at 0.03 instead of d_model^-1/2 as in original nGPT.

#### 3.2.2. Depth Corrections (m_depth)

- **Heuristic from Residual Networks:** Drawing from literature on residual networks (h_ℓ+1 = h_ℓ + n_layers^-α_depth F_ℓ(h_ℓ)), where n_layers^-α_depth F_ℓ(h_ℓ) should scale as 1/n_layers to prevent blowup.
- **νGPT Specifics:** Instead of scaling η_hidden with depth, the authors scale the initial values of the LERP trainable parameters α_A,init and α_M,init as 0.05 * m_depth^-1 (proportional to n_layers^-1). This corresponds to an implicit α_depth = 1.
- **Empirical Observation:** Crucially, empirical experiments show that scaling η_hidden with depth (e.g., m_depth^-1/2 as in Depth-μP) *breaks* transfer, and η_hidden should not be depth-scaled in νGPT.

#### 3.2.3. Token Horizon Corrections (m_data)

- **Empirical Power Law:** Based on prior work (Bjorck et al., 2025) and their own power-law fits (Figure 9), the optimal global learning rate is observed to decrease as (iteration count)^-1/3.
- **νGPT Scaling:** The global learning rate (η_global) is scaled by m_data^-1/3.

#### 3.3. Experimental Setup

- **Dataset:** FineWeb-Edu, sequence length 4096, batch size 64.
- **Tokenizer:** OLMo 2 (vocabulary size 100,352).
- **Optimization:** Adam (AdamW with weight decay 0.0) with β1=0.9, β2=0.95, ϵ=10^-16, no warmup, cosine learning rate decay to 10% of peak.
- **Base Model:** 10 layers, 10 heads (d_model is derived from head dimension and number of heads).
- **Scaling Experiments:**
 - Fixed iteration count (80,000 steps, ~21B tokens).
 - "Compute-optimal" 20 tokens per parameter.
- **Evaluation:** Validation loss, learning rate sensitivity, and direct measurement of alignment exponents. Experiments are averaged over three initialization seeds where noted.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yields several findings regarding learning rate transfer in Normalized Transformers:

#### 4.1. Learning Rate Transfer in νGPT

- **Width Transfer:** The baseline nGPT configuration does not exhibit learning rate transfer across model width (specifically, scaling the number of heads while keeping head dimension and depth fixed) (Figure 2a). In contrast, the proposed νGPT parametrization demonstrates consistent learning rate transfer across model width (Figure 2b).
- **Depth Transfer:** Interestingly, the baseline nGPT already shows a decent degree of learning rate transfer across depth (scaling the number of layers) at a fixed iteration count, although models with the highest depth exhibit some instability (Figure 3a). νGPT also exhibits good depth transfer and shows lower learning rate sensitivity, contributing to increased stability in deep models (Figure 3b).
- **Token Horizon Transfer:** The optimal learning rate in nGPT (without explicit token count corrections) is found to scale approximately as (iteration count)^-1/3 (Figure 9, 15). This empirical finding leads to the inclusion of a global learning rate multiplier of m_data^-1/3 in νGPT, which enables transfer across token horizons.
- **Combined Transfer:** νGPT achieves learning rate transfer across combinations of depth, width, and token horizon, with performance that is comparable to or slightly better than a well-tuned original nGPT baseline (Figure 1).

#### 4.2. Empirical Validation of Alignment Exponents

- Measurements of alignment exponents (α, ω, ν, as defined in Definition 3.1) in νGPT models (Figures 4, 5, 6, 12, 13, 14) provide empirical grounding for the proposed scaling rules.
- **ω Exponents:** The exponents ω_hidden and ω_output are consistently observed to be approximately 1/2. This finding is significant as it contradicts the ω_output = 1 assumption often used in classical μP derivations, which would lead to logits becoming negligible at initialization. The ω=1/2 observation supports parametrizations where logits are order-1 from initialization.
- **α and ν Exponents:** The α and ν exponents vary significantly during training, starting higher and settling closer to 0.5-0.6. When weighted by loss decrease (giving more importance to early training phases), these exponents average around 3/4. This empirical observation supports the "mid alignment assumption" (max{α, ν} = 3/4) adopted for νGPT's width corrections.

#### 4.3. Comparison with μP-style Corrections

- When compared to a μP-style parameterization (referred to as "CompleteP" in the paper, which assumes full alignment), νGPT exhibits somewhat better learning rate transfer over width (Figure 7). This suggests that incorporating empirically measured alignment exponents provides more accurate scaling rules for nGPT than generalized μP assumptions.
- The choice of ω_output = 1/2 allows for logits to be order-1 at initialization, unlike classical μP where they are O(d_model^-1/2). Ablations (Figures 17, 18) indicate that this specific logits scaling choice (Θ(1) at init vs. Θ(d_model^-1/2) at init) does not significantly impact overall performance when η_output is tuned, but the Θ(1) scaling aligns better with the observed ω_output = 1/2.

#### 4.4. Depth Scaling Details

- While the original nGPT already shows reasonable depth transfer, a "Depth-μP" style correction (scaling η_hidden with m_depth^-1/2) is found to *break* learning rate transfer over depth in this architecture (Figure 8a). This indicates that the optimal scaling for hidden learning rates in nGPT is independent of depth.
- Empirical analysis of the trainable α_A and α_M components in the baseline nGPT suggests that their average values at the end of training decrease roughly as n_layers^-0.5 (Figure 10), implying an implicit α_depth = 1/2 behavior within the trainable LERP parameters.

#### 4.5. Hyperparameter Sensitivity

- The performance of both baseline nGPT and νGPT is sensitive to the learning rate applied to unembedding weights (η_output). Tuning the ratio of η_output to other learning rates can yield performance improvements (Figure 16), suggesting that η_output might be too large by default. The authors propose multiplying η_output by a tuned coefficient (e.g., 2^-1) for better performance.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of large-scale neural network training, particularly for Transformer architectures, in several ways:

1. **Enabling Efficient Scaling of Normalized Transformers:** The primary significance lies in providing a robust parameterization (νGPT) that enables learning rate transfer across critical scaling dimensions (width, depth, token horizon) for the Normalized Transformer (nGPT). nGPT is an architecture known for its training speedups and reduced hyperparameter complexity (e.g., no need for weight decay or warmup). By addressing the lack of learning rate transfer in nGPT, this work makes the architecture more practical and accessible for training very large models, significantly reducing the computational cost and time associated with hyperparameter search.
2. **Refinement of Hyperparameter Transfer Theory:** The work refines existing hyperparameter transfer theories, such as μP, by integrating empirical observations from a modern, normalized architecture. The empirical measurement and validation of alignment exponents, particularly the consistent finding of ω_output = 1/2, challenge some foundational assumptions of classical μP and lead to a novel, empirically grounded parametrization. This contributes to a more accurate theoretical understanding of scaling laws in diverse neural network architectures.
3. **Empirical Grounding in Hyperparameter Research:** The methodology emphasizes rigorous empirical validation alongside theoretical heuristics. By systematically measuring alignment exponents, analyzing the behavior of trainable LERP parameters, and conducting extensive ablation studies across various scaling factors, the authors provide strong empirical evidence for their proposed scaling rules. This approach can serve as a model for future research in hyperparameter optimization.
4. **Addressing Architectural Nuances:** The study highlights specific architectural properties of nGPT, such as the behavior of its trainable LERP parameters and the observed optimal learning rate scaling for hidden layers with respect to depth. These findings suggest that scaling rules are not universally applicable and must be carefully tailored to the specific architectural design, fostering a deeper understanding of how normalization impacts model dynamics and transferability.
5. **Direction for Future Research:** The paper identifies several open theoretical questions, including a more comprehensive analytical understanding of depth-wise transfer in normalized models, the dynamics of weight decay when used in conjunction with these scaling laws, a more detailed analytical explanation for the breakdown of certain μP assumptions in specific models, and a theoretical derivation of the observed ~1/3 exponent for token count corrections. These questions provide clear avenues for future theoretical and empirical investigations in the field.

In summary, this research provides a practical solution for scaling nGPT models efficiently, while also advancing the theoretical and empirical understanding of hyperparameter transfer in highly normalized deep learning architectures. This is expected to accelerate the development and deployment of large-scale AI models.
