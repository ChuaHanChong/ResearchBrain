# Visual Representation Alignment for Multimodal Large Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from KAIST AI and collaborators introduced VIsual Representation ALignment (VIRAL), a novel regularization strategy that directly supervises the visual pathway in Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) by aligning internal representations with Vision Foundation Models. This approach prevents the loss of fine-grained visual information, leading to consistent performance improvements of up to 17.3% on specific vision-centric tasks and a 9.4% average gain over baselines like LLaVA-1.5.

## Problem

- Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) often underperform on vision-centric tasks demanding fine-grained visual understanding and are prone to visual hallucinations.
- The dominant text-only supervision paradigm for MLLMs indirectly supervises the visual pathway, leading to the loss of critical fine-grained visual information within the model.
- Internal visual representations in MLLMs exhibit "visual representation misalignment," diverging significantly from initial vision encoder features in deeper layers.

## Method

- A regularization strategy named ==VIsual Representation ALignment (VIRAL)== is introduced to directly supervise the internal visual pathway of MLLMs.
- VIRAL aligns the MLLM's intermediate visual representations with features extracted from a robust pre-trained ==Vision Foundation Model (VFM)== using a ==learnable projection== and a negative cosine similarity loss.
- This alignment is applied to specific intermediate layers of the MLLM, integrating rich, vision-centric knowledge from models like ==DINOv2== to prevent the loss of fine-grained visual details.

## Results

- VIRAL achieved consistent performance gains, with an average improvement of **9.4%** over LLaVA-1.5 baselines and up to **17.3%** on specific multimodal benchmarks.
- The approach substantially enhanced vision-centric reasoning, showing over **5%** gains on tasks like MMVP which demand fine-grained visual understanding.
- VIRAL proved robust across diverse vision encoders and LLM backbones, demonstrating broad applicability and achieving faster convergence with only a **3%** increase in training overhead.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - MLLMs' reliance on text-only supervision causes them to implicitly discard fine-grained visual details, leading to limitations in vision-centric tasks.
> - Directly supervising internal visual pathways by aligning them with features from Vision Foundation Models (VFMs) like DINOv2 can effectively preserve detailed visual semantics.
> - Applying alignment loss at specific intermediate layers (e.g., the 16th layer of LLaVA-1.5) is critical for optimal visual information retention and processing within MLLMs.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) has been a significant breakthrough in AI, enabling models to process and reason with both text and visual inputs. Techniques like visual instruction tuning (e.g., LLaVA) have propelled MLLMs to impressive performance across a wide array of tasks. However, a persistent challenge remains: MLLMs often struggle with *vision-centric tasks* that demand fine-grained visual understanding, such as precise object counting, detailed spatial reasoning, or accurately identifying specific attributes within an image. This limitation manifests in phenomena like visual hallucination, where models confidently generate text that is inconsistent with the visual input.

Previous research efforts to mitigate these shortcomings have largely concentrated on improving the *input stage* of MLLMs. This includes adopting more powerful vision encoders (e.g., from CLIP to SigLIPv2) or designing more expressive vision-language projectors to deliver richer visual representations to the language model. While these advancements have yielded notable improvements, they are often constrained by scalability and efficiency, and fundamentally, they do not address how visual information is *processed and preserved* once it enters the language model.

This paper positions itself by identifying a crucial, yet under-explored, problem: the *internal visual pathway* within MLLMs is often under-supervised due to the prevailing text-only supervision paradigm. The authors hypothesize that MLLMs, when trained predominantly with a language-modeling objective, tend to prioritize visual details that immediately aid text prediction, inadvertently *discarding* other fine-grained visual information that is essential for deeper visual comprehension. This leads to a "visual representation misalignment," where internal visual features diverge significantly from the rich representations initially provided by the vision encoder.

This work builds upon recent studies that have begun to analyze the *internal information flow* in MLLMs, highlighting the critical role of intermediate layers in aggregating global context, capturing fine-grained spatial features, and integrating multimodal information. By focusing on explicitly regularizing these internal representations, this paper departs from purely input-centric or output-reconstruction-based approaches. Unlike methods that focus on endpoint supervision or reconstruction objectives (which excel at low-level fidelity but may fall short on higher-level semantics), this work emphasizes *semantic alignment* with robust, pre-trained Vision Foundation Models (VFMs). This novel perspective aims to directly address the degradation of fine-grained visual semantics during the MLLM's internal processing, thereby filling a significant gap in the current MLLM research landscape and pushing towards more robust and visually intelligent multimodal models.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation of this research stems from an identified limitation in the current training paradigm of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs):
- **Under-supervision of the Visual Pathway:** MLLMs are primarily fine-tuned with a language-modeling objective, meaning supervision signals are almost entirely concentrated on textual outputs. While visual tokens constitute a substantial part of the multimodal input, their learning is mediated indirectly through language. The authors hypothesize that this text-only supervision paradigm compels the model to retain only visual details immediately conducive to text prediction, leading to the gradual *discarding of other potentially useful, fine-grained visual information*.
- **Observed Visual Representation Misalignment:** To empirically validate their hypothesis, the authors conducted an experiment measuring the similarity between the internal visual representations of an MLLM (LLaVA) and those from its original vision encoder (CLIP). They observed a sharp drop in similarity in deeper layers, confirming that internal visual representations rapidly diverge from the encoder's rich input features. This misalignment, they argue, directly undermines the model's capacity for complex visual reasoning, such as object counting and spatial understanding.

Given this motivation, the **key objectives** of the paper are:
1. **To propose a novel regularization strategy, VIsual Representation ALignment (VIRAL),** that directly supervises the visual pathway in MLLMs.
2. **To prevent the model from discarding fine-grained visual attributes** provided by the vision encoder during training.
3. **To enable the model to retain critical visual details** from its own vision encoder and simultaneously **complement additional visual knowledge** by aligning with features from stronger Vision Foundation Models (VFMs).
4. **To significantly enhance the MLLM's ability to reason over complex visual inputs,** particularly in vision-centric tasks and in mitigating visual hallucinations, while preserving general multimodal understanding capabilities.
5. **To provide a simple yet effective solution** that is broadly applicable across different vision encoders, LLM backbones, and model scales, without requiring substantial architectural changes.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology of this paper centers on identifying and rectifying the visual information loss within MLLMs through a targeted regularization strategy.

#### 3.1. Problem Identification and Empirical Validation

The authors begin by hypothesizing that existing MLLMs, trained predominantly with a text-only language modeling objective, inadvertently discard fine-grained visual details. This is because the visual pathway receives only indirect, language-mediated supervision. To validate this, they employ CKNNA (a metric for representational similarity) to measure the alignment between the internal visual representations of LLaVA and the original visual features extracted by its CLIP vision encoder. Their empirical findings confirm a significant drop in similarity in deeper layers, termed "visual representation misalignment," indicating a loss of rich visual information. Interestingly, they note a slight increase or attenuation of this drop in the *middle layers*, suggesting these layers are crucial for retaining visual information.

#### 3.2. Initial Exploration of Visual Information Preservation

Before introducing VIRAL, the authors explore two preliminary approaches to preserving visual information:
1. **Residual Connection (Post-projection):** They attempt to re-inject the projected visual features (P_ϕ(z)) into an intermediate layer (specifically, the 16th layer of a 32-layer LLM) via a residual path. This simple addition, trained solely with the original text loss, shows improved alignment and general performance, supporting the hypothesis that retaining encoder-aligned visual information is beneficial. However, concerns about the projector's ability to fully preserve original information remain.
2. **Residual Connection (Pre-projection):** A variant where raw encoder features (z) are re-injected into an intermediate layer via a lightweight adapter. This approach performs worse than the baseline, suggesting that raw features, unaligned with the language space, can disrupt existing vision-language alignment.

#### 3.3. Visual Representation Alignment (VIRAL)

Motivated by the limitations of residual connections and the desire for a more principled approach, the authors propose **VIsual Representation ALignment (VIRAL)**.
- **Core Idea:** Instead of merely re-injecting features, VIRAL explicitly *aligns* the MLLM's internal visual representations with features from a powerful, pre-trained **Vision Foundation Model (VFM)**. This moves beyond just retaining the original encoder's features to integrating richer, vision-centric knowledge from state-of-the-art visual models.
- **Formalization:**
 - Let `e_img_ℓ` be the visual representations at the ℓ-th layer of the MLLM.
 - Let `E(·)` be a pre-trained VFM encoder, which produces target features `y = E(I)` for an input image `I`.
 - A learnable projection `P_π(·)` maps `e_img_ℓ` into the VFM feature space.
 - The **Visual Representation Alignment (L_VRA) loss** is defined as the negative cosine similarity between `P_π(e_img_ℓ,i)` and `y_i`, encouraging alignment:
 `L_VRA = - (1/N) Σ sim(P_π(e_img_ℓ,i), y_i)` (gradients do not flow into `y`).
 - The total objective combines the standard language modeling loss (`L_LM`) with `L_VRA` using a weighting factor `λ`:
 `L_total = L_LM + λ L_VRA`.
- **Implementation Details:**
 - **Base Model:** LLaVA-1.5, utilizing Vicuna-1.5 as the LLM and CLIP as the vision encoder. LoRA is used for efficient fine-tuning.
 - **VFM Selection:** DINOv2 is chosen as the default VFM due to its demonstrated effectiveness in ablation studies. Other VFMs like SAM, DAv2, and RADIOv2.5 were also explored.
 - **Projection Module:** `P_π` is a lightweight three-layer MLP with SiLU activations.
 - **Alignment Layer:** The alignment loss is applied to the 16th layer of the 32-layer LLM, based on ablation studies identifying this as an optimal layer for visual information processing.
 - **Data:** The original LLaVA-665K dataset is used.
 - **Training:** The overall process involves optimizing both the LLM and the vision-language projector (and `P_π`) during visual instruction tuning.

The methodology is characterized by its empirical foundation, careful step-by-step validation, and the principled introduction of a novel regularization term that leverages strong pre-trained visual models to enhance the internal visual processing of MLLMs.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research paper presents a compelling set of findings that consistently support the effectiveness of the VIRAL strategy:

- **Consistent Performance Improvements Across Benchmarks:** VIRAL consistently outperforms baseline MLLMs (LLaVA-1.5) across all widely adopted multimodal benchmarks. The gains are substantial, reaching up to 17.3% on specific tasks and an impressive average improvement of 9.4% over the baseline. This includes significant boosts on vision-centric tasks (CV-Bench2D, MMVP, What's Up), hallucination detection (POPE), and general multimodal understanding (MME, MMStar).

- **Enhanced Vision-Centric Capabilities:** The most pronounced improvements are observed in tasks requiring fine-grained visual understanding, such as object counting and spatial reasoning. For instance, on MMVP (which targets cases where CLIP-like features underperform), VIRAL achieves gains of 5.13% with a CLIP encoder and 4.89% with a SigLIPv2 encoder, directly addressing a known weakness of MLLMs. This validates the core hypothesis that preserving detailed visual attributes is crucial for these tasks.

- **Robustness Across Different Architectures:**
 - **Vision Encoders:** The benefits of VIRAL extend beyond CLIP, showing consistent improvements even when using a stronger, self-supervised vision encoder like SigLIPv2, demonstrating that the gains stem from the alignment mechanism itself, not merely compensating for a weaker encoder.
 - **LLM Backbones:** VIRAL proves effective across different scales (Vicuna-1.5-7B vs. 13B) and alternative LLM backbones (Qwen2.5-7B), indicating its broad applicability as a general strategy for MLLM improvement.

- **Comprehensive Ablation Studies:**
 - **Optimal VFM Choice:** DINOv2 emerges as the most effective Vision Foundation Model for providing the alignment target, consistently outperforming other VFMs like CLIP, SAM, DAv2, and RADIO. This highlights the importance of leveraging VFMs with rich, vision-centric representations.
 - **Critical Alignment Layer:** The 16th layer of the 32-layer LLaVA-1.5 model is identified as the most effective target for applying the alignment loss. This supports prior findings about the importance of intermediate layers for fine-grained visual processing and confirms that strategic placement of supervision is more effective than uniform application.
 - **Alignment Objective:** A simple cosine similarity-based alignment loss yields better performance than a feature relation alignment objective (e.g., MSE on self-similarity matrices), favoring direct feature correspondence over structural distillation.

- **Attention Analysis:** Qualitative and quantitative analyses of attention maps reveal that VIRAL encourages more focused, semantically aligned text-to-image attention. Models trained with VIRAL exhibit consistently lower spatial entropy in their attention patterns, indicating more selective and meaningful attention to relevant image regions, especially at the aligned intermediate layer.

- **Robustness to Token Permutation:** Experiments on randomly permuting visual tokens demonstrate that VIRAL-trained models are more sensitive to spatial structure. They suffer larger performance drops compared to the baseline when visual token order is disrupted, confirming that VIRAL enables MLLMs to capture and exploit fine-grained spatial relationships more effectively.

- **Qualitative Results and Visualizations:** Qualitative comparisons showcase VIRAL's ability to correctly answer challenging counting and spatial reasoning questions where the baseline fails. PCA visualizations of internal visual representations demonstrate that VIRAL leads to more structured and semantically coherent embeddings at the 16th layer, indicating better preservation of critical visual details.

- **Training Efficiency:** Despite introducing an additional regularization term, VIRAL incurs only about a 3% overhead in total training time. Importantly, models trained with VIRAL achieve faster convergence and quickly surpass baseline performance within fewer training steps, suggesting improved scalability.

Overall, the findings strongly validate VIRAL as a powerful and widely applicable method for enhancing the visual understanding capabilities of MLLMs by directly addressing the issue of internal visual representation misalignment.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "Visual Representation Alignment for Multimodal Large Language Models" paper introduces a significant and timely contribution to the field, with several impactful implications:

- **Addresses a Core Limitation of MLLMs:** The paper pinpoints and effectively addresses a fundamental flaw in the prevailing text-only supervision paradigm for MLLMs – the loss of fine-grained visual information within the model's internal processing. This problem leads to common MLLM weaknesses like visual hallucination and poor performance on vision-centric tasks. By tackling this root cause, VIRAL paves the way for more reliable and accurate multimodal understanding.

- **Enhances Vision-Centric Reasoning:** A major impact is the substantial improvement in complex vision-centric tasks, including object counting and detailed spatial reasoning. These capabilities are crucial for real-world applications such as image analysis for scientific research, advanced robotics, medical imaging interpretation, and sophisticated visual question answering systems where precision matters.

- **Establishes a New Regularization Strategy:** VIRAL offers a "simple yet effective regularization strategy" that can be readily integrated into existing MLLM training pipelines. Its modular nature, only requiring a lightweight projection and alignment loss with a VFM, makes it highly practical and accessible for researchers and practitioners. This could become a standard practice in MLLM development.

- **Leverages the Power of Vision Foundation Models:** By effectively distilling knowledge from pre-trained Vision Foundation Models (VFMs) like DINOv2 into the internal representations of MLLMs, VIRAL acts as a bridge, enriching the MLLM's visual pathway with robust, vision-centric semantics. This synergistic approach maximizes the utility of powerful, specialized vision models for general-purpose MLLMs.

- **Broad Applicability and Scalability:** The demonstrated effectiveness of VIRAL across various vision encoders (CLIP, SigLIPv2), LLM backbones (Vicuna-1.5, Qwen2.5), and different scales (7B, 13B) underscores its generalizability. This suggests that the principle of visual representation alignment is a fundamental improvement that can benefit a wide range of current and future MLLM architectures. The observation that the problem of visual information loss persists even in advanced architectures like LLaVA-NeXT (with tiled images) further extends the potential impact of VIRAL's core idea.

- **Opens New Research Directions:** This work prompts further investigation into the internal mechanisms of MLLMs. Understanding *why* specific intermediate layers are optimal for alignment, and how different VFMs contribute distinct types of visual knowledge, could lead to more sophisticated and finely-tuned alignment strategies. It encourages a shift in focus from solely improving input representations to actively managing and enhancing the processing of visual information *within* the multimodal transformer.

- **Towards More Trustworthy AI:** By mitigating visual hallucinations and improving the accuracy of visually grounded responses, VIRAL contributes to building more trustworthy and explainable MLLMs. Models that accurately perceive and reason about visual inputs are less likely to generate factually incorrect or misleading information, which is critical for sensitive applications.

In essence, VIRAL offers a principled and effective method to infuse MLLMs with deeper visual intelligence, moving beyond superficial visual grounding towards a more robust and nuanced understanding of complex visual scenes. This represents a crucial step forward in the development of truly capable multimodal AI.
