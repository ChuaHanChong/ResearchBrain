# Exploring Transferability of Self-Supervised Learning by Task Conflict Calibration

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from the Institute of Software Chinese Academy of Sciences and the University of Chinese Academy of Sciences developed Task Conflict Calibration (TC²), a framework enhancing the transferability of self-supervised learning representations by addressing gradient conflicts in multi-task training. This approach consistently improves performance in unsupervised, semi-supervised, and various transfer learning scenarios, including notable gains in out-of-distribution generalization and visual understanding tasks.

## Problem

- Existing self-supervised learning (SSL) methods often struggle to transfer learned representations effectively to out-of-distribution data or diverse downstream tasks.
- A direct integration of meta-learning principles into SSL, while conceptually promising for transferability, leads to unstable and inconsistent performance due to conflicting gradients between tasks.
- Task-specific causal factors from other tasks can act as confounders, 'contaminating' the feature representations for a target task.

## Method

- The ==Task Conflict Calibration (TC²)== framework is introduced, which reformulates SSL training from an instance-centric to a ==task-centric perspective== by splitting mini-batches into distinct SSL tasks.
- TC² learns a union of ==global causal generative factors== and identifies task-specific relevant factors for each sample, using these to calibrate representations by filtering out irrelevant semantics.
- A ==two-stage bi-level optimization framework== integrates TC² into the SSL pipeline, optimizing both the factor extraction networks and the SSL model to ensure transferability and mitigate task conflicts.

## Results

- TC² consistently improved unsupervised classification performance across various datasets, with SimCLR+TC² achieving approximately **4.74%** higher Top-1 accuracy on ImageNet-100.
- In semi-supervised learning, Barlow Twins+TC² showed a **~2.9%** Top-1 accuracy improvement on ImageNet with **1%** labeled data, from **55.0%** to **57.9%**.
- The method enhanced transfer learning capabilities across diverse tasks, including up to a **~3.3%** boost in VOS (J-mean) for video tasks and significant gains in object detection/instance segmentation and out-of-distribution generalization (e.g., **~2.5%** average accuracy on PACS for MAE+TC²).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Empirical experiments demonstrate that a simple multi-task SSL setup, though yielding average improvements, suffers from significant instability due to frequent and detrimental 'task conflicts' arising from gradient misalignment.
> - Causal analysis using Structural Causal Models (SCMs) reveals that task-specific causal factors from other tasks act as confounders, degrading target task representation learning.
> - Calibrating sample representations to retain only task-relevant semantics, by learning and selectively applying causal generative factors, effectively alleviates these identified task conflicts.
