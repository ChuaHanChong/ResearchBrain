# Harness-Zero: Harness Distillation via Agent-as-Harness

> [!summary] Summary
> Harness-Zero distills an optimized agent harness into model weights so its gains persist under a minimal fixed harness. A harnessing agent, guided by a private reference harness adapted from the evolved one, reviews each student response and passes or minimally replaces it within the target action space; SFT on these reviewed trajectories lifts Qwen3.5-9B from 23.3% to 44.3% macro success, beating the 41.7% achieved with the harness attached.

## Problem

- Harness optimization methods like Meta-Harness improve the external scaffolding rather than the model, so their gains vanish once the specialized harness is removed at deployment.
- Because the best harness varies across domains, instances, and models, general agents must accept a suboptimal shared harness or route among many costly specialized harnesses.
- Trajectories collected under an optimized harness use different actions and information than the target harness, so direct imitation fails; prior internalization work covered only isolated mechanisms.

## Method

- ==Harness evolution and adaptation==: a ==skill-guided loop== evolves a DeepAgents-style harness of tools, middleware, skills, and memory, then adapts it into a ==private reference harness== of review rules and action recipes.
- **Agent-as-harness**: a ==harnessing agent== consults the reference harness, reviews each student proposal before execution, and either passes it or substitutes the ==smallest coherent full replacement== valid in the ==target harness==.
- ==Reviewed-trajectory SFT==: ==LoRA== fine-tuning on accepted responses, with leaked ==reviewer-perspective reasoning masked== from the loss, so the student deploys under a single-Bash mini-SWE-agent harness with no reviewer or reference harness.

## Results

- Without training, on frontier models agent-as-harness with the adapted reference averages **81.1%**, versus **78.1%** for the code-based meta-harness and **69.2%** for review with an empty reference.
- Distilled Qwen3.5-9B reaches **44.0%** on SpreadsheetBench, **58.9%** on AppWorld, and **30.0%** on USPTO under the minimal harness, trailing the mounted evolved harness only on USPTO (**38.0%**).
- USPTO ablations: SFT on teacher rollouts stays at **12%**, teacher-under-evolved-harness drops to **3%**, and oracle-answer review gives **15%**, versus **30%** for Harness-Zero; it recovers **82.3%** of **28** harness-exclusive behaviors.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Rollout success does not predict distillation value: oracle-answer corrections reach 98.6% collection success yet teach shortcuts, while lower-success procedural guidance transfers far better.
> - Supervision must live in the student's own action space and state; imitating trajectories from a richer harness teaches calls to tools that are unavailable and collapses performance.
> - Procedural habits like inspecting, verifying, and reloading internalize readily, but deep domain knowledge and executable tooling resist SFT, so harnesses shrink rather than disappear.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Position Within the Broader Research Landscape

#### 1.1. Agent harnesses as a source of capability

The paper addresses a growing area of research on **LLM agent harnesses**. A harness is the external software system that mediates interaction between a language model and an environment. It may provide tools, file systems, memory, skills, context management, middleware, workflow control, verification procedures, and persistent state.

Recent coding and research agents increasingly rely on such scaffolding. For example, a coding harness may combine shell access, file manipulation, subagents, background jobs, and persistent workspaces. Research-oriented harnesses may organize hypothesis generation, experimentation, and evidence gathering. Prior work has also investigated the automatic evolution of prompts, workflows, context, tools, and harness code.

However, harness improvements normally remain external to the model. A model may perform better when paired with a specialized harness, but the improvement disappears if the harness is removed or replaced. Since the appropriate harness can vary by domain, task, and model, system designers face a choice between using one general-purpose harness that may be suboptimal or maintaining multiple specialized harnesses and routing tasks among them. The latter increases orchestration, model-call, context, and tool-use costs.

#### 1.2. Relation to harness optimization and model training

The work is related to methods that jointly optimize models and harnesses, including harness–weight co-evolution, self-improving agents, and distillation from privileged workflows. Its distinction is that it aims to transfer **complete harness-induced behavior** into model parameters while retaining only a minimal target harness at deployment.

The central technical issue is that the optimized harness and the deployment harness may expose different tools and action spaces. Trajectories generated under the optimized harness therefore cannot necessarily be directly imitated by a model operating under the target harness. HARNESS-ZERO addresses this mismatch through an intermediate language-model-based reviewer called the **harnessing agent**.

### 2. Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective is to formulate and test **agent harness distillation**: using a specialized or evolved harness during training to generate supervision, then internalizing the behaviors it induces through fine-tuning so that the specialized harness can be removed during deployment.

The motivation has several parts. First, harness engineering can reveal useful procedures that are difficult for a base model to discover independently. These procedures may include inspecting files before editing, preserving pre-filled spreadsheet cells, validating chemical structures, enumerating alternatives, checking deliverables, or recovering from failed tool calls.

Second, external harnesses are costly and difficult to maintain. A deployment system with many specialized harnesses requires routing logic, separate prompts and tools, and potentially different compatibility adjustments for each model. Distillation could reduce this fragmentation by moving some of the capability into the model.

Third, direct imitation is difficult when the source and target harnesses differ. A source harness may provide specialized tools, enforce middleware rules, inject memory, or alter the model’s context. The target harness may offer only a general-purpose execution tool. The paper therefore seeks a method that translates source-harness guidance into demonstrations that are valid in the target harness’s native action space.

The study evaluates three main questions:

1. Can an agent acting as a harness transfer guidance across different harness action spaces?
2. Can a model retain the performance gains of an evolved harness after that harness is removed?
3. Which types of harness behavior are most readily internalized, and which remain difficult to distill?

### 3. Methodology and Approach

#### 3.1. Harness definitions

The framework uses three harnesses:

- **Target harness \(h\):** A fixed, minimal mini-SWE-agent-style harness with a system prompt and one Bash-based `execute` tool.
- **Evolved harness \(h^\star\):** A domain-specific harness optimized on training tasks. It may contain tools, middleware, skills, and memory.
- **Private reference harness \(K\):** An adaptation of \(h^\star\) for the harnessing agent. It provides review rules and action recipes but does not directly interact with the environment.

The target harness defines what actions the student can execute. The reference harness instead tells the harnessing agent when intervention is appropriate and how to express a correction using the target harness.

#### 3.2. Harness evolution and adaptation

For each benchmark domain, the authors first evolve a shared harness over three rounds. Failed training tasks are analyzed by a group of agents, and a main evolution agent consolidates reusable changes into tools, middleware, skills, or memory. The evolution process is designed to address classes of failures rather than individual task-specific errors.

The resulting components are adapted into \(K\). Tools in \(h^\star\) become **action recipes** explaining how to construct equivalent Bash or Python commands under \(h\). Student-side middleware becomes **review middleware** that detects corresponding failure conditions from the visible trajectory and privately alerts the harnessing agent. Skills and memory are converted from direct instructions for the student into diagnostic criteria and intervention guidance for the reviewer.

For example, a spreadsheet middleware component may reject a finish if pre-filled cells have been overwritten. The corresponding reference-harness rule cannot directly inspect the student’s sandbox. Instead, it checks whether the visible trajectory contains evidence of an input–output comparison. If not, the harnessing agent may replace the student’s proposed final response with a Bash command that performs the comparison through the target harness.

#### 3.3. Agent-as-harness trajectory collection

During data collection, the base student model operates under \(h\). At each turn it proposes a response, which may contain one execution command or a final answer. Before execution, the harnessing agent reviews the proposal using:

- The current student-visible context.
- The unexecuted student response.
- The private reference harness \(K\).
- Its own history from earlier reviews.

The harnessing agent chooses either **PASS** or **REPLACE**. A passed response is executed unchanged. A replacement must be a complete response in the student’s action space, normally a single Bash command or a final answer. The accepted response is executed through \(h\), and only its output becomes part of the student’s subsequent context.

The reviewer is constrained from inspecting hidden solutions, verifier feedback, or private environment state. If it needs additional evidence, it must generate a student-executable action whose result becomes visible in the trajectory. The reviewer is also instructed to make the smallest coherent correction, preserve the student’s intent where possible, and return control to the student rather than solving the whole task itself.

This differs from **code-as-harness**, in which the student model directly runs under \(h^\star\). Agent-as-harness instead uses a language model to translate the guidance of \(h^\star\) into target-compatible responses.

#### 3.4. Fine-tuning and deployment

The authors apply supervised fine-tuning to the accepted trajectories. Both unchanged student responses and harness-guided replacements are included. Reviewer-perspective reasoning is masked when it contains references to proposals, review decisions, or private harness components. Trajectories that expose private paths or metadata are filtered.

The distillation experiments use Qwen3.5-9B as the student. Training is performed separately for each domain using LoRA SFT for two epochs. The reported configuration uses rank 32, scaling parameter 32, batch size 8, a 65,536-token sequence length, a peak learning rate of \(2 \times 10^{-4}\), five percent warmup, and seed 42.

At deployment, only the fine-tuned model and the minimal target harness \(h\) remain. The evolved harness \(h^\star\), reference harness \(K\), and harnessing agent are removed.

#### 3.5. Evaluation setup

The experiments cover:

- **SpreadsheetBench Verified:** Spreadsheet manipulation, with 300 training tasks and 100 evaluation tasks.
- **AppWorld:** Interactive tool use across simulated applications, with 147 training tasks and 168 evaluation tasks grouped into scenarios.
- **USPTO Retrosynthesis:** Single-step precursor prediction, with 500 training tasks and 100 test tasks.

SpreadsheetBench and USPTO use task success or pass@1. AppWorld uses scenario goal completion. The authors evaluate frontier models for the training-free agent-as-harness comparison and Qwen3.5-9B for distillation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

#### 4.1. Agent-as-harness compared with code-as-harness

With evolved guidance available, agent-as-harness achieves an average score of **81.1%** across six benchmark–model settings, compared with **78.1%** for code-as-harness and **68.6%** for the minimal target harness alone. The advantage is particularly visible for DeepSeek-V4-Pro on SpreadsheetBench, AppWorld, and USPTO. For GPT-5.6 Sol, the methods are closer, with agent-as-harness matching or modestly exceeding the code-based harness.

An empty reference harness produces an average of only **69.2%**, indicating that the main benefit does not come simply from adding a second reviewing agent. The evolved procedural guidance in \(K\) is important.

The additional analysis shows that the advantage depends on model capability. Agent-as-harness outperforms code-as-harness for the stronger models but becomes inferior for weaker models. For Qwen3.6-35B-A3B on USPTO, the evolved code harness scores 48%, whereas agent-as-harness scores 36%. The weaker reviewer intervenes excessively, replacing 66% of reviewed steps, and its corrections are harmful overall.

#### 4.2. Distillation results

Under the minimal harness \(h\), the untrained Qwen3.5-9B base model achieves a macro-average of **23.3%** across the three domains. Adding the evolved harness raises this to **41.7%**. After HARNESS-ZERO distillation, the model reaches **44.3% under \(h\) alone**.

The domain-specific results are:

- SpreadsheetBench: base 31.0%, evolved harness 39.0%, distilled model 44.0%.
- AppWorld: base 26.8%, evolved harness 48.2%, distilled model 58.9%.
- USPTO: base 12.0%, evolved harness 38.0%, distilled model 30.0%.

Thus, the distilled model exceeds the base model with the evolved harness on the macro-average and on SpreadsheetBench and AppWorld. On USPTO, it improves substantially over the base model but does not fully recover the performance of the specialized harness.

The paper interprets this difference as evidence that procedural behavior is easier to internalize than deep domain knowledge. Spreadsheet and application tasks benefit from learnable workflows such as inspection, controlled modification, and verification. USPTO also depends on chemical priors, candidate-generation logic, and specialized executable validation, which are more difficult to transfer through limited SFT data.

#### 4.3. Ablation of supervision sources

The USPTO ablation shows that stronger demonstrations alone are not sufficient. Direct distillation from GPT-5.6 Sol produces a student score of only 12%, despite a 52% collection success rate. Teacher trajectories generated under \(h^\star\) produce 3%, and base-student trajectories under \(h^\star\) return to 12%.

Review with an empty reference harness reaches 11%. Review with oracle task answers reaches 15%, even though its collection success is 98.6%. HARNESS-ZERO, using \(K\) without access to answers, reaches **30%**. This suggests that supervision is most useful when it begins from the student’s own trajectory and introduces reusable corrections in the target action space. Direct access to answers may encourage shortcuts that do not generalize.

#### 4.4. Behavioral recovery

The authors identify 28 behaviors that appear under \(h^\star\) but not under \(h\) for the base model. HARNESS-ZERO recovers these behaviors at an average rate of **82.3%**. Recovered patterns include preserving spreadsheet examples, inspecting workbooks before editing, canonicalizing SMILES, validating chemical structures, checking heavy-atom coverage, avoiding repeated failed calls, and selecting correctly between action and answer completion.

Recovery is uneven. Spreadsheet behaviors such as inspecting before editing and reloading saved workbooks are recovered at or near 100%, while AppWorld behaviors such as complete pagination and reading back mutations are recovered at 40% and 50%, respectively. These measurements provide behavioral evidence beyond aggregate task success, although the detectors are designed by the authors and only include patterns with sufficient support.

### 5. Significance, Potential Impact, and Limitations

HARNESS-ZERO presents a framework for treating harness development as a source of training data rather than only as deployment-time infrastructure. Its main contribution is the response-boundary translation performed by agent-as-harness, which makes supervision compatible with a fixed action space despite differences between source and target harnesses.

If validated at larger scale, the approach could reduce dependence on collections of specialized deployment harnesses. Domain-specific tools and procedures could be used during training and then partially absorbed into shared model parameters. It could also provide a mechanism for accumulating improvements discovered through automated harness evolution.

The results do not show that harnesses can be eliminated entirely. The method still requires a minimal execution harness, and some mechanisms—especially context management, external memory, and privileged state—may not be expressible through ordinary student responses. Training-time review also adds cost: USPTO collection latency is reported as approximately 2.4 times that of the unreviewed agent. The method depends on a sufficiently capable harnessing model, and its performance is evaluated primarily with one 9B student model, separate domain-specific training sets, and single-run benchmark results. Deep domain knowledge remains only partially transferable.

Overall, the paper provides evidence that specialized harness behavior can be converted into model-level behavior when interventions are incremental, target-compatible, and grounded in the student’s own trajectory. Its broader implication is that future agent systems may combine minimal runtime scaffolding with models trained from the accumulated procedural knowledge of many evolved harnesses.
