# Imitation Learning via Simultaneous Optimization of Policies and Auxiliary Trajectories

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at NVIDIA, University of Washington, and Georgia Institute of Technology introduce Collocation for Demonstration Encoding (CoDE), an imitation learning method that improves policy learning from fixed offline demonstrations. It achieves this by simultaneously optimizing the policy and auxiliary trajectories, leading to reduced long-horizon rollout errors and enhanced data efficiency by up to an order of magnitude on robotic manipulation tasks.

## Problem

- Existing imitation learning methods like Behavior Cloning (BC) suffer from covariate shift, where compounded one-step errors cause policies to diverge from expert trajectories over long horizons.
- Many advanced imitation learning algorithms, such as DAgger, require impractical online interaction with an 'oracular expert' to correct policy deviations.
- Inconsistencies often exist between raw demonstrations (e.g., due to noise or non-smoothness) and the learner's policy class, leading to irreducible one-step errors even with optimal BC.

## Method

- The proposed CoDE (Collocation for Demonstration Encoding) framework jointly optimizes both the policy parameters and a set of parameterized auxiliary trajectories.
- CoDE minimizes two objectives: the deviation between original demonstrations and auxiliary trajectories, and the one-step prediction error of the policy with respect to the auxiliary trajectories.
- For acceleration-based robotic systems, auxiliary trajectories are derived from parameterized continuous-time position trajectories, and a single neural network generates all auxiliary trajectories for parameter efficiency.

## Results

- CoDE policies exhibited substantially smaller error growth over long horizons compared to BC and BC+NOISE, with state deviation remaining nearly flat, validating its ability to mitigate covariate shift.
- The method achieved high performance with an order of magnitude fewer training demonstrations; for instance, CoDE with 10 trajectories matched BC+NOISE with over 150 trajectories on a lifting task.
- On robotic manipulation tasks, CoDE yielded significantly lower mean target errors (e.g., 0.01m vs. 0.89m for BC) and drastically reduced collision rates (**5.3%** vs. **49.0%** for BC) in obstacle avoidance scenarios.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - CoDE theoretically bounds trajectory deviation, demonstrating that by finding auxiliary trajectories consistent with the policy class, the uniform one-step error can be significantly reduced compared to standard BC, mitigating exponential error growth.
> - Optimizing auxiliary trajectories creates a 'smoother' and policy-compatible version of the demonstrations, effectively providing an easier learning problem for the policy network.
> - The joint optimization balances faithfulness to original demonstrations with consistency for the policy, allowing the policy to learn robustly without online expert interaction or assumptions about expert actions.
