# Elucidating the SNR-t Bias of Diffusion Probabilistic Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers characterized a "Signal-to-Noise Ratio-timestep (SNR-t) bias" in Diffusion Probabilistic Models (DPMs), where denoising samples consistently exhibit lower SNR than ideal, leading to inaccurate noise predictions. A training-free differential correction method, Differential Correction in Wavelet domain (DCW), was developed to address this, achieving substantial FID score reductions, such as a 42.6% decrease for IDDPM on CIFAR-10 with 20 sampling steps.

## Problem

- Diffusion Probabilistic Models (DPMs) suffer from inherent biases that degrade the quality of generated outputs.
- A newly identified "Signal-to-Noise Ratio-timestep (SNR-t) bias" causes a misalignment between the SNR of denoising samples and their expected timesteps during the inference phase.
- This misalignment leads to inaccurate noise predictions by the DPM network, particularly an overestimation of noise in reverse-denoising samples, due to accumulated prediction and discretization errors.

## Method

- The researchers characterized the ==SNR-t bias== empirically and theoretically, demonstrating that reverse-denoising samples consistently possess a lower ==Signal-to-Noise Ratio (SNR)== than ideal forward samples at corresponding timesteps.
- A ==training-free differential correction== method was introduced, which applies a dynamic differential signal to guide biased samples toward ideal ones during each denoising step without incurring additional ==Neural Function Evaluations==.
- This correction was enhanced in the ==wavelet domain== (**DCW**) by decomposing samples into ==frequency subbands== and applying ==independently weighted differential corrections==, aligning with the coarse-to-fine denoising process of DPMs.

## Results

- The DCW method reduced the FID score for IDDPM on CIFAR-10 by **42.6%** (from **13.19** to **7.57**) for **20-step** generation and by **25%** (from **5.55** to **4.16**) for **50-step** generation, demonstrating improved generation quality.
- DCW consistently outperformed recent exposure bias correction methods and further enhanced the performance of already bias-corrected models (e.g., **47.1%** FID reduction for EDM with **13 NFE**) across various DPM architectures and sampling strategies.
- Qualitative assessments revealed that DCW mitigated visual artifacts such as over-smoothing and overexposure, yielding images with improved coherence, semantic fidelity, and clearer details, particularly with a small number of sampling steps, while incurring negligible computational overhead (**0.08%** to **0.47%**).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - DPM networks exhibit sensitivity to a mismatch between input sample SNR and its associated timestep, leading to inaccurate noise predictions.
> - The reverse-denoising process in DPMs inherently produces samples with a lower Signal-to-Noise Ratio than ideal forward-perturbed samples at the same timestep.
> - Applying differential correction in the wavelet domain, with dynamic weighting for different frequency components, is an effective and computationally efficient strategy to mitigate this bias and improve generation quality.
