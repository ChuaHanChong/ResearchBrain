# SWE-RM: Execution-free Feedback For Software Engineering Agents

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from The Hong Kong University of Science and Technology and Alibaba Group developed SWE-RM, an execution-free reward model that provides reliable, fine-grained feedback for software engineering agents. This model substantially improves agent performance, boosting accuracy by 7.6 to 10.4 absolute percentage points in test-time scaling and increasing resolve rates by 3 absolute percentage points in reinforcement learning on SWE-Bench Verified.

## Problem

- Traditional execution-based feedback for software engineering agents (via unit tests) is sparse and binary, providing insufficient granularity for efficient reinforcement learning.
- The reliance on unit tests for feedback is often hindered by their unreliability, limited coverage, and high computational cost for large-scale training.
- Existing execution-free reward models for software engineering tasks primarily focus on test-time scaling, lacking a comprehensive understanding of their suitability and properties for reinforcement learning.

## Method

- Formulated reward modeling as a ==generative classification== task, where the model outputs  or  tokens indicating task resolution, with continuous scores derived from token log probabilities.
- Utilized the Qwen3-30B-A3B model as the backbone and trained it with supervised fine-tuning on over 400k diverse, multi-turn agent trajectories, featuring a crucial 256k context length.
- Established a comprehensive evaluation framework including Test-Time Scaling (TTS), Area Under the Curve (AUC) for discriminative ability, and Expected Calibration Error (ECE) for confidence reliability, to assess reward models' versatility for both ranking and reinforcement learning.

## Results

- SWE-RM achieved state-of-the-art Test-Time Scaling performance on SWE-Bench Verified, improving Qwen3-Coder-Flash accuracy by 10.4 absolute percentage points (to **62.0%**) and Qwen3-Coder-Max accuracy by 7.6 absolute percentage points (to **74.6%**).
- Integrating SWE-RM's execution-free feedback with execution-based signals for reinforcement learning improved resolve rates by 3 absolute percentage points (**54.8%** vs. **51.8%**) on SWE-Bench Verified, demonstrating enhanced training efficiency and stability.
- Extensive ablation studies showed that factors like increased data scale, a 2:1 positive-to-negative training ratio, and a 256k context length are critical for building robust reward models with superior discriminative ability (AUC) and confidence reliability (ECE).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Test-Time Scaling (TTS) performance alone is insufficient for evaluating reward models, as high TTS does not guarantee effective or stable Reinforcement Learning (RL); AUC and ECE are critical for assessing discriminative ability and confidence reliability.
> - Training data scale and the ratio of positive-to-negative examples significantly impact reward model quality, with larger datasets and a 2:1 positive-to-negative ratio yielding better calibration and discrimination.
> - Employing a 256k context length in the reward model is vital for processing long, complex agent trajectories, enabling 100% scoring coverage and substantially boosting overall performance.
