# Not All Tokens See Equally: Perception-Grounded Policy Optimization for Large Vision-Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Harbin Institute of Technology and Huawei Technologies Co., Ltd. developed Perception-Grounded Policy Optimization (PGPO), a reinforcement learning framework for large vision-language models (LVLMs) that assigns credit at a fine-grained, token-level based on visual dependency. PGPO enhances multimodal reasoning, achieving an average 18.7% performance boost across seven benchmarks and demonstrating improved training stability by reducing gradient variance.

## Problem

- Existing reinforcement learning from verifiable rewards (RLVR) frameworks for LVLMs apply a uniform credit assignment, broadcasting the same advantage to all generated tokens regardless of their visual reliance.
- This uniform credit assignment overlooks the heterogeneous nature of multimodal reasoning, where only a fraction of tokens genuinely depend on visual information.
- The lack of perception-grounded credit assignment leads to suboptimal visual reasoning, increased gradient variance, and diluted learning signals for critical visual information.

## Method

- The authors quantify ==Token Visual Dependency== for each token using the ==Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence== between the model's predictive distributions with and without visual conditioning.
- A ==threshold-gated, piecewise function== reshapes the reinforcement learning advantages at the token level, actively amplifying learning signals for visually-dependent tokens and suppressing gradient noise from visually-independent tokens.
- A ==sum-preserving normalization== mechanism ensures that the total advantage mass remains consistent, which is critical for maintaining stable optimization and preventing training collapse.

## Results

- **PGPO** achieved state-of-the-art performance, boosting LVLMs by an average of **18.7%** compared to the original **Qwen2.5-VL** series and outperforming existing RLVR methods across multimodal reasoning benchmarks.
- The framework demonstrated superior training stability, preventing late-stage training collapse observed in some baselines and achieving faster initial convergence and higher performance.
- Models trained with **PGPO** showed a rapid and steady increase in the average visual dependency of generated tokens, correlating with rising accuracy and indicating enhanced perceptual grounding, with a modest **10%** computational overhead.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Token visual dependency is sparse across generated sequences, with only a small fraction of tokens being critically dependent on visual input for their accurate generation.
> - Suppression of gradient noise from visually irrelevant tokens (nuisance tokens) is as crucial, if not more, than boosting signals for visually dependent tokens, for overall performance improvement.
> - Strict maintenance of advantage mass through sum-preserving normalization is essential to prevent a non-zero mean shift in advantages, which can lead to significant gradient variance and training instability.
