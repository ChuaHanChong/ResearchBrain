# Not All Tokens See Equally: Perception-Grounded Policy Optimization for Large Vision-Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Harbin Institute of Technology and Huawei Technologies Co., Ltd. developed Perception-Grounded Policy Optimization (PGPO), a reinforcement learning framework for large vision-language models (LVLMs) that assigns credit at a fine-grained, token-level based on visual dependency. PGPO enhances multimodal reasoning, achieving an average 18.7% performance boost across seven benchmarks and demonstrating improved training stability by reducing gradient variance.

## Problem

- Existing reinforcement learning from verifiable rewards (RLVR) frameworks for LVLMs apply a uniform credit assignment, broadcasting the same advantage to all generated tokens regardless of their visual reliance.
- This uniform credit assignment overlooks the heterogeneous nature of multimodal reasoning, where only a fraction of tokens genuinely depend on visual information.
- The lack of perception-grounded credit assignment leads to suboptimal visual reasoning, increased gradient variance, and diluted learning signals for critical visual information.

## Method

- The authors quantify ==Token Visual Dependency== for each token using the ==Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence== between the model's predictive distributions with and without visual conditioning.
- A ==threshold-gated, piecewise function== reshapes the reinforcement learning advantages at the token level, actively amplifying learning signals for visually-dependent tokens and suppressing gradient noise from visually-independent tokens.
- A ==sum-preserving normalization== mechanism ensures that the total advantage mass remains consistent, which is critical for maintaining stable optimization and preventing training collapse.

## Results

- **PGPO** achieved state-of-the-art performance, boosting LVLMs by an average of **18.7%** compared to the original **Qwen2.5-VL** series and outperforming existing RLVR methods across multimodal reasoning benchmarks.
- The framework demonstrated superior training stability, preventing late-stage training collapse observed in some baselines and achieving faster initial convergence and higher performance.
- Models trained with **PGPO** showed a rapid and steady increase in the average visual dependency of generated tokens, correlating with rising accuracy and indicating enhanced perceptual grounding, with a modest **10%** computational overhead.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Token visual dependency is sparse across generated sequences, with only a small fraction of tokens being critically dependent on visual input for their accurate generation.
> - Suppression of gradient noise from visually irrelevant tokens (nuisance tokens) is as crucial, if not more, than boosting signals for visually dependent tokens, for overall performance improvement.
> - Strict maintenance of advantage mass through sum-preserving normalization is essential to prevent a non-zero mean shift in advantages, which can lead to significant gradient variance and training instability.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) is experiencing rapid advancements, particularly with the application of Reinforcement Learning from Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) to enhance reasoning capabilities. Initially successful in text-oriented Large Language Models (LLMs) through methods like Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), RLVR has been extended to LVLMs. This extension primarily focuses on improving training data design, reward construction, and optimization strategies.

Despite these advancements, a prevailing limitation in current RLVR frameworks for LVLMs is the coarse-grained nature of credit assignment. Existing methods often distribute a uniform, sequence-level advantage across all generated tokens, regardless of their specific reliance on visual information. This approach is identified as a methodological flaw because multimodal reasoning, by its nature, demands precise perception that is not uniformly distributed across a generated text sequence. Only a subset of tokens, often those describing critical visual entities or numerical values derived from an image, truly depend on visual grounding. By treating all tokens equally, these methods dilute learning signals for the critical, visually-groundued reasoning steps and introduce noise from linguistically-driven, visually-independent tokens.

This paper addresses this gap by proposing a fine-grained, token-level credit assignment mechanism. It builds upon existing RLVR techniques by introducing a method to quantify and leverage the visual dependency of individual tokens, thereby aiming to refine the optimization process and improve the perception-grounded reasoning abilities of LVLMs. The work directly contributes to improving the efficiency and robustness of RL for multimodal tasks by focusing on the underlying mechanism of token-level policy optimization.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to develop a novel policy optimization framework for LVLMs that effectively addresses the issue of non-uniform visual dependency across generated tokens during multimodal reasoning. The motivation stems from the recognition of a "foundational methodological flaw" in existing RLVR frameworks:

- **Problem Identification:** Current RLVR methods for LVLMs, such as GRPO, assign identical advantage scores to all tokens within a generated sequence. This uniform credit assignment is problematic because multimodal reasoning steps do not all rely equally on visual input. Many tokens are primarily driven by linguistic priors, while only a "critical minority" (e.g., specific numbers, geometric terms, or descriptive adjectives extracted from an image) exhibit high visual dependency.
- **Consequence of Uniform Credit Assignment:** By distributing identical advantages, these methods "inherently dilute the learning signals essential for optimizing the critical, visually-grounded steps." This dilution makes it harder for the model to learn to extract and reason with visual information effectively. Furthermore, it introduces "gradient noise" from tokens that are largely independent of the visual context, potentially destabilizing training and hindering the development of robust visual perception.
- **Need for Quantification:** To overcome this limitation, the authors identify the imperative to "quantify the causal contribution of the visual modality to each individual reasoning step." This quantification is crucial for enabling a fine-grained credit assignment mechanism that can differentiate between visually-dependent and visually-independent tokens.
- **Enhancing Perception-Grounded Reasoning:** The ultimate goal is to enable LVLMs to perform more robust, perception-grounded multimodal reasoning by ensuring that the reinforcement learning signal is appropriately amplified for tokens that truly rely on visual perception, while simultaneously suppressing noise from tokens that do not. This is expected to lead to improved performance on complex multimodal tasks, particularly those requiring precise visual information extraction and integration.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, Perception-Grounded Policy Optimization (PGPO), introduces a token-level credit assignment framework into multimodal RLVR. It operates by dynamically reshaping advantages based on the visual dependency of individual tokens.

#### 3.1. Token Visual Dependency Formulation

The core of PGPO is the quantification of "Token Visual Dependency." This is formalized using the Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence. For a state $s_t = (q, o_{<t})$ and an image $I$, the visual dependency score at step $t$ is defined as:

$S(s_t, I):= D_{KL} (\pi_\theta(\cdot|s_t, I) \parallel \pi_\theta(\cdot|s_t))$

Here, $\pi_\theta(\cdot|s_t, I)$ is the model's next-token predictive distribution conditioned on both the text context $s_t$ and the image $I$, while $\pi_\theta(\cdot|s_t)$ is the distribution conditioned only on the text context. A larger $S$ value indicates a greater causal information gain from the visual input for predicting the token at step $t$. This metric is described as capturing "Bayesian surprise" and is inherently non-negative, providing a robust signal.

To obtain the visually-unconditioned distribution $\pi_\theta(\cdot|s_t)$ without altering positional encodings, an attention masking technique is employed during a secondary forward pass. The attention mask corresponding to all visual tokens is set to `False`, effectively blinding the text tokens to the visual prefix. The exact KL divergence, which involves summing over the entire vocabulary, is approximated using a low-variance, unbiased Monte-Carlo estimator based on the generated token $x_t \sim \pi_I$.

#### 3.2. Transformation of Visual Dependency Score

The raw KL divergence $S_t$ can be unbounded and heavy-tailed, making it unsuitable for direct advantage reshaping. To address this, a two-stage monotonic transformation is applied to create a bounded visual dependency score $I_t \in$:

1. **Logarithmic Compression:** The raw score is first dampened using a shifted logarithmic function:
 $\tilde{S}_t = \log(1 + S_t)$
 This ensures non-negativity and reduces the impact of extreme outliers.
2. **Min-Max Normalization:** The dampened signal is then normalized sequence-wise:
 $I_t = \frac{\tilde{S}_t - \min_j \tilde{S}_j}{\max_j \tilde{S}_j - \min_j \tilde{S}_j + \epsilon}$
 This resolves numerical instability and provides a standardized relative scale.

#### 3.3. Threshold-Gated Advantage Reshaping

PGPO introduces a piece-wise base scaling weight $\omega(I_t)$ that reallocates credit based on the normalized visual dependency score $I_t$:

$\omega(I_t) = \begin{cases} \frac{I_t}{\tau + \epsilon}, & \text{if } I_t < \tau \\ 1 + \beta \cdot \frac{I_t - \tau}{1 - \tau + \epsilon}, & \text{if } I_t \geq \tau \end{cases}$

Here, $\tau \in (0, 1)$ is a relative threshold that distinguishes visually-dependent tokens from "noise tokens" (low dependency). $\beta \geq 0$ is a boosting coefficient that controls the amplification of signals for highly dependent tokens. This mechanism aims to suppress gradient noise from low-dependency tokens and actively boost learning signals for perceptually pivotal tokens.

#### 3.4. Mass-Conserving Normalization

To ensure stable optimization and prevent policy update magnitudes from becoming unbounded, a sum-preserving normalization is applied to the base weights:

$\tilde{\omega}_t = \omega(I_t) \cdot \frac{|o_i|}{\sum_{j=1}^{|o_i|} \omega(I_j)}$

This normalization mathematically guarantees that the total advantage "mass" across the entire trajectory remains consistent with the original, preserving the zero-mean property of intra-group advantages (i.e., $\sum_{t=1}^{|o_i|} \tilde{\omega}_t = |o_i|$).

The final PGPO advantage for the $t$-th token in the $i$-th trajectory is then defined as:
$\tilde{A}_{i,t} = A_i \cdot \tilde{\omega}_t$
where $A_i$ is the original GRPO sequence-level advantage. This $\tilde{A}_{i,t}$ is substituted into the GRPO clipped surrogate objective for policy updates.

#### 3.5. Theoretical Analysis

The paper provides theoretical justifications for PGPO's design:
- **Gradient Noise Suppression:** PGPO is shown to strictly mitigate policy gradient variance by attenuating the contribution of visually-independent tokens, reducing the nuisance-token component in the gradient second moment by a multiplicative factor.
- **Necessity of Mass Conservation:** The sum-preserving normalization is proven essential for training stability. Unnormalized scaling can introduce a non-zero mean shift, potentially leading to a quadratic explosion in total gradient variance.
- **Monotonicity and Rank Preservation:** The transformation from raw visual dependency score $S_t$ to the modulated weight $\tilde{\omega}_t$ is proven to be monotonically non-decreasing and to preserve the relative ordinal importance of tokens within a sequence, ensuring that credit assignment faithfully reflects visual dependency.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

#### 4.1. Empirical Analysis of Visual Dependency

- **Distributional Sparsity:** Analysis on MathVerse (vision-dominant subset) revealed that token-level visual dependency $S_t$ exhibits a highly sparse, long-tailed distribution. Only a limited subset of tokens showed high visual dependency, suggesting that visually-grounded reasoning is driven by a critical minority of tokens.
- **Higher Dependency for Visual Anchors:** Tokens identified as "visual anchors" (those impossible to infer without the image, as annotated by GPT-5) exhibited significantly higher average $S_t$ values compared to non-anchor tokens. This indicates that $S_t$ effectively captures semantic visual grounding.
- **Lower Dependency in Hallucinations:** On the CHAIR benchmark, truthful tokens demonstrated higher average $S_t$ than hallucinated ones. This suggests that hallucinated concepts are primarily driven by the model's language prior, not actual visual input, further validating $S_t$ as a proxy for factual visual reliance.

#### 4.2. Performance on Multimodal Reasoning Benchmarks

- **State-of-the-Art Performance:** PGPO consistently achieved state-of-the-art results across seven challenging multimodal reasoning benchmarks (DynaMath, Geo3k, MathVerse, MathVision, MMK12, LogicVista, MMMU-Pro). When applied to Qwen2.5-VL-7B, PGPO boosted models by 18.7% on average compared to the base Qwen2.5-VL-7B and outperformed existing methods like GRPO, DAPO, PAPO, and VPPO. Similar gains were observed with Qwen2.5-VL-3B.
- **Pronounced Gains on Visually Demanding Tasks:** Performance gains were particularly notable on benchmarks requiring rigorous spatial reasoning and high visual dependency, such as Geo3k, MathVerse, and MMMU-Pro, indicating its effectiveness in real perception-grounded reasoning.

#### 4.3. Training Stability and Dynamics

- **Faster Convergence and Stability:** PGPO demonstrated faster initial convergence and superior training stability compared to baselines like DAPO, which suffered from severe late-stage training collapse characterized by a sharp drop in accuracy and a spike in policy entropy. PGPO's approach reduced gradient variance and stabilized the training trajectory without requiring ad-hoc entropy regularization.
- **Increased Visual Dependency:** During training, the average visual dependency ($\tilde{S}_t$) of generated tokens under PGPO exhibited a rapid and steady upward trend, correlating with increased accuracy. This suggests that PGPO acts as an implicit regularizer, actively guiding the policy to rely more on visual evidence for multimodal reasoning.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies and Hyperparameter Analysis

- **Threshold-Gated Mechanism:** Ablation studies confirmed the necessity of the full threshold-gated mechanism. The "suppression-only" strategy (reducing gradients for irrelevant tokens) yielded stronger performance than "boosting-only," indicating the importance of filtering noise. Removing the sum-preserving normalization ("PGPO w/o Norm.") significantly degraded performance, empirically verifying its role in maintaining stable policy updates.
- **Hyperparameter Sensitivity:** An optimal configuration was found at a threshold $\tau = 0.4$ and boosting coefficient $\beta = 2.0$. This configuration precisely partitioned trajectories, allowing for effective amplification of true perceptual anchors without inflating global gradients.

#### 4.5. Performance on General Benchmarks and Computational Overhead

- **Generalizability:** PGPO also demonstrated superior performance on three unseen, general VQA benchmarks (MMBench, MMStar, MME), outperforming baselines and confirming that the method does not impair general visual-language capabilities but rather sharpens perceptual grounding.
- **Limited Computational Overhead:** The additional computational cost introduced by PGPO (due to a second forward pass for visual dependency estimation) was approximately 10% relative to the DAPO baseline. This small overhead was deemed a worthwhile trade-off for the substantial performance gains and improved learning effectiveness on complex visual reasoning tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a significant advancement in the application of reinforcement learning to Large Vision-Language Models for complex multimodal reasoning. By addressing the fundamental issue of coarse-grained credit assignment, PGPO offers a more principled and effective approach to training LVLMs.

The key significance and potential impact include:

- **Enhanced Multimodal Reasoning:** PGPO directly improves LVLMs' ability to ground their reasoning in visual evidence. This is particularly crucial for tasks requiring precise extraction of information from images (e.g., numerical values, spatial relationships, object attributes) where linguistic priors alone are insufficient or misleading.
- **Improved Training Stability and Efficiency:** The theoretical and empirical demonstrations of gradient variance reduction and prevention of training collapse are vital. This makes the RLVR process more robust and easier to manage, potentially reducing the need for heuristic regularization techniques that complicate model tuning.
- **Foundation for Future LVLM Development:** By introducing the concept and quantification of "Token Visual Dependency," the work provides a novel interpretable metric for understanding how LVLMs utilize visual information. This could pave the way for more explainable and controllable multimodal AI systems.
- **Mitigation of Hallucinations:** The finding that hallucinated tokens exhibit lower visual dependency suggests PGPO's potential in inherently mitigating object hallucinations by de-emphasizing linguistically-driven (but visually ungrounded) outputs.
- **Generalizability Across Tasks:** The consistent performance gains across diverse specialized reasoning benchmarks and general VQA tasks indicate that PGPO's mechanism is broadly applicable, enhancing both specific and general visual-language capabilities without detrimental trade-offs.
- **Optimizing Resource Usage:** The fine-grained credit assignment ensures that computational resources are more efficiently directed towards learning critical, visually-grounded steps, rather than being diluted across irrelevant linguistic segments.

While the current work is validated on models up to 7B parameters, its positive scaling trends suggest potential for larger models, provided computational resources allow. The methodology establishes a foundation for building more perceptually intelligent and reliable multimodal AI.
