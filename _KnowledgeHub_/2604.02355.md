# From Broad Exploration to Stable Synthesis: Entropy-Guided Optimization for Autoregressive Image Generation

> [!summary] Summary
> Entropy-Guided Group Relative Policy Optimization (EG-GRPO) is introduced as an enhanced fine-tuning strategy for autoregressive text-to-image (T2I) models that quantifies token-level uncertainty using entropy and dynamically reallocates optimization efforts. This approach significantly reduces generative instability and uncertainty, leading to state-of-the-art performance on compositional generalization benchmarks like T2I-CompBench, where it improves metrics such as Color (84.11), Shape (60.88), and Texture (77.38).

## Problem

- Balancing the generation of diverse outputs (exploration) with high-fidelity, reward-aligned images (exploitation) in text-to-image (T2I) generation.
- Ensuring stability and consistency of generated content across repeated sampling in T2I models.
- Lack of systematic understanding of how Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning and Reinforcement Learning (RL) interact to influence uncertainty and generation stability in autoregressive T2I.

## Method

- An entropy-based analysis was performed to quantitatively investigate the role of ==token-level uncertainty== in both textual CoT reasoning and subsequent image token generation.
- Entropy-Guided Group Relative Policy Optimization (EG-GRPO) was developed as a token-level modification to the standard GRPO objective, guided by principles of uncertainty focus and confidence protection.
- EG-GRPO dynamically categorizes tokens by entropy, suppressing reward-driven updates for low-entropy tokens and adding an entropy bonus to high-entropy tokens to prioritize uncertainty reduction.

## Results

- EG-GRPO achieved competitive performance on T2I-CompBench, surpassing baselines in Color (84.11), Shape (60.88), and Texture (77.38), and showed improvements on the WISE benchmark in Culture (49.00) and Science (46.33).
- The method systematically reduced both the mean and variance of entropy for textual CoT and image tokens compared to T2I-R1, fostering more confident and stable token generation.
- EG-GRPO demonstrated superior convergence efficiency, achieving higher reward scores earlier in the training process with only a marginal increase in computational overhead (**1.35%** step time, **0.38 GB** GPU memory).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Chain-of-Thought (CoT) expands the generative exploration space, while Reinforcement Learning (RL) (specifically GRPO) subsequently contracts it towards narrower, higher-reward, lower-entropy regions.
> - A strong negative correlation exists between final image reward and both the mean and variance of image-token entropy, implying that reducing generative uncertainty and instability is crucial for high-quality images.
> - The intrinsic uncertainty of textual CoT directly influences downstream image quality, with lower-entropy CoTs correlating with higher-reward images, highlighting the importance of coherent semantic planning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of text-to-image (T2I) generation has experienced rapid advancements, largely driven by large-scale pretraining and the development of sophisticated autoregressive and diffusion architectures. However, the progression of T2I models continues to face two persistent challenges: effectively balancing the generation of diverse outputs (exploration) with the production of high-fidelity, reward-aligned images (exploitation), and ensuring the stability and consistency of generated content across repeated sampling.

Recent research has explored the integration of Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting with Reinforcement Learning (RL) to enhance T2I generation. CoT methods aim to provide richer semantic planning by generating intermediate reasoning steps, thereby improving the model's understanding of complex prompts. Concurrently, RL is employed to directly optimize models based on task-specific or human preference rewards, aligning generative outputs more closely with desired criteria. Prior works, such as Visual-CoG, ReasonGen-R1, and T2I-R1, have incorporated rationale-augmented data and multi-level reasoning chains, often utilizing Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) for refinement. Other studies, like PromptEnhancer, have demonstrated the benefits of CoT-based prompt rewriting with RL for improving image quality, while methods such as DPOK focus on preference alignment within diffusion models. These collective efforts highlight a promising trajectory for enhancing the controllability, interpretability, and human alignment of T2I systems.

Despite these advancements, a systematic understanding of the precise interaction between CoT's tendency to broaden the generative search space and RL's role in optimizing within that space remains incompletely characterized, particularly concerning its impact on uncertainty and generation stability. This paper addresses this gap by conducting an entropy-based analysis to quantify token-level uncertainty in both textual CoT reasoning and the subsequent image token generation processes. It then proposes a novel fine-tuning strategy, Entropy-Guided Group Relative Policy Optimization (EG-GRPO), which refines the existing GRPO framework by dynamically reallocating its optimization budget based on this quantified token-level uncertainty. This work builds upon the foundation of autoregressive T2I models and GRPO, aiming to enhance their performance by explicitly managing and controlling token-level uncertainty and stability throughout the generation pipeline.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to achieve a systematic and quantitative understanding of the intricate dynamics between Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning and Reinforcement Learning (RL) optimization within autoregressive text-to-image (T2I) generation. A central focus is placed on elucidating how these interactions collectively govern the model's predictive uncertainty and the stability of its generated outputs. The motivation for this analytical approach stems from the observation that while the combined application of CoT and RL has been empirically shown to improve T2I generation, the underlying mechanisms by which CoT's exploratory nature interacts with RL's optimization capabilities, and their influence on uncertainty propagation and generation consistency, have not been thoroughly investigated.

To address this, the authors quantitatively analyze entropy dynamics, employing Shannon entropy as a metric to measure token-level uncertainty in both the textual CoT (semantic planning) and the subsequent image token generation (visual decoding). This analysis is motivated by the hypothesis that a precise understanding of how uncertainty evolves and is managed throughout the T2I pipeline can lead to the development of more effective and targeted optimization strategies.

Based on the insights derived from this foundational analysis, a secondary, practical objective is to develop and implement an improved fine-tuning strategy. This strategy, named Entropy-Guided Group Relative Policy Optimization (EG-GRPO), is designed to specifically address the identified issues related to uncertainty and instability. The detailed motivations for the architectural and algorithmic design of EG-GRPO are as follows:

- **Managing the Exploration-Exploitation Trade-off:** The analysis revealed that CoT tends to broaden the generative exploration space, while RL typically contracts it toward desired outputs. EG-GRPO is motivated to create an optimization method that effectively navigates this dynamic, aiming to prevent the premature collapse of exploration while efficiently guiding the model towards stable, high-reward regions.
- **Minimizing Uncertainty and Instability:** The observed strong negative correlation between final image reward and both the mean and variance of image-token entropy indicated that reducing these uncertainty metrics is critical for generating high-quality images. EG-GRPO is motivated to directly mitigate these factors at a token-specific level.
- **Leveraging Upstream Influence of CoT:** The finding that the entropy of textual CoT directly influences downstream image quality motivates incorporating a mechanism to control uncertainty originating from the semantic planning stage. The analysis suggested that lower-entropy CoTs consistently lead to better image generations, highlighting the importance of CoT coherence.
- **Optimizing Gradient Allocation:** Standard GRPO broadcasts a single advantage value across all tokens within a generated sequence, which may result in inefficient allocation of computational effort. This approach might waste gradients on already confident predictions or fail to adequately address highly uncertain tokens. EG-GRPO is motivated to reallocate this optimization budget by prioritizing updates for high-entropy (uncertain) tokens and, conversely, protecting low-entropy (confident) tokens from potentially disruptive reward-driven updates, thereby preserving stable knowledge.
- **Achieving State-of-the-Art Performance:** The ultimate motivation is to develop a method that not only provides a theoretical understanding of T2I dynamics but also translates these analytical insights into tangible performance improvements on standard T2I benchmarks, particularly for challenging compositional generalization tasks.

In summary, the research pursues a dual objective: first, to systematically analyze the fundamental interplay of CoT and RL in T2I through the quantitative lens of entropy, and second, to translate these analytical insights into a practical, uncertainty-aware optimization algorithm that enhances generative quality, consistency, and stability.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research methodology is structured into two primary phases: an entropy-based analysis of CoT-RL dynamics in text-to-image (T2I) generation, followed by the development, implementation, and evaluation of an Entropy-Guided Group Relative Policy Optimization (EG-GRPO) strategy.

#### 3.1. Entropy-Based Analysis of T2I Generation (Section 4)

The analytical phase aimed to quantitatively investigate the role of uncertainty in autoregressive T2I models that combine Chain-of-Thought (CoT) with Reinforcement Learning (RL), specifically utilizing Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO).
- **Model Configurations for Analysis:** The study used a Janus-Pro autoregressive backbone operating in a discrete latent space. For comparative analysis, three configurations were examined: the base Janus-Pro model, Janus-Pro augmented with CoT reasoning (Janus-Pro+CoT), and a GRPO-finetuned variant built upon Janus-Pro+CoT (referred to as T2I-R1).
- **Uncertainty Quantification:** Shannon entropy, defined as H(π_θ(· | z_<i, c)), was employed to quantify token-level predictive uncertainty. This metric reflects the predictability of the next token in a sequence, where lower entropy signifies higher confidence and higher entropy indicates greater uncertainty. Entropy was computed for both textual CoT tokens and image tokens.
- **Exploration-Exploitation Dynamics Assessment (Section 4.1):** The distributions of generated images were visualized in a two-dimensional space, plotting each image's output mean entropy against its reward score for the three model configurations. This enabled the observation of how CoT and GRPO individually and in combination affected the generative exploration space.
- **Investigation of Upstream CoT Influence (Section 4.2):** To ascertain how the intrinsic uncertainty of the textual CoT impacts downstream image generation, a subset of samples exhibiting stable image generation (defined by an entropy variance below a threshold of 0.011 across multiple runs) was isolated. The correlation between the mean entropy of the textual CoT and the average reward of the resulting images was then analyzed. Additionally, reward distributions for images conditioned on different CoTs for the same prompt were visualized to highlight consistency.
- **Analysis of RL's Learned Objective for Reward Optimization (Section 4.3):** The study examined how the fully RL-trained model (T2I-R1) implicitly controlled entropy to optimize for reward. For each prompt-CoT pair, multiple images were generated, and their mean entropy (representing overall uncertainty) and standard deviation of entropy (capturing instability across runs) were aggregated. Correlations between reward and these entropy statistics were analyzed, with particular attention to behaviors observed in high-variance generative regimes.

#### 3.2. Entropy-Guided Group Relative Policy Optimization (EG-GRPO) (Section 5)

Motivated by the analytical findings, EG-GRPO was designed as a token-level modification to the standard GRPO objective.
- **Design Principles:** The development of EG-GRPO was guided by three core principles derived from the entropy analysis:
 1. **Focus on uncertainty:** Allocate a greater proportion of the update mass to high-entropy tokens to reduce instability where it is most pronounced.
 2. **Protect confidence:** For tokens identified as having low entropy (high confidence), reward-driven updates are suppressed, allowing only the KL-to-reference regularization term to act, thereby preserving stability and previously acquired knowledge.
 3. **Maintain Reward-Driven Optimization:** The fundamental group-relative reward objective of GRPO is retained, but it is augmented with an additive per-token entropy bonus applied exclusively to high-entropy tokens, ensuring that the optimization remains aligned with reward signals.
- **Token Selection and Advantage Modification (Section 5.3, 5.4):**
 - For each generated sequence `i` and for each modality (textual CoT or image), tokens `t` were dynamically categorized based on their normalized Shannon entropy, `H_bar(i)_t = H(i)_t / log|V|`:
 - `S^(i,m)_lo`: Comprising the bottom 20% of tokens by entropy (low-entropy tokens).
 - `S^(i,m)_hi`: Comprising the top 50% of tokens by entropy (high-entropy tokens).
 - `S^(i,m)_mid`: Encompassing the remaining 30% of tokens.
 - Binary masks, `M^(i)_t` and `U^(i)_t`, were introduced. `M^(i)_t` is set to 0 for tokens in `S^(i,m)_lo` and 1 otherwise, effectively removing reward-driven updates for low-entropy tokens. `U^(i)_t` is set to 1 for tokens in `S^(i,m)_hi` and 0 otherwise, marking them to receive an entropy bonus.
 - The standard GRPO advantage `A^(i)` (broadcast uniformly to all tokens) was modified to `A_tilde(i)_t` for each token `t`:
 `A_tilde(i)_t = M^(i)_t * A^(i) + U^(i)_t * λ * sg[H_bar(i)_t]`
 where `sg[·]` denotes the stop-gradient operator. This formulation implies that low-entropy tokens receive zero reward-driven gradient, while high-entropy tokens receive an additional entropy bonus proportional to their normalized entropy, augmenting the original GRPO advantage. Mid-entropy tokens receive the unmodified `A^(i)`.
- **Batch-level Budget Calibration (Section 5.3):** A scaling factor `λ` was introduced to manage the magnitude of the entropy bonus. Proposition 1 established a `λ_star` that ensures the batch-wise coefficient budget of EG-GRPO is approximately proportional to GRPO’s, with a configurable scaling factor `κ`. Corollary 5.1 provided a formal proof that the entropy bonus term vanishes at GRPO equilibrium (i.e., when `A^(i) = 0` for all sequences), thereby preserving the stationary points of the base objective.
- **Loss Function:** The overall EG-GRPO loss function is formulated as:
 `L_EG-GRPO(θ) = - (1/G) * Σ_i (1/T^(i)) * Σ_t A_tilde(i)_t * log π_θ(o^(i)_t | c, o^(i)_<t) + β D_KL(π_θ || π_ref)`
 where `β` is a coefficient controlling the KL regularization term towards a reference policy `π_ref`, ensuring that the policy does not drift too far from its initial state.

#### 3.3. Experimental Settings and Evaluation (Section 6)

- **Training Details:** The policy was trained using 6,786 text prompts sourced from T2I-CompBench, augmented with structured object-attribute annotations automatically extracted using GPT-4o mini. The policy backbone was initialized from Janus-Pro-7B. Optimization was performed with a learning rate of 1 × 10^(-6) and a KL regularization coefficient `β = 0.01`.
- **Reward Pipeline:** A multi-component reward system was employed, integrating Human Preference Score (HPS) for human aesthetic and preference alignment, GroundingDINO for accurate object detection and localization, GIT for Visual Question Answering (VQA) to assess semantic understanding, and an Object-Relation Module (ORM) implemented by fine-tuning LLaVA-OneVision-7B to evaluate relational understanding.
- **Benchmarks:** Evaluations were conducted on two established T2I evaluation suites: T2I-CompBench (6,000 prompts, designed to test compositional generalization across attribute binding, object relations, and complex compositions) and WISE (1,000 prompts, focusing on knowledge-intensive reasoning, including cultural commonsense, spatio-temporal reasoning, and natural science).
- **Ablation Studies:** To assess the contribution of different components, ablation studies were performed, testing variants of EG-GRPO where entropy guidance was applied solely to textual CoT tokens (w/ only sem) or exclusively to image tokens (w/ only tok).
- **Diversity Analysis:** Generative diversity was quantified using the Vendi Score, a reference-free metric. This analysis compared EG-GRPO to the T2I-R1 baseline, particularly focusing on subsets of generated samples where both models achieved comparable quality scores to isolate diversity effects.
- **Entropy Analysis of Proposed Method:** The mean and variance of entropy distributions for both textual CoT and image tokens were directly compared between EG-GRPO and T2I-R1 to demonstrate the explicit effects of the proposed optimization strategy on token-level uncertainty.
- **Computational Efficiency:** To evaluate practical applicability, computational overhead was assessed by benchmarking wall-clock time and peak GPU memory usage against T2I-R1. Convergence efficiency, measured by reward progression over training steps, was also compared.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded several empirical findings from its entropy analysis and demonstrated performance improvements with the proposed EG-GRPO method.

#### 4.1. Insights from Entropy Dynamics Analysis

- **Dichotomy of CoT and RL:** The analysis indicated a complementary relationship between CoT and RL. CoT expanded the generative exploration space, leading to a broader distribution of output entropies and a wider range of generated outcomes, including both low and high-quality samples. In contrast, GRPO fine-tuning subsequently contracted this expanded space, guiding the model towards narrower, lower-entropy regions that consistently yielded higher rewards. This suggested that CoT facilitates broad exploration, while RL acts as a refinement mechanism to exploit this landscape.
- **Upstream CoT Influence on Image Quality:** A negative correlation was observed between the mean entropy of textual CoT and the average reward of generated images, particularly in scenarios where image generation itself was stable. This indicated that lower-entropy CoTs correlated with higher-reward images, suggesting that coherent and confident textual reasoning is a significant factor in achieving high-quality visual outputs. Furthermore, different CoTs for the same prompt were shown to form distinct clusters in the reward space, with certain CoTs consistently leading to lower-reward image generations.
- **RL's Implicit Objective: Minimizing Uncertainty and Instability:** Empirical data revealed a consistent negative correlation between the final image reward and both the mean entropy and standard deviation of image-token entropy. This suggested that generative instability (higher entropy standard deviation) is detrimental to quality. The analysis further indicated that instability amplified the negative impact of high mean entropy on reward. In exploratory, high-variance generation regimes, reducing mean entropy was particularly effective in improving image quality. These findings collectively implied that the RL agent implicitly learns to minimize both overall generative uncertainty and generation instability across samples.

#### 4.2. Performance of EG-GRPO

- **State-of-the-Art Benchmarking:** EG-GRPO achieved competitive results on established T2I benchmarks. On T2I-CompBench, it surpassed all baselines, including T2I-R1, in categories such as Color (84.11), Shape (60.88), and Texture (77.38), with notable improvements in Shape binding. On the WISE benchmark, EG-GRPO demonstrated improvements in Culture (49.00 vs. 48.00) and Science (46.33 vs. 45.00) while maintaining comparable performance in Spatio-temporal reasoning (56.00 vs. 55.50) when compared to T2I-R1. These results suggest that entropy-guided updates enhance compositional reasoning and overall robustness.
- **Ablation Study Confirmation:** Ablation studies confirmed the efficacy of the full EG-GRPO model, which applied entropy guidance to both textual CoT tokens and image tokens, achieving the best overall performance. Variants that applied guidance solely to textual CoT tokens or exclusively to image tokens performed less effectively than the full model. This indicates that balanced uncertainty regulation across both modalities is beneficial, and uncoordinated entropy control can be counterproductive. The baseline without entropy guidance (w/o All) consistently performed worse than the full EG-GRPO model, confirming the utility of the proposed entropy-aware updates.
- **Diversity Preservation:** While the natural exploration-exploitation trade-off inherent in RL training led to a slight decrease in overall diversity (measured by Vendi Score) as the model converged to high-reward regions, EG-GRPO demonstrated its ability to maintain diversity comparable to the baseline (T2I-R1) when samples of similar quality were considered. This indicates that the method effectively reduces "bad" uncertainty contributing to instability without collapsing the generative model into narrow, undesirable modes.
- **Entropy Reduction:** Compared to T2I-R1, EG-GRPO systematically reduced both the mean and variance of entropy for textual CoT tokens and, more significantly, for image tokens. This outcome aligns with the method's design to foster more confident and stable token generation, where high entropy at the token level is often indicative of decoding instability or local artifacts rather than meaningful variation.
- **Computational Efficiency:** EG-GRPO introduced a marginal increase in computational overhead, with approximately a 1.35% increase in step time and a 0.38 GB increase in GPU memory usage compared to T2I-R1. However, it exhibited superior convergence efficiency, achieving higher reward scores earlier in the training process, which suggests a better time-to-convergence ratio.
- **Qualitative Consistency:** Qualitative evaluations demonstrated that EG-GRPO consistently produced high-quality images that accurately rendered fine-grained attributes, preserved coherent spatial layouts in complex scenes, and maintained stability across multiple objects.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the understanding and advancement of text-to-image (T2I) generation in several ways, with implications for future generative model development and application.

- **Fundamental Insight into Generative Dynamics:** The systematic, entropy-based analysis provides a quantitative framework for understanding the intricate interplay between Chain-of-Thought (CoT) exploration and Reinforcement Learning (RL) exploitation in autoregressive T2I models. By clarifying how CoT expands the generative space and how RL refines it by minimizing uncertainty and instability, the work enhances the theoretical comprehension of complex multimodal generative systems. This deeper understanding can inform the design of more principled and effective training paradigms for other sequence-to-sequence generative tasks.
- **Enhanced T2I Performance and Robustness:** The proposed Entropy-Guided Group Relative Policy Optimization (EG-GRPO) demonstrates state-of-the-art performance on challenging compositional and knowledge-intensive T2I benchmarks. The significant gains in compositional generalization (e.g., attribute binding, object relations) suggest that the method produces images that adhere more accurately and consistently to complex textual descriptions. This leads to more reliable and controllable T2I models, which are critical for diverse applications ranging from creative content generation to visual design and scientific illustration.
- **Improved Generative Stability and Fidelity:** By specifically targeting the reduction of mean and variance of token-level entropy, EG-GRPO directly addresses the issue of generative instability. This leads to more confident and stable visual token generation, potentially reducing common artifacts and improving the overall fidelity and perceived quality of synthesized images. The ability to preserve learned knowledge in confident regions while focusing updates on uncertain areas offers a robust mechanism for stable and incremental model refinement.
- **Efficient Optimization Strategy:** The token-level reallocation of optimization budget in EG-GRPO, which protects low-entropy tokens and prioritizes high-entropy ones, represents an advancement in RL fine-tuning efficiency. Despite introducing minimal computational overhead, the method demonstrates faster convergence to higher reward scores compared to baselines. This makes it a practical approach for fine-tuning large-scale autoregressive models, potentially reducing the computational resources and time required for achieving high-quality results.
- **Broader Applicability of Uncertainty-Aware Learning:** The core concept of entropy-guided optimization and selective gradient application based on token-level confidence is not limited to T2I. It could be extended to other autoregressive generation tasks, such as natural language generation, code synthesis, or even video generation, where managing the trade-off between exploration and exploitation and controlling uncertainty are crucial for performance and quality. This paradigm shift towards uncertainty-aware gradient allocation could inspire new research directions in reinforcement learning for generative models.

In conclusion, this research provides both fundamental insights into T2I dynamics and a practical, high-performing optimization algorithm. Its significance lies in advancing the capabilities of T2I models to generate more accurate, stable, and compositionally complex images, while also offering a transferable methodology for improving other generative AI systems.
