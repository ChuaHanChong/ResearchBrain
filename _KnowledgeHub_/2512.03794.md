# AdaptVision: Efficient Vision-Language Models via Adaptive Visual Acquisition

> [!summary] Summary
> The AdaptVision framework from Tencent Hunyuan allows Vision-Language Models to adaptively acquire visual information through a coarse-to-fine strategy and a novel reinforcement learning algorithm, achieving 97.9% of vanilla model performance while consuming only 33% of visual tokens and demonstrating a 1.67x inference speedup.

## Problem

- Large Vision-Language Models (VLMs) incur substantial computational and memory overhead due to processing a high volume of visual tokens from high-resolution images.
- Existing VLM efficiency methods employ passive, fixed-ratio compression or limited dynamic decisions, lacking fine-grained, adaptive visual token acquisition tailored to specific input needs.
- Training multi-turn VLM policies with standard reinforcement learning (RL) faces challenges like ambiguous credit assignment and imbalanced optimization between tool invocation and answer generation.

## Method

- **AdaptVision** employs a two-turn ==coarse-to-fine== processing strategy: initially analyzing low-resolution images and then adaptively invoking a bounding box tool to crop specific high-resolution regions for detailed analysis only when necessary.
- A composite reward function is designed to balance accuracy and token efficiency, including rewards for correct answers, proper output format, penalties for excessive tool use or 'lucky' low-resolution guesses, and rewards for relevant, minimally-sized crop areas.
- Decoupled Turn Policy Optimization (DTPO) is introduced, which decouples policy loss and advantage estimation by turns, providing more precise credit assignment and balanced optimization for tool-related and outcome-related decisions in the RL framework.

## Results

- AdaptVision achieved **97.9%** of the vanilla model's performance across various benchmarks while reducing visual token consumption to **33%** (compared to **100%**), outperforming all other efficient VLM baselines.
- The framework demonstrated a **1.67x** overall speedup in end-to-end inference time compared to the vanilla model and other dynamic methods.
- DTPO ensured stable and efficient training, with AdaptVision learning to adaptively regulate tool usage, increasing calls for complex tasks and reducing them for simpler ones, unlike standard GRPO which led to unstable and suboptimal tool call ratios.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Emulating human-like active vision, where models adaptively acquire visual information, significantly enhances VLM efficiency and accuracy compared to passive compression methods.
> - Decoupling policy loss and advantage estimation in multi-turn reinforcement learning is crucial for stable and effective optimization of policies that balance multiple, potentially conflicting objectives (e.g., accuracy vs. efficiency).
> - VLMs can learn to autonomously determine the minimum visual information required for specific tasks, adapting their token consumption based on image complexity and question type.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Vision-Language Models (VLMs) have demonstrated capabilities in various visual question answering (VQA) tasks and practical applications by integrating visual tokens into large language models (LLMs). However, a significant challenge within this field is the substantial computational and memory overhead associated with processing the large number of visual tokens required, particularly for high-resolution images. For instance, an image might generate thousands of visual tokens, making inference computationally expensive.

Existing research has explored various strategies to enhance VLM efficiency by compressing visual tokens. These approaches generally fall into two categories:
1. **Fixed-ratio compression**: Methods like FastV, SparseVLM, and VisionZip prune or merge a predefined percentage of visual tokens based on metrics such as importance or similarity. These methods offer a static reduction in tokens.
2. **Limited dynamic processing**: Some approaches, such as VisionThink, adapt visual token usage to a limited extent, for example, by deciding between using 100% or 25% of tokens based on task type (e.g., OCR-related vs. simpler tasks).

A common limitation across these existing methods is their largely passive nature. They either apply a fixed compression ratio or make coarse-grained decisions, lacking the ability for fine-grained, adaptive token acquisition that responds to the specific demands of each input sample. This contrasts with human visual perception, which operates through an active, coarse-to-fine mechanism, first grasping the scene's gist from low-spatial-frequency information and then directing attention to salient regions for detailed analysis.

The "thinking-with-images" paradigm in VLMs, where models invoke tools like zoom and crop to enhance fine-grained understanding, represents a related direction focused on improving accuracy. This work aims to bridge the gap by applying this active reasoning capability not only for accuracy but also for computational efficiency, specifically by enabling VLMs to dynamically determine and acquire the minimum necessary visual tokens. AdaptVision introduces a framework that leverages tool use and a specialized reinforcement learning approach to address the adaptive visual token reduction problem, positioning it as an advancement in developing more efficient and biologically inspired VLMs.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation for this research stems from the computational burden introduced by the high volume of visual tokens in modern VLMs, which hinders their efficiency. While current methods attempt to mitigate this, they are often characterized by their passive or limited adaptive capabilities. This leads to a fundamental inquiry: can VLMs autonomously determine the minimum number of visual tokens required for each sample, adapting to varying task requirements and image complexities?

The key objectives and motivations are detailed as follows:

- **Addressing Computational Overhead**: VLMs often process thousands of visual tokens per image, leading to substantial memory and computational costs. The primary goal is to reduce this overhead without compromising performance.
- **Emulating Human Active Vision**: The research is inspired by the human visual system's active, sequential, and adaptive "coarse-to-fine" processing. Humans first extract general scene information from low-resolution input and then selectively focus on specific regions for detailed analysis when necessary. The aim is to imbue VLMs with a similar capability.
- **Advancing Beyond Passive Token Compression**: Existing efficient VLM methods typically employ fixed-ratio token compression or make binary decisions (e.g., low vs. high resolution), which are not sufficiently flexible. A key motivation is to move beyond these passive approaches to enable truly adaptive and dynamic visual token acquisition.
- **Leveraging Visual Tool Use for Efficiency**: Previous work has shown the utility of visual tools (like bounding box cropping) for enhancing VLM accuracy by enabling fine-grained visual understanding. This work aims to re-purpose this "thinking-with-images" paradigm to achieve computational efficiency by using tools to acquire *only* the necessary visual information.
- **Optimizing Dual Objectives (Accuracy and Efficiency)**: The core objective is to learn a policy that effectively balances achieving high answering accuracy with minimizing visual token consumption.
- **Overcoming Reinforcement Learning Challenges**: Training a policy with these dual, often conflicting, objectives using standard reinforcement learning (RL) algorithms like Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) presents specific challenges:
 - **Ambiguous Credit Assignment**: Standard RL assigns a single, sequence-level reward, making it difficult to differentiate the contribution of tool invocation decisions from that of final answer generation. This can lead to suboptimal learning.
 - **Imbalanced Optimization**: In multi-turn sequences (e.g., tool invocation followed by an answer), gradient signals for early tokens (tool tokens) can be diluted by normalization factors, leading to their under-optimization compared to later tokens (answer tokens).

To address these motivations and challenges, the research proposes AdaptVision, an efficient VLM paradigm, and introduces Decoupled Turn Policy Optimization (DTPO) as a specialized RL algorithm designed to enable adaptive visual acquisition and effectively balance accuracy and efficiency during training.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

AdaptVision proposes an efficient VLM framework designed for adaptive visual token acquisition. The methodology integrates a coarse-to-fine processing strategy with a novel reinforcement learning optimization technique.

#### 3.1. Framework Overview

AdaptVision operates in two potential turns:
1. **Initial Processing**: The model first processes an image at a low resolution (1/4th of the original), which reduces visual token usage to 25% of the full-resolution input.
2. **Decision Point**: Based on this initial, compressed visual information and the input question, the VLM decides:
 - To provide a direct answer if sufficient information is available.
 - To invoke a bounding box tool to crop specific key regions from the original high-resolution image (`I_crop`) for further detailed analysis. This second turn acquires additional visual tokens only when necessary.

The overarching goal is to minimize the total number of visual tokens (`n_img = n_low + I_tool * n_crop`, where `I_tool` is an indicator for tool-call responses) while maintaining high answer accuracy. This involves learning a policy that both selectively invokes the tool and accurately identifies the minimal necessary regions.

#### 3.2. Reinforcement Learning Formulation

The decision-making process within AdaptVision is framed as a reinforcement learning problem. The policy `π_θ(o|x)` is optimized to select between a direct answer sequence (`o_1:N`) or a two-turn sequence involving a tool call (`o_1:T`, where `T` are tool tokens) followed by an answer (`o_T+1:N`).

#### 3.3. Reward Design

To balance efficiency and accuracy, a composite reward function `R = R_oc + R_tool` is designed:

- **Outcome Reward (`R_oc`)**:
 - **Accuracy Reward (`R_acc`)**: A binary reward (1 for correct, 0 for incorrect) based on an LLM-as-judge (GPT-4o) evaluating the generated answer against the ground truth.
 - **Format Reward (`R_form`)**: A reward of 0.5 for adhering to specified output formats (e.g., `<think>`, `<answer>`, `<tool call>`), otherwise 0.
 - **Balance Reward (`R_bal`)**: Designed to prevent excessive tool usage or "lucky guesses":
 - A penalty of -0.1 for correct answers that invoke a tool call.
 - A penalty of -0.1 for direct answers that are correct when the probability of a correct response from low-resolution images (`r`) is below a threshold (θ = 0.2). This encourages tool use when low-resolution is insufficient.

- **Tool Reward (`R_tool = R_crop - α * R_area`)**:
 - **Crop Reward (`R_crop`)**: A binary reward (1 for correct, 0 for incorrect) from GPT-4o, assessing if the cropped region `I_crop` contains information relevant to the question.
 - **Relative Area Reward (`R_area`)**: Penalizes oversized bounding boxes. It is calculated as `I(R_acc=1) * I(R_crop=1) * clip((r_a / μ_a) - 1, 0, 1)`, where `r_a` is the area ratio of the cropped region, and `μ_a` is the mean `r_a` for successful tool calls within a batch. This encourages the selection of the smallest informative region. The hyperparameter `α` is set to 2 to balance `R_crop` and `R_area`.

#### 3.4. Decoupled Turn Policy Optimization (DTPO)

To address the challenges of ambiguous credit assignment and imbalanced optimization inherent in multi-turn RL with standard GRPO, DTPO is introduced:

1. **Decoupled Policy Loss**: The policy loss is decoupled by turns. Instead of a single normalization factor for the entire sequence, tool tokens and answer tokens are normalized separately. This prevents tool tokens from being under-optimized due to their shorter length relative to the full sequence.
 `J_DTPO(θ) = E_x,o_i [ (1/ΣT_i) Σ(L_i,t_tool) + (1/Σ(N_i-T_i)) Σ(L_i,t_answer) ]`
2. **Decoupled Advantage Estimation**: DTPO computes distinct advantage estimates for tool-related and outcome-related rewards. The advantage for the `t`-th token `A_i,t` is given by `A^(i)_oc + λ * A^(i)_tool * I(1 <= t <= T_i)` for tool calls, and `A^(i)_oc + λ * A^(i)_tool` for direct answers.
 - `A^(i)_tool` is computed using `R^(i)_tool` (the tool reward).
 - `A^(i)_oc` is computed using `R^(i)_oc` (the outcome reward).
 - `λ` is a hyperparameter (set to 0.3) that controls the trade-off between the two advantages.

This decoupling provides more precise credit assignment and balanced optimization, facilitating more effective learning of the adaptive policy.

#### 3.5. Implementation Details

AdaptVision is built upon the Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct model. Training is performed using the veRL framework, with a batch size of 512, mini-batch size of 32, and a learning rate of 1e-6 for 80 steps. The KL term is dropped during policy optimization. During inference, the vLLM framework is used with a temperature of 0.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of AdaptVision involved comparisons with existing efficient VLM methods across several general VQA benchmarks, including ChartQA, OCRBench, DocVQA, MME, MMVet, RealWorldQA, POPE, MathVista, and MathVerse. The baseline models included the vanilla Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct (100% visual tokens), a down-sample version (25% visual tokens), and various static (FastV, SparseVLM, VisionZip at 50% token retention) and dynamic (VisionThink) efficient VLM methods.

#### 4.1. Performance and Efficiency

- **Superior Performance with Reduced Tokens**: AdaptVision achieved an average performance of 97.9% relative to the vanilla model across all benchmarks, while consuming only 33% of the visual tokens. This performance surpasses all other efficient VLM methods, which had higher token consumption (e.g., VisionThink at 52% or 99%, static methods at 50%) or lower average performance (e.g., Down-Sample at 92.1% with 25% tokens).
- **Enhanced Coarse-to-Fine Reasoning**: Compared to the Down-Sample model, AdaptVision improved accuracy by 5.8% (from 92.1% to 97.9%) with only a 7% increase in visual token consumption (from 25% to 33%), indicating its effectiveness in selectively acquiring necessary visual details.
- **Inference Latency Reduction**: AdaptVision demonstrated a 1.67x overall speedup in end-to-end inference time compared to the vanilla model and VisionThink†. While it required additional token generation for reasoning and tool calls compared to the Down-Sample model, the resulting inference time remained within acceptable limits.

#### 4.2. Ablation Studies on Reward Design

- **Importance of Balance Reward**: The absence of the balance reward `R_bal` caused the model to rapidly collapse into excessive tool use. With `R_bal`, the model learned to adaptively regulate tool usage, only invoking tools when genuinely necessary.
- **Necessity of Tool Reward**: Without the tool reward `R_tool`, the model failed to explore tool use, collapsing to direct answering within a few training steps. `R_tool` was crucial for incentivizing and guiding effective tool exploration.

#### 4.3. GRPO vs. DTPO Training Dynamics

- **GRPO Instability**: Training with vanilla GRPO exhibited unstable dynamics. It struggled to optimize rewards early on, leading to near-zero tool call ratios. Later, it rapidly shifted to excessive tool use, eventually collapsing, demonstrating issues with ambiguous credit assignment and imbalanced optimization.
- **DTPO Stability and Efficiency**: In contrast, DTPO showed a stable and efficient optimization process. Both outcome and tool rewards increased steadily from the beginning, reflecting effective tool exploration. The model converged to a balanced and reasonable tool call ratio, confirming DTPO's effectiveness in learning to balance tool use and direct answering. AdaptVision trained with DTPO significantly outperformed an AdaptVision variant trained without DTPO in both accuracy and token efficiency.

#### 4.4. Adaptive Tool-use Analysis

- **Task-Specific Adaptation**: AdaptVision demonstrated adaptive tool-use behavior. For complex visual tasks requiring fine-grained details (e.g., MathVerse, ChartQA), it frequently invoked the tool. For general understanding tasks (e.g., POPE) where low-resolution information was sufficient, it rarely called the tool, maintaining high efficiency.
- **Stable Learning with DTPO**: Training curves showed that the DTPO-trained model learned to selectively increase tool calls for challenging samples while decreasing them for simpler ones, unlike the GRPO-trained model which resulted in a near 100% tool call ratio across all samples.

#### 4.5. Qualitative Case Studies

Case studies illustrated AdaptVision's intelligent visual reasoning. While a down-sample model might fail to answer correctly due to insufficient low-resolution information, and a vanilla model might succeed but at a high token cost, AdaptVision effectively combines efficiency and accuracy. It initially processes a low-resolution image, identifies the need for more detail, and then intelligently crops only the most relevant region from the high-resolution image, producing correct answers with minimal additional visual token consumption.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The AdaptVision framework presents advancements in the field of Vision-Language Models, particularly concerning computational efficiency and adaptive reasoning. The significance and potential impact of this work can be summarized as follows:

- **Enabling Truly Adaptive VLM Efficiency**: This research provides a paradigm shift from passive or coarsely dynamic visual token compression methods to a truly adaptive approach. By allowing VLMs to autonomously determine the minimum necessary visual tokens for each task, AdaptVision significantly reduces computational overhead and memory consumption, a critical step towards practical deployment of VLMs in diverse environments.
- **Biologically Inspired Design**: The integration of a coarse-to-fine visual reasoning mechanism, inspired by human active vision, enhances the model's ability to process visual information in a more intuitive and efficient manner. This biological inspiration can guide future research in designing more cognitively aligned AI systems.
- **Advancement in Reinforcement Learning for Multi-Objective Optimization**: The introduction of Decoupled Turn Policy Optimization (DTPO) offers a robust solution to the challenging problems of ambiguous credit assignment and imbalanced optimization in multi-turn, multi-objective reinforcement learning tasks. This algorithmic contribution extends beyond VLM applications and could be beneficial for training other complex generative models with sequential decision-making components.
- **Improved Model Deployment and Accessibility**: By drastically reducing the visual token consumption and, consequently, inference time (demonstrating a 1.67x speedup), AdaptVision makes powerful VLMs more accessible and deployable in resource-constrained settings or for real-time applications where latency is a critical factor. This facilitates broader adoption of VLMs in areas such as mobile devices, edge computing, and large-scale enterprise solutions.
- **Foundation for Future Research**: The identified limitations, such as reliance on a single visual tool, fixed initial compression ratio, and a two-turn reasoning constraint, open clear avenues for future research. Expanding the toolset (e.g., deblurring, varied resolution selection), enabling dynamic multi-round tool calls for deeper reasoning, and exploring its applicability to other VLM architectures represent direct extensions of this work. Such extensions could lead to even more sophisticated and versatile efficient VLMs.
- **Enhanced Robustness and Stability in Training**: The demonstrated stability and efficiency of DTPO in training multi-turn policies signify an improvement in the practical aspects of VLM development, allowing for more reliable and controlled optimization of complex, dual-objective models.

In conclusion, AdaptVision contributes a novel, efficient, and biologically inspired VLM framework alongside a robust RL optimization algorithm. Its impact lies in advancing the state-of-the-art in VLM efficiency, making these powerful models more practical and scalable for real-world applications, while also offering a methodological contribution to the broader field of reinforcement learning.
