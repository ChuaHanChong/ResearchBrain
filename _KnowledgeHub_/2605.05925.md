# DexSynRefine: Synthesizing and Refining Human-Object Interaction Motion for Physically Feasible Dexterous Robot Actions

> [!summary] Summary
> DexSynRefine is a framework that synthesizes and refines human-object interaction (HOI) motion into deployable, physically feasible dexterous robot actions. It utilizes a generative prior for task- and state-conditioned HOI trajectories, which are then physically grounded via task-space residual reinforcement learning and robustly transferred to real robots using coupled contact and dynamics adaptation, achieving high success rates across various manipulation tasks.

## Problem

- Collecting robot-specific teleoperation datasets for dexterous manipulation is resource-intensive and does not scale well, particularly for complex, contact-rich tasks.
- Human-object interaction (HOI) data, though accessible, cannot be directly applied to robots due to the 'embodiment gap' between human and robot hands and the lack of explicit dynamic/contact information in kinematic motion.
- Policies trained in simulation struggle during real-world deployment due to sim-to-real discrepancies and the unavailability of privileged information (e.g., object properties, contact forces) that is accessible in simulation.

## Method

- Develops HOI-MMFP, a task- and initial object state-conditioned generative prior, which uses a Transformer-based autoencoder and a conditional flow-matching model to synthesize smooth and generalizable HOI trajectories from sparse demonstrations.
- Formulates a task-space residual Reinforcement Learning approach to refine these synthesized kinematic HOI motions, adding a learned residual signal to the wrist and fingertip components to ensure physical feasibility and robust interaction under contact constraints.
- Implements a contact and dynamics-aware adaptation mechanism for robust sim-to-real transfer, employing GRU-based modules to infer hidden contact states and object dynamics from robot proprioceptive history for the deployed student policy.

## Results

- HOI-MMFP synthesized trajectories with the lowest first-frame wrist error (0.015m translation, 2.50° rotation) and jerk, leading to higher downstream RL success rates (52.6% on 'Pick Up and Hammer' task).
- The Task-Space Residual action representation achieved the highest mean success rate (68.1%) across tasks in simulation, outperforming kinematic retargeting (max 5.8%) and other baselines, particularly on contact-rich tasks.
- The full DexSynRefine pipeline demonstrated robust real-world performance, achieving significant success rate gains (50-70 percentage points) over kinematic retargeting, with up to 9/10 successes on 'Bowl' and 7/10 on 'Hammer'.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Task- and state-conditioned generative priors can effectively synthesize smooth, geometrically consistent, and task-relevant human-object interaction trajectories from sparse human data, overcoming data sparsity limitations.
> - A task-space residual reinforcement learning approach provides a robust and effective method for physically grounding kinematic human demonstrations into robot actions, enabling stable object control and precise finger fine-tuning.
> - Coupled adaptation modules that infer both contact states and object dynamics from proprioceptive history are essential for recovering privileged refinement behaviors, significantly enhancing sim-to-real transfer for dexterous manipulation policies.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Dexterous manipulation by robots, particularly involving multi-fingered hands and contact-rich interactions, has seen advancements through imitation learning from large-scale teleoperation datasets. However, the collection of such datasets is resource-intensive and challenging to scale. An alternative approach involves leveraging more accessible human-object interaction (HOI) recordings, such as internet videos or motion-capture data, as a source of motion priors for robots.

This approach presents two primary difficulties. First, HOI data are often sparse, meaning a limited number of recorded interactions may not cover the diversity of task configurations or initial object states encountered during robot deployment. This sparsity can lead to a lack of a suitable human reference for novel object poses. Second, HOI data are purely kinematic, providing only geometric hand and object motions without explicit information about unobserved contact forces, friction, actuation limits, or other contact-rich dynamics crucial for successful manipulation. These issues are further complicated by the embodiment gap, which refers to the differences in kinematic structure, actuation, and contact geometry between human and robotic hands.

Previous research has attempted to mitigate this gap by using human or object motion as references for goal-conditioned reinforcement learning. However, these methods typically assume the existence of an appropriate reference interaction for each task instance, which limits their applicability under sparse data conditions. The broader challenge in the field, which this work addresses, is to develop a framework capable of synthesizing task- and initial-object-state-conditioned interaction priors from sparse HOI data, physically grounding these priors into feasible robot actions, and enabling their deployment on real robots without reliance on privileged simulation states. DexSynRefine aims to contribute a coupled framework that transforms sparse HOI data from structured motion priors into deployable dexterous robot actions.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to convert sparse and purely kinematic human-object interaction (HOI) demonstrations into executable and physically feasible dexterous robot actions for real-world deployment. This overarching goal is motivated by three specific challenges inherent in using HOI data for robot learning:

1. **Addressing the Sparsity of HOI Demonstrations**: Human demonstrations, even when collected, are typically sparse and cannot encompass the full range of initial object poses or task configurations that a robot might encounter. Direct use of such limited data often means a suitable human reference is unavailable for a given new task instance. Therefore, a key motivation is to develop a method that can synthesize novel, consistent, and smooth HOI reference trajectories conditioned on the task and initial object state, thereby extending the utility of sparse demonstrations beyond their observed scope.

2. **Bridging the Kinematic-to-Dynamic Gap and Embodiment Mismatch**: HOI data are kinematic, describing motion geometry without capturing the underlying contact forces, friction, and actuation dynamics essential for successful physical interaction. Furthermore, human and robot hands differ significantly in their kinematics, actuation mechanisms, and contact properties, creating an "embodiment gap." Directly retargeting human kinematic motions to a robot often results in physically infeasible or unstable actions. The motivation here is to physically ground these synthesized kinematic references through a learning process that accounts for robot-specific embodiment constraints and contact-rich dynamics, transforming them into feasible robot actions. The paper specifically aims to investigate which action representation is most effective for this physical grounding.

3. **Enabling Robust Real-World Deployment without Privileged Information**: In simulation, privileged information such as precise object properties and contact states are readily available to a robot policy. However, these are not directly observable from onboard sensors in real-world environments. Policies trained in simulation often struggle with "sim-to-real" transfer due to this information asymmetry. The motivation is to develop an adaptation mechanism that allows the robot to infer or estimate missing physical context (e.g., contact forces, object dynamics) from its proprioceptive history, enabling the physically grounded policies to operate robustly on a real robot without relying on privileged simulation states.

By addressing these challenges, the research aims to provide a more scalable and robust pipeline for teaching dexterous manipulation skills to robots using human demonstrations.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

DexSynRefine operates through a coupled, three-stage framework: HOI motion collection and augmentation, conditional HOI motion prior synthesis, and task-space residual refinement with contact-and-dynamics adaptation.

#### 3.1. HOI Motion Collection and Object-Centric Augmentation

The initial stage involves collecting a small number of human-object interaction (HOI) demonstrations and then augmenting them to expand coverage.
- **Data Collection**: Seven HOI demonstrations were collected per task for five dexterous manipulation tasks, including pick-and-place, tool use, and object reorientation. A multi-camera setup was used with FoundationPose for object tracking and Manus Gloves for hand and wrist tracking. Initial object yaw was varied across demonstrations.
- **Object-Centric Augmentation**: To overcome data sparsity and increase the range of initial object poses, collected trajectories underwent object-centric augmentation. Initial object poses were perturbed using planar SE(2) transformations (varying x, y, and yaw). The reaching phase of the trajectory was then interpolated from a canonical hand start pose to the augmented initial hand configuration, while the manipulation phase was rigidly transformed to preserve the relative hand-object geometry. For symmetric objects, additional random rotations around the symmetry axis were applied. This process expanded the dataset to approximately 300 augmented trajectories per task, each resampled to a fixed length of 220 frames.

#### 3.2. HOI-MMFP: Conditional HOI Motion Prior Synthesis

This stage synthesizes spatially consistent and smooth HOI reference trajectories conditioned on task and initial object state. HOI-MMFP extends Motion Manifold Flow Primitives (MMFP) to coupled hand-object motion.
- **Manifold Learning**: A Transformer autoencoder maps an augmented trajectory to a compact latent code, **z***, and reconstructs the trajectory. The autoencoder is optimized with a loss function combining reconstruction error, a latent norm penalty, and a smoothness penalty applied to decoded interpolated latents, encouraging manifold continuity.
- **Conditional Latent Flow Matching**: With the autoencoder fixed, a latent flow-matching model is trained. This model is conditioned on a variable **c** = (**u**, **o₀**), where **u** is a task embedding derived from a pretrained text encoder, and **o₀** is the initial object pose. A Diffusion Transformer-based vector field **v_θ** learns to map a noisy latent **z_s** to the true latent **z***.
- **Inference**: To generate a new HOI trajectory, the system solves an Ordinary Differential Equation (ODE) using the learned vector field to progress from a noisy latent (sampled from a normal distribution) to a target latent, which is then decoded by the autoencoder's decoder into a full, task-consistent kinematic HOI reference trajectory **ˆτ**.

#### 3.3. Task-Space Residual Refinement with Contact-and-Dynamics Adaptation

The synthesized HOI reference **ˆτ** is kinematically plausible but not directly executable by a robot. This stage physically grounds the reference and adapts it for real-world deployment.
- **Task-Space Residual Action Integration**: At each policy step, a refinement policy predicts a task-space residual signal **u_t**. This signal is smoothed using an exponential moving average (EMA), integrated, and clipped to predefined bounds to produce a residual action **∆a_t**. This **∆a_t** is added to the wrist and fingertip targets derived from **ˆτ_t** to obtain a refined task-space target. A PD controller then converts these targets into joint commands for the robot, using quintic task-space interpolation and inverse kinematics to bridge the 20 Hz policy rate to the 120 Hz simulation rate.
- **Privileged Teacher Policy Learning**: A teacher policy is trained in a simulation environment using Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) with an asymmetric actor-critic architecture.
 - The **critic** observes a comprehensive "privileged" state, including simulator-only information (e.g., true contact states and forces, object dynamics parameters) in addition to proprioception, the synthesized reference, and object dynamics parameters.
 - The **actor** receives a dynamics latent (compressed representation of object intrinsic parameters) and predicts the residual action.
 - The reward function encourages object tracking, fingertip and wrist guidance, stable contact, and penalizes unintended collisions and excessive residual changes.
- **Deployable Student Contact-and-Dynamics Adaptation**: To enable real-world deployment where privileged information is unavailable, a student policy is distilled from the teacher.
 - This involves maintaining hand-centric and full-body proprioceptive histories over a recent window of policy steps.
 - A GRU-based **contact module** is trained to predict binary contact states and contact forces from the hand-centric history.
 - A GRU-based **dynamics module** is trained to infer the dynamics latent from the full-body proprioceptive history.
 - The student actor, initialized from the teacher, then uses these inferred contact and dynamics estimates, along with proprioception and the synthesized HOI reference, to predict its residual actions. The student policy is trained with losses for imitating the teacher's actions, matching inferred dynamics latents to true dynamics latents, and accurately predicting contact states and forces. This allows the student policy to refine motions on a real robot using only proprioceptive history.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments were designed to evaluate the effectiveness of DexSynRefine across its three main stages, focusing on reference synthesis quality, physical grounding, deployable adaptation, and real-world transfer across five dexterous manipulation tasks.

#### 4.1. Motion Synthesis Quality and Downstream RL Usability (Q1)

- **Spatial Perturbation Robustness**: HOI-MMFP demonstrated superior spatial consistency when generating motions for perturbed initial object poses. It achieved the lowest first-frame wrist error (0.015 m / 2.50°), outperforming DiT-Full (0.018 m / 2.65°) and significantly surpassing a Task-Conditioned VAE (TC-VAE) (0.121 m / 12.88°). Qualitatively, HOI-MMFP and DiT-Full preserved a consistent canonical hand start, while TC-VAE produced distorted trajectories.
- **Temporal Smoothness and Downstream Utility**: HOI-MMFP generated trajectories with the lowest translational and rotational wrist jerk (29.83 m/s³ and 101.08 rad/s³, respectively), indicating smoother motions compared to TC-VAE and DiT-Full, which were more prone to high-jerk movements. In downstream residual RL on the "Pick Up and Hammer" task, HOI-MMFP references led to a higher success rate (60.3% vs. 49.1% for DiT-Full) and lower object errors, demonstrating that manifold-based synthesis produces references that are both spatially grounded and smooth enough for subsequent physical refinement.

#### 4.2. Comparing Action Representations for Physical Grounding (Q2)

- A comparative study of five action representations was conducted, keeping the synthesized HOI reference and reward design constant. Direct kinematic retargeting, without learned refinement, resulted in low success rates (≤ 5.8%), confirming the necessity of physical grounding.
- Absolute action baselines, such as "Task-Space Absolute," largely failed across tasks, while "Joint-Space Absolute" performed adequately only on simpler pick-and-place tasks ("Book," "Bowl") but poorly on contact-rich tasks (≤ 13% SR).
- The proposed "Task-Space Residual" representation achieved the highest mean success rate (68.1%) and performed best on all contact-rich tasks ("Pringles Bottle," "Hammer," "Watering Can"). While "Joint-Space Absolute" exhibited slightly better performance on "Book" due to a larger direct action space in low-contact scenarios, "Task-Space Residual" demonstrated robustness across diverse manipulation types. This indicated that local corrections to task-space wrist and fingertip targets were more effective for physical grounding than regenerating full motions.

#### 4.3. Effectiveness of Contact-and-Dynamics Adaptation (Q3)

- Ablation studies on two contact-rich tasks ("Pick Up and Hammer," "Pick and Pour Watering Can") investigated the necessity of jointly adapting for contact and dynamics.
- The "Full" teacher policy (with both adaptations) achieved the highest success rates and lowest errors. Ablations that adapted only dynamics ("w/o Contact") or only contact ("w/o Dynamics") showed significantly reduced performance, often failing, indicating that both forms of adaptation are jointly necessary for effective residual refinement. Dynamics adaptation addresses object-level physical variations, while contact adaptation handles rapid finger-object interaction changes.
- The "Full" student policy, distilled from the teacher, largely maintained performance, closely matching the teacher on "Watering Can" (51.8% vs. 52.4%). A larger performance gap was observed on "Hammer" (44.3% vs. 60.3%), suggesting challenges in inferring complex pre-swing contact transitions from proprioceptive history alone.

#### 4.4. Real-World Performance Evaluation (Q4)

- The complete DexSynRefine pipeline was evaluated on a real robot across all five tasks, with success rates reported over 10 trials per task.
- Direct kinematic retargeting of the synthesized HOI-MMFP reference achieved a maximum of 2/10 successful trials and completely failed on two tasks (0/10), confirming its inadequacy for real-world deployment.
- In contrast, DexSynRefine delivered absolute improvements of 50–70 percentage points in success rates across tasks. It achieved 9/10 successes on "Bowl," 8/10 on "Pringles Bottle," and 7/10 on "Hammer," and transferred to all five tasks.
- Remaining failures in tasks like "Watering Can" (finger clearance issues during release) and "Book" (toppling during reorientation) indicated that contact clearance and dynamic balancing persisted as primary sources of sim-to-real error.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

DexSynRefine presents a comprehensive framework that addresses several bottlenecks in transferring human dexterous manipulation skills to robots. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Scalable Skill Acquisition**: The work offers a scalable alternative to the labor-intensive and expensive process of collecting large-scale robot teleoperation datasets. By demonstrating the ability to synthesize coherent and executable robot actions from sparse, kinematic human-object interaction (HOI) data, DexSynRefine reduces the data requirements for teaching complex dexterous skills. This could enable faster deployment of new manipulation abilities in various robotic applications.

- **Robust Physical Grounding**: The framework highlights the importance of physically grounding kinematic human demonstrations. It identifies task-space residuals as an effective action representation for this purpose, particularly in contact-rich scenarios. This approach allows the robot policy to make local corrections to human-like motions while respecting the robot's specific embodiment and the physical dynamics of interaction, leading to more stable and feasible robot behaviors.

- **Enhanced Sim-to-Real Transfer**: The inclusion of a contact-and-dynamics adaptation module is a key contribution to improving the robustness of policies transferred from simulation to real-world robots. By inferring crucial but unobservable physical context (like contact states and object dynamics) solely from proprioceptive history, the system mitigates the "sim-to-real gap," enabling more reliable deployment without reliance on privileged simulation information or additional specialized sensors (e.g., tactile sensors).

- **Generalization within Tasks**: HOI-MMFP's ability to synthesize new HOI references conditioned on unseen initial object states contributes to the generalization capabilities of the robot within a given task. This means the robot can execute the task even when the object is presented in configurations not explicitly demonstrated by a human, enhancing autonomy and flexibility.

The integration of motion synthesis, physical grounding, and deployable adaptation into a single, coordinated pipeline demonstrates a path towards making sparse human motion priors truly executable dexterous robot actions. This could accelerate the development of robotic systems capable of performing intricate manipulation tasks in unstructured environments.

Future work directions identified by the authors include extending the approach to a single multi-task residual policy, exploring the integration of tactile sensing to improve contact reconstruction, and enabling reactive re-planning by periodically updating the HOI prior based on online perception of object perturbations. These advancements could further enhance the framework's versatility and robustness.
