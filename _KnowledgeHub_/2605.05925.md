# DexSynRefine: Synthesizing and Refining Human-Object Interaction Motion for Physically Feasible Dexterous Robot Actions

> [!summary] Summary
> DexSynRefine is a framework that synthesizes and refines human-object interaction (HOI) motion into deployable, physically feasible dexterous robot actions. It utilizes a generative prior for task- and state-conditioned HOI trajectories, which are then physically grounded via task-space residual reinforcement learning and robustly transferred to real robots using coupled contact and dynamics adaptation, achieving high success rates across various manipulation tasks.

## Problem

- Collecting robot-specific teleoperation datasets for dexterous manipulation is resource-intensive and does not scale well, particularly for complex, contact-rich tasks.
- Human-object interaction (HOI) data, though accessible, cannot be directly applied to robots due to the 'embodiment gap' between human and robot hands and the lack of explicit dynamic/contact information in kinematic motion.
- Policies trained in simulation struggle during real-world deployment due to sim-to-real discrepancies and the unavailability of privileged information (e.g., object properties, contact forces) that is accessible in simulation.

## Method

- Develops HOI-MMFP, a task- and initial object state-conditioned generative prior, which uses a Transformer-based autoencoder and a conditional flow-matching model to synthesize smooth and generalizable HOI trajectories from sparse demonstrations.
- Formulates a task-space residual Reinforcement Learning approach to refine these synthesized kinematic HOI motions, adding a learned residual signal to the wrist and fingertip components to ensure physical feasibility and robust interaction under contact constraints.
- Implements a contact and dynamics-aware adaptation mechanism for robust sim-to-real transfer, employing GRU-based modules to infer hidden contact states and object dynamics from robot proprioceptive history for the deployed student policy.

## Results

- HOI-MMFP synthesized trajectories with the lowest first-frame wrist error (0.015m translation, 2.50° rotation) and jerk, leading to higher downstream RL success rates (52.6% on 'Pick Up and Hammer' task).
- The Task-Space Residual action representation achieved the highest mean success rate (68.1%) across tasks in simulation, outperforming kinematic retargeting (max 5.8%) and other baselines, particularly on contact-rich tasks.
- The full DexSynRefine pipeline demonstrated robust real-world performance, achieving significant success rate gains (50-70 percentage points) over kinematic retargeting, with up to 9/10 successes on 'Bowl' and 7/10 on 'Hammer'.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Task- and state-conditioned generative priors can effectively synthesize smooth, geometrically consistent, and task-relevant human-object interaction trajectories from sparse human data, overcoming data sparsity limitations.
> - A task-space residual reinforcement learning approach provides a robust and effective method for physically grounding kinematic human demonstrations into robot actions, enabling stable object control and precise finger fine-tuning.
> - Coupled adaptation modules that infer both contact states and object dynamics from proprioceptive history are essential for recovering privileged refinement behaviors, significantly enhancing sim-to-real transfer for dexterous manipulation policies.
