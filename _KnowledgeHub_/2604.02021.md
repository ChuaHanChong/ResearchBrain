# Bridging Discrete Planning and Continuous Execution for Redundant Robot

> [!summary] Summary
> A framework bridges the gap between discrete reinforcement learning path planning and continuous execution for redundant manipulators, achieving a 100% planning success rate in challenging environments and reducing joint increment norms by an order of magnitude. This is accomplished through a lightweight interface that regularizes path geometry and enhances execution stability.

## Problem

- Direct execution of discrete, voxel-based reinforcement learning (RL) paths on redundant manipulators using point-wise inverse kinematics (IK) leads to unstable and dynamically implausible motions.
- Anisotropic action sets in discrete RL planners create 'saw-tooth' paths, step-size jitter, and unnecessary turns, reducing path efficiency and smoothness.
- Lack of a coherent interface design between discrete path planning and continuous robot control, often treated as simple concatenation, leads to issues like abrupt joint transitions and instability near kinematic singularities.

## Method

- A two-layer bridging framework is introduced, comprising a ==geometry layer== for path regularization and a ==physics layer== for robust execution, without modifying the core RL planner.
- The geometry layer uses ==step-normalized 26-connected Cartesian actions== and a ==geometric tie-breaking== mechanism to generate shorter, smoother, and more efficient discrete paths, followed by cubic spline smoothing and micro-step control.
- The physics layer employs ==Task-Priority Damped Least-Squares (TP-DLS)== inverse kinematics with ==null-space joint-centering==, trust-region clipping, and joint velocity constraints to ensure smooth, stable, and singularity-robust continuous execution.

## Results

- The improved planner achieved a **100%** success rate across sparse, medium, and dense obstacle environments, substantially outperforming baselines (**56-70%** success rate).
- Generated paths were significantly shorter and had fewer turns (e.g., in dense environments, **0.68m** path length and **5.02** turns vs. baseline 396m and 3901 turns).
- The **TP-DLS** executor demonstrated an order-of-magnitude advantage in joint increment norm (DQ95 in range of 10^-2 rad vs. 10^-1 to 10^0 rad for baseline) and reduced maximum joint velocities and accelerations, indicating much smoother joint movements.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Regularizing the action space of discrete RL planners through step normalization and geometric tie-breaking significantly improves the quality and navigability of generated paths.
> - A task-priority inverse kinematics approach, combined with null-space objectives and robust step control, is essential for translating discrete plans into dynamically feasible and smooth joint trajectories for redundant manipulators.
> - Addressing the interface mismatch as a dedicated design problem, rather than a mere concatenation, is crucial for practical deployment of discrete planning algorithms in continuous robotic systems.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in this paper is positioned at the intersection of reinforcement learning (RL) based motion planning and continuous robot control for redundant manipulators.

**Reinforcement Learning for Motion Planning:**
Recent advancements in deep reinforcement learning (DRL) have led to its adoption in robotic manipulator motion planning, offering advantages in dynamic and semi-structured environments where classical planners might be less adaptable. Voxel-grid representations and finite action sets are frequently employed in DRL-based frameworks to simplify the state-action space, enabling stable policy learning and reliable deployment for tasks such as obstacle avoidance (Jia et al., 2020; Landgraf et al., 2021; James et al., 2022). These methods often focus on the generation of discrete end-effector paths. However, the existing literature frequently treats the subsequent execution of these discrete paths on continuous-time dynamic systems, particularly redundant manipulators, as a secondary concern, often relying on simple point-wise inverse kinematics (IK) solvers. This approach often overlooks the potential for misalignment between the discrete plan and continuous execution, leading to issues like joint instability and dynamically implausible motions.

**Redundancy Resolution and Continuous Control:**
For redundant manipulators (e.g., 7-Degrees of Freedom arms), techniques like Damped Least-Squares (DLS) inverse kinematics (Deo and Walker, 1995) and Task-Priority (TP) control (Nakamura et al., 1987; Basso and Pettersen, 2020) are established methods for handling singularities, resolving redundancy, and managing multiple objectives (e.g., end-effector tracking, posture optimization, joint-limit avoidance). While these classical control strategies are robust, they are typically applied to already smoothed reference trajectories or as point-wise solvers, assuming a continuous and well-behaved input. They do not intrinsically address the artifacts that can arise from direct execution of paths generated by discrete-action planners.

**Trajectory Smoothing and Optimization:**
A separate area of research focuses on trajectory smoothing and optimization, which takes piecewise-linear paths (e.g., from sampling-based planners) and converts them into continuous, dynamically feasible trajectories. Methods include collision-aware spline construction, time-parameterization, and optimization-based approaches (Pan et al., 2012; Kim et al., 2020; Liu et al., 2021). These techniques often involve computationally intensive dedicated optimization problems for each path, which may not be suitable for real-time or frequently changing tasks where RL is often preferred for its look-up table efficiency.

**The Identified Gap:**
This paper specifically addresses a structural mismatch: the direct concatenation of discrete, voxel-based RL path planners with continuous robot execution. The authors identify that anisotropic action sets in discrete planners and the point-wise nature of conventional numerical inverse kinematics lead to issues such as "saw-tooth" paths, step-size jitter, abrupt joint transitions, and instability near singular configurations when attempting to execute discrete RL-generated paths. The work aims to bridge this specific gap by introducing a lightweight interface that regularizes both the geometric properties of the discrete planning output and the differential update law of the continuous execution layer, without requiring modification of the core RL planning algorithm or computationally expensive full trajectory optimization.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a framework that effectively bridges the gap between discrete path planning generated by voxel-based reinforcement learning and the continuous execution requirements of redundant robot manipulators. The motivation for this work stems from several challenges observed when directly deploying discrete RL-generated paths:

1. **Execution Instability and Poor Quality:** When discrete end-effector paths, typical outputs of voxel-grid RL planners, are executed on 7-DoF redundant manipulators using conventional point-wise numerical inverse kinematics (IK), several issues arise. These include fluctuating step sizes, abrupt changes in joint configurations, numerical instability near kinematic singularities, and sensitivity to joint limits. The resulting trajectories, while numerically convergent, are often dynamically implausible or undesirable for real-world robotic operations.
2. **Anisotropic Action Set Artifacts:** Standard 26-neighbor action sets in 3D voxel grids consist of moves with varying Euclidean lengths (axis-aligned, face-diagonal, space-diagonal). When combined with approximate value functions and exploration strategies like ε-greedy, this anisotropy can lead to suboptimal path geometries. This manifests as "saw-tooth" patterns, paths with unnecessary turns, and end-effector oscillations around obstacles, resulting in longer paths and increased wear on the robot.
3. **Lack of Coherent Interface:** The interface between discrete path planning and continuous execution is frequently treated as a direct concatenation, rather than a design problem. Existing path smoothing or advanced IK techniques often operate in isolation or assume a smooth reference input, failing to address the fundamental misalignments introduced by discrete planning.
4. **Desire for Improved Practical Deployment:** Beyond merely generating a collision-free path (Question 1: How to reach the goal?), practical deployment requires a path that is short, smooth, and compliant with safety and actuation constraints (Question 2: How to reach the goal better?). This work aims to enhance the quality of these paths to improve real-world executability.

To address these motivations, the paper sets out to achieve the following specific objectives:

- **Analyze the Mismatch:** Clearly identify and characterize the structural mismatch between voxel-based RL paths and continuous execution on redundant manipulators, specifically detailing how anisotropic action metrics and point-wise IK contribute to observed artifacts.
- **Propose a Lightweight Bridge Architecture:** Design a modular framework that modifies the interface components rather than the core RL planner. This involves regularization on the planning side and a robust execution layer on the control side.
- **Regularize Discrete Path Geometry:** Introduce modifications to the planning process, specifically step-normalized 26-neighbor Cartesian actions and a geometric tie-breaking mechanism, to generate geometrically smoother and more efficient discrete paths.
- **Enhance Continuous Execution Robustness:** Implement a Task-Priority Damped Least-Squares (TP-DLS) inverse kinematics layer that tracks smoothed reference paths, incorporates joint-centering in the null space, and applies trust-region clipping and joint velocity constraints for improved joint-space smoothness and singularity robustness.
- **Benchmark and Validate Improvements:** Develop a comprehensive benchmark suite and a set of quantitative metrics for a 7-DoF manipulator in various cluttered environments to rigorously demonstrate improvements in path length, joint-space smoothness, and physical executability.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology introduces a two-layer bridging framework, operating at the interface between a voxel-based Q-learning planner and the continuous control of a 7-DoF redundant manipulator. The core RL planner itself is utilized as a black box without internal modifications.

#### 3.1. Geometry Layer: Voxel-Based Q-Learning and Action Regularization

This layer is responsible for generating and smoothing the end-effector path in task space.

- **Q-learning Planner on a 3-D Voxel Grid:** A tabular Q-learning agent is trained on a uniform 3D grid of cubic voxels representing the robot's reachable workspace. The agent learns a policy to move from a start voxel to a goal voxel while avoiding obstacles. The reward function includes a positive reward for reaching the goal, a large negative reward for collisions or exiting the grid, and a small step penalty to encourage shorter paths. The output of this layer, prior to smoothing, is a sequence of discrete end-effector Cartesian locations, `x_RL_k`.

- **Normalized 26-Connected Actions with Geometric Tie-Break:** This is a key modification to the action space compared to baseline approaches.
 - **Action Normalization:** Instead of using raw index-space displacements `v = (Δi, Δj, Δk)` (e.g., `(1,0,0)` vs `(1,1,1)`) with varying Euclidean lengths, each `v` is mapped to a Cartesian displacement `a(v)` with a fixed physical step length `δ`. This makes the planner "isotropic" in physical space, ensuring all axial, face-diagonal, and space-diagonal moves produce the same end-effector displacement magnitude.
 - **Geometric Tie-Break:** When multiple actions at a given state `s` yield Q-values that are within a small threshold `ε_Q` of the maximum Q-value (i.e., they are "near-optimal"), a tie-breaking rule is invoked. This rule selects an action that minimizes the turning angle relative to the previous action `θ(a, a_k-1)` and maximizes an approximate look-ahead clearance `d_obs(x, a)`. A geometric score `J_geo(a)` is computed, and the action maximizing this score is chosen. This mechanism biases the planner towards smoother and safer paths without altering the fundamental Q-value update rule.

- **Baseline Planners for Comparison:**
 - `Original`: Uses a 6-connected action set (axis-aligned movements) with a step length equal to the grid resolution.
 - `NoNorm`: Uses the raw 26-connected index-space directions as unnormalized physical steps, without a tie-breaking rule.

- **Task-Space Spline Smoothing and Micro-Step Control:** The discrete `x_RL_k` polyline from the Q-learning planner contains sharp corners. To provide a smooth reference for the execution layer:
 - **Cubic Spline Fitting:** Three independent cubic splines are fitted to the (x, y, z) coordinates of `x_RL_k`, resulting in a continuous parametric curve `x_spl(t)`.
 - **Macro-Waypoint Resampling:** The continuous curve `x_spl(t)` is then resampled into `M` "macro" waypoints, `x_ref_i`, chosen to be approximately arc-length uniform with a desired step size (e.g., 5 mm).
 - **Micro-Step Constraint:** Within the inverse kinematics routine, a micro-step constraint ensures that the Euclidean distance between consecutive points delivered to the IK solver does not exceed a predefined threshold (e.g., < 1 mm). If needed, intermediate points are inserted via linear interpolation. This guarantees bounded end-effector displacement per IK iteration for stability.

#### 3.2. Physics Layer: Task-Priority Damped Least-Squares Execution

This layer computes the joint trajectory `q_i` for the 7-DoF manipulator, tracking the smoothed reference sequence `x_ref_i`.

- **Task-Priority Damped Least-Squares (TP-DLS) Inverse Kinematics:** For each reference point `x_ref_i`, the solver iteratively computes joint updates.
 - **Primary Task: Position Control:** The end-effector position error `e_p = x_ref_i - p(q)` is addressed using a damped least-squares pseudo-inverse of the translational Jacobian `J_p(q)`. The joint update `Δq_1` is calculated with a proportional gain `k_p`. The rotational component of the Jacobian is not actively controlled, allowing the end-effector orientation to adapt as redundancy is exploited.
 - **Null-Space Joint-Centering Objective:** A secondary objective is introduced to keep joint configurations away from their limits. An error `e_c = q_mid - q` (where `q_mid` is the midpoint of joint limits) is projected into the null space of the primary task's Jacobian `N_1(q)`. This generates a null-space joint update `Δq_2 = α_c N_1^T e_c` that affects posture without disturbing the primary end-effector position task to first order.
 - **Combined Update:** The total joint update is `Δq = Δq_1 + Δq_2`.

- **Step-Length and Velocity Clipping:** To maintain dynamic feasibility and prevent large, abrupt joint movements:
 - A global scalar bound `Δq_max` is applied to the Euclidean norm of `Δq`. If `||Δq||_2` exceeds this, `Δq` is scaled down.
 - Per-joint bounds `Δq_max,j` are enforced, clipping each `Δq_j` component individually. These bounds correspond to maximum joint velocities for a given time step `Δt`.

- **Iterative Solving:** For each `x_ref_i`, the TP-DLS process iterates until the position error `||e_p||` falls below a specified tolerance (1 mm) or a maximum iteration count is reached.

- **Baseline Executor for Comparison:**
 - `Num-IK`: A conventional numerical inverse kinematics solver. It solves a full-pose IK problem for each reference waypoint independently, using the previous solution as an initial guess. This baseline does not incorporate task priority, null-space joint centering, or explicit step-length limits. Both executors (`Num-IK` and `TP-DLS`) operate on the *same* smoothed reference paths generated by the `Improved` planner for a fair comparison of execution quality.

#### 3.3. Experimental Setup

- **Simulation Platform:** MATLAB 7-DoF robotic arm simulation.
- **Environments:** Static environments categorized by obstacle density: Sparse (0-30 obstacles), Medium (31-99 obstacles), Dense (>= 100 obstacles). Spherical obstacles with randomized positions and radii.
- **Tasks:** 1,000 start-goal pairs per density class (3,000 total tasks), with collision-free start and goal configurations.
- **Metrics:**
 - **Planner-level:** Success rate, path length, number of turns, number of discrete steps, minimum end-effector-obstacle distance. (Failed tasks assigned penalty values for length, turns, steps).
 - **Execution-level:** 95th percentile of joint increment norm (DQ95), maximum joint velocity, maximum joint acceleration, minimum joint-limit margin, number of backtracks/retries, total computation time.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of the proposed bridging framework involved comparisons against baseline planners and execution layers across various obstacle densities.

#### 4.1. Planning Interface Regularization

The results of the planner comparison, summarized in Table 1, highlight the impact of the proposed modifications to the discrete planning layer.

- **Success Rate:** The `Improved` planner achieved a 1.00 (100%) success rate across all sparse, medium, and dense obstacle environments. This significantly outperforms the `Original` (0.56–0.68) and `NoNorm` (0.58–0.70) baselines, which showed a higher failure rate, particularly in dense scenarios. This indicates enhanced navigability and robustness in complex environments.
- **Path Length and Turns:** The `Improved` method consistently generated shorter paths and fewer turns compared to both baselines. For instance, in dense environments, the `Improved` planner produced paths averaging approximately 0.68 m with 5.02 turns, whereas `Original` and `NoNorm` planners resulted in paths of 396.468 m and 499.377 m with 3901.6 and 3237.8 turns, respectively. This demonstrates a substantial improvement in path efficiency and geometric quality.
- **Minimum Clearance:** The minimum end-effector-obstacle clearance values remained nearly identical across all methods and density classes (e.g., approximately 0.21 m for sparse, 0.16 m for medium, and 0.13 m for dense). This suggests that the improvements in path length and turns were not achieved by compromising safety margins or proximity to obstacles.
- **Mechanism Effectiveness:** The findings confirm that step normalization and the geometric tie-breaking mechanism effectively suppress irregular step alternation and unnecessary backtracking, which are inherent issues in unnormalized 26-neighbor expansions. The `Improved` planner also produced highly uniform waypoint spacing, which is beneficial for subsequent processing.

#### 4.2. Impact of Execution Layer on Trajectory Quality

For the execution layer comparison, both the `Num-IK` (baseline numerical IK) and `TP-DLS` (proposed) solvers tracked identical smoothed reference paths generated by the `Improved` planner for 150 tasks. Both solvers achieved a 100% success rate in tracking these paths, maintaining end-effector position errors within 1 mm for all reference points. Metrics such as path length, number of turns, and minimum clearance remained unchanged, as expected since the input paths were identical.

Table 2 details the execution-level metrics:

- **Joint Increment Norm (DQ95):** The `TP-DLS` method exhibited an order-of-magnitude advantage over `Num-IK` in the 95th percentile of the joint increment norm. `TP-DLS` kept `DQ95` in the range of 10^-2 rad, while `Num-IK` ranged from 10^-1 to 10^0 rad. This indicates that `TP-DLS` generated significantly smoother joint movements, with individual joint steps remaining below typical warning thresholds (e.g., 0.1 rad).
- **Maximum Joint Velocity (MaxVel) and Acceleration (MaxAcc):** `TP-DLS` substantially reduced both `MaxVel` and `MaxAcc` compared to `Num-IK`. For instance, `MaxAcc` was reduced by over an order of magnitude (e.g., in dense environments, from 15659.236 for `Num-IK` to 4438.005 for `TP-DLS`). This suppression of abrupt joint transitions and dynamic spikes contributes to more dynamically feasible and physically realistic motions.
- **Minimum Joint-Limit Margin (JointMargin):** Both solvers maintained comparable margins from joint limits, with `TP-DLS` showing a slight advantage in most tasks (e.g., 0.014 rad for `TP-DLS` vs 0.0002 rad for `Num-IK` in dense scenes). This indicates that the null-space joint-centering objective in `TP-DLS` improved joint health without compromising overall safety.
- **Computational Cost (Backtracks and Time):** `TP-DLS` incurred a higher computational cost, reflected in increased `Backtracks` and `Time` metrics. This is attributed to its strategy of employing multiple small-step iterations and retries in locally challenging sections, ensuring stable convergence within the specified error tolerance. For offline evaluation, this overhead was considered acceptable.
- **Convergence Stability:** `TP-DLS` demonstrated stable convergence, with final position errors for interpolated reference points typically stabilizing within the 10^-4 to 10^-3 m range, consistently below the 1 mm threshold. This confirms its reliable trajectory tracking performance across various scenarios without numerical divergence.

Qualitatively (Figure 3), the `Proposed bridge` (normalized 26-neighbor + TP-DLS) equalizes step sizes and maintains joints away from limits, in contrast to the `Baseline` (unnormalized actions + Num-IK) which exhibits irregular steps and configurations close to joint limits.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a framework that offers a solution to a persistent challenge in robotics: effectively translating discrete, high-level plans into smooth, dynamically feasible continuous robot motions. The significance and potential impact of this work are manifold:

1. **Enhanced Practicality of Voxel-based RL:** The bridging framework makes voxel-grid reinforcement learning more practical and deployable for redundant robot manipulators. By regularizing path geometry and improving execution smoothness, it addresses common shortcomings that have limited the direct application of such discrete planners in real-world scenarios.
2. **Improved Trajectory Quality and Robot Longevity:** The substantial reduction in peak joint accelerations and velocities, along with more uniform joint increments, contributes to smoother robot operation. This can lead to decreased mechanical wear and tear, lower energy consumption, and extended operational lifespan for robotic manipulators.
3. **Increased Planning Reliability and Success Rate:** The improvements on the planning side, particularly the 100% success rate in challenging, dense environments, indicate a more robust and reliable planning capability. This is critical for autonomous systems operating in dynamic and cluttered spaces.
4. **Decoupling of Planning and Control:** The design of the bridging layer as an agnostic interface between the planner's output and the robot's control system allows for independent development and optimization of both components. This means existing or future grid-based RL planners can benefit from this execution bridge without internal modifications to their learning algorithms or network structures.
5. **Foundation for Advanced Applications:** By providing a reliable method for generating high-quality executable trajectories, this work lays a foundation for more complex robotic tasks. Future integration with formal safety guarantees, online execution, and application to physical robot platforms, multi-arm coordination, and dexterous manipulation becomes more feasible.
6. **Addressing a Fundamental Interface Problem:** The paper explicitly treats the interface between discrete planning and continuous execution as a design problem, rather than a trivial concatenation. This highlights the importance of considering the entire system's behavior, from abstract planning to physical execution, for successful robotic deployment.

In summary, this research contributes to the field by providing a robust, lightweight, and generic solution that improves the quality and executability of paths generated by discrete reinforcement learning planners on redundant manipulators, thereby advancing the state of the art in autonomous robotics.
