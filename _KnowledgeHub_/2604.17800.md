# ReFineVLA: Multimodal Reasoning-Aware Generalist Robotic Policies via Teacher-Guided Fine-Tuning

> [!summary] Summary
> VinRobotics researchers introduce ReFineVLA, a framework that fine-tunes pre-trained Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models using teacher-guided natural-language rationales to instill explicit multimodal reasoning. This approach improves VLA models' interpretability and generalization capabilities, achieving up to a 9.6% increase in task success on complex manipulation tasks in SimplerEnv benchmarks compared to baselines.

## Problem

- Existing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models often learn direct functional input-action mappings, bypassing explicit, step-by-step reasoning processes.
- This limitation hinders interpretability and generalization, particularly in complex, long-horizon manipulation tasks or under out-of-distribution conditions.
- Traditional VLAs exhibit a narrow focus on immediate visual cues for action, indicating a reactive mapping rather than a comprehensive multimodal understanding grounded in explicit rationale.

## Method

- **ReFineVLA** augments robotic datasets with explicit ==natural-language reasoning annotations== (observation, situation analysis, spatial reasoning, task planning) generated by an expert teacher model (Gemini 2.0).
- Employs ==selective transfer fine-tuning==, freezing lower layers of pre-trained VLA models (e.g., **SpatialVLA**) while adapting upper layers and the policy head to efficiently integrate reasoning.
- Uses a ==multi-objective loss== that jointly optimizes for ==behavioral cloning== (action prediction) and ==language modeling== (reasoning generation) to instill both action competence and explicit rationales.

## Results

- ReFineVLA achieved an average success rate of **47.7%** on WidowX tasks, a **+5.0%** improvement over SpatialVLA, with a notable **+21.4%** gain on the 'Put Spoon on Towel' task.
- On Google Robot tasks, ReFineVLA surpassed baselines by **+2.3%** in visual matching (**76.6%** avg) and **+3.5%** in variant aggregation (**68.8%** avg), including a **+9.6%** gain on the 'Move Near' task.
- Attention-map visualizations showed a shift from narrow focus to semantically meaningful regions post-fine-tuning, indicating improved holistic multimodal understanding and the ability to generate coherent step-by-step reasoning rationales.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Injecting explicit multimodal reasoning supervision, derived from an expert teacher, effectively guides VLA models to learn sophisticated decision-making logic, enhancing their interpretability and generalization.
> - Selective fine-tuning targeting the upper layers of VLA models enables efficient adaptation and integration of high-level reasoning without compromising the foundational low-level feature extraction from pre-training.
> - Jointly optimizing for action prediction and reasoning generation leads to more robust and structured multimodal representations, improving performance in complex and compositional manipulation tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotics research has seen advancements in developing policies that generalize beyond specific training data, particularly with the emergence of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. These models aim to leverage the generalization capabilities of large Vision-Language Models (VLMs), such as CLIP, LLaVA, and Phi-3-Vision, and adapt them for robotic control by training on extensive robotic datasets. VLAs like RT-2, OpenVLA, and SpatialVLA represent a significant direction towards generalist robot policies capable of executing diverse tasks across various environments and embodiments.

Despite their ability to translate multimodal observations with linguistic instructions into robotic actions, a limitation of current VLA models is their tendency to learn direct functional input-action mappings. This approach often bypasses explicit, step-by-step reasoning processes. Consequently, while these models may perform adequately in familiar scenarios, their interpretability and generalization can diminish in complex, long-horizon manipulation tasks or under out-of-distribution conditions that require a deeper understanding of sequential logic and adaptability. Previous research on Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning in large language models (LLMs) and VLMs has demonstrated its utility in breaking down complex problems into intermediate steps, which has inspired applications in embodied AI for tasks such as textual planning or generating robotic trajectories. However, the explicit integration of multimodal reasoning directly into the policy learning process of VLA models, by training them to jointly generate actions and explanatory rationales derived from a teacher, remains an area with potential for further exploration.

This work addresses the identified gap by proposing a framework, ReFineVLA, that injects explicit multimodal reasoning into pre-trained VLA models. By leveraging an expert teacher to generate detailed natural-language rationales, the research aims to guide VLA models to learn to reason about their actions, thereby enhancing their multimodal understanding, interpretability, and generalization capabilities for complex robotic manipulation tasks. The approach situates itself at the intersection of generalist robot policies and CoT reasoning for embodied AI, seeking to combine the broad generalization of VLAs with structured, explicit reasoning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the reasoning capabilities of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, thereby improving their interpretability and generalization performance on complex, long-horizon robotic manipulation tasks. The authors identify a significant limitation in existing VLA models: they typically learn direct functional mappings from multimodal observations to actions, often omitting the explicit logical steps required for sophisticated decision-making. This absence of explicit reasoning can be particularly problematic for tasks demanding nuanced understanding, adaptability to novel conditions, and robust performance in out-of-distribution scenarios.

The motivation for ReFineVLA stems from several observations:
- **Lack of Explicit Reasoning in VLAs:** Current VLAs, despite inheriting robustness from VLMs and training on large datasets, often lack sophisticated and adaptive multimodal reasoning. Their training objectives primarily optimize for accurate next-action prediction without incorporating reasoning-based Chain-of-Thought (CoT). This leads to models finding correlations sufficient for seen tasks but without explicitly learning the underlying causal or sequential logic.
- **Impact on Interpretability and Generalization:** The direct input-action mapping limits a VLA model's ability to provide interpretable decision-making processes. Furthermore, this limitation negatively impacts their generalization performance when faced with novel objects, environments, instructions, or cross-embodiments, where a deeper understanding of observation-action relationships is critical.
- **Narrow Focus of Attention:** Through attention map visualizations, the authors observed that traditional VLAs tend to focus narrowly on specific visual cues directly associated with immediate actions. This indicates a reactive, direct mapping rather than a comprehensive multimodal understanding grounded in explicit rationale, hindering performance in tasks requiring broader contextual and spatial reasoning.
- **Potential of Reasoning Supervision:** The authors hypothesize that injecting explicit multimodal reasoning supervision can mitigate these limitations. By guiding VLA models to learn the sequential logic and physical intelligence behind robotic actions within the context of language-based instructions, the model's understanding of observation-action relationships could be improved, leading to enhanced performance on complex and compositional manipulation tasks.

Therefore, ReFineVLA aims to instill explicit reasoning capabilities directly into learned robotic policies by fine-tuning pre-trained VLAs with teacher-guided rationales. This approach is intended to jointly optimize policy learning and explicit reasoning adaptation, preserving the pre-trained generalization while enhancing reasoning capacity.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

ReFineVLA proposes a multimodal reasoning-aware framework that fine-tunes existing VLA models by incorporating teacher-guided reasoning rationales. The methodology consists of three main components: multimodal reasoning annotation generation, selective transfer fine-tuning, and a multi-objective learning formulation.

#### 3.1. Multimodal Reasoning Annotation Generation

The core of ReFineVLA's approach is to augment standard robotic datasets, which typically contain only observation-action pairs (o_i, a_i), with explicit reasoning annotations (r_i).
1. **Teacher Model:** An expert reasoning-based teacher model, specifically Gemini 2.0, is utilized to generate these rationales.
2. **Structured Prompting:** For each observation (o_i), which includes visual images (I_i) and language instructions (l_i), the teacher model is prompted with a structured query. This prompt guides the teacher to articulate reasoning across four categories:
 - **Observation:** Identifying objects and features in the images.
 - **Situation Analysis:** Interpreting the current scene and task context.
 - **Spatial Reasoning:** Analyzing spatial relationships between objects relevant to the task.
 - **Task Planning:** Formulating a sequence of logical steps and sub-actions to achieve the goal.
3. **Dataset Enrichment:** The generated natural-language rationales (r_i) are paired with the original (o_i, a_i) tuples, creating an enriched dataset D' = {(o_i, a_i, r_i)}. This dataset serves as structured multimodal reasoning signals, bridging the gap between perception and action and providing insight into the decision-making process. Approximately 125,000 robot trajectories from the BridgeData-v2 and Google RT1 Robot datasets were annotated using this pipeline.

#### 3.2. Selective Transfer Fine-Tuning for Efficient Adaptation

ReFineVLA employs a selective fine-tuning strategy to adapt pre-trained VLA models. This approach is designed to:
1. **Preserve General Features:** Most parameters, particularly the lower layers responsible for basic feature extraction in the vision and language encoders, are frozen. This prevents overfitting to the specialized reasoning-enriched dataset and retains the broad, generalizable representations learned during pre-training.
2. **Efficient Adaptation:** Fine-tuning only a subset of parameters significantly reduces computational costs (time, memory, FLOPs), making the process more scalable and practical.
3. **Targeted Knowledge Infusion:** Higher-level cognitive processing, such as abstract reasoning, is hypothesized to reside in the upper layers of VLA models, which are typically involved in decision-making and complex feature integration. Therefore, selective fine-tuning targets these upper layers and the policy head, enabling efficient integration of explicit reasoning capabilities without compromising foundational low-level feature extraction. Empirical validation (Figure 8) indicated that freezing the first 24 transformer layers provided an optimal balance.

#### 3.3. Learning Objectives

The training objective of ReFineVLA is formulated as a multi-objective loss function that jointly optimizes for accurate action prediction and reasoning generation:
L_ReFineVLA = L_action + λ_r * L_reasoning

1. **Action Prediction Loss (L_action):** This is a behavioral cloning loss, calculated as the negative log-likelihood of the ground-truth actions (a_i) given the multimodal inputs (o_i). It ensures the model learns to predict the correct robotic actions.
2. **Reasoning Generation Loss (L_reasoning):** This is a standard language modeling negative log-likelihood loss, guiding the model to produce the teacher-generated rationales (r_i) given the multimodal inputs (o_i). It encourages step-by-step thinking and fosters structured multimodal reasoning.
3. **Hyperparameter (λ_r):** This weighting factor controls the influence of the reasoning loss. Its optimal value was empirically determined through ablation studies to be λ_r = 0.3 (Figure 8).

The training process (Algorithm 1) iteratively updates the selected learnable parameters of the pre-trained VLA model, refining it to jointly predict actions and generate rationales.

#### 3.4. VLA Backbone and Implementation

For the VLA backbone, the authors utilized **SpatialVLA** (Qu et al., 2025), a 3.5B-parameter model based on the PaliGemma 2 VLM backbone. SpatialVLA was pre-trained on large datasets like Open X-Embodiment and RHT20, providing a robust foundation with established capabilities in generalist policies and spatial awareness. Training was conducted on multi-node clusters with NVIDIA H100 GPUs.

#### 3.5. Attention Map Visualization

As part of their analytical approach, the authors also conducted attention map visualizations (Section 3 and Section 5.3). This involved extracting and aggregating attention weights for action tokens across all layers and attention heads during inference. A top-k aggregation strategy was used to highlight salient input regions. This qualitative analysis was employed to observe changes in the model's focus before and after ReFineVLA fine-tuning, aiming to empirically reveal enhanced multimodal understanding.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of ReFineVLA was conducted across a suite of simulated manipulation benchmarks on SimplerEnv, encompassing both WidowX and Google Robot tasks. The framework consistently outperformed existing state-of-the-art VLA baselines, demonstrating improvements in task success rates and enhanced interpretability.

#### 4.1. Overall Performance

Across the SimplerEnv benchmarks, ReFineVLA achieved the highest average success rates in nearly all settings, indicating improved learning behavior and generalization capacity.

#### 4.2. WidowX Robot Tasks (Table 1)

- ReFineVLA achieved an average success rate of **47.7%**, which is a **5.0% improvement** over the second-best method, SpatialVLA (42.7%).
- On the "Put Eggplant in Yellow Basket" task, ReFineVLA achieved 95.2% success, closely approaching SpatialVLA's 100.0%.
- Significant improvements were observed in tasks requiring complex spatial reasoning:
 - "Put Spoon on Towel": ReFineVLA scored 42.9%, a 21.4% improvement over SpatialVLA (20.8%).
 - "Put Carrot on Plate": ReFineVLA achieved 33.3%, an 8.3% improvement over SpatialVLA (25.0%).
These results suggest enhanced spatial awareness and manipulation capabilities due to reasoning-guided supervision.

#### 4.3. Google Robot Tasks (Table 2)

- **Visual Matching Setting:** ReFineVLA achieved an average success rate of **76.6%**, outperforming SpatialVLA (74.3%) by **2.3%**.
 - Notably, ReFineVLA surpassed all baselines in the "Move Near" task with **95.3% accuracy**, a **9.6% improvement** over SpatialVLA.
- **Variant Aggregation Setting:** ReFineVLA yielded an average success rate of **68.8%**, surpassing SpatialVLA (65.3%) by **3.5%**.
 - In this setting, ReFineVLA showed improved performance on "Open/Close Drawer" with 34.4%, an **8.2% gain** over SpatialVLA (26.2%).
These experimental results highlight ReFineVLA's robustness across spatial, visual, and temporal variations, which the authors attribute to its structured reasoning learning objective.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies

1. **Effect of Reasoning Supervision (Table 3):**
 - Introducing explicit reasoning supervision through ReFineVLA led to performance increases. For instance, in visual matching settings, task accuracy improved from 70.8% (transfer fine-tuning only) to 76.6% (with reasoning learning).
 - The most substantial improvements were observed in high-level tasks such as "Move Near" (9.6% over SpatialVLA) and "Open/Close Drawer" (8.2% in variant aggregation). This supports the hypothesis that targeted fine-tuning with explicit reasoning loss promotes more structured and transferable policies.

2. **Training Loss and Action Accuracy (Figure 4):**
 - During fine-tuning on the Fractal dataset, ReFineVLA's training L1 loss decreased more rapidly and maintained consistently lower values compared to the baseline.
 - ReFineVLA's training action accuracy progressively increased and consistently surpassed the baseline across all training steps. These observations suggest that explicit multimodal reasoning supervision provides meaningful learning signals, guiding the optimization process towards more accurate and stable multimodal policy representations.

3. **Reasoning Accuracy (Figure 5):**
 - Reasoning accuracy improved rapidly during early training steps and then stabilized, indicating effective incorporation and robust internalization of reasoning-guided signals within the model.

4. **Attention Behavior (Figure 6):**
 - Prior to ReFineVLA fine-tuning, VLA models exhibited a narrow focus on immediate action targets, often neglecting broader contextual and spatial relationships.
 - Post-fine-tuning with ReFineVLA, a consistent shift in attention was observed towards semantically meaningful regions, indicating that the model learned to reason more holistically by integrating visual and linguistic information over time. This suggests improved structured, multimodal representations.

5. **Chain-of-Thought Reasoning (Figure 7, Figure 9 in Appendix):**
 - ReFineVLA demonstrated the ability to generate step-by-step reasoning procedures alongside robotic actuator actions for complex and long-horizon tasks, such as "Close the drawer" or "Move the coke can near the orange." The model could identify objects, estimate relative positions, and guide the robot through sub-actions to achieve the goal task.

6. **Reasoning Loss Weight (λ_r) (Figure 8, left):**
 - An ablation study on the reasoning loss weight (λ_r) showed that setting λ_r = 0.3 achieved the best performance, yielding a 9.7% improvement in task success. Both excessively low and high values of λ_r led to decreased performance, indicating the importance of balancing action prediction and reasoning learning.

7. **Number of Frozen Layers (Figure 8, right):**
 - Freezing the first 24 transformer layers resulted in an optimal 8.2% improvement in task success rate. This suggests that while lower layers should retain pre-trained perceptual grounding, upper layers are critical for absorbing high-level reasoning supervision. Freezing too few or too many layers led to suboptimal performance.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of ReFineVLA marks an advancement in the development of generalist robotic policies by addressing a critical limitation of traditional Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models: the absence of explicit, step-by-step multimodal reasoning. By injecting teacher-guided natural-language rationales into the fine-tuning process, ReFineVLA demonstrates that VLA models can be trained to not only predict actions but also to reason about them, thereby enhancing their interpretability and generalization capabilities.

The significance of this work lies in several areas:
- **Enhanced Interpretability:** By explicitly generating reasoning rationales, ReFineVLA offers a more transparent view into the model's decision-making process. This interpretability can be crucial for debugging, verifying safety, and increasing user trust in autonomous robotic systems.
- **Improved Generalization:** The empirical results indicate that models fine-tuned with ReFineVLA exhibit superior generalization to novel and diverse environmental variations, particularly in complex and long-horizon manipulation tasks. This is evidenced by consistent performance gains across different tasks and robot embodiments in the SimplerEnv benchmarks.
- **Robustness to Variations:** The framework's improved performance in "variant aggregation" and "visual matching" settings suggests greater robustness to changes in backgrounds, lighting conditions, distractors, and camera poses, which are common challenges in real-world robotic deployments.
- **Efficient Adaptation:** The selective transfer fine-tuning approach ensures that the benefits of explicit reasoning are integrated efficiently, preserving the generalizable features of large pre-trained VLA models while minimizing computational overhead.

The potential impact of ReFineVLA is substantial for the field of robotics:
- **Foundation for More Capable Robots:** By fostering deeper multimodal understanding, ReFineVLA contributes to building more intelligent and adaptable robotic agents capable of handling a wider array of tasks in unstructured environments without extensive re-training.
- **Advancement in Human-Robot Interaction:** More interpretable robotic behavior can facilitate more effective human-robot collaboration, as humans can better understand why a robot is performing a certain action.
- **New Research Avenues:** This work establishes a promising direction for reasoning-driven generalist VLA-based robot policies. It opens avenues for future research into scaling reasoning supervision through techniques like human-in-the-loop refinement or self-improving teacher models using reinforcement learning.
- **Real-World Application Potential:** While currently evaluated in simulation, the framework's enhanced robustness and reasoning capabilities suggest a stronger foundation for sim-to-real transfer, potentially accelerating the deployment of sophisticated robotic systems in real-world scenarios.
- **Long-Horizon Task Solving:** The demonstrated ability to reason through and plan for complex, multi-step tasks suggests that ReFineVLA could be a component in developing robots capable of tackling more intricate and extended manipulation sequences.

Future explorations could involve incorporating memory mechanisms or temporal context to further enhance reasoning across extended task horizons and rigorously evaluating the framework's robustness in real-world robotic systems.
