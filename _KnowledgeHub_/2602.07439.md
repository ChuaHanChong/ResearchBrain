# TextOp: Real-time Interactive Text-Driven Humanoid Robot Motion Generation and Control

> [!summary] Summary
> TextOp introduces a two-level architecture enabling real-time interactive text-driven motion generation and control for humanoid robots. This system allows a Unitree G1 robot to execute diverse, complex behaviors and smooth transitions based on live text commands, achieving an average user interaction latency of 0.73 seconds.

## Problem

- Developing flexible, intuitive, and real-time control interfaces for advanced humanoid robots remains a persistent challenge.
- Existing methods rely on predefined motion trajectories or constant human teleoperation, which limits adaptability and real-time command revision.
- Bridging high-level natural language intent with low-level, high-frequency control signals for stable humanoid robot execution is difficult.

## Method

- A two-level architecture is employed: a high-level motion latent diffusion model (LDM) for interactive, text-driven kinematic trajectory generation, and a low-level reinforcement learning (RL) policy for dynamic motion tracking.
- The high-level generator uses a specialized robot-skeleton DoF-based local incremental motion representation, tailored for robotic kinematics and invariant to global pose.
- The low-level tracking policy's training data is augmented with motions produced by the high-level generator to enhance robustness to online-generated trajectories and mitigate the sim-to-real gap.

## Results

- TextOp enabled a Unitree G1 robot to perform seamless, diverse, and complex behaviors (e.g., dancing, jumping) controlled by real-time interactive text commands with an average user interaction latency of **0.73** seconds.
- The motion generator achieved competitive performance in offline evaluations, with an FID of **3.072** and R-precision (R@1) of **0.300**, outperforming various baselines in semantic alignment and motion quality.
- The motion tracking policy demonstrated high success rates (around **0.99**) and low tracking errors on generator-produced motions, and the robot maintained stability and recovered gracefully from external perturbations during physical execution.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A two-level cognitive-physical architecture effectively separates semantic understanding from real-time physical execution, enabling interactive language-driven control.
> - Designing motion representation specific to robot kinematics (DoF-based local incremental) significantly improves the quality and physical plausibility of generated motions.
> - Augmenting the motion tracker's training data with samples from the motion generator improves its ability to robustly execute novel, dynamically generated reference motions in real-world deployment.
