# TextOp: Real-time Interactive Text-Driven Humanoid Robot Motion Generation and Control

> [!summary] Summary
> TextOp introduces a two-level architecture enabling real-time interactive text-driven motion generation and control for humanoid robots. This system allows a Unitree G1 robot to execute diverse, complex behaviors and smooth transitions based on live text commands, achieving an average user interaction latency of 0.73 seconds.

## Problem

- Developing flexible, intuitive, and real-time control interfaces for advanced humanoid robots remains a persistent challenge.
- Existing methods rely on predefined motion trajectories or constant human teleoperation, which limits adaptability and real-time command revision.
- Bridging high-level natural language intent with low-level, high-frequency control signals for stable humanoid robot execution is difficult.

## Method

- A two-level architecture is employed: a high-level motion latent diffusion model (LDM) for interactive, text-driven kinematic trajectory generation, and a low-level reinforcement learning (RL) policy for dynamic motion tracking.
- The high-level generator uses a specialized robot-skeleton DoF-based local incremental motion representation, tailored for robotic kinematics and invariant to global pose.
- The low-level tracking policy's training data is augmented with motions produced by the high-level generator to enhance robustness to online-generated trajectories and mitigate the sim-to-real gap.

## Results

- TextOp enabled a Unitree G1 robot to perform seamless, diverse, and complex behaviors (e.g., dancing, jumping) controlled by real-time interactive text commands with an average user interaction latency of **0.73** seconds.
- The motion generator achieved competitive performance in offline evaluations, with an FID of **3.072** and R-precision (R@1) of **0.300**, outperforming various baselines in semantic alignment and motion quality.
- The motion tracking policy demonstrated high success rates (around **0.99**) and low tracking errors on generator-produced motions, and the robot maintained stability and recovered gracefully from external perturbations during physical execution.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A two-level cognitive-physical architecture effectively separates semantic understanding from real-time physical execution, enabling interactive language-driven control.
> - Designing motion representation specific to robot kinematics (DoF-based local incremental) significantly improves the quality and physical plausibility of generated motions.
> - Augmenting the motion tracker's training data with samples from the motion generator improves its ability to robustly execute novel, dynamically generated reference motions in real-world deployment.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of humanoid robotics has made significant strides in whole-body motion tracking, enabling complex and coordinated movements on physical hardware. However, a persistent challenge lies in developing flexible, intuitive, and real-time control interfaces for these advanced robots. TextOp directly addresses this gap by introducing a novel control paradigm that leverages natural language as a continuous and interactive driving signal.

Historically, humanoid controllers have largely operated under two main paradigms:

- **Predefined Motion Trajectories:** These systems rely on pre-programmed sequences of movements, offering high fidelity for specific tasks but lacking adaptability to changing user intent or dynamic environments. While valuable for reproducing known motions, their "fixed plan" nature limits their use in interactive scenarios.
- **Continuous Human Teleoperation:** Methods like motion capture suits or VR devices allow direct human guidance, providing real-time flexibility. However, they demand constant human involvement, making long-term or autonomous operation impractical and labor-intensive.

More recently, **text-conditioned motion generation** has emerged as a powerful technique, translating semantic descriptions into robot movements. Early approaches often generated full motion sequences *offline*, which were then tracked by the robot. While these demonstrated the power of language, they inherently suffered from the "fixed plan" limitation, offering minimal support for real-time command revision. Other end-to-end models attempting direct language-to-control mapping also typically design behavior upfront.

Separately, the **humanoid animation community** has explored interactive motion generation for virtual characters, where users can steer motions on the fly. These systems treat conditioning signals and generated motions as time-varying streams, enabling responsive synthesis. However, they traditionally lack integration with real-robot whole-body control, often neglecting dynamic constraints and the complexities of the sim-to-real gap.

TextOp positions itself at the intersection of these advancements, explicitly bridging the gap between interactive language-based intent expression and real-time, physically executable humanoid control. It aims to combine the semantic richness and flexibility of text-driven generation with the real-time responsiveness of interactive systems, all while ensuring physical stability and smooth execution on a real robot. This work represents a significant step towards creating more autonomous and human-friendly humanoid robot interfaces, moving beyond static plans and constant manual intervention towards truly intelligent, adaptable, and language-guided agents.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of TextOp is to enable **real-time interactive text-driven humanoid motion generation and control**. This overarching goal can be broken down into several specific motivations and sub-objectives:

1. **Bridging High-Level Intent with Low-Level Execution:** The core motivation stems from the recognized gap between expressing complex human intent (e.g., "dance," "jump," "strum guitar") and the low-level, high-frequency control signals required for stable humanoid robot execution. TextOp seeks to create a seamless interface where natural language commands directly translate into robust physical movements.
2. **Achieving Real-time Responsiveness and On-the-Fly Modification:** Current methods either rely on static, pre-defined trajectories or constant teleoperation. TextOp aims to overcome this by allowing users to stream language commands and modify instructions *during execution*, enabling dynamic and adaptive robot behavior in response to evolving user intent.
3. **Enabling Smooth Transitions Across Diverse, Challenging Behaviors:** A critical challenge in humanoid control is ensuring fluidity and stability when switching between disparate motion types (e.g., from dancing to jumping). TextOp seeks to facilitate smooth and continuous transitions across a wide range of complex skills within a single, unbroken motion execution, without requiring restarts or explicit reprogramming.
4. **Ensuring Physical Plausibility and Robustness on Real Hardware:** Unlike interactive motion generation for virtual characters, TextOp is designed for physical robots. This necessitates robust whole-body control that accounts for dynamic constraints, maintains balance, and can recover from perturbations, thereby addressing the sim-to-real gap.
5. **Developing a Universal and Flexible Control Framework:** The goal is to create a system that is not limited to a few specific actions but can interpret and execute a broad vocabulary of natural language commands, making the humanoid controller more versatile and widely applicable.

In essence, TextOp is motivated by the desire to unlock free-form intent expression for humanoid robots, making them more intuitive, interactive, and autonomous partners in diverse real-world applications.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

TextOp employs a novel **two-level architecture** that elegantly separates high-level cognitive understanding from low-level physical execution, allowing for real-time interactive control:

1. **High-Level: Interactive Motion Generation (6.25 Hz)**
 - **Model:** This level uses an autoregressive motion latent diffusion model (LDM) combined with a Variational Autoencoder (VAE). The VAE first learns a compact latent space for motion, and the LDM then models the text-conditioned distribution of these motion latents. Both are Transformer-based networks.
 - **Functionality:** Conditioned on the current language input (processed by a CLIP encoder) and recent motion context (past 2 frames), the model continuously generates short-horizon (8 future frames) kinematic trajectories. This autoregressive nature allows for continuous generation and dynamic updates based on streaming language commands. Classifier-free guidance is used to enhance semantic alignment.
 - **Key Design Choice 1: Robot-Skeleton Motion Representation:** Instead of traditional human skeleton (e.g., SMPL) representations which use 3-DoF ball joints, TextOp uses a specialized DoF-based local incremental motion representation tailored for robotic skeletons (which typically have single-DoF rotational joints). This representation is compact, enforces kinematic constraints, and is invariant to global pose, making it more suitable for the Unitree G1 robot. It encodes root orientation (roll, pitch, incremental yaw), contact indicators, local root translation increments, root height, joint positions, and joint-wise increments.

2. **Low-Level: Dynamic Motion Tracking (50 Hz)**
 - **Model:** This level consists of a robust whole-body motion tracking policy, implemented as an MLP-based network, trained using goal-conditioned Reinforcement Learning (RL) in a large-scale physical simulation environment (IsaacLab).
 - **Functionality:** The policy receives the robot's proprioceptive state (joint positions, velocities, base velocities, etc.) and the short-horizon kinematic reference motion generated by the high-level module (5 future frames). It then translates these kinematic references into high-frequency joint-level control commands (actions) to be executed on the physical robot, ensuring stable and smooth whole-body motion while minimizing tracking error. PPO is used as the optimization algorithm.
 - **Key Design Choice 2: Data Augmentation via Motion Generation:** To mitigate the distribution gap between offline motion datasets and the online, potentially novel or noisy, trajectories generated by the high-level diffusion model, the training data for the tracking policy is augmented. Realistic text streams are used to produce synthetic motions with the high-level generator, which are then used to train the tracker. This exposes the policy to the "generator's dialect" of motion, enhancing its robustness during real-robot deployment.

**Data Preparation:** The training data is built upon the large-scale AMASS motion capture dataset, enriched with a proprietary dataset containing complex dance and martial arts motions. All motions are retargeted from SMPL to the robot skeleton using GMR and resampled to 50 Hz. A filtering process discards motions that cannot be reliably tracked, ensuring data quality for both generation and tracking. Language annotations for motion generation are sourced from BABEL, which provides frame-level descriptions essential for natural switching. Mirror augmentation is also applied.

**Deployment:** The system is deployed on a **Unitree G1 humanoid robot** (29 DoF). The motion tracker runs onboard at 50 Hz using ONNX Runtime, while the motion generator runs on an external workstation at 6.25 Hz using TensorRT. Communication between the modules is via network, with a motion buffer synchronizing execution. User text commands are streamed in real time to the generator, enabling interactive control.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

TextOp's effectiveness is validated through extensive real-robot experiments and comprehensive offline evaluations, addressing key research questions about its precision, stability, responsiveness, and component contributions.

**A. Real-world Deployment (Addressing Q1: Can TextOp achieve precise, stable, and responsive whole-body behaviors in the real world?)**

- **Complex Skill Demonstrations & Interactive Control:** TextOp successfully enables the Unitree G1 robot to execute seamless sequences of diverse skills—from expressive gestures (bow, wave) to complex physical tasks (dancing, jumping, playing instruments)—driven by real-time interactive text commands. This "one-take" capability for long-horizon tasks highlights coherence and stability. Users can modify commands on the fly, demonstrating smooth composition and transitions between skills.
- **Robustness Analysis:** Quantitative evaluation over 30-second continuous trials with both randomly sampled and looping commands shows high success rates (e.g., 16/20 for random, 10/10 for "punch") and strong tracking fidelity metrics (low E_g-mpjpe, E_mpjpe, E_acc, E_vel). Furthermore, the robot demonstrates graceful recovery from external perturbations (pushes/pulls) while maintaining its ongoing text-driven motions, underscoring its real-world stability.
- **Real-Time Performance:** The system exhibits fast and consistent response times. Text encoding takes 7.64 ± 2.56 ms, motion generation 29.63 ± 3.56 ms, and tracking policy 2.15 ± 0.11 ms. Critically, the average user interaction latency (from command input to physical response) is 0.73 ± 0.10 s, which is sufficiently low for responsive real-time interaction.

**B. Offline Evaluation (Addressing Q2 & Q3)**

**1. Motion Generation Evaluation (Addressing Q2: Can the motion generator produce high-quality and semantically consistent motions from text commands in an interactive setting?)**

- **Effectiveness of Robot-Skeleton Representation:** TextOp's proposed robot-skeleton motion representation achieves state-of-the-art performance across most metrics for both segment-level quality (matching text commands) and transition-level smoothness.
 - It significantly outperforms baselines like DART+Retarget (trained on SMPL then retargeted), BeyondMimic, HumanML3D-style, and RobotMDM in FID (lower is better, e.g., TextOp 3.072 vs. DART+Retarget 4.837) and R-precision (higher is better, e.g., TextOp R@1 0.300 vs. RobotMDM 0.262).
 - It demonstrates superior Diversity and lower Multimodal Distance, indicating better distributional similarity to real data and stronger semantic alignment.
 - While DART+Retarget shows slightly better smoothness in AUJ (due to retargeting post-processing), TextOp remains competitive.
- **Reasoning for Improvement:** The compact DoF-based representation reduces modeling complexity, and the carefully designed incremental root-state representation improves data efficiency.

**2. Motion Tracking Evaluation (Addressing Q3: Can the motion tracking policy robustly execute diverse reference motions, especially those produced by the generator?)**

- **Robustness to Generator-Produced Motions:** All TextOp variants (TextOp-M+G, TextOp-G, TextOp-M) achieve high success rates (around 0.99) and low tracking errors on generator-produced motion data, significantly outperforming prior general motion trackers (TWIST2, GMT, Any2Track) which show much lower success rates (e.g., TWIST2 0.922, GMT 0.834, Any2Track 0.714) and higher errors. This confirms the tracker's ability to robustly execute text-conditioned references.
- **Impact of Data Augmentation:**
 - **TextOp-G (trained exclusively on generator-produced motions)** performs best on generator-produced motions, highlighting the benefit of aligning the tracker's training distribution with deployment-time inputs.
 - **TextOp-M (trained exclusively on motion capture data)** shows the strongest generalization to *unseen* motion capture data (SnapMoGen dataset), but performs slightly worse on generator-produced motions.
 - **TextOp-M+G (trained on a combination of motion capture and generated motions)** offers the best trade-off, maintaining strong performance on generator-produced motions while exhibiting competitive generalization to unseen motion capture data (Succ 0.814 on SnapMoGen vs. TextOp-M 0.823, but much better on generator data). This validates the mixed data strategy for deployment-oriented robustness and generalization.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

TextOp introduces a highly significant advancement in humanoid robotics by establishing a **new control paradigm** where natural language serves as a continuously revisable, real-time control signal. This shifts the interaction model from discrete, predefined tasks or constant teleoperation to a more intuitive, adaptive, and human-centric form of guidance.

The primary significance and potential impact include:

- **Enhanced Human-Robot Interaction:** By enabling fluid, real-time linguistic control, TextOp dramatically lowers the barrier for human users to command complex robot behaviors. This paves the way for more natural, collaborative, and expressive interactions with humanoid robots in diverse settings, from domestic assistance to industrial applications.
- **Increased Autonomy and Adaptability:** The system's ability to dynamically interpret and respond to changing text commands on-the-fly moves humanoid robots closer to true autonomy. Robots can adapt their behavior to evolving situations or user preferences without needing human intervention for every decision, enhancing their utility in dynamic environments.
- **Foundation for General-Purpose Humanoid Robots:** The versatility demonstrated in executing a wide range of skills (dancing, jumping, gesturing) from text suggests a significant step towards general-purpose humanoid robots capable of learning and performing diverse tasks through natural language instruction. This is a critical component for future AI agents embodied in physical forms.
- **Novel Design Contributions:** The two key design choices—the **robot-skeleton motion representation** and **data augmentation via motion generation**—are impactful contributions to the field. The former ensures that motion generation models are well-aligned with robot kinematics, leading to higher quality and more physically plausible motions. The latter specifically addresses the sim-to-real gap and distribution mismatch, a crucial challenge in deploying learning-based control policies to real hardware.
- **Catalyst for Future Research:** The paper explicitly identifies future research directions, such as integrating environment perception and interactive physical reasoning. Overcoming these limitations would allow TextOp to operate in more complex, unconstrained real-world scenarios, adapting to obstacles and interacting with objects. Combining these capabilities with advanced language models into intelligent agent systems could lay a solid foundation for fully autonomous, instruction-driven humanoid robots.

In conclusion, TextOp represents a crucial step towards creating intelligent, interactive, and robust humanoid robots that can seamlessly understand and execute complex human intentions expressed through natural language, marking a significant milestone in the journey towards sophisticated human-robot collaboration.
