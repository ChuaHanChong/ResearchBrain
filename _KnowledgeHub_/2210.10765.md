# When to Ask for Help: Proactive Interventions in Autonomous Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> A framework named Proactive Agent INTerventions (PAINT) enables autonomous reinforcement learning agents to proactively detect and manage irreversible states in complex environments. This approach substantially reduces the need for human interventions and label queries while achieving comparable or superior task performance across various continuous control benchmarks.

## Problem

- Autonomous reinforcement learning agents struggle in real-world environments characterized by irreversible states from which they cannot recover independently, requiring constant human supervision.
- Existing safe RL methods often assume prior knowledge of safety labels or require a high frequency of human feedback (e.g., querying labels at every time-step), which is impractical and costly.
- The high cost of human monitoring and manual resets limits the scalability and practical deployment of RL agents, particularly in physical robotic systems.

## Method

- **PAINT** modifies the reward function to strongly penalize actions leading to ==irreversible states==, assigning them a fixed, extremely low Q-value to discourage their visitation.
- A neural network classifier learns to estimate ==state reversibility==, utilizing a highly label-efficient ==binary search algorithm== that queries only ==O(log T) labels per trajectory== instead of O(T).
- The agent proactively requests a human reset when its learned classifier predicts it has entered an irreversible state, optionally performing additional ==exploration steps== before the actual intervention.

## Results

- PAINT significantly reduced human interventions (e.g., **80 resets** in Tabletop Organization vs. **3000+** for baselines) and label queries (e.g., **3,260 labels** for **200,000 states** in Maze vs. **200,000** for baselines) across various tasks.
- The framework achieved task success rates comparable to or better than existing safe RL and reversibility-aware methods, with intervention counts **plateauing over training**, indicating effective learning to avoid irreversible states.
- Ablation studies confirmed the **critical role** of post-prediction exploration steps in enhancing the learned reversibility classifier and the overall efficiency of the PAINT framework.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - By modifying the Bellman update to assign a fixed, low Q-value to actions leading to irreversible states, the agent is effectively incentivized to avoid them without requiring an oracle.
> - The sequential nature of irreversible states within a trajectory allows for a binary search-based labeling strategy, drastically reducing the number of expert queries needed to identify the point of irreversibility.
> - Performing short, random exploration steps after the agent predicts an irreversible state but before a human reset provides valuable data to refine the reversibility classifier, improving its accuracy and the agent's overall learning efficiency.
