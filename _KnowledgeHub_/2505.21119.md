# Universal Value-Function Uncertainties

> [!summary] Summary
> The Universal Value-Function Uncertainties (UVU) method estimates epistemic uncertainty in deep reinforcement learning value functions using a single-model architecture. It employs a modified Temporal Difference learning objective with a fixed random target network, providing theoretically grounded uncertainty estimates comparable to deep ensembles with reduced computational cost.

## Problem

- Quantifying epistemic uncertainty in deep reinforcement learning is challenging but crucial for efficient exploration, scalable offline learning, and safe decision-making.
- Existing Bayesian RL methods are computationally intensive, and deep ensembles for uncertainty estimation incur significant computational and memory overhead.
- Single-model uncertainty methods often capture only myopic (short-term) uncertainty, requiring heuristic propagation mechanisms to infer policy-dependent value uncertainty, often lacking theoretical support.

## Method

- A single-model architecture, ==Universal Value-Function Uncertainties== (**UVU**), is introduced, consisting of an online learner and a fixed target network.
- The online learner is trained with a Temporal Difference (TD) loss, predicting synthetic rewards generated by the fixed target network, to capture policy-dependent value uncertainty.
- Epistemic uncertainty is quantified by the squared prediction error between the online learner and the fixed target network for a given state, action, and policy encoding.

## Results

- UVU achieved performance comparable to deep ensembles (e.g., BDQNP with **15**-35 models) in an offline multi-task RL setting (Minigrid GoToDoor environment) while exhibiting substantially lower computational runtime.
- The method demonstrated robust scaling with network width, indicating that theoretical insights from the infinite-width NTK regime translate effectively to practical finite-width neural networks.
- UVU effectively quantified policy-dependent, long-term value uncertainty, outperforming standard DQN and RND-based baselines, and matching or exceeding careful RND variants that use intrinsic reward priors.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Neural Tangent Kernel (NTK) theory can be extended to analyze the learning dynamics of wide neural networks trained with semi-gradient TD losses, yielding closed-form solutions for convergence.
> - In the infinite-width limit, the expected squared prediction error of UVU is theoretically equivalent to the variance of an ensemble of universal Q-functions, providing a strong theoretical basis for its uncertainty estimates.
> - Practical multi-headed UVU architectures can estimate ensemble variance with finite samples, where the sample mean squared prediction error is identically distributed as the sample variance of an ensemble with one more head.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Deep reinforcement learning (RL) has demonstrated efficacy in various sequential decision-making problems, yet its broad application to real-world scenarios faces ongoing challenges. These challenges include the need for efficient exploration in complex environments, the scalability of offline learning from pre-collected datasets, and ensuring safe decision-making by RL agents. A central component for addressing these issues is the accurate quantification of epistemic uncertainty, which refers to the agent's uncertainty stemming from limited data. Within RL, estimating the epistemic uncertainty of the value function is particularly important as it reflects an agent's knowledge about the long-term consequences of its actions.

Existing approaches to value-function uncertainty estimation exhibit various limitations. Bayesian RL methods, both model-based and model-free, offer theoretical foundations but typically incur significant computational costs due to the intractability of posterior inference. Their theoretical guarantees can also be complicated by the use of temporal difference (TD) learning with bootstrapping. Deep ensembles have emerged as a practical and reliable method for quantifying value uncertainty in deep RL. However, they introduce substantial computational and memory overhead, limiting their scalability, particularly for larger models.

Conversely, single-model methods such as Random Network Distillation (RND), pseudo-counts, or intrinsic curiosity mechanisms are computationally more efficient. These methods, however, typically quantify *myopic* epistemic uncertainty, reflecting immediate prediction errors or novelty. To derive *value* uncertainties from these myopic estimates, additional propagation mechanisms are required, which often rely on heuristics and may lack a thorough theoretical basis. This gap in the literature highlights a need for computationally efficient single-model approaches that can directly estimate policy-dependent value uncertainties, supported by a strong theoretical framework.

This work introduces Universal Value-Function Uncertainties (UVU) as a method addressing this identified research gap. UVU aims to provide a single-model solution for policy-dependent value uncertainty quantification, conceptually similar to Universal Value Function Approximators (UVFAs) that condition value functions on specific policy contexts. The paper further contributes to the theoretical understanding of deep RL by applying Neural Tangent Kernel (NTK) theory, traditionally used for analyzing supervised learning with squared losses, to the more complex setting of TD-learning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a novel, computationally efficient, single-model method capable of directly estimating epistemic uncertainty in value functions for any given policy. This method is designated as Universal Value-Function Uncertainties (UVU).

The motivation for this objective stems from several limitations observed in current methods for uncertainty quantification in reinforcement learning:
- **Computational Overhead of Ensembles:** Deep ensembles, while empirically effective for value uncertainty estimation, necessitate training and maintaining multiple independent models. This imposes a significant computational burden and memory footprint, which can hinder the scalability of RL agents for complex tasks or large-scale deployments. The authors aim to reduce this overhead by proposing a single-model alternative.
- **Indirect Nature of Single-Model Myopic Methods:** Existing single-model methods often quantify only myopic uncertainty (e.g., novelty or immediate prediction errors). Deriving *value uncertainty*, which inherently depends on long-term future trajectories dictated by a policy, from these myopic estimates typically requires heuristic propagation mechanisms. These mechanisms can be complex to design, may not provide tight bounds, and frequently lack robust theoretical grounding, potentially leading to underestimation of true uncertainties. UVU is motivated to provide a direct measure of policy-dependent value uncertainty.
- **Lack of Strong Theoretical Foundation for Efficient Methods:** Many practical single-model uncertainty methods in RL operate without a comprehensive theoretical understanding of what types of uncertainty they truly capture or how their estimates relate to more theoretically grounded Bayesian or ensemble approaches. A core motivation for UVU is to address this by providing a rigorous theoretical analysis, specifically by leveraging Neural Tangent Kernel (NTK) theory, to establish a formal connection between its uncertainty estimates and those from deep ensembles.
- **Importance of Policy-Conditional Value Uncertainty:** Accurate policy-dependent value uncertainty is crucial for various aspects of RL. This includes guiding efficient exploration strategies (e.g., favoring actions with high uncertainty), enabling safe decision-making (e.g., avoiding actions with potentially catastrophic uncertain outcomes), and facilitating scalable offline RL (e.g., identifying tasks or state-action regions where the agent's knowledge is insufficient). The ability to provide universal, policy-conditioned uncertainty estimates is therefore a key driver.

In essence, the research is motivated by the desire to combine the practical benefits of computationally efficient single-model architectures with the reliability and theoretical soundness typically associated with ensemble-based or Bayesian methods for quantifying long-term, policy-dependent value uncertainty in RL.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The Universal Value-Function Uncertainties (UVU) method is designed to measure epistemic value uncertainty using a single-model architecture, drawing inspiration from Random Network Distillation (RND) but with modifications tailored to capture long-term policy-dependent uncertainties.

**Core UVU Mechanism:**
UVU employs two primary neural networks:
1. **Online Learner (`u`):** A neural network parameterized by `ϑ_t`, which is trained over time. Its input includes state (`s`), action (`a`), and a policy encoding (`z`).
2. **Fixed Target Network (`g`):** A neural network parameterized by `ψ_0`, which is initialized randomly and remains fixed throughout the training process. Its architecture is identical to the online learner, taking `(s, a, z)` as input.

The training process for the online learner `u` is distinct from standard RND:
1. **Synthetic Reward Generation:** For any given transition `(s, a, s')` and policy encoding `z`, subsequent actions `a'` are drawn from the policy `π(·|s', z)`. The fixed target network `g` then generates a synthetic reward `r_g^z` as:
 `r_g^z(s, a, s', a') = g(s, a, z, ψ_0) - γg(s', a', z, ψ_0)`
 where `γ` is the discount factor. This formulation implies that the target network `g` itself satisfies the Bellman equation for this synthetic reward and the policy `π(·|s, z)`.
2. **Online Network Training (TD Learning):** The online network `u` is trained to minimize a Temporal Difference (TD) loss, similar to a Q-learning objective, but using the synthetic rewards `r_g^z`. Given a dataset `X = {s_i, a_i, z_i}`, the loss function is:
 `L(ϑ_t) = 1/2ND Σ_i (γ[u(s'_i, a'_i, z_i, ϑ_t)]_sg + r_g^z(s_i, a_i, s'_i, a'_i) - u(s_i, a_i, z_i, ϑ_t))^2`
 The `[·]_sg` denotes a stop-gradient operation, which prevents gradients from flowing through the bootstrapped target value `u(s'_i, a'_i, z_i, ϑ_t)`.
3. **Uncertainty Quantification:** The epistemic uncertainty for any `(s, a, z)` tuple is quantified by the squared prediction error between the online learner and the fixed target network:
 `ϵ(s, a, z, ϑ_t, ψ_0)^2 = (u(s, a, z, ϑ_t) - g(s, a, z, ψ_0))^2`

The rationale is that `g` is inherently a "random value function" for the synthetic rewards `r_g^z`. If the online network `u` can perfectly learn `g` based on available data for a given policy `π(·|s, z)`, the prediction error will be zero. However, if the data is insufficient or does not adequately cover trajectories induced by `π(·|s, z)`, `u` will fail to fully recover `g`, and the resulting non-zero `ϵ^2` quantifies the epistemic uncertainty regarding that policy's value function. This "universal" aspect means `u` and `g` take `z` (policy encoding) as input, allowing for policy-conditioned uncertainty estimates.

**Theoretical Analysis using Neural Tangent Kernel (NTK) Theory:**
A key contribution of the paper is a rigorous theoretical analysis of UVU within the framework of NTK theory. This involves:
- **Extending NTK to TD Learning:** The authors derive the learning dynamics of wide neural networks optimized with semi-gradient TD losses under gradient descent. This yields closed-form solutions for the convergence and generalization behavior of neural network value functions in the infinite-width limit.
- **Equivalence to Ensemble Variance:** In the limit of infinite network width, the paper mathematically demonstrates that the prediction errors generated by UVU are equivalent to the variance of an ensemble of independent universal value functions. This equivalence holds both in expectation (Corollary 1) and for finite sample estimators when using a multi-headed UVU architecture (Corollary 2).
- **Multi-headed UVU:** In practical implementations, UVU uses a multi-headed architecture where a single shared-hidden-layer network produces multiple output heads for both `u` and `g`. The sample mean squared prediction error across these heads is used, and Corollary 2 provides theoretical justification for its equivalence to ensemble variance estimation.

**Empirical Validation:**
The methodology is empirically validated on an offline multi-task RL benchmark from the Minigrid suite, specifically the GoToDoor environment. The experimental setup involves:
- **Offline Learning:** Agents are trained on a pre-collected dataset where the data-collection policy exhibits systematic failures for certain task/grid combinations.
- **Task Rejection Protocol:** Agents are evaluated on their ability to reject tasks for which they have high uncertainty, indicating insufficient data or unreliable value estimates, to maximize overall performance.
- **Baselines:** UVU is compared against standard DQN, Bootstrapped DQN with randomized priors (BDQNP, a deep ensemble method), and RND-based agents (DQN-RND and DQN-RND-P, which uses intrinsic reward priors).
- **Metrics:** Performance is assessed based on average evaluation returns with task rejection, computational runtime, and an ablation study on network width to assess robustness to finite-size networks.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents both theoretical characterizations and empirical validations of the Universal Value-Function Uncertainties (UVU) method.

**Theoretical Findings:**
- **NTK for TD Learning:** The paper successfully extends the Neural Tangent Kernel (NTK) theory to analyze the learning dynamics of wide neural networks trained with semi-gradient Temporal Difference (TD) losses. Theorem 1 provides closed-form expressions for the mean and covariance of converged function evaluations for such networks under gradient flow in the infinite-width limit. This result is significant as it formalizes the behavior of value functions trained with TD losses within the NTK framework, which previously primarily focused on supervised learning with squared losses.
- **Equivalence of UVU Errors and Ensemble Variance:** Corollary 1 establishes a fundamental theoretical justification for UVU. It demonstrates that, in the limit of infinite network width and considering random initializations, the expected squared prediction error of UVU is exactly equivalent to the variance of an ensemble of universal Q-functions. This implies that UVU, despite being a single-model approach, captures the same epistemic uncertainty as a full deep ensemble in this idealized setting.
- **Finite Sample Equivalence for Multi-headed Architectures:** Corollary 2 extends this theoretical understanding to practical scenarios involving finite sample estimators. It shows that for a multi-headed UVU model with `M` independent heads, the sample mean squared prediction error is identically distributed as the sample variance of an ensemble comprising `M+1` independently trained Q-functions. This provides a theoretical basis for using multi-headed architectures in UVU as an effective estimator for ensemble variances.

**Empirical Findings:**
- **Performance in Offline Multi-task RL:** UVU demonstrated effective quantification of value function uncertainty in a challenging offline multi-task RL setting (GoToDoor environment with task rejection). Its performance, measured by average evaluation returns, was comparable to, and in some cases exceeded, that of large deep ensembles (e.g., BDQNP with 15 or 35 models) across various environment complexities (grid sizes 5-10).
- **Computational Efficiency:** A key empirical result is the substantial computational savings offered by UVU. Runtime analysis showed that UVU, even with a multi-headed architecture (e.g., 512 heads), had a significantly lower runtime per 100,000 gradient steps compared to deep ensembles (e.g., 3-model BDQNP), making it a more scalable solution for uncertainty quantification.
- **Robustness to Finite Network Width:** An ablation study on network width confirmed that UVU's performance scales similarly to DQN and BDQNP baselines. This indicates that the theoretical insights from the infinite-width NTK regime translate robustly to practical finite-width neural networks, provided sufficient representational capacity.
- **Comparison to Baselines:**
 - A standard DQN agent performed significantly worse, highlighting the necessity of effective uncertainty estimation for task rejection in data-scarce or suboptimal data regions.
 - RND-based methods (DQN-RND) performed poorly without specific mechanisms for propagating myopic uncertainty to value uncertainty.
 - DQN-RND-P, which incorporates intrinsic reward priors to address underestimation issues in RND, achieved competitive results, suggesting that careful design is required for propagating myopic uncertainties. UVU generally matched or outperformed DQN-RND-P.
- **Intuitive Illustration:** A tabular example and neural network visualizations in a chain MDP successfully build intuition, demonstrating how UVU's TD-learning objective enables it to capture policy-dependent, long-term value uncertainty when the policy deviates from available data, leading to a measurable discrepancy between the online and target networks.

In summary, the paper's findings establish UVU as a theoretically grounded and empirically competitive method for value-function uncertainty estimation in RL, offering significant computational advantages over traditional deep ensemble approaches.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of Universal Value-Function Uncertainties (UVU) offers several significant contributions and potential impacts on the field of reinforcement learning:

- **Addressing Computational Bottlenecks:** A primary impact of UVU is its potential to mitigate the computational and memory overhead associated with deep ensembles, which are currently a standard for reliable uncertainty quantification in deep RL. By providing a single-model, multi-headed architecture that achieves comparable performance to large ensembles, UVU can enable the development of more scalable and computationally feasible uncertainty-aware RL agents. This is crucial for deploying RL systems in resource-constrained environments or for large-scale, complex applications where training and maintaining multiple deep networks is prohibitive.
- **Advancing Single-Model Uncertainty Quantification:** UVU represents an advancement in single-model methods for uncertainty quantification in RL. Unlike previous single-model approaches that primarily capture myopic uncertainty and require additional heuristic propagation mechanisms, UVU directly estimates policy-dependent value uncertainty through its specialized TD-learning objective. This directness provides a more integrated and potentially more accurate approach to understanding long-term consequences of actions.
- **Strong Theoretical Foundation for TD Learning:** The paper's extension of Neural Tangent Kernel (NTK) theory to analyze semi-gradient TD learning is a notable theoretical contribution. Establishing a formal equivalence between UVU errors and the variance of universal Q-function ensembles provides a robust theoretical backing for a practical algorithm. This theoretical grounding can increase confidence in the reliability of UVU's uncertainty estimates and may inspire further theoretical investigations into the behavior of neural networks in various RL settings.
- **Enabling Robust Offline and Multi-task RL:** The empirical validation in challenging offline multi-task RL environments, particularly with a task-rejection protocol, highlights UVU's utility for scenarios where agents must discern their limitations. By providing reliable estimates of value uncertainty, UVU can empower agents to make more informed decisions, such as rejecting tasks for which they lack sufficient data or confidence, thus leading to improved overall performance and safety. This capability is vital for real-world applications where data might be sparse, biased, or where agents need to operate across diverse, potentially novel tasks.
- **Facilitating Efficient Exploration and Safe Decision-Making:** Accurate and computationally efficient value uncertainty estimates are fundamental to designing more efficient exploration strategies and ensuring safe decision-making in online RL. UVU's ability to provide policy-conditioned uncertainty could guide agents to explore state-action pairs that promise high information gain or avoid actions with high uncertain risks, contributing to more robust and adaptable RL systems.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The work opens several avenues for future research. Bridging the gap between the idealized infinite-width NTK theory and the practical performance of finite-width networks is an ongoing challenge. Furthermore, integrating UVU with advanced representation learning techniques (e.g., self-predictive auxiliary losses) could enhance its applicability to complex exploration problems, and combining it with unsupervised RL methods might lead to novel ways of generating diverse policies and their uncertainty profiles.

In conclusion, UVU offers a method that is both theoretically sound and empirically effective for quantifying policy-dependent value uncertainty in RL, while significantly improving computational efficiency. This could accelerate the development and deployment of more intelligent, adaptable, and robust RL agents across a wider range of applications.
