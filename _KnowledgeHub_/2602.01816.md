# Seeing Is Believing? A Benchmark for Multimodal Large Language Models on Visual Illusions and Anomalies

> [!summary] Summary
> VIA-Bench, a new benchmark designed to evaluate Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) on visual illusions and anomalies, reveals a substantial performance gap between MLLMs and human perception. The best MLLM achieved 69.23% accuracy compared to human 93.30%, and Chain-of-Thought reasoning often degrades MLLM performance in these challenging visual contexts.

## Problem

- Current Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) achieve high performance on standard vision-language benchmarks, potentially masking fundamental limitations in true visual intelligence.
- Existing evaluations primarily focus on typical visual contexts and lack the rigor to stress-test MLLMs with visual illusions and anomalies that defy common-sense priors.
- A critical gap exists in discerning whether MLLMs genuinely perceive visual signals or merely perform sophisticated pattern matching against internalized canonical knowledge.

## Method

- Developed ==VIA-Bench==, a novel benchmark comprising ==1,004 high-quality multiple-choice question-answer pairs== specifically focused on diverse visual illusions and anomalies.
- Constructed VIA-Bench following a ==multi-stage human-in-the-loop pipeline==, categorizing content into six distinct illusion and anomaly types grounded in cognitive psychology.
- Conducted comprehensive evaluations on over ==20 state-of-the-art MLLMs==, including proprietary and open-source models, analyzing performance metrics and the specific impact of Chain-of-Thought reasoning.

## Results

- Human performance on VIA-Bench averaged **93.30%** accuracy, while the best MLLM (Gemini-3-pro) achieved only **69.23%**, demonstrating a significant **24.07%** performance gap.
- MLLMs showed inconsistent performance across the six illusion categories and model types, with no single model exhibiting uniformly strong accuracy across all challenging scenarios.
- Chain-of-Thought (CoT) strategies typically failed to improve accuracy and, in many cases, degraded MLLM performance by rationalizing initial misperceptions rather than correcting them.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Current MLLMs exhibit a substantial gap in perceptual and reasoning capabilities compared to humans when faced with visual illusions and anomalies.
> - Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting frequently fails to enhance, and often degrades, MLLM performance in these challenging visual contexts due to 'hallucination reinforcement'.
> - MLLMs' failures often stem from fundamental visual misperceptions, leading to high uncertainty and 'overthinking' phenomena where models struggle to resolve visual ambiguity.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) represents a significant frontier in artificial intelligence, with models like GPT, Gemini, and Claude demonstrating impressive capabilities across a wide array of vision-language tasks. These models have shown remarkable proficiency on general-purpose vision-language benchmarks, often reaching or even exceeding human-level performance on standard in-distribution data. This success has fueled the perception that MLLMs are rapidly approaching human-like intelligence in multimodal understanding.

However, the authors identify a critical gap in the existing evaluation landscape. Most current benchmarks primarily focus on standard visual contexts and stepwise reasoning, or, while probing counterfactual logic or visual puzzles, often lack the sufficient difficulty and discriminative power required to truly challenge frontier models. This leads to a situation where "superficial benchmark success" might mask fundamental limitations in genuine visual intelligence. The core issue, as highlighted by the authors, is that these evaluations rarely stress-test MLLMs under atypical conditions that defy common-sense priors, such as cognitive illusions, perceptual conflicts, and intuition-breaking scenarios. In such situations, reliance on canonical priors, often learned from vast datasets, can directly contradict visual evidence. For example, a model might predict "five" for a six-fingered hand, prioritizing a learned prototype over the actual visual input.

This paper directly addresses this critical deficiency by introducing VIA-Bench. It posits that to truly assess whether MLLMs genuinely "perceive visual signals" or merely perform "sophisticated pattern matching," they must be challenged with scenarios where "seeing is believing" is fundamentally tested. By focusing on visual illusions and anomalies, VIA-Bench pushes MLLMs beyond their comfort zone of standard data distributions, forcing them to reconcile observed visual evidence with their internalized knowledge. This work distinguishes itself from previous efforts like Illusory VQA, The Art of Deception, or GVIL by providing a systematic, comprehensive testbed spanning diverse categories of illusions and anomalies, moving beyond localized or edited datasets to a more holistic diagnostic suite for assessing perceptual robustness. In essence, this research provides a much-needed diagnostic tool to uncover the "persistent bottleneck in machine perception" that standard benchmarks overlook, thus guiding the community towards developing MLLMs with more human-like, robust, and deliberate reasoning capabilities.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to introduce **VIA-Bench**, a novel and challenging benchmark specifically designed to probe the robustness of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) when confronted with visual illusions and anomalies. This objective is driven by several key motivations:

1. **Expose Limitations Beyond Standard Benchmarks:** Current MLLMs often achieve high performance on general-purpose vision-language benchmarks, some even claiming human-level capabilities. However, these benchmarks typically rely on "standard in-distribution data." The authors are motivated to test MLLMs' performance in "scenarios that defy common-sense priors," thereby exposing vulnerabilities that standard evaluations miss.
2. **Discern True Perception from Pattern Matching:** A fundamental motivation is to answer the question: "Do MLLMs truly perceive visual signals, or are they merely performing sophisticated pattern matching against internalized canonical priors?" Visual illusions and anomalies serve as ideal probes because they inherently challenge conventional assumptions and force a reconciliation between observed visual evidence and internal knowledge.
3. **Quantify the Gap Between Human and Machine Perception:** The paper aims to quantify how far current MLLMs lag behind human perception in these challenging contexts. While humans can eventually interpret and understand illusions, the hypothesis is that MLLMs, despite their advanced capabilities, will struggle, revealing a significant divergence between machine and human perception.
4. **Evaluate Reasoning Robustness, Especially Chain-of-Thought (CoT):** The research seeks to specifically investigate the robustness of MLLMs' reasoning mechanisms, particularly the efficacy of Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting. The authors hypothesize that in counter-intuitive visual scenarios, CoT might not offer the expected robustness and could even degrade performance by reinforcing initial misperceptions.
5. **Incentivize Future Research towards Artificial General Intelligence (AGI):** By highlighting these fundamental perceptual bottlenecks, the authors aim to "incentivize the community to explore the headroom of MLLMs" and provide clear directions for future research. Addressing these challenges is deemed critical for the advancement of MLLMs toward more robust, human-like perception and ultimately, artificial general intelligence.

In summary, the core motivation is to move beyond superficial evaluations to diagnostically stress-test MLLMs where their "seeing" might be "deceiving," thereby fostering the development of models that exhibit genuine visual intelligence rather than brittle pattern recognition.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed in this research revolves around the meticulous construction of the VIA-Bench dataset and comprehensive evaluation of state-of-the-art MLLMs.

#### 3.1. Dataset Construction (VIA-Bench Dataset)

VIA-Bench consists of 1,004 high-quality multiple-choice question-answer (QA) pairs, carefully designed to isolate failure modes where MLLM internal priors conflict with raw visual evidence. The construction follows a systematic, multi-stage, human-in-the-loop pipeline:

- **Taxonomy and Categorization:** A taxonomy grounded in cognitive psychology (Eagleman, 2001) and MLLM failure-mode analysis (Comanici et al., 2025) was established. Six primary categories were defined:
 - **Color Illusions (CI):** Focuses on color constancy, contrast, and illumination (e.g., Checker Shadow illusion, Ishihara plates).
 - **Motion Illusions (MI):** Static images eliciting subjective perceptions of motion (e.g., stripe-induced drift).
 - **Gestalt Illusions (GI):** Detecting fine-grained objects where overall composition creates perceptual biases (e.g., odd-one-out patterns).
 - **Geometric and Spatial Illusions (GSI):** Probing geometric biases in judgments of length, parallelism, and 3D existence (e.g., Penrose triangle, Zöllner illusion).
 - **General Visual Illusions (VI):** Broader misperceptions caused by perspective, composition, or occlusion (e.g., leaves resembling birds).
 - **Visual Anomalies (VA):** Scenarios violating common-sense priors, particularly biological anomalies like polydactyly (extra fingers/toes).
- **Data Curation and Unification:** A hybrid acquisition strategy combined manual web-crawling from specialized public sources with aggregation from existing high-resolution perceptual datasets (e.g., Turing Eye Test (TET)). Images were filtered for resolution, clarity, and non-ambiguity, and metadata was unified into a structured schema.
- **QA Pair Annotation:** The task is multiple-choice. Annotation focused on three critical dimensions:
 - **Inquisitive Precision:** Questions are tailored to specific illusion types, providing localized visual cues (arrows, labels) for spatial comparisons to minimize grounding errors.
 - **Distractor Design:** Plausible distractors were manually crafted to capture common machine-learning shortcuts or stereotypical priors. A "Not Sure" option was included in every set to gauge uncertainty, though it's never the ground truth.
 - **Ground-Truth Verification:** All annotations were cross-verified by a secondary expert annotator to ensure the "intrinsic truth" of the image was undisputed.
- **Quality Assurance and Debiasing:** Two techniques were applied to mitigate shortcut learning:
 - Randomizing the permutation of options to eliminate positional bias.
 - For binary questions, randomly flipping question polarity (e.g., "Is X true?" vs. "Is X false?") to prevent correlation between correctness and label frequency.
- **Dataset Statistics:** The dataset maintains a balanced distribution across the six categories. It covers seven capability dimensions, testing both low-level perception (counting, color identification) and high-level reasoning (judgment, object recognition). Question lengths vary, and ultra-high-resolution images are included to ensure continued challenge.

#### 3.2. Evaluation on VIA-Bench

- **Benchmark Models:** Over 20 diverse MLLMs were evaluated, ranging from 3B to 235B parameters, grouped into:
 - **Proprietary Systems:** Gemini-3-pro, OpenAI o4-mini, Gemini-2.5-pro, GPT-5-mini, GPT-4o, ChatGPT-4o, GPT-5-chat.
 - **Open-source Models:** Qwen-VL-max, Qwen2.5-VL (3B, 7B, 72B), InternVL3.5 (8B, 38B, without thinking).
 - **Reasoning-enhanced Models:** OpenAI o3, Claude (Opus, Sonnet, 3.5 Sonnet), InternVL3.5 (with thinking), GLM-4.5V, Qwen3-VL-Thinking (30B, 235B). A blind evaluation baseline (GPT-4-Turbo with vision disabled) and human performance were also included for comparison.
- **Evaluation Criteria and Metrics:**
 - **Accuracy:** Standard accuracy ($a = n/N$).
 - **Parsing Protocols:** Two protocols for comparing model responses to ground truth:
 - **Match:** Rule-based evaluation via regular expression matching of the final option label.
 - **Judge:** LLM-as-a-Judge (GPT-4.1-mini) serving as an arbiter for correctness, particularly for more nuanced responses.
 - **Experimental Setup:** Models were run five times independently, with results averaged to reduce randomness. Output format instructions were included in prompts for reliable parsing. Temperature was set to 0.8 (or model defaults) for diverse outputs. Locally deployed models used 8 H20 GPUs without quantization.
- **CoT Reasoning Analysis:** The impact of Chain-of-Thought (CoT) strategies was specifically investigated using two variants injected via system prompts:
 - **Zero-shot CoT:** General instruction like "Let's think step by step."
 - **Manual CoT:** Providing explicit guidelines for systematic analysis.
- **Qualitative Analysis:** Detailed case studies of model responses and reasoning traces were conducted for selected frontier MLLMs (OpenAI o3, OpenAI o4, Gemini-2.5-pro, Qwen3-VL-30B-A3B) to understand primary bottlenecks and error types.
- **Ablation Study:** Explored the impact of temperature settings on model performance for selected models.

This comprehensive approach ensures that VIA-Bench is a robust, high-quality benchmark capable of rigorously testing the perceptual and reasoning capabilities of current and future MLLMs against human performance in challenging, counter-intuitive visual scenarios.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The extensive evaluation on VIA-Bench revealed several critical insights into the current capabilities and limitations of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs):

1. **Significant Performance Gap with Human Perception:**
 - Humans achieved an average accuracy of **93.30%** on VIA-Bench.
 - The best MLLM (Gemini-3-pro) reached only **69.23%** average accuracy.
 - This represents a substantial gap of at least **24.07%**, indicating that current MLLMs remain significantly below human-level perception and deliberate reasoning in these challenging scenarios. VIA-Bench successfully serves as a rigorous testbed to quantify this divergence.

2. **Inconsistent Performance Across MLLM Categories and Illusion Types:**
 - **Proprietary MLLMs:** While generally outperforming open-source models, proprietary systems like Gemini-3-pro showed inconsistent performance across categories. For instance, OpenAI o4-mini achieved an impressive 97.16% on Geometric and Spatial Illusions (GSI) but struggled significantly in other areas. No single model demonstrated uniformly strong accuracy across all six categories, highlighting a lack of generalized robustness.
 - **Open-source MLLMs:** Top-tier open-source models lagged behind proprietary ones. Several models (e.g., Qwen2.5-VL-3B-Instruct and InternVL3.5-38B) scored even lower than the blind evaluation baseline in some categories, suggesting unreliable visual perception and reasoning. Performance did not consistently scale with model parameters.
 - **Reasoning-enhanced MLLMs:** Models explicitly designed for enhanced reasoning (e.g., OpenAI o3, Qwen3-VL-235B-A22B-Thinking) showed better average accuracy within their group but still exhibited pronounced weaknesses, particularly in Visual Anomalies (VA) and Color Illusions (CI), with highest accuracies of only 25.02% and 51.84%, respectively.

3. **The "CoT Paradox": Chain-of-Thought Reasoning Often Fails or Degrades Performance:**
 - Contrary to its typical benefit in complex reasoning tasks, Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting strategies (both zero-shot and manual) generally failed to improve accuracy on VIA-Bench. In many cases, CoT even *degraded* performance.
 - The qualitative analysis revealed that CoT often leads to "hallucination reinforcement." Instead of correcting an initial visual misperception, the extended reasoning process would "rationalize deceptive priors," generating plausible-sounding justifications for an incorrect premise, thus creating "brittle mirages" of reasoning.
 - This suggests that "purely text-based CoT is insufficient for resolving visual illusions" as it lacks continuous visual grounding to verify intermediate steps, instead relying on ungrounded textual shortcuts or amplifying perceptual errors.

4. **Qualitative Insights into MLLM Failure Modes:**
 - **Initial Visual Misperception:** Most errors stemmed from a fundamental misidentification of a key visual element (e.g., missing a subtle illusory cue). Once this flawed premise was established, subsequent logical deductions were inevitably incorrect.
 - **High Uncertainty and Self-Negation:** MLLMs often exhibited high uncertainty, evidenced by phrases like "I'm a bit uncertain..." or "maybe...", indicating a conflict between visual evidence and internalized priors. They tended to prioritize canonical knowledge over the actual visual input.
 - **Overthinking Phenomenon:** A severe "overthinking phenomenon" was prevalent, especially in reasoning-enhanced models. Visual illusions triggered repetitive or self-contradictory CoT traces (e.g., loops of "wait," "but," "no"), where models struggled to resolve visual ambiguity, leading to lengthy, yet ultimately flawed, reasoning.

In essence, the findings consistently reveal a fundamental divergence in perceptual and reasoning capabilities between humans and current MLLMs when faced with visually deceptive or anomalous inputs. MLLMs tend to be "blind" to the nuances of illusions, prioritizing learned prototypes over actual visual evidence, and their reasoning mechanisms are often not robust enough to overcome these initial perceptual errors.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of VIA-Bench and its comprehensive evaluation carries significant implications for the future direction of Multimodal Large Language Model (MLLM) research and the broader pursuit of Artificial General Intelligence (AGI).

1. **Redefining MLLM Robustness and Intelligence:** VIA-Bench serves as a stark reminder that impressive performance on standard benchmarks does not equate to genuine visual intelligence or robust perception. By exposing systematic failure modes in MLLMs when confronted with visual illusions and anomalies, this work compels the research community to re-evaluate and redefine what it means for an MLLM to be "intelligent" and "robust." It highlights that human-like perception involves not just pattern recognition but also the ability to critically evaluate and often override common-sense priors based on direct visual evidence.

2. **Guiding Future MLLM Development:** The findings provide concrete diagnostic insights, revealing that current MLLMs suffer from fundamental perceptual bottlenecks. The "CoT Paradox" is particularly impactful, demonstrating that merely adding more textual reasoning steps (Chain-of-Thought) is insufficient and can even be detrimental if the initial visual grounding is flawed. This suggests a critical need for advancements in how MLLMs process and interpret visual information at a foundational level, potentially requiring:
 - **Improved Visual Grounding:** Better mechanisms for MLLMs to consistently and accurately ground their understanding in raw visual evidence, especially when it contradicts learned priors.
 - **Adaptive Reasoning:** Development of reasoning architectures that can detect perceptual conflicts, handle ambiguity, and adapt their reasoning process rather than reinforcing initial errors.
 - **Enhanced Uncertainty Awareness:** Models that can more accurately recognize when they are uncertain due to conflicting information, rather than producing confident but incorrect answers.

3. **Advancing Trustworthy AI:** In real-world applications (e.g., autonomous driving, medical imaging, robotics), MLLMs will inevitably encounter atypical, anomalous, or visually deceptive scenarios. The brittleness exposed by VIA-Bench underscores that current MLLMs are not yet reliably trustworthy in such edge cases. Addressing these perceptual vulnerabilities is crucial for building safer, more reliable, and more accountable AI systems.

4. **Community Engagement and Benchmark Legacy:** The public release of VIA-Bench (data and code) will provide a valuable, accessible resource for researchers to rigorously test new MLLM architectures and training methodologies. This initiative is designed to "accelerate breakthroughs" and "push the boundaries of MLLMs." The authors' commitment to continuously updating the testbed and exploring more diverse formats (e.g., open-ended QA, complex compositional illusions) ensures its long-term relevance and impact.

5. **A Stepping Stone Towards AGI:** Ultimately, the work posits that resolving these perceptual bottlenecks is "critical for the advancement of artificial general intelligence." The ability to navigate and correctly interpret the complexities of visual illusions is a hallmark of human cognitive flexibility and robustness, and successfully endowing MLLMs with this capability would represent a significant step towards truly intelligent machines that can reason deliberately and perceive the world more like humans.

In conclusion, "Seeing Is Believing?" provides a powerful lens through which to scrutinize the foundational perceptual and reasoning capabilities of MLLMs. By uncovering where these models stumble in the face of visual deception, it offers clear guidance and a crucial benchmark for developing the next generation of MLLMs that are not only proficient but also genuinely robust, perceptive, and ultimately, more intelligent.
