# From Simulation to the Real-World: An In-Field 6D Pose Dataset and Baseline for Robotic Strawberry Harvesting

> [!summary] Summary
> An in-field 6D pose dataset for robotic strawberry harvesting: 12,040 real farm images with 16,037 instances, ground truth built from checkerboard PnP camera poses, metric COLMAP reconstruction, and manual 3D box annotation, plus a 35,118-image Isaac Sim synthetic set with domain randomization. A DETR-style RGB-only baseline shows synthetic-only training gives 0.0% pose accuracy, quantifying a large sim-to-real gap.

## Problem

- Existing strawberry harvesting systems recover only 3D fruit centroid position, not orientation, so grasp approach angles for soft asymmetric fruit stay unplanned.
- Prior strawberry 6D pose models train purely on synthetic data rendered without scene context, leaving real-field performance and the sim-to-real gap entirely unquantified.
- Agricultural 6D pose ground truth exists only from controlled lab collections, excludes strawberries, and no in-field annotation pipeline had been demonstrated.

## Method

- Real ground-truth pipeline composes T_local→cam = T_world→cam · T_local→world from checkerboard ==EPnP== camera poses, ==COLMAP== metric dense reconstruction, and manual 3D bounding-box annotation on the point cloud.
- ==Isaac Sim== synthetic set renders full farm scenes with HDRI lighting, plant geometry randomization, RealSense-matched intrinsics, hemisphere camera sampling at 0.2-1.0 m, and jittered look-at targets.
- ==DETR==-style baseline: backbone encoder plus ==transformer decoder== with object queries, predicting 2D boxes, continuous 6D rotation, and decoupled in-plane/depth translation under warmup-gated geodesic and SmoothL1 pose losses.

## Results

- Synthetic-only training yields **0.0%** pose accuracy on real images for every backbone, with mean rotation error above **90** degrees and IoU@0.5 of only **28%**, **29%**, and **12%**.
- Adding **10%** real data cuts DINOv2-B rotation error from **91.90** to **25.00** degrees, a **73%** drop, and lifts IoU@0.5 from **28%** to roughly **85%**.
- Frozen DINOv2-B beats fine-tuned ViT-B/16 and ResNet-101 under real-only training: **5.04** vs **7.49** vs **11.92** degrees, and **46.0%** vs **25.9%** vs **9.4%** at 3 cm/5 degrees.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Scene-level rendering realism and domain randomization alone do not close a sim-to-real gap; distributional mismatch in depth range and viewpoint coverage still dominates.
> - Coarse 2D detection transfers across domains far faster than 6D pose, so detection metrics badly overstate readiness for precision manipulation.
> - Monocular depth is the binding constraint: depth error stays roughly 4x in-plane error, capping useful translation thresholds regardless of backbone quality.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The agricultural sector, particularly strawberry production, faces increasing challenges due to labor shortages and high production costs, with harvesting alone accounting for approximately 40% of total expenses. Robotic harvesting presents a potential solution to these issues by automating the labor-intensive process. However, fully autonomous harvesting at a commercial scale remains an unrealized goal, primarily due to technical challenges in robotic perception and manipulation.

Existing robotic harvesting systems have made progress in fruit detection and 3D position estimation, typically relying on RGB-D cameras to locate the centroid of fruits. While these systems enable coarse pick-and-place operations, they generally do not explicitly recover fruit orientation. For soft and asymmetrically shaped fruits like strawberries, precise, damage-free grasping requires knowledge of the full 6D pose (position and orientation) to allow an end-effector to approach the fruit from an optimal angle.

Previous studies have explored strawberry orientation estimation and full 6D pose estimation. However, these approaches have predominantly relied on synthetic datasets for training their models. This reliance on synthetic data introduces a significant "sim-to-real gap," meaning that the performance of these models in real-world agricultural field conditions remains unquantified. The transferability of models trained in simulated environments to actual, uncontrolled field settings is a known challenge in agricultural robotics, even for simpler tasks like object detection. For more geometrically complex tasks like 6D pose estimation, this gap is expected to be more pronounced.

Furthermore, while datasets with 6D pose annotations exist for agricultural produce, they either do not include strawberries or were collected in controlled laboratory settings rather than natural, in-field environments. The inherent challenges of acquiring accurate 6D pose ground truth in uncontrolled field conditions have limited the development and evaluation of robust 6D pose estimation methods for robotic strawberry harvesting. This research addresses this critical void by providing the first in-field 6D pose ground truth dataset for strawberries, thereby enabling more realistic evaluation and advancement of robotic harvesting technologies.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the recognized gap in existing literature and resources concerning 6D pose estimation for robotic strawberry harvesting. Accurate 6D pose estimation is crucial for enabling precise and damage-free grasping of strawberries by robotic manipulators, a capability currently lacking in most robotic harvesting systems that primarily focus on 3D fruit localization.

The specific objectives and underlying motivations can be summarized as follows:

1. **Address the Lack of In-Field 6D Pose Ground Truth Data:** Current 6D pose estimation studies for strawberries have largely depended on synthetic data. This reliance means that the performance of these models under the varied and challenging conditions of real agricultural fields is unknown. Collecting ground truth 6D pose data in such environments is inherently difficult. The paper aims to overcome this challenge by creating and presenting the first real-world 6D pose ground truth dataset of strawberries collected directly from actual agricultural fields. This dataset is intended to provide a robust benchmark for evaluating the real-world efficacy of 6D pose estimation models.

2. **Develop a More Realistic Synthetic Dataset:** While acknowledging the limitations of synthetic data, the researchers recognize its value in reducing annotation costs and enabling diverse training scenarios. Previous synthetic datasets for strawberries often lacked sufficient scene-level realism. A key objective was to introduce a new synthetic dataset rendered in NVIDIA Isaac Sim, featuring enhanced scene-level realism and incorporating domain randomization techniques. This aims to create a synthetic environment that more closely mimics real-world conditions, potentially narrowing the sim-to-real gap.

3. **Quantify the Sim-to-Real Gap for Strawberry 6D Pose Estimation:** Despite advancements in synthetic environment realism, a domain gap typically persists. A central motivation for this work was to empirically quantify the magnitude of this sim-to-real gap specifically for strawberry 6D pose estimation under real agricultural field conditions. By evaluating models trained on synthetic-only, mixed, and real-only data, the research seeks to provide a quantitative understanding of how much real-world data is necessary to bridge this gap and to what extent improved synthetic data can contribute.

4. **Establish Baseline Performance for Future Research:** To serve as a reference point for future studies, the paper aims to provide baseline 6D pose estimation results using various backbone encoders (representing different architectural paradigms: CNNs, supervised transformers, and self-supervised vision foundations). These baselines are intended to illustrate current capabilities and limitations, particularly in the context of different training data configurations, thereby guiding subsequent research efforts in model development and domain adaptation.

In essence, the research is motivated by the need to move beyond purely synthetic evaluations and develop robust, real-world capable 6D pose estimation systems for robotic strawberry harvesting, thereby contributing to the practical deployment of autonomous agricultural robots.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research addresses its objectives through the creation of two complementary datasets—one real-world and one synthetic—and the evaluation of baseline 6D pose estimation models using these datasets.

**A. Dataset Collection**

1. **Real-World Dataset:**
 - **Data Acquisition:** Video sequences of red-stage strawberry plants were recorded in an actual agricultural field using an Intel RealSense D435i camera at a resolution of 640x480 pixels. A checkerboard (11x8 squares, 25mm square size) was placed near the plants during recording.
 - **Camera Calibration:** The intrinsic matrix (K) of the camera was determined through calibration, a prerequisite for the PnP algorithm.
 - **World-to-Camera Transformation (T_world→cam):** For frames where the checkerboard was visible, the Perspective-n-Point (PnP) algorithm was applied to estimate the camera's pose relative to the world coordinate system for each frame.
 - **3D Reconstruction and Metric Scaling:** Sparse 3D reconstruction was performed using COLMAP, aligning the reconstructed model to the world coordinate system by leveraging the per-frame `T_world→cam` estimates. This step recovered the metric scale of the scene. Subsequently, dense reconstruction was carried out using PatchMatch stereo and stereo fusion to generate a detailed metric point cloud.
 - **3D Bounding Box Annotation (T_local→world):** Manual annotation of 3D bounding boxes was performed on the reconstructed point cloud for each visible strawberry instance. These annotations defined the object's position, orientation, and dimensions in the world coordinate system.
 - **6D Pose Ground Truth (T_local→cam):** The final 6D pose of each strawberry instance relative to the camera was computed by composing the `T_world→cam` and `T_local→world` transformations. A conversion was applied to align with the OpenGL coordinate convention used by NVIDIA Isaac Sim.
 - **2D Bounding Box Annotation:** Manual 2D bounding boxes were also annotated on the RGB frames for instances with sufficient reconstruction quality.
 - **Dataset Size:** The resulting real-world dataset comprises 12,040 images and 16,037 annotated strawberry instances.

2. **Synthetic Dataset:**
 - **Environment Creation:** A strawberry farm environment was constructed within NVIDIA Isaac Sim. This environment included a custom-developed red-stage strawberry plant model, CC0-licensed ground material from AmbientCG, and a farm field HDRI from Poly Haven to ensure scene-level realism.
 - **Camera Configuration:** The synthetic camera was configured to precisely match the Intel RealSense D435i, using identical image resolution (640x480 pixels) and camera intrinsics.
 - **Camera Posing and Randomization:** The camera was positioned on a hemisphere centered on the strawberry plant, with distances sampled uniformly between 0.2 and 1.0 meters. A random offset was applied to the look-at target point to ensure strawberries appeared at varying 2D positions within the image.
 - **Domain Randomization:** To increase diversity and reduce the sim-to-real gap, domain randomization was applied to lighting conditions (intensity and direction) and strawberry plant geometry (size and shape), with a randomized seed for each scene.
 - **Annotation Generation:** RGB images, 2D bounding boxes, 3D bounding boxes (providing `T_local→world`), and camera extrinsics (`T_world→cam`) were directly obtained from the simulator. The 6D pose ground truth was then derived using the same formula as the real-world dataset.
 - **Dataset Size:** The synthetic dataset consists of 35,118 images and 127,910 annotated strawberry instances.

**B. 6D Pose Estimation Baseline Model and Training**

1. **Architecture:** The baseline model employs a shared architecture, where spatial features extracted by a backbone encoder are fed into a DETR-style transformer decoder. This decoder uses learnable object queries to jointly predict 2D bounding boxes, rotation (using the continuous 6D representation), and translation (decoupled into in-plane `x, y` and depth `z` components).
2. **Backbone Encoders:** Three distinct backbone encoders were evaluated:
 - **ResNet-101:** A convolutional neural network (CNN) serving as a traditional baseline.
 - **ViT-B/16:** A Vision Transformer (ViT) pre-trained with supervised learning.
 - **DINOv2-B:** A self-supervised vision foundation model.
3. **Loss Function:** The training objective involved a combination of losses:
 - **Hungarian Matching:** Object queries were matched to ground-truth annotations using a cost function that included L1 distance and negative GIoU for bounding boxes.
 - **Total Loss:** The total loss combined detection loss (classification, L1 bbox, GIoU) with pose losses (geodesic loss for rotation, SmoothL1 for in-plane and depth translation).
 - **Warmup Strategy:** Pose losses were activated gradually using a linear warmup factor over 20 epochs to stabilize early training, as object queries initially learn to localize.
 - **Loss Coefficients:** Specific weights were applied to different loss components, with higher weights for pose losses, especially depth translation, to reflect its difficulty.
4. **Experimental Setup:**
 - **Hardware:** Training was conducted on a single NVIDIA B200 GPU.
 - **Dataset Splits:** The real-world dataset was split into 10,040 training, 1,000 validation, and 1,000 test images. Validation and test sets comprised only real-world images.
 - **Training Configurations:** Models were trained under synthetic-only, real-only, and mixed settings (R1-R9, representing 10% to 90% real-world data in the training set, complemented by synthetic data).
 - **Training Parameters:** Models were trained for 100 epochs using the AdamW optimizer, a learning rate of 1e-4, weight decay of 1e-4, a StepLR scheduler, gradient clipping, and a batch size of 128. DINOv2 was used with a frozen backbone, while others were fine-tuned with a lower learning rate for their backbones.

**C. Evaluation Metrics**

Given the shape variability of organic objects, standard metrics like ADD/ADD-S were deemed unsuitable. The following metrics were employed:
- **Pose Accuracy:** The percentage of predictions where both translation error (fixed at `τ_t = 3 cm`) and rotation error (varied at `τ_r ∈ {5°, 10°, 20°}`) fell below their respective thresholds.
- **Detection Quality:** Evaluated using IoU@0.5 and IoU@0.75 for 2D bounding box overlap.
- **Mean Rotation Error:** Average geodesic error in degrees between predicted and ground-truth rotations.
- **Mean Translation Error:** Average error in centimeters, decomposed into in-plane (`x, y`) and depth (`z`) components.

**D. Dataset Analysis**

An analysis of ground-truth accuracy accounted for errors from camera calibration, PnP estimation, and COLMAP reconstruction. The pose distributions of both real-world and synthetic datasets were analyzed to identify differences in in-plane translation spread, mean depth range, and rotation viewpoint coverage, which could contribute to the sim-to-real gap.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted provided several key findings regarding 6D pose estimation for strawberry harvesting:

1. **Substantial Sim-to-Real Gap:**
 - Models trained exclusively on the synthetic dataset (even with scene-level realism and domain randomization) demonstrated severe performance degradation when evaluated on real agricultural field images.
 - Synthetic-only training yielded 0.0% pose accuracy across all evaluated backbones and rotation thresholds (3 cm translation threshold).
 - Mean rotation errors exceeded 90° for all backbones (e.g., DINOv2-B: 91.90°).
 - 2D detection quality was also very low (e.g., IoU@0.5 of 28% for DINOv2-B).
 - This finding quantifies that the domain gap between simulation and reality is substantial for 6D pose estimation in agricultural environments, affirming the necessity of real-world data.

2. **Rapid Domain Adaptation with Minimal Real Data:**
 - The introduction of a small amount of real-world data (as little as 10% in the R1 configuration) resulted in a dramatic improvement in performance across all backbone architectures and metrics.
 - For DINOv2-B, the mean rotation error dropped significantly from 91.90° (synthetic-only) to 25.00° (R1), representing a 73% reduction. Similarly, IoU@0.5 for detection rose from 28% to approximately 85%.
 - Performance continued to improve with increasing proportions of real data, although the most substantial gains were observed with the initial addition of real data, indicating a rapid but partial alignment to the real-world domain.

3. **Differential Adaptation for Detection and Pose Estimation:**
 - 2D detection quality demonstrated faster adaptation and earlier saturation compared to 6D pose accuracy. DINOv2-B and ViT-B/16 achieved high IoU@0.5 scores (around 85% and 81% respectively) at the R1 stage, eventually converging near 95% and 91% with full real training.
 - In contrast, 6D pose accuracy (e.g., at 3 cm/20°) was only around 16% for DINOv2-B and 12% for ViT-B/16 at R1, continuing to improve substantially as more real data was incorporated.
 - This asymmetry suggests that coarse 2D localization is more robust to domain shifts than the precise geometric understanding required for accurate 6D pose estimation.

4. **Superior Performance of DINOv2-B:**
 - DINOv2-B consistently outperformed ViT-B/16 and ResNet-101 across all training configurations and evaluation metrics.
 - Under real-only training, DINOv2-B achieved a mean rotation error of 5.04°, significantly lower than 7.49° for ViT-B/16 and 11.92° for ResNet-101.
 - The performance advantage of DINOv2-B became more pronounced at stricter rotation thresholds; for instance, at 3 cm/5°, DINOv2-B achieved 46.0% accuracy, while ViT-B/16 and ResNet-101 reached 25.9% and 9.4%, respectively.
 - This outcome indicates that self-supervised pretraining on large-scale visual data provides particularly beneficial features for fine-grained pose estimation in complex agricultural scenes.

5. **Depth Error as the Primary Translation Bottleneck:**
 - Analysis of translation errors revealed that the depth component (`ϵ_z`) consistently accounted for a disproportionately large share of the total translation error, significantly exceeding the in-plane (`x, y`) error across all conditions.
 - For DINOv2-B under real-only training, the in-plane error was 0.83 cm, whereas the depth error was 3.23 cm, approximately four times larger.
 - In-plane error was observed to be manageable even with limited real data (around 2 cm at R1), with only marginal improvements thereafter. Depth error, however, decreased more gradually and remained the primary limiting factor.
 - This finding suggests that for monocular RGB-only 6D pose estimation, accurate depth prediction is the most challenging aspect and a key bottleneck for achieving overall high pose accuracy relevant for robotic grasping.

6. **Qualitative Observations:**
 - Visualizations of predicted 6D poses confirmed that synthetic-only trained models exhibited significant axis misalignments.
 - As the proportion of real data in the training set increased, the predicted poses progressively converged closer to the ground truth, aligning with the quantitative results.

7. **Dataset Distribution Differences:**
 - An analysis of pose distributions between the real-world and synthetic datasets revealed discrepancies in in-plane translation spread, mean depth range, and rotation angle coverage. These distributional mismatches contribute to the observed sim-to-real gap.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of agricultural robotics and computer vision in several significant ways:

1. **Filling a Critical Data Gap:** The primary significance of this work is the introduction of the first real-world 6D pose ground truth dataset for strawberries collected in actual agricultural fields. This dataset, comprising 12,040 images and 16,037 annotated instances, addresses a long-standing need for realistic evaluation data in robotic strawberry harvesting. Its availability will enable researchers to develop and test perception systems under conditions representative of real-world deployment, moving beyond the limitations of synthetic or laboratory-based data.

2. **Quantifying the Sim-to-Real Gap:** The study provides a quantitative measurement of the sim-to-real gap for 6D pose estimation of strawberries. By demonstrating the severe performance drop of synthetically trained models on real data, even with improved synthetic realism, the research underscores the persistent challenges in domain adaptation. This quantification serves as a critical baseline for future research aiming to bridge this gap, whether through enhanced simulation, advanced domain adaptation techniques, or novel hybrid training strategies.

3. **Advancing Robotic Grasping Capabilities:** Accurate 6D pose estimation is fundamental for precise, damage-free robotic grasping of soft and fragile fruits like strawberries. The availability of this dataset and the established baselines can accelerate the development of more sophisticated perception modules for agricultural robots. This could lead to more efficient and less wasteful harvesting operations, directly impacting production costs and addressing labor shortages in the industry.

4. **Guiding Future Research and Development:** The detailed analysis of baseline performance across various backbone encoders and training configurations offers valuable insights. The findings highlight that:
 - Even a small amount of real data significantly improves performance, suggesting the potential for few-shot or semi-supervised learning approaches.
 - Detection and pose estimation tasks adapt differently across domains, with depth estimation being a particularly challenging bottleneck for monocular RGB-only systems. This identifies depth estimation as a key area for focused research and potential integration of depth sensors or multi-view systems.
 - The superior performance of self-supervised vision models (DINOv2-B) suggests that leveraging large-scale pre-trained features can be highly beneficial for fine-grained tasks in complex, unstructured environments like agricultural fields.

5. **Community Resource:** The public release of the real-world dataset will provide a valuable, standardized benchmark for the broader computer vision and robotics communities. This can foster collaborative research and accelerate innovation in agricultural robotics, object pose estimation, and sim-to-real transfer learning.

In conclusion, this research provides essential infrastructure and foundational knowledge that directly supports the development of robust, practical, and autonomous robotic systems for strawberry harvesting, contributing to the sustainability and efficiency of agricultural practices.
