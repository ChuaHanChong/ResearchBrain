# Breaking the SFT Plateau: Multimodal Structured Reinforcement Learning for Chart-to-Code Generation

> [!summary] Summary
> Meituan researchers developed Multimodal Structured Reinforcement Learning (MSRL) to address the performance limitations of Supervised Fine-Tuning in chart-to-code generation. Leveraging a new large-scale realistic dataset and multi-granularity rewards, MSRL achieved a new state-of-the-art among open-source models, outperforming a saturated SFT model by 6% on ChartMimic and showing competitive performance against proprietary models like GPT-4o.

## Problem

- Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) in multimodal large language models (MLLMs) reaches a performance plateau for chart-to-code generation, failing to generalize effectively from simplistic synthetic datasets.
- Generating precise, executable plotting code from complex visual charts is challenging for MLLMs, as critical information is sparse within boilerplate code, and SFT treats all tokens uniformly.
- Existing chart-to-code datasets are often limited in scale, diversity, and realism, hindering robust model development and generalization.

## Method

- A large-scale (3 million pairs), realistic ==chart-to-code== training corpus was constructed from arXiv tables, with a high-quality subset curated for reinforcement learning (RL) training through code content and visual fidelity filtering.
- Multimodal Structured Reinforcement Learning (MSRL) was developed, featuring a two-stage training strategy and a multi-granularity reward system that integrates rule-based textual accuracy and MLLM-based visual fidelity assessments.
- The approach fine-tuned an SFT-trained Qwen2.5-VL base model using Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), leveraging code normalization and weighted rewards for detailed code aspects and execution success.

## Results

- MSRL achieved state-of-the-art performance for open-source MLLMs on chart-to-code generation, with a **96.5%** execution rate and **83.8%** high-level score on ChartMimic, and **98.2%** execution rate and **89.9%** high-level score on ReachQA.
- The MSRL strategy significantly broke the SFT plateau, yielding an average improvement of **6%** on the ChartMimic benchmark over the saturated SFT model.
- MSRL demonstrated strong generalization capabilities, improving execution rates and high-level scores on unseen Seaborn and Plotly charts, and performed comparably to larger proprietary models like GPT-4o on detailed low-level metrics.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Simply scaling supervised fine-tuning data leads to diminishing returns, confirming an SFT performance plateau in complex structured output tasks like chart-to-code generation.
> - Reinforcement learning, when equipped with multi-granularity, structured rewards combining both textual and visual feedback, can effectively push MLLM performance beyond the SFT plateau for code generation from complex visual inputs.
> - A two-stage RL curriculum, prioritizing computationally cheaper textual rewards before incorporating more expensive visual rewards, balances optimization efficiency with overall performance gain.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses limitations in multimodal large language models (MLLMs) when applied to tasks involving information-dense images and structured output generation, specifically chart-to-code generation. Large language models (LLMs) have demonstrated advanced reasoning, often enhanced by reinforcement learning (RL) for tasks like code generation and mathematical problem-solving. While MLLMs have progressed in visual reasoning tasks such as visual question answering, their capabilities remain constrained when interpreting complex visual data like charts and producing structured outputs such as executable code.

Chart-to-code generation is identified as a particularly challenging task with practical implications, as it involves converting visual chart data into programming code that can reproduce the visualization. Existing approaches, predominantly Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) or Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) applied to synthetic datasets, have shown limited performance. This is attributed to the often simplistic patterns within synthetic corpora and a lack of effective generalization strategies. The current research aims to advance this area by developing robust strategies that leverage more realistic data and refined reinforcement learning techniques to move beyond the performance ceiling observed with conventional SFT methods. It also seeks to systematically analyze the SFT plateau rather than applying RL directly after SFT, which allows for isolating the specific benefits of the proposed RL strategy.

The paper contextualizes its work within ongoing research in several related areas:
- **Chart MLLMs:** These models focus on various aspects of chart understanding, from low-level data extraction to high-level question answering and summarization. Recent efforts include training MLLMs on extensive, chart-specific datasets and employing RL to enhance reasoning capacities for charts.
- **Reasoning MLLMs:** Building on the success of LLMs in reasoning, research extends to improving MLLM long-chain reasoning using RL with rule-based rewards. Algorithms like Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) are adapted for multimodal contexts, and enhanced RL strategies are being developed.
- **MLLMs for Code:** This domain involves generating various types of code from visual inputs, including execution code for visual problems and HTML from webpages. Chart-to-code generation is a specific and evolving task within this area, with benchmarks evaluating reproduction, editing, and other functionalities.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives of this research are multi-faceted:

- **Systematically Investigate the SFT Performance Plateau:** The authors aim to empirically demonstrate and analyze the performance limitations of Supervised Fine-Tuning when scaling data for chart-to-code generation. The motivation stems from the observation that SFT alone often proves insufficient for tasks requiring deep understanding of complex visual information and the generation of structured, precise outputs like code. Prior methods, relying on SFT or DPO with synthetic data, were found to yield suboptimal performance due to simplistic data patterns and limited generalization. The paper posits that merely increasing SFT data volume eventually leads to diminishing returns, hence the need for a systematic investigation to confirm this "SFT plateau."

- **Propose Multimodal Structured Reinforcement Learning (MSRL) to Break the SFT Plateau:** The core motivation for developing MSRL is to overcome the identified SFT limitations. The SFT objective treats all tokens in a target sequence with uniform importance. This approach is considered ineffective for optimizing plotting code generation, where critical information (e.g., specific data values, styling parameters) appears infrequently amidst boilerplate code snippets. Reinforcement learning, with a reward function specifically designed to prioritize the accuracy and correctness of such critical content, is proposed as a promising avenue to enhance code generation capacity beyond what SFT can achieve. MSRL is designed to integrate feedback from both textual and visual modalities to guide the learning process.

- **Construct a Large-Scale, Realistic Training Corpus for Chart-to-Code Generation:** The researchers recognized that existing chart-to-code datasets are often constrained by their reliance on purely synthetic data and limited scale. This results in charts that exhibit simplistic trends and lack diversity, which can hinder the generalization capabilities of MLLMs. To address this, a significant motivation was to curate a comprehensive dataset derived from real-world sources, ensuring a more realistic distribution of chart types and complexity. This corpus would serve as a foundation for thoroughly investigating SFT scaling and for training the MSRL system.

- **Evaluate and Demonstrate the Effectiveness and Generalization of MSRL:** A final objective is to rigorously evaluate MSRL against state-of-the-art open-source and proprietary MLLMs on established benchmarks. This evaluation is intended to quantify the performance gains achieved by MSRL, particularly its ability to improve high-level metrics and to generalize to unseen plotting libraries and complex chart structures. The motivation is to establish MSRL as a robust and effective strategy for advancing MLLM capabilities in visual understanding and structured code generation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology involves three main components: training corpus construction, Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT), and Multimodal Structured Reinforcement Learning (MSRL).

**Training Corpus Construction (Section 3.1):**
The authors constructed a large-scale chart-to-code training corpus to address limitations of previous synthetic datasets.
- **Data Source:** Real-world tables were extracted from arXiv papers published in 2023 and earlier. This temporal constraint was applied to ensure no overlap with the ChartMimic benchmark.
- **Code Generation:** LLMs were employed to generate diverse and complex plotting code from these tables.
- **Scale:** The initial corpus comprised 3 million chart-code pairs.
- **RL Data Curation:** A separate high-quality subset for RL training was created through a two-stage filtering process:
 - **Stage 1 (Code Content Filtering):** From an initial 130,000 candidate samples, code was filtered based on chart type (using plot functions and tree-structured parsing for complex multi-chart types) and data type (retaining only 1D arrays and non-nested dictionaries). This reduced the set to 45,000 samples.
 - **Stage 2 (Visual Quality Filtering):** GPT-4.1-mini, acting as a visual LLM judge, filtered for high-fidelity chart images. Manual inspection of a subset showed over 90% consistency with human judgment. This yielded a final RL training corpus of 33,000 samples. The remaining samples were designated for SFT.
- **Dataset Characteristics:** The resulting dataset features 24 distinct chart types, a completely realistic data source, multi-chart content, and support for 1,555 Matplotlib API types, indicating rich visual diversity.

**Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) (Section 3.2):**
- **Base Model:** Qwen2.5-VL was chosen as the base model.
- **Data Scaling Experiments:** To investigate the SFT plateau, the model was fine-tuned on six distinct training corpus sizes: 200k, 400k, 800k, 1.6M, 2M, and 2.8M samples, all derived from the constructed dataset.
- **Training Objective:** The model was trained by minimizing the standard autoregressive negative log-likelihood of the target sequence.

**Multimodal Structured Reinforcement Learning (MSRL) (Section 3.3):**
MSRL was designed to enhance the SFT-trained model by leveraging multi-granularity reward functions.
- **Code Normalization:** A code normalization step was introduced to map generated outputs to a canonical representation. This step aims to mitigate issues arising from stylistic diversity in generated code, facilitating consistent reward computation.
- **Multi-Granularity Reward System:**
 - **Textual Reward:** A rule-based accuracy score, computed as a weighted average across five dimensions:
 - Data values: Assessed via soft value matching (with a ±5% relative error tolerance).
 - Chart types: Evaluated by hard string matching.
 - Layout: Assessed via hard value matching.
 - Titles and labels: Evaluated using edit distance.
 - Execution Reward: A binary score indicating whether the generated code executes successfully.
 - **Visual Reward:** This reward assesses the overall structural similarity of the rendered chart.
 - Process: Generated code is executed to render a chart image. An MLLM (Qwen2.5-VL-72B) then evaluates the visual similarity between this rendered image and the original input chart across six aspects: chart type, layout, text content, data, style, and clarity. The scores are normalized. Code failing to render receives a reward of 0.
- **Two-Stage RL Training Strategy:**
 - **Stage 1:** The model is optimized using only the textual reward. This stage prioritizes computational efficiency.
 - **Stage 2:** A hybrid reward, combining both textual and visual rewards, is introduced. This stage uses a reduced number of samples to fine-tune the model for visual fidelity, balancing performance and computational cost.
 - **Total Reward:** The total reward R for a completed trajectory is calculated as a weighted sum: R = w_t * R_text + w_v * R_vis + w_e * R_exec, where w_t, w_v, and w_e are hyperparameters.
 - **RL Algorithm:** Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) was adopted for RL, which leverages group-wise relative advantages among sampled responses, aligning with the customized reward designs.

**Implementation Details (Appendix B):**
- **SFT:** Qwen2.5VL-7B-Instruct with LLaMA-Factory, trained for one epoch with 1e-5 learning rate, batch size 32, on 16 H800 GPUs (60 hours).
- **RL:** Built upon the SFT model using the MM-EUREKA framework. Stage 1 used 22k data pairs with textual-only reward (weights: wt=1, wv=0, we=0.5). Stage 2 used 11k pairs with hybrid reward (weights: wt=0.5, wv=0.5, we=0.5). Textual reward components had specific weights. Both stages trained for one episode, learning rate 1e-6, batch size 128, temperature 1, 8 rollouts per sample. Stage 1 required 10 hours and Stage 2 required 24 hours on 24 H800 GPUs.

**Evaluation (Section 4.1):**
Models were evaluated in a zero-shot setting on ChartMimic (600 examples from Direct Mimic task) and ReachQA (500 plotting scripts). Baselines included proprietary models (e.g., GPT-4o, Claude-3-opus), general-domain open-source MLLMs (e.g., Qwen2-VL, InternVL2), and chart-specific open-source MLLMs (e.g., ChartCoder, ChartLlama).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presented several findings regarding the performance of SFT and the effectiveness of the proposed MSRL strategy:

- **SFT Performance Plateau:** Experiments scaling SFT data from 200k to 2.8M samples demonstrated a clear performance plateau. Model performance improved significantly up to approximately 2 million data points but showed negligible further gains with additional data. This indicates that simply increasing the volume of SFT data eventually leads to diminishing returns and is insufficient to overcome inherent limitations in structured output generation.

- **MSRL Achieves State-of-the-Art Performance:** The MSRL model demonstrated a new state-of-the-art among open-source MLLMs for chart-to-code generation. On the ChartMimic benchmark, MSRL achieved an execution rate of 96.5%, a low-level score of 78.6%, and a high-level score of 83.8%. On the ReachQA benchmark, it achieved an execution rate of 98.2%, a low-level score of 86.1%, and a high-level score of 89.9%. These results surpass all previous open-source models, including specialized chart-domain MLLMs like ChartCoder.

- **MSRL Breaks the SFT Plateau:** Applying the MSRL strategy to a saturated SFT model resulted in substantial performance improvements. Specifically, MSRL yielded an average improvement of 6% on the ChartMimic benchmark, with notable increases in execution rate, low-level, and high-level scores (e.g., a 6.2% improvement in high-level metrics). This suggests that the MSRL approach successfully moves beyond the limitations of SFT alone.

- **Competitive with Proprietary Models:** MSRL's performance was comparable to, and in some detailed low-level metrics, surpassed that of larger proprietary models such as GPT-4o. This outcome is notable given that MSRL is based on a 7B-parameter open-source model, while GPT-4o is a significantly larger model.

- **Detailed Low-Level Performance:** A detailed evaluation of low-level metrics (Text, Layout, Type, Color) on both ChartMimic and ReachQA showed MSRL outperforming all open-source models and surpassing GPT-4o on a majority of these fine-grained measures, indicating strong capacity in generating precise code details.

- **RL Training Plateau:** The two-stage RL training process also exhibited a performance curve. The first stage (textual rewards only) achieved approximately 5% performance improvement, converging around 22k training samples. The second stage (multimodal rewards) provided an additional 1.5% improvement, converging around 11k training samples. This demonstrates the efficiency and effectiveness of the two-stage curriculum, allowing for targeted optimization.

- **Ablation Studies Validating Methodology:**
 - **SFT/RL Interaction:** Ablations showed that an RL-only model performed worse than an SFT-only approach on high-level scores, while RL applied to a strong SFT baseline delivered significant gains. This indicates that the proposed multi-granularity reward function effectively surpasses the upper limit of SFT.
 - **Reward Strategy:** Both textual and visual rewards independently improved model performance over the SFT baseline, with visual rewards being more effective for visual fidelity but demanding more computational resources. The two-stage RL strategy was found to balance performance and efficiency by initially leveraging lower-cost textual rewards before incorporating higher-cost visual rewards.
 - **Visual Evaluator Choice:** Ablations on visual evaluators indicated that MLLMs like Qwen2.5-VL-72B correlated well with human judgment in assessing chart fidelity, outperforming pure vision models which captured similarity at a coarser level.
 - **GRPO vs. DPO:** The Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) algorithm significantly outperformed Direct Preference Optimization (DPO), validating its suitability for the task and the designed structured reward system.
 - **Dataset Quality:** Comparison with the ChartCoder dataset confirmed that the authors' newly constructed dataset, derived from real-world tables with its diversity and multi-chart content, contributed to superior performance in both SFT and RL stages.

- **Generalization to Unseen Plotting Libraries:** MSRL demonstrated an ability to generalize to out-of-domain Seaborn and Plotly chart images, which were not part of its Matplotlib-focused training. It significantly improved execution rates and high-level scores compared to the base Qwen2.5-VL-7B model on these unseen libraries.

- **Qualitative Analysis:** Visualizations of generated charts confirmed that MSRL produced higher-fidelity reproductions compared to leading open-source MLLMs and even GPT-4o, exhibiting greater precision in data values and layouts, and addressing issues like incorrect chart type recognition or layout syntax errors observed in other models.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work presents several contributions with potential significance and impact within the field of multimodal AI and code generation:

- **Addressing the SFT Performance Plateau:** The research systematically demonstrates and quantifies the performance limitations of Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) for complex structured output generation tasks like chart-to-code. This finding is significant as it highlights that merely scaling data is insufficient for continuous performance improvement in such domains, thereby motivating the development of more advanced learning paradigms.

- **Advancing Chart-to-Code Generation:** The proposed Multimodal Structured Reinforcement Learning (MSRL) strategy establishes a new state-of-the-art for open-source models in chart-to-code generation. Its competitive performance with advanced proprietary models like GPT-4o, despite using a smaller base model, indicates a substantial leap in capability for automated chart interpretation and code synthesis. This advancement can contribute to more reliable and accurate conversion of visual data into actionable code.

- **Novel Multimodal Reinforcement Learning Framework:** The MSRL framework introduces a sophisticated multi-granularity reward system that integrates both rule-based textual feedback for fine-grained code correctness and MLLM-based visual feedback for overall chart fidelity. Coupled with a two-stage curriculum training strategy, this methodology offers a template for optimizing MLLMs on tasks requiring detailed structured outputs from rich multimodal inputs. This approach could potentially be adapted to other visual-to-code generation tasks or complex reasoning problems where both precise local details and global structural coherence are critical.

- **Creation of a Large-Scale, Realistic Dataset:** The construction of a 3-million-pair training corpus derived from real-world tables in arXiv papers represents a significant resource for the research community. This dataset addresses the limitations of previous synthetic or smaller-scale datasets by offering greater diversity and realism in chart types and complexity. Its availability can foster more robust model development and benchmarking in chart-to-code generation and related areas.

- **Improved Generalization Capabilities:** The demonstrated ability of MSRL to generalize its capabilities to unseen plotting libraries (Seaborn and Plotly), despite being trained primarily on Matplotlib-style code, suggests that the learned representations and reasoning skills are not narrowly confined to the training domain. This improved generalization is crucial for the practical deployment of such models in varied real-world scenarios.

- **Practical Applications in Scientific and Data Visualization:** The enhanced accuracy and robustness of chart-to-code generation can have a direct impact on scientific research and data analysis. Automating the reproduction of scientific visualizations from published charts can accelerate research validation, aid in data exploration, and facilitate the creation of interactive data dashboards, potentially reducing manual effort and errors.
