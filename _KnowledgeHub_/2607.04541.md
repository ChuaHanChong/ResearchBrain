# CRISP: A Spatiotemporal Camera-Radar Backbone for Driving via Forecasting-Based World-Model Pretraining

> [!summary] Summary
> CRISP introduces a camera-radar (CR) spatiotemporal Bird's-Eye-View (BEV) backbone that learns robust, motion-aware representations for autonomous driving. It achieves state-of-the-art performance across a suite of downstream perception, prediction, and planning tasks by pretraining on future LiDAR point cloud forecasting, enabling LiDAR-free inference during deployment.

## Problem

- The high cost and form factor of LiDAR limit the scalable deployment of LC fusion systems in production autonomous vehicles.
- Existing autonomous driving perception models rely on extensive, costly task-specific 3D annotations for supervised training.
- Radar's inherent motion-sensing capabilities are underexplored in transferable, forecasting-based pretraining for learning general scene dynamics.

## Method

- A Camera-Radar (CR) spatiotemporal Bird's-Eye-View (BEV) backbone, named **CRISP**, is developed to fuse camera and radar data into a unified representation.
- The backbone is pretrained using a ==forecasting-based objective==: predicting future 3D LiDAR point clouds, with LiDAR used solely for supervision during this phase.
- An **enhanced radar encoder**, ==radar-enhanced temporal self-attention==, and ==modality innovation gating== in multimodal feature rendering enable effective integration of radar's motion cues and selective fusion.

## Results

- CRISP achieves superior long-horizon point cloud forecasting, outperforming camera-only ViDAR (e.g., **0.58 m²** CD at **1.5s** vs. **1.25 m²**) and LRS4Fusion baselines.
- The pretrained backbone leads to state-of-the-art or improved performance on downstream tasks, including 3D object detection (**53.2 mAP**, **61.3 NDS**), tracking, motion forecasting, and planning on the nuScenes dataset.
- The pretraining framework improves label efficiency, yielding **over 3 percentage point** gains in NDS and mAP for 3D detection when using reduced training data.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating radar's direct motion cues into a forecasting-based world-model pretraining framework fundamentally improves spatiotemporal representation learning for future prediction.
> - Leveraging LiDAR as privileged supervision exclusively during pretraining can effectively transfer comprehensive 3D geometric and temporal understanding to a CR backbone without requiring LiDAR at inference.
> - Tailored fusion mechanisms, such as radar-enhanced temporal self-attention and modality innovation gating, are essential for robustly combining sparse radar data with dense visual information in a unified BEV representation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Autonomous driving systems require robust perception to understand complex environments, relying on various sensor modalities such as cameras, LiDAR, and radar. Each modality offers distinct advantages: cameras provide dense semantic information, LiDAR delivers accurate 3D geometry, and radar offers robustness to adverse weather and lighting while directly measuring motion through Doppler velocity. Given the limitations of any single sensor, multimodal fusion has become a significant area of research.

Historically, LiDAR-camera (LC) fusion has demonstrated strong performance, but the cost and form factor of LiDAR systems present challenges for large-scale deployment in production vehicles. In response, camera-radar (CR) fusion has gained increasing attention as a more scalable and cost-effective alternative, with applications in 3D object detection, scene segmentation, mapping, and occupancy prediction.

Despite advances in CR fusion, many autonomous vehicle (AV) perception models traditionally rely on task-specific supervised training, which necessitates expensive and time-consuming manual annotation of 3D data. This has driven interest in pretraining methods, which aim to learn reusable representations from large amounts of unlabeled or weakly labeled data, subsequently reducing the need for extensive downstream task annotations. Early pretraining efforts in autonomous driving often focused on static reconstruction of masked LiDAR points, voxels, or Bird's-Eye-View (BEV) features. More recently, multimodal pretraining has emerged, utilizing LiDAR as privileged supervision for camera-based or LC systems, often through depth prediction or masked scene rendering.

Concurrently, forecasting-based pretraining and "world-model" learning have been recognized as effective strategies for encoding scene dynamics, particularly for tasks requiring future prediction. However, these forecasting approaches have predominantly been explored in camera-only (CO) or LC settings, leaving radar largely unexplored in transferable representation learning. This is a notable gap, as radar's direct measurements of range and Doppler velocity are inherently suited for predictive scene modeling. Existing CR methods, while beneficial, are mostly trained for supervised downstream heads, rather than learning a general, transferable multimodal backbone.

CRISP addresses this gap by integrating camera-radar fusion with forecasting-based pretraining. It seeks to leverage radar's intrinsic motion-sensing capabilities within a world-model pretraining framework to learn a unified spatiotemporal BEV representation. Unlike previous methods that either pretrain a single modality or perform separate pretraining before fusion, CRISP aims for joint CR pretraining, with LiDAR serving as privileged supervision *only* during the pretraining phase to forecast future 3D scene structure. This approach positions CRISP as an advancement in developing scalable, low-cost sensor configurations for autonomous driving while enabling robust, motion-aware predictive capabilities.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a transferable spatiotemporal camera-radar (CR) Bird's-Eye-View (BEV) backbone, named CRISP, through forecasting-based representation learning. This backbone is designed to learn unified representations that capture multimodal geometry, radar motion cues, temporal dynamics, and BEV semantics, while maintaining the practical advantage of LiDAR-free inference during deployment.

The motivation for CRISP arises from several key observations and existing limitations in autonomous driving perception:

- **Cost and Scalability of Sensing:** While LiDAR-camera (LC) fusion offers strong performance, the high cost and form factor of LiDAR limit its widespread deployment in production vehicles. Camera-radar (CR) configurations, already prevalent in Advanced Driver-Assistance Systems (ADAS), provide a more scalable and cost-effective alternative. Developing robust CR fusion systems is crucial for practical autonomous driving.
- **Limitations of Task-Specific Supervised Training:** Many existing AV perception models, including CR fusion networks, are trained with extensive task-specific 3D annotations (e.g., for detection, segmentation). Acquiring these annotations at scale is expensive and laborious. Pretraining offers a more scalable approach to learn effective representations from abundant unlabeled or weakly labeled data, which can then be fine-tuned for various downstream tasks with less specialized supervision.
- **Underexplored Role of Radar in Forecasting-Based Pretraining:** Recent advances in forecasting-based pretraining and world models have shown promise in encoding scene dynamics for driving. However, these methods have largely focused on camera-only (CO) or LC settings. Radar, with its direct measurements of range and Doppler velocity, is uniquely suited for predicting future motion and scene evolution, yet its potential in transferable representation learning through forecasting-based pretraining remains largely unexplored. Integrating radar can anchor future scene prediction by complementing visual semantics with instantaneous motion evidence.
- **Need for Reusable, Multimodal Backbones:** Prior CR methods primarily optimize for specific downstream heads (e.g., detection, segmentation) and often introduce temporal modeling to enhance a particular task. There is a need for a unified CR backbone that can learn fused spatiotemporal representations that are broadly transferable across a wide range of perception, prediction, and planning tasks, rather than being specialized.
- **Bridging Modality Gaps without LiDAR Inference:** CRISP aims to leverage LiDAR as "privileged supervision" during the pretraining phase to teach the CR backbone strong 3D geometric and temporal understanding. Critically, LiDAR is *not* required at inference time, allowing the deployed model to remain a practical CR-only system. This approach facilitates learning comprehensive representations by bridging camera, radar, and LiDAR during pretraining, without incurring LiDAR costs during operation.

In summary, CRISP seeks to bridge the gap between practical, low-cost CR sensing and advanced, scalable representation learning paradigms by introducing a forecasting-based pretraining framework. The objective is to produce a versatile CR backbone that not only performs well on various downstream tasks but also fundamentally understands scene dynamics and geometry, learned through the predictive task of future LiDAR point cloud forecasting.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

CRISP is designed as a Camera-Radar (CR) spatiotemporal Bird's-Eye-View (BEV) backbone pretrained via future point cloud forecasting. The overall approach involves encoding historical multi-view images and radar sweeps into a unified spatiotemporal BEV feature, which is then used by a future prediction decoder to forecast LiDAR point clouds. LiDAR is exclusively used for supervision during pretraining and is removed during downstream inference.

The methodology can be broken down into the following key components:

**A. Overall Architecture:**
CRISP's architecture consists of:
1. **Image Encoder:** Extracts multi-view image features.
2. **Enhanced Radar Encoder:** Converts radar sweeps into BEV-aligned radar features.
3. **CR Spatiotemporal BEV Backbone:** The core component that fuses camera and radar features to produce a unified spatiotemporal BEV representation. This backbone iteratively refines BEV tokens over multiple layers.
4. **Future Prediction Decoder:** Used *only during pretraining*, this autoregressive decoder predicts current and future scene occupancy, supervised by LiDAR point clouds.

**B. Enhanced Radar Encoder:**
This module processes radar sweeps to make them more suitable for BEV perception, addressing the ego-centric nature of Doppler velocity measurements and modality gaps.
1. **Ego-Motion Awareness:** Given pillar-based radar BEV features and ego-motion information, a lightweight Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) encodes the ego state. This embedding is broadcast and additively fused with the radar BEV feature map. This ego-motion-dependent bias allows the radar representation to adapt to the vehicle's instantaneous motion, making Doppler cues contextually interpretable.
2. **Residual Spatial Calibration:** To reduce the feature distribution gap between radar and camera, a residual spatial calibrator is introduced. It projects the ego-motion-aware radar feature into a lower-dimensional space, concatenates it with a BEV positional encoding, and predicts a location-aware correction that is added back to the original radar feature. This spatially varying refinement improves compatibility with camera BEV features without distorting the original radar signal.

**C. Camera-Radar Spatiotemporal BEV Backbone:**
This is the central innovation where CR interaction occurs directly within the BEV encoder, replacing camera-centric latent rendering. The backbone comprises `L` (typically 6) repeated layers, each performing radar-enhanced temporal self-attention, multimodal feature rendering, and a position-wise feed-forward network.

1. **Radar-Enhanced Temporal Self-Attention:**
 - **Gated Radar Conditioning:** Before temporal aggregation, the calibrated radar BEV feature modulates the current BEV query through a learned query-conditioned radar gate (sigmoid activation on a linear projection of the query). This gated residual update, `Q_t^(l) = Q_t^(l-1) + g_t,q^(l) ⊙ r_t^(l)`, primes the BEV query with radar range and motion cues, allowing radar to influence subsequent temporal attention.
 - **Guided Temporal Offset:** The radar-primed query, combined with positional encoding, is used in ego-aligned deformable temporal attention. This means the prediction of sampling offsets and attention weights for historical BEV memory becomes radar-aware through the query itself, enabling radar to naturally benefit temporal sampling.
 - **Query-Conditioned Temporal Gating:** Instead of uniformly averaging current and historical outputs from temporal deformable attention, CRISP introduces a spatial gate, `g_t,temp^(l)`, predicted from the query. This gate balances the contributions of current-frame evidence (`O_t,cur^(l)`) and propagated historical memory (`O_t,hist^(l)`), allowing flexible fusion.

2. **Multimodal Feature Rendering with Modality Innovation Gating (MIG):**
 - After temporal aggregation, each BEV token incorporates evidence from both cameras and radar. This replaces ViDAR's camera-centric latent rendering, as radar already provides metric BEV observations.
 - **Camera Spatial Cross-Attention (SCA):** A BEVFormer-style SCA renders multi-view image features into the BEV representation by sampling image features from valid camera projections of 3D reference points associated with BEV queries. This produces a camera-specific residual update (`∆Q_t,cam^(l)`).
 - **Radar Cross-Attention (RCA):** A BEV-to-BEV deformable RCA directly samples from the calibrated radar BEV feature map using 2D BEV reference points, generating a radar-specific residual update (`∆Q_t,rad^(l)`).
 - **Modality Innovation Gating (MIG):** This crucial component predicts *independent* query-conditioned gates (`g_t,cam^(l)` and `g_t,rad^(l)`) for the camera and radar residual updates. The final BEV query is updated as `Q_t,rend^(l) = Q_t,pre^(l) + g_t,cam^(l) ⊙ ∆Q_t,cam^(l) + g_t,rad^(l) ⊙ ∆Q_t,rad^(l)`. The independent gates allow each layer to selectively fuse camera, radar, both, or neither, based on local scene context, preserving the existing BEV state while admitting useful sensor evidence.

**D. Forecasting-Based Pretraining Objective:**
CRISP adopts ViDAR's objective, using an autoregressive occupancy prediction head *only during pretraining*.
1. **Occupancy Prediction Head:** This head, composed of transformer layers, takes the multimodal BEV states (from `B_fused_t`) and a learned embedding of future ego-motion to predict future BEV occupancy logits (`O_t+τ`) over a grid with vertical bins.
2. **Pretraining Loss:** LiDAR point clouds serve as privileged supervision. The loss combines a sparse LiDAR occupancy classification loss (encouraging high probability for observed surfaces and low for empty space) and a dense voxel occupancy reconstruction loss (promoting spatially coherent geometry).

**E. Downstream Finetuning:**
After pretraining, the future prediction decoder is discarded. The pretrained camera branch, radar encoder, and CR spatiotemporal BEV backbone are retained as initialization for various downstream tasks (e.g., 3D detection, tracking, planning). Task-specific heads are then attached and fine-tuned. This ensures that the finetuned model remains a practical CR system without requiring LiDAR at inference.

**Implementation Details:**
Experiments are conducted on the nuScenes dataset. The camera encoder uses a ResNet-101 and FPN. Radar points are voxelized and processed by the enhanced radar encoder built on PointPillars. The BEV grid is 200x200. The backbone has six encoder layers. Pretraining runs for 24 epochs. For downstream tasks, the pretrained backbone initializes standard models like BEVFormer and UniAD.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

CRISP was evaluated on point cloud forecasting and a comprehensive suite of downstream autonomous driving tasks using the nuScenes dataset. The results demonstrate the effectiveness of forecasting-based CR pretraining.

**A. Point Cloud Forecasting:**
- **Superior Long-Horizon Forecasting:** CRISP consistently outperforms the camera-only (CO) ViDAR baseline and even the LiDAR-camera (LC) LRS4Fusion baseline for long-horizon (1.5s to 3.0s) point cloud forecasting. For instance, with a 3s history, CRISP achieved Chamfer Distances (CD) of 0.58 m² at 1.5s and 0.84 m² at 3.0s, significantly better than ViDAR (1.25 m² at 1.5s, 1.73 m² at 3.0s) and outperforming LRS4Fusion from 1.5s onwards.
- **Radar's Role in Motion:** This indicates that the CR backbone, especially with its radar-enhanced temporal attention and multimodal feature rendering, effectively learns motion-aware BEV representations for future prediction, going beyond simple auxiliary radar cues.
- **Outperforming Language-Augmented Models:** CRISP also outperformed HERMES (a language-augmented world model) at reported horizons under the 0s history setting, suggesting its sensor-only pretraining strategy yields competitive predictive accuracy.

**B. Downstream Finetuning (UniAD-CRISP or BEVFormer-CRISP):**
The pretrained CRISP backbone was integrated into BEVFormer (for detection) and UniAD (for multi-task driving) and fine-tuned.

- **3D Object Detection (Table II):** CRISP achieved 53.2 mAP and 61.3 NDS, outperforming both CO pretraining baselines (e.g., ViDAR-pretrained BEVFormer: 45.8 mAP, 54.8 NDS) and a strong CR multi-task baseline (SpaRC-AD: 46.6 mAP, 57.0 NDS). This suggests that CR forecasting-based pretraining provides a stronger BEV initialization for object-centric 3D perception.
- **Online Map Segmentation (Table III):** UniAD-CRISP achieved the best IoU for lane (35.4%) and divider (29.3%) categories, while remaining competitive on drivable area and crossing. This indicates that CR pretraining does not degrade performance on static scene elements, even if radar's primary benefit is in dynamic tasks.
- **Online Tracking (Table IV):** UniAD-CRISP improved AMOTA (48.6%), AMOTP (1.12 m), and Recall (0.57) over both CO baselines (e.g., ViDAR: 43.46% AMOTA, 1.25 m AMOTP) and SpaRC-AD (46.9% AMOTA, 1.13 m AMOTP). Radar's direct motion cues are noted as beneficial for maintaining object continuity during tracking.
- **Motion Forecasting (Table V):** UniAD-CRISP achieved the best minADE (0.53 m), minFDE (0.85 m), and EPA (0.571), and second-best MR (0.134). This demonstrates that the pretraining objective encourages encoding dynamic scene evolution, and radar observations contribute to accurate agent prediction.
- **Future Occupancy Prediction (Table VI):** UniAD-CRISP achieved the best results across near and far regions for both VPQ and IoU, with particularly strong gains in the far field (e.g., VPQ-f 47.5% vs. ViDAR's 37.8%). This highlights radar's value at long ranges where camera depth estimates are less reliable.
- **Planning (Table VII):** UniAD-CRISP reduced both average L2 error (0.80 m) and average collision rate (0.15%), outperforming ViDAR-pretrained UniAD (0.94 m L2, 0.20% collision) and SpaRC-AD (0.93 m L2, 0.23% collision). This suggests that the improved CR BEV representation translates to better planning performance and safety metrics.

**C. Label-Efficiency of Pretraining (Fig. 5):**
- CRISP consistently improved mAP and NDS across different training data fractions (1/4, 1/2, full nuScenes dataset) for 3D detection.
- Significant gains were observed in the low-label regime, with over 3 percentage point improvements in NDS and mAP when using only 1/4 or 1/2 of the training data. This indicates that CRISP pretraining provides a stronger initialization, reducing dependence on extensive 3D box annotations.

**D. Ablation Study (Table VIII, IX, X, XI):**
- **Component Ablation (Table VIII):** Adding radar-enhanced temporal self-attention yielded significant forecasting gains. Multimodal feature rendering (replacing ViDAR's latent rendering) provided the largest downstream improvement, particularly in NDS and mAVE (velocity error). The enhanced radar encoder further contributed a consistent gain.
- **Radar Encoder Design (Table IX):** Ego-motion awareness and residual spatial calibration cumulatively improved performance, particularly in AEE and NDS, by making radar features more context-aware and compatible with camera features.
- **Radar-Enhanced Temporal Self-Attention (Table X):** Gated radar conditioning and temporal gating individually and cumulatively improved forecasting and detection metrics, especially AEE and mAVE, supporting the benefit of radar-primed queries and selective temporal fusion.
- **Multimodal Feature Rendering (Table XI):** Innovation gating, which selectively gates modality-specific residual updates, achieved the best downstream transfer while maintaining competitive forecasting, outperforming adaptive scalar weighting and full gating.

**E. Qualitative Analysis (Figures 6, 7, 8):**
- **Robustness in Low-Light (Fig. 6):** In a low-light scene, CRISP maintained a stable prediction of a forward vehicle's trajectory, while the CO ViDAR baseline lost the vehicle and exhibited spatial drift. This highlights radar's value under challenging illumination.
- **Anchoring to Metric Structure (Fig. 7):** CRISP better preserved physical scene layout compared to ViDAR, which propagated erroneous structures from billboard-like regions, indicating radar-enhanced BEV features help anchor predictions to metric scene structure rather than purely visual texture.
- **Failure Mode - High-Level Semantics (Fig. 8):** CRISP's predictions could drift when future behavior depended on subtle, high-level traffic semantics (e.g., unobservable traffic light influencing a stopped vehicle). This suggests limitations when causal reasoning, traffic rules, or agent intent are not explicitly modeled.

In conclusion, the findings indicate that CRISP effectively integrates camera and radar data through a novel pretraining paradigm, yielding a robust spatiotemporal BEV representation. This representation not only improves fundamental tasks like 3D point cloud forecasting, especially at longer horizons and under challenging conditions, but also significantly enhances performance across a broad spectrum of perception, prediction, and planning tasks crucial for autonomous driving, while also demonstrating improved label efficiency.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The CRISP framework represents a significant contribution to autonomous driving research by bridging the gap between practical, low-cost sensor configurations and scalable, predictive representation learning. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Enabling Scalable and Cost-Effective Autonomous Driving:** By demonstrating that a camera-radar (CR) system can achieve high-performance perception, prediction, and planning capabilities without LiDAR at inference time, CRISP paves the way for more affordable and widely deployable autonomous vehicles. This aligns with the industry's drive towards more production-ready and cost-optimized sensor suites.
- **Advancing Multimodal Pretraining for Driving:** CRISP innovates by being among the first to successfully integrate radar into a forecasting-based world-model pretraining framework. It establishes that radar's direct motion cues are not merely auxiliary but can fundamentally improve the learning of spatiotemporal dynamics and geometry, especially for long-horizon prediction. This provides a new direction for multimodal representation learning in driving.
- **Learning Reusable and Transferable Representations:** The broad transferability of the pretrained CRISP backbone across diverse downstream tasks (3D detection, tracking, mapping, motion forecasting, occupancy prediction, and planning) is a key achievement. This suggests that the learned representation encodes general, fundamental properties of the driving environment, reducing the need for extensive task-specific model architectures and supervised training.
- **Improving Label Efficiency:** The demonstrated gains in performance, particularly in low-label regimes, highlight CRISP's potential to alleviate the burden of costly and time-consuming manual 3D data annotation. A strong pretrained backbone can accelerate the development and deployment of new autonomous driving features and systems.
- **Enhanced Understanding of Dynamic Scenes:** CRISP's robust performance in point cloud forecasting, especially for dynamic objects and in challenging conditions (e.g., low light), underscores its ability to capture complex scene evolution. This is critical for safety-critical applications requiring accurate understanding and prediction of moving agents.
- **Novel Architectural Contributions:** The specific design elements, such as the enhanced radar encoder, radar-enhanced temporal self-attention, and multimodal feature rendering with modality innovation gating, provide practical solutions for integrating sparse, ego-motion-sensitive radar data with dense visual semantics. These architectural innovations could inspire similar fusion strategies in other multimodal contexts.
- **Foundation for Future Driving World Models:** CRISP's success in forecasting 3D scene structure from CR inputs serves as a strong foundation for more advanced driving world models. Future work could build upon this by incorporating higher-level reasoning (traffic rules, agent intent, causality), uncertainty estimation, and counterfactual planning, moving beyond purely geometric prediction.
- **Broader Impact in Robotics:** The core principle of learning deployment-friendly sensor representations from richer, training-only supervision has implications beyond autonomous driving. This approach could benefit various robotic systems operating under constraints of cost, form factor, partial observability, and sensor degradation, fostering more robust and adaptable robotic perception.

In conclusion, CRISP significantly pushes the boundaries of autonomous driving perception by demonstrating a viable path towards scalable and robust CR systems. Its contribution lies not only in achieving state-of-the-art performance but also in establishing a novel paradigm for sensor fusion and representation learning that can accelerate the development of future autonomous technologies.
