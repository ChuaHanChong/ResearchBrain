# Dynamic robotic cloth folding with efficient Koopman operator-based model predictive control

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers developed a Model Predictive Control framework combining a high-fidelity physics-based simulator with an efficient Koopman operator-based data-driven model to enable a robot to perform fast, accurate cloth folding with a single corner grasp and continuous motion. The system achieved successful zero-shot sim-to-real transfer, demonstrating average relative folding errors of 5.18% for wool and 2.93% for denim on a UR5 robot.

## Problem

- Cloth exhibits highly nonlinear and complex dynamics under fast motions, making accurate system identification and trajectory planning challenging for robotic manipulation.
- Existing robotic cloth folding methods are often quasi-static, too slow, require bimanual setups, or lack precision for dynamic single-manipulator tasks, failing to emulate human-like efficiency.
- The "sim-to-real gap" is particularly problematic for dynamic cloth manipulation, as high speeds amplify inertial effects and lead to discrepancies between simulated and real-world behavior.

## Method

- A ==Model Predictive Control== (MPC) framework was developed, integrating a high-fidelity physics-based cloth simulator (SOM) with an efficient data-driven surrogate model.
- Koopman operator regression, combined with the Nyström method, was used to learn a linear, lower-dimensional surrogate model (COM) from training data generated by the SOM.
- The linear dynamics from the Koopman COM were embedded into an MPC framework to solve finite-horizon optimal control problems, generating optimal robot trajectories while enforcing physical and robot constraints.

## Results

- The control strategy achieved successful zero-shot sim-to-real transfer to a UR5 robotic arm, performing fast folding actions (under **1.5** seconds) for different cloth materials.
- The system demonstrated an average relative folding error (*E_fold*) of **5.18%** for wool and **2.93%** for denim, with denim generally yielding lower errors.
- Simulation experiments showed that increasing the number of Nyström landmarks (*m*) and the prediction horizon (*T*) reduced final folding error, while the cumulative cost grew at a sublinear rate.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The dimensionality of the Koopman surrogate state and the MPC prediction horizon are crucial parameters that directly impact the accuracy and effectiveness of the dynamic cloth folding trajectories.
> - An initial 'motion towards the center' of the cloth is a pivotal strategy for successful dynamic folding, which naturally emerges when the MPC prediction horizon is sufficiently long.
> - Leveraging Koopman operator regression allows for the linearization of complex, nonlinear cloth dynamics, enabling efficient, derivative-free trajectory computation suitable for real-time optimal control.
