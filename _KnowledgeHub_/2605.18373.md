# Dynamic robotic cloth folding with efficient Koopman operator-based model predictive control

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers developed a Model Predictive Control framework combining a high-fidelity physics-based simulator with an efficient Koopman operator-based data-driven model to enable a robot to perform fast, accurate cloth folding with a single corner grasp and continuous motion. The system achieved successful zero-shot sim-to-real transfer, demonstrating average relative folding errors of 5.18% for wool and 2.93% for denim on a UR5 robot.

## Problem

- Cloth exhibits highly nonlinear and complex dynamics under fast motions, making accurate system identification and trajectory planning challenging for robotic manipulation.
- Existing robotic cloth folding methods are often quasi-static, too slow, require bimanual setups, or lack precision for dynamic single-manipulator tasks, failing to emulate human-like efficiency.
- The "sim-to-real gap" is particularly problematic for dynamic cloth manipulation, as high speeds amplify inertial effects and lead to discrepancies between simulated and real-world behavior.

## Method

- A ==Model Predictive Control== (MPC) framework was developed, integrating a high-fidelity physics-based cloth simulator (SOM) with an efficient data-driven surrogate model.
- Koopman operator regression, combined with the Nyström method, was used to learn a linear, lower-dimensional surrogate model (COM) from training data generated by the SOM.
- The linear dynamics from the Koopman COM were embedded into an MPC framework to solve finite-horizon optimal control problems, generating optimal robot trajectories while enforcing physical and robot constraints.

## Results

- The control strategy achieved successful zero-shot sim-to-real transfer to a UR5 robotic arm, performing fast folding actions (under **1.5** seconds) for different cloth materials.
- The system demonstrated an average relative folding error (*E_fold*) of **5.18%** for wool and **2.93%** for denim, with denim generally yielding lower errors.
- Simulation experiments showed that increasing the number of Nyström landmarks (*m*) and the prediction horizon (*T*) reduced final folding error, while the cumulative cost grew at a sublinear rate.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The dimensionality of the Koopman surrogate state and the MPC prediction horizon are crucial parameters that directly impact the accuracy and effectiveness of the dynamic cloth folding trajectories.
> - An initial 'motion towards the center' of the cloth is a pivotal strategy for successful dynamic folding, which naturally emerges when the MPC prediction horizon is sufficiently long.
> - Leveraging Koopman operator regression allows for the linearization of complex, nonlinear cloth dynamics, enabling efficient, derivative-free trajectory computation suitable for real-time optimal control.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Robotic cloth manipulation is a domain presenting significant challenges for conventional robot control algorithms due to the deformable nature of textiles. This field encompasses diverse tasks, broadly categorized into quasi-static and dynamic manipulation. Quasi-static approaches typically involve slow motions where inertial effects are negligible, often requiring multiple motion primitives or bimanual setups to achieve a desired fold. In contrast, dynamic manipulation leverages the cloth's inertia and dynamics through fast motions, mirroring the efficiency observed in human experts, particularly in industrial settings such as textile factories.

However, dynamic cloth manipulation introduces considerable complexities. The intricate and nonlinear dynamics of cloth make accurate system identification challenging and planning effective folding trajectories difficult. A notable issue is the "sim-to-real gap," where control strategies developed in simulation often do not transfer effectively to physical robots, especially when dealing with the high speeds that amplify inertial phenomena. Existing robotic solutions for dynamic folding often utilize small, relatively rigid garments, or are either too slow to be practical or fast but lack precision, necessitating multiple attempts for successful folding.

This work contributes to the landscape of dynamic cloth manipulation by addressing the need for fast, accurate, and resource-efficient robotic folding. It distinguishes itself by focusing on single-manipulator, single-motion folding tasks, which demand sophisticated control strategies to effectively manage cloth dynamics. The integration of advanced physics-based simulation with data-driven modeling techniques, specifically Koopman operator regression, positions this research within the intersection of control theory, machine learning, and deformable object manipulation. The paper aims to bridge the gap between human-level dexterity in dynamic folding and the capabilities of robotic systems by proposing a framework that can generate robust control trajectories despite the inherent challenges of cloth's nonlinear dynamics and the sim-to-real transfer problem.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop and evaluate a control methodology enabling a robotic manipulator to perform fast, accurate cloth folding to previously unseen target poses, utilizing only a single corner grasp and a single continuous motion.

This objective is motivated by several challenges and limitations identified in the field of robotic cloth manipulation:

- **Complexity of Cloth Dynamics:** Cloth exhibits highly nonlinear and complex dynamics, especially under fast motions. This inherent complexity makes accurate system identification, which is crucial for model-based control, difficult. Developing precise physical models is computationally intensive, and deriving analytical control laws for such systems is often intractable.
- **Difficulty in Trajectory Planning:** The high-dimensional and non-smooth state space of deformable objects like cloth complicates the planning of folding trajectories. Conventional methods struggle to account for the dynamic evolution of the cloth during fast manipulations, often leading to imprecise folds or requiring numerous corrective actions.
- **Sim-to-Real Transfer Gap:** Strategies designed in physics-based simulators frequently encounter difficulties when deployed on real robotic platforms. This "sim-to-real gap" is particularly pronounced in dynamic tasks, where inertial effects of the cloth become significant and can lead to discrepancies between simulated and real-world behavior. Current robotic approaches often require extensive real-world experimentation or significant model simplifications to mitigate this gap.
- **Limitations of Current Robotic Folding Approaches:** Existing robotic solutions for cloth folding often fall short in terms of speed, precision, or resource efficiency. Many techniques are quasi-static, too slow for practical applications, or require bimanual setups, which increase hardware complexity. Single-handed approaches often rely on multiple sequential motion primitives or exhibit insufficient accuracy. The goal is to emulate human-like efficiency and dexterity, where dynamic, fast motions are employed for rapid and precise folding.
- **Industrial and Domestic Relevance:** Fast and reliable cloth folding is highly desirable in industrial settings, such as textile manufacturing, where automation can significantly improve efficiency. Similarly, in domestic and assistive robotics, autonomous cloth manipulation skills are essential for expanding robotic capabilities in household tasks. The lack of efficient dynamic folding methods hinders the widespread adoption of robots in these areas.
- **Need for Efficient Control Models:** High-fidelity physics-based simulators, while accurate, are computationally expensive and not suitable for real-time model predictive control. There is a need for efficient surrogate models that can approximate cloth dynamics accurately but with significantly reduced computational cost, allowing for effective online trajectory generation. This motivated the exploration of techniques like Koopman operator regression, which can linearize nonlinear dynamics for more efficient control synthesis.

By addressing these motivations, the research aims to contribute a robust and efficient framework that advances the state-of-the-art in dynamic robotic cloth folding, paving the way for more dexterous and capable robotic systems in various applications.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research proposes a novel Model Predictive Control (MPC) framework for dynamic robotic cloth folding. This framework integrates a high-fidelity physics-based simulator for accurate cloth dynamics with an efficient data-driven surrogate model derived using Koopman operator regression.

#### 3.1. Physical Model of the Cloth (Simulation-Oriented Model - SOM)

The foundation of the approach is a detailed inextensible cloth model, adapted from prior work.
- **Discretization:** The cloth is represented as a quadrilateral mesh with *N* vertices. The state of the cloth *φ(t)* is defined by the 3D positions of all its vertices.
- **Dynamics Equations:** The motion of the cloth is governed by a set of Ordinary Differential Equations (ODEs) that account for:
 - **Inertia:** Represented by a mass matrix *M* and cloth density *ρ*.
 - **Gravity:** Modeled with a term involving a virtual mass *δ* and gravitational acceleration *g*. The distinction between inertial and virtual mass allows for a simplified aerodynamic model, introducing an upward lift force.
 - **Stiffness:** An isometric bending model with a stiffness constant *κ* and an approximation of the Laplacian operator.
 - **Damping:** Rayleigh damping term *αMφ̇* models slow oscillations and air drag.
 - **Inextensibility Constraints:** Lagrange multipliers *λ(t)* enforce the inextensibility of the cloth, ensuring quadratic equality constraints *C(φ) = 0*.
 - **Collision Modeling:** The model includes collisions with the table and self-collisions using Signorini’s contact model, which introduces non-smooth forces and inequality constraints *H(φ) ≥ 0*.
- **Numerical Integration:** The system is integrated numerically using an iterative process that solves a sequence of quadratic programs (QPs) at each time step. This QP minimizes a quantity related to acceleration and friction, subject to linearized inextensibility and collision constraints.
- **Parameter Estimation:** Physical parameters like virtual mass *δ* and damping *α* are estimated using *a priori* empirical formulas that depend on cloth density, size, and average velocity, derived from Motion Capture (MoCap) data. The stiffness parameter *κ* is empirically tuned for the dynamic folding task, as it was not reliably estimable from the MoCap data for the formulas.

#### 3.2. Data-Driven Surrogate Model (Control-Oriented Model - COM)

To enable efficient control, a linear, data-driven surrogate model is learned from high-fidelity data generated by the SOM. This is achieved using Koopman operator regression combined with the Nyström method.
- **Control Input:** The robot controls the displacement (Δu ∈ ℝ⁶) of two adjacent points on the grasped corner of the cloth, maintaining a fixed end-effector orientation.
- **Koopman Operator Regression:** The core idea is to lift the nonlinear cloth dynamics *φ_{j+1} = f(φ_j, Δu_j)* into an infinite-dimensional Hilbert space (via a kernel feature map *ψ*), where the dynamics become linear.
- **Nyström Approximation:** To make the problem computationally tractable, the infinite-dimensional space is approximated by a finite-dimensional one using the Nyström method. This involves sub-sampling *m_nys* "landmarks" from the training data. A regularized least-squares problem is solved to learn a linear operator *G* that describes the evolution of the transformed state *z*.
- **Surrogate Dynamics:** The learned operator *G* yields a linear state-space model in the lifted space: *z_{t+1} = G * [z_t; u_t]*. The specific closed-form vector-valued dynamics are provided involving kernel matrices *K_m*, *K_nm*, and a block-diagonal operator *B*.
- **State Reconstruction:** A reconstruction matrix *C* is learned to map the lifted state *z* back to an approximated physical state *φ ≈ Cz*, which is necessary for evaluating the control performance in the original state space.
- **Training Data Generation:** The COM is trained using 100 simulated folding trajectories (1.5 seconds each, 0.01s sampling time) generated by the SOM. These trajectories are parabolic, tilted towards the center of the cloth, a shape deemed effective for dynamic folding.

#### 3.3. Model Predictive Control (MPC)

The linear dynamics provided by the Koopman COM are then embedded into a linear MPC framework.
- **Optimal Control Problem (OCP):** At each time step, a finite-horizon OCP is solved to determine the optimal sequence of control inputs (Δu₀..., Δu_T). The objective is to minimize a quadratic cost function that penalizes deviations from a target cloth pose (in the lifted state space) and the control effort. The cost function *J* is defined as *Σ⟨(z_t - z_r), Q(z_t - z_r)⟩ + ⟨Δu_t, RΔu_t⟩*.
- **Constraints:** The OCP includes several types of constraints:
 - **Dynamic Constraints:** The Koopman linear dynamics *z_{t+1} = G * [z_t; u_t]* are enforced.
 - **Control Space Constraints:** To ensure stability and feasibility on the real robot, and to prevent the controller from operating in unexplored regions of the state space:
 - **Spatial Bounds:** Constraints prevent the grasped corner from going too close to or below the table, or too far into the cloth center prematurely.
 - **Smoothness:** Constraints limit the change in control inputs (Δu_t - Δu_{t-1}) to ensure smooth robot motion.
 - **Fixed Orientation:** Constraints enforce that the two controlled points on the corner move by the same displacement, simulating a rigid gripper with constant orientation.
 - **Velocity Limits:** Bounding the maximum displacement of the grasped corner per time step (w ≤ Δu_t ≤ v) to avoid unstable behavior.
- **Receding Horizon:** The MPC operates in a receding horizon manner. Only the first optimal control action (Δu*₀) is applied to the SOM (which then provides the new *φ_κ+1* for the next iteration), and the OCP is re-solved at the subsequent time step with the updated state.
- **Implementation:** The simulation pipeline is implemented in Matlab, with the OCP solved using the YALMIP library.

#### 3.4. Experimental Evaluation

- **Simulation Experiments:** Assessed the impact of *m* (number of Nyström landmarks) and *T* (prediction horizon) on folding error and cumulative cost index.
- **Sim-to-Real Evaluation:** Trajectories generated by the MPC pipeline are executed on a UR5 robotic arm with two different cloth materials (wool and denim). The evaluation uses an open-loop execution due to the high speed of motions and latency of visual feedback. Performance is measured by the relative folding error (*E_fold*) based on visible surface area and qualitatively through visual inspection.

This comprehensive methodology aims to combine the fidelity of physics-based modeling with the efficiency of data-driven linear approximations to achieve robust and performant dynamic cloth manipulation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents findings from both simulation experiments and real-robot evaluations, demonstrating the efficacy of the proposed control pipeline.

#### 4.1. Simulation Experiments

The simulation experiments aimed to characterize the behavior of the Model Predictive Control (MPC) algorithm under varying parameters:

- **Impact of Surrogate State Size (m):** The number of Nyström landmarks, *m*, which determines the dimensionality of the Koopman surrogate state, significantly influenced the folding accuracy. Results showed that increasing *m* led to a reduction in the final folding error across 10 target folds. This indicates that a larger surrogate state provides a more accurate control-oriented model (COM), thereby improving the MPC algorithm's performance. For instance, increasing *m* from 58 to 158 resulted in a notable improvement in accuracy (Figure 4a).
- **Impact of Prediction Horizon (T):** The length of the MPC prediction horizon, *T*, was found to be critical for generating effective folding trajectories. For short horizons, the generated trajectories did not exhibit an initial motion towards the center of the cloth. Conversely, for larger values of *T*, this "motion towards the center" emerged as a characteristic feature of the trajectories (Figure 5a). The empirical observation that this initial inward motion is "pivotal" for successful folding was corroborated by a reduction in the final folding error for larger *T* values (Figure 5b). This suggests that a sufficiently long prediction horizon allows the MPC to plan more strategically, accounting for the cloth's dynamics over a longer temporal window.
- **Running Cost:** The cumulative cost index (*J*), which includes both state error and control effort, was evaluated over the simulation horizon (*T_max*). The results indicated that *J* grew at a sublinear rate with respect to *T_max* (Figure 4b). This sublinear growth suggests that the MPC framework is efficient in minimizing its defined cost, despite the receding horizon approach and the complexity of the underlying task.

#### 4.2. Sim-to-Real Evaluation

The efficacy of the generated trajectories was further assessed on a real UR5 robotic arm, demonstrating a successful transfer from simulation to reality:

- **Zero-Shot Sim-to-Real Transfer:** The control strategy, trained exclusively on simulated data, achieved successful zero-shot transfer to a real robot for fast folding actions, with motion durations less than 1.5 seconds. This indicates that the combination of the high-fidelity physics-based simulator (SOM) and the Koopman-based COM generated sufficiently realistic and robust trajectories to counteract the inertial effects of cloth at high speeds.
- **Performance Across Materials:** Two different cloth materials, wool and denim (Table II), were used to test the algorithm's performance in diverse scenarios. The robot successfully folded both garments to given target poses.
- **Quantitative Performance (E_fold):** The performance was quantitatively measured using the relative folding error (*E_fold*), calculated as the difference between the actual and target folding ratios (visible surface area).
 - For **wool**, *E_fold* ranged from 2.21% to 7.23% across 5 target folds, with an average of approximately 5.18%.
 - For **denim**, *E_fold* ranged from 0.769% to 4.34%, with an average of approximately 2.93%.
 Denim generally exhibited lower *E_fold* values compared to wool (Table III).
- **Qualitative Observations:** Visualizations of the folds (Figure 7) provided additional insights. While denim folds yielded a lower *E_fold*, the woolen garments often appeared flatter and demonstrated more accurate corner placement relative to the reference fold. This qualitative difference suggests that *E_fold*, based solely on area ratio, may not fully capture all nuances of folding quality, particularly with respect to cloth configuration and overall flatness, which are influenced by the material's physical properties.

Overall, the main findings indicate that the proposed framework can efficiently generate dynamic folding trajectories for complex deformable objects like cloth. The simulation results demonstrate the importance of model complexity and prediction horizon, while the real-robot experiments confirm the practical applicability and zero-shot transfer capabilities of the approach, providing satisfactory folding accuracy for different cloth materials.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research contributes to the field of robotic manipulation of deformable objects, specifically addressing the challenging task of dynamic cloth folding. The significance and potential impact of this work can be summarized as follows:

- **Advancement in Dynamic Deformable Object Manipulation:** This work provides a method for achieving fast and accurate dynamic folding of cloth with a single robotic manipulator and a single motion. This capability moves beyond traditional quasi-static or multi-primitive approaches, addressing a core challenge in robotic dexterity for deformable materials. It pushes the boundaries of what robots can achieve in complex manipulation tasks that require handling high-dimensional, nonlinear dynamics.
- **Bridging the Sim-to-Real Gap for High-Speed Tasks:** A notable achievement is the successful "zero-shot" sim-to-real transfer of control strategies for motions lasting less than 1.5 seconds. This is particularly significant because high-speed dynamic movements amplify inertial effects, typically exacerbating the sim-to-real gap. The integration of an accurate physics-based simulator (SOM) with an efficient data-driven model (COM) proves effective in generating trajectories realistic enough for direct deployment on physical robots without real-world fine-tuning.
- **Efficiency through Koopman Operator Regression and MPC:** The use of Koopman operator regression to linearize the nonlinear cloth dynamics into a lower-dimensional surrogate model offers several advantages:
 - **Computational Efficiency:** The resulting linear model allows for the formulation and efficient solution of linear quadratic optimal control problems within an MPC framework. This contrasts with computationally expensive nonlinear optimization required by direct use of the physics-based model.
 - **Derivative-Free Control:** The Koopman-based model facilitates derivative-free trajectory computation with respect to the complex nonlinear simulator, simplifying control design.
 - **Reduced Dimensionality:** The Koopman lifting and Nyström approximation can reduce the dimensionality of the state space, further improving computational performance.
- **Practical Applicability in Industry and Domestic Settings:** The ability to perform fast and accurate cloth folding has direct implications for automation in industries such as textile manufacturing, where speed and efficiency are paramount. In domestic and assistive robotics, this research contributes to the development of more versatile robots capable of autonomously performing household tasks like laundry, enhancing quality of life and autonomy for individuals.
- **Robustness via Constraint-Aware Control:** Embedding various constraints (e.g., preventing collisions with the table, ensuring smoothness of robot motions, maintaining fixed gripper orientation, bounding control actions) within the MPC framework enhances the robustness and safety of the generated trajectories, making them more suitable for real-world robotic execution.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The proposed control pipeline opens avenues for subsequent research. Future work could focus on integrating real-time visual feedback for closed-loop control, which would enhance robustness to initial pose variations and external disturbances. Exploring different grasping strategies and developing adaptive methods to account for various cloth shapes, materials, and unknown parameters (e.g., through online parameter estimation or material recognition) are also potential directions.

In summary, this research provides a robust, efficient, and practically validated approach to dynamic robotic cloth folding, addressing critical challenges in the field and laying groundwork for more capable and autonomous robotic systems in diverse applications.
