# High-Fidelity Simulated Data Generation for Real-World Zero-Shot Robotic Manipulation Learning with Gaussian Splatting

> [!summary] Summary
> RoboSimGS creates high-fidelity, physically interactive simulation environments from real-world images using 3D Gaussian Splatting for photorealism and Multi-modal Large Language Models to infer object articulation and physical properties. This approach enables successful zero-shot Sim2Real transfer for robotic manipulation tasks and significantly boosts policy performance when augmenting limited real-world datasets.

## Problem

- The prohibitive cost and labor of collecting large-scale real-world robot interaction data severely limit the scalability of generalist robot policies.
- Traditional Sim2Real approaches suffer from a significant "domain gap" in visual appearance and physical properties, hindering transferability to real robots.
- Prior Real2Sim2Real (R2S2R) methods, while offering photorealistic scene reconstructions, largely create static environments, lacking the physical interactivity needed for training complex, contact-rich manipulation tasks.

## Method

- A hybrid scene representation is employed, using 3D Gaussian Splatting for photorealistic backgrounds and explicit meshes for interactive objects.
- Multi-modal Large Language Models (MLLMs like GPT-4o) are utilized to automatically infer complex articulation (e.g., joint types, limits) and physical material properties (e.g., density, stiffness) for interactive objects directly from multi-view renderings.
- A comprehensive scene augmentation strategy, including randomization of object poses, camera views, lighting conditions, and robot trajectories, is applied to generate diverse training data.

## Results

- Policies trained exclusively on RoboSimGS-generated data demonstrated successful zero-shot transfer across eight diverse real-world robotic manipulation tasks.
- Augmenting limited real-world datasets (e.g., 50 demonstrations) with synthetic RoboSimGS data (e.g., 50 demonstrations) consistently and substantially improved the performance of state-of-the-art visuomotor policies like Diffusion Policy and π0 across various tasks.
- RoboSimGS achieves a high data generation throughput, producing over 10,000 demonstrations per day on a single GPU, representing a 10-fold increase compared to manual teleoperation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating 3D Gaussian Splatting for high visual fidelity with MLLM-driven inference for physical interactivity effectively transforms static scene reconstructions into dynamic, interactive robot training environments.
> - Multi-modal Large Language Models can automate the complex and traditionally manual process of inferring articulated structures and physical properties of objects from visual input.
> - A holistic augmentation strategy, encompassing diverse environmental factors like camera views, lighting, and action trajectories, is essential for enabling robust Sim2Real transfer, beyond simple object randomization.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper addresses one of the most fundamental challenges in modern robotics and Embodied AI: the **data acquisition bottleneck** for training generalist robot policies capable of open-world manipulation. The ambition of creating robots that learn directly from raw sensory input and exhibit human-like generalization and language understanding is a grand goal, yet it is severely constrained by the prohibitive cost and labor associated with collecting large-scale real-world robot interaction data.

Historically, researchers have explored several avenues to tackle this data scarcity:
- **Teleoperation systems:** These allow humans to provide expert demonstrations. While effective, they are inherently limited by human effort, making them unscalable for the massive datasets required by contemporary deep learning models.
- **Simulated data (Sim2Real):** This approach leverages computational resources to generate synthetic data. However, it suffers from a significant "domain gap" between simulation and reality across visual appearance, physical properties, and interaction dynamics. Early efforts like domain randomization and domain adaptation aimed to bridge this gap by introducing variability or aligning feature spaces, but their effectiveness is ultimately constrained by the fidelity of the underlying simulator, which often lacks photorealism and accurate physics.

A more recent and transformative paradigm is **Real2Sim2Real (R2S2R)**. This approach aims to automatically reconstruct high-fidelity digital twins of real-world scenes, often using neural radiance fields (e.g., NeRF, 3D Gaussian Splatting), to create simulated environments for policy learning. Pioneering R2S2R works, such as Robo-GS and SplatSim, have demonstrated remarkable success in achieving visually stunning, photorealistic reconstructions of real scenes.

**However, this is where RoboSimGS carves out its unique niche and significant contribution:** existing R2S2R methods, despite their visual fidelity, largely produce static, non-interactive "digital snapshots." Their focus on photorealism often comes at the cost of physical interactivity, meaning interactions are limited to predefined, rigid assets. This fundamental limitation prevents them from being effectively used to train policies for complex, contact-rich manipulation tasks that require realistic physical dynamics and articulated object behaviors (e.g., opening a drawer, grasping a deformable object).

RoboSimGS directly addresses this critical **"physical interactivity gap"** within the R2S2R landscape. It builds upon the visual fidelity offered by 3D Gaussian Splatting but crucially augments it with physically accurate, interactive, and *articulated* objects. By integrating Multi-modal Large Language Models (MLLMs) to automate the inference of physical properties and complex kinematic structures, RoboSimGS transforms visually realistic but static scene reconstructions into dynamic, interactive "robotic playgrounds." This innovation represents a significant advancement, enabling the generation of data that is both photorealistic and physically plausible, thereby pushing the boundaries for zero-shot Sim2Real transfer in complex manipulation scenarios.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The development of RoboSimGS is driven by a clear set of motivations stemming from the limitations of current robot learning paradigms and aims to achieve specific objectives to overcome them.

**Motivation:**
1. **Data Acquisition Bottleneck:** The primary driver is the recognition that the scalability of robot learning, particularly for developing generalist policies capable of open-world manipulation, is fundamentally constrained by the high cost, labor, and time involved in collecting real-world interaction data. Traditional methods like teleoperation are not scalable enough for the immense data demands of modern deep learning models.
2. **Domain Gap in Simulation:** While simulated data offers exponential scalability, existing Sim2Real approaches suffer from a significant "domain gap." This gap manifests in differences in visual appearance, geometric representation, and realistic physical material behaviors between simulated and real environments, making policies trained purely in simulation difficult to transfer to the real world.
3. **Limitations of Current R2S2R Methods:** Even cutting-edge Real2Sim2Real (R2S2R) frameworks, which utilize radiance fields (like NeRF and 3DGS) to reconstruct photorealistic scenes, fall short in a critical dimension: physical interaction. They tend to create visually stunning but static "digital snapshots" where objects are largely non-interactive or rigid, thus preventing the training of policies for complex, contact-rich manipulation tasks that are ubiquitous in real-world scenarios.

**Key Objectives:**
1. **Generate High-Fidelity, Physically Interactive Simulation Environments:** The central objective is to develop a novel R2S2R framework that can convert multi-view real-world images into simulation environments that are not only photorealistic but also possess accurate physical properties and support complex interactions.
2. **Bridge the Physical Interactivity Gap:** Specifically, to overcome the limitation of existing R2S2R frameworks by endowing reconstructed objects with physically plausible, articulated structures and material properties, moving beyond static scene representations.
3. **Automate Asset Creation using MLLMs:** To pioneer the use of Multi-modal Large Language Models (MLLMs) to automatically infer object attributes like density, stiffness, joint types (hinges, sliders), and motion limits directly from visual data, thereby streamlining the creation of dynamic, interactive assets.
4. **Enable Zero-Shot Sim2Real Transfer:** To demonstrate that policies trained entirely on data generated by RoboSimGS can achieve successful, zero-shot transfer to real-world robotic manipulation tasks without any further fine-tuning in the real environment.
5. **Enhance Performance and Generalization of SOTA Methods:** To show that data generated by RoboSimGS can significantly augment limited real-world datasets, thereby boosting the performance, robustness, and generalization capabilities of state-of-the-art visuomotor policies.
6. **Provide a Scalable Data Generation Solution:** To offer a highly efficient and scalable alternative to manual data collection, dramatically increasing the throughput of synthetic demonstration generation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

RoboSimGS proposes a novel Real2Sim2Real (R2S2R) framework that transforms multi-view real-world images into scalable, high-fidelity, and physically interactive simulation environments. The methodology is structured into three main stages: Scene Reconstruction, Sim2Real Environment Alignment, and Holistic Scene Augmentation.

#### 3.1. Scene Reconstruction: Hybrid Scene Representation

To overcome the limitations of physics simulators (poor photorealism) and neural radiance fields (poor physics compatibility), RoboSimGS adopts a decoupled, hybrid representation:
- **Input Data:** Approximately 10 minutes of manual multi-view scanning is performed for each task-specific real-world scene.
- **Background Reconstruction (3D Gaussian Splatting - 3DGS):**
 - The static background of the scene is reconstructed using 3DGS (Kerbl et al., 2023). This method represents the scene as a set of 3D Gaussians, each with position, opacity, color (Spherical Harmonics), and a covariance matrix. This allows for high-quality, real-time photorealistic rendering of novel views through alpha-blending.
 - To enable precise spatial and semantic alignment, a learnable semantic feature vector is appended to each Gaussian. These features are trained using language-driven supervision, leveraging a frozen CLIP text encoder and multi-class 2D ground-truth semantic masks. A contrastive loss minimizes the difference between rendered 2D feature maps and target text embeddings.
 - The differentiable nature of 3DGS is leveraged for rendering both color and semantic features.
- **Object Reconstruction (Explicit Meshes with MLLM-driven Interaction):**
 - Foreground interactive objects are reconstructed as explicit meshes, initially segmented using tools like ARCode.
 - **MLLM-driven Articulation Inference (Key Innovation):** To imbue these static meshes with physical interactivity, a Multi-modal Large Language Model (MLLM, specifically GPT-4o) is employed.
 - The MLLM is provided with multi-view renderings of the reconstructed mesh.
 - It identifies the object category and proposes its potential articulation (e.g., joint type like prismatic/revolute, and semantic labels for parts like "drawer body," "main cabinet").
 - An open-vocabulary segmentation method (AffordDex) uses these textual labels to partition the mesh into static and mobile parts.
 - The MLLM is then prompted again to determine precise joint parameters (axis, motion limits), which are used to define a URDF-compatible joint, creating a fully articulated object.
 - **MLLM-driven Physics Estimation (Key Innovation):** To accurately simulate physical interactions, a physics expert agent, powered by GPT-4o, estimates material properties.
 - Given four orthographic views of a 3D asset, the MLLM infers fundamental physical parameters: density (ρ), Young’s modulus (E), and Poisson’s ratio (ν). These parameters are crucial for governing mass distribution, stiffness, and deformation response in the physics simulator.
 - The robot itself is excluded from this reconstruction, as its model is readily available in URDF format.

#### 3.2. Sim2Real Environment Alignment

This stage integrates the hybrid scene representation into a physics simulator.
- **World Coordinate Frame Alignment:**
 - The coordinate system of the 3DGS scene is aligned with the simulator's.
 - The robot's geometry (fixed at a default joint configuration) serves as a common anchor.
 - Point clouds are generated from both the robot’s URDF model and the 3DGS reconstruction.
 - The Iterative Closest Point (ICP) algorithm is used to find the optimal rigid transformation (rotation R, translation t) between these point clouds, effectively mapping the entire 3DGS scene into the simulator's coordinate system.
- **Camera Pose Alignment:**
 - The simulated camera is precisely aligned with its real-world counterpart.
 - This is formulated as an optimization problem minimizing the photometric error (L1 norm) between a real-world reference image and the image rendered by the differentiable 3DGS function.
 - Starting from a rough initial pose, gradient descent is used to iteratively refine the camera's position and rotation parameters until convergence.

#### 3.3. Holistic Scene Augmentation

To generate a robust policy that can generalize to real-world variability, RoboSimGS employs a comprehensive augmentation strategy on the simulated environment:
- **Object-level Augmentation:** Randomizes the 6-DoF pose (position and orientation) and uniform scale of interactive objects within predefined ranges.
- **Camera View Augmentation:** Applies random perturbations (translations and rotations) to the precisely aligned real-world camera extrinsics. This generates diverse yet realistic camera perspectives, centered around the deployment view, using 3DGS's novel view synthesis capabilities.
- **Lighting Condition Augmentation:** Directly modifies the visual attributes of 3D Gaussians (random scaling, offset, and noise) to simulate global changes in color contrast, brightness, and sensor noise characteristics.
- **Trajectory Augmentation:** Introduces randomized intermediate via-points (small positional offsets to the final goal) using an inverse kinematics (IK) solver. This breaks deterministic motion paths, exposing the policy to a richer distribution of trajectories and enhancing robustness to execution errors.

**Policy Training and Deployment:** Policies (e.g., Diffusion Policy, π0) are trained exclusively on this augmented synthetic data and then deployed directly in the real world for zero-shot transfer.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of RoboSimGS demonstrates its effectiveness in generating high-fidelity simulated data for robotic manipulation, leading to remarkable zero-shot Sim2Real transfer and enhanced policy performance.

1. **Zero-Shot Sim2Real Transfer:**
 - Policies trained *entirely* on data generated by RoboSimGS achieve successful zero-shot transfer across eight diverse real-world manipulation tasks (Stack Cubes, Pick & Place, Deformable Pick & Place, Upright Bottle, Move Bottle, Drawer Close, Box Close, Wiping).
 - For instance, a Diffusion Policy (DP) trained on 100 RoboSimGS demonstrations achieved a 0.83 success rate for PickPlace, outperforming a DP trained on 50 real demonstrations (0.71). For Move Bottle, 100 RoboSimGS DP achieved 0.91, slightly better than 50 real DP (0.89). This validates RoboSimGS's ability to create training environments that are sufficiently realistic for direct deployment.

2. **Synergistic Effect of Real and Simulated Data:**
 - Augmenting limited real-world data (50 demonstrations) with an equal amount of RoboSimGS data (50 synthetic demonstrations) consistently and substantially boosts the performance of both Diffusion Policy and the generalist VLA model π0 across all evaluated tasks.
 - For example, for the "Wiping" task, π0 trained on 50 real demonstrations had a 0.00 success rate, while adding 50 RoboSimGS demonstrations increased it dramatically to 0.86. Similarly, for "Stack Cubes" with DP, performance improved from 0.60 (50 Real) to 0.69 (50 Real + 50 RoboSimGS). This indicates that RoboSimGS data is a valuable supplement, enhancing the utility and generalization of real-world datasets.

3. **Data Scaling and Efficiency Analysis:**
 - A policy trained on just 200 demonstrations generated by RoboSimGS achieved performance comparable to one trained on 100 real-world demonstrations. This highlights the high quality and sample efficiency of the synthetic data, suggesting that RoboSimGS can significantly reduce the need for extensive real-world data collection.

4. **Generalization to Challenging Settings:**
 - Policies trained with RoboSimGS data, particularly with its holistic augmentation strategy, demonstrate superior generalization capabilities when faced with unforeseen real-world variations (e.g., changes in lighting, object size, scene clutter, camera pose, and desktop appearance).
 - For instance, in challenging lighting conditions, DP trained on 50 real demonstrations failed (0.00 success rate), whereas DP trained on 50 RoboSimGS data achieved 0.46, and the combination (50 Real + 50 RoboSimGS) reached 0.54. This underscores the importance of the comprehensive augmentation for building robust policies.

5. **Sim2Real Transfer Fidelity:**
 - A comprehensive cross-domain evaluation showed that policies trained exclusively in the RoboSimGS simulator (Sim-to-Real) achieved success rates nearly identical to policies trained and evaluated entirely in the real world (Real-to-Real). This strong equivalence confirms that RoboSimGS effectively serves as a high-fidelity digital twin of reality, capable of accurately predicting real-world policy performance.

6. **Data Generation Efficiency and Scalability:**
 - RoboSimGS can generate over 10,000 demonstrations per day on a single NVIDIA RTX 5060 Ti GPU. This represents a more than 10-fold increase in data collection throughput compared to human teleoperation (typically around 1,000 demonstrations per day), making it a highly scalable and cost-effective solution.

7. **Ablation Studies:**
 - **Effect of Physics Estimation:** Removing the MLLM-driven physics estimation led to a significant degradation in real-world performance, especially for contact-rich tasks (e.g., Wiping success rate dropped from 0.85 to 0.51). This confirms that accurate physical properties are as crucial as visual fidelity for learning effective manipulation policies.
 - **Effect of Holistic Scene Augmentation:** Ablating the holistic augmentation (i.e., only randomizing object pose) resulted in a severe performance collapse, indicating that simple domain randomization is insufficient. The comprehensive approach of randomizing camera views, lighting conditions, and trajectories is indispensable for preventing overfitting and ensuring robustness.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

RoboSimGS represents a significant advancement in the field of robot learning, with profound implications for the development of generalist robot policies and the scalability of robotics research. Its key contributions and potential impacts are:

1. **Bridging the Photorealism-Interactivity Gap:** The most critical impact is the successful bridging of the long-standing gap between visually photorealistic scene reconstructions and physically interactive simulation environments within the Real2Sim2Real (R2S2R) paradigm. Previous methods excelled at visual fidelity but lacked dynamic interactivity. RoboSimGS now enables the creation of "robot playgrounds" that are both stunningly realistic and physically plausible, allowing for training complex, contact-rich manipulation tasks previously unfeasible with R2S2R.

2. **Scalable and Cost-Effective Data Generation:** RoboSimGS offers a highly scalable and efficient solution to the pervasive data bottleneck in robotics. By generating over 10,000 demonstrations per day on a single GPU, it provides a 10-fold increase in throughput compared to manual teleoperation. This dramatically reduces the cost, labor, and time associated with acquiring large-scale datasets, democratizing access to high-quality training data for robot learning.

3. **Enabling Zero-Shot Sim2Real Transfer:** The demonstration of successful zero-shot Sim2Real transfer is a monumental achievement. It means that policies trained entirely in the RoboSimGS environment can be deployed directly into the real world without any real-world fine-tuning. This capability can accelerate research cycles, reduce hardware reliance, and significantly lower the barrier to deploying learned robotic skills in practical applications.

4. **Enhancing Policy Robustness and Generalization:** The holistic scene augmentation strategy is crucial for learning robust policies. By systematically randomizing object poses, camera views, lighting conditions, and action trajectories, RoboSimGS effectively prepares policies for the inherent variability and unpredictability of real-world environments. This leads to superior generalization capabilities, which is vital for robots operating in unstructured and dynamic settings.

5. **Pioneering MLLM Integration for Asset Creation:** The novel application of Multi-modal Large Language Models (MLLMs) to automatically infer complex physical properties (density, stiffness, Poisson’s ratio) and kinematic structures (joint types, axes, limits) from visual data is a groundbreaking innovation. This automates a traditionally manual, expert-intensive, and time-consuming process of creating physically accurate articulated assets, transforming static meshes into dynamic, interactive components. This MLLM-driven approach opens new avenues for intelligent scene understanding and autonomous environment generation.

6. **Accelerating Embodied AI and Generalist Robotics:** By providing a powerful tool for generating vast quantities of high-fidelity, physically interactive data, RoboSimGS can significantly accelerate the development of generalist robot policies and other embodied AI agents. It lays crucial groundwork for creating more intelligent, adaptable, and autonomous robots capable of learning and operating in diverse, open-world scenarios, from industrial automation and logistics to assistive robotics and smart homes.

**Limitations and Future Work (as identified by the authors):**
The primary limitation highlighted is the current need for a "time-consuming and complex manual scene reconstruction pipeline" as a one-time setup effort per scene. While significant, this manual step remains a bottleneck for widespread deployment to many novel environments. Future work will focus on developing faster and more automated 3D reconstruction methods to streamline this initial setup phase, further enhancing the scalability and practical utility of the framework.
