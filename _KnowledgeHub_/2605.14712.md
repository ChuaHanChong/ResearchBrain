# IntentVLA: Short-Horizon Intent Modeling for Aliased Robot Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> IntentVLA introduces a framework for Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models that uses recent visual history to infer and maintain short-horizon intent, improving execution stability in aliased observational scenarios. This approach enhances task success and inter-chunk consistency across various manipulation benchmarks, including the newly developed AliasBench.

## Problem

- Existing VLA policies often infer robot actions based only on the current visual frame and language instruction, lacking crucial contextual information.
- This frame-conditioned approach leads to unstable and inconsistent execution when visually similar observations correspond to different short-horizon intentions (observation aliasing).
- Policies fail to maintain "local commitment" within an episode, resampling different intents across adjacent replanning steps, which causes contradictory action chunks.

## Method

- **IntentVLA** integrates ==recent visual history== with current visual-language context to model ==short-horizon intent== for robot manipulation.
- A frozen ==VGGT-1B encoder== extracts ==compact, geometry-aware tokens== from a window of past head-camera frames to represent historical context.
- This historical context is fused with current visual-language features using ==gated cross-attention== to guide action chunk generation via a ==DiT-based flow-matching head==.
- A new diagnostic benchmark, **AliasBench**, was developed to rigorously test a policy's ability to maintain consistent local continuation under ==observation aliasing==.

## Results

- IntentVLA achieved an average success rate of **45.8%** on the novel AliasBench, a substantial improvement over the **9.0%** baseline, alongside a **17.6%** reduction in inter-chunk consistency error (ICC-L2).
- The framework secured the highest average success rates on the SimplerEnv (**72.9%**) and RoboCasa (**57.0%**) benchmarks, outperforming previous baselines and state-of-the-art methods.
- Ablation studies confirmed that integrating recent visual history through gated fusion and a compact intent-evidence token are key contributors to the observed improvements in both task success and execution stability.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Frame-conditioned VLA policies are vulnerable to short-horizon observation aliasing, leading to unstable execution when the current frame alone is ambiguous.
> - Explicitly conditioning on recent visual history via a compact, learned intent representation helps VLA policies maintain local commitment and generate more consistent action chunks.
> - A specialized history encoder focusing on global geometric information and inter-frame relations (using VGGT) can efficiently provide the necessary disambiguating context.
