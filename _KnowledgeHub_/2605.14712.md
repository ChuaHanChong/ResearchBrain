# IntentVLA: Short-Horizon Intent Modeling for Aliased Robot Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> IntentVLA introduces a framework for Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models that uses recent visual history to infer and maintain short-horizon intent, improving execution stability in aliased observational scenarios. This approach enhances task success and inter-chunk consistency across various manipulation benchmarks, including the newly developed AliasBench.

## Problem

- Existing VLA policies often infer robot actions based only on the current visual frame and language instruction, lacking crucial contextual information.
- This frame-conditioned approach leads to unstable and inconsistent execution when visually similar observations correspond to different short-horizon intentions (observation aliasing).
- Policies fail to maintain "local commitment" within an episode, resampling different intents across adjacent replanning steps, which causes contradictory action chunks.

## Method

- **IntentVLA** integrates ==recent visual history== with current visual-language context to model ==short-horizon intent==, using a frozen ==VGGT-1B encoder== to extract ==compact, geometry-aware tokens== from a window of past head-camera frames.
- This historical context is fused with current visual-language features using ==gated cross-attention== to guide action chunk generation via a ==DiT-based flow-matching head==.
- A new diagnostic benchmark, **AliasBench**, was developed to rigorously test a policy's ability to maintain consistent local continuation under ==observation aliasing==.

## Results

- IntentVLA achieved an average success rate of **45.8%** on the novel AliasBench, a substantial improvement over the **9.0%** baseline, alongside a **17.6%** reduction in inter-chunk consistency error (ICC-L2).
- The framework secured the highest average success rates on the SimplerEnv (**72.9%**) and RoboCasa (**57.0%**) benchmarks, outperforming previous baselines and state-of-the-art methods.
- Ablation studies confirmed that integrating recent visual history through gated fusion and a compact intent-evidence token are key contributors to the observed improvements in both task success and execution stability.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Frame-conditioned VLA policies are vulnerable to short-horizon observation aliasing, leading to unstable execution when the current frame alone is ambiguous.
> - Explicitly conditioning on recent visual history via a compact, learned intent representation helps VLA policies maintain local commitment and generate more consistent action chunks.
> - A specialized history encoder focusing on global geometric information and inter-frame relations (using VGGT) can efficiently provide the necessary disambiguating context.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation has seen significant advancements with the development of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. These models aim to provide a direct interface between robot perception (visual observations), high-level commands (language instructions), and low-level control (robot actions). Recent iterations, often built upon transformer backbones and trained on large robot datasets augmented with vision-language pretraining, have demonstrated improved generalization capabilities across various tasks and robot embodiments. Key examples include RT-2, OpenVLA, Octo, and models utilizing diffusion or flow matching for continuous multi-step control.

Despite these advancements, a notable challenge persists in the reliability and stability of VLA policies, particularly under conditions of partial observability. Robotic imitation datasets, while reflecting the inherent multimodality of human behavior across different episodes, often exhibit local commitment within a single episode; that is, once a demonstrator embarks on a particular task phase or strategy, subsequent actions tend to remain consistent with that choice. However, current VLA policies predominantly infer actions based solely on the current visual frame and language instruction. This frame-conditioned approach can lead to issues when similar visual observations correspond to different short-horizon intentions or task phases that are not discernible from the current frame alone. Consequently, policies may resample different intents across adjacent replanning steps, resulting in contradictory action chunks and unstable execution.

This work positions itself within the broader landscape by specifically addressing this failure mode: the inability of frame-conditioned VLA models to maintain *local commitment* when faced with *short-horizon observation aliasing*. While existing "intent-based VLA models" (e.g., DIAL, ACoT-VLA, MINT) aim to bridge the semantic-kinematic gap by incorporating concepts of intent, the paper notes that these methods largely rely on the current observation frame and may still struggle with ambiguity that requires recent historical context for disambiguation. IntentVLA differentiates itself by explicitly integrating recent visual history to learn and condition on short-horizon intent, thereby aiming to preserve within-episode consistency during local replanning, rather than attempting to eliminate multimodality or infer discrete intent labels explicitly.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from an identified vulnerability in existing VLA policies: their performance degradation when encountering short-horizon observation aliasing under partial observability. The authors observe that while human demonstrations exhibit multimodality across distinct episodes (e.g., multiple ways to achieve a goal), within any given episode, human actions are locally committed to a specific task phase, path, or completion strategy. Current VLA models, which typically infer action chunks based solely on the current visual frame and language instruction, lack the contextual information to discern this local commitment.

Specifically, the key objectives and motivations are:

1. **Identify and Characterize a Failure Mode:** The research aims to formally identify and describe a specific failure mode where identical or highly similar frame-level observations can correspond to different short-horizon intents (e.g., "put bread in skillet" versus "put bread on plate" when holding bread over a cooking area). A frame-conditioned policy, lacking historical context, might then oscillate between these plausible but contradictory intents across successive replanning steps, leading to unstable and inconsistent robot behavior. The goal is not to remove the inherent multimodality of demonstrations but to enable the policy to adhere to the *active, episode-specific commitment*.
2. **Develop a Diagnostic Benchmark:** To rigorously test this identified failure mode, the authors aimed to create a specialized benchmark. Existing manipulation benchmarks often focus on overall task success but do not specifically isolate or measure a policy's ability to maintain consistent local continuation in scenarios where the current observation is ambiguous. The motivation for AliasBench is to provide a controlled environment where visually aliased states require different next actions, and the disambiguating information is available only from recent task history, thereby directly evaluating intent consistency.
3. **Propose a History-Conditioned Framework:** Based on the identified problem, the research proposes to develop a novel imitation learning framework, IntentVLA, that explicitly incorporates recent visual history. The motivation is to move beyond current-frame-only conditioning by learning a compact representation of short-horizon intent from past observations, which can then guide consistent action generation for future chunks. This approach seeks to provide the "missing context" that frame-conditioned policies lack.
4. **Validate the Proposed Framework:** A critical objective is to empirically validate IntentVLA's effectiveness. This involves demonstrating improvements in both task success rates and, crucially, inter-chunk consistency, not only on the newly designed AliasBench (which directly tests the core problem) but also on established standard manipulation benchmarks (SimplerEnv, LIBERO, RoboCasa) to show generalizability beyond controlled ambiguity scenarios.

In essence, the research is motivated by the need for VLA models to exhibit more stable and human-like committed behavior in partially observable, multimodal environments, moving beyond merely predicting plausible actions for individual frames to generating action sequences consistent with an ongoing, short-horizon intent.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology of this research involves identifying a specific problem in VLA models, designing a benchmark to expose this problem, and then developing a novel architecture to address it.

**Problem Formulation:**
The work models robot manipulation as a partially observable decision process. The issue is articulated as current VLA policies modeling `p_θ(τ_t | o_t, ℓ)`, where `τ_t` is an action chunk, `o_t` is the current observation, and `ℓ` is the language instruction. This formulation marginalizes over a latent short-horizon intent `z_t` (`p_θ(τ_t | o_t, ℓ) = ∫ p_θ(τ_t | o_t, ℓ, z_t) p(z_t | o_t, ℓ) dz_t`). The core problem arises when `p(z_t | o_t, ℓ)` is multimodal or uncommitted due to observation aliasing. IntentVLA proposes conditioning action generation on recent visual history `h_t`, specifically `h_K_t = o_t-K:t-1`, to infer a deterministic short-horizon intent representation `m_t = f_ϕ(o_t, ℓ, h_K_t)`. This `m_t` then conditions the action chunk generation `p_θ(τ_t | o_t, ℓ, h_t)`.

**IntentVLA Architecture:**
The IntentVLA framework, illustrated in Figure 4, integrates current visual-language context with recent visual history through a specialized architecture:
1. **Current Visual-Language Context Encoding:** The current image `o_t` and language instruction `ℓ` are processed by a Qwen3-VL 4B backbone (`q_ψ`). This generates a current-condition representation `F_t` (a sequence of tokens from the last hidden layer), which serves as the primary conditioning source for the action model.
2. **Recent Visual History Encoding:** A separate, frozen VGGT-1B encoder (`g_ϕ`) processes a finite window of recent visual observations `h_K_t = o_t-K:t-1`. Crucially, this history encoder utilizes only head-camera frames (when multiple camera views are available) and specifically retains one camera token and four register tokens per frame. These tokens are chosen for their ability to capture global geometric information, inter-frame relations, and recent viewpoint changes, which are considered instrumental for inferring active short-horizon intent. This process yields history evidence tokens `U_t` and a compact summary representation `e_t = Pool(U_t)`. These are then projected into the action-model hidden space as `˜U_t` and `e_t`.
3. **Intent Information Fusion:** The current visual-language context `F_t` is fused with the fine-grained history tokens `˜U_t` using gated cross-attention. This operation (`F'_t = F_t + σ(α) MHA(Q=LN(F_t), K=˜U_t, V=˜U_t)`) enriches the current observation context with relevant historical cues. Subsequently, the compact history-evidence token `e_t` is appended to `F'_t` as a single context token `e_tok_t`. The combined sequence `C_t = [F'_t; e_tok_t]` forms the condition-dependent short-horizon intent representation `m_t`.
4. **Action Generation:** The action chunk `τ_t` is generated by a standard DiT-based flow-matching action head, conditioned on the fused context `C_t`. During inference, `C_t` is fixed for the current decision step, and actions are obtained by denoisifying Gaussian noise using an Euler-style solver, similar to GR00T.

**Training Objective:**
The model is trained using the standard conditional flow-matching objective. Given a target action chunk `τ_t`, Gaussian noise `ϵ`, and a sampled flow time `s`, an interpolated chunk `X_s` is formed. The conditional velocity field `ˆV_θ(X_s, s | C_t)` is trained to match the ground-truth displacement `τ_t - ϵ` using an L2 loss: `L_flow = E[ ||ˆV_θ(X_s, s | C_t) - (τ_t - ϵ)||^2_2 ]`. All components, including the history encoder projections and the fusion module, are learned jointly with this policy objective without explicit supervision on intent labels.

**AliasBench: Ambiguity-Aware Benchmark Design:**
To specifically evaluate short-horizon observation aliasing, the authors developed AliasBench on top of RoboTwin2. This benchmark comprises 12 manipulation tasks with matched simulation training and evaluation environments. Its design focuses on scenarios where `o_t^(1) ≈ o_t^(2)` but `a_t^(1) ≠ a_t^(2)`, with the disambiguating context residing in recent observations.
- **Ambiguity Categories:** Tasks are categorized into four families:
 - **Back-and-forth ambiguity (4 tasks):** Repeated local routines where similar states recur in different phases (e.g., picking up an object and then returning it).
 - **Crossing-path ambiguity (3 tasks):** Source-dependent routing where similar in-flight transport states arise from different origins, influencing the destination.
 - **Bimanual ambiguity (2 tasks):** Dual-arm settings where symmetric handoff configurations require different continuations based on transfer direction.
 - **Multi-goal ambiguity (3 tasks):** Scenes with multiple plausible targets, where a transient cue or recently observed property dictates the correct target but is no longer visible in the current frame.
- **Observation-Aliasing Diagnostic:** To quantitatively confirm aliasing, a diagnostic was developed. Images within ambiguity windows are encoded into visual embeddings, and nearest-neighbor retrieval (intra-episode for back-and-forth, cross-episode for others) is performed. The diagnostic measures the median cosine distance to same-intent and different-intent neighbors, and the percentage of top-k neighbors (k=5) from a different intent. This ensures that the benchmark tasks genuinely exhibit strong current-frame aliasing.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of IntentVLA was conducted across the newly introduced AliasBench and several established simulation benchmarks, focusing on task success rates and execution stability.

**Results on AliasBench:**
IntentVLA demonstrated improved performance on AliasBench, which is specifically designed to isolate short-horizon observation aliasing:
- **Success Rate:** IntentVLA achieved an average success rate of 45.8% across the 12 tasks, a notable improvement over the Qwen3VL-GR00T baseline (9.0%). Direct history-as-context baselines, which feed raw past frames into the Qwen backbone, showed limited effectiveness; for instance, the best feasible variant (4 frames uniformly sampled from the last 16) reached only 28.1% average success, with longer histories often leading to out-of-memory errors. IntentVLA's largest gains were observed in crossing-path and back-and-forth tasks, suggesting the effectiveness of its compact intent-conditioned history in these scenarios. The benchmark, however, remains challenging, with average success rates below 50%.
- **Inter-chunk Consistency (ICC-L2):** A key finding was the improvement in inter-chunk consistency, measured by the ICC-L2 metric (squared L2 overlap error between adjacent replanned chunks). IntentVLA reduced the mean ICC-L2 from 0.219 to 0.181, a 17.6% relative reduction, indicating more consistent local intent across replanning steps in ambiguous regions. The 90th-percentile ICC-L2 also showed consistent reductions across all ambiguity families, implying improved stability in difficult ambiguous windows. This result supports the hypothesis that recent visual history helps maintain commitment to a consistent short-horizon intent.

**Results on Standard Benchmarks:**
IntentVLA's advantages extended to standard manipulation benchmarks:
- **SimplerEnv (WidowX tasks):** IntentVLA achieved the highest overall average success rate of 72.9%, outperforming the Qwen3-VL-GR00T baseline (65.3%) and exceeding the previously reported best average (3D-Mix, 68.2%) by 4.7 points. Significant gains were observed on "Put Carrot on Plate," "Stack Green Block on Yellow Block," and "Put Eggplant in Yellow Basket." Performance on "Put Spoon on Towel" was slightly lower than the baseline, which the authors suggest might be due to the history pathway diluting attention to fine visual details where ambiguity is minimal.
- **LIBERO:** On the LIBERO benchmark, where several recent VLAs already achieve high scores, IntentVLA demonstrated competitive performance, particularly on the LIBERO-Long suite. It reached 97.4% success, compared to 92.0% for Qwen3-VL-GR00T and 92.4% for π0.5. This indicates that recent visual history helps infer active local continuations within longer, multi-stage routines, contributing to reduced inconsistent chunk generation.
- **RoboCasa (GR1 Tabletop tasks):** IntentVLA achieved the best overall average success rate of 57.0% on the RoboCasa GR1 Tabletop Manipulation Benchmark, surpassing other baselines like TwinBrainVLA (54.6%), VP-VLA (53.8%), and LangForce (52.6%). Gains were particularly noted in close-placement tasks (e.g., "PnP Bottle To Cabinet Close") and various novel transfer settings, suggesting the general utility of the short-horizon history signal.

**Ablation Studies on SimplerEnv:**
Ablation studies confirmed the contributions of IntentVLA's components:
- Removing VGGT and temporal history reverted performance to the frame-only baseline.
- Using VGGT on only the current frame did not improve average scores, suggesting its value is in processing *recent history* for geometric and inter-frame evidence, not as a standalone single-frame encoder.
- History fusion alone improved the average success rate from 65.3% to 69.5%.
- Adding the compact intent-evidence token further increased the average to 72.9%. These results indicate that both the gated history fusion and the explicit compact history summary are beneficial for robust chunk generation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a framework, IntentVLA, that addresses a specific and critical limitation of current Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models: their susceptibility to inconsistent behavior when faced with short-horizon observation aliasing. The work's significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Addressing a Fundamental Instability in VLA Models:** By identifying and providing a solution for the "failure mode of frame-conditioned chunk policies under partial observability," IntentVLA contributes to making robot manipulation more stable and reliable. Current policies often struggle with multimodal demonstrations because they cannot maintain local commitment across adjacent replanning steps when the current observation is ambiguous. IntentVLA’s approach of conditioning on recent visual history offers a mechanism to infer and adhere to the active short-horizon intent, thereby reducing inter-chunk conflict and improving execution stability. This is crucial for real-world robot deployment where consistent behavior is paramount.

2. **Introduction of a Novel Diagnostic Benchmark (AliasBench):** The creation of AliasBench represents a significant contribution to the field of robotic learning. Unlike existing benchmarks that primarily measure overall task success, AliasBench is specifically designed to isolate and quantify the problem of short-horizon observation aliasing. By providing 12 tasks across four categories of ambiguity with matched training and evaluation data, it offers a controlled environment to rigorously test a policy’s ability to maintain decision consistency. This benchmark can serve as a valuable tool for future research, enabling direct evaluation and comparison of new VLA architectures in addressing this specific challenge.

3. **Practical and Effective History-Conditioned Architecture:** IntentVLA introduces a compact and efficient method for incorporating historical context. By using a frozen VGGT-based history encoder to extract specific camera and register tokens, and fusing these with the current visual-language context through gated cross-attention, the framework avoids the computational overhead of feeding raw long visual histories directly into large VLMs. The empirical results across AliasBench, SimplerEnv, LIBERO, and RoboCasa demonstrate that this design consistently improves both task success rates and inter-chunk consistency, indicating its practical utility and generalizability beyond the specific ambiguity tasks.

4. **Advancing Generalist Robot Capabilities:** The improved robustness and stability offered by IntentVLA move generalist robot policies closer to real-world applicability. In complex environments, robots frequently encounter situations where visually similar states demand different immediate actions based on prior context. By enabling VLAs to infer and commit to short-horizon intents, IntentVLA enhances their ability to perform multi-stage and intricate manipulation routines more coherently. This is a step towards more capable and autonomous embodied AI systems that can execute long-horizon tasks with greater fluidity and fewer errors stemming from local inconsistencies.

5. **Guiding Future Research Directions:** The work also highlights limitations and potential avenues for future research. The focus on short-horizon intent suggests a need for exploring longer-term memory mechanisms for tasks requiring recall of sparse events or recovery from significant closed-loop deviations. Additionally, investigations into more interpretable intent probes and adaptive history selection could further refine VLA policies, allowing them to dynamically assess ambiguity and leverage historical evidence more intelligently.
