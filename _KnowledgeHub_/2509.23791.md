# CaRe-BN: Precise Moving Statistics for Stabilizing Spiking Neural Networks in Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Peking University researchers developed CaRe-BN (Confidence-adaptive and Re-calibration Batch Normalization) to stabilize Spiking Neural Networks (SNNs) in online Reinforcement Learning. This method provides more precise normalization statistics, enabling SNNs to achieve faster convergence and higher returns, outperforming traditional ANNs by an average of 5.9% in continuous control tasks while preserving energy efficiency.

## Problem

- Spiking Neural Networks (SNNs) inherently suffer from unstable gradient propagation during training, relying on Batch Normalization (BN) for stability.
- Traditional BN, which uses Exponential Moving Averages (EMA), struggles to precisely track the continuously shifting data distributions in non-stationary online Reinforcement Learning (RL) environments.
- Existing SNN-BN methods, designed for static supervised learning, are inadequate for the dynamic nature of online RL, leading to a negative feedback loop that hinders exploration and policy improvement.

## Method

- A ==Confidence-adaptive== update (Ca-BN) mechanism, inspired by the Kalman estimator, adaptively reweights batch statistics to minimize the mean-squared error of BN statistics under non-stationary RL dynamics.
- A periodic Re-calibration mechanism (Re-BN) corrects accumulated estimation errors by sampling multiple calibration batches from the replay buffer at fixed intervals.
- **CaRe-BN** is integrated into general online RL algorithms, applying Ca-BN during policy updates and Re-BN periodically, ensuring no additional inference overhead by fusing layers into synaptic weights for deployment.

## Results

- CaRe-BN consistently reduced the Wasserstein distance of BN statistics, leading to more precise estimates and higher exploration returns across MuJoCo tasks.
- SNNs with CaRe-BN achieved faster convergence, higher final returns, and significantly lower training variance across various spiking neuron models and RL algorithms, outperforming existing SNN-RL methods and setting a new state-of-the-art for SNN normalization.
- SNNs equipped with CaRe-BN demonstrated an average performance gain of **5.9%** over their ANN counterparts in continuous control, maintaining substantial energy efficiency (e.g., 16.48 nJ for SNNs vs. 1775.36 nJ for ANNs) with negligible training overhead.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Precise Batch Normalization statistics are critical for effective exploration and learning in online SNN-RL, directly influencing an agent's ability to select optimal actions.
> - Combining adaptive weighting of current batch statistics (Ca-BN) with periodic global corrections from a replay buffer (Re-BN) effectively addresses the non-stationarity challenges in SNN-RL.
> - Robust, SNN-specific normalization can enable SNNs to match or exceed the performance of Artificial Neural Networks (ANNs) in dynamic RL settings while retaining their inherent energy efficiency.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Spiking Neural Networks (SNNs) represent a class of neural models designed to emulate the event-driven computational principles of biological brains. Their event-driven nature offers potential advantages in low-latency and energy-efficient inference, particularly when deployed on neuromorphic hardware platforms. Concurrently, Reinforcement Learning (RL) has demonstrated success across a range of complex control tasks, aligning with applications in embodied AI and robotics. The integration of SNNs with RL (SNN-RL) is an emerging field that aims to develop agents capable of learning complex behaviors while operating with reduced energy consumption, making them suitable for resource-constrained edge devices.

However, training SNNs presents challenges, largely due to their discrete spike dynamics and the reliance on surrogate gradients for backpropagation, which can lead to unstable gradient propagation, including vanishing or exploding gradients. Batch Normalization (BN) is a technique commonly employed in Artificial Neural Networks (ANNs) to stabilize training by regulating activation statistics and improving gradient flow. It has also been adapted for SNNs in supervised learning contexts to mitigate training instability and enhance performance.

Despite its utility in supervised SNN training, traditional BN encounters difficulties in online RL environments. The inherent non-stationarity of data distributions in online RL, where the agent's policy continuously evolves, causes activation statistics to drift. This makes it challenging for BN's moving statistics, typically estimated via exponential moving averages (EMA), to precisely track the true population statistics. In ANNs, BN layers are often omitted in standard online RL algorithms because shallower ANN architectures can frequently achieve stable training without normalization. In contrast, SNNs critically depend on normalization to stabilize membrane potentials and surrogate-gradient backpropagation. Removing BN from SNN-based RL systems results in severe gradient instability and substantial performance degradation.

This research addresses a specific gap: while various BN extensions exist for SNNs, they are typically designed under the assumption of static data distributions characteristic of supervised learning. These methods are therefore ill-suited for the dynamic, non-stationary conditions of online RL. Furthermore, existing SNN-RL methodologies have largely overlooked the fundamental challenge of robust normalization. This work aims to bridge this gap by developing a BN strategy specifically tailored for SNNs in online RL settings, thereby enabling more stable and effective training of energy-efficient neuromorphic agents for dynamic control tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enable stable and high-performance Spiking Neural Networks (SNNs) for Reinforcement Learning (RL) by addressing the limitations of conventional Batch Normalization (BN) in non-stationary online learning environments.

The motivation for this work stems from several critical observations:
1. **Dependency of SNNs on Normalization:** SNNs, due to their discrete and non-differentiable spike dynamics, inherently suffer from unstable gradient propagation during training, a problem that is typically mitigated by BN. Unlike many Artificial Neural Networks (ANNs) used in RL, which can often train stably without BN, SNNs rely heavily on normalization to regulate membrane potentials and facilitate effective surrogate-gradient backpropagation. The absence or inadequacy of normalization leads to significant gradient instability and performance degradation in SNN-based RL.
2. **Breakdown of Traditional BN in Online RL:** Standard BN relies on approximating population statistics using moving averages. In online RL, the agent's policy continuously changes as it interacts with the environment, leading to continuously shifting data distributions. Under these non-stationary dynamics, traditional BN's moving statistics fail to precisely track the true underlying distributions. This inaccuracy manifests as a "noise-delay trade-off": low momentum leads to stable but slow adaptation, while high momentum adapts quickly but amplifies noise from small batches.
3. **Negative Feedback Loop in RL:** Imprecise BN statistics in online RL are particularly detrimental. During exploration and exploitation, inaccurate normalization causes the agent to select suboptimal actions. These suboptimal actions generate poor trajectories, which are then used for training, further exacerbating the problem and hindering policy improvement. This creates a negative feedback loop that impedes learning and convergence.
4. **Limitations of Existing SNN-BN Methods:** Prior BN variants developed for SNNs are predominantly designed for supervised learning contexts, where data distributions are assumed to be static. These methods do not account for the dynamic shifts inherent in online RL, rendering them unsuitable for SNN-RL applications.
5. **Preserving Energy Efficiency:** A core advantage of SNNs is their potential for low-latency and energy-efficient operation on neuromorphic hardware. Any stabilization technique introduced must not compromise this efficiency during inference.

Therefore, the key objective is to propose a novel BN strategy, CaRe-BN, that can:
- Accurately estimate BN statistics under the non-stationary dynamics of online RL.
- Stabilize SNN optimization and improve gradient flow without disrupting the RL training process.
- Enhance exploration and accelerate convergence to achieve higher-performing policies.
- Preserve the energy efficiency of SNNs during inference for deployment on resource-constrained devices.
- Be generalizable across various spiking neuron models and RL algorithms.

By addressing these challenges, the work aims to advance the practical adoption of SNNs for energy-efficient, high-performance control in real-world applications such as robotics and autonomous systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors propose Confidence-adaptive and Re-calibration Batch Normalization (CaRe-BN), a BN strategy specifically designed for Spiking Neural Networks (SNNs) in online Reinforcement Learning (RL). CaRe-BN integrates two complementary mechanisms: a confidence-adaptive update strategy and a re-calibration mechanism.

#### 3.1. Confidence-Adaptive Update of BN Statistics (Ca-BN)

Traditional BN approximates population statistics (mean and variance) using an Exponential Moving Average (EMA) of batch statistics. This approach presents a noise-delay trade-off: a low momentum parameter results in stable but slow adaptation to distribution shifts, while a high momentum parameter leads to quick adaptation but amplifies noise from small batch estimates. This trade-off is particularly problematic in online RL due to its non-stationary dynamics.

To overcome this, Ca-BN introduces a confidence-guided mechanism, inspired by the Kalman estimator, that adaptively reweights estimators to minimize the mean-squared error (MSE) of BN statistics.
- **Optimal Linear Estimator:** The method defines optimal linear estimators for the population mean ($\hat{\mu}_i$) and variance ($\hat{\sigma}^2_i$) at iteration *i*. These estimators combine the current mini-batch statistics ($\mu_i, \sigma^2_i$) and previous moving estimates ($\hat{\mu}_{i|i-1}, \hat{\sigma}^2_{i|i-1}$) using adaptive weights ($K_{\mu_i}, K_{\sigma_i}$). The weights are computed based on the generalized variance (D) of the estimation errors from both the current batch and the previous estimate.
- **Confidence of Mini-batch Statistics:** Assuming activations follow a Gaussian distribution (standard in BN), the variance of the sample mean ($D(\mu^*_i - \mu_i)$) is approximated as $\sigma^2_i / N$, and the variance of the sample variance ($D(\sigma^{*2}_i - \sigma^2_i)$) is approximated as $2\sigma^4_i / (N-1)$, where $N$ is the batch size.
- **Confidence of Previous Estimates:** Since the true population statistics are unknown, direct computation of the error variances of previous estimates ($D(\mu^*_i - \hat{\mu}_{i|i-1})$ and $D(\sigma^{*2}_i - \hat{\sigma}^2_{i|i-1})$) is not feasible. The method approximates these by maintaining smoothed recursive estimators ($D_{\mu_i}, D_{\sigma_i}$) through an EMA of the squared deviations between the current mini-batch statistics and the previous moving estimates.
- **Adaptive Weighting:** When distributional shifts are rapid, the smoothed deviations ($D_{\mu_i}, D_{\sigma_i}$) increase, which in turn increases the adaptive weights ($K_{\mu_i}, K_{\sigma_i}$), accelerating the adaptation of the moving statistics. Conversely, when statistics are stable, these terms shrink, lowering the weights and reducing noise from small mini-batches.

#### 3.2. Re-calibration Mechanism of BN Statistics (Re-BN)

While Ca-BN provides online, adaptive estimates, these estimates can still drift from true population values due to inherent stochasticity. A periodic re-calibration mechanism (Re-BN) is introduced to correct these accumulated estimation errors.
- **Periodic Correction:** At fixed intervals ($T_{cal}$), the method draws $M$ calibration batches from the replay buffer. For each batch, its mean ($\mu_j$) and variance ($\sigma^2_j$) are computed.
- **Aggregated Statistics:** The re-calibrated BN statistics (mean $\hat{\mu}_i$ and variance $\hat{\sigma}^2_i$) are then computed by averaging the means of the calibration batches and using a formula to combine their second moments for the variance.
- **Computational Overhead:** This re-calibration requires additional forward passes. However, by setting $T_{cal} \gg M$, the computational overhead is kept negligible relative to the total training cost.

#### 3.3. Integrating with RL

The CaRe-BN framework integrates these two components into a general online RL algorithm:
1. At each iteration, after selecting an action and storing the transition (using current inference BN statistics), the agent updates its policy by sampling a mini-batch.
2. During the policy update, the confidence-adaptive update (Ca-BN) is applied to update the moving BN statistics using the current mini-batch statistics and the recursively estimated error variances.
3. Periodically, at pre-defined intervals, the re-calibration mechanism (Re-BN) is invoked, sampling multiple mini-batches from the replay buffer to refine the BN statistics.
A key aspect of CaRe-BN is that its inference procedure remains identical to conventional BN. The CaRe-BN layer is designed to be fused into synaptic weights during deployment, ensuring no additional inference overhead and preserving the energy efficiency of SNNs.

#### 3.4. Experimental Setup

The methodology includes extensive experimental evaluation on:
- **Discrete Action Spaces:** Atari 2600 games (Pong, Breakout, SpaceInvaders, Freeway, Seaquest) using a Deep Q-Network (DQN) framework with a Spiking Q-Network.
- **Continuous Control:** MuJoCo benchmarks (InvertedDoublePendulum, Ant, HalfCheetah, Hopper, Walker2d) using a hybrid framework with a spiking actor network and a deep ANN critic, trained with DDPG, TD3, and SAC algorithms.
- **Spiking Neuron Models:** Leaky Integrate-and-Fire (LIF), Current-based LIF (CLIF), and Dynamic Neuron (DN) models.
- **Training Details:** All SNN agents are trained via Spatio-Temporal Backpropagation (STBP), with CaRe-BN layers inserted between adjacent layers. SNN agents perform a single forward inference of 5 simulation time steps per environment step.
- **Evaluation Metrics:** Wasserstein distance for statistics precision, exploration returns, learning curves, final policy returns, relative variance, and comparison with ANN baselines and existing SNN-BN methods.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of CaRe-BN yielded several findings across various benchmarks and configurations:

#### 4.1. More Precise BN Statistics Lead to Better Exploration

- **Reduced Wasserstein Distance:** CaRe-BN consistently reduced the Wasserstein distance between the estimated BN statistics and the true feature distribution across all layers during training in continuous control tasks (e.g., InvertedDoublePendulum-v4 with CLIF neurons and TD3 algorithm, Figure 3). This indicates that CaRe-BN provides more precise normalization statistics compared to traditional BN.
- **Higher Exploration Returns:** The improved precision in BN statistics translated into higher exploration returns across five MuJoCo tasks (Figure 4). This suggests that accurate normalization enables the SNN agent to select better actions during exploration, leading to higher-quality trajectories for policy updates and establishing a positive feedback loop for learning.

#### 4.2. Adaptability of CaRe-BN

- **Improved Final Returns and Faster Convergence:** CaRe-BN consistently outperformed standard SNNs by achieving faster convergence and higher final returns across different spiking neuron models (LIF, CLIF, DN) and RL algorithms (TD3, DDPG) in continuous control tasks (Figure 5, Tables 10-15).
- **Lower Variance:** CaRe-BN significantly reduced the variance of SNN-RL training compared to standard SNNs (Figure 6a, 6b). The relative variance of the final policy returns for SNNs equipped with CaRe-BN was lower than that of ANN baselines (17.71% for DDPG and 21.24% for TD3). This indicates enhanced stability and reproducibility of SNN-RL training.
- **Generalization Across RL Domains:** CaRe-BN also demonstrated strong generalization capabilities in discrete-action settings, showing markedly improved performance in Atari benchmarks using a deep spiking Q-network (Figure 7).

#### 4.3. Exceeding State-of-the-Art

- **Superior to Other SNN-RL Methods:** In comparisons using the TD3 algorithm and CLIF neuron model on MuJoCo, CaRe-BN outperformed existing state-of-the-art SNN-RL approaches (e.g., ANN-SNN conversion methods, pop-SAN, MDC-SAN, ILC-SAN) (Table 1). This suggests that robust normalization is more impactful than architectural modifications in improving SNN-RL performance.
- **New SOTA for SNN-BN:** CaRe-BN also surpassed other SNN-specific BN variants (tdBN, BNTT, TEBN, TABN), establishing a new state-of-the-art normalization strategy for SNN-RL.
- **Outperforming ANN Counterparts:** SNNs equipped with CaRe-BN trained with TD3 achieved an average performance gain (APG) of 5.9% over their ANN counterparts on MuJoCo tasks (Table 1). Similar results were observed with the SAC algorithm (Figure 9). This indicates that properly normalized SNNs can not only match but exceed the performance of traditional ANN-based RL agents while retaining energy efficiency.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies

Ablation studies revealed that both components of CaRe-BN—the Confidence-adaptive update (Ca-BN) and the Re-calibration mechanism (Re-BN)—contribute positively to performance (Figure 6c). The combination of both mechanisms provided the most significant improvement, suggesting that Ca-BN handles online estimation while Re-BN corrects accumulated errors, leading to more precise and consistent normalization.

#### 4.5. SNN-Friendly Design

When applied to standard ANNs, CaRe-BN did not provide significant improvements over ANN baselines without CaRe-BN (Figure 6d, Tables 18-19). This is attributed to shallow ANNs often being able to train stably without additional normalization, and it confirms that the observed benefits of CaRe-BN are primarily due to its addressing SNN-specific normalization challenges rather than enhancing the general RL mechanism.

#### 4.6. Energy Consumption and Training Costs

- **Inference Energy Efficiency:** SNNs with CaRe-BN demonstrated substantially reduced energy consumption per inference (e.g., average 16.48 nJ for SNNs vs. 1775.36 nJ for ANNs on MuJoCo tasks), leveraging the inherent energy efficiency of SNNs (Table 21).
- **Negligible Training Overhead:** CaRe-BN introduced no significant additional training time or GPU memory usage compared to other BN variants, making it a lightweight solution (Table 20).
- **Impact of Simulation Time Steps:** While the default SNN simulation time step was 5, experiments showed that SNNs generally benefit from larger time steps, and CaRe-BN achieved even stronger results (up to 6.32% improvement over ANNs) with 8 SNN simulation steps (Table 17).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research makes a significant contribution to the field of Spiking Neural Networks (SNNs) in Reinforcement Learning (RL) by introducing CaRe-BN, a tailored batch normalization method. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Enabling High-Performance SNN-RL:** CaRe-BN directly addresses a critical challenge in SNN-RL: the instability of traditional Batch Normalization (BN) in non-stationary online learning environments. By providing more precise and stable normalization, it enables SNNs to achieve higher performance, faster convergence, and reduced training variance. This moves SNN-RL closer to practical viability for complex control tasks.

2. **Surpassing ANN Baselines:** The demonstration that SNNs equipped with CaRe-BN can outperform their Artificial Neural Network (ANN) counterparts in continuous control tasks (by up to 5.9% in average performance gain) is a notable finding. This suggests that with appropriate architectural and training considerations, SNNs can not only match but potentially exceed the performance of ANNs while retaining their inherent energy-efficient advantages.

3. **Facilitating Neuromorphic Computing Adoption:** SNNs are designed for deployment on energy-efficient neuromorphic hardware. By stabilizing SNN training without introducing additional inference overhead, CaRe-BN paves the way for the development and deployment of robust, high-performing, and energy-efficient neuromorphic agents in real-world applications such as robotics, autonomous systems, and edge computing, where computational resources are often constrained.

4. **Opening New Research Directions:** The work highlights the critical importance of normalization strategies tailored to the unique dynamics of SNNs, particularly in dynamic learning paradigms like RL. This could stimulate further research into SNN-specific regularization, optimization techniques, and architectural designs that leverage the strengths of neuromorphic computation while mitigating its challenges.

5. **Practical Applicability and Ease of Integration:** CaRe-BN is designed as a lightweight, drop-in replacement for existing SNN-RL pipelines, introducing no significant additional computational cost during training and preserving the low-latency, energy-efficient inference of SNNs. This ease of integration enhances its practical appeal for researchers and developers working on SNN-based systems.

In conclusion, CaRe-BN represents a technical advancement that stabilizes and enhances SNN-RL, thereby strengthening the foundation for more capable and efficient brain-inspired artificial intelligence.
