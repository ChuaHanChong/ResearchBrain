# RFS: Reinforcement Learning with Residual Flow Steering for Dexterous Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> This research introduces Residual Flow Steering (RFS), a data-efficient reinforcement learning framework that adapts pretrained generative policies for dexterous manipulation. RFS integrates latent noise steering for global behavioral modulation with residual actions for local refinement, achieving an average success rate of 0.861 across six simulated tasks and enabling robust real-world generalization to unseen objects with limited human corrective data.

## Problem

- Policies pretrained via imitation learning often lack robustness and generalization capabilities for successful deployment in diverse, real-world conditions, especially for dexterous manipulation.
- Many established reinforcement learning (RL) algorithms are incompatible with expressive generative policy architectures (e.g., diffusion and flow models) that represent multimodal action distributions.
- Existing policy adaptation methods typically focus on either local corrections (residual policies) or global behavioral shifts (latent modulation), failing to efficiently combine both for comprehensive policy improvement.

## Method

- The authors propose **RFS** (Residual Flow Steering), a unified ==policy modulation framework== that jointly learns an ==initial latent noise variable== ($a_0$) for global steering and a ==residual action== ($a_r$) for local adjustments.
- **RFS** uses the latent noise $a_0$ to steer a pretrained ==flow-matching base policy==, generating a base action, to which the learned residual action $a_r$ is then added.
- The framework is implemented with a ==sim-to-reality pipeline==, involving online ==PPO== fine-tuning in simulation and offline ==TD3+BC== fine-tuning in the real world using limited human-corrected data.

## Results

- **RFS** achieved an average success rate of **0.861** across six simulated dexterous manipulation tasks, outperforming various baselines including Diffusion/Flow RL (e.g., DPPO: **0.178**), offline RL (e.g., IQL: **0.457**), and other policy adaptation methods.
- In real-world experiments, RFS significantly improved generalization to unseen objects, achieving **74.0%** grasping and **70.0%** pick-and-place success with only 50 human corrections, compared to zero-shot performance (**30.0%** grasping, **40.0%** pick-and-place) and supervised fine-tuning baselines.
- Ablation studies confirmed that the combined RFS approach consistently outperformed individual components (e.g., Residual RL or DSRL) in both simulation and real-world settings, demonstrating the synergistic benefits of joint local and global policy modulation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Unifying latent-noise steering (global adaptation) with residual actions (local refinement) on top of flow-matching policies significantly enhances policy robustness and generalization compared to using either method in isolation.
> - For effective offline reinforcement learning adaptation of modulated policies, conditioning the critic on the final executed action (a=ab+ara = a_b + a_ra=ab​+ar​) is crucial for stability and learning efficacy.
> - Simulation pretraining provides a broad coverage of behaviors and perturbations, which, when combined with a small set of real-world human corrections via RFS, leads to substantially greater improvements and generalization than fine-tuning policies trained solely on real data.
