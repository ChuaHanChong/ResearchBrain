# CLEVRER: CoLlision Events for Video REpresentation and Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> CLEVRER introduces a synthetic video dataset designed to assess AI models' temporal and causal reasoning abilities in explaining, predicting, and counterfactually reasoning about collision events. The research also presents the Neuro-Symbolic Dynamic Reasoning (NS-DR) model, which achieves substantial performance improvements over existing baselines across these complex reasoning tasks.

## Problem

- Existing video understanding benchmarks primarily focus on complex visual and linguistic pattern recognition, neglecting deeper temporal and causal reasoning.
- Current AI models struggle to explain 'why' events occur, predict 'what will happen,' or imagine 'what if' scenarios, indicating a gap in causal inference capabilities.
- A lack of diagnostic datasets that simplify visual input to isolate and rigorously test models' understanding of underlying temporal and causal mechanisms.

## Method

- Created **CLEVRER**, a synthetic video dataset featuring 20,000 videos of object collisions with ground-truth motion traces and event histories, along with machine-generated questions for descriptive, explanatory, predictive, and counterfactual reasoning.
- Developed the **Neuro-Symbolic Dynamic Reasoning (NS-DR)** model, which integrates a ==neural video frame parser==, a ==neural dynamics predictor== (==Propagation Network==), a ==question parser==, and a ==symbolic program executor==.
- The dataset generation leverages a ==physics engine== (Bullet) for realistic object interactions and Blender for rendering, enabling explicit control over causal structures and detailed annotations.

## Results

- Baseline models showed significantly degraded performance on causal questions, with MAC(V) achieving only **12.5%** per-option accuracy on explanatory and **13.7%** on counterfactual tasks.
- The NS-DR model demonstrated substantial improvements, achieving **79.6%** per-option accuracy on explanatory questions, **68.7%** on predictive questions, and **42.2%** on counterfactual questions.
- NS-DR also surpassed all baselines on descriptive questions with **88.1%** accuracy, showcasing superior integration of perception and compositional logic for fundamental video understanding.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - State-of-the-art visual reasoning models, while competent at descriptive tasks, fundamentally lack the ability to model dynamics and infer causal relationships, particularly for unobserved or counterfactual situations.
> - Object-centric representations are crucial for robust video reasoning, as evidenced by improved performance when models process individual objects and their attributes.
> - A neuro-symbolic architecture that combines neural perception and dynamics prediction with symbolic logic provides an effective framework for addressing complex causal video understanding challenges.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This paper positions itself within the rapidly evolving landscape of visual reasoning and video understanding, specifically addressing a critical gap: the emphasis on *causal structure* and *temporal reasoning* over mere pattern recognition. The authors argue that while much progress has been made in general video understanding (e.g., video captioning, action recognition) and visual question answering (VQA) on both static images and videos, these benchmarks often focus on complex visual and linguistic perception rather than the underlying logical, temporal, and causal mechanisms.

The work draws inspiration from several key areas:
- **CLEVR Dataset (Johnson et al., 2017a):** CLEVRER builds upon the philosophy of CLEVR, which simplified visual appearance in static images to isolate and focus on compositional reasoning with language. CLEVRER extends this by simplifying visual input in videos to focus on *temporal and causal reasoning*.
- **Developmental Psychology and Human Causal Judgment:** The dataset's design, particularly the inclusion of explanatory, predictive, and counterfactual questions, is explicitly motivated by theories of human causal judgment (Gerstenberg et al., 2015; Ullman, 2015). This distinguishes CLEVRER from many purely data-driven AI benchmarks, embedding it within a framework of understanding human-like intelligence.
- **Physical and Causal Reasoning:** The paper situates itself alongside research on learning scene dynamics and intuitive physics (e.g., interaction networks, propagation networks). However, CLEVRER uniquely integrates dynamics modeling with compositional causal reasoning, grounding these complex reasoning tasks within the language domain. It complements concurrent works like CoPhy (Baradel et al., 2020) which studies physical dynamics prediction in counterfactual settings, and CATER (Girdhar & Ramanan, 2020) which focuses on compositional actions and temporal reasoning. CLEVRER's distinct contribution is its comprehensive range of causal questions (explanation, prediction, counterfactual) alongside descriptive tasks, making it a powerful diagnostic tool.

By introducing CLEVRER, the authors aim to shift the research focus from high-fidelity visual recognition and complex natural language understanding (where deep learning models often excel) towards the deeper cognitive abilities of explaining *why* events happen, predicting *what will happen*, and imagining *what if* scenarios. This aligns with the broader push in AI towards more robust, interpretable, and human-like reasoning capabilities that go beyond superficial correlations.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to advance the field of video reasoning by systematically studying and evaluating computational models on their ability to understand temporal and causal events, a cornerstone of human intelligence. The authors identify a significant limitation in existing video reasoning benchmarks: a pervasive focus on pattern recognition from complex visual and linguistic inputs, rather than on the intricate causal structures governing events.

The key motivations driving this work are:

1. **Address the Gap in Causal Reasoning:** Most current video datasets, despite their complexity, do not adequately probe a model's understanding of "why" events occur or "what if" circumstances were different. CLEVRER is designed to fill this void by providing a controlled environment where causal relationships are explicit and testable.
2. **Develop a Diagnostic Dataset:** The goal is to create a benchmark that simplifies visual recognition (similar to CLEVR for images) to minimize perceptual biases, allowing researchers to isolate and focus on the complexity of temporal and causal reasoning. This diagnostic nature is crucial for pinpointing the strengths and weaknesses of AI models in specific reasoning capacities.
3. **Explore Human-Inspired Reasoning Categories:** Drawing inspiration from cognitive science, the dataset is structured around four distinct types of questions:
 - **Descriptive:** To assess basic perception and understanding of "what is happening."
 - **Explanatory:** To evaluate the ability to infer causes and "what's responsible for" events.
 - **Predictive:** To test the capacity for forecasting "what will happen next."
 - **Counterfactual:** To gauge the ability to imagine alternative outcomes under hypothetical "what if" conditions. These latter three are particularly challenging and directly target causal reasoning.
4. **Evaluate State-of-the-Art Models:** By benchmarking current leading visual reasoning models on CLEVRER, the authors aim to expose their limitations in causal understanding, providing clear evidence that current approaches often fall short beyond superficial pattern matching.
5. **Propose a Principled Approach:** Motivated by the shortcomings of existing models, a further objective is to explore a "principled approach" to causal reasoning. This led to the development of the Neuro-Symbolic Dynamic Reasoning (NS-DR) model, which explicitly integrates perception, dynamics modeling, and symbolic logic to address the identified challenges.

In essence, the paper seeks to push the boundaries of AI from mere observation and recognition towards a deeper, more human-like understanding of dynamic, interactive environments, capable of explaining, predicting, and imagining scenarios.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology centers around the creation of the **CLEVRER dataset** and the evaluation of various baseline models, culminating in the introduction of a novel **Neuro-Symbolic Dynamic Reasoning (NS-DR) model**.

#### 3.1. The CLEVRER Dataset

CLEVRER is a synthetic, diagnostic video dataset meticulously designed to evaluate temporal and causal reasoning.

- **Video Generation:**
 - **Quantity:** 20,000 synthetic videos (10,000 for training, 5,000 for validation, 5,000 for testing), each lasting 5 seconds.
 - **Environment:** A fully controlled, synthetic environment with objects moving and colliding on a flat tabletop.
 - **Physics Simulation:** The Bullet physics engine simulates realistic object motion and collisions.
 - **Rendering:** Blender is used to render realistic video frames from the simulated motion traces.
 - **Objects:** Objects possess compositional intrinsic attributes (shape: cube, sphere, cylinder; material: metal, rubber; color: 8 distinct colors), ensuring each object is uniquely identifiable. All objects are of the same size.
 - **Events:** Three types: enter, exit, and collision, each with defined participants.
 - **Causal Structure Generation:** A recursive process generates complex causal structures by starting with a moving object and iteratively adding objects whose initial conditions lead to collisions, creating rich event histories and motion traces.
 - **Annotations:** Each video comes with ground-truth motion traces and full event histories, crucial for model diagnostics and the generation of causal questions. Two seconds of simulation are held out for predictive tasks.

- **Question Generation:**
 - Machine-generated questions are paired with ground-truth, tree-structured functional programs, representing their underlying logic.
 - **Descriptive Questions (219,918):** Evaluate understanding of dynamic content and temporal relations (e.g., "what color," "how many"). These are open-ended, single-word answers, with sub-types like count, exist, query color/material/shape.
 - **Explanatory Questions (33,811):** Query causal responsibility (e.g., "what's responsible for"). Multiple-choice, with models needing to select all correct options.
 - **Predictive Questions (14,298):** Test prediction of future events beyond the video's end (e.g., "what will happen next"). Multiple-choice.
 - **Counterfactual Questions (37,253):** Assess reasoning about hypothetical scenarios, such as removing an object (e.g., "what will happen if"). Multiple-choice.
 - **Bias Mitigation:** Questions are sampled to balance answer distributions and minimize question-answer correlations across the dataset.

#### 3.2. Baseline Evaluation

A wide range of state-of-the-art models are evaluated:

- **Language-only Models:** Q-type (random, frequent), LSTM (to establish language biases).
- **Video Question Answering Models:** CNN+MLP, CNN+LSTM, TVQA+ (a variant of TVQA using attribute-aware object-centric features), Memory (Fan et al., 2019).
- **Compositional Visual Reasoning Models:** IEP(V) (neural program execution), TbD-net(V) (neural network assembly from parsed programs), MAC(V) (joint attention with temporal attention), and MAC(V+) (MAC augmented with object segmentation masks and attributes).
- **Implementation Details:** Models use ResNet-50 features, uniform sampling of 25 frames per video, and some models leverage object segmentation masks and attributes.
- **Metrics:** Descriptive questions use accuracy. Multiple-choice questions use per-option accuracy (correctness of individual options) and per-question accuracy (all options correctly selected).

#### 3.3. Neuro-Symbolic Dynamic Reasoning (NS-DR) Model

Motivated by the limitations of baselines, NS-DR combines neural networks for perception and dynamics prediction with symbolic logic for causal reasoning.

- **Video Frame Parser (Figure 4-I):** A Mask R-CNN (ResNet-50 FPN backbone) performs object detection, segmentation, and attribute classification on each video frame, generating an object-centric representation.
- **Neural Dynamics Predictor (Figure 4-II):** A Propagation Network (PropNet) models object- and relation-centric dynamics. It takes object proposals from the parser, learns motion across frames, and predicts future motion traces and collision events. It represents the dynamic system as a graph of objects and relations. A variation, NS-DR (NE), identifies collisions via velocity changes, rather than explicit relation prediction.
- **Question Parser (Figure 4-III):** An attention-based sequence-to-sequence LSTM model translates natural language questions (and choices for multiple-choice questions) into functional programs.
- **Program Executor (Figure 4-IV):** Implemented in Python, this component explicitly executes the functional programs on the symbolic motion and event traces provided by the dynamics predictor, producing the final answer. It includes input (Objects, Events, UnseenEvents), filter (e.g., Filter_color, Filter_collision, Filter_ancestor, Get_counterfact), and output (e.g., Query_color, Count, Exist, Belong_to) modules.

This modular, neuro-symbolic approach aims to disentangle perception, dynamics, and reasoning, allowing each component to operate on appropriate representations and address specific challenges.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation on CLEVRER reveals significant insights into the capabilities and limitations of current AI models for causal video reasoning.

#### 4.1. Baseline Model Performance (Table 2)

- **Language-only Models:** Perform poorly (e.g., LSTM at 34.7% descriptive accuracy), confirming that CLEVRER has minimal language-only biases and requires visual input.
- **Descriptive Reasoning:**
 - Models designed for compositional visual reasoning (TbD-net(V): 79.5%, MAC(V+): 86.4%) achieve strong performance on descriptive questions. This demonstrates their proficiency in combining visual and language perception with compositional logic for "what is" type questions.
 - Video QA models like TVQA+ (72.0%) also perform reasonably well, but less effectively than dedicated compositional models.
- **Causal Reasoning (Explanatory, Predictive, Counterfactual):**
 - **Universal Struggle:** All baseline models, including the best-performing compositional ones, show significantly degraded performance on explanatory, predictive, and especially counterfactual questions.
 - **Marginal Gains:** MAC(V) and TbD-net(V) achieve only marginal improvements over random or language-only baselines on causal tasks. For instance, MAC(V) gets 12.5% per-option on explanatory and 16.5% on predictive, far from descriptive performance.
 - **Counterfactual Weakness:** Performance on counterfactual questions is particularly low across all baselines, often barely above random chance (e.g., MAC(V) at 13.7% per-option accuracy), highlighting their inability to reason about unobserved or hypothetical scenarios.
 - **Importance of Object-Centric Representations:** MAC(V+), which uses augmented object-aware features, shows a notable performance boost over MAC(V) on all tasks (e.g., 22.3% vs 12.5% on explanatory, 42.9% vs 16.5% on predictive per-option accuracy). TVQA+ also achieves relatively higher scores on predictive questions (48.9%), suggesting the critical role of understanding individual objects and their attributes.

**Key takeaway from baselines:** While current models can recognize objects and events and perform simple logical operations, they fundamentally lack the ability to model dynamics and infer causal relationships, especially for unobserved or counterfactual situations.

#### 4.2. Neuro-Symbolic Dynamic Reasoning (NS-DR) Performance (Table 3)

NS-DR demonstrates substantial improvements across all reasoning categories, validating its neuro-symbolic approach.

- **Descriptive Questions:** NS-DR achieves 88.1% accuracy, outperforming all baselines by a clear margin, even with a question parser trained on only 1,000 programs. This indicates superior integration of perception and compositional logic.
- **Explanatory Questions:** NS-DR dramatically improves to 79.6% per-option and 82.9% per-question accuracy, showcasing its ability to effectively capture and reason about causal relationships within the video's observed events.
- **Predictive Questions:** Performance reaches 68.7% per-option and 74.1% per-question accuracy. The explicit dynamics predictor allows the model to forecast future events, a capability largely absent in baselines.
- **Counterfactual Questions:** While still the most challenging, NS-DR achieves 42.2% per-option accuracy, a significant leap over baseline models which struggled immensely with "what if" scenarios. This highlights the power of a dynamics model that can simulate alternative realities.
- **NS-DR (NE) Variation:** Performing comparably to the full NS-DR model (e.g., 54.1% predictive per-option), NS-DR (NE) shows the system's adaptability to different dynamics modeling approaches, where collisions are inferred from velocity changes.
- **Ablation Study on Question Parser (Figure 5):** NS-DR demonstrates robust performance even with a relatively small number of programs (around 1,000) for training its question parser, indicating its efficiency in learning the mapping from language to symbolic logic.

**Overall Findings:** The results strongly suggest that object-centric representations, explicit dynamics modeling, and a neuro-symbolic architecture—which combines neural perception with symbolic reasoning—are crucial components for tackling complex temporal and causal reasoning tasks in videos.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The CLEVRER dataset and the accompanying Neuro-Symbolic Dynamic Reasoning (NS-DR) model represent a significant contribution to the field of artificial intelligence, with broad implications for future research and applications.

1. **A Novel Diagnostic Benchmark:** CLEVRER provides a sorely needed diagnostic tool for evaluating AI models on a spectrum of reasoning tasks, from basic descriptive understanding to complex causal inference (explanation, prediction, counterfactuals). By simplifying visual complexity, it forces models to engage with the underlying logic and dynamics, exposing the limitations of current pattern-recognition-focused approaches. This diagnostic capability is crucial for guiding the development of more intelligent systems.

2. **Shift Towards Deeper Understanding:** The work champions a paradigm shift in AI research, urging the community to move beyond passive knowledge extraction and superficial correlations towards building an "internal understanding of the dynamics and causal relations." This is a fundamental step towards AI that can reason like humans, who instinctively explain, predict, and imagine alternative scenarios.

3. **Validation of Neuro-Symbolic AI:** The exceptional performance of the NS-DR model, which explicitly combines neural perception, a learned physics engine for dynamics prediction, and symbolic program execution, provides strong evidence for the efficacy of neuro-symbolic approaches. It demonstrates that integrating the strengths of deep learning (for perception) with classical AI (for symbolic reasoning and planning) is a powerful strategy for tackling tasks that require both rich perception and complex logical inference.

4. **Identification of Essential AI Capabilities:** The study clearly identifies two critical capabilities for advanced video reasoning:
 - **Object-centric Representations:** The improved performance of models leveraging object-aware features (MAC(V+)) and the core design of NS-DR underscore the importance of understanding individual objects and their attributes within a scene.
 - **Dynamics Modeling:** The ability of NS-DR to perform well on predictive and counterfactual tasks directly stems from its integrated dynamics predictor. This highlights that robust AI for physical environments must be able to simulate and reason about how objects interact over time and under varying conditions.

5. **Opening New Research Avenues:** The limitations of NS-DR, particularly on long-term counterfactual predictions and its reliance on extra supervision for parsing, point to fertile grounds for future research. This includes developing:
 - More accurate and stable dynamics models for longer horizons and greater complexity.
 - Unsupervised or weakly-supervised methods for scene decomposition, object discovery, and concept grounding, reducing the need for extensive manual annotations.
 - Hybrid models that can better generalize to more realistic and diverse visual environments.

6. **Potential for Real-World Applications:** While CLEVRER is a synthetic benchmark, the reasoning capabilities it promotes—such as understanding causal chains, predicting future states, and evaluating hypothetical outcomes—are essential for real-world applications. These include robust robotic manipulation (e.g., predicting how an object will move if pushed), autonomous driving (e.g., anticipating collisions and planning evasive actions), and intelligent agents that can explain their decisions and learn from counterfactual experiences.

In conclusion, CLEVRER and NS-DR represent a concerted effort to push AI towards a deeper, more cognitive form of intelligence. By focusing on the "why" and "what if" of video events, this work lays a crucial foundation for building AI systems that are not just excellent pattern recognizers but truly capable reasoners about the dynamic physical world.
