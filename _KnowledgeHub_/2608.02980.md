# Qwen-3D: A Generalist 3D Vision-Language Model for Spatial Understanding

> [!summary] Summary
> Qwen-3D is a generalist 3D Vision-Language Model from Carnegie Mellon University that enhances spatial understanding from multi-view visual streams by integrating geometry-aware representations and a direct decoding mechanism. It achieves state-of-the-art performance among 3D LMMs on referential grounding, instance segmentation, and VQA, significantly narrowing the performance gap with specialist 3D systems while preserving its base model's 2D capabilities.

## Problem

- Large Multimodal Models (LMMs) struggle with long, multi-view video streams due to high computational costs from frame-centric tokenization and limited context windows.
- Existing 3D LMMs exhibit performance gaps compared to specialist 3D perception systems on tasks like grounding and segmentation, primarily due to an "information bottleneck" in decoding dense 3D predictions.
- A lack of a unified and direct interface between the language reasoning capabilities of LMMs and the need for dense, spatially precise 3D geometric predictions.

## Method

- **Qwen-3D**'s ==Geometry-Aware Scene Representation== employs voxel-based token merging of unprojected 2D image features with depth, creating compact 3D world-space tokens.
- ==Geometry-Aware Vision-Language Attention== integrates ==3D Rotary Positional Embeddings (3D RoPE)== with uniform frequency distribution for each spatial dimension, alongside a full attention mask for visual tokens within the Qwen2.5-VL backbone, adapted via LoRA.
- A ==Geometry-Aware Decoding== mechanism directly connects the VLM backbone's contextualized vision-language features to a Mask2Former-style segmentation decoder, enabling dense and precise 3D geometric predictions.

## Results

- Achieved state-of-the-art among 3D LMMs on the ScanRefer benchmark with **62.8%** Acc@25, improving over 30% compared to 3D-LLM and 12% over LLaVA-3D, substantially narrowing the gap with specialist models.
- Demonstrated superior out-of-domain generalization on the Locate-3D benchmark, achieving **55.7%** Acc@25 and **43.4%** Acc@50, significantly outperforming specialist models like UniVLG.
- Maintained strong performance on 2D vision-language tasks, matching or slightly outperforming its Qwen2.5-VL base model, indicating successful preservation of 2D capabilities during joint 2D-3D training.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Aggregating multi-view observations into a shared 3D world coordinate system through voxel-based token merging effectively compresses visual information, promoting efficient and consistent spatial reasoning.
> - A direct, full-token interface between the VLM backbone and a mask-based decoder overcomes the "geometry-aware decoding bottleneck" prevalent in prior 3D LMMs, enabling more precise and dense 3D predictions.
> - Careful design of 3D Rotary Positional Embeddings, ensuring uniform frequency distribution across all spatial dimensions, is critical for robust 3D grounding capabilities, outperforming naive 3D encoding methods.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of large multimodal models (LMMs) has achieved success in processing 2D images and short video segments. However, extending these capabilities to long, multi-view video streams presents challenges primarily due to the computational cost associated with frame-centric tokenization and the inherent limitations of context windows in existing architectures. 3D geometry offers a potential solution by providing a natural mechanism for compressing visual information. By integrating depth and camera pose, observations from multiple views and time steps can be fused into a persistent, world-aligned representation, which can mitigate the computational burden and enable more effective long-range spatio-temporal reasoning.

Previous work in integrating 3D information into LMMs generally follows two paradigms. One approach involves introducing 3D point clouds as auxiliary inputs, either alongside or in place of multi-view images. While this provides explicit geometric structure, it often treats point clouds as a separate modality, potentially overlooking inherent alignment between point cloud features and image features with corresponding depth. Examples include LL3DA, Scene-LLM, and Grounded-3D-LLM. The second paradigm, exemplified by methods such as LLaVA-3D and Video-3D-LLM, integrates geometry directly into the visual token representation by modifying positional encodings to reflect 3D world coordinates. This allows models to reason over multi-view observations within a shared spatial coordinate system, leveraging strong visual representations from large VLM backbones.

Despite these advancements, existing 3D LMMs have generally shown performance gaps when compared to specialist 3D perception systems on tasks like grounding, detection, and segmentation. This performance disparity is attributed, in part, to limitations in how 3D predictions are decoded. Current 3D LMMs typically communicate 3D predictions through language tokens, proposal selection from predefined sets, or lightweight grounding queries via special tokens. These methods can create an information bottleneck between the robust language reasoning capabilities of the LMM backbone and the requirement for dense, spatially precise geometric predictions in a continuous 3D world coordinate system. This work aims to address this identified bottleneck by proposing a more direct and unified interface between language reasoning and 3D geometric perception.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a generalist 3D Vision-Language Model (VLM), named Qwen-3D, that enhances spatial understanding capabilities over multi-view visual streams, particularly long-horizon videos and static scenes. The model aims to overcome current limitations of LMMs in 3D perception by integrating geometry-aware representations and decoding mechanisms more effectively.

The motivations driving this work are multi-faceted:

- **Addressing Computational and Context Limitations for Long Videos:** Current VLMs struggle with long multi-view video streams due to the quadratic computational cost of attention mechanisms and limited context windows. The authors propose that 3D geometry, specifically depth and camera poses, can serve as a compression mechanism, mapping video frames into a shared 3D coordinate system to create persistent scene representations. This would allow for efficient reasoning over longer sequences by aggregating observations in world space.
- **Improving 3D Perception Performance:** Existing 3D LMMs lag behind specialist 3D perception systems on fundamental tasks such as grounding, detection, and segmentation. The researchers contend that this gap is largely due to the "geometry-aware decoding bottleneck," where current methods for communicating 3D predictions (e.g., text generation of coordinates, selection from proposals, or single grounding tokens) are insufficient for dense and precise spatial outputs.
- **Unifying Language Reasoning and Dense Geometric Prediction:** A core motivation is to bridge the gap between high-level language understanding and low-level, spatially precise 3D perception. Current 3D LMMs often maintain a strict separation, leading to information loss. The authors seek to develop an architecture where the language model and the geometric grounding decoder share dense visual representations, enabling rich bidirectional interactions.
- **Developing a Generalist Model:** The objective is to create a single, unified architecture that can perform a diverse set of vision-language tasks, including referential grounding, instance segmentation, and visual question answering, across both 2D images and 3D scenes. This generalist approach aims to leverage the strengths of large 2D VLM backbones while significantly improving 3D spatial reasoning without sacrificing 2D capabilities.
- **Enhancing Out-of-Domain Generalization:** While specialist 3D models often perform well in-domain, LMMs are hypothesized to offer superior generalization to novel or out-of-distribution scenes and language instructions. The authors aim to validate this by designing a model that is robust to shifts in sensor modalities and scene characteristics.

In summary, the key objective is to build a robust, efficient, and versatile 3D VLM by fundamentally rethinking how geometry is integrated into the model's visual token representation and, critically, how dense 3D predictions are decoded, thereby closing the performance gap with specialist systems and enabling broader generalist capabilities.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Qwen-3D extends the Qwen2.5-VL model with three core components designed to facilitate geometry-aware reasoning and decoding.

#### 3.1. Geometry-Aware Scene Representation

The process begins by taking a set of posed RGB-D (color and depth) observations.
1. **Feature Extraction and Unprojection:** The Qwen2.5-VL vision encoder is used to extract 2D image features. For each feature, its corresponding depth value is unprojected into a 3D world-space coordinate using known camera intrinsics and poses. This yields a set of feature-coordinate pairs {(f_i, p_i)}, where f_i is a visual feature and p_i is its 3D world coordinate.
2. **Voxel-based Token Merging:** To address redundancy arising from multi-view observations capturing the same physical locations, the unprojected feature-coordinate pairs undergo voxel-based token merging. The 3D space is discretized into voxels (with a specified size of 5cm). Features and coordinates within each occupied voxel are mean-pooled, resulting in a compact, unordered set of geometry-aligned tokens {(f'_j, p'_j)}, where the number of tokens M' is significantly less than the original M. This step effectively compresses visual information in 3D space, promoting efficiency and spatial consistency.

#### 3.2. Geometry-Aware Vision–Language Attention

Unlike traditional VLMs that operate on 2D image-plane coordinates, Qwen-3D performs attention directly within a shared 3D world coordinate system.
1. **Text Tokenization:** The input natural language query is embedded into a sequence of text tokens using the Qwen2.5-VL language tokenizer.
2. **Concatenation and Multi-modal Attention:** The voxelized 3D point features and text tokens are concatenated and fed into the Qwen2.5-VL's multi-modal attention layers.
3. **3D Rotary Positional Embeddings (3D RoPE):** To enable 3D spatial reasoning, Qwen's Multimodal RoPE, originally designed for 1D or 2D grids, is adapted to encode 3D world coordinates (x, y, z). The positional encoding for a point p is defined as PE_3D(p) = [PE(t); PE(x); PE(y); PE(z)], where 't' represents temporal position. For text, 't' increments sequentially, and (x, y, z) are treated as standard 1D RoPE. For point cloud tokens, 't' is constant, and their world coordinates are used as spatial IDs. Crucially, the 3D RoPE in Qwen-3D initializes the same range of frequencies for each spatial dimension (x, y, z), ensuring a uniform distribution of high and low-frequency channels. This contrasts with a "naive 3D MRoPE" that uses Qwen2.5-VL's default partitioning, which assigns an imbalanced frequency spectrum, degrading 3D grounding capabilities.
4. **Full Attention Masking:** Qwen2.5-VL typically uses autoregressive causal masking. For 3D point clouds, which are permutation-invariant, this is replaced with a full attention mask over visual tokens, allowing all vision and language tokens to attend to each other except during autoregressive text generation. This modification improves 3D performance while being shown not to degrade the backbone's original capabilities.
5. **LoRA Adaptation:** The vision-language attention layers of the frozen Qwen2.5-VL backbone are fine-tuned using LoRA (Low-Rank Adaptation) to efficiently adapt the pre-trained model to 3D tasks with a minimal number of trainable parameters (∼50M).

#### 3.3. Geometry-Aware Decoding

This component addresses the identified bottleneck in existing 3D LMMs by providing a direct connection between the VLM backbone and a dense geometric prediction mechanism.
1. **Direct Connection to Mask-based Decoder:** Instead of relying on text-space decoding, proposal selection, or special grounding tokens, Qwen-3D directly exposes the full contextualized vision–language representation (V for visual tokens, T for language tokens) from the backbone to a Mask2Former-style segmentation decoder.
2. **Object Queries:** A set of learnable object queries (Q) are processed through the decoder. Each decoder layer performs cross-attention with visual and language tokens and self-attention among object queries. This allows each query to jointly reason about scene geometry and linguistic context using the complete visual representation.
3. **Positional Embeddings in Decoder:** All attention operations involving visual tokens in the decoder utilize positional embeddings corresponding to the underlying modality (2D image coordinates or 3D world coordinates), maintaining geometric awareness.
4. **Dual Prediction Heads:** After the final decoder layer, each object query predicts:
 - A segmentation mask through a dot product with the updated visual tokens.
 - A language grounding score through a dot product with the language tokens.
 This unified decoder supports both 2D and 3D grounding, associating text spans with visual regions. For question answering and captioning, the original Qwen2.5-VL language head performs next-token prediction over the contextualized scene–language representation.

#### 3.4. Training Objectives and Joint 2D-3D Training

Qwen-3D is supervised with three losses:
- **Mask loss:** Binary Cross-Entropy (BCE) and Dice loss for segmentation masks, matched to ground truth via Hungarian matching.
- **Text-span grounding loss:** Binary Cross-Entropy for predicted text spans matched to ground-truth text spans. Unmatched queries are supervised to predict low probability.
- **Text generation loss:** Token-level cross-entropy for question-answering and captioning tasks.

The model is trained jointly on a diverse mixture of 2D and 3D datasets. 3D datasets include referential grounding (SR3D, NR3D, ScanRefer), instance segmentation (ScanNet200, Matterport), and question answering (ScanQA, SQA3D). To prevent catastrophic forgetting of the base model's 2D capabilities, co-training also includes 2D datasets: referential grounding (RefCOCO family), instance segmentation (COCO), captioning/QA (LLaVA-Instruct-150k), and instruction fine-tuning (Alpaca). During training, 2D data can be lifted to 3D with a certain probability using reconstruction models to improve alignment between 2D and 3D representations.

The model introduces only about 50M trainable parameters, with the Qwen2.5-VL ViT backbone frozen, vision-language attention layers fine-tuned via LoRA, and the mask decoder trained from scratch. Inference typically takes approximately 2 seconds per scene for the model forward pass.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

Qwen-3D demonstrates notable advancements across a range of 3D vision-language tasks and maintains strong performance on 2D tasks.

#### 4.1. 3D Referential Grounding

- **State-of-the-Art among 3D LMMs:** On the ScanRefer benchmark, Qwen-3D models (3B and 7B variants) established a new state-of-the-art among 3D LMMs. The 3B model achieved 62.2% Acc@25 and 55.3% Acc@50, while the 7B model achieved 62.8% Acc@25 and 54.7% Acc@50. This represents an improvement of over 30% compared to 3D-LLM, 12% over LLaVA-3D, and 4% over Video-3D-LLM. The models also show strong performance on SR3D and NR3D, with no prior 3D LLM-based models reporting results on these benchmarks.
- **Closing Gap with Expert 3D Grounding Models:** Qwen-3D's performance on ScanRefer approaches that of the state-of-the-art expert model UniVLG (63.5% Acc@25, 56.4% Acc@50), substantially narrowing the performance difference between generalist LMMs and specialist 3D models.
- **Out-of-Domain Generalization:** On the Locate-3D (ScanNet++) benchmark, which represents an out-of-domain evaluation using iPhone LiDAR data, Qwen-3D significantly outperformed UniVLG (32.3% Acc@25, 24.6% Acc@50) and Video-3D-LLM (33.2% Acc@25, 27.6% Acc@50), achieving 55.7% Acc@25 and 43.4% Acc@50. This suggests enhanced robustness to domain shifts, attributed to its direct decoding from VLM features rather than reliance on pre-trained detectors. On the 3EED outdoor benchmark, Qwen-3D matched or surpassed UniVLG in certain splits (e.g., Drone and Quad).

#### 4.2. 3D Instance Segmentation

- On the ScanNet200 instance segmentation benchmark, Qwen-3D (3B) achieved 25.3% mAP and 41.5% mAP25. This performance significantly surpassed Grounded-3D-LLM (12.1% mAP, 16.8% mAP25) by 13% mAP and 25% mAP25, demonstrating its capability as a full scene detector and approaching the performance of language-prompted specialist models like UniVLG (27.9% mAP, 46.1% mAP25).

#### 4.3. 3D Visual Question Answering (VQA)

- On the ScanQA and SQA3D benchmarks, the Qwen-3D 3B variant outperformed the state-of-the-art expert model UniVLG on ScanQA and achieved comparable performance to LLaVA-3D-7B. The 7B variant of Qwen-3D demonstrated superior performance across all baselines on both ScanQA (31.5 EM@1, 99.0 CIDEr) and SQA3D (59.6 EM@1).

#### 4.4. 2D Vision-Language Tasks

- Qwen-3D was evaluated on 2D referential grounding datasets (RefCOCO, RefCOCO+, RefCOCOg) and the RealWorldQA dataset. It demonstrated strong performance, closely matching or slightly outperforming its base model, Qwen2.5-VL 3B, and significantly surpassing other 2D VLMs. For instance, on RefCOCO, Qwen-3D achieved 88.1% compared to Qwen2.5-VL-3B's 89.1% (with a mask decoder variant of Qwen2.5-VL-3B achieving 89.7%). This indicates that the joint 2D-3D training successfully preserves the strong 2D capabilities of the base model.

#### 4.5. Additional Analysis and Ablations

- **Geometry-Aware Decoding:** Ablation studies showed that the direct, full token interface between the VLM backbone and the object mask decoder (Qwen-3D: 53.5% Top1 Acc@0.5) significantly outperformed a variant using a special grounding token like `<REF>` (Qwen-3D <REF>: 39.7% Top1 Acc@0.5). This supports the hypothesis that the proposed decoding mechanism avoids an information bottleneck.
- **Geometry-Aware Attention (3D RoPE):** The aligned 3D Rotary Positional Embeddings (53.5% Top1 Acc@0.5) outperformed both 2D RoPE (53.2%) and a naive 3D RoPE implementation (49.9%), indicating the importance of uniform frequency distribution across 3D dimensions.
- **Causal Mask vs. Full Attention Mask:** Replacing the causal masking in Qwen2.5-VL's attention layers with a full attention mask for visual tokens led to a substantial performance increase (Full: 53.5% vs. Causal: 36.4% Top1 Acc@0.5).
- **VLM Tuning:** Fine-tuning the underlying Qwen VLM backbone via LoRA was critical for strong grounding performance (Finetune: 53.5% vs. Frozen: 37.0% Top1 Acc@0.5).
- **Robustness to Noise:** Qwen-3D demonstrated high robustness to both depth and camera pose noise, avoiding catastrophic failure and even outperforming UniVLG under extreme misalignments in some conditions.

These results indicate that Qwen-3D represents a methodological advancement in integrating 3D spatial understanding into generalist vision-language models, achieving competitive performance across diverse tasks and demonstrating improved generalization capabilities.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of Qwen-3D represents an advancement in the field of 3D vision-language models, addressing several identified limitations of prior approaches.

- **Bridging the Gap between LMMs and Specialist 3D Systems:** A key significance of Qwen-3D is its ability to substantially narrow the performance gap between general-purpose 3D LMMs and highly specialized 3D perception systems. By achieving competitive performance on 3D referential grounding and instance segmentation, Qwen-3D demonstrates that LMMs can extend their generalist reasoning capabilities to dense 3D perception tasks without a significant trade-off in accuracy compared to models specifically designed for these tasks. This suggests a potential for unified models to replace or complement specialized pipelines.
- **Enhanced Spatial Understanding for Long-Horizon Visual Streams:** The geometry-aware scene representation, particularly voxel-based token merging and 3D Rotary Positional Embeddings, enables efficient processing and spatial reasoning over long multi-view video streams. This capability is crucial for applications requiring extended context, such as autonomous navigation, robotics, and complex scene analysis in dynamic environments.
- **Improved 3D Grounding and Segmentation Mechanisms:** The novel geometry-aware decoding mechanism, which directly connects contextualized vision-language features to a mask-based segmentation decoder, is a significant contribution. This approach overcomes the "information bottleneck" of previous decoding strategies (e.g., text generation, proposal selection, single grounding tokens), allowing for more precise and dense geometric predictions. This could set a new standard for how LMMs interface with 3D grounding systems.
- **Generalist Capabilities with 2D Retention:** The joint 2D-3D training framework is impactful because it allows Qwen-3D to achieve state-of-the-art 3D performance while simultaneously preserving the strong 2D vision-language capabilities of its base Qwen2.5-VL model. This demonstrates the feasibility of creating truly generalist multimodal models that operate seamlessly across different visual modalities and tasks without significant catastrophic forgetting.
- **Robustness and Out-of-Domain Generalization:** The model's demonstrated robustness to depth and camera pose noise, coupled with its superior out-of-domain performance on benchmarks like Locate-3D, suggests a higher degree of generalization. This is particularly valuable for real-world applications where sensor data can be noisy or originate from diverse capture modalities.

**Potential Impact:**
The advancements embodied in Qwen-3D could significantly impact several domains:
- **Robotics and Embodied AI:** Improved 3D spatial understanding and grounding are fundamental for robots to interpret and interact with their environments, perform tasks specified by natural language instructions, and navigate complex spaces.
- **Augmented Reality (AR) and Virtual Reality (VR):** Better understanding of real-world 3D environments could enhance AR/VR applications, enabling more realistic scene reconstruction, object interaction, and language-guided content placement.
- **Content Creation and Analysis:** The ability to densely ground language in 3D scenes could facilitate automatic annotation, semantic search, and intelligent content generation for 3D models and environments.
- **Next-Generation Multimodal AI:** Qwen-3D's architecture and training paradigm provide a foundation for future multimodal models that integrate diverse sensory inputs and reasoning capabilities more cohesively, moving towards more holistic scene understanding.

**Limitations and Future Directions:**
The paper acknowledges limitations, including the assumption of largely static 3D coordinate systems, posing challenges for dynamic environments with non-rigid motion. Future work could focus on extending geometry-aware attention and grounding to dynamic 4D scene representations, integrating temporal motion models, or dynamic scene reconstruction. Another limitation is the focus on single-step grounding; future research could explore compositional reasoning over multiple grounded entities for complex instructions. Finally, Qwen-3D's reliance on externally estimated depth maps and camera poses suggests a future direction in jointly optimizing geometry estimation and language-guided scene understanding within a single end-to-end framework.
