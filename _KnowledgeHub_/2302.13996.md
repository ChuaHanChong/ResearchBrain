# Aligning Bag of Regions for Open-Vocabulary Object Detection

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Nanyang Technological University and SenseTime Research developed BARON (BAg of RegiONs), an approach for open-vocabulary object detection that explicitly aligns embeddings of grouped visual regions with a Vision-Language Model's compositional knowledge. This method yielded 34.0 box AP50 on novel categories on OV-COCO, an increase of 4.6 points, and surpassed prior state-of-the-art performance on OV-LVIS and cross-dataset transferability benchmarks.

## Problem

- Traditional object detectors are limited to recognizing a fixed, pre-defined set of categories, hindering their application in real-world scenarios with dynamic object concepts.
- Existing distillation-based open-vocabulary object detection (OVD) methods primarily align individual region embeddings with Vision-Language Model (VLM) features, under-exploiting the VLM's implicit understanding of compositional structures and object co-occurrences.
- This focus on individual regions limits the detector's ability to leverage scene-level context, which is beneficial for identifying novel objects that often appear in specific contextual relationships.

## Method

- **BARON** groups contextually interrelated visual regions into "bags" based on spatial proximity and size balance using a ==neighborhood sampling strategy==.
- For each bag, the detector's region features are projected into ==pseudo-words==, augmented with ==positional embeddings== representing spatial context (relative box centers and shapes), and then fed into a VLM's text encoder to form a unified "bag-of-regions embedding."
- These student bag-of-regions embeddings are aligned with corresponding teacher VLM image encoder features (derived from masked image crops of the bags) using a ==contrastive learning== objective, complementing individual region-level ==knowledge distillation==.

## Results

- Achieved **34.0 box AP50** on novel categories on the OV-COCO dataset, marking a **4.6 point** increase over the previous best reported result.
- Consistently outperformed state-of-the-art methods across various settings on the OV-LVIS dataset, achieving **22.6 mask AP** on rare categories using ensemble and learned prompts.
- Demonstrated improved generalization, with BARON-trained models exhibiting higher AP metrics when transferred to external datasets including Pascal VOC 2007, COCO validation, and Objects365 v2 validation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Large-scale Vision-Language Models (VLMs) implicitly capture compositional structures and object co-occurrence from image-text pre-training, which can be explicitly leveraged to enhance open-vocabulary object detection.
> - Encoding spatial information, such as relative positions and shapes of regions, into pseudo-word embeddings is crucial for the VLM's text encoder to effectively process grouped visual regions while maintaining their spatial context.
> - Aligning groups of contextually related regions (bags) with VLM knowledge, rather than solely individual regions, significantly improves the detector's scene-level understanding and performance on novel category detection.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Traditional object detectors are designed to recognize a fixed set of categories encountered during their training phase. This limitation restricts their applicability in real-world environments where an expansive and dynamic range of object concepts may exist. Open-Vocabulary Object Detection (OVD) addresses this challenge by enabling detectors to identify objects belonging to categories not seen during training.

A prominent approach within OVD is distillation-based learning, which leverages the extensive knowledge embedded in pre-trained Vision-Language Models (VLMs) such as CLIP. VLMs are trained on vast datasets of image-text pairs, learning to align visual and linguistic representations. Current distillation-based OVD methods typically extract features from VLMs and align these with embeddings of individual regions proposed by the object detector. While effective, this approach primarily focuses on individual object recognition.

The broader research landscape indicates that VLMs, through their large-scale pre-training on image-text pairs, implicitly capture complex compositional structures of semantic concepts within a scene. For example, previous work like MaskCLIP has shown that a VLM's image encoder can implicitly understand "stuff and things" in a complex scene, aligning pixel embeddings with corresponding text representations, despite not being explicitly trained for such dense prediction tasks. This suggests an untapped potential within VLMs for understanding relationships and co-occurrences of multiple concepts.

This paper positions itself by proposing to explicitly harness this implicit compositional knowledge. It argues that existing OVD methods under-exploit the compositional structure of multiple semantic concepts within a scene, focusing predominantly on individual region-level alignment. By introducing the concept of aligning "bag of regions" embeddings, this work aims to move beyond individual object recognition towards a more scene-level understanding, thereby improving the detection of novel objects which often appear in specific contexts. This represents an evolution in distillation-based OVD, seeking to more fully leverage the rich, context-aware representations learned by VLMs.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the observation that while pre-trained Vision-Language Models (VLMs) implicitly learn compositional structures and co-occurrences of semantic concepts from large-scale image-text datasets, existing open-vocabulary object detectors (OVDs) largely under-exploit this capability. Current distillation-based OVD approaches primarily focus on aligning individual region embeddings with VLM features, overlooking the potential benefits of understanding the interplay and context among multiple visual concepts in a scene.

The key objectives of this work are:

1. **To leverage compositional knowledge in VLMs for OVD:** The core objective is to move beyond individual region-level alignment and explicitly incorporate the compositional structure of semantic concepts, such as the co-existence of objects, into the OVD framework. The hypothesis is that VLMs, trained on image-text pairs where each image often contains a "bag of semantic concepts" described by its caption, have implicitly learned these compositional relationships.
2. **To develop a novel method for aligning "bag of regions" embeddings:** The research aims to design and implement a mechanism, termed BARON (BAg of RegiONs), that groups contextually interrelated visual regions into a "bag." This method involves projecting the embeddings of these grouped regions into a word embedding space (creating "pseudo words") and then processing them with a VLM's text encoder to obtain a unified "bag-of-regions embedding."
3. **To align student and teacher representations at the bag level:** The generated bag-of-regions embeddings from the detector (student) are intended to be aligned with corresponding features extracted by a frozen VLM's image encoder (teacher). This alignment is performed using a contrastive learning approach, encouraging the detector to learn representations that capture the co-occurrence of multiple visual concepts as understood by the VLM.
4. **To enhance open-vocabulary detection performance, particularly for novel categories:** By explicitly learning the co-existence of visual concepts, the method seeks to improve the detector's ability to understand the scene holistically, which is hypothesized to be beneficial for recognizing novel objects that might appear in specific contexts or alongside known objects.
5. **To demonstrate the method's flexibility and generalization:** The objectives include evaluating BARON's effectiveness across challenging OVD benchmarks (OV-COCO and OV-LVIS) and demonstrating its adaptability to different supervision signals, such as image captioning, beyond direct VLM image feature distillation. Furthermore, the work aims to show improved transferability to other datasets, indicating robust generalization.
6. **To understand and visualize the VLM's implicit compositional knowledge:** A secondary motivation involves gaining insights into how VLMs capture object co-occurrence and how this knowledge is transferred to the detector, through qualitative analysis and visualizations.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, BARON (BAg of RegiONs), extends distillation-based open-vocabulary object detection by explicitly aligning embeddings of grouped regions, rather than just individual regions. The approach is built upon a standard Faster R-CNN detector and leverages a pre-trained Vision-Language Model (VLM) for knowledge distillation.

### 4. Preliminaries and Base Detector Modification

BARON instantiates its ideas on Faster R-CNN. To enable open-vocabulary detection, the standard classification head of Faster R-CNN is replaced with a linear layer. This layer projects the detector's region features into a "pseudo word" embedding space. These pseudo words are then fed into the text encoder of a frozen VLM (e.g., CLIP) to produce category-agnostic object representations. Final classification is achieved by computing cosine similarity between these representations and the text embeddings of predefined categories (generated by the VLM's text encoder using prompt templates). The model is trained on base categories using conventional regression and classification losses, with additional distillation for novel categories.

### 5. Forming Bag of Regions

The core innovation is to group contextually interrelated regions into "bags." This strategy is designed to ensure that the VLM's image encoder effectively represents the combined concepts. Two key properties guide the formation of bags:
- **Proximity:** Regions within a bag should be spatially close to minimize redundant image content when an encompassing crop is formed.
- **Size Balance:** Regions should have similar sizes to prevent larger regions from dominating the VLM's representation of the bag.

A **neighborhood sampling strategy** is employed:
1. **Candidate Generation:** For each region proposal generated by the RPN, eight surrounding candidate boxes are generated. These candidates are given the same shape as the original region proposal.
2. **Filtering:** Candidates exceeding image boundaries beyond a certain proportion (e.g., 2/3 of their area) are discarded.
3. **Sampling:** The remaining candidates are independently sampled to form a bag.
4. **Aspect Ratio Adjustment:** To prevent bags from forming extreme aspect ratios, the sampling probability for left/right or upper/bottom candidates is adjusted based on the region proposal's height-to-width ratio.
5. **Multiple Bags:** Multiple groups (G) are sampled for each region proposal to enrich the bag-of-regions representations.

### 6. Representing Bag of Regions

Embeddings for these bags are generated from both the student detector and the teacher VLM.
- **Student Bag-of-Regions Embedding ($f^t_i$):**
 - The pseudo words ($w^i_j$) corresponding to the regions ($b^i_j$) in a bag are first obtained from the linear projection layer.
 - **Spatial Information Encoding:** Crucially, positional embeddings ($p^i_j$) representing the relative box center positions and box shapes of each region are generated and added to their respective pseudo words. This ensures spatial context is retained.
 - These augmented pseudo words ($w^i_j + p^i_j$) are then concatenated and fed into the VLM's text encoder ($T$) to produce the student bag-of-regions embedding.
- **Teacher Bag-of-Regions Embedding ($f^v_i$):**
 - An image crop that tightly encloses all regions within a bag is created.
 - This image crop is fed into the VLM's image encoder ($V$).
 - To focus the teacher on the regions, any image content outside the grouped regions within the crop is masked out in the attention layers of the image encoder.

### 7. Aligning Bag of Regions

A **contrastive learning approach** (InfoNCE loss) is used to align the student and teacher bag-of-regions embeddings.
- The loss ($L_{bag}$) pulls positive pairs (matching student and teacher embeddings for the same bag) closer while pushing away negative pairs (student embedding for one bag, teacher embedding for another).
- To provide a sufficient number of negative pairs, especially given a small number of bags per image, two queues are maintained to store embeddings from previous iterations.

### 8. Aligning Individual Regions (Complementary)

In addition to bag-level alignment, the method also incorporates individual-level distillation.
- Student individual embeddings are derived by averaging the pseudo-word embeddings of a single region from the text encoder's last attention layer.
- Teacher individual embeddings are obtained by applying RoiAlign on the feature map of the VLM's image encoder's last attention layer for each region.
- An InfoNCE loss ($L_{individual}$) is applied to these individual region embeddings, similar to existing methods. This is considered complementary to the bag-level alignment.

### 9. Caption Supervision

BARON is designed to be general and can adapt to caption supervision. In this setting:
- The teacher image embedding ($f^v$) is replaced by an embedding obtained by feeding the image's captions to the VLM's text encoder ($T$).
- Region groups are formed by randomly sampling RPN proposals.
- A soft cross-entropy loss is used to align the student bag-of-regions embedding to multiple caption embeddings, following practices in UniCL.
- Individual region alignment is omitted because direct correspondence between individual region pseudo words and words in a caption (grounding) is typically unavailable in caption supervision.

### 10. Implementation Details

- **Backbone:** ResNet50-FPN for CLIP distillation, ResNet50-C4 for caption supervision.
- **Pre-training:** Backbone weights initialized with SOCO.
- **VLM:** CLIP model based on ViT-B/32.
- **Hyperparameters:** Specific temperature values ($\tau_{cls}, \tau_{bag}, \tau_{individual}$) are tuned for OV-COCO and OV-LVIS.
- **Pseudo Words:** A single linear layer maps region features to pseudo words, with an optimal number of 6 pseudo words per region.
- **Random Word Dropout:** Applied to pseudo words (probability 0.5) to mitigate overfitting.
- **Suppression Alleviation:** Strategies include detaching the objectness prediction branch, caching sampled region proposals, and using different VLM output layers for classification and alignment to reduce competition between losses.

### 11. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several key findings across quantitative benchmarks, ablation studies, and qualitative analyses.

### 12. Benchmark Results

- **OV-COCO Dataset:**
 - BARON, when distilling knowledge from CLIP with a Faster R-CNN detector, achieved a box AP$_{50}$ of 34.0 on novel categories. This represents a 4.6 point increase over the previously reported best result (OV-DETR), despite OV-DETR using a stronger Deformable DETR backbone.
 - When using caption supervision alone, BARON achieved 33.1 box AP$_{50}$ on novel categories, outperforming previous approaches like VLDet.
 - With a combination of CLIP image features, COCO captions, and MAVL proposals, BARON achieved 42.7 box AP$_{50}$ on novel categories, significantly surpassing Rasheed et al.. (Table 1)
- **OV-LVIS Dataset:**
 - BARON consistently outperformed state-of-the-art methods across various settings. For instance, without using ensemble or learned prompts, BARON achieved 18.0 mask AP on rare categories, which was higher than ViLD*.
 - With ensemble and learned prompts, BARON achieved 22.6 mask AP on rare categories, outperforming DetPro. (Table 2)
- **Transferability to Other Datasets:**
 - Models trained on OV-LVIS by BARON exhibited improved generalization ability when transferred to Pascal VOC 2007, COCO validation, and Objects365 v2 validation datasets, showing higher AP metrics across the board compared to ViLD* and DetPro. (Table 3)

### 13. Ablation Studies

- **Effectiveness of Aligning Bag of Regions:**
 - A baseline using only individual-level distillation ($L_{individual}$) achieved 25.7 AP$_{50}$ on novel categories.
 - Replacing this with bag-of-regions alignment ($L_{bag}$) but without positional embeddings (PE) yielded similar performance (25.7 AP$_{50}$), indicating that simple concatenation of pseudo words is insufficient.
 - The inclusion of positional embeddings with $L_{bag}$ dramatically increased novel category performance to 32.8 AP$_{50}$ (a 7.1 point gain), highlighting the crucial role of spatial information.
 - Combining both individual-level and bag-of-regions alignment with positional embeddings resulted in the best performance of 34.0 AP$_{50}$, demonstrating their complementary nature. (Table 4)
- **Sampling Strategies for Bags of Regions:**
 - Neighborhood sampling (reduced version) achieved 32.2 AP$_{50}$ on novel categories.
 - This significantly outperformed grid sampling (25.4 AP$_{50}$) and random sampling (27.3 AP$_{50}$), supporting the rationale that grouping spatially close and similarly sized regions enhances VLM's representation. (Table 5)
- **Box Overlap between Regions in a Bag:**
 - An optimal overlap (IOF) of 0.1 between sampled boxes led to the best performance (34.0 AP$_{50}$), suggesting a balance between semantic continuity and diverse content coverage. Negative overlap (interval) or excessive overlap led to reduced performance. (Table 6)
- **Sampling Probability ($p_b$) and Number of Bags Per Proposal:**
 - An optimal sampling probability $p_b$ of 0.3 for candidate boxes yielded the best result (34.0 AP$_{50}$). (Table 7)
 - Sampling 3 bags of regions per proposal was found to be optimal (34.0 AP$_{50}$). (Table 8)
- **Number of Pseudo Words Per Region:**
 - Performance on novel categories increased as the number of pseudo words per region was increased from 2 to 6, reaching 34.0 AP$_{50}$. Beyond 6 (e.g., 8 pseudo words), performance slightly decreased, indicating an optimal representation length. (Table 9)

### 14. Further Analysis (Qualitative)

- **Co-occurrence of Objects in VLMs:** Incremental addition of object categories to text descriptions of images resulted in increased similarity scores between image and text embeddings from CLIP. This finding supports the premise that large-scale VLMs implicitly capture the co-occurrence of multiple concepts and even simple relationships. (Figure 6)
- **Visualization of Predictions:** Grad-CAM++ visualizations demonstrated that the BARON-trained model generated stronger, more localized, and complete feature map responses for novel categories compared to a baseline with only individual-level distillation. It also showed that the model could capture relationships between objects of imbalanced sizes or large distances, generalizing beyond the specific neighborhood sampling strategy used in training. (Figure 5)

### 15. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of open-vocabulary object detection (OVD) and the broader understanding of vision-language models (VLMs) in several significant ways.

### 16. Novelty in OVD Methodology

The work introduces a novel paradigm for knowledge distillation in OVD by moving beyond individual region-level alignment to explicitly align "bag of regions" embeddings. This represents a methodological advancement, directly addressing the under-exploitation of compositional structures implicitly learned by VLMs. By treating a group of contextually related regions as a "sentence" of visual concepts, the method effectively leverages the VLM's text encoder to understand scene-level information.

### 17. Enhanced Performance and Generalization

BARON achieves new state-of-the-art results on challenging OVD benchmarks, including OV-COCO and OV-LVIS, particularly for novel categories. The significant performance gains, such as a 4.6 box AP$_{50}$ increase on OV-COCO novel categories, demonstrate its practical effectiveness. Furthermore, the improved transferability to other datasets like Pascal VOC, COCO validation, and Objects365 indicates a robust generalization ability, making the trained detectors more applicable in diverse real-world scenarios.

### 18. Deeper Understanding of VLM Capabilities

The research provides empirical evidence that large-scale VLMs, without explicit training for object co-occurrence or compositional scene understanding, implicitly learn these complex relationships from image-text pairs. The qualitative analysis showing increased image-text similarity with more complete text descriptions and more focused Grad-CAM++ responses for novel objects highlights the VLM's latent ability to represent the compositional structure of multiple concepts. This insight encourages further exploration into how VLMs acquire and represent such sophisticated knowledge.

### 19. Technical Contributions to Feature Representation

The introduction of a neighborhood sampling strategy ensures that grouped regions are both spatially proximate and of similar scale, optimizing the input for the VLM's image encoder. Critically, the method of encoding spatial information (relative box centers and shapes) into positional embeddings, which are then added to projected pseudo words before being fed to the text encoder, is a key technical contribution. This allows the text encoder to process visual regions while maintaining their spatial context, bridging the gap between vision and language representation in a novel way.

### 20. Foundations for Future Research

The paper explicitly acknowledges that the compositional structure explored primarily pertains to object co-occurrence (analogous to a "bag of words"). This opens avenues for future research to investigate more complex linguistic compositional structures (e.g., semantic relationships, actions, attributes) and how VLMs might capture or be endowed with such human-like compositional representations. Further exploration could lead to more nuanced scene understanding and even greater generalization in object detection and related vision-language tasks.
