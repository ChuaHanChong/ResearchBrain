# Dexterous Manipulation Policies from RGB Human Videos via 3D Hand-Object Trajectory Reconstruction

> [!summary] Summary
> The VIDEOMANIP framework learns dexterous multi-finger robot manipulation policies by reconstructing 3D hand-object trajectories from readily available RGB human videos. This approach achieved an average grasping success rate of 63.75% across 20 diverse objects in simulation and a 62.86% average success rate on seven real-world manipulation tasks with a LEAP Hand.

## Problem

- Dexterous multi-finger robot manipulation is challenging due to high-dimensional action spaces and difficulty in acquiring large-scale, high-quality training data.
- Traditional data collection methods rely on specialized hardware (e.g., teleoperation, motion capture) or direct robot demonstrations, which are costly, time-consuming, and limit data scalability and diversity.
- Existing human-to-robot approaches often suffer from reconstruction inaccuracies, lack physical plausibility, or produce data insufficient for robust policy learning without further robot-specific fine-tuning.

## Method

- A device-free framework, **VIDEOMANIP**, reconstructs ==3D object meshes==, human hand poses, and ==metric depth== from monocular RGB human videos, then retargets human motions to robot hands.
- Reconstructed grasps are refined using ==contact optimization== with ==interaction-centric grasp modeling== to ensure physically plausible and stable robot-object interactions.
- A ==demonstration synthesis== strategy generates multiple, varied training trajectories from a single reconstructed video, randomizing object positions and applying transformations to enhance policy generalization.

## Results

- Achieved an average grasping success rate of **63.75%** across **20 diverse objects** in the IsaacGym simulator, with contact optimization improving success from **30.7% to 63.75%**.
- Demonstrated an average success rate of **62.86%** across **seven real-world manipulation tasks** using a **LEAP Hand**, outperforming retargeting-based baselines that frequently failed or produced erroneous motions.
- Gravity-alignment calibration was crucial for in-the-wild videos, preventing task failures, while increasing synthesized demonstrations for manipulation improved success rates from **1/15 to 13/15** for a pour tea task.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicit 3D hand-object trajectory reconstruction from RGB videos provides more effective and physically grounded supervision for dexterous robot policies compared to pixel-level video generation approaches.
> - Contact optimization is critical for correcting inherent inaccuracies in 3D reconstruction, significantly improving the physical plausibility and success rate of robot grasps.
> - Synthesizing diverse training trajectories from a single human video demonstration through spatial randomization is effective for enabling robust policies that generalize to varied object poses and locations.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation, particularly for multi-finger dexterous hands, faces significant challenges stemming from the high-dimensional action spaces involved and the difficulty in acquiring large-scale, high-quality training data. Traditional approaches for gathering robot demonstrations largely depend on human teleoperation, which often necessitates specialized hardware such as wearable devices, multi-camera motion capture systems, or other dedicated sensing equipment. These methods typically require controlled capture environments and direct human involvement, which inherently limits their scalability and the diversity of data that can be collected.

Prior research has explored leveraging human-generated data for manipulation learning. Some approaches use egocentric human videos, but these have often been restricted to coarser manipulation tasks or gripper-based robots, or they require robot demonstrations for fine-tuning after initial training on in-the-wild datasets. Other methods might rely on pre-scanned object models or focus on extracting intermediate representations like object affordances or hand poses without demonstrating effective direct application for dexterous robotic hand manipulation or imitation learning. Furthermore, existing human-object interaction reconstruction techniques from images or videos, while advancing, sometimes suffer from reconstruction inaccuracies, long processing times, or produce data that is insufficient for robust policy learning without further robot demonstrations or carefully designed reward functions.

This work addresses these limitations by proposing a device-free framework that aims to learn dexterous multi-finger manipulation directly from readily available RGB human videos, including those recorded "in-the-wild." This approach seeks to overcome the scalability barriers and hardware dependencies of conventional methods by leveraging computer vision advancements to reconstruct detailed 3D hand-object trajectories, subsequently retargeting them for robot policy training. The objective is to enable scalable robot learning from ubiquitous video data, without requiring specialized sensing equipment, robot-specific data collection, or pre-scanned object models, thus differentiating itself from methods that rely on either explicit robot demonstrations or constrained data acquisition setups.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to ascertain the extent to which dexterous robot manipulation can be effectively learned using only human video supervision. This overarching goal is motivated by the persistent challenges in multi-finger robotic manipulation, specifically the high-dimensional nature of its action space and the labor-intensive process of acquiring sufficient training data through conventional means.

The specific key objectives driving this work are:

1. **Develop a device-free framework:** To create a system, named VIDEOMANIP, that can learn dexterous multi-finger manipulation policies directly from RGB human videos without requiring specialized hardware such as wearable devices, external sensors, or even robot demonstrations for post-training. This aims to significantly enhance the scalability of data acquisition.
2. **Reconstruct explicit 3D robot-object trajectories:** To leverage recent advancements in computer vision to estimate human hand poses, object meshes, and object scales from monocular RGB videos. The framework then retargets these reconstructed human motions to robot hands, generating explicit 3D trajectories suitable for robot learning. This includes handling videos from both controlled "in-scene" environments and "in-the-wild" settings without pre-existing camera-robot calibration.
3. **Ensure physically plausible robot interactions:** To address the inherent inaccuracies that can arise from 3D reconstruction by introducing a hand-object contact optimization process. This process uses interaction-centric grasp modeling to refine the reconstructed grasps, thereby encouraging physically feasible and stable robot-object interactions during policy training.
4. **Enable generalizable policy learning from limited data:** To overcome the challenge of learning robust manipulation policies from a single human video demonstration, which typically lacks sufficient diversity. This objective is achieved by implementing a demonstration synthesis strategy that generates multiple, varied training trajectories from a single reconstructed video.

The motivation for these objectives stems from several factors:
- **Scalability limitations of current methods:** Existing robot data collection pipelines often involve specialized hardware or extensive human teleoperation, which are costly, time-consuming, and do not scale to the vast amount of diverse manipulation data required for generalizable policies.
- **Ubiquity of RGB videos:** Internet-scale availability of RGB videos of human-object interactions presents a potentially rich, low-cost data source. However, extracting actionable robot supervision from such data is challenging due to the lack of robot actions and precise 3D information.
- **Deficiencies in existing reconstruction-to-robot approaches:** While some methods reconstruct human hand poses or object meshes, they often struggle with accuracy, physical plausibility (e.g., interpenetration), or provide data that is insufficient for direct imitation learning without further robot-specific fine-tuning.
- **Need for robust and generalizable policies:** A single human demonstration, even if perfectly reconstructed, provides limited variations in object pose, hand approach, or environmental conditions, which can lead to policies that do not generalize well to novel scenarios.

By addressing these points, the research seeks to provide a more scalable, device-free, and robust paradigm for learning dexterous robotic manipulation, directly from human observation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The VIDEOMANIP framework is designed to learn dexterous grasping and manipulation policies from RGB human videos without requiring specialized hardware, external sensors, or robot demonstrations. The approach proceeds in two main stages: 3D hand-object trajectory reconstruction from video, followed by dexterous grasp and manipulation policy learning.

**Assumptions:**
The framework operates under specific assumptions:
- Input videos are egocentric and captured with a static camera, including both in-scene and in-the-wild recordings. Only one video is available per task or object.
- No prior access to pre-scanned object meshes, object size information, depth measurements, or camera intrinsics.
- No robot data or wearable/external sensing devices are used.

**A. 3D Hand-Object Trajectory Reconstruction from Video**
Given an RGB video of a human interacting with objects, the system reconstructs the object mesh (O) and its 6D pose, recovers the human hand pose, and then retargets this motion to a robot hand.

1. **Metric Depth and Camera Intrinsics Estimation:** The process begins by employing MoGe-2 to estimate metric depth maps and camera intrinsics for each video frame. This step establishes a consistent, joint, and metric 3D coordinate frame for subsequent reconstructions, ensuring physical consistency between the hand and object.

2. **Object Mesh Reconstruction and Pose Estimation:**
 - **Object Masking:** The Segment Anything Model 2 (SAM 2) identifies and masks the manipulated objects within the video frames.
 - **Image-to-Mesh Generation:** Masked object-centric images are fed into MeshyAI to generate a complete 3D object mesh (O). These meshes capture shape and appearance but initially lack accurate metric scale.
 - **Two-stage Scale Estimation:**
 - **Coarse Estimation:** The GPT-4.1 language model provides an initial coarse estimate of the object's physical dimensions, which is used to rescale the mesh.
 - **Refinement:** FoundationPose is then used to refine the object scale. It evaluates multiple candidate scalings (e.g., 0.5× to 2×) by minimizing the rendering error between the scaled mesh and the SAM 2 object mask, leveraging the previously estimated metric depth information.

3. **Human Hand Mesh Estimation and Robot Hand Retargeting:**
 - **Hand Mesh Recovery:** The HaMeR model reconstructs the human hand mesh (H) for all video frames, representing it with pose (θ) and shape (β) parameters.
 - **Depth Alignment:** To ensure the reconstructed hand mesh is spatially consistent with the object, its depth is corrected by averaging the metric depths at 2D keypoints predicted by HaMeR, using the same MoGe-2 metric depth maps as for the object.
 - **Robot Hand Retargeting:** The aligned human hand poses are then retargeted to the target robot hand configuration (q), which includes the wrist pose and finger joint angles. This is achieved through an optimization process that minimizes the error between selected robot link keypoints and corresponding human hand joints, as described in.

4. **In-the-Wild Videos Calibration:**
 - For videos captured outside a robot setup without extrinsic camera-robot calibration, actions are initially expressed in the camera frame, misaligned with the physical world frame.
 - **Gravity Alignment:** GeoCalib, a single-image camera calibration method, is applied to the first video frame to estimate the gravity direction in the camera frame. This yields a rotation matrix (grav\_R\_cam) that aligns gravity with the negative z-axis. This rotation is then applied to all reconstructed meshes (R, O) and robot configurations (q), producing gravity-aligned trajectories that are suitable for policy training and can be aligned with in-scene recordings.

**B. Dexterous Grasp and Manipulation Learning**
This stage focuses on making the reconstructed robot data suitable for robust policy learning.

1. **Grasp and Manipulation Skill Decomposition:** The reconstructed trajectory is divided into a grasping stage (from hand approach to stable grasp) and a manipulation stage (object operation after grasping).

2. **Contact Optimization and Interaction-Centric Grasp Modeling:**
 - **Contact Optimization:** During the grasping stage, pre-trained ContactOpt refines the reconstructed grasps. It predicts desirable contact regions on the hand and object meshes and optimizes human hand pose parameters (h) to align current contacts with these targets using differentiable contact maps. The optimized human hand poses are then retargeted to robot configurations (q\_opt\_t) to serve as improved grasp demonstrations.
 - **Interaction-Centric Grasp Modeling:** The DRO model is used to learn a robust grasp model from the optimized robot mesh (R) and object mesh (O). DRO predicts dense point-to-point distances between robot and object point clouds, capturing interaction patterns. A training loss minimizes the difference between predicted and ground-truth distance matrices. This model is then used to compute the optimal grasp configuration (q\_grasp) for a given object.

3. **Manipulation Demonstration Synthesis and Training:**
 - **Demonstration Synthesis:** To address the limited diversity from a single video, DemoGen is employed. It synthesizes additional manipulation demonstrations by spatially randomizing object positions and applying consistent SE(3) transformations to both object point clouds and robot trajectories. This preserves hand-object contact and fine-grained finger motions, creating diverse training data from a single source video.
 - **Manipulation Policy Learning:** The 3D diffusion-based policy (DP3) is adopted for manipulation. It takes as input the robot hand point clouds and proprioceptive state at the grasp pose (P\_R\_grasp, q\_grasp), along with the object point clouds (P\_O). The policy learns to output actions as predicted changes in the robot configuration (∆q) and is executed in a closed-loop manner. During execution, due to potential occlusion of the grasped object by the robot hand, the object point cloud is updated by assuming a fixed hand-object relative pose established at the grasp.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents quantitative and qualitative results for two categories of experiments: object grasping in simulation and object manipulation in the real world.

**A. Grasping Experiments**
Twenty human videos, one per object, were collected and used to train models for grasping diverse objects with an 18-DoF Inspire robot hand in the IsaacGym simulator. Grasps were evaluated over 100 trials, considered successful if the object's displacement under 0.5 times its mass force disturbance remained within 3 cm.

- **Generalization Across Object Categories (Q1):** The grasping model, trained on video-reconstructed and optimized grasp data, achieved an average success rate of 82.13% across 15 successfully grasped objects and 63.75% across all 20 objects. The predicted grasps demonstrated fidelity to human grasps, such as the finger placement on a soap dispenser.
- **Impact of Grasp Optimization:** An ablation study showed that training the DRO grasping model with unoptimized grasps resulted in only a 30.7% success rate. In contrast, using ContactOpt-optimized grasps significantly improved the success rate to 63.75%. Unoptimized grasps frequently suffered from object penetration or failed to establish proper contact, whereas optimized grasps achieved more accurate hand-object interactions.
- **Effect of Additional Videos for Challenging Objects (Q2):** Five objects initially exhibited lower success rates due to issues like object pose estimation errors (e.g., pan handle occlusion) or a lack of force closure (e.g., glasses case). By collecting two additional human videos per failed object and augmenting the training dataset, the success rate for these five objects improved from 8.6% to 40.8%. The overall success rate across all objects increased from 63.75% to 70.25%. This indicated that increased video diversity could enhance model robustness, although one object (Hand Bag) did not show improvement due to grasp sensitivity to disturbances.

**B. Manipulation Experiments**
Seven manipulation tasks were evaluated using a four-finger LEAP Hand mounted on a 7-DoF xArm in the real world, each trained from a single human demonstration video. Three tasks utilized in-scene videos (Pour Tea, Close Drawer, Pick&Place Can), and four used in-the-wild videos with gravity-based calibration (Pour Tea, Hang Hat, Move Jenga Box, Screw Bulb). A DP3 manipulation policy was trained for each task, using 1000 DemoGen-synthesized trajectories from the single reconstructed source trajectory.

- **Performance with Single In-Scene/In-the-Wild Video (Q3):** VIDEOMANIP achieved an average success rate of 62.86% across all seven tasks. The reconstructed trajectories closely aligned with the human videos, providing spatiotemporal supervision.
 - **Comparison to Baselines:**
 - **LVP (Large Video Planner) and LVP(-H):** These retargeting-based baselines performed significantly worse, often failing to generate feasible grasps or exhibiting motion failures (e.g., missing rotational motions for screwing, object not placed correctly). LVP(-H), which lacked human hand cues in the initial observation, frequently produced erroneous future frames, hallucinating wrong robots, failing tasks, or confusing hands. This indicated the benefit of VIDEOMANIP's explicit 3D geometry reconstruction over pixel-level video generation.
 - **π\_0.5:** This vision-language-action model showed limited success, attributed to its primary training on parallel-gripper datasets and insufficient fine-tuning demonstrations for dexterous manipulation with the LEAP Hand.
- **Impact of In-the-Wild Calibration and Synthesized Demonstrations (Q4):**
 - **In-the-Wild Calibration:** For the Pour Tea task, there was no significant performance difference between in-scene and in-the-wild videos when gravity-alignment calibration was applied. However, without gravity alignment, the success rate for the in-the-wild Pour Tea task dropped to 0%, highlighting the critical role of this calibration for real-world applicability.
 - **DemoGen Synthesized Trajectories:** An ablation on the in-scene Pour Tea task demonstrated that increasing the number of DemoGen-synthesized trajectories (from 1 to 1000) for varied target object locations improved the success rate from 1/15 to 13/15. This confirms that DemoGen effectively enables DP3 to learn policies that generalize across different target object positions.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a framework, VIDEOMANIP, that addresses significant challenges in learning dexterous multi-finger robotic manipulation. Its primary significance lies in demonstrating that robust grasping and manipulation policies can be learned directly from RGB human videos, including those recorded in uncontrolled "in-the-wild" settings, without requiring specialized sensing devices, robot-specific demonstrations, or pre-scanned object models.

The potential impact of this work is multifaceted:
- **Enhanced Scalability and Accessibility:** By leveraging ubiquitous RGB videos, the framework offers a scalable and cost-effective alternative to traditional data collection methods. This could democratize access to advanced robot learning paradigms, reducing the financial and logistical barriers for developing complex dexterous manipulation skills.
- **Physically Grounded Learning:** The explicit reconstruction of 3D hand-object trajectories provides physically meaningful supervision, which is shown to be more reliable and effective for training robot policies compared to approaches based on generative video models. This grounding helps in learning more robust and physically plausible robot actions.
- **Improved Generalization:** The integration of contact optimization and a demonstration synthesis strategy (DemoGen) from a single human video trajectory substantially improves the physical validity of reconstructed grasps and the generalizability of learned manipulation policies to varied object poses and locations. This moves towards enabling robots to perform tasks in diverse, unconstrained environments.
- **Broader Application of Human Data:** The ability to extract actionable robot policies from common human video content opens avenues for utilizing the vast and ever-growing pool of internet videos as a rich source of training data for a wide array of robotic tasks.

Despite these advancements, the authors acknowledge limitations, such as the potential for compounding errors from relying on multiple 3D vision models and the current assumption of static camera setups. Future work includes incorporating trajectory verification to filter errors, extending the approach to dynamic camera settings, and exploring alternative trajectory synthesis modalities to address object occlusion issues during manipulation. Addressing these limitations could further broaden the applicability and robustness of this device-free, video-only robot learning paradigm, potentially accelerating the development of more intelligent and adaptable robotic systems.
