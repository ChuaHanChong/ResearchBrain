# Dexterous Manipulation Policies from RGB Human Videos via 3D Hand-Object Trajectory Reconstruction

> [!summary] Summary
> The VIDEOMANIP framework learns dexterous multi-finger robot manipulation policies by reconstructing 3D hand-object trajectories from readily available RGB human videos. This approach achieved an average grasping success rate of 63.75% across 20 diverse objects in simulation and a 62.86% average success rate on seven real-world manipulation tasks with a LEAP Hand.

## Problem

- Dexterous multi-finger robot manipulation is challenging due to high-dimensional action spaces and difficulty in acquiring large-scale, high-quality training data.
- Traditional data collection methods rely on specialized hardware (e.g., teleoperation, motion capture) or direct robot demonstrations, which are costly, time-consuming, and limit data scalability and diversity.
- Existing human-to-robot approaches often suffer from reconstruction inaccuracies, lack physical plausibility, or produce data insufficient for robust policy learning without further robot-specific fine-tuning.

## Method

- A device-free framework, **VIDEOMANIP**, reconstructs ==3D object meshes==, human hand poses, and ==metric depth== from monocular RGB human videos, then retargets human motions to robot hands.
- Reconstructed grasps are refined using ==contact optimization== with ==interaction-centric grasp modeling== to ensure physically plausible and stable robot-object interactions.
- A ==demonstration synthesis== strategy generates multiple, varied training trajectories from a single reconstructed video, randomizing object positions and applying transformations to enhance policy generalization.

## Results

- Achieved an average grasping success rate of **63.75%** across **20 diverse objects** in the IsaacGym simulator, with contact optimization improving success from **30.7% to 63.75%**.
- Demonstrated an average success rate of **62.86%** across **seven real-world manipulation tasks** using a **LEAP Hand**, outperforming retargeting-based baselines that frequently failed or produced erroneous motions.
- Gravity-alignment calibration was crucial for in-the-wild videos, preventing task failures, while increasing synthesized demonstrations for manipulation improved success rates from **1/15 to 13/15** for a pour tea task.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicit 3D hand-object trajectory reconstruction from RGB videos provides more effective and physically grounded supervision for dexterous robot policies compared to pixel-level video generation approaches.
> - Contact optimization is critical for correcting inherent inaccuracies in 3D reconstruction, significantly improving the physical plausibility and success rate of robot grasps.
> - Synthesizing diverse training trajectories from a single human video demonstration through spatial randomization is effective for enabling robust policies that generalize to varied object poses and locations.
