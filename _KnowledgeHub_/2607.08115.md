# RadLoc: Radar-based 3-DoF Global Localization via Fast, Robust, and Lightweight Spatial Descriptor Across Diverse Environmental Scenarios

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Inha University developed RadLoc, an end-to-end pipeline for 3-DoF global localization using spinning radar sensors, featuring fast pre-processing, a compact descriptor, and hierarchical retrieval. The system achieved the fastest runtime and smallest descriptor size among state-of-the-art methods, while maintaining top-tier place recognition recall and competitive 3-DoF pose estimation accuracy across diverse weather and environmental conditions.

## Problem

- Existing vision and LiDAR-based localization systems struggle in adverse weather conditions due to sensor attenuation and noise.
- Prior radar-based localization methods often lack computational efficiency, require extensive training data, or provide only limited 1-DoF pose estimation.
- Many current approaches do not jointly consider descriptor design, retrieval efficiency, and full 3-DoF pose estimation within a unified, lightweight, and deployable framework.

## Method

- **RadLoc** integrates a lightweight ==1D Cell-Averaging Constant False Alarm Rate (CA-CFAR)== filter for efficient radar image pre-processing to suppress noise.
- It generates a compact, inherently rotation-invariant ==1D spatial descriptor== by aggregating intensities across the azimuth dimension and pooling over range segments.
- A ==hierarchical coarse-to-fine== search scheme and 3-DoF pose estimation via sequential ==phase correlation== in log-polar and Cartesian domains are employed for efficient retrieval and comprehensive localization.

## Results

- RadLoc achieved the fastest total runtime and generated the smallest descriptor size among all compared state-of-the-art methods, demonstrating superior scalability for large-scale databases.
- It consistently obtained top-tier Recall@1 performance across **15** diverse sequences, outperforming competitors in multi-session scenarios and maintaining robustness under adverse weather conditions.
- The system provided competitive 3-DoF pose estimation accuracy with a high success rate (e.g., over **99%**) and significantly reduced computational overhead (approximately **90% faster**) compared to feature-based GICP.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The inherent characteristics of spinning radar data, particularly near-range dominance and azimuthal symmetry, can be leveraged to create compact and rotation-invariant spatial descriptors.
> - A hierarchical retrieval strategy based on the decreasing spatial information density with radar range significantly reduces search time without compromising recognition performance.
> - Decomposing 3-DoF pose estimation into sequential rotation and translation steps using phase correlation in appropriate coordinate systems (log-polar for rotation, Cartesian for translation) enhances stability and accuracy.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Global localization, encompassing place recognition and relative pose estimation, constitutes a fundamental capability for autonomous mobile robots. This capability is critical for applications such as Simultaneous Localization and Mapping (SLAM) and multi-session/multi-robot mapping. While vision- and LiDAR-based localization methods are widely studied, they exhibit limitations under adverse weather conditions (e.g., rain, fog, snow, dust) due to the attenuation of their shorter wavelengths.

Radar sensors offer an alternative by leveraging longer wavelengths, enabling penetration through challenging environmental obscurants and thereby providing robustness in diverse weather conditions. Consequently, radar-based global localization has been an active area of research. Prior work in this domain has primarily focused on enhancing individual components, such as place recognition algorithms or pose estimation techniques, often without considering the entire localization pipeline's computational efficiency or seamless integration.

Recent learning-based approaches, exemplified by Kidnapped Radar and SHeRLoc, have demonstrated strong recognition performance. However, these methods typically necessitate extensive training data and significant computational resources, which can restrict their practical deployability in novel or rapidly changing environments. Handcrafted descriptor-based methods, including Radar Scan Context (RSC), RaPlace, RadVLAD, RadFFTVLAD, and ReFeree, have also advanced the field. Nevertheless, these often face challenges related to robustness, efficiency, or are limited in the scope of pose estimation, frequently providing only 1-DoF (yaw) estimation rather than full 3-DoF (translation and yaw). For instance, ReFeree, while effective, relies on computationally intensive feature extraction during pre-processing.

The present work, RadLoc, addresses these identified gaps by adopting a holistic perspective on radar sensor-based global localization. It explicitly aims to jointly consider descriptor design, retrieval efficiency, and 3-DoF pose estimation within a unified framework. This approach distinguishes RadLoc from prior methods by striving for an end-to-end pipeline that prioritizes speed, robustness, and lightweight design, particularly for real-world deployment where computational resources and diverse environmental conditions are significant factors.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop RadLoc, a fast, robust, and lightweight end-to-end global localization pipeline specifically designed for spinning radar sensors. This pipeline aims to integrate place recognition with accurate 3-DoF pose estimation, ensuring applicability across diverse environmental scenarios.

The detailed objectives and motivations are:
1. **Accelerate Pre-processing**: Previous radar localization methods often rely on computationally costly feature extraction techniques (e.g.,) to mitigate noise in raw radar images. This research is motivated by the need to replace these slow techniques with a lightweight and efficient pre-processing step that effectively suppresses radar-specific noise (e.g., speckle noise, receiver saturation) without incurring significant computational overhead. The goal is to make the initial processing of radar images faster and more discriminative.
2. **Design a Compact and Rotation-Invariant Spatial Descriptor**: The authors observed that existing descriptors for radar images sometimes struggle with robustness due to noise and variations, or are not inherently rotation-invariant. The motivation here is to leverage the inherent characteristics of spinning radar images, specifically the near-range dominance, to construct a compact 1D descriptor. This descriptor is intended to be inherently rotation-invariant through azimuth aggregation, thereby simplifying matching, and to be highly memory-efficient, which is crucial for large-scale and multi-session applications.
3. **Develop an Efficient Retrieval Strategy**: Traditional global descriptor retrieval methods, such as brute-force search or flat Kd-trees, can become computationally prohibitive as the database size grows. Recognizing that radar spatial information density decreases with range, the authors are motivated to design a hierarchical coarse-to-fine retrieval scheme. This strategy aims to efficiently narrow down the search space by initially focusing on more informative near-range data, thus reducing overall retrieval time without compromising recognition performance.
4. **Integrate 3-DoF Pose Estimation**: Many existing radar-based localization methods either focus solely on place recognition or provide limited pose estimation, often restricted to 1-DoF (yaw). The objective is to extend the localization capability to full 3-DoF (translation in X, Y, and rotation around Z, i.e., $\Delta x, \Delta y, \Delta \theta$). This is motivated by the requirement for more comprehensive pose information for practical applications like SLAM and precise robot navigation, utilizing phase correlation in appropriate coordinate domains.
5. **Ensure Versatility and Deployability**: The ultimate motivation is to create a versatile global localization module that is not only robust and efficient but also readily applicable to various robotic systems. This includes seamless integration into SLAM systems for drift correction, multi-session SLAM for long-term consistency, and cross-weather map management, thereby addressing a critical need for deployable, resource-efficient solutions in diverse and challenging real-world environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The RadLoc pipeline is an end-to-end system for 3-DoF global localization using spinning radar sensors. It comprises fast pre-processing, compact descriptor generation, hierarchical place recognition, and 3-DoF pose estimation.

**Overall Pipeline (Figure 2):**
The pipeline begins with a polar radar image. This image is simultaneously converted into a Cartesian representation for subsequent pose estimation and retained in its polar form for descriptor construction. The image then undergoes lightweight pre-processing to suppress noise. The processed polar image is used to generate a compact descriptor, which is stored in a descriptor database. Corresponding polar and Cartesian images are also stored in their respective databases. During a query, a hierarchical coarse-to-fine place retrieval strategy is employed to find the best match in the database. Upon retrieval of a matching place, the corresponding polar and Cartesian images are used for 3-DoF pose estimation via phase correlation. The output is a global localization result suitable for applications like SLAM and multi-session SLAM.

**A. Fast Radar Image Pre-processing:**
Raw radar images are characterized by noise sources such as speckle noise and receiver saturation, which can degrade localization performance. While prior methods often utilize feature extraction techniques (e.g.,) or binarization for noise suppression, these can introduce computational overhead or parameter sensitivity (Figure 3a).
RadLoc replaces these approaches with a lightweight 1D Cell-Averaging Constant False Alarm Rate (CA-CFAR) filtering. This filter is applied along the range direction of the polar radar image. The output of this filtering is retained as a continuous intensity representation, rather than a binarized one. This continuous representation, coupled with 1D CA-CFAR, is designed to be both faster and more discriminative than previous pre-processing methods (Figure 3b).

**B. Compact and Rotation-Invariant Descriptor Generation:**
Following pre-processing, the filtered polar radar image, $\tilde{I}_{\text{polar}} \in \mathbb{R}^{N_a \times N_r}$, is used to construct a compact descriptor. This is achieved by applying average pooling over the entire azimuth dimension ($N_a$) for predefined range patches ($p_r$). Each descriptor element $d_n$ is computed by averaging intensities within a specific range segment across all azimuth bins:
$d_n = \frac{1}{N_a \cdot p_r} \sum_{i=1}^{N_a} \sum_{j=(n-1)p_r+1}^{n \cdot p_r} \tilde{p}_{ij}$, where $n \in \{1, \dots, E\}$ and $E = \frac{N_r}{p_r}$ is the descriptor dimension. The resulting elements are concatenated into a 1D descriptor $d = [d_1, d_2, \dots, d_E]^\top \in \mathbb{R}^E$.
This design incorporates two key features:
1. **Rotational Invariance**: Aggregating values across the full azimuth dimension inherently eliminates dependence on the vehicle's yaw orientation, making the descriptor rotation-invariant.
2. **Compactness**: The range pooling factor $p_r$ directly controls the descriptor's dimensionality, allowing for a compact representation suitable for long-range spinning radars (e.g., reducing $N_r = 3360$ to $E = 40$ with $p_r = 84$).

**C. Simple yet Effective Hierarchical Searching:**
The spatial information density in polar radar measurements decreases with increasing range due to signal decay and larger Cartesian areas covered by distant bins. This concentrates discriminative structural cues in the near-to-mid range. The proposed descriptor inherently preserves this radial ordering.
RadLoc employs a hierarchical coarse-to-fine retrieval strategy based on this observation. Each descriptor is partitioned into a near-range segment ($d_{\text{near}} = [d_1, \dots, d_K]$) and the full descriptor ($d_{\text{full}} = [d_1, \dots, d_E]$). The partition index $K$ is set to 20 based on analysis of descriptor element contributions (Figure 4).

- **Coarse stage**: To mitigate the near-range dominance caused by range-dependent attenuation, a monotonically increasing weighting $w = [w_1, \dots, w_K]^\top$ is applied to the near-range segment: $\tilde{d}_{\text{near}} = w \odot d_{\text{near}}$. A simple weighting of $w_k = \sqrt{k}$ is used to moderately compensate for attenuation, balancing the contribution of different range bins. A Kd-tree index, $T_{\text{near}}$, is constructed using these weighted near-range descriptors. For a query descriptor, its weighted near-range component $\tilde{d}_{\text{near}}^{(q)}$ is used to retrieve the top-$N_c$ nearest neighbors from $T_{\text{near}}$ by minimizing the $L_2$ distance, $s_{\text{coarse}}(m) = \Vert \tilde{d}_{\text{near}}^{(q)} - \tilde{d}_{\text{near}}^{(m)} \Vert_2$. This stage significantly reduces the search space with logarithmic query complexity.
- **Fine stage**: The top-$N_c$ candidates identified in the coarse stage are re-ranked using the full descriptor $d_{\text{full}}$ without range-dependent weighting. The $L_2$ distance, $s_{\text{fine}}(m) = \Vert d_{\text{full}}^{(q)} - d_{\text{full}}^{(m)} \Vert_2$, is used for re-ranking. The top-ranked candidate is selected as the final retrieval result. The full unweighted descriptor is used to avoid amplifying low signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) in far-range bins, which could degrade matching stability.

**D. 3-DoF Pose Estimation via Phase Correlation:**
Upon successful place retrieval, RadLoc estimates the relative 3-DoF pose ($\Delta x, \Delta y, \Delta \theta$) between the query and the matched radar image using phase correlation. This process is decomposed into two sequential steps:
1. **Rotation estimation**: The Cartesian radar images of the query and matched candidate ($I_{\text{cart}}^{(q)}$ and $I_{\text{cart}}^{(m)}$) are converted into log-polar coordinates. In this domain, a planar rotation corresponds to a horizontal translational shift. Phase correlation is then applied to the log-polar representations to directly obtain the metric-scale rotational offset $\Delta \theta$.
2. **Translation estimation**: After estimating $\Delta \theta$, the matched polar image is compensated for this rotation by circularly shifting it along the azimuth axis. The rotation-compensated polar image is then converted back to the Cartesian coordinate system ($I_{\text{cart}}^{(m, \text{rot})}$). Finally, 2D phase correlation is applied between $I_{\text{cart}}^{(q)}$ and the rotation-aligned $I_{\text{cart}}^{(m, \text{rot})}$ to recover the metric-scale translational offset ($\Delta x, \Delta y$).
This two-stage decomposition is designed to prevent coupling between rotation and translation, contributing to stable and efficient 3-DoF pose estimation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of RadLoc involved extensive experiments across 15 sequences from 5 public radar datasets: Oxford Radar Robotcar, OORD, MulRan, Boreas, and Hercules. These datasets encompass diverse environments (urban, off-road), weather conditions (sunny, cloudy, rainy, snowy), and three different Navtech radar sensors. RadLoc's performance was compared against state-of-the-art learning-based methods (SHeRLoc, Kidnapped Radar) and handcrafted descriptor-based methods (RSC, RaPlace, RadVLAD, RadFFTVLAD, ReFeree). For 3-DoF pose estimation, a feature extraction with Generalized Iterative Closest Point (GICP) registration pipeline served as a baseline.

**Place Recognition Performance:**
- **Runtime Analysis (Figure 5)**: RadLoc demonstrated the fastest total runtime among all compared methods, on both the smallest (Parking 01) and largest (Maree) datasets. This efficiency becomes more pronounced with increasing database size, confirming the scalability of the proposed hierarchical retrieval strategy for large-scale and real-time global localization.
- **Single-session Evaluation (Table II)**: RadLoc consistently achieved top-tier Recall@1 (R@1) performance across all 15 sequences under 10m and 20m loop closure thresholds. It outperformed competing methods in unstructured off-road trajectories (e.g., OORD), demonstrating robustness to sparse geometric landmarks and reverse traversal. In structured urban environments (MulRan and Hercules datasets), RadLoc remained among the top performers, effectively suppressing false positives despite dense loop candidates. Notably, it maintained stable recall performance under adverse weather conditions in Boreas (e.g., 21-01-26, 21-04-29), indicating robustness to weather-induced intensity attenuation.
- **Multi-session Evaluation (Table III, Figure 6)**: In multi-session scenarios (OORD dataset), where methods often degrade, RadLoc exhibited the highest overall and most consistent R@1, Area Under the Curve (AUC), and F1 scores. Qualitative results (Figure 6) illustrate RadLoc's ability to maintain a lower rate of false loop closures compared to SHeRLoc, RSC, and ReFeree.

**3-DoF Global Localization:**
- **Pose Estimation Accuracy (Table IV)**: RadLoc achieved competitive Relative Translation Error (RTE) and Relative Rotation Error (RRE) across all evaluated Hercules sequences. It maintained a high success rate (above 99% in most cases, under 5m translation and 10° rotation thresholds), indicating reliable pose initialization even in complex environments. Compared to the feature-based GICP alignment baseline, RadLoc provided more stable initialization with a substantial reduction in computational overhead (e.g., 0.036s vs 0.342s for Mountain 01).
- **SLAM Integration (Figure 7)**: RadLoc was successfully integrated into a radar sensor-based SLAM pipeline (KAIST 03 sequence). The results demonstrated its ability to enable accurate global re-localization and consistent trajectory alignment, effectively correcting odometry drift and producing globally consistent maps. This confirms its suitability for practical mapping and loop closure applications.
- **Multi-session and Cross-weather Alignment (Figure 8, Figure 9)**: The proposed method successfully aligned trajectories acquired at different times (8 days apart on Oxford Radar Robotcar dataset, Figure 8) and under significantly different weather conditions (sunny vs. snowy on Hercules dataset, Figure 9). This indicates that the descriptor retains sufficient distinctiveness to enable accurate global alignment despite long-term structural and appearance variations.
- **Descriptor Scalability (Table V)**: RadLoc generated the smallest descriptor size among all compared methods (e.g., 0.73 MB for a single session, 1.22 MB for two sessions on Oxford Radar Robotcar dataset). This compactness is presented as crucial for efficient storage and retrieval in scalable multi-session map management, where memory constraints are significant.

**Ablation Study:**
- **Effect of Range-Dependent Weighting (Figure 10)**: Comparing weighted vs. unweighted descriptors using the Kolmogorov–Smirnov (KS) statistic, the weighted formulation in RadLoc resulted in lower KS statistics for both positive (0.2600 vs 0.4880) and negative (0.1260 vs 0.1720) descriptor pairs. This indicates a closer alignment between descriptor distances and ground truth spatial separation, demonstrating that the weighting mitigates range-dependent bias while preserving consistency with underlying spatial relationships.
- **Effect of Hierarchical Retrieval (Figure 11, Figure 12)**: The hierarchical retrieval scheme was shown to preserve recall performance at levels comparable to exhaustive KD-tree flat search (Figure 11). Concurrently, it achieved substantial computational savings in retrieval runtime, particularly for larger databases (Figure 12). This confirms that the hierarchical strategy effectively reduces search complexity without degrading recognition quality.
- **Effect of Radial Partition K (Table VI)**: An analysis of the partition index K (defining the near-range segment boundary for coarse retrieval) revealed a trade-off. Increasing K generally improved R@1 by incorporating more structural information. However, AUC and F1 scores gradually decreased with larger K, suggesting that including more far-range bins in the weighted coarse stage could amplify low SNR and reduce precision. K=20 was selected as a balanced configuration.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

RadLoc offers a significant contribution to the field of radar-based global localization by providing a holistic, end-to-end pipeline. Its primary impact lies in demonstrating that robust, fast, and lightweight 3-DoF global localization is achievable with spinning radar sensors across diverse and challenging environmental conditions, addressing a long-standing gap in the literature.

The demonstrated robustness across various environments (urban, off-road), weather conditions (sunny, rainy, snowy), and different radar types highlights the practical utility of RadLoc for real-world autonomous systems. This versatility suggests enhanced reliability for operations where traditional vision and LiDAR sensors are compromised.

The computational efficiency, stemming from fast pre-processing (1D CA-CFAR filtering), a compact descriptor design, and an efficient hierarchical retrieval strategy, is a key aspect of this work. The achievement of the fastest retrieval times and smallest descriptor sizes among state-of-the-art methods implies significant advantages for scalability in large-scale mapping and long-term deployments. This is particularly relevant for applications that demand continuous operation and efficient resource management, such as cloud-based mapping or systems with limited onboard computing power.

Furthermore, RadLoc's capability to provide accurate 3-DoF pose estimation, integrated within the localization pipeline, marks an advance for radar-based systems that previously often offered only 1-DoF (yaw) estimation. This comprehensive pose information is critical for integrating with SLAM systems for drift correction and for enabling precise robot navigation and multi-robot coordination. The successful integration into single-session and multi-session SLAM frameworks, including cross-weather map alignment, confirms its practical applicability and potential for managing dynamic and evolving environments over extended periods.

By reducing the reliance on extensive training data and substantial computational resources typically associated with learning-based methods, RadLoc presents a more deployable solution. This characteristic could facilitate faster adoption and deployment in new operational areas or by entities with limited access to large datasets and high-performance computing infrastructure.

The compact descriptor and lightweight pipeline also open avenues for future research and applications, such as multi-robot SLAM, where communication bandwidth and computational resources per robot are often constrained. RadLoc's design inherently supports such scenarios, potentially fostering advancements in coordinated autonomous multi-robot systems.
