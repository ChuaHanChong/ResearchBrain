# Autonomous Character-Scene Interaction Synthesis from Text Instruction

> [!summary] Summary
> LINGO synthesizes multi-stage human-scene interaction motion (locomotion, hand-reaching, object interaction) from a single text instruction and goal location, using an auto-regressive diffusion model with an autonomous stage scheduler and dual voxel scene encoding, outperforming TRUMANS and GOAL baselines on realism and scene-awareness metrics.

## Problem

- Prior HSI synthesis splits locomotion, reaching, and object interaction into separate specialized systems, breaking coherence across long-term multi-stage sequences.
- Existing methods require complex extra inputs like object trajectories, human keypoints, or predefined waypoints and motion-phase labels, limiting autonomous deployment.
- Available scene-interaction datasets lack text-annotated, long-horizon sequences spanning grasping, HOI, and fine-grained scene geometry constraints together.

## Method

- ==Auto-regressive diffusion model== generates successive motion segments conditioned on scene, text, and goal embeddings, using only prior frames instead of full pre-planned trajectories.
- **Dual voxel scene encoder** builds current and predictive ==occupancy grids== processed by a ==Vision Transformer==, giving the character awareness of both immediate and upcoming local geometry.
- **Autonomous scheduler**, a ==Transformer encoder== over frame-embedded text and motion tokens, predicts per-segment ==stage-transition probability==, removing manual stage-boundary annotation.

## Results

- On interactive motion synthesis LINGO beats TRUMANS with FID **2.048** vs **2.438** and F1 score **0.622** vs **0.552**, while frame-embedding ablation raises FID to **2.368**.
- Locomotion evaluation shows LINGO cuts mean scene penetration to **0.402** versus TRUMANS **1.011** and max penetration to **0.948** versus **7.441**, with lower foot sliding **0.432** vs **0.472**.
- Object-reaching task shows LINGO nearly triples GOAL's precision, reducing error distance to **0.061** vs **0.156** and completion time to **3.073s** vs **4.880s**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Embedding a frame-position signal directly into language conditioning lets auto-regressive generation track progress within a semantic action, avoiding disordered repetition.
> - Dual (current plus predictive) local scene representations generalize the single-voxel idea, letting a model anticipate geometry it has not reached yet rather than only reacting locally.
> - Treating goal locations as soft conditioning rather than hard kinematic constraints lets a generative model discover natural approach and transition motion on its own.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of human motion synthesis, especially within 3D environments, is a critical area of research with applications spanning animation, virtual reality, robotics, and embodied AI. Historically, synthesizing realistic human-scene interactions (HSIs) has been a complex challenge due to the intricacies of human biomechanics, environmental constraints, and the contextual understanding required for natural interactions.

The paper identifies several key limitations in the existing research landscape:
1. **Fragmented Frameworks:** Current methods often treat different stages of HSI (e.g., locomotion, hand-reaching, human-object interaction or HOI) as separate problems, leading to a lack of coherence and seamless transitions in long-term sequences. Specialized systems are typically used for distinct actions, making it difficult to automate complex, multi-stage activities from high-level instructions.
2. **Complex Input Dependencies:** Many state-of-the-art models require extensive user inputs beyond simple commands, such as predefined waypoints, object trajectories, human keypoints, or explicit motion phase labels. This complexity limits flexibility and practical deployment, demanding significant manual effort from animators or domain experts.
3. **Lack of Comprehensive Datasets:** A significant bottleneck has been the scarcity of large-scale, scene-level datasets that are richly annotated with natural language descriptions and cover diverse, multi-stage character-scene interactions, including fine-grained grasping and context-rich HOI. While datasets for object manipulation (e.g., BEHAVE, GRAB) or human motion in scenes (e.g., SAMP, TRUMANS) exist, they often lack the unification, long-term coherence, or textual annotations crucial for developing truly autonomous, language-guided systems. TRUMANS, for instance, provides frame-wise action labels but lacks semantically separable clips and requires predefined waypoints.

This paper positions itself directly against these challenges. It aims to bridge the gap by proposing a **unified framework** that can generate multi-stage, scene-aware motions autonomously from **minimal, high-level textual instructions** and a goal location. Furthermore, a major contribution to the landscape is the introduction of **LINGO**, a comprehensive, language-annotated motion-captured (MoCap) dataset, specifically designed to address the data scarcity issue for unified HSI research.

By integrating locomotion, hand-reaching, and HOI into a single pipeline and reducing user input to simple text, this work directly advances towards more intuitive and automated character animation. It moves beyond specialized solutions for individual action types, seeking to emulate human-like intuitive interpretation of verbal commands in diverse contexts. The method's engagement with diffusion models, scene representation, and language embedding techniques aligns with current trends in generative AI, pushing the boundaries of what is possible in language-conditioned motion synthesis.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of this research is to develop a comprehensive framework for **synthesizing multi-stage scene-aware interaction motions directly from a single text instruction and a goal location**. This ambitious goal is motivated by the significant limitations and unmet needs in the current landscape of human motion synthesis:

1. **Achieve Unified, Seamless Multi-Stage HSI Synthesis:** A primary motivation is to overcome the fragmentation prevalent in existing motion synthesis methods. Current approaches typically model different stages of HSI (e.g., walking, reaching, interacting with objects) using separate, specialized systems. This leads to disjointed motions that lack coherence, particularly in long-term interaction sequences that demand contextual alignment. The authors aim to integrate locomotion, hand-reaching, and human-object interaction (HOI) into a seamless pipeline, ensuring both the realism and functional coherence of the synthesized motions.

2. **Reduce Dependency on Complex User Inputs:** Many state-of-the-art methods necessitate intricate and time-consuming manual inputs, such as specifying exact trajectories, human keypoints, or precise motion phase labels. This reliance on highly detailed user-defined waypoints and stage transitions severely limits the flexibility and practical deployment of these systems. The paper is motivated to drastically simplify the control signals, moving towards an autonomous synthesis process driven by only a single natural language text instruction and a high-level goal location (e.g., "pick up the apple" or "sit on the chair").

3. **Address the Lack of Comprehensive, Language-Annotated HSI Datasets:** A critical barrier to developing robust, generalizable language-guided motion synthesis models has been the absence of suitable training data. Existing datasets often fall short by not covering the full spectrum of character-scene interaction, lacking precise language descriptions, or failing to capture long-term, context-rich sequences in diverse indoor scenes with dynamic objects. The motivation here is to curate and release a large-scale, meticulously annotated motion-captured (MoCap) dataset to bridge this data gap, thereby enabling the training of models that can accurately interpret and execute complex motion instructions.

In essence, the paper seeks to enable "autonomous character-scene interaction synthesis" that is intuitive for users, contextually aware of the 3D environment, and capable of generating realistic, multi-stage human behaviors from minimal linguistic input, pushing the field closer to intuitive digital character animation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework tackles the intricate task of autonomous character-scene interaction synthesis through a multi-faceted approach, combining an auto-regressive diffusion model with novel scene and language representations, an autonomous scheduler, and a custom-built dataset.

#### 3.1. Overall Framework

The core of the method is an **auto-regressive diffusion model**. This model generates subsequent motion segments based on previously generated motions, a current text instruction, scene information, and a goal location. An **autonomous scheduler** works in conjunction to predict the optimal transition timing between different action stages (e.g., transitioning from locomotion to interaction).

#### 3.2. Data Representation

- **Human Motion:** Character motion is represented using the **SMPL-X model**, providing parameterized human body, hand, and facial expressions. The output is a sequence of 3D joint positions, which are then used to pose the SMPL-X mesh.
- **Scene:** The 3D scene is represented as a binary **voxel grid** (occupancy grid). For dynamic objects, additional information is captured by sampling points from their surfaces with normal vectors.
- **Goal Location:** A 3D coordinate (G) is specified by the user, whose interpretation varies based on the action stage (e.g., walking direction, hand target, pelvis target).
- **Text Instruction:** Natural language descriptions (V) provide high-level guidance.

#### 3.3. Motion Diffusion Module

The generative engine is a **Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Model (DDPM)**. It operates auto-regressively, generating fixed-length motion segments.
- **Forward Process:** Gradually adds noise to canonicalized input motion data (joint locations relative to the pelvis of the first frame).
- **Reverse Process:** A neural network (ϵ_θ) learns to denoise the corrupted motion data, conditioned on the noisy motion, timestep (t), and combined condition terms (C).
- **Conditions (C):** Scene embedding (S_emb), text embedding (V_emb), and goal embedding (G_emb) are concatenated as input to guide the denoising process. An embedding of the diffusion timestep is also added.

#### 3.4. Dual Voxel Scene Encoder

To ensure scene-aware and collision-free motion, the method introduces a novel **dual voxel scene representation**:
- **Current Scene Voxel:** A 32x32x32 voxel grid centered around the character's pelvis at the start of the current motion segment, capturing immediate surroundings.
- **Predictive Scene Voxel:** Its position varies: for locomotion, it's placed 0.8 meters ahead in the goal direction; for object interactions, it's aligned with the object's location. This provides anticipatory scene context.
- Both voxels are concatenated and fed into a **Vision Transformer (ViT)** to extract a 512-dimensional scene embedding (S_emb). Masking is applied for scene-independent actions.

#### 3.5. Frame-embedded Text Encoder

To maintain semantic coherence across auto-regressively generated segments without predefined timing, a unique **frame embedding** approach is used:
- The raw text instruction (V) is encoded using a **CLIP encoder**, then transformed by an MLP.
- The **frame number** (of the first frame in the current segment, relative to the original full semantic clip) is converted into a 512-dimensional vector via a sinusoidal positional encoder.
- This frame embedding is added to the text embedding, and the result is passed through another MLP to form the final text conditional token (V_emb). This allows the model to learn temporal patterns within semantic actions (e.g., distinguishing "turning page" from "reading" within "read a book").

#### 3.6. Stage-specific Goal Encoder

The goal location (G) is interpreted differently based on the interaction stage:
- **Locomotion:** A 2D vector representing the walking direction and speed for the current segment, derived from a user-specified goal location.
- **Hand Grasping/Manipulation:** The 3D position of the index finger (for "pick up" or "put down"). Penetration resolution is applied during the last diffusion steps.
- **Scene-level Interaction (e.g., sitting):** The precise pelvis position for the interaction.
- These goals are processed by separate MLPs to yield the goal embedding (G_emb). Goals are masked (zeroed) if irrelevant to the current action type.

#### 3.7. Autonomous Scheduler

This module determines when to transition from one action stage to the next, based on the generated motion segment, current frame number, and language instruction. It is also a **Transformer Encoder** (3 layers, 8 attention heads), taking the frame-embedded language instruction and encoded motion frames as input. It predicts a likelihood (0-1) of the current stage concluding, dictating whether to maintain the current semantic or initiate a new one.

#### 3.8. LINGO Dataset (Language-annotated INteraction, Grasping, and lOcomotion)

A critical contribution is the creation of this comprehensive MoCap dataset to train the model.
- **VR-assisted MoCap Setup:** Addresses challenges of diverse scene capture and dynamic object interaction. Actors wear VR headsets, seeing synthetic scenes while performing motions in a physical VICON MoCap space. Physical placeholders support interactions with static virtual objects. A human assistant helps synchronize dynamic object interactions (grasp/release points).
- **High Quality:** Utilizes VICON system for superior motion data compared to multi-view cameras or IMUs, and VR minimizes occlusions.
- **Statistics:** 16 hours of motion sequences, 120 diverse indoor scenes, 40 types of motions, all precisely text-annotated. Language descriptions are augmented using GPT-4 for generalizability.
- **Data Representation:** SMPL-X for human body, MANO for hands.

This integrated approach, leveraging advanced generative models, custom data, and intelligent conditioning, allows for the synthesis of complex, context-aware human motions from simple textual commands.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation demonstrates the efficacy of the proposed method across three distinct categories of human-scene interaction: locomotion, object reaching, and interactive motion, with comparisons against state-of-the-art baselines and ablation studies.

#### 4.1. Interactive Motion Synthesis (Table 1)

- **Overall Performance:** The proposed method achieved the highest scores in motion realism (FID↓), how closely generated motions match references (Precision↑), and coverage of reference motions (Recall↑), leading to the best F1 score (↑). This indicates that the synthesized multi-stage motions are high-quality, realistic, and semantically coherent with textual instructions.
- **Diversity:** While the proposed method shows slightly lower diversity compared to real motions and TRUMANS, the authors attribute this to the frame embedding ensuring semantic coherence. Without the frame embedding, the model produces "disordered" motions that repeat actions without a coherent flow, leading to higher but less meaningful diversity. This trade-off suggests the model prioritizes semantic correctness over arbitrary variations.
- **Ablation (w/o frame embedding):** Removing the frame embedding significantly degraded performance across FID, Precision, Recall, and F1 score, confirming its crucial role in linking motion segments into semantically meaningful, long-term sequences.

#### 4.2. Locomotion (Table 2)

- **Collision Avoidance:** The method significantly outperformed TRUMANS in all scene penetration metrics (Pene%scene↓, Penemean↓, Penemax↓). This highlights its superior ability to autonomously plan and adjust trajectories to avoid collisions in cluttered environments, rather than relying on predefined paths. Qualitative results (Figure 3, 5a vs. 5b) visually support this, showing characters actively navigating obstacles.
- **Motion Quality:** The foot sliding (FS↓) metric for the proposed method was lower than TRUMANS, indicating more stable and realistic locomotion without unnatural slippage.
- **Ablation (flattened voxel):** Replacing the dual voxel scene representation with a simplified, flattened walkable map resulted in higher penetration and foot sliding. This ablation powerfully validates the effectiveness of the dual voxel scene encoder in providing comprehensive 3D environmental information, enabling more precise and realistic motion adjustments for collision avoidance.

#### 4.3. Object Reaching (Table 3)

- **Reaching Accuracy and Efficiency:** The proposed method achieved the lowest error distance (Error dist.↓) between the intended goal and the actual hand position, demonstrating high precision in object reaching. It also completed tasks faster (Time used↓) and with lower scene penetration (Pene%scene↓) compared to GOAL, a baseline focused on grasping. This indicates robust and efficient hand-oriented motions that are aware of the surrounding environment.
- **Ablations:** Both ablations (w/o dual voxel and w/o frame embedding) led to degraded performance in error distance, penetration, and/or time used. This further reinforces the importance of both the dual voxel scene encoder for environmental awareness and the frame embedding for precise temporal semantic guidance, even in tasks primarily involving hand movements.

**Generalizability:** The paper notes that the method can generalize to unseen environments, which is a significant practical advantage for deployment in various virtual settings.

In summary, the results consistently demonstrate that the proposed framework generates high-quality, multi-stage human motions that are closely aligned with both environmental constraints (collision avoidance, natural interaction) and textual conditions (semantic coherence, accurate task execution). The qualitative comparisons further underscore the naturalness and scene awareness of the generated animations, particularly in complex scenarios requiring autonomous navigation and interaction.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research presented in "Autonomous Character-Scene Interaction Synthesis from Text Instruction" holds significant implications across several fields, pushing the boundaries of automated character animation and embodied AI.

#### 5.1. Core Significance

- **Unified, Autonomous HSI Synthesis:** The most significant contribution is the development of a comprehensive generative framework that unifies locomotion, hand-reaching, and human-object interaction into a single, seamless pipeline. This overcomes the fragmentation of previous approaches, allowing for the autonomous generation of complex, multi-stage actions from a simple textual instruction and goal location. This reduction in user input complexity is a major leap towards more intuitive and less labor-intensive animation.
- **Novel Conditioning Mechanisms:** The introduction of the **Dual Voxel Scene Encoder** provides a sophisticated mechanism for detailed, anticipatory scene awareness, enabling realistic collision avoidance and natural interaction with both static and dynamic environments. The **Frame-embedded Text Encoder** is crucial for maintaining semantic coherence over long, auto-regressively generated sequences, allowing the model to understand the temporal progression within a single natural language instruction. These novel components are key to the method's superior performance in realism and contextual accuracy.
- **LINGO Dataset Contribution:** The creation and public release of **LINGO**—a large-scale, language-annotated, VR-assisted motion-captured dataset—is a vital resource for the research community. It addresses a critical data scarcity problem, offering unprecedented diversity in motion types, scene variations, and detailed textual descriptions. This dataset will undoubtedly serve as a benchmark and training foundation for future research in human motion synthesis, embodied AI, and language-conditioned generation, accelerating advancements in the field.

#### 5.2. Potential Impact

- **Animation and Content Creation:** The ability to generate realistic, multi-stage character animations from simple text instructions could revolutionize the animation industry. It promises to significantly reduce the manual effort and expertise required for creating virtual characters in games, films, virtual reality (VR), and augmented reality (AR) applications, enabling faster prototyping and more diverse content.
- **Virtual Avatars and Digital Humans:** For applications involving virtual avatars in metaverses, social VR platforms, or digital assistants, this work paves the way for more responsive, intelligent, and context-aware characters that can naturally interact with their virtual surroundings based on natural language commands.
- **Robotics and Embodied AI:** The insights into scene-aware motion generation and language-guided physical interaction are highly relevant for embodied AI and robotics. Training robots or virtual agents with such capabilities could lead to more intelligent agents that can interpret high-level human instructions and execute complex tasks in real-world or simulated environments.
- **Human-Computer Interaction (HCI):** By translating natural language into complex physical actions, this research contributes to the development of more intuitive and accessible human-computer interfaces, enabling users to control virtual entities with greater ease and naturalness.
- **Academic Research Catalyst:** The methodological innovations, particularly in integrating diffusion models with robust scene and language conditioning, along with the provision of the LINGO dataset, will stimulate further research. It provides a strong foundation for exploring generalizability to unseen interaction types, incorporating more fine-grained hand manipulation, and improving physical plausibility.
