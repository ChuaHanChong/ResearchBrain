# Behind Every Domain There is a Shift: Adapting Distortion-aware Vision Transformers for Panoramic Semantic Segmentation

> [!summary] Summary
> A new distortion-aware Vision Transformer, Trans4PASS+, combined with Mutual Prototypical Adaptation (MPA) for unsupervised learning, advances panoramic semantic segmentation, achieving state-of-the-art performance with mIoU gains of up to 17.44% over prior methods in outdoor urban scenes and matching fully-supervised results in indoor environments. This work also introduces the SynPASS dataset to facilitate synthetic-to-real adaptation studies.

## Problem

- Panoramic images suffer from significant geometric distortions and object deformations, particularly at the poles, which degrade the performance of standard semantic segmentation models.
- The creation of pixel-wise semantic annotations for 360-degree images is labor-intensive and expensive, resulting in a severe scarcity of labeled panoramic datasets.
- Existing Unsupervised Domain Adaptation (UDA) strategies are not fully tailored to the unique distortion challenges and ultra-wide field-of-view of panoramic imagery.

## Method

- Developed **Trans4PASS+**, a Vision Transformer architecture incorporating ==Deformable Patch Embedding (DPE)== and a novel ==Deformable MLP v2 (DMLPv2)== to inherently handle geometric distortions in panoramic images.
- Implemented **Mutual Prototypical Adaptation (MPA)**, an Unsupervised Domain Adaptation (UDA) strategy, which leverages ==dual-domain prototypes== and integrates the ==Segment Anything Model (SAM)== for rectifying noisy pseudo-labels.
- Established **SynPASS**, a new large-scale synthetic panoramic dataset with 9,080 annotated images, to facilitate Synthetic-to-Real (SYN2REAL) domain adaptation studies in 360-degree vision.

## Results

- The Trans4PASS+ architecture achieved **40.72% mIoU** on the SynPASS test set, outperforming CNN-based and other transformer-based methods by up to **5.07% mIoU**.
- Mutual Prototypical Adaptation (MPA) combined with Trans4PASS+ yielded state-of-the-art Unsupervised Domain Adaptation (UDA) results, with a **+17.44% mIoU** gain over prior methods in outdoor scenes (e.g., **59.43%** on DensePASS).
- The approach, particularly with SAM-enhanced MPA, nearly matched fully-supervised performance in indoor UDA settings (**53.49% mIoU** vs. **54.1%**), marking the first time an unsupervised model reached this level on Stanford2D3D Panoramic.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Embedding distortion-aware mechanisms like Deformable Patch Embedding and Deformable MLP v2 into Vision Transformers significantly enhances their capability to process and generalize across highly distorted panoramic imagery.
> - A mutual prototypical adaptation scheme, augmented by Segment Anything Model for pseudo-label refinement, proves highly effective in aligning feature distributions and distilling knowledge across source and target domains for panoramic UDA.
> - Synthetic panoramic datasets can provide valuable spatial distribution priors, leading to better segmentation of highly distorted objects compared to adaptation from real pinhole images, highlighting the utility of SYN2REAL adaptation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Semantic segmentation is a fundamental task in computer vision, aiming to assign a semantic label to each pixel in an image. Significant advancements have been made in this field, particularly with the advent of Fully Convolutional Networks (FCNs) and later, transformer-based architectures. Most of these methods, however, are developed and evaluated on images captured by standard pinhole cameras, which typically have a narrow field of view (FoV).

This work specifically addresses **panoramic semantic segmentation**, which involves processing 360-degree imagery. Panoramic images offer a comprehensive understanding of surrounding scenes, making them valuable for applications such as autonomous vehicles, robotics, and augmented/virtual reality (AR/VR). However, panoramic images present two primary challenges that distinguish them from pinhole images:
1. **Image Distortions and Object Deformations:** Due to the equirectangular projection commonly used to represent 360-degree spherical data in a 2D flat image, objects and scene elements appear severely distorted, particularly at the poles of the projection. This distortion can degrade the performance of models trained on undistorted pinhole images.
2. **Scarcity of Annotated Data:** Creating dense, pixel-wise semantic annotations is labor-intensive and expensive. This challenge is exacerbated for panoramic images due to their ultra-wide FoV and the presence of numerous small and complex scene elements, leading to a limited availability of labeled 360-degree datasets.

Existing approaches for panoramic segmentation have explored distortion-mitigated representations or multi-task schemes, but many still assume the availability of labeled panoramic data. While dynamic and deformable Vision Transformers have emerged to handle variable spatial information, their application has primarily been limited to narrow-FoV images.

To address the data scarcity, **Unsupervised Domain Adaptation (UDA)** is a common strategy that aims to transfer knowledge from a label-rich source domain to an unlabeled target domain. While UDA has been extensively studied in pinhole imagery (e.g., adapting from synthetic to real data or between different real-world datasets), its application to panoramic segmentation, particularly considering the unique distortion challenges, remains less explored in the existing literature.

This research contributes to the broader landscape by:
- Proposing a novel Vision Transformer architecture explicitly designed to handle panoramic distortions.
- Developing an enhanced UDA strategy tailored for panoramic imagery, bridging the gap between existing labeled resources (pinhole or synthetic) and unlabeled real-world panoramas.
- Introducing a new large-scale synthetic panoramic dataset, thereby expanding the resources available for studying panoramic segmentation and domain adaptation in this context.
By tackling both the architectural and data-scarcity challenges specific to 360-degree vision, this work advances the state of holistic scene understanding.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to advance panoramic semantic segmentation by overcoming its two critical challenges: image distortions and object deformations inherent in panoramas, and the scarcity of corresponding semantic annotations.

The key motivations and detailed objectives are as follows:

1. **Addressing Image Distortions and Object Deformations:**
 - **Motivation:** Panoramic images, specifically those using equirectangular projection, exhibit significant geometric distortions and object deformations. Standard semantic segmentation models, typically designed for pinhole images, struggle to process these deformed features effectively, leading to suboptimal performance. Performance degradation is observed with increasing field of view.
 - **Objective:** To design and implement a novel distortion-aware Vision Transformer architecture capable of inherently handling the characteristics of panoramic images. This involves enabling the model to adapt to spatial variations and deformations from the initial patch embedding stages through deeper feature processing.

2. **Mitigating the Impact of Limited Labeled Data:**
 - **Motivation:** Generating pixel-level semantic annotations for 360-degree images is exceptionally time-consuming and expensive. This data scarcity severely limits the training of robust panoramic semantic segmentation models. Leveraging existing label-rich datasets through Unsupervised Domain Adaptation (UDA) offers a viable alternative.
 - **Objective:** To develop and enhance an effective UDA strategy tailored for panoramic segmentation. This strategy should enable models to adapt from readily available labeled data (e.g., pinhole images or synthetic panoramas) to unlabeled real-world panoramic target domains.

Specifically, the paper outlines the following detailed objectives:

- **Propose Trans4PASS+ Architecture:** Introduce an upgraded Transformer for Panoramic Semantic Segmentation (Trans4PASS+). This architecture is equipped with:
 - **Deformable Patch Embedding (DPE):** To learn distortion-aware prior knowledge during the initial patchifying of the image, allowing flexible sampling of spatial connections in distorted patches.
 - **Deformable MLP (DMLPv2):** To enable adaptive feature parsing by adaptively mixing and interpreting feature tokens. DMLPv2 is designed with a lightweight yet effective parallel token mixing mechanism (Channel Mixer and Pooling Mixer) to enhance flexibility in modeling discriminative information.
- **Enhance Unsupervised Domain Adaptation:** Develop a robust UDA strategy named Mutual Prototypical Adaptation (MPA).
 - **Dual-domain Prototypes:** Generate prototypes from both source (labeled) and target (unlabeled with pseudo-labels) domains to facilitate mutual knowledge distillation and complementary alignment of semantic similarities in feature space.
 - **Pseudo-label Rectification with SAM:** Integrate the Segment Anything Model (SAM) to rectify noisy and incomplete pseudo-labels generated for the target domain, thereby improving the quality of self-training.
- **Establish a New Dataset and Adaptation Scheme:**
 - **Create SynPASS Dataset:** Develop a new large-scale synthetic panoramic dataset (SynPASS) containing 9,080 panoramic images with dense annotations for urban street scenes.
 - **Enable SYN2REAL Adaptation:** Facilitate a new Synthetic-to-Real (SYN2REAL) domain adaptive panoramic segmentation scheme in 360-degree imagery, in addition to revisiting the Pinhole-to-Panoramic (PIN2PAN) adaptation paradigm.
- **Conduct Comprehensive Evaluations:** Perform extensive experiments across both indoor and outdoor scenarios, investigating both PIN2PAN and SYN2REAL adaptation regimens to benchmark the proposed methods against state-of-the-art approaches.

These objectives collectively aim to provide a comprehensive solution for robust panoramic semantic segmentation under realistic conditions of distortion and data scarcity.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology addresses panoramic semantic segmentation through two main thrusts: an architectural design for distortion awareness and an adaptation strategy for unsupervised learning.

#### 3.1. Trans4PASS+ Architecture

The core of the proposed solution is the Transformer for Panoramic Semantic Segmentation (Trans4PASS), which is then upgraded to Trans4PASS+. The architecture maintains a multi-scale pyramid feature structure, typical in modern semantic segmentation models, consisting of an encoder and a decoder.

- **Distortion Awareness:** The design is motivated by the geometric distortions inherent in equirectangular panoramic projections. The area distortion (AD) in such projections is proportional to `cos(phi)` (where `phi` is latitude), indicating that areas away from the equator are increasingly stretched. Standard patch embedding (PE) modules with fixed sampling grids fail to account for these deformations.
- **Deformable Patch Embedding (DPE):**
 - This module replaces standard PE in both the encoder and decoder.
 - Instead of fixed offsets for patchifying positions, DPE learns data-dependent and distortion-aware offsets. These adaptive offsets are predicted by a function `g(f)` (implemented via deformable convolution) based on the input feature map `f`.
 - The learned offsets `Δ_DPE` allow the model to adaptively sample spatial connections within distorted patches, thereby explicitly considering the panorama's characteristics from the initial feature extraction stage.
- **Deformable MLP (DMLP) Token Mixers:**
 - **Motivation:** Standard MLP-based token mixers lack adaptivity, which can limit their effectiveness in processing panoramic data with severe deformations.
 - **DMLPv1:** This token mixer uses DPE-processed feature maps and applies adaptive spatial offsets (`Δ_DMLP`) for token mixing. The offsets are predicted channel-wise using a deformable convolution operation, allowing for flexible sampling of tokens. This version features a residual structure with DPE, two DMLPs, and an MLP module.
 - **DMLPv2 (for Trans4PASS+):** This is an enhanced, more lightweight, and effective version. DMLPv2 incorporates a **parallel token mixing mechanism** by combining:
 - **Channel Mixer (CX):** A Squeeze&Excite (SE) based module that performs space-consistent, channel-wise feature reweighting, highlighting informative channels.
 - **Pooling Mixer (PX):** A non-parametric multi-scale pooling operator (using 3x3, 5x5, and 11x11 average pooling kernels) that focuses on spatial-wise sampling.
 - DMLP: Still present, focusing on adaptive spatial sampling.
 - This parallel design in DMLPv2 aims to improve flexibility in modeling discriminative information and enhance generalization against domain shifts.

#### 3.2. Mutual Prototypical Adaptation (MPA)

MPA is an Unsupervised Domain Adaptation (UDA) strategy designed to transfer knowledge from a label-rich source domain to an unlabeled panoramic target domain.

- **Preliminaries:** The model is initially trained on a source dataset `D_s` using a standard segmentation loss (`L_s_SEG`). For the unlabeled target dataset `D_t`, pseudo-labels `ˆy_t` are generated based on the most probable class predictions from the source-trained model. Self-Supervised Learning (SSL) loss (`L_t_SSL`) is then applied using these pseudo-labels.
- **Core Idea:** MPA aims to soften the effect of potentially noisy hard pseudo-labels by aligning features in a prototypical feature space, rather than relying solely on pixel-wise classification on pseudo-labels in the output space. It seeks to distill mutual knowledge by explicitly considering prototypes from both source and target domains.
- **Prototype Construction and Update:**
 - Prototypes `P_k` for each class `k` are computed as the mean of all feature vectors (pixel-embeddings) across both source (using ground truth) and target (using pseudo-labels) domains that share the class `k`.
 - Prototypes are stored in a memory bank `M` and dynamically updated during training using a momentum-based mechanism.
 - Multi-scale feature embeddings are used to construct these prototypes, enhancing their robustness and expressiveness.
- **SAM-enhanced Pseudo-Label Rectification:**
 - **Motivation:** Pseudo-labels generated by source-only models for the target domain can be incomplete or noisy, especially for complex objects or background regions.
 - **Approach:** The Segment Anything Model (SAM), pre-trained on a large-scale dataset (SA-1B), is employed as an external mask generator. SAM is used to rectify and complete the missing or erroneous parts of the pseudo-labels generated by the segmentation model, before they are used for prototype generation and target domain adaptation. This helps alleviate the negative effects of low-quality pseudo-labels.
- **MPA Loss:** A knowledge distillation loss (`L_MPA`) is applied to drive feature alignment between the actual feature embeddings (`f`) and a reconstructed feature map (`ˆf`) composed of class prototypes. This is applied to both source (`L_s_MPA`) and target (`L_t_MPA`) domains.
- **Overall Loss:** The final loss function is a weighted sum of the source segmentation loss, target SSL loss, and the dual-domain MPA losses.

#### 3.3. SynPASS Dataset Creation

To facilitate the study of Synthetic-to-Real (SYN2REAL) adaptation in panoramic segmentation, a new large-scale synthetic dataset named SynPASS was created.

- **Simulator:** The CARLA urban driving simulator was used.
- **Data Generation:**
 - Virtual sensor suite: 6 pinhole cameras positioned at a single viewpoint to capture cubemap panoramas.
 - Projection: Cubemap panoramas were re-projected into the equirectangular format (1024x2048 resolution).
 - Diversity: Data was collected across 8 open-source city maps, with 100-120 initial collection points per map. Vehicle drives followed simulator traffic rules, sampling every 50 frames and keeping 10 key-frames per point.
 - Environmental Conditions: Varied weather (sunny, cloudy, foggy, rainy, each 25%) and time of day (daytime 85%, nighttime 15%) conditions were modulated.
- **Scale and Content:** SynPASS contains 9,080 panoramic RGB images with corresponding dense semantic labels (22 classes), including 9 additional classes compared to common real-world datasets like Cityscapes.

#### 3.4. Adaptation Paradigms Investigated

The research thoroughly investigates two primary UDA paradigms in both indoor and outdoor scenarios:
- **Pinhole-to-Panoramic (PIN2PAN):** Adapting from label-rich pinhole image datasets (e.g., Cityscapes, Stanford2D3D Pinhole) to unlabeled real panoramic image datasets (e.g., DensePASS, Stanford2D3D Panoramic).
- **Synthetic-to-Real (SYN2REAL):** Adapting from label-rich synthetic panoramic image datasets (e.g., SynPASS, Structured3D) to unlabeled real panoramic image datasets (e.g., DensePASS, Stanford2D3D Panoramic).

The models are evaluated on overlapping classes between source and target domains (8 classes for indoor, 13 for outdoor).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted across various indoor and outdoor scenarios, and under both PIN2PAN and SYN2REAL adaptation regimens, yielded several findings.

#### 4.1. SynPASS Benchmark Performance

- Trans4PASS+ (Small model) achieved 40.72% mean Intersection over Union (mIoU) on the SynPASS test set, outperforming CNN-based methods (e.g., HRNet by +5.07%) and other transformer-based methods (e.g., SegFormer-B2 by +3.48%).
- The model demonstrated robustness across different weather conditions (cloudy, foggy, rainy, sunny) and illuminations (day, night), with the largest improvement in the rainy condition (+5.03% mIoU over SegFormer-B2).
- The benchmark remains challenging, as indicated by the best performance of 40.72% mIoU on the test set.

#### 4.2. PIN2PAN and SYN2REAL Domain Gaps

- **Outdoor PIN2PAN (Cityscapes to DensePASS):**
 - Off-the-shelf CNN- and transformer-based models exhibited significant performance drops (∼40-50% mIoU) when directly applied to panoramic data.
 - Trans4PASS+ (Small) achieved 46.5% mIoU on DensePASS in the source-only setting, outperforming SegFormer-B2 (42.4%) with comparable computational complexity. When employing a Mask2Former head, Trans4PASS+ reached 48.4% mIoU.
 - The results suggested that distortion awareness in the architecture design (DMLPv2) improved generalization from pinhole to panoramic data.
- **Indoor PIN2PAN (Stanford2D3D Pinhole to Panoramic):**
 - Trans4PASS+ (Small) obtained 49.76% mIoU on Stanford2D3D Panoramic, with a smaller performance gap (-1.72%) compared to previous models like Trans4Trans (-21.36%).
- **SYN2REAL Gaps:**
 - For outdoor SYN2REAL (SynPASS to DensePASS), Trans4PASS+ (Small) achieved 43.83% mIoU in source-only setting, a +5.09% gain over PVT baseline.
 - For indoor SYN2REAL (Structured3D to Stanford2D3D Panoramic), Trans4PASS+ (Small) achieved 52.09% mIoU in source-only setting.
- **PIN2PAN vs. SYN2REAL (Source-Only):**
 - PIN2PAN (e.g., CS to DP) models generally performed better than SYN2REAL (e.g., SP to DP) in source-only settings, particularly in outdoor scenarios. This was attributed to the richer detailed texture cues in real pinhole datasets.
 - However, SYN2REAL models sometimes showed advantages on highly distorted objects like sidewalks, which present uncommon shapes and positions in panoramas, benefiting from the spatial distribution priors of synthetic panoramic data.
- **Zero-shot Generalization:** Trans4PASS+ (Small), trained solely on synthetic Structured3D (S3D8), achieved 52.09% mIoU on real panoramic Stanford2D3D (SPan8), surpassing the previous supervised state-of-the-art HRNet (52.00%) which was trained with additional real annotated data.

#### 4.3. Ablation Study of Trans4PASS+ Structure

- **Components:** The Deformable Patch Embedding (DPE), DMLPv1, and DMLPv2 modules consistently improved performance. DMLPv2, due to its enhanced token mixing, yielded additional gains (e.g., +1.8% mIoU over DMLPv1 in a non-DPE setting, and +4.05% with DPE).
- **PX and CX in DMLPv2:** Both the Pooling Mixer (PX) and Channel Mixer (CX) contributed significantly. Their combined use in DMLPv2 led to a substantial performance boost (+10.92% mIoU over baseline on DensePASS).
- **Multi-scale Pooling:** A multi-scale pooling combination of {3x3, 5x5, 11x11} kernels in PX yielded superior results.
- **Comparison of PE and MLP Methods:** DPE with DMLPv2 consistently outperformed other combinations of patch embedding (vanilla PE, deformable conv) and MLP variants (vanilla MLP, CycleMLP, ASMLP), demonstrating its adaptivity and effectiveness for panoramas.
- **Token Mixing Methods:** DMLPv2's parallel token mixing outperformed other existing token mixers (PoolFormer, FAN, or their combination), indicating an optimal balance for robust panoramic segmentation.

#### 4.4. Mutual Prototypical Adaptation (MPA) Strategy

- **Outdoor (Cityscapes to DensePASS):**
 - MPA-Trans4PASS+ (Small) achieved 59.43% mIoU, representing a +17.44% boost over the previous best method, P2PDA. It also surpassed other UDA methods like PCS (+5.60%) and DAFormer (+4.87%).
 - It outperformed multi-supervision methods that require significantly more data.
- **Indoor (Stanford2D3D Pinhole to Panoramic):**
 - MPA-Trans4PASS+ (Small) achieved 53.49% mIoU, performing comparably to a fully-supervised model (54.1%) trained with full access to target domain annotations. This marks the first time an unsupervised model reached this level of performance on Stanford2D3D.
- **SAM-enhanced MPA:** Integrating SAM for pseudo-label rectification was effective. While SAM alone or SAM combined with SSL showed limited improvements, SAM-enhanced MPA provided significant gains (e.g., +4.39% mIoU for SP13→DP13 and +2.47% for CS13→DP13 over standalone SAM), demonstrating that SAM effectively rectifies pseudo-labels and MPA leverages this improved quality.
- **Omnidirectional Segmentation:** MPA consistently improved mIoU across all eight angular directions of a panorama, indicating comprehensive adaptation.

#### 4.5. Qualitative Analysis

- Visualizations confirmed that Trans4PASS and Trans4PASS+ models produced more accurate segmentation results for distorted objects (e.g., trucks, sidewalks, pedestrians outdoors; doors, tables indoors) compared to baseline models.
- SAM-based MPA showed improved segmentation for objects like trucks and sidewalks that were missed by standalone SAM-enhanced predictions.
- Visual comparisons between PIN2PAN and SYN2REAL revealed that source-trained PIN2PAN models struggled with highly distorted objects (e.g., sidewalks) due to shape differences, while SYN2REAL models performed better in such cases due to learning shape priors from synthetic panoramas. Conversely, PIN2PAN models excelled at recognizing objects with rich textures (e.g., traffic signs) due to appearance similarity with real pinholes. MPA successfully bridged these domain gaps in both paradigms.
- t-SNE visualizations of feature embeddings demonstrated that MPA successfully aligned feature distributions between source and target domains, bringing mutual prototypes closer in the feature space.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of computer vision by addressing specific and critical challenges in panoramic semantic segmentation, offering advancements with significant implications:

1. **Robust Panoramic Scene Understanding:** The introduction of the Trans4PASS+ architecture, with its Deformable Patch Embedding (DPE) and Deformable MLP v2 (DMLPv2), provides an architecture designed to inherently handle the geometric distortions and object deformations prevalent in 360-degree images. This architectural innovation can lead to more accurate and reliable semantic understanding of panoramic scenes, which is crucial for real-world applications.

2. **Effective Unsupervised Domain Adaptation for Panoramas:** The Mutual Prototypical Adaptation (MPA) strategy offers a robust framework for transferring knowledge from label-rich source domains to unlabeled panoramic target domains. The integration of the Segment Anything Model (SAM) for pseudo-label rectification is a notable enhancement, demonstrating a method for improving the quality of self-training in UDA, which is a common challenge. This significantly reduces the dependency on expensive and time-consuming manual annotation of panoramic data.

3. **New Resource for Research:** The creation of the SynPASS dataset fills a gap in publicly available large-scale synthetic panoramic datasets for urban scenes. This dataset provides a valuable resource for training data-hungry models and, more importantly, facilitates systematic research into Synthetic-to-Real (SYN2REAL) domain adaptation for panoramic imagery, offering an alternative to traditional Pinhole-to-Panoramic (PIN2PAN) adaptation.

4. **Advancement of Vision Transformers:** This work demonstrates the adaptability and effectiveness of Vision Transformers for specialized vision tasks that involve complex geometric transformations, extending their utility beyond standard image recognition benchmarks. The deformable modules showcase how Transformers can be modified to better cope with specific data characteristics.

5. **Practical Applications:** The advancements in robust panoramic semantic segmentation have direct and substantial impact on several practical domains:
 - **Autonomous Driving:** Enabling holistic 360-degree scene perception for autonomous vehicles, enhancing safety and navigation capabilities by providing dense, pixel-level understanding of the entire surroundings.
 - **Augmented and Virtual Reality (AR/VR):** Improving immersive viewing experiences by providing accurate semantic context for 360-degree environments, facilitating more realistic object placement and interaction.
 - **Robotics:** Enhancing environmental perception for mobile robots operating in complex and unstructured 360-degree environments, aiding in navigation, object interaction, and task execution.

6. **Benchmarking and Future Directions:** The comprehensive experimental evaluation across multiple datasets and adaptation paradigms establishes new state-of-the-art benchmarks. The insights gained regarding PIN2PAN versus SYN2REAL adaptation provide guidance for future research, suggesting avenues for combining multiple source domains or integrating additional modalities like LiDAR data to further improve panoramic scene understanding.
