# Early to Share, Late to Save: Synchronisation-Driven Communication Gating in Bandwidth-Constrained Cooperative VLN

## Problem

- Existing cooperative Vision-Language Navigation (VLN) methods implicitly assume unlimited communication bandwidth between agents, making them impractical for real-world resource-constrained deployments.
- Learning effective communication policies for multi-agent systems using reinforcement learning (e.g., REINFORCE) often suffers from high variance due to delayed and noisy credit assignment.
- The 'when' of communication under bandwidth constraints, particularly in language-guided navigation and how it influences recurrent neural network states, remains largely unexplored.

## Method

- A novel problem formulation for bandwidth-constrained cooperative VLN is introduced, extending the R2R benchmark to a two-agent scenario with explicit per-agent transmission budgets and asymmetric path assignments.
- A stable, lightweight **hindsight communication gate** is proposed, trained using ==Binary Cross-Entropy (BCE)== loss on post-hoc labels derived from navigation failures, transforming communication learning into a supervised task.
- The gate's inputs are limited to the agent's ==hidden state== and remaining budget, allowing for empirical investigation into whether agents learn to communicate based on uncertainty or other emergent patterns.

## Results

- The hindsight-gated policy with **B=3** transmissions achieved **8.9%** SR and **8.4%** SPL on val-unseen, matching the performance of an unconstrained communication scenario (B=∞) and slightly surpassing no communication.
- Analysis revealed that **82.6%** of communications occurred within the first 0-2 steps of an episode, and agents fired messages more often when confident in their actions (**0.453** average confidence when sending vs. **0.413** when not sending).
- Learned gating (B=3) yielded a cumulative alignment gain of **+0.072** on val-unseen, significantly outperforming random gating (**+0.020**) and entropy-based gating (**+0.017**), demonstrating its efficiency in aligning agent internal states.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Agents operating under bandwidth constraints learn a 'synchronisation-driven' communication regime, prioritizing early communication when confident to align internal models, rather than communicating reactively when uncertain.
> - Early communication significantly boosts hidden-state alignment between agents, and this alignment effect persists and compounds over subsequent timesteps due to the recurrent nature of the GRU.
> - Hindsight gating provides a stable and efficient method for learning communication policies, avoiding the high variance of traditional policy gradient approaches in multi-agent reinforcement learning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated within the field of Vision-Language Navigation (VLN) and specifically addresses challenges in multi-agent cooperative VLN within bandwidth-constrained environments.

Traditional VLN research, exemplified by benchmarks such as Room-to-Room (R2R), focuses on a single embodied agent interpreting natural language instructions to navigate photorealistic indoor environments. Methods like Seq2Seq, Speaker-Follower, DUET, and HAMT have advanced single-agent navigation by improving instruction encoding, action planning, and handling continuous environments.

The extension of VLN to cooperative multi-agent settings has been explored by works such as Co-NavGPT and CAMON. These methods utilize Large Language Models (LLMs) to facilitate coordination and communication between multiple robots for visual semantic or multi-object navigation. A key characteristic of most prior cooperative navigation research, including these LLM-based approaches and earlier multi-agent communication frameworks like CommNet and MADDPG, is the implicit assumption of unlimited communication bandwidth. Agents are often presumed to share observations or messages freely at every step without incurring significant cost or constraint.

A subset of Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning (MARL) research has investigated learned communication. IC3Net, for instance, introduced a binary communication gate trained with REINFORCE, demonstrating that gated communication can be beneficial when agents are already competent. TarMAC explored attention-based message targeting. However, these methods typically do not focus on language-guided navigation, nor do they extensively analyze temporal communication patterns or hidden-state alignment. Furthermore, methods employing REINFORCE for gate learning often encounter high variance due to the delayed and noisy credit assignment problem in attributing episode success or failure to individual communication decisions. Farooq et al. addressed bandwidth efficiency through message compression in MARL, but their work focused on agents navigating to coordinates without language grounding and primarily on message size rather than timing.

This paper distinguishes itself by explicitly introducing and focusing on the problem of bandwidth-constrained cooperative VLN. It addresses the "when" of communication under a fixed budget, a question largely unexplored in language-guided navigation. By proposing a supervised learning approach for communication gating, it seeks to mitigate the training difficulties associated with high-variance policy gradients found in prior methods like IC3Net. The research also moves beyond examining *what* agents communicate (as in emergent communication) to characterize *when* they choose to communicate under resource limitations and *why* specific temporal patterns emerge, particularly in the context of recurrent neural networks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to investigate and address the challenge of communication management in bandwidth-constrained cooperative Vision-Language Navigation (VLN) systems. This objective is motivated by real-world application scenarios for multi-robot systems, such as search-and-rescue operations, warehouse automation, or multi-drone inspections. In these deployments, communication channels (e.g., radio, mesh networks) have inherent limitations, imposing strict constraints on the frequency of data transmission due to factors like energy budgets, network slot availability, or privacy regulations. Existing cooperative VLN methods generally assume unlimited communication, rendering them impractical for such resource-constrained environments.

A secondary, but significant, objective is to develop a more stable and efficient method for training communication gates compared to established reinforcement learning techniques. Prior approaches, particularly those employing REINFORCE, suffer from high variance in learning communication policies. This is because the causal link between a single communication decision at a specific timestep and the eventual success or failure of an episode (which may span 15-20 steps) is long and noisy, making credit assignment difficult. The paper aims to circumvent this issue by reframing the gate learning problem.

The specific contributions outlined in the paper, which align with these objectives, are:

1. **Introduction of Bandwidth-Constrained Cooperative VLN:** This work formalizes a new problem setting that extends the R2R benchmark to a two-agent scenario with explicit, hard per-agent transmission budgets. This formulation is designed to reflect realistic deployment constraints previously absent from cooperative VLN research.
2. **Proposal of Hindsight Gating:** To address the high variance in training communication policies, the authors propose a lightweight, stable, and Binary Cross-Entropy (BCE)-supervised communication gate. This gate is trained using post-hoc labels derived from observed navigation failures, converting a reinforcement learning problem into a supervised classification task. The gate's design intentionally limits its inputs to the agent's hidden state and remaining budget, without explicit uncertainty measures, to empirically investigate its learned firing patterns.
3. **Characterization of a Synchronisation-Driven Communication Regime:** Through empirical analysis of the trained gate's behavior, the research aims to characterize the temporal patterns of communication. Specifically, it seeks to determine whether agents learn to communicate when uncertain (the common "uncertainty-recovery hypothesis") or if another strategy emerges under bandwidth constraints. The observed outcome of early, confident communication is posited to represent a novel synchronisation-driven regime, where agents align their internal representations to leverage the recurrent nature of their policies.

In summary, the motivation is to bridge the gap between theoretical cooperative VLN methods and real-world resource limitations, while also improving the stability and interpretability of communication learning in multi-agent systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed in this research involves a novel problem formulation for bandwidth-constrained cooperative VLN, a three-phase training procedure for a communication gate based on hindsight supervision, and a detailed analysis of emergent communication patterns and hidden-state alignment.

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation

- **Setting:** The system involves two agents (N=2) navigating Matterport3D indoor environments, guided by a shared natural language instruction. At each timestep *t*, agent *i* receives a visual observation (CLIP ViT-B/32 features), any messages from its partner, and its remaining normalized transmission budget *b_rem(t)*.
- **Asymmetric Path Assignment:** To create a realistic information asymmetry, agents are assigned complementary sub-paths from the same R2R episode. Agent 0 navigates the full path, while Agent 1 starts at the midpoint and navigates the second half. Both agents receive the full instruction. This design positions Agent 1 as an information source with privileged goal-region knowledge, providing context to Agent 0, the primary navigator. Navigation Success Rate (SR) is primarily reported for Agent 0.
- **Bandwidth Constraint:** Each agent is restricted to a maximum of *B* messages per episode. Communication incurs a fixed cost per message. A binary indicator *g_i(t) ∈ {0, 1}* denotes whether agent *i* transmits at time *t*. The message transmitted is the agent's cross-modal context vector *c_i(t) ∈ R^512*, which is a language-conditioned visual representation.

#### 3.2. Navigation Backbone

Each agent utilizes a common architecture:
- **Feature Extraction:** Frozen CLIP ViT-B/32 is used for encoding visual observations (36 panoramic directions × 512 dimensions) and language instructions.
- **CrossModalAttention:** A module generates a context vector *c_i(t) ∈ R^512* by attending over language tokens using the current visual observation as a query.
- **Recurrent State Management:** A Gated Recurrent Unit (GRU) maintains the agent's hidden state *h_i(t)*. The GRU update function incorporates the previous hidden state *h_i(t-1)*, the current context vector *c_i(t)*, any aggregated partner message *m_i(t)* (zero if no message received), and the embedding of the previous action *a_t-1*.
- **NavigationHead:** Candidate actions are scored via a direct dot product between the agent's hidden state *h_i(t)* and the CLIP features of candidate viewpoints *φ_k*.

#### 3.3. Hindsight Communication Gating

The training of the communication gate proceeds in three distinct phases:

1. **Phase 1: Single-Agent Navigation Pre-training:**
 - The navigation backbone is initially trained using imitation learning (cross-entropy loss against ground-truth paths with teacher forcing).
 - This phase establishes a baseline navigation policy (π_nav) without any inter-agent communication, setting up the failure modes that will later be used to supervise the gate.

2. **Phase 2: Hindsight Label Collection and Gate Training:**
 - The pre-trained π_nav is executed on paired training episodes without any messages exchanged between agents.
 - At each timestep *t*, a binary label *y_i(t)* is assigned to agent *i*. A step is labeled as "communication-critical" (*y_i(t)=1*) only if two conditions are met: (1) agent *i*'s predicted action *ˆa_i(t)* is incorrect, and (2) its partner agent *j* (where *j≠i*) already knows the correct action *a_j*(t)*. If agent *i* is correct, or if both agents are incorrect, the step is labeled *y_i(t)=0*. This labeling scheme serves as a proxy for the expected value of communication, identifying situations where a partner's message could genuinely aid navigation.
 - A lightweight, 3-layer Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) gate (π_gate) is then trained. Its inputs are agent *i*'s hidden state *h_i(t)* and the remaining budget *b_rem(t)*. The gate is trained with Binary Cross-Entropy (BCE) loss, predicting the probability of sending a message (*p_send*). This supervised approach avoids the high variance associated with policy gradients. The explicit input of *b_rem(t)* allows the gate to implicitly consider the budget depletion over time.
 - The gate is designed without explicit entropy or confidence scores as inputs to determine empirically whether it learns to fire under high or low uncertainty.
 - **Inference:** During evaluation, at each timestep, the gate computes *p_send*. If *p_send* exceeds a threshold (τ = 0.4) and the budget permits, agent *i* broadcasts its context vector *c_i(t)* and decrements its budget.

3. **Phase 3: Joint Fine-Tuning:**
 - After the gate (π_gate) is trained, both agents' navigation policies (π_nav) are jointly fine-tuned. During this phase, the trained gate is deployed to control communication, allowing the navigation policies to adapt to receiving partner messages at the communication-critical steps identified by the gate. The gate itself is frozen to prevent catastrophic forgetting of its learned communication policy.

#### 3.4. Evaluation Metrics and Baselines

- **Navigation Performance:** Evaluated using Success Rate (SR) (agent stops within 3 meters of goal) and Success weighted by Path Length (SPL). Evaluation is conducted on val_seen (buildings seen during training) and val_unseen (novel buildings) splits of the R2R dataset.
- **Communication Patterns and Alignment:** The core analysis involves measuring the cosine similarity between Agent 0's and Agent 1's GRU hidden states at each timestep. The metric *Δ* is defined as the difference in similarity between conditions with and without communication.
- **Communication Policies for Comparison:**
 - **Learned gate (ours):** The proposed hindsight-gated policy.
 - **Random:** Fires at the same rate as the learned gate but at uniformly random steps.
 - **Entropy-based:** Fires when the agent's action entropy is highest (implementing the uncertainty-recovery hypothesis as a heuristic).
 - **Always:** Communicates every step until the budget is exhausted.
 - **None:** No communication (baseline).
- **Statistical Significance:** Paired t-tests are used to confirm the significance of reported differences in alignment gains.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several findings regarding navigation performance, emergent communication patterns, and hidden-state alignment, particularly under bandwidth constraints.

#### 4.1. Navigation Performance

- The baseline single-agent navigation policy achieved 43.2% SR on val-seen and 9.2% SR on val-unseen. The relatively low val-unseen SR is attributed to training without speaker-augmented data, indicating that the backbone agents exhibit limited generalization initially.
- In the cooperative setting, with a budget of B=3 transmissions, hindsight-gated communication for Agent 0 (the full-path navigator) achieved 8.9% SR and 8.4% SPL on val-unseen. This marginally exceeded the no-communication baseline (8.7% SR, 8.2% SPL) and matched the full-communication (B=∞) scenario (8.9% SR, 8.4% SPL).
- While the absolute SR improvement was modest, the ability of the B=3 learned gate to match full-communication performance and slightly surpass the no-communication baseline suggests that selective early communication, even from a partner with only partial path knowledge, can be beneficial for navigation. Agent 1's SR was not directly comparable due to its shorter sub-path.

#### 4.2. Emergent Communication Patterns

Analysis of the trained gate's firing patterns revealed two consistent observations across all tested budget levels (B ∈ {1, 3, 5}) on val-unseen data:
- **Early Concentration:** Communication transmissions predominantly occurred in the initial steps of an episode (steps 0-2), with a significant reduction in firing rate from step 3 onwards. For B=3, 82.6% of transmissions occurred in steps 0-2.
- **High Confidence at Send:** Contrary to the intuitive "uncertainty-recovery hypothesis," the gate fired more often when the agent exhibited higher confidence in its action choice (higher maximum action softmax probability) compared to when it did not send a message. For B=3, the average confidence when sending was 0.453, versus 0.413 when not sending.
These patterns indicate a "synchronisation-driven" communication regime, where agents prioritize early information exchange to align their internal models, rather than communicating reactively when facing high uncertainty or confusion.

#### 4.3. Hidden-State Alignment Analysis

To empirically test the synchronisation hypothesis, the cosine similarity between Agent 0's and Agent 1's GRU hidden states was measured. The cumulative alignment gain (sum of ∆ over timesteps) was compared across various communication policies at a matched budget of B=3 transmissions:
- **Superiority of Learned Gating:** Learned gating achieved a cumulative alignment gain of +0.072 on val-unseen, significantly outperforming heuristic policies. This represented a 260% greater alignment gain than random gating (+0.020) and a 320% greater gain than entropy-based gating (+0.017).
- **Ineffectiveness of Uncertainty-Recovery:** The entropy-based gating strategy, which explicitly implements the uncertainty-recovery hypothesis by communicating when action entropy is highest, performed worse than random gating in terms of alignment gain. This provides direct evidence against the efficacy of communicating primarily when uncertain under bandwidth constraints.
- **Consistency Across Splits:** The performance hierarchy (learned > always > random > entropy > none) remained consistent across both val-seen and val-unseen datasets.
- **Alignment Scalability with Budget:** Cumulative alignment gain increased sharply from B=1 (+0.034) to B=3 (+0.057) but showed signs of saturation beyond B=3 (+0.059 for B=5). This suggests that early synchronisation might yield diminishing returns after a certain level of initial communication.
- **Efficient Approximation of Unconstrained Communication:** Learned gating with only B=3 transmissions achieved a cumulative alignment gain (+0.072) that approached the performance of always-communicate (B=∞) with full budget (+0.078). This indicates high communication efficiency.
- **Recurrent Propagation Effect:** The alignment gain (∆) increased over the course of an episode even after communication largely ceased in later steps. For example, at step 5, the learned gate showed ∆=+0.020, significantly higher than random gating's +0.006, despite low gate firing rates at that stage. This effect supports the hypothesis that early messages inject alignment into the GRU hidden state, which then propagates and compounds through subsequent recurrent updates without requiring further transmissions.
- **Statistical Significance:** Differences in ∆ values between learned and random gating were statistically significant (p < 0.001 at steps 1 and 2, p < 0.01 at step 5), confirming the robustness of the recurrent propagation effect.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces a new perspective on communication strategies for multi-agent systems, particularly in environments with resource limitations. Its findings have several implications for both theoretical understanding and practical applications.

#### 5.1. Novel Communication Regime

The primary significance lies in identifying and empirically validating a "synchronisation-driven" communication regime. This contrasts with the more commonly assumed "uncertainty-recovery" paradigm. The finding that agents learn to communicate early and when confident, rather than reactively when uncertain, challenges intuitive notions of multi-agent coordination. For recurrent policies, early communication of grounded trajectory representations significantly aligns hidden states, and this alignment persists and compounds over time due to the recurrent nature of the GRU. This insight suggests that effective communication under tight budgets is about proactive alignment of internal models rather than reactive problem-solving.

#### 5.2. Methodological Contribution: Hindsight Gating

The introduction of hindsight gating offers a stable and low-variance alternative to traditional reinforcement learning methods (like REINFORCE) for training communication gates. By transforming the complex credit assignment problem into a supervised classification task, this approach facilitates more reliable and interpretable learning of communication policies. This methodological innovation could be applied to other multi-agent tasks where communication timing is critical and policy gradient-based methods are challenging.

#### 5.3. Practical Implications for Resource-Constrained Systems

The identified synchronisation regime provides a guiding principle for designing communication protocols in bandwidth-limited multi-robot systems. Instead of continuously monitoring uncertainty and communicating reactively, systems could be optimized to prioritize early, foundational information exchange to establish a shared understanding or aligned internal states among agents. This could lead to more energy-efficient, robust, and effective multi-robot deployments in areas like search-and-rescue, logistics, or remote exploration where communication bandwidth or energy is a critical constraint.

#### 5.4. Broader Applicability

While demonstrated in the context of VLN with recurrent policies, the principles derived from this research, particularly the benefits of early synchronisation and the impact of recurrent propagation, could be relevant to a wider range of multi-agent tasks involving sequential decision-making and shared context, especially where agents rely on internal state representations.

#### 5.5. Future Research Directions

The research also highlights areas for future investigation. The observation that navigation SR improvement was modest in the low-competence baseline suggests a prerequisite condition: communication benefits might be more pronounced when base agents possess a higher individual navigation competence, consistent with prior work. Further research could explore this competence threshold. Additionally, the current formulation uses a fixed asymmetric role assignment and N=2 agents in discrete environments. Extensions to symmetric roles, larger numbers of agents (N>2), continuous environments with kinematic constraints, and the integration of message targeting mechanisms could provide further insights into bandwidth-constrained cooperative navigation.
