# HALOMI: Learning Humanoid Loco-Manipulation with Active Perception from Human Demonstrations

> [!summary] Summary
> HALOMI presents a framework for enabling humanoid robots to learn complex loco-manipulation skills, including active perception, directly from human demonstrations. The method utilizes a manifold-constrained whole-body controller and a specialized data processing pipeline to bridge the embodiment gap, achieving success rates up to 90% on various real-world tasks with a Unitree G1 humanoid.

## Problem

- Acquiring scalable and high-quality demonstration data for complex humanoid loco-manipulation policies is challenging and resource-intensive using traditional real-robot teleoperation.
- Existing robot-free human demonstration frameworks often do not effectively transfer active perception (human-like gaze shifts and head movements) to humanoids and struggle with the embodiment gap in observations and actions.
- Directly applying sparse, world-frame head-hand trajectories from human demonstrations to humanoid whole-body controllers can lead to instability and difficulties in inferring stable full-body motion due to under-specification.

## Method

![[2606.18772-method.png]]
*The control architecture utilizes a learned latent behavior manifold (BFM-Zero) to translate sparse head-hand targets into stable, natural whole-body motions.*

- An augmented robot-free human demonstration system, featuring bimanual grippers and egocentric sensing with an active neck on the humanoid, is used to capture detailed hand-eye coordination.
- A ==manifold-constrained whole-body controller==, based on a learned latent behavior space (BFM-Zero), is developed to generate natural and stable full-body motions from sparse head and hand targets, ensuring robust execution.
- An automated human data processing pipeline, including ego-view alignment and controller-aware reference trajectory adaptation, systematically reduces visual and action-execution discrepancies between human demonstrations and humanoid capabilities.

## Results

- The manifold-constrained whole-body controller achieved average tracking errors (E_MGBP) of **0.0409-0.1163m** on unseen human trajectories, and HALOMI reached high real-world success on a Unitree G1 for Bag Transfer (**90%**), Pick Bread and Place (**85%**), and Transfer Towel to Basket (**80%**).
- Ego-view alignment was essential, improving Bag Transfer success from **10%** to **90%**, and controller-aware trajectory adaptation reduced tracking errors by approximately **6.7%** and improved task success rates (e.g., Pick Bread and Place from **75%** to **85%**).
- Disabling active perception significantly reduced task success rates (e.g., Bag Transfer from **90%** to **30%**), even in cases where a static view might seem sufficient, highlighting its importance for preserving learned hand-eye coupling.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Active perception, specifically dynamic head movements and gaze shifts, is crucial for humanoids to successfully perform multi-stage, long-horizon manipulation tasks by maintaining task-relevant visual context.
> - Bridging the embodiment gap through both observation alignment (human to humanoid ego-view) and action adaptation (optimizing human trajectories for robot execution) is critical for robust and successful skill transfer.
> - Learning whole-body control within a latent behavior manifold effectively generates stable and natural full-body motions from sparse, high-level commands, overcoming the under-specification challenges in direct joint-space control.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Humanoid robots are recognized for their potential to operate in human-centric environments due to their human-like morphology and capacity for whole-body coordination. However, a significant challenge in robotics research is the acquisition of large-scale, high-quality demonstration data for training complex loco-manipulation policies. Traditional methods, such as teleoperation, are often expensive, time-consuming, and demand access to physical humanoid platforms and skilled operators.

In response to these limitations, robot-free human demonstrations have emerged as a scalable alternative. These demonstrations leverage interfaces like the Universal Manipulation Interface (UMI) to collect human interaction data without requiring a physical robot. Prior work has successfully applied UMI-style interfaces to fixed-base manipulators and, more recently, to mobile platforms. A key aspect of human demonstrations is the natural integration of active perception, where humans coordinate their head and hand movements to maintain task-relevant visual context throughout a task. This active perception is critical for learning long-horizon manipulation tasks involving multi-stage gaze changes.

Some research has extended robot-free demonstrations to humanoid loco-manipulation by augmenting UMI interfaces with additional lower-body references, such as pelvis or foot targets. While these methods help specify humanoid whole-body motion, they require demonstrators to provide lower-body information during data collection, which can be burdensome. Furthermore, these systems often do not explicitly address the transfer of active ego-view (head motion and gaze changes) from humans to humanoids, which is a limiting factor for tasks requiring continuous gaze shifts.

HALOMI positions itself within this landscape by focusing on a task-centric head-hand demonstration interface that prioritizes manipulation-relevant hand motions and active head movements, allowing a whole-body controller to infer the remaining full-body motion. This approach directly confronts several challenges: the embodiment gap between humans and humanoids in both observation (ego-view discrepancies due to morphology and camera differences) and action execution, the brittleness of directly executing world-frame head-hand trajectories without specified lower-body commands, and the potential for non-negligible tracking errors when human-derived targets are directly applied to humanoid controllers. By addressing these issues, HALOMI aims to provide a more scalable and robust framework for transferring complex loco-manipulation skills with active perception from human demonstrations to humanoid robots.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of HALOMI is to establish a scalable and robust framework for learning humanoid loco-manipulation skills, including active perception, directly from human demonstrations. This overarching goal is motivated by several specific challenges inherent in current humanoid robotics research:

1. **Scalability of Data Collection**: Traditional methods for collecting humanoid locomotion and manipulation data, such as real-robot teleoperation, are resource-intensive. The research is motivated by the need for a more scalable data source that does not require access to a physical humanoid robot or highly skilled teleoperators. Human demonstrations, being collectable at scale, offer a promising alternative for training vision-language-action (VLA) models for humanoids.

2. **Integration of Active Perception**: Human demonstrations naturally capture active hand-eye coordination and gaze shifts, which are essential for long-horizon, multi-stage tasks involving visual search, grasping, and placement. A key motivation is to enable humanoid robots to replicate this active perception, allowing them to dynamically adjust their viewpoint to maintain task-relevant visual context. Previous robot-free demonstration frameworks often focused on fixed-base or wheeled platforms, or did not explicitly integrate active ego-view transfer for humanoids.

3. **Bridging the Human-to-Humanoid Embodiment Gap**: Significant differences exist between human and humanoid morphology, observation capabilities (e.g., camera placement relative to head), and action execution dynamics. Direct transfer of human demonstrations to humanoids often fails due to:
 - **Ego-view Observation Mismatch**: Differences in viewpoint between a human demonstrator and a robot's head-mounted camera can lead to visual discrepancies that mislead learned policies.
 - **Action Execution Mismatch**: Raw human head-hand trajectories may be difficult or infeasible for a humanoid whole-body controller to track precisely, leading to unstable or inaccurate movements, especially when lower-body motions are underspecified.

4. **Robust Whole-Body Control for Sparse Targets**: The chosen task-centric interface, which provides only head and hand targets, leaves the majority of the humanoid's whole-body motion (torso, legs, feet) underspecified. This requires the robot's controller to infer feasible and stable whole-body movements from sparse world-frame keypoint targets. Developing a controller that can achieve precise and robust tracking of these sparse, potentially out-of-distribution (OOD) targets, while maintaining natural and stable whole-body motion, is a significant technical motivation.

To address these motivations, HALOMI proposes:
- An **augmented UMI-style egocentric data collection protocol** to capture detailed hand-eye coordination.
- A **manifold-constrained whole-body controller** designed for precise and robust tracking of head-hand targets by planning in a learned latent behavior manifold, thus managing the under-specification of whole-body motion.
- An **automated human data processing procedure**, including ego-view alignment and controller-aware reference trajectory adaptation, to systematically reduce the human-to-humanoid embodiment gap in both observation and action spaces.
- A **hierarchical learning framework** that leverages these components for effective skill transfer.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

HALOMI is structured around four main components: a human data collection and robot hardware system, a manifold-constrained whole-body controller, an automated human data processing pipeline, and a high-level VLA policy training and deployment framework.

**A. Data Collection & Robot System**
- **Human Data Collection**: A robot-free system is employed for data acquisition. Demonstrators utilize bimanual Agilex Robotics Pika Sense handheld grippers, which capture 6-DOF trajectories and gripper-width signals. Wearable egocentric sensing is achieved via a helmet equipped with an Intel RealSense D435i camera and an HTC VIVE Tracker 3.0. External Lighthouse base stations provide millimeter-level accurate 6-DOF tracking of grippers and head. The system collects multi-view visual observations (local gripper-centric RGB from Pika Sense, global egocentric RGB from RealSense) and action streams, all synchronized at 30 Hz.
- **Robot Hardware**: The Unitree G1 humanoid robot serves as the platform. It is augmented with robot-mounted Pika grippers, designed to mimic the human handheld grippers in geometry, layout, and camera placement, thereby reducing the gripper-centric visual gap. A custom 3-DoF active neck is designed and mounted on the robot's head. This servo-driven neck provides yaw, pitch, and roll control for the head-mounted camera, with rotational axes approximately aligned with the camera's optical center to minimize translational offsets.

**B. Manifold-Constrained Whole-Body Control System**
- **Control Interface**: The system tracks sparse, manipulation-aware world-frame head and hand targets (6-DoF for hands, 3D position for head). The 3-DoF active neck controller analytically converts head orientation targets into joint commands, decoupled from the body controller.
- **Problem Statement**: Directly learning a world-frame tracking policy in raw joint action space is prone to instability and aggressive, unnatural motions due to its multi-modal and under-constrained nature when only sparse targets are provided.
- **Solution: Latent Behavior Manifold**: HALOMI addresses this by planning over a learned behavior manifold. It utilizes BFM-Zero, a model that learns a spherical latent behavior space, where each latent action decodes into a natural and stable whole-body motion. The RL tracking policy is trained to output latent actions within this BFM-Zero space.
- **Training Strategy**: A teacher-student training approach is used, trained on a large-scale loco-manipulation motion dataset (>6,000 sequences).
 - **Teacher Policy (π_tea)**: Trained via RL, it observes task tracking observations (whole-body reference targets, tracking errors, root dynamics), privileged simulation states, and future reference motions. It outputs a 128-D latent command, projected onto the BFM-Zero spherical latent space, and decoded by the frozen BFM-Zero policy into joint commands. Rewards encourage tracking accuracy for hand poses and head position, alongside regularization for smooth behaviors.
 - **Student Policy (π_stu)**: Distilled from the teacher using DAgger. It observes deployable proprioceptive history and only world-frame head-hand tracking errors as task observations. The student predicts a 128-D latent command, which is similarly projected and decoded by BFM-Zero. The student policy is optimized to minimize the L2 distance between its predicted latent command and that of the teacher.

**C. Human Data Processing to Bridge the Embodiment Gap**
An offline, two-step data processing module is implemented to reduce visual and action-execution discrepancies between human demonstrations and humanoid capabilities.
- **1) Ego-view Alignment**: This procedure adapts human egocentric observations to the humanoid's viewpoint. It first reconstructs the human ego-view into a 3D scene using a learning-based single-view depth estimation model. This 3D representation is then reprojected to the humanoid's viewpoint, and image inpainting fills in regions revealed by the new viewpoint.
- **2) Controller-Aware Reference Trajectory Adaptation**: This step adjusts raw human-derived head-hand reference trajectories (`x_ref_t = [p_L_t, p_R_t, p_H_t, R_L_t, R_R_t]`) to ensure better reproduction by the humanoid's whole-body controller.
 - The adaptation modifies the translational components (`p_ref_t`) using a residual trajectory `r_t`, parameterized by B-splines (`˜p_t = p_ref_t + r_t`).
 - An initial residual `r_0_t` is obtained by simulating the raw reference trajectory with the controller and calculating the tracking error (`e_t = p_ref_t - p_exec_t`).
 - A coarse-to-fine adaptation strategy is employed, decomposing `r_t` into `r_global` (full-horizon) and `r_local` (chunk-wise). Initial B-spline control points are fitted from `r_0`.
 - Optimization of these B-spline control points is performed using CEM-based sampling. Candidate control point sets are evaluated in parallel simulation environments, scored based on tracking accuracy and executed trajectory smoothness. The sample distribution is iteratively updated from the elite set of best-performing candidates.
 - The best adapted trajectory, if it improves over the original, is then selected.

**D. Loco-Manipulation Policy Learning and Deployment**
- **High-level Policy Training**: The processed human demonstration data is used to fine-tune a pre-trained VLA model (π_0.5). This high-level policy takes synchronized RGB images (egocentric and gripper-centric) and language instructions as input.
- **Action Representation**: The policy predicts action chunks, which are represented as relative trajectories for the left hand, right hand, and head. For each target, the k-th relative pose within an action chunk at time `t` is defined as `p_k_t = p_t+k - p_t` and `R_k_t = R_t^-1 R_t+k`. Continuous gripper values are appended to the hand relative poses.
- **Deployment**: During inference, an asynchronous image acquisition thread provides synchronized camera streams to the VLA policy. The VLA predicts an action chunk, which is then converted into a sequence of world-frame Cartesian targets. These targets are streamed at 50 Hz to the manifold-constrained whole-body controller, which tracks them while maintaining balance and generating the remaining whole-body motion.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of HALOMI comprised both simulation and real-world experiments on a Unitree G1 humanoid, addressing loco-manipulation capabilities, the effectiveness of human data processing, the role of active perception, and generalization abilities across five diverse tasks.

**A. Loco-Manipulation Capability (Q1)**
- **Controller Performance**: The manifold-constrained whole-body controller demonstrated precise and robust tracking of sparse head-hand targets. On unseen human trajectories, it achieved average global tracking errors (E_MGBP) ranging from 0.0409m to 0.1163m across three tasks (Pick Bread and Place, Transfer Towel to Basket, Squat-and-Grasp), with adaptation further reducing these errors. Crucially, the controller maintained stable and non-aggressive behavior even under challenging out-of-distribution (OOD) target commands, in contrast to raw action-space tracking which lost balance.
- **Overall System Success Rates**: HALOMI achieved high success rates on three quantitatively evaluated real-world tasks:
 - Bag Transfer: 90%
 - Pick Bread and Place: 85%
 - Transfer Towel to Basket: 80%
- **Qualitative Capabilities**: For "Tossing," the robot performed a dynamic object-release motion, and for "Squat-and-Grasp," it executed a deep squat to grasp a low-positioned object, stood up, and placed it. These qualitative demonstrations indicated the system's ability to support dynamic behaviors and whole-body posture adaptation from sparse head-hand targets.

**B. Human Data Processing Improvement (Q2)**
- **Ego-view Alignment**: This component proved critical for task success. In the Bag Transfer task, removing ego-view alignment caused the success rate to drop from 90% to 10%. Failures typically involved the robot approaching the target but stopping with a constant offset, indicating that the human-to-humanoid ego-viewpoint gap biased the learned visual-action mapping.
- **Controller-Aware Reference Trajectory Adaptation**:
 - **Tracking Error Reduction**: Reference adaptation consistently reduced tracking errors across all three quantitatively evaluated tasks, yielding an average error reduction of approximately 6.725% in E_MGBP.
 - **Task Success and Quality**: In "Pick Bread and Place," adaptation improved the success rate from 75% to 85%, leading to more accurate grasps and precise placements. For "Transfer Towel to Basket," the success rate improved from 75% to 80%, with qualitative observations indicating more accurate grasps, smoother whole-body execution, and steadier ego-view observations during object transport and placement.

**C. Role of Active Perception (Q3)**
- **Tasks Requiring Active Viewpoint Changes**: For "Bag Transfer," disabling active neck control reduced the success rate to 30%, with failures occurring when the cabinet moved out of the central field of view. For "Transfer Towel to Basket," disabling active neck control reduced the success rate from 80% to 10%, as the robot could not actively search for the basket during placement. These results highlight the importance of active head-view control for multi-stage tasks involving changing visual foci.
- **Tasks Solvable with a Static View**: Even in "Pick Bread and Place," where a static ego-view could theoretically suffice, disabling active head control reduced the success rate from 85% to 20%. This suggests that active perception is not only for expanding the visual field but also for preserving the hand-eye coupling learned from human demonstrations.

**D. Generalization Ability (Q4)**
- **Unseen Object Appearances**: In "Transfer Towel to Basket," HALOMI achieved a 60% success rate with novel towels, demonstrating object-level visual generalization.
- **Unseen Spatial Configurations**:
 - "Bag Transfer": The policy maintained a 60% success rate with OOD cabinet placements, indicating generalization to unseen target locations through the relative head-hand pose interface.
 - "Pick Bread and Place": The system was robust to global translations when the relative bread-to-plate layout was preserved (6/10 success), enabling the robot to reach far-away targets beyond the demonstrated workspace. However, when the relative bread-to-plate layout was changed, the success rate dropped to 0/10, as the required hand-target trajectory for placement was not covered by the demonstration dataset.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The HALOMI framework presents advancements in learning complex humanoid loco-manipulation skills, with several areas of significance and potential impact:

1. **Scalable Skill Acquisition for Humanoids**: HALOMI demonstrates a practical and scalable approach to acquire complex whole-body loco-manipulation skills for humanoids by leveraging readily available human demonstrations. This reduces the dependency on costly and time-consuming real-robot teleoperation, making it feasible to collect larger and more diverse datasets for humanoid skill learning.

2. **Effective Transfer of Active Perception**: The explicit integration and validation of active perception capabilities, through an active neck and ego-view alignment, represents a step in enabling humanoids to exhibit human-like gaze coordination. This capability is crucial for long-horizon tasks, complex environments, and dynamic interactions, where robots must actively acquire relevant visual information.

3. **Robust Embodiment Gap Bridging**: The proposed human data processing pipeline, encompassing ego-view alignment and controller-aware reference trajectory adaptation, addresses fundamental challenges in transferring human movements to robots with different morphologies and dynamics. These techniques systematically reduce discrepancies in observation and action execution, which is critical for successful and robust sim-to-real and human-to-robot transfer.

4. **Enhanced Humanoid Control Robustness**: The manifold-constrained whole-body controller offers a robust solution for executing sparse, high-level head-hand targets on under-actuated humanoid platforms. By planning in a learned latent behavior manifold, the controller generates stable, natural, and feasible whole-body motions, even when confronted with challenging or out-of-distribution commands. This contributes to safer and more reliable humanoid operation in real-world settings.

5. **Foundation for Advanced Humanoid Robotics**: The HALOMI framework provides a robust foundation for future research in several directions. It can facilitate the development of more generally capable humanoid robots that can adapt to unstructured human-centric environments, learn from a broader range of human experiences, and execute more complex and dynamic tasks. The hierarchical learning structure also provides a clear pathway for integrating advanced VLA models with low-level, high-fidelity robot control.

6. **Impact on VLA Model Grounding**: By providing a reliable method to ground VLA models trained on human data into real-world humanoid robot execution, HALOMI can accelerate research in vision-language-action learning, making human demonstrations a more potent data source for developing intelligent robotic agents.
