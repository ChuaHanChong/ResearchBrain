# Satellite Trajectory Optimization via Proximal Policy Optimization for Space Debris Avoidance

> [!summary] Summary
> A PPO agent for autonomous satellite collision avoidance in GEO. It is trained inside an open-source astrodynamics simulator with Newtonian two-body dynamics, Sun and Moon third-body perturbations, and fuel-aware thrust, using curriculum learning plus shaped rewards for survival, projected miss distance, and delta-v conservation. Over 1,000 deterministic episodes it avoids collisions 97.5% of the time versus 27.5% for an impulsive planner and 20.7% rule-based.

## Problem

- Conjunction handling stays manual and human-in-the-loop, leaving analysts roughly two actionable alerts per satellite weekly, a process that cannot scale to megaconstellation traffic.
- Fixed collision-probability thresholds force crude trade-offs: conservative settings drove roughly 50,000 Starlink maneuvers in six months, depleting propellant without globally optimizing safety against mission lifetime.
- Prior learning-based avoidance work is largely proprietary or unreleased, offering no open simulator, shared seeds, or reproducible evaluation pipeline for comparing controllers.

## Method

- A Gym-compatible ==astrodynamics simulator== propagates two-body motion with Sun and Moon third-body perturbations, semi-implicit Euler steps of 1 second, rocket-equation fuel depletion, and CelesTrak TLE-seeded debris.
- A ==PPO== actor-critic MLP of 256-256-128 units, with ==clipped surrogate objective== and ==GAE==, outputs continuous three-axis thrust from satellite state, residual fuel, and up to 100 debris positions.
- A three-stage ==curriculum== raises collision probability from 0.4 to 1.0 and debris radius from 25 to 100 m, paired with shaped survival, projected-miss, milestone, delta-v, large-burn and jitter terms.

## Results

- PPO reached **97.5%** success across **1,000** deterministic GEO episodes with **25** collisions and **5** fuel depletions, versus **38%** for DQN, **27.5%** impulsive, **20.7%** rule-based, **0%** no-action.
- Collision rate fell **76.8** percentage points below the rule-based controller and **70.0** below the impulsive planner; mean episode length rose to **976** steps against roughly **277** to **321**.
- Safety cost propellant: PPO burned **254 kg** and **888 m/s** delta-v against the impulsive planner's **4 kg** and **12 m/s**, but held **1,429 m** mean minimum separation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Continuous-action policies beat discretized ones on long-horizon orbital control: a six-direction fixed action set drove unstable wide orbits and 62% collisions under identical rewards.
> - Reward weighting is the real design knob; penalizing collision far above propellant yields aggressive early burns and a sixty-fold fuel gap over impulsive control.
> - Results assume noise-free full state and an idealized thrust model, so reported success rates upper-bound what partially observed onboard deployment would achieve.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The Earth's orbital environment, particularly Low Earth Orbit (LEO) and Geosynchronous Equatorial Orbit (GEO), is experiencing increasing congestion due to the proliferation of satellites, including large megaconstellations, and accumulating space debris. This congestion leads to a growing frequency of conjunction alerts and collision risks, threatening the long-term sustainability of space activities and accelerating the potential for a cascading collision scenario known as the Kessler Syndrome. Current collision avoidance practices often involve manual or rule-based systems, which are increasingly challenged by the dynamic and rapidly expanding orbital population. These conventional methods are typically non-autonomous, proprietary, and may lack the long-term orbital considerations and scalability required for future space traffic management.

Existing manual and human-in-the-loop approaches rely on space surveillance networks to issue Conjunction Data Messages (CDMs), which operators then filter and assess to plan avoidance maneuvers. This process is time-consuming, resource-intensive, and prone to delays, as demonstrated by incidents such as the 2019 near-miss between ESA’s Aeolus and a SpaceX Starlink satellite. Limitations of these traditional methods include their lack of scalability, potential for suboptimal fuel consumption due to fixed probability thresholds, challenges in coordination between multiple operators, and insufficient transparency in data sharing.

In response to these challenges, the broader research landscape is increasingly exploring the application of machine learning (ML) and artificial intelligence (AI) for space traffic management. Early applications have focused on enhancing conjunction analysis and decision support, such as the European Space Agency's (ESA) Kessler library for predicting collision threats using Bayesian deep learning, and NASA's Conjunction Assessment Risk Analysis (CARA) program, which investigates AI/ML classifiers for automated maneuver decisions. Reinforcement Learning (RL) has emerged as a promising approach for autonomous maneuver planning and execution, particularly for sequential decision-making under uncertainty, where trade-offs between safety, fuel efficiency, and mission objectives must be balanced. Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO), a deep RL algorithm, has gained attention for its stability and efficiency in complex decision spaces, with recent studies applying it to LEO satellite collision avoidance.

This research contributes to this evolving landscape by developing an RL-driven autonomous collision avoidance system, specifically for geostationary satellites, using PPO. A key aspect of this work is the creation of a publicly available, high-fidelity astrodynamics simulation environment and a reproducible pipeline, which addresses the noted limitation of many industrial ML implementations remaining proprietary and opaque. By open-sourcing their framework, the authors aim to facilitate community validation, peer review, and the development of unified avoidance strategies, thereby advancing the field of autonomous space traffic management.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the escalating orbital congestion and the inherent limitations of current manual and rule-based collision avoidance systems. The rapid growth of satellite populations, particularly megaconstellations, combined with existing space debris, is increasing the frequency of close conjunctions and fragmentation events, which poses a significant threat to the long-term sustainability and safety of orbital operations. Traditional methods, characterized by human-in-the-loop decision-making, manual coordination, and fixed operational thresholds, are demonstrating a lack of scalability, responsiveness, and optimality in managing the increasing complexity of the orbital environment. This situation necessitates a transition towards more autonomous and intelligent solutions.

Based on this motivation, the key objectives of the study are defined as follows:

1. **Develop an Autonomous Collision Avoidance System:** The central objective is to design and implement a machine learning-driven system capable of autonomously planning and executing collision avoidance maneuvers for geostationary satellites. This system aims to overcome the scalability issues and human dependency of current practices.
2. **Create a High-Fidelity Astrodynamics Simulator:** A fundamental objective is to build an open-source, high-fidelity astrodynamics simulation environment. This simulator is intended to serve as a realistic platform for training, testing, and monitoring autonomous agents, accurately modeling the dynamic and debris-rich orbital environments, including real-world and simulated debris.
3. **Train a Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) Agent:** Within the developed simulation environment, a specific objective is to train a reinforcement learning agent using the Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) algorithm. This agent is designed to learn a policy that effectively balances multiple critical factors: ensuring collision avoidance, maintaining orbital stability, and conserving fuel.
4. **Establish an Open-Source and Reproducible Framework:** A significant objective is to ensure that the entire framework, including the simulation environment, training pipeline, and evaluation methodologies, is publicly available and fully deterministic. This commitment to openness and transparency aims to lower the barrier for comparative studies, facilitate peer review, and promote reproducibility within the orbital reinforcement learning research community.

The explicit contributions outlined in the paper align with these objectives: the development of a realistic orbital simulator (with Newtonian gravity, third-body perturbations, and fuel-aware thrust), a Gym-compatible RL environment focused on safety and efficiency, and an open-source pipeline for training and evaluating agents in GEO scenarios. The study also aims to demonstrate that its simulated results lead to reduced collisions and improved stability compared to baselines, while addressing the nuanced trade-offs involving fuel consumption.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research employs a multi-faceted methodology that integrates astrodynamic simulation with deep reinforcement learning techniques for autonomous satellite trajectory optimization.

**A. Astrodynamic Satellite Simulation**
The core of the methodology is a custom-built, high-fidelity astrodynamic simulator designed to model the complex orbital environment.

1. **Initialization:** The simulation environment can be initialized in two ways: either by randomly generating debris and active satellites or by retrieving real-time orbital debris state vectors and Two-Line Element (TLE) entries from the CelesTrak database. TLEs are converted into orbital state vectors using simplified perturbation models. The simulation duration and discrete time steps (∆t = 1s) are defined for trajectory propagation. To prevent overfitting on real-world data, a simulated dataset for debris positions is generated for training, with configurable ranges for debris count, initial relative positions/velocities, and time-to-collision (TTC) based on real data trends. Astropy and Poliastro libraries are utilized for astrodynamic computations.
2. **Physical Dynamics Modeling:** Orbital propagation is based on Newtonian two-body dynamics, augmented with third-body perturbations from the Moon and Sun (Algorithm 1). The continuous-time equations of motion account for Earth's gravity, gravitational influences from perturbing bodies, and the satellite's thrust force. These dynamics are integrated using a semi-implicit Euler step. Propellant expenditure is modeled using the rocket equation, tracking instantaneous mass and cumulative delta-v (∆V). Collision detection is determined when the instantaneous separation distance between the satellite and any debris falls below an effective safety radius ($r_{eff}$), leading to episode termination.
3. **Environment Observation Space:** The agent's observation vector comprises the satellite's position, velocity, remaining fuel, and the positions of up to 100 surrounding debris objects. A scenario blueprint, including debris states, target index, deterministic flags, and initial fuel, is saved for exact replication of episodic outcomes and trajectory visualization. Auxiliary telemetry, such as elapsed time, cumulative delta-v, minimum distance to debris, collision flags, and projected miss distance, is logged to support reward shaping and post-evaluation analytics.

**B. Reinforcement Learning Environment Design**
The simulation environment is integrated with the OpenAI Gym API to facilitate the training and evaluation of reinforcement learning agents.

1. **Policy Architecture and Training:** The chosen agent architecture is Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO), implemented using Stable-Baselines3. The PPO agent utilizes a shared multilayer perceptron (MLP) with three dense layers (256, 256, and 128 nodes) to process state inputs and produce both policy and value outputs. PPO's clipped surrogate objective function (Eq. 6), combined with a value function loss and an entropy bonus (Eq. 7), ensures stable policy updates. Generalized Advantage Estimation (GAE) is employed to reduce variance in gradient estimates.
 A curriculum learning schedule is implemented during training, progressively increasing scenario difficulty over 1,000,000 timesteps. This includes stages of "Basic avoidance," "Intermediate," and "Advanced," with increasing collision probabilities and debris radii, designed to gradually challenge the agent.
2. **Reward Design and Shaping:** The reward function (Algorithm 3) is intricately shaped to encourage safe, fuel-efficient, and stable orbital behavior. Key components include a survival reward, a projected-miss reward, penalties for delta-v usage (linear, quadratic, and for large burns), a smoothness penalty for action changes (jitter), a milestone bonus for surviving certain durations, and a severe penalty for collisions. The delta-v cost is context-aware, increasing when the satellite is closer to debris.
3. **Model and Simulation Integration:** The environment presents a continuous observation space and a continuous action space, allowing the agent to make fine-grained velocity adjustments. The agent's primary task is to make continuous orbital control decisions to avoid collisions while striving for stable and efficient trajectories.

**C. Evaluation and Testing**
The PPO agent's performance is rigorously evaluated against four baseline controllers in a fully deterministic pipeline.

1. **Baseline Controllers:**
 - **Risk-aware rule-based controller:** This controller assesses relative kinematics (distance, time-to-closest-approach) to calculate a scalar risk score and applies thrust based on this metric. An improved multi-factor decision rule was implemented to enhance its competitiveness.
 - **Impulsive ∆v Planner:** This baseline computes the instantaneous relative state and predicts the time to closest approach. It then applies a constant lateral acceleration to achieve a required clearance, saturating at environment action bounds.
 - **Deep Q-Network (DQN):** A learning-based baseline implemented with a discrete action space (discretizing continuous thrust into a fixed set of directions and magnitudes) and a smaller two-layer MLP (64 nodes each). It shares the same reward structure and hyperparameters as the PPO agent for comparative purposes.
 - **No-action controller:** This serves as a control, representing a passive satellite that performs no maneuvers, used to verify the inherent collision risk of the simulated scenarios.
2. **Deterministic Evaluation Pipeline:** The evaluation involves 1,000 episodes per policy using a fixed test configuration. The pipeline enforces shared seeds across all controllers and re-instantiates environments per policy to ensure reproducibility. Comprehensive metrics are recorded, including collision/success rates, episode length, total reward, delta-v usage, minimum approach distance, and fuel consumption. Detailed per-episode logs, seeds, and per-step time-series data are exported for in-depth analysis and transparency.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of 1,000 deterministic GEO episodes per policy yielded a quantitative comparison of the proposed PPO agent against the specified baselines.

**Overall Performance:**
The PPO agent demonstrated a 97.5% collision avoidance success rate, successfully avoiding collisions in 975 out of 1,000 trials. This performance significantly surpassed all baseline controllers:
- The rule-based baseline achieved a 20.7% success rate.
- The impulsive ∆v planner baseline achieved a 27.5% success rate.
- The Deep Q-Network (DQN) baseline achieved a 38.0% success rate.
- The no-action controller resulted in a 0.0% success rate (100% collisions), confirming the non-trivial nature of the simulated scenarios.
The PPO agent effectively reduced the collision rate by 76.8 percentage points compared to the rule-based policy and 70.0 percentage points compared to the impulsive baseline.

**Policy Behavior Analysis:**
- **PPO Agent:** The PPO agent exhibited a proactive burn strategy. Upon observing debris, it initiated maneuvers early to widen the projected miss window, typically increasing separation to over 500 meters within the first 150 seconds, before coasting for the remainder of the simulation. This behavior consistently ensured safe avoidance. The PPO agent recorded higher cumulative ∆v (888.410 m/s) and fuel consumption (254.492 kg) than the baselines. This is attributed to the reward function design, which prioritized successful collision avoidance, tolerating more aggressive maneuvers when in close proximity to debris.
- **Rule-Based Policy:** This policy's 20.7% success rate was deemed insufficient. Its behavior was characterized by a lack of long-horizon planning, often exhausting its maneuver budget early without sufficient late-stage correction capabilities. Spacing rarely exceeded 300 meters, and its actions consisted of discrete ∆v bursts without sustained follow-through.
- **Impulsive ∆v Planner:** Similar to the rule-based policy, the impulsive baseline's actions and ∆v spending quickly tapered off. It initiated large burns at the onset of an encounter but these were often insufficient for sustained collision avoidance. While more accurate and fuel-efficient than the rule-based approach, it did not outperform the PPO agent. It maintained very low cumulative ∆v (12.175 m/s) and minimal fuel usage (4.125 kg), highlighting its prioritization of conservation over consistent safety in dynamic, multi-body environments.
- **Deep Q-Network (DQN):** The DQN agent exhibited high-variance behavior, with trajectories often diverging into unstable orbits. It demonstrated difficulty with long-horizon planning and failed to apply the necessary early corrective burns. Its ∆v usage was characterized as noisy and inconsistent, primarily small, unstructured thrusts rather than coordinated maneuvers. Many episodes terminated due to reaching maximum simulation steps or drifting into wide orbits rather than controlled avoidance. Despite being conservative with fuel, the policy failed to learn a stable avoidance strategy, indicating the limitations of DQN for continuous, long-duration orbital tasks in this context.

**Fuel Performance:**
The PPO agent's fuel consumption (254 kg) was higher compared to the rule-based controller (138 kg) and the impulsive controller (4 kg). The authors indicate that this increased fuel usage represents a trade-off for significantly enhanced collision avoidance performance, with the PPO's average reward being nearly five times higher than the best baseline. They suggest that this delta-v efficiency is acceptable for high-priority avoidance maneuvers where mission safety is paramount.

**Cumulative Rewards:**
An analysis of the constituent reward terms showed that the PPO agent's superior total reward (3.47 × 10^7) was driven by substantially positive survival and distance components, which effectively outweighed the incurred fuel penalties. In contrast, the baseline policies accrued only modest survival rewards and proportionally smaller penalties, which were insufficient to offset the losses from frequent collisions.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a framework that offers advancements in autonomous space debris avoidance, with several areas of potential impact:

**Advancement in Autonomous Collision Avoidance:** The PPO agent's demonstrated 97.5% collision avoidance success rate significantly surpasses that of traditional rule-based (20.7%), impulsive ∆v (27.5%), and even another reinforcement learning baseline, DQN (38.0%). This performance indicates progress in developing more reliable and effective autonomous systems for safeguarding operational satellites in increasingly congested orbital environments.

**Addressing Orbital Congestion and Sustainability:** By providing a highly effective autonomous collision avoidance solution, this work directly addresses the critical challenge of escalating orbital congestion and the associated risks of cascading collisions. The development of scalable and intelligent systems is crucial for ensuring the long-term sustainability of space activities, particularly with the continued deployment of satellite megaconstellations.

**Open-Source Contribution and Reproducibility:** The public availability of the simulation environment and a fully deterministic evaluation pipeline is a notable contribution. This transparency lowers the entry barrier for other researchers, fosters community validation, encourages peer review, and facilitates rapid iterative development. It provides a foundational tool for comparative studies and the development of standardized avoidance strategies across different operators.

**Foundation for Future Space Traffic Management (STM):** The framework established in this study serves as a robust foundation for the training and evaluation of reinforcement learning-based agents in contested orbital environments. This can contribute to the evolution of advanced space traffic management systems, enabling more proactive and coordinated responses to potential conjunctions and enhancing overall operational resilience.

**Demonstration of RL Applicability in Astrodynamics:** The study effectively showcases the capabilities of Proximal Policy Optimization in solving complex, continuous-control problems in astrodynamics. The PPO agent's ability to learn proactive, distributed burn strategies for smoother and more consistent avoidance, as opposed to the sporadic actions of baselines, highlights the potential of deep reinforcement learning for optimal trajectory planning.

**Identified Pathways for Future Research:** The paper explicitly details limitations, such as the agent's perfect sensing, simplified thrust model, and lack of real-time deployment considerations (e.g., onboard compute, latency, safety guarantees). This transparent identification of future work directions (realistic sensing, operational constraints, hybrid control, advanced baselines, multi-agent swarm modeling) provides a clear roadmap for further development, contributing to the continued maturation of autonomous space systems.

While the PPO agent exhibits higher fuel consumption compared to some baselines, its significantly superior collision avoidance performance presents a critical trade-off, especially for high-priority missions where safety is paramount. This work advances the field towards a more automated, data-driven, and sustainable approach to managing space traffic.
