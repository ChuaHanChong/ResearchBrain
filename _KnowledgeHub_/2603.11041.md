# DynVLA: Learning World Dynamics for Action Reasoning in Autonomous Driving

> [!summary] Summary
> DynVLA introduces a Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model that uses a novel Dynamics Chain-of-Thought (CoT) to explicitly reason about future world dynamics in autonomous driving. This approach enables more informed and physically grounded decision-making by learning compact, discrete representations of future motion, achieving a PDM Score of 91.7 on the NAVSIM benchmark and a Driving Score of 88.34 on Bench2Drive, while maintaining low inference latency of 0.37s.

## Problem

- Existing Textual CoT methods in autonomous driving VLA models struggle to capture fine-grained spatiotemporal relationships and suffer from high inference latency.
- Visual CoT approaches, which predict future visual frames, incur significant computational overhead and redundancy by generating decision-irrelevant pixel-level details, leading to high latency.
- Autonomous driving models require an efficient, interpretable, and physically grounded reasoning mechanism to handle complex, interactive, and safety-critical scenarios requiring long-horizon planning.

## Method

- **DynVLA** introduces a novel Dynamics Chain-of-Thought (CoT) paradigm that learns compact, discrete representations of future world dynamics using a specialized Dynamics Tokenizer.
- The Dynamics Tokenizer employs decoupled ego-centric and environment-centric dynamics, regularized by action-based and cross-view consistency losses, to ensure physically grounded and disentangled dynamics modeling.
- A three-stage training pipeline (Dynamics Tokenizer pre-training, Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) on Dynamics CoT, and Reinforcement Fine-Tuning (RFT) with Group Relative Policy Optimization) integrates dynamics reasoning into the action generation process.

## Results

- DynVLA achieved state-of-the-art performance with a PDM Score of **91.7** on the NAVSIM benchmark and a Driving Score of **88.34** on the Bench2Drive closed-loop benchmark, outperforming existing VLA and end-to-end methods.
- Dynamics CoT demonstrated superior efficiency with an inference latency of 0.37s, significantly lower than Textual CoT (3.04s) and Visual CoT (2.29s), while yielding higher planning performance (PDMS **87.2**).
- Ablation studies confirmed that dynamics decoupling and action regularization were critical for preventing codebook collapse and learning meaningful dynamics tokens, which directly improved downstream planning performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly modeling and predicting compact future world dynamics, rather than dense pixel frames or abstract text, significantly enhances autonomous driving decision quality and safety while maintaining low inference latency.
> - Decoupling ego-centric and environment-centric dynamics, combined with action-based and cross-view consistency regularization, is crucial for learning interpretable, disentangled, and physically grounded dynamics representations.
> - A structured Dynamics CoT generation process, reinforced through a sequential SFT and RFT pipeline, effectively trains VLA models to reason about future world evolution before generating driving actions, improving planning and robustness.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of autonomous driving has seen significant advancements, with a recent shift towards end-to-end learning paradigms. Within this context, Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models have emerged as a promising approach, offering enhanced cross-modal grounding and improved decision quality by integrating visual observations, linguistic instructions, and direct action generation.

A key aspect of improving VLA models for complex tasks like autonomous driving is incorporating explicit reasoning steps, often inspired by the "reason-then-act" cognitive process observed in humans. Chain-of-Thought (CoT) techniques, initially successful in large language models, have been adapted to VLA models in robotics and autonomous driving to enable models to generate intermediate reasoning traces before producing final actions.

Existing CoT paradigms in autonomous driving VLA models primarily fall into two categories:
- **Textual CoT:** This approach involves generating high-level decision logic in natural language. While providing interpretability, textual representations can struggle to capture the fine-grained spatiotemporal relationships critical for real-world driving maneuvers. Furthermore, generating long textual reasoning traces can introduce substantial inference latency.
- **Visual CoT:** This paradigm attempts to address the spatiotemporal limitations of textual CoT by predicting future visual frames. While more expressive in representing visual changes, it often involves generating a large number of pixel-level tokens, leading to significant redundancy (predicting decision-irrelevant background details) and high computational overhead, which increases inference latency.

This work, DynVLA, positions itself by proposing an alternative CoT paradigm, termed Dynamics CoT, designed to overcome the limitations of both Textual and Visual CoT. It aims to capture the essential spatiotemporal evolution of the driving world in a compact and efficient manner, thereby improving decision quality while maintaining practical inference speeds. The paper also relates to broader efforts in learning latent action tokenizers for embodied AI and developing foresight-driven policies in robotics, adapting these concepts to the specific challenges of multi-agent and viewpoint-changing driving scenarios.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to introduce DynVLA, a driving VLA model that incorporates a novel Chain-of-Thought (CoT) paradigm referred to as Dynamics CoT. This new paradigm is designed to enable VLA models for autonomous driving to perform more informed and physically grounded decision-making by explicitly reasoning about future world dynamics.

The motivation for proposing Dynamics CoT stems from identified limitations in existing CoT approaches within autonomous driving VLA models:
- **Addressing the shortcomings of Textual CoT:** Current textual CoT methods, which describe scene reasoning in text, are limited in their ability to capture the fine-grained spatiotemporal relationships essential for complex driving maneuvers. Driving decisions, such as intricate lane changes or yielding in dense traffic, depend on precise understanding of vehicle positions, speeds, and interaction dynamics, which discrete linguistic representations often fail to convey adequately. Additionally, generating detailed textual reasoning can introduce high inference latency, hindering real-time application.
- **Overcoming the redundancy and overhead of Visual CoT:** While visual CoT, which predicts future visual frames, provides more direct spatiotemporal reasoning, it involves generating pixel-level information for the entire scene. This includes predicting static background elements and texture details that are often irrelevant to critical driving decisions. Such dense prediction leads to substantial reasoning redundancy, increased learning difficulty for the model, and significant computational overhead, consequently resulting in high inference latency.
- **Achieving compact, interpretable, and efficient reasoning:** The authors are motivated to develop a reasoning mechanism that distills the complex future evolution of the driving world into a minimal yet informative representation. This representation should not only capture the critical changes in the environment (dynamics) but also be interpretable, allowing for a better understanding of the model's decision-making process. Crucially, this reasoning process must be computationally efficient to be viable for real-time autonomous driving applications.
- **Enabling physically grounded and disentangled dynamics modeling:** The motivation extends to ensuring that the learned dynamics are physically meaningful and not ambiguous. This involves explicitly decoupling ego-centric dynamics (arising from the ego vehicle's own motion) from environment-centric dynamics (changes originating from other traffic participants or static obstacles). This disentanglement is intended to lead to more accurate and robust world dynamics modeling, preventing misinterpretations where, for instance, ego forward motion is confused with a leading vehicle moving backward.
- **Enhancing decision quality and safety:** By providing the VLA model with explicit, compact, and accurate forecasts of future world dynamics, the objective is to improve the quality, safety, and reliability of generated driving actions, particularly in interaction-intensive and safety-critical scenarios that require long-horizon planning.

In summary, the key motivation is to bridge the gap between abstract reasoning and concrete spatiotemporal understanding in autonomous driving VLA models, through a novel CoT paradigm that is simultaneously more informative, less redundant, and more efficient than existing approaches.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

DynVLA is designed as a three-stage training pipeline, integrating a novel Dynamics Tokenizer with supervised and reinforcement fine-tuning for action generation.

#### 3.1. Stage 1: Dynamics Tokenizer Training

The first stage focuses on learning to extract compact, discrete representations of future world dynamics.

- **Encoder with Decoupled Dynamics:**
 - **Input Processing:** The system takes sequential images, specifically the current observation ($O_t$) and the next observation ($O_{t+1}$), which are converted into patch sequences ($x_t$, $x_{t+1}$) using a Vision Transformer (ViT) patchifier.
 - **Dynamics Encoding:** A Dynamics Encoder ($E_{dyn}$), composed of Transformer layers, processes these patch sequences.
 - **Decoupling:** To separate the ego vehicle's motion from environmental changes, two sets of learnable queries, $Q_{ego}$ and $Q_{env}$, are introduced. These queries aggregate the encoded information into continuous ego-centric ($e^t_{ego}$) and environment-centric ($e^t_{env}$) dynamics representations. Both $N_{ego}$ and $N_{env}$ (number of tokens for each type) are kept small to ensure compactness.
 - **Discretization:** Separate Vector Quantization (VQ) codebooks, $C_{ego}$ and $C_{env}$, are used to discretize the continuous dynamics. This process assigns the continuous dynamics to their nearest neighbors in the respective codebooks, yielding discrete ego-centric ($D^t_{ego}$) and environment-centric ($D^t_{env}$) tokens. These are then concatenated to form the final Dynamics Tokens ($D_t$). The discrete tokens are then mapped back to continuous embeddings ($z_t$) for decoding.

- **Decoder with Action-based Regularization:**
 - To prevent the dynamics learning from being under-constrained and to ensure physical grounding, an action-based regularization is introduced.
 - An MLP action decoder predicts the ego vehicle's action ($\hat{a}_{t \to t+1}$) directly from the discrete ego-centric dynamics ($D^t_{ego}$).
 - A loss function ($L_{act-reg}$) penalizes the squared difference between the predicted and ground-truth ego actions, encouraging the ego-centric branch to explicitly encode ego motion and promoting disentanglement from environment dynamics.

- **Decoder with Cross-view Consistency Regularization:**
 - To ensure the dynamics representations are semantically consistent across different sensory modalities, a cross-view consistency regularization is applied.
 - The same dynamics representation ($z_t$) is used to predict both the future image ($O_{t+1}$) and the future Bird's Eye View (BEV) map ($BEV_{t+1}$), conditioned on their respective current observations ($O_t$, $BEV_t$).
 - This is achieved using two modality-specific dynamics decoders: an image decoder ($D^{img}_{dyn}$) and a BEV decoder ($D^{bev}_{dyn}$), both comprising Transformer layers. This regularization forces the dynamics tokens to capture underlying scene evolution consistent across different perspectives.

- **Dynamics Tokenizer Training Objective:** The tokenizer is trained by minimizing a combined loss function:
 - Image reconstruction loss ($L_{img\_recon}$): Combines Mean Squared Error (MSE) and perceptual similarity loss (LPIPS) for both low-level structure and high-level semantics.
 - BEV reconstruction loss ($L_{bev\_recon}$): A cross-entropy loss for BEV map reconstruction.
 - VQ-VAE loss ($L_{VQ}$).
 - Action regularization loss ($L_{act-reg}$).
 - The overall loss function ($L$) is a weighted sum of these components.

#### 3.2. Stage 2: Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) on Dynamics CoT

After the Dynamics Tokenizer is trained, the next stage involves fine-tuning the entire VLA model to integrate dynamics reasoning into action generation.

- **Structured Dynamics CoT Sequence:** The model is trained to generate a structured output sequence. This sequence begins with special tokens (⟨BOD⟩), followed by a sequence of predicted future dynamics tokens ($D_{t:t+K-1}$), then an end-of-dynamics token (⟨EOD⟩), a beginning-of-action token (⟨BOA⟩), the predicted action tokens ($A_{t:t+N-1}$), and finally an end-of-action token (⟨EOA⟩).
 - Dynamics tokens $D_{t+k}$ are extracted from the trained Dynamics Encoder by observing $O_{t+k}$ and $O_{t+k+1}$ over a future horizon of K steps.
 - Action tokens $A_{t:t+N-1}$ are discretized from continuous actions using a FAST tokenizer.
- **SFT Training:** The model is trained to maximize the likelihood of this structured output sequence, conditioned on current observations ($O_t$, $O_{t-1}$), text instructions ($T_t$), and ego state ($S_t$). This is formulated as a standard next-token prediction loss, comprising a dynamics prediction loss ($L_{dyn}$) and an action prediction loss ($L_{act}$). This process teaches the model a causal generation order: first reason about dynamics, then generate actions.

#### 3.3. Stage 3: Reinforcement Fine-Tuning (RFT) on Dynamics CoT

The final stage employs reinforcement learning to further refine the policy, addressing limitations of purely imitation-based learning such as generating unsafe or suboptimal trajectories.

- **Reward Design:**
 - **Trajectory-level reward ($r_{traj}$):** The PDM Score (PDMS) (a scalar in) is used, which aggregates multiple safety and efficiency metrics (e.g., No At-Fault Collision, Drivable Area Compliance, Ego Progress, Time-to-Collision, Comfort).
 - **Format reward ($r_{fmt}$):** A binary reward (0 or 1) is given if the generated token sequence adheres to the required structured format (Dynamics CoT template).
 - The final reward ($r$) is a weighted sum of $r_{traj}$ and $r_{fmt}$.
- **RFT Training:** The policy is optimized using Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO).
 - For each input, multiple candidate sequences (dynamics and actions) are rolled out, and their corresponding rewards are computed.
 - The GRPO objective aims to improve high-reward sequences while regularizing the policy to stay close to a frozen reference model (from SFT) using a KL divergence term. This process helps to enhance planning safety and decision quality while preserving the structured generation of Dynamics CoT.

The base model used for DynVLA is EMU3, leveraging its unified architecture for handling the new dynamics modality.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of DynVLA was conducted across three benchmarks: NAVSIM (a real-world benchmark), Bench2Drive (a closed-loop benchmark), and a large-scale in-house dataset. The results demonstrate the effectiveness of Dynamics CoT compared to existing CoT and non-CoT methods.

- **Overall Performance Comparison:**
 - **NAVSIM Benchmark:** DynVLA achieved the highest PDM Score (PDMS) of 91.7, outperforming a range of traditional end-to-end methods and various VLA-based methods. For instance, it surpassed strong baselines like DriveDPO (90.0), AutoVLA (89.1), and FSDrive (85.1). This indicates that reasoning over future dynamics provides more effective and reliable guidance for decision-making in real-world scenarios.
 - **Bench2Drive Benchmark:** In challenging closed-loop, long-horizon, and interactive scenarios, DynVLA achieved the best performance across all metrics: Driving Score (DS) of 88.34, Success Rate (SR) of 72.73, and Mean Multi-Ability of 72.23. These results significantly surpassed other VLA models like TF++ (64.39) and AutoVLA, highlighting the advantages of Dynamics CoT in complex interactive environments.
 - **In-house Dataset:** On a large-scale in-house dataset (700k frames), DynVLA demonstrated superior scalability and robustness, achieving the lowest Average Displacement Error (ADE) of 1.215m and the lowest Collision Rate of 4.04‱. This performance was better than reimplemented Transfuser (1.746m, 5.63‱) and DriveVLA-W0 (1.344m, 5.13‱).

- **Ablation Studies and Analysis:**
 - **Transferability of Learned Dynamics Tokens:** Visualizations (Fig. 4 and 8) demonstrated that the learned ego-centric dynamics reliably preserve ego motion, while environment-centric dynamics govern the motions of surrounding agents. These dynamics tokens were shown to be transferable across different scenarios, suggesting they capture disentangled and interpretable representations of motion.
 - **CoT Design and Latency Comparison:** An analysis of different CoT designs revealed that Dynamics CoT offers a favorable balance of performance and efficiency.
 - Textual CoT (scene description) incurred high latency (3.04s) and resulted in lower PDMS (85.3).
 - Meta-action CoT showed marginal improvement (PDMS 86.0) with moderate latency (0.43s).
 - Visual CoT (future image prediction) provided moderate gains (PDMS 86.3-86.4) but suffered from substantial latency (2.29s).
 - Dynamics CoT achieved the best PDMS (87.2) with significantly lower latency (0.37s) compared to Textual and Visual CoT, attributed to its compact representation.
 - **Impact of Training Stages:** The study on training stages (Table 5) showed that applying Dynamics CoT SFT consistently improved planning metrics over a non-CoT baseline. Subsequent RFT further enhanced performance, leading to the highest PDMS. For the EMU3 base model, the PDMS increased from 85.6 (w/o CoT) to 87.2 (with Dynamics CoT SFT) and further to 91.7 (with Dynamics CoT RFT). Similar trends were observed with the Qwen2.5-VL base model, verifying the generality of Dynamics CoT.
 - **Dynamics Decoupling:** Decoupling ego and environment dynamics, combined with action-based regularization, was found to be critical. Without decoupling, the Dynamics Tokenizer exhibited codebook collapse (Fig. 5), indicating an inability to learn discriminative dynamics representations. Decoupling forced the tokens to learn more meaningful, disentangled representations.
 - **Dynamics Tokenizer Design:** Ablations on the Dynamics Tokenizer designs (Table 6) indicated that decoupling dynamics and applying cross-view consistency regularization (using both image and BEV supervision) were essential for optimal planning performance.
 - **Prediction Horizon and Token Count:** Experiments on the prediction horizon (Table 7) determined that a 2-second future horizon (K=2) offered the best balance between performance and latency. Increasing the horizon further introduced diminishing returns due to increased uncertainty. Similarly, using 8 dynamics tokens (4 ego-centric, 4 environment-centric) was found to be optimal (Table 8), balancing capacity and compactness.

- **Qualitative Analysis:**
 - **Safer Intent-aware Interaction:** Dynamics CoT allowed the model to anticipate the intentions of other agents (e.g., a front car stopping) and adjust its plan accordingly, preventing collisions (Fig. 6, top row).
 - **Foresighted Planning:** The model could reason about future scene evolution (e.g., a leading vehicle moving right to open a path), enabling foresighted and safer trajectory planning that prevented off-road excursions (Fig. 6, middle row).
 - **Road-geometry Awareness:** By predicting the evolution of road constraints (e.g., an upcoming curb), Dynamics CoT guided the model to make timely steering adjustments, preventing collisions with road boundaries (Fig. 6, bottom row).
 - **Decoded Visualization:** The decoded future images and BEV maps from the Dynamics Tokenizer closely aligned with ground-truth evolutions across diverse scenarios, confirming that the tokenizer effectively compresses planning-relevant dynamics (Fig. 7).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The DynVLA research introduces a novel Dynamics Chain-of-Thought (CoT) paradigm that contributes to the field of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for autonomous driving. This work carries several implications and potential impacts:

- **Advancement in Autonomous Driving Decision-Making:** Dynamics CoT offers a structured intermediate reasoning step that is physically grounded and spatiotemporally aware. By explicitly forecasting compact world dynamics before action generation, DynVLA enables VLA models to make more informed decisions compared to approaches that either rely on abstract linguistic reasoning or dense, pixel-level future predictions.
- **Improved Safety and Efficiency in Autonomous Systems:** The experimental results consistently demonstrate that DynVLA achieves higher safety and efficiency metrics (e.g., PDMS, Driving Score, lower collision rates) across diverse benchmarks. Crucially, it achieves these gains while significantly reducing inference latency compared to Textual and Visual CoT methods. This efficiency is critical for real-time deployment in autonomous vehicles, where rapid and reliable decision-making is paramount for safety.
- **Disentangled and Interpretable Dynamics Representation:** The introduction of the Dynamics Tokenizer, with its explicit decoupling of ego-centric and environment-centric dynamics and regularization mechanisms, yields a transferable and interpretable representation of future world evolution. This disentanglement can enhance the model's ability to understand and react to specific aspects of the driving scene, potentially simplifying debugging and analysis of model failures. The transferability across scenarios suggests a more generalizable understanding of motion.
- **Foundation for Future Reasoning-Based VLA Models:** Dynamics CoT establishes a promising direction for future research in reasoning-based VLA models. It demonstrates that a compact, dynamics-focused representation can effectively serve as an intermediate reasoning variable, offering a middle ground between abstract symbolic reasoning and raw perceptual prediction. This could inspire further work on efficient and effective reasoning paradigms for complex embodied AI tasks.
- **Practical Value for Large-Scale Deployment:** The performance on a large-scale in-house dataset, which is designed to represent diverse and challenging real-world scenarios, underscores the practical applicability and scalability of DynVLA. Its ability to maintain low collision rates and accurate motion prediction in such varied conditions suggests its potential for integration into real-world autonomous driving systems.

**Limitations and Future Work:** The paper acknowledges limitations, primarily that inaccurate predicted dynamics (e.g., misinferred agent intentions, incorrect road structure reasoning, degraded visual observations) can propagate errors to action generation. Future work could focus on enhancing robustness to such uncertainties by integrating richer domain priors, world knowledge, rule-based reasoning, or map-aware commonsense. Another potential direction is to incorporate Dynamics CoT into a dual fast-slow system architecture, where long-horizon dynamics reasoning updates tokens at a lower frequency, guiding a lightweight fast planner for real-time control.
