# Improving Vision-Language-Action Model with Online Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Tsinghua University and UC Berkeley researchers present iRe-VLA, a groundbreaking framework that enables continuous improvement of Vision-Language-Action models through online reinforcement learning, demonstrating significant performance gains across both seen and unseen robotic manipulation tasks while maintaining training stability through a novel two-stage approach.

## Problem

- Existing VLA models lack the ability to improve through environmental interaction
- Traditional reinforcement learning approaches face stability issues when applied to large vision-language models

## Method

- Developed a ==two-stage training framework== alternating between online RL and supervised learning
- **iRe-VLA** implements ==parameter-efficient fine-tuning== using ==LoRA== while keeping core VLM parameters frozen

## Results

- Successfully demonstrated improved performance on original expert tasks, new RL-trained tasks, and unseen generalization tasks
- Validated framework across multiple environments including **Metaworld**, **Franka Kitchen**, and real-world **Panda** robot tasks
- Achieved stable training while maintaining computational efficiency suitable for robotics applications

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Combining frozen VLM parameters with RL training provides crucial stability
> - Iterative retraining on successful trajectories enables continuous improvement without degrading original capabilities
> - Parameter-efficient methods are essential for practical robotic applications

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

- Builds on recent work integrating large vision-language models (VLMs) into robotics
- Addresses a key gap in the field: how to improve VLA models through environmental interaction
- Connects two major research threads:
 - Vision-language models for robotics (e.g., RT-2, HiRT, Roboflamingo)
 - Reinforcement learning for large models (e.g., RLHF for language models)

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

- Develop methods to improve Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models through online reinforcement learning
- Address challenges of training instability when applying RL to large models
- Create computationally efficient training approaches suitable for robotic systems
- Enable continuous improvement of VLA models through environmental interaction

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The iRe-VLA framework consists of three main stages:
1. Initial supervised fine-tuning on expert demonstrations
2. Online RL stage with frozen VLM parameters
3. Supervised learning stage using both original expert data and newly collected successful trajectories
- Iterates between stages 2 and 3 to progressively improve performance
- Uses parameter-efficient LoRA method for VLM fine-tuning

### 4. Main Findings and Results

- Successfully stabilized RL training for large VLA models
- Achieved improved performance across three categories of tasks:
 - Original expert tasks
 - New RL-trained tasks
 - Unseen generalization tasks
- Validated on multiple environments:
 - Metaworld benchmark
 - Franka Kitchen benchmark
 - Real-world Panda robot manipulation tasks

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

- Technical Advances:
 - First successful application of online RL to large VLA models
 - Novel two-stage training approach that balances stability and performance
 - Computationally efficient training pipeline suitable for robotics
- Practical Impact:
 - Enables continuous improvement of robotic systems through interaction
 - Improves generalization to unseen tasks
 - Provides a framework for scaling up robotic learning with large models

The paper represents a significant advance in making large vision-language models more practical for robotic applications, with demonstrated success in both simulated and real-world environments.
