# Compute as Teacher: Turning Inference Compute Into Reference-Free Supervision

> [!summary] Summary
> A new method, Compute as Teacher (CaT), generates supervision signals for large language models in post-training scenarios lacking traditional ground truth or programmatic verifiers. It leverages inference compute to synthesize reference-free supervision, leading to relative performance increases of up to 33% on MATH-500 and 30% on HealthBench.

## Problem

- Post-training large language models (LLMs) often lack sufficient human-labeled reference data or external programmatic verifiers for specialized tasks.
- This data scarcity is particularly acute in "non-verifiable" domains like creative writing or open-ended dialogue, where objective ground truth is elusive.
- Existing alternatives like costly human annotation or LLM-as-a-judge methods suffer from inconsistencies and can introduce biases.

## Method

- ==Compute as Teacher (CaT)== synthesizes a high-quality reference by leveraging multiple parallel rollouts generated by the current policy for a given prompt.
- A ==frozen anchor policy== then integrates evidence from these rollouts to construct an improved reference response, without seeing the original prompt.
- This synthesized reference is converted into reward signals (binary for verifiable tasks, ==rubric-based== for non-verifiable tasks) to fine-tune the policy using Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO).

## Results

- CaT-RL consistently improved model performance across tasks, achieving up to **33%** relative gain on MATH-500 and **30%** on HealthBench for Llama 3.1 8B, surpassing the initial policy and its own inference-time teacher signal.
- Self-proposed rubrics for non-verifiable tasks were highly effective, outperforming direct LLM-as-a-judge methods and demonstrating quality comparable to, or sometimes exceeding, human expert annotations.
- CaT exhibited a unique self-correction ability, producing correct answers that disagreed with all of the initial rollouts it was conditioned on in approximately **1%** of questions, demonstrating its generative power beyond simple selection.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - CaT synthesizes entirely new reference answers by reconciling multiple rollouts, enabling it to correct errors even when individual model generations are flawed or disagree with the majority.
> - For non-verifiable tasks, the method uses self-proposed rubrics, decomposing complex judgments into atomic, verifiable criteria, which leads to more robust and less biased reward signals than direct LLM judging.
> - The approach fosters a virtuous learning cycle where an improving policy generates higher quality rollouts, leading to better synthesized supervision and further model enhancement.
