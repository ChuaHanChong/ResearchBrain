# Beyond Spatial Benchmarks: From Spatial Reasoning to Navigation

> [!summary] Summary
> Spatial-Nav asks whether spatial-reasoning benchmark gains transfer to ObjectNav. A diagnostic study shows benchmark-tuned spatial specialists hurt navigation, while goal-coupled, phase-matched spatial supervision helps. The authors build SPATIAL-NAV-100K, fine-tune in two stages (shared spatial-navigation foundation, then Exploration and Approach experts), and add Spatial-NPD, distilling a spatially-primed teacher's action preferences into a reasoning-free student. The 8B policy hits 77.4 SR on HM3D-v0.2 at 148 ms per step.

## Problem

- Existing spatial benchmarks probe isolated question answering from images or videos, so high scores never test whether the ability supports sustained goal-directed embodied behavior.
- Interactive embodied spatial benchmarks still conclude once an answer is produced, leaving them disconnected from long-horizon downstream tasks such as object goal navigation.
- Prior navigation methods apply spatial priors uniformly across a task, so which spatial ability each navigation phase needs, and how supervision meets decision learning, stays unclear.

## Method

- **SPATIAL-NAV-100K**: over 100K annotated records from HM3D ObjectNav rollouts, split into Navigation Decision, Spatial Reasoning, and Spatial2Nav Bridge targets across five spatial abilities.
- Two-stage ==LoRA== fine-tuning: Stage 1 learns a shared spatial-navigation foundation, Stage 2 specializes an Exploration expert (Relation/Topology/Affordance) and an Approach expert (Relation/Visibility/Geometry).
- **Spatial-NPD**: a frozen teacher conditioned on spatial annotations produces action preferences over legal actions; a ==temperature-scaled KL loss== distills them into a reasoning-free student.

## Results

- The 8B policy reaches **77.4/35.4** SR/SPL on HM3D-v0.2, **60.2/30.5** on HM3D-v0.1 and **47.9/20.6** on unseen MP3D, using **45** A100-hours.
- Component ablation climbs **59.2** to **63.7** to **68.7** to **74.5** to **77.4** SR as decision-spatial SFT, phase experts, shared foundation, then Spatial-NPD are added.
- Aligned teacher priors beat shuffled ones by **2.4** to **3.2** SR; step-wise CoT costs **8.0** SR and **12.7** SPL while raising latency to **929 ms**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Benchmark spatial skill is not usable spatial skill: gains appear only when spatial evidence is coupled to the downstream task goal.
> - Different task phases need different spatial abilities; training one model on all five underperforms the best phase-matched single-ability variant.
> - Keep reasoning as a parallel auxiliary target, not a serialized prefix to the action; distill its preferences rather than decoding it at inference.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Overview

Object Goal Navigation (ObjectNav) asks an agent to find a named object in an unfamiliar environment. The practical bottleneck is not producing spatial answers in isolation. It is using relevant spatial evidence to make repeated navigation decisions over a long episode. The authors' central claim is that benchmark-tuned spatial ability does not by itself solve that problem. Supervision must connect spatial evidence to the goal, to the phase of navigation, and to action learning.

Panel (a) shows SR-versus-SPL scatterplots for VLMNav and WMNav on HM3D-v0.2 and HM3D-v0.1. The paired spatial-reasoning specialists (cyan) sit below their Qwen2.5-VL-7B base points (red). Panel (b) shows changes from baseline for reasoning themes and individual spatial abilities. Navigation-coupled spatial supervision is positive on both sources, while generic spatial results are mixed or small.

### 2. Spatial cues must serve the goal

[[2511.05491|Visual Spatial Tuning]] (VST) is trained to improve broad spatial benchmark performance. In the paper's paired navigation tests, benchmark-oriented specialists do not transfer that improvement to ObjectNav. Every tested specialist underperforms its paired base model across the four combinations of two navigation frameworks and two HM3D versions.

To find what does help, the authors ran a lightweight fine-tuning study using Qwen3-VL-8B in VLMNav. They compared annotation schemas across three seeds, and no variant received direct navigation-action labels. Two metrics measure the outcome:

- **Success Rate (SR)** is the share of episodes reaching the target within the 1.0 m success radius.
- **Success Weighted by Path Length (SPL)** weights success by path length.

Navigation-coupled spatial supervision improved SR/SPL by **+2.9/+1.4 points** with HM3D training data and **+2.5/+1.2 points** with VSI-Bench training frames.

The other schemas act as controls:

- Appearance supervision lowers performance.
- Conventional, goal-agnostic spatial description yields almost no HM3D gain and falls below baseline on the VSI-based condition.
- Goal-oriented non-spatial supervision helps on HM3D but falls below baseline on VSI-Bench.

Only navigation-coupled spatial supervision improves both. The VSI-based condition uses 2,700 ARKitScenes training frames paired with ObjectNav goals and is evaluated on the same HM3D-v0.2 episodes. It is not a direct VSI-Bench navigation evaluation. These controls support the authors' interpretation that linking goal information to spatial evidence matters. They do not establish a universal causal law.

### 3. Specialize by phase, and keep the teacher off the action path

The policy is built in two steps: a shared model first, then specialized experts. Navigation is split into two phases. **Exploration** chooses candidate directions to search, and **Approach** decides whether to stop or continue. A shared foundation learns decision and spatial targets separately. Two later experts then specialize:

- the Exploration expert on Relation, Topology, and Affordance;
- the Approach expert on Relation, Visibility, and Geometry.

On top of this, the method uses Spatial-inspired Navigation Policy Distillation (Spatial-NPD). A frozen teacher sees the navigation context plus an aligned spatial annotation and supplies preferences over legal actions. The student sees only the navigation context. It is trained both on the direct action target and to match the teacher's preferences. The deployed student acts without generating spatial text at inference.

Panel (a) shows a Stage 1 shared spatial-navigation foundation, followed by an Exploration expert and an Approach expert, with separate spatial and decision targets. Panel (b) shows a teacher conditioned on spatial priors plus navigation input and a student receiving navigation input alone. Its supervision is labeled self-distillation/KL and action cross-entropy.

The decisive test fixes spatial fine-tuning and varies the teacher's prior. On HM3D-v0.2, aligned priors beat shuffled priors by **+2.4 SR/+2.8 SPL**. Shuffled priors score 75.0/32.6, versus 74.5/32.9 without Spatial-NPD. The evidence therefore favors state-aligned prior information over a generic distillation effect. It does not prove that spatial reasoning is the only causal factor.

### 4. Results and limits

The final Qwen3-VL-8B policy reaches **77.4% SR and 35.4% SPL** on HM3D-v0.2. In the paper's component-ablation setup, the base model scores 59.2/22.1. These are four-seed means over 1,000 evaluation episodes. The paper also reports 60.2/30.5 on HM3D-v0.1 and 47.9/20.6 on train-unseen MP3D. The method does not lead every baseline on every metric; for example, Qwen-RobotNav's MP3D SR is 48.8.

The established contribution is controlled evidence that spatial supervision helps ObjectNav when it is tied to the goal and the navigation phase. That supervision is then transferred into a direct-action student. The main boundary is scope: the experiments cover simulated ObjectNav with a fixed depth-based candidate proposer and controller. Other sensing and action setups and physical robots remain untested.
