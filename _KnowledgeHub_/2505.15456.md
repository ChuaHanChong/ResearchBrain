# Teaching Language Models to Evolve with Users: Dynamic Profile Modeling for Personalized Alignment

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Harbin Institute of Technology and Du Xiaoman Financial developed the Reinforcement Learning for Personalized Alignment (RLPA) framework, enabling large language models to dynamically infer and adapt to individual user profiles. This approach, using a dual-level reward system and trained via PPO, significantly surpassed static personalization methods by up to 29.06 points in alignment scores and achieved competitive performance with commercial LLMs, demonstrating robust long-term coherence and efficient profile-based reasoning.

## Problem

- Large Language Models struggle with personalized alignment, relying on static user profiles that fail to adapt to evolving user preferences over time.
- Existing personalization methods, such as prompt-based and offline optimization techniques, offer superficial adaptation, face cold-start limitations, and show poor long-term coherence.
- General reasoning capabilities of LLMs are often inefficient when applied to the specific task of dynamic personalized dialogue, leading to suboptimal performance.

## Method

- The Reinforcement Learning for ==Personalized Alignment== (**RLPA**) framework was introduced, framing personalized dialogue as a multi-turn Markov Decision Process (MDP) for dynamic user adaptation.
- A simulated user model, initialized with a 'golden profile' and revealing information incrementally, was designed using GPT-4o-mini to facilitate scalable and controllable training.
- A dual-level reward system combines a Profile Reward (F1-score based) for accurate user profile inference with a Response Reward (multi-dimensional, binary criteria based) for generating profile-consistent, high-quality responses.
- The agent (Qwen-2.5-3B-Instruct) is trained using Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) with this combined reward signal to learn optimal personalization strategies, resulting in the Qwen-RLPA model.

## Results

- Qwen-RLPA achieved the highest overall alignment scores on both Vanilla and Extended ALOE benchmarks, significantly outperforming prompt-based and offline optimization baselines (e.g., +**29.06** over SFT, +**28.11** over DPO).
- The model demonstrated superior long-term coherence, showing a consistently rising alignment trend over **10** dialogue turns and stable profile accuracy over **70** turns, while rapidly adapting to mid-dialogue preference conflicts.
- Qwen-RLPA was competitive with commercial LLMs like GPT-4o and Claude-**3.5**, exhibiting superior consistency and long-term coherence, especially in challenging cross-format generalization scenarios.
- The framework achieved higher response alignment using significantly fewer reasoning tokens compared to other reasoning-centric LLMs, indicating more efficient and focused profile-aware reasoning for personalized dialogue.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Personalized alignment can be effectively modeled as a dynamic, interactive process through a multi-turn MDP and optimized using reinforcement learning, allowing continuous user profile inference and adaptation.
> - A synergistic dual-level reward mechanism, integrating both profile inference accuracy and personalized response quality, is crucial for robust and fluent personalized dialogue generation.
> - Dynamic profile modeling within the RLPA framework enables LLMs to overcome cold-start challenges and maintain long-term coherence, successfully adapting to evolving user preferences across extended interactions.
> - Specialized profile-based reasoning, learned through RLPA, is more efficient for personalized dialogue tasks, achieving superior alignment with fewer reasoning tokens compared to general-purpose LLMs.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The rapid advancement of Large Language Models (LLMs) has led to their deployment across numerous interactive applications, making "alignment" with human values and intentions a paramount concern. Early alignment techniques, such as instruction tuning and Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF), primarily focused on achieving general helpfulness, harmlessness, and adherence to broad human preferences. This "one-size-fits-all" approach, while effective for general-purpose LLMs, inherently falls short when individual user needs, goals, and interaction styles diverge. The paper identifies this limitation as a critical gap, emphasizing the need for **personalized alignment** to enable LLMs to engage effectively in user-centric dialogue, particularly in domains like long-term conversation, personalized education, and user-tailored decision support.

Within the landscape of personalized LLMs, existing methods generally fall into two categories:
- **Prompt-based methods**: These involve injecting user profiles, chat history, or retrieved information directly into the LLM's prompt at inference time (e.g., profile-augmented prompting, retrieval-augmented prompting). While straightforward, these methods are criticized for offering only superficial personalization, being constrained by context window limitations, and lacking mechanisms for long-term memory or adaptive behavior. They rely on static templates and cannot dynamically evolve with the user.
- **Offline optimization methods**: Techniques like Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) and Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) aim to train models on large datasets of desired personalized responses. However, these methods are data-intensive, making them impractical for "cold-start" scenarios where initial user data is scarce. Moreover, their static nature leads to poor generalization across diverse users and limited real-time adaptation during ongoing interactions.

This paper positions its **Reinforcement Learning for Personalized Alignment (RLPA)** framework as a novel paradigm that addresses the fundamental limitations of static and shallow personalization approaches. By framing personalized alignment as a multi-turn Markov Decision Process (MDP) and employing reinforcement learning, the authors propose a system capable of continuous, dynamic adaptation to evolving user preferences. This moves beyond merely *using* a user profile to actively *inferring* and *refining* it through interaction, thereby enabling robust personalization from a cold start and sustaining it across extended dialogues. This work represents a significant step towards truly adaptive and user-centric conversational AI, pushing the boundaries of what LLMs can achieve in personalized interactions.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enable Large Language Models (LLMs) to achieve **dynamic and effective personalized alignment** with users, particularly in challenging scenarios like cold-start interactions and evolving user preferences over long-term dialogues. The authors are motivated by the inherent limitations of existing LLM alignment strategies:
1. **Static and Shallow Personalization:** Current prompt-based and offline optimization methods treat user profiles as static inputs or learn from predefined, fixed datasets. This prevents LLMs from truly understanding and adapting to a user's changing needs, preferences, and interaction styles over time.
2. **Cold-Start Problem:** Many existing approaches require substantial prior user data or explicit profiles, making them ineffective when interacting with a new user where such information is scarce or non-existent.
3. **Lack of Long-Term Adaptation:** Even with some initial personalization, models struggle to maintain coherence and adapt to subtle or explicit shifts in user preferences across extended conversational turns. This leads to a degradation in the quality of personalization over time.
4. **Inefficiency of General Reasoning:** While advanced LLMs possess powerful reasoning capabilities, applying these broadly for personalized dialogue can be inefficient. The authors hypothesize that a dedicated "profile-based reasoning" paradigm could be more effective and efficient for this specific task.

To address these motivations, the paper sets forth the following key objectives:
- **Formulate Personalized Alignment as a Multi-Turn MDP:** Conceptualize the problem as an interactive process where the LLM (agent) learns to infer and adapt to user attributes through sequential dialogue turns.
- **Develop a Reinforcement Learning Framework (RLPA):** Design an online learning mechanism that allows the LLM to learn personalization strategies through interaction with a simulated user model, without relying on extensive labeled datasets.
- **Introduce a Dual-Level Reward System:** Create a reward structure that simultaneously incentivizes accurate user profile inference (Profile Reward) and the generation of responses consistent with that inferred profile (Response Reward).
- **Empirically Validate Dynamic Personalization:** Demonstrate that the proposed RLPA framework significantly outperforms existing static baselines and competes with state-of-the-art commercial LLMs in terms of personalization quality, robustness to preference conflicts, and long-term coherence.
- **Show Efficiency of Profile-Based Reasoning:** Illustrate that dynamically constructed user profiles, guided by the RLPA framework, lead to more efficient and effective reasoning for personalized dialogue compared to general-purpose reasoning LLMs.

Through these objectives, the research aims to pave the way for more sophisticated, adaptive, and truly user-centric conversational AI systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of this work is the **Reinforcement Learning for Personalized Alignment (RLPA)** framework, which conceptualizes personalized dialogue as a multi-turn Markov Decision Process (MDP). In this MDP, a language model acts as an agent, interacting with a user to infer and adapt to user-specific attributes dynamically.

The RLPA framework consists of three primary components:

#### 3.1. Simulated User Design

To enable scalable and controllable training, the RLPA framework utilizes a **simulated user model**. Each simulated user is initialized with a predefined **golden profile (P)**, comprising various attributes like preferences, personality traits, and goals, which are embedded into the user model's system prompt.
- **Profile Groundedness:** The simulated user's responses are consistently conditioned on this profile. Crucially, the user model is instructed to reveal profile information gradually across turns, rather than disclosing it all at once. This incremental exposure forces the dialogue agent to progressively infer and refine its understanding of the user.
- **Behavioral Consistency:** The simulated user maintains stable preferences and conversational style throughout the interaction, allowing the agent to benefit from cumulative reasoning.
- **Model Choice:** Through human evaluations, **GPT-4o-mini** was selected as the most suitable user model, balancing performance in profile fidelity and behavioral consistency with computational efficiency.

#### 3.2. Profile Reward Function Design

The **Profile Reward ($R_{profile}$)** guides the LLM to accurately infer and maintain a user profile ($\hat{P}_t$) at each dialogue turn ($t$). User attributes are not explicitly observable, so the model must learn to extract them implicitly from dialogue history.
- **Structured Profile Representation:** The user profile is represented in a slot-value format (e.g., Age, Gender, Interests, Educational Background), enabling explicit supervision. The model is trained to output its current estimate of the user profile ($\hat{P}_t$) in this structured format.
- **F1-Score Based Reward:** The reward is calculated using a slot-wise matching score, effectively an F1-score, which combines precision and recall:
 $R_{profile_t} = \frac{2 \cdot |\hat{P}_t \cap P|}{|\hat{P}_t| + |P|}$
 This formulation encourages the model to infer complete and accurate profiles by rewarding correct matches and penalizing omissions or incorrect predictions.

#### 3.3. Response Reward Function Design

The **Response Reward ($R_{response}$)** ensures that the LLM's generated responses ($r_t$) are consistent with the currently inferred user profile ($\hat{P}_t$). An external reward model evaluates this alignment.
- **Multi-dimensional Evaluation:** The reward model outputs a scalar score $R_{response_t} \in$ based on four core personalization dimensions: preference expression, style consistency, goal alignment, and persona coherence.
- **Binary Quality Criteria:** To enforce overall response quality, five binary criteria are also applied: Naturalness (N), Relevance (R), Logical consistency (L), Engagement (G), and Informativeness (F). The final response reward is computed as a product:
 $R_{response_t} = N \cdot R \cdot L \cdot G \cdot F$
 This strict criterion ensures that only fully satisfactory responses across all aspects receive a positive reward.
- **Reward Model Choice:** Similar to the user simulator, **GPT-4o-mini** was chosen as the response reward model after benchmarking against human judgments, prioritizing efficiency while maintaining high agreement.

#### 3.4. Training with Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO)

The agent (LLM) is trained using **Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO)**, a well-established policy gradient reinforcement learning algorithm.
- **Combined Reward Signal:** At each dialogue turn, the model receives a combined reward: $R_t = R_{profile_t} + R_{response_t}$. This turn-level feedback encourages continuous inference, refinement, and utilization of user profiles.
- **Optimization Objective:** PPO maximizes a clipped surrogate objective function that leverages advantage estimates derived from the reward sequence, ensuring stable policy updates within a trust region.

The entire framework is instantiated by fine-tuning **Qwen-2.5-3B-Instruct**, resulting in **Qwen-RLPA**. This end-to-end interactive learning approach allows the model to progressively learn to track and adapt to user preferences from scratch, addressing the cold-start and dynamic adaptation challenges.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The empirical evaluations demonstrate the superior performance of Qwen-RLPA across various personalized alignment tasks and challenging scenarios.

#### 4.1. Overall Personalized Alignment Performance

- **State-of-the-Art Performance:** Qwen-RLPA achieves the highest overall alignment scores on both the **Vanilla ALOE** (in-format generalization) and **Extended ALOE** (cross-format generalization) benchmarks. This indicates its robust ability to personalize responses under both familiar and entirely new profile schemas.
- **Outperformance over Baselines:**
 - **Prompt-based methods** (Reminder, Self-Critic, CoT, RAG) showed limited personalization, often struggling with dynamic adaptation.
 - **Offline optimization methods** (SFT, DPO) demonstrated improvements over prompt-based methods but were significantly outperformed by RLPA. For instance, in the Vanilla setting, RLPA improved over SFT by +29.06 and DPO by +28.11 in alignment score.
- **Competitive with Commercial LLMs:** Qwen-RLPA is highly competitive with, and in some aspects surpasses, leading proprietary models such as Claude-3.5 and GPT-4o. While GPT-4o sometimes achieved slightly higher raw alignment in the Vanilla setting, Qwen-RLPA exhibited superior consistency (higher N-R²) and long-term coherence, especially in the more challenging Extended ALOE setting where it led in all metrics.
- **Long-Term Coherence:** Turn-wise alignment scores illustrated that while SFT and DPO showed early improvement followed by degradation, RLPA demonstrated a consistently rising alignment trend across all 10 turns. This highlights its capacity to continually refine inferred profiles and adapt responses as dialogue evolves.

#### 4.2. Ablation Study

The ablation study, which removed either the Profile Reward (PR) or Response Reward (RR) during training, confirmed the complementary and crucial roles of both components:
- **Profile Reward is essential for accurate user modeling:** Training with only Response Reward led to reasonable response-level personalization but weaker early-turn alignment and instability, indicating a lack of explicit guidance for building structured user representations.
- **Response Reward is essential for fluent personalization:** Training with only Profile Reward resulted in good profile inference but significantly lower average alignment, demonstrating the model's struggle to effectively translate inferred attributes into natural, personalized responses.
The synergy between PR and RR ensures both accurate user representation and its effective utilization in dialogue generation.

#### 4.3. Adaptation to Preference Conflict

Qwen-RLPA exhibited remarkable adaptability when user preferences were deliberately shifted mid-dialogue (at turn 6):
- It showed only a minor drop in alignment immediately after the shift and rapidly recovered in subsequent turns, demonstrating its ability to detect and promptly adjust to evolving user preferences.
- In contrast, baselines like DPO struggled significantly, showing sharp drops and limited recovery, indicating their poor responsiveness to dynamic user intent.

#### 4.4. Stable Profile Modeling in Long-Term Interaction

Evaluations over 70 dialogue turns with a consistently fixed user profile revealed:
- Qwen-RLPA's profile accuracy steadily increased over time, nearly converging to the theoretical maximum of revealed user attributes. This signifies its robust ability to accumulate and preserve user information throughout extended interactions.

#### 4.5. Comparison with Reasoning LLMs

Considering user profile inference as a form of domain-specific reasoning, Qwen-RLPA was compared against several reasoning-centric LLMs (DeepSeek-R1, GPT-o3-mini, QwQ-32B):
- Qwen-RLPA consistently achieved higher response alignment scores while using significantly fewer reasoning tokens.
- Other reasoning models, despite generating more tokens, often yielded lower response quality, suggesting inefficiencies or misaligned reasoning processes for personalized dialogue. This finding supports the argument that RLPA facilitates more focused, efficient, and profile-aware reasoning for this specific task.

In summary, the findings strongly validate the RLPA framework's effectiveness in achieving dynamic, adaptive, and efficient personalized alignment, positioning it as a leading approach in conversational AI.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research presented in this paper, "Teaching Language Models to Evolve with Users: Dynamic Profile Modeling for Personalized Alignment," carries significant implications and potential impact across both academic research and practical applications of conversational AI.

#### 5.1. Advancement in Personalized AI Research

- **Novel Paradigm for Dynamic Personalization:** By reformulating personalized alignment as a multi-turn Markov Decision Process and employing reinforcement learning, the paper introduces a fundamentally dynamic and interactive paradigm. This moves beyond static profile injection or offline fine-tuning, offering a more realistic and adaptive approach to user modeling.
- **Addressing Cold-Start and Long-Term Challenges:** The RLPA framework directly tackles two critical challenges in personalization: the cold-start problem (where initial user data is minimal) and the maintenance of coherence over long, evolving dialogues. Its ability to infer and refine user profiles continuously is a substantial improvement over prior methods.
- **Dual-Level Reward System:** The innovative dual-level reward mechanism, combining profile inference accuracy with personalized response quality, provides a powerful and granular training signal. This ensures that the model not only understands *who* the user is but also *how* to effectively communicate with them in a personalized manner.
- **Efficiency of Profile-Based Reasoning:** The finding that RLPA can achieve superior personalized alignment with fewer reasoning tokens compared to general-purpose reasoning LLMs highlights the efficiency of its specialized, profile-based reasoning. This suggests a more optimized approach for specific dialogue tasks, potentially influencing future LLM architectural designs for personalization.

#### 5.2. Practical Applications and Societal Impact

- **Enhanced User Experience:** The primary benefit of dynamic personalized alignment is a drastically improved user experience. LLMs capable of truly adapting to individual needs can offer more relevant, engaging, and empathetic interactions across various applications.
- **Personalized Education:** In educational settings, an LLM that evolves with a student's learning style, knowledge gaps, and preferences could provide highly customized tutoring, content recommendations, and feedback, making learning more effective and engaging.
- **Mental Health Support and Coaching:** For sensitive domains like mental health support or life coaching, an adaptive LLM could offer more nuanced, consistent, and user-sensitive advice, potentially increasing trust and efficacy.
- **User-Centered Decision Support:** In complex decision-making scenarios, personalized LLMs could act as intelligent assistants that understand a user's goals, risk tolerance, and information preferences, providing tailored recommendations and explanations.
- **Industry Adoption:** The collaboration with an industry partner (Du Xiaoman Financial) suggests the potential for real-world adoption, particularly in customer service, financial advisory, or other user-facing applications where long-term customer relationships and personalized interaction are key. The competitive performance against commercial models further strengthens its practical viability.

#### 5.3. Limitations and Future Directions (as identified by authors)

While impactful, the authors acknowledge several limitations that pave the way for future research:
- **Single-User Focus:** The current RLPA framework assumes a single-user interaction thread. Future work could extend this to multi-user, multi-session, or cross-domain personalization, which would better reflect the complexity of real-world usage patterns.
- **Theoretical Understanding:** The theoretical underpinnings of long-term alignment dynamics and convergence properties within this MDP framework remain underexplored. More principled frameworks for continual user modeling and policy generalization could offer deeper insights.
- **Ethical Considerations (Broader Impact Statement):** The authors also highlight the ethical implications of dynamic user modeling, such as the potential for inferring sensitive attributes without consent, misuse for manipulation or surveillance, and the risk of amplifying filter bubbles. Future work needs to focus on privacy-preserving mechanisms, transparency (e.g., user consent, profile inspection), and safeguards to ensure equitable, respectful, and safe use of personalized AI.

Overall, this work presents a compelling and empirically strong case for reinforcement learning as a powerful approach to dynamic personalized alignment in LLMs. Its findings open new avenues for building more intelligent, adaptive, and genuinely user-centric conversational agents, while also prompting critical consideration of the ethical responsibilities inherent in such advanced personalization capabilities.
