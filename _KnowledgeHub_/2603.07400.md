# Perceptive Variable-Timing Footstep Planning for Humanoid Locomotion on Disconnected Footholds

> [!summary] Summary
> A framework for humanoid robot locomotion integrates online perception, mixed-integer optimization, and variable step timing to enable navigation on disconnected footholds. The approach leverages DCM-based constraints and within-step replanning to achieve stable walking at approximately 1.0 m/s on challenging terrains, with MIQP solve times averaging 13 ms.

## Problem

- Humanoid robots struggle with bipedal locomotion on complex, unstructured terrains featuring disconnected safe regions, such as stepping-stone fields.
- Effective navigation requires concurrently addressing underactuated robot dynamics, adapting step timing, and making discrete, non-convex decisions about foothold placement.
- Existing methods often lack a unified formulation that integrates real-time perception, dynamic feasibility guarantees, and robustness to disturbances within a single planning framework.

## Method

- An onboard perceptive Mixed-Integer Quadratic Program (MIQP) framework jointly plans foot placement and step duration for a short horizon (N steps) using step-to-step Divergent Component of Motion (DCM) dynamics.
- A probabilistic ego-centric terrain perception module processes depth images to extract a union of convex steppable regions in real-time for the MIQP planner.
- The framework embeds explicit DCM-based capturability and viability constraints, and includes a within-step replanning mechanism that continuously updates the initial DCM from sensor measurements to enhance robustness.

## Results

- The framework successfully enabled stable and dynamically consistent locomotion of a Digit humanoid model on randomized stepping-stone fields in simulation, achieving a forward velocity of approximately **1.0** m/s.
- The MIQP planner demonstrated real-time feasibility with an average solve time of approximately 13 ms, making it suitable for onboard computation.
- Ablation studies confirmed that variable step duration, a sufficient planning horizon (N>2), and explicit DCM-based viability constraints are all essential for successful navigation on complex, disconnected terrains.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Jointly optimizing discrete foothold selection and variable step duration within a reduced-order, capturability-aware template is critical for enabling humanoid locomotion on highly constrained and disconnected terrains.
> - Explicitly embedding DCM-based safety constraints (one-step lateral and infinite-step sagittal bounds) provides rigorous guarantees for dynamic feasibility and robustness against instability.
> - Continuous within-step replanning, by backward-propagating measured instantaneous DCM, is effective in increasing robustness to model mismatch and external disturbances in real-time operation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Bipedal locomotion on complex and unstructured terrains, characterized by obstacles, unsafe ground patches, or discontinuous safe regions (e.g., stepping stones), represents a significant challenge in robotics. Addressing this challenge requires control systems that can simultaneously manage underactuated robot dynamics, adapt to varying environmental conditions, and make discrete, often non-convex decisions about foothold placement and step timing.

Prior research in footstep planning has explored various methodologies. Graph search and sampling-based methods have been developed for long-horizon terrain reasoning, exemplified by approaches that plan discrete footholds across rough terrain. While effective for global pathfinding, these methods often operate decoupled from the robot's underlying dynamics, which can make real-time feasibility guarantees at the control level challenging.

Optimization-based methods, particularly those employing mixed-integer formulations, have emerged as a principled approach for integrating terrain constraints with dynamic planning. Early work introduced convex segmentation and mixed-integer footstep planning for uneven ground, which was later extended to aggressive or rough-terrain motions and phase-based end-effector parameterizations. The integration of reduced-order dynamics with perceptive terrain constraints and kinodynamic constraints for rigorous foothold selection has also been demonstrated.

Another critical aspect of robust bipedal locomotion is timing adaptation. Research has shown that optimizing step duration can significantly improve robustness and feasibility. This includes classic preview control ideas, switching time optimization in whole-body Model Predictive Control (MPC), and Divergent Component of Motion (DCM)-based step timing adaptation on restricted footholds.

In parallel, perceptive walking capabilities, particularly using elevation maps or heightmaps, have been widely adopted for modeling complex terrains. Integrating elevation map-based plane segmentation with whole-body MPC has enabled quadrupeds to navigate various terrains. For bipeds, vision-derived terrain information has been coupled with mixed-integer optimization for walking on rough terrain. However, a unified formulation that simultaneously optimizes discrete foothold selection on disconnected regions and variable step duration within a reduced-order, capturability-aware template has been an area of ongoing exploration.

More recently, data-driven approaches, such as learning-based foothold selection or locomotion policies, have shown empirical capabilities for dynamic walking across stepping stones. While these methods can achieve impressive performance, certifying viability and capturability while strictly enforcing membership in disconnected regions at rapid replanning rates often remains challenging.

This work contributes to the landscape by proposing a mixed-integer MPC framework that integrates multiple previously explored concepts: real-time online perception, step-to-step DCM dynamics, variable step duration, discrete foothold selection on disconnected regions, and explicit capturability/viability constraints. Furthermore, it introduces a within-step replanning mechanism that utilizes backward-propagated measured DCM, addressing robustness to model mismatch and external disturbances, which distinguishes it from previous approaches that may not concurrently optimize all these aspects in a single formulation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the challenges associated with enabling humanoid robots to navigate real-world environments that often contain obstacles, unstable surfaces, or clutter. Such scenarios typically reduce the available ground for stepping to a disconnected set of admissible footholds, akin to stepping-stone fields. Effective locomotion in these conditions necessitates a control framework that can concurrently address several coupled, complex problems:

1. **Underactuated Dynamics:** Bipedal robots are inherently underactuated, requiring careful management of their balance and center of mass (CoM) motion to maintain stability during walking.
2. **Adaptive Step Timing:** The duration of each step significantly influences the robot's stability and its ability to span gaps or adjust to dynamic conditions. Optimizing step duration alongside foot placement is crucial, as longer steps can increase unstable mode amplification, while shorter steps might limit progress or flexibility.
3. **Discrete, Nonconvex Foothold Feasibility:** The admissible footholds are not continuously distributed but form a union of disconnected, non-convex regions. The planner must make discrete choices about which region to step on for each foot.
4. **Online Perception:** In untethered, real-world operation, the precise locations and shapes of these safe regions are not known *a priori*. They must be identified and mapped online using onboard perception systems.

The authors identify several specific gaps in existing research that motivate their proposed framework:

- **Integrated Optimization of Discrete Foothold Selection and Variable Step Duration:** While some methods addressed either discrete foothold selection or timing adaptation, a formulation that simultaneously optimizes both within a reduced-order, capturability-aware template, especially for disconnected regions, was considered under-explored.
- **Explicit Capturability and Viability Constraints:** Previous mixed-integer footstep planners did not always explicitly embed capturability and viability-based constraints derived from the DCM space. Such constraints are essential for shaping the feasible set with interpretable safety margins and ensuring dynamic stability.
- **Robustness to Model Mismatch and Disturbances:** Existing approaches often lacked mechanisms for robust real-time adaptation to disturbances or inaccuracies in the robot model. A method for within-step replanning by continuously updating the initial DCM from sensor measurements was identified as a necessary improvement for practical deployment.

Based on these motivations, the key objective of this work is to develop an onboard, perceptive mixed-integer model predictive control (MPC) framework that jointly plans foot placement and step duration for humanoid robots traversing disconnected footholds. The framework aims to achieve this by integrating four main components:
1. Step-to-step DCM dynamics with variable step duration.
2. Explicit DCM-based capturability and viability constraints to ensure dynamic safety.
3. An onboard terrain perception interface that processes ego-centric depth images into a union of convex steppable regions in real-time.
4. Slack variable regularization of stride differences to promote smooth and consistent locomotion.
Additionally, a specific goal is to enhance robustness by implementing a within-step replanning mechanism that updates the initial DCM using measured instantaneous DCM.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework integrates perception, planning, and control modules to enable dynamic bipedal locomotion on disconnected footholds. The overall architecture consists of a perception module processing depth images, an MIQP planner optimizing foot placement and step duration, and a task-space controller executing the plan.

**A. Probabilistic Ego-Centric Terrain Perception**
The perception module's function is to convert ego-centric depth images into a union of convex steppable region constraints for the MIQP planner. This involves three stages:

1. **Depth Image Processing:** Multiple body-mounted depth cameras capture depth images, which are back-projected into 3D point clouds. These point clouds are then transformed into the current stance foot frame.
2. **Probabilistic Local Heightmap:** A 2.5D elevation grid (heightmap `H`) is maintained in the stance foot frame. Each cell `H(r,s)` stores a Gaussian height belief (`μ_rs`, `σ^2_rs`), an observation count, and a transform count.
 - **Frame Motion Compensation:** When the stance foot pose changes between cycles, the existing grid is transformed. Safeguards include motion thresholds to prevent unnecessary resampling, resampling penalties (variance inflation and observation count decay) to reduce confidence in propagated data, and staleness clearing for old cells.
 - **ICP Registration:** Iterative Closest Point (ICP) is used to refine the alignment of new point cloud observations against the existing heightmap, mitigating odometry drift. Height-aware correspondence filtering and outlier rejection enhance robustness.
 - **Observation Variance Model:** The uncertainty of depth observations (`σ^2_obs`) is modeled as a function of distance and viewing angle.
 - **Bayesian Cell Update:** For multiple depth points in the same cell, heights are aggregated via precision-weighted averaging, then fused with the cell's prior belief using a Kalman-style update.
 - **Temporal Uncertainty Decay:** For unobserved cells, variance grows at a motion-adaptive rate, reflecting reduced confidence when the robot is moving.
3. **Convex Region Constraints:**
 - **Approximate Convex Region Extraction:** Steppable regions are identified by thresholding the heightmap, and contours are extracted. Small contours are filtered, and remaining ones are approximated by polygons using the Ramer–Douglas–Peucker (RDP) algorithm. These polygons are then converted into convex hulls using Sklansky's algorithm and represented as half-space constraints (`A_j p ≤ b_j`).
 - **Velocity-Adaptive Region Selection:** If the number of detected regions exceeds the planner's capacity, a rectangular "beam mask" aligned with the walking direction selects a relevant subset. This beam's position is shifted based on the commanded goal and robot speed, ensuring the stance region is always included.

**B. Variable Time Foot Placement via MIQP**
The core planning component is a Mixed-Integer Quadratic Program (MIQP) that optimizes foot placements and step durations over a short horizon (N steps) using step-to-step DCM dynamics.

1. **DCM and Step-to-step Dynamics:** The DCM (`ξ_0,k`) at the start of step `k` and the stance foot/CoP location (`p_k`) during step `k` are related by a linear recursion: `ξ_0,k+1 = e^(λT_k) ξ_0,k + (1 - e^(λT_k)) p_k`. Here `T_k` is the step duration and `λ` is the natural frequency. The term `σ_k = e^(λT_k)` is introduced as a decision variable to linearize the form for MIQP.
2. **Decision Variables:** The MIQP optimizes over foot placements (`p_k`), DCM states (`z_k`), and `σ_k` for `N` steps. Slack variables (`s_x,k`, `s_y,k`) are introduced for stride length and width, regularizing inter-step differences. Step durations are bounded: `σ_min ≤ σ_k ≤ σ_max`.
3. **Objective Function:** The MIQP minimizes a quadratic cost function `J(·)` that combines:
 - Tracking of the DCM state (`z_k`) to a goal (`z_goal`).
 - Penalization of deviations of `σ_k` from a nominal step duration (`σ_nom`).
 - Penalization of deviations of stride length (`s_x,k`) and width (`s_y,k`) from nominal values (`p_x,nom`, `p_y,nom`).
4. **Safety and Capturability Constraints:** To ensure dynamic feasibility and prevent instability, the following linear constraints are imposed on the DCM:
 - **Inner-side Lateral Corridor:** A one-step capturability condition ensures the DCM remains on the inner side of the stance foot with a margin (`∆_y`), preventing leg crossing.
 - **Longitudinal Capturability:** An infinite-step capturability bound limits the unstable state growth in the sagittal direction, assuming future steps take maximum length at minimum duration, tightened by a robustness margin (`∆_x`).
 - **Step-to-step Growth:** Limits unrealistic forward amplification of the unstable state between successive steps.
5. **Stepping-Stone (Disconnected) Region Constraints:** Membership in the union of `M` convex regions (`R_j`) is enforced using binary assignment variables (`δ_kj`) and a big-M relaxation. For each step `k`, exactly one region `j` must be selected (`Σ δ_kj = 1`).
6. **Bounds and Slack Constraints:** Box bounds (`L_min`, `L_max`, `W_min`, `W_max`) are applied to stride lengths and widths, reflecting kinematic reach and self-collision limits.
7. **Variable Step Duration MIQP Specialization:** To maintain tractability, only the first step's timing (`σ_1`) is optimized as a decision variable, while subsequent timings are fixed to `σ_nom`.
8. **Within-step Replanning:** The planner runs continuously during a step. At each replanning instant (`~t`), the initial DCM (`z_1`) for the current step is updated by backward-propagating the measured instantaneous DCM (`ξ(~t)_mea`), improving robustness to disturbances and model mismatch.

**C. Low-Level Controller**
A task-space, whole-body controller tracks the robot's base height, torso orientation, and swing foot position/orientation. Swing-foot reference trajectories are generated as minimum-jerk profiles. If the target landing pose or duration changes mid-swing due to replanning, the remaining trajectory segment is updated to smoothly reach the new target. The controller uses a dimensionless phase variable, allowing stable updates without transients regardless of timing variations.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The proposed perceptive mixed-integer predictive control framework was evaluated through simulations using the Digit humanoid robot model within the MuJoCo physics engine. The simulation timestep was 1 ms, the low-level controller ran at 1 ms, and the MIQP planner operated at a cycle of 30 ms. The depth-based perception module updated at the beginning of each step and then every 180 ms during the step. Gurobi (Python interface) was used for solving the MIQP online.

**A. Performance on Randomized Stepping-Stone Fields**
The framework was tested on randomized stepping-stone fields, designed to present irregular gaps and sparsity, thereby challenging discrete selection and timing.
- The planner successfully generated terrain-aware and dynamically consistent footstep sequences.
- It demonstrated adaptive timing, where step durations were actively adjusted throughout the swing phase to maintain capturability, particularly when facing scarce or irregular footholds.
- The system achieved a forward (x) velocity of approximately 1.0 m/s, indicating capabilities for high-speed dynamic walking even on constrained terrains. The y-velocity showed periodic oscillations consistent with bipedal gait.
- Analysis of the CoM and DCM trajectories in a top-down view showed smooth evolution, indicating stable balance control throughout the challenging terrain navigation.

**B. Computational Efficiency**
- The average per-iteration solve time for the MIQP (including formulation, update, and solve) in Python was approximately 13 ms, with a standard deviation of 3 ms and a range of ms. These times are indicative of real-time feasibility for onboard computation.

**C. Ablation Studies**
To assess the contribution of key components, the proposed framework (denoted as A) was compared against three modified versions:
- **Fixed Step Duration (B):** This variant failed to navigate the randomized terrain. The lack of variable step duration over-constrained the DCM evolution, preventing the planner from finding viable foot placements and timings to traverse sparse or irregular footholds.
- **Reduced Preview Horizon (N=2) (C):** With a shortened preview horizon of only two steps, this approach also failed. The limited foresight proved insufficient for determining a feasible sequence of footsteps through the complex, random regions, especially under high-speed dynamic walking conditions.
- **No Viability Constraints (D):** This version, lacking explicit capturability and viability bounds on DCM evolution, likewise failed. Without these constraints, the DCM state could be pushed too close to the stability boundaries, leading to rapid degradation into infeasible regions when subjected to system noise and uncertainties.

These ablation studies collectively indicate that jointly optimizing variable step duration, maintaining a sufficient planning horizon, and enforcing explicit DCM-based viability constraints are critical for enabling successful and robust bipedal locomotion on highly constrained and disconnected terrains.

**D. Robustness to Disturbances**
The within-step replanning mechanism, which backward-propagates the measured instantaneous DCM to update the initial DCM for the planner, contributed to robustness. The planned initial DCM was observed to converge stably to the measured one by the end of a swing phase, even when abrupt in-step changes occurred due to newly emerging tight region constraints. This demonstrates the framework's ability to continuously correct its internal state and adapt to real-time measurements.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a significant advancement in the field of humanoid robot locomotion by integrating several critical capabilities into a single, cohesive framework. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Enabling Advanced Humanoid Locomotion:** The work directly addresses the challenge of humanoid robots navigating complex, unstructured, and often hazardous real-world environments characterized by disconnected, stepping-stone-like footholds. This capability is crucial for deploying humanoid robots in domains such as disaster response, construction, or exploration, where terrain is rarely perfectly flat or continuous.
2. **Unified Planning for Discrete and Continuous Variables:** The proposed Mixed-Integer Quadratic Program (MIQP) framework uniquely unifies the optimization of discrete foothold selection (choosing among disconnected regions) and continuous step duration (adaptive timing) within the same problem formulation. This joint optimization is critical, as these aspects are deeply coupled in dynamic locomotion. Previous methods often addressed these components separately or did not fully integrate them with real-time perception.
3. **Enhanced Dynamic Stability and Robustness:** The explicit embedding of Divergent Component of Motion (DCM)-based capturability and viability constraints, including both lateral one-step and sagittal infinite-step bounds, provides a rigorous guarantee of dynamic feasibility and safety margins. Furthermore, the novel within-step replanning mechanism, which continuously updates the initial DCM based on real-time measurements, significantly improves the system's robustness to model mismatch and external disturbances. This is a crucial step towards reliable operation in unpredictable environments.
4. **Real-Time Perceptive Capabilities:** The integration of an onboard, probabilistic depth-sensing-based perception pipeline that robustly extracts convex steppable regions from ego-centric sensor data allows the robot to adapt to unknown terrains online. This removes the reliance on pre-mapped environments, expanding the autonomy of humanoid robots.
5. **Computational Efficiency for Onboard Deployment:** The achieved millisecond-level solve times for the MIQP, even in a Python implementation, indicate the practical feasibility of deploying this complex planning framework on onboard hardware. This paves the way for direct implementation on physical humanoid robots without requiring offboard computational resources.
6. **Foundation for Future Research:** This work lays a foundation for several future research directions. These include the development of uncertainty-aware footstep planning to explicitly account for perception and control uncertainties, extension to more complex terrain types (e.g., highly compliant surfaces, moving obstacles), and, critically, the experimental realization and validation on physical humanoid robot hardware such as Digit. The success in simulation suggests a strong potential for transferring these capabilities to real-world applications, moving humanoid robotics closer to general-purpose agile locomotion.
