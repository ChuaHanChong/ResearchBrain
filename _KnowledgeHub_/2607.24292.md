# Learning Adaptive Multi-Task Guidance, Navigation, and Control via Hypernetworks

> [!summary] Summary
> The University of Luxembourg and Georgia Institute of Technology developed HYPER-GNC, a multi-task reinforcement learning framework utilizing hypernetworks and physics-informed semantic embeddings for unified Guidance, Navigation, and Control (GNC) of free-flying robots. This system achieved autonomous adaptation to diverse mission profiles, demonstrated zero-shot generalization to novel compositional tasks, and successfully transferred policies to physical hardware, showing superior performance and training efficiency compared to existing multi-task baselines.

## Problem

- Traditional space robot GNC relies on separate, task-specific controllers, leading to high computational costs, architectural brittleness, and limited operational flexibility due to the need for ground-based intervention.
- Existing Multi-Task Reinforcement Learning (MTRL) methods struggle with conflicting objectives among tasks and diverse state spaces, often resulting in unstable training, large models, or an inability to generalize to new scenarios.
- Current approaches using discrete task identifiers cannot synthesize novel behavioral combinations, hindering autonomous adaptation to unforeseen mission configurations without retraining.

## Method

- Introduced **HYPER-GNC**, a multi-task reinforcement learning system featuring a ==hypernetwork architecture== that dynamically modulates the weights of a shared actor-critic policy based on task context.
- Employs ==physics-informed semantic embeddings== as continuous 5-dimensional vectors of control priorities (e.g., position, attitude, velocity, braking, safety) to define tasks and enable zero-shot generalization via interpolation.
- Utilizes ==Rank-1 Vector Factorization== within the hypernetwork's modulation heads to significantly reduce parameter count and incorporates comprehensive ==domain randomization== during training to facilitate robust sim-to-real transfer.

## Results

- HYPER-GNC demonstrated superior performance across four primary GNC tasks (Docking, Velocity Tracking, Inspection, Navigation with Obstacles) in simulation, achieving, for instance, a **0.005m** (±**0.003**) position error for Docking, outperforming other multi-task baselines.
- The framework enabled zero-shot generalization to novel compositional tasks, such as "Docking with Obstacles" and "Stabilization," by leveraging the continuous semantic embedding space.
- Successfully transferred learned policies to a physical satellite emulator, showing robustness to payload mass and center of mass variations, and exhibiting fuel-efficient thruster activations in real-world maneuvers.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Physics-informed continuous semantic embeddings effectively resolve conflicting GNC objectives and allow for the composition of novel behaviors by interpolating physical priorities, enabling zero-shot adaptation without explicit retraining.
> - The hypernetwork architecture with Rank-1 factorization provides a compact and efficient method for multi-task control, mitigating gradient interference and promoting stable learning without relying on auxiliary optimization techniques.
> - Extensive domain randomization during training is crucial for successful sim-to-real transfer, enabling policies to generalize and maintain robustness against real-world uncertainties and disturbances on physical hardware.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Broader Research Landscape

The current era of space exploration necessitates advancements in orbital autonomy, particularly for free-flying robots operating in complex, multi-agent environments such as the International Space Station (ISS) and the prospective Lunar Gateway. These robots require robust Guidance, Navigation, and Control (GNC) systems to perform diverse tasks like inspection, logistics, velocity tracking, and docking. Traditionally, this versatility has been addressed by maintaining a library of discrete, task-specific controllers. However, this approach presents significant limitations, including high computational cost, architectural brittleness, and a reduced operational lifetime due due to the need for ground-based intervention and controller updates as mission requirements evolve.

Reinforcement Learning (RL) has emerged as a data-driven alternative for mastering complex, non-linear dynamics in spacecraft GNC, with prior work demonstrating its utility in specific mission phases such as planetary landing, small-body operations, rendezvous and docking, and attitude stabilization. However, these applications often result in specialist models that lack generalization capabilities and are data-inefficient for novel scenarios.

Multi-Task Reinforcement Learning (MTRL) aims to address these issues by learning a single, generalized policy that can adapt to diverse tasks, theoretically improving data efficiency and leveraging shared knowledge. Nonetheless, MTRL faces its own set of challenges, including conflicting objectives among tasks (e.g., precision for docking versus speed for obstacle avoidance), diverse state spaces, and architectural complexities. Naïve approaches, such as scaling up model capacity or merging datasets, can lead to unstable training or conflicting gradients, degrading performance. Furthermore, for resource-constrained environments like orbital robotics, MTRL architectures must be compact, memory-efficient, and computationally lean.

Various MTRL methods have been explored in robotics, ranging from Goal-Conditioned RL (appending task identifiers to observations) to optimization-based techniques like Gradient Surgery (PCGrad) and architectural approaches such as Soft Modularization, Routing Networks, and Parameter-Compositional RL (PaCo). While these methods aim to mitigate task interference, they often rely on learning discrete routing strategies or a large number of expert parameters, which can be computationally intensive for flight hardware, and typically treat tasks as categorical indices.

Hypernetworks, which generate the weights for a main network, have been applied in Meta-RL and Continual Learning to enable rapid adaptation to new tasks and improve memory stability. In robotics, hypernetworks have been utilized for context-aware control, adapting policies to varying physics or capabilities, and handling heterogeneous robot teams. This paper positions its contribution within this landscape by introducing a multi-task reinforcement learning framework that leverages hypernetworks and physics-informed semantic embeddings to create a unified GNC policy, bridging the gap in robustly resolving conflicting GNC objectives in microgravity environments without relying on task-specific retraining.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a unified Guidance, Navigation, and Control (GNC) policy for free-flying spacecraft that can master distinct and complex mission profiles while synthesizing novel behavioral combinations on demand. This goal is driven by several key motivations:

1. **Enhance Versatility and Resource Efficiency:** Current reliance on a library of separate, task-specific controllers for missions such as velocity tracking, docking, inspection, and navigation with obstacle avoidance is architecturally brittle, computationally expensive, and limits operational flexibility. The authors aim to replace this with a single, compact, and resource-efficient controller.

2. **Achieve Autonomous Adaptation and Extend Operational Lifetime:** The need for ground operators to upload new or reconfigured controllers when mission requirements evolve (e.g., unexpected obstacles, unplanned rendezvous) incurs communication delays, mission downtime, and periods of reduced autonomy. A core motivation is to enable the robot to autonomously adapt to evolving mission profiles without ground intervention, thereby directly extending its effective operational lifetime.

3. **Overcome Multi-Task Learning Challenges:** Standard MTRL approaches often struggle with conflicting objectives (e.g., aggressive movement for inspection vs. precise stabilization for docking) and diverse state spaces, leading to unstable training or large, monolithic models unsuitable for constrained orbital environments. The paper aims to develop an MTRL architecture that achieves generalization and shared knowledge without excessive model size or sacrificing training stability.

4. **Enable Zero-Shot Generalization to Novel Mission Configurations:** Traditional MTRL methods that use discrete task IDs cannot synthesize novel behavioral combinations because they treat tasks as isolated categories. A key objective is to allow the controller to generalize to novel mission configurations at deployment time without any retraining by leveraging a continuous task embedding space. This approach contrasts with Meta-RL, which typically requires additional trial-and-error transitions for adaptation, introducing non-deterministic behavior and computational overhead. Instead, this work aims for adaptation through semantic interpolation for immediate deployment readiness.

5. **Validate Sim-to-Real Transfer:** To ensure real-world deployability, a significant motivation is to demonstrate the framework's effectiveness on physical hardware. This involves addressing the domain gap between simulation and reality by employing comprehensive domain randomization during training and validating the policy on a physical satellite emulator.

6. **Leverage Physics-Informed Embeddings:** The work is motivated by the hypothesis that incorporating physics-informed semantic embeddings can resolve potential task conflicts and enable robust behavioral composition by projecting mission objectives into a continuous semantic manifold, thereby guiding the policy's modulation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework, named HYPER-GNC, is a multi-task reinforcement learning system designed to generate adaptive GNC policies for free-flying robots. It is based on a hypernetwork architecture that uses physics-informed task embeddings to modulate a shared actor-critic policy.

**Problem Formulation:** The work frames multi-task reinforcement learning as learning a policy $\pi$ that maximizes the expected return across a set of N tasks, where each task is defined as a Markov Decision Process (MDP) with task-specific dynamics and reward functions.

**Robot Dynamics and Tasks:** The framework models JAXA’s Int-Ball2, a 6-DOF spherical free-flying robot, in simulation. For physical validation, policies are deployed onto a 5kg cylindrical floating platform (satellite emulator) that emulates planar microgravity dynamics.
The robot is trained on four distinct real-world mission profiles:
- **Velocity Tracking:** The robot matches target linear and angular velocities across six degrees of freedom.
- **Docking:** The robot approaches a target position and aligns its heading with the target direction.
- **Inspection:** The robot sequentially navigates through a predefined sequence of target poses.
- **Navigation with Obstacles:** The robot reaches a target position while avoiding collisions.
These tasks involve different and sometimes conflicting control objectives (e.g., precise stabilization for docking vs. maintaining momentum for inspection).

**Key Challenges and Architectural Solutions:**
1. **Conflicting Gradient Dynamics:** To mitigate issues arising from fundamentally opposing control laws (e.g., aggressive braking for docking vs. high-speed tracking for inspection), HYPER-GNC avoids a naive shared policy by dynamically modulating the main network's weights based on task context.
2. **Behavioral Composition for Novel Mission Profiles:** Instead of using discrete task IDs that limit generalization, the framework projects mission objectives into a continuous semantic manifold. This enables "zero-shot task composition," allowing the controller to synthesize novel behaviors (e.g., "Safe Docking with Obstacles") by interpolating known physical priorities at inference time without retraining.
3. **Sim2Real Gap:** For robust transfer to physical hardware (e.g., the Floating Platform with its binary action space), comprehensive **Domain Randomization** is applied during training. This involves perturbing physical parameters such as the center of mass (CoM) position, total mass variation, and the magnitude of external wrenches. Additionally, the policy's output distribution is switched from a Beta distribution (for continuous actions in Int-Ball2 simulation) to a Gaussian distribution, which is then discretized for binary thruster control on the Floating Platform, to ensure stable convergence for real-world application.

**Context-Aware Weight Modulation (HYPER-GNC Architecture):**
The architecture consists of two main components:
- **Main Network (Policy):** This serves as the actor-critic agent. Both the actor and critic are separate networks comprising a sequence of Adaptive Linear Layers. The weights and biases of these layers are dynamically modulated during inference by corresponding Hypernetworks. The actor's output heads parameterize a Beta distribution (via α and β shape parameters) for continuous action spaces, which is changed to a Gaussian for binary action spaces during sim-to-real.
- **Hypernetwork:** An individual hypernetwork is attached to each adaptive layer. It takes a **semantic task embedding `e_k`** as input. This embedding is processed by a shared Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP), referred to as the Semantic Encoder, and then fed into four distinct Modulation Heads: Weight Scaling ($\Psi_{W_{scale}}$), Weight Additive ($\Psi_{W_{add}}$), Bias Scaling ($\Psi_{b_{scale}}$), and Bias Additive ($\Psi_{b_{add}}$).
 - **Rank-1 Vector Factorization:** Each modulation head uses a Rank-1 Vector Factorization mechanism. Instead of predicting full modulation matrices, it predicts two vectors (Key `k` and Query `q`) whose outer product generates the modulation matrix/vector. This significantly reduces the number of parameters (e.g., 16x reduction for a 32x32 layer) while maintaining expressivity.
 - **Stable Initialization:** To prevent disruption of the base network at the beginning of training, Key/Query head weights are initialized with near-zero variance, and the scale head output is shifted by +1.0. This ensures that the Hypernetwork initially acts as an identity modulation, allowing task-specific adaptations to emerge gradually.

**Physics-Informed Hypernetwork Framework:**
The context vector `e_k` is a 5-dimensional vector `[α_pos, α_att, α_vel, α_brake, α_safe]`, where each dimension represents a normalized physical control priority between 0 and 1. These dimensions directly map to distinct, independent control objectives in classical GNC mission design. For instance, `α_pos` and `α_att` govern position and attitude errors, `α_vel` dictates velocity maintenance, `α_brake` enables negative acceleration, and `α_safe` activates obstacle avoidance behaviors.
- **Continuous Sampling:** During training, each dimension of `e_k` is uniformly sampled from a defined range (Table V in Appendix). This continuous sampling acts as a regularization technique, preventing the policy from overfitting to discrete task IDs and promoting generalized weight modulations.
- **Deployment:** The semantic embedding `e_k` can be dynamically set by a human operator, a higher-level mission planner, or hard-coded for specific mission phases, requiring no online learning or retraining.

**Training Details:** The policies are trained using Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) within the Isaac Lab simulator. Training is conducted across 4,096 parallel environments (1,024 per task), with PPO updates computed over all tasks in each iteration. A curriculum learning strategy, employing a sigmoid-based difficulty scheduler, is used to adjust obstacle density during training, facilitating policy convergence.

**Baselines:** The framework's performance is compared against several baselines:
- **Single-Task RL:** Task-specific policies trained from scratch.
- **Multi-Task RL (MTRL):** A baseline concatenating all task information with task IDs into a single observation vector.
- **PCGrad:** MTRL with gradient surgery to manage conflicting gradients.
- **Multi-Critic (MTCR):** PPO with a separate critic for each task.
Ablations on HYPER-GNC, such as using Gaussian PDF sampling or incorporating task IDs/semantic IDs in the observation space, are also performed.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several findings regarding the HYPER-GNC framework's performance in simulation and its transferability to real-world hardware.

**Simulation Performance (Int-Ball2):**
- **Overall Task Mastery (Table I):** HYPER-GNC demonstrated superior performance across the four primary GNC tasks (Docking, Velocity Tracking, Inspection, and Navigation with Obstacles) compared to Multi-Task RL, PCGrad, and Multi-Critic baselines. For instance, in Docking, HYPER-GNC achieved a position error of 0.005m (±0.003), significantly lower than Single-Task RL (0.017m ±0.008). Similarly, it showed competitive or improved results in other metrics and tasks.
- **Training Efficiency (Fig. 3):** The Hypernetwork-based approach exhibited sample efficiency comparable to single-task experts, converging at a similar rate, and substantially outperforming other multi-task baselines (MTRL, MTCR, PCGrad) in terms of convergence speed and final return.
- **Generalization vs. Overfitting:** While single-task experts often achieved marginally higher training returns, HYPER-GNC consistently outperformed them in deployment evaluations (Table I). This is attributed to the regularization effect of the multi-task architecture, leading to a more robust policy that generalizes effectively, rather than overfitting to specific training conditions.
- **Semantic Embeddings and Behavioral Composition (Table II):** The continuous semantic manifold enabled HYPER-GNC to achieve competitive performance on novel compositional tasks not explicitly trained for, such as "Docking with Obstacles," "Stabilization," and "Point Navigation." This validates the framework's ability to synthesize new behaviors through interpolation of physical priorities, supporting zero-shot generalization.

**Ablation Studies:**
- **Beta Distribution Superiority (Table X):** Using a Beta distribution for continuous bounded action spaces in HYPER-GNC resulted in a 50.97% overall performance improvement across tasks compared to using a Gaussian distribution, indicating its suitability for the problem domain.
- **Task Identifiers in Observations (Table X):** Including redundant task identifiers (either one-hot encoded Task IDs or the semantic embedding itself) in the observation space actually led to a slight performance degradation for HYPER-GNC. This suggests that the hypernetwork effectively captures the necessary task information directly through its weight modulation, making additional observation inputs redundant.
- **Compatibility with Auxiliary Multi-Task Methods (Table X):** Combining HYPER-GNC with auxiliary multi-task optimization techniques like PCGrad or Multi-Critic generally resulted in performance degradation or negligible gains compared to the standalone HYPER-GNC model. This indicates that HYPER-GNC's intrinsic inductive bias effectively manages task-specific nuances, rendering these additional methods less beneficial and computationally more expensive.
- **Curriculum Learning:** Implementing a sigmoid-based difficulty scheduler for obstacle density during training consistently enhanced performance, likely by providing a structured learning progression.

**Robustness Analysis (Fig. 4, Table IV):**
- The controller demonstrated resilience to internal inertial changes, such as center of mass (CoM) and mass offsets, with relative performance degradation remaining relatively low (near 1.2 factor).
- However, the system exhibited higher sensitivity to external disturbances, where increasing wrench and torque forces led to a sharper, non-linear increase in relative error. When all disturbances were applied simultaneously, performance degraded gracefully and linearly with severity.

**Sim-to-Real Transfer (Floating Platform):**
- **Successful Transfer (Table III, Fig. 5):** HYPER-GNC successfully transferred the learned policies to a physical Floating Platform for all primary mission profiles and novel compositional tasks.
- **Domain Randomization Impact:** Policies trained with domain randomization (U(0.0, 0.15) for mass and CoM offsets, U(0.0, 0.2) for wrench and torque forces) significantly reduced the sim-to-real gap, particularly for Navigation with Obstacles and Docking tasks, outperforming policies trained without randomization.
- **Fuel Efficiency (Fig. 7):** The framework produced policies characterized by smooth, sparse, and purposeful thruster firings in the real world, indicative of an efficient thruster activation scheme and energy-optimal maneuvers.
- **Sensitivity Analysis (Table IV, Fig. 6):** Real-world tests on the Docking task showed robustness to payload mass variations (1kg, 2kg, 3kg) and CoM changes (10cm, 20cm, 30cm offset with 2kg added weight), maintaining reasonable position and orientation errors.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The HYPER-GNC framework represents an advancement in autonomous control for free-flying robots in orbital environments, offering several significant implications and potential impacts:

1. **Unified and Resource-Efficient Control:** By introducing a single, compact, and low-level control policy that masters diverse GNC tasks, the work addresses a fundamental bottleneck in space robotics. This unified approach eliminates the need for maintaining multiple task-specific controllers, reducing computational overhead, memory requirements, and architectural complexity on resource-constrained flight hardware.

2. **Enhanced Operational Flexibility and Autonomy:** The framework's ability to synthesize novel behavioral combinations through a continuous semantic task manifold enables zero-shot generalization to unforeseen mission configurations. This allows robots to autonomously adapt to evolving requirements without requiring ground intervention or on-orbit retraining, directly extending their effective operational lifetime and enhancing overall mission flexibility. This capability moves towards a paradigm where robots can dynamically adjust their behavior based on changing physical priorities.

3. **Robustness and Real-World Deployability:** Extensive simulations with Int-Ball2 and successful sim-to-real transfer validation on a physical satellite emulator demonstrate the framework's robustness. The integration of domain randomization during training significantly narrows the simulation-to-reality gap, making the learned policies more resilient to real-world uncertainties such as inertial perturbations, external forces, and actuator dynamics. This is crucial for the safe and reliable deployment of autonomous systems in space.

4. **Improved Training Efficiency and Stability:** HYPER-GNC achieves sample efficiency comparable to single-task specialists, outperforming traditional multi-task learning baselines. The architecture, particularly the physics-informed semantic embeddings and rank-1 factorization in the hypernetwork, effectively mitigates conflicting gradients and promotes stable learning without resorting to computationally intensive external optimization techniques.

5. **Foundation for Future Space Robotics:** This work provides a scalable foundation for future developments in modular free-flyers. While currently focused on 6-DOF rigid body control, the hypernetwork architecture's potential for generating weights for policies governing coupled multi-body dynamics (e.g., robotic manipulators for active debris removal or on-orbit servicing) is a promising avenue for future research.

6. **Contribution to Reproducible Research:** The public release of code, trained models, and deployment scripts supports reproducibility and facilitates further research in multi-task reinforcement learning for space applications, encouraging community engagement and accelerated development.
