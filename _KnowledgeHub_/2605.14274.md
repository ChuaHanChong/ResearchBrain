# CreFlow: Corrective Reflow for Sparse-Reward Embodied Video Diffusion RL

> [!summary] Summary
> CreFlow is a framework that post-trains embodied video generation models for robotic manipulation by employing a compositional, LTL-based reward model for diagnostic feedback and an online reinforcement learning framework with localized updates. This approach achieved an average success rate of 53.1% across eight bimanual manipulation tasks, representing a 23.8 percentage point improvement over the base model, and demonstrated high fidelity in reward judgment.

## Problem

- Videos generated by contemporary models often lack physical plausibility for embodied manipulation due to general pretraining data and likelihood-surrogate objectives.
- Existing video reinforcement learning rewards for manipulation are insufficient, failing to capture compositional task requirements or subtle, pixel-level errors critical for task success.
- Applying uniform reinforcement learning updates across high-dimensional video rollouts is inefficient for localized task errors, leading to wasted gradients and potential off-manifold drift.

## Method

- A ==compositional constraint monitor== leverages ==Linear Temporal Logic (LTL)== to specify complex task requirements, providing a binary success signal and spatio-temporal diagnostic information (where and why a rollout fails).
- A ==credit-aware NFT loss== uses a ==spatio-temporal mask== to confine reinforcement learning updates to reward-relevant regions, preventing perturbations to unrelated pixels and allocating gradients efficiently.
- A ==corrective reflow loss== explicitly estimates a correction direction for failed rollouts by using the empirical mean of within-group successful rollouts as a lower-variance target, stabilizing and accelerating post-training.

## Results

- CreFlow achieved a **53.1%** average success rate on eight RoboTwin bimanual manipulation tasks, a **23.8 percentage point** improvement over the Vidar base model and superior to other reinforcement learning baselines.
- The compositional reward model demonstrated high fidelity, with **88.0%** agreement with human labels and **91.0%** agreement with simulator labels, outperforming existing reward models in capturing manipulation success.
- CreFlow exhibited faster and more stable reward improvement during training compared to DanceGRPO and DiffusionNFT, especially on long-horizon, multi-stage tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Formalizing task requirements with LTL and providing spatio-temporal diagnostic information allows for more faithful and effective reward signals than general visual metrics for manipulation tasks.
> - Localizing reinforcement learning updates to task-relevant spatio-temporal regions in high-dimensional video is crucial for efficient gradient allocation and preventing off-manifold drift.
> - Leveraging within-group positive samples as explicit corrective targets for failed rollouts leads to more stable and faster training by providing a direct supervisory signal for error correction.
