# CreFlow: Corrective Reflow for Sparse-Reward Embodied Video Diffusion RL

> [!summary] Summary
> CreFlow is a framework that post-trains embodied video generation models for robotic manipulation by employing a compositional, LTL-based reward model for diagnostic feedback and an online reinforcement learning framework with localized updates. This approach achieved an average success rate of 53.1% across eight bimanual manipulation tasks, representing a 23.8 percentage point improvement over the base model, and demonstrated high fidelity in reward judgment.

## Problem

- Videos generated by contemporary models often lack physical plausibility for embodied manipulation due to general pretraining data and likelihood-surrogate objectives.
- Existing video reinforcement learning rewards for manipulation are insufficient, failing to capture compositional task requirements or subtle, pixel-level errors critical for task success.
- Applying uniform reinforcement learning updates across high-dimensional video rollouts is inefficient for localized task errors, leading to wasted gradients and potential off-manifold drift.

## Method

- A ==compositional constraint monitor== leverages ==Linear Temporal Logic (LTL)== to specify complex task requirements, providing a binary success signal and spatio-temporal diagnostic information (where and why a rollout fails).
- A ==credit-aware NFT loss== uses a ==spatio-temporal mask== to confine reinforcement learning updates to reward-relevant regions, preventing perturbations to unrelated pixels and allocating gradients efficiently.
- A ==corrective reflow loss== explicitly estimates a correction direction for failed rollouts by using the empirical mean of within-group successful rollouts as a lower-variance target, stabilizing and accelerating post-training.

## Results

- CreFlow achieved a **53.1%** average success rate on eight RoboTwin bimanual manipulation tasks, a **23.8 percentage point** improvement over the Vidar base model and superior to other reinforcement learning baselines.
- The compositional reward model demonstrated high fidelity, with **88.0%** agreement with human labels and **91.0%** agreement with simulator labels, outperforming existing reward models in capturing manipulation success.
- CreFlow exhibited faster and more stable reward improvement during training compared to DanceGRPO and DiffusionNFT, especially on long-horizon, multi-stage tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Formalizing task requirements with LTL and providing spatio-temporal diagnostic information allows for more faithful and effective reward signals than general visual metrics for manipulation tasks.
> - Localizing reinforcement learning updates to task-relevant spatio-temporal regions in high-dimensional video is crucial for efficient gradient allocation and preventing off-manifold drift.
> - Leveraging within-group positive samples as explicit corrective targets for failed rollouts leads to more stable and faster training by providing a direct supervisory signal for error correction.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The integration of video generation models (VGMs) into robotic manipulation systems is a developing area, offering a foundation for planning and control. Despite progress in perceptual fidelity, videos synthesized by contemporary VGMs often exhibit physical implausibility in embodied manipulation contexts. This limitation arises from web-scale pretraining data, which includes heterogeneous sources that may violate physical rules, and likelihood-surrogate objectives that prioritize global plausibility over task-critical robot-object interactions.

Reinforcement Learning (RL) has emerged as a method to align general-purpose VGMs with specific reward targets, such as visual quality, 3D consistency, or action executability. However, the design of effective rewards for manipulation video RL presents particular challenges. Generated videos can appear globally coherent yet contain subtle, pixel-level errors that lead to downstream failures. Existing video RL rewards, often based on single visual metrics, may not adequately capture these crucial violations because manipulation video evaluation inherently requires compositional assessment—multiple requirements must be simultaneously met for task success. Furthermore, high-dimensional video rollouts often contain a large proportion of reward-irrelevant pixels and time intervals. Uniformly applying RL updates across the entire video can lead to inefficient gradient allocation, potentially perturbing plausible or irrelevant regions and causing off-manifold drift.

This work addresses these challenges by proposing a novel approach that combines a compositional constraint-based reward model with a specialized online RL framework. It extends beyond existing methods that use compositional constraints for filtering implausible videos (e.g., EmboAlign) by focusing on improving the VGM itself through constraint-guided post-training. It also differentiates from prior video reward models that rely on image-level metrics, learned preference rewards for general video quality, or specific physical properties by introducing Linear Temporal Logic (LTL) for compositional task specification and localized failure diagnosis. In the context of RL algorithms for generative models (e.g., DiffusionNFT, DanceGRPO), this work introduces mechanisms to handle sparse, high-dimensional rewards more effectively by localizing updates and leveraging within-group sample relationships.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives of this research are to:

1. **Develop a compositional constraint-based reward model:** The goal is to create a reward system for post-training embodied video generation models that provides not only a faithful success signal (i.e., whether a video rollout could lead to successful task execution) but also diagnostic information regarding *where* and *why* a rollout fails. This addresses the limitations of existing reward models that often simplify manipulation video evaluation to low-level visual metrics, failing to capture the compositional nature of task requirements or the subtle, decisive errors in robot-object interactions.
2. **Formulate task requirements using Linear Temporal Logic (LTL):** The motivation is to automatically translate complex, compositional task specifications (e.g., "put the object into the cabinet's drawer" involving object persistence, kinematic validity, action causality, and goal satisfaction) into a structured, verifiable format. LTL constraints, evaluated over robot and object state traces, aim to capture the causal logic within generated videos and provide spatio-temporal diagnostic information necessary for pinpointing violations.
3. **Propose an online RL framework, CreFlow, for effective post-training with sparse, localized signals:** The objective is to convert the sparse, binary feedback from the compositional constraint monitor into actionable supervision for high-dimensional video diffusion models. This is motivated by the inefficiency and instability of applying uniform RL updates across entire video rollouts when task-critical errors are localized in space and time, leading to wasted gradients and potential off-manifold drift. CreFlow aims to achieve effective improvement by focusing updates on reward-relevant regions and leveraging relational information among rollouts.

Specifically, CreFlow aims to achieve this through two key designs:
 - **Credit-aware NFT loss:** To confine RL updates to reward-relevant regions. This prevents perturbations to unrelated regions during post-training, which is crucial given that manipulation success often hinges on small spatio-temporal details while most pixels are irrelevant.
 - **Corrective reflow loss:** To explicitly estimate a correction direction using within-group positive samples. This aims to stabilize and accelerate training by providing a direct target for failed rollouts, moving beyond merely suppressing the generation probability of negative samples.

By achieving these objectives, the research seeks to improve the physical plausibility and downstream execution success of videos generated by VGMs in robotic manipulation tasks, addressing a fundamental gap between visually plausible generation and practical robotic control.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The CreFlow framework introduces a compositional constraint-based reward model and an online RL training methodology to post-train embodied video generation models (VGMs). It focuses on converting sparse task violations into localized corrective updates.

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation and Preliminaries

The work considers online reinforcement learning post-training of a pretrained flow-matching text-image-to-video (TI2V) model for robot manipulation. A trainable video generator `pθ` samples rollouts `x0`, which are then mapped to robot actions `a` by a frozen inverse-dynamics decoder `IIDM`. The objective is to maximize the binary success indicator `Succ(a; y)`, which is expensive to evaluate. During post-training, this is surrogated by a compositional constraint reward. CreFlow builds on the DiffusionNFT framework, which uses positive (`v+θ`) and negative (`v−θ`) branches to reduce the squared error between model predictions and rollout velocities, weighted by the rollout's reward `ri`.

#### 3.2. Compositional Constraint Monitor

To provide faithful and diagnostic reward signals, CreFlow introduces a compositional constraint monitor `Mc` that evaluates generated rollouts `x0` based on task-specific requirements. This process involves two stages:

- **Specification Synthesis:** For a given task context `y` (language instruction `c` and first-frame observation `I`), an offline coding agent synthesizes:
 - A set of task-relevant entities `Ec` (e.g., robot parts, specific objects).
 - An entity-grounded predicate library `Pc` (e.g., `GRASP(arm, cup)`, `IN(cup, drawer)`).
 - A Linear Temporal Logic (LTLf) conjunction `Φc = VKk=1 φk`, where each clause `φk` instantiates from a template library covering persistence (`G p`), terminal placement (`FG p`), causal coupling (`G(p → q)`), and ordering (`p U q`). This structured approach ensures template-driven witness extraction.

- **State Lifting and Clause Evaluation:** For each generated rollout `x0`, the video is lifted into a per-entity state trace `σ(x0; y)`. This lifting is performed by cooperating vision foundation models: SAM3 for object masks and trajectories, an Inverse Dynamics Model (IDM) for robot and gripper states, and a Visual Language Model (VLM) for attribute checks. As a byproduct, a SAM3 mask atlas `S` (swept masks for each entity) is collected. Each predicate `p` from `Pc` is evaluated as a Boolean stream, and each LTL clause `φk` is evaluated by a finite-trace monitor. This returns a truth value `rk` and a violation witness `Wk` (set of entities and frames responsible for failure). The overall reward `r(x0; y)` is binary (1 if `Φc` is satisfied, 0 otherwise). The violation records `V` and mask atlas `S` provide spatio-temporal diagnostic information.

#### 3.3. Credit-Aware NFT Loss

To address the issue of gradient accumulation on reward-irrelevant pixels, CreFlow modifies the DiffusionNFT loss with a spatio-temporal mask.

- **Spatio-Temporal Credit Mask:** A single binary mask `M` is shared across all rollouts within a group. This mask is aggregated from the per-rollout violation traces and SAM3 atlases. The temporal component `MT` collects all frames implicated by any failing clause across the group. The spatial component `MS` unions the entity atlases of all rollouts (successful or failed) to preserve a common support for competition between positive and negative loss branches. `M = MT ⊗ MS`. If all rollouts in a group succeed, `M` is effectively zero, reflecting a vanishing group-relative advantage.

- **Loss Formulation:** The Credit-Aware NFT loss (`LCA_NFT`) gates both positive and negative branches of the original DiffusionNFT loss with `M`. This restricts the squared-error residual to the identified task-relevant regions, preventing updates on non-violating or unrelated pixels. This localization aims to prevent off-manifold drift and efficiently allocate gradients.

#### 3.4. Corrective Reflow Loss

Existing RL methods often process rollouts in isolation. CreFlow leverages the relational structure within a group of rollouts generated under the same initial condition.

- **Corrective Target:** For failed rollouts, the empirical mean of the within-group successful rollouts (`¯x+0`) serves as an explicit corrective target. Since positive rollouts under the same conditioning `y` are i.i.d. samples from the successful-rollout distribution, `¯x+0` is an unbiased estimator of the within-condition positive mean. This aggregated target reduces variance compared to using a single positive sample.

- **Loss Formulation:** The Corrective Reflow loss (`LCR`) applies only to negative rollouts. It regresses the model's one-step `x0`-prediction (`ˆx(i)0`) toward `¯x+0` on the masked regions `M`. This is equivalent to a velocity-space regression, where the corrective velocity target `ev⋆i` is derived from `x(i)t` and `¯x+0`. This provides an explicit supervisory signal for how a failed region should be corrected, leading to more stable and faster post-training.

#### 3.5. Overall Training Objective

The full CreFlow objective combines these components:
`LCreFlow(θ) = LCA_NFT(θ) + λCR LCR(θ) + λKL LKL(θ; θref)`
where `λCR` and `λKL` are scalar weights, and `LKL` is a KL regularizer to a frozen reference policy (`θref`), typically the pretrained VGM itself.

#### 3.6. Theoretical Analysis (Appendix A Summary)

The theoretical analysis in the appendix provides formal justifications for CreFlow's designs. It shows that:
- The violation-localized estimator concentrates update strength on failure-relevant coordinates, avoiding off-mask noise.
- The masked estimator is unbiased under reward locality.
- The corrective branch yields a strictly lower-variance parameter-gradient estimator than the NFT branch at small noise levels. This is due to being structurally EMA-free (eliminating a persistent noise floor from reflected EMA plug-ins) and the `x0`-space loss formulation which introduces a `t^2` down-weighting of the gradient, effectively converting a `Θ(1/t^2)` divergent target covariance into an `O(t^2)` vanishing gradient floor at small `t`.
- The active-coordinate update combines an NFT pull along the reward-induced direction `Δ` and a corrective pull along the marginal-prototype direction `¯Δ`, guided by the mask `M`.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments were conducted on eight RoboTwin bimanual manipulation tasks, using a pretrained Vidar text-image-to-video model as the base VGM and its IDM as the frozen action decoder.

#### 4.1. Benchmark Comparison (Downstream Task Success)

- **Superior Performance:** CreFlow achieved the highest success rate across all eight tasks, averaging 53.1%, which is a 23.8 percentage point improvement over the Vidar base (30.4%) and significantly higher than other RL post-training baselines.
- **Comparison with Baselines:**
 - `π0.5` (policy-level reference) and Vidar base performed at 30.1% and 30.4% average success, respectively.
 - EVA (an existing manipulation-video-RL method with IDM-based continuous reward) showed only marginal improvement (34.3%), suggesting that low-level motion artifact penalties are insufficient for compositional manipulation tasks.
 - DanceGRPO and DiffusionNFT, when using the same binary compositional reward as CreFlow, performed at 29.4% and 42.4% respectively, indicating that CreFlow's localized optimization strategy is more effective than generic policy gradients or unlocalized negative-aware finetuning.
- **Long-Horizon Task Gains:** The improvements were particularly substantial in long-horizon, multi-stage tasks (e.g., `put_bottles_dustbin`, `stack_bowls_three`), supporting the hypothesis that LTL-based verification and localized updates are beneficial for complex sequential tasks.

#### 4.2. Training Convergence

- CreFlow demonstrated faster and more stable reward improvement compared to DanceGRPO and DiffusionNFT on representative tasks (`rank_blocks_rgb`, `put_bottles_dustbin`, `stack_bowls_three`). This suggests that its localized correction strategy enhances optimization efficiency and final performance.
- DanceGRPO exhibited unstable or limited improvement, attributed to its non-targeted policy-gradient updates under sparse binary rewards.
- DiffusionNFT was more stable than DanceGRPO but plateaued at a lower reward, indicating its limitation when negative-aware updates are applied over the full high-dimensional rollout without failure localization.

#### 4.3. Reward Fidelity to Downstream Success

- **High Agreement:** On a set of 100 held-out videos with human and simulator labels, CreFlow's compositional reward showed 88.0% agreement with human labels and 91.0% agreement with simulator labels, outperforming all other reward models.
- **Pairwise Ranking Accuracy:** CreFlow achieved a pairwise ranking accuracy of 0.893, which was notably higher than the strongest continuous baseline, EVA (0.792).
- **LTL Advantage:** The VLM-as-judge baseline (74.0% accuracy vs. simulator) indicated that binary scoring alone is insufficient; the advantage of CreFlow stems from its LTL-based temporal verification over perception-grounded predicates.
- **Implication:** These results suggest that global appearance, motion, or executability scores are inadequate proxies for manipulation success, which requires verifying compositional task specifications.

#### 4.4. Computational Overhead of the LTL Monitor

- The per-rollout cost for evaluating the compositional LTL reward was approximately 15 seconds on a single H100 GPU. This includes SAM3 mask extraction (dominant, ~8s), IDM gripper trace (~1s), and LTL evaluation/VLM queries.
- This overhead is considered a small fraction of the overall per-step wall-clock budget, which also includes video sampling (~60s) and optimizer steps (~70s).

#### 4.5. Ablation Study

- **Component Contribution:**
 - Vanilla DiffusionNFT yielded an average success rate of 36.0%.
 - Adding only the credit-aware NFT loss improved the average to 43.0%, closing a significant portion of the credit-assignment gap and confirming the importance of localizing updates.
 - Adding only the corrective reflow loss resulted in a smaller gain (39.0%), suggesting that without the violation-trace mask, the positive prototype still drives gradients on off-task coordinates.
 - Combining both (CreFlow) achieved the full improvement to 49.3% (average across ablated tasks), demonstrating the synergistic effect of the two mechanisms.
- **Qualitative Ablation:** Visual results showed that vanilla DiffusionNFT without masking degraded irrelevant regions (e.g., dustbin) during training. CreFlow, by restricting updates to task-relevant entities (arms, bottle) identified by the mask, preserved off-task visual quality by anchoring unrelated parts of the scene to the pretrained prior.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces CreFlow, a framework that addresses critical limitations in post-training video generation models for robotic manipulation. Its primary significance lies in the development of a **compositional constraint-based reward model** that leverages Linear Temporal Logic (LTL) to provide a faithful binary success signal alongside fine-grained, spatio-temporal diagnostic information about task violations. This is a notable advancement over existing reward systems that often rely on global visual metrics or continuous scores, which have been shown to be insufficient for the nuanced and compositional nature of embodied manipulation tasks.

The **CreFlow online RL framework** itself represents an important contribution by effectively converting this sparse, localized feedback into actionable supervision for high-dimensional video diffusion models. The **credit-aware NFT loss** is designed to mitigate off-manifold drift and efficiently allocate gradients by confining updates to reward-relevant regions identified by a spatio-temporal mask. This mechanism is particularly impactful for high-dimensional video generation, where task-critical errors are often subtle and localized. The **corrective reflow loss** further refines the training process by explicitly leveraging within-group successful rollouts as a direct corrective target for failed samples. This approach, supported by theoretical analysis demonstrating lower-variance parameter-gradient estimation, contributes to more stable and accelerated training compared to methods that merely suppress negative samples.

The experimental results demonstrate that CreFlow yields **reward judgments better aligned with human and simulator success labels**, leading to a substantial improvement in downstream execution success (23.8 percentage points) across a range of bimanual manipulation tasks. This suggests a practical impact on the reliability and performance of robot learning systems that rely on video generation for planning and control. The method's effectiveness on long-horizon, multi-stage tasks highlights its potential for more complex robotic applications.

A noted **limitation** is the current monitor's reliance on 2D image-plane states, which can restrict its ability to resolve fine-grained 3D spatial relations. Future work in extending constraint evaluation with depth or 3D state estimation could further enhance the framework's capabilities, potentially leading to even more robust and physically accurate video generation for robotics. Overall, CreFlow provides a robust and theoretically grounded approach to align generative models with complex, real-world robotic objectives, pushing the boundaries of what physically plausible and task-executable videos can achieve in embodied AI.
