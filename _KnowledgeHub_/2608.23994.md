# Bridging Teacher Expectations and Robot Learning via Coupling Dynamics

> [!summary] Summary
> A conceptual taxonomy paper for human-robot teaching. It proposes a four-point coupling scale - no, singular, iterative, step, plus hybrid variants - classifying how tightly a teacher's interaction is bound to the robot's learning updates, and scores each study twice: ground-truth update schedule versus what the teacher perceives. Applied to 20 studies, it shows perceived learning, not actual learning, drives teacher behavior.

## Problem

- Prior human-robot teaching work optimizes the robot's policy while ignoring the teacher's experience level, constraints, and cognitive load, so unintuitive interaction degrades demonstration quality.
- Offline batch learning separates data collection from learning, and even online interactive frameworks assume end users understand how the robot updates, which they rarely do.
- No existing lens compares teaching systems by when learning updates are linked to interaction, so mismatches between teacher mental models and real update schedules go unmeasured.

## Method

- A four-point **coupling scale** - no, singular, iterative, and step coupling - grading interaction designs by the frequency with which teaching events are tied to robot learning updates.
- ==Hybrid coupling==, an extra category for systems mixing several learning processes at different levels, such as real-time grip control alongside end-of-day path-map updates.
- A two-step application protocol: establish the ground-truth update schedule, then reconstruct the teacher's inferred model from the first observable behavior change; their gap is the diagnostic signal.

## Results

- Screening **195 references** against three inclusion criteria retained **20 studies**, each placed on the scale twice: by designed update schedule and by teacher-perceived coupling.
- No reviewed system implemented true step coupling; the closest used gaze cues simulating continuous learning without any model update, yet still reshaped teacher demonstration behavior.
- Singular coupling consistently underperformed across **four** compared studies; adding preference queries, robot repositioning, feedback requests, or pretraining raised data diversity and learning speed while lowering workload.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Teachers infer learning from observable behavior, not model state, so convincing cues can drive teaching adaptation even when nothing is actually being learned.
> - Learning complexity need not raise interaction complexity: hiding hybrid processes lowers teacher effort but costs transparency and an accurate teacher mental model.
> - Gains come from where human feedback lands within the learning process, not from how much feedback or which modality is used.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of human-robot teaching (HRT) focuses on enabling individuals without specialized technical expertise to customize robots according to their specific needs after deployment. This capability is considered crucial for overcoming barriers to public robot adoption, such as the perception of robots as "single-use" tools or requiring significant training for customization. Early HRT approaches primarily relied on offline learning, where data collection from a human teacher was separated from the robot's learning process. This batch learning often led to limitations, including the compounding of small execution errors in unfamiliar states, as the robot's policy remained static during interaction.

The research landscape evolved with the advent of online learning frameworks, exemplified by Dataset Aggregation (DAgger) in 2011. These frameworks allowed robots to proactively query experts for feedback and integrate real-time teacher input, shifting the paradigm towards iterative human-robot dialogue. While this transition improved robot policy optimization and adaptability, a critical gap remained in addressing the human element of HRT. Existing online HRT systems often prioritize robot learning efficiency without adequately considering the teacher's experience, cognitive load, or mental model of how the robot learns. This oversight can lead to unintuitive or taxing teaching interactions, which in turn can degrade the quality of demonstrations and the robot's learning outcomes.

This paper positions itself within this evolving landscape by addressing the critical need to bridge the gap between human teacher expectations and robot learning processes. It acknowledges that recent advancements mean robot learning is increasingly shaped by the structure, timing, and content of human teaching. The work draws upon established human learning theories, such as Constructivism, to inform the design of more natural and effective HRT interactions. By proposing a new framework—the "coupling scale"—for classifying HRT interactions, the paper aims to systematically analyze how the temporal and behavioral dynamics of learning influence both robot performance and human perception and teaching behavior. It seeks to move beyond purely technical optimization of robot learning by integrating insights into human pedagogy, thus enhancing the naturalness of human-robot collaboration and reducing misalignment between human and robot mental models.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation for this research stems from the observation that despite advancements in human-robot teaching (HRT), a significant disconnect persists between how humans intuitively teach and how robots are designed to learn. This misalignment often arises because human teachers, who are typically non-technical experts, lack formal training on the intricacies of robot learning algorithms. Consequently, their expectations regarding the robot's learning process (e.g., when and how it updates its knowledge) may not align with the actual robot learning system, leading to inefficient teaching, frustration, and suboptimal robot performance.

The paper identifies that while online HRT frameworks have improved robot learning by allowing for interactive feedback, they often overlook the human teacher's experience level, cognitive load, and mental model. If teaching interactions are unintuitive or demanding, the quality of human input and, subsequently, robot learning can suffer. This highlights a critical need to design HRT systems that are not only efficient for the robot but also natural and comprehensible for the human teacher.

Informed by established human learning theories, particularly Constructivism, the authors propose that understanding how humans acquire, process, and retain information can provide valuable insights into how robots should solicit and receive knowledge. Constructivism emphasizes that teaching and learning are inseparable, and learning is a continuous, meaning-making process where knowledge structures are incrementally refined through interaction. These principles suggest that teachers inherently expect responsiveness and visible adaptation during the teaching interaction, contrasting with many robot learning systems that may have delayed or invisible updates.

The key objectives of this work are therefore:

- **To propose a new conceptual scale:** The primary objective is to introduce a novel framework, termed the "HRT coupling scale," which classifies human-robot teaching interactions based on the level of coupling dynamics present between the human teacher and the robot learner. This scale is designed to characterize when and how robot learning processes are perceived by the human teacher.
- **To apply the coupling scale to existing literature:** The authors aim to utilize this proposed scale to analyze a subset of the human-robot teaching literature. This application seeks to demonstrate the utility of the scale in categorizing diverse HRT designs and understanding their underlying learning dynamics.
- **To investigate the effects of coupling dynamics and mental model mismatches:** A core objective is to investigate how varying levels of coupling dynamics, and the resulting misalignments between a human teacher's mental model and the ground truth robot learning system, affect both the effectiveness of the teaching process and human perceptions towards it (e.g., user trust, workload, preferences, interaction quality, and performance).
- **To inform the design of future HRT systems:** By analyzing these effects, the research intends to offer insights into designing HRT interactions that are more natural, intuitive, and efficient for human teachers. This involves better aligning the robot's learning mechanisms with human pedagogical expectations, thereby bridging the gap between their respective mental models and facilitating a more effective transfer of knowledge.

Ultimately, the motivation is to enhance the pedagogical naturalness of HRT interactions, improve robot learning outcomes, and foster greater adoption of robots by enabling non-technical users to effectively customize them.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed in this paper involves a multi-faceted approach, combining theoretical framework development with a literature-based analysis.

**A. Theoretical Foundation from Human Learning Theories**
The authors ground their work in human learning theories, specifically **Constructivism**. Constructivism emphasizes that learning is an active, meaning-making process built through engagement with tasks and social partners, rather than a passive reception of information. Two core principles from Constructivism are highlighted:
1. **Experience as Meaningful:** Learning occurs continuously through engagement, and every experience (including failed ones) is expected to have a meaningful effect within the teaching interaction. In contrast, robots often treat experience as mere data points, creating a potential misalignment.
2. **Incremental Learning:** Knowledge structures are continuously refined throughout an experience, not just updated at discrete boundaries. Teachers expect learners to adapt behavior within the course of an interaction (e.g., through feedback, correction), which contrasts with robot systems that may have delayed or invisible adaptations.

This theoretical lens provides the basis for understanding teacher expectations regarding the timing and visibility of learning in HRT.

**B. Human-Robot Teaching Taxonomy**
To bridge the continuous nature of human teaching with the discrete nature of robot learning, the authors define a HRT scenario and its components:
- **HRT Scenario:** The environment where the human teacher and robot learner interact.
- **Human Teacher:** Source of expertise, translating intuitive knowledge into structured guidance.
- **Robot Learner:** Processes teaching into actionable learning updates.
- **HRT Interaction:** The mechanism of knowledge exchange, defined by:
 - **Target Competency:** The overarching goal for the robot.
 - **Operational Task:** Specific actions required to achieve the goal.
 - **Exchange of Information:** Pedagogical bridge (teacher interventions like feedback, corrections, repetition) to align robot behavior with expectations.

**C. HRT Coupling Scale**
The core methodological contribution is the introduction of a **4-point HRT coupling scale**, based on the "frequency of learning"—the density of coupled robot knowledge acquisitions and learning updates.
1. **No Coupling:** Robot learning system is not updated by the interaction. Robot knowledge remains static. (e.g., pre-programmed behavior).
2. **Singular Coupling:** A set of experiences or a completed interaction is followed by a *single* learning update, typically offline after the session. (e.g., batch learning).
3. **Iterative Coupling:** Higher frequency of learning, with updates occurring at episodes or specific milestones within a session. (e.g., after completing a sub-task).
4. **Step Coupling:** Continuous learning throughout the interaction, with incremental updates at all times, mimicking fluid human learning. (Potentially real-time behavior adaptation).
A fifth category, **Hybrid Coupling**, is introduced to describe HRT designs that mix multiple learning processes at different coupling levels (e.g., step coupling for motor control and singular coupling for high-level task logic).

**D. Application of the Scale**
The application of this scale involves a two-step process for analysis:
1. **Establish Ground Truth:** Systematically analyze how often the robot's knowledge updates occur, when they are triggered, and what conditions cause them (e.g., explicit teacher corrections, accumulated experience). This objectively classifies the robot's actual coupling level.
2. **Reconstruct Teacher's Perspective:** Infer the teacher's understanding of when and whether the robot has learned based on observable robot behavior. The teacher's mental model of learning is considered to originate from the first explicit indication of a knowledge update in the robot's behavior.
By comparing the ground truth update schedule with the teacher's inferred mental model, researchers can identify misalignments and understand how timing, information exchange, and frequency of learning influence the teaching interaction and the teacher's instructional decisions.

**E. Literature Review and Analysis**
To investigate the effects of coupling, the authors applied their scale to a subset of the HRT literature:
- **Source:** References from, a paper defining human-interactive robot learning, were used (initially 195 papers).
- **Inclusion Criteria:** Papers were selected if they involved: 1) at least one robot-human interaction, 2) a robot learning interaction, and 3) human actions/communication influencing robot behavior.
- **Dataset:** A total of 20 papers were retained for detailed analysis.
- **Data Collection:** Each paper was categorized twice on the scale: once for the robot's *ground truth* coupling design and once for the *teacher's perceived* coupling. Additionally, data related to HRT interaction outcomes (e.g., user trust, workload, preferences, interaction quality, performance) was extracted.
- **Analysis:** Patterns in these outcomes were investigated within and across coupling positions, and specifically in cases of misalignment between ground truth and perceived coupling. This allowed the authors to explore how coupling dynamics impact robot learning and human teacher behavior.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The application of the HRT coupling scale to the selected literature revealed distinct patterns and implications across different coupling levels, particularly concerning teacher perception, mental model alignment, and learning efficiency.

**A. No Coupling**
In systems with no coupling, where the robot's internal learning system is not updated by human interaction, teachers often develop an **illusion of perceived learning** and an **illusion of influence**. Robot gaze cues, pre-scripted behaviors, and responsiveness can create the *appearance* of adaptation. This leads teachers to infer learning from behavioral cues, prompting them to modify demonstrations, exaggerate actions, and adjust timing. However, this results in a one-sided adaptation loop: teacher effort accumulates without affecting future robot behavior, transforming interaction into an overhead cost. The richness of feedback modalities (e.g., physical correction, verbal feedback) does not compensate for the absence of actual coupling, leading to systematic misalignment between teacher expectations and system capabilities. Teachers form false mental models based on perceived responsiveness, masking the system's functional stagnation.

**B. Singular Coupling**
Singular coupling, characterized by a single learning update after a completed interaction or set of experiences, exhibits several limitations:
- **Reduced Data Diversity and Learning Performance:** The lack of real-time visibility into model updates prevents the system from shaping teacher input towards more informative demonstrations. This often leads to limited and less robust training data, with demonstrations tending to be redundant, overly safe, or suboptimal, resulting in poor state-space coverage and generalization.
- **Mental Model Misalignment:** The absence of displayed knowledge updates hinders the teacher's ability to form an accurate mental model of the robot's knowledge state. This leads to inefficient and incorrect human teaching behavior, as teachers struggle to understand what the robot has learned or needs to learn.
- **Temporal Separation Inefficiency:** The "teach first, learn later" sequence limits co-adaptation, imposing higher mental workload on participants and leading to redundant inputs and longer completion times. This temporal gap prevents real-time correction and refinement.
- **Benefits of Augmentation:** Singular coupling is identified as incomplete. Augmenting it with mechanisms like iterative queries, movements (appearing as iterative learning), feedback requests, or pretraining consistently improved outcomes. These additions enhance data diversity and leverage human expertise more effectively.

**C. Iterative Coupling**
Iterative coupling transforms HRT into a co-adaptive process where robot learning and teacher strategies recursively influence each other. This dynamic shapes both robot performance and teacher adaptation:
- **Cognitive Effects:** Teachers adapt their strategies, such as valuing data diversity, simplifying demonstrations based on robot success, or developing preferences for specific feedback modalities. Novice participants may even develop emergent task understanding during iterative teaching.
- **Control Structure:** The channel of iteration (e.g., evaluative feedback, physical interventions, natural language) dictates the interaction type. A distinction is made between system-regulated iterations (teacher evaluates system actions) and teacher-regulated iterations (human supervises and corrects robot behavior). This control structure impacts how teachers perceive robot intelligence.
- **Perceived Learning:** The perception of iteration and learning can influence teacher behavior, even if system-level learning is limited or not directly observable, suggesting that coupling is also a cognitive construct.

**D. Iterative Hybrid Coupling**
This level involves multiple iterative or iterative and lower-level couplings within a single system (e.g., initial demonstration followed by preference queries).
- **Shifting Teacher Roles:** Hybrid coupling often consists of distinct learning systems that alter the teacher's role, such as transitioning from demonstrator to evaluator or interaction partner. This typically shifts teachers away from open-ended demonstrations towards more structured interactions.
- **Addressing Singular Learning Limitations:** Hybrid approaches often emerge to address insufficient data diversity common in singular coupling. By introducing structured guidance (e.g., queries) into the teaching process, they improve robustness and performance.
- **Fragmented vs. Unified Perception:** Teachers may experience hybrid learning as distinct sequential activities (fragmented), or multiple learning mechanisms can perceptually collapse into a single continuous loop (unified), depending on the visibility of learning complexity within the interaction.
- **Transparency vs. Effort Trade-off:** Visible hybrid coupling promotes transparency but often at the cost of increased interaction effort (e.g., labeling data). Hidden hybrid coupling preserves interaction simplicity and reduces cognitive load, but at the expense of teacher awareness and interpretability of the learning process.

**E. Step Coupling**
Step coupling is defined by continuous, real-time learning. The review found no system that implemented "true" step coupling in the literature. The closest examples involved robot gaze cues creating the *appearance* of ongoing learning without actual internal model updates.
- **Perceived Step Coupling:** Such perceived continuous learning can increase teacher engagement and promote adaptive teaching behavior (e.g., forming dynamic mental models, altering demonstrations).
- **Misalignment Risk:** However, this also introduces the possibility of misalignment, as teachers may overestimate the robot's knowledge and expend unnecessary effort based on perceived rather than actual learning. Design consideration is required to align feedback cues with actual learning processes to avoid misleading teachers.

**F. Hybrid Step Coupling**
Representing the highest point on the scale, hybrid step coupling involves the concurrent learning processes.
- **Concurrent Learning:** In these systems, learning is not divided into distinct phases but emerges from the real-time coupling of autonomous and interactive learning, often with continuous learning during task execution and integrated teacher feedback.
- **Feedback Timing as Constraint:** Learning is structured around key moments where feedback is most informative. Control over feedback timing (teacher-initiated vs. system-requested, interaction budgets) becomes a defining constraint. Tighter alignment between feedback timing and the robot's learning process reduces redundancy and increases efficiency.
- **Feedback Integration over Modality:** Hybrid step coupling is defined by *how* feedback is integrated into an ongoing learning process, rather than the specific modality of feedback. The teacher's role shifts from a primary knowledge source to a learning process regulator, providing targeted corrections while the system maintains continuous learning responsibility. This reduces misalignment and enables more efficient, lower-burden interaction.

Overall, the findings indicate that the level of coupling significantly influences teaching effectiveness, teacher perceptions, and the quality of human-robot collaboration. Misalignment between ground truth and perceived coupling often leads to inefficiencies and misinterpretations.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research offers several significant contributions and has the potential for considerable impact on the field of human-robot teaching (HRT) and human-robot interaction (HRI).

Firstly, the paper introduces a novel **conceptual framework and taxonomy—the HRT coupling scale**—for classifying robot learning systems based on the temporal and behavioral dynamics of their learning processes. This scale moves beyond traditional classifications of learning paradigms (e.g., offline vs. online) to focus on the frequency and visibility of learning updates from the perspective of the human teacher. This provides a standardized methodology for researchers to benchmark robotic adaptability and understand the pedagogical implications of different HRT designs.

Secondly, the work **highlights the critical role of coupling dynamics and mental model alignment** in determining the effectiveness and naturalness of HRT interactions. By systematically comparing the robot's "ground truth" learning behavior with the human teacher's "perceived" learning, the authors provide a diagnostic tool for identifying sources of misalignment. This is crucial because, as demonstrated in the findings, mismatches between teacher expectations and robot capabilities can lead to wasted human effort, suboptimal data collection, reduced learning performance, and negative teacher perceptions (e.g., frustration, inaccurate mental models).

Thirdly, the findings **inform the design of more intuitive, effective, and human-centric HRT systems**. By grounding the analysis in human learning theories like Constructivism, the research emphasizes that robots should be designed to learn in ways that align with natural human pedagogy. The analysis across different coupling levels offers concrete insights:
- **Avoiding Misleading Cues:** The "No Coupling" findings highlight the danger of creating an illusion of learning without actual updates, suggesting that feedback cues must be carefully aligned with genuine learning processes.
- **Structuring Teacher Input:** The limitations of "Singular Coupling" underscore the need for mechanisms to guide teachers towards providing diverse and informative demonstrations, rather than simply collecting data.
- **Promoting Co-adaptation:** "Iterative Coupling" and its hybrid forms reveal the benefits of bidirectional learning dynamics where both teacher and robot adapt, fostering more engaging and efficient interactions.
- **Balancing Transparency and Effort:** "Iterative Hybrid Coupling" introduces a design trade-off that system designers must consider: whether to expose learning complexity for greater teacher understanding or to hide it for reduced cognitive load.
- **Optimizing Feedback Timing:** "Hybrid Step Coupling" emphasizes that performance gains are driven less by the sheer volume of human input and more by its strategic placement and integration within the continuous learning process.

Finally, this research contributes to a broader shift in HRT from merely optimizing robot policy to comprehensively **integrating human cognitive, perceptual, and pedagogical factors**. By providing a rigorous framework to understand how humans perceive and react to different robot learning dynamics, the paper facilitates the development of robots that are not only capable learners but also effective and natural collaborators. This can ultimately enhance user satisfaction, reduce training barriers, and accelerate the widespread adoption of robots in diverse real-world applications by enabling non-technical users to confidently and efficiently teach robots to meet their evolving needs.
