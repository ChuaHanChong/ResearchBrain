# Thinking With Videos: Multimodal Tool-Augmented Reinforcement Learning for Long Video Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Tsinghua University, University of Chinese Academy of Sciences, and Bytedance developed VITAL, a framework that enhances Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) for video reasoning by integrating a visual toolbox and multimodal Chain-of-Thought (CoT). This approach achieves state-of-the-art results on challenging long and complex video understanding benchmarks while reducing hallucination.

## Problem

- Current MLLMs for video reasoning primarily rely on text-based Chain-of-Thought (CoT), which leads to insufficient cross-modal interaction and limits dynamic leverage of visual information.
- Without robust visual grounding, MLLMs are prone to hallucinating facts or generating inconsistent reasoning steps, a problem exacerbated in long and complex videos.
- Long video understanding poses challenges due to high computational complexity and the difficulty in precisely grounding reasoning over extended temporal durations.

## Method

- ==VITAL==, an end-to-end agentic framework, enables ==multimodal Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning== by integrating MLLMs with a =='visual toolbox'== for dynamic interaction with video content.
- The framework employs a =='video clipping' tool==, allowing the MLLM to dynamically sample dense frames from specified video segments to focus attention on critical visual details.
- A two-stage training process is used, comprising Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) on MTVR-CoT-72k and Reinforcement Learning (RL) with ==Difficulty-aware Group Relative Policy Optimization (DGRPO)== on MTVR-RL-110k datasets for multi-task learning.

## Results

- VITAL-7B achieved state-of-the-art performance across various long video benchmarks, including **79.3%** accuracy on LongVideo-Reason and an improvement of R@0.5 to **34.7%** on VidChapters-7M.
- The integration of the visual toolbox consistently provided substantial performance gains across all benchmarks, such as a **9.1%** increase in LVR accuracy and a **10.2%** improvement on VidChapters-7M R@0.3.
- Ablation studies confirmed that DGRPO improved difficult long video tasks, and multi-task training combining temporal grounding, reasoning VQA, and grounded VQA yielded the best overall performance, increasing the average score from **37.9%** to **57.1%**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Dynamic interaction with a visual toolbox is crucial for MLLMs to achieve fine-grained, multimodal reasoning, effectively moving beyond static text-based approaches.
> - The 'video clipping' tool, enabling MLLMs to actively query and focus on specific video segments, is highly effective for reducing hallucination and improving reasoning accuracy, especially in long videos.
> - The Difficulty-aware Group Relative Policy Optimization (DGRPO) algorithm effectively balances diverse task difficulties and sample complexities during multi-task reinforcement learning, leading to more stable and efficient training.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Video understanding remains a fundamental and challenging problem in artificial intelligence, with applications spanning recommendation, surveillance, content generation, and autonomous driving. Recent years have seen significant advancements with the rise of multimodal large language models (MLLMs), which can jointly process visual and textual information. These models have shown promise in complex video reasoning tasks such as video question answering (VQA) and temporal grounding.

The current research landscape for video reasoning in MLLMs primarily relies on **text-based Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning**. While effective in some scenarios, these methods face two critical limitations, particularly when dealing with longer videos or more complex reasoning chains:
1. **Insufficient cross-modal interaction:** MLLMs often struggle to fully leverage visual information directly within their reasoning steps, leading to a disconnect between the textual reasoning and the underlying visual evidence.
2. **Increased hallucination:** Without robust grounding in visual evidence, MLLMs are prone to generating factually incorrect or inconsistent reasoning steps and answers, a problem exacerbated by the length and complexity of video content.

This paper directly addresses these limitations by proposing a shift from text-based CoT to **multimodal CoT reasoning**, augmented with a "visual toolbox." This paradigm draws inspiration from two key areas:
- **Reasoning-Enhanced MLLMs:** Works like DeepSeek-R1 and OpenAI-o1 have shown that reinforcement learning (RL)-based post-training can significantly boost the reasoning abilities of LLMs. This paper extends this idea to MLLMs for video, building on contemporary methods like Video-R1 and VideoChat-R1, which apply similar RL post-training to enhance video reasoning. However, VITAL differentiates itself by moving beyond purely text-based CoT in these models.
- **Tool-Augmented LLMs:** The concept of equipping LLMs with external tools (e.g., search engines, code interpreters, visual foundation models) to expand their capabilities beyond pure text processing is a rapidly evolving field. VITAL aligns with this trend by integrating a "visual toolbox" specifically designed for video content. This builds upon recent efforts like DeepEyes and OpenThinkImg, which explore "thinking with images" through tools like zoom-in, sketching, and detection, but VITAL specifically targets "thinking with videos" by focusing on video grounding and clipping tools.

Furthermore, the paper tackles the inherent challenges of **long video understanding**, a domain where MLLMs struggle due to high computational complexity. While existing approaches propose techniques like iterative temporal selection, visual token compression, and long context extension fine-tuning, these often remain computationally intensive or lack the precision needed for fine-grained reasoning. VITAL introduces a multimodal tool-augmented RL framework as a more efficient and accurate solution for long video reasoning, contrasting with prior works that often rely on dynamic frame sampling strategies.

In summary, VITAL positions itself at the intersection of advanced MLLM reasoning, tool augmentation, and efficient long video processing, aiming to bridge the gap between high-level textual reasoning and fine-grained visual evidence through dynamic, tool-driven interaction.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core motivation behind this research stems from the identified limitations of current MLLMs in video reasoning, specifically:
- **Limited Cross-Modal Interaction:** Existing MLLMs, primarily relying on text-based Chain-of-Thought (CoT), often process visual information at an initial stage and then conduct reasoning mostly within the textual modality. This restricts their ability to dynamically query and leverage specific visual evidence throughout a complex reasoning process.
- **Increased Hallucination:** Due to this insufficient cross-modal interaction, especially when handling long videos or multi-step reasoning chains, MLLMs are prone to "hallucinating" facts or producing incorrect inferences that are not grounded in the video's visual content.

To address these challenges, the authors set out with the following key objectives:
1. **Enable Multimodal Chain-of-Thought Reasoning:** To evolve from purely text-based CoT to a multimodal CoT paradigm, where the model can explicitly incorporate visual information and dynamically attend to relevant video content at various stages of reasoning.
2. **Develop an End-to-End Agentic Video Reasoning Framework:** To design a framework (VITAL) that allows MLLMs to actively interact with video content by sampling new frames on demand using a "visual toolbox." This agentic approach aims to facilitate efficient multi-round multimodal tool-augmented training and evaluation.
3. **Enhance Reasoning for Long Videos:** To specifically improve the precision and reduce hallucination in reasoning over long videos, where the density and duration of visual information pose significant challenges for conventional MLLMs.
4. **Construct High-Quality Multi-Task Video Reasoning Datasets:** To create tailored, large-scale datasets (MTVR-CoT-72k for supervised fine-tuning and MTVR-RL-110k for reinforcement learning) that cover various video reasoning tasks, including temporal grounding, video question answering (VQA), and grounded VQA, thereby supporting the proposed training paradigm.
5. **Mitigate Difficulty Imbalance in Multi-Task Reinforcement Learning:** To develop a robust reinforcement learning algorithm (Difficulty-aware Group Relative Policy Optimization, DGRPO) that can effectively handle the varying difficulty levels across different tasks and samples in multi-task video reasoning, ensuring stable training and improved generalization.

By achieving these objectives, the authors aim to significantly advance the video reasoning capabilities of MLLMs, making them more accurate, interpretable, and less prone to hallucination, especially in challenging long video scenarios.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of this work is the **Video Intelligence via Tool-Augmented Learning (VITAL)** framework, an end-to-end agentic system designed to enable multimodal Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning for long videos.

#### 3.1. Tool-Augmented Learning Framework

VITAL operates through a multi-round generation process, integrating a Multimodal Large Language Model (MLLM) backbone with a visual toolbox.
- **Multi-round Generation:** Given a user question and an input video, the MLLM dynamically decides whether to invoke a tool from the visual toolbox or directly output an answer. If a tool is called, the MLLM generates a textual thinking step and a tool call request. A parser extracts this information, and the visual toolbox executes the tool, returning new visual information (e.g., densely sampled frames) or an error message. This process forms a multimodal CoT trajectory, allowing the MLLM to refine its reasoning with updated visual evidence.
- **Visual Toolbox:** The paper identifies the "video clipping" tool as the most effective. This tool takes start and end time parameters and returns a densely sampled sequence of frames from the specified video segment. This allows the MLLM to "zoom in" on relevant temporal segments of a long video, focusing its attention on critical visual details.

#### 3.2. Multi-Task Video Reasoning Training Data

To facilitate tool calling and multimodal reasoning, VITAL relies on two high-quality, large-scale multi-task video reasoning datasets:
- **MTVR-CoT-72k:** Used for the initial Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) cold-start phase.
- **MTVR-RL-110k:** Used for the subsequent Reinforcement Learning (RL) phase.

These datasets are designed to cover three key video reasoning tasks:
- **Temporal Grounding:** Identifying the start and end times of an event described by a natural language query.
- **Video Question Answering (VQA):** Answering general questions about video content.
- **Grounded VQA:** Simultaneously predicting relevant time ranges and answering questions.

The data generation pipeline includes a crucial **rollout filtering process**:
- For each sample, an MLLM generates `k` (default 8) diverse rollouts.
- Samples that are either too easy (all rollouts pass, `PassAll@k`) or too hard (none pass, `PassNone@k`) are filtered out. This curates a dataset with balanced difficulty, maximizing learning signals.
- For long video temporal grounding, visual tool parameters are predefined with a small noise added to ground truth ranges. For long video VQA, the reasoning MLLM autonomously selects tool parameters.
- The final dataset consists of four subsets: MTVR-CoT (54k) and MTVR-RL (94k) for basic video reasoning, and MTVR-CoT-Tool (18k) and MTVR-RL-Tool (16k) for multi-round tool-augmented long video reasoning.

#### 3.3. Difficulty-aware GRPO Training

To address challenges in multi-task RL training, the paper proposes **Difficulty-aware Group Relative Policy Optimization (DGRPO)**:
- **Reward Design:** A composite reward function is used, comprising:
 - `R_acc(τ)`: A multi-task accuracy reward (e.g., IoU for temporal grounding, Exact Match for MCQ, ROUGE for open-ended QA).
 - `R_format(τ)`: A strict rule-based reward for adhering to the specified output format (`<think>...</think><tool call>...</tool call><answer>...</answer>`).
 - `R_tool(τ)`: A reward for successfully calling at least one tool.
- **Difficulty Balancing:** DGRPO mitigates two types of imbalance:
 - **Task-wise imbalance:** The accuracy reward (IoU) for temporal grounding tasks is scaled based on task-specific difficulty parameters (`α_i`, `β_i`). This addresses the slower reward increase observed in harder tasks with continuous IoU metrics.
 - **Sample-wise imbalance:** For each sample, a difficulty indicator `D_i,j` is calculated by averaging the rewards of all `G` rollouts. A soft linear scaling (`w_k,i,j`) is then applied to `D_i,j` to adjust the reward, giving more weight to harder samples and preventing the model from over-optimizing on easy ones.
- **Optimization:** The scaled rewards are then used within a modified GRPO algorithm to optimize the MLLM's policy, encouraging stable training and improved generalization across diverse tasks and difficulties.

The training process involves four stages: two cold-start SFT stages (one without tools, one with tools) followed by two DGRPO stages (one without tools, one with tools), jointly optimizing for temporal grounding, VQA, and grounded VQA.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The VITAL framework, particularly VITAL-7B, demonstrates superior performance across a wide array of challenging video understanding benchmarks, showcasing its advanced reasoning abilities for both long and short video scenarios.

#### 4.1. Long Video Understanding

- **Long Video Question Answering:** VITAL-7B achieved state-of-the-art results on benchmarks like LongVideo-Reason (LVR) and Video-MME. It notably outperformed the previous best open-source models on LVR by a significant margin (Acc: 79.3% vs. 67.9%). The "Toolbox" mechanism alone contributed substantially to this improvement (e.g., +9.1% on LVR accuracy).
- **Long Video Temporal Grounding:** On benchmarks such as VidChapters-7M and VUE-TR-Vision, VITAL-7B showed considerable gains. For instance, on VidChapters-7M, it improved R@0.5 from 27.4% to 34.7%, indicating better precision in localizing events within long videos. The integration of the toolbox yielded significant improvements, such as +10.2% on VidChapters-7M R@0.3 and +3.7 IoU on VUE-TR-Vision.

#### 4.2. Complex Video Reasoning

VITAL-7B consistently surpassed strong baselines on multi-step reasoning benchmarks including VSI-Bench, Video-MMMU, and MMVU. The toolbox enhanced accuracy across these tasks (e.g., +4.3% on VSI-Bench, +2.1% on Video-MMMU, +6.1% on MMVU), confirming its ability to handle diverse reasoning tasks that require spatial understanding and multi-discipline knowledge.

#### 4.3. Video Temporal Grounding and Grounded VQA

For short video tasks, VITAL-7B demonstrated strong foundational capabilities:
- **Temporal Grounding:** Achieved SOTA performance on Charades-STA and ActivityNet-MR, indicating robust basic temporal grounding.
- **Grounded VQA:** Set new SOTA on NExT-GQA and ReXTime, illustrating that the multimodal CoT design effectively integrates accurate temporal grounding with deep video reasoning for more reliable understanding.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies

- **Effectiveness of DGRPO:** The Difficulty-aware Group Relative Policy Optimization (DGRPO) algorithm improved difficult long video understanding tasks (LVR, VidChapters-7M) while maintaining performance on easier tasks, increasing the average score from 50.3 to 52.1 (Table 1). This confirms DGRPO's role in balancing task complexities for stable training.
- **Analysis of Tool-Augmented Reinforcement Learning:** Comparing models with and without the visual toolbox, the introduction of tool-augmented RL consistently led to substantial performance improvements across all benchmarks, especially for long video perception (Tabs 2-4, ∆Toolbox rows). This highlights the crucial role of external tools in enhancing MLLM capabilities.
- **Analysis of Training Dataset Composition:** Experiments showed that combining all three task types – temporal grounding (TG), reasoning VQA (RQA), and grounded VQA (GQA) – yielded the best overall performance, improving the average score from 37.9% (no training data) to 57.1% (Table 5). This underscores the synergistic benefits of multi-task training.
- **Analysis of Visual Tools:** Among various visual tools, the "video clipping" tool was identified as the most effective for temporal grounding and reasoning tasks. Other tools like "clip captioning" or "clip QA" did not yield improvements in zero-shot settings for GPT or Gemini models, suggesting that specialized training is essential for effective tool utilization.

Qualitative analysis (Fig. 2) further illustrated that multimodal CoT, by integrating relevant, densely sampled video frames, significantly reduces hallucination and leads to more accurate temporal grounding compared to text-based CoT.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The VITAL framework represents a significant advancement in the field of multimodal large language models for video understanding, with broad implications and potential impacts:

1. **Enhanced Long Video Reasoning:** VITAL effectively tackles the long-standing challenge of long video understanding, where MLLMs traditionally struggle with cross-modal interaction and hallucination. By enabling models to "think with videos" through dynamic visual tool interaction, it pushes the boundaries of how MLLMs can process and reason over extended temporal sequences. This has direct implications for applications dealing with long-form content, such as documentaries, lectures, surveillance footage, and entertainment.

2. **Reduced Hallucination and Improved Grounding:** The core innovation of multimodal Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning, powered by a visual toolbox, allows MLLMs to query specific visual evidence on demand. This direct visual grounding dramatically reduces the tendency for hallucination, leading to more reliable, factually accurate, and interpretable reasoning outputs. This improved trustworthiness is critical for deploying MLLMs in sensitive applications.

3. **A Stepping Stone Towards Agentic MLLMs:** VITAL’s agentic framework, where the MLLM actively decides when and how to use external tools, signifies a move towards more autonomous and interactive AI agents. This capability extends beyond passive understanding, enabling models to perform more complex, goal-oriented tasks that require adaptive information gathering.

4. **Robust Multi-Task Learning:** The introduction of Difficulty-aware Group Relative Policy Optimization (DGRPO) is a notable contribution to reinforcement learning for MLLMs. By effectively balancing diverse task difficulties and sample complexities, DGRPO ensures more stable and efficient training, leading to models with improved generalization capabilities across a spectrum of video reasoning tasks. This algorithm could be broadly applicable to other multi-task RL scenarios in multimodal AI.

5. **High-Quality Dataset Contributions:** The creation of the MTVR-CoT-72k and MTVR-RL-110k datasets, specifically designed for multi-task video reasoning and tool-augmented learning, provides valuable resources for the research community. These datasets, especially with their emphasis on multimodal CoT and rollout filtering, can accelerate future research in this domain.

**Potential Future Directions:**
The authors acknowledge limitations and outline clear directions for future work:
- **Expanded Visual Toolbox:** Integrating a wider array of tools beyond temporal grounding and question answering (e.g., for spatial grounding, object detection, or action recognition) would broaden the framework's applicability to more complex spatio-temporal reasoning tasks.
- **Multimodal Sensory Integration:** Incorporating other modalities, such as audio, into the reasoning process would allow for a more holistic understanding of video content, crucial for scenarios where sound cues are vital for context (e.g., identifying speakers, detecting events based on sound).
- **Adaptive Tool Selection Strategies:** Developing more sophisticated and adaptive strategies for tool selection would enhance the robustness and versatility of multimodal reasoning, allowing the MLLM to intelligently choose the best tool for a given sub-problem.

In conclusion, VITAL represents a significant leap forward in video understanding by MLLMs, addressing key limitations through an innovative tool-augmented, multimodal reasoning paradigm. Its contributions lay foundational groundwork for developing more capable, reliable, and agentic AI systems for complex video analysis.
