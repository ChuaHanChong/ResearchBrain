# Efficient Sequence Packing without Cross-contamination: Accelerating Large Language Models without Impacting Performance

> [!summary] Summary
> The researchers at Graphcore Inc. developed efficient sequence packing algorithms and model adjustments to accelerate Large Language Model training. This approach reduces computational waste from padding by over 50%, achieving more than a 2x speedup while preserving model accuracy and avoiding hardware-specific custom kernels.

## Problem

- Significant computational waste in LLM training due to padding shorter sequences to a fixed maximum length, accounting for up to 89% of tokens in some datasets.
- Existing "greedy" sequence packing methods risk "cross-contamination," where information from different logical sequences within a packed input negatively impacts model accuracy.
- Advanced "un-padding" techniques, while efficient, often require hardware-specific low-level kernel implementations, introduce dynamic computational graph complexities, and lead to load imbalance in distributed training.

## Method

- Developed two novel offline ==histogram-based packing algorithms==, **SPFHP** and **NNLSHP**, to efficiently combine variable-length sequences into fixed-size batches, achieving up to 99.7% packing efficiency.
- Introduced **packedBERT**, a set of three critical model adjustments: explicit ==positional embedding lookups==, ==block-diagonal attention masking== to prevent cross-contamination, and ==per-sequence loss calculation==.
- Provided guidelines for hyperparameter adjustments (e.g., ==LAMB optimizer== decay) to maintain training stability and leverage increased effective batch sizes from packing.

## Results

- Achieved up to **99.7%** packing efficiency and a packing factor of **2.00** with NNLSHP, demonstrating near-optimal utilization of sequence length capacity.
- Delivered a realized speedup of **1.913x** to over **2x** for BERT phase 2 pre-training on Graphcore IPUs, significantly reducing computational cost and training time.
- Preserved downstream task performance, with packed models achieving comparable F1 and Exact Match scores to unpacked BERT models on SQuAD 1.1 fine-tuning, with only marginal differences.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Simple data packing without careful model adjustments to positional embeddings and attention masking inevitably leads to severe performance degradation.
> - Efficient, histogram-based offline packing can achieve near-optimal data density (e.g., 99.7%) and double effective throughput for LLMs like BERT.
> - The deterministic nature of pre-packing provides inherent load balancing across distributed accelerators, overcoming the scaling limitations of dynamic un-padding methods.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The rapid advancements in Large Language Models (LLMs) like BERT, GPT-3, and T5 have highlighted a critical computational challenge: efficiently processing variable-length sequences. Modern hardware accelerators, optimized for fixed-size tensor operations, often resort to "padding" shorter sequences to match the maximum length in a batch. This paper precisely quantifies this inefficiency, demonstrating that padding can account for up to 50% of tokens in the Wikipedia BERT pre-training dataset (at sequence length 512) and an astonishing 89% in datasets like GLUE-cola (at sequence length 128). This substantial waste of computation power, energy, and time forms the core problem addressed by this research.

The research landscape has seen several attempts to mitigate this "padding problem," each with its own trade-offs:

- **Naive Batching with Padding:** This is the simplest and most widespread approach (e.g., vanilla BERT, Hugging Face framework). While easy to implement, it leads to the significant inefficiencies outlined above.
- **"Greedy" Packing/Concatenation:** Approaches used in models like BERT, T5, GPT-3, and RoBERTa attempt to concatenate multiple sentences or segments from the same or different documents into a single sequence to fill the maximum length. While improving density, these methods often don't explicitly optimize for *overall dataset packing efficiency*. Crucially, they risk "cross-contamination" where information from one logical sequence (or document segment) influences another within the same packed input, potentially degrading accuracy. RoBERTa, for instance, reported a 0.35% reduction in F1 scores due to this.
- **"Un-padding" or Dynamic Batching:** Advanced techniques like "Effective Transformer," NVIDIA's Faster Transformer, Lingvo, and Fairseq dynamically remove padding during computation or group sequences of similar lengths (sorted batching). While highly effective at eliminating padding compute, these methods introduce new complexities:
 - **Hardware-Specific Optimizations:** They often require custom, low-level kernel implementations, usually tailored for specific hardware (e.g., GPUs), limiting portability.
 - **Dynamic Computational Graphs:** Changing sequence lengths or batch sizes necessitates re-optimization and recompilation of the computational graph (e.g., with XLA), introducing overhead.
 - **Load Imbalance in Distributed Training:** In multi-accelerator setups, variable processing times for different batches (due to varying actual sequence lengths) lead to accelerators waiting for the slowest one to finish, reducing overall efficiency gains.

This paper carves out a unique niche by introducing a generalized, hardware-agnostic, and mathematically equivalent solution to sequence packing. It formalizes packing as a bin-packing problem, develops efficient offline algorithms, and crucially, provides model modifications that prevent cross-contamination and preserve model accuracy. By tackling the padding issue at the data preprocessing stage and through minimal, yet essential, model adjustments, the authors aim to deliver significant speedups without incurring the drawbacks of existing methods, thus offering a more universally applicable and robust solution for the AI community.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to **significantly accelerate the training and inference of Large Language Models (LLMs) by efficiently packing variable-length sequences, without compromising model performance or requiring hardware-specific custom kernel implementations.**

This overarching objective is driven by several key motivations:

- **Addressing Widespread Computational Waste:** The authors empirically demonstrate that padding tokens constitute a substantial portion of LLM training data. For example, up to 50% of tokens in the Wikipedia pre-training dataset for BERT (at a maximum sequence length of 512) are padding, and in some GLUE tasks, this figure can soar to 89%. This represents a massive opportunity for efficiency gains, with potential speedups of 2x or more by simply avoiding processing these "dead" tokens.
- **Overcoming Limitations of Existing Solutions:** While prior work attempted to tackle padding, each method presented its own set of problems:
 - **Accuracy Degradation:** Existing "greedy" packing strategies, particularly those mixing segments from different documents without proper isolation, can lead to "cross-contamination" in self-attention layers, negatively impacting downstream task accuracy.
 - **Hardware-Specificity and Complexity:** "Un-padding" approaches, while effective, often rely on highly optimized, low-level kernels tailored for specific hardware architectures (e.g., GPUs). This makes them difficult to implement, debug, port across different hardware, and integrate with advanced compilers like XLA, which prefer static tensor shapes.
 - **Load Imbalance in Distributed Systems:** The dynamic nature of un-padding can lead to variable processing times for batches across multiple accelerators, causing idle time and diminishing scaling efficiency in large distributed training setups.
- **Ensuring Mathematical Equivalence and Reproducibility:** A critical motivation is to ensure that any packing strategy does not alter the fundamental mathematical operations of the original model. This is crucial for maintaining convergence behavior, achieving target accuracies (e.g., in benchmarks like MLPerf™), and ensuring that existing pre-training checkpoints and fine-tuning practices remain valid. The goal is to provide a drop-in acceleration that doesn't force re-evaluation of model configurations or retraining from scratch.
- **Promoting Generality and Broad Applicability:** The authors aim for a solution that is not confined to specific LLM architectures or text-based tasks. By framing packing as a generalized bin-packing problem, they motivate its applicability to other sequence-based machine learning domains, such as audio processing (e.g., LibriSpeech) and molecular modeling (e.g., QM9), and even hint at future extensions to computer vision.

In essence, the motivation is to unlock significant, universally accessible computational efficiency for modern deep learning, making large model training faster, cheaper, and more environmentally friendly, without introducing new hurdles for researchers and practitioners.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology is structured into three interdependent components: efficient data packing during preprocessing, essential model modifications to maintain mathematical equivalence, and heuristic adjustments for hyperparameters.

#### 3.1. Packing Algorithms

The core idea is to formalize the sequence packing problem as a variation of the well-studied **bin packing problem**, where variable-length sequences (items) are packed into fixed-capacity bins (maximum sequence length batches) to minimize the number of used bins (or maximize density). To handle the massive scale of modern datasets (millions of sequences), the authors propose two novel heuristic offline algorithms that operate on the *histogram* of sequence lengths rather than individual sequences:

- **Shortest-Pack-First Histogram-Packing (SPFHP):** This algorithm processes the histogram of sequence lengths in sorted order, from longest to shortest sequences. It then applies a "worst-fit" heuristic, placing the current histogram bin (representing a quantity of sequences of a certain length) into the "pack" (bin) that currently has the *most* remaining space. This approach also limits the maximum number of sequences allowed in a single pack (packing depth). SPFHP boasts a time complexity of O(n + s_m^2) and space complexity of O(s_m^2), where *n* is the number of sequences and *s_m* is the maximum sequence length.
- **Non-Negative Least Squares Histogram-Packing (NNLSHP):** This more sophisticated algorithm re-frames the packing problem as a (weighted) non-negative least squares (NNLS) problem. It first pre-generates a "packing matrix" (A), where each column represents a "strategy" – a combination of sequence lengths that sum up to the maximum sequence length, respecting a maximum packing depth (e.g., at most 3 sequences per pack). The algorithm then solves *wAx = wb* (where *b* is the histogram of sequence counts and *w* is a residual weight vector) for *x*, the non-negative repetition count for each strategy. The solution vector *x* (rounded to integers) provides the optimal mixture of packing strategies. A residual weighting scheme (e.g., lower weight for very short sequences) is used to prioritize packing of longer sequences. NNLSHP has a time complexity of O(n + s_m^5) and space complexity of O(s_m^3). Both algorithms are deterministic and designed for high efficiency even on large datasets.

#### 3.2. packedBERT: Model Changes

Crucially, efficient packing alone is insufficient; the model must be adjusted to correctly interpret and process these packed sequences without degradation in performance. The authors introduce three key model modifications, collectively termed "packedBERT," that ensure mathematical equivalence with the original BERT implementation:

- **Adjust Positional Embeddings:** In standard BERT, positional embeddings are often added as a bias based on a linearly increasing index. With packed sequences, this index needs to reset for each new logical sequence within a pack (e.g., `` for a pack of two sequences). This requires replacing the simple bias-add operation with an explicit embedding lookup, using an additional input that specifies each token's position within its original sequence.
- **Adjust Attention Masking:** To prevent "cross-contamination," where tokens from one sequence inadvertently attend to tokens from another sequence within the same pack, a **block-diagonal attention mask** is applied *before* the softmax operation in the self-attention layer. This mask effectively isolates the attention computation for each sequence, ensuring that tokens only attend to other tokens from their original sequence. This is implemented directly within standard deep learning frameworks, avoiding the need for custom hardware kernels.
- **Adjust Per-Sequence Loss and Accuracy:** Many NLP tasks (e.g., SQuAD, or Next Sentence Prediction in BERT pre-training) require loss and accuracy calculations on a per-sequence basis, not per-pack. Simply applying the loss function to packed inputs would result in a per-pack weighted loss, leading to a different convergence optimum. The proposed solution involves "unpacking" the logits and labels to compute individual sequence losses in parallel, effectively re-averaging them to match the canonical per-sequence behavior, minimizing overhead.

#### 3.3. Adjust Hyperparameters

Packing effectively increases the "real" batch size (number of actual sequences or non-padding tokens processed per step). This can affect hyperparameters sensitive to batch size, especially in optimizers like LAMB. The authors offer two strategies:

- **Reducing Computational Batch Size:** The simplest approach is to reduce the gradient accumulation count (or micro-batch size) by the average packing factor. This keeps the effective number of sequences per optimizer step constant, allowing the use of existing hyperparameters.
- **Heuristic for LAMB Optimizer Decay:** To fully utilize hardware and maintain a larger effective batch size, they propose adjusting the decay parameters (β1, β2) of the LAMB optimizer. For a packing factor *p*, the new decay parameters are set as β1 := β1^p and β2 := β2^p. They provide a theoretical justification for this heuristic, showing it approximately preserves momentum and velocity in the optimizer.

Together, these components form a comprehensive approach to efficiently accelerate LLMs by intelligently reorganizing data and making precise, performance-preserving model adjustments.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research paper presents a compelling set of findings that validate the efficacy and importance of their proposed sequence packing methodology.

- **Prevalence of Padding:** The initial analysis vividly illustrates the substantial waste due to padding in NLP datasets. For the Wikipedia BERT pre-training dataset at a maximum sequence length of 512, approximately 50% of tokens are padding, leading to a theoretical 2x speedup opportunity. In more extreme cases, such as GLUE-cola with a max sequence length of 128, padding can reach 89%. This confirms the critical need for efficient packing across various NLP tasks and beyond, as similar skewed distributions were observed in audio (LibriSpeech) and molecular (QM9) datasets.

- **Efficiency of Packing Algorithms:**
 - Both proposed algorithms, SPFHP and NNLSHP, are highly efficient in maximizing data density. NNLSHP, with a packing depth of 3, achieved an impressive **99.7% packing efficiency** (meaning 99.7% of tokens in a batch are actual data, not padding). This resulted in a **packing factor of 2.00**, very close to the theoretical upper bound of 2.001 for the Wikipedia dataset at sequence length 512.
 - SPFHP also performed exceptionally well, achieving 99.6% efficiency with maximum packing depth.
 - In stark contrast, "greedy" packing, as used in models like T5, showed significantly lower efficiency (around 78.24%), translating to a speedup of only ~1.566x.
 - The algorithms are remarkably fast, processing millions of sequences in seconds, making them practical for preprocessing large datasets.

- **Realized Speedup and Throughput:**
 - For BERT phase 2 pre-training on Graphcore IPUs, NNLSHP (depth 3) delivered a **realized speedup of 1.913x**, confirming that the theoretical efficiency gains translate directly into practical throughput improvements.
 - Remarkably, in optimized setups, the total speedup from packing **exceeded 2x**. This was attributed not only to reduced computational work but also to decreased latency in transferring data to the device, highlighting an often-overlooked benefit of data density.

- **Necessity of Model Adjustments:**
 - Ablation studies emphatically demonstrated that both the **attention masking** and **positional embedding adjustments** are indispensable. Training without these modifications led to a drastic worsening of training loss and accuracy, with the model failing to converge to the target accuracy. This validates the claim that simply packing data without careful model adaptation will degrade performance.
 - The paper also showed that removing the Next Sentence Prediction (NSP) loss, as done in some alternative approaches, reduced downstream SQuAD F1 and EM scores by 1.31% and 1.15% respectively. This underscores the importance of the proposed cross-contamination avoidance mechanism, especially for models that rely on such inter-sentence relationships.

- **Convergence Behavior and Downstream Performance:**
 - When applied to the MLPerf™ BERT large phase 2 pre-training benchmark, packed models, even with heuristic hyperparameter adjustments (e.g., adjusting LAMB decay parameters), exhibited **learning curves that closely matched** those of unpacked models. This demonstrates that the packing strategy and model modifications effectively preserve the original model's training dynamics.
 - Full pre-training of BERT-base and BERT-large followed by SQuAD 1.1 fine-tuning showed **comparable downstream F1 and Exact Match scores** to the original BERT reference, with only marginal differences (e.g., BERT-base F1 reduced by 0.2%). This is a critical result, proving that the acceleration comes without a detrimental impact on end-task performance.

- **Load Balancing and Scaling Advantages:**
 - A significant finding is the **inherent load balancing** offered by the packing approach. Since each "pack" always fills the maximum sequence length, the processing time per batch is consistent. This is a crucial advantage in distributed training.
 - In contrast, "un-padding" approaches suffer from declining speedups as the number of accelerators increases (e.g., dropping to 50% speedup on 32 accelerators, 30% on 2048). This is because devices often wait for the slowest batch (containing the longest sequence) to finish, creating load imbalance. Packing's consistent batch processing eliminates this bottleneck.

In summary, the main findings robustly support the paper's central hypothesis: efficient sequence packing, when coupled with carefully designed model modifications, delivers substantial speedups (exceeding 2x in practice) while maintaining mathematical equivalence, convergence behavior, and downstream task performance, all without the complexities and scaling issues of alternative dynamic un-padding methods.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research presented by Krell et al. holds significant implications across several dimensions of large-scale machine learning, particularly for training large language models.

- **Dramatic Efficiency Gains and Reduced Carbon Footprint:** The most immediate and impactful contribution is the ability to **halve the computational cost** of training models like BERT (for phase 2 pre-training on Wikipedia) by eliminating up to 50% of wasted computation due to padding. This 2x acceleration directly translates to faster research cycles, lower cloud computing expenses, and a significantly reduced carbon footprint for AI development. In an era where model sizes and training times are continually escalating, such fundamental efficiency improvements are paramount for sustainable AI growth.

- **Hardware and Software Agnosticism:** Unlike many existing "un-padding" or dynamic batching techniques that rely on highly specialized, hardware-specific custom kernels (often for GPUs), the proposed sequence packing approach is implemented at the framework level with standard operations. This makes it **portable and readily applicable** across a wide array of hardware accelerators (including Graphcore IPUs, NVIDIA GPUs, Google TPUs) and deep learning frameworks, greatly broadening its accessibility and adoption potential within the ML community. Researchers and engineers can leverage these benefits without rewriting low-level code or being locked into specific vendor ecosystems.

- **Performance Preservation and Reproducibility:** A critical aspect of this work is its meticulous focus on **maintaining mathematical equivalence** with the original model implementations. The rigorous adjustments to positional embeddings, attention masking, and loss calculation ensure that packing does not alter the model's fundamental behavior, convergence properties, or downstream task performance. This is crucial for established benchmarks like MLPerf™ and allows researchers to apply these optimizations without worrying about tedious hyperparameter re-tuning or sacrificing accuracy. The provision of public code and detailed explanations further enhances reproducibility.

- **Enhanced Scaling in Distributed Training:** The inherent **load-balancing** property of the proposed packing method addresses a major bottleneck in large-scale distributed training. By ensuring that each "pack" always fills the maximum sequence length, processing times across multiple accelerators become uniform, eliminating idle time caused by slower batches in dynamic un-padding approaches. This makes the speedup from packing highly scalable, delivering consistent benefits even across thousands of accelerators.

- **Broader Applicability Beyond NLP:** The paper effectively demonstrates that the problem of variable-length data and the benefits of packing are not exclusive to natural language processing. By showing skewed length distributions and potential speedups in audio processing (LibriSpeech) and molecular data (QM9), the research opens avenues for applying these principles to diverse sequence-based machine learning domains. The authors even suggest future work in computer vision (e.g., packing images of different sizes for Vision Transformers), hinting at a generalizable paradigm for data efficiency across AI.

- **Simplified Development Workflow:** By providing robust packing algorithms and clear guidelines for model modifications, the paper simplifies the process for practitioners to optimize their LLM training pipelines. It removes the need for complex custom kernel development or intricate strategies to handle dynamic tensor shapes, allowing developers to focus more on model innovation rather than low-level optimization.

In conclusion, the work by Krell et al. offers a foundational improvement in how sequence data is processed for large language models. Its blend of algorithmic innovation, careful model design, and practical hardware considerations results in a solution that is efficient, robust, generalizable, and immediately impactful, poised to accelerate the development and deployment of AI technologies across various fields.
