# Dynamic Multimodal Prototype Learning in Vision-Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from USTC, NTU, and collaborators developed ProtoMM, a training-free framework that constructs and dynamically updates multimodal prototypes by integrating textual descriptions with visual knowledge from streaming test data. This method improved average zero-shot classification accuracy by 0.93% on 11 cross-domain datasets using CLIP-RN50 and achieved 1.08% higher accuracy on OOD benchmarks with CLIP-ViT-B/16 compared to previous state-of-the-art baselines.

## Problem

- Textual prototypes in Vision-Language Models (VLMs) suffer from semantic ambiguity, leading to confusion between visually distinct classes with similar textual names (e.g., 'sword lily' vs. 'blackberry lily').
- Existing VLM test-time adaptation methods primarily focus on textual prompt refinement and largely overlook crucial visual information present in streaming test images.
- VLMs struggle to dynamically and continuously adapt to evolving, streaming test data distributions without expensive, gradient-based re-training or complex updates.

## Method

- ProtoMM constructs ==multimodal prototypes== by combining LLM-enhanced textual descriptions with dynamically updated ==visual 'particles'== extracted from the test stream.
- It re-conceptualizes images and prototypes as discrete distributions in the CLIP embedding space, assigning importance weights to individual visual augmentations and prototype components via ==Optimal Transport==.
- Visual 'particles' within the multimodal prototypes are dynamically updated through a ==weighted moving average== of high-confidence visual features from incoming test samples, enabling continuous, training-free adaptation.

## Results

- ProtoMM achieved an average zero-shot classification accuracy of **63.10%** across 11 cross-domain datasets with a CLIP-RN50 backbone, representing a **0.93%** improvement over the previous best training-free method, AWT.
- The method demonstrated robust generalization to out-of-distribution data, achieving an OOD average accuracy of **65.69%** with a CLIP-ViT-B/16 backbone, surpassing the training-based DPE by **1.03%**.
- Ablation studies confirmed the critical contributions of both the distributed feature construction and the dynamic multimodal prototype learning modules, showing incremental performance gains with each component.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Augmenting textual prototypes with dynamically acquired visual knowledge is crucial for resolving semantic ambiguities and significantly enhancing VLM generalization to both in-distribution and out-of-distribution data.
> - Optimal Transport provides an effective and interpretable mechanism for quantifying the importance of diverse visual features and prototype components, guiding the intelligent integration of multimodal information.
> - A training-free framework for dynamic multimodal prototype learning offers an efficient and robust approach for VLMs to continuously adapt and improve their zero-shot classification performance during inference.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated at the forefront of Vision-Language Models (VLMs), particularly focusing on enhancing their performance in **zero-shot classification** and **test-time adaptation (TTA)** scenarios. VLMs like CLIP have revolutionized multimodal AI by learning powerful representations from vast quantities of image-text pairs, enabling remarkable zero-shot capabilities. However, their generalization to new, potentially out-of-distribution, test data remains a significant challenge.

The broader research landscape for VLM adaptation includes several key areas:
1. **Prompt Learning/Tuning:** This approach (e.g., CoOp, CoCoOp) aims to optimize learnable prompt vectors or generate better textual descriptions to condition the VLM. While effective, many prompt tuning methods involve gradient-based optimization, which can be computationally expensive during inference, or require a small amount of training data.
2. **Test-Time Adaptation (TTA):** This specific branch of prompt learning (e.g., TPT, DiffTPT, C-TPT) focuses on adapting the model or its prompts to unlabeled test data on the fly. Existing TTA methods, however, predominantly focus on the textual modality, either by tuning text prompts or creating better textual descriptions via large language models (LLMs) (e.g., CuPL, VisDesc, MetaPrompting).
3. **Non-Parametric/Training-Free Adaptation:** Methods like TDA and DOTA avoid gradient updates by employing strategies such as caching high-confidence samples or estimating test-sample distributions. While computationally efficient, these methods often rely on unimodal information or heuristic logit fusion, which can limit their robustness and accuracy.
4. **Optimal Transport (OT) Applications:** OT has gained traction in deep learning for measuring distances between distributions, particularly in domain adaptation, clustering, and even aligning visual and language features. AWT is a very closely related work that uses OT to calculate image-text distances for VLM adaptation.

ProtoMM distinguishes itself from this landscape by addressing a critical oversight: the **inherent ambiguity in purely textual prototypes**. Previous methods, even those leveraging LLMs for richer descriptions, are still confined to the textual domain. ProtoMM argues that class names, despite descriptive efforts, can be semantically similar in text space while visually distinct (e.g., "sword lily" vs. "blackberry lily"). It bridges this gap by explicitly introducing **multimodal prototypes** that integrate both textual descriptions and visual information dynamically extracted from the test stream. By employing Optimal Transport not just for alignment but for *enhancing textual prototypes with visual knowledge* and quantifying importance, ProtoMM offers a novel, training-free, and highly effective solution that pushes the boundaries of VLM generalization. This approach aligns with a growing trend towards multimodal understanding, but uniquely applies it to dynamic prototype refinement in TTA.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core motivation behind ProtoMM stems from the observed limitations of existing Vision-Language Models (VLMs) like CLIP, particularly when performing zero-shot classification and test-time adaptation. While VLMs excel at leveraging vast text-image pairings for general representations, their reliance on *textual prototypes* for classification presents a significant challenge:

1. **Semantic Ambiguity in Class Names:** The paper highlights that class names, even with detailed textual prompts, can be inherently ambiguous in the semantic embedding space of VLMs. For instance, "sword lily" and "blackberry lily" are textually similar (both contain "lily," both are flowers from the Iridaceae family) but visually distinct. Similarly, "laptop" and "desktop computer" are textually related (both "computers") but represent different visual concepts. Such textual similarities lead to confusion, making it difficult for text-only prototypes to accurately differentiate between visually distinct classes (Figure 1).
2. **Overlooking Crucial Visual Information:** Most prior test-time adaptation methods for VLMs, including prompt tuning and non-parametric approaches, primarily operate within the textual modality. They attempt to refine textual prompts or generate better text descriptions but largely neglect the rich, complementary visual information available in the streaming test images themselves. This constitutes a missed opportunity for robust prototype construction.
3. **Need for Dynamic and Continuous Adaptation:** In real-world scenarios, test data often arrives in a streaming manner, and the distribution might shift over time. Existing methods often struggle to continuously learn and adapt without expensive re-training or complex gradient-based updates, limiting their practical applicability.

To address these limitations, ProtoMM sets forth the following key objectives:

- **Construct Multimodal Prototypes:** The primary objective is to move beyond unimodal (textual) prototype learning by creating prototypes that effectively combine knowledge from both textual descriptions and visual features.
- **Enable Dynamic Adaptation at Test Time:** To allow prototypes to continuously learn and improve, the framework aims to dynamically update the multimodal prototypes as new test samples stream in, accumulating valuable visual knowledge over time.
- **Develop a Training-Free Framework:** The method should operate entirely during inference, without requiring any gradient-based optimization or re-training of the VLM, ensuring efficiency and practicality for real-time applications.
- **Leverage Optimal Transport for Refined Prototype Learning:** Utilize Optimal Transport to quantify the semantic importance of both individual image augmentations and prototype components, enabling a more intelligent integration of visual knowledge into the multimodal prototypes.
- **Boost Generalization in Unseen Scenarios:** Ultimately, the goal is to enhance the VLM's ability to generalize across diverse datasets and out-of-distribution settings, achieving superior zero-shot classification accuracy.

By achieving these objectives, ProtoMM seeks to overcome the "ambiguous semantics" problem and make VLMs more robust and adaptable in dynamic, real-world inference environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

ProtoMM (Prototype Multimodal) is a novel, training-free framework designed to adapt Vision-Language Models (VLMs) during test time by constructing and dynamically updating multimodal prototypes. The methodology comprises two main modules: **Distributed Feature Construction** and **Multimodal Prototype Learning**.

#### 3.1. Preliminaries

The framework builds upon a pre-trained VLM, specifically CLIP, which consists of a vision encoder $f(\cdot)$ and a text encoder $g(\cdot)$. For zero-shot classification, CLIP uses textual prototypes $z_c = g(\Phi(z_c))$ (e.g., "a photo of a $z_c$") for each class $c$. Optimal Transport (OT) is employed to measure the distance between discrete distributions. Given two distributions $P = \sum a_i \delta_{v_i}$ and $Q = \sum b_j \delta_{u_j}$, the OT distance $d_{OT}(P, Q; C) = \min_T \langle T, C \rangle - \epsilon h(T)$ is computed, where $T$ is the transport plan and $C$ is the cost matrix. The Sinkhorn algorithm is used for efficient computation.

#### 3.2. Distributed Feature Construction

This module re-conceptualizes images and prototypes not as single points but as discrete distributions in the CLIP embedding space to capture diverse details.

1. **Image Distribution ($P_t$):** For an incoming test image $x_t$, it is augmented into $N$ different views $\{x_n^t\}_{n=1}^N$ using standard techniques like random crop, flip, and resize. Each view $x_n^t$ is processed by the vision encoder $f(\cdot)$ to obtain a visual feature $x_n^t$. The image distribution $P_t$ is then formed as $P_t = \sum_{n=1}^N a_n^t \delta_{x_n^t}$, where $a_n^t$ are importance weights.
2. **Textual Prototype Enhancement:** For each class $z_c$, its base textual prototype is augmented with $M-1$ additional descriptive sentences generated by a Large Language Model (LLM) like GPT-3.5. These $M$ descriptions $\{z_m^c\}_{m=1}^M$ are encoded by the text encoder $g(\cdot)$.
3. **Multimodal Prototype ($Q_c$):** The crucial innovation is to expand the textual prototype with visual information. The multimodal prototype for class $c$ is defined as $Q_c = \sum_{m=1}^M w_m^c \delta_{z_m^c} + \sum_{s=1}^S w_{M+s}^c \delta_{e_s^c}$.
 - The first term represents the enhanced textual descriptions.
 - The second term $\{e_s^c\}_{s=1}^S$ represents $S$ **visual particles** (features) that are appended to the prototype set. These particles initially start as an average of the textual descriptions (since no visual data is available at the start) and are dynamically updated by accumulating visual knowledge from the test stream.
 - $w_c = [w_1^c, \dots, w_{M+S}^c]$ are importance weights for all points (textual and visual) in the multimodal prototype.
4. **Importance Weight Calculation:**
 - **Image Augmentation Weights ($a_t$):** For a test image $x_t$, the importance $a_n^t$ for each visual augmentation $x_n^t$ is calculated based on its semantic proximity to all current mean multimodal prototypes $\bar{z}_c$ and its "entropy" across these prototypes. This ensures informative augmentations contribute more.
 - **Prototype Point Weights ($w_c$):** Similarly, the importance $w_m^c$ for each point (textual description $z_m^c$ or visual particle $e_s^c$) in the multimodal prototype $Q_c$ is derived by considering its correlation with all visual augmentations of the current test image $x_t$. This makes the prototype instance-specific.

#### 3.3. Multimodal Prototype Learning

This module describes how classification is performed using the constructed distributions and how the visual particles are dynamically updated.

1. **Classification via Optimal Transport:** Given the image distribution $P_t$ and multimodal prototype distributions $Q_c$ for all classes $c$, the semantic distance between $P_t$ and each $Q_c$ is computed using the Optimal Transport distance $d_{OT}(P_t, Q_c; C_{tc})$. The cost matrix $C_{tc}$ consists of cosine distances between visual augmentations $\{x_n^t\}$ and multimodal prototype points $\{z_m^c, e_s^c\}$. The Sinkhorn algorithm efficiently solves for the transport plan $T_{tc}$.
2. **Prediction:** The prediction probability for class $c$ given image $x_t$ is inversely proportional to its OT distance: $p(y_t = c|x_t) = \frac{\exp(-d_{OT}(P_t, Q_c; C_{tc}))}{\sum_{c'=1}^C \exp(-d_{OT}(P_t, Q_{c'}; C_{tc'}))}$.
3. **Dynamic Visual Particle Update:** This is a crucial step for test-time adaptation.
 - **High-Confidence Filtering:** Only test samples $x_t$ whose predicted probability $p(y_t = c|x_t)$ for the most probable class $c$ exceeds a threshold $\tau$ are considered for updating the prototypes. This ensures that only reliable visual information is incorporated.
 - **Top-S Selection:** For these high-confidence samples, a "score function" $\Theta_t = T_{tc} w_{y_t}$ (where $y_t$ is the predicted class) is used to quantify the importance of each visual augmentation $x_n^t$. The top-S augmentations with the highest scores are selected.
 - **Weighted Averaging Update:** The visual particles $e_s^c$ (which are part of $Q_c$) for the *predicted class* $c$ are updated using a weighted moving average: $e_s^c \leftarrow \frac{w_{M+s}^t e_s^c + \theta_{(s)}^t x_{(s)}^t}{w_{M+s}^t + \theta_{(s)}^t}$. This mechanism ensures that the multimodal prototypes progressively accumulate high-quality, discriminative visual knowledge from the streaming test data, making them more robust and generalized over time.

The entire process is training-free and adapts to each incoming test sample, making ProtoMM highly suitable for dynamic environments.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

ProtoMM demonstrates significant performance improvements across a wide range of benchmarks, consistently outperforming state-of-the-art test-time adaptation (TTA) methods. The key findings are:

1. **Superior Performance on Cross-Domain Benchmarks:**
 - Across 11 diverse classification datasets (e.g., Aircraft, Caltech101, ImageNet, Flowers102), ProtoMM achieves an average accuracy of **63.10% with a CLIP-RN50 backbone**, which is a **0.93% improvement over AWT**, the previous best training-free method. It also significantly outperforms TDA (2.04% gain) and DOTA (0.94% gain).
 - With a more powerful **CLIP-ViT-B/16 backbone, ProtoMM reaches an average accuracy of 70.70%**, surpassing DPE (the best overall baseline) by **1.08%**. This indicates its effectiveness across different backbone architectures.
 - Particularly strong performance is noted on fine-grained datasets like Flowers102, where ProtoMM outperforms AWT by over 2.3% with ViT-B/16.

2. **Robust Generalization to Out-of-Distribution (OOD) Data:**
 - Evaluations on ImageNet and its four OOD variants (ImageNet-A, ImageNet-V2, ImageNet-R, ImageNet-Sketch) further highlight ProtoMM's generalization capabilities.
 - Using the **RN50 backbone, ProtoMM achieves an OOD average accuracy of 48.21%**, leading TDA by 2.58% and AWT by 0.71%.
 - With the **ViT-B/16 backbone, ProtoMM reaches an OOD average of 65.69%**, surpassing DOTA by 1.17% and even the training-based DPE by 1.03%. These results underscore the method's adaptability to varied data distributions without explicit tuning for OOD scenarios.

3. **Effectiveness of Multimodal Prototype Components:**
 - **Ablation studies confirm the individual contributions of ProtoMM's modules.** Using only the "Distributed Feature Construction" (Eq. 5, expanding textual prototypes with importance weights) yields about 1% accuracy gain over vanilla CLIP on ImageNet (RN50).
 - Adding the "Multimodal Prototype Learning" (Eq. 10, dynamic visual particle updates) further improves performance by over 1% (e.g., on Caltech101 with ViT-B/16), demonstrating the critical role of dynamically incorporating visual knowledge.

4. **Optimal Hyperparameter Settings:**
 - **Number of Augmentations (N, M):** Performance initially improves with an increasing number of visual (N) and textual (M) augmentations, stabilizing around 40-50 for visual and 50-70 for textual. This suggests a balance where sufficient diverse information is captured without introducing noise.
 - **Threshold ($\tau$):** An optimal threshold of $\tau=0.8$ for confidence-based filtering is identified. Values too low (less filtering) or too high (insufficient updates) lead to decreased performance, confirming the importance of selecting reliable samples for prototype updates.
 - **Top-S Selection:** Performance generally improves and then stabilizes as more top-S confident samples are selected, implying that a reasonable number of high-quality samples are sufficient to enrich the prototypes.

5. **Interpretability and Efficiency:**
 - **Visualization of Transport Plan:** Heatmaps of the transport plan demonstrate that ProtoMM effectively focuses on discriminative object regions within images (e.g., hamster, mountain bike), indicating that relevant visual knowledge is indeed transferred to the prototypes.
 - **Inference Efficiency:** As a training-free method, ProtoMM is significantly faster during inference than gradient-based prompt tuning methods (e.g., TPT, DiffTPT, DPE), which can take hours or days. ProtoMM's inference time (approx. 21 minutes on RN50 for ImageNet) is comparable to other training-free methods like AWT and DOTA, while achieving higher accuracy. The parallelizable nature of the Sinkhorn algorithm contributes to its competitive runtime.

In summary, ProtoMM provides compelling evidence that dynamically updated multimodal prototypes, driven by optimal transport, effectively address the limitations of textual ambiguities in VLMs, leading to substantial and consistent improvements in zero-shot generalization across diverse and out-of-distribution datasets, all within an efficient training-free framework.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of ProtoMM holds significant implications for the field of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) and broader multimodal AI:

1. **Overcoming a Fundamental VLM Limitation:** ProtoMM directly tackles the critical issue of semantic ambiguity in purely textual class prototypes, which has long been a bottleneck for VLM generalization. By integrating dynamic visual knowledge into prototypes, it allows VLMs to differentiate between concepts that are textually similar but visually distinct, thereby enhancing their robustness and reliability in real-world scenarios. This moves beyond merely improving textual prompts to fundamentally enriching the representation of class concepts.

2. **Advancing Test-Time Adaptation (TTA):** The training-free nature of ProtoMM makes it highly practical for test-time adaptation. Unlike methods that require gradient backpropagation, ProtoMM can adapt to streaming, unlabeled test data efficiently, without significant computational overhead or the need for retraining. This is crucial for applications in dynamic environments, edge devices, or situations where continuous model updates are not feasible. Its superior performance over existing training-free TTA methods sets a new benchmark for efficiency and effectiveness.

3. **Pioneering Dynamic Multimodal Prototype Learning:** The concept of dynamically updating multimodal prototypes, particularly through the accumulation of "visual particles" guided by optimal transport, is a novel and impactful contribution. It introduces a mechanism for VLMs to continuously learn from their environment during inference, much like humans refine their understanding of concepts through experience. This dynamic learning capability improves generalizability to unseen scenarios and distribution shifts.

4. **Novel Application of Optimal Transport:** ProtoMM showcases an innovative application of Optimal Transport (OT) beyond its typical use for domain alignment or distance measurement. Here, OT is used to quantify the "importance" of different visual augmentations and prototype components, facilitating a more intelligent and weighted integration of information. This opens up new avenues for using OT in multimodal learning, particularly for data fusion, representation learning, and adaptive weighting schemes.

5. **Impact on Downstream Tasks:** The enhanced generalization and robust class representations achieved by ProtoMM can have a cascading positive impact on a variety of downstream VLM tasks. Improved zero-shot classification is foundational for applications like zero-shot object detection, image retrieval, content moderation, and even more complex multimodal reasoning tasks where accurate concept understanding is paramount. For instance, the ability to better distinguish fine-grained categories (demonstrated on Flowers102) is vital for specialized applications in botany, medicine, or quality control.

6. **Future Research Directions:** The paper itself points to future work, including designing even more efficient multimodal prototype learning frameworks and applying them to higher-level downstream tasks such as image/video understanding. The success of ProtoMM suggests that dynamic, multimodal, and training-free adaptation is a highly promising research direction, potentially inspiring new paradigms for continuous learning and robust AI systems that can effectively operate in complex, evolving real-world settings.

In essence, ProtoMM not only addresses a critical technical challenge in VLM performance but also introduces a powerful conceptual framework for multimodal learning and adaptation that could significantly influence the development of more intelligent and adaptable AI systems.
