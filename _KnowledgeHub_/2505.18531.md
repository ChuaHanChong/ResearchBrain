# Generative RLHF-V: Learning Principles from Multi-modal Human Preference

> [!summary] Summary
> Generative RLHF-V enhances multi-modal Large Language Models' alignment with human preferences by replacing traditional score-only reward models with Generative Reward Models (GRMs) that generate reasoning traces and principles. This framework leads to improved alignment quality and superior out-of-distribution generalization compared to conventional approaches.

## Problem

- Aligning Multi-modal Large Language Models (MLLMs) with human preferences and intentions remains challenging due to the complexity of multi-modal understanding and generation.
- Traditional Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) methods rely on score-only reward models, which struggle with low accuracy and weak generalization in multi-modal tasks.
- Scalar reward scores from traditional RLHF offer poor interpretability, providing no insight into why certain multi-modal responses are preferred or not.

## Method

- Introduces **Generative RLHF-V**, a framework that replaces score-only reward models with ==Generative Reward Models== (GRMs) trained to learn principles from multi-modal preference data.
- GRMs generate a predicted principle, a reasoning trace, and pairwise scores for responses, leveraging the language model's capabilities to explain evaluation criteria.
- Employs RL Optimization from Grouped Comparison, where the GRM evaluates multiple candidate responses simultaneously to provide more accurate and robust reward signals for policy optimization.

## Results

- The Generative RLHF-V framework achieved superior out-of-distribution generalization, with scores of **0.79** on LLaVA-Critic, **0.78** on Beavertails-V, and **0.68** on MLLM-as-a-Judge.
- The approach demonstrated consistent alignment quality improvements, showing 5-**20%** gains across various evaluation metrics and different model architectures and sizes.
- Grouped comparison exhibited near-linear performance improvements as the number of candidate responses increased from 3 to 7, indicating a scalable and efficient evaluation method.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Learning principles and generating reasoning traces from human preferences using Generative Reward Models (GRMs) enhances multi-modal reward modeling accuracy and interpretability.
> - A grouped comparison approach for evaluating multiple candidate responses simultaneously provides more robust reward signals and exhibits near-linear performance improvements with increasing candidates.
> - While generative reward models offer better interpretability, they remain susceptible to reward hacking, where models learn to generate misleading but high-scoring responses.
