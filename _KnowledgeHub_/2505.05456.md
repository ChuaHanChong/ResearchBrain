# SITE: towards Spatial Intelligence Thorough Evaluation

> [!summary] Summary
> This research introduces SITE (Spatial Intelligence Thorough Evaluation), a benchmark dataset designed to rigorously assess the visuospatial abilities of large vision-language models (VLMs). Evaluation with SITE reveals a substantial performance gap between current VLMs and humans, particularly in novel view-taking and dynamic scene understanding, and demonstrates a strong correlation between VLM spatial intelligence proficiency and success in robot manipulation tasks.

## Problem

- Existing vision-language model (VLM) benchmarks lack comprehensive and systematic evaluation of fine-grained spatial intelligence (SI).
- Current benchmarks are often limited to static scenes, single viewpoints, or treat spatial reasoning as a minor component among many tasks.
- Key aspects of human spatial cognition, such as view-taking (perspective transformation) and reasoning in dynamic contexts, are largely unaddressed in VLM evaluation.

## Method

- Developed ==SITE==, a novel benchmark, by combining a bottom-up survey of existing datasets with a top-down, ==cognitive science-inspired taxonomy== to identify evaluation gaps.
- Designed new tasks, including ==Ego-exo View Association and Shuffled Frames Reordering==, using the Ego-Exo4D dataset to cover underrepresented areas like view-taking and dynamic scenes.
- Standardized tasks into a multiple-choice visual question answering (VQA) format and used ==Chance-Adjusted Accuracy (CAA)== for robust and fair evaluation of various state-of-the-art VLMs.

## Results

- GPT-4o, the best-performing VLM, achieved an overall Chance-Adjusted Accuracy (CAA) of **37.8%** on SITE, trailing human performance (**67.5%** on a tiny subset) by approximately **32%**.
- VLMs struggled significantly with Ego-exo View Association tasks, with GPT-4o scoring only **28.20%** CAA compared to human perfect accuracy (**100%**), and with Shuffled Frames Reordering tasks, where human performance remained high (**96-98%**).
- A strong positive Pearson correlation coefficient (**0.902**) was found between a VLM's overall SITE CAA score and its success rate in robot manipulation tasks on the LIBERO-Spatial dataset.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - VLMs exhibit a significant spatial intelligence gap compared to humans, performing especially poorly in tasks requiring perspective transformation and reasoning in dynamic, multi-view contexts.
> - Integrating cognitive science classification systems helps identify critical gaps in AI's spatial understanding and enables the creation of more targeted and comprehensive benchmarks.
> - Stronger spatial intelligence in VLMs, as measured by SITE, correlates directly with improved performance in embodied AI tasks, specifically robot manipulation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of SITE is a critical response to the evolving landscape of artificial intelligence, particularly the rapid advancements in large vision-language models (VLMs). While VLMs have demonstrated impressive general capabilities in visual reasoning and question answering, their ability to perform fine-grained spatial intelligence (SI) has remained largely underexplored and inadequately evaluated. This paper positions itself at the intersection of computer vision, natural language processing, and cognitive science, aiming to bridge existing gaps in the assessment of AI's understanding of the physical world.

Historically, computer vision has tackled various components of SI through tasks like object detection, object referring, 2.5D visual relationships, and basic counting/localization. Similarly, advancements in 3D vision and autonomous driving have produced datasets with 3D annotations and multi-view inputs, but these efforts have often been fragmented, focusing on specific aspects or static scenes. The advent of general-purpose VLMs, however, necessitates a unified and holistic testbed capable of assessing SI across its diverse facets.

Existing benchmarks for VLMs, such as MME, MMBench, SpatialEval, CVBench, Blink, 3DSRBench, VSI-Bench, MLVU, MVBench, and VideoMME, have acknowledged the importance of SI by including relevant questions. However, the authors argue that these benchmarks often treat spatial reasoning as one among many tasks, lacking a systematic and comprehensive focus. They are frequently limited in scope, either restricted to single-image questions, neglecting dynamic contexts, or failing to incorporate critical aspects like viewpoint transformation. For instance, 3DSRBench is limited to single-image questions, and VSI-Bench is confined to indoor scenes. Crucially, tasks requiring reasoning across multiple viewpoints—a fundamental aspect of human spatial cognition—have been largely unaddressed.

SITE differentiates itself by systematically drawing upon established classification systems from cognitive science to define and categorize spatial intelligence. This top-down approach, combined with a bottom-up survey of existing datasets, allows the authors to identify specific underrepresented areas, particularly view-taking (spatial orientation) and dynamic scenes. By designing novel tasks to fill these gaps, SITE provides a far more structured and comprehensive evaluation than previous efforts. This work is not merely another benchmark; it represents a foundational step towards building AI models that can truly "understand" and interact with space in a manner akin to human cognition, thereby pushing the boundaries of embodied AI and robotics.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to introduce **SITE (Spatial Intelligence Thorough Evaluation)**, a novel and comprehensive benchmark dataset designed to rigorously assess the visuospatial abilities of large vision-language models (VLMs).

The motivations underpinning SITE are multifaceted:

1. **Fundamental Cognitive Ability:** Spatial intelligence (SI) is a foundational cognitive capacity in humans, encompassing visualization, manipulation, and reasoning about spatial relationships. It is critical for a wide array of disciplines, from architecture and engineering to neuroscience and robotics. For AI, developing robust SI is equally crucial for enabling models to effectively navigate, interact, and understand complex physical environments.
2. **Addressing VLM Limitations:** Despite significant advancements, existing VLMs exhibit limitations in fine-grained spatial reasoning. Their impressive general visual reasoning capabilities do not necessarily translate to a deep understanding of spatial relationships, especially in complex or dynamic scenarios. A comprehensive evaluation is needed to pinpoint these weaknesses.
3. **Filling Gaps in Existing Benchmarks:** Current VLM benchmarks are largely insufficient for a thorough evaluation of SI. They tend to be fragmented, cover only limited aspects of spatial reasoning (e.g., static scenes, single viewpoints), or relegate SI to a secondary role among many other tasks. Key SI factors, such as perspective transformation (view-taking) and reasoning in dynamic contexts, are often neglected.
4. **Systematic and Cognitively Grounded Design:** The authors are motivated to move beyond ad-hoc task collection by grounding their benchmark in cognitive science literature. By adopting three established classification systems for SI, they aim to create a structured and psychologically informed framework for assessing AI capabilities, ensuring broad coverage of SI factors.
5. **Standardized Evaluation:** To facilitate easier and more consistent evaluation across diverse tasks and models, a standardized multiple-choice visual question answering (VQA) format is a key objective.
6. **Understanding Real-World Impact:** Beyond simply identifying weaknesses, the research aims to empirically demonstrate the practical importance of SI in AI. By correlating VLM spatial reasoning proficiency with performance on embodied AI tasks (specifically robot manipulation), the authors seek to highlight why improving SI is vital for developing more capable and generalist AI agents.
7. **Catalyzing Future Research:** Ultimately, SITE is intended to serve as a diagnostic tool that reveals specific areas where VLMs fall short. This, in turn, will guide future research and development efforts towards building more spatially intelligent AI systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology for curating SITE is a meticulously designed two-stage process, combining both **Bottom-Up** and **Top-Down** approaches, followed by a robust evaluation protocol.

#### 3.1. Data Curation: Bottom-Up Path

1. **Dataset Survey:** The process began by surveying 30 representative vision-language datasets, comprising 22 image-based and 8 video-based benchmarks (e.g., VSR, CV-Bench, VSI-Bench, VideoMME). The focus was on validation and test splits.
2. **Filtering for SI Relevance:**
- **LLM-based Filtering:** To efficiently identify spatial intelligence-related tasks, a pretrained Large Language Model (LLM), GPT-4o, was utilized. Prompts were designed using the textual components of tasks (questions, answers, options, descriptions) from existing datasets, along with a few-shot examples, to classify tasks into six coarse categories or to deem them irrelevant to SI. This reduced the initial pool of tasks.
- **Manual Screening:** The LLM-filtered tasks underwent a second phase of manual screening, where human annotators jointly reviewed the vision-language modalities to ensure relevancy and quality.
3. **Coarse Categorization:** The LLM was prompted to generate six coarse-level spatial intelligence categories from the filtered tasks:
- Counting & Existence (Count.)
- Spatial Relationship Reasoning (Rel.)
- Object Localization & Positioning (Loc.)
- 3D Information Understanding (3D Inf.)
- Multi-View Reasoning (MultiV.)
- Movement Prediction & Navigation (Mov.)
4. **Standardization:** All selected tasks, originally in diverse QA formats, were standardized into a multiple-choice visual question answering (VQA) format for consistency and ease of evaluation.
5. **Result:** This bottom-up path yielded 6,943 tasks (3,135 image-based and 3,808 video-based QA pairs).

#### 3.2. Data Curation: Top-Down Path & Novel Tasks

1. **Cognitive Science-Inspired Taxonomy:** The authors leveraged three major classification systems of SI from cognitive science literature to analyze the coverage of the bottom-up dataset:
- **Scales:** Figural, vista, and environmental space (Hegarty et al.).
- **View-taking:** Spatial visualization and spatial orientation (Hegarty and Waller).
- **Structures/Scenes:** Intrinsic vs. extrinsic and static vs. dynamic (Uttal et al.).
2. **Gap Identification:** Analysis revealed a significant underrepresentation of tasks involving "view-taking" (perspective transformation or spatial orientation) and "dynamic" factors in the existing datasets.
3. **Novel Task Design:** To address these gaps, two new types of tasks were designed using the Ego-Exo4D dataset, which provides synchronized egocentric and exocentric views of dynamic events:
- **Ego-exo View Association (Extrinsic-Static):** Requires VLMs to associate egocentric and exocentric views of the same visual scene, selecting the best-matching image from candidates. This assesses static spatial reasoning under viewpoint changes.
- **Shuffled Frames Reordering (Extrinsic-Dynamic):** Challenges models to infer the correct temporal order of shuffled frames from one viewpoint (e.g., exocentric) based on reference frames from another viewpoint (e.g., egocentric), or vice versa. This evaluates dynamic spatial reasoning across different perspectives.
4. **Annotation for Novel Tasks:** Qualified human annotators were employed to select challenging distractor frames and remove ambiguous cases, ensuring task quality and difficulty.

#### 3.3. Final SITE Benchmark Construction

1. **Union and Stratified Sampling:** The tasks from both the bottom-up collection and the newly designed top-down tasks were unified. Stratified sampling was performed to ensure a balanced representation across the identified SI factors and categories.
2. **Final Dataset:** SITE comprises a total of 8,068 QA pairs, including 4,260 image-based and 3,808 video-based tasks, covering 30 existing benchmarks and 1 newly annotated dataset (Ego-Exo4D).

#### 3.4. Evaluation Protocol

1. **Models Evaluated:** Nine state-of-the-art VLMs were selected, including both open-source (LLaVA-OneVision, InternVL-2.5, Qwen2.5-VL, Phi-3.5V) and proprietary models (GPT-4o, Gemini 1.5).
2. **Evaluation Framework:** The `lmms-eval` framework was used for standardized and reproducible benchmarking.
3. **Evaluation Metric:** **Chance-Adjusted Accuracy (CAA)** was chosen to account for varying numbers of answer choices across multiple-choice questions, providing a fairer measure of true reasoning ability beyond random guessing.
4. **Baselines and Upper Bounds:** Random chance (CAA = 0) served as the baseline. Human performance, evaluated by 7 participants on a randomly sampled tiny subset (50 QA pairs per task category), established the upper bound.
5. **Downstream Task Correlation:** To demonstrate the real-world relevance, selected VLMs (LLaVA-OneVision, Qwen2.5-VL variants) were fine-tuned and evaluated on the LIBERO-Spatial robot manipulation dataset in a few-shot setting. The mean L2 distance and overall success rate were used to correlate with SITE performance.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The extensive experiments conducted on the SITE benchmark yielded several key findings regarding the spatial intelligence capabilities of state-of-the-art Vision-Language Models:

1. **Significant Performance Gap with Humans:** The most striking finding is the substantial disparity between human performance and that of leading VLMs. On a tiny subset of the benchmark, humans achieved an overall Chance-Adjusted Accuracy (CAA) of 67.5%, while the best-performing VLM, GPT-4o, managed only 35.6%. Across the full SITE benchmark, GPT-4o's overall CAA was 37.8%, indicating a performance gap of approximately 32% compared to human capabilities. This large gap suggests that current VLMs still lack a robust understanding of spatial relationships.

2. **Struggles in Novel View-Taking and Dynamic Tasks:**
- **View Association:** Humans achieved perfect accuracy (100%) on the Ego-exo View Association tasks (matching egocentric and exocentric views). In stark contrast, GPT-4o achieved only 28.20% and Gemini-1.5-pro 7.03% on the full split. Open-source models like LLaVA-OneVision-7B and Qwen2.5-VL-7B also performed poorly (15.95% and 0.65% average CAA, respectively). This highlights a severe deficiency in VLMs' ability to perform perspective transformations.
- **Frames Reordering:** On the more complex Shuffled Frames Reordering tasks (inferring temporal order across viewpoints), human performance remained high (96-98%). GPT-4o's performance dropped significantly compared to view association, suggesting difficulty in mapping temporal events across different viewpoints. While InternVL-2.5-8B and Qwen2.5-VL-7B showed slightly better results among VLMs, their average performance remained very low (e.g., Qwen2.5-VL-7B at 5.93%). This indicates challenges in understanding both temporal and multi-view dynamics.

3. **Specific Weaknesses Across SI Categories:**
- **Multi-View Reasoning:** This category proved to be exceptionally challenging for all VLMs. On the full split, even state-of-the-art models obtained CAA scores of less than 10%, with GPT-4o underperforming human performance by over 70% on the tiny subset. This suggests VLMs are not effectively trained with data involving different viewpoints of the same scene.
- **3D Information Understanding:** Models consistently scored low (mostly below 15%) in tasks requiring understanding of depth relationships, 3D transformations, and inferring 3D object properties. This indicates a persistent struggle with grasping three-dimensional spatial structures.
- **Human Performance Nuances:** Interestingly, humans also showed comparatively lower performance in some categories like Counting & Existence (e.g., counting objects in long videos), 3D Information Understanding (e.g., inferring camera matrices), and Movement Prediction & Navigation (e.g., navigating in long videos). This is attributed to human attention bottlenecks in tracking multiple objects and complex transformations over time.

4. **Benefits of Model Scale and Pretraining Recipes:**
- **Scaling:** Generally, larger models within the same family (e.g., LLaVA-OneVision-7B over 0.5B, Qwen2.5-VL-7B over 3B) showed performance gains, indicating that increasing model parameters helps improve spatial reasoning, though not enough to close the human-AI gap.
- **Specialized Pretraining:** Qwen2.5-VL-7B, which includes image and video grounding tasks in its pretraining, performed best among open-source models in localization and comparatively better in frames reordering. This suggests that specific pretraining data recipes can enhance certain aspects of spatial intelligence.

5. **Strong Correlation with Embodied AI Tasks:** A crucial finding is the empirical demonstration of a strong positive Pearson correlation coefficient (0.902) between a VLM's overall SITE CAA score and its performance on a robot manipulation task using the LIBERO-Spatial dataset (measured by negated mean L2 distance). Qwen2.5-VL-7B, the top-performing open-source model in several SITE categories, achieved a 38% success rate in robot manipulation, while others failed completely. This robust correlation underscores the direct relevance of spatial intelligence for successful interaction in the physical world and highlights the benchmark's utility in predicting performance in embodied AI.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of SITE represents a significant advancement in the evaluation of large vision-language models and carries profound implications for the broader field of artificial intelligence.

1. **Comprehensive Diagnostic Tool:** SITE provides the most comprehensive and systematically curated benchmark to date for evaluating spatial intelligence in VLMs. By drawing upon cognitive science taxonomies and combining bottom-up data collection with top-down gap analysis, it offers an unprecedented level of detail and structure for assessing visuospatial reasoning. This goes beyond existing benchmarks that often treat spatial reasoning superficially or in a fragmented manner.

2. **Guiding Future VLM Development:** The stark performance gap identified between humans and state-of-the-art VLMs, particularly in spatial orientation, multi-view reasoning, and 3D understanding, serves as a clear roadmap for future research. It unequivocally highlights critical areas where current models are deficient, prompting researchers to develop novel architectures, training methodologies, and data augmentation strategies specifically aimed at bolstering these foundational spatial capabilities. This will shift research focus from merely scaling models to addressing specific cognitive shortcomings.

3. **Advancement of Embodied AI and Robotics:** The strong empirical correlation between VLM performance on SITE and success in robot manipulation tasks is a cornerstone finding. It concretely demonstrates that enhancing a VLM's spatial intelligence directly translates to more capable and effective embodied AI agents. This insight is invaluable for robotics, where spatial reasoning is paramount for tasks like navigation, object manipulation, and human-robot interaction in unstructured environments. SITE will accelerate the development of robots that can perceive, understand, and interact with the physical world more intelligently.

4. **Interdisciplinary Bridge:** By systematically integrating insights from cognitive science into AI benchmark design, SITE fosters a deeper interdisciplinary dialogue. It not only allows AI researchers to leverage decades of cognitive psychology research on human spatial abilities but also provides a framework for cognitive scientists to study how artificial systems acquire and apply spatial reasoning, potentially offering new perspectives on human cognition itself.

5. **Standardization and Reproducibility:** The standardization of tasks into a multiple-choice VQA format and the use of the Chance-Adjusted Accuracy metric provide a consistent and fair framework for comparing models. This promotes reproducibility and enables researchers worldwide to objectively evaluate their models' spatial intelligence.

6. **Catalyst for New Datasets and Tasks:** The methodology for identifying gaps and designing novel tasks (e.g., view-taking, dynamic scene reordering) will inspire the creation of more targeted and challenging benchmarks in the future, pushing the boundaries of what is expected from multimodal AI. The Ego-Exo4D dataset, for example, is highlighted as a rich resource for such dynamic, multi-view tasks.

In conclusion, SITE is more than just a dataset; it is a meticulously constructed framework that redefines how we evaluate spatial intelligence in AI. Its findings offer critical insights into the current limitations of VLMs and, more importantly, provide a clear direction for building truly intelligent systems capable of understanding and navigating our complex, three-dimensional world.
