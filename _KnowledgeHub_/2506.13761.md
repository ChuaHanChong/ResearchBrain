# Prompting with the Future: Open-World Model Predictive Control with Interactive Digital Twins

> [!summary] Summary
> This paper presents "Prompting with the Future" (PWTF), a Model Predictive Control framework that enables open-world robotic manipulation by using interactive digital twins to provide Vision-Language Models (VLMs) with explicit physical foresight. The system consistently achieved higher success rates on eight diverse real-world tasks compared to existing VLM-based and VLA robot control methods, without requiring task-specific robot demonstration data or VLM fine-tuning.

## Problem

- Vision-Language Models (VLMs) struggle with precise, low-level robotic control due to their inherent lack of fine-grained physical insight and implicit reasoning capabilities for dynamics and contact.
- Existing VLM-based robotic control methods often generalize poorly to novel environments and unseen tasks, requiring extensive robot demonstration data or failing in complex, open-world scenarios.
- Traditional Model Predictive Control (MPC) methods typically rely on manually designed task-specific cost functions, limiting their adaptability to open-world, high-level language instructions.

## Method

- Introduces **Prompting with the Future (PWTF)**, an MPC framework that integrates Vision-Language Models with explicit physics modeling provided by an ==interactive digital twin==.
- Automatically constructs a high-fidelity, interactive digital twin of the real-world workspace from a quick handheld video scan, using a hybrid 3D representation of ==Gaussian Splatting== for photorealistic rendering and meshes for accurate physical simulation.
- Leverages the VLM as a 'critic' within a sampling-based planner (==Cross-Entropy Method==), where the VLM evaluates multi-view renderings of diverse simulated future outcomes against high-level language instructions to select optimal robot actions.

## Results

- Achieved consistently higher **success rates** across **eight** diverse real-world manipulation tasks compared to state-of-the-art open-vocabulary VLM-based methods (VoxPoser*, MOKA) and Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models (OpenVLA, π0).
- Ablation studies confirmed that adaptive multi-view observations, subgoal decomposition, and the Cross-Entropy Method (CEM) are crucial components, each significantly contributing to performance.
- Qualitative results demonstrated accurate physical simulation, effective VLM evaluation of simulated outcomes, and strong alignment between digital twin predictions and real-world robot trajectories.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decoupling semantic understanding (VLM) from explicit physical reasoning, which is handled by an interactive digital twin, allows VLMs to operate in-distribution by evaluating predicted future observations.
> - Providing VLMs with simulated future outcomes as visual prompts, generated by a physics-aware digital twin, enables robust evaluation of candidate actions for robot control.
> - Adaptive multi-view rendering and task decomposition into subgoals enhance the VLM's spatial reasoning and improve planning robustness for complex, multi-stage manipulation tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation is rapidly advancing towards enabling robots to perform complex tasks in unstructured, "open-world" environments using natural language instructions. This paper addresses a critical challenge within this landscape: bridging the gap between the high-level semantic reasoning capabilities of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) and the precise, physics-aware low-level control required for real-world robotic interaction.

**Existing Approaches and Their Limitations:**

- **VLM-based Robotic Control:**
 - **Imitation Learning:** Many approaches fine-tune VLMs on massive demonstration trajectories. While effective for learned tasks, their generalization to novel environments and unseen tasks is severely limited by the scale of robotics datasets, which are orders of magnitude smaller than VLM training datasets.
 - **Zero-shot VLMs with Visual Prompts:** Other works employ VLMs in zero-shot settings by providing observed images or images overlaid with sampled actions. These methods require the VLM to implicitly infer future physical interactions and dynamics. This implicit physical reasoning is a significant weakness for VLMs, as they are primarily trained for visual question-answering, not physical simulation, leading to struggles in complex scenarios involving intricate contact, dynamics, and precise motion.
- **Model Predictive Control (MPC):** MPC is a powerful optimization-based control strategy widely used in robotics. It relies on a dynamic model to predict future states and an optimization process to find optimal actions. Recent MPC methods for open-world planning have explored using video generation models to predict future outcomes. However, these generative models often introduce visual artifacts, fail to generalize beyond their training distribution, and cannot guarantee precise conditioning on low-level actions. Traditional MPC also typically requires manual design of task-specific cost functions, limiting its adaptability to open-world scenarios.
- **3D Scene Reconstruction for Robot Control:** Advances in 3D reconstruction methods like NeRFs and Gaussian Splatting offer photorealistic rendering and dense geometry. While these are promising for robot motion planning, most prior works leveraging them often assume static scenes or use reconstruction primarily as a virtual playground for data collection or reinforcement learning, rather than for *online, interactive* prediction and planning in real time.

**PWTF's Contribution and Position:**

"Prompting with the Future" (PWTF) fundamentally differentiates itself by **decoupling semantic understanding (VLM) from explicit physical reasoning (interactive digital twin)**. Instead of forcing VLMs to implicitly infer dynamics, PWTF provides the VLM with *simulated future outcomes* as visual prompts. This allows the VLM to operate "in-distribution" by performing what it excels at: evaluating and describing visual scenes.

PWTF addresses the limitations of prior work by:
1. **Providing Explicit Physics Modeling:** Utilizing an interactive digital twin to accurately simulate physical interactions, eliminating the need for VLMs to perform implicit physical reasoning.
2. **Leveraging VLMs as Critics:** Transforming open-world manipulation into an evaluation task for VLMs, enabling them to assess diverse simulated outcomes against high-level language instructions.
3. **Integrating Hybrid 3D Representation:** Combining the efficient photorealistic rendering of Gaussian Splatting with the accurate geometric modeling of meshes, enabling dynamic and interactive scene representation.
4. **Enabling Zero-Shot Manipulation:** Performing effectively without extensive robot demonstration data collection, VLM fine-tuning, or in-context examples, relying instead on the VLM's pre-trained commonsense knowledge and the digital twin's physical fidelity.

This work represents a significant step towards truly general-purpose robotic manipulation by combining the strengths of large pre-trained models with robust physics-based simulation, creating a powerful framework for complex, open-world tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of "Prompting with the Future" (PWTF) is to enable general-purpose robots to perform novel, human-specified manipulation tasks in unstructured and highly varied real-world settings. The core problem it aims to solve is the fundamental disconnect between the strong high-level semantic reasoning capabilities of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) and their inherent lack of fine-grained physical insight necessary for precise, low-level robotic control.

The motivation for this work stems from the observation that while VLMs have made impressive strides in understanding language and visual semantics (e.g., visual question answering), they struggle with the implicit physical reasoning required for tasks involving intricate contact, dynamics, and motion. For example, a robot tidying a shoe rack (as illustrated in Figure 1) needs not just to identify shoes and understand the task, but also to accurately plan gripper motions, avoid collisions, and precisely translate/rotate objects. Current VLM-based approaches often falter in such scenarios because they ask the VLM to infer dynamic consequences, which is outside their training distribution.

To address this critical gap, the authors set out to achieve the following key objectives:

1. **Augment VLM-based Policies with Explicit Physics Modeling:** Introduce a Model Predictive Control (MPC) framework that integrates VLMs with direct physical simulation. This allows the VLM to bypass the need for internal physical reasoning.
2. **Develop an Interactive Digital Twin Pipeline:** Create a system capable of automatically constructing a high-fidelity, interactive digital twin of a real-world workspace from a quick handheld video scan. This digital twin must support both accurate physical simulation of object dynamics and photorealistic rendering of future states.
3. **Formulate Manipulation as Simulation-Informed VLM Evaluation:** Instead of having the VLM predict actions or dynamic results, the framework would simulate diverse possible outcomes within the digital twin. These simulated future outcomes would then be rendered as visual prompts for the VLM.
4. **Implement Adaptive Visual Prompting:** Design a mechanism where the framework adaptively selects optimal camera viewpoints for rendering the simulated future outcomes. This aims to expose the most informative physical context to the VLM, enhancing its spatial reasoning.
5. **Utilize Sampling-Based Planning with VLM as Critic:** Employ a sampling-based planner (specifically, Cross-Entropy Method) where the VLM acts as a "critic," evaluating the simulated outcomes for their alignment with the task objective. The planner then uses these VLM evaluations to select the optimal action sequence.
6. **Validate on Real-World, Contact-Rich Tasks:** Demonstrate the framework's effectiveness on challenging real-world manipulation tasks that involve significant physical interaction, object reorientation, and clutter removal, showcasing superior success rates compared to existing state-of-the-art VLM-based control methods.

Ultimately, the motivation is to enable truly open-world manipulation by seamlessly fusing the semantic understanding strengths of VLMs with the fine-grained, physics-aware control capabilities that real-world robotic tasks inherently demand, all without requiring extensive task-specific robot data collection or VLM fine-tuning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The "Prompting with the Future" (PWTF) framework is an innovative Model Predictive Control (MPC) approach that tackles open-world manipulation by leveraging the semantic reasoning of a Vision-Language Model (VLM) and complementing it with explicit physics modeling provided by an interactive digital twin. The overall pipeline takes a natural language instruction and an RGB video scan of the scene as input, and outputs an action sequence for the robot.

The methodology can be broken down into three key stages:

#### 3.1. A. Construction of Interactive Digital Twins

The first crucial step is to build a high-fidelity, interactive digital twin of the real-world environment. This twin must be capable of both accurate physical simulation and photorealistic rendering of future states.

1. **Hybrid Reconstruction:** Given a handheld video scan of the real-world environment (approximately 200 images for reconstruction), the system first reconstructs a hybrid scene representation.
 - **Gaussian Representation:** 2D Gaussian Splatting is applied to the extracted RGB frames to create a Gaussian representation (G). This provides photorealistic visual appearance.
 - **Mesh Representation:** To ensure precise geometry critical for physical simulation, the Gaussian representation (G) is converted into a mesh representation (M) using TSDF volume integration.
 - **Camera Pose Estimation:** COLMAP is used to estimate relative camera poses, which are then aligned to the real-world coordinate system using a chessboard pattern.
2. **Interactive Objects Identification and Modeling:** For dynamic manipulation, the digital twin needs to differentiate and model movable objects.
 - **Object Segmentation:** Multi-view images are rendered from the reconstructed mesh (M). A front-view image is then prompted to GPT-4o with the task instruction to identify relevant movable objects (e.g., "drum stick"). The identified object names are passed to Molmo, a VLM, to point to these objects in the 2D image. SAM2 is then used to segment and track the identified object masks across all multi-view images. These 2D masks are projected back to 3D to label and segment movable object meshes within M.
 - **Dynamic Gaussian Association:** To ensure realistic rendering as objects move, each Gaussian point in G is associated with its nearest movable mesh. During simulation, these "anchored" Gaussians inherit the translation and rotation of their corresponding meshes, maintaining visual consistency.
3. **Physical Simulation Integration:** A physics simulator S is integrated, along with the robot's URDF (Unified Robot Description Format) model (U). This simulator computes physically plausible state transitions, allowing the digital twin to accurately predict action-conditioned outcomes.

This hybrid approach ensures that the digital twin provides both the structural geometry necessary for physics and the visual fidelity required for VLM evaluation.

#### 3.2. B. Simulation and Rendering for Visual Prompting

Once the interactive digital twin is constructed, it is used to generate visual prompts for the VLM.
1. **Action Simulation:** For a given candidate robot action `a`, the physics simulator `S` predicts the resulting transformation of the robot and scene objects within the mesh representation `M`, denoted as `T = S(M, a)`.
2. **State Update:** This transformation `T` is then applied to the Gaussian scene representation `G`, yielding an updated state `G(T)` that encapsulates the physical consequences of the action.
3. **Multi-view Rendering:** To facilitate 3D understanding for the VLM (which operates on 2D images), multi-view observations of the predicted future are synthesized.
 - Background scene images `I_g` are rendered from `G(T)` using a set of candidate camera configurations `C`.
 - The robot executing action `a` is rendered separately `I_r` using the simulator and its URDF model.
 - `I_g` and `I_r` are composited based on depth information to generate the final multi-view observations `I = I_i ∈ R^(W×H×3)`. These diverse viewpoints allow the VLM to better perceive the manipulation scene and reason about future states.

#### 3.3. C. Motion Planning via Simulation-Informed Prompting (MPC)

The control problem is formulated as an MPC framework where the VLM serves as an evaluator of predicted future states.

1. **Subgoal Decomposition (High-level Planning):**
 - Initially, the VLM is prompted with multi-view observations of the initial state and the overall task instruction `l`.
 - The VLM decomposes the complex task into a set of structured subtasks `τ = {τ1, τ2, ...}`, defining intermediate objectives.
 - At each planning step `t`, the VLM, prompted with current observations `I_t` and the subtask set `τ`, selects the currently active subgoal `τ_i`. This decomposition helps localize the optimization objective and improves planning robustness.
2. **Adaptive Viewpoint Selection:**
 - Since robot actions are 6-DoF but the VLM uses 2D inputs, viewpoint is crucial.
 - At each step, observations of the updated scene `G(T)` are rendered from a set of candidate camera configurations `C` (e.g., front, left, right, top-down).
 - Conditioned on the active subgoal `τ_i` and current observations `I_t`, the VLM selects the viewpoint `C_t` that provides the most informative observation for evaluating action outcomes. This adaptive rendering strengthens the VLM's ability to reason about geometric and physical variations.
3. **Sampling-Based Planning (Cross-Entropy Method - CEM):**
 - For low-level action generation, the Cross-Entropy Method (CEM) is employed.
 - **Sampling:** Candidate actions `a_k` are initially sampled from a multivariate Gaussian distribution.
 - **Simulation & Evaluation:** Each `a_k` is simulated in the digital twin to obtain future observations `o_t,k`. These simulated outcomes are grouped (e.g., 5 per group) and individually prompted to the VLM, along with the subgoal `τ_i`. The VLM evaluates and selects the outcome that best advances the subgoal.
 - **Distribution Update:** The "elite" actions (those corresponding to the VLM-selected best outcomes) are used to update the sampling distribution.
 - **Iteration:** This sampling and refinement process is repeated for a set number of iterations (e.g., three).
 - **Optimal Action:** The mean of the final optimized distribution is taken as the action `a_t` to be executed by the robot in the real world.

The VLM used is GPT-4o for its strong reasoning and evaluation capabilities. This comprehensive approach ensures that the VLM's semantic understanding is grounded in accurate physical predictions, leading to robust and precise robotic manipulation in complex, open-world settings without explicit robotic data collection or fine-tuning.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The authors conducted extensive experiments to validate PWTF, focusing on eight diverse real-world manipulation tasks requiring 6-DoF control, semantic understanding, and varied manipulation skills. These tasks included "water plant," "play drum," "press spacebar," "pair up shoes," "cucumber basket," "lowest tune," "unplug charger," and "clean up." For each task, five different manipulation scenes with randomized object layouts and distractors were created, and each method was rolled out twice per scene for 10 trials total per task. The primary metric used was success rate.

#### 4.1. Quantitative Results

The quantitative comparison against several state-of-the-art baselines is presented in Table II:

- **PWTF vs. Open-Vocabulary VLM-based Methods (VoxPoser*, MOKA):** PWTF consistently achieved significantly higher success rates. Baselines like VoxPoser* and MOKA, which rely on open-vocabulary detectors for object perception, often failed when specific object parts (e.g., a spacebar or a xylophone key) could not be precisely recognized. PWTF overcomes this by directly leveraging the VLM to reason about *simulated future states*, removing the need for a separate, potentially failure-prone, perception module for object parts.
- **PWTF vs. Vision-Language-Action (VLA) Models (OpenVLA, π0):** OpenVLA and π0, even with their training on large-scale robotic datasets, showed limited generalization, particularly in zero-shot settings for complex tasks. Even after task-specific fine-tuning with 20 expert demonstrations, their performance remained poor for many tasks. The authors hypothesize that these methods require far more data to generalize effectively to unseen layouts. In contrast, PWTF's strength lies in leveraging the VLM's inherent commonsense reasoning, combined with the "rehearsal" capability of the digital twin, which allows it to adapt to broader task coverage and layouts without additional robot data. PWTF particularly excelled in tasks demanding precise gripper pose alignment due to its simulation-based optimization.

#### 4.2. Qualitative Results

The paper provides compelling qualitative examples demonstrating the PWTF framework's capabilities:

- **Action Optimization (Figure 4):** Visualizations of the CEM process show that the digital twin can simulate diverse action outcomes with accurate physics (e.g., object rotation, collisions). The VLM effectively evaluates these simulated results, allowing the CEM to iteratively refine the action distribution, guiding the robot towards the desired goal (e.g., wiping spilled tea).
- **Real-World Trajectories (Figures 5, 8):** Examples of successful task rollouts in both the digital twin and the real world confirm the strong alignment between simulation and reality. This visual alignment validates the fidelity of the digital twin and the effectiveness of the planning process.
- **Adaptive Rendering:** Key planning steps demonstrate the VLM's ability to adaptively change camera viewpoints (e.g., to cross-validate gripper alignment from different perspectives for grasping or placing). This highlights the benefit of the adaptive viewpoint selection component in improving VLM spatial reasoning and action precision.

#### 4.3. Ablation Study

A thorough ablation study investigated the contribution of PWTF's core components (Table III):

- **"w/o Views" (fixed top-down camera):** This setting resulted in a significant performance drop, especially for tasks requiring precise control (e.g., "Press spacebar," "Play drum"). This confirms that adaptive multi-view observations are crucial for enhancing the VLM's spatial perception and ability to distinguish between precise action outcomes.
- **"w/o Subtasks" (direct user instruction as goal):** Performance decreased, particularly for multi-stage tasks (e.g., "Play drum," "Clean up"). This demonstrates that decomposing complex tasks into structured subgoals allows the VLM to focus on more concrete evaluation criteria for each step, improving planning robustness and efficiency.
- **"w/o CEM" (mean of selected actions without optimization):** This led to the most substantial performance degradation across almost all tasks. It underscores that the Cross-Entropy Method (CEM) is indispensable for sampling efficiency and effectively optimizing the action distribution, validating its central role in the model predictive control framework.

#### 4.4. Failure Breakdown

The authors analyzed the failure cases, categorizing them into four groups (Figure 6):

- **Sampling error (45.4%):** The most frequent failure, stemming from the inherent randomness of action sampling and occasional inaccuracies in VLM reasoning, preventing the system from finding a successful action within the step budget.
- **Execution error (27.3%):** Failures occurring in the real world due to robot gripper misalignment during grasping or objects dropping due to insufficient grip or friction.
- **Reconstruction error (15.2%):** Issues with the digital twin's quality, such as visual artifacts caused by noisy camera pose estimation or 3D reconstruction, which can mislead the VLM.
- **Planning error (12.1%):** Cases where the VLM's high-level subtask decomposition was incorrect, leading to illogical action sequences (e.g., trying to hit a drum without first picking up the drumstick).

These findings collectively demonstrate that PWTF's novel integration of interactive digital twins with VLM-based evaluation significantly outperforms existing methods, highlighting the critical role of explicit physics modeling and adaptive visual prompting for robust, open-world robotic manipulation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

"Prompting with the Future" (PWTF) presents a significant advancement in the field of open-world robotic manipulation, offering a novel paradigm that effectively bridges the gap between high-level semantic understanding and low-level physical control.

**Key Contributions and Significance:**

1. **Decoupling Semantic and Physical Reasoning:** The most significant contribution is the strategic decoupling of the VLM's semantic understanding from the physical reasoning, which is instead handled by an interactive digital twin. This allows the VLM to operate "in-distribution" by evaluating *predicted future observations*, rather than attempting to infer complex dynamics or directly predict actions. This approach leverages the VLM's strengths more effectively and mitigates its inherent weaknesses in physical common sense.
2. **Explicit Physics Modeling for Robustness:** By building high-fidelity interactive digital twins from real-world scans, PWTF provides a physically grounded world model. This enables accurate simulation of diverse candidate actions, including contact-rich interactions, object reorientation, and clutter removal. The explicit modeling of dynamics significantly enhances the robustness and precision of robotic manipulation, which is a major challenge for purely VLM-driven methods.
3. **Zero-Shot Open-World Manipulation:** PWTF demonstrates the capability for zero-shot manipulation in novel tasks and unstructured environments without requiring task-specific robot demonstrations or fine-tuning of the VLM. This greatly reduces the data collection burden and increases the adaptability of robots to new scenarios.
4. **Adaptive Visual Prompting:** The adaptive viewpoint selection for rendering simulated outcomes is a clever mechanism. It allows the VLM to obtain the most informative visual context for evaluating physical interactions, compensating for the VLM's limited inherent 3D spatial reasoning and improving the accuracy of action selection.
5. **Model Predictive Control with VLM as Critic:** The integration into an MPC framework, where the VLM acts as a flexible and generalizable "cost function" or "critic," is powerful. It replaces the need for manually designed cost functions for each task, enabling the robot to pursue high-level, free-form language goals effectively.

**Potential Impact:**

The implications of PWTF extend across several dimensions:

- **Accelerated Robotic Deployment:** By reducing the reliance on large, costly, and task-specific robot demonstration datasets, PWTF paves the way for faster deployment of general-purpose robots in diverse applications, from industrial settings to service robotics and even domestic environments.
- **Enhanced Robot Autonomy:** The ability to understand free-form language instructions and autonomously plan in complex, unstructured settings moves robots closer to true general intelligence and autonomy, capable of responding flexibly to human commands.
- **New Research Directions:** This work opens up new avenues for research at the intersection of large foundational models, physics-based simulation, and robotic control. Future efforts could focus on improving the efficiency of digital twin creation, integrating more sophisticated physics models, and exploring multi-robot or multi-task scenarios.
- **Bridging the Sim-to-Real Gap:** PWTF effectively utilizes the "real-to-sim-to-real" paradigm by constructing digital twins from real-world scans and then using simulations to inform real-world actions. This approach helps to overcome some of the traditional challenges of sim-to-real transfer.

**Limitations and Future Work:**

Despite its significant contributions, the paper acknowledges several limitations, primarily related to computational efficiency.
1. **Latency:** Reconstructing 3D representations (20 minutes per scene) and VLM inference (6 seconds per planning step) introduce considerable latency, limiting real-time applicability for very fast-paced tasks. However, the authors note that the framework is "backbone-agnostic" for 3D reconstruction (showing improved speed with Sparse Voxels Rasterization) and could benefit from smaller, more efficient open-source VLMs.
2. **VLM Capability Dependency:** The framework's performance is inherently bounded by the capabilities of the underlying VLM. Inaccuracies in VLM perception or reasoning can lead to suboptimal action evaluations and planning variance. Nevertheless, as VLMs continue to evolve rapidly, the system is designed to directly benefit from these advancements, promising improved robustness and efficiency over time.

In conclusion, "Prompting with the Future" represents a crucial step towards robust and versatile open-world robotic manipulation. By intelligently combining the strengths of vision-language models with explicit physical simulation, it offers a compelling framework for closing the critical gap between high-level semantic understanding and precise low-level control, setting a promising direction for future research in embodied AI.
