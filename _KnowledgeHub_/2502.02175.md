# VLA-Cache: Efficient Vision-Language-Action Manipulation via Adaptive Token Caching

> [!summary] Summary
> VLA-Cache introduces an adaptive token caching framework that reduces the computational overhead of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models by intelligently reusing static and non-task-relevant visual tokens across consecutive frames. The method achieves up to 1.63x speedup in CUDA latency and a 27.31% FLOP reduction in simulations with minimal performance drop, and improves real-world robot success rates by 2.4%.

## Problem

- State-of-the-art VLA models have high computational costs, making real-time deployment in time-critical robotic control applications challenging.
- Existing VLA models redundantly recompute all visual tokens at every timestep, even for static scene elements, leading to inefficiencies.
- Prior acceleration methods often require architectural modifications, extensive retraining, or degrade spatial fidelity crucial for precise robotic manipulation.

## Method

- **VLA-Cache** implements a training-free inference acceleration method that reuses ==Key-Value (KV)== representations of visually static tokens across consecutive frames.
- It identifies static tokens based on patch-level cosine similarity and filters out 'task-relevant' static tokens by analyzing cross-attention scores from the language decoder.
- A layer-adaptive token reusing strategy, guided by attention entropy, dynamically adjusts the reuse proportion across different decoder layers to further optimize efficiency.

## Results

- Achieved up to **1.63x** speedup in CUDA latency and a **27.31%** reduction in FLOPs for OpenVLA, increasing control frequency from **4.23** Hz to **4.59** Hz with only a **0.3%** average drop in success rate.
- Demonstrated additive gains when applied to high-frequency models, boosting OpenVLA-OFT's control frequency from **65.10** Hz to **78.98** Hz.
- Improved the average success rate by **2.4%** (from **82.1%** to **84.6%**) on a real Kinova Jaco2 robot across four manipulation tasks and maintained baseline performance under dynamic backgrounds while reducing FLOPs by **42%**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Temporal continuity and spatial redundancy in sequential visual observations within robotic tasks offer significant opportunities for cross-frame computational reuse.
> - Distinguishing between visually static but task-relevant tokens and genuinely irrelevant static tokens is crucial; naive reuse of all static tokens degrades performance.
> - Cross-attention scores from the VLA model's language decoder can effectively and efficiently serve as a proxy for identifying task-relevant visual regions.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation has traditionally faced significant challenges in developing robust and generalizable policies, with conventional reinforcement learning often struggling with robustness and limited generalization capabilities. The recent surge in large-scale Vision-Language Models (VLMs) has revolutionized multimodal understanding, and this success has extended to Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. These VLA models integrate visual perception and linguistic instructions to directly generate robotic actions end-to-end, holding immense promise for adaptable and generalized robotic control. Pioneering works like RT-2, Octo, and OpenVLA have demonstrated these capabilities, leveraging large-scale robotic datasets.

However, a major bottleneck for the real-world deployment of VLA models, particularly in time-critical robotic control applications, is their substantial computational cost. Existing acceleration techniques for VLM/VLA models primarily fall into two categories:
1. **Generic acceleration methods:** These include model lightweighting (e.g., TinyVLA), quantization (e.g., QAIL), and early-exit strategies (e.g., DeeR-VLA). While somewhat effective, they often require architectural modifications or extensive retraining, limiting their flexibility and generalizability.
2. **Intra-frame token-level techniques:** Methods like FastV, SparseVLM, and ToMe reduce redundancy *within a single image frame* by pruning or merging tokens. While useful for general VLMs, they often disregard the temporal continuity inherent in robotic tasks. Applying these directly to VLA models can disrupt spatial fidelity crucial for precise manipulation, leading to performance degradation, as demonstrated in the paper's ablation studies.

VLA-Cache strategically differentiates itself by exploiting the *temporal continuity* and *spatial redundancy* across consecutive frames in robotic manipulation sequences. Unlike intra-frame methods, it focuses on reusing computations *between* frames, addressing the language model decoding bottleneck that often dominates VLA inference time. This approach is training-free and "plug-and-play," making it highly compatible with existing VLA architectures and complementary to other high-frequency frameworks (like OpenVLA-OFT) that tackle different aspects of the control frequency challenge. By focusing on cross-frame token reuse, VLA-Cache directly tackles a critical, yet often overlooked, source of computational inefficiency in sequential robotic perception, thereby making a significant contribution to enabling real-time, high-frequency control with complex VLA models.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind VLA-Cache stems from the inherent conflict between the powerful, multimodal reasoning capabilities of state-of-the-art VLA models and the strict real-time computational demands of robotic control. While VLA models excel at understanding complex instructions and visual scenes to generate actions, their substantial computational overhead (e.g., large language model decoders) makes real-time deployment challenging, especially in scenarios requiring rapid decision-making and high control frequencies.

The authors identify a key inefficiency: VLA tasks involve processing a continuous stream of visual observations, where a significant portion of the scene (e.g., background, stationary objects) remains static or changes minimally across adjacent frames. Existing VLA models typically recompute all visual tokens from scratch at every timestep, leading to redundant computations that contribute significantly to latency without proportional utility for action generation. This leads to the following key objectives:

1. **Reduce Computational Overhead:** Develop a method to significantly decrease the FLOPs and CUDA latency associated with VLA model inference, thereby boosting control frequency.
2. **Maintain Action Precision and Success Rate:** Ensure that efficiency gains do not come at the expense of task performance or action accuracy, which is critical for reliable robotic manipulation.
3. **Training-Free and Plug-and-Play:** Design an acceleration method that does not require architectural modifications or costly retraining, allowing for easy integration with existing and future VLA models.
4. **Exploit Temporal Redundancy:** Leverage the temporal continuity in robotic visual inputs by adaptively caching and reusing computations for static visual tokens across frames.
5. **Intelligently Filter Task-Relevant Information:** Address the challenge that some visually static tokens might still be semantically important for the task, requiring a mechanism to differentiate and recompute these critical tokens.
6. **Optimize Reuse Strategically:** Implement a layer-adaptive caching strategy that dynamically adjusts token reuse based on the attention patterns within the VLA decoder, further refining efficiency without sacrificing precision.

In essence, the paper aims to bridge the gap between the theoretical capabilities of large VLA models and their practical applicability in real-world, real-time robotic systems by developing an intelligent, context-aware inference acceleration technique.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

VLA-Cache introduces a training-free inference acceleration method that intelligently reuses static visual tokens across consecutive frames to reduce computational overhead in VLA models. The core idea is built upon three main mechanisms: Static Token Selection, Task-Relevant Token Eviction, and Layer-Adaptive Token Reusing.

#### 3.1. KV Cache for VLA Token Reusing

The method extends the concept of Key-Value (KV) caching, commonly used in autoregressive language models to store and reuse past token computations, to the visual modality across time steps. Instead of discarding visual encodings and recomputing them entirely at each new frame, VLA-Cache selectively reuses the KV representations of visual tokens that haven't changed.

#### 3.2. Temporal Redundancy in Robotic Perception

The approach leverages the observation that in robotic manipulation, a large portion of the visual scene (e.g., background, distant objects) remains consistent between adjacent frames.

- **Static Token Selection:** For each image frame $I_t$, it is divided into non-overlapping patches. The cosine similarity is computed between corresponding patches $P_{i,j}^t$ and $P_{i,j}^{t-1}$ from the current and previous frames, respectively. Patches with a similarity above a threshold $\tau$ are initially deemed "visually static." A `Top-k` filter then selects the `k` most stable static tokens, forming the set $P_{static}$.

#### 3.3. Retaining Task-Relevant Information

A crucial insight is that not all visually static tokens are semantically irrelevant. Tokens near the gripper or target object might appear visually unchanged but are highly sensitive to subtle environmental dynamics and crucial for accurate action generation. Naive reuse of all static tokens significantly degrades performance.

- **Evicting Task-Relevant Tokens:** To prevent reusing semantically critical tokens, VLA-Cache employs a lightweight filtering mechanism based on the **cross-attention scores from the language decoder**. Specifically, for each decoder layer, the text-to-vision attention matrix is extracted. These attention scores are averaged across attention heads and selected decoder layers to compute a `task-relevance` score for each visual token. Tokens with a `task-relevance` score exceeding a threshold $\tau_{task}$ are identified as $P_{task-relevant}$. The final set of reusable tokens, $P_{reuse}$, is then determined by removing task-relevant tokens from the static set: $P_{reuse} = P_{static} \setminus P_{task-relevant}$. This ensures that critical regions are always recomputed with up-to-date features.

#### 3.4. Layer Adaptive Token Reusing

The attention distribution within the VLA decoder varies across layers, with deeper layers often exhibiting more concentrated focus. To further optimize reuse, VLA-Cache introduces a layer-adaptive strategy:

- **Attention Entropy for Reuse Ratio:** The concentration of attention at each layer $l$ is quantified using an entropy measure $E_l$. An entropy ratio $R_l = (E_{l-1} - E_l) / E_{l-1}$ captures the change in attention focus between layers. These ratios are accumulated to define a layer-specific reuse proportion $\alpha_l$. Layers with a larger cumulative reduction in entropy (i.e., more focused attention) are allowed to reuse a higher fraction of the identified static tokens, as fewer tokens are likely to require recomputation in such layers.

#### 3.5. Implementation Details and Computational Complexity

During inference, VLA-Cache modifies the VLA decoder's forward pass:
- For tokens in $P_{reuse}$, their KV representations from the previous timestep are directly reused.
- For dynamic or task-relevant tokens, their KV representations are freshly recomputed.
- Position embeddings and attention masks are managed to accommodate the reduced set of active tokens.

The theoretical analysis shows that the overheads for static token identification (O($H^2$)), task-relevance filtering (O($L_tL_vD$)), and layer-adaptive entropy computation (O($L^2D$)) are significantly smaller than the computational savings achieved by reducing the effective token count in each Transformer layer. This leads to substantial FLOP reduction without complex architectural changes.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

VLA-Cache was extensively evaluated on three state-of-the-art VLA models (OpenVLA, CogAct, and OpenVLA-OFT) across two simulation platforms (LIBERO and SIMPLER) and a real-world robotic system (Kinova Jaco2), demonstrating consistent and significant improvements.

#### 4.1. Simulation Environment Results

**LIBERO Benchmark (OpenVLA and OpenVLA-OFT):**
- **Speedup and Efficiency:** VLA-Cache achieved up to **1.63× speedup** in CUDA latency and a **27.31% reduction in FLOPs** over standard OpenVLA. This translated to an increase in control frequency from 4.23 Hz to **4.59 Hz** (a 15% increase relative to the latency reduction).
- **Performance Maintenance:** Crucially, this efficiency came with only a **0.3% average drop in success rate** (from 75.0% to 74.7%), and in some goal-oriented tasks, VLA-Cache even slightly outperformed the baseline.
- **Compatibility with High-Frequency Architectures:** When applied to OpenVLA-OFT, a model already optimized for high-frequency control (65.10 Hz), VLA-Cache further boosted control frequency to **78.98 Hz**, demonstrating additive gains and strong compatibility.
- **Comparison with Other Acceleration Methods:** VLA-Cache significantly outperformed token-level acceleration techniques like SparseVLM and FastV, which often degraded task performance or provided marginal speedups in the VLA context due to their intra-frame operation and disruption of spatial fidelity.

**SIMPLER Environment (CogAct):**
- **Generalizability Across Action Heads:** VLA-Cache maintained success rates comparable to the CogAct baseline (e.g., 74.8% vs 74.4% in Visual Matching) while achieving a **1.37× reduction in inference latency** and approximately **20% fewer FLOPs**. This showcased the portability and effectiveness of VLA-Cache across different VLA models, including those with diffusion policy heads for continuous control.

#### 4.2. Real Robot Evaluation Results

**Kinova Jaco2 Robot Arm:**
- **Practical Speedup:** VLA-Cache achieved practical speedup in real-world scenarios, demonstrating substantial reductions in FLOPs and inference time (e.g., from 64.16ms to 51.85ms latency).
- **Enhanced Robustness and Success Rate:** Across four diverse manipulation tasks (PickPot, PlaceCube, PutSausage, WipeTable), VLA-Cache improved the average success rate by **2.4%** (from 82.1% to 84.6%). This suggests that by reducing interference from redundant visual tokens, VLA-Cache can enhance decision robustness.
- **Performance Under Dynamic Background:** In the PickPot task with simulated background motion (e.g., human hands), VLA-Cache maintained the baseline's success rate (80%) while reducing FLOPs by **42%** and latency by **35%**. This highlights its ability to filter out transient or irrelevant tokens, preserving efficiency and stability in dynamic real-world settings.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies and Sensitivity Analysis

- **Importance of Task-Relevant Token Eviction:** Naive reuse of all visually static tokens resulted in a significant performance drop (84.4% to 74.2% success rate with OpenVLA on LIBERO Spatial). Filtering out task-relevant tokens based on decoder attention largely recovered performance to 82.6%.
- **Impact of Layer-Adaptive Strategy:** The layer-adaptive token reusing strategy further improved accuracy to 83.8% with minimal additional latency.
- **Token Budget and Relevance Threshold:** Studies varying the number of reused tokens (`k`) and the task-relevance threshold ($\tau_{task}$) showed that VLA-Cache maintains robust performance across a wide range of these parameters, with a default setting (k=100, $\tau_{task}$=0.5) offering a strong balance.
- **Attention vs. Object Mask Proxies:** Cross-attention scores from the decoder proved to be a more effective proxy for task relevance than object masks (e.g., from Efficient Track Anything), yielding higher success rates and lower latency.

Overall, the results conclusively demonstrate that VLA-Cache effectively addresses the computational bottleneck of VLA models, enabling faster and more efficient robotic control with negligible or even positive impacts on task success rates, both in simulation and real-world environments.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

VLA-Cache represents a significant advancement in the field of robotic control, particularly for the practical deployment of sophisticated Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Enabling Real-time Robotic Control:** The primary and most direct impact is making large, computationally intensive VLA models viable for real-time robotic manipulation. By achieving up to 1.7× speedup in CUDA latency and a 15% increase in control frequency, VLA-Cache helps overcome a major barrier to deploying these powerful models in applications requiring rapid decision-making and high-frequency control loops. This can lead to more responsive, agile, and safer robotic systems.

2. **Increased Accessibility and Deployability:** As a training-free and plug-and-play solution, VLA-Cache drastically lowers the bar for integrating advanced VLA capabilities into existing robotic platforms. Researchers and practitioners can leverage its benefits without costly retraining, architectural modifications, or extensive hardware upgrades, making VLA models more accessible to a wider range of applications and resource-constrained environments. This democratizes the use of state-of-the-art AI in robotics.

3. **Enhanced Robustness and Performance:** The method's ability to intelligently filter out irrelevant or redundant visual information, even under dynamic backgrounds, contributes to improved model robustness. By focusing computational resources on task-relevant and dynamic elements, VLA-Cache can lead to more stable and reliable action predictions, as demonstrated by the slight increase in success rates in real-world experiments and under noisy conditions. This suggests that computational efficiency can go hand-in-hand with, and even improve, task performance.

4. **Novel Approach to Temporal Redundancy:** VLA-Cache introduces a novel and highly effective strategy for exploiting temporal continuity in sequential visual data for inference acceleration. Unlike prior art that primarily focused on intra-frame redundancy, its cross-frame token caching mechanism is uniquely suited for closed-loop control systems, opening new avenues for efficiency research in time-series multimodal processing.

5. **Foundation for Future VLA Development:** By alleviating the computational burden, VLA-Cache can accelerate the research and development of even more complex and capable VLA models. Researchers can now design larger, more powerful VLA architectures with less concern about immediate inference bottlenecks, knowing that methods like VLA-Cache can help manage the computational load.

**Limitations and Future Work:**
The paper acknowledges certain limitations. VLA-Cache's efficiency gains might diminish in highly dynamic environments where a large proportion of the scene changes between frames. Additionally, its applicability has been validated on VLA models built on LLaMA2 decoders (OpenVLA, CogAct, OpenVLA-OFT); its performance with emerging VLA systems using different backbones (e.g., Gemma2 in $\pi^0$) or more complex architectures remains an open direction for future work.

Despite these limitations, VLA-Cache provides a strong foundation. Future work could involve extending its adaptive mechanisms to handle highly dynamic scenes more efficiently, exploring its integration with various emerging multimodal architectures, and potentially adapting the core principles to other time-series data processing tasks in AI.

In conclusion, VLA-Cache is a significant contribution that directly addresses a critical bottleneck in robotic AI. By intelligently balancing computational efficiency with action precision through adaptive token caching, it moves VLA models closer to widespread practical deployment, unlocking new possibilities for intelligent and autonomous robotic systems in real-world settings.
