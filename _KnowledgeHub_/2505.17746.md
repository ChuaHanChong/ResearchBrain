# Fast Quiet-STaR: Thinking Without Thought Tokens

> [!summary] Summary
> Fast Quiet-STaR introduces a multi-stage training method for large language models to internalize token-level reasoning, achieving significant inference speedups while maintaining or improving accuracy. It enables models like Mistral 7B to show a 9% average accuracy increase on reasoning benchmarks at the same inference latency as standard pre-trained models.

## Problem

- The Quiet Self-Taught Reasoner (Quiet-STaR) paradigm, while effective for token-level reasoning, incurs substantial inference overhead and increased latency due to explicit thought trace generation for every predicted token.
- The high computational cost of Quiet-STaR hinders its practical deployment in real-world, latency-sensitive applications.
- Directly reducing the number of thought tokens in Quiet-STaR leads to a significant degradation in reasoning performance, creating a trade-off between efficiency and accuracy.

## Method

- A ==multi-stage curriculum learning strategy==, called Fast Quiet-STaR, progressively reduces the number of thought tokens during training, starting with more and gradually moving to fewer, to compress explicit reasoning.
- The final checkpoint from the curriculum learning is used to initialize a standard ==Next Token Prediction (NTP) model==.
- This NTP model is then fine-tuned using ==reinforcement learning (REINFORCE)== to internalize the explicit thought process, enabling enhanced reasoning capabilities without generating explicit thought tokens during inference.

## Results

- Fast Quiet-STaR NTP improves average accuracy by **9%** for Mistral 7B (from **32.0%** to **41.0%**) and **5.7%** for Qwen2.5 7B (from **48.7%** to **54.4%**) on reasoning benchmarks, with identical inference latency to pre-trained baselines.
- End-to-end generation latency is drastically reduced, with Fast Quiet-STaR NTP achieving only **6%** of Quiet-STaR 16-8's generation time (**3.2s** vs. **52.7s** for 128 tokens) by fully eliminating explicit thought tokens.
- Ablation studies confirm the effectiveness of the curriculum learning strategy and the importance of initializing the RL phase with a highly compressed thought-reasoner, showing that a 'hard-to-easy' progression or direct training performs worse.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A progressive curriculum learning approach, moving from 'easy' (more thought tokens) to 'hard' (fewer thought tokens), is crucial for effectively compressing and abstracting token-level reasoning into efficient patterns.
> - Reinforcement learning can successfully distill complex, explicit reasoning capabilities into implicit knowledge within a standard Next Token Prediction framework, eliminating the need for separate thought generation during inference.
> - The long-term inference cost savings from 'thinking without thought tokens' (Fast Quiet-STaR NTP) are substantial and quickly offset the initial training cost, making the approach economically viable for large-scale deployments.
