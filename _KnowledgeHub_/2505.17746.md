# Fast Quiet-STaR: Thinking Without Thought Tokens

> [!summary] Summary
> Fast Quiet-STaR introduces a multi-stage training method for large language models to internalize token-level reasoning, achieving significant inference speedups while maintaining or improving accuracy. It enables models like Mistral 7B to show a 9% average accuracy increase on reasoning benchmarks at the same inference latency as standard pre-trained models.

## Problem

- The Quiet Self-Taught Reasoner (Quiet-STaR) paradigm, while effective for token-level reasoning, incurs substantial inference overhead and increased latency due to explicit thought trace generation for every predicted token.
- The high computational cost of Quiet-STaR hinders its practical deployment in real-world, latency-sensitive applications.
- Directly reducing the number of thought tokens in Quiet-STaR leads to a significant degradation in reasoning performance, creating a trade-off between efficiency and accuracy.

## Method

- A ==multi-stage curriculum learning strategy==, called Fast Quiet-STaR, progressively reduces the number of thought tokens during training, starting with more and gradually moving to fewer, to compress explicit reasoning.
- The final checkpoint from the curriculum learning is used to initialize a standard ==Next Token Prediction (NTP) model==.
- This NTP model is then fine-tuned using ==reinforcement learning (REINFORCE)== to internalize the explicit thought process, enabling enhanced reasoning capabilities without generating explicit thought tokens during inference.

## Results

- Fast Quiet-STaR NTP improves average accuracy by **9%** for Mistral 7B (from **32.0%** to **41.0%**) and **5.7%** for Qwen2.5 7B (from **48.7%** to **54.4%**) on reasoning benchmarks, with identical inference latency to pre-trained baselines.
- End-to-end generation latency is drastically reduced, with Fast Quiet-STaR NTP achieving only **6%** of Quiet-STaR 16-8's generation time (**3.2s** vs. **52.7s** for 128 tokens) by fully eliminating explicit thought tokens.
- Ablation studies confirm the effectiveness of the curriculum learning strategy and the importance of initializing the RL phase with a highly compressed thought-reasoner, showing that a 'hard-to-easy' progression or direct training performs worse.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A progressive curriculum learning approach, moving from 'easy' (more thought tokens) to 'hard' (fewer thought tokens), is crucial for effectively compressing and abstracting token-level reasoning into efficient patterns.
> - Reinforcement learning can successfully distill complex, explicit reasoning capabilities into implicit knowledge within a standard Next Token Prediction framework, eliminating the need for separate thought generation during inference.
> - The long-term inference cost savings from 'thinking without thought tokens' (Fast Quiet-STaR NTP) are substantial and quickly offset the initial training cost, making the approach economically viable for large-scale deployments.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The rapid advancements in Large Language Models (LLMs) have demonstrated their impressive capabilities across diverse natural language processing tasks. However, scaling up model sizes or training data alone has proven insufficient for excelling in complex reasoning tasks. This has spurred a significant research direction focused on enabling LLMs to engage in explicit "thinking" or reasoning processes before generating final answers.

This paper is a direct extension and significant improvement upon a recent and influential paradigm called **Quiet Self-Taught Reasoner (Quiet-STaR)** (Zelikman et al., 2024). Quiet-STaR introduced a novel, finer-grained "token-level" reasoning process, where the model generates an internal "thought trace" for each token it predicts, greatly enhancing its reasoning abilities. This contrasts with earlier reasoning methods like Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting (Kojima et al., 2022), Tree of Thoughts (ToT) (Yao et al., 2023), or reinforcement learning (RL) based methods (e.g., OpenAI o1, DeepSeek-R1, Kimi-1.5, QwQ), which primarily operate at a problem or sequence level.

While Quiet-STaR showed remarkable performance gains, it suffers from a critical practical limitation: substantial inference overhead due to the generation of a thought trace for *every* predicted token. This dramatically increases the Time-to-First-Token (TTFT) and overall generation latency, hindering its deployment in real-world, latency-sensitive applications.

"Fast Quiet-STaR" addresses this critical efficiency gap. It operates within the established framework of token-level reasoning but aims to make it computationally viable. The work also taps into the growing trend of **curriculum learning** in LLMs, where models are gradually introduced to more complex tasks or data, and **reinforcement learning (RL)** for fine-tuning and distilling knowledge, particularly for shifting reasoning from explicit thought generation to implicit internalization within the standard Next Token Prediction (NTP) paradigm. By tackling the efficiency problem of token-level reasoning, Fast Quiet-STaR seeks to bridge the gap between advanced reasoning capabilities and practical deployment considerations, a crucial challenge in the current LLM landscape.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives of "Fast Quiet-STaR" are to:

1. **Reduce the inference overhead of Quiet-STaR:** The most significant motivation stems from the high computational cost and latency associated with Quiet-STaR's token-level thought trace generation. The authors aim to significantly decrease the Time-to-First-Token (TTFT) and overall inference time, making this powerful reasoning paradigm more practical.
2. **Preserve the reasoning benefits of Quiet-STaR:** Crucially, the goal is not to sacrifice performance for efficiency. Fast Quiet-STaR seeks to maintain or even improve the strong reasoning abilities imparted by Quiet-STaR, even with fewer explicit thought tokens.
3. **Enable more abstract and concise reasoning:** By reducing the number of thought tokens, the authors aim to encourage the model to internalize a more efficient and compact reasoning process, skipping non-essential steps without compromising accuracy.
4. **Extend to standard Next Token Prediction (NTP) setting:** A key objective is to fully eliminate the need for explicit thought token generation during inference, transitioning the reasoning to an implicit mode within the conventional NTP framework. This would allow the model to achieve enhanced reasoning capabilities at the same inference latency as a standard pre-trained model.

The motivation for these objectives is rooted in the dilemma faced by Quiet-STaR: while effective, its high inference costs severely undermine its practical value. As Figure 1 in the paper illustrates, Quiet-STaR's TTFT can be over 10 times higher than conventional NTP, and reducing thought tokens directly leads to significant performance degradation. The authors are inspired by prior work showing LLMs can learn to skip reasoning steps, believing that a similar compression is possible for token-level thoughts. This work is thus motivated by the need to find a sweet spot where advanced reasoning is both powerful and practically deployable.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Fast Quiet-STaR introduces a novel multi-stage training strategy leveraging curriculum learning and reinforcement learning to achieve its objectives. The methodology can be broken down into two main parts: Fast Quiet-STaR (for efficient explicit thought generation) and Fast Quiet-STaR NTP (for implicit reasoning).

#### 3.1. Quiet-STaR (Background)

Fast Quiet-STaR builds upon the Quiet-STaR framework, which consists of three phases:
- **Think Process:** For each token `x_i`, the model generates a thought trace `T_i` of length `n-1` (where `n` is the number of thought tokens), enclosed by special meta-tokens `<|start_of_thought|>` and `<|end_of_thought|>`. This thinking attends to previous context and prior thought tokens.
- **Talk Process:** A shallow MLP head calculates an interpolation weight `w` to mix logits from the base model (before thought) and the thought-influenced model (after thought), producing `log p_talk`.
- **Learn Process:** The REINFORCE algorithm is used to optimize thought selection based on a reward `r_j`, which is the difference between the log-likelihood of `m` ahead ground-truth tokens given the thought `T_j` and the mean log-likelihood across sampled rationales. A standard log-likelihood loss `L_NLL` is also included for the base language model head.

#### 3.2. Fast Quiet-STaR: Curriculum Learning for Thought Compression

The core innovation of Fast Quiet-STaR is a multi-stage training strategy inspired by curriculum learning. The key insight is that directly training with fewer thought tokens (a harder setting) is less effective than gradually adapting the model.
- **Progressive Difficulty:** The training proceeds from "easy" to "hard" settings by progressively reducing the number of thought tokens (`n`) and ahead tokens (`m`).
 - **Initial Stage:** The model starts with a larger number of thought tokens (e.g., 16 thought tokens and 8 ahead tokens, denoted as 16-8), similar to the original Quiet-STaR. This allows the model to learn a detailed reasoning process.
 - **Subsequent Stages:** As training progresses, the number of thought tokens and ahead tokens are gradually reduced (e.g., to 12-4, then 8-4). In each subsequent stage, the model is initialized with the checkpoint from the previous stage. This forces the model to learn more concise and abstract reasoning patterns to complete the thought process within shorter sequences, internalizing efficient reasoning.

#### 3.3. Fast Quiet-STaR NTP: Transitioning to Implicit Reasoning via Reinforcement Learning

To achieve the ultimate goal of NTP-level efficiency without explicit thought tokens, Fast Quiet-STaR extends its approach with a reinforcement learning-based fine-tuning step:
- **Initialization:** An NTP model is initialized using the checkpoint from the *final stage* of Fast Quiet-STaR's curriculum learning (e.g., Fast Quiet-STaR 8-4). This means the NTP model already inherits the reasoning abilities learned through thought compression.
- **Reward Definition:** The negative log-likelihood loss of the Fast Quiet-STaR model after its thinking process (`L_FastQuietSTaR`) serves as a reference. The reward `r_j` for the NTP model at token `j` is defined as the difference between `L_FastQuietSTaR` and the negative log-likelihood loss of the Fast Quiet-STaR NTP model at token `j` (`L_FastQuietSTaR-NTP`). A higher reward means the NTP model's prediction quality is closer to that of the explicit thought process.
- **RL Optimization:** The model is then fine-tuned using a REINFORCE loss, maximizing this reward. This encourages the NTP model to emulate the superior prediction quality of the Fast Quiet-STaR model *without* explicitly generating intermediate reasoning tokens. Essentially, the model learns to compress and integrate the explicit "thinking" into its latent representations, performing implicit reasoning during standard NTP inference.

The entire training pipeline for Fast Quiet-STaR NTP (from Quiet-STaR 16-8 to Fast Quiet-STaR 12-4, then 8-4, and finally NTP) is designed to be highly data-efficient, requiring only 0.5M tokens for continued pre-training and taking approximately 54 minutes on 8 H800 GPUs.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of Fast Quiet-STaR and Fast Quiet-STaR NTP provides strong evidence for their effectiveness across various benchmarks, demonstrating improvements in both efficiency and accuracy.

#### 4.1. Performance Comparison (Table 1 & Figure 1)

- **Fast Quiet-STaR vs. Quiet-STaR:** Fast Quiet-STaR consistently outperforms Quiet-STaR when compared under the same inference time budget (i.e., the same number of thought tokens). For Mistral 7B, Fast Quiet-STaR with 8 thought tokens achieves an average accuracy of 41.7%, surpassing Quiet-STaR 16-8 (39.2%) even though the latter uses twice as many thought tokens and has a significantly higher TTFT (0.738s vs. 0.305s). This demonstrates the success of curriculum learning in enabling efficient thought compression.
- **Fast Quiet-STaR NTP vs. Pre-Trained Baseline:** Fast Quiet-STaR NTP achieves significant performance gains over the original pre-trained models without incurring *any* additional inference time overhead, maintaining the same TTFT (0.028s for Mistral 7B, 0.026s for Qwen2.5 7B).
 - On Mistral 7B, Fast Quiet-STaR NTP shows an average accuracy improvement of **9%** (from 32.0% to 41.0%).
 - On Qwen2.5 7B, it achieves an average accuracy improvement of **5.7%** (from 48.7% to 54.4%).
- **Model Agnosticism:** The improvements are observed across two different open-source LLMs, Mistral 7B and Qwen2.5 7B, suggesting the generalizability of the proposed method.
- **Benchmark Performance:** Significant improvements are seen across all four evaluated datasets: PIQA, SIQA, CommonsenseQA, and GSM8K. Notably, on CommonsenseQA, Fast Quiet-STaR NTP improves Mistral 7B accuracy by 13.8%.

#### 4.2. Generation Latency (Table 2)

- Fast Quiet-STaR NTP drastically reduces end-to-end generation latency. For a prefix length of 256 tokens and generating 128 tokens, Fast Quiet-STaR NTP achieves just 6% of the generation time of Quiet-STaR 16-8 (3.2s vs. 52.7s), effectively matching the speed of the pre-trained baseline. This underscores its practical viability.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

- **Curriculum Learning:** Reversing the curriculum learning strategy ("hard-to-easy" progression, termed Rev Quiet-STaR) did not yield performance improvements. In fact, Rev Quiet-STaR 16-8 underperformed Fast Quiet-STaR 8-4, confirming that the "easy-to-hard" progressive reduction of thought tokens is crucial for effective learning and thought compression (Figure 3).
- **RL Initialization:** Initializing the RL-based fine-tuning (for Fast Quiet-STaR NTP) with the checkpoint from Fast Quiet-STaR 8-4 yielded the best performance compared to initializing with the pre-trained model or Quiet-STaR 16-8. This indicates that the compact, informative thought trace learned by Fast Quiet-STaR 8-4 is an optimal starting point for distilling reasoning into the NTP paradigm (Figure 4).
- **Fast Quiet-STaR NTP Without Curriculum Learning:** Directly training an NTP model from Quiet-STaR 16-8 (bypassing intermediate curriculum stages, creating "Quiet-STaR NTP") resulted in a 5.2% drop in average accuracy compared to the full Fast Quiet-STaR NTP. This further validates the critical role of the multi-stage curriculum learning process in enhancing overall model performance (Table 3).

#### 4.4. Data Efficiency (Figure 5)

- Fast Quiet-STaR achieves strong results with only 20-40 additional training steps beyond the initial Quiet-STaR training. This suggests that performance gains are due to the progressive learning mechanism rather than increased data exposure.

#### 4.5. Performance on Generative Tasks (Table 4)

- Fast Quiet-STaR NTP, when applied with Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning and majority voting (cot-maj@6) on GSM8K, improves accuracy from 43.3% (pre-trained) to 52.4%. This demonstrates that Fast Quiet-STaR enhances reasoning in generative contexts and is complementary to CoT, not redundant.

#### 4.6. Long-Term Inference Savings and Thought Token Analysis

- The entire training pipeline is highly efficient (0.5M tokens, 54 minutes on 8 H800 GPUs). The inference time saved from just 67 runs of Fast Quiet-STaR NTP compared to Quiet-STaR 16-8 (55 minutes total speedup) is sufficient to offset the entire training cost, highlighting significant long-term cost advantages in real-world deployments.
- Visualizations of thought tokens (Figure 6 & 7) show that Fast Quiet-STaR 8-4 generates more abstract and goal-directed reasoning compared to the unstructured thoughts of Quiet-STaR 8-4. This confirms that curriculum learning enables the model to acquire effective reasoning under resource constraints.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

"Fast Quiet-STaR" represents a significant advancement in the field of efficient LLM reasoning, particularly for models employing token-level thought processes. Its primary significance lies in **bridging the critical gap between sophisticated reasoning capabilities and practical inference efficiency.**

The potential impacts are substantial:

1. **Democratization of Advanced Reasoning:** By drastically reducing the inference overhead of Quiet-STaR, Fast Quiet-STaR makes powerful token-level reasoning more accessible for real-world applications where latency and computational costs are paramount. This could lead to wider adoption of more complex reasoning paradigms in interactive systems, chatbots, and AI assistants.
2. **Enabling Implicit Reasoning:** The successful transition to the Next Token Prediction (NTP) setting with Fast Quiet-STaR NTP is a major breakthrough. It demonstrates that explicit, internal thought processes can be effectively internalized and compressed into the model's latent representations, leading to enhanced reasoning without requiring any additional inference steps or specialized decoding mechanisms. This "thinking without thought tokens" is highly desirable for deployment.
3. **Validation of Curriculum Learning for Reasoning Compression:** The work provides compelling evidence for the effectiveness of curriculum learning in guiding LLMs to develop concise and abstract reasoning abilities under resource constraints. This methodology could inspire similar approaches for compressing other complex behaviors or knowledge within LLMs.
4. **Cost-Effectiveness and Scalability:** The analysis of long-term inference savings clearly demonstrates that the upfront training cost of Fast Quiet-STaR NTP is quickly recouped, especially in scenarios involving millions of inference runs. This makes the approach economically attractive for large-scale deployments.
5. **Complementary to Existing Techniques:** The finding that Fast Quiet-STaR NTP enhances performance even when combined with Chain-of-Thought reasoning (e.g., on GSM8K) suggests its broad applicability and potential to synergize with other reasoning methods, further boosting overall LLM intelligence.

**Limitations acknowledged by the authors:**
- **Domain Specificity:** The evaluation primarily focuses on mathematical and logical reasoning tasks. Its generalization capability to other complex domains (e.g., scientific reasoning, creative writing, nuanced common sense beyond QA) remains to be thoroughly validated.
- **Method Specificity:** The method is currently tailored specifically for the Quiet-STaR reasoning paradigm. Adapting it to other fine-grained reasoning or planning frameworks might require further research.

Despite these limitations, Fast Quiet-STaR offers a promising direction for developing more intelligent, efficient, and deployable Large Language Models, paving the way for LLMs that can "think" effectively without imposing prohibitive computational burdens.
