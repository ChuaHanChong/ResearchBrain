# Delay-Aware Active Triangulation with Uncertainty-Driven Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning for Counter-UAS

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at KAIST introduced a multi-agent reinforcement learning framework for Counter-UAS active triangulation, specifically designed to mitigate challenges from communication delays and diverse uncertainty sources. This framework enhanced 3D target localization precision, achieving a 14% reduction in RMSE and a 10.6 percentage point increase in triangulation validity against a learning evader, under realistic operational constraints.

## Problem

- Achieving precise and continuous 3D localization of small unmanned aerial systems (UAS) for Counter-UAS applications presents a challenge.
- Existing multi-agent active triangulation methods often neglect cumulative, asymmetric system delays (e.g., between ego-state and inter-agent observations), which are prevalent in real-world deployments.
- Current uncertainty models for triangulation typically simplify error sources, leading to less robust performance under realistic sensing conditions where multiple uncertainties contribute.

## Method

- The problem is formalized as a ==Decentralized Partially Observable Markov Decision Process (Dec-POMDP)== augmented with ==Age-of-Information (AoI)== tags in observations and a ==recurrent policy (GRU)== to manage stochastic communication delays.
- A ==dual-path delay architecture== evaluates policies trained with 'privileged' (clean ground-truth) versus 'perception-consistent' (noisy, delayed) rewards to understand trade-offs in stability and robustness.
- A comprehensive ==multi-source analytical covariance model== for triangulation is developed, integrating uncertainties from pixel detection, pose estimation, gimbal calibration, and camera intrinsics.

## Results

- The inclusion of AoI metadata and a GRU policy reduced RMSE to **0.547 m** (from **0.701 m** without AoI) and increased triangulation validity by **10.6 percentage points** (to **78.1%**).
- Policies trained with perception-consistent rewards achieved **14%** lower RMSE (**0.547 m** vs. **0.633 m**), **27%** fewer track losses, and **38%** fewer collisions compared to those using privileged rewards.
- The multi-source analytical covariance model yielded a **2.8-fold** improvement in RMSE (from **1.546 m** to **0.547 m**) and a **45.9 percentage point** increase in triangulation validity over angular-only uncertainty models.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly accounting for information freshness via AoI-augmented observations and using recurrent policies (GRUs) is crucial for effective multi-agent coordination and delay compensation in stochastic environments.
> - Aligning the reward function with the agents' actual noisy and delayed observations ('perception-consistent rewards') can lead to superior aggregate performance and robustness against noise, albeit with a more reactive coordination style.
> - Accurate and comprehensive modeling of all uncertainty sources (pixel, pose, gimbal, intrinsics) significantly improves triangulation accuracy and guides agents towards more robust geometric configurations.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The proliferation of small unmanned aerial systems (UAS) has increased the demand for Counter-UAS (C-UAS) technologies, which require precise and continuous 3D target localization. Vision-based tracking offers a passive, semantic-rich, and scalable solution for this challenge. Within this domain, multi-agent active triangulation, where mobile observers dynamically adjust their positions to optimize measurement geometry, is a method for obtaining instantaneous 3D position estimates. This approach is further enhanced by platforms equipped with controllable gimbal-stabilized cameras that feature optical zoom, allowing for independent control of viewing direction and subject distance.

However, a critical challenge in real-world multi-agent systems is the presence of cumulative system delays. These latencies, stemming from frame acquisition, object detection, inter-agent communication, and control propagation, can significantly impact system performance. Existing perception-aware methods often focus on single-agent scenarios, failing to address the complex multi-agent delay structures, particularly the asymmetry between ego-state observations (low latency) and inter-agent observations (higher latency).

This work positions itself as an extension and enhancement of previous research in multi-agent reinforcement learning (RL) for triangulation, notably building upon the framework introduced by Gavin et al., which utilized analytical covariance-based rewards. The paper aims to integrate more realistic deployment conditions, including stochastic communication delays with asymmetric staleness, controllable gimbal-zoom sensors with field-of-view constraints, and a comprehensive multi-source uncertainty propagation model. By doing so, it addresses limitations in existing methods that assume instantaneous feedback or simplified uncertainty models, thereby contributing to the development of more robust and deployable C-UAS technologies. The research specifically leverages concepts from Age-of-Information (AoI) in delayed-observation Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning (MARL) and advanced multi-view geometry for uncertainty modeling.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the necessity for precise and continuous 3D localization of aerial targets in Counter-UAS (C-UAS) applications. While multi-agent active visual triangulation offers a promising approach, existing methods often operate under idealized assumptions that do not reflect the complexities of real-world deployments. Specifically, these methods commonly disregard cumulative latency arising from various stages of data processing and communication, ranging from tens to thousands of milliseconds. This latency is particularly problematic in multi-agent scenarios due to its asymmetric nature: ego-state observations typically benefit from low-latency onboard sensing, whereas inter-agent observations incur greater delays due to communication channels.

To address these limitations and enhance the realism and robustness of multi-agent active triangulation, the research sets forth three key objectives:

1. **Develop a Delay-Aware Dec-POMDP Formulation with AoI-Augmented Observations:** The first objective is to formalize the active triangulation problem as a Decentralized Partially Observable Markov Decision Process (Dec-POMDP) that explicitly accounts for stochastic communication delays. This involves incorporating Age-of-Information (AoI) metadata into agent observations, which quantifies the freshness of received information. The motivation is to enable agents to reason about the staleness of inter-agent observations, thereby facilitating delay-aware coordination. The hypothesis is that explicit delay modeling is essential for effective coordination under realistic communication conditions.

2. **Conduct a Comparative Evaluation of Privileged and Perception-Consistent Rewards:** The second objective is to investigate the impact of different reward computation strategies on policy performance and robustness. Using a dual-path delay architecture, the study compares policies trained with "privileged" rewards, derived from clean, ground-truth (but delayed) states, against policies trained with "perception-consistent" rewards, derived from the same noisy and delayed observations available to the policy. Both policies are trained under identical observation noise. The motivation is to understand the trade-offs between optimizing for an ideal state versus optimizing for the perceptually available state, particularly concerning stability and robustness in deployment.

3. **Implement Multi-Source Analytical Covariance Propagation:** The third objective is to enhance the uncertainty modeling within the triangulation process. This involves extending existing angular-noise covariance models to incorporate a broader range of uncertainty sources, including pixel detection noise, pose estimation error, gimbal calibration error, and camera intrinsics uncertainties. The motivation is that a comprehensive model of uncertainty is crucial for accurately estimating target position and guiding agents towards configurations that minimize estimation error, particularly under realistic sensing conditions where multiple error sources contribute to overall uncertainty.

By pursuing these objectives, the research aims to establish a more robust and practical framework for multi-agent active visual triangulation in challenging C-UAS environments, ultimately improving the precision and continuity of 3D target localization.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors frame the multi-agent active visual triangulation problem as a Decentralized Partially Observable Markov Decision Process (Dec-POMDP), augmented with a stochastic communication model. This framework considers C camera-equipped drones ("agents") tasked with localizing a single target. The system operates in discrete time steps.

**A. Dec-POMDP Formulation with Delayed Observations**
- **Global State (sₖ)**: Comprises the individual states of each agent (3D position, velocity, 4D quaternion orientation, 3D angular velocity, 2D gimbal angles, 1D zoom) and the target state (3D position, 3D velocity).
- **Actions (a⁽ⁱ⁾ₖ)**: Each agent executes a 7-dimensional continuous action, mapped to physical commands for 3D velocity, yaw rate, gimbal pan/tilt rates, and zoom rate.
- **Stochastic Delay Model**: All state observations are subject to a three-stage delay pipeline:
 1. **Staleness**: Arises from discrete sampling intervals.
 2. **Latency**: Represents computation time or network propagation delays.
 3. **Dropout**: Models packet loss with a probability `p_drop`.
 This model generates cumulative delays ranging from 50 to 1000 ms. A key feature is the **asymmetry in delays**: ego-state observations experience minimal latency (simulating onboard sensors), while inter-agent observations incur full communication delays.
- **Age-of-Information (AoI)**: For each inter-agent observation (˜x⁽ʲ→ⁱ⁾ₖ), an AoI tag (∆⁽ʲ→ⁱ⁾ₖ) is maintained. This tag measures the elapsed time since the source generated the currently held information. The AoI is included in agent i's observation (oᵢₖ), allowing policies to consider information freshness.
- **Recurrent Policy**: To handle delayed and asynchronously updated observations, a Gated Recurrent Unit (GRU) is employed. This recurrent neural network maintains a temporal belief based on recent information history, acting as an implicit delay compensator.

**B. Triangulation Geometry**
Each agent is equipped with a gimbaled camera featuring pinhole projection and linear optical zoom. Given detected pixel coordinates for the target, unit ray directions (d̂ᵢ) from each camera's position (pᵢ) are derived. The 3D target position (X̂) is then calculated using a closed-form multi-view ray-intersection formula, which minimizes the sum of squared perpendicular distances from X̂ to all rays. This method requires at least two cameras with non-parallel rays for a valid estimate.

**C. Objectives (Reward Function)**
The goal is to learn a joint policy that maximizes a shared team reward, which decomposes into several per-agent components:
- **Triangulation Quality (r_tri)**: Rewards agents for configurations that minimize the trace of the triangulated position's covariance matrix (Σ_X), inversely proportional to its square root.
- **Image-Space Tracking (r_img)**: Encourages keeping the target centered in the image and maintaining an appropriate bounding-box size through exponential decay terms for deviations.
- **Safety Constraints (r_coll)**: Penalizes agents for pairwise distances falling below a minimum threshold, preventing collisions.
- **Action Regularization (r_act)**: Penalizes the magnitude and jerk of agent actions.

**D. Dual-Path Delay Architecture**
This architecture separates the observation path, which is always noisy and delayed for the decentralized actors, from the reward path. This design allows for a controlled comparison between two reward computation strategies:
- **Noisy Path (Perception-Consistent Rewards)**: Rewards are computed using the same noisy and delayed states that the policy observes. Gaussian noise is added to state fields, scaled by a curriculum factor.
- **Clean Path (Privileged Rewards)**: Rewards are computed from delayed ground-truth states without noise.
Both paths apply the same underlying delay structure, but the difference in noise injection for reward computation is central to evaluating the stability-robustness tradeoff. Ego-state paths have minimal latency, while inter-agent paths have full communication delays.

**E. Analytical Triangulation Covariance (Σ_X)**
The framework extends previous work by deriving a closed-form covariance for the triangulated position (Σ_X) that incorporates multiple uncertainty sources:
- **Pixel detection noise**: Error in identifying target pixels.
- **Pose estimation error**: Inaccuracy in agent position and orientation.
- **Gimbal calibration error**: Errors in gimbal angle measurements.
- **Camera intrinsics uncertainties**: Errors in focal length and principal point.
The approach linearizes the camera projection and combines these uncertainties into an effective residual covariance (Σ_resid = Σ_pix + J_θ Σ_θ Jᵀ_θ). The target position covariance is then calculated as the inverse of the weighted least-squares information matrix H = Jᵀ_X Σ⁻¹_resid J_X, i.e., Σ_X = H⁻¹. This comprehensive model guides agents toward robust geometric configurations.

**F. Curriculum Learning**
To facilitate learning in a complex environment, an eight-dimensional staged curriculum is employed across three phases:
- **Phase 1 (0–60k steps)**: Agents learn ego motion and sensor control for visual tracking of a stationary target under clean observations.
- **Phase 2 (20k–100k steps)**: Target motion and the triangulation coordination reward are introduced.
- **Phase 3 (80k–200k steps)**: Observation noise, communication delays, detection dropout, and dynamics randomization are progressively introduced.
This curriculum allows agents to master foundational skills before tackling full environmental complexity.

**G. Training Setup**
Experiments are conducted using Isaac Sim 4.5 / Isaac Lab 2.1 with SKRL 1.4.3. Training uses 4096 parallel environments on an NVIDIA RTX 3090 GPU. The learning algorithm is MAPPO (Multi-Agent Proximal Policy Optimization) in a Centralized Training Decentralized Execution (CTDE) setup, featuring parameter sharing among agents. The policy consists of a shared recurrent network: a two-layer MLP encoder (47-dimensional observations to 64-dimensional embedding) followed by a 64-unit GRU (sequence length 32). Training spans 200k steps, equivalent to approximately 819 million transitions.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research conducted experiments under five distinct conditions at full curriculum difficulty to evaluate the contributions of each methodological component. Performance was assessed using metrics such as Root Mean Square Error (RMSE), Triangulation Validity, Visibility, Convergence steps, Collisions, and Track Loss. The findings are summarized below:

- **Impact of Temporal Modeling (AoI and Recurrence):**
 - **MLP (stacked observations)**: Policies trained with a 3-layer MLP and frame stacking (providing ~1.2 s of temporal context) achieved near-zero triangulation validity (0.7%). This indicates that fixed temporal windows are insufficient to compensate for non-uniform, stochastic, and variable-latency observation delays inherent in the model.
 - **GRU with AoI**: Replacing the MLP with a GRU (recurrent policy) significantly improved performance. Furthermore, the inclusion of AoI metadata in the observation space for the GRU policy led to a reduction in RMSE from 0.701 m (without AoI) to 0.547 m (perception-consistent reward, with AoI) and an increase in triangulation validity by 10.6 percentage points (from 68.5% to 78.1%). This suggests that both recurrent memory (for implicit delay compensation) and explicit staleness information (AoI) are crucial for robust coordination under stochastic delays.

- **Comparative Evaluation of Privileged and Perception-Consistent Rewards:**
 - **Aggregate Metrics**: The policy trained with **perception-consistent rewards** (using noisy, delayed states for reward computation) achieved superior aggregate performance compared to the **privileged reward** policy (using clean, delayed states). Specifically, the perception-consistent policy showed an RMSE of 0.547 ± 0.217 m, which was 14% lower than the privileged reward policy (0.633 ± 0.345 m). It also resulted in 27% fewer track losses and 38% fewer collisions.
 - **Stability-Robustness Tradeoff**: A per-timestep analysis (Fig. 5) revealed a qualitative difference in behavior. The privileged reward policy tended to converge to a more stable geometric equilibrium with monotonically decreasing RMSE and sustained viewing angles, implying a more consistent optimization target. In contrast, the perception-consistent reward policy learned reactive coordination that continuously readjusted to current (stochastic) observations. While this yielded better aggregate noise robustness, it also led to a gradual increase in RMSE and visibility decay over extended episodes, lacking a fixed attractor. This suggests that neither reward formulation strictly dominates, and the choice is deployment-dependent.

- **Contribution of Multi-Source Analytical Covariance:**
 - **Angular-only Covariance**: Policies trained with an angular-only covariance model (similar to) exhibited substantial degradation. They achieved an RMSE of 1.546 ± 1.134 m (a 2.8-fold increase compared to the perception-consistent multi-source policy) and a triangulation validity of only 32.2% (a 45.9 percentage point drop). Track losses also increased by 2.8 times.
 - **Multi-Source Covariance**: The comprehensive multi-source covariance model, incorporating pixel, pose, gimbal, and intrinsics uncertainties, significantly improved localization accuracy and validity. This finding confirms that accurately modeling a broad range of uncertainty sources is essential for guiding policies toward robust configurations under realistic sensing conditions.

- **Robustness Envelopes:**
 - **Communication Delay**: The perception-consistent policy demonstrated graceful performance degradation up to approximately 200 ms of communication delay, beyond its training default of 100 ms.
 - **Target Speed**: The policy maintained consistent tracking quality across a range of target speeds (0–12 m/s), indicating generalization beyond its training speed of 5 m/s.

In summary, the experiments validate that explicit delay modeling with AoI, recurrent policy architectures, the careful alignment of rewards with observations, and comprehensive multi-source uncertainty propagation are all critical for achieving robust and precise multi-agent active triangulation in Counter-UAS scenarios under realistic communication and sensing constraints.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes a comprehensive framework for delay-aware multi-agent active visual triangulation in Counter-UAS applications, demonstrating how to achieve precise aerial target localization under realistic operational constraints. The unification of a Dec-POMDP formulation with Age-of-Information (AoI)-augmented observations, a dual-path reward architecture, and multi-source analytical covariance propagation within a Multi-Agent Proximal Policy Optimization (MAPPO) framework represents a significant step towards deployable cooperative robotic systems.

The systematic ablation studies provide empirical validation for each component's importance:
- The integration of **AoI-augmented observations** improves triangulation validity by 10.6 percentage points, confirming the necessity of explicit information freshness metadata for effective delay compensation in multi-agent coordination.
- The comparison between **perception-consistent and privileged rewards** reveals a crucial trade-off. While perception-consistent rewards yield superior aggregate metrics (e.547 m RMSE and 27% fewer track losses), they induce a reactive coordination style. Privileged rewards, conversely, lead to more temporally stable behavior. This finding identifies reward-observation alignment as a critical design axis, offering guidance for tailoring policies to specific deployment requirements concerning either overall robustness or temporal stability.
- The **multi-source analytical covariance propagation** demonstrates a substantial reduction in RMSE (2.8-fold) compared to angular-only uncertainty modeling. This underscores that comprehensive uncertainty modeling, accounting for pixel, pose, gimbal, and intrinsics errors, is vital for guiding agents towards geometrically robust configurations under realistic sensing noise.

The potential impact of this work is multifaceted:
- **Enhanced C-UAS Capabilities**: By addressing the challenges of latency and uncertainty, the framework can lead to more accurate and reliable 3D localization of aerial threats, which is foundational for effective detection, tracking, and neutralization in C-UAS operations.
- **Advancements in Multi-Agent Robotics**: The approach provides a principled methodology for designing and training multi-agent systems to perform cooperative tasks under partial observability and stochastic communication delays, conditions prevalent in many real-world robotic applications.
- **Improved Networked Control Systems**: The insights into delay-aware coordination and the role of AoI can inform the design of networked control strategies for distributed systems beyond C-UAS, where information freshness and robustness to communication imperfections are paramount.
- **Bridging Research Fields**: The work successfully integrates concepts from networked control, multi-view geometry, and multi-agent reinforcement learning, fostering interdisciplinary research and development.

Despite these advancements, the authors identify several areas for future work. The current study is simulation-based, necessitating **real-flight validation** with onboard sensing and wireless networking to confirm real-world performance. Scaling beyond the tested two-agent scenario introduces challenges, particularly a sharp increase in collision rates in preliminary three-agent experiments, highlighting the need for **effective pairwise collision encodings** for larger teams. Further research will explore **hybrid reward strategies** that combine the benefits of both privileged and perception-consistent signals. Additionally, **online estimation of deployment noise and delay conditions** (rather than assuming them known), **uncertainty models accounting for measurement staleness**, and strategies for **sim-to-real transfer** are critical next steps to transition this research into practical applications.
