# On the Geometry of On-Policy Distillation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at HKUST, UT Austin, Zhejiang University, and others characterized the parameter-space optimization geometry of On-Policy Distillation (OPD) for large language models, finding it occupies a "relaxed off-principal" regime distinct from SFT and RLVR. OPD exhibits "subspace locking" where cumulative updates rapidly converge into a low-dimensional, functionally sufficient channel, primarily controlled by the objective's composition.

## Problem

- Large language model (LLM) post-training methods like Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) and Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) exhibit distinct parameter-space optimization geometries.
- On-Policy Distillation (OPD), a hybrid post-training approach, combines aspects of both SFT and RLVR, making its parameter trajectory and underlying optimization geometry unclear.
- A comprehensive understanding of OPD's parameter-space dynamics was lacking, hindering the development of more efficient and robust OPD algorithms.

## Method

- The study systematically characterized OPD's ==parameter-space== trajectory using comparative analysis against SFT and RLVR on Qwen3-family models.
- Four parameter-space diagnostics (update sparsity, principal-angle rotation, normalized spectral shift, update-mask overlap) were used to compare static endpoint geometries.
- Dynamic update trajectories were analyzed over training with metrics like stable rank, Frobenius norm, subspace similarity, and functional sufficiency tests. Control experiments perturbed token density, rollout policy, and objective function composition to identify governing factors.

## Results

- OPD demonstrated an intermediate bf16-aware update sparsity of **51.6%** (unchanged weights), compared to SFT's **8.1%** and RLVR's **77.2%**, alongside intermediate subspace rotation and spectral drift.
- The cumulative updates in OPD rapidly formed a low-dimensional update channel, with an early-emerging rank-16 right singular subspace proving functionally sufficient to preserve performance when subsequent training was constrained to it.
- Mixing the OPD teacher-correction signal with the RLVR reward signal significantly altered the stable-rank dynamics, while token sparsification and off-policy rollouts primarily affected update scale without changing the spectral shape.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - On-Policy Distillation (OPD) establishes a "relaxed off-principal" optimization regime, intermediate between the dense, principal updates of SFT and the sparse, off-principal updates of RLVR.
> - OPD's cumulative updates rapidly converge into a low-dimensional, persistent, and functionally sufficient subspace, a phenomenon termed "subspace locking."
> - The composition of OPD's objective function is the primary control mechanism for this subspace locking, whereas runtime perturbations like token sparsification or off-policy rollouts largely preserve the update geometry.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses a gap in the understanding of post-training dynamics for large language models (LLMs), specifically focusing on On-Policy Distillation (OPD). Recent advancements in LLM reasoning capabilities have been significantly driven by post-training techniques such as supervised fine-tuning (SFT) and reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR). SFT involves training on offline demonstrations using cross-entropy objectives, while RLVR optimizes policies using sparse outcome-level feedback, often incorporating mechanisms to maintain policy stability, such as KL regularization.

On-Policy Distillation (OPD) has emerged as a complementary post-training paradigm. It trains a smaller "student" model on trajectories generated by itself (on-policy), guided by dense, token-level supervision from a stronger "teacher" model. Despite its empirical utility in improving LLM reasoning, the precise parameter-space dynamics and the intrinsic update trajectory of OPD have remained largely uncharacterized.

Prior research has established distinct "geometric footprints" for SFT and RLVR in the parameter space. SFT typically induces dense, principal-aligned updates, which can significantly distort the pretrained spectral structure of the model. In contrast, online reinforcement learning methods, including RLVR, tend to produce sparse, off-principal updates that are observed to better preserve the pretrained spectral properties, often attributed to conservative projection mechanisms that limit policy-level KL divergence.

OPD combines elements of both paradigms: its dense token-level distillation signal resembles SFT, while its on-policy sampling and policy-gradient-like optimization connect it to RLVR. This hybrid nature makes it difficult to infer OPD's parameter trajectory solely from the established characteristics of SFT or RLVR. This paper aims to empirically locate OPD within this established spectrum and characterize its unique training dynamics, thereby contributing to a more comprehensive understanding of LLM post-training optimization.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research is to demystify the training dynamics of On-Policy Distillation (OPD) in the parameter space of large language models, given its increasing use in improving LLM reasoning capabilities and the lack of understanding regarding its underlying optimization trajectory. The paper seeks to move beyond empirical observations of OPD's performance and analyze the geometric changes it induces in model weights.

The authors articulate three specific research questions (RQs) to guide their investigation:

- **RQ1: Where does OPD lie within the parameter-space spectrum between SFT and RLVR?**
 - This objective aims to quantitatively position OPD relative to the two well-understood post-training paradigms, SFT and RLVR, using established parameter-space diagnostics. The motivation is to determine if OPD's updates align more closely with the dense, principal-aligned updates of SFT or the sparse, off-principal, geometry-preserving updates of RLVR, or if it occupies a distinct intermediate position.

- **RQ2: What intrinsic update trajectory does OPD follow during training?**
 - This objective extends beyond a static comparison of endpoints to characterize the evolution of OPD's cumulative updates throughout the training process. The motivation is to identify any unique patterns or phases in OPD's parameter-space trajectory, such as changes in dimensionality, scale, or subspace stability, which might distinguish it from the dynamics observed in SFT or RLVR.

- **RQ3: Which component of OPD controls this trajectory?**
 - This objective investigates the specific factors within the OPD framework that govern its observed parameter-space dynamics. By systematically perturbing different aspects of OPD (e.g., teacher supervision density, rollout policy, objective composition), the researchers aim to identify the causal levers that determine its characteristic update geometry and trajectory. The motivation is to inform the design of more effective and geometry-aware OPD algorithms.

Overall, the motivation is to provide a parameter-space interpretation of OPD-based post-training, moving beyond behavioral observations to understand the underlying mechanics of how OPD modifies LLM parameters. This understanding is intended to guide the development of future OPD algorithms that can leverage or control these geometric properties for improved stability, interpretability, and transferability.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research employs a comparative, empirical methodology, utilizing a suite of parameter-space diagnostics to analyze the weight updates of models undergoing On-Policy Distillation (OPD), Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT), and Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR).

#### 3.1. Experimental Setup

- **Model Family:** Qwen3-family checkpoints are used, primarily Qwen3-8B as the student model.
- **Initialization:** OPD and RLVR models are initialized from the same SFT checkpoint (Qwen3-8B-SFT), which itself is derived from a pretrained Qwen3-8B-Base model. SFT updates are measured from the pretrained base model to the SFT checkpoint.
- **Data Domain:** The main comparisons are conducted on a math-domain prompt distribution (dapo-math-17k dataset for OPD/RLVR, Dolci-Think SFT data for SFT anchor).
- **Teacher Models:** Qwen3-32B serves as the teacher for OPD.
- **Precision:** All training and analysis are performed with bf16 precision.

#### 3.2. Parameter-Space Diagnostics (for RQ1)

Building on prior work by Zhu et al. (2025), the authors use four diagnostics to compare the post-training weight geometry:
- **Update Sparsity (bf16-aware):** Measures the percentage of weights that remain unchanged when considering bfloat16 rounding. A higher value indicates fewer visible weight changes. The threshold for "unchanged" is defined as `|w_i - ŵ_i| ≤ η max(|w_i|, |ŵ_i|)` with `η = 10^-3`.
- **Principal-Angle Rotation:** Quantifies the rotation of the dominant singular subspaces (top-k left and right singular vectors) between the initial (`W_0`) and post-training (`W_+`) weight matrices. Smaller angles indicate stronger preservation of pretrained dominant subspaces. `k=512` is used by default.
- **Spectral Drift (Normalized Spectral Shift, NSS):** Measures the L2 norm of the difference in singular value vectors, normalized by the L2 norm of the initial singular value vector. Smaller NSS indicates stronger preservation of the pretrained singular value spectrum.
- **Update-Mask Overlap:** Compares the bf16-visible update mask (locations of changed weights) with two reference masks derived from `W_0`:
 - **Principal Mask (`M_princ`):** Top-α fraction of entries by magnitude in the rank-k SVD reconstruction of `W_0` (proxy for high-curvature regions).
 - **Low-Magnitude Mask (`M_low`):** Bottom-α fraction of entries by absolute magnitude in `W_0`.
 - Overlap is defined as `|M* ∩ M_upd| / |M_upd|`. Sub-random overlap with `M_princ` suggests depletion from principal weights, while super-random overlap with `M_low` indicates concentration in low-magnitude regions. `α=0.5` and `r=64` are used.

#### 3.3. Intrinsic Update Trajectory Analysis (for RQ2)

To track how cumulative updates `∆W_t = W_t - W_0` evolve over training checkpoints:
- **Stable Rank:** `srank(∆W_t) = ||∆W_t||_F^2 / ||∆W_t||_op^2`. This measures the effective number of dominant singular directions carrying the update energy, providing a scale-aware measure of effective dimensionality.
- **Frobenius Norm:** `||∆W_t||_F` quantifies the magnitude of cumulative updates, ruling out small-update artifacts for low stable rank.
- **Hill Tail Estimate:** `bα_Hill` (Hill, 1975) is used as an auxiliary diagnostic to inspect the spectral shape of `∆W_t`.
- **Subspace Similarity:** `Sim_K(t, t_end)` measures the alignment of the top-K right singular subspace of `∆W_t` with the final update subspace `V_K(t_end)`. Values near 1 indicate early emergence of the final update channel. `K=16` is used.
- **Rank-Constrained Training:** To test functional sufficiency, training is constrained to an early-identified low-dimensional subspace. Gradients `g` are projected onto `V_16` (extracted around 20% training progress) as `g ← gV_16 V_16^⊤`. This is applied to both OPD and SFT, and performance is evaluated.

#### 3.4. Control Experiments (for RQ3)

To determine factors controlling subspace locking, three components of OPD are perturbed:
- **Token Sparsification:** Teacher supervision density is altered by retaining either top-KL or randomly selected tokens at 25% and 50% density.
- **Off-Policy Rollouts:** Rollouts are generated by the teacher policy (`y ~ π_T(|x)`) instead of the student policy (`y ~ π_θ(|x)`), while keeping the OPD objective fixed.
- **Objective Interpolation:** The OPD teacher-correction signal (`A_i,OPD`) is mixed with the RLVR reward signal (`A_i,RLVR`) using a weighting parameter `α` (`A^(α)_i = αA_i,OPD + (1-α)A_i,RLVR`). This changes the gradient source itself.

All diagnostics are computed offline per matrix and then averaged, to avoid domination by large matrices and preserve layer/module variations. CPU float64 SVD is used for principal angles to ensure stability.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The study yielded several findings regarding the parameter-space geometry and training dynamics of On-Policy Distillation (OPD), positioning it distinctly from Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) and Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR).

#### 4.1. OPD Occupies a Relaxed Off-Principal Regime (RQ1)

- **Update Sparsity:** OPD exhibits intermediate sparsity compared to SFT and RLVR. In controlled Qwen3-8B comparisons, SFT leaves 8.1% of weights unchanged, RLVR leaves 77.2% unchanged, and OPD falls in between at 51.6%. This pattern remained stable across various OPD variants (teacher/student scale, data domain, MoE teacher).
- **Subspace Rotation and Spectral Drift:** SFT induces the largest rotation of pretrained singular subspaces (principal angles > 10°) and the highest spectral drift (NSS at 10^-3 level). RLVR preserves geometry most strongly, with minimal rotation (< 0.5°) and drift (NSS at 10^-5 level). OPD consistently lies between them, showing smaller but non-zero rotation (around 1°) and drift (NSS at 10^-4 level).
- **Update Localization:** SFT shows high overlap with principal masks and low overlap with low-magnitude masks, indicating updates in high-curvature regions. RLVR shows the opposite, with low principal-mask overlap and high low-magnitude overlap, indicating updates in low-magnitude, off-principal regions. OPD again lies in between, shifting updates away from principal weights and towards low-magnitude regions more than SFT, but less selectively than RLVR.
- **Relaxed Three-Gate Account:** The authors interpret these findings through a "relaxed Three-Gate account" inspired by RLVR. OPD is anchored by local quadratic budget and steered by pretrained model geometry like RLVR, but the dense token-level teacher supervision provides more descent directions, leading to larger effective updates and broader accessible directional support. This results in more coordinates crossing the bf16 realization threshold, yielding intermediate sparsity while preserving an off-principal bias.

#### 4.2. OPD Learns Through Subspace Locking (RQ2)

- **Low-Dimensional Update Band:** Tracking cumulative updates (∆W_t) across training reveals distinct trajectories. SFT progressively expands its update subspace (increasing stable rank), and RLVR contracts towards a low-dimensional endpoint. In contrast, OPD rapidly enters and stays within a narrow, low-dimensional update band throughout training. This is not due to small updates; OPD accumulates a substantially larger Frobenius norm than RLVR while achieving a comparable stable rank. Hill tail estimates also support OPD's mildly evolving spectral shape.
- **Early Subspace Emergence:** Subspace similarity diagnostics show that OPD's final low-dimensional update channel emerges early in training, with strong alignment to the final subspace from the first measured checkpoint. SFT and RLVR converge to their final subspaces more gradually.
- **Functional Sufficiency:** Constraining subsequent training to this early-emerging rank-16 subspace preserves OPD's performance, indicating that this low-dimensional channel is functionally sufficient for OPD training. The same constraint substantially degrades SFT performance across multiple reasoning benchmarks, confirming this sufficiency is not a generic property of the projection dimension.

#### 4.3. Objective Composition Controls Subspace Locking (RQ3)

- **Runtime Perturbations (Robustness):** Perturbing runtime factors like token supervision density (e.g., top-KL or random sparsification at 25% or 50% retention) or rollout policy (off-policy generation by teacher) did not alter the characteristic OPD stable-rank trajectory. These changes primarily affected the update scale rather than the underlying spectral shape.
- **Objective Mixing (Sensitivity):** Interpolating the OPD teacher-correction signal with the RLVR reward signal (mixing `A_OPD` and `A_RLVR`) exposed a boundary of the locked regime. OPD-dominant mixtures (high `α`) retained the OPD-like stable-rank trajectory. However, when the OPD component became sufficiently weak (low `α`), the trajectory departed from the baseline, entering a distinct spectral regime. This suggests that the fundamental source of the gradient, rather than just the sampled data or token coverage, controls the update geometry. The mechanistic view attributes this to changes in the dominant covariance geometry of the gradients when objective sources are mixed.

In summary, OPD exhibits a distinct parameter-space geometry characterized by a relaxed off-principal localization and an early-emerging, persistent, and functionally sufficient low-dimensional update channel, whose dynamics are primarily controlled by the objective's composition.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research provides a fundamental understanding of the parameter-space dynamics of On-Policy Distillation (OPD), distinguishing it as a unique post-training paradigm rather than a simple intermediate point between SFT and RLVR. The findings offer several significant implications and potential impacts:

- **Refined Understanding of Post-Training:** By characterizing OPD's "relaxed off-principal regime" and "subspace locking" phenomenon, the paper contributes to a more nuanced comprehension of how different post-training methods modify large language models. This geometric perspective complements behavioral analyses and provides insights into why OPD achieves its observed empirical utility.
- **Guidance for Algorithm Design:** The identification of subspace locking, particularly its early emergence and functional sufficiency, suggests a crucial guiding principle for future OPD algorithms: **design OPD as geometry control, not merely as denser token supervision.** This implies that developers should monitor and potentially exploit the locked update channel.
- **Improved OPD Stability and Interpretability:** By understanding that objective composition is the primary lever controlling the update trajectory, practitioners can strategically regulate objective-induced update geometry. This could lead to more stable and interpretable OPD training processes, where the impact of various design choices (e.g., token selection, rollout policy, teacher scale) is evaluated through their effect on the model's update geometry rather than solely on performance metrics.
- **Enhanced Transferability:** Preserving a functionally sufficient update channel could potentially make OPD models more robust and transferable across tasks or domains, as the core learning mechanism is understood to operate within a constrained, effective subspace.
- **Basis for Geometry-Aware Optimization:** The work lays the groundwork for developing new optimization techniques specifically tailored to OPD's unique geometric properties. For instance, techniques that explicitly encourage or maintain low-dimensional, off-principal updates could be explored, potentially leading to more efficient or performant OPD variants.
- **Mechanistic Explanations for Hybrid Methods:** For other hybrid post-training methods that combine elements of SFT and RL, this work provides a framework for analyzing their parameter-space geometry, potentially revealing similar "relaxed" or "locked" regimes.

**Limitations:**
The authors acknowledge that their analysis primarily focuses on controlled Qwen3-family reasoning settings. The observed geometric patterns might vary across different model families, modalities, or task distributions. Furthermore, the "Three-Gate account" and covariance analysis are presented as mechanistic explanations consistent with the empirical evidence, rather than complete causal or formal theories of OPD optimization. The diagnostics characterize trajectories from stored checkpoints, meaning the full, continuous dynamics are approximated.
