# On the Geometry of On-Policy Distillation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at HKUST, UT Austin, Zhejiang University, and others characterized the parameter-space optimization geometry of On-Policy Distillation (OPD) for large language models, finding it occupies a "relaxed off-principal" regime distinct from SFT and RLVR. OPD exhibits "subspace locking" where cumulative updates rapidly converge into a low-dimensional, functionally sufficient channel, primarily controlled by the objective's composition.

## Problem

- Large language model (LLM) post-training methods like Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) and Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) exhibit distinct parameter-space optimization geometries.
- On-Policy Distillation (OPD), a hybrid post-training approach, combines aspects of both SFT and RLVR, making its parameter trajectory and underlying optimization geometry unclear.
- A comprehensive understanding of OPD's parameter-space dynamics was lacking, hindering the development of more efficient and robust OPD algorithms.

## Method

- The study systematically characterized OPD's ==parameter-space== trajectory using comparative analysis against SFT and RLVR on Qwen3-family models.
- Four parameter-space diagnostics (update sparsity, principal-angle rotation, normalized spectral shift, update-mask overlap) were used to compare static endpoint geometries.
- Dynamic update trajectories were analyzed over training with metrics like stable rank, Frobenius norm, subspace similarity, and functional sufficiency tests. Control experiments perturbed token density, rollout policy, and objective function composition to identify governing factors.

## Results

- OPD demonstrated an intermediate bf16-aware update sparsity of **51.6%** (unchanged weights), compared to SFT's **8.1%** and RLVR's **77.2%**, alongside intermediate subspace rotation and spectral drift.
- The cumulative updates in OPD rapidly formed a low-dimensional update channel, with an early-emerging rank-16 right singular subspace proving functionally sufficient to preserve performance when subsequent training was constrained to it.
- Mixing the OPD teacher-correction signal with the RLVR reward signal significantly altered the stable-rank dynamics, while token sparsification and off-policy rollouts primarily affected update scale without changing the spectral shape.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - On-Policy Distillation (OPD) establishes a "relaxed off-principal" optimization regime, intermediate between the dense, principal updates of SFT and the sparse, off-principal updates of RLVR.
> - OPD's cumulative updates rapidly converge into a low-dimensional, persistent, and functionally sufficient subspace, a phenomenon termed "subspace locking."
> - The composition of OPD's objective function is the primary control mechanism for this subspace locking, whereas runtime perturbations like token sparsification or off-policy rollouts largely preserve the update geometry.
