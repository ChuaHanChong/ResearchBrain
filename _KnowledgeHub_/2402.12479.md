# In value-based deep reinforcement learning, a pruned network is a good network

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Google DeepMind, Mila, and Université de Montréal demonstrate that gradual magnitude pruning dramatically enhances deep reinforcement learning agent performance, achieving up to 60% improvement on Atari, and enables robust scaling of network architectures by improving learning stability and plasticity.

## Problem

- Deep reinforcement learning networks often struggle with training instabilities, parameter under-utilization, and diminishing returns or degradation as network size increases.
- Existing deep RL networks commonly suffer from pathologies like dormant neurons, loss of plasticity, and a tendency to overfit early experiences.
- Scaling neural network parameters in deep RL does not reliably lead to performance gains, contrasting with 'scaling laws' observed in supervised learning.

## Method

- The authors systematically applied ==gradual magnitude pruning (GMP)==, a technique that progressively removes low-magnitude parameters during training, to various deep RL agents.
- They investigated GMP across diverse value-based agents (==DQN, Rainbow, IQN==), offline RL settings (CQL), and actor-critic methods (SAC, PPO), primarily using an Impala-based ResNet architecture.
- Experiments were conducted on the Arcade Learning Environment, Atari 100k, and MuJoCo, varying ==network widths, sparsity levels==, and other hyperparameters to assess generality and scalability.

## Results

- Gradual magnitude pruning achieved up to a **60%** performance improvement for DQN and **50%** for Rainbow over dense baselines on Atari, consistently using only **5%** of the original network parameters.
- Pruned networks exhibited monotonically increasing performance with increased network width, effectively mitigating the performance degradation typically observed in larger dense RL models.
- The technique consistently outperformed alternative regularization methods, plasticity injection techniques, and other sparse training approaches like RigL across various RL algorithms and regimes.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Gradual magnitude pruning acts as a potent regularization mechanism, reducing Q-value variance, average parameter norms, and the fraction of dormant neurons within the network.
> - Pruning enhances network plasticity by increasing the effective rank of network parameters and significantly reducing gradient correlation and interference during training.
> - The benefits of pruning extend beyond simple compression, actively shaping learning dynamics to enable robust performance scaling with larger initial network architectures.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper addresses a critical and persistent challenge in deep reinforcement learning: the effective utilization and scalability of neural network parameters. Despite the remarkable successes of deep RL in complex domains like Atari games, Go, and StarCraft II, there is a growing body of evidence indicating inherent difficulties in how RL agents effectively use their large neural networks. This contrasts sharply with supervised learning, where increasing model size often leads to predictable performance gains, often encapsulated by "scaling laws." In deep RL, larger networks frequently introduce training instabilities, parameter under-utilization, and diminishing returns or even performance degradation.

Previous research has illuminated several pathologies in deep RL networks:
- **Underparameterization/Redundancy:** Kumar et al. (2021a) showed implicit underparameterization, meaning that despite having many parameters, the networks don't fully leverage them.
- **Dormant Neurons:** Sokar et al. (2023) identified that a significant number of neurons become inactive or "dormant" during training, effectively reducing the network's capacity.
- **Plasticity Loss:** Nikishin et al. (2022) and Sokar et al. (2023) demonstrated that RL networks often lose plasticity—their ability to adapt to new data—over time, hindering continuous learning and performance.
- **Overfitting:** Song et al. (2019) and Zhang et al. (2018) highlighted the tendency of RL networks to overfit, particularly to early experiences (primacy bias).

Against this backdrop, sparse training techniques have emerged as a promising avenue. The general idea is to train or use only a subset of a neural network's parameters, driven by motivations such as computational efficiency, reduced memory footprint, and sometimes, improved generalization. In RL, Graesser et al. (2022) made a key observation: applying *gradual magnitude pruning* (Zhu & Gupta, 2017) to a Deep Q-Network (DQN) with a ResNet backbone (Espeholt et al., 2018) yielded a surprising 50% performance improvement over its dense counterpart, using only 10% of the original parameters. However, the same technique applied to the original CNN architecture showed no such benefit, only maintaining performance.

This paper builds directly on this intriguing observation. Instead of merely demonstrating that sparse networks can *maintain* performance—a common goal in network compression—it investigates whether gradual magnitude pruning can be a *general technique for dramatically improving* the performance of deep RL agents, especially as networks scale. It positions itself as an exploration into the utility of non-standard network topologies (sparse ones) as a core mechanism for enhancing RL performance, rather than just an optimization for deployment.

The work contrasts with other approaches to improve RL scaling and stability:
- **Architectural Changes:** Shifting from CNN to ResNet architectures (Cobbe et al., 2020; Farebrother et al., 2023; Schwarzer et al., 2023) has shown some success in enabling scaling.
- **Regularization Techniques:** Spectral normalization (Bjorck et al., 2021), data augmentation (Yarats et al., 2021), dropout (Gal & Ghahramani, 2016), layer/batch normalization (Ba et al., 2016), and L2 weight regularization (Liu et al., 2020) aim to mitigate overfitting and instability.
- **Plasticity Injection:** Methods like periodic network re-initialization (Nikishin et al., 2022) or recycling dormant neurons (Sokar et al., 2023) directly address plasticity loss.
- **Dynamic Sparse Training:** Other dynamic sparse methods like RigL (Evci et al., 2020), where network topology continually changes, have been explored for efficiency and performance.

This paper differentiates itself by showing that gradual magnitude pruning, despite resulting in a *static* sparse mask after a certain training phase, not only outperforms these alternative regularization and plasticity-enhancing methods but also enables robust performance scaling. This suggests that the benefits of pruning go beyond simple compression or generic regularization, possibly by shaping the learning dynamics in a uniquely advantageous way for RL's non-stationary environment.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from a peculiar observation by Graesser et al. (2022): gradual magnitude pruning, when applied to a DQN agent with an Impala-based ResNet architecture, led to a substantial 50% performance improvement while using only 10% of the original network parameters. Crucially, this benefit was absent when the same pruning technique was applied to the original CNN architecture, where performance was maintained but not improved. This qualitative difference, leading to non-negative results across architectures, suggested that training deep RL agents with induced sparse network topologies could be broadly beneficial and warranted a deeper, more systematic investigation.

Based on this motivation, the authors set forth the following key objectives:

1. **Establish Gradual Magnitude Pruning as a General Technique for Parameter Efficiency:** The main goal is to demonstrate that gradual magnitude pruning is not an isolated trick but a broadly applicable method for maximizing parameter efficiency across different value-based RL agents. This involves testing its efficacy beyond the specific DQN + ResNet setup where it was initially observed.

2. **Demonstrate Scalability and Enhanced Performance with Larger Networks:** A central hypothesis is that the performance gains from pruning are not merely about compression but actively facilitate better learning, especially as the initial network architectures are scaled. The objective is to show that pruned networks yield stronger agents than their dense counterparts and that these improvements *continue* as the base network size increases, directly addressing the notorious difficulty of scaling deep RL networks without performance degradation.

3. **Investigate Across Diverse Agents and Training Regimes:** To affirm the generality of the technique, the authors aim to apply gradual magnitude pruning to a varied set of agents, including different value-based methods (DQN, Rainbow, IQN, M-IQN), online and offline RL settings (e.g., DrQ(ϵ), DER, CQL), and even actor-critic methods (SAC, PPO). This broad scope helps ascertain the technique's robustness.

4. **Conduct In-depth Analyses to Understand the Underlying Benefits:** Beyond empirical performance, a key objective is to delve into *why* gradual magnitude pruning is so effective. This involves analyzing various intrinsic network properties and learning dynamics, such as Q-value variance, parameter norms, dormant neuron counts, effective rank of parameters, and gradient correlation, to uncover the mechanisms behind its advantages.

In essence, the paper aims to move beyond merely reporting an interesting observation to systematically characterizing the utility, generality, and mechanistic explanations for why "a pruned network is a good network" in value-based deep reinforcement learning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research employs a comprehensive experimental methodology to systematically investigate the impact of gradual magnitude pruning on deep reinforcement learning agents.

**Core Technique: Gradual Magnitude Pruning (GMP)**
The central technique explored is gradual magnitude pruning, as proposed by Zhu & Gupta (2017). This method involves progressively removing network parameters with low weight magnitudes over the course of training. The sparsity level increases polynomially from an initial dense state to a final desired sparsity ($s_F$) between a start iteration ($t_{start}$) and an end iteration ($t_{end}$). In most experiments, the pruning schedule adopted from Graesser et al. (2022) sets $t_{start}$ at 20% and $t_{end}$ at 80% of total training steps, with the final sparse network topology remaining fixed for the remainder of training. A target sparsity of 95% was predominantly used after initial explorations identified it as consistently yielding the best performance. The experiments leveraged the JaxPruner library (Lee et al., 2024) for seamless integration with Dopamine.

**Reinforcement Learning Agents and Architectures:**
- **Value-Based Online RL:** The primary agents investigated were DQN (Mnih et al., 2015) and Rainbow (Hessel et al., 2018), utilizing the JAX implementations from the Dopamine library (Castro et al., 2018). More modern value-based agents like IQN (Dabney et al., 2018) and Munchausen-IQN (Vieillard et al., 2020) were also tested to demonstrate broader applicability.
- **Architectures:** The default network architecture was the 15-layer Impala-based ResNet (Espeholt et al., 2018), which is known for better scaling properties compared to the original CNN architecture (Mnih et al., 2015). Experiments were also conducted with the original CNN for comparison.
- **Low Data Regime:** DrQ(ϵ) (Kostrikov et al., 2020; Agarwal et al., 2021) and Data-Efficient Rainbow (DER) (Van Hasselt et al., 2019) were used to assess performance in sample-constrained settings.
- **Offline RL:** Conservative Q-Learning (CQL) (Kumar et al., 2020) and CQL+C51 (Kumar et al., 2022) were employed to evaluate the technique's effectiveness when learning solely from fixed datasets without environment interactions.
- **Actor-Critic Methods:** Soft Actor-Critic (SAC) (Haarnoja et al., 2018) and Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) (Schulman et al., 2017) were included to explore if the benefits extend beyond value-based approaches.

**Experimental Settings and Environments:**
- **Online RL & Low Data:** Primarily evaluated on the Arcade Learning Environment (ALE) (Bellemare et al., 2013). A diverse 15-game subset was initially used, with verification on the full 60 Atari 2600 games and the 26-game Atari 100k benchmark. Most experiments ran for 40 million environment frames (with some extending to 100M or 200M for specific analyses).
- **Offline RL:** Trained on 17 Atari games for 200 million frames iterations using a random 5% sample of data collected by a DQN agent.
- **Actor-Critic:** Evaluated on five continuous control environments from the MuJoCo suite (Todorov et al., 2012).

**Scaling and Hyperparameter Variation:**
- **Network Width:** The impact of scaling network widths (multiplying the number of features in convolutional layers) was systematically investigated (e.g., width scales of 1, 3, 5) to understand how pruning interacts with larger models.
- **Sparsity Levels:** Varying target sparsity levels (e.g., 0.9, 0.95, 0.99) were explored.
- **Replay Ratio:** The replay ratio (gradient updates per environment step) was varied to test how pruned networks handle more frequent updates.
- **Other Hyperparameters:** Sweeps were performed on learning rates, batch sizes, and update horizons to ensure the robustness of the observed benefits.

**Evaluation and Analysis:**
- **Performance Metric:** Human-normalized interquantile mean (IQM) (Agarwal et al., 2021) was the primary metric, aggregated across games and configurations, with 95% stratified bootstrap confidence intervals. Each experiment was run with 5 or 10 independent seeds.
- **Empirical Analyses:** To understand *why* pruning is effective, the authors measured and analyzed:
 - Q-target variance
 - Average norm of network parameters (ParametersNorm)
 - Average norm of Q-values (QNorm)
 - Effective rank of the parameters (Srank, as per Kumar et al., 2021a)
 - Fraction of dormant neurons (as per Sokar et al., 2023)
 - Gradient covariance matrices (Lyle et al., 2023) to assess plasticity and gradient interference.

**Comparative Baselines:**
The efficacy of gradual magnitude pruning was compared against several alternative methods:
- **Dense Networks:** Unpruned baseline models.
- **Lottery Ticket Hypothesis:** Retraining a subnetwork with the final mask but original initialization (Frankle & Carbin, 2018).
- **Dynamic Sparse Training:** RigL (Evci et al., 2020), a method that maintains sparsity throughout training by dynamically adjusting the network topology.
- **Normalization Baselines:** Standard Weight Decay (WD) and L2 regularization (Kumar et al., 2022) to enforce parameter norm constraints.
- **Plasticity Injection Baselines:** Periodic Network Resets (Nikishin et al., 2022) and Recycling Dormant Neurons (ReDo) (Sokar et al., 2023), methods designed to directly combat plasticity loss.

This methodical approach allowed the authors to not only confirm the empirical effectiveness of gradual magnitude pruning across a wide spectrum of RL problems but also to begin unraveling the underlying reasons for its success.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded several significant findings, demonstrating the broad utility and profound impact of gradual magnitude pruning in deep reinforcement learning.

1. **Dramatic Performance Improvements with Parameter Efficiency:**
 - The most striking result is that gradual magnitude pruning consistently led to substantial performance gains. For DQN, pruned networks achieved nearly a **60% performance improvement**, and for Rainbow, a **50% improvement** over their dense, unscaled counterparts on 15 diverse Atari games (Figure 1). This was achieved while using only a small fraction (e.g., 5%) of the original network parameters (95% sparsity).
 - These gains were not restricted to the 15-game subset but were confirmed on the full suite of 60 Atari 2600 games (Figure 6).

2. **Enabled Scaling and Mitigated Performance Degradation:**
 - A critical finding is that while the performance of *unpruned* (dense) architectures often decreased or plateaued with increasing network widths, the performance of *pruned* counterparts **increased monotonically** with width (Figure 1). This directly addresses a long-standing challenge in deep RL, where scaling network size often leads to instability and performance degradation.
 - This suggests pruning not only compresses networks but actively facilitates better learning in larger models.

3. **Generality Across RL Algorithms and Regimes:**
 - **Value-Based Agents:** The benefits of pruning extended beyond DQN and Rainbow to more modern value-based agents like IQN and Munchausen-IQN, showing significant gains across different widths (Appendix G.2).
 - **Low Data Regime:** While no immediate gains were observed in the initial Atari 100k (100k agent steps) setting, when evaluated over 40 million environment interactions, pruned agents (DrQ(ϵ) and DER) demonstrated significant gains. Notably, pruned DrQ(ϵ) agents avoided the performance degradation seen in the baseline when trained for longer (Figure 7).
 - **Offline RL:** Both CQL and CQL+C51 showed substantial improvements with pruned networks, especially with wider architectures. Pruning also helped prevent performance collapse in shallow networks (width scale 1) in the offline regime (Figure 8).
 - **Actor-Critic Methods:** For SAC on MuJoCo, advantages from pruning persisted in some environments (Walker2d-v2, Ant-v2) but were less pronounced or absent in others (HalfCheetah-v2, Hopper-v2, Humanoid-v2). Similar mixed trends were observed for PPO (Figure 9, Appendix E, G.1). The authors hypothesize this might be due to the use of dense layers in MuJoCo experiments versus convolutional layers in Atari.

4. **Enhanced Stability and Robustness:**
 - Pruned networks demonstrated superior stability. Even with a "compressed" pruning schedule (e.g., a 40M schedule applied to 100M training frames), the pruned network maintained its strong performance, suggesting that the final sparse topology is robust and can sustain learning over longer periods (Figure 10).
 - The advantages of pruning remained consistent across various hyperparameter sweeps, including different learning rates, batch sizes, and update horizons (Figure 28, Figure 29, Figure 30). Although adjusting learning rates for wider networks improved dense baselines, pruning still maintained its performance lead.

5. **Insights into the Mechanisms of Effectiveness:**
 - Empirical analyses revealed several key factors contributing to pruning's success (Figure 11):
 - **Reduced Variance:** Pruning effectively reduced the variance of Q-value estimates.
 - **Regularization of Norms:** It decreased the average norm of network parameters and Q-values, acting as a form of regularization.
 - **Reduced Dormant Neurons:** The number of inactive ("dormant") neurons was significantly reduced.
 - **Increased Effective Rank:** Pruning increased the effective rank (Srank) of the network parameters, suggesting more diverse and useful feature representations.
 - **Improved Plasticity and Gradient Dynamics:** Analysis of gradient covariance matrices (Figure 14) showed that pruning induced **weaker gradient correlation and less gradient interference** compared to dense networks. This aligns with recent findings that reduced gradient interference is associated with increased plasticity and better performance (Lyle et al., 2023).

6. **Superiority Over Existing Baselines:**
 - Gradual magnitude pruning consistently **outperformed a range of alternative methods** designed to address scaling, regularization, or plasticity issues (Figure 12):
 - It was more effective than direct regularization methods like Weight Decay and L2 normalization.
 - It surpassed explicit plasticity injection techniques like periodic Network Resets and Recycling Dormant Neurons (ReDo).
 - It yielded better results than applying the Lottery Ticket Hypothesis (retraining with a fixed mask from original initialization) and dynamic sparse training methods like RigL (Figure 13, Appendix G.3).

These findings collectively establish gradual magnitude pruning as a powerful, general, and mechanistically beneficial technique for improving deep RL agent performance, especially in the context of scaling network architectures.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The findings presented in "In value-based deep reinforcement learning, a pruned network is a good network" hold significant implications for the field of deep reinforcement learning, offering both practical benefits and deeper theoretical insights.

#### 5.1. A General and Effective "Drop-in" for Performance Improvement

The most immediate impact is the identification of gradual magnitude pruning as a broadly applicable and highly effective "drop-in" mechanism for enhancing the performance of value-based deep RL agents. Unlike complex algorithmic changes, this technique can be readily integrated into existing training pipelines, offering dramatic performance boosts (up to 60%) without requiring extensive hyperparameter tuning. This simplicity and effectiveness make it an attractive tool for practitioners and researchers alike.

#### 5.2. Unlocking Scalability in Deep RL

A critical contribution of this work is demonstrating that gradual magnitude pruning not only improves performance but also **enables robust scaling of network architectures** in deep RL. Historically, larger networks in RL have often led to training instabilities and performance degradation, in stark contrast to supervised learning's "scaling laws." This paper shows that pruned networks continue to improve performance as their initial width increases, effectively overcoming this barrier. This breakthrough is crucial for developing RL agents capable of tackling increasingly complex, real-world tasks that necessitate highly expressive models.

#### 5.3. Deeper Understanding of RL Network Pathologies

The empirical analyses provide valuable insights into *why* gradual magnitude pruning is so effective. By showing that pruning reduces Q-target variance, parameter norms, and dormant neurons, while increasing effective rank and reducing gradient interference, the paper sheds light on the internal dynamics of deep RL networks. These insights contribute to a better understanding of the pathologies (e.g., plasticity loss, parameter under-utilization) that plague current RL training methods, pointing towards improved stability and sustained learning capacity. The observation of reduced gradient correlation is particularly significant, linking pruning's benefits to enhanced network plasticity.

#### 5.4. Challenging Conventional Wisdom on Sparsity

This work challenges the prevailing view that network pruning is primarily a post-training compression technique for deployment or, at best, a way to maintain performance with fewer parameters. Instead, it positions sparsity, when introduced gradually during training, as a powerful regularization and performance-enhancing mechanism that can actively improve the learning process itself. This shift in perspective could encourage a broader exploration of sparse network topologies as a fundamental design choice in RL, rather than just an afterthought.

#### 5.5. Future Research Directions

The findings open up several promising avenues for future research:
- **Integration with Advanced RL Agents:** Incorporating gradual magnitude pruning into state-of-the-art agents designed for multi-task generalization, sample efficiency, and broader generalizability (e.g., Taiga et al., 2023; Schwarzer et al., 2023; Hafner et al., 2023) could yield further breakthroughs.
- **Implications for Transfer Learning and Fine-tuning:** The observed stability and sustained performance of pruned networks might have significant implications for methods reliant on fine-tuning or "reincarnation" (Agarwal et al., 2022), where pre-trained knowledge is adapted to new tasks.
- **Hardware Acceleration and Edge Deployment:** As hardware accelerators specifically optimized for sparse network computations become more prevalent, the efficiency benefits of pruned networks will translate directly into faster training times. Furthermore, the reduced parameter count makes these agents highly appealing for deployment on resource-constrained edge devices.
- **Exploration of Non-Standard Topologies:** The success of gradual magnitude pruning serves as an invitation to extensively explore other non-standard network architectures and topologies. Since RL operates in non-stationary environments, networks designed primarily for stationary supervised learning problems may not be optimal. This work suggests that tailoring network structure could be a potent, yet under-explored, design lever.

In conclusion, "In value-based deep reinforcement learning, a pruned network is a good network" makes a compelling case for the transformative potential of gradual magnitude pruning. It not only offers a highly practical tool for improving RL performance and scalability but also deepens our understanding of the intricate learning dynamics within neural networks, setting a new direction for future research in network architecture and efficiency for reinforcement learning.
