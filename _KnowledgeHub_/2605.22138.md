# Efficient Agentic Reasoning Through Self-Regulated Simulative Planning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from the Institute of Foundation Models (IFM) and Carnegie Mellon University developed SR²AM, an AI agent architecture that decomposes decision-making into self-regulation, simulative planning, and reactive execution. This approach enabled SR²AM-v1.0-30B to achieve an overall Pass@1 score of 71.3, competitive with much larger models, while consuming 25.8–95.3% fewer reasoning tokens than other 30-32B agentic LLMs.

## Problem

- Current agentic Large Language Models (LLMs) often use unconstrained chain-of-thought, leading to inefficient token consumption without reliable accuracy gains.
- The lack of explicit mechanisms to control the presence, horizon, or structure of planning within these systems results in undifferentiated reasoning.
- Existing methods, whether unregulated or partially regulated, fail to integrate self-regulation and grounded simulative planning into a unified, efficient architecture.

## Method

- Proposed a three-system decomposition for agentic decision-making: Reactive Execution (System I), Simulative Reasoning (System II), and Self-Regulation (System III).
- Implemented SR²AM (Self-Regulated Simulative Reasoning Agentic LLM), where the LLM acts as the ==world model== and the configurator (System III) and simulative planner (System II) are explicit stages in the ==chain-of-thought==.
- Trained SR²AM using supervised finetuning (SFT) based on plan reconstruction from existing LLM traces, followed by refinement through ==reinforcement learning== (RL) to optimize task success and inter-system coordination.

## Results

- SR²AM-v1.0-30B achieved an overall Pass@1 score of 71.3 across 11 benchmarks, performing competitively with systems up to 1.0T parameters while significantly outperforming other 30-32B agentic LLMs.
- The approach dramatically improved reasoning efficiency, with SR²AM-v1.0-30B consuming 25.8–**95.3%** fewer reasoning tokens than comparable 30-32B baselines for similar or better accuracy.
- Ablation studies confirmed the critical and complementary roles of all three proposed systems, showing that RL refinement leads to agents learning to engage in longer-horizon, deeper planning rather than just increasing planning frequency.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decomposing agentic reasoning into distinct, self-regulated planning, simulative reasoning, and reactive execution systems provides superior accuracy-efficiency tradeoffs.
> - A learned 'configurator' (System III) can dynamically decide when and how deeply to engage in planning, avoiding the inefficiency of constant deliberation.
> - Grounded deliberation in future-state prediction via a world model offers a unified and explicit planning structure applicable across diverse tasks without domain-specific engineering.
