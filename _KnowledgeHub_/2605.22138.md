# Efficient Agentic Reasoning Through Self-Regulated Simulative Planning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from the Institute of Foundation Models (IFM) and Carnegie Mellon University developed SR²AM, an AI agent architecture that decomposes decision-making into self-regulation, simulative planning, and reactive execution. This approach enabled SR²AM-v1.0-30B to achieve an overall Pass@1 score of 71.3, competitive with much larger models, while consuming 25.8–95.3% fewer reasoning tokens than other 30-32B agentic LLMs.

## Problem

- Current agentic Large Language Models (LLMs) often use unconstrained chain-of-thought, leading to inefficient token consumption without reliable accuracy gains.
- The lack of explicit mechanisms to control the presence, horizon, or structure of planning within these systems results in undifferentiated reasoning.
- Existing methods, whether unregulated or partially regulated, fail to integrate self-regulation and grounded simulative planning into a unified, efficient architecture.

## Method

- Proposed a three-system decomposition for agentic decision-making: Reactive Execution (System I), Simulative Reasoning (System II), and Self-Regulation (System III).
- Implemented SR²AM (Self-Regulated Simulative Reasoning Agentic LLM), where the LLM acts as the ==world model== and the configurator (System III) and simulative planner (System II) are explicit stages in the ==chain-of-thought==.
- Trained SR²AM using supervised finetuning (SFT) based on plan reconstruction from existing LLM traces, followed by refinement through ==reinforcement learning== (RL) to optimize task success and inter-system coordination.

## Results

- SR²AM-v1.0-30B achieved an overall Pass@1 score of 71.3 across 11 benchmarks, performing competitively with systems up to 1.0T parameters while significantly outperforming other 30-32B agentic LLMs.
- The approach dramatically improved reasoning efficiency, with SR²AM-v1.0-30B consuming 25.8–**95.3%** fewer reasoning tokens than comparable 30-32B baselines for similar or better accuracy.
- Ablation studies confirmed the critical and complementary roles of all three proposed systems, showing that RL refinement leads to agents learning to engage in longer-horizon, deeper planning rather than just increasing planning frequency.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decomposing agentic reasoning into distinct, self-regulated planning, simulative reasoning, and reactive execution systems provides superior accuracy-efficiency tradeoffs.
> - A learned 'configurator' (System III) can dynamically decide when and how deeply to engage in planning, avoiding the inefficiency of constant deliberation.
> - Grounded deliberation in future-state prediction via a world model offers a unified and explicit planning structure applicable across diverse tasks without domain-specific engineering.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of artificial intelligence agents capable of long-horizon planning and goal-oriented behavior is a significant area of research. A dominant approach in recent embodied and language-based systems involves treating the agent as a reactive policy with adaptive computation, such as chain-of-thought reasoning in large language models (LLMs). These systems are typically trained end-to-end, with the expectation that planning capabilities will emerge implicitly from sufficient data and computational resources. This approach is evident in current agentic LLMs that utilize unconstrained chain-of-thought, sometimes refined with reinforcement learning (RL), to solve complex problems in various domains like web browsing, software engineering, and scientific reasoning.

However, the paper identifies a fundamental limitation of this dominant approach: the lack of explicit mechanisms to control the presence, horizon, or structure of planning within the undifferentiated chain-of-thought. This often leads to inefficient token consumption during training, where increased reasoning length does not reliably translate to improved accuracy. Furthermore, this approach offers no explicit planning structure that can be independently analyzed, regulated, or improved.

Prior research has addressed aspects of this problem but has not integrated all components into a unified architecture. Efforts have focused on controlling the amount of reasoning (e.g., effort-adaptive approaches), selecting execution modes at the initiation of a task (e.g., mode routing), distilling rule-based workflows, or using world models for obligatory simulation. This paper positions itself by proposing a more comprehensive decomposition of agentic decision-making that integrates self-regulation, simulative planning, and reactive execution, aiming to overcome the limitations of existing methods that are either unregulated, partially regulated, or obligatorily simulative.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central argument of this research is that efficient agentic reasoning can be achieved through a decomposition of decision-making into three interacting systems, moving beyond the prevalent paradigm of unconstrained, reactive policies. This decomposition is motivated by observed inefficiencies in current agentic LLMs, where increased computational expenditure for reasoning does not consistently correlate with performance gains. The paper argues for the distinct roles of:

1. **Reactive Execution (System I):** This system is responsible for fine-grained reasoning and direct action, handling immediate responses and executing plans. It captures execution patterns that may be difficult to encode in structured plans and allows for rapid responses when extensive deliberation is not required.
2. **Simulative Reasoning (System II):** This system grounds deliberation in future-state prediction by utilizing a world model. Unlike unconstrained chain-of-thought, which implicitly hopes for planning to emerge, simulative reasoning provides a unified and explicit planning structure. It operates by proposing candidate actions, predicting their consequences through the world model, and selecting action sequences that maximize expected long-term progress. This mechanism is intended to be general-purpose and applicable across diverse tasks without requiring domain-specific engineering. The paper suggests that augmenting any baseline policy with an accurate world model for simulative reasoning can improve behavior.
3. **Self-Regulation (System III):** This system, implemented through a learned "configurator," determines when and how deeply the agent should engage in planning at each decision point. Inspired by human cognitive processes that modulate deliberation based on factors like urgency and complexity, the configurator aims to avoid the inefficiency of unregulated, always-on deliberation and the rigidity of fixed planning cycles. It decides whether to initiate a new plan, continue an existing one, or bypass planning entirely to act directly.

The key objective of this work is to empirically investigate whether this tripartite System I+II+III decomposition can yield superior accuracy-efficiency tradeoffs compared to existing unregulated or partially regulated agentic reasoning paradigms, particularly in the context of language-based interactive reasoning. By formalizing and instantiating this decomposition, the researchers aim to demonstrate that explicit control over planning processes and grounding deliberation in future-state prediction can lead to more effective and resource-conscious AI agents.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The researchers developed SR²AM (Self-Regulated Simulative Reasoning Agentic LLM) to instantiate their proposed three-system decomposition. In SR²AM, a Large Language Model (LLM) itself serves as the world model within the language space. The configurator (System III) and the simulative planner (System II) are implemented as distinct, explicit stages within the LLM’s chain-of-thought reasoning. These stages operate in conjunction with the LLM’s capacity for free-form reasoning and acting (System I).

**Environment and Tools:**
The agent operates in interactive reasoning environments encompassing mathematical problem-solving, scientific reasoning, tabular data analysis, and web information seeking. It is equipped with three tools:
- `web_search`: A search engine for querying web-scale information.
- `visit_tool`: A web browser that crawls and summarizes page content given a goal.
- `python_repl_tool`: A stateless Python sandbox for computation and data processing.
The agent interacts with these tools over multiple turns, forming a belief state from observations and selecting actions.

**Supervised Data Construction:**
The training process begins with supervised finetuning (SFT) on data that encodes self-regulated simulative reasoning. Two distinct approaches were developed for collecting this supervised data:

1. **Approach 1: Multi-Module Inference (v0.1):** This approach demonstrates feasibility by implementing the configurator (System III) and simulative planner (System II) as separate prompted LLMs. Additional LLM-based modules (e.g., for user intent interpretation, progress summarization, plan reflection) are integrated. The configurator can invoke these modules before deciding on the next action. Trajectories are constructed by interleaving the configurator’s thoughts with the outputs of invoked modules, then filtered for answer correctness and minimum reasoning complexity. For main experiments, `o4-mini` was used.

2. **Approach 2: Plan Reconstruction (v1.0):** This is the primary approach, designed for better scalability. It leverages chain-of-thought traces from a pretrained reasoning LLM (DeepSeek-V3.2). An annotator LLM (`𝜓`) is instructed to reconstruct configurator decisions (`𝑢_t`) (i.e., whether planning is necessary) and structured plan content (`𝑐_t`) from these traces. A plan `𝑐_t` includes a summary of relevant conditions (`̂𝑠_c_t`), proposed actions (`𝑎'_t`), and predicted future states (`̂𝑠_c_t+1`). This allows the LLM to jointly infer the current state, propose actions, and predict consequences within a single generation pass, implicitly serving as encoder, policy, and world model. Annotated plans are appended to the original model thoughts, preserving free-form reasoning while augmenting it with structured, self-regulated planning. For web-browsing tasks, plans are truncated to a maximum of two steps due to high environmental uncertainty.

**Reinforcement-Learning-Based Refinement:**
Following SFT, models are further refined using reinforcement learning (RL) to optimize for task success and coordinate the three systems.
- **Reward Function:** A piecewise function combines three binary signals:
 - `r_answer`: Measures answer correctness using an LLM judge.
 - `r_struct`: Assesses format compliance across the trajectory.
 - `r_final`: Checks for final-answer extractability.
 This function prioritizes answer correctness while providing a gradient signal for structural compliance in all trajectories.
- **Optimization:** An adapted version of Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) with asymmetric clipping is used. Trajectories are sampled in groups, and group-normalized advantages are computed. For larger models (30B+), truncated trajectories are filtered to mitigate format collapse.

**Training Data and Base Models:**
The training dataset is composed of open-source math, science, tabular, and web reasoning benchmarks. Difficulty-based filtering is applied to the RL datasets to ensure informative gradient signals. SR²AM-v0.1-8B is trained from Qwen3-8B, while SR²AM-v1.0-30B is trained from Qwen3-30B-A3B-Thinking-2507.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of SR²AM involved comparing its performance against various baselines on 11 benchmarks across four categories (math, science, tabular analysis, web information seeking), focusing on overall Pass@1 accuracy and average reasoning tokens per trajectory.

**Task Performance:**
- **SR²AM-v0.1-8B:** Achieved an overall Pass@1 score of 57.0. This performance surpassed other systems of comparable parameter scale (7-8B) and was competitive with unregulated agentic LLMs ranging from 30-32B and pretrained LLMs with tools at 120-355B parameters.
- **SR²AM-v1.0-30B:** Achieved an overall Pass@1 score of 71.3. This places it competitively with larger systems such as DeepSeek-V3.2 (685B, 73.2 Pass@1) and Kimi-K2.5 (1.0T, 70.9 Pass@1) when utilizing the same tool harness. It also exceeded GPT-5.4-xhigh (68.4 Pass@1) as a text-only reasoning LLM and approached its performance with tool integration (78.3 Pass@1). Among other 30-32B agentic LLMs, SR²AM-v1.0-30B outperformed a range of baselines, including both unregulated and partially-regulated approaches, and demonstrated competitive accuracy with MiroThinker-v1.5-30B (74.2 Pass@1).

**Reasoning Efficiency:**
- **SR²AM-v0.1-8B:** Consumed an average of 3,698 reasoning tokens per trajectory. This is fewer or comparable to most systems at the 7-8B scale (which ranged from 601–11,206 tokens), while simultaneously achieving higher Pass@1 scores.
- **SR²AM-v1.0-30B:** Consumed an average of 5,518 reasoning tokens per trajectory. This represents a significant reduction, using 25.8–95.3% fewer reasoning tokens compared to competitive 30-32B agentic LLMs, while maintaining or achieving better accuracy. Specifically, it used 51.2% fewer tokens than MiroThinker-v1.5-30B (5,518 vs. 11,295 tokens) for competitive Pass@1 performance. These results suggest that self-regulated simulative planning can achieve strong task success while controlling reasoning length effectively.

**Analysis of Self-Regulated Simulative Reasoning:**
- **Component Ablation (SR²AM-v1.0-30B):** Ablation studies confirmed the individual contributions of each system. Removing free-form reasoning (System I) led to the largest accuracy drop (from 66.6 to 46.8 Pass@1), indicating the complementary role of structured plans and fine-grained reasoning. Disabling selective planning (System III) resulted in the highest increase in token consumption (from 4,925 to 5,451), confirming the configurator's role in efficiency. Removing simulative planning structure (System II) or plan horizon control (System III) also reduced accuracy, supporting the value of grounded state prediction and uncertainty-aware planning. The full decomposition (SFT) showed a modest improvement over direct SFT on teacher Chain-of-Thought (66.6 vs. 65.3 Pass@1), providing a more effective foundation for RL.
- **RL Refinement of Planning Horizon:** RL training of SR²AM-v1.0-30B led to a clear shift towards longer-horizon plans. The percentage of turns with 2-step or 3+-step plans increased (from 5.3% to 14.9%), while the frequency of unplanned turns remained stable (from 15.6% to 13.6%). This indicates that RL incentivizes deeper planning when planning is invoked, rather than simply increasing planning frequency. This trend was consistent across all task categories, with the largest increase in science (32.7%) and the smallest in web tasks (20.9%), aligning with the inherent uncertainty in web environments.
- **Persistence of Self-Regulated Simulative Reasoning Advantage:** A comparison of SR²AM-v0.1-8B (self-regulated) against a Qwen3-8B base model (unregulated deliberation) during RL training revealed that unregulated deliberation led to significant increases in reasoning tokens without proportional accuracy gains, and experienced rising context overflow. In contrast, SR²AM-v0.1-8B consistently used fewer reasoning tokens and achieved higher accuracy with minimal context overflow, demonstrating that the I+II+III decomposition amplifies benefits through RL by channeling improvement into planning quality rather than reasoning volume.
- **Disentangling Structure from Supervised Initialization:** By comparing SFT data derived from the multi-module inference pipeline (v0.1) with data encoding unregulated reasoning from the same teacher LLM (`o4-mini`), the structured I+II+III approach consistently outperformed unregulated reasoning across varying data scales. This suggests that the value of the structured decomposition itself, rather than solely the quality of the teacher LLM, contributes to improved performance, and this benefit scales with both data quantity and teacher quality.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research presented in this paper carries several significant implications for the development of advanced AI agents.

Firstly, the empirical validation of the proposed System I+II+III decomposition for agentic reasoning suggests a viable alternative to the prevalent approach of relying on implicitly emerging planning within undifferentiated chain-of-thought. The demonstrated capability of SR²AM to achieve competitive accuracy while consuming substantially fewer reasoning tokens indicates that explicit architectural design for self-regulation and simulative planning can lead to more efficient and resource-aware agents. This efficiency gain addresses a critical limitation of current agentic models, where increasing reasoning length often does not reliably translate into performance improvements and can lead to context window overflow.

Secondly, the ablation studies reinforce the distinct and complementary roles of reactive execution (System I), simulative planning (System II), and self-regulation (System III). The finding that removing any of these components degrades performance or efficiency supports the architectural rationale. The observation that reinforcement learning refines planning behavior by increasing the average planning horizon rather than merely the frequency of planning suggests that agents can learn to engage in deeper, more anticipatory deliberation when appropriate, rather than simply expanding the volume of reasoning. This qualitative shift in planning behavior represents an advancement in how agents can adapt their cognitive processes.

Thirdly, the development of a unified simulative planning framework that performs strongly across diverse task categories (math, science, tabular analysis, and web information seeking) without requiring per-domain engineering highlights its potential as a general-purpose planning mechanism. This generality contrasts with domain-specific or rule-based approaches, offering a more flexible foundation for building intelligent agents.

Finally, the concept of the "configurator" as a learned mechanism for autonomous regulation of reasoning processes has broader implications. While demonstrated here for inference-time planning, the principle of an agent deciding when and how deeply to engage a cognitive process could extend to other forms of internal agency. This includes governing its own learning, adapting its internal models, revising long-term goals, or engaging in mental simulation. Such an extension moves towards agents with a more sophisticated capacity for self-organization and adaptability, potentially leading to more robust and autonomous AI systems that emulate aspects of natural intelligence. Future work involving embodied or multi-agent settings, integrating multimodal world models, and further refining context management techniques will be important next steps in realizing this potential.
