# FoundationMotion: Auto-Labeling and Reasoning about Spatial Movement in Videos

> [!summary] Summary
> FoundationMotion introduces an automated pipeline to generate a large-scale dataset of 500K question-answering pairs and captions for fine-grained object motion and spatial reasoning in videos. This dataset enables Vision-Language Models to achieve substantial performance improvements on "how" motion benchmarks, outperforming larger generalist models in specific spatial understanding tasks.

## Problem

- Current Vision-Language Models (VLMs) lack fine-grained understanding of object motion and complex spatial reasoning, particularly for "how" questions about movements and interactions.
- There is a critical scarcity of large-scale, high-granularity datasets for object trajectories, interactions, and detailed motion descriptions due to the prohibitive cost and time of manual annotation.
- This data limitation hinders the effective deployment of VLMs in embodied AI applications like robotics and autonomous driving, which require precise understanding of spatial dynamics for safe operation.

## Method

- Introduces **FoundationMotion**, a fully automated and unified data curation pipeline for constructing large-scale, fine-grained motion datasets.
- The pipeline processes videos by filtering camera motion and performing multi-object detection and robust temporal tracking for general objects, persons, and detailed hand motions using specialized vision models.
- Leverages GPT-4o-mini to transform structured tracking outputs into natural language captions covering seven dimensions of motion, then generates multi-choice question-answer pairs targeting five specific motion understanding skills.

## Results

- The automated pipeline generated approximately 500K question-answering pairs and captions from 46.7K videos, forming the FoundationMotion Dataset, an unprecedented scale for fine-grained motion data.
- Four new zero-shot "how" motion benchmarks (AV-Car, AV-Hand, Daily, Robotics) were manually curated to evaluate models' zero-shot generalizability to out-of-distribution videos, specifically targeting complex spatial reasoning.
- Models fine-tuned with the FoundationMotion Dataset achieved substantial performance improvements across various benchmarks (e.g., NVILA-Video-15B improved by +14.9% on Robotics) and often surpassed larger generalist models like Gemini-2.5-Flash on these targeted motion understanding tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - An automated pipeline effectively generates large-scale, high-quality datasets for fine-grained object motion and spatial reasoning, overcoming the limitations of costly manual annotation.
> - Providing structured bounding box trajectories as JSONs alongside video frames to Large Language Models is crucial for generating accurate and detailed motion captions and high-quality QA pairs.
> - Different types of "how" motion questions (e.g., motion recognition, action order, repetition count) provide complementary benefits and collectively enhance VLM understanding, targeting distinct error modes.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) has made remarkable strides in recent years, enabling machines to understand and interpret visual information in conjunction with natural language. State-of-the-art models like Gemini and Qwen-VL demonstrate impressive capabilities in identifying objects, scenes, and events in videos. However, this paper identifies a critical gap: current VLMs still struggle with **fine-grained motion understanding** and **spatial reasoning**, particularly in answering "how" questions about object movements and interactions. While they excel at "what" (e.g., "a person is pouring water"), they falter at "how" (e.g., "pouring water from a bottle into a glass" or "the car is turning right").

This limitation is a significant barrier for deploying VLMs in **embodied AI applications** such as robotics and autonomous driving, where precise understanding of spatial dynamics and object motion is paramount for safe and effective operation. The authors attribute this struggle primarily to the **scarcity of large-scale, fine-grained motion datasets**. Existing datasets are typically constructed through costly and time-consuming manual annotation, which limits their scalability and granularity. Manually labeling object trajectories, interactions, and detailed motion descriptions for millions of video frames is simply infeasible.

FoundationMotion directly addresses this critical data scarcity problem by introducing a **fully automated data curation pipeline**. This innovative approach positions it within the broader trend of automated dataset construction, building upon prior work such as VideoEspresso (LLM-generated VideoQA), CinePile (QA for long movies), VoCap (object-centric captioning), and UltraVideo (motion-based clip filtering). FoundationMotion distinguishes itself by specifically focusing on *fine-grained object motions* and *spatial relations*, leveraging multi-object tracking to generate detailed captions and QA pairs about trajectories, a granularity largely unaddressed by previous automated methods.

Furthermore, the paper situates itself in the context of existing motion-focused video understanding benchmarks. **MotionBench** and **FAVOR-Bench** have highlighted the deficiency of current VLMs in motion reasoning, showing scores below human levels. While these benchmarks focus on "what moves, when, and how detailed," FoundationMotion aims to fill the gap by enabling models to capture *spatial relations* and by providing the *large-scale training data* necessary to improve performance on such benchmarks. By creating both a dataset and a set of "how" motion benchmarks, FoundationMotion seeks to establish a new standard for the field, fostering the development of VLMs that can truly reason about the physical world.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of FoundationMotion is to **enable machines to effectively describe and reason about object motion and spatial relationships** in videos, thereby bridging the gap between current VLM capabilities and human-level physical reasoning. This overarching goal is driven by several key motivations:

1. **Addressing the Data Scarcity Problem:** The most significant motivation is the critical lack of large-scale, fine-grained motion datasets. Manual annotation of detailed object trajectories and spatial interactions is prohibitively expensive and time-consuming, severely limiting the scalability and granularity of existing motion datasets. FoundationMotion aims to overcome this by introducing a fully automated data curation pipeline.

2. **Enhancing Motion and Spatial Understanding in VLMs:** While state-of-the-art VLMs excel at recognizing objects and general events, they struggle with the nuances of "how" motion occurs (e.g., direction, velocity, trajectory, specific interactions) and complex spatial reasoning. The project seeks to create a dataset that specifically targets these fine-grained aspects, allowing VLMs to learn more robust physical reasoning capabilities.

3. **Improving Embodied AI Applications:** A core motivation is the urgent need for enhanced motion understanding in real-world embodied applications like robotics and autonomous driving. These domains require machines not only to identify objects but also to precisely understand their movements, interactions, and spatial relationships to operate safely and effectively. FoundationMotion aims to provide the foundational data for building VLMs capable of meeting these stringent requirements.

4. **Establishing a Standard for Motion Understanding:** Beyond data generation, the project aims to raise awareness about the importance of motion understanding, establish a standard for the field, and foster community development by releasing all code, data, and benchmarks. This includes creating new, challenging "how" motion benchmarks to rigorously evaluate model progress.

In summary, FoundationMotion is motivated by the conviction that spatial thinking is fundamental to intelligence, and that enabling machines to master fine-grained motion and spatial reasoning is crucial for the next generation of intelligent systems, particularly those interacting with the physical world.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

FoundationMotion introduces a fully automated and unified data curation pipeline designed to construct large-scale motion datasets. The pipeline consists of four main stages:

1. **Video Preprocessing:**
- **Temporal Cropping:** Raw input videos are processed by extracting short, motion-rich segments of 5-10 seconds. For longer videos, a segment is sampled around the midpoint with some temporal variation to capture diverse moments. This strategy manages computational costs while focusing on informative clips.
- **Camera Motion Filtering:** To ensure clear spatial relations and accurate motion descriptions, videos with significant camera motion are detected and filtered out. This is achieved using VGGT (Wang et al., 2025), which analyzes camera poses across frames to compute a motion score. Videos exceeding a predefined threshold (τ_motion = 0.3) are excluded.

2. **Object Detection and Tracking:** This stage precisely identifies and tracks moving objects within the preprocessed video segments.
- **Open-Vocabulary Object Detection:** The Qwen2.5-VL-7B model (Bai et al., 2025) is used to identify salient object categories in the first frame. Subsequently, Grounded-DINO (Liu et al., 2023) localizes these objects, querying individual object classes to ensure accurate bounding box detection with semantic labels.
- **Human-Centric Detection:** A specialized hierarchical pipeline is employed for human motion. Cascade Mask R-CNN with a ViTDet-H backbone (Li et al., 2022) detects persons. ViTPose+ (Xu et al., 2022) extracts whole-body keypoints, including hand regions. Hands23 (Cheng et al., 2023) then performs detailed hand detection, including identifying hand side (left/right) and contact states (e.g., object contact), along with any held objects. IoU matching associates hands with persons.
- **Temporal Tracking:** SAM2 (Ravi et al., 2024) propagates the detected bounding boxes and segmentation masks across video frames. A hierarchical ID scheme assigns unique identifiers: persons (0-99), their body parts (e.g., ID×10+1 for left hand), and general objects (starting from 1000). This ensures consistent tracking and maintains semantic relationships. Tracking is refined by incorporating new hand and hand-object detections at keyframes (every 5th frame) to prevent drift.

3. **Caption Generation:**
- GPT-4o-mini (Hurst et al., 2024) is employed to transform the structured tracking outputs into natural language captions.
- Inputs to GPT-4o-mini include sampled video frames (2 fps), JSON files containing normalized bounding box trajectories, and visual overlays with color-coded bounding boxes. The structured JSON data is crucial for providing explicit spatial and temporal information.
- The LLM is prompted to generate captions covering seven dimensions of motion: action/gesture recognition, temporal sequencing, object–action associations, spatial context, repetition patterns, motion dynamics (direction, distance, velocity, trajectory), and evolving spatial relationships. This detailed prompting ensures comprehensive and consistent captions.

4. **Question-Answer Generation:**
- GPT-4o-mini is again used to create multi-choice question–answer (QA) pairs based on the generated captions and video frames.
- The questions target five specific skills: (1) motion recognition, (2) temporal ordering, (3) action–object association, (4) location-based motion, and (5) repetition counting.
- Each question comes with four options, with distractors drawn from video content, and correct answers are randomly distributed across options to avoid bias.
- This dual use of LLMs (first for descriptive captions, then for analytical QA pairs) ensures that the dataset not only describes motion but also provides valuable evaluative content.

This multi-stage, multi-model approach leverages the strengths of specialized vision models for robust detection and tracking, combined with the advanced reasoning and natural language generation capabilities of Large Language Models, to automate the creation of a vast, fine-grained motion understanding dataset.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

FoundationMotion successfully constructs a large-scale, fine-grained motion dataset and demonstrates its effectiveness in significantly improving the motion understanding capabilities of Vision-Language Models.

1. **FoundationMotion Dataset Creation:** The automated pipeline generated approximately **500K question-answering (QA) pairs and captions** from 46.7K videos (approximately 467K video-QA pairs), which collectively form the FoundationMotion Dataset. This scale is unprecedented for fine-grained motion data. The dataset statistics reveal a high annotation density (1.671 questions/second) over concise, motion-rich video clips (mostly 3-7 seconds), with balanced answer distributions and informative question lengths.

2. **Creation of "How" Motion Benchmarks:** To address the lack of targeted evaluation for fine-grained motion, the authors manually curated four new zero-shot benchmarks:
- **AV-Car:** Focuses on car motion in autonomous driving scenarios (1,968 QA pairs from Nuscenes).
- **AV-Hand:** Focuses on hand motion in autonomous driving contexts (108 QA pairs from Nuscenes).
- **Daily:** Evaluates hand motions and hand-object interactions in human daily activities (832 QA pairs from 100 Days of Hands).
- **Robotics:** Assesses robot motion, primarily hands, during manipulation tasks (102 QA pairs from YouTube).
 These benchmarks are designed to test zero-shot generalizability to out-of-distribution videos, ensuring no overlap with the training data.

3. **Significant Performance Improvements from Fine-tuning:** Models fine-tuned with the FoundationMotion Dataset consistently achieved substantial gains across various motion understanding benchmarks:
- **NVILA-Video-15B:** Saw improvements of +1.0% on MotionBench, +7.1% on AV-Car, and a remarkable +14.9% on Robotics, with smaller but consistent gains on VLM4D (+0.1%), AV-Hand (+0.6%), and Daily (+2.4%).
- **NVILA-Video-8B:** Gained +0.6% on MotionBench, +1.7% on AV-Car, +6.8% on AV-Hand, +2.0% on Daily, and +17.8% on Robotics.
- **Qwen2.5-VL-7B:** Showed broad improvements: +2.1% on MotionBench, +3.2% on VLM4D, +1.8% on AV-Car, +5.6% on AV-Hand, +11.7% on Daily, and +4.2% on Robotics.

4. **Outperforming Stronger Baselines:** A particularly notable finding is that models fine-tuned with FoundationMotion data, even smaller open-source models, could **outperform larger open-source models and strong closed-source baselines**:
- NVILA-Video-15B + FoundationMotion achieved 91.5% on AV-Car, surpassing Gemini-2.5-Flash (84.1%) and Qwen-2.5-VL-72B (83.3%).
- The same model also exceeded Qwen-72B on VLM4D (51.9% vs. 50.5%) and AV-Hand (58.7% vs. 56.5%).
 These results demonstrate that targeted, high-quality motion data can enable smaller models to achieve superior performance in specific domains compared to much larger, more generalist models.

5. **Ablation Studies Confirming Pipeline Effectiveness:**
- **Bounding Box JSONs Improve QA Quality:** An ablation study showed that providing LLMs with structured bounding box JSONs alongside video frames (as opposed to video-only input) significantly improved QA quality. This led to substantial gains in fine-grained action accuracy (+2.6), motion detail and specificity (+2.6), and temporal coherence (+2.4), highlighting the critical role of explicit spatial signals.
- **Complementary Benefits of Different QA Types:** The five distinct question types (Motion Recognition, Action Order, Motion-related Objects, Location-related Motion, Repetition Count) provided complementary benefits. Fine-tuning with each type individually improved performance over the baseline, with "Repetition Count" yielding the largest single gain (+14.6%). Combining all types achieved the best overall performance, indicating that they target distinct error modes and collectively enhance understanding.

These findings collectively underscore the high quality and effectiveness of the FoundationMotion dataset and its automated curation pipeline in advancing the state of the art in fine-grained video motion understanding.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The FoundationMotion project carries significant implications and potential impact across several domains of AI and computer vision:

1. **Scalable Data Generation for Fine-Grained Motion:** The most immediate and profound impact is providing a fully automated, scalable solution for generating large-scale, high-quality datasets for fine-grained object motion and spatial reasoning. This addresses a critical bottleneck in VLM development, which has historically been hampered by the prohibitive cost and time of manual annotation for such complex data. By overcoming this, FoundationMotion paves the way for rapid advancements in motion understanding.

2. **Bridging the "What" vs. "How" Gap in VLMs:** FoundationMotion directly tackles the limitation of current VLMs that struggle with the "how" of motion. By providing training data rich in specific motion dynamics, trajectories, and spatial relationships, it enables models to develop a deeper, more human-like understanding of physical interactions in videos. This enhanced capability is crucial for moving beyond basic object and event recognition.

3. **Advancing Embodied AI and Robotics:** The improved motion understanding facilitated by FoundationMotion has a direct and substantial impact on embodied AI applications. For autonomous driving, it means vehicles can better perceive and predict the movements of other cars, pedestrians, and cyclists, leading to safer navigation. In robotics, a more granular understanding of dexterous hand movements and object manipulation can significantly enhance robot learning, control, and human-robot interaction.

4. **Democratizing High-Performance Motion Understanding:** The demonstration that mid-sized open-source models, when fine-tuned with FoundationMotion data, can surpass much larger closed-source and open-source models (like Gemini-2.5-Flash and Qwen2.5-VL-72B) is highly significant. This suggests that access to high-quality, domain-specific data can be more impactful than simply scaling model size, potentially democratizing access to state-of-the-art motion understanding capabilities for a wider research community.

5. **Fostering Community Development:** The authors' commitment to releasing all code, data, and benchmarks is a powerful catalyst for future research. This open-science approach will allow other researchers to build upon FoundationMotion, validate its findings, and accelerate innovation in motion understanding, potentially leading to new models, methodologies, and applications. The new "how" motion benchmarks provide standardized evaluation tools for tracking progress in this challenging area.

**Limitations and Future Work:** The paper acknowledges that its current spatial understanding is primarily limited to 2D space. The authors highlight that understanding "how" objects move in 3D, including dexterous hand motions and joint-level movements, remains an essential next step for a more comprehensive understanding of the real world. Addressing this 3D challenge is explicitly stated as a direction for future work, indicating a clear path for expanding FoundationMotion's capabilities.

In conclusion, FoundationMotion represents a pivotal contribution to the field of AI by providing a scalable, automated solution for a critical data problem. Its impact will be felt in the improved capabilities of Vision-Language Models, the advancement of embodied AI applications, and the acceleration of research in fine-grained motion and spatial reasoning.
