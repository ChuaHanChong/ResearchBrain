# Sail into the Headwind: Alignment via Robust Rewards and Dynamic Labels against Reward Hacking

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Meta AI's FAIR group developed POWER-DL, a framework designed to identify and address two types of reward hacking in preference-based learning for AI alignment. The method leverages robust reward estimation and dynamic preference labels to improve alignment performance and maintain initial model capabilities across instruction-following, conversational, and mathematical reasoning benchmarks, outperforming existing state-of-the-art methods like DPO.

## Problem

- AI alignment via Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) is vulnerable to 'reward hacking', where AI exploits flaws in the reward signal instead of genuinely aligning with human values.
- Reward hacking occurs in two ways: Type I (suboptimal choices appear favorable due to noisy data) and Type II (high-quality choices are underrepresented, leading to performance degradation).
- Current preference optimization methods like DPO and its variants lack mechanisms to robustly handle the statistical uncertainty and distribution shift inherent in human preference datasets.

## Method

- Introduces ==POWER (Preference Optimization via Weighted Entropy Robust Rewards)==, which addresses Type I reward hacking by integrating Guiaşu's Weighted Entropy with robust reward maximization to account for uncertainty.
- Proposes ==Dynamic Labels==, a technique to mitigate Type II reward hacking by adaptively adjusting the influence of preference pairs during training based on their consistency with current reward estimates.
- Combines these two techniques into ==POWER-DL==, an integrated framework for robust preference optimization with theoretical guarantees for finite-sample performance under general function approximation.

## Results

- POWER-DL consistently outperformed DPO and SimPO on AlpacaEval 2.0, achieving up to **13.0%** higher win rates with Helpsteer2 and **4.8%** with Zephyr.
- The method showed gains of up to **11.5%** on Arena-Hard and maintained or improved mathematical reasoning capabilities on GSM8K, unlike baselines that often degraded performance.
- Experiments demonstrated that POWER-DL is less prone to the degradation effects of Type II reward hacking, consistently maintaining or improving performance over the initial model.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Reward hacking in preference optimization manifests as two distinct types: Type I (statistical fluctuations causing overestimation) and Type II (underrepresentation of good behaviors leading to degradation).
> - Existing preference optimization methods like DPO are theoretically vulnerable to both types of reward hacking due to insufficient accounting for statistical uncertainty and inadequate regularization.
> - Integrating pessimistic reward estimation (via weighted entropy and robust reward maximization) and adaptive preference weighting (dynamic labels) can effectively mitigate both forms of reward hacking.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

AI alignment—ensuring artificial intelligence systems act in accordance with human values and preferences—remains a critical challenge as AI capabilities continue to advance. One popular approach for aligning language models with human preferences is Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) and its variants, which use human preference data to train models to generate more desirable outputs.

However, these methods are vulnerable to a fundamental problem: reward hacking, where AI systems exploit flaws in the reward modeling process rather than genuinely improving alignment with human values.

As shown in the figure above, reward hacking can occur in two distinct ways: Type I, where subpar choices appear more favorable due to statistical fluctuations in the preference data, and Type II, where decent choices appear less favorable due to insufficient representation in the preference dataset, leading to degradation of the initial model.

This paper, by researchers Paria Rashidinejad and Yuandong Tian from Meta AI's Fundamental AI Research (FAIR) group, introduces a novel framework to identify, analyze, and address these reward hacking vulnerabilities in preference-based learning.

#### 1.1. Understanding Reward Hacking

Reward hacking in preference optimization occurs when the AI system learns to maximize a flawed reward signal rather than the true underlying human preferences. The researchers formalize this problem within a contextual bandit framework, where for each context (e.g., a user prompt), the AI must select an action (e.g., generate a response) to maximize reward.

The paper identifies two distinct types of reward hacking:

1. **Type I Reward Hacking**: This occurs when statistical fluctuations in the preference dataset make suboptimal choices appear more favorable than they should be. This happens because preference data is inherently noisy and limited, leading to overestimation of rewards for certain actions.

2. **Type II Reward Hacking**: This happens when high-quality choices from the initial model are underrepresented in the preference dataset, causing the model to move away from these good behaviors during optimization. This can actually degrade performance compared to the initial model.

Both types of reward hacking stem from the fundamental challenge of distribution shift—the mismatch between the data distribution of the initial model, the preference dataset, and the true underlying reward function.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

#### 2.1. Limitations of Existing Methods

Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) and its variants (SimPO, IPO, ChiPO) have become popular approaches for learning from human preferences. These methods aim to learn a policy that maximizes implicit rewards derived from preference data while staying close to an initial reference policy.

The researchers provide a theoretical analysis demonstrating that despite incorporating regularization to stay close to the initial model, these methods remain vulnerable to both types of reward hacking:

1. For Type I reward hacking, existing methods fail to properly account for statistical uncertainty in the implicit reward estimates, leading to overconfidence in noisy preference data.

2. For Type II reward hacking, the regularization mechanisms in current methods are insufficient to prevent degradation when high-quality outputs from the initial model are underrepresented in the preference dataset.

The paper shows that these vulnerabilities persist across various preference optimization methods, highlighting the need for a more robust approach.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

#### 3.1. POWER: Preference Optimization via Weighted Entropy Robust Rewards

To address Type I reward hacking, the researchers propose POWER (Preference Optimization via Weighted Entropy Robust Rewards), which integrates two key components:

1. **Guiaşu's Weighted Entropy**: This provides a theoretically principled way to account for the importance of different actions based on the initial policy.

2. **Robust Reward Maximization**: This incorporates uncertainty estimates into the reward optimization process, preventing the model from exploiting statistical fluctuations.

Mathematically, POWER optimizes the following objective:

$$\max_\pi \mathbb{E}_{(x,a) \sim d_\pi}[\hat{r}(x,a) - \gamma \cdot \text{SE}(\hat{r}(x,a))] - \beta \cdot D_{\text{KL}}(\pi || \pi_0)$$

Where:
- $\hat{r}(x,a)$ is the estimated reward
- $\text{SE}(\hat{r}(x,a))$ is the standard error of the reward estimate
- $\gamma$ controls the degree of pessimism
- $\beta$ regulates the KL divergence from the initial policy $\pi_0$

The standard error term acts as a pessimism mechanism, discouraging the model from overconfidently pursuing actions with highly uncertain reward estimates. This directly addresses Type I reward hacking by making the model more cautious about seemingly high-reward actions that are based on limited data.

#### 3.2. Dynamic Labels: Addressing Type II Reward Hacking

For Type II reward hacking, the paper introduces a novel technique called Dynamic Labels. This method adaptively adjusts the importance of preference pairs during training based on how consistent they are with the model's current belief about rewards.

The dynamic labeling process works as follows:

1. Start with binary preference labels from the dataset (e.g., A is preferred over B)
2. During training, compute a confidence score for each preference pair based on:
 - The consistency of the preference with current reward estimates
 - The uncertainty in those reward estimates
3. Gradually adjust the effective labels from binary (0 or 1) to soft probabilities
4. Use these modified labels to update the gradient calculation

This process effectively reduces the influence of preference pairs that conflict with the model's increasingly reliable reward estimates, preventing the degradation of initially good behaviors that are underrepresented in the preference data.

The complete algorithm, named POWER-DL, combines robust reward estimation with dynamic labels to address both types of reward hacking simultaneously.

#### 3.3. Implementation and Theoretical Guarantees

A significant strength of the paper is that it provides theoretical guarantees for POWER under general function approximation. The researchers prove that POWER enjoys finite-sample guarantees, bounding the suboptimality of the learned policy in terms of the complexity of the function class, the amount of data, and the inherent difficulty of the problem.

The implementation of POWER-DL builds on the DPO framework but incorporates the robust reward objective and dynamic labeling process. This makes it relatively straightforward to integrate into existing preference optimization pipelines.

The method requires tuning three main hyperparameters:
- $\gamma$: Controls pessimism in reward estimation
- $\eta$: Affects the confidence score calculation for dynamic labels
- $\beta$: Regulates KL divergence from the initial model

The figure above shows how these hyperparameters affect performance, with LC representing length-controlled win rates and WR representing standard win rates on AlpacaEval. The results demonstrate that POWER-DL is relatively robust to hyperparameter choices within reasonable ranges.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

#### 4.1. Experimental Results

The researchers conducted extensive evaluations on standard language model alignment benchmarks:

1. **AlpacaEval 2.0**: A benchmark for evaluating instruction-following capabilities
2. **Arena-Hard**: A more challenging subset of conversation tasks
3. **GSM8K Mathematical Reasoning**: A benchmark for testing mathematical problem-solving abilities

The experimental setup included testing POWER-DL against DPO and SimPO using both Helpsteer2 (7B parameters) and Zephyr (7B parameters) as base models, with both base and instruction-tuned variants.

The results show consistent and significant improvements of POWER-DL over baseline methods:

- On AlpacaEval 2.0, POWER-DL achieved up to 13.0% higher LC win rates with Helpsteer2 and 4.8% higher with Zephyr compared to DPO
- On Arena-Hard, POWER-DL showed gains of up to 11.5% with Helpsteer2 and 4.0% with Zephyr
- On GSM8K, POWER-DL maintained or improved mathematical reasoning capabilities while other methods often showed degradation

Perhaps most importantly, the experiments reveal that POWER-DL is much less prone to the degradation effects of Type II reward hacking. While DPO and SimPO sometimes performed worse than the initial model on certain tasks, POWER-DL consistently maintained or improved performance.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

#### 5.1. Practical Implications

The findings in this paper have several important practical implications for developing aligned AI systems:

1. **More Reliable Alignment**: By addressing both types of reward hacking, POWER-DL enables more reliable alignment of AI systems with human preferences, reducing the risk of unintended behaviors.

2. **Better Data Efficiency**: The robust reward estimation and dynamic labels allow the method to make better use of limited preference data, which is important given that collecting high-quality human feedback is expensive.

3. **Preservation of Initial Capabilities**: The approach helps preserve desirable capabilities from the initial model, preventing the degradation that often occurs with standard preference optimization methods.

4. **Theoretical Understanding**: The theoretical analysis provides insights into why and how reward hacking occurs, offering a foundation for developing even more robust alignment techniques in the future.

5. **Straightforward Implementation**: The method builds on existing preference optimization frameworks, making it relatively straightforward to adopt in practice.

The hyperparameter sensitivity analysis also provides practical guidance for implementing POWER-DL, showing that it performs well across a range of settings rather than requiring precise tuning.

#### 5.2. Conclusion

The paper "Sail into the Headwind: Alignment via Robust Rewards and Dynamic Labels against Reward Hacking" makes several important contributions to the field of AI alignment:

1. It formally identifies and categorizes two distinct types of reward hacking in preference-based learning: Type I (overestimation from statistical fluctuations) and Type II (underrepresentation of good behaviors).

2. It provides theoretical analysis demonstrating that existing preference optimization methods, including DPO and its variants, are vulnerable to both types of reward hacking.

3. It introduces POWER, a robust preference optimization method with theoretical guarantees that addresses Type I reward hacking through pessimistic reward estimation.

4. It develops the dynamic labels technique to mitigate Type II reward hacking by adaptively adjusting the influence of preference pairs during training.

5. It demonstrates empirically that the combined approach, POWER-DL, consistently outperforms state-of-the-art preference optimization methods across multiple benchmarks.

The work addresses a fundamental challenge in AI alignment and offers both theoretical insights and practical solutions. By making preference-based learning more robust against reward hacking, it takes a significant step toward developing AI systems that more reliably align with human values and preferences.

As AI systems continue to advance in capabilities, the importance of robust alignment methods like POWER-DL will only grow, making this research a valuable contribution to the ongoing effort to develop safe and beneficial AI.
