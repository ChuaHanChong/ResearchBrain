# KineDex: Learning Tactile-Informed Visuomotor Policies via Kinesthetic Teaching for Dexterous Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> A kinesthetic teaching framework enables collection of tactile-enriched demonstrations for training dexterous robotic manipulation, combining ring-shaped finger straps for force feedback with an inpainting-based visuomotor policy that achieves 74.4% success rate across nine contact-rich tasks while demonstrating 2x faster data collection compared to teleoperation.

## Problem

- Existing methods like teleoperation suffer from kinematic mismatches and lack real-time tactile feedback
- Collecting high-quality demonstrations for contact-rich manipulation tasks is inefficient and challenging

## Method

- Hand-over-hand kinesthetic teaching with ring-shaped finger straps for tactile feedback
- Inpainting-based visuomotor policy that combines visual, proprioceptive and tactile information
- ==Modified PD== controller for force-informed action execution

## Results

- **74.4%** average success rate across nine challenging manipulation tasks
- More than **2x** faster data collection compared to teleoperation
- Significant performance drops when removing force control or tactile sensing components
- User studies confirm improved operability and data quality versus teleoperation

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Direct physical guidance with tactile feedback enables more natural and efficient demonstration collection
> - Force control and tactile sensing are crucial for successful contact-rich manipulation
> - Removing human hand occlusions through inpainting prevents domain shift during deployment
