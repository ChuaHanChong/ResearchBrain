# TPRU: Advancing Temporal and Procedural Understanding in Large Multimodal Models

> [!summary] Summary
> A new dataset and reinforcement learning fine-tuning methodology, TPRU, significantly improves the temporal and procedural understanding of Multimodal Large Language Models. Developed by researchers at East China Normal University and Shanghai AI Lab, this approach enhanced a 7B-parameter model's accuracy from 50.33% to 75.70% on a new evaluation benchmark, outperforming larger models like GPT-4o on certain temporal reasoning tasks.

## Problem

- Smaller, resource-efficient MLLMs exhibit limitations in comprehending temporal and procedural visual data sequences.
- Current MLLM training paradigms primarily focus on single-image-text alignment or treat multiple images as an unordered collection, lacking systematic procedural flow.
- Existing evaluation benchmarks identify MLLMs' deficiencies in temporal reasoning but do not provide structured, large-scale training data to address these limitations.

## Method

- Introduced **TPRU**, a large-scale ==dataset== (24,750 QA pairs, 126,000 images) constructed from diverse embodied scenarios with inherent procedural and temporal structures.
- Designed three complementary task formats: Temporal Reordering, Next-Frame Prediction, and Previous-Frame Review, including challenging negative samples to foster deep procedural understanding.
- Employed a reinforcement learning (RL) fine-tuning strategy, specifically the Group-wise Preference Optimization (GRPO) algorithm, on Qwen2.5-VL models to enhance their temporal and procedural reasoning capabilities.

## Results

- TPRU-finetuned models achieved substantial improvements on the TPRU-Test dataset; for example, TPRU-**7B**'s accuracy increased from **50.33%** to **75.70%**.
- TPRU-**7B** surpassed GPT-4o on TPRU-Test and demonstrated competitive or superior performance on established temporal benchmarks like MuirBench (TPRU-**32B** at **68.42%** vs. GPT-4o at **68.00%**) and LEGO-Puzzles, with notable gains in 'Ordering' and 'Backwards' reasoning.
- Ablation studies confirmed the synergistic effect of all three task formats, the critical role of negative samples, and the superior efficacy of the GRPO reinforcement learning approach compared to standard supervised fine-tuning.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Targeted training with procedurally-grounded data and a reinforcement learning fine-tuning approach can enable smaller MLLMs to achieve advanced temporal and procedural reasoning capabilities, surpassing larger models in specific tasks.
> - The explicit inclusion of challenging negative samples during training is crucial for MLLMs to perform robust cross-modal validation and avoid superficial pattern recognition.
> - Specialized training focused on discrete state changes and causal relationships is vital for fine-grained procedural understanding, differentiating it from general video comprehension.
