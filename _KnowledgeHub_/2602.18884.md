# TPRU: Advancing Temporal and Procedural Understanding in Large Multimodal Models

> [!summary] Summary
> A new dataset and reinforcement learning fine-tuning methodology, TPRU, significantly improves the temporal and procedural understanding of Multimodal Large Language Models. Developed by researchers at East China Normal University and Shanghai AI Lab, this approach enhanced a 7B-parameter model's accuracy from 50.33% to 75.70% on a new evaluation benchmark, outperforming larger models like GPT-4o on certain temporal reasoning tasks.

## Problem

- Smaller, resource-efficient MLLMs exhibit limitations in comprehending temporal and procedural visual data sequences.
- Current MLLM training paradigms primarily focus on single-image-text alignment or treat multiple images as an unordered collection, lacking systematic procedural flow.
- Existing evaluation benchmarks identify MLLMs' deficiencies in temporal reasoning but do not provide structured, large-scale training data to address these limitations.

## Method

- Introduced **TPRU**, a large-scale ==dataset== (24,750 QA pairs, 126,000 images) constructed from diverse embodied scenarios with inherent procedural and temporal structures.
- Designed three complementary task formats: Temporal Reordering, Next-Frame Prediction, and Previous-Frame Review, including challenging negative samples to foster deep procedural understanding.
- Employed a reinforcement learning (RL) fine-tuning strategy, specifically the Group-wise Preference Optimization (GRPO) algorithm, on Qwen2.5-VL models to enhance their temporal and procedural reasoning capabilities.

## Results

- TPRU-finetuned models achieved substantial improvements on the TPRU-Test dataset; for example, TPRU-**7B**'s accuracy increased from **50.33%** to **75.70%**.
- TPRU-**7B** surpassed GPT-4o on TPRU-Test and demonstrated competitive or superior performance on established temporal benchmarks like MuirBench (TPRU-**32B** at **68.42%** vs. GPT-4o at **68.00%**) and LEGO-Puzzles, with notable gains in 'Ordering' and 'Backwards' reasoning.
- Ablation studies confirmed the synergistic effect of all three task formats, the critical role of negative samples, and the superior efficacy of the GRPO reinforcement learning approach compared to standard supervised fine-tuning.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Targeted training with procedurally-grounded data and a reinforcement learning fine-tuning approach can enable smaller MLLMs to achieve advanced temporal and procedural reasoning capabilities, surpassing larger models in specific tasks.
> - The explicit inclusion of challenging negative samples during training is crucial for MLLMs to perform robust cross-modal validation and avoid superficial pattern recognition.
> - Specialized training focused on discrete state changes and causal relationships is vital for fine-grained procedural understanding, differentiating it from general video comprehension.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) have demonstrated advancements in integrating visual and linguistic information, achieving performance across various vision-language tasks. However, a limitation exists, particularly in smaller and more resource-efficient MLLMs, regarding their ability to comprehend temporal and procedural visual data sequences. Existing training paradigms often focus on single-image-text alignment or treat multiple images as an unordered collection, even in datasets that incorporate multi-frame inputs from videos. This approach typically emphasizes general sequential content rather than the fine-grained temporal and procedural understanding required for dynamic, interactive, and embodied AI applications.

The current research landscape shows efforts to enhance multi-image capabilities through instruction-tuning datasets like Mantis-Instruct and LLaVA-NeXT-Interleave, which provide multi-frame inputs. However, these datasets often lack a systematic framework for teaching explicit procedural flow. Concurrently, various evaluation benchmarks such as MuirBench, LEGO-Puzzles, STRIPCIPHER, and TempVS have been developed to assess multi-image reasoning, narrative, temporal logic, and multi-step planning. While these benchmarks effectively expose the MLLMs' deficiencies in temporal understanding, they primarily serve as evaluation tools rather than providing structured training data to address the root cause of these limitations.

This work addresses the identified gap by introducing TPRU, a dataset and training methodology designed to systematically cultivate temporal and procedural understanding in MLLMs. It aims to bridge the divide between existing evaluation benchmarks and the scarcity of suitable large-scale, high-quality, real-world sequential data for training. By focusing on resource-constrained models, TPRU attempts to enable the deployment of MLLMs in practical embodied AI scenarios like robotic manipulation and GUI navigation, where the comprehension of state changes and procedural logic is essential. The paper positions TPRU as a means to move beyond merely evaluating a failure to actively addressing it through targeted data and training.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the temporal and procedural understanding capabilities of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs), particularly smaller, resource-efficient variants. This enhancement is motivated by a critical deficiency observed in these models when processing complex temporal and procedural visual data, a bottleneck that significantly hinders their application in real-world embodied AI systems.

The motivation for this work stems from several key observations:
- **Performance Gap in Smaller MLLMs:** While large-scale MLLMs have shown emerging competence in vision-language tasks, smaller and more efficient counterparts exhibit significant limitations in understanding temporal and procedural image sequences. This gap is not solely a matter of model scale but points to a fundamental limitation in their training.
- **Importance for Embodied AI:** Downstream tasks in embodied AI, such as robotic manipulation, embodied navigation, and instruction following, demand a robust understanding of sequential actions, state changes, and procedural logic. These applications often operate on resource-constrained edge devices, making the deployment of massive, parameter-heavy models impractical. The inability of smaller models to grasp temporal dynamics therefore represents a significant impediment to progress in this field.
- **Systemic Failure in Prevailing Training Paradigms:** Current MLLM training paradigms predominantly focus on aligning text with single images or treating multiple images as an unordered collection. Even datasets that incorporate multi-frame inputs, such as LLaVA-NeXT-Interleave, typically emphasize general sequential content comprehension rather than the fine-grained temporal and procedural understanding critical for complex tasks. This oversight is attributed to the inherent difficulty of capturing the continuous transformations of real-world actions in a structured, large-scale dataset suitable for training.
- **Need for Training-Oriented Data:** The community has developed numerous evaluation benchmarks that confirm the MLLMs' failure in temporal reasoning but has largely neglected to create large-scale, high-quality, real-world sequential data specifically designed for *training* this capability. This creates a scenario where models are evaluated on a skill they were not systematically taught.

To address these motivations, the key objectives are:
- **Develop a Large-Scale, Procedurally Coherent Dataset:** Construct TPRU, a novel, large-scale dataset comprising 24,750 QA pairs and 126,000 images, sourced from diverse embodied scenarios. This dataset is designed to inherently possess clear procedural and temporal structures.
- **Cultivate Temporal Reasoning through Structured Tasks:** Systematically design the TPRU dataset to foster deep procedural understanding through three complementary task formats: Temporal Reordering, Next-Frame Prediction, and Previous-Frame Review.
- **Enhance Robustness with Challenging Negative Samples:** Incorporate a significant number of challenging negative samples with deliberate inconsistencies. This aims to compel models to move beyond superficial pattern recognition to active, cross-modal validation and true comprehension.
- **Establish a Rigorous Evaluation Benchmark:** Create TPRU-Test, a manually curated test set of 461 challenging instances, for robust evaluation of temporal and procedural understanding, ensuring it presents novel generalization challenges.
- **Validate an Effective Training Methodology:** Leverage TPRU with a reinforcement learning (RL) fine-tuning methodology, specifically targeting the enhancement of resource-efficient MLLMs.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed in this research focuses on constructing a novel dataset, TPRU, and leveraging a reinforcement learning (RL) strategy to fine-tune MLLMs for enhanced temporal and procedural understanding.

#### 3.1. TPRU Dataset Construction

The TPRU dataset is composed of two main parts: TPRU-25k for fine-tuning and TPRU-Test for evaluation.

#### 3.1.1. TPRU-25k (Fine-tuning Set)

TPRU-25k consists of 24,750 samples with 126,000 images across four procedural scenarios.

- **Data Sources:** Data was collected from diverse, real-world embodied scenarios to ensure a broad scope of procedural logic:
 - **Robotic Manipulation:** Video frames were sampled from "planning" tasks within the ShareRobot dataset to create discrete action sequences.
 - **LEGO Assembly:** Curated from 36 stop-motion YouTube videos, providing distinct, blur-free images suitable for part-to-whole reasoning.
 - **GUI Operation:** A new dataset derived from four-step screenshot sequences from GUI Odyssey, capturing goal-driven digital workflows.
 - **Embodied Navigation:** Ordered visual observations from agents navigating in simulated environments like Habitat.

- **Generation Pipeline:** The raw sequential data from these sources underwent a three-stage pipeline:
 - **Filtering and Quality Control:**
 - Diverse inputs (video frames, screenshots) were converted into coherent image sequences, each containing three to four images.
 - Qwen2.5-VL-72B was used as an automated assessor to filter out sequences exhibiting blurriness, abrupt transitions, or a lack of discernible temporal progression. This ensured high-fidelity, logically coherent sequences.
 - **Description Generation and Robustness Enhancement:**
 - Qwen2.5-VL-72B generated corresponding textual descriptions for each filtered image sequence to reinforce vision-language alignment.
 - A negative sampling strategy was introduced: a subset of instances featured deliberately mismatched textual descriptions and visual content (e.g., "pick up fork" with "put down knife"). For these cases, the correct output was "None of the choices provided," compelling the model to perform cross-modal verification rather than relying on textual priors.
 - **Task Formulation:** Three distinct and complementary tasks were designed:
 - **Temporal Ordering:** For a shuffled group of images and a textual description, the model was required to output the correct permutation to restore the chronological sequence.
 - **Next-Frame Prediction:** Given the initial, second, and terminal frames of a four-frame sequence, the model had to select the correct intervening third frame from a set of candidates, including distractors from similar scenarios. This simulates immediate action planning.
 - **Previous-Frame Review:** Given the final three frames of a four-frame sequence, the model was tasked with identifying the correct initial frame from candidates. This aims to improve understanding of procedural prerequisites and trace events to their origin.

#### 3.1.2. TPRU-Test (Evaluation Benchmark)

TPRU-Test is a manually curated, held-out evaluation set of 461 instances designed for rigorous assessment.
- **Composition:** It includes challenging instances from the four core training domains (Robotic Manipulation, LEGO Assembly, GUI Operation, Embodied Navigation) and incorporates human activities from the EPIC-KITCHENS dataset to test robustness and generalization.
- **Curation Protocol:** Each instance underwent a multi-stage human review process where annotators verified ground-truth sequences, created plausible distractors, and ensured question clarity. Each instance was cross-verified by at least one other expert.
- **Task Structure:** It inherits the same three task formats (Temporal Ordering, Next-Frame Prediction, Previous-Frame Review) as the training set.

#### 3.2. Training Methodology

- **Model Backbone:** Qwen2.5-VL models of varying parameter counts (3B, 7B, 32B) were selected as the base MLLMs.
- **Fine-tuning Strategy:** A reinforcement learning (RL) strategy was employed for fine-tuning on the TPRU-25k dataset.
- **RL Algorithm:** The Group-wise Preference Optimization (GRPO) algorithm was implemented using the Easy-R1 framework.
- **Hyperparameters:** Key settings included KL regularization with a coefficient of 0.01, generation of 5 rollouts per training sample, AdamW optimizer with a learning rate of 1e-6, and training for 2 epochs on 8 NVIDIA A800 GPUs. Reward curves showed stable convergence during training.
- **Evaluation Framework:** The open-source VLMEvalKit framework was used for standardized and reproducible evaluation across TPRU-Test and other public benchmarks.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted demonstrate the effectiveness of the TPRU dataset and the reinforcement learning fine-tuning methodology in enhancing the temporal and procedural understanding of MLLMs.

#### 4.1. Performance on TPRU-Test

The core evaluation on the TPRU-Test dataset showed substantial performance improvements for the fine-tuned models.
- The accuracy of TPRU-7B improved from 50.33% to 75.70%.
- TPRU-3B demonstrated a significant improvement from 37.96% to 60.95%.
- TPRU-7B surpassed the performance of the larger proprietary model GPT-4o, indicating that targeted training on procedurally-grounded data can yield competitive results even in smaller models.

#### 4.2. Performance on Temporal-Related Benchmarks

The fine-tuned models also exhibited generalization capabilities to established benchmarks that require temporal and procedural reasoning.

- **MuirBench:**
 - TPRU-finetuned models consistently improved over their Qwen2.5-VL base models across all scales.
 - TPRU-32B achieved an overall accuracy of 68.42%, outperforming GPT-4o (68.00%) and approaching Qwen2.5-VL-72B (69.35%).
 - The most notable gains were in the "Ordering" sub-task, with TPRU-32B reaching 45.31%, significantly higher than its base model and GPT-4o (23.44%). TPRU-7B's score in "Ordering" more than doubled from 14.06% to 34.38%.
 - Improvements were also observed in broader relational reasoning tasks such as "Difference Spotting" and "Visual Retrieval."

- **LEGO-Puzzles:**
 - Models consistently outperformed their base versions. TPRU-7B achieved an overall score of 42.8%, indicating positive generalization to complex, structured assembly tasks.
 - Pronounced improvements were seen in "Multi-Step Reasoning" categories.
 - TPRU-7B's performance on the "Ordering" task quadrupled from 8.0% to 32.0%.
 - Its "Backwards" reasoning capability more than doubled from 22.0% to 49.0%, and significant gains were observed in the "Next-Step" sub-task.

#### 4.3. Generalization to General Benchmarks

Evaluation on a range of broad multi-image benchmarks confirmed that the specialized training did not degrade general capabilities. The TPRU-finetuned models maintained or slightly improved performance (e.g., MMMU (+2.6) and MMCR (+1.08) for TPRU-7B) across these diverse tasks, indicating successful avoidance of catastrophic forgetting.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies

Ablation studies provided insights into the contributions of key components:

- **Task Composition:** Training with all three complementary tasks (Temporal Ordering, Next-Frame Prediction, Previous-Frame Review) yielded the highest performance, confirming a synergistic effect where the diversity of reasoning skills contributes to comprehensive temporal understanding.
- **Efficacy of Negative Samples:** Training without negative samples resulted in a significant degradation of performance on temporal reasoning benchmarks (LEGO-Puzzles and MuirBench). This indicates that teaching models to explicitly reject invalid logic is critical for robust procedural understanding.
- **Impact of Data Volume:** Performance on MuirBench and LEGO-Puzzles improved with increasing data size but began to plateau near the full 24,750 samples, suggesting that the dataset is sufficiently comprehensive.
- **Impact of Training Strategy:** The GRPO reinforcement learning approach consistently achieved superior results across benchmarks compared to standard Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT), indicating its effectiveness in cultivating advanced procedural reasoning.

#### 4.5. Comparison with Video-Temporal Baselines

A comparison with state-of-the-art Video Large Language Models (LLaVA-Video, SmolVLM2, Long-VITA, Qwen2.5-Omni) revealed that TPRU-7B significantly outperformed these models on MuirBench, LEGO-Puzzles, and TPRU-Test. This highlights a distinction between general video understanding (recognizing events) and precise procedural understanding (inferring sequences and consequential relationships), which TPRU's targeted training addresses. For example, on the MuirBench Ordering subtask, TPRU-7B achieved 34.38, compared to Qwen2.5-Omni's 18.8.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents TPRU, a large-scale dataset and an associated reinforcement learning fine-tuning methodology, addressing a recognized deficiency in Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs): the understanding of temporal and procedural visual data sequences. The demonstrated performance gains suggest that the limitations of MLLMs, particularly smaller, resource-efficient variants, in procedural reasoning are not inherent to their scale but are surmountable through targeted data and training approaches.

The primary significance of this work lies in its contribution to enabling robust sequential reasoning capabilities in MLLMs that are practical for real-world deployment. By achieving competitive, and in some cases superior, performance compared to significantly larger models like GPT-4o on temporal understanding tasks, TPRU indicates that high-level procedural comprehension can be instilled in models suitable for resource-constrained environments. This could facilitate the deployment of MLLMs in critical embodied AI applications such as robotic manipulation, where understanding complex sequences of actions is fundamental, and in GUI navigation, where tracking screen state changes and user interactions is essential.

The introduction of the TPRU dataset, with its diverse sources, structured task formats (Temporal Reordering, Next-Frame Prediction, Previous-Frame Review), and the inclusion of challenging negative samples, provides a valuable resource for the research community. This systematic approach to data construction, aimed at cultivating active cross-modal validation rather than passive observation, represents a methodological advancement in training MLLMs for genuine comprehension of procedural logic. The release of the codebase further promotes transparency and reproducibility, potentially accelerating future research in this area.

The findings also contribute to a deeper understanding of the differences between general video understanding and fine-grained procedural temporal understanding. The superior performance of TPRU-7B over general video LLMs on procedural tasks suggests that specialized training focused on discrete state changes and causal relationships is crucial for specific embodied AI scenarios.

In summary, this work shifts the paradigm for MLLM development in temporal reasoning. Instead of focusing solely on increasing model size, it demonstrates that carefully curated, procedurally-grounded data combined with an effective reinforcement learning strategy can unlock advanced reasoning capabilities in smaller, more deployable models. This has the potential to move the frontier of capable AI from primarily massive, high-resource systems towards practical, efficient, and deployable agents for real-world applications.
