# Understanding Self-Supervised Learning via Latent Distribution Matching

> [!summary] Summary
> The Latent Distribution Matching (LDM) framework unifies diverse self-supervised learning algorithms, demonstrating how existing methods derive from it and enabling the design of new models, such as a Kalman-based predictive approach for uncertainty quantification. This framework further establishes formal identifiability guarantees for learned latent representations, proving their capacity to recover true underlying variables up to affine transformations.

## Problem

- A cohesive theoretical framework for self-supervised learning (SSL) was missing, hindering the understanding and systematic development of diverse SSL methods.
- Existing interpretations, such as geometric alignment and mutual information maximization, provided incomplete or insufficient explanations for the success of various SSL algorithms.
- There was a need to formally explain the empirical success of predictive SSL models and provide guarantees for the identifiability of their learned representations.

## Method

- Formulated self-supervised learning as a ==Latent Distribution Matching== (**LDM**) objective, minimizing the ==KL divergence== between an empirical latent distribution and an assumed latent model.
- Decomposed the **LDM** objective into alignment and entropy maximization terms, connecting it to maximum likelihood and encouraging encoder invertibility on the data manifold.
- Demonstrated that various existing SSL algorithms arise from specific choices of latent models and entropy estimation techniques within the LDM framework, and used it to derive a novel ==Kalman-based predictive SSL== model.

## Results

- Unified a wide range of existing SSL algorithms (e.g., SimCLR, VICReg, BYOL) by demonstrating their derivation as specific instances of the LDM framework based on choices of latent models and entropy estimators.
- Empirically confirmed that explicit Mutual Information maximization offers no systematic performance advantage over LDM, suggesting its success stems from implicit distribution matching and entropy maximization.
- Derived a novel Kalman-based predictive SSL model that achieved R^2=0.99 for latent variable recovery in synthetic data, quantified uncertainty in real-world time-series, and was theoretically proven to yield identifiable latent representations (up to affine transformations).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Latent Distribution Matching (LDM) serves as a unifying theoretical framework, explaining how diverse SSL methods are specific instantiations of matching empirical and assumed latent distributions.
> - Explicit mutual information (MI) maximization contributes little to representation quality beyond the entropy maximization inherent in LDM, with MI-based successes often stemming from implicit distribution matching.
> - Latent entropy maximization within LDM encourages the encoder to be approximately invertible on the data manifold, a property crucial for learning effective and identifiable representations.
