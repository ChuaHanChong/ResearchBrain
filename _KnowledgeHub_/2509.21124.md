# Expanding Reasoning Potential in Foundation Model by Learning Diverse Chains of Thought Patterns

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Peking University and Meituan developed the CoTP framework, a dual-granularity approach to select high-value Chain-of-Thought (CoT) data during mid-training, enhancing the “reasoning potential” of foundation models. This method substantially improved performance on challenging mathematical reasoning tasks and elevated the upper bound of downstream reinforcement learning.

## Problem

- Reinforcement learning (RL) performance in large reasoning models is often limited by the inherent reasoning capacity of the foundation model, leading to unstable performance.
- Existing mid-training methodologies use Chain-of-Thought (CoT) data indiscriminately, without identifying which specific types or characteristics most effectively improve model reasoning capabilities.
- The deeper paradigmatic characteristics and essence of reasoning patterns within CoT sequences remained unexplored, leaving no principled way to identify high-value data for training.

## Method

- Defined "==reasoning potential==" as the inverse of the expected number of attempts required to solve a problem, providing a quantifiable metric for improvement.
- Constructed a high-value core reference set of CoT data by abstracting atomic reasoning patterns and applying importance weighting.
- Developed a dual-granularity data selection algorithm, CoTP, using Weighted Dynamic Time Warping on both reasoning pattern chains and token entropy chains to select high-value CoT data for mid-training.

## Results

- Models mid-trained with CoTP data achieved a **9.58%** average improvement on AIME 2024 and 2025, and an overall **6.39%** gain on challenging mathematical reasoning benchmarks compared to baseline approaches.
- The CoTP framework increased the upper bound of downstream Reinforcement Learning (RL) performance by an average of **7.81%** compared to models trained on general long-CoT data.
- Ablation studies confirmed that both token-level entropy features and importance weighting of reasoning patterns are critical for effectively selecting high-value data and improving model performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Enhancing a foundation model's intrinsic reasoning potential during mid-training is crucial for improving downstream RL performance and overall problem-solving efficacy.
> - Effective data curation, focusing on diverse and high-value reasoning patterns, is more impactful than merely increasing the volume of mixed reasoning data.
> - A dual-granularity representation combining abstract reasoning patterns and fine-grained token entropy captures the essential characteristics of effective CoT sequences.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Recent advancements in large reasoning models (LRMs), particularly for complex tasks such as mathematical reasoning, have been significantly influenced by post-training optimization techniques, especially those employing reinforcement learning (RL) frameworks. Prior research has indicated that the parameter space within foundation models inherently possesses latent pathways for challenging reasoning, and RL training functions to operationalize these implicit capabilities. This suggests that the foundational reasoning capacity of a model directly impacts and sets limits on the achievable upper bounds of RL performance, as evidenced by unstable RL performance in certain open-source foundation models.

Simultaneously, studies have shown that incorporating Chain-of-Thought (CoT) data, particularly long CoT sequences, during the mid-training phase can substantially enhance the depth of a model's reasoning. However, existing methodologies often treat CoT data uniformly, without a detailed investigation into which specific types or characteristics of CoT data most effectively contribute to improving model reasoning. This gap in understanding the inherent paradigmatic features and deeper essence of reasoning within CoT sequences represents a key area for further exploration.

This paper addresses this specific challenge by proposing a targeted approach to enrich reasoning patterns in foundation models during mid-training. It positions itself within the landscape by moving beyond indiscriminate use of CoT data, instead focusing on identifying and leveraging high-value reasoning patterns. By enhancing the foundation model's reasoning potential prior to or in conjunction with RL, this work aims to create a more robust base that can lead to superior and more stable performance in complex reasoning tasks, thereby complementing and extending the benefits of existing RL-based optimization strategies. The paper builds upon observations from related work, such as OctoThinker, which demonstrated the benefits of integrating CoT QA data in mid-training, and large-scale open-source long-CoT datasets like OpenR1-Math-220k and AM-DeepSeek-R1-Distilled.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to improve the reasoning capabilities of foundation models by expanding their "reasoning potential" during the mid-training stage. This is motivated by two primary observations:

1. **Limitation of RL Performance by Foundation Model Capacity:** While reinforcement learning (RL) frameworks have proven effective in improving problem-solving abilities in large reasoning models (LRMs) through exploratory feedback, empirical evidence suggests that the inherent reasoning capacity of the foundation model directly influences and ultimately limits the upper bounds of subsequent RL performance. Certain open-source foundation models have exhibited unstable RL performance, underscoring the need to thoroughly explore and enhance the core reasoning capabilities of these models.
2. **Indiscriminate Use of Chain-of-Thought (CoT) Data:** Prior studies have indicated that blending question-answer (QA) data with long Chain-of-Thought (CoT) sequences during mid-training can significantly deepen reasoning abilities. However, current approaches often utilize CoT data without differentiation, leading to an unanswered question regarding which specific data types or reasoning paradigms are most effective in substantially improving model reasoning capabilities. There is a lack of investigation into the nuanced paradigmatic characteristics and deeper essence of reasoning patterns within CoT sequences.

To address these motivations, the paper sets out the following specific objectives:
- **Define Reasoning Potential:** For the first time, to theoretically define the "reasoning potential" of a foundation model as the inverse of the number of independent attempts required to correctly answer a given question. This definition establishes a quantifiable metric, where expanding the potential is equivalent to reducing the average number of attempts needed for a correct answer.
- **Abstract Atomic Reasoning Patterns:** To abstract atomic reasoning patterns from CoT sequences, characterizing them by their commonality and inductive capabilities. These patterns serve as fundamental building blocks of complex reasoning.
- **Construct a High-Value Core Reference Set:** To utilize these abstracted patterns to construct a core reference set. This set is envisioned as an approximation of an ideal oracle dataset, enriched with valuable reasoning patterns that exhibit high reasoning efficiency (low expected first-passage time).
- **Develop a Dual-Granularity Data Selection Algorithm:** To propose a dual-granularity algorithm that leverages both chains of reasoning patterns and token entropy. This algorithm aims to efficiently select high-value CoT data (termed CoTP data) from a larger data pool that aligns with the established core reference set. The selected data would then be used to train models to master effective reasoning.
- **Empirical Validation:** To demonstrate that mid-training with this selected 10B-token CoTP data can significantly expand the reasoning potential of a Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) foundation model, leading to measurable improvements on challenging mathematical reasoning benchmarks and raising the upper bound of downstream RL performance.

In essence, the research aims to provide a principled, data-driven methodology for enhancing foundation models' intrinsic reasoning abilities by intelligently curating their mid-training diet, thereby improving their overall problem-solving efficacy and preparing them for more advanced tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The CoTP (Chain-of-Thought Patterns) framework is designed to train foundation models by exposing them to diverse, high-value reasoning patterns during mid-training. This is achieved through a multi-stage process involving theoretical definition, core set construction, and a dual-granularity data selection algorithm.

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation and Reasoning Potential Definition

The paper first defines "Model Potential" (Φ(M, q_i)) as the probability that a model M generates the correct answer for question q_i when sampling from its output distribution. The overall model potential Φ(M, D_eval) is the expected potential over an evaluation dataset. A crucial corollary establishes that model potential is the inverse of the expected first-passage time (E[K_i]), where K_i is the number of independent attempts needed to solve q_i. Therefore, expanding reasoning potential is equivalent to reducing E[K_i]. The objective is to select a training subset D*_train from a source dataset D_source that minimizes the gap between the reasoning potential of a model trained on D*_train and one trained on an (unobtainable) ideal oracle dataset D*_oracle.

#### 3.2. Core Set Approximation of Oracle

Given the difficulty of directly obtaining D*_oracle, the paper proposes approximating it with a carefully constructed "core set" (C_core) of CoT data rich in diverse, high-value reasoning patterns.
- **Dual Granularity Representation:** Each CoT sample is characterized at two levels of granularity:
 - **Reasoning Pattern Chains (ξ(c)):** An atomic reasoning pattern (ρ) is defined as a fundamental cognitive operation applicable across diverse problem domains. A pattern chain C = [ρ_1..., ρ_n] is an ordered sequence of these patterns extracted from a CoT. Deepseek-V3 is used for annotating these chains.
 - **Token Entropy Chains (η(c)):** The entropy (h_t) of each token in a CoT sequence is computed as h_t = − Σ p_t(v) log p_t(v), capturing token features with high reasoning gain.
- **Core Set Construction Steps:**
 1. **Question Filtering:** Questions from the source dataset (D_source) are filtered, annotated with difficulty/problem types, and randomly sampled to match an expected distribution.
 2. **CoT Generation and Pattern Extraction:** For each selected question, multiple strong reasoning models generate 'r' CoT sequences. Low-quality or unsolvable data is filtered. Pattern chains (ξ(c_i,j)) are extracted.
 3. **Pattern Importance Weighting:** The importance of each pattern (ρ_k) for a given question (q_i) is assessed using a TF-IDF weighting scheme (ω(ρ_k | q_i, Q)), balancing frequency within CoT sequences against global rarity.
 4. **Manual Selection:** CoT sequences with correct answers exhibiting distinctive, high-importance patterns are manually selected to form C_core. Each instance in C_core is associated with its pattern importance weights (Ω_i).

#### 3.3. Selecting Training Data with High-Value Reasoning Patterns

To build the training dataset D_train, instances with CoT sequences similar to those in C_core are selected from D_source. This is framed as an assignment problem with capacity constraints.
- **Distance Metric:** The similarity between a core CoT (c_c_i) and a source CoT (c_s_j) is quantified by a distance D_ij, which is a weighted sum of pattern chain and entropy chain distances:
 `D_ij = λ * d_pattern(ξ(c_c_i), ξ(c_s_j)) + (1 − λ) * d_entropy(η(c_c_i), η(c_s_j))`
 where λ is a weighting parameter.
- **Dynamic Time Warping (DTW):** DTW is employed for both distance computations:
 - `d_pattern`: Uses `WeightedDTW` with pattern chain (ξ), importance weights (Ω_i) from the core instance, and a character n-gram cosine distance (`d_ngram`) as the base distance function (Algorithm 2). Chinese character-level n-grams are used due to their semantic richness.
 - `d_entropy`: Uses `WeightedDTW` with entropy chain (η), uniform weights (w=1), and absolute difference (`d_abs`) as the base distance.
- **Optimization:** The assignment problem (Eq. 7) is reformulated into a minimum weight perfect bipartite matching problem by replicating each core instance `o` times. This transformed problem is then solved efficiently using the Hungarian algorithm (Algorithm 1). This ensures that each core instance is matched with exactly `o` source instances, and each source instance is assigned at most once.

#### 3.4. Experimental Setup

- **Data Construction:** A LongCoTPool is created by integrating OpenR1-Math-220k, AM-DeepSeek-R1-Distilled, and high-difficulty (H4/H5) BoostQA questions with long CoT traces generated by DeepSeek-R1. This pool undergoes rigorous n-gram deduplication and filtering for truncated/unanswered data. Contamination with benchmark data is mitigated using 10-gram matching and embedding similarity.
- **Model Training:** The 85A6B Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) foundation model, pre-trained on 14T tokens, is used. Mid-training involves decaying on 30B tokens of specialized reasoning data (CoTP or baselines) and general-domain KnowEdu data at a 1:2 ratio. Scaling experiments extend this to 60B tokens. All models undergo Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) with 60k long-CoT entries to ensure a fair comparison for long CoT generation. The RL stage uses the GSPO algorithm (Zheng et al., 2025).
- **Evaluation:** Models are assessed for general performance (MMLU, C-Eval, etc.) and on challenging mathematical reasoning tasks (AIME 2025/2024, HMMT 2025, BeyondAIME, MATH500). Pass@k metrics and RL performance are also evaluated.
- **Baselines:** Include general corpora (KnowEdu), QA datasets without CoT (BoostQA), short-CoT QA datasets (JiuZhang3.0, MegaMathQA, OMInstruct-2), and long-CoT QA datasets (OpenR1-Math, AM-Distilled, LongCoTPool).
- **Ablation Studies:** Investigate the impact of entropy-based selection (λ parameter), n-gram length, and importance weighting.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrate that the CoTP framework significantly enhances the reasoning capabilities of foundation models.

#### 4.1. Performance on Challenging Mathematical Reasoning Tasks

- Mid-training with only 10B tokens of high-value reasoning data selected by CoTP leads to a substantial improvement in performance across multiple challenging mathematical reasoning benchmarks.
- On AIME 2024 and 2025, the 85A6B MoE model achieved a 9.58% improvement using CoTP data compared to baseline approaches.
- Overall, CoTP provides an average gain of 6.39% over the LongCoTPool (a simple mixture of existing long-CoT data) and 4.21% and 5.10% over OpenR1-Math and AM-Distilled, respectively.
- The LongCoTPool, without selective curation, showed a slight decline in average performance compared to OpenR1-Math and AM-Distilled, indicating that merely increasing the volume of mixed reasoning data may not be sufficient. CoTP's curated approach from the LongCoTPool achieved optimal performance.
- The model trained with CoTP also sustained general performance across various general benchmarks, demonstrating that specialized reasoning enhancement does not detriment broad capabilities.

#### 4.2. Enhancement of Downstream Reinforcement Learning (RL) Performance

- The expanded reasoning potential from CoTP mid-training translates into a notable increase in the upper bound of downstream RL performance.
- CoTP-trained models achieved an average improvement of 7.81% in RL performance compared to models trained on LongCoTPool, and a 42.04% improvement over those trained on KnowEdu.
- Pass@k evaluations and RL training step curves illustrate that models mid-trained on CoT datasets, particularly CoTP, exhibit consistent and progressive improvements with increasing attempts and training steps, indicating a more robust and expandable reasoning foundation.

#### 4.3. Scalability

- When the mid-training data volume was scaled from 30B to 60B tokens (by relaxing similarity thresholds for CoTP data selection), the model continued its upward performance trajectory. This resulted in an additional 4.72% average improvement on AIME 2025 & 2024, suggesting the continuous effectiveness and scalability of the CoTP framework in recalling high-value reasoning data while maintaining general performance.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies

- **Entropy-based selection:** Removing the entropy component (i.e., setting λ=1 for only pattern chain distance) resulted in a reduction in average performance, indicating that token-level entropy features contribute to capturing valuable reasoning patterns.
- **N-gram length:** Using n-gram lengths of n=1 or 2 yielded better outcomes for pattern similarity evaluation, providing a balanced representation. Analysis showed that Chinese character-level n-grams are particularly effective in distinguishing unrelated patterns due to the semantic meaning of individual characters, reducing misjudgment cases observed with English pattern representations.
- **Importance weighting:** Models trained without importance weighting for reasoning patterns showed poorer performance, underscoring the critical role of TF-IDF scores in distinguishing between normal and high-value reasoning patterns and their differential contribution to reasoning potential.

#### 4.5. Analysis of Reasoning Patterns

- CoTP-trained models demonstrated an enhanced mastery of a broader array of correct and key reasoning patterns, showing a 7.25% increased overlap with patterns mastered by a strong reference model (DeepSeek-R1) compared to LongCoTPool.
- A pattern distance analysis using DTW showed a 31.4% reduction in the average reasoning pattern chain distance between the CoTP-trained model and DeepSeek-R1, indicating a convergence to sophisticated, expert-level reasoning paradigms.
- The composition of the CoTP dataset closely matched the distribution of the manually curated core set, with a significant reduction in KL divergence for problem type distributions (from 0.18 for LongCoTPool to 0.04 for CoTP) and better alignment in token length distributions, which generally featured longer CoT sequences.
- The paper also noted that reasoning patterns inherently encompass reflective characteristics, which can be integrated within discrete patterns or manifest as recursive loops in pattern chains, suggesting an intertwined nature between reasoning and introspective processes.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces a significant advancement in enhancing the reasoning capabilities of large language models, particularly in complex domains like mathematics. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Novel Theoretical Framework:** The explicit definition of "reasoning potential" as the inverse of the expected number of attempts required to solve a problem provides a new, quantifiable metric for evaluating and understanding model capabilities. This theoretical foundation helps frame the goal of improving reasoning in a measurable way.
- **Targeted Data Curation for Mid-Training:** The CoTP framework offers a principled methodology for selecting high-value Chain-of-Thought (CoT) data during the mid-training phase. By moving beyond indiscriminate data use, this approach highlights the critical importance of data quality and relevance in foundational model training, demonstrating that "more data" is not necessarily "better data" without careful curation.
- **Substantial Performance Gains:** The demonstrated improvements on challenging mathematical reasoning benchmarks (e.g., 9.58% on AIME) and the significant increase in the upper bounds of downstream reinforcement learning (RL) performance (7.81%) indicate a practical and effective strategy for developing more capable and robust LLMs. These gains suggest that a well-curated mid-training dataset can unlock latent reasoning abilities.
- **Improved Understanding of Reasoning Mechanisms:** The dual-granularity approach, utilizing both abstract reasoning patterns and fine-grained token entropy, provides a deeper understanding of what constitutes effective reasoning. The analysis of pattern importance and the alignment of CoTP data with a reference core set contribute to identifying and leveraging key cognitive operations in problem-solving.
- **Scalability and Generalizability:** The observed scalability of the CoTP framework with increased data volumes suggests its potential for application in larger-scale industrial settings. Furthermore, its domain-agnostic nature, with visualization of patterns across various STEM fields, implies broad applicability to any complex problem-solving scenario that can be decomposed into atomic reasoning steps.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** This work establishes a strong foundation for future research in several areas, including:
 - Developing more sophisticated methods for identifying and extracting high-value reasoning patterns.
 - Exploring adaptive mid-training strategies that dynamically adjust data selection based on model learning progress.
 - Investigating the interplay between different levels of reasoning granularity (e.g., token, pattern, and global chain) to further refine model training.
 - Enhancing the interpretability of LLM reasoning by explicitly tracking the acquisition and utilization of specific reasoning patterns.

In conclusion, this research provides both a theoretical underpinning and a practical framework for expanding the reasoning potential of foundation models, demonstrating that strategic data curation during mid-training is a crucial factor in pushing the boundaries of AI capabilities in complex problem-solving.
