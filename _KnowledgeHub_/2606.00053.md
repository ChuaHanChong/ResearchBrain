# VLAMotor: Test-Guided Enhancement of Vision-Language-Action Models via Agent-BasedData Synthesis

> [!summary] Summary
> VLAMotor is a test-and-repair framework for vision-language-action robot policies. It ranks randomly generated simulation test cases by latent-space distance to training data, prunes near-duplicates, then uses a VLM agent to abstract each failure trajectory and compose parameterized repair skills into successful executions. These auto-labeled trajectories fine-tune the model, raising simulated success by 49.25% and real-robot success by 57.50%.

## Problem

- Manually curated VLA robustness benchmarks demand heavy human effort and cover only limited configuration dimensions, so they cannot expose failures at scale.
- Automated random test generators ignore input diversity, so many selected cases stem from one underlying defect and waste the testing budget on redundancy.
- Prior work stops at failure detection with no mechanism to turn discovered failure trajectories into supervisory data, leaving repair and enhancement unaddressed.

## Method

- **VLAMotor**'s ==distance-aware test selection== scores each candidate by ==cosine distance in the fused hidden state== before the action head, ranks by uncertainty, then prunes latent-space neighbors for diversity.
- ==Training support degree== with exponential distance weights defines uncertainty separately for discrete action classes (==support ratio==) and continuous action values (==negative log support==).
- A ==VLM agent== abstracts failures into causal relations, state changes and subgoals, plans sequences from a ==16-primitive perception/motion/interaction repair library==, grounds them via ==inverse kinematics==, and loops on diagnostic feedback.

## Results

- **92.33%** of 1,200 generated test cases triggered failures across RT-1-X, OpenVLA-7B and pi-0.5; the hierarchical pi-0.5 failed most, at **96.25%**.
- Against VLATest under equal budget, failure detection rose from **83.62%** to **90.38%** and trajectory coverage from **63.75%** to **82.68%**, an **18.93 point** coverage gain.
- Fine-tuning lifted overall simulated success by **49.25%** (per-task **37.00-62.67%**); on a Franka arm, pi-0.5 scored **0/80** while the enhanced model scored **46/80**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Distance to nearest training samples serves double duty: candidate-to-training distance predicts failure likelihood, candidate-to-candidate distance flags redundancy from a shared defect.
> - Repair scales better as composition over a small parameterized skill library than as per-case scripts or direct continuous-action prediction by a language model.
> - Simulation-only repair data transferred to real hardware, but jitter and erratic motion rooted in architecture or training stayed unrepairable by data augmentation alone.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models represent a foundational technology for end-to-end robotic manipulation, enabling robots to interpret natural language instructions and visual observations to execute complex tasks. These models, like other deep neural network (DNN)-based systems, are data-driven, meaning their performance is heavily reliant on the coverage and quality of their training data. Consequently, VLA models are susceptible to failures in edge-case or long-tail configurations not sufficiently represented in their training datasets, which poses a challenge for their reliable deployment in real-world robotic applications.

Existing research largely addresses the issue of VLA failures through two main avenues for failure exposure. The first involves manually curated benchmarks (e.g., LIBERO-Plus, RobustVLA, Eva-VLA, ViFailback, VLA-Arena). While useful, these benchmarks demand significant human effort, cover a limited range of configurations, and are thus not scalable for discovering a broad spectrum of failures. The second approach utilizes automated testing frameworks (e.g., VLATest) which randomly generate test inputs. A limitation of these frameworks, consistent with broader DNN testing concerns, is their insufficient attention to the diversity of generated inputs; many discovered failures may stem from the same underlying defect, leading to redundant test cases and inefficient testing.

Crucially, prior studies primarily focus on failure detection but generally lack mechanisms for systematically leveraging these discovered failures to repair or enhance the VLA models. There is a gap in translating failure trajectories into supervisory data that can be used to improve model robustness and mitigate identified risks. VLAMotor aims to bridge this gap by integrating effective failure exposure with an agent-based data synthesis method for model finetuning, providing a closed-loop framework for VLA enhancement.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a comprehensive analysis framework, named VLAMotor, for the enhancement of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. This framework is designed to address the critical challenges of VLA model reliability and robustness in real-world deployment scenarios.

The key motivations driving this work are:

1. **Mitigating Deployment Risks of VLA Models**: VLA models, being data-driven, are prone to exhibiting unexpected or erroneous behavior when encountering configurations not well-represented in their training data. Such "edge-case" failures can lead to significant risks in real-world robotic applications, including physical damage or task incompletion. The researchers are motivated to develop a method that can efficiently identify these high-quality failure modes *before* deployment.
2. **Bridging the Gap Between Failure Detection and Model Repair**: A significant limitation of existing VLA testing approaches is that they predominantly focus on detecting failures but do not provide a scalable mechanism to leverage these discoveries for model improvement. The paper highlights the absence of a system that can automatically convert failure trajectories into valuable supervisory data to correct the model's erroneous behaviors. VLAMotor is motivated to establish a closed-loop system that transforms detected failures into actionable information for model enhancement.
3. **Improving Efficiency and Diversity in Failure Exposure**: Current automated VLA testing methods often rely on random input generation, which can result in redundant test cases that expose the same underlying defects repeatedly. This inefficiency limits the diversity of discovered failure modes. The research is motivated to introduce a more effective and efficient test input selection strategy that prioritizes inputs with high failure-revealing potential and reduces redundancy, thereby maximizing the coverage of distinct failure modes under a constrained testing budget.
4. **Enabling Agent-Based, Scalable Data Synthesis for Finetuning**: Manually annotating successful trajectories for model retraining is labor-intensive and costly. The paper is motivated to develop an automated, agent-based approach that can synthesize supervisory data from failed executions. This involves abstracting failure information into semantic representations and leveraging a library of repair skills to generate successful corrective trajectories, which can then be automatically labeled and used to finetune the VLA model.
5. **Achieving Low-Cost VLA Enhancement through Sim-to-Real Transfer**: The cost and safety concerns associated with testing and data collection on physical robots are substantial. The researchers aim to develop a simulation-based enhancement framework that can effectively transfer improvements to real-world hardware. This would provide a low-cost and scalable direction for improving VLA model robustness without extensive real-world experimentation.

In essence, VLAMotor seeks to provide a comprehensive, automated, and scalable solution for testing and repairing VLA models, ultimately leading to more robust and reliable robotic manipulation systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

VLAMotor operates as an in-simulation framework designed to test and repair VLA models, producing an enhanced model with improved robustness. It comprises two main phases: Distance-aware Test Selection for failure exposure and Agent-based Data Synthesis for model finetuning.

#### 3.1. Distance-aware Test Selection

This phase aims to identify a compact and diverse set of failure-inducing test cases efficiently.

1. **Preprocessing and Test Candidate Generation**: An initial pool of candidate test cases ($D_C$) is generated using VLATest, an automated testing tool that samples randomized testing configurations based on the target VLA model.
2. **Latent Representation**: To compute distance-based uncertainty, VLAMotor uses the fused hidden state immediately preceding the action head of the VLA model as the latent feature space. This space captures the integrated visual and linguistic information crucial for action prediction. For an input $x_i$, its latent feature $z_i = f(x_i)$, where $f$ is the composite function of VLA layers before the action head.
3. **Distance Metric**: Cosine distance is employed in the latent space, defined as $d(x_i, x_j) = 1 - \frac{z_i \cdot z_j}{\|z_i\|\|z_j\|}$. This angular metric is chosen for its robustness to norm variations and its ability to measure semantic dissimilarity in VLA embeddings.
4. **Training Support Degree**: This metric quantifies how strongly the training data supports a VLA prediction for a given test input. It is based on the distance to the K_T nearest neighbors in the training set ($N_T(x_c^i)$).
 - For **discrete action types**: The weighted nearest neighbor support probability $p^*_c(x_c^i)$ for an action class $c$ is calculated using an exponential decay function of the cosine distance. The support degree $S(\hat{c}_c^i)$ for the VLA's predicted action $\hat{c}_c^i$ is this probability.
 - For **continuous action values**: The training support degree $S(\hat{a}_c^i)$ considers both latent space distance and the Euclidean distance between the predicted action $\hat{a}_c^i$ and expert actions $a_T^j$ of nearest training samples.
5. **Uncertainty Score**: Derived from the training support degree, a higher uncertainty score indicates weaker training support and a higher likelihood of prediction error.
 - For **discrete action outputs**: Uncertainty $U(x_c^i) = \frac{p^*_{c'}(x_c^i)}{p^*_{\hat{c}_c^i}(x_c^i)}$, where $c'$ is the non-predicted action class with the highest support.
 - For **continuous action outputs**: Uncertainty $U(x_c^i) = -\log(S(\hat{a}_c^i) + \epsilon)$.
6. **Redundancy-aware Diversity Score**: To ensure the selected test inputs are diverse, a redundancy-aware diversity score $R(x_i)$ is computed for each candidate $x_i$ within a preliminary subset. This score is the sum of cosine distances to its K_R nearest neighbors within that subset. A larger $R(x_i)$ suggests higher diversity.
7. **Test Selection**:
 - **Uncertainty-based ranking**: Candidates from $D_C$ are ranked by their uncertainty scores in descending order, and the top $\beta$ candidates form a preliminary subset $D'_S$.
 - **Redundancy elimination**: $D'_S$ is further filtered by removing inputs that are close in the latent space (i.e., have low diversity scores) to reduce redundancy. If filtering results in fewer than $\beta$ inputs, the remaining slots are filled from the unselected candidates in $D_C$ based on their uncertainty scores.

#### 3.2. Agent-based Data Synthesis and Model Repair

This phase utilizes the discovered failures to generate synthetic supervisory data and fine-tune the VLA model.

1. **Abstraction Generation**: For each identified failure case, VLAMotor replays the failure trajectory in the simulator. It then uses ICAL to generate a high-level structured semantic abstraction ($L_t$) of the failure. $L_t$ includes causal relations, state changes, subgoal decomposition, and state abstraction, providing semantic guidance for repair.
2. **Failure Reflection and Skill Sequencing**: Instead of direct low-level action prediction, repair is formulated as constructing an executable program from a predefined repair skill library.
 - **Repair Skill Library**: Comprises 16 parameterized repair primitives categorized into:
 - **Perception Repair**: e.g., `relocalize object`, `recenter grasp point`.
 - **Motion Repair**: e.g., `Move-to to pregrasp`, `adjust end effector pose`.
 - **Interaction Repair**: e.g., `retry grasp`, `change gripper width`, `verify success`.
 - **VLM Repair Planner**: A VLM-based planner takes $L_t$, current observation, and task instruction as input to produce a parameterized repair skill sequence ($P_t$). This sequence specifies which skills to invoke and with what arguments.
3. **Skill Grounded Motion Execution**: Each symbolic skill invocation $(s_i, \alpha_i)$ in $P_t$ is translated into physically executable robot actions through a fixed pipeline:
 - **Target Pose Computation**: Maps the skill type and parameters to a goal pose ($u_i$) in Cartesian space.
 - **Inverse Kinematics (IK) Solving**: Converts the target pose $u_i$ into a feasible robot joint configuration $q_i$.
 - **Motion Generation**: Produces a collision-free trajectory $\tau_i$ between successive configurations.
4. **Environment Execution and Feedback Generation**:
 - The grounded trajectories are executed in the simulator.
 - **Success Check**: Task-specific success checkers evaluate environmental conditions (e.g., object position) to determine if the repair succeeded.
 - **Iterative Repair**: If the repair fails, a VLM analyzes observations to generate diagnostic feedback (error signal, state deviation). This feedback is sent back to the abstraction generation module, initiating a new round of analysis and planning, forming an iterative repair loop until success or maximum attempts are reached.
5. **Automatic Trajectory Annotation**: Upon successful repair, the complete execution trajectory is automatically converted into supervised training data ($D_{new}$). This data is failure-aware and requires no manual labeling.
6. **Model Enhancement**: The new dataset $D_{new}$ is merged with the original training data $D_{orig}$ and used to fine-tune the original VLA model ($F$), yielding an enhanced model ($F'$). This process helps correct errors, improve robustness, and expand the training distribution.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of VLAMotor addressed three research questions using four robotic manipulation tasks (Pick up an object, Move object A to object B, Put object A on top of object B, and Put object A into object B) and three VLA models (OpenVLA, RT-1, and π_0.5).

#### 4.1. RQ1: Effectiveness in Revealing VLA Failures

- **Overall Effectiveness**: VLAMotor demonstrated high effectiveness in revealing failures, with an average of **92.33%** (1108 out of 1200) of its generated in-simulation test cases causing VLA models to fail the task.
- **Adaptation to Different VLA Models**: The framework maintained strong performance across diverse VLA models:
 - Monolithic models: RT-1 (90.50% failure rate), OpenVLA (90.25% failure rate).
 - Hierarchical model: π_0.5 (96.25% failure rate).
- **Adaptation to Different Tasks**: VLAMotor achieved consistently high failure rates across tasks with varying difficulty levels, all exceeding 88%:
 - Pick-up: 89.67% failure rate.
 - Move-to: 88.33% failure rate.
 - Put-on: 99.67% failure rate.
 - Put-into: 91.67% failure rate.
- **Comparison with Prior Work (VLATest)**: Under the same test budget, VLAMotor significantly outperformed the state-of-the-art automated testing tool VLATest:
 - **Failure Detection**: VLAMotor achieved a 90.38% failure detection rate, improving over VLATest's 83.62% by **6.76%**.
 - **Trajectory Coverage (Diversity)**: VLAMotor achieved 82.68% trajectory coverage, improving over VLATest's 63.75% by **18.93%**.
- **Analysis of Ineffective Cases**: Manual inspection of the small number of test cases (63.04% of which were ineffective) that did not trigger failures suggested limitations primarily stemmed from the candidate generation tool (VLATest) integrated into VLAMotor, rather than VLAMotor's selection strategy itself.

#### 4.2. RQ2: Effectiveness in Repairing VLA Failures

- **Overall Repair Effectiveness**: By fine-tuning VLA models with synthetic data derived from failed test cases, VLAMotor enhanced the overall success rate of VLA models by **49.25%** (from 92 initial successful cases to 683 successful cases out of 1200 new unseen tests).
- **Adaptation to Different Robotic Manipulation Tasks**: Substantial improvements were observed across all tasks:
 - Pick-up: Success rate improved by 54.33%.
 - Move-to: Success rate improved by 62.67%.
 - Put-on: Success rate improved by 37.00%.
 - Put-into: Success rate improved by 43.00%.
- **Adaptation to Different Models**: VLAMotor showed strong repair performance across different VLA model architectures:
 - RT-1-X-VLAMotor: Success rate increased by 44.00%.
 - OpenVLA-VLAMotor: Success rate increased by 48.50%.
 - π_0.5-VLAMotor: Success rate increased by 55.25%.
- **Analysis of Non-repaired Tests**: Manual inspection indicated that most persistent failures after enhancement were attributable to inherent issues in the model architecture or training process (e.g., unexplained jitter, erratic robot motion causing target loss), which cannot be directly addressed by data-augmentation-based repair.

#### 4.3. RQ3: Real-world Performance of Optimized VLA Models (Sim-to-Real Transfer)

- **Real-World Failure Exposure**: For the π_0.5 model tested on 80 new unseen real-world test inputs, VLAMotor's generated inputs proved highly effective, with **100%** (80 out of 80) of the original π_0.5 model's executions resulting in failures.
- **Real-World Success Rate Improvement**: When the simulation-enhanced π_0.5-VLAMotor model was deployed on real hardware, it significantly outperformed the original model. The enhanced model achieved **46 successful cases** out of 80, corresponding to an improvement in success rate of **57.50%** (from 0 successful cases for the original model).
- **Conclusion on Sim-to-Real**: These results indicate that VLA models enhanced solely with simulation-generated data through VLAMotor remain effective in real-world deployment, demonstrating a promising and low-cost approach for VLA model improvement.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

VLAMotor introduces a novel and comprehensive framework that addresses critical limitations in the testing and enhancement of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, offering several significant contributions and potential impacts:

1. **First Integrated VLA Enhancement Framework**: VLAMotor is presented as the first analysis framework that integrates both effective failure exposure and subsequent model enhancement through discovered failures. This closed-loop approach, from identifying diverse failure modes to leveraging them as supervisory data for finetuning, represents a methodological advancement over prior work that largely separated failure detection from repair.
2. **Improved Robustness and Generalization**: By automatically synthesizing failure-aware training data and using it to fine-tune VLA models, VLAMotor directly targets the root cause of VLA failures. The enhanced models exhibit increased robustness to edge-case configurations and improved generalization capabilities, as evidenced by significant increases in success rates across diverse tasks and model architectures. This directly contributes to more reliable VLA model deployment in practical robotic applications.
3. **Enhanced Testing Efficiency and Diversity**: The distance-aware test selection strategy employed by VLAMotor significantly improves upon existing automated testing methods. By prioritizing high-uncertainty inputs and eliminating redundancy based on latent space distances, VLAMotor generates more diverse and failure-revealing test sets under equivalent budgets. This maximizes the discovery of distinct failure modes, leading to more comprehensive model evaluation and targeted repair.
4. **Low-Cost Sim-to-Real Transfer for VLA Enhancement**: A key impact of VLAMotor is its demonstration of effective sim-to-real transfer. Enhancing VLA models solely with data generated within a simulation environment and observing substantial performance gains in real-world hardware deployment offers a promising pathway to reduce the prohibitive costs and logistical challenges associated with real-world data collection and experimentation for robotic systems. This opens a scalable and economical direction for developing more capable robots.
5. **Agent-Based Data Synthesis**: The framework's reliance on a VLM-based agent for abstracting failure trajectories into semantic representations and synthesizing repair skill sequences automates the generation of high-quality supervisory data. This overcomes the bottleneck of manual data annotation, enabling scalable model improvement.
6. **Addressing Gaps in Prior Research**: VLAMotor directly addresses the shortcomings of current VLA testing (limited coverage of manual benchmarks, redundancy in random testing) and repair (lack of mechanisms to leverage failures for model improvement) methods, providing a holistic solution.
7. **Facilitating Future Research**: The stated intention to release the artifact and dataset of VLAMotor will provide a valuable resource for the research community, enabling further exploration, benchmarking, and development of VLA testing and enhancement techniques.

**Potential Limitations and Threats to Validity**:
The authors acknowledge certain limitations. One external threat to validity is the reliance on a predefined repair-skill library. However, the authors argue this is mitigated by the library's design for generality, its coverage of key repair primitives for the target tasks, and its backward-compatible organization allowing for lightweight additions. An internal threat is the potential for subjective bias in manual analysis during evaluation. This was mitigated by standardizing procedures, involving multiple research groups for cross-validation, and calculating a Cohen's kappa coefficient of 0.764, indicating substantial agreement. Furthermore, architectural or fundamental training issues (e.g., unexplained jitter or erratic motion) that are beyond the scope of data-augmentation-based repair are identified as persistent failure modes.
