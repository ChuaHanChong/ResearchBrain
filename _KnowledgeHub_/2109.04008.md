# Graph Based Network with Contextualized Representations of Turns in Dialogue

> [!summary] Summary
> TUCORE-GCN tackles dialogue-based relation extraction by encoding dialogues with speaker embeddings, applying masked multi-head self-attention over a surrounding-turn window, and running a GCN over a heterogeneous dialogue graph of dialogue, turn, subject and object nodes with BiLSTM-injected turn order. It also recasts emotion recognition in conversation as relation extraction, reaching 73.1 F1 on DialogRE and state-of-the-art results on MELD and EmoryNLP.

## Problem

- Sentence-level relation extraction fails on dialogue, where 65.9% of relational triples in DialogRE have arguments never appearing in the same turn.
- BERT-based dialogue models learn token representations that blur subject-object roles, so asymmetric inverse relations are predicted far worse than symmetric ones.
- Prior graph approaches build latent token-level multi-view graphs that model all possible token pairs without encoding speakers, turns, or argument mentions as meaningful units.

## Method

- ==Encoding module== adds ==speaker embeddings== to a speaker-marked input sequence, letting BERT or RoBERTa produce speaker-sensitive token representations of the whole dialogue.
- ==Turn attention module== applies ==masked multi-head self-attention== with a surrounding-turn mask, so each turn's representation is reinforced only by neighbours within a window size c.
- ==Heterogeneous dialogue graph== with dialogue, turn, subject, object nodes plus dialogue, argument, speaker edges; turn nodes pass through ==BiLSTM== before each ==GCN== layer to inject sequence order.

## Results

- On DialogRE test, the BERT variant beats all BERT baselines by **5.3-7.6** F1; the RoBERTa variant reaches **73.1** F1 and **65.9** F1c, **+1.8**/**+2.2** over RoBERTa-s.
- Recast as relation extraction, the model scores **65.36** on MELD and **39.24** on EmoryNLP, beating COSMIC by **0.15** and **1.13**; DailyDialog reaches **61.91**, below CESTa's **63.12**.
- Ablations drop F1 by **1.1** (turn attention), **0.4** (speaker embedding) and **0.6** (turn-level BiLSTM) on DialogRE, with larger EmoryNLP drops of **2.17**, **1.65** and **0.89**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Giving arguments their own graph nodes rather than pooled token spans preserves subject-object asymmetry, cutting the asymmetric-symmetric F1 gap that pure attention encoders leave open.
> - Restricting attention to a small window of neighbouring turns beats unrestricted context: locality is the strongest single mechanism in the ablation.
> - Reformulating classification tasks as relation triples transfers architecture across tasks, but sequence-tagging models still win where labels are strongly autocorrelated across adjacent utterances.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Relation Extraction (RE) is a fundamental task in natural language processing (NLP) aimed at identifying semantic relationships between entities within text. Historically, RE research primarily focused on sentence-level extraction. However, a significant portion of relational facts are expressed across multiple sentences, necessitating the development of cross-sentence and document-level RE methods. Within this broader context, dialogue-based relation extraction presents a unique set of challenges. Dialogues are characterized by high frequencies of personal pronouns, lower information density compared to formal written texts, and relations often requiring reasoning over multiple turns. Recent work, such as the introduction of the DialogRE dataset by Yu et al. (2020), has highlighted the need for models capable of understanding multi-turn conversational dynamics. Existing approaches for dialogue-based RE, including BERT-based models like BERT_s and graph-based methods like GDPNet, have made progress, but often exhibit limitations, particularly with asymmetric inverse relations.

Concurrently, Emotion Recognition in Conversations (ERC) has emerged as another important dialogue-based NLP task. ERC aims to identify the emotional state of utterances within a dialogue, with applications in user behavior analysis and fake news detection. Various ERC models, including recurrence-based (e.g., DialogueRNN, COSMIC) and graph-based (e.g., DialogueGCN, RGAT) methods, have been proposed. However, these ERC-specific models are typically not designed for or directly applicable to broader dialogue-based RE tasks.

This research positions itself by addressing the complexities of dialogue-based RE through a novel graph-based architecture. A key contribution is its attempt to generalize this architecture by demonstrating its effectiveness on ERC, uniquely framing ERC as a dialogue-based RE problem. This approach aims to bridge the gap between task-specific models and more generalizable dialogue understanding frameworks, providing a unified methodology for different dialogue-based NLP tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an effective model for dialogue-based relation extraction that comprehensively addresses the inherent challenges posed by conversational data. A secondary, but significant, objective is to introduce and validate a novel approach for treating the task of Emotion Recognition in Conversations (ERC) as an instance of dialogue-based relation extraction.

The detailed motivations for these objectives stem from specific characteristics of dialogues and limitations of existing models:

- **Comprehensive Understanding of Dialogues for Relation Extraction:** Dialogues require a deeper level of understanding than individual sentences. The authors identify three key challenges that motivate their model design:
 - **Speaker Information:** The identity of the speaker for each utterance is crucial. Relational triples often depend on who is speaking, meaning a change in speaker can alter the subject or object of a relation (e.g., `(S2, per:alternate_names, Pheebs)` would change if a different speaker `S3` uttered the same line as `S2`). Existing models might not adequately capture this speaker dependency.
 - **Contextual Meaning of Turns:** Understanding an individual turn often necessitates considering its surrounding context. Pronouns or elliptical expressions in one turn are often resolved by information present in preceding or subsequent turns. For instance, "No, but he's always late" is ambiguous without the preceding question about "S2's brother." This highlights the need for models to integrate local turn context.
 - **Multi-Turn Information and Sequential Characteristics:** Relational facts in dialogues frequently span multiple turns, meaning arguments may not appear in the same utterance. The sequential nature of dialogue turns provides crucial temporal and causal links that are essential for extracting these cross-turn relations. Effectively leveraging this sequential information is a key challenge.

- **Addressing Limitations in Existing RE Models:** Current BERT-based models for dialogue-based RE, such as BERT_s, have shown good performance but struggle with asymmetric inverse relations (where swapping the subject and object arguments results in a different or non-existent relation). This limitation arises because these models, relying on self-attention, may not adequately distinguish between the roles of subject and object arguments within the input sequence.

- **Novel Framing of Emotion Recognition in Conversations (ERC):** The authors note that ERC is a challenging task with considerable practical utility (e.g., analyzing user behavior, detecting fake news). While many successful ERC models exist, they are typically task-specific. The motivation here is to demonstrate that ERC can be effectively re-framed as a dialogue-based RE problem. By defining the emotion of an utterance as a relation (speaker, emotion, utterance), the authors aim to show that a generalized dialogue understanding framework can tackle diverse NLP tasks, potentially offering a more unified and extensible approach than highly specialized models. This proposition, to the authors' knowledge, had not been explored in previous studies.

By addressing these motivations, the research aims to contribute a robust and generalizable model for intricate dialogue understanding tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed model, TUrn COntext awaRE Graph Convolutional Network (TUCORE-GCN), is structured into four main modules designed to systematically process and understand dialogues: the Encoding Module, Turn Attention Module, Dialogue Graph with Sequential Nodes Module, and Classification Module.

#### 3.1. Encoding Module

The Encoding Module prepares the input dialogue for subsequent processing, focusing on incorporating speaker information.
- **Input Representation:** It adopts the BERT_s input sequence format. A dialogue `d` consisting of `M` turns (`s_1: t_1, ..., s_M: t_M`) and an argument pair (`a_1, a_2`) are transformed. Speaker IDs `s_i` are mapped to special tokens `[S1]` or `[S2]` if they correspond to argument `a_1` or `a_2`, respectively; otherwise, they remain `s_i`. The final input sequence is constructed as `[CLS] ˆd [SEP] ˆa_1 [SEP] ˆa_2 [SEP]`, where `ˆa_k` represents the arguments, potentially as `[S_k]`.
- **Speaker Embeddings:** To explicitly capture speaker change information, additional speaker embeddings `E_s(ˆs_i)` are added to the token representations for each turn. Tokens without specific speaker information (e.g., separator tokens) receive a default embedding `E_s(])`.
- **Context Encoder:** The combined token representations, enriched with speaker and positional embeddings, are then fed into a pre-trained transformer-based encoder (e.g., BERT or RoBERTa) to generate speaker-sensitive contextualized token representations.

#### 3.2. Turn Attention Module

This module is designed to enhance the representation of each turn by incorporating context from surrounding turns.
- **Mechanism:** It applies a Masked Multi-Head Self-Attention mechanism to the output of the Encoding Module.
- **Surrounding Turn Mask:** A crucial component is the "surrounding turn mask." For any token `x_m` belonging to turn `F(x_m)`, the mask allows it to attend only to other tokens `x_n` that fall within its own turn or within a specified "surround turn window size" `c` (i.e., turns from `F(x_m)-c` to `F(x_m)+c`). Tokens outside this window or outside the dialogue are masked with `-∞`, effectively preventing them from contributing to the attention score. This ensures that each turn's representation is refined based on its immediate conversational context.

#### 3.3. Dialogue Graph with Sequential Nodes Module

This module constructs a heterogeneous graph to model complex interactions within the dialogue at various levels and integrate sequential information.
- **Node Types:** The graph comprises four types of nodes:
 - **Dialogue Node:** Represents the overall dialogue context. Its initial representation is derived from the `[CLS]` token's output from the Turn Attention Module.
 - **Turn Nodes:** One node for each of the `M` turns. The initial representation for the `i`-th turn node is the average of the token representations corresponding to `ˆs_i: t_i` from the Turn Attention Module's output.
 - **Subject Node:** Represents the subject argument (`a_1`). Its initial representation is the average of the token representations of `ˆa_1`.
 - **Object Node:** Represents the object argument (`a_2`). Its initial representation is the average of the token representations of `ˆa_2`.
- **Edge Types:** Three types of edges connect these nodes:
 - **Dialogue Edge:** Connects all turn nodes to the single dialogue node, allowing the dialogue node to aggregate turn-level information.
 - **Argument Edge:** Links a turn node to the subject node and/or object node if the corresponding argument is explicitly mentioned within that turn (`ˆs_i: t_i`). This models interactions between turns and specific arguments.
 - **Speaker Edge:** Fully connects all turn nodes that were uttered by the same speaker. This captures inter-turn dependencies specific to a particular participant.
- **GCN with BiLSTM Integration:** To process this heterogeneous graph, a Graph Convolutional Network (GCN) is employed. Critically, to inject sequential information into the turn nodes, a Bidirectional LSTM (BiLSTM) is applied *to the turn nodes' features* before each GCN layer's aggregation step. This means that the turn nodes first have their representations updated based on their sequential order in the dialogue, and then these "sequentially aware" turn node representations are used in the GCN's aggregation. The GCN aggregates features from neighbors based on the different edge types, `k`, allowing nodes to learn representations informed by their local graph structure.

#### 3.4. Classification Module

The final module is responsible for predicting relations based on the learned node features.
- **Feature Concatenation:** To provide a comprehensive view for classification, the representations of the dialogue node, subject node, and object node are concatenated from *all GCN layers* (including the initial, pre-GCN layer). This multi-layer concatenation `C = [h^(0)_d; h^(0)_s; h^(0)_o; ...; h^(G)_d; h^(G)_s; h^(G)_o;]` ensures that features at different levels of abstraction are utilized.
- **Relation Prediction:** For each possible relation type `r`, a dedicated vector `W_r` is introduced. The probability `P_r` of the existence of relation `r` between the arguments is computed using a sigmoid activation function: `P_r = sigmoid(C W_r^T)`.
- **Training:** The entire model is trained end-to-end using a cross-entropy loss function.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluates TUCORE-GCN on two distinct tasks: dialogue-based Relation Extraction (RE) and Emotion Recognition in Conversations (ERC), using two model variants: TUCORE-GCN_BERT (based on BERT-base) and TUCORE-GCN_RoBERTa (based on RoBERTa-large).

#### 4.1. Dialogue-Based Relation Extraction

Experiments were conducted on the DialogRE dataset, using F1 and F1c scores as evaluation metrics.
- **Performance Comparison (BERT-based models):**
 - TUCORE-GCN_BERT significantly outperformed all BERT-based baselines. On the DialogRE test set, it achieved 65.5 F1 and 60.2 F1c. This represents improvements of 6.0 F1 and 6.0 F1c over BERT_s (59.5 F1, 54.2 F1c), and 5.3 F1 and 2.9 F1c over GDPNet (60.2 F1, 57.3 F1c), which was a previous state-of-the-art BERT-based model.
- **Performance Comparison (RoBERTa-based models):**
 - TUCORE-GCN_RoBERTa further improved performance, achieving new state-of-the-art results on DialogRE with 73.1 F1 and 65.9 F1c on the test set. This is an improvement of 1.8 F1 and 2.2 F1c over the strong baseline RoBERTa_s (71.3 F1, 63.7 F1c).
- **Conclusion:** The results indicate that TUCORE-GCN is effective in dialogue-based RE, demonstrating robust performance improvements across different encoder backbones and setting new benchmarks on the DialogRE dataset.

#### 4.2. Analysis on Inverse Relations

An analysis was conducted on the DialogRE development set to assess the model's performance on asymmetric and symmetric inverse relations, a known challenge for BERT-like models.
- **Problem with BERT:** Previous BERT-based models struggled with asymmetric inverse relations, showing a large performance gap compared to symmetric inverse relations, as they do not inherently learn the precise roles of subject and object arguments.
- **TUCORE-GCN Improvement:** TUCORE-GCN_BERT significantly reduced the F1 score difference between asymmetric and symmetric inverse relations to 5.8, which was the smallest difference among BERT-based models tested. For RoBERTa-based models, TUCORE-GCN_RoBERTa reduced this difference by 2.9 compared to RoBERTa_s.
- **Conclusion:** The explicit modeling of subject and object arguments as separate nodes within the graph structure of TUCORE-GCN helps mitigate the limitations of previous models in predicting inverse relations.

#### 4.3. Emotion Recognition in Conversations (ERC)

The paper introduced a novel approach of treating ERC as a dialogue-based RE task, framing emotion prediction as a (speaker, emotion, utterance) triple. This approach was tested on MELD, EmoryNLP, and DailyDialog datasets.
- **Validation of ERC as RE:** The baseline RoBERTa_r (RoBERTa using the proposed RE-like input format) outperformed the standard RoBERTa baseline, suggesting that framing ERC as RE is a beneficial approach.
- **Performance Comparison (TUCORE-GCN_RoBERTa):**
 - **MELD:** TUCORE-GCN_RoBERTa achieved 65.36 weighted-F1, slightly outperforming COSMIC (65.21 F1), the previous state-of-the-art for this dataset.
 - **EmoryNLP:** TUCORE-GCN_RoBERTa obtained 39.24 weighted-F1, surpassing COSMIC (38.11 F1) by a notable margin.
 - **DailyDialog:** TUCORE-GCN_RoBERTa achieved 61.91 micro-F1, showing competitive performance, although it was slightly lower than CESTa (63.12 F1). The authors attribute CESTa's higher performance on DailyDialog to its CRF layer, which benefits from the dataset's high emotional consistency, a characteristic less prominent in MELD.
- **Conclusion:** The proposed method for treating ERC as a dialogue-based RE task is effective. TUCORE-GCN_RoBERTa achieved state-of-the-art performance on MELD and EmoryNLP, and competitive results on DailyDialog, demonstrating its versatility across different dialogue understanding tasks.

#### 4.4. Ablation Study

An ablation study was performed to assess the contribution of key modules and mechanisms within TUCORE-GCN_RoBERTa on DialogRE, MELD, and EmoryNLP.
- **Removal of Speaker Embedding:** Eliminating speaker embeddings resulted in performance drops across all datasets (0.4 F1 on DialogRE, 0.72 F1 on MELD, 1.65 F1 on EmoryNLP). This confirms the importance of speaker change information for encoding dialogue context.
- **Removal of Turn Attention Module:** Disabling the turn attention module led to the most significant performance drops (1.1 F1 on DialogRE, 0.77 F1 on MELD, 2.17 F1 on EmoryNLP). This highlights the critical role of the surrounding turn mask in capturing contextualized turn representations.
- **Removal of Turn-Level BiLSTM:** Removing the BiLSTM layer for turn nodes in the graph module also caused performance degradation (0.6 F1 on DialogRE, 0.34 F1 on MELD, 0.89 F1 on EmoryNLP). This indicates that integrating sequential information into the turn nodes before GCN processing is beneficial for learning node features.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research presents TUCORE-GCN, a model that enhances the understanding of dialogues for natural language processing tasks, demonstrating its significance through several key contributions and potential impacts.

- **Advanced Dialogue Understanding:** TUCORE-GCN is explicitly designed to address fundamental challenges in dialogue understanding, such as the critical role of speaker identity, the need for contextualized turn representations, and the importance of multi-turn reasoning. By modeling these aspects through its unique architecture, the model provides a more comprehensive approach to dialogue comprehension.

- **State-of-the-Art Performance:** The model established new state-of-the-art results on the DialogRE dataset for dialogue-based relation extraction and on the MELD and EmoryNLP datasets for emotion recognition in conversations. This indicates the robust performance and effectiveness of the proposed architecture across distinct, complex dialogue-based NLP tasks.

- **Novel Task Framing for Generalization:** The innovative approach of treating Emotion Recognition in Conversations (ERC) as a dialogue-based relation extraction problem is a significant contribution. This re-framing allows a single model architecture to tackle different NLP tasks, suggesting a potential for greater generalization and efficiency compared to developing separate, task-specific models. It demonstrates that relation extraction frameworks can be extended to other fine-grained classification problems within conversational contexts.

- **Architectural Contributions:** The introduction of specific architectural elements contributes to the field:
 - The **surrounding turn mask** in the Turn Attention Module effectively captures context-sensitive turn representations, which is crucial for resolving ambiguities and understanding turn meanings in dialogues.
 - The **heterogeneous dialogue graph**, with its distinct node types (dialogue, turn, subject, object) and specialized edge types (dialogue, argument, speaker), provides a structured way to model intricate interactions and dependencies inherent in multi-party conversations.
 - The **integration of Bidirectional LSTMs with Graph Convolutional Networks** for sequential nodes combines the strengths of sequence modeling with graph-based reasoning, allowing the model to leverage both the temporal order of turns and their complex relational dependencies.

- **Broader Applicability:** Given TUCORE-GCN's design principles, which are rooted in generalized dialogue characteristics, the model shows promise for application in other dialogue-based natural language understanding tasks beyond relation extraction and emotion recognition. This suggests a pathway for developing more versatile and robust models for the expanding domain of conversational AI.

In summary, this work advances the state of dialogue-based NLP by providing a performant, principled, and generalizable architecture that addresses core challenges in understanding conversational data, with implications for a wide range of downstream applications.
