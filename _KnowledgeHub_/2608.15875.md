# GigaBrain-0.7: Scaling Embodied Foundation Models to Emergent Capabilities with a Three-System Architecture

> [!summary] Summary
> GigaBrain-0.7 is an embodied foundation model that unifies a vision-language planner, a flow-matching action expert, and a world-value model predicting future subgoal images and progress scores to condition action generation. Trained in one stage on 37,000+ hours of multi-embodiment data, it beats pi-0.5 and prior GigaBrain models on real-robot language-following and complex manipulation across two platforms.

## Problem

- Most VLAs remain reactive observation-to-action predictors with no mechanism to anticipate future states or judge whether current behavior is making progress.
- Heterogeneous robot data differ in embodiment, action space, and execution conditions; without alignment, greater diversity can introduce interference rather than transfer.
- Multi-stage training recipes separate vision-language representation learning from continuous action optimization, fragmenting understanding, prediction, action, and improvement across the lifecycle.

## Method

- **Three-System architecture**: System 2 (PaliGemma2 VLM) handles understanding and subtask planning, System 1 (PaliGemma2 plus 0.5B Action Expert MoT) generates flow-matching actions, System 3 (GigaWorld-1-based World Value Model) predicts subgoal images and task-progress values.
- One-stage joint pretraining with ==Soft Knowledge Insulation==: jointly optimizes next-token-prediction and flow-matching losses while attenuating, not blocking, action gradients flowing into the VLM backbone.
- System 3 conditioning with ==stochastic condition dropout==: subgoal images and a binary advantage signal derived from predicted value differences are injected into System 1's prompt during post-training, later refined via offline AWR and online actor-critic RL with human corrective rollouts.

## Results

- Post-training language-following success averages **91.5%** on AgileX PiPER and **84.2%** on Maker H01, versus **88.8%** and **75.2%** for pi-0.5 and **76.1%** and **69.6%** for GigaBrain-0.1.
- Post-training complex manipulation success averages **84.9%** on PiPER and **74.1%** on H01, versus pi-0.5's **76.6%** and **45.2%**, with the largest gains on deformable and multi-stage household tasks.
- System 3 ablation on gift-wrapping raises success from **0%** (base) to **80%** and score from **61.1%** to **96.7%** when both subgoal-image and value conditioning are combined.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Unifying discrete and continuous action objectives in one training stage avoids the optimization fragmentation that staged VLM-then-action pipelines introduce.
> - Predictive and value conditioning can improve task quality and execution efficiency even when binary success is already saturated, so richer metrics than success rate reveal gains that success alone hides.
> - Heterogeneous data sources such as robot, near-hand, and egocentric human demonstrations are complementary rather than redundant, so scaling source diversity matters as much as scaling volume.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of vision-language-action (VLA) models has established a prominent paradigm for generalist embodied agents. These models extend large-scale vision-language representations to facilitate robot control, demonstrating the ability to complete complex and long-horizon tasks, particularly in structured environments. The existing landscape of VLA architectures includes autoregressive VLM-as-Actor models (e.g., RT-2, OpenVLA), serial (cascaded) VLM–action-expert models (e.g., GR00T N1, Qwen-RobotManip), and parallel Mixture-of-Transformers (MoT) models (e.g., π0, π0.5, GigaBrain-0). Training paradigms range from multi-stage adaptation to one-stage joint optimization of vision-language and action objectives, often incorporating task-specific post-training and reinforcement learning.

Despite progress, several challenges persist in scaling VLA systems. These include the need for more effective architectural designs, the ability to generalize across substantially larger and more heterogeneous data regimes, and broader generalization across diverse tasks and robot embodiments. Many current VLA systems primarily focus on reactive observation-to-action prediction, with limited mechanisms for anticipating future states or evaluating behavioral progress. Integrating heterogeneous robot data, which varies significantly across embodiments and action spaces, also presents challenges without appropriate alignment and contextualization.

GigaBrain-0.7 positions itself within this landscape by building upon the parallel VLM-Action Expert architecture and the GigaBrain-0 series. It aims to address the identified limitations by proposing a unified learning system that integrates heterogeneous experience and multi-embodiment pretraining with coordinated understanding, prediction, action, and continual improvement. Specifically, it extends the architectural design by introducing a three-system framework and scales pretraining data significantly to enhance generalization capabilities. The work also incorporates world model-powered components for explicit prediction and evaluation, an area that has seen increasing interest for improving embodied foundation models by using predictive structure for data generation, unified modeling, and policy conditioning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind GigaBrain-0.7 stems from recognized limitations in current VLA systems. While existing VLA models have demonstrated capabilities in specific tasks, they face ongoing challenges in effectively designing architectures, scaling to substantially larger and more diverse datasets, and achieving broad generalization across a wider array of tasks and robot embodiments. A critical gap identified is the prevalent focus on reactive observation-to-action prediction, which often lacks mechanisms for anticipating future states or evaluating the progress of ongoing behaviors. Such reactive approaches can hinder robust performance in complex, long-horizon tasks where planning and foresight are beneficial. Furthermore, integrating diverse robot data from various embodiments and action spaces without inducing interference remains a significant challenge. The authors contend that building truly capable embodied foundation models requires a unified learning system that can integrate heterogeneous experience and multi-embodiment pretraining with a coordinated approach to understanding, prediction, action, and continuous improvement.

To address these motivations, the key objectives of GigaBrain-0.7 are:
1. **Develop an effective architectural design:** To unify understanding, prediction, and action, moving beyond reactive policies by coordinating these functions within a three-system architecture.
2. **Scale pretraining data:** To leverage substantially larger and more heterogeneous data regimes, aiming to overcome data diversity challenges and improve learning.
3. **Enhance generalization:** To achieve broader generalization capabilities across diverse robot embodiments and real-world scenarios, thereby improving out-of-the-box performance and adaptability.
4. **Introduce an aligned training methodology:** To implement one-stage alignment training that jointly optimizes vision-language understanding and multi-embodiment action generation, aiming to prevent optimization fragmentation often seen in multi-stage pipelines.
5. **Demonstrate performance improvements:** To achieve substantial improvements in foundation zero-shot capabilities, language-conditioned instruction following, and post-training task success rates compared to existing state-of-the-art models and previous iterations of the GigaBrain series.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

GigaBrain-0.7 employs a structured approach encompassing a novel three-system architecture, extensive data curation, a multi-stage training pipeline, and comprehensive evaluation.

**Architecture:**
The system is organized into a three-system architecture:
- **System 1 (Action and Control):** This system is a Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model based on a PaliGemma2 (3B) vision-language model (VLM) backbone coupled with a dedicated Action Expert (0.5B). It generates continuous action chunks using a flow matching (FM) formulation. The system is designed to be embodiment-aware, accommodating varying degrees of freedom and control modes across different robot morphologies. Inputs include current multimodal observations, short-term visual memory, subtask instructions from System 2, proprioceptive states, a Robot ID, and predictive signals from System 3. A Mixture-of-Transformers (MoT) design couples the VLM and Action Expert, with Soft Knowledge Insulation (Soft KI) applied to gradients from the action expert entering the VLM backbone to allow controlled adaptation while preserving general VLM capabilities. It supports both next-token prediction (NTP) for discrete actions and FM for continuous actions.
- **System 2 (Understanding and Planning):** This system is a Vision Language Model (VLM), also based on PaliGemma2 (3B). Its role is to interpret current and historical visual observations through temporal context modeling, track task progress, and decompose long-horizon instructions into executable subtasks using hierarchical prompts. It generates chain-of-thought reasoning that describes the visual context and produces explicit subtask instructions for System 1.
- **System 3 (Prediction and Evaluation):** This component utilizes a world value model built upon GigaWorld-1 (5B). It provides System 1 with prospective information by predicting future visual states (subgoal images) and estimating scalar state values representing task progress. The subgoal image is the last frame of a short predicted video sequence, and the value estimate is converted into a binary advantage condition (indicating increasing task progress). These signals are supplied to System 1 as additional conditioning interfaces.

**Data Curation:**
GigaBrain-0.7 is pretrained on a multi-source training corpus comprising over 37,000 hours of heterogeneous embodied trajectory data and approximately 270 million vision-language samples.
- **Embodied Trajectory Data:** This corpus includes 20,535.65 hours of real-robot data (55.12%) spanning 16 robot morphologies, 8,251.83 hours of Universal Manipulation Interface (UMI) demonstrations (22.15%) for near-hand manipulation, 2,862.36 hours of egocentric (EGO) human demonstrations (7.68%) for first-person interaction priors, 1,453.92 hours of simulation data (3.90%), and 4,153.22 hours of world model-generated data (11.15%) to expand diversity.
- **VLM Image–Text/Question-Answering Data:** Comprising 271,976,674 samples, this dataset covers image captioning, general visual question answering, spatial-relation reasoning, region grounding, point prediction, affordance understanding, and robotic task understanding. It includes self-collected embodied VLM annotations derived from trajectory data using LLM-assisted methods (e.g., Qwen3.6-27B).
- **Data Processing Pipeline:** A unified pipeline converts raw data into a standardized LeRobot v3.0 format. This involves constructing a unified robot state and action representation (with masks for missing dimensions and shared parameters for different embodiments), LLM-assisted language instruction rewriting and subtask annotation, and multi-stage quality control (e.g., extreme-value filtering, stationary segment removal, kinematic consistency correction, and training-loss-based anomaly filtering).

**Training Stages:**
The training process unfolds in four distinct stages:
1. **VLA Pre-Training:** Systems 1 and 2 are jointly trained on the full heterogeneous corpus. This one-stage framework optimizes both NTP-based objectives for language, subtask, and discrete actions, and FM-based objectives for continuous actions. Soft KI is applied to control gradient flow from the Action Expert to the VLM backbone. Temporal-Spatial Blocks in the visual encoder incorporate short-term visual memory. Hierarchical task supervision mixes task-level and subtask-level instructions.
2. **World Model Pre-Training:** System 3, based on GigaWorld-1, is trained in two stages. First, robot-centric video pre-training adapts its visual dynamics understanding to robotic manipulation to generate future observations. Second, a value pathway is trained to estimate scalar task progress.
3. **World-Model-Powered VLA Post-Training:** The pre-trained VLA (Systems 1 and 2) is fine-tuned on task-specific demonstrations. During this stage, System 3 remains frozen, providing its generated subgoal images and value-derived binary advantage conditions as additional inputs to System 1. Stochastic condition dropout is applied to these signals.
4. **Experience-Driven Reinforcement Learning:** The post-trained policy is further refined. An offline stage uses an Advantage-Weighted Regression (AWR) style objective on human-in-the-loop (HIL) rollout data, weighting successful and high-progress segments more heavily. This is followed by an online reinforcement learning stage, where the refined policy is redeployed, and human corrections at difficult states are incorporated into an actor-critic framework.

**Evaluation:**
The evaluation involved real-world assessments on AgileX PiPER/PiPER-X and Maker H01 robot platforms, comparative analyses against various VLA baselines (e.g., π0.5, GigaBrain-0.1, Xiaomi-Robotics-1, Galaxea G0.5), and benchmark evaluations on embodied vision-language tasks (MiMo-Embodied) and simulation environments (RoboTwin 2.0, EBench, RoboColiseum). Metrics included success rates, task scores, and completion times, across in-distribution and out-of-distribution tasks, and zero-shot, language-following, and complex manipulation scenarios.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of GigaBrain-0.7 yielded several findings regarding architectural design, data scaling, and performance across various benchmarks and real-world scenarios.

**Backbone and Architecture Comparison:**
Comparative studies of VLM backbones for System 1 indicated that PaliGemma2 (3B) exhibited strong performance across structured manipulation, language-conditioned object interaction, and deformable-object manipulation, including non-zero success on shirt folding tasks. This backbone was subsequently selected for further experiments. Regarding VLM–action expert coupling, the Dual Stream architecture demonstrated the strongest real-robot performance across evaluated tasks, including the more challenging shirt-folding task, compared to Last-Layer or Multi-Layer Cross Attention variants.

**Data Scaling Effects:**
Increased pretraining data consistently corresponded to lower validation loss, suggesting continued optimization benefits with larger data scales. In real-robot evaluations, increasing robot-data scale improved task success rates, with a more pronounced effect observed on complex tasks such as chaotic clothes folding. The inclusion of diverse human demonstration data (UMI and EGO) in the pretraining mixture, alongside robot trajectories, resulted in stronger overall performance, and this benefit persisted after task-specific post-training.

**Temporal Context Analysis:**
Qualitative analysis indicated that the incorporation of short-term visual memory through Temporal-Spatial Blocks helped the policy resolve ambiguities in visually similar, recurring states during manipulation. This enabled the policy to break out of repeated-action cycles and continue task execution, whereas policies without temporal context could become trapped in such loops.

**System 3 Prediction and Evaluation Analysis:**
System 3, the World Value Model, demonstrated the ability to predict future visual observations and estimate scalar task progress values. The inferred advantage signals showed a decrease during imagined failure trajectories and an increase during recovery, suggesting effective discrimination of favorable states. Ablation studies confirmed that both future subgoal image conditioning and value-derived advantage conditioning improved task success rates, average task scores, and reduced completion times across tasks like clothes folding, gift wrapping, and cube sorting. Their combined use generally yielded the highest performance, indicating that these predictive and evaluative signals contributed to more robust task execution.

**Foundation Model Evaluation (Out-of-the-Box Capabilities):**
- **Language Following:** The pretrained GigaBrain-0.7 policy demonstrated the ability to follow diverse language instructions on both AgileX PiPER and Maker H01 without task-specific adaptation, performing object selection, scene rearrangement, and multi-step manipulation.
- **Generalization:** The policy exhibited target understanding, spatial grounding, and compositional generalization to out-of-distribution (OOD) object instances, spatial arrangements, and task compositions (e.g., unseen pepper instances with randomized container placements). However, a reduction in success rates was observed in OOD settings compared to in-distribution tasks.
- **Complex Manipulation:** The pretrained policy demonstrated non-trivial out-of-the-box performance on complex manipulation tasks requiring sustained interaction, multi-stage execution, and deformable-object handling, such as folding unseen garment instances from unstructured initial states. This indicated transfer of reusable interaction structure beyond memorized object appearances or initial states.

**Post-Training Evaluation:**
- **Multi-Task Language Following:** After task-specific post-training, GigaBrain-0.7 showed improved average success rates over the preceding GigaBrain-0.1 model and was competitive with or exceeded π0.5 across a range of language-conditioned manipulation tasks (e.g., color discrimination, target selection, directional reasoning) on both AgileX PiPER and Maker H01.
- **Complex Manipulation:** In tasks requiring sustained contact, multi-stage execution, or deformable-object manipulation, GigaBrain-0.7 provided consistent improvements over GigaBrain-0.1 and remained competitive with or stronger than π0.5 on both robot embodiments. This included tasks such as playdough kneading, tableware arranging, and household item storage.

**Benchmark Evaluation:**
- **Embodied Vision-Language Evaluation (MiMo-Embodied):** GigaBrain-0.7 achieved the highest overall and Spatial Understanding averages among compared open-source models before the incorporation of MiMo data into pretraining, indicating strong visual-semantic and spatial reasoning capabilities.
- **Simulation Benchmark Evaluation:**
 - **RoboTwin 2.0:** GigaBrain-0.7 achieved the strongest overall performance and ranked first in the challenging Hard (domain-randomized) setting under the multi-task Co-Train protocol, demonstrating robustness to visual and environmental variations.
 - **EBench:** The model obtained the highest overall task success rate and aggregate task score among publicly available VLA models, extending its advantage to mobile manipulation, long-horizon tasks, and precise interaction.
 - **RoboColiseum:** GigaBrain-0.7 showed the strongest performance across all four evaluated dimensions: instruction following, spatial reasoning, robustness, and general manipulation, among the compared open-source models.

**Experience-Driven Reinforcement Learning:**
A staged experience-reinforcement pipeline demonstrated consistent, incremental improvements on real-robot tasks. Starting from an SFT baseline, offline reinforcement learning using advantage-weighted regression improved average success rates from 30.0% to 57.5%. Subsequent online reinforcement learning, incorporating human corrections, further increased average success rates to 100% across all four evaluated tasks, including long-horizon and precision-critical scenarios (e.g., Link Installation, Cable Tie Insertion and Tying, Bearing Installation).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

GigaBrain-0.7 represents an advancement in the field of embodied artificial intelligence by addressing several challenges in scaling VLA models for generalist robot control. The proposed three-system architecture, which explicitly coordinates understanding, prediction, and action, contributes to the development of more capable and robust embodied agents. The findings suggest that this architectural design, combined with large-scale, heterogeneous data pretraining, can lead to emergent capabilities in diverse real-world and simulated scenarios.

The demonstrated improvements in generalization across multiple robot embodiments and task types, including zero-shot and out-of-distribution performance, indicate progress towards more adaptable and less task-specific robotic systems. The incorporation of a dedicated prediction and evaluation system (System 3) is a notable contribution, as it provides the policy with foresight and progress awareness, which proved beneficial for enhancing task success rates, execution efficiency, and overall robustness, especially in complex and long-horizon tasks. This moves beyond purely reactive control towards more proactive and guided behavior.

Furthermore, the integration of a multi-stage, experience-driven reinforcement learning pipeline allows for continuous policy improvement from real-robot rollouts and human corrections. This iterative refinement process, which leverages both offline experience and targeted online interventions, has the potential to make robot deployment and adaptation more efficient and effective, particularly for overcoming precision-critical failure states encountered in real-world environments.

The comprehensive data curation strategy, encompassing real-robot, human demonstration (UMI, EGO), simulation, and world-model-generated data, along with sophisticated data processing, contributes to the scalability and diversity of the learned representations. The commitment to open-sourcing the training code and pretrained model weights is expected to facilitate further research and development within the embodied AI community, fostering reproducibility and accelerating progress. The demonstrated capabilities across household and industrial scenarios suggest a potential for broader deployment of general-purpose robots in various practical applications.
