# RH20T: A Comprehensive Robotic Dataset for Learning Diverse Skills in One-Shot

> [!summary] Summary
> A comprehensive real-world robotic manipulation dataset, RH20T, comprising over 110,000 multi-modal sequences across 147 diverse tasks, has been collected by researchers at Shanghai Jiao Tong University. This dataset enables robotic policies to achieve improved few-shot learning and generalization capabilities compared to non-pretrained models.

## Problem

- Robotics lacked large, diverse real-world datasets for complex, contact-rich manipulation tasks, hindering the development of generalizable robotic policies.
- Existing robot learning methods often relied solely on visual input, overlooking crucial multi-modal sensory data like tactile or force feedback for intricate interactions.
- Traditional tele-operation methods for data collection were inefficient and unsafe for capturing intricate contact-rich interactions, limiting data quality and scale.

## Method

- Developed **RH20T**, a dataset of over 110,000 real-world robotic manipulation sequences featuring 147 tasks and 42 skills, emphasizing complex, contact-rich interactions.
- Employed an advanced ==tele-operation system== utilizing ==haptic devices== and ==force-torque sensors== for precise, safe, and intuitive data collection.
- Captured ==multi-modal sensory information==, including visual (RGB, depth, IR), tactile, force-torque, audio, and proprioceptive data, alongside paired human demonstration videos and language descriptions.

## Results

- Pre-training robot learning models on RH20T consistently improved task success rates and accelerated model convergence for various manipulation tasks.
- Models pre-trained on RH20T demonstrated superior few-shot learning capabilities, achieving higher performance with significantly fewer new demonstrations compared to non-pretrained models.
- The dataset enabled improved generalization, allowing pre-trained models to perform effectively with novel objects and environmental variations not seen during initial training.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Collecting real-world, large-scale, and multi-modal robotic manipulation data, particularly for contact-rich tasks, requires an advanced and intuitive tele-operation system.
> - Exposure to a wide variety of tasks, robot embodiments, and environmental conditions during pre-training is critical for enabling few-shot learning and generalization in robotic policies.
> - Integrating diverse sensory inputs (visual, tactile, force, audio, proprioception) allows for learning more robust and versatile manipulation skills, especially for complex interactions.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper "RH20T" directly addresses a critical bottleneck in the field of robotic manipulation: the scarcity of large, diverse, and comprehensive real-world datasets. This limitation has historically hindered the development of generalizable and robust robotic policies, particularly for complex, contact-rich tasks in open domains.

**Current Landscape Challenges:**
- **Data Scarcity:** Despite growing interest in robot learning, the community has long lacked large-scale, diverse robotic manipulation datasets in the real world. Data acquisition is notoriously challenging, requiring significant effort in configuring diverse robot platforms, setting up varied environments, and gathering high-quality manipulation trajectories.
- **Simplicity of Tasks:** Most existing research and datasets for imitation learning and foundation models have primarily focused on "simple" cases, such as pushing or pick-and-place tasks. While these tasks are foundational, they do not encompass the vast complexity of real-world human-like manipulation.
- **Over-reliance on Visual Modality:** Many contemporary robot learning approaches rely *solely* on visual guidance. However, as the authors rightly point out, human manipulation often integrates multiple senses, particularly tactile perception, which is crucial for contact-rich interactions and scenarios where visual information is occluded or insufficient. Previous datasets largely neglect these multi-modal aspects, especially force and tactile feedback.
- **Inefficient Tele-operation:** Traditional methods for collecting expert robot trajectories, such as 3D mice, keyboards, or VR remotes, are often inefficient, unintuitive, and pose safety risks for contact-rich tasks due to lack of force feedback and motion drift.

**RH20T's Contribution to the Landscape:**
RH20T positions itself as a transformative contribution by directly tackling these limitations. It provides:
- **Unprecedented Scale and Diversity:** It is presented as the largest real-world robotic manipulation dataset to date, featuring over 110,000 sequences across 147 tasks/42 skills, covering a wide range of complex, contact-rich interactions (e.g., cutting, plugging, slicing, pouring). This scale is essential for training robust, generalizable policies and fostering the development of "foundation models."
- **Multi-Modal Richness:** Unlike predominantly visual datasets, RH20T comprehensively integrates visual (RGB, depth, IR), tactile (6DoF force-torque, fingertip tactile), audio, and proprioceptive information. This multi-modal emphasis acknowledges the importance of diverse sensory inputs for complex manipulation, drawing parallels to human dexterity.
- **Foundation for Generalization:** The dataset's design, including diversity in robots, grippers, environments, and human interference, is explicitly aimed at enabling robots to learn transferable skills and generalize to novel scenarios with minimal effort, aligning with the "one-shot imitation learning" paradigm.
- **Enabling Foundation Models:** The authors explicitly draw parallels to the success of large-scale datasets in natural language processing (NLP) and computer vision (CV) that have led to the emergence of powerful foundation models. RH20T aims to play a similar role in robotics, moving beyond task-specific models towards more general skill learning.
- **Improved Data Collection:** By employing a haptic device with force rendering and force-torque sensors for tele-operation, the authors demonstrate a more intuitive, precise, and safe data collection methodology for contact-rich tasks, overcoming a significant barrier for creating such datasets.

In essence, RH20T addresses a fundamental need in robot learning by providing the data infrastructure required to push the boundaries of robotic intelligence from narrow, pre-programmed tasks to broad, adaptable, and human-like manipulation capabilities in complex, real-world environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of the RH20T project is to **unlock the potential for a robotic agent to generalize to hundreds of real-world skills with multi-modal perception.** This ambitious goal is motivated by several critical limitations in the current state of robot learning research:

1. **Bridging the Gap from Simple to Complex Tasks:** Current robotic manipulation research, especially in one-shot imitation learning and robotic foundation models, predominantly demonstrates algorithms on simple tasks (e.g., pushing, picking, placing). The primary motivation is to enable robots to acquire more complex, practical skills found in everyday human activities that often involve intricate contact dynamics and require fine motor control.

2. **Addressing Data Scarcity and Diversity:** A major impediment to exploring complex tasks is the severe lack of large, diverse robotic manipulation datasets collected in the real world. Existing datasets are often small, limited in task variety, or confined to specific robot platforms and environments. The authors are motivated to overcome the "huge barriers associated with data acquisition," including configuring diverse robot platforms, creating varied environments, and gathering robust manipulation trajectories.

3. **Incorporating Multi-Modal Perception Beyond Vision:** The vast majority of current methods focus solely on visual guidance. However, human manipulation relies heavily on tactile feedback, especially in contact-rich interactions. The paper is motivated by the physiological observation that humans with impaired digital sensibility struggle with manipulations relying only on vision. Therefore, a key objective is to integrate crucial sensory information beyond vision, such as force and audio, to enable learning of diverse manipulations in open environments, where visual occlusion or ambiguity might occur.

4. **Improving Data Collection Efficiency and Safety:** Traditional tele-operation methods (3D mice, VR remotes) are often inefficient, unintuitive, and unsafe for collecting data involving rich-contact interactions, frequently leading to collisions and emergency stops. The motivation here is to develop and utilize a more precise, intuitive, and safe tele-operation system (haptic device with force feedback) to efficiently gather high-quality data for contact-rich tasks without compromising robot safety or data fidelity.

5. **Facilitating Robotic Foundation Models:** Inspired by the transformative impact of large-scale datasets in NLP and CV that led to foundation models, the authors are motivated to provide a dataset of similar scale and diversity for robotics. Their ultimate objective is to accelerate the emergence of powerful foundation models capable of general skill learning, which can transfer learned policies to novel tasks with minimal new demonstrations.

In summary, the core objective is to create a comprehensive, high-quality, and multi-modal real-world dataset that serves as a foundational resource for training robotic agents capable of learning and generalizing hundreds of diverse, complex, and contact-rich manipulation skills with minimal effort, thereby advancing the field towards truly intelligent and adaptable robots.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology of RH20T revolves around a meticulously designed real-world data collection and processing pipeline, aimed at maximizing diversity, scale, and multi-modality, coupled with an experimental validation using a state-of-the-art imitation learning model.

#### 3.1. Data Collection Strategy

The authors implemented a novel and comprehensive strategy to overcome the challenges of collecting large-scale, diverse, and multi-modal robotic manipulation data:

- **Advanced Tele-operation Setup:** Unlike previous approaches relying on simplified interfaces (3D mice, VR remotes), the core of their collection method involved equipping the robot with a **force-torque sensor** and employing a **haptic device with force rendering**. This setup provides intuitive, precise, and safe control, crucial for recording contact-rich interactions without triggering emergency stops. Volunteers were trained in less than an hour, demonstrating its user-friendliness.
- **Skill Diversity:** The dataset comprises 147 tasks (categorized into 42 distinct skills or verbs). These tasks were sourced from established benchmarks like RLBench (48 tasks) and MetaWorld (29 tasks), along with 70 self-proposed tasks that represent common and complex real-world manipulations. Emphasis was placed on **contact-rich interactions** such as cutting, plugging, slicing, pouring, and folding, moving beyond simple pick-and-place.
- **Hardware and Embodiment Diversity:** To enhance generalization across different robot configurations, data was collected using:
 - 4 popular robot arms (e.g., Flexiv, URS, Franka, Kuka).
 - 4 different robotic grippers (e.g., Dahuan AG95, Robotiq-85, Franka).
 - 3 types of force-torque sensors (e.g., OptoForce, ATI Axia80-M20).
 This resulted in 7 distinct robot configurations.
- **Environmental and State Diversity:**
 - Over 50 different table covers with varied textures and materials were used.
 - Irrelevant objects were introduced to create visual distractions.
 - Tens of volunteers performed manipulations to ensure diverse trajectories.
 - For each skill, volunteers repeated manipulations 10 times with variations in object instances, locations, and other environmental conditions.
 - Experiments included robotic manipulation with human interference (both adversarial and cooperative).
- **Multi-Modal Data Acquisition:** Each robot sequence is rich in sensory information, collected from multiple well-calibrated sensors:
 - **Visual:** RGB, depth, and binocular IR images from multiple (8-10 global, 1-2 in-hand) multi-view cameras.
 - **Tactile/Force:** 6DoF force-torque measurements at the robot's wrist, with some sequences also including fingertip tactile information (from a uSkin sensor).
 - **Audio:** Recordings from both in-hand and global microphones.
 - **Proprioception:** Joint angles/torques, end-effector Cartesian pose, and gripper states.
 All data were collected at the highest possible frequency supported by the workstation and synchronized with timestamps.
- **Human Demonstrations and Language Descriptions:** For every robot sequence (110,000+), a corresponding human demonstration video was collected on the same platform (with an ego-centric camera), and a language description was provided. This pairing is crucial for one-shot imitation learning from human examples and language-conditioned policies.
- **Quality Control:** Significant effort was invested in calibrating all sensors, ensuring extrinsic calibration of all cameras relative to the robot. Force-torque sensors were carefully tared. Manual validation was performed for each scene to ensure camera calibration quality, ensuring high fidelity for multi-modal fusion.
- **Data Hierarchy and Compositionality:** The dataset is organized in a tree hierarchy based on intra-task similarity, allowing for flexible pairing of human and robot demonstrations across various levels of similarity (different configs, users, scenes, viewpoints). It includes both short, single-manipulation sequences and long, compositional tasks (e.g., plugging in a lamp, which involves multiple sub-tasks), enabling research into hierarchical skill learning.
- **Rating System:** Volunteers provided a quality rating (0-9) for each manipulation, with 0 for emergency stops and 1 for task failure, providing valuable meta-data for filtering or weighting demonstrations.

#### 3.2. Data Processing

Collected raw data underwent preprocessing to provide a coherent and unified data interface:
- Coordinate frames of all robots and force-torque sensors were aligned.
- End-effector Cartesian pose and force-torque data were transformed into each camera's coordinate system.
- All recorded data were synchronized in the temporal domain.

#### 3.3. Experimental Validation Approach

To demonstrate the dataset's effectiveness, the authors chose a few-shot learning framework using **Action Chunking with Transformers (ACT)**, a recent, powerful baseline model for robot manipulation.

- **Experimental Platform:** A Flexiv robot arm with an Intel RealSense RGB-D camera and a Dahuan-95 gripper, set up in a *new environment* (different camera pose, table cover) distinct from the RH20T collection environments to rigorously test generalization.
- **Target Task:** Grasping a block and placing it on a weight.
- **Data Splits for Training:**
 - **Target Data:** 75 newly collected robot manipulation sequences (RGB images + actions) for the specific task in the new environment.
 - **RH20T Data (Same Task):** 335 sequences from RH20T for the *same* task (but with different camera views, table covers, objects, robot embodiments).
 - **RH20T Data (Similar Tasks):** 195 sequences from RH20T for 3 *different but similar* tasks (e.g., pick up block, pick and place at designated location, move block left to right).
- **Training Protocol:**
 - **Pre-training Phase:** ACT model pre-trained on different subsets of the selected RH20T data.
 - **Fine-tuning Phase:** Pre-trained (or non-pre-trained) ACT model fine-tuned on the 75 newly collected sequences for the target task.
- **Evaluation Metrics:** Success rates were measured on a real robot over 20 trials for three stages: reaching the block, grasping it, and placing it on the weight. Evaluation time limit was 60 seconds.
- **Generalization Test:** Models were tested with novel objects (metal vs. pink weights) and table covers (white vs. blue) not seen during training, to assess generalization ability to unseen environment variations.
- **Implementation Details:** ACT hyper-parameters were specified (hidden/feedforward channels, learning rates, epochs, chunk size, image scale). Temporal ensembling was applied during evaluation.

This methodical approach allowed the authors to isolate and evaluate the benefits of pre-training on RH20T for both few-shot learning and generalization across diverse conditions, underscoring the dataset's practical utility.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted using the ACT model demonstrate clear advantages of pre-training on the RH20T dataset, particularly for improving task success rates, accelerating convergence, enabling few-shot learning, and enhancing generalization.

- **Enhanced Success Rate with Pre-training:**
 - When the ACT model was fine-tuned with 75 demonstrations of the target task, pre-training on selected data from RH20T consistently led to higher success rates. For example, without pre-training (75 demos, 500 epochs), the place success rate was 0%, but with pre-training on "Same Task" data from RH20T, it rose to 15%.
 - Including data from "Multiple Similar Tasks" during pre-training further improved success rates (e.g., place success rate improved from 15% to 20% compared to "Same Task" pre-training at 500 epochs). This highlights the benefit of diverse skill exposure.

- **Accelerated Model Convergence:**
 - Comparing models trained for fewer epochs (500) with pre-training against those trained for more epochs (750) without pre-training showed that pre-training on RH20T data accelerates model convergence. A model pre-trained for 500 epochs could achieve comparable or better performance to a non-pretrained model trained for 750 epochs, indicating faster learning and potentially reduced training time.

- **Superior Few-Shot Learning Capability:**
 - The results strongly support RH20T's utility in few-shot learning scenarios. When the number of new demonstrations for fine-tuning was reduced from 75 to 40 or even 10, models pre-trained on RH20T significantly outperformed non-pretrained counterparts.
 - For instance, with only 40 new demonstrations, a model pre-trained on multiple similar tasks from RH20T achieved a 15% place success rate, which is better than a non-pretrained model trained with 75 demonstrations (0% place success rate).
 - Even with just 10 new demonstrations, a pre-trained model achieved a 5% place success rate, showcasing its remarkable ability to generalize from very limited new data, a crucial step towards practical robot deployment.

- **Improved Generalization to Novel Environments:**
 - When tested in environments with novel objects (different weights) and table covers, the model pre-trained on RH20T consistently demonstrated superior generalization ability compared to the non-pretrained model.
 - For example, when placing the block on a "metal weight," the non-pretrained model had a 0% place success rate, while the pre-trained model achieved 10%. Similar improvements were observed with "pink weight" and different table covers (white vs. blue), indicating that the diversity of RH20T enables the model to learn more robust and transferable features across variations in the environment.

In essence, the experimental results unequivocally demonstrate that the RH20T dataset, through its scale and diversity, serves as a powerful resource for pre-training robot learning models, leading to significant improvements in task success, training efficiency, few-shot adaptability, and generalization capabilities in real-world robotic manipulation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The RH20T dataset represents a significant leap forward in the field of robot learning, with the potential to profoundly impact several areas:

- **Enabling Foundation Models in Robotics:** The most significant potential impact of RH20T is its role in fostering the development of "foundation models" for robotic manipulation. Inspired by the success of large-scale datasets in NLP (e.g., Common Crawl for LLMs) and computer vision (e.g., ImageNet for vision transformers), RH20T provides the necessary scale and diversity for training large, general-purpose robotic policies. These models could dramatically reduce the effort required to teach robots new skills, allowing them to adapt to novel tasks and environments with minimal demonstrations, akin to how LLMs generalize to new prompts. This could revolutionize robotic deployment by shifting from task-specific programming to flexible, adaptable learning.

- **Advancing Complex Skill Learning:** By emphasizing contact-rich interactions and collecting data for hundreds of diverse and complex skills (beyond simple pick-and-place), RH20T opens new avenues for research into more sophisticated robotic behaviors. This dataset provides the necessary data to train models capable of tasks requiring fine motor control, precise force application, and intricate environmental interaction, bringing robots closer to human-level dexterity in daily tasks.

- **Driving Multi-Modal Robot Learning:** RH20T's comprehensive integration of visual, force, audio, and proprioceptive data is crucial. It acknowledges that human-like manipulation often requires integrating information from multiple senses. This dataset will spur research into novel multi-modal fusion architectures and learning algorithms that can effectively combine asynchronous sensory streams to achieve robust and adaptive control, especially in scenarios where one modality might be occluded or insufficient.

- **Facilitating Few-Shot and Zero-Shot Learning:** The experimental results clearly show RH20T's capacity to significantly improve few-shot learning performance and generalization. This is highly significant for practical robotics, as it means robots can acquire new skills with far fewer human demonstrations or in novel environments, thereby reducing deployment costs and accelerating adoption in new applications. The ability to generalize to unseen objects and environments based on pre-training is a critical step towards true robotic autonomy.

- **Standardization and Benchmarking:** By being publicly available and meticulously curated (with calibration and detailed metadata), RH20T provides a standardized benchmark for evaluating new robot learning algorithms. This allows researchers worldwide to compare their methods on a common, high-quality dataset, fostering rapid progress and ensuring fair comparisons of different approaches.

- **Inspiration for Next-Generation Tele-operation:** The paper's successful data collection methodology, leveraging haptic devices with force feedback, highlights an effective paradigm for acquiring high-quality interaction data. This could inspire the development of more advanced and intuitive tele-operation systems, crucial for future datasets and real-time robot control.

- **Bridging Human and Robot Learning:** The inclusion of paired human demonstration videos and language descriptions is vital. It enables research into learning from human examples, leveraging human intuition and knowledge, and developing language-conditioned policies that allow natural language commands to guide robot behavior, making robots more accessible and easier to instruct.

While the paper acknowledges limitations (high data collection cost, unfulfilled potential of foundation models due to computational limits), RH20T undeniably provides a robust foundation. It is a critical resource that will accelerate research towards more intelligent, versatile, and adaptable robotic systems, pushing the boundaries of what robots can learn and accomplish in the real world.
