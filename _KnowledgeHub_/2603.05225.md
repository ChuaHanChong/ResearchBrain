# AI+HW 2035: Shaping the Next Decade

> [!summary] Summary
> An extensive collaborative effort from 30 leading experts across academia and industry presents a 10-year vision and roadmap for AI and hardware co-design, aiming for a 1000x improvement in AI training and inference efficiency. The work redefines progress by prioritizing 'intelligence per joule' and proposes a multi-layered co-evolution framework to overcome current sustainability and technological fragmentation challenges.

## Problem

- The unsustainable exponential growth of AI models leads to escalating energy consumption and environmental footprint, making current scaling approaches economically and ecologically unfeasible.
- A critical disconnect exists between AI algorithm development and hardware design, resulting in fragmented innovation where systems and workloads are misaligned.
- Persistent hardware bottlenecks, particularly the 'memory wall' where data movement costs dominate energy and latency, severely limit further AI performance gains.

## Method

- A multi-layered vision for AI+HW ==co-design== and co-evolution is proposed, fostering deep co-innovation across hardware technologies, algorithms, and application layers.
- The concept of 'intelligence per joule' is championed, redefining AI scaling to prioritize energy efficiency and meaningful capability over brute-force computational power.
- Future computing paradigms are advocated, including memory-centric architectures, 3D integration, and leveraging AI to accelerate and optimize hardware design itself.

## Results

- A strategic roadmap outlines a path to achieve a **1000x** improvement in AI training and inference efficiency within 10 years by optimizing across algorithms, silicon, and system levels.
- A multi-layered framework for AI+HW co-evolution is established, emphasizing continuous cross-layer co-design for future breakthroughs.
- Recommendations include shifting to memory-centric architectures, leveraging AI for hardware design, fostering adaptive self-optimizing systems, and a call to action for public-private partnerships to address the impending power crisis and democratize AI infrastructure.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Achieving a 1000x improvement in AI efficiency within the next decade is an urgent necessity and is possible through combined gains across the algorithmic, silicon, and system levels.
> - Data movement, not computation, now constitutes the primary energy and latency bottleneck in AI systems, necessitating a fundamental shift to memory-centric architectures.
> - AI itself is poised to revolutionize hardware design, with 'AI-in-the-loop' methodologies and generative AI for EDA becoming critical for faster, more optimized system development.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper "AI+HW 2035" positions itself at the critical intersection of two rapidly advancing fields: Artificial Intelligence and Hardware. It arises from a growing recognition that the current trajectories of AI and hardware development, while individually impressive, are often uncoordinated, leading to significant inefficiencies and an unsustainable path forward for AI.

**Addressing the Disconnect:** The core problem addressed is the "critical gap" where "today's algorithms are designed around yesterday's systems, and tomorrow's chips are optimized for today's workloads." This fragmentation, termed "siloed research," stifles progress toward holistic, sustainable, and adaptive AI systems. The paper argues for a paradigm shift from isolated advancements to tightly integrated co-design and co-evolution. This aligns with existing but nascent efforts in AI+HW co-design (e.g., conferences like MLCAD, MLSys, ICLAD), aiming to make such integration mainstream.

**The Unsustainable Scaling of AI:** A central theme is the alarming energy consumption and environmental footprint of large AI models. The exponential growth of frontier models, demanding massive compute and memory bandwidth, has reached "environmentally and economically unsustainable levels." This concern is echoed across the AI community, prompting calls for more efficient, "green AI" solutions. The paper directly tackles this by proposing a redefinition of "scaling" from brute-force computation to "intelligence per joule" – maximizing meaningful capability per unit of energy. This reframing is a crucial contribution to the ongoing debate about the ethical and practical implications of ever-larger AI models.

**Overcoming Hardware Bottlenecks:** The paper confronts the persistent "memory wall" where data movement costs now dominate energy consumption and latency. While specialized hardware like GPUs, FPGAs, and TPUs have driven AI forward, they are often optimized for particular, rapidly evolving workloads. The vision proposes moving beyond these towards memory-centric, 3D-integrated, and compute-in-memory architectures that fundamentally address data movement challenges. This builds upon established concepts in computer architecture (e.g., Illusion, N3XT, Megatrends) but integrates them within a holistic AI co-design framework.

**Long-Term Vision and Roadmap:** Unlike many research papers that focus on specific technical advancements, "AI+HW 2035" provides a comprehensive 10-year roadmap. It synthesizes current trends, anticipated challenges, and promising solutions across the entire computing stack—from materials and devices to algorithms and applications. This forward-looking perspective, backed by a large group of experts, aims to set a strategic direction for research funding, industry investment, and academic curricula for the next decade. It serves as a unifying framework for understanding and tackling the intertwined challenges of AI and hardware development, promoting a collaborative ecosystem across academia, industry, and government.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of "AI+HW 2035: Shaping the Next Decade" is to lay out a cohesive, long-term vision and a 10-year roadmap for the strategic coordination of AI and hardware (HW) co-design and co-development. This overarching goal is driven by several critical motivations and aims to achieve specific transformative outcomes:

**Core Motivation: Addressing Unsustainable AI Growth and Fragmentation:**
The authors are acutely motivated by the "profound imbalance" in AI's current trajectory. While AI has achieved "unprecedented capability," its exponential scaling, fueled by vast compute and data, has become "increasingly unsustainable." This unsustainability manifests in:
- **Environmental and Economic Cost:** Training frontier models consumes enormous energy, comparable to "hundreds of households" or even "nations," leading to significant carbon emissions and escalating economic costs.
- **Technological Fragmentation:** A critical mismatch exists where AI algorithms are designed for outdated hardware, and new hardware is optimized for obsolete workloads. This "siloed" innovation prevents optimal system-level efficiency and adaptability.
- **Physical Bottlenecks:** The "memory wall," where data movement is more energy-intensive than computation, and limits in interconnects, power delivery, and thermal management, constrain further progress.

**Key Objectives for the Next Decade:**
To counteract these challenges and harness AI's full potential responsibly, the paper articulates several concrete objectives for the next ten years:

1. **Reinventing Computing for 1000x Efficiency:** The most ambitious quantitative goal is to achieve a **1000x improvement in efficiency for AI training and inference**. This necessitates "deep co-innovation" between AI models and hardware architectures, focusing on "intelligence per joule" rather than raw compute.
2. **Enabling Energy-Aware, Self-Optimizing Systems:** To move beyond static and inefficient systems, the paper aims for the development of adaptive AI systems that can seamlessly operate and optimize their performance and energy consumption across diverse environments (cloud, edge, and physical world).
3. **Democratizing Access to Advanced AI Infrastructure:** Acknowledging the escalating costs and complexity, a key objective is to make advanced AI infrastructure accessible to a broader range of researchers and organizations, preventing AI progress from being limited to a few hyperscale players.
4. **Revolutionizing Design Productivity and Adaptability:** The paper seeks to accelerate hardware and system design cycles by embedding "AI-in-the-loop design workflows," enabling systems to learn, adapt, and co-evolve with algorithms.
5. **Building Reliable and Trustworthy AI Systems:** As AI becomes ubiquitous, a critical objective is to ensure reliability and trustworthiness by explicitly managing trade-offs among accuracy, robustness, and efficiency, and embedding human-centric principles into design.
6. **Unifying AI+HW Evolution for Physical AI:** A major motivation is to enable "Physical AI" (robotics, autonomous agents, scientific discovery) by coupling data-driven learning with physics laws, requiring tight integration of learning, control, and highly efficient hardware.
7. **Fostering Coordinated Global Impact:** The paper calls for sustained public–private partnerships, coordinated national initiatives, shared infrastructure, and workforce development to ensure AI+HW co-design becomes a unifying, long-term mission that benefits humanity sustainably.

In essence, the motivation is to transform AI from a resource-intensive, fragmented technological marvel into a sustainable, efficient, adaptive, and broadly accessible platform that delivers profound societal benefits responsibly.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

"AI+HW 2035: Shaping the Next Decade" is a vision paper, and as such, its methodology is not based on empirical experimentation but rather on a systematic, collaborative, and expert-driven process of foresight and strategic planning. The approach taken by the authors is highly structured and comprehensive, designed to synthesize a broad range of expert perspectives into a coherent roadmap.

The core methodology can be broken down as follows:

1. **NSF-Sponsored Workshop as a Catalyst:** The paper explicitly states that it "emerged from an NSF-sponsored workshop with the same title." This workshop brought together the 30 co-authors, all leading experts from academia, industry, and government research labs. This collective brainstorming and discussion formed the foundation of the vision. Each co-author presented insights, ensuring a diverse range of perspectives on challenges and opportunities in AI+HW co-design.

2. **Multi-Stakeholder Expert Consensus:** The extremely broad and distinguished author list—spanning various subfields of AI (ML theory, NLP, robotics, deep learning), hardware (computer architecture, EDA, photonics, neuromorphic, quantum computing), and systems—is central to the methodology. This ensures that the roadmap is not biased by a single research paradigm or institutional agenda but reflects a consensus view from leaders across the entire AI and computing ecosystem.

3. **Layered Abstraction Framework:** The paper organizes its vision around a multi-layered framework for AI+HW co-design and co-evolution (Figure 1), encompassing:
 - **Hardware Technologies Layer:** Focusing on physical limits, new materials, and architectural innovations.
 - **Algorithms and Paradigms Layer:** Addressing model efficiency, learning techniques, and software abstractions.
 - **Applications and Societal Impact Layer:** Concentrating on real-world deployment, sustainability, and human-centric design.
 This structured approach allows for detailed analysis within each layer while explicitly emphasizing their interdependencies and feedback loops.

4. **Systematic Analysis within Each Layer:** For each abstraction layer, the authors employ a consistent analytical structure to elaborate on the vision:
 - **Key Insights:** Fundamental understanding derived from current trends and future predictions.
 - **Key Challenges and Opportunities:** Identification of current hurdles and potential breakthroughs.
 - **Key Questions and Answers:** Addressing critical, often debated, topics to clarify strategic directions.
 - **Important Future Trends:** Delineation of near-term (2-5 years) and medium/long-term (6-10 years) technological and algorithmic shifts.
 - **Potential Obstacles, Pitfalls, and Solutions:** A candid assessment of risks and proposed mitigation strategies.
 - **What Does Success Mean in 10 Years:** Defining measurable outcomes and milestones for the vision.
 - **Suggested Action Items:** Concrete recommendations for academia, industry, government, and the broader community.

5. **Data-Informed Projections:** To ground the future trends, the paper leverages "representative data from 2020–2025, with trends extrapolated toward 2030." This data is visualized in 2D and 3D plots (Figures 2 and 3) to show the joint evolution of model size, accuracy, and time. This empirical grounding adds credibility to the predictions and highlights the shift towards optimizing for efficiency rather than just scale.

6. **Call to Action and Living Document:** The paper concludes with a clear "Call to Action," providing specific, actionable recommendations for all stakeholders. It also proposes using the report "as a living reference, capturing ongoing community input," indicating an adaptive and evolving methodology for guiding future research and policy.

By combining expert consensus, a structured analytical framework, data-informed projections, and actionable recommendations, the methodology aims to produce a comprehensive, realistic, and impactful roadmap for the future of AI+HW co-design.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The paper "AI+HW 2035: Shaping the Next Decade" delivers a profound and multi-faceted vision, presenting several key findings and proposed outcomes that redefine the future trajectory of artificial intelligence and its underlying hardware. These "results" are not experimental data but rather a comprehensive synthesis of expert consensus, future predictions, and strategic recommendations.

1. **The 1000x Efficiency Imperative and "Intelligence per Joule":** A central finding is the urgent need and a viable path to achieve a **1000x improvement in AI training and inference efficiency within the next 10 years.** This ambitious goal is predicated on combined gains from algorithmic optimization (10x), silicon utilization and advances (20x), and system-level efficiency (5x). This redefines success from raw computational power (FLOPs) to "intelligence per joule"—meaningful capability, insight, or task performance per unit of energy—making efficiency, sustainability, and cost first-class objectives.

2. **Multi-Layered AI+HW Co-Evolution Framework:** The paper's most significant conceptual contribution is its "Multi-Layered Vision for AI+HW Co-Design and Co-Evolution" (Figure 1). It asserts that future breakthroughs will stem not from isolated advances but from synergistic innovation across three tightly coupled abstraction layers: **Hardware Technologies, Algorithms and Paradigms, and Applications and Societal Impact.** This framework emphasizes continuous cross-layer co-design, where AI helps design hardware, hardware accelerates AI, and both co-evolve in response to societal needs.

3. **Fundamental Shifts in Computing Paradigm:**
 - **Memory-Centricity:** A crucial finding is that "data movement has become the primary bottleneck." This mandates a fundamental shift from compute-centric to **memory-centric and data-centric architectures**, leveraging innovations like compute-in-memory (CIM), dense 3D integration, and photonic/optoelectronic interconnects to minimize energy and latency costs.
 - **AI-in-the-Loop Design:** The paper identifies the necessity for **AI to design hardware**. AI-driven Electronic Design Automation (EDA) and generative AI tools are presented as essential to accelerate design cycles, explore vast design spaces, and optimize trade-offs across layers.
 - **Adaptive and Self-Optimizing Systems:** Future systems must be **energy-aware, self-optimizing, and architecturally adaptive**, capable of dynamically adjusting execution strategies, precision, and resource allocation based on real-time telemetry.

4. **Key Technological and Algorithmic Directions:**
 - **Hardware:** Near/in-memory computing, 3D monolithic integration, heterogeneous packaging (chiplets), photonic/optical interconnects, quantum-classical integration, and advanced cooling/power delivery.
 - **Algorithms:** Hardware-aware training, low-complexity and compact models (e.g., hybrid, Shannon-inspired, neuro-inspired, approximate, probabilistic, state-space models), algorithmic sparsity, physics-informed learning (neural operators, differentiable simulators), and latent world models (JEPA).
 - **System Software:** Hardware-aware compilers, auto-tuners, unified programming frameworks, and runtime schedulers that optimize across heterogeneous units and fleet-scale deployments.

5. **The Emergence and Demands of Physical AI:** The vision highlights that the "next leap in AI innovation lies in coupling data-driven learning with the laws of physics." Physical AI systems (robotics, autonomous agents, scientific discovery) place stringent demands on energy efficiency, real-time responsiveness, and robustness, making tight end-to-end co-design a prerequisite. The paper predicts that physical AI will account for the vast majority of real-world inference by 2035, driven by highly efficient small models.

6. **Looming "Power Crisis" and Infrastructure Challenges:** A stark finding is the impending "power crisis" in the U.S., with datacenter demand set to outstrip generation and grid capacity within five years. This, along with challenges in data sovereignty, regulatory complexity, and a fragmented ecosystem, threatens to slow transformative AI adoption.

7. **Actionable Roadmap for Stakeholders:** The paper concludes with detailed, actionable recommendations for academia, industry, government, and the broader community, emphasizing:
 - **Collaboration:** Deep interdisciplinary research and sustained public-private partnerships.
 - **Infrastructure:** Shared AI+HW testbeds, open platforms, and national initiatives (e.g., extending NAIRR).
 - **Workforce:** Training students fluent across AI and hardware boundaries.
 - **Metrics:** Shifting evaluation to system-level efficiency and societal impact.
 - **Policy:** Government investment in energy infrastructure and policies attracting global talent.

In essence, the main "results" are a validated strategic direction for the AI and hardware communities, a blueprint for achieving radical efficiency gains, and a warning about critical infrastructure challenges that must be addressed collaboratively to ensure AI's sustainable and equitable future.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "AI+HW 2035: Shaping the Next Decade" paper carries immense significance and has the potential for transformative impact across research, industry, government, and society at large. Its significance stems from its comprehensive nature, the authority of its contributors, and its direct engagement with the most pressing challenges facing the future of artificial intelligence.

**1. A Unified and Coherent Roadmap:** The most immediate impact of this paper is providing a much-needed, unified, and coherent roadmap for AI and hardware co-design. In fields often characterized by rapid, yet fragmented, advancements, this vision paper serves as a strategic compass. It bridges disparate research silos, offering a holistic perspective that can guide funding agencies, university research programs, and industry R&D efforts for the next decade, ensuring that innovation is synergistic rather than isolated.

**2. Redefining AI Scaling and Sustainability:** By introducing and championing "intelligence per joule" as the paramount metric, the paper fundamentally redefines what constitutes progress in AI. This paradigm shift moves the focus from brute-force model scaling and raw computational power to energy efficiency, environmental sustainability, and economic viability. Its success would dramatically lower AI's carbon footprint, making advanced AI more environmentally responsible and accessible. This has profound implications for the long-term societal acceptance and deployability of AI technologies.

**3. Accelerating Innovation through Co-Design:** The advocacy for deep cross-layer co-design and "AI-in-the-loop" hardware design promises to significantly accelerate the pace of innovation. By fostering continuous feedback loops between algorithms, architectures, and systems, the paper envisions a future where hardware adapts to evolving AI paradigms as quickly as AI models adapt to new data. This could shrink hardware design cycles, unlock new performance frontiers, and enable novel computing paradigms (e.g., memory-centric, photonic, analog AI) that are currently constrained by traditional design workflows.

**4. Enabling Transformative Applications, Especially Physical AI:** Achieving the 1000x efficiency target would unlock entirely new classes of applications currently infeasible due to energy, latency, and cost constraints. This is particularly impactful for **Physical AI**—robotics, autonomous vehicles, intelligent manufacturing, scientific discovery, and healthcare. These real-world systems demand real-time responsiveness, extreme energy efficiency, and inherent trustworthiness, which the proposed co-design approach directly addresses. This will lead to a broader and deeper integration of AI into physical environments, from smart cities to personalized medicine.

**5. Addressing Critical Infrastructure and Societal Challenges:** The paper bravely confronts the looming "power crisis" and the escalating energy demands of AI infrastructure. By highlighting this as a national and global competitive risk, it provides crucial impetus for policymakers to invest in energy infrastructure, streamline regulations, and promote diversified energy sources. Furthermore, its emphasis on democratizing AI access, embedding human-centric principles, and fostering global collaboration underscores a commitment to equitable, ethical, and socially responsible AI development, mitigating potential inequalities and promoting trust.

**6. Fostering Academia-Industry-Government Partnerships:** The detailed action items for each stakeholder group provide a blueprint for coordinated action. By explicitly calling for national AI+HW initiatives, shared infrastructure, and robust workforce development, the paper aims to create a powerful ecosystem that leverages the strengths of all sectors. This collaborative model is essential for tackling complex, systemic challenges that no single entity can solve alone, ensuring that the United States remains competitive in the global AI landscape.

In conclusion, "AI+HW 2035" is more than a technical forecast; it is a strategic manifesto for the sustainable and responsible advancement of artificial intelligence. Its impact will be measured not only in technological breakthroughs but also in its ability to steer the entire AI ecosystem towards a future where intelligence is not just powerful, but also efficient, accessible, reliable, and fundamentally aligned with human and planetary needs.
