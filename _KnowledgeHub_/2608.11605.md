# Foresight Without Seeing: Latent Futures for World Action Models

> [!summary] Summary
> ForeWAM is a direct-policy World Action Model that gives its Action DiT predictive context without generating future video. A single Video DiT prefill over the clean current latent plus noisy future slots yields layer-wise key-value states (Future-KV) reused across action denoising, while dynamics registers distilled from a frozen latent-action teacher encode interaction-induced transitions. It reaches 96.7% on LIBERO and 61.6% on LIBERO-Plus without embodied pretraining.

## Problem

- Cascaded and joint world action models expose predicted scene evolution to the action pathway, but iterative video denoising adds heavy inference cost and generation errors can propagate into actions.
- Direct-policy world action models drop future rollout for efficiency, yet leave no explicit inference-time interface through which predictive dynamics reach the Action DiT.
- Conditioning only on current-observation features forces the action expert to infer both present scene state and contact-induced transition consequences from the same visual context.

## Method

- **Future-KV** runs one ==Video DiT== prefill at noise level 1.0 over the clean current visual latent plus noise-initialized future slots, caching layer-wise key-value states reused across all action-denoising steps.
- Sixteen ==dynamics registers== are mean-pooled and projected to match a frozen ==LaWM== latent-action teacher target, biasing implicit future states toward object motion, contact changes, and task progress.
- A structured attention mask routes current-frame, register, future-slot, and action tokens; joint ==flow-matching== loss sums video, action, and latent-action distillation terms, with gradients crossing the video-to-action interface.

## Results

- ForeWAM scores **96.7%** average on the four LIBERO suites and ForeWAM-Flash **96.9%**, using **2B** policy parameters versus Fast-WAM's **6B** and no embodied pretraining.
- On LIBERO-Plus, ForeWAM reaches **61.6%** overall, **10.1** points above Fast-WAM, with the largest margins on camera viewpoint (**+46.1**) and sensor noise (**+21.1**) perturbations.
- Action-generation latency drops from **667 ms** to **568 ms**, and to **220 ms** after OneDP distillation; the ablation gives **61.6%** for both pathways versus **58.5%** and **58.0%** for each alone.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Predictive dynamics can condition a policy as cached internal attention states rather than decoded pixels, decoupling foresight benefits from video-generation latency.
> - Generic future-video objectives do not preferentially retain interaction-relevant change; an explicit latent-action distillation target is needed to shape a compact transition bottleneck.
> - Distributed cached visual context and compact transition summaries are complementary, though the reported gains stay within LIBERO-scale sim and are not causally isolated.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in this paper addresses a challenge within the field of robotic control, specifically concerning World Action Models (WAMs). The broader landscape of robot control policies includes Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, which learn reactive observation-to-action mappings. WAMs represent an evolution of this paradigm by integrating future visual prediction with action generation, allowing policies to account for how the physical world changes during interaction.

Existing WAMs generally fall into two categories based on how they expose predictive dynamics to the action pathway:
1. **Explicit-future WAMs (Cascaded or Joint WAMs):** These models either generate future observations before conditioning action prediction (cascaded) or generate future video and actions together (joint). While they provide direct access to predicted scene evolution, they often incur substantial inference costs due to iterative video denoising and are susceptible to errors propagating from future generation into action prediction. Examples include methods that explicitly generate future visual observations or co-model future states and actions within a shared architecture.
2. **Direct-policy WAMs:** These models, such as Fast-WAM, aim to improve efficiency by predicting actions directly from the current observation representation, skipping explicit future video generation during inference. However, this approach inherently limits the action pathway's direct access to predictive dynamics, leaving it without an explicit interface to understand how the scene might evolve.

ForeWAM aims to bridge the gap between these two paradigms. It seeks to combine the inference efficiency of direct-policy WAMs with the benefits of incorporating predictive dynamics, a characteristic of explicit-future WAMs. The paper positions ForeWAM as a method that enables direct-policy WAMs to access predictive context without explicitly generating future observations at inference time, thereby addressing a fundamental trade-off between predictive context and inference efficiency.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to develop a direct-policy World Action Model that can provide its action generation component (Action DiT) with access to predictive dynamics without explicitly generating future visual observations during deployment.

The motivation for this objective stems from the limitations observed in current WAM paradigms:

- **High Inference Costs and Error Propagation in Explicit-Future WAMs:** Models that iteratively generate future video for action prediction, whether cascaded or joint, face significant computational overhead during inference. This iterative denoising process is time-consuming. Furthermore, any inaccuracies in the generated future visual predictions can propagate and negatively impact the accuracy of the downstream action generation, potentially leading to suboptimal or incorrect robot behaviors.
- **Lack of Explicit Predictive Context in Direct-Policy WAMs:** While direct-policy WAMs, such as Fast-WAM, successfully mitigate the inference cost by avoiding future video generation at deployment, they inherently remove the explicit mechanism through which predictive dynamics inform the action pathway. This design choice implies that the Action DiT primarily relies on features from the current observation. For tasks requiring interaction-dependent behaviors (e.g., grasping, pushing, placing), the appropriate action often depends on anticipating the state transition induced by physical contact. Without an explicit interface for predictive dynamics, the policy must infer these complex, interaction-induced changes solely from the current visual context, which can be a challenging task.

Therefore, the authors were motivated to investigate how to enable a direct-policy WAM to retain its inference efficiency while explicitly exposing action-relevant predictive dynamics to its action pathway. This includes providing hidden features that encode task-relevant temporal structure without requiring future observations or decoded future video at inference. The proposed ForeWAM aims to achieve this by establishing an implicit interface for predictive context, thereby offering a more efficient yet dynamically aware robotic control policy.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

ForeWAM addresses its objectives through a combination of a video diffusion transformer (Video DiT), a dedicated Action DiT, a hidden future-slot Key-Value (K/V) cache, and latent-action-supervised dynamics registers. The model is designed to operate without future video rollouts or a teacher model at deployment.

**Problem Formulation:**
The policy predicts an action chunk $a_{1:H}$ given a multi-camera observation $o$, an instruction $l$, and a proprioceptive state $p$. Unlike explicit-future WAMs that might generate future visual trajectories $u_{1:T}$, ForeWAM aims for a direct policy while exposing predictive context. At inference, ground-truth future observations and teacher outputs are unavailable. ForeWAM constructs a stochastic substrate $ez_{Fsub}$ by concatenating the VAE encoding of the current observation $z_{cur}(o)$ with pure noise $\epsilon_F$ for future slots. A single prefill of the Video DiT with this substrate produces dynamics-register states $D_\theta$ and layer-wise K/V states $H_{KV}$, which then condition the Action DiT.

**Model Architecture and Design Rationale:**
The model employs two complementary pathways to provide predictive context to the Action DiT:

1. **Future-KV:** This pathway provides distributed visual context. It processes the clean latent of the current frame and noisy future slots through a single Video DiT prefill. The resulting layer-wise key and value states are cached and reused by the Action DiT throughout its denoising steps. This mechanism exposes spatiotemporal context in feature space, avoiding iterative future-video rollout or pixel-space reconstruction costs.
2. **Latent-Action (LA)-Supervised Dynamics Registers:** This pathway provides a compact, transition-oriented summary of scene changes. A set of dynamics registers are introduced and supervised by a frozen LaWM (Latent World Action Model) latent-action encoder during training. The LaWM teacher, trained as an inverse-dynamics component, encodes demonstrated visual transitions (before and after states) into a quantized latent-action target $z_{LA}$. A trainable projection head maps the mean-pooled dynamics registers to match this target, thereby encouraging the registers to capture interaction-relevant changes like object motion or contact changes. This target is non-executable and used solely during training.

**Token Groups and Routing:**
The model utilizes four token groups: current-frame tokens (C), dynamics registers (D), future-slot tokens (F), and action tokens (A). The Video DiT processes C, D, and F. The Action DiT then reads the complete video sequence (derived from C and F via the cached K/V states) along with the dynamics registers. A structured attention mask dictates information flow, allowing future-slot tokens to integrate current frame and dynamics registers, and action tokens to access the full video sequence and registers. The proprioceptive state is incorporated as an additional context token.

**Future-KV Prefill:**
During training, the video branch learns to predict future latents from demonstrated data. At inference, to avoid iterative future generation, the clean current latent is combined with noise for future slots. The Video DiT is run once in a "prefill" step ($\sigma=1.0$) to generate and cache the key and value tensors from every layer. These cached K/V states are then utilized by the Action DiT for all its denoising steps. This incurs a single video prefill cost per action query, not an iterative rollout.

**Training Objective:**
The model is trained with continuous flow matching, a generative modeling technique. The total loss function $L$ is a sum of three terms:
- **$L_{video}$:** A flow-matching loss for predicting the demonstrated future video latent $z_{1:T}$.
- **$L_{action}$:** A flow-matching loss for generating the demonstrated executable action chunk $a_{1:H}$.
- **$L_{LA}$:** A distillation loss for the dynamics registers. This term penalizes the difference between the mean-pooled dynamics registers (after a trainable projection) and the detached latent-action target $z_{LA}$ provided by the frozen LaWM teacher. This target represents a visual transition and guides the registers to encode interaction-relevant dynamics.

Gradients from the action objective are permitted to flow through the video-to-action K/V interface during training, allowing for end-to-end learning. Critically, during deployment, the model operates without access to ground-truth future observations or the LaWM teacher, performing no future video generation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted on the LIBERO and LIBERO-Plus benchmarks demonstrate ForeWAM's performance, efficiency, and robustness.

**Performance on Standard LIBERO Suites:**
- Both ForeWAM and its accelerated variant, ForeWAM-Flash, achieved competitive in-distribution control performance without requiring embodied robot-data pretraining.
- ForeWAM obtained an average success rate of 96.7% across the four standard LIBERO suites (Spatial, Object, Goal, Long), with performance ranging from 92.8% on Long tasks to 99.6% on Object tasks.
- ForeWAM-Flash achieved an average success rate of 96.9%, showing a maximum difference of 0.8 percentage points from the standard ForeWAM on any given suite.
- Compared to Fast-WAM, ForeWAM and ForeWAM-Flash were 0.9 and 0.7 percentage points lower, respectively, in overall success rates, indicating that the accelerated variant largely preserves the performance of the full inference configuration on standard LIBERO tasks.

**Robustness on LIBERO-Plus:**
- On the evaluated subset of LIBERO-Plus, which includes various out-of-distribution perturbations, ForeWAM achieved an overall success rate of 61.6%. It exhibited its highest success rates under lighting and language perturbations, and its lowest under robot-initial-state shifts.
- ForeWAM surpassed the reported Fast-WAM result of 51.5% by 10.1 percentage points overall. Notable gains were observed in specific perturbation categories: 46.1 points higher on camera viewpoint shifts and 21.1 points higher on sensor noise, with smaller improvements on object layout, language, and background texture. However, ForeWAM showed lower success on robot-initial-state shifts and lighting compared to Fast-WAM.
- ForeWAM-Flash achieved an overall success rate of 58.2%, which is 3.4 points below ForeWAM but 6.7 points above Fast-WAM overall. It remained lower than Fast-WAM on robot-initial-state, language, lighting, and background shifts.

**Inference Efficiency:**
- ForeWAM demonstrated improved action-denoising latency compared to Fast-WAM. On a single NVIDIA A800 GPU, ForeWAM reduced the 10-step latency from 667 ms (Fast-WAM) to 568 ms, representing a 14.8% reduction.
- ForeWAM-Flash, which distills the action-denoising process from 10 to 2 steps, further reduced the latency to 220 ms. This corresponds to a 61% reduction relative to ForeWAM and a 67% reduction relative to Fast-WAM.
- The parameter count for ForeWAM was approximately one-third of Fast-WAM's (2 billion parameters versus 6 billion), contributing to its efficiency.

**Ablation Study:**
- A component comparison on LIBERO-Plus indicated that combining both proposed pathways (Future-KV and LA supervision) yielded the strongest overall result.
- ForeWAM (with both components) achieved 61.6% success.
- Configurations using "Future-KV only" achieved 58.5% success, while "LA supervision only" achieved 58.0% success.
- The "Base policy" (without either Future-KV or LA supervision) achieved 53.6%, serving as a contextual reference. These results suggest that the two conditioning paths provide complementary benefits to the policy.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The significance of ForeWAM lies in its demonstration that direct-policy World Action Models can effectively integrate predictive dynamics into their action pathway without incurring the costs associated with explicit future video generation at inference time. This addresses a trade-off inherent in prior WAM designs, potentially advancing the development of more efficient and robust robot control policies.

**Specific impacts include:**

- **Improved Inference Efficiency:** By introducing the Future-KV mechanism and latent-action-supervised dynamics registers, ForeWAM reduces action-generation latency compared to models like Fast-WAM, while also operating with a smaller parameter count. This efficiency is critical for real-time robotic applications where rapid action generation is required. The ForeWAM-Flash variant further amplifies these efficiency gains.
- **Enhanced Robustness:** The observed performance improvements on LIBERO-Plus suggest that ForeWAM can achieve higher robustness to various out-of-distribution perturbations, particularly in aspects like camera viewpoint and sensor noise. This enhanced robustness is a valuable attribute for deploying robotic systems in varied and unpredictable real-world environments.
- **Bridging the Gap in WAM Paradigms:** ForeWAM offers a conceptual and architectural solution to the problem of providing predictive context to direct-policy WAMs. It indicates that implicit, latent representations of future dynamics can be leveraged effectively, rather than relying solely on explicit, pixel-level future video predictions.
- **Reduced Training Resource Requirements:** The model achieves competitive performance without requiring embodied robot-data pretraining, which can be a resource-intensive process. This may broaden access to and accelerate the development of advanced robot control models.
- **Complementary Context Pathways:** The ablation study suggests that providing both distributed visual context (Future-KV) and compact, interaction-relevant transition cues (LA-supervised dynamics registers) is more effective than using either pathway alone. This highlights the value of multi-faceted conditioning for action generation.

**Limitations and Future Directions:**
The paper acknowledges that the evaluation is currently limited to LIBERO and LIBERO-Plus. While these benchmarks are comprehensive for manipulation tasks and robustness perturbations, they may not fully represent the diversity of embodiments, interaction dynamics, visual conditions, and long-horizon behaviors encountered in broader robotic settings. Therefore, the generalizability of ForeWAM to different robot morphologies, task distributions, or real-world deployment scenarios remains an area for further investigation. The robustness gains on LIBERO-Plus are also noted to be within the evaluated subset and not necessarily indicative of universal out-of-distribution generalization.

Despite these limitations, ForeWAM's contributions represent a step towards more capable and deployable robotic AI systems by demonstrating a method for foresight in action generation without requiring explicit, and often costly, future visual predictions.
