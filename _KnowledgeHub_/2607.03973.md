# MANCE: Manifold Aware Concept Erasure

> [!summary] Summary
> A method called MANCE (Manifold Aware Concept Erasure) improves the surgical removal of target concepts from neural representations by constraining erasure updates to an estimated local manifold. Its MANCE++ variant demonstrates state-of-the-art performance in nonlinear concept erasure, achieving near-chance target leakage while minimally affecting other encoded information across 119 diverse NLP and vision benchmarks.

## Problem

- Surgicality: Removing a target concept from neural representations without inadvertently damaging other valuable, often unknown, information.
- Entangled Concepts: Neural representations encode multiple attributes that are often entangled due to data correlations or phenomena like superposition, making precise erasure difficult.
- Nonlinear Concepts: Existing methods struggle with effectively removing nonlinearly encoded concept information while preserving representation dimensionality.

## Method

- The ==Manifold Constraint Hypothesis (MCH)== posits that natural representations lie on a lower-dimensional manifold, predicting that interventions constrained to this manifold will preserve other concepts better.
- The **MANCE** algorithm iteratively estimates the ==local tangent space== of the representation manifold using ==k-nearest neighbors== and projects ==gradient-based erasure updates== onto this estimated space.
- The **MANCE++** variant combines the iterative **MANCE** method with closed-form preprocessing steps (==LEACE== and ==CovMatch==) to exhaust simple linear and second-moment signals before **MANCE** tackles residual nonlinearities.

## Results

- MANCE consistently improved target leakage for existing erasure methods (INLP, LEACE, IGBP), reducing it by up to **17.6 percentage points** on NLP settings while maintaining or increasing surgicality budget coverage.
- The MANCE++ variant achieved new state-of-the-art performance in nonlinear concept erasure, reaching near-chance leakage (e.g., **0.0 pp**) across a high number of settings (e.g., **35/39** NLP, **39/40** CelebA-CLIP least-correlated) with minimal control degradation.
- Ablation studies confirmed that the manifold constraint is crucial, as the unconstrained AmbCE++ variant consistently left higher leakage (**6-10 pp**) and failed to stay within surgicality budgets on many settings.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Neural representations derived from natural data concentrate on a structured, lower-dimensional manifold, and respecting this intrinsic geometry improves the surgicality and effectiveness of concept erasure.
> - Constraining erasure updates to the local tangent space of this manifold significantly reduces collateral damage to other encoded information while effectively removing the target concept.
> - A combination of simple linear and covariance-based preprocessing with iterative, manifold-aware gradient updates is highly effective for achieving state-of-the-art nonlinear concept erasure across diverse modalities and concepts.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in "MANCE" addresses the challenge of concept erasure in neural representations, a subfield within interpretable and ethical AI. Neural representations, or embeddings, are known to encode a wide range of attributes from their input data, including semantic content, style, demographic information, and task-specific labels. In various applications, it becomes necessary to remove specific attributes, such as gender, toxicity, or privacy-sensitive information, from these representations while ideally preserving all other encoded information. This task is inherently difficult due to the entangled nature of concepts within high-dimensional neural spaces, where removing one concept may inadvertently damage others, a phenomenon referred to as "surgicality" in the literature.

Previous research in concept erasure can be broadly categorized into linear and nonlinear methods. Linear methods, such as Iterative Nullspace Projection (INLP) by Ravfogel et al. (2020) and LEACE by Belrose et al. (2023), focus on removing linearly decodable information. More recent efforts, including Iterative Gradient-Based Projection (IGBP) by Iskander et al. (2023) and Obliviator by Akbari et al. (2025), have aimed at addressing nonlinear concept information. Obliviator, for example, approaches the problem by iteratively training an encoder to minimize statistical dependence between the representation and the erased attribute, followed by an RKHS-based disentanglement step. However, some existing nonlinear methods have practical limitations, such as not guaranteeing the preservation of the original representation dimensionality, which can restrict their direct integration into downstream model computations.

MANCE positions itself as a complementary approach rather than a direct competitor to existing methods. It operates by projecting the erasure update onto an estimated manifold of natural representations, thereby enhancing the surgicality of edits performed by various prior erasure techniques. The core premise is that neural representations of natural inputs tend to concentrate on a structured, lower-dimensional manifold. By constraining interventions to this manifold, the method aims to remove target concepts while minimizing unintended damage to other, potentially unknown, information. This focus on the intrinsic geometry of representation spaces represents a distinct contribution to the field, suggesting that interventions respecting this geometry can lead to more precise and surgical concept erasure, particularly for nonlinear concepts where current methods face significant challenges. The paper claims that MANCE++, a variant incorporating closed-form preprocessing, establishes a new state-of-the-art for nonlinear concept erasure.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a method for concept erasure that can effectively remove a target concept from neural representations while preserving other information encoded within them to a high degree. This objective is driven by several motivations and challenges inherent in current concept erasure techniques:

1. **Surgicality:** A central tension in concept erasure is the need for "surgicality"—removing information about the target concept without causing unnecessary damage to other, potentially valuable, concepts. Existing methods often struggle with this, particularly when concepts are correlated or entangled in the representation space (e.g., professional information and gender).
2. **Entanglement and Superposition:** Neural representations are known to encode multiple attributes, which can be entangled due to data correlations or phenomena like superposition within the model. Intervening on one concept in such a space can have cascading negative effects on others, making precise erasure difficult.
3. **Unknown Control Information:** A significant challenge is that the "other information" to be preserved (control concepts) is often unknown or not explicitly labeled. This precludes formulating concept erasure as a standard constrained optimization problem where preservation of specific, known attributes could be enforced. The absence of explicit preservation targets necessitates a more general approach to minimize collateral damage.
4. **The Manifold Constraint Hypothesis (MCH):** To address the aforementioned challenges, the authors propose the Manifold Constraint Hypothesis (MCH). This hypothesis posits that representations derived from natural input distributions do not uniformly fill the entire high-dimensional representation space but rather concentrate on a structured, lower-dimensional manifold. The core motivation behind MCH is the prediction that interventions on these representations, if constrained to move along this manifold, would be as effective in concept erasure while demonstrably preserving other encoded concepts better than unconstrained interventions. This hypothesis provides the theoretical foundation and guiding principle for the proposed methodology.
5. **Operationalizing MCH for Nonlinear Erasure:** The overarching objective is to operationalize MCH into a practical and effective method for nonlinear concept erasure. This involves developing an algorithm that can estimate the local geometry of the representation manifold and use this estimation to constrain the direction of erasure updates. The goal is to build a method that can improve the surgicality and leakage trade-offs of existing erasure techniques, especially for complex, nonlinearly encoded concepts.
6. **Benchmarking and State-of-the-Art Performance:** A further objective is to conduct a comprehensive evaluation across diverse modalities (text and vision), models, and concepts, demonstrating that the proposed method (MANCE and its variants) consistently improves erasure performance and, specifically, that MANCE++ can achieve state-of-the-art results in nonlinear concept erasure.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The Manifold Aware Concept Erasure (MANCE) family of algorithms operationalizes the Manifold Constraint Hypothesis (MCH) by constraining concept erasure updates to an estimated representation manifold. MANCE is an iterative, gradient-based method that can be applied independently or on top of existing erasure techniques.

#### 3.1. The Manifold Constraint Hypothesis (MCH)

The MCH posits that natural representations occupy a structured, lower-dimensional manifold within the full representation space. It predicts that interventions constrained to this manifold will preserve other encoded concepts better than unconstrained interventions, given a matched effect on the target concept.

#### 3.2. The MANCE Method

MANCE is an iterative algorithm that modifies representations (X) to remove a target concept (y). It consists of three primary ingredients:

1. **Estimate the Manifold Locally:**
 - For each representation $x_i$ to be edited, MANCE estimates its local tangent space $T_{x_i}(\mathcal{M})$ using its $k$ nearest neighbors from the *original, natural* (unedited) representations ($X^{(0)}$).
 - A local PCA matrix $S_i \in \mathbb{R}^{k \times d}$ is formed by mean-centering these $k$ neighbors.
 - Singular Value Decomposition (SVD) is applied to $S_i$ to obtain right singular vectors $V_i$ and singular values $\sigma_i$. The top $r$ right singular vectors form the tangent-basis matrix $B_i \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times r}$, which spans the estimated tangent space. The rank $r$ is typically estimated once using the TwoNN method.
 - Crucially, while $x_i$ updates in each round, the neighborhood for tangent space estimation is always drawn from the fixed initial natural representations $X^{(0)}$, anchoring the manifold estimate to the original data distribution.

2. **Build the Tangent Erasure Direction:**
 - At each round $t$, a nonlinear MLP concept probe $f_t: \mathbb{R}^d \rightarrow \mathbb{R}$ is trained on the current representations ($X^{(t-1)}$) and target labels. This probe is refitted every $\tau$ rounds for efficiency.
 - The gradient of the probe's prediction with respect to each input representation $\nabla f_t(x_i)$ is computed and normalized to $u_i$.
 - This normalized gradient $u_i$ is then projected onto the local tangent basis $B_i$ to obtain tangent-basis coordinates $c_i = B_i^\top u_i$.
 - The tangent-basis coordinates are reweighted by their corresponding singular values $\sigma_{i, \ell}$ (raised to an exponent $\alpha$, typically $\alpha=1$) to form a spectrally-weighted tangent direction $d_i = B_i \text{diag}(\sigma_i^\alpha) c_i$. This weighting gives more emphasis to directions well-supported by nearby natural representations.
 - The representation $x_i$ is then updated by subtracting a scaled component aligned with the normalized $d_i$: $\tilde{x}_i = x_i - \lambda_i \langle x_i, \hat{u}_i \rangle \hat{u}_i$, where $\hat{u}_i = d_i / \|d_i\|_2$.

3. **Choose a Per-Sample Local-Neighborhood Cap:**
 - A unique step size $\lambda_i$ is determined for each sample to ensure the update remains within the local manifold approximation.
 - The local neighborhood radius $r_i$ is defined as the mean Euclidean distance from $x_i$ to its $k$ nearest neighbors in $X^{(0)}$.
 - The update displacement is capped at an $\epsilon$ fraction of this radius: $\|\tilde{x}_i - x_i\|_2 \le \epsilon \cdot r_i$. This ensures the first-order tangent approximation remains valid.
 - The step size $\lambda_i$ is chosen as the largest value that satisfies this cap, up to a global maximum $\lambda_{\max}$. The parameter $\epsilon$ is fixed across all settings ($0.1$) because $r_i$ is scale-aware.

#### 3.3. MANCE$^+$ and MANCE$^{++}$ Variants

These variants prepend closed-form preprocessing steps before the iterative MANCE loop:
- **MANCE$^+$:** Prepends LEACE, a linear eraser that removes first-moment linear signal related to class means.
- **MANCE$^{++}$:** Further prepends CovMatch, a rank-2 projection that removes second-moment class-conditional covariance asymmetry, specifically the leading eigenvectors of $\Delta\Sigma = \Sigma_+ - \Sigma_-$. These preprocessing steps are affine projections of low effective rank (at most 3) designed to exhaust simple signal before MANCE tackles residual nonlinearities.

#### 3.4. Experimental Setup

- **Metrics:**
 - **Target Leakage ($S, D_S$):** Measured by retraining a nonlinear MLP probe on the edited representations and reporting its accuracy $S$. $D_S = S - S_{\text{floor}}$ (majority-vote baseline), where values near 0 indicate effective erasure.
 - **Surgicality ($\Delta Y, D_Y$):** Measured as the change in accuracy of a probe on a set of "control" concepts, comparing performance on original ($Y_{\text{clean}}$) vs. edited ($Y_{\text{edit}}$) representations. $\Delta Y = Y_{\text{edit}} - Y_{\text{clean}}$. $D_Y = \max(0, -\Delta Y)$ represents control degradation.
 - **Surgicality Budget:** Methods are evaluated under fixed limits ($b \in \{1, 3, 5, 10\}$ percentage points) on control degradation ($D_Y \le b$). For iterative methods, the trajectory step yielding the strongest target erasure within budget is selected.
 - **Coverage:** The number of settings out of the total where a method achieves at least one within-budget edit.
- **Settings:**
 - **NLP:** 39 settings (13 LLM families, 3 concepts: sycophancy, gender, safety) at the 50%-depth layer. Control concepts: answer preference (sycophancy), profession (gender), helpfulness (safety).
 - **Vision (CelebA-CLIP):** 80 settings (CLIP ViT-B/32 embeddings, 40 binary facial attributes) under two surgicality regimes: 5 most-correlated and 5 least-correlated control concepts, selected by Pearson correlation with the target.
- **Baselines:** LEACE, INLP, IGBP, Obliviator (a state-of-the-art nonlinear method).
- **Ablation:** AmbCE$^{++}$, a variant of MANCE$^{++}$ that removes the tangent projection and uses a global, unconstrained gradient step size, designed to isolate the contribution of the manifold constraint.
- **Hyperparameters:** All step-controlling hyperparameters (e.g., $\epsilon=0.1$, $\lambda_{\max}=64$, $H=60$ rounds) are fixed across all 119 settings without per-setting tuning.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation across 119 diverse settings (39 NLP, 80 CelebA-CLIP) provides empirical support for the Manifold Constraint Hypothesis and demonstrates the effectiveness of the MANCE family of algorithms.

1. **MANCE Complements Prior Erasers:**
 - Applying MANCE as an additional step on representations previously intervened upon by other erasure methods (INLP, LEACE, IGBP) consistently improved target leakage results.
 - For example, at a surgicality budget of $D_Y \le 1$ percentage point (pp) on NLP settings, LEACE's leakage dropped from 19.1 pp to 1.5 pp with MANCE, INLP's from 15.2 pp to 1.8 pp, and IGBP's from 11.5 pp to 1.6 pp. Similar reductions were observed at looser budgets.
 - MANCE also maintained or increased the number of settings staying within the surgicality budget (coverage).
 - The only exception was Obliviator, where no significant improvement in leakage was observed; however, this was attributed to Obliviator already reaching near-chance leakage on the limited number of "easier" settings it covered within budget, leaving little room for further improvement.

2. **MANCE$^{++}$ Achieves State-of-the-Art Leakage–Surgicality Trade-offs:**
 - Across both NLP and vision modalities, MANCE$^{++}$ demonstrated superior performance compared to all baselines, establishing a new state-of-the-art for nonlinear concept erasure.
 - **NLP Settings:** MANCE$^{++}$ was the only method that consistently achieved near-chance leakage across various surgicality budgets, often reaching 0.0 pp leakage (at chance) and covering a high number of settings (e.g., 35/39 settings at $D_Y \le 10$ pp). In contrast, Obliviator, the strongest baseline, retained residual leakage (e.g., +4.3 pp at $D_Y \le 1$ pp) and covered fewer settings at chance.
 - **Vision (CelebA-CLIP) Settings:** MANCE$^{++}$ combined high coverage with near-floor leakage. In the "least-correlated" control regime, MANCE$^{++}$ covered 39/40 to 40/40 attributes at $D_Y \le 1$ pp and $D_Y \le 3$ pp, respectively, while Obliviator covered 15/40 to 29/40. In the "most-correlated" regime (a more challenging scenario), MANCE$^{++}$ covered 19/40 to 34/40 attributes, whereas Obliviator covered only 2/40 to 15/40. MANCE$^{++}$ was able to achieve near-chance erasure even on the harder, most-correlated attributes, where Obliviator often failed to stay within budget.

3. **Manifold Constraint Enhances Precision and Surgicality:**
 - The ablation study with AmbCE$^{++}$ (MANCE$^{++}$ without the manifold constraint but with comparable effective step magnitude) highlighted the importance of the manifold constraint. AmbCE$^{++}$ consistently left higher leakage (6–10 pp) and failed to stay within surgicality budgets on many settings. This directly supports the MCH prediction that constraining updates to the manifold improves surgical concept erasure.

4. **Greatest Gains on Difficult-to-Remove Concepts:**
 - MANCE$^{++}$'s advantage was most pronounced for concepts that are inherently harder to remove surgically, particularly those with high correlations to control concepts.
 - For NLP, this was evident with the "gender" concept, where profession information strongly correlates with gender. MANCE$^{++}$ reached chance on significantly more models compared to Obliviator at various surgicality budgets.
 - In the vision setting, MANCE$^{++}$ demonstrated a stronger performance on attributes with high target–control correlation, showing its ability to handle representation entanglement more effectively.

5. **Computational Cost:**
 - MANCE is computationally more intensive than one-shot linear erasers, requiring about eight minutes per setting on a dedicated GPU for fitting and application. This cost is primarily due to per-round k-NN queries and local SVDs, and periodic nonlinear probe refitting. However, this is an offline cost for fitting the eraser, and the resulting edited representations can be used for downstream tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research on MANCE contributes to the field of representation editing with several significant implications:

1. **Empirical Validation of the Manifold Constraint Hypothesis (MCH):** The extensive experimental results provide strong empirical evidence supporting MCH. The finding that interventions constrained to the natural representation manifold lead to more surgical and effective concept erasure is a core contribution. This hypothesis suggests a fundamental property of neural representation spaces that can be leveraged for various editing tasks.
2. **Advancement in Nonlinear Concept Erasure:** MANCE, particularly its MANCE$^{++}$ variant, achieves state-of-the-art performance in nonlinear concept erasure. This capability is critical for addressing complex and often sensitive concepts that are nonlinearly entangled within representations, such as biases related to demographics or safety attributes. The ability to remove these concepts with high surgicality improves the trustworthiness and ethical alignment of AI systems.
3. **Generalizable Framework:** MANCE's design as a complementary method that can be applied on top of existing erasure techniques makes it a versatile tool. It offers a generalizable framework for enhancing the surgicality of interventions, rather than proposing an entirely new, self-contained eraser.
4. **Broader Implications for Representation Editing:** The principles underlying MCH may extend beyond concept erasure to other forms of representation editing, such as activation steering or counterfactual generation. The insight that respecting the intrinsic geometry of representation spaces improves interventions could inform the development of more precise and controlled methods for manipulating neural models.
5. **Future Research Directions:** The work opens avenues for future research into more sophisticated manifold estimation techniques, including non-local or global models of the representation manifold. Establishing formal links between the quality of manifold estimation and guarantees on intervention outcomes could further solidify the theoretical foundations and practical utility of geometry-aware interventions.

**Limitations noted by the authors include:**
- **Measurement Scope:** Erasure and surgicality are quantified through empirical probe-based evaluations, which do not offer formal guarantees of complete unrecoverability or preservation of all unknown control concepts.
- **Local Manifold Estimation:** The reliance on local, first-order tangent space estimates, particularly in sparse or highly curved regions of the manifold, could limit the method's effectiveness. The advantage of the manifold constraint may also diminish if the intrinsic dimension of the representations approaches the full representation dimension.
- **Computational Cost:** The iterative nature and per-sample local calculations make MANCE more computationally expensive than one-shot linear methods, impacting the speed of fitting and deployment.
