# Thinking with Generated Images

> [!summary] Summary
> Shanghai Jiao Tong University and collaborating researchers develop "Thinking with Generated Images" (TwGI), a paradigm enabling large multimodal models to spontaneously generate intermediate visual steps during reasoning through native long-multimodal thought processes, achieving up to 50% relative improvement on complex multi-object vision generation tasks by implementing two complementary mechanisms—decomposing visual tasks into progressive subgoals and self-critiquing generated images for iterative refinement—while demonstrating substantial performance gains on GenEval and DPG-Bench benchmarks compared to baseline Anole models that rely solely on text-based chain-of-thought reasoning.

## Problem

- Current large multimodal models primarily rely on text-based chain-of-thought reasoning, which fails to capture the nuances of human visual cognition and limits their ability to engage dynamically with visual information
- Existing "Thinking with Images" approaches are constrained by relying on user-supplied images and limited operations, restricting the model's thinking space
- Multi-agent agentic frameworks for visual tasks are fragmented and limit generalizability and scalability while placing burden on test-time and post-training scaling methods

## Method

- Introduce =="Thinking with Generated Images" paradigm== where LMMs spontaneously generate visual steps or subgoals during reasoning, implemented as a unified autoregressive framework using ==Anole LMM architecture== generating interleaved text-image token sequences
- Develop two complementary mechanisms: ==vision generation with intermediate visual subgoals== for progressive task decomposition, and ==vision generation with self-critique== for iterative refinement
- Curate synthetic dataset for supervised fine-tuning with vision generation prompts designed to elicit self-critique and intermediate visual subgoal generation capabilities

## Results

- TwGI-Anole-7b-Obj achieved up to **50%** relative improvement in complex multi-object scenarios, consistently outperforming baseline Anole-7b on prompts involving multiple entities, spatial reasoning, and visual composition
- TwGI-Anole-7b-Crit model demonstrated notable performance increases after self-critique steps, indicating effective error detection and correction capabilities
- Substantial improvements demonstrated on vision generation benchmarks GenEval and DPG-Bench compared to text-based chain-of-thought baselines

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Visual thinking plays a crucial role in human creativity, analysis, and problem-solving, suggesting AI systems need dynamic visual reasoning capabilities rather than treating images as fixed inputs
> - Native long-multimodal thought processes can be implemented effectively on unified autoregressive LMMs by leveraging interleaved text-image token training
> - Self-critique mechanisms enable models to analyze shortcomings in generated visual hypotheses and produce refined outputs, while visual reconstruction loss combined with standard cross-entropy loss enhances image integrity and coherence during training

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research falls within the rapidly growing field of large multimodal models (LMMs) and vision-language models (VLMs). Specifically, it addresses the challenge of enhancing visual reasoning capabilities in these models. The paper builds upon existing research in chain-of-thought (CoT) prompting for language models and multi-step or tool-augmented approaches for LMMs.

**Prior Research and Limitations:**

- **Text-based CoT:** Existing LMMs primarily rely on text-based CoT for reasoning, which the authors argue is limited as it doesn't fully capture the nuances of human visual cognition.
- **Thinking with Images:** Previous work on "Thinking with Images" involves models processing or manipulating user-provided images as intermediate reasoning steps. The authors argue that this approach restricts the model's thinking space.
- **Agentic Approaches:** Recent studies use multi-component agentic frameworks for complex visual tasks. These frameworks incorporate planning, captioning, generating, critiquing, and rewarding components. While effective, the paper contends that their fragmented nature limits generalizability and scalability and places an unwarranted burden on test-time scaling and post-training scaling methods.

**Novelty and Contribution:**

The paper introduces a novel paradigm called "Thinking with Generated Images," which aims to overcome the limitations of existing approaches. This paradigm enables LMMs to spontaneously generate intermediate visual thoughts as part of their reasoning process, allowing them to explore visual hypotheses, critique their own outputs, and refine them iteratively.

**Broader Context:**

The research acknowledges the trend toward building more capable end-to-end generalist models instead of relying on complex multi-agent systems. It positions itself as a step toward realizing superintelligence by integrating various capabilities into a single, coherent model.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

- **Objective:** To develop a generalized, scalable model that can naturally think and reason across modalities (text and vision).
- **Motivation:**
 - Human cognition is natively multimodal, and visual thinking plays a crucial role in creativity, analysis, and problem-solving.
 - Current LMMs are limited in their ability to reason with visual information, as they primarily process images as fixed inputs rather than engaging with them in a dynamic and iterative manner.
 - Existing approaches to "Thinking with Images" are constrained by relying on user-supplied images and limited operations on those images.
 - Agentic approaches using multiple models complicate generalizability, scalability, and the application of scaling methods.

The authors argue that AI systems need to be able to generate intermediate visual representations to effectively tackle complex tasks in domains like design, intuitive physics, and 3D modeling.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

- **Core Idea:** The paper proposes "Thinking with Generated Images," where LMMs can spontaneously generate visual steps or subgoals during reasoning.
- **Native Long-Multimodal Thought Process:** The authors introduce a "native long-multimodal thought process" implemented on unified autoregressive LMMs (specifically Anole). This process allows the model to generate sequences of interleaved multimodal tokens (text and image patches).
- **Model Choice:** The authors utilize the Anole LMM architecture because it is pre-trained on interleaved text-image tokens, offers relatively efficient image representation, and closely resembles existing LLMs in its modeling strategy.
- **Data Curation:** The authors carefully curate a synthetic dataset for supervised fine-tuning (SFT). This dataset includes vision generation prompts designed to elicit the model's ability to (1) critique its own generated visual steps and (2) generate intermediate visual subgoals.
- **Two Complementary Mechanisms:**
 - **Vision Generation with Intermediate Visual Subgoals:** Models decompose complex visual tasks into manageable components that are generated and integrated progressively.
 - **Vision Generation with Self-Critique:** Models generate an initial visual hypothesis, analyze its shortcomings through textual reasoning, and produce refined outputs based on their own critiques.
- **Training Loss:** The paper introduces a visual reconstruction loss to enhance image integrity and coherence, in addition to the standard cross-entropy loss.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

- **Significant Improvements on Vision Generation Benchmarks:** The proposed approach demonstrates substantial improvements over baseline approaches on vision generation benchmarks, such as GenEval and DPG-Bench.
- **Improved Handling of Complex Multi-Object Scenarios:** The models achieved up to a 50% relative improvement in handling complex multi-object scenarios.
- **Benefit of Intermediate Visual Subgoals:** The TwGI-Anole-7b-Obj model consistently outperforms the baseline Anole-7b model, achieving a significant boost in the ability to handle complex prompts involving multiple entities, better spatial reasoning, and better visual composition.
- **Effectiveness of Self-Critique:** The TwGI-Anole-7b-Crit model shows notable increase in performance after the self-critique step, suggesting that analyzing generated images and reflecting on them allows for better error detection and correction.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

- **Transformative Possibilities for AI Systems:** The "Thinking with Generated Images" paradigm has the potential to revolutionize AI systems across diverse real-world applications.
- **Applications:** The paper suggests applications in fields like biochemistry (protein structure exploration), architecture (spatial design iteration), forensic analysis (crime scene reconstruction), and sports strategy (envisioning strategic plays).
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The work establishes a foundation for future research in multimodal cognition and complex visual reasoning tasks.
- **Open-Source Release:** The release of an open-source suite encourages further exploration and development by the research community.

**Potential Impact:**

This research could lead to AI systems that are capable of more creative, analytical, and strategic thinking by leveraging visual imagination and iterative refinement. It could also enable more intuitive and interactive AI tools for various domains.

**Limitations and Future Directions:**

The authors acknowledge that the current implementation is limited by the developmental stage of unified LMMs, especially in the open-source domain. Future research directions include:

- **Better Benchmarking:** Developing more realistic benchmarks for evaluating "Thinking with Generated Images" on real-world tasks.
- **Test-Time and Post-Training Scaling:** Exploring test-time and post-training scaling techniques for unified LMMs.
- **Efficient Vision Representation:** Investigating efficient vision representation methods to achieve scalable scaling in the vision modality.

**Overall Assessment:**

The research presents a promising approach to enhancing visual reasoning in LMMs. The "Thinking with Generated Images" paradigm and the "native long-multimodal thought process" offer a novel way to integrate visual information into the reasoning process. The experimental results demonstrate the potential of this approach to improve performance on vision generation tasks. The open-source release will likely stimulate further research and development in this area.

While the current implementation has limitations, the paper lays a solid foundation for future work on multimodal cognition and visual reasoning in AI.
