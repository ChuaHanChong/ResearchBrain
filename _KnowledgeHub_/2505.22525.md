# Thinking with Generated Images

> [!summary] Summary
> Shanghai Jiao Tong University and collaborating researchers develop "Thinking with Generated Images" (TwGI), a paradigm enabling large multimodal models to spontaneously generate intermediate visual steps during reasoning through native long-multimodal thought processes, achieving up to 50% relative improvement on complex multi-object vision generation tasks by implementing two complementary mechanisms—decomposing visual tasks into progressive subgoals and self-critiquing generated images for iterative refinement—while demonstrating substantial performance gains on GenEval and DPG-Bench benchmarks compared to baseline Anole models that rely solely on text-based chain-of-thought reasoning.

## Problem

- Current large multimodal models primarily rely on text-based chain-of-thought reasoning, which fails to capture the nuances of human visual cognition and limits their ability to engage dynamically with visual information
- Existing "Thinking with Images" approaches are constrained by relying on user-supplied images and limited operations, restricting the model's thinking space
- Multi-agent agentic frameworks for visual tasks are fragmented and limit generalizability and scalability while placing burden on test-time and post-training scaling methods

## Method

- Introduce =="Thinking with Generated Images" paradigm== where LMMs can spontaneously generate visual steps or subgoals during reasoning through native long-multimodal thought processes
- Implement unified autoregressive framework using ==Anole LMM architecture== that generates sequences of interleaved multimodal tokens (text and image patches)
- Develop two complementary mechanisms: ==vision generation with intermediate visual subgoals== for progressive task decomposition, and ==vision generation with self-critique== for iterative refinement
- Curate synthetic dataset for supervised fine-tuning with vision generation prompts designed to elicit self-critique and intermediate visual subgoal generation capabilities

## Results

- Achieved up to **50%** relative improvement in handling complex multi-object scenarios compared to baseline approaches
- TwGI-Anole-7b-Obj model consistently outperformed baseline Anole-7b model with significant improvements in complex prompts involving multiple entities, spatial reasoning, and visual composition
- TwGI-Anole-7b-Crit model demonstrated notable performance increases after self-critique steps, indicating effective error detection and correction capabilities
- Substantial improvements demonstrated on vision generation benchmarks GenEval and DPG-Bench compared to text-based chain-of-thought baselines

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Visual thinking plays a crucial role in human creativity, analysis, and problem-solving, suggesting AI systems need dynamic visual reasoning capabilities rather than treating images as fixed inputs
> - Native long-multimodal thought processes can be implemented effectively on unified autoregressive LMMs by leveraging interleaved text-image token training
> - Self-critique mechanisms enable models to analyze shortcomings in generated visual hypotheses and produce refined outputs based on their own analysis
> - Visual reconstruction loss combined with standard cross-entropy loss enhances image integrity and coherence during training
