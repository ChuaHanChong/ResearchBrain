# UniForce: A Unified Latent Force Model for Robot Manipulation with Diverse Tactile Sensors

> [!summary] Summary
> UniForce introduces a unified latent force model that learns a shared, physically grounded representation across diverse tactile sensors for robot manipulation. It leverages quasi-static force equilibrium as a label-free supervisory signal, enabling zero-shot transfer for force estimation and enhancing force-aware policies in tasks like whiteboard wiping with up to an 80% success rate.

## Problem

- Robotic manipulation struggles with the wide heterogeneity of tactile sensors, which vary in sensing principles, modalities, and form factors, limiting generalizable robotic skills.
- Developing force-aware capabilities for each new tactile sensor requires costly sensor-specific data collection, calibration, and model training.
- Existing tactile representation learning often lacks sensor-invariant, physically grounded contact force representations, leading to entanglement with sensor-specific features.

## Method

- **UniForce** utilizes a ==conditional variational autoencoder (CVAE)== architecture, learning an encoder for ==inverse dynamics== (mapping tactile observations to latent force) and a decoder for ==forward dynamics== (reconstructing observations from latent force).
- It leverages the physical principle of ==quasi-static force equilibrium== during bilateral grasps, where opposing forces are equal, as a ==label-free supervisory signal== to align diverse tactile observations.
- Tactile observations from heterogeneous sensors are canonicalized into a ==unified 2D marker image== representation before being processed by a ==causal spatiotemporal transformer== to extract deformation features and infer a probabilistic force manifold.

## Results

- The learned latent dimension z5 showed a strong negative correlation (**r = -0.74 ± 0.024**) with normal force (Fz), explaining approximately **55.2%** of its variance (R^2).
- UniForce achieved superior **zero-shot** force estimation, outperforming baselines like ResNet, AnyTouch, T3, and UniT in cross-sensor transfer, and achieving comparable MAE to a non-zero-shot method (GenForce).
- Integrating UniForce into a VLA model for a whiteboard wiping task improved success rates from **20%** (vision-only) to **50-60%** with single-sensor tactile input, and up to **80%** with bilateral tactile input, demonstrating effective zero-shot policy transfer.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The latent space learned by UniForce successfully encodes physically meaningful contact forces, with specific latent dimensions correlating strongly with normal and lateral shear forces.
> - Leveraging implicit physical constraints (force equilibrium) enables efficient learning of a sensor-invariant tactile representation without requiring explicit ground-truth force labels.
> - The unified representation allows for zero-shot transfer of force prediction and force-aware policies across different tactile sensors, including both vision-based and magnetic-based types.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Dexterous robot manipulation often benefits from high-quality tactile feedback, particularly force sensing, to enable nuanced interactions with objects. However, a significant challenge in developing generalizable robotic skills is the inherent heterogeneity of tactile sensors. These sensors vary widely in their sensing principles (e.g., optical, magnetic, capacitive), signal modalities (e.g., images, multi-channel signals), form factors (e.g., flat, curved), and material properties (e.g., soft, hard elastomers). This diversity typically necessitates sensor-specific data collection, calibration, and model training, which limits the transferability of force-aware policies and models across different sensor types.

Existing efforts in cross-sensor representation learning can be broadly categorized. One line of research focuses on learning shared latent spaces within a family of similar sensors, such as vision-based tactile sensors like GelSight. Methods like AnyTouch, T3, and UniT have shown success in transfer within these narrow hardware regimes, but they often do not explicitly ground their latent spaces in physical variables like contact force, limiting their applicability to non-vision-based sensors or broader robotic skill learning. Another approach involves cross-sensor transfer through self-supervised learning or image-to-image translation between tactile domains. While these methods enable some transfer, they are often trained for specific sensor pairs and may not scale efficiently as the number of sensor types increases, especially across different transduction principles. Prior work like GenForce has addressed transferable force sensing by transferring force labels and using per-sensor translation and training, but this still requires multiple stages and sensor-specific processing.

In the context of force-aware robot manipulation, tactile information is increasingly integrated into models such as Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. Current strategies often involve using pretrained tactile encoders to provide tactile tokens for policy conditioning or fusing tactile information into perception and action decoding. However, many existing tactile encoders are grounded in language semantics or rely on wrist force/torque sensors, which do not directly capture distributed contact forces or slippage. This leaves a gap in providing a universal, force-grounded tactile representation that can function across diverse and heterogeneous tactile sensors without extensive sensor-specific engineering or data collection.

UniForce addresses this gap by proposing a framework that explicitly learns a shared latent force space across heterogeneous tactile sensors. It aims to disentangle sensor-invariant contact force from sensor-specific tactile signals by leveraging a physical principle—force equilibrium during bilateral grasps—as a scalable, label-free supervisory signal. This approach distinguishes itself by focusing on a physics-grounded objective to ensure the learned representation is invariant to sensor hardware, thereby enabling zero-shot transfer of force prediction and force-aware policies across different tactile modalities.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this work is to develop UniForce, a unified tactile representation learning framework capable of learning a shared latent force space that is common across diverse and heterogeneous tactile sensors. This framework aims to reduce the domain shift typically observed across different sensors, thereby facilitating generalizable robot manipulation.

The motivation for this work stems from several key challenges in current robotic manipulation:

1. **Sensor Heterogeneity as a Barrier to Generalization**: Modern tactile sensors exhibit significant differences in their underlying sensing principles (e.g., optical, magnetic), signal modalities (e.g., images, multi-channel signals), physical form factors (e.g., flat, curved), and material properties (e.g., soft, hard elastomers). These differences mean that force-aware policies and force prediction models trained on one sensor typically perform poorly when deployed on another, hindering widespread adoption and scalability.
2. **High Cost of Sensor-Specific Training**: Developing force-aware capabilities for each new tactile sensor usually requires extensive and costly processes, including collecting large datasets with ground-truth force labels, performing sensor-specific calibration, and training separate models. This per-sensor approach is impractical for scaling robotic manipulation across a wide range of tasks and hardware.
3. **Lack of Sensor-Invariant Force Representations**: Existing tactile representation learning methods often focus on tasks like texture recognition or pose estimation, or they aim for transfer within a narrow family of sensors. Many of these approaches do not explicitly disentangle the physically meaningful contact force from the sensor-specific visual or signal characteristics. Consequently, their latent spaces may entangle force information with sensor appearance, pixels, or semantics, limiting true cross-sensor transferability.
4. **Need for Force-Grounded Policies**: While tactile feedback is crucial, current integration of tactile sensing into high-level robot control policies (such as Vision-Language-Action models) often relies on general tactile tokens or wrist force/torque measurements. There is a need for more direct, physically grounded contact force information from the fingertips that can robustly transfer across different tactile sensors to enable dexterous, force-aware manipulation.

To address these motivations, UniForce sets forth specific objectives:
- To learn an encoder that maps sensor-specific tactile observations to a shared, physically meaningful latent force space (inverse dynamics).
- To learn a decoder that can reconstruct tactile observations from this latent force, conditioned on sensor-specific reference images (forward dynamics).
- To achieve this representation learning without relying on expensive external force/torque (F/T) sensors or explicit force labels. Instead, it exploits the principle of quasi-static force equilibrium during bilateral grasping as an implicit, scalable supervisory signal.
- To enable zero-shot transfer capabilities, where the learned universal tactile encoder can be directly integrated into downstream force estimation and force-aware policy learning tasks on new, unseen sensors without any retraining or finetuning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

UniForce is designed as a conditional variational autoencoder (CVAE) that leverages the physical principle of quasi-static force equilibrium to learn a shared latent force representation across heterogeneous tactile sensors.

**Core Principle**: The central idea is that during a quasi-static bilateral grasp of an object, two opposing fingertips equipped with tactile sensors will experience approximately equal and opposite contact forces. While the resulting tactile measurements (e.g., images) from these heterogeneous sensors can differ significantly, the underlying contact force is physically constrained to be consistent. This physical constraint provides a label-free, implicit supervisory signal to align the diverse tactile observations in a common latent force space.

**Problem Formulation**:
The framework considers a set of heterogeneous tactile sensors, each with distinct taxel layouts or marker patterns. Raw sensor signals are canonicalized into a unified 2D marker image representation, where a tactile observation `x` consists of a reference frame `I_ref` (undeformed state) and a contacted frame `I_cur` (deformed state). For a synchronized sensor pair {`s_L`, `s_R`} grasping an object, the framework seeks to learn a sensor-invariant encoder `E_θ` that maps sensor-specific observations `x_L` and `x_R` to a shared latent force space. The contact force is represented as a patch-wise latent force map `z ∈ R^(N_p × 6)`, where `N_p` is the number of spatial patches, and each patch corresponds to a 6D latent force vector. The objective is to align the encoder posteriors inferred from the left and right observations, i.e., `q_θ(z | x_L) ≈ q_θ(z | x_R)`.

**UniForce Model Architecture**:
The UniForce model consists of an encoder (approximating inverse dynamics) and a decoder (modeling forward dynamics).

1. **Encoder – Inverse Dynamics**:
 - **Input**: A tactile observation `x = ⟨I_ref, I_cur⟩`, represented as a 2-frame stack of 3-channel images of size `H×W`.
 - **Patchification**: The two frames are initially patchified into tokens `V_0 ∈ R^(T × N_p × D)`, where `T=2` (reference and current), `N_p` is the number of patches, and `D` is the embedding dimension. Sinusoidal positional encodings are injected to preserve temporal order.
 - **Causal Spatiotemporal Transformer**: This module extracts deformation features:
 - **Spatial attention**: Operates within each frame, using multi-head self-attention to capture local marker structures, followed by LayerNorm and residual connections.
 - **Causal temporal attention**: Operates along the temporal axis for spatially aligned tokens. A causal mask prevents the reference frame from attending to the current contact frame, while allowing the contacted frame to attend to the reference.
 - **Probabilistic Force Manifold**: The output of the transformer, `h_last ∈ R^(N_p × D)`, is projected to a diagonal-Gaussian posterior over the patch-wise force map: `[μ, log σ^2] = Linear(LayerNorm(h_last)) ∈ R^(N_p × 12)`. During training, the latent force `z` is sampled as `μ + σ ⊙ ϵ`, where `ϵ ∼ N(0, I)`. During inference, `z = μ`.

2. **Decoder – Physics-Conditioned Forward Dynamics**:
 - **Input**: The sensor-specific reference image `I_ref` and the inferred latent force `z`.
 - **Fusion**: The latent force `z` is linearly projected and additively injected into the patch embedding of `I_ref`, forming `V_dec = PatchEmbed(I_ref) + Linear_proj(z)`.
 - **Reconstruction**: A spatial transformer then processes `V_dec` to predict the pixel-wise reconstruction of the contacted image `I_cur`, denoted as `ˆI_cur`. The conditioning on `I_ref` implicitly provides sensor-specific appearance information.

**Cross-Sensor Force Equilibrium Training**:
UniForce is trained using a three-branch objective function applied to a paired grasp involving a left sensor (`L`) and a right sensor (`R`). Let `x_i = ⟨I_i_ref, I_i_cur⟩` for `i ∈ {L, R}`, and let `z_i` be sampled from `q_θ(z | x_i)`. The decoder `D_ψ` is used to reconstruct `ˆI_i→j_cur = D_ψ(I_j_ref, z_i)`. This includes self-reconstruction (`i=j`) and cross-sensor reconstruction (`i≠j`), where the supervision target for `ˆI_i→j_cur` is `I_j_cur`. This setup encourages `z_i` to capture force-relevant information while discarding sensor-specific appearance.

The total loss function `L_total` comprises three components:
- **Reconstruction Loss (`L_recon`)**: Combines an `ℓ1` loss with LPIPS loss across all four reconstruction branches (self and cross for both left and right sensors). This encourages accurate reconstruction of marker deformations.
- **KL Divergence (`L_KL`)**: Regularizes the encoder's posterior `q_θ(z | x)` towards a standard normal distribution `N(0, I)`, similar to standard VAEs.
- **Equilibrium Loss (`L_eq`)**: Enforces patch-wise consistency between the latent force maps inferred from the left and right sensors: `L_eq = (1/N_p) ||z_L - z_R||^2_2`.

**Force-Equilibrium Data Collection**:
A custom setup was designed for data collection:
- **Hardware**: A Franka FR3 robot arm with a Franka Hand, symmetric indenters, and tactile sensors mounted on the two fingers.
- **Tactile Sensors**: Three heterogeneous sensors were used:
 - **GelSight 2017 (vision-based)**: In-house fabricated, high-resolution RGB images with a square marker pattern.
 - **TacTip (vision-based)**: Biomimetic, curved surface with internal pins, sensitive to shear and contact forces.
 - **uSkin (magnetic-based)**: XELA Robotics patch version with a 4×4 taxel array, providing multi-channel deformation signals.
- **Protocol**: TacTip was fixed on the right finger as an anchor, while the left finger alternated between GelSight and uSkin, creating two paired datasets (`⟨GelSight, TacTip⟩` and `⟨uSkin, TacTip⟩`). The robot performed slow drags and rotations with gradual indentation using various symmetric indenters to induce tangential deformation while maintaining a quasi-static state. Approximately 8,000 frames were collected per sensor pair.
- **Data Preprocessing**:
 - **Coordinate Alignment**: Right-finger tactile images were horizontally flipped to align coordinate systems.
 - **Canonicalization**: Raw signals from all sensors (RGB images for vision-based, multi-channel for uSkin) were converted into a unified binary marker-image representation using image segmentation or signal-to-marker rendering, respectively, enabling a single image-based architecture.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of UniForce focused on four key questions regarding its ability to learn force-grounded representations, perform cross-sensor reconstruction, enable zero-shot force estimation, and facilitate zero-shot force-aware policy transfer.

**Q1: Learned Latent Space Encodes Contact Force**
To assess if the learned latent space (z0–z5) encoded contact force, the frozen UniForce encoder was tested on the GenForce-Hetero dataset, which provides ground-truth force labels for TacTip, GelSight-D, and uSkin. Pearson correlations were computed between averaged latent dimensions and force components (Fx, Fy, Fz).
- **Normal Force (Fz)**: A strong negative correlation was observed between Fz and latent dimension z5 (r = -0.74 ± 0.024), indicating a stable representation of normal force across sensors, with z5 alone explaining approximately 55.2% of the variance (R^2).
- **Lateral Shear Force (Fx)**: Fx showed a moderate correlation with z2 (r = 0.58 ± 0.056), with an R^2 of 34.2%.
- **Lateral Shear Force (Fy)**: Fy was weakly encoded (mean |r| ≈ 0.21, R^2 = 4.7%), attributed to less excitation of this force component in the training dataset.
These correlations provide evidence that UniForce successfully recovers physically meaningful latent factors from self-supervised training.

**Q2: Cross-Sensor Image Reconstruction (Forward Dynamics)**
The ability of UniForce's decoder to reconstruct deformed tactile observations, including cross-sensor reconstructions, was demonstrated.
- **Self and Cross-Sensor Reconstruction**: UniForce successfully reconstructed both self-sensor images (e.g., TacTip latent force reconstructing TacTip image) and cross-sensor images (e.g., TacTip latent force reconstructing GelSight or uSkin images, conditioned on their respective reference observations).
- **Preservation of Geometry**: The reconstructions generally preserved consistent contact location and geometry, enabled by the patch-wise latent design.
- **Limitations**: Some failure cases were observed, particularly with uSkin cross-reconstructions showing inconsistent marker displacement and TacTip reconstructions (conditioned on GelSight) being incomplete under large deformations. These instances highlight challenges in precise cross-sensor rendering with sparse signals or high deformation.
Despite limitations, the overall success indicates that the decoder learns a reference-conditioned mapping consistent with the intended forward model.

**Q3: Zero-Shot Force Estimation**
UniForce's performance in zero-shot force estimation was evaluated by freezing its pretrained encoder, attaching a trainable force prediction head, and training the head on one source sensor before testing on other unseen sensors without finetuning.
- **Superior Zero-Shot Transfer**: UniForce demonstrated consistent improvements in zero-shot transfer over several baselines (ResNet, AnyTouch, T3, UniT) across various sensor-pair settings, as measured by R^2 and Mean Absolute Error (MAE).
- **Comparison with Non-Zero-Shot Method**: In specific transfers, such as from TacTip to GelSight (T→G), UniForce achieved R^2 values of {0.77, 0.62, 0.83} for (Fx, Fy, Fz), outperforming GenForce (a non-zero-shot method requiring per-sensor translation and training) which achieved {0.48, 0.49, 0.67}. UniForce's average MAE was also comparable to GenForce across six transfer settings. This was attributed to UniForce's end-to-end, force-grounded latent space training.
- **Resolution Dependency**: Weaker transfer was observed when uSkin (a lower-resolution sensor) was used as the source sensor, consistent with similar findings in prior work like GenForce.
The strong improvements in force error and R^2 values validate UniForce's effectiveness in zero-shot force estimation across heterogeneous sensors.

**Q4: Zero-Shot Force-Aware Policy Transfer**
UniForce was integrated into a Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model (π0.5) for a whiteboard wiping task to assess its utility in force-aware skill learning with zero-shot policy transfer. The UniForce encoder remained frozen, mapping marker-based tactile images into force-grounded tactile tokens.
- **Improved Success Rates**: Adding tactile sensing generally improved task success rates compared to vision-only policies (V: 20% success rate, V+T: 50%).
- **Zero-Shot Policy Transfer**: A policy trained with TacTip (50% SR) could be transferred zero-shot to GelSight (45% SR) and uSkin (60% SR) without finetuning the tactile encoder, indicating the generalizability of the UniForce representation.
- **Multi-Sensor Coordination Benefits**: Using bilateral tactile feedback from both fingers (V+T+G: 75% SR, V+T+U: 80% SR) yielded higher success rates than single-sensor tactile input, suggesting that UniForce enables the VLA model to effectively exploit complementary contact information (e.g., normal and shear cues from both sides) for stable contact regulation.
- **Stable Contact Regulation**: Analysis of end-effector z-position showed flatter profiles during wiping phases with UniForce and tactile sensing, indicating more stable vertical contact regulation and fewer instances of inappropriate force (e.g., slip-induced height changes or over-pressing).
These results confirm UniForce's capability to support zero-shot force-aware policy learning and multi-sensor coordination in robot manipulation tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The UniForce framework presents several significant contributions and has the potential to impact various aspects of robotic manipulation:

1. **Bridging Sensor Heterogeneity**: UniForce directly addresses a fundamental challenge in tactile robotics by providing a unified framework that can learn a shared, force-grounded representation across diverse tactile sensors, including both vision-based and non-vision-based modalities. This is a step towards overcoming the fragmentation caused by sensor-specific data and models.

2. **Reduced Data Collection and Training Costs**: By leveraging quasi-static force equilibrium as an implicit, label-free supervisory signal, UniForce eliminates the need for expensive and labor-intensive collection of explicit force labels during representation learning. This significantly reduces the overhead associated with deploying new tactile sensors or developing force-aware capabilities.

3. **Enabling Zero-Shot Transfer**: The framework demonstrates zero-shot transferability for both force estimation and force-aware policy learning. This means that a universal encoder, once trained, can be plugged into new downstream tasks or deployed with entirely different tactile sensors without requiring further retraining or finetuning of the tactile representation module. This capability is critical for scaling robotic applications and improving adaptability.

4. **Improved Robot Dexterity and Robustness**: By providing access to physically meaningful, sensor-invariant latent force representations, UniForce can enhance a robot's ability to perform delicate, contact-rich manipulation tasks. The demonstrated improvements in force estimation accuracy and policy success rates in tasks like wiping suggest that robots can achieve more stable and precise interactions, reducing failures due to slip or excessive force.

5. **Foundation for Generalizable Robotic Skills**: UniForce contributes to the development of more generalizable robotic skills. By standardizing the tactile input through a shared force space, it simplifies the integration of tactile feedback into broader robot learning architectures, such as Vision-Language-Action models, allowing policies to benefit from tactile cues regardless of the specific sensor hardware.

6. **Future Research Directions**: The identified limitations, such as reliance on the quasi-static assumption, the lack of explicit material property modeling, challenges in generalizing to entirely new sensing modalities, and potential for more sophisticated tactile integration into large models, open avenues for future research. Addressing these could further enhance the robustness, adaptability, and applicability of tactile sensing in robotics.

In summary, UniForce offers a scalable and efficient approach to integrate diverse tactile sensing into robotic systems, paving the way for more dexterous, robust, and adaptable robots capable of performing complex manipulation tasks across varying environmental conditions and hardware setups.
