# VibeWorlding: Can Multimodal Agents Construct 3D Open Worlds End-to-End?

> [!summary] Summary
> VibeWorlding formulates end-to-end 3D open-world construction as a multi-turn multimodal agent task: given a text or image+text query, a policy invokes asset retrieve/add/delete/rotate/translate tools and reflects on rendered feedback. Trained via cold-start SFT plus joint multimodal GRPO on a dual-constraint (physics + intent) verifier, VibeWorlder-30B-A3B beats GPT-5.5 and Qwen3.8-Max on the new VWE-Bench.

## Problem

- Existing MLLM 3D-world agents are evaluated only on idealized, simple queries, leaving intent inference, tool use, and 3D reasoning under-analyzed on realistic, open-ended requests.
- 3D asset retrieval, editing, and rendering operations are scattered across incompatible tools, so no unified sandbox exists for end-to-end agentic construction.
- No open-source framework unifies benchmarking and agentic RL training for 3D world construction, so whether post-training improves these capabilities remains unstudied.

## Method

- **VWE-Bench**: 2,616 artist-guided synthetic 3D assets, 323 human-annotated seed worlds, and 6,828 reverse-synthesized queries split into verified (ground-truth) and unverified (rubric-judged) sets.
- **VibeWorlding-Gym** sandbox unifies asset retrieve/add/delete/rotate/translate and Blender multi-view rendering as ==MCP tools==, paired with a ==dual-constraint verifier== combining physical feasibility checks and an MLLM rubric judge.
- Joint multimodal post-training pipeline: cold-start ==SFT== on reward-filtered reverse-synthesized trajectories followed by ==GRPO== reinforcement learning jointly over text-only and multimodal (rendered-image) queries with outcome-based rewards.

## Results

- Frontier MLLMs plateau below **60%** overall Pass@1 (GPT-5.5 **57.3%**, Qwen3.8-Max **56.9%**), while untrained Qwen3-VL-8B/30B-A3B score only **5.3%**/**13.6%**.
- RL post-training lifts VibeWorlder-30B-A3B from **13.6%** (base) to **34.5%** (SFT) to **59.3%** (RL), surpassing GPT-5.5 and Qwen3.8-Max; VibeWorlder-8B reaches **41.4%**, matching Gemini 3.1-pro's **42.7%**.
- Verifier agrees with blind human judgment at Cohen's kappa **0.54** (case-level **83.4%** agreement) and Spearman rho **0.88** system-level ranking correlation across **17** evaluated systems.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Collision-free spatial editing, not intent understanding or retrieval, is the persistent bottleneck: collision pass rates stay at 59-68% even after RL across all models.
> - SFT and RL unlock distinct capability axes: SFT builds physical/ecological competence and partial 3D understanding, while RL is what drives 3D reasoning and understanding gains.
> - Verifiable, ground-truth-checkable rewards yield larger and more stable RL gains than MLLM-as-judge rewards, suggesting reward fidelity bounds achievable policy improvement.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses the construction of interactive 3D open worlds from user queries, a task significant for gaming, simulation, and embodied AI. Recently, multimodal large language models (MLLMs) have been leveraged to automate this process. Existing MLLM-powered 3D world construction methods primarily fall into two categories:

1. **Sequential Pipeline Approaches**: These methods decompose the construction process into distinct stages, each handled by a specialized sub-agent. Examples include SceneCraft (Hu et al., 2024) and 3D-GPT (Sun et al., 2025), which typically involve a planner agent, followed by iterative critique and refinement stages. Some works also integrate MLLMs with game engines to compile textual descriptions into executable scene programs, such as UnrealLLM (Tang et al., 2025), LatticeWorld (Duan et al., 2025), and WorldGen (Wang et al., 2026).
2. **Autonomous Agentic Workflows**: These approaches enable a single agent to autonomously plan, invoke asset operation tools, and self-refine the 3D world through multi-turn interactions. SAGE (Xia et al., 2026), SceneWeaver (Yang et al., 2026), and SceneAssistant (Luo et al., 2026) integrate asset editing, procedural content generation, and rendering into a unified tool set. SceneReVis (Zhao et al., 2026) has explored multi-turn agentic reinforcement learning for world-building.

Despite these advancements, the authors identify several limitations in the current landscape that VIBEWORLDING aims to address:
- **Limited Query Complexity**: Most existing methods are evaluated on idealized, simple queries, struggling with complex, open-ended user intents common in real-world scenarios.
- **Lack of Unified Open-Source Frameworks**: The absence of an open-source framework hinders systematic benchmarking, comparison, and training of 3D world construction agents, particularly for agentic reinforcement learning (RL) post-training. Many existing frameworks remain closed-source.
- **Fragmented 3D Asset Environment**: High-quality, physically consistent 3D assets are difficult to obtain at scale, and necessary asset retrieval, editing, and rendering operations are often scattered across incompatible tools, impeding end-to-end construction.
- **Challenges in 3D World Verification**: A feasible 3D world must meet physical constraints (e.g., collision-free layouts, realistic heights) and user intent (e.g., aesthetic coherence, task requirements), which are difficult to evaluate systematically.

VIBEWORLDING contributes to the field by proposing a unified, open-source framework designed to overcome these limitations, enabling systematic benchmarking and training of MLLM-powered agents for end-to-end 3D open world construction, particularly focusing on complex and multi-turn interactions with multimodal feedback. This work aims to push the boundaries of MLLM capabilities in 3D spatial reasoning and tool use by leveraging reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards, an approach not extensively explored for multimodal 3D world construction.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of this research is to enable multimodal agents to autonomously construct interactive 3D open worlds from user queries in an end-to-end manner. This goal is referred to as "vibe worlding."

The key objectives and motivations driving this work are as follows:

1. **Developing a Unified Benchmarking and Training Framework**: A primary objective is to create a unified, open-source framework, termed VIBEWORLDING, for both benchmarking and training vibe worlding agents. This addresses the current fragmentation in the research landscape, where systematic analysis and comparison of agents are difficult due to a lack of standardized tools and datasets.
2. **Enabling Autonomous Intent Understanding, Planning, and Tool Use**: The research aims to develop agents capable of autonomously inferring user intent, planning scene layouts, invoking 3D tools (e.g., asset retrieval, editing), and reflecting on multimodal feedback (e.g., 3D map, rendered images) over a multi-turn agent-environment interaction process. This moves beyond simpler, pipeline-based approaches.
3. **Addressing Challenges of Complex, Open-Ended User Queries**: Existing methods are often limited to idealized or simple queries. A key motivation is to support complex user queries with open-ended intents, simulating real-world scenarios more effectively. This necessitates robust multimodal understanding and reasoning capabilities in the agents.
4. **Unifying Fragmented 3D Asset Environments and Tools**: The authors aim to overcome the fragmentation of 3D asset environments. This involves building a high-quality, physically consistent 3D asset library and unifying disparate asset retrieval, editing, and rendering operations under a single sandbox interface to facilitate end-to-end construction.
5. **Establishing Robust Verification and Reward Mechanisms**: Constructing feasible 3D worlds requires adherence to physical constraints (e.g., no collisions, proper grounding) and fulfillment of user intent. The objective is to develop a dual-constraint verifier that combines physical feasibility checks with an MLLM-based judge for intent fulfillment, providing reliable evaluation and scalable reward signals for RL training.
6. **Investigating the Impact of Agentic RL Post-training**: A core motivation is to systematically study whether training methodologies, specifically agentic reinforcement learning (RL) post-training, can enhance the underlying capabilities of multimodal agents in understanding, reasoning, and tool use for 3D world construction. This includes exploring if RL can enable open-source MLLMs to match or surpass closed-source frontier models.
7. **Systematically Analyzing Agent Capabilities**: The work seeks to systematically measure and analyze the strengths and defects of multimodal agents across various capabilities pertinent to 3D world construction, such as physical feasibility, ecological plausibility, 3D understanding, 3D reasoning, and retrieval plausibility.

These objectives are motivated by the recognition of the importance of interactive 3D open world construction for various applications and the current limitations in existing MLLM-powered solutions, prompting the need for a comprehensive framework for evaluation and improvement.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

VIBEWORLDING is presented as a unified framework designed for benchmarking and training "vibe worlding agents." The approach formulates the task as a multi-turn, multimodal, tool-integrated reasoning process where an agent infers user intent, plans scene layouts, invokes 3D tools, and reflects on multimodal feedback until a final interactive 3D world is generated. The methodology comprises two main components: VWE-BENCH for evaluation and VIBEWORLDING-GYM for training.

#### 3.1. VWE-BENCH Construction (VibeWorlding Evaluation Benchmark)

VWE-BENCH is a benchmark built through a collaboration between MLLMs and human annotators, designed to provide high-quality 3D assets, seed 3D worlds, and multimodal queries.

- **Stage 1: High-Quality 3D Asset Synthesis**:
 - An artist-guided generative pipeline was used to create a physically consistent asset library.
 - **Asset Textual and Image Annotation**: Art-annotators defined an inventory of 3,148 native concept assets (names, categories) and obtained reference asset images via text-to-image generation using the Gemini 3.1-flash-image model.
 - **Image-to-3D Generation**: Each asset image was transformed into a 3D mesh (``.glb`` file) using the Hunyuan3D 3.1 model.
 - **Quality Filtering and Size Annotation**: Generated 3D assets were filtered for fidelity to concept images. Surviving assets (2,616 in total) were annotated with real-world scales, defining native bounding boxes and semantic descriptions. These assets span 20 semantic categories and various physical sizes.

- **Stage 2: High-Quality Seed 3D World Annotation**:
 - Professional art-annotators constructed 323 coarse but functional seed 3D worlds directly from the synthesized assets. These seed worlds jointly cover the entire asset library and exhibit diverse complexity, ranging from 8 to 258 placed assets.

- **Stage 3: Reverse Multimodal Query Synthesis**:
 - Using the constructed assets and seed worlds, 6,828 multimodal user queries were reverse-synthesized, categorized into "3D world construction" (from scratch) and "3D world refinement" (editing existing worlds).
 - **3D World Construction Queries (1,364 queries)**: An MLLM was prompted to read a seed world and generate requests to build it from an empty map.
 - *Theme only*: High-level atmosphere (e.g., "an eerie wasteland shrine").
 - *Theme + elements*: Theme plus a coarse list of required elements.
 - *Full blueprint*: Detailed elements and spatial organization.
 - *Distractor*: Queries with infeasible sub-requests (e.g., missing asset, physics violation) to test agent clarification.
 - **3D World Refinement Queries (5,464 queries)**:
 - *Asset perturbation*: Directly perturbing seed world assets (deleting/moving) and using an MLLM to describe the changes.
 - *Asset-level edit (precise)*: Fine-grained edits with ground truth verification (1,710 queries).
 - *Asset-level edit (fuzzy)*: Vague, underspecified instructions (e.g., "tidy up the trees a bit").
 - *MLLM-as-critic*: An MLLM critiques a seed world holistically to synthesize improvement queries.
 - *Scene critique*: Identifies shortcomings (e.g., empty, cluttered).
 - *Scene guidance*: High-level directional guidance (e.g., "make it feel more lively").
 - *Scene restatement*: Reiteration of desired end state.
 - *Complex description*: Involving multiple coordinated edits or rich requirements.
 - Queries are split into Verified (1,710 precise asset-level edits with ground truth) and Unverified (5,118 open-ended queries evaluated by rubrics). Art-annotators reviewed and filtered low-quality queries.

#### 3.2. VIBEWORLDING-GYM (Joint Multimodal RL Training Framework)

VIBEWORLDING-GYM is designed to support scalable agentic RL training.

- **4.2.1. 3D Sandbox Construction**:
 - **Unified 3D Tools**: Five tools for world construction and editing: `asset_retrieve`, `asset_add`, `asset_delete`, `asset_rotate`, and `asset_translate`.
 - `asset_retrieve` is built on Qwen3-Embedding-4B, trained with InfoNCE loss on a synthetic positive–negative pair dataset, augmented with an Error-Cost-Ordered (ECO) hinge chain for graded relevance. It ensures retrieved candidates are placeable.
 - **Unified 3D Rendering**: Blender is used as a rendering service. After each agent action, the modified 3D world is rendered from five fixed camera viewpoints (front, back, left, right, top-down) and returned as multimodal feedback.

- **4.2.2. Dual-Constraint Verifier**:
 - A valid 3D world must satisfy physical feasibility and user intent fulfillment. The verifier integrates Python-based geometric checks and an MLLM-based judge.
 - **Physical Feasibility Verification**:
 - *Collision*: Geometric check for interpenetration, clipping, or overlap (minimum 10m³ overlap beyond 0.5m per-axis slack).
 - *Height*: Checks for unsupported floating or objects sunk into the ground, with exemptions for legitimate elevations (e.g., objects on surfaces, flying creatures).
 - **Intent Fulfillment Verification (MLLM-based judge, Gemini 3.5-flash backbone)**:
 - *Ecological plausibility*: Assesses global coherence, stylistic consistency, and absence of out-of-context objects.
 - *3D understanding*: Evaluates if the agent correctly grasps user requests (entities, layout).
 - *3D reasoning*: Determines if the agent correctly uses 3D tools to modify the world according to intent.
 - *Retrieval plausibility*: Checks if the agent selects semantically appropriate assets during retrieval.
 - **Multi-Dimension Reward Calculating**: For Unverified queries, all checks (physical and intent) must pass for a full reward (binary 0/1). For Verified queries, a rule-based score (proportion of correctly modified assets in) is used, with a strict reward verification mechanism that penalizes unauthorized edits.

- **4.2.3. Joint Multimodal RL Post-training**:
 - **Cold-start Data Synthesis for SFT**: Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) trajectories are synthesized. For Unverified queries, Gemini 3.1-pro generates high-quality outcomes, then reverses to rewrite complete reasoning trajectories. For Verified queries, trajectories are reverse-synthesized from ground-truth maps. Full-parameter SFT is applied to these trajectories.
 - **Joint Multimodal RL**: Uses GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) to train the cold-started agent policy. It learns from both pure-text (from-scratch construction) and multimodal (refinement with renders) queries. The outcome-based reward (binary for unverified, for verified) guides optimization.

#### 3.3. Experimental Setup

- **Dataset Partition**: VWE-BENCH is split into training (SFT cold-start, RL) and testing sets with disjoint seed 3D worlds to ensure generalization.
- **Metrics**: Pass@1 (fraction of queries solved in a single rollout) is the primary automatic metric. An Unverified query passes if all verifier dimensions pass. A Verified query's Pass@1 is the mean rule-based score.
- **Human Evaluation**: A blind human study with professional 3D artists complements automatic evaluation, assessing intent fulfillment, ecological plausibility, physical feasibility, factual hallucination, and response intelligence. A strong correlation (Spearman’s ρ = 0.88) between human and verifier rankings is reported.
- **Baselines**: Compared against frontier MLLMs (GPT-5.5, Gemini models, Claude-Opus-4.8, Kimi-K3, Qwen3.8-Max), agent-scaffold frameworks (SceneWeaver, SAGE, SceneAssistant with GPT-5.5 backbone), and open-source MLLMs (Qwen3-VL-8B/30B-A3B) and their post-trained variants.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments reveal the capabilities and limitations of current MLLMs in end-to-end 3D world construction and demonstrate the effectiveness of multimodal RL post-training.

#### 4.1. Current MLLM Performance and the Impact of Multimodal RL

- **Limited Performance of Frontier MLLMs**: Even the strongest frontier MLLMs, such as GPT-5.5 and Qwen3.8-Max, achieved Pass@1 scores below 60% on VWE-BENCH (GPT-5.5 at 57.3%, Qwen3.8-Max at 56.9%). This indicates that end-to-end 3D world construction remains a challenging task. Untrained open-source models performed substantially worse, with Qwen3-VL-8B achieving 5.3% and Qwen3-VL-30B-A3B achieving 13.6% Pass@1.
- **Significant Improvement from Multimodal RL Post-training**: The combination of cold-start SFT and joint multimodal RL significantly improved the performance of open-source backbones. The VibeWorlder-30B-A3B model improved from a base of 13.6% to 34.5% (SFT) and then to 59.3% (after RL), achieving the best overall Pass@1 score among all evaluated models. VibeWorlder-8B (41.4%) demonstrated parity with Gemini 3.1-pro (42.7%).
- **Superiority on Verified Tasks**: VibeWorlder-30B-A3B notably excelled on Verified tasks (64.5% Pass@1), outperforming GPT-5.5 (60.4%), suggesting that agentic RL is particularly effective in instilling precise, rule-checkable editing capabilities.

#### 4.2. Capability Analysis and Bottlenecks

- **Challenges in 3D World Construction**: Building a 3D world from scratch proved challenging for all MLLMs, requiring complex coordination of asset retrieval, spatial reasoning, and physically plausible placement while avoiding collisions.
- **Difficult Refinement Queries**: Among refinement tasks, "Complex Description" and "Scene Restatement" were identified as the most difficult, as they involve interpreting long, underspecified instructions, with frontier models achieving below 50% Pass@1. RL consistently improved performance across all refinement sub-types.
- **Dominant Bottleneck: Collision-Free 3D Editing**: A six-capability analysis revealed that collision detection/avoidance remains the weakest capability across all models, with scores ranging from 59% to 68% even after RL training. This highlights precise, collision-free 3D spatial editing as a key unresolved challenge.
- **RL's Impact on Specific Capabilities**: RL training substantially improved:
 - **3D reasoning**: From 6–20% (base) to 56–85% (VibeWorlder-30B-A3B reaching 85%).
 - **Retrieval usage**: To 91–99%, surpassing frontier models.
 - **3D understanding**: To 90–98%.
 - **Ecological plausibility**: To 84–94%.
 - This indicates that RL enables models to better understand scenes and retrieve appropriate assets, but accurate, collision-aware placement remains an issue.

#### 4.3. Agent Capability Improvement in Multimodal RL Post-training

- **Complementary Roles of SFT and RL**:
 - **Cold-start SFT**: Primarily established foundational physical and ecological competence, with initial scores around 60% for physical feasibility and 90% for ecological plausibility, which remained largely stable during subsequent RL. It also provided limited improvements in 3D understanding (from 0.02 to 0.17).
 - **Multimodal RL**: Primarily unlocked 3D spatial capabilities, significantly improving 3D understanding (from 0.17 to 0.80) and 3D reasoning (from 0.04 to 0.69), which was initially the weakest capability.
- **Learning Regimes for Verified vs. Unverified Queries**:
 - **Verified Queries**: Showed substantial and stable performance improvements (from 0.14 to 0.64 reward) due to the precise and reliable nature of ground-truth-derived rewards.
 - **Unverified Queries**: Also showed improvement (from 0.04 to 0.37) but with considerably more unstable training reward curves, suggesting that the reliability of RL improvement is constrained by reward fidelity from MLLM-based judges.

#### 4.4. Failed Case Analysis

Manual inspection of failures revealed two dominant and persistent failure modes:

- **Imprecise 3D Distance Editing**: Agents frequently emitted incorrect 3D coordinates despite selecting the correct asset and semantic target. For example, an object might be translated by the correct magnitude but in the opposite direction (e.g., mapping "forward" to +Y instead of −Y), leading to positional errors and collisions. This suggests a misunderstanding of coordinate frames or precise spatial relationships.
- **Overediting on Constructed 3D Worlds**: During multi-turn refinement, agents sometimes modified assets the user did not request. For instance, an agent might correctly add a new object but simultaneously delete an unrelated existing object. This "scope violation" leads to zero reward, highlighting weak grounding of the existing 3D world state and poor preservation of untouched elements.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The VIBEWORLDING framework, benchmark, and associated findings offer several contributions and potential impacts on the field of multimodal AI and 3D content generation:

1. **Unified and Open-Source Framework**: VIBEWORLDING represents the first agentic RL framework designed for end-to-end 3D world construction. Its open-source release of 2,616 high-quality 3D assets, 323 human-annotated seed 3D worlds, 6,828 multimodal user queries, an asset retrieval embedding model, an interactive sandbox environment, a dual-constraint verifier, and a unified agent post-training framework (SFT and RL) provides a comprehensive stack. This infrastructure is expected to facilitate systematic research, benchmarking, and comparison of 3D world construction agents.
2. **Advanced Benchmarking for Complex Scenarios**: The VWE-BENCH benchmark moves beyond idealized or simple queries common in prior work. By including diverse query types (from-scratch construction, multi-turn refinement, precise/fuzzy edits, distractor queries), it provides a more realistic and challenging evaluation platform for agents to demonstrate capabilities in understanding complex user intents and operating within varied constraints.
3. **Demonstrated Efficacy of Multimodal RL Post-training**: The research empirically demonstrates that agentic reinforcement learning, particularly when combined with cold-start SFT, can significantly enhance the 3D spatial reasoning and tool-use capabilities of open-source MLLMs. The finding that VibeWorlder-30B-A3B surpasses leading closed-source frontier models (like GPT-5.5) on overall Pass@1, especially on verified tasks requiring precise editing, suggests a powerful paradigm for developing highly capable 3D world agents.
4. **Systematic Capability Analysis**: The detailed six-capability analysis provides insights into where current multimodal agents excel and where they struggle. Identifying collision-free 3D editing as the dominant bottleneck and differentiating the contributions of SFT (foundational competence) and RL (spatial reasoning) offers targeted directions for future research and model development.
5. **Identification of Persistent Challenges**: The analysis of failure modes (imprecise 3D distance editing, overediting) highlights fundamental challenges in 3D spatial grounding and persistent state awareness that need to be addressed in future MLLM architectures and training methodologies. The limitations related to time-consuming 3D rendering and the subjectivity of end-to-end verification in open-domain tasks are also critical for the field.
6. **Guidance for Future Research and Development**: The paper outlines several promising directions for improvement, including the development of richer and more integrated 3D tools and higher-level agent skills, methods for larger-scale 3D world construction, more efficient multimodal RL techniques (e.g., potential reward credit assignment), and the use of more diverse data sources and user queries (e.g., image-to-3D-world tasks).
7. **Practical Application Potential**: The VibeWorlding CLI prototype demonstrates the immediate usability of the post-trained agent for interactive 3D world creation and refinement, hinting at practical applications in game development, simulation environments, and tools for embodied AI research.

By providing a comprehensive framework, robust benchmarks, and empirical evidence for the effectiveness of multimodal RL, this work aims to advance the frontier of end-to-end 3D world construction and inform the development of more intelligent and capable multimodal agents.
