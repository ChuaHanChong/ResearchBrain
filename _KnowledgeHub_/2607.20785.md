# Robostral Navigate

> [!summary] Summary
> Mistral AI developed Robostral Navigate, a scalable navigation system for embodied robots that leverages a single monocular RGB camera and natural language instructions. The system achieved new state-of-the-art results on both R2R-CE and RxR-CE benchmarks, outperforming methods that use more extensive sensory inputs.

## Problem

- High-performing embodied navigation systems often rely on specialized sensors (depth, LiDAR, multi-camera, pre-built maps), limiting compatible hardware and increasing deployment costs.
- Existing navigation systems commonly output robot-specific metric displacements, requiring recalibration for different platforms and hindering generalization across robot embodiments.
- Training embodied navigation systems from real-world data is costly and logistically challenging, impeding large-scale data collection and efficient model development.

## Method

- Developed **Robostral Navigate**, an 8-billion-parameter ==Vision-Language Model (VLM)== for high-level path planning that operates solely on monocular RGB images, paired with a ==diffusion policy== for low-level motor control.
- Implemented a navigation strategy that prioritizes predicting ==waypoints in image space== for robustness to camera intrinsics and world scale, with a fallback to metric displacement for off-screen targets, enabling cross-robot generalization.
- Created an efficient simulation-based data pipeline generating 2.4 million expert trajectories and introduced a ==prefix-caching technique== with a ==tree-based attention mask== for VLM training, reducing training time by 22x.

## Results

- Achieved a new state-of-the-art on the R2R-CE benchmark with a **77.4%** Success Rate (SR) and **74.2%** SPL, surpassing prior single-camera methods by **10.5** percentage points and multi-sensor systems by **5.3** percentage points.
- Established a new state-of-the-art on the RxR-CE benchmark with a **75.1%** SR and **68.7%** SPL, outperforming previous single-camera methods by **1.7** percentage points and demonstrating superior path efficiency over multi-camera baselines.
- Online reinforcement learning (CISPO) contributed significant improvements, increasing success rates by **4.03%** on R2R-CE and **3.9%** on RxR-CE over supervised fine-tuning alone.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decomposing navigation into VLM-based high-level planning and a diffusion policy for low-level control allows for efficient and robust execution with minimal sensory input.
> - Predicting navigation targets in image space (pixel coordinates) is effective for enabling cross-robot generalization across diverse embodiments, eliminating the need for robot-specific recalibration.
> - Combining large-scale simulation data with advanced training techniques, including prefix-caching and online reinforcement learning (CISPO), efficiently scales VLM training and improves agent robustness in challenging scenarios.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Embodied navigation, specifically the ability of a robot to follow natural language instructions through an environment, is identified as a fundamental capability for general-purpose robotics. Recent advancements in vision-language models (VLMs) have contributed to progress in this area, with several systems demonstrating improved performance. However, a common limitation among many high-performing existing systems is their reliance on specialized or extensive sensory inputs, such as depth sensors, LiDAR, multi-camera rigs, or pre-built environmental maps. This dependency introduces several constraints: it narrows the range of compatible robotic platforms, increases per-unit hardware costs, and often necessitates environment-specific calibration. These factors collectively hinder the broad deployment and scalability of navigation policies.

The current work positions itself within this landscape by addressing the challenge of scalability. It aims to develop a navigation system that minimizes sensor assumptions, specifically by using only a monocular RGB camera stream, which is a ubiquitous sensor across various robotic platforms. Furthermore, it seeks to generalize across different robot embodiments and be trainable efficiently, primarily from simulated data. By focusing on these aspects, Robostral Navigate attempts to advance the field towards more practical and widely deployable robotic navigation solutions, moving beyond approaches that achieve high accuracy at the expense of hardware complexity or specialized data requirements. This aligns with the broader trend towards developing general-purpose robots capable of operating in diverse, unstructured environments with minimal specialized adaptations.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of Robostral Navigate is to develop a scalable navigation system for embodied robots that can follow natural language instructions. This overarching objective is broken down into several specific motivations and design principles:

1. **Minimize Sensor Assumptions:** A core motivation is to reduce reliance on complex and costly sensors. The paper argues that current state-of-the-art systems often depend on depth sensors, multi-camera setups, or pre-built maps, which limit hardware compatibility and increase deployment expenses. To address this, Robostral Navigate is designed to operate solely on a stream of monocular RGB images, identified as the most universally available sensor across diverse robotic platforms.

2. **Generalize Across Robot Embodiments:** Traditional navigation systems often output metric displacements tied to a robot's specific geometry, requiring recalibration for different platforms. A key objective is to create a policy robust to variations in robot morphology and camera configurations. This is achieved by having the model predict waypoints in image space (pixel coordinates) rather than robot-specific metric coordinates. This approach is intended to make the policy inherently robust to changes in camera intrinsics and scene scale, enabling deployment across wheeled, legged, and aerial robots without re-calibration.

3. **Efficient Training from Simulation Alone:** The high cost and logistical challenges of real-world data collection for training embodied navigation systems are significant barriers to scalability. Therefore, a major motivation is to enable efficient training exclusively within simulation environments. This involves generating large-scale synthetic datasets and developing training methodologies that can leverage this data effectively to reduce training time from months to days.

4. **Improve Navigation Performance:** While focusing on scalability, the project also aims to achieve high performance on established benchmarks. The objective is not merely to build a scalable system but one that can surpass existing methods, including those that use more privileged sensing, in terms of success rate and path efficiency.

In summary, the motivation behind Robostral Navigate is to provide a comprehensive solution for embodied navigation that is not only accurate but also practical for widespread deployment by addressing the fundamental challenges of sensor dependency, robot-specific calibration, and data collection costs.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Robostral Navigate employs a two-stage decomposition for the navigation task and integrates several techniques for efficient and robust training.

#### 3.1. System Architecture

The system decomposes navigation into two main challenges: high-level path planning and low-level motor control.
- **High-Level Planning:** An 8-billion-parameter Vision-Language Model (VLM), named Robostral Navigate, is responsible for understanding natural language instructions, reasoning about the scene from visual input, and predicting a coarse path. It takes a language instruction and a history of monocular RGB frames as input.
- **Low-Level Control:** A smaller, 121-million-parameter diffusion policy translates the VLM's coarse path into a dense trajectory. This trajectory is then converted into high-frequency actuator torques by an embodiment-specific motion-tracking controller. The VLM operates at 0.5 Hz, while the diffusion policy generates trajectories at 10 Hz, and the motion controller produces motor commands at 100 Hz.

#### 3.2. Navigation via Pointing with Displacement Fallback

The VLM predicts the next destination using two modes:
- **Visual Pointing Mode:** When the target is visible within the camera's field of view, the model infers its image coordinates (u, v) and the desired change in heading (Δθ) upon arrival. This mode is prioritized due to its robustness to camera intrinsics and world scale, facilitating generalization across platforms.
- **Metric Displacement Fallback Mode:** For destinations outside the current field of view, the model predicts local translational (Δx, Δy) and rotational (Δθ) displacements relative to the current frame. This ensures the robot can continue to make progress even when the target is not directly visible.
During training, the system jointly predicts all five quantities (u, v, Δx, Δy, Δθ) when the destination is visible. If the destination is invisible, only metric displacements are predicted. The model also predicts a STOP action upon task completion.

#### 3.3. Pointing to Actions

The VLM's predicted waypoints (pointing coordinates or displacements) are converted into robot actions by a diffusion transformer model. This model receives the VLM's prediction, robot physical parameters (height, radius), the RGB frame used by the VLM, and the current RGB frame. It outputs a sequence of 30 delta coordinates (dx, dy, dθ) representing relative 2D displacements and 1D rotations for the next second, forming an action chunk. This action chunk is then fed to a motion controller specific to the robot platform, which generates high-frequency motor commands.

#### 3.4. Cross-Robot Generalization

Generalization across different robot platforms is inherent in the image-space pointing approach. To further enhance this, training data for both the VLM and diffusion policies incorporates randomized robot configurations, including variations in robot height (0.4-1.8m), radius (0.15-0.45m), camera height (70-100% of robot height), and camera pitch (0-25°). This randomization aims to reduce dependency on specific hardware setups, enabling deployment on diverse platforms like the Galaxea R1 and Hiwonder JetAuto using the same model weights, with only the low-level platform controller adapted.

#### 3.5. Training

- **Data Generation:** An efficient simulation-based pipeline generated approximately 2.4 million expert navigation trajectories across 350,000 diverse 3D scenes (offices, residential, commercial, outdoor). Farthest point sampling ensures varied start and goal positions, leading to diverse trajectory lengths and complexities.
- **Supervised Training with Prefix Trees:** To address the computational inefficiency of processing long sequential episodes (O(T²) token complexity), a prefix-caching technique is introduced. This method encodes entire episodes as single training sequences (instruction | observation₀ | action₀ | ... | observationₙ | actionₙ), reducing token complexity to O(T) and training time significantly (22× token reduction, from months to days). To prevent the model from conditioning on previous ground-truth actions during training, a tree-based attention mask is implemented. This mask ensures that a token can only attend to prior tokens in a shared prefix or within the same branch, maintaining visual grounding for action prediction.
- **Online Reinforcement Learning via CISPO:** After supervised fine-tuning (SFT), the model undergoes online reinforcement learning using CISPO to mitigate exposure bias and improve exploration and recovery capabilities. The reward function is defined as `-max(2, dist_to_goal)`, which penalizes distance to the goal but clips the penalty at 2 meters to encourage the STOP action. A hard subset of 35,000 tasks, identified as failures by the SFT policy, is used for RL training to focus computational resources on challenging scenarios. An implicit visual curriculum is induced by packing tasks by scene, ensuring that batches contain multiple episodes sharing the same visual context. This RL phase significantly improved the success rate.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

Robostral Navigate was evaluated on the Room-to-Room in Continuous Environments (R2R-CE) and Room-Across-Room in Continuous Environments (RxR-CE) benchmarks, which require agents to follow unseen natural language instructions in novel photorealistic environments. Performance was assessed using standard navigation metrics: Navigation Error (NE↓), Oracle Success (OS↑), Success Rate (SR↑), and Success weighted by Path Length (SPL↑).

#### 4.1. R2R-CE Benchmark (Validation Unseen Split)

- Robostral Navigate achieved a Success Rate (SR) of 77.4% and an SPL of 74.2%.
- This performance established a new state of the art, surpassing the best prior single-camera method (Qwen-RobotNav-4B, 66.9% SR) by 10.5 percentage points.
- It also outperformed the best system utilizing depth or multiple cameras (Qwen-RobotNav-8B, 72.1% SR) by 5.3 percentage points, despite relying solely on a single RGB camera.
- The integration of online reinforcement learning (RL) contributed a significant improvement, increasing the success rate on R2R-CE validation unseen by 4.03% (from a Supervised Fine-Tuning baseline of 73.40% to 77.43%).

#### 4.2. RxR-CE Benchmark (Validation Unseen, English-only Split)

- Robostral Navigate achieved an SR of 75.1% and an SPL of 68.7%, with a Navigation Error (NE) of 3.47 meters.
- It surpassed the prior state-of-the-art single-camera method (Qwen-RobotNav-8B, 73.4% SR, 63.5% SPL) by 1.7 percentage points in success rate and 5.2 percentage points in path efficiency (SPL).
- Notably, the single-camera RGB-only model demonstrated superior path efficiency (68.7% SPL vs. 65.7% SPL) and lower navigation error (3.47m vs. 3.58m) compared to the best depth- and multi-camera-assisted baseline (Qwen-RobotNav-8B with depth), while maintaining competitive success rates (75.1% vs. 76.5%).
- The online RL phase yielded a substantial 3.9% improvement in SR on RxR-CE, from an SFT baseline of 71.2% to 75.1%.

These results indicate that Robostral Navigate, despite its minimal sensing requirements, achieved higher performance across critical metrics than many methods utilizing additional sensory inputs or more complex hardware configurations. The high SPL values on both benchmarks also suggest that successes were achieved through efficient, optimal paths.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of Robostral Navigate presents several significant contributions and potential impacts on the field of embodied AI and robotics:

1. **Advancement in Minimal-Sensor Navigation:** By achieving state-of-the-art performance on challenging benchmarks using only a monocular RGB camera, Robostral Navigate demonstrates that high-level navigation capabilities do not inherently require complex, multi-modal sensor setups. This challenges the prevailing reliance on depth sensors, LiDAR, or multi-camera rigs in many advanced navigation systems.

2. **Enhanced Scalability and Generalization:** The system's design, which includes image-space waypoint prediction and training data randomization, significantly enhances its cross-robot generalization capabilities. The ability to deploy the same model weights on diverse platforms without recalibration reduces deployment friction and cost, making advanced navigation more accessible for various robotic embodiments, from wheeled to legged and aerial systems. This directly addresses a critical scalability challenge in robotics.

3. **Efficient Training Paradigms:** The introduction of the prefix-caching training recipe, combined with the tree-based attention mask, represents a notable advancement in VLM training efficiency. By reducing training tokens by 22x and converting training times from months to days, this methodology accelerates research and development cycles, making it feasible to train large-scale models with extensive simulation data.

4. **Effective Integration of Reinforcement Learning:** The successful application of online reinforcement learning (CISPO) on top of a supervised fine-tuned VLM, specifically tailored to hard subsets of tasks, demonstrates an effective strategy for improving robustness, exploration, and recovery capabilities. This hybrid training approach addresses the limitations of behavioral cloning (exposure bias) and enables the agent to learn from interaction in out-of-distribution states.

5. **Foundation for General-Purpose Robotics:** The work frames Robostral Navigate as a foundational step towards building general-purpose embodied agents. By demonstrating that large-scale simulation, efficient training, and reinforcement learning can produce a high-performing navigation model with minimal sensing requirements, it lays groundwork for developing more capable and versatile robots that can operate autonomously in complex, real-world environments with reduced hardware and operational overhead. The potential impact extends to various applications where cost-effective, adaptable navigation is crucial, such as logistics, exploration, and service robotics.
