# ForesightNav: Learning Scene Imagination for Efficient Exploration

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from ETH Zurich and the University of Zurich developed ForesightNav, an exploration strategy that enables autonomous robots to predict complete geometric and semantic scene representations from partial observations using a learned imagination module. This method improves navigation efficiency for both PointNav and ObjectNav tasks, achieving a 100% completion rate and a 1.5x speedup in PointNav, and an SPL of 0.67 and a Success rate of 0.73 in ObjectNav on the Structured3D dataset.

## Problem

- Existing end-to-end reinforcement learning approaches for robotic navigation struggle with generalization to novel environments, requiring extensive retraining for each new scenario.
- Zero-shot ObjectNav methods often face computational bottlenecks due to text generation or require significant pre-exploration and mapping, limiting real-time applicability.
- Robotic navigation systems commonly operate reactively, making decisions based only on immediate observations without leveraging anticipatory reasoning about unexplored areas.

## Method

- **ForesightNav** integrates a ==GeoSem Map== to store jointly captured geometric (occupancy) and semantic (CLIP embeddings) information, serving as the agent's memory.
- An Imagination Module, a learnable function, predicts the complete GeoSem Map of an entire scene from partially observed GeoSem Maps, including occupancy probability and high-level semantic attributes.
- The predicted complete GeoSem Map is used to extract long-term navigation goals, guiding the agent to efficiently locate target objects by planning paths using the imagined geometry and semantics.

## Results

- In the PointNav task, the imagination agent achieved a **100%** completion rate and an approximate **1.5x** speedup, reducing mean timesteps to **439.38** compared to **640.65** for a vanilla agent.
- For ObjectNav, ForesightNav demonstrated higher overall performance with an SPL of **0.67** and a Success rate of **0.73**, outperforming several frontier-based baselines on the Structured3D validation dataset.
- The UNet architecture, supervised with Binary Cross-Entropy (BCE) loss, was identified as the most effective imagination network configuration.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Equipping robots with a learned imagination capability allows them to proactively infer unseen geometric and semantic scene elements, enhancing understanding and interpretation of complex, unknown environments.
> - A novel training methodology generates realistic partial observations for the imagination network by simulating an agent navigating a 2D grid world, ensuring data reflects real-world exploration patterns.
> - Utilizing predicted complete scene representations enables the selection of more strategic and long-term navigation goals, moving beyond short-sighted, purely local or reactive exploration strategies.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Autonomous robotic navigation in novel and unknown environments is a central challenge in robotics research, drawing inspiration from human cognitive abilities. Humans effectively leverage prior knowledge and experience to infer spatial layouts, anticipate object locations, and reason about unexplored areas, even with limited visual information. This capability, often referred to as imagination or anticipatory reasoning, allows for goal-directed and efficient exploration.

The existing landscape of robotic navigation approaches has made progress by integrating scene information, frequently through end-to-end reinforcement learning (RL) pipelines or the use of Vision-Language Models (VLMs). However, these methods often encounter limitations. End-to-end RL approaches typically struggle with generalization to novel environments, necessitating extensive retraining for each new scenario. While advanced VLMs offer zero-shot solutions that bypass task-specific training, they can exhibit limitations in navigation precision due to their inherent challenges in understanding detailed spatial information.

Specifically for Object Goal Navigation (ObjectNav), where an agent must locate a specified object in a new environment, common strategies include reinforcement learning, learning from demonstrations, or constructing semantic top-down maps. These are often restricted to predefined object categories or simulated data, which limits their real-world applicability. Zero-shot ObjectNav methods, such as those employing frontier-based exploration with CLIP features or open-vocabulary object detectors (e.g., CLIP on Wheels, LGX, CM², ESC, StructNav, VLFM), attempt to overcome these category limitations. However, some of these involve computational bottlenecks due to text generation from visual features or require significant pre-exploration and mapping, hindering real-time use.

Regarding scene representation, various methods exist, including topological maps from image segments or multi-modal scene representations combining CLIP and DINO embeddings. Many of these require initial exploration and mapping of the environment, which is not suitable for immediate navigation in unseen spaces. Previous work has explored predicting explicit map representations from partial observations. Some approaches use generative models to extend maps or predict top-down semantic maps with random masking, but these often lack complete floor plan generation or do not accurately simulate real-world partial observations. Other methods infer unseen walls as 2D line segments from LIDAR data, yet their evaluation metrics may not directly align with practical navigation tasks.

ForesightNav addresses these gaps by proposing an exploration strategy that explicitly incorporates a learned imagination capability, directly predicting complete geometric and semantic scene representations from partial observations. This approach is distinct from previous methods by integrating human-inspired anticipatory reasoning into the robotic exploration loop, aiming for enhanced generalization and efficiency in unknown environments without extensive pre-mapping or retraining. The paper also contributes a specific training methodology for this imagination capability and a new closed-loop evaluation benchmark for ObjectNav, aiming to provide a more rigorous assessment of method generalization.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of this work is to develop autonomous robotic agents capable of navigating unseen environments efficiently by leveraging a learned imagination capability, mirroring human cognitive processes. The researchers are motivated by the observation that humans effectively infer spatial layouts and anticipate object presence based on prior knowledge, even from partial views. Extending this "imagination" to robots is hypothesized to improve their understanding and interpretation of complex environments, directly translating to more efficient and generalizable autonomous navigation.

The specific objectives and motivations for ForesightNav are detailed as follows:

- **To enable prediction of contextual information for unexplored regions:** Current navigation systems often operate reactively, making decisions based solely on immediate observations. The work aims to equip robots with the ability to predict unseen occupancy and semantic details beyond their current field of view. This predictive capacity is motivated by the need for proactive decision-making and long-term planning, moving beyond reactive strategies.
- **To facilitate efficient selection of meaningful long-term navigation goals:** By predicting what lies in unexplored areas, the robot can identify more strategic and long-term navigation goals, rather than relying on short-sighted frontier exploration. This is motivated by the inefficiency of purely local exploration in complex environments, which can lead to redundant paths or missed opportunities for finding target objects.
- **To enhance exploration efficiency and generalization in unseen environments:** A primary motivation is to overcome the generalization challenges faced by many existing end-to-end learning-based navigation methods, which often require extensive retraining for new environments or tasks. By explicitly learning a generalized imagination model, the goal is to enable robust performance in environments not encountered during training, leading to faster completion rates and more successful object discovery.
- **To propose a novel method for training imagination networks:** Accurately predicting unseen regions from limited observations is complex. The paper is motivated to develop a suitable training strategy that generates realistic partial observations. Unlike prior works that use random masking, this approach simulates an agent navigating a 2D grid world to create partially observed occupancy masks, ensuring that the training data reflects real-world observation patterns during navigation.
- **To introduce a new closed-loop evaluation benchmark for Object Goal Navigation:** Existing benchmarks for ObjectNav sometimes lack the comprehensiveness needed to evaluate the generalization capabilities of new methods, particularly regarding complex indoor scenes. The motivation here is to provide a more robust and comprehensive evaluation framework using the Structured3D dataset, enabling a better assessment of how methods generalize across diverse indoor scenes.

In summary, the motivation stems from the desire to imbue autonomous systems with a form of anticipatory reasoning, akin to human imagination, to address fundamental limitations in current robotic exploration and navigation, thereby moving towards more intelligent and adaptable robotic agents for real-world applications such as home service, search and rescue, delivery, and industrial inspections.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, ForesightNav, is designed to enable efficient exploration in unseen environments by integrating an imagination capability into an autonomous navigation system. The approach addresses the Object Goal Navigation (ObjectNav) task.

**Problem Formulation**
An agent starts at a random location in a novel indoor environment and is given a target object category (e.g., 'bed'). At each timestep, it receives first-person RGB-D images and pose readings. The agent's action space includes `MOVE FORWARD` (0.25m), `TURN LEFT` (30°), `TURN RIGHT` (30°), `LOOK UP` (30°), `LOOK DOWN` (30°), and `STOP`. The objective is to navigate to within 1 meter of the target object and execute the `STOP` action within a maximum of 500 steps.

**System Overview**
ForesightNav comprises two main building blocks:
1. **GeoSem Map:** A scene representation serving as the agent's memory, storing jointly captured geometric and semantic information from past observations. This work uses occupancy for geometry and CLIP embeddings for semantics.
2. **Imagination Module:** An exploration strategy responsible for deducing long-term navigation goals by predicting the complete GeoSem Map of the entire scene from the currently observed, partial one.

As the agent moves, it updates its GeoSem Map. The Imagination Module then processes this partial map to predict a complete scene representation. This predicted map is subsequently used to select a long-term navigation goal to efficiently locate the target object category. The agent plans its path using the predicted geometry. Once the target object is within the agent's field of view, it navigates directly to the object without further exploration.

#### 3.1. GeoSem Map Construction

A GeoSem Map is a Bird's-Eye-View (BEV) projection of 3D language-aligned voxel features, functioning as a structured grid-based representation $M \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times W \times (D+1)}$. Here, $H \times W$ defines the spatial resolution, and $D$ is the dimensionality of the CLIP embedding space.
- **Semantic Features ($f_{sem}$):** The first $D$ channels at each grid cell store $D$-dimensional CLIP feature vectors. These are initialized to zeros and updated as the episode progresses.
 - At each timestep $t$, a dense per-pixel semantic embedding is computed from the RGB image $I_{RGB}^{(t)}$ using the LSeg encoder.
 - The corresponding depth image $I_{Depth}^{(t)}$ is used to project each pixel to a local 3D point cloud.
 - Each 3D point inherits the CLIP embedding of its pixel. These points are transformed into the world frame using the camera pose $T_{wc}^{(t)}$ and discretized into the grid map $M$. If multiple points fall into the same grid cell, their embeddings are averaged.
- **Occupancy Features ($f_{occ}$):** The last channel stores occupancy features, defined as $f_{occ}(x) \in \{0, 0.5, 1\}$, representing free, unknown, and occupied cells, respectively. It is initialized to 0.5 (unknown) for all cells.
 - Occupancy is computed using a density map derived from the 3D points. A height-based filter ($z_{min}$, $z_{max}$) is applied to consider only points relevant to the agent's navigability. A cell is classified as occupied if its density exceeds 10% of the maximum density.
- **Zero-Shot Object Segmentation:** For object localization, given $K$ language queries, each query is encoded using the CLIP text encoder. The dot product of these query vectors with every cell of the GeoSem Map yields $K$ similarity scores. The grid cell is assigned to the category with the highest similarity score.

#### 3.2. The Imagination Module

The Imagination Module, denoted as $f_{imagine}$, is a learnable function that predicts the ground-truth GeoSem Map of a scene from a partially observed GeoSem Map. It predicts both complete occupancy and semantics.
- **Input:** $M \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times W \times (D+1)}$, a partially observed GeoSem Map where unobserved semantic channels are zero and unobserved occupancy channels are 0.5.
- **Output:** $J \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times W \times (D+2)}$, a 3D grid representation containing predicted high-level semantic attributes ($p_{semantic}$), occupancy probability ($p_{occ} \in$), and interior mask probability ($p_{interior} \in$). The interior mask is predicted to distinguish between true unobserved regions within the scene and external regions.

**Generating Training Data: Partially Observed Occupancy Masks**
Training pairs $(M, G)$ are generated, where $M$ is a partial GeoSem Map and $G$ is the ground truth. To create realistic partial observations for training $f_{imagine}$, an agent is simulated in a 2D grid world of each scene's ground truth occupancy map $C$.
- The agent is initialized randomly and tasked with reaching randomly generated waypoints using the A* algorithm.
- At each timestep, the agent simulates a depth sensor (LIDAR) within its field of view (FOV) and sensor range, updating its 'predicted map' $P_t$, which is initialized with all cells as unobserved (0.5). The observation mask $O_t$ determines the regions observed at $t$.

**Network Architecture and Training Setup**
- On-the-fly data augmentations (rotation, scaling) are applied.
- Two architectures are explored: a Vision Transformer (ViT)-based architecture using Masked Autoencoders (MAE) and a Convolutional Neural Network (CNN)-based architecture using a U-Net.
- The imagination network is supervised using a multi-task loss with three components:
 - $L_{CLIP}$: Cosine similarity loss for CLIP embeddings, applied only to interior regions.
 - $L_{occ}$: Weighted binary cross-entropy (BCE) loss for occupancy prediction to account for sparsity.
 - $L_{interior}$: Standard BCE loss for the interior mask prediction.
- The final loss is a weighted sum: $L = \lambda_{CLIP} \cdot L_{CLIP} + \lambda_{occ} \cdot L_{occ} + \lambda_{interior} \cdot L_{interior}$.

#### 3.3. Long-Term Navigation Goal Extraction

The predicted GeoSem Map $J$ from $f_{imagine}$ is used to select the navigation goal.
- A cosine similarity heatmap is computed between the predicted semantic features in $J$ (filtered by the predicted interior mask) and the CLIP encoding of the goal query.
- To refine the goal location, the heatmap is normalized and thresholded to create a binary map of potential goal locations. Outlier removal is performed using DBSCAN, and an optimal number of clusters ($K$) is found using the silhouette score.
- A Gaussian Mixture Model (GMM) with $K$ Gaussians is fitted to identify candidate zones for the goal query. If $K > 1$, the closest cluster to the robot's current position is chosen as the navigation goal.
- Imagination and goal selection are triggered when a new GeoSem Map is predicted, which occurs when the agent's current field of view has low overlap with the last updated field of view, based on a predefined threshold.

**GeoSem Maps from Equirectangular RGBD (Supplementary Details)**
For the Structured3D dataset, which provides panoramic RGB-D images, a specific process is used to generate ground truth GeoSem Maps:
- **Perspective Projection:** Equirectangular RGB images are converted into 12 perspective images (90° FOV, strategically chosen horizontal and vertical viewing directions) to mitigate geometric distortions when processed by the LSeg encoder.
- **2D-3D Correspondence:** An indexing scheme links pixels in the original equirectangular depth maps to unique global 3D points. This index is also projected to perspective images, allowing each pixel in the perspective image to retain the identity of its original 3D point.
- **CLIP Embedding Generation and Mapping:** LSeg processes the perspective images to derive pixel-wise CLIP embeddings. These embeddings are then aggregated into the grid representation of the GeoSem Map, maintaining correspondence via the indexing.
- **Handling Spurious Observations:** To ensure realism, the ground truth occupancy maps are generated from a top-down projection of the global point cloud. An interior-exterior mask, derived from Structured3D annotations, is applied to the occupancy map to mark external points as unobserved (0.5), preventing "leaks" through walls in simulations.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of ForesightNav was conducted using the Structured3D dataset, a large-scale photo-realistic dataset comprising 3500 indoor scenes. The Imagination module was trained on the dataset's train-validation split (3000 scenes for training, 500 for validation). All performance metrics were reported on the Structured3D validation split, which was not used during training.

**Network Details:**
The GeoSem Map had spatial dimensions $H=224, W=224$. A height-based filter set $z_{min}=30\%$ and $z_{max}=70\%$ of the scene height, simulating a drone agent's occupancy map. The ViT-B/32 CLIP model variant provided $D=512$-dimensional embeddings, resulting in a GeoSem Map size of $224 \times 224 \times 513$. For the UNet architecture, convolution depths were set to. The threshold for converting predicted occupancy probability to a binary map was 0.72. Loss coefficients were $\lambda_{CLIP}=10$, $\lambda_{occ}=1$, and $\lambda_{interior}=1$.

**Evaluation Setup:**
The evaluation was performed in a closed-loop simulation for the ObjectNav task, as defined in Section 3, with an episode timeout of 500 seconds. Agents were initialized randomly in a 2D grid world, and observations were simulated using precomputed ground truth GeoSem Maps. Evaluation metrics included Success, Success weighted by Path Length (SPL), and Distance to Goal, as proposed by Anderson et al. (2018).

#### 4.1. Evaluation on Occupancy Prediction (PointNav)

Prior to ObjectNav, the Imagination strategy was assessed on the Point Goal Navigation (PointNav) task, where the agent aims to reach a specific point-goal (x, y, z). Two agents, both using the A* algorithm for planning, were compared:
- **Imagination Agent:** Reimagines the occupancy map using the imagination layer every 10 timesteps and plans its path using this imagined map.
- **Vanilla Agent:** Replans using only the currently observed occupancy map every 10 timesteps.

**Table 1: Closed-loop simulation evaluation results for PointNav.**
| Method | Completion Rate (↑) | Mean Timesteps (↓) |
|:-------------- |:------------------ |:----------------- |
| Vanilla | 0.99 | 640.65 |
| UNet + BCE | **1.0** | **439.38** |
| UNet + MSE | **1.0** | 448.23 |
| ViT + BCE | 0.85 | 465.72 |
| ViT + MSE | 0.89 | 499.78 |

The imagination agents, particularly the UNet architecture supervised with BCE Loss, demonstrated a completion rate of 1.0 (100%) and a reduction in mean timesteps to 439.38, compared to the Vanilla agent's 0.99 completion rate and 640.65 mean timesteps. This indicates an approximate 1.5x speedup in reaching goals for the best imagination agent. The results suggest that predicting unobserved walls effectively assists the agent in navigating more quickly and completing tasks. A visual representation in Figure 7 further supported that the imagination agent could predict scene structure and choose a more efficient path.

#### 4.2. Evaluation on ObjectNav

ForesightNav's imagination exploration strategy was evaluated against several frontier-based baseline methods, which are modular-learning approaches. The target object categories for ObjectNav included: ["chair", "couch", "potted plant", "bed", "toilet", "tv"].

**Baselines:**
- **VLFM-CLIP:** VLFM agent using CLIP for language grounding.
- **StructNav-Frontiers:** Inspired by StructNav, using a top-down semantic map with fixed object categories and a prior distance matrix to score frontiers.
- **Random Agent:** Selects a random frontier for exploration.
- **Greedy Agent:** Chooses the closest frontier to its current position.

**Table 2: Average metrics for different exploration strategies in the ObjectNav task.**
| Method | SPL (↑) | Distance To Goal (↓) | Success (↑) |
|:------------------------ |:------ |:------------------- |:---------- |
| ForesightNav (Ours) | **0.67**| 25.32 | **0.73** |
| VLFM-CLIP | 0.66 | 27.36 | 0.71 |
| StructNav-Frontiers | 0.63 | 33.38 | 0.66 |
| Random Agent | 0.62 | 29.30 | 0.68 |
| Greedy Agent | 0.58 | 37.06 | 0.60 |

ForesightNav demonstrated higher performance across all three metrics in the ObjectNav task. It achieved an SPL of 0.67 and a Success rate of 0.73, outperforming VLFM-CLIP (SPL 0.66, Success 0.71) and StructNav-Frontiers (SPL 0.63, Success 0.66). The Random and Greedy agents showed lower performance, as anticipated. These results suggest that ForesightNav's ability to anticipate unseen regions and use imagination-aided GeoSem Maps contributes to improved navigation efficiency and success rates in finding target objects.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

ForesightNav introduces an exploration strategy for autonomous robots centered on a learned imagination capability, drawing inspiration from human cognitive processes. The core contribution is equipping robotic agents with the capacity to predict unobserved contextual information, including occupancy and semantic details, within novel environments. This predictive ability informs the selection of long-term navigation goals.

The reported results indicate improvements in navigation efficiency for both PointNav and ObjectNav tasks. For PointNav, the imagination-driven agent achieved a 100% completion rate and an approximate 1.5x speedup compared to a baseline agent relying only on current observations. In the ObjectNav task, ForesightNav demonstrated a higher Success weighted by Path Length (SPL) of 0.67 and a Success rate of 0.73 on the Structured3D validation dataset, outperforming comparative modular-learning approaches. These findings suggest that incorporating anticipatory reasoning through an imagination module can enhance an agent's ability to generalize and navigate efficiently in previously unseen environments.

The methodology's integration of CLIP's visual-semantic understanding with an anticipatory reasoning framework offers a path toward more informed navigation decisions. By enabling agents to infer promising frontiers and prioritize areas likely to contain target objects, ForesightNav addresses challenges related to exhaustive search in complex environments.

The work proposes a step toward developing foundational models for indoor scene understanding, analogous to the impact of large vision-language models on perception tasks. This has potential implications for real-world applications requiring autonomous navigation in dynamic or unknown indoor settings, such as home service robots, search and rescue operations, automated delivery systems, and industrial inspections.

**Limitations and Future Work:**
The current approach, while effective for indoor environments with Bird's-Eye-View (BEV) style GeoSem Maps, may face scalability challenges for larger scenes or higher-resolution maps due to increased memory usage and network size requirements for the imagination model. Future research directions suggested by the authors include exploring hierarchical memory structures, potentially combining GeoSem Maps with representations like 3D Scene Graphs, to improve scalability and retrieval efficiency for navigating extensive environments or responding to non-object-centric language queries.
