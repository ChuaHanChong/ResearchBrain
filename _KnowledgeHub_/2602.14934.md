# Activation-Space Uncertainty Quantification for Pretrained Networks

> [!summary] Summary
> GAPA (Gaussian Process Activations) is a post-hoc uncertainty method for frozen networks. It swaps selected nonlinearities for Gaussian-process activations whose posterior mean equals the original activation, preserving point predictions exactly while yielding closed-form epistemic variance. k-means inducing points, local kNN conditioning and delta-method propagation give single-pass uncertainty that matches or beats Laplace-family baselines on calibration and OOD at far lower test cost.

## Problem

- Weight-space Bayesian methods such as variational BNNs, MC dropout and ensembles need retraining, labels or multiple stochastic forward passes, impractical for frozen pretrained backbones.
- Laplace approximations rely on curvature estimates that scale poorly as models and output spaces grow, becoming prohibitive for large-vocabulary language models and dense segmentation.
- Existing post-hoc methods alter the backbone's point predictions, while cheap temperature scaling captures no epistemic uncertainty, so no baseline is simultaneously single-pass, mean-preserving and epistemic.

## Method

- ==GP activations== replace an elementwise nonlinearity with per-neuron ==Gaussian processes== whose prior mean is that nonlinearity, conditioned on noiseless pseudo-observations, so the posterior mean provably equals the original activation.
- Two-stage sparse inference: offline ==k-means== or ==farthest-point compression== of cached pre-activations into inducing points, then test-time ==FAISS== conditioning on K=50 neighbours, giving O(log M) plus O(K^3) queries.
- Deterministic variance propagation: diagonal rules for linear layers, ==delta method== for nonlinearities, an ==NIGP correction== for stacked **GAPA** layers, plus ==attention==, ==RMSNorm== and ==softmax== rules for transformers.

## Results

- Best NLL on all three regression benchmarks (Airline **4.946**, Year **3.470**, Taxi **3.112**) and best CQM calibration overall, including **0.014** on Year against VaLLA200's **0.041**.
- CIFAR-10 ResNet-56 reaches **0.953** OOD AUROC at **3.30s** test time versus VaLLA's **0.960** at **363.8s**; FMNIST to MNIST BALD AUROC hits **0.993** with accuracy unchanged.
- On LLaMA-3.2-3B, activation-space epistemic uncertainty exceeds the oracle logit-temperature AUROC bound once inducing points exceed roughly **1000**, while last-layer Laplace stays at chance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Using the original nonlinearity as GP prior mean makes uncertainty strictly additive: accuracy and decisions are untouched by construction, though calibration metrics can still degrade.
> - Conditioning on a subset of inducing points provably inflates, never shrinks, posterior variance, so local kNN approximation stays conservative rather than silently overconfident.
> - Layer placement trades off: final layers maximise OOD detection, intermediate layers preserve calibration, and stacking more uncertain layers degrades NLL through compounding variance.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Overview

A frozen classifier may keep making confident predictions when an input is far from what it saw in training. Common uncertainty methods can demand more computation or alter the model's predictions. The practical problem is how to know when a trusted, already-trained model faces unfamiliar inputs without retraining it, running many model evaluations, or accepting changed predictions.

GAPA (Gaussian Process Activations) adds an uncertainty signal around the existing prediction rather than replacing that prediction. The paper's toy binary-classification illustration shows the idea: GAPA retains the classifier's decision boundary while uncertainty rises away from training examples.

Toy binary-classification task. Panels, left to right: MAP (deterministic backbone), MC Dropout, Last-Layer Laplace, and GAPA. Darker shading indicates more confident predictions, and the black line marks the decision boundary. GAPA preserves the backbone's boundary while adding epistemic uncertainty that grows smoothly away from training data.

### 2. Uncertainty without moving the prediction

Most uncertainty methods place uncertainty on network weights. GAPA instead replaces selected nonlinear activations with Gaussian-process (GP) activations.

The first step happens offline. Reference training inputs run through the frozen network, and GAPA caches the pre-activations at selected layers. A pre-activation is the value entering a nonlinearity.

At a selected layer, the nonlinearity becomes a GP whose prior mean is the original function, such as tanh or ReLU. The GP's targets are the original activation values at the cached inputs. Because the prior mean already agrees with those targets, the GP posterior mean remains the original activation.

The added information is the posterior variance. It signals how poorly the test activation is supported by cached training activations. The paper uses diagonal output covariance, treating activation dimensions independently for tractability.

To scale up, GAPA compresses the cached activations into a smaller inducing set using k-means. At test time, the GP conditions on the $K$ nearest inducing activations for the query, with $K = 50$ in the paper's complexity discussion.

The resulting diagonal variances propagate forward through the remaining frozen network using deterministic rules:

- Linear layers propagate variance using squared weights.
- Nonlinearities use a first-order, local linearization.
- When another GAPA layer receives an uncertain input, a first-order noisy-input correction accounts for that input uncertainty.

These rules yield approximate moments, not an exact full-covariance distribution.

Top: a network carrying its mean activations, contrasted with GAPA's mean-plus-variance propagation. Orange arrows show the mean signal and blue arrows the variance signal. Bottom: a deterministic tanh with orange cached training activations, and GAPA-tanh, whose posterior mean matches tanh; the shaded band is ±2 standard deviations.

### 3. A measured trade-off, not a universal win

The closest conceptual prior is VaLLA, Ortega et al.'s variational linearized Laplace method. It approximates uncertainty for a pretrained network with a sparse GP and retains the network output as the predictive mean, as described in its [paper](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2302.12565). VaLLA models uncertainty through a linearized, weight-space/Laplace GP construction and involves optimizing variational parameters. GAPA places uncertainty at hidden activations and uses cached activation statistics and local inducing-point conditioning.

The experiment uses pretrained CIFAR-10 ResNet backbones, with SVHN as the out-of-distribution (OOD) data. OOD AUROC measures how well an uncertainty score separates ID from OOD examples, and higher is better.

On ResNet-56, GAPA scores 0.953 AUROC at 3.30 seconds test time, while VaLLA scores 0.960 at 363.8 seconds. These are reported test times for 1,000 examples. GAPA therefore has slightly lower OOD AUROC in this comparison, while using much less reported test time. Its CIFAR-10 accuracy of 94.4% matches the MAP backbone's reported accuracy, which is consistent with the mean-preserving design.

Test time (seconds, log scale) versus OOD AUROC for ResNet-20, ResNet-32, ResNet-44, and ResNet-56. Dashed lines mark Pareto frontiers, where better means higher AUROC and lower test time. GAPA is on or near the frontier across the four panels; a frontier position does not mean GAPA has the highest AUROC.

This is a test-time comparison, not an end-to-end accounting of offline setup. GAPA requires an offline forward pass to cache training activations, an inducing set, and indexing. Its classification test-time procedure is deterministic and single-pass through the network. Its approximate variance propagation and diagonal covariance are design choices, not guarantees of capturing all uncertainty dependencies.

### 4. Keep the mean, add a signal

GAPA's central design adds a warning signal without replacing the frozen network's mean activation. In the reported CIFAR setup, it retains a strong OOD score at low reported test-time cost, although VaLLA scores slightly higher on AUROC. The method's primary limitation is the memory cost of storing inducing activations.
