# AffordGen: Generating Diverse Demonstrations for Generalizable Object Manipulation with Afford Correspondence

> [!summary] Summary
> AffordGen introduces a framework to generate diverse robot manipulation demonstrations by leveraging semantic affordance correspondences across 3D object meshes. This method enables training robust visuomotor policies that achieve zero-shot generalization to novel objects and categories, outperforming baseline methods by an average of 24.1% in unseen object tests in simulation and demonstrating meaningful success rates in real-world cross-category tasks where others failed.

## Problem

- High cost and labor intensity associated with collecting large-scale, high-quality human demonstrations for robot manipulation.
- Limited generalization capabilities of learned policies to novel objects, varying shapes, and unseen environments not encountered during training.
- Existing synthetic data generation methods often expand spatial relationships for a single object instance, limiting semantic diversity and cross-category generalization.

## Method

- **AffordGen** repurposes ==semantic affordance correspondence== as a ==generative prior== to synthesize diverse, full 6D manipulation trajectories from minimal source demonstrations.
- The method establishes ==3D semantic keypoint correspondence== between source and target meshes using ==canonicalization and DINOv2==, then generates diverse demonstrations through ==keypoint-constrained trajectory replay== and motion planning.
- It creates a large-scale, ==affordance-aware dataset== that covers a broad spectrum of object instances and 6D poses, enabling the training of robust, closed-loop visuomotor policies.

## Results

- **AffordGen** policies achieved an average performance boost of **24.1%** over the best baseline (CPGen) on unseen object tests in simulation, consistently outperforming other methods.
- In zero-shot cross-category generalization tasks, **AffordGen** was the only method to achieve meaningful non-zero success rates (e.g., **55.00%** for simulated mug pouring) where baselines showed negligible success.
- Real-world experiments validated **AffordGen**'s superior performance on unseen real objects and successful cross-category generalization, such as achieving **14/27** successes for real-world mug pouring compared to **3/27** for CPGen.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Semantic affordance information can be effectively leveraged as a generative prior for synthesizing diverse training data, rather than solely for online planning, to enhance visuomotor policy learning.
> - Integrating advanced vision foundation models like DINOv2 for 3D semantic keypoint matching significantly improves the ability to transfer manipulation skills across geometrically diverse object shapes and categories.
> - Training policies on large-scale, affordance-aware datasets generated from minimal expert demonstrations facilitates robust zero-shot generalization to entirely unseen objects, including those from different categories.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation has seen advancements through visuomotor imitation learning; however, its practical deployment is constrained by the cost of collecting extensive human demonstrations and the limited generalization of learned policies to novel objects and scenarios. This work positions itself within the context of addressing these challenges, particularly the issue of data diversity and generalization.

Previous research efforts to mitigate data scarcity and improve generalization can be broadly categorized. One paradigm involves **synthetic data generation through trajectory adaptation**, exemplified by methods like MimicGen, DemoGen, and CPGen. These approaches augment existing demonstrations by replaying skills in new spatial configurations or by deforming the source object. However, these methods primarily focus on expanding spatial relationships for a single object instance, consequently inheriting the restricted semantic scope of the source demonstration. This limitation results in poor generalization beyond the original object, particularly concerning diverse object shapes or orientations.

A second paradigm utilizes **large-scale generative models** (e.g., GenSim, RoboGen) to create data from scratch, often employing Large Language Models (LLMs) to define tasks and scenes. While effective at generating task-level diversity, these methods are limited by the capabilities and quality of their underlying automated solvers. Other works, such as those leveraging diffusion models, primarily augment the *visual appearance* of existing data to enhance visual robustness, rather than addressing generalization to new spatial configurations or object types.

Another relevant area involves **affordance-based methods**, such as Robo-ABC, DenseMatcher, and FUNCTO. These leverage semantic correspondence to transfer affordance knowledge to unseen objects, facilitating one-shot imitation learning and cross-category generalization. However, these approaches are often planning-centric, relying on the precision of mapped affordance points and the planning algorithm. The execution typically follows pre-computed, open-loop trajectories, lacking the reactive capabilities inherent in learning-based closed-loop policies. This implies a gap in effectively integrating semantic affordance knowledge into robust, reactive learning pipelines.

AffordGen aims to bridge these gaps by **repurposing affordance information as a generative prior for policy learning**. It distinguishes itself by not using affordances for online planning but as a guide for synthesizing diverse and semantically grounded demonstrations. By generating a large, affordance-aware dataset spanning novel object instances and full 6D spatial relations, AffordGen seeks to train closed-loop visuomotor policies that combine the semantic generalizability of affordances with the reactive robustness characteristic of end-to-end learning. This approach extends beyond the single-object instance limitations of prior data augmentation techniques and seeks to overcome the reactivity limitations of traditional affordance-based planning methods.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The motivation for AffordGen stems from two fundamental challenges in visuomotor imitation learning for robot manipulation:
1. **High cost of data collection:** Obtaining large-scale, high-quality human demonstrations is a resource-intensive process, which restricts the applicability of imitation learning in diverse real-world scenarios.
2. **Limited generalization:** Policies learned through imitation often exhibit poor generalization to novel objects, variations in shape, and unseen environments not encountered during training. This issue is particularly pronounced when dealing with geometric variations and limited data diversity.

Existing approaches have attempted to address these challenges with partial success. Synthetic data generation methods, while capable of expanding data volume, typically augment spatial relationships for a single object instance, which limits their semantic scope and their ability to generalize to novel object shapes or categories. Affordance-based methods offer a mechanism for semantic transfer across objects, but they often rely on open-loop planning, which compromises the reactive robustness essential for real-world robotic tasks. There is a recognized need for a systematic methodology to integrate this valuable semantic knowledge into learning-based, closed-loop policy training.

Given these limitations, the key objectives of the AffordGen framework are:
- **To leverage semantic correspondence of meaningful keypoints across a large collection of 3D meshes** to generate diverse and semantically grounded robot manipulation trajectories. This aims to overcome the data diversity constraints imposed by geometric variations.
- **To create a large-scale, affordance-aware dataset** from a minimal number of source demonstrations. This dataset is designed to cover a broad spectrum of object instances and full 6D spatial relations, including objects from different categories.
- **To train a robust, closed-loop visuomotor policy** that effectively integrates two key aspects: the semantic generalizability derived from affordance understanding and the reactive robustness characteristic of end-to-end learning.
- **To enable zero-shot generalization** of the learned policies to truly unseen objects, including those from different categories, thus significantly improving data efficiency in robot learning.
- **To establish a novel paradigm** where affordance knowledge is utilized not for online planning, but primarily as a powerful guide for generating high-quality training data, thereby maximizing its impact on policy learning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The AffordGen framework is structured around three main stages: Source Demonstration Pre-processing, Semantic Correspondence on 3D Meshes, and Diverse Demonstration Generation.

**Problem Formulation:**
The framework models a manipulation task as a sequence of three distinct stages: Grasp (Ω_G), Skill (Ω_S), and Transition (Ω_T). During these stages, the robot perceives its environment through visual (point clouds) and proprioceptive observations, subsequently executing an action. Point clouds are specifically chosen as the visual input modality due to their inherent 3D structure, which facilitates data manipulation and generation. A core assumption underlying the generation strategy is that semantic information embedded in manipulation trajectories remains consistent, even when the shapes and sizes of manipulated objects vary.

**Source Demonstration Pre-processing:**
From an expert demonstration, three types of information are extracted:
1. **Grasping time (t_grasp):** The specific moment the gripper closes, determined from the end-effector state.
2. **Skill segment (τ_s):** The sequence of actions critical for task completion, identifiable through video reasoning capabilities of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) or human annotation.
3. **Keypoints:** These include the "affording point," representing the contact point between the gripper and the object, and the "function point," denoting the interaction point between the manipulated tool and other objects. Both keypoints are defined in 3D space and can be annotated by VLMs or humans.

Visual input consists of point clouds derived from an RGB-D camera. SAM2, a segmentation model, segments relevant objects (robot, active object, goal object, others) in RGB images. These 2D segmentations are then mapped onto the 3D point cloud. Subsequent steps involve removing background and floor points and down-sampling the point cloud using Farthest Point Sampling (FPS) to obtain a concise workspace point cloud.

**Semantic Correspondence on 3D Meshes:**
A critical component is establishing accurate 3D correspondences of keypoints between the source object mesh (M_src) and a large set of target object meshes (M_tg). This is achieved by:
1. **Canonicalization:** Each 3D mesh is normalized into a unified canonical space using a 6D pose estimator to standardize orientation and scale.
2. **Rendering:** Parallel rendering is employed to generate RGB-D images from multiple camera viewpoints for both source and target meshes.
3. **Semantic Feature Extraction:** DINOv2, a visual foundation model, processes each rendered image to extract its semantic representation.
4. **Keypoint Matching:** For a given keypoint `x` on the source mesh, its neighboring vertices are projected onto rendered images. The corresponding pixels on target images are identified by maximizing cosine similarity in the DINOv2 feature space. A similarity score is recorded for each match.
5. **3D Target Keypoint Estimation:** The matched pixels on target images are then unprojected back into 3D space, yielding candidate correspondences. The final target keypoint `x'` on M_tg is computed as a weighted average of these candidates, using the similarity scores as weights.

**Diverse Demonstration Generation:**
This stage focuses on synthesizing complete manipulation trajectories for new object meshes.
1. **Keypoint-constrained Trajectory Replay:** The core principle is that the relative trajectory of the end-effector to the affording point, and the relative trajectory of the function point to the goal object, remain similar for objects sharing a functional class.
 - **Grasp Segment (τ_g) Transfer:** The source grasp segment is first normalized into the local frame of the source mesh. For a new target mesh M_tg with its corresponding affording point `x'_aff`, the new grasp segment in M_tg's local frame is computed by translating the normalized source segment by the vector `(x'_aff - x_aff)`.
 - **Skill Segment (τ_s) Transfer:** The trajectory of the function point during the skill stage is derived and normalized into the source mesh's local frame. For M_tg, the new function trajectory in its local frame is obtained by a similar translation using the function points `(x'_fun - x_fun)`. These localized segments are then transformed back to the world frame based on the target object's pose, and joint positions are computed via an Inverse Kinematics (IK) solver.
2. **Motion Planning for Transition Segment:** The transition segments (between grasp and skill, or between skills in multi-stage tasks) are considered free-space motions. These are generated using standard motion planning algorithms or spherical linear interpolation to ensure collision-free paths.
3. **Point Cloud Digital Cousin Generation:** To create the visual observations for the generated trajectories, point clouds of the robot and the manipulated object are rendered directly from simulation. These simulated components are then combined with point clouds of the goal object and other static elements from the source demonstration. This hybrid approach aims to mitigate the sim-to-real gap without requiring full reconstruction of the real environment in simulation. For the skill segment, goal object point clouds are replayed from corresponding source demonstration timestamps to preserve visual authenticity of occlusions.

**Policy Training and Evaluation:**
Policies are trained using these generated datasets with consistent hyperparameters across tasks (e.g., 1000 epochs, specific observation and action horizons). Experiments are conducted in both simulation (ManiSkill3) and the real world across four tasks: Teapot Pouring, Mug Hanging, Knife Cutting, and Shoe Organizing. Evaluations include both in-category generalization (on source and unseen meshes) and zero-shot cross-category generalization. Performance is compared against baselines like DemoGen, CPGen (adapted for point clouds), and FUNCTO (for real-world experiments), with metrics focusing on success rates.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted in both simulation and real-world environments demonstrate AffordGen's capabilities in generating diverse data for robot manipulation and improving policy generalization.

**Simulation Experiments:**
- **In-category Generalization:** AffordGen policies exhibited comparable performance to DemoGen on the source mesh, indicating that the generation process preserved the task-relevant information for the original object. Crucially, AffordGen significantly outperformed both DemoGen and CPGen on *unseen object tests* across all four manipulation tasks (Teapot Pouring, Mug Hanging, Knife Cutting, Shoe Organizing). Specifically, AffordGen achieved an average performance boost of 24.1% over the best baseline (CPGen) on unseen object tests in its most effective configuration (100 meshes x 10 demonstrations per mesh). The performance generally decreased as evaluation meshes became increasingly dissimilar from the source, but AffordGen consistently maintained higher generalization performance compared to baselines.
- **Zero-shot Cross-Category Generalization:** AffordGen demonstrated the ability to repurpose source demonstrations to generate training data for objects from entirely different categories but sharing functional affordances (e.g., mug pouring from teapot pouring demonstrations). In these zero-shot cross-category tasks, AffordGen was the only method to achieve meaningful non-zero success rates, significantly outperforming CPGen and DemoGen, which showed negligible success. For example, in the simulated mug pouring task, AffordGen achieved a 55.00% success rate, while CPGen and DemoGen yielded 2.70% and 0.70% respectively.

**Real-World Experiments:**
- **Experiment Settings:** Real-world validation mirrored the simulation setup, utilizing 10 expert demonstrations to generate 1000 training demonstrations. Evaluations were conducted on a diverse set of previously unseen real objects, and FUNCTO, a planning-based affordance method, was included as an additional baseline.
- **In-category Generalization:** Similar to simulation, AffordGen, DemoGen, and CPGen achieved high success rates on the source object. However, AffordGen again surpassed DemoGen and CPGen in performance on most *unseen real objects*. For instance, in real-world teapot pouring on unseen instances, AffordGen recorded 74/162 successful rollouts, compared to 2/162 for DemoGen and 15/162 for CPGen.
 FUNCTO's performance was observed to be highly contingent on the clarity and visibility of key correspondence points. It performed relatively well in tasks with clear key regions (e.g., knife cutting) but showed reduced success rates in scenarios involving significant orientation variations or occlusions (e.g., mug hanging), highlighting a limitation of planner-based methods reliant on explicit keypoint mapping. AffordGen, by contrast, implicitly learned robust relationships between keypoints and object shapes through large-scale generated data, mitigating these occlusion challenges.
- **Zero-shot Cross-Category Generalization:** AffordGen successfully demonstrated the generation of cross-category object manipulation data in real-world settings (e.g., mug pouring from teapot demonstrations, handbag hanging from mug demonstrations, saw cutting from knife demonstrations). These real-world cross-category experiments reaffirmed AffordGen's ability to expand robot capabilities with low data cost, achieving notably higher success rates than the baseline methods. For example, in real-world mug pouring, AffordGen achieved 14/27 successes, compared to 3/27 for CPGen and 0/27 for DemoGen.

**General Observations:**
AffordGen successfully generated thousands of diverse trajectories across hundreds of 3D meshes from a minimal number of source demonstrations. The framework improved policy performance on both objects similar to those used in generation and truly novel, unseen objects. The study also indicated that the effectiveness of object-level generation for generalization initially increases and then declines as the diversity of generated objects extends further, providing insights for optimizing cross-instance data generation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

AffordGen represents an advancement in addressing two persistent challenges in robotic manipulation: the prohibitive cost of data collection and the limited generalization capabilities of learned policies to novel objects and scenarios. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Novel Data Generation Paradigm:** The work introduces a new paradigm by leveraging affordance correspondence not for online planning, but as a systematic generative source for synthesizing diverse, semantically-grounded, and full 6D manipulation trajectories. This approach scales minimal human demonstrations into thousands of training examples across varied object categories and poses.
- **Enhanced Generalization Capabilities:** AffordGen enables policies to achieve robust zero-shot generalization to truly unseen objects, including those from different categories, in both simulation and real-world environments. This is a crucial step toward developing more adaptable and general-purpose robots that can operate in unstructured and varied environments without requiring extensive re-training or new demonstrations for every novel object encountered.
- **Improved Data Efficiency:** By generating large-scale, high-quality synthetic data from a few initial demonstrations, the framework significantly reduces the burden and cost associated with manual data collection in robot learning. This improvement in data efficiency can accelerate research and development in robotics.
- **Robust Closed-Loop Control:** Policies trained with AffordGen-generated data exhibit robust, closed-loop control. This combines the semantic understanding derived from affordance information with the reactive capabilities of end-to-end learning, which is a key advantage over traditional open-loop, planning-centric affordance methods that may lack adaptability to real-time variations.
- **Practical Applicability:** The demonstrated success in real-world experiments, particularly the cross-category generalization, suggests a strong potential for the practical deployment of robots in a wider array of applications. Robots equipped with such policies could perform complex manipulation tasks on unfamiliar objects in dynamic settings.
- **Guidance for Future Research:** The observed trend regarding the object-level generation ability (initially increasing then decreasing with increasing dissimilarity of generated objects) provides empirical guidance for optimizing data diversity strategies in future cross-instance generation research. Furthermore, the integration of powerful vision foundation models like DINOv2 for semantic correspondence highlights the potential for combining advanced perception capabilities with robust policy learning.

In conclusion, AffordGen contributes to robot learning by providing a scalable and data-efficient method for generating diverse manipulation demonstrations. This approach has the potential to foster the development of more generalizable and autonomous robotic systems.
