# Why Does RL Generalize Better Than SFT? A Data-Centric Perspective on VLM Post-Training

> [!summary] Summary
> This research reveals that Reinforcement Learning's superior out-of-distribution generalization in Vision-Language Models stems from an implicit data filtering mechanism, prioritizing medium-difficulty training samples. The authors introduce Difficulty-Curated Supervised Fine-Tuning (DC-SFT), which explicitly filters data based on difficulty, matching or exceeding RL's generalization performance while offering significantly higher computational efficiency and training stability.

## Problem

- Reinforcement Learning (RL) generally achieves better out-of-distribution (OOD) generalization than Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) in Vision-Language Models (VLMs), with the reasons primarily attributed to RL's algorithmic properties.
- Standard SFT often overfits in-distribution (ID) training data, resulting in suboptimal performance on unseen or OOD examples.
- RL-based VLM adaptation is computationally intensive and frequently suffers from training instability, hindering its practical application.

## Method

- Proposed a data-centric hypothesis that RL's generalization stems from an implicit ==data filtering== mechanism that assigns higher advantage signals to medium-difficulty training samples, effectively focusing updates on them.
- Developed Difficulty-Curated SFT (DC-SFT), an SFT variant that explicitly filters training data into easy, medium-difficulty, and hard categories based on initial VLM responses.
- Introduced SFT-M (training on medium-difficulty samples) and SFT-EM (training on easy and medium-difficulty samples, excluding hard ones) to test the hypothesis and improve SFT generalization.

## Results

- DC-SFT (SFT-EM) consistently outperformed standard SFT and the GRPO (RL) baseline on OOD tasks, achieving up to a **4.48%** gain over SFT and **2.62%** over GRPO for Qwen2.5-VL-**7B** models.
- DC-SFT demonstrated substantially higher computational efficiency, achieving a **4.9x** speedup on ImageNet and **3.2x** speedup on RefCOCO compared to GRPO, while maintaining superior training stability.
- Training SFT exclusively on 'hard' samples resulted in a **14.07%** OOD performance drop on ImageNet-R, highlighting their detrimental effect, while SFT-M matched or slightly exceeded GRPO's OOD performance by **0.93%**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - RL implicitly filters training data, concentrating model updates on medium-difficulty samples that yield high-variance rewards and meaningful advantage signals, while largely ignoring easy and hard examples.
> - Hard training samples are particularly detrimental to SFT's OOD generalization, leading to overfitting to noisy patterns due to their disproportionately large gradient norms.
> - Explicitly curating SFT training data by removing hard examples (DC-SFT) allows SFT to achieve OOD generalization comparable to or surpassing RL, with improved stability and efficiency.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work situates itself within the rapidly evolving field of large-scale Vision-Language Models (VLMs) and their post-training methodologies. Adapting pre-trained foundation models to specific downstream tasks is a critical step, commonly achieved through Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) or Reinforcement Learning (RL).

**Addressing the SFT vs. RL Generalization Gap:** A well-documented phenomenon in this landscape is that RL-trained models often exhibit superior generalization to out-of-distribution (OOD) data compared to SFT-trained models, which tend to overfit the in-distribution (ID) training set. Previous explanations for RL's advantage typically focused on its algorithmic properties, such as promoting exploration and learning from reward feedback. This paper challenges and extends this conventional understanding by proposing a **data-centric explanation**. It argues that RL's generalization stems from an *implicit data filtering mechanism* that prioritizes medium-difficulty training samples, rather than solely from its optimization objective. This directly contrasts with standard SFT's uniform treatment of all training data.

**Connections to Data Curation and Curriculum Learning:** The paper connects to the broader research on data curation for SFT, where techniques like rejection sampling and model distillation are used to construct high-quality instruction datasets. The authors position their Difficulty-Curated SFT (DC-SFT) as a complementary approach that can further enhance these methods by focusing on optimal data subsets for generalization. While related to curriculum learning, which focuses on the *sequencing* of training data from easy to hard, this work emphasizes *filtering* specific data subsets (i.e., removing hard examples) to improve generalization.

**Implications for Test-Time Scaling and Reasoning:** The study also touches upon test-time scaling, where RL has been shown to encourage complex reasoning in models, leading to better OOD performance on tasks requiring intricate thought processes. While SFT with model distillation can sometimes match or outperform RL for reasoning in smaller models, its generalization limitations have been a challenge. This paper aims to bridge that generalization gap for SFT, making it a more viable and efficient option for enhancing reasoning capabilities and test-time scaling. By offering a stable and efficient SFT variant that generalizes well, the work provides a practical alternative to computationally intensive RL methods, which is highly relevant given the resource demands of large-scale model training.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to provide a **data-centric explanation** for the empirically observed phenomenon that Reinforcement Learning (RL) models generalize better to out-of-distribution (OOD) data than Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) models in Vision-Language Model (VLM) post-training.

The primary motivations driving this work are:

1. **Unraveling the Generalization Gap:** Despite the consistent observation of RL's superior OOD generalization, the underlying mechanisms have predominantly been attributed to RL's unique optimization objectives (e.g., exploration, reward maximization). The authors aim to investigate whether a more fundamental, data-related factor is at play.
2. **Developing Efficient and Stable Generalization Methods:** RL-based training, while effective, is known for its computational intensity and training instability. If the generalization advantage can be linked to data characteristics, it might be possible to achieve similar or better generalization using simpler, more stable, and computationally efficient SFT approaches. This would democratize access to robust VLM adaptation.
3. **Improving VLM Robustness:** In real-world applications, VLMs frequently encounter data that deviates from their training distribution. Enhancing OOD generalization is crucial for the reliability and practical utility of these models.

The paper's core hypothesis is that **RL's superior generalization arises from an implicit data filtering mechanism that inherently prioritizes medium-difficulty training samples.** The authors contend that RL updates the model more significantly on samples with high-variance rewards (medium-difficulty), while effectively ignoring "easy" (consistently high reward) or "hard" (consistently low reward) samples that yield uniform or negligible advantage signals. This implicit selection process, they hypothesize, is key to RL's robust performance.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research adopts a systematic, data-centric approach to test its hypothesis and propose an improved SFT method.

#### 3.1. Data-Centric Hypothesis Formulation

The authors begin by categorizing training samples into three difficulty levels for a given prompt, based on the correctness of eight generated responses (G=8) from an initialized VLM:
- **Easy:** All G responses are correct.
- **Hard:** All G responses are incorrect.
- **Medium-difficulty:** Responses contain a mixture of correct and incorrect answers.

They hypothesize that RL, specifically using algorithms like Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), implicitly filters data. For easy and hard instances, where all generated responses yield uniform (maximal or minimal) rewards, the advantage term (A_k in Eq. 5) becomes zero. Consequently, these samples contribute negligible gradients, and the model updates are effectively concentrated on medium-difficulty samples that produce diverse rewards and meaningful advantage estimates.

#### 3.2. Empirical Validation of Data Difficulty Impact

To validate this hypothesis, the authors conduct experiments training SFT models on distinct data subsets curated by difficulty (easy, medium, hard).
- **VLMs Used:** Qwen2.5-VL-3B and Qwen2.5-VL-7B as foundational models, with additional scalability tests on MiniCPM-V-4.
- **Tasks:**
 - **Image Classification:** Training on ImageNet-1K (100 categories), evaluating on ID ImageNet-1K and OOD ImageNet-R, ImageNet-A.
 - **Visual Grounding:** Training on RefCOCO (10,000 instances), evaluating on ID RefCOCO validation and OOD Ref-L4, Lisa.
 - **Reasoning Tasks:** Training on MMK12 (teacher-model distilled reasoning chains), evaluating on OOD MMK12, MMMU, WeMath, MathVerse, MathVista, and MathVision.
- **Training Settings:** Parameter-efficient LoRA fine-tuning (rank 32, alpha 64) for most experiments, with some full-parameter fine-tuning for robustness checks. AdamW optimizer, cosine learning rate scheduler.
- **Evaluation Metrics:** Accuracy for image classification and reasoning, IoU ≥ 0.5 for visual grounding. Greedy decoding (temperature 0).

#### 3.3. Difficulty-Curated SFT (DC-SFT) Proposal

Based on the empirical findings, the authors propose DC-SFT, which explicitly filters the training set based on sample difficulty. Two variants are introduced:
- **SFT-M:** Trains exclusively on medium-difficulty instances, mimicking RL's implicit filtering.
- **SFT-EM:** Trains on both easy and medium-difficulty examples, explicitly removing only the hard samples.

#### 3.4. Comparative Analysis

DC-SFT variants are compared against:
- **Standard SFT:** Trained on the full dataset.
- **GRPO:** A representative RL algorithm, trained on the full dataset with similar generation settings for fair comparison (8 responses per prompt, temperature 0.9, top-p 1.0) and a KL divergence penalty.
- **SFT-RS (Rejection Sampling):** For reasoning tasks, where the model is trained only on its own self-generated correct responses.

The comparison focuses not only on performance but also on **training stability** (analyzing performance curves over steps) and **computational efficiency** (measuring training time).

#### 3.5. Ablation Studies and Scalability Analysis

Further experiments quantify the impact of hard data by incrementally adding them to SFT. Gradient norms are analyzed across different difficulty subsets to understand why hard data is detrimental. Scalability is tested by using a larger training set (100k samples) and different base models (MiniCPM-V-4), as well as evaluating full-parameter fine-tuning.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yields several significant findings that collectively support the data-centric hypothesis and validate the effectiveness of DC-SFT:

1. **Hard Samples Degrade OOD Generalization in SFT:** Training SFT models exclusively on "hard" samples significantly improves in-distribution (ID) performance but leads to a substantial degradation in out-of-distribution (OOD) performance. For instance, Qwen2.5-VL-7B saw a 7.08% ID gain but a severe 14.07% OOD drop on ImageNet-R when trained on hard data.
2. **Medium-Difficulty Samples Promote Balanced Performance:** Training SFT models on "medium-difficulty" samples achieves a more balanced performance profile, with good ID improvements and preserved or slightly enhanced OOD generalization. Training on "easy" samples also maintains OOD performance but yields lower ID scores compared to medium-difficulty data.
3. **DC-SFT Outperforms Standard SFT and RL on OOD Tasks:**
 - **SFT-M (Medium-difficulty only):** This variant, designed to mimic RL's implicit filtering, consistently matches or exceeds the OOD performance of GRPO (RL baseline). For Qwen2.5-VL-7B, SFT-M showed an average OOD accuracy gain of 2.79% over SFT and 0.93% over GRPO.
 - **SFT-EM (Easy + Medium-difficulty):** This variant, which explicitly removes only hard samples, achieves the strongest OOD performance overall, delivering substantial improvements over standard SFT (up to 4.48% for 7B model) and significantly outperforming GRPO (by 2.62% for 7B model). This suggests that the detrimental effect of hard samples on standard SFT's generalization is a primary culprit.
4. **Superior Training Stability:** GRPO exhibits notable training instability, with sharp performance drops during training, as shown in the performance curves. In contrast, DC-SFT maintains consistently stable performance throughout the training process.
5. **Significantly Higher Computational Efficiency:** DC-SFT demonstrates substantially higher efficiency compared to GRPO, achieving 4.9x speedup on ImageNet and 3.2x speedup on RefCOCO. This efficiency is maintained even when including the time required for data curation.
6. **Scalability and Generalizability:**
 - The effectiveness of DC-SFT extends to other VLM architectures (MiniCPM-V-4) and larger training datasets (100k samples), consistently outperforming SFT and GRPO on OOD benchmarks.
 - DC-SFT also maintains its benefits under full-parameter fine-tuning, not just LoRA.
7. **Hard Data's Disproportionate Impact:** Even a small proportion (e.g., 5%) of hard samples can severely degrade OOD generalization in SFT. This is attributed to the significantly larger gradient norms induced by hard samples, which dominate the optimization trajectory and potentially lead to overfitting to noisy or ambiguous patterns.
8. **Enhanced Reasoning Capabilities:** When applied to tasks requiring complex reasoning (e.g., mathematical reasoning, general knowledge), SFT-M consistently achieves the highest performance across various benchmarks, surpassing standard SFT, GRPO, and Rejection Sampling baselines.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work offers a profound shift in understanding the generalization capabilities of post-trained Vision-Language Models, moving from an algorithmic-centric view to a data-centric one. Its significance and potential impact are multifaceted:

1. **Fundamental Insight into Generalization:** By identifying RL's implicit data filtering mechanism as a key driver of its generalization, the paper provides a new theoretical lens through which to understand and improve VLM adaptation. This insight challenges prevailing beliefs and opens new avenues for research into how data characteristics influence model learning and generalization.
2. **Practical and Efficient VLM Post-Training:** The introduction of Difficulty-Curated SFT (DC-SFT) presents a highly practical and accessible method for achieving robust OOD generalization. DC-SFT's ability to outperform strong RL baselines while offering significantly greater training stability and computational efficiency is a major breakthrough. This means that developers can achieve high-performing, generalized VLMs without incurring the high costs and complexities traditionally associated with RL.
3. **Democratization of Advanced VLM Capabilities:** The improved efficiency and stability of DC-SFT can make state-of-the-art generalization more accessible to researchers and practitioners with limited computational resources. This could accelerate innovation in various applications requiring robust VLMs, such as robotics, medical imaging, and scientific discovery.
4. **Improved Data Curation Strategies:** The findings underscore the critical importance of training data quality and composition, particularly highlighting the detrimental effect of "hard" samples. This will likely inspire more sophisticated data curation and filtering techniques for SFT, going beyond simple data quantity to focus on data "utility" for generalization.
5. **Enhanced Reasoning for VLMs:** The demonstration that DC-SFT also improves reasoning capabilities extends its utility beyond basic OOD generalization. This suggests a powerful, yet simpler, pathway to developing VLMs that can perform complex multi-modal reasoning tasks more effectively and reliably.
6. **Guidance for Future Research:** The paper's limitations, such as restricting validation to a relatively narrow set of model architectures and scales, and primarily using LoRA fine-tuning, also point to clear directions for future work. Expanding validation to a broader array of VLMs, larger models, and diverse fine-tuning strategies will be crucial to further solidify these findings. Investigating more dynamic or adaptive difficulty curation methods could also be a promising area.

In conclusion, "Why Does RL Generalize Better Than SFT?" is a highly impactful paper that not only provides a compelling data-centric explanation for a long-standing observation but also introduces an efficient, stable, and effective method (DC-SFT) to address the VLM generalization gap, thereby paving the way for more robust and practical VLM applications.
