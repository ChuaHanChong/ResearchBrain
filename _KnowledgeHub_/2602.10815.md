# Why Does RL Generalize Better Than SFT? A Data-Centric Perspective on VLM Post-Training

> [!summary] Summary
> This research reveals that Reinforcement Learning's superior out-of-distribution generalization in Vision-Language Models stems from an implicit data filtering mechanism, prioritizing medium-difficulty training samples. The authors introduce Difficulty-Curated Supervised Fine-Tuning (DC-SFT), which explicitly filters data based on difficulty, matching or exceeding RL's generalization performance while offering significantly higher computational efficiency and training stability.

## Problem

- Reinforcement Learning (RL) generally achieves better out-of-distribution (OOD) generalization than Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) in Vision-Language Models (VLMs), with the reasons primarily attributed to RL's algorithmic properties.
- Standard SFT often overfits in-distribution (ID) training data, resulting in suboptimal performance on unseen or OOD examples.
- RL-based VLM adaptation is computationally intensive and frequently suffers from training instability, hindering its practical application.

## Method

- Proposed a data-centric hypothesis that RL's generalization stems from an implicit ==data filtering== mechanism that assigns higher advantage signals to medium-difficulty training samples, effectively focusing updates on them.
- Developed Difficulty-Curated SFT (DC-SFT), an SFT variant that explicitly filters training data into easy, medium-difficulty, and hard categories based on initial VLM responses.
- Introduced SFT-M (training on medium-difficulty samples) and SFT-EM (training on easy and medium-difficulty samples, excluding hard ones) to test the hypothesis and improve SFT generalization.

## Results

- DC-SFT (SFT-EM) consistently outperformed standard SFT and the GRPO (RL) baseline on OOD tasks, achieving up to a **4.48%** gain over SFT and **2.62%** over GRPO for Qwen2.5-VL-**7B** models.
- DC-SFT demonstrated substantially higher computational efficiency, achieving a **4.9x** speedup on ImageNet and **3.2x** speedup on RefCOCO compared to GRPO, while maintaining superior training stability.
- Training SFT exclusively on 'hard' samples resulted in a **14.07%** OOD performance drop on ImageNet-R, highlighting their detrimental effect, while SFT-M matched or slightly exceeded GRPO's OOD performance by **0.93%**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - RL implicitly filters training data, concentrating model updates on medium-difficulty samples that yield high-variance rewards and meaningful advantage signals, while largely ignoring easy and hard examples.
> - Hard training samples are particularly detrimental to SFT's OOD generalization, leading to overfitting to noisy patterns due to their disproportionately large gradient norms.
> - Explicitly curating SFT training data by removing hard examples (DC-SFT) allows SFT to achieve OOD generalization comparable to or surpassing RL, with improved stability and efficiency.
