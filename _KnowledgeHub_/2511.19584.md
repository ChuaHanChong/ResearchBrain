# Learning Massively Multitask World Models for Continuous Control

> [!summary] Summary
> This research demonstrates that online reinforcement learning can scale to hundreds of diverse continuous control tasks, introducing Newt, a model-based agent, and MMBench, a benchmark with 200 tasks. Newt achieves superior multitask performance and data-efficiency, demonstrating strong transferability and open-loop control capabilities, challenging prior assumptions about online RL scalability.

## Problem

- Online reinforcement learning (RL) was widely considered not to scale efficiently to continuous control problems involving hundreds of diverse tasks.
- Existing generalist agents primarily rely on imitation learning, which is limited by the availability of costly expert demonstrations and an inherent performance ceiling.
- A lack of comprehensive benchmarks for massively multitask online RL hindered progress in developing truly general-purpose control agents.

## Method

- Introduces MMBench, a novel benchmark comprising 200 diverse ==continuous control tasks== across 10 domains, with language instructions and expert demonstrations.
- Develops Newt, a language-conditioned, multitask world model built upon the data-efficient TD-MPC2 framework.
- Newt leverages pre-trained vision/language encoders, a self-predictive world model, and a four-pronged strategy to integrate demonstrations for efficient online learning.

## Results

- Newt achieved superior multitask performance (**0.438** normalized score at **100M** steps) and data-efficiency on MMBench compared to strong baselines like PPO and FastTD3.
- The learned world model in Newt enabled open-loop control for up to 48 steps (**16x** its training horizon), with performance often closely matching closed-loop control.
- Newt demonstrated strong transferability to 20 held-out tasks, achieving an average score of **0.868** after only 100k finetuning steps, significantly outperforming a from-scratch baseline (**0.480**).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Massively multitask online reinforcement learning is feasible and highly effective for continuous control when combined with model-based approaches, language conditioning, and strategic use of demonstrations.
> - Language instructions are crucial for task differentiation and improving performance, particularly in domains where tasks cannot be distinguished solely by observations.
> - The learned world model in Newt enables robust open-loop control over surprisingly long time horizons and facilitates rapid few-shot adaptation to unseen tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper positions itself at the intersection of several rapidly evolving fields: large-scale reinforcement learning, generalist agents, and continuous control robotics.

Historically, reinforcement learning (RL) for continuous control has largely focused on single-task environments or strictly offline learning regimes. While impressive progress has been made in learning diverse control policies, the prevailing view has been that online RL does not scale efficiently to a large number of diverse continuous control tasks. The dominant approach for creating "generalist agents" capable of many tasks, as seen in works like Reed et al. (2022) or Open X-Embodiment Collaboration et al. (2023), has often involved large-scale supervised learning on enormous datasets of expert demonstrations (imitation learning). This approach, while powerful, has inherent limitations: it relies on the availability and quality of demonstrations, which are often costly to collect and fundamentally bound the policy's performance by the expert's skill level.

Inspired by the success of "foundation models" in other domains like natural language processing and computer vision, which involve large-scale pretraining followed by fine-tuning with lighter RL, this work challenges the aforementioned limitations. The authors explicitly ask whether this "foundation model recipe" can be successfully applied to *online continuous control* across *hundreds of tasks*. This is a critical departure, aiming to leverage online interaction for continuous improvement and potentially superhuman performance, rather than being confined by demonstrations.

To facilitate this shift, the paper introduces **MMBench**, a novel benchmark designed for massively multitask online RL. This addresses a significant gap in the research landscape, as existing benchmarks are often limited to single tasks, narrow task families, or offline settings. By providing a diverse suite of 200 tasks spanning 10 distinct domains with varying embodiments, observation modalities (state, image), and accompanying language instructions and demonstrations, MMBench creates an unprecedented testbed for generalist control agents.

The proposed algorithm, **Newt**, directly builds upon the robust and data-efficient TD-MPC2 framework, adapting it to the massively multitask, language-conditioned, and online learning scenario. By combining model-based RL with strategic integration of demonstrations and language conditioning, Newt attempts to bridge the gap between efficient single-task learning and scalable multi-domain generalization, pushing the boundaries of what is considered feasible for online reinforcement learning in continuous control. This work thus represents a significant step towards developing more truly general and adaptable robotic and control agents, moving beyond imitation learning's constraints and proving the scalability of online RL in this complex domain.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to challenge the prevailing assumption that online reinforcement learning (RL) does not scale effectively to continuous control problems involving a vast number of tasks. Specifically, the authors pose the central question: **"can a single policy be trained with online RL on hundreds of control tasks at once?"**

This overarching goal is driven by several key motivations:

1. **Overcoming Limitations of Imitation Learning:** While large-scale imitation learning has produced impressive "generalist agents," it suffers from two major drawbacks:
 - **Data Scarcity:** It is heavily reliant on enormous datasets of near-expert trajectories, which are difficult and expensive to collect.
 - **Performance Ceiling:** The performance of the resulting policy is inherently bounded by the quality of the demonstrations, preventing the agent from achieving superhuman capabilities or continuously improving beyond expert behavior. The authors advocate for online RL as a means to move beyond this limitation.
2. **Developing General-Purpose Control Agents:** The long-term ambition in robotics and AI is to create agents capable of performing a wide variety of tasks across many embodiments. Achieving this requires moving beyond single-task or narrow-domain training paradigms towards massively multitask learning.
3. **Advancing Online RL for Continuous Control:** The community needs robust and scalable online RL algorithms that can operate effectively in complex, high-dimensional continuous control environments. The current focus on narrow tasks or offline regimes has reinforced the view that online RL struggles with scalability in this domain, a view the authors aim to refute.
4. **Facilitating Research:** The current lack of a comprehensive benchmark for massively multitask online RL hinders progress in this direction. Therefore, a crucial objective is to introduce **MMBench**, providing a standardized, diverse, and accessible environment to accelerate research.
5. **Leveraging Model-Based RL Strengths:** The authors motivate the choice of a model-based RL approach (Newt, based on TD-MPC2) due to its inherent advantages in:
 - **Data-Efficiency:** Model-based methods can often learn effective policies with less environmental interaction.
 - **Robustness:** They tend to be robust across varied observation/action spaces, reward functions, and task horizons.
 - **Task Differentiation:** The world model can integrate language and visual cues to differentiate tasks effectively.
 - **Computational Feasibility:** Aiming for a method that trains within a reasonable timeframe, especially for hundreds of tasks.

In essence, the paper aims to demonstrate the feasibility and benefits of training a truly generalist control agent through online reinforcement learning on a massive scale, supported by a novel benchmark and a data-efficient, model-based algorithmic framework.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology centers around two core contributions: the **MMBench** benchmark for massively multitask reinforcement learning and the **Newt** agent, a language-conditioned multitask world model.

#### 3.1. MMBench: A Benchmark for Massively Multitask RL

**MMBench** is designed to enable and evaluate massively multitask online RL.
- **Scale and Diversity:** It comprises 200 unique continuous control tasks for training (and 20 for testing) across 10 distinct task domains, including DMControl, Meta-World, ManiSkill3, MuJoCo, MiniArcade (a new suite introduced in this work), Box2D, RoboDesk, OGBench, and Atari. This includes 159 existing tasks, 22 new variants, and 19 entirely new MiniArcade tasks.
- **Observation Modalities:** All tasks support low-dimensional state observations, 224x224 RGB images, or both.
- **Language Instructions:** Crucially, every task is accompanied by a natural language instruction, differentiating tasks even when observation spaces are similar (e.g., in RoboDesk). These instructions describe the embodiment and task goal, with embeddings generated by CLIP-ViT/B.
- **Demonstrations:** To address exploration challenges in large-scale online RL, MMBench provides 10-40 expert demonstrations per task (totaling over 4000), collected by single-task TD-MPC2 agents. These serve as a strong behavior prior.
- **Accessibility:** The benchmark offers a ready-to-use Docker image, fast environment wrappers for asynchronous stepping, batched frame-stacking/image encoding, cached language embeddings, and unified handling of diverse observation/action spaces, significantly reducing computational barriers for researchers.
- **Problem Formulation:** The goal is to learn a single policy for an infinite-horizon Partially Observable Markov Decision Process, where the state is augmented with low-dimensional state, image, and language instruction, to maximize expected return across all tasks.

#### 3.2. Newt: A Multitask World Model for Continuous Control

Newt extends **TD-MPC2**, a model-based RL algorithm known for its data-efficiency and robust performance in continuous control. TD-MPC2 performs trajectory optimization (planning) in the latent space of a self-predictive world model. Newt adapts this to the massively multitask, language- and optionally image-conditioned online setting through several algorithmic improvements:

- **Refined Architecture (Eq. 1):**
 - **Language Encoder:** `g = CLIP_text(s_lang)` (frozen, pretrained).
 - **Image Encoder:** `x = DINOv2(s_img)` (optional, frozen, pretrained).
 - **State Encoder:** `z = h(s_state, x, g)` (computes latent state representation).
 - **Latent Dynamics:** `z' = d(z, a, g)` (predicts next latent state).
 - **Reward Predictor:** `r̂ = R(z, a, g)` (predicts task-specific reward).
 - **Terminal Value Predictor:** `q̂ = Q(z, a, g)` (predicts discounted sum of rewards).
 - **Policy Prior:** `â = p(z, g)` (predicts optimal action, warm-starts planning).
 All learnable components (h, d, R, Q, p) are implemented as MLPs, with inputs fused by concatenation.
- **World Model Learning Objective (Eq. 2):**
 Newt jointly optimizes `h, d, R, Q` using gradient descent on a combined loss function:
 - **Self-prediction:** `||z'_t - sg(h(s'_state_t, x'_t, g))||^2` (latent dynamics match next encoded state, with stop-gradient).
 - **Reward Prediction:** `ℓ_CE(r̂_t, r_t)` (cross-entropy for discrete reward regression, handling diverse reward scales).
 - **Value Prediction:** `ℓ_CE(q̂_t, q_t)` (cross-entropy for discrete value regression).
 - **TD-Target:** `q_t = r_t + γQ_tgt(z'_t, p(z'_t), g)` (one-step TD-target).
 - Uses an ensemble of Q-networks and per-task discount factors based on episode length.
- **Policy Prior Objective (Eq. 3):**
 The policy `p` is a stochastic maximum entropy policy maximizing Q-values, but with a crucial addition:
 - **Model-based Behavior Cloning (BC) Loss:** `||p(z_t, g) - a_t||^2`. This term directly supervises the policy with expert actions from demonstrations and distills planned actions, improving performance when Q-value estimates are noisy.
- **Leveraging Demonstrations (Fourfold Strategy):**
 1. **Model-based Pretraining:** Before any online interaction, all learnable components are pretrained on demonstrations by minimizing `L(θ) + L_p(θ)` (with Q-value term in `L_p(θ)` temporarily disabled).
 2. **Constrained Planning:** Early in online RL, the planner is biased towards the pretrained policy's action distribution, with this bias linearly annealed to zero as the online Q-function improves (Fig. 11).
 3. **Oversampling of Demonstrations:** Demonstrations are kept in a separate buffer and sampled at equal proportions (50%) with online interaction data, ensuring their continued influence.
 4. **Action Supervision in RL Policy Updates:** The explicit BC loss term in Equation 3.
- **Computational Acceleration:** Newt incorporates significant engineering efforts to accelerate training, including distributed model updates, environment interactions, and replay buffers across multiple processes/GPUs, and using `torch.compile` for optimized execution.

In summary, Newt combines a robust model-based RL framework with powerful pre-trained encoders, a novel integration of language instructions, and a sophisticated strategy for leveraging demonstrations to enable scalable and data-efficient learning across hundreds of diverse continuous control tasks in an online setting.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted on the MMBench benchmark yielded several significant findings regarding Newt's capabilities and the feasibility of massively multitask online RL.

- **Q1: Performance of Massively Multitask Online RL:**
 - **Overall Superiority:** Newt demonstrates significantly better multitask performance and data-efficiency compared to a strong set of baselines, including Behavior Cloning (BC), Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO), FastTD3, and a simpler version of TD-MPC2 without Newt's specific enhancements. Newt achieves a higher average score across 200 tasks (0.438 normalized score at 100M steps) than all baselines (e.g., PPO at ~0.2, FastTD3 at ~0.3, TD-MPC2 at ~0.35).
 - **Domain-Specific Strengths:** Newt shows particular strength and data-efficiency in domains like DMControl, DMControl Extended, ManiSkill, and MiniArcade.
 - **Challenging Domains:** All RL methods, including Newt, exhibited subpar performance in MuJoCo, Box2D, and Atari domains, sometimes performing similarly to the simpler BC baseline. This is conjectured to be due to the relative uniqueness of tasks within these domains, limiting cross-task transfer. The authors note there is still room for improvement in consistency across tasks.

- **Q2: Impact of Learning from Demonstrations:**
 - Demonstrations proved to be highly beneficial for alleviating exploration difficulties and improving asymptotic performance in the massively multitask setting.
 - All four proposed methods of leveraging demonstrations—model-based pretraining, constrained planning, oversampling in the replay buffer, and an additional behavior cloning loss term in policy updates—contributed positively. The combination of all four strategies yielded the best overall performance, highlighting their synergistic effect.

- **Q3: Model Capabilities (Transfer and Open-Loop Control):**
 - **Rapid Adaptation (Few-shot Finetuning):** Newt exhibited strong transferability to unseen tasks and embodiments. When finetuned on 20 held-out tasks (without demonstrations), the pretrained Newt agent achieved a zero-shot score of 0.192 compared to 0.013 for an agent trained from scratch. After only 100k environment steps of finetuning, Newt reached an average score of 0.868, significantly outperforming the from-scratch baseline at 0.480. This demonstrates non-trivial transfer.
 - **Open-Loop Control:** The learned world model enabled Newt to perform open-loop control (planning and executing actions without environment feedback) over surprisingly long time horizons (up to 48 steps, 16x its training horizon of 3). In many tasks, its open-loop performance closely matched closed-loop control, providing strong evidence that the world model learns meaningful and accurate representations of environment dynamics.

- **Q4: Analysis and Ablations:**
 - **Scaling Model and Batch Size:** In contrast to some single-task RL findings, scaling both model size (up to 20M parameters for the default, and 80M tested) and batch size was found to be beneficial for performance in the multitask setting, suggesting a compute-optimal size exists for a given number of tasks.
 - **Language Instructions:** Conditioning the agent on language instructions provided clear performance benefits (average normalized score improvement from 0.371 to 0.438). This improvement was most pronounced in domains where tasks cannot be easily differentiated by observations alone (e.g., RoboDesk). Language conditioning also yielded lower training loss and matched the performance of explicit task IDs while enabling generalization.
 - **Visual Observations:** Adding high-resolution RGB inputs resulted in only a marginal overall performance improvement (+0.004). However, vision significantly improved performance in manipulation tasks (RoboDesk, Meta-World) but led to a decrease in domains like DMControl, where visual RL is known to be more challenging.
 - **Training Cost:** The authors provide detailed benchmarks, showing that training on 200 tasks for 100M environment steps with state observations is feasible within days on modern GPUs (e.g., 4.6 days on 2x RTX 5090).

In conclusion, the results strongly affirm that online RL for massively multitask continuous control is not only feasible but also highly effective when combined with model-based approaches, language conditioning, and strategic use of demonstrations. Newt, coupled with the MMBench benchmark, offers a powerful new framework for advancing generalist agent capabilities.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work makes a highly significant contribution to the fields of reinforcement learning, robotics, and embodied AI, with substantial potential impact:

1. **Redefining Scalability in Online RL for Continuous Control:** The most significant impact is demonstrating that **online reinforcement learning can indeed scale to hundreds of diverse continuous control tasks simultaneously**. This directly challenges a prevailing pessimistic view in the community and opens new avenues for developing general-purpose robotic agents that can continuously learn and improve in complex, real-world scenarios, moving beyond the limitations of purely imitation-based approaches.
2. **Introduction of a Foundational Benchmark (MMBench):** MMBench is a critical contribution. By providing a comprehensive, diverse, and accessible benchmark of 200 tasks across 10 domains, complete with language instructions, demonstrations, and optional visual observations, it fills a major void in the research landscape. This benchmark will serve as a standardized testbed, accelerating the development, evaluation, and comparison of new algorithms for massively multitask, multi-embodiment, and multi-domain RL. Its robust infrastructure (Docker, fast wrappers) further enhances its utility and adoption.
3. **Advancing Generalist Agent Architectures (Newt):** Newt, built upon the TD-MPC2 framework, showcases a powerful recipe for constructing generalist agents. Its integration of:
 - Pre-trained large language and vision models (CLIP, DINOv2) for robust representations.
 - A control-centric, self-predictive world model for data efficiency and planning.
 - Strategic and multi-faceted leveraging of expert demonstrations for bootstrapping and regularization.
 - Effective handling of diverse observation/action spaces and reward functions.
 ...provides a strong baseline and blueprint for future generalist policy learning. The demonstrated open-loop control capabilities and rapid adaptation to unseen tasks highlight the power of learned world models.
4. **Enabling Future Research Directions:** The authors' detailed "Recommendations for Future Work" section itself is a valuable contribution, outlining specific, high-impact research avenues that MMBench and Newt directly enable:
 - **Visual RL Scalability:** Improving visual RL's practicality and performance for large-scale multitask settings (e.g., better pretraining, novel data pipelines).
 - **Enhanced Language Understanding:** Moving beyond fixed instructions to improve generalization to novel task descriptions or more nuanced commands.
 - **Advanced Pretraining Strategies:** Exploring more sophisticated pretraining beyond simple demonstration-based supervised learning, akin to reasoning models in NLP.
 - **Novel Neural Architectures:** Integrating recent innovations like Transformers or Diffusion Policies into model-based RL.
 - **Deeper Model Analysis:** Understanding the emergent capabilities, learned representations, and limitations of massively multitask agents.
 - **Adaptive Learning Strategies:** Developing dynamic curricula and non-uniform sampling methods to optimize learning across tasks with varying complexities.
 - **Scaling Environments and Datasets:** Continued expansion of training data through procedural generation and integration of external large-scale datasets.
5. **Commitment to Open Science:** The release of the benchmark, 200+ model checkpoints, 4000+ demonstrations, and code for training/evaluation is a crucial act of open science. This transparency and provision of resources will significantly lower the barrier to entry for researchers, fostering collaboration and accelerating progress in this complex domain.

In essence, this paper doesn't just present a new method; it lays down a foundational challenge, provides the tools (MMBench), and offers a compelling solution (Newt) that collectively push the frontier of general-purpose embodied AI. It establishes massively multitask online RL as a viable and powerful paradigm, likely inspiring a new wave of research focused on building increasingly intelligent and adaptable agents.
