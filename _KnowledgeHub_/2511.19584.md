# Learning Massively Multitask World Models for Continuous Control

> [!summary] Summary
> This research demonstrates that online reinforcement learning can scale to hundreds of diverse continuous control tasks, introducing Newt, a model-based agent, and MMBench, a benchmark with 200 tasks. Newt achieves superior multitask performance and data-efficiency, demonstrating strong transferability and open-loop control capabilities, challenging prior assumptions about online RL scalability.

## Problem

- Online reinforcement learning (RL) was widely considered not to scale efficiently to continuous control problems involving hundreds of diverse tasks.
- Existing generalist agents primarily rely on imitation learning, which is limited by the availability of costly expert demonstrations and an inherent performance ceiling.
- A lack of comprehensive benchmarks for massively multitask online RL hindered progress in developing truly general-purpose control agents.

## Method

- Introduces MMBench, a novel benchmark comprising 200 diverse ==continuous control tasks== across 10 domains, with language instructions and expert demonstrations.
- Develops Newt, a language-conditioned, multitask world model built upon the data-efficient TD-MPC2 framework.
- Newt leverages pre-trained vision/language encoders, a self-predictive world model, and a four-pronged strategy to integrate demonstrations for efficient online learning.

## Results

- Newt achieved superior multitask performance (**0.438** normalized score at **100M** steps) and data-efficiency on MMBench compared to strong baselines like PPO and FastTD3.
- The learned world model in Newt enabled open-loop control for up to 48 steps (**16x** its training horizon), with performance often closely matching closed-loop control.
- Newt demonstrated strong transferability to 20 held-out tasks, achieving an average score of **0.868** after only 100k finetuning steps, significantly outperforming a from-scratch baseline (**0.480**).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Massively multitask online reinforcement learning is feasible and highly effective for continuous control when combined with model-based approaches, language conditioning, and strategic use of demonstrations.
> - Language instructions are crucial for task differentiation and improving performance, particularly in domains where tasks cannot be distinguished solely by observations.
> - The learned world model in Newt enables robust open-loop control over surprisingly long time horizons and facilitates rapid few-shot adaptation to unseen tasks.
