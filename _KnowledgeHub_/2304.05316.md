# OccFormer: Dual-path Transformer for Vision-based 3D Semantic Occupancy Prediction

> [!summary] Summary
> OccFormer predicts camera-based 3D semantic occupancy via a dual-path transformer encoder splitting local windowed attention and global ASPP context, plus a Mask2Former-style decoder with preserve-pooling and class-guided sampling, beating TPVFormer on SemanticKITTI and nuScenes.

## Problem

- 3D convolution for occupancy prediction uses fixed receptive fields, poorly matching varied spatial distributions of different semantic classes.
- 3D convolution's spatial invariance cannot handle sparse, discontinuous voxel features produced by camera-to-3D lifting methods.
- Tri-plane representations like TPVFormer sacrifice fine-grained semantic detail, and naive Mask2Former adaptation suffers sparsity and class imbalance in 3D.

## Method

- ==Dual-path transformer block==: local ==windowed self-attention== per BEV slice plus global ==ASPP==-processed pooled BEV feature, fused via sigmoid-weighted summation with skip connection.
- Transformer occupancy decoder adapting ==Mask2Former== with multi-scale ==3D deformable attention== pixel decoder and iterative ==masked-attention== query decoding for 3D mask classification.
- ==Preserve-pooling== (max-pooling instead of trilinear interpolation for downsampling attention masks) and ==class-guided sampling== weighting rare classes by inverse frequency.

## Results

- Outperforms MonoScene by **1.24%** mIoU (**11%** relative) on SemanticKITTI test, ranking first among monocular methods with **12.32%** SSC mIoU.
- Surpasses TPVFormer by **1.4%** mIoU on nuScenes LiDAR segmentation test set (**70.8%** vs **69.4%**) using a single joint model.
- Ablations show class-guided sampling lifts mIoU from **12.13%** to **13.46%** over uniform sampling, and dual-path fusion beats single-path and 3D convolution baselines.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decomposing 3D volume processing into parallel local and global 2D pathways can match or beat full 3D convolution/attention at lower parameter and compute cost.
> - Naively porting 2D segmentation techniques (bilinear downsampling, uniform sampling) to sparse 3D voxel supervision degrades performance; the sampling/pooling strategy must respect 3D sparsity.
> - A single model jointly trained for occupancy and LiDAR segmentation generalizes better across both tasks than task-specific models trained separately.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Broader Research Landscape

The field of 3D perception for autonomous driving has witnessed a significant evolution in its representations and methodologies. Historically, LiDAR-based systems have dominated due to their accurate explicit depth measurements. However, vision-based approaches offer compelling advantages in terms of cost-effectiveness, scalability, and generality, driving a strong research push in this direction.

Early vision-based 3D perception often relied on Bird's-Eye-View (BEV) representations. Methods like LSS and BEVFormer transform multi-view camera images into a 2D BEV feature map, suitable for tasks like 3D object detection or semantic map construction. While efficient, BEV representations inherently collapse the 3D scene into a 2D plane, losing crucial structural information along the vertical (height) dimension. This limitation restricts the holistic understanding of complex 3D environments, especially for obstacles with variable shapes or for tasks requiring fine-grained geometry.

The advent of "3D semantic occupancy prediction" (also known as 3D semantic scene completion) addresses this gap by aiming to reconstruct the surrounding 3D environment with both geometry and semantics at a voxel level. For instance, initial works like SSCNet focused on LiDAR-based inputs. The seminal "MonoScene" marked a significant step by proposing the first monocular framework for this task, utilizing a 3D UNet to process voxel features. However, conventional 3D convolutions, as used in MonoScene, suffer from limitations: they operate with fixed receptive fields, are computationally expensive, and struggle to efficiently process sparse and discontinuous 3D features generated from 2D-to-3D transformations.

More recently, "TPVFormer" introduced a tri-perspective view representation (three orthogonal planes) to describe the 3D scene. While innovative, the paper notes that the tri-plane format can be susceptible to a deficiency of fine-grained semantic information, leading to suboptimal performance compared to dense 3D features.

"OccFormer" positions itself precisely within this evolving landscape. It addresses the limitations of prior vision-based 3D semantic occupancy methods by introducing a transformer-based approach that is both efficient and capable of capturing long-range, dynamic dependencies in 3D feature volumes. By leveraging the power of transformers and adapting state-of-the-art segmentation models (Mask2Former) to the 3D domain, OccFormer seeks to overcome the drawbacks of 3D convolutions and sub-optimal 3D representations, paving the way for more accurate and comprehensive vision-only 3D scene understanding.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of "OccFormer" is to achieve **accurate and fine-grained 3D semantic occupancy prediction from multi-view camera images**. This task aims to reconstruct the entire surrounding 3D environment, predicting both the geometric presence and semantic class for each voxel in the 3D space.

The motivations behind this work are multifaceted:

1. **Enhancing 3D Scene Understanding:** Traditional BEV representations, while efficient, discard vertical structural information. A holistic understanding of a 3D scene, critical for safe and robust autonomous driving (e.g., perceiving overhanging objects, understanding road infrastructure in depth), necessitates dense 3D semantic occupancy maps.
2. **Transitioning to Vision-Only Perception:** While LiDAR-based methods excel in depth measurement, camera-based systems offer significant advantages in terms of cost, widespread availability, and richness of semantic information. The paper aims to push the boundaries of vision-based 3D perception to match or exceed LiDAR-based performance for this task.
3. **Overcoming Limitations of Existing Vision-based 3D Models:**
 - **3D Convolutions:** Previous vision-based approaches like MonoScene often rely on 3D UNets. However, 3D convolutions are computationally intensive, have fixed receptive fields (struggling with varying object scales and patterns), and are spatially invariant, making them sub-optimal for sparse and discontinuous 3D features generated from image-to-3D lifting.
 - **Alternative 3D Representations:** Approaches like TPVFormer, which use tri-plane representations, may simplify the 3D problem but can compromise fine-grained semantic detail, leading to inferior performance in dense occupancy prediction.
4. **Achieving Efficiency and Dynamic Processing:** To address the computational burden of processing large 3D voxel grids, the authors aim to develop a method for encoding 3D features that is long-range (capturing global context), dynamic (adapting receptive fields to content), and computationally efficient.
5. **Mitigating Data Challenges:** 3D semantic occupancy data is inherently sparse (most voxels are empty or unknown) and suffers from significant class imbalance (e.g., roads are abundant, while specific objects like bicycles are rare). The method must robustly handle these challenges during training and inference.

In essence, OccFormer seeks to develop a robust, efficient, and accurate vision-based framework for generating comprehensive 3D semantic occupancy maps, leveraging the strengths of transformer architectures to overcome the limitations of prior methods.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

OccFormer proposes a dual-path transformer network to effectively process 3D voxel features for semantic occupancy prediction. The overall pipeline consists of an image encoder, an image-to-3D transformation module, a dual-path transformer encoder, and a transformer occupancy decoder.

#### 3.1. Overall Pipeline

- **Image Encoder:** A standard backbone (EfficientNetB7 for SemanticKITTI, ResNet-101 for nuScenes) extracts multi-scale 2D features from multi-view camera images.
- **Image-to-3D Transformation:** Inspired by the LSS paradigm, this module lifts 2D image features into a 3D feature volume. It predicts a pixel-wise depth distribution for each camera view, which is then used to project image context features into a point cloud representation, finally voxelized into a dense 3D feature volume (e.g., 128x128x16 resolution with 128 channels).

#### 3.2. Dual-path Transformer Encoder

This is a core innovation designed to efficiently process the heavy 3D feature volumes while capturing long-range and dynamic dependencies. It decomposes the 3D processing into two parallel pathways along the horizontal plane, followed by an adaptive fusion:

- **Local Path:** Focuses on extracting fine-grained semantic structures. It processes each 2D BEV slice (across the height dimension) independently using **shared windowed self-attention**. By effectively "merging" the height dimension into the batch dimension, it allows for parallel processing across different heights while benefiting from the dynamic and long-range capabilities of attention, but with reduced quadratic complexity due to windowing.
- **Global Path:** Aims to capture scene-level semantic layouts efficiently. It first creates a single BEV feature by **average pooling along the height dimension** from the 3D feature volume. This collapsed 2D BEV feature is then processed using either the same windowed self-attention (weights shared with the local path) or, more practically, an **ASPP (Atrous Spatial Pyramid Pooling)** module within a bottleneck structure to efficiently capture global contexts across the BEV plane.
- **Adaptive Fusion:** The outputs from the local ($F_{local}$) and global ($F_{global}$) paths are fused using a sigmoid-weighted summation: $F_{out} = F_{local} + \sigma(W F_{local}) \cdot \text{unsqueeze}(F_{global}, -1)$. This allows the network to adaptively propagate scene-level information from the global path to the entire 3D volume, guided by the local features.
- **Structure:** The encoder consists of a series of these dual-path transformer blocks, with 3D convolution layers interspersed for locality and downsampling. This hybrid structure aims for efficiency by performing most attention-based reasoning in 2D, but still benefiting from 3D convolutions for hierarchical feature learning.

#### 3.3. Transformer Occupancy Decoder

OccFormer is the first to adapt the state-of-the-art mask classification model, Mask2Former, for 3D semantic occupancy prediction. It operates by predicting a set of binary 3D masks, each associated with a class label.

- **Pixel Decoder:** This component aggregates multi-scale 3D features (from the encoder) to generate high-resolution per-voxel embeddings. It uses a **multi-scale 3D deformable attention** module, tailored for 3D, to facilitate effective intra-scale and inter-scale interactions, enhancing each feature volume with multi-scale semantic information. The highest resolution feature volume is then projected into per-voxel embeddings ($E_{voxel}$).
- **Transformer Decoder:** Following Mask2Former, this part iteratively refines a set of parameterized query features. Each query attends to its corresponding foreground regions via **masked attention**. Self-attention and FFNs are applied for context exchange and feature projection. Finally, each query predicts its semantic logits and a mask embedding, which, when dot-producted with $E_{voxel}$ and passed through a sigmoid, yields the binary 3D mask.
- **Preserve-Pooling:** A crucial modification for 3D sparsity. Unlike Mask2Former's default bilinear interpolation for downsampling high-resolution mask predictions into low-resolution attention masks for the next iteration, OccFormer uses **max-pooling**. This simple change is critical for 3D data, as trilinear interpolation can dilute or remove sparse local structures (e.g., small objects), while max-pooling preserves them.
- **Class-Guided Sampling:** To combat the sparsity and extreme class imbalance in 3D occupancy data, the paper proposes a class-guided sampling method for selecting voxel positions for matching costs and losses during training. Instead of uniform sampling, voxels are sampled based on their ground-truth class frequencies, giving higher sampling weights to rarer classes ($w_c = (1/n_c)^\beta$). This ensures that foreground and minority classes are sufficiently represented in the optimization process.

#### 3.4. Loss Functions

The total loss combines:
- **Mask Classification Loss ($L_{mask-cls}$):** Computed based on bipartite matching (Hungarian algorithm) between predicted and ground-truth segments at sampled positions.
- **Depth Distribution Loss ($L_{depth}$):** Binary cross-entropy loss to supervise the intermediate depth distribution generated by the image-to-3D transformation, following BEVDepth.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

OccFormer demonstrates significant advancements in vision-based 3D semantic occupancy prediction, outperforming existing state-of-the-art methods on prominent autonomous driving datasets.

#### 4.1. Quantitative Results

- **Semantic Scene Completion on SemanticKITTI:**
 - OccFormer significantly outperforms all existing monocular methods for semantic scene completion.
 - It achieves an SSC mIoU of **12.32%** on the SemanticKITTI test set, which is a **1.24% absolute improvement (11% relative improvement)** over MonoScene (11.08% mIoU) and a **1.06% absolute improvement** over the more recent TPVFormer (11.26% mIoU).
 - OccFormer ranks first among all monocular methods on the SemanticKITTI test leaderboard for this task.
 - For class-agnostic Scene Completion (SC IoU), OccFormer also shows a slight improvement (34.53% vs. 34.25% for TPVFormer).
 - The per-class IoU results highlight improvements across many categories, particularly for challenging, smaller objects.

- **LiDAR Semantic Segmentation on nuScenes:**
 - Following TPVFormer, this task is used as a quantitative indicator for the quality of 3D semantic occupancy.
 - OccFormer achieves an mIoU of **70.8%** on the nuScenes test set, outperforming the vision-based TPVFormer (69.4% mIoU) by **1.4%**.
 - Remarkably, OccFormer's performance becomes competitive with several state-of-the-art LiDAR-based methods, despite using only multi-view camera inputs.
 - The paper notes that OccFormer uses a single model for both LiDAR segmentation and 3D semantic occupancy, unlike TPVFormer which may require separate models or have less reasonable occupancy predictions from its LiDAR segmentation model. It is also the first method to achieve 70%+ mIoU for LiDAR segmentation using only multi-view images.

#### 4.2. Ablation Studies

Ablation studies on the SemanticKITTI validation set confirm the effectiveness of OccFormer's key components:

- **Dual-path Encoder:** Both local and global pathways contribute positively to performance, demonstrating their complementary roles in capturing fine-grained structures and scene-level layouts. The dual-path transformer encoder also achieves a better performance-to-efficiency trade-off compared to vanilla 3D convolutions (3D ResNet-16) and 3D windowed attention (3D Swin-T).
- **Pixel Decoder:** The multi-scale 3D deformable attention mechanism outperforms a standard 3D FPN, highlighting its efficacy in fusing multi-level semantics and generating high-resolution voxel embeddings.
- **Transformer Decoder Adaptations:**
 - **Preserve-Pooling:** Using max-pooling instead of trilinear interpolation for downsampling attention masks yields a notable improvement of approximately 0.5 mIoU (from 11.61 to 12.13 mIoU with uniform sampling). This validates its importance for preserving sparse 3D structures.
 - **Class-Guided Sampling:** This method significantly boosts performance by almost 1 mIoU (from 12.13 to 13.46 mIoU with max-pooling), demonstrating its critical role in addressing sparsity and class imbalance, particularly for rare foreground objects.

#### 4.3. Qualitative Results

- **SemanticKITTI:** Visualizations show OccFormer's ability to better hallucinate invisible regions and achieve a more accurate scene-level semantic layout compared to MonoScene. Predicted objects like buildings are more complete and properly located, with better reasoning about interactions among neighboring semantic classes.
- **nuScenes:** OccFormer produces more contiguous, complete, and realistic 3D semantic occupancy predictions than TPVFormer. Foreground objects like cars and traffic cones have more accurate structures, and background elements like drivable surfaces are rendered more coherently. The ability of a single OccFormer model to produce both high-quality LiDAR segmentation and dense occupancy is also a key qualitative strength.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

OccFormer represents a substantial advancement in vision-based 3D perception for autonomous driving, with several significant implications:

1. **Enabling Holistic Scene Understanding:** By providing dense, fine-grained 3D semantic occupancy maps, OccFormer moves beyond sparse object detection or limited 2D BEV representations. This comprehensive understanding of the 3D environment is crucial for more robust planning, navigation, and decision-making in complex autonomous driving scenarios, including obstacle avoidance, understanding road structure, and predicting agent behavior.
2. **Driving Vision-Centric Autonomous Driving:** The paper reinforces the feasibility and increasing competitiveness of vision-only perception systems. By achieving state-of-the-art performance comparable to or exceeding LiDAR-based methods in certain aspects, OccFormer contributes to reducing the reliance on expensive LiDAR sensors, making autonomous driving technology more cost-effective and scalable for mass deployment.
3. **Advancing Transformer Applications in 3D:** OccFormer successfully adapts the powerful transformer architecture, previously dominant in 2D vision tasks, to the challenging 3D domain. Its dual-path design and clever 2D-centric processing strategy demonstrate an efficient way to handle large 3D voxel volumes, overcoming the computational bottlenecks of traditional 3D convolutions. This could inspire future research into hybrid 2D/3D transformer architectures.
4. **Robustness to Data Challenges:** The innovations in the decoder (preserve-pooling and class-guided sampling) are critical for handling the inherent sparsity and severe class imbalance of real-world 3D occupancy data. These techniques make the training process more stable and effective, leading to better predictions for rare but safety-critical objects.
5. **Multi-Task Capability:** The ability of a single OccFormer model to generate both high-quality LiDAR semantic segmentation and dense 3D semantic occupancy predictions streamlines the perception pipeline, offering a unified and efficient solution for multiple downstream tasks.

In conclusion, OccFormer pushes the boundaries of vision-based 3D perception, demonstrating that camera inputs alone can provide a rich, detailed, and robust understanding of the surrounding environment. Its efficiency and accuracy make it a promising component for future autonomous driving systems, accelerating the transition towards more capable and economically viable self-driving solutions.
