# PhysGaia: A Physics-Aware Benchmark with Multi-Body Interactions for Dynamic Novel View Synthesis

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Seoul National University introduce PhysGaia, a physics-aware dataset for Dynamic Novel View Synthesis (DyNVS) that includes complex multi-body interactions and comprehensive ground-truth physics parameters across various material types. Evaluation on PhysGaia reveals that current state-of-the-art DyNVS models largely fail to achieve physical realism, despite efforts toward photorealism.

## Problem

- Existing Dynamic Novel View Synthesis (DyNVS) datasets primarily focus on photorealism and lack crucial information about underlying physical dynamics, multi-body interactions, and ground-truth physics parameters.
- A critical gap exists in datasets specifically designed to enable physics-aware modeling for DyNVS, particularly for complex scenarios involving diverse materials like liquids, gases, viscoelastic substances, and textiles.
- Current DyNVS models lack the ability to reason about how scenes behave physically, hindering the quantitative evaluation of physical realism without access to ground-truth 3D trajectories and material properties.

## Method

- The **PhysGaia** dataset was constructed using **SideFX** Houdini 20.5, offering 17 distinct scenes with complex multi-body interactions across four material types: liquids, gases, viscoelastic substances, and textiles.
- It provides comprehensive ground-truth physical data, including 3D particle trajectories and specific physics parameters (e.g., viscosity, Young's modulus, temperature) for each material.
- Material-specific physics solvers (Fluid-Implicit Particle for liquids, Pyro for gases, ==Material Point Method== for viscoelastic substances, and Vellum for textiles) were employed to ensure strict adherence to physical laws and generate realistic dynamic behaviors.

## Results

- State-of-the-art DyNVS algorithms (D-3DGS, 4DGS, STG, SOM) achieved low PSNR scores (frequently below 30) on PhysGaia, even with multiview inputs, indicating significant challenges in reconstructing complex multi-body dynamics.
- Qualitative analysis showed that evaluated methods struggled to accurately capture multi-body interactions, often exhibiting visual artifacts such as 'needle-like artifacts' and 'under-reconstruction of dynamic regions'.
- A fundamental limitation was identified where current DyNVS models failed to reconstruct physically reliable motions, instead producing only localized fluctuations of Gaussian primitives or erratic movements, especially in scenes with fluids or gases.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Achieving photorealism through RGB reconstruction loss in DyNVS does not inherently guarantee physical realism or an accurate understanding of underlying physical dynamics.
> - Current state-of-the-art DyNVS methods primarily reconstruct localized visual fluctuations or erratic motion of primitives rather than capturing and representing physically reliable and consistent motions.
> - Comprehensive datasets that include ground-truth 3D trajectories and precise physics parameters are essential for driving progress in physics-aware dynamic scene reconstruction and enabling quantitative evaluation of physical reasoning in models.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in "PhysGaia" addresses a recognized challenge in the field of Dynamic Novel View Synthesis (DyNVS), particularly the integration of physical realism into scene reconstruction. DyNVS, a rapidly evolving area following the advent of Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF), aims to reconstruct complex 4D spatiotemporal scenes from video inputs to enable photorealistic image synthesis from novel viewpoints and time steps.

Initially, DyNVS research focused on achieving photorealistic appearance from multi-view or monocular video inputs. Existing benchmarks for DyNVS have primarily supported this objective by providing training videos, with varying degrees of object motion complexity or camera setups (e.g., fixed multi-view, handheld monocular). Recent advancements, such as the extension of 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) to 4D scenes (4DGS), have further enhanced photorealistic rendering capabilities.

A subsequent direction in DyNVS involves incorporating physical laws to govern motion, moving beyond mere visual fidelity to enable models to reason about scene behavior. Pioneering works have started to integrate differentiable physics simulations into 4DGS frameworks. However, a significant limitation of existing efforts in physics-aware DyNVS is their confinement to simplified scenarios, often involving single objects with homogeneous materials, predominantly rheological substances. Real-world videos, while capturing complex dynamics, lack the necessary ground-truth physical information (e.g., 3D trajectories, material parameters) for quantitative evaluation of physical realism.

Existing physics-simulated 4D datasets, while providing ground-truth physics, often exhibit limitations in terms of material diversity, complexity of multi-object interactions, or the richness of dynamic behaviors. For instance, some datasets support multi-object interactions but are restricted to specific materials (e.g., textiles in clothed human datasets), while others offer physics parameters and 3D trajectories but are confined to single-material settings (e.g., highly viscous liquids or viscoelastic materials).

PhysGaia positions itself as a benchmark designed to address these gaps by providing a physics-aware dataset featuring complex multi-body interactions across a diverse range of physical materials, coupled with comprehensive ground-truth physical information. This aims to facilitate the development and evaluation of DyNVS models that can achieve both photorealism and physical consistency in complex dynamic environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of PhysGaia is to introduce a novel physics-aware benchmark for Dynamic Novel View Synthesis (DyNVS) that facilitates the development and evaluation of models capable of physics-consistent dynamic reconstruction, extending beyond traditional photorealism.

The motivation for creating PhysGaia stems from several limitations observed in the existing research landscape:

- **Limited Physical Realism in DyNVS:** Current DyNVS datasets and methods largely prioritize photorealistic appearance. While models can render how scenes look, there is a recognized gap in their ability to reason about how scenes physically behave. This limits the application of DyNVS in scenarios requiring physical understanding, such as immersive AR/VR applications where interactive and physically plausible responses are crucial.
- **Lack of Complex Multi-Body Interactions:** Existing physics-aware DyNVS research often focuses on simplified scenarios involving isolated objects with a single material type. Complex environments characterized by interactions among multiple objects and diverse physical materials (e.g., liquids, gases, textiles, rheological substances) remain largely unexplored. Modeling such intricate interactions presents significant challenges for current DyNVS approaches.
- **Absence of Ground-Truth Physical Information:** While real-world videos can capture complex dynamic scenes, they inherently lack the underlying ground-truth physical data, such as 3D particle trajectories and precise material parameters. This absence makes rigorous, quantitative evaluation of physical realism in reconstructed scenes challenging or impossible. A benchmark that provides this data is essential for objectively assessing physics-aware models.
- **Narrow Coverage of Materials and Dynamics in Physics-Simulated Datasets:** Existing 4D datasets generated via physics simulation, while providing some ground truth, often have limitations. These include a narrow range of physical materials, oversimplified dynamics, or a lack of rich multi-object interactions. This restricts the generalizability and applicability of models trained on such datasets to real-world complexity.
- **Need for Physics-Aware Evaluation Metrics:** To advance physics-consistent reconstruction, new evaluation metrics are needed that can quantify how well reconstructed scene dynamics align with fundamental physical laws and ground-truth motion, rather than solely relying on image-based metrics.

By addressing these limitations, PhysGaia aims to accelerate research in several directions: developing DyNVS models that can perform faithful, physics-consistent dynamic reconstruction; advancing physics-based scene understanding; and fostering the integration of deep learning with advanced physical simulation techniques.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

PhysGaia is constructed as a physics-aware benchmark designed to provide comprehensive data for evaluating dynamic novel view synthesis and physics-based scene understanding. The methodology involves generating scenes using material-specific physics solvers and offering extensive ground-truth information, along with supporting infrastructure for research adoption.

#### 3.1. Benchmark Properties

- **Complex Scenarios with Physics-Aware Dynamics:** The benchmark comprises 17 scenes featuring multi-object interactions across four diverse material types: liquids, gases, rheological materials, and textiles. To ensure physical fidelity and accurate force exchange, material-specific physics solvers are employed:
 - **Liquids:** Fluid-Implicit Particle (FLIP) solver, a hybrid particle-grid method, suitable for incompressible fluids and capturing phenomena like splashing.
 - **Gases (Smoke):** Pyro solver, which accurately models temperature fields crucial for buoyancy effects and adheres to Navier-Stokes equations for fluid mechanics.
 - **Rheological Substances (e.g., snow, jelly):** Material Point Method (MPM), an extension of FLIP, designed for chunk-based materials, capturing deformation and internal force propagation.
 - **Textiles (e.g., cloth):** Vellum solver, based on Extended Position Based Dynamics (XPBD), which handles deformable objects by iteratively projecting particle positions to satisfy geometric constraints like stretch and bend.
 These solvers are integrated within SideFX Houdini 20.5, ensuring consistent multi-body interactions under uniform boundary conditions. The scenes capture various physical phenomena beyond multi-body collisions, including non-locally rigid motion, specular reflection, and refraction.

- **Providing Physics Parameters and 3D Trajectories:** Unlike real-world video datasets, PhysGaia, being simulated, offers complete access to underlying physical information. This includes 3D particle trajectories and physics parameters such as viscosity, Young's modulus, Poisson's ratio, and temperature (for gas scenes). This ground-truth data is crucial for quantitative evaluation of physical reasoning and inverse physics problems.

- **Supporting Diverse DyNVS Tasks:** The benchmark supports both multi-view and monocular DyNVS tasks. Training data can be configured from full sequences (multi-view) or single sequences (monocular). Evaluation is performed using two static cameras with a large baseline to ensure diverse viewpoints. Camera trajectories for training are typically spirals, with 2 or 4 cameras depending on the scene's 180° or 360° nature.

- **Customizability:** Complete source files, including physics solvers, source geometries, materials, and texture controls, are provided. This allows researchers to generate customized datasets with higher resolution imagery and diverse modalities (e.g., depth maps, surface normals, relighted scenes) to suit specific downstream tasks.

- **Accessibility:** PhysGaia includes integration pipelines for state-of-the-art 4D Gaussian Splatting models and provides COLMAP-reconstructed point clouds for each scene. This aims to facilitate adoption by the research community.

#### 3.2. Metrics for Physical Realism

To quantitatively evaluate physical realism, the paper introduces two metrics that assess how well the motions of Gaussian primitives in 4DGS models align with ground-truth physical trajectories:

- **Trajectory Distance (TD):** Measures the average Euclidean distance between matched reconstructed primitive trajectories and ground-truth trajectories over all frames. Lower TD values indicate closer alignment and higher physical realism.
- **Area Under the Outlier Percentages (AUOP):** A percentage-based metric that captures outlier behavior over time. An outlier is defined as a primitive whose trajectory deviation from the ground truth exceeds a specified threshold, and once marked, it remains an outlier. AUOP quantifies the area under the curve of outlier percentages over time. Lower AUOP values reflect better compliance with ground-truth physics.

These metrics provide complementary perspectives on trajectory accuracy and the persistence of deviations, enabling a robust evaluation of physical realism.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The empirical analysis conducted with PhysGaia highlights its unique properties as a benchmark and exposes limitations of existing DyNVS approaches in achieving physical realism.

#### 4.1. Comparison with Physics-based Benchmarks

- **Motion Complexity:** PhysGaia demonstrates superior motion complexity compared to existing physics-based benchmarks (e.g., ScalarFlow, PAC-NeRF, Spring-Gaus). Using the dynamic score metric, PhysGaia achieved a score of 0.444, which was higher than ScalarFlow (0.391) and Spring-Gaus (0.372). This indicates its capacity to capture more intricate multi-body interactions.
- **Photorealism:** PhysGaia also exhibits higher visual fidelity. When evaluated using FID (Frechet Inception Distance) and KID (Kernel Inception Distance) by flattening video sequences into image sets, PhysGaia yielded the lowest scores (FID: 207.8, KID: 0.118) compared to ScalarFlow (FID: 293.5, KID: 0.255), PAC-NeRF (FID: 242.6, KID: 0.164), and Spring-Gaus (FID: 261.8, KID: 0.171). These results suggest that PhysGaia effectively integrates physical accuracy with high photorealism and complex motion dynamics.

#### 4.2. Analysis on Dynamic Novel View Synthesis (DyNVS)

Several recent DyNVS methods (D-3DGS, 4DGS, STG, MoSca, SoM) were evaluated on PhysGaia for both photorealism and physical realism.

##### 4.2.1. Evaluation of Photorealism

- **Performance Challenges:** Existing methods struggle to achieve high photorealism on PhysGaia, particularly in scenes with complex multi-object interactions. Average PSNR scores across all 17 scenes were below 30 even for multi-view setups (e.g., D-3DGS: 24.2, 4DGS: 24.4), and lower for monocular setups (e.g., D-3DGS: 21.7, 4DGS: 22.7). This contrasts with their performance on simpler DyNVS benchmarks.
- **Motion Complexity Impact:** This difficulty is attributed to the high motion complexity of PhysGaia, evidenced by its high dynamic score compared to other DyNVS datasets like Nerfies, DNeRF, HyperNeRF, and DyCheck.
- **Method-Specific Limitations:**
 - MLP- and grid-based representations (D-3DGS, 4DGS) showed limitations in capturing fine-grained dynamics such as fluid splashing.
 - Polynomial motion models (STG) were found to be too restrictive for complex trajectories.
 - Motion factorization or As-Rigid-As-Possible (ARAP) based constraints (MoSca, SoM) broke down under rich multi-object interactions.
- **Qualitative Observations:** Qualitative results showed that methods often produce needle-like artifacts and fail to accurately reconstruct dynamic elements, especially in scenes like "jelly party."

##### 4.2.2. Evaluation of Physical Realism

- **Discrepancy between Photorealism and Physical Plausibility:** While methods might achieve reasonable photorealism, they often fail to ensure physical plausibility, particularly in highly dynamic scenes.
- **Trajectory Distance (TD) and Area Under Outlier Percentages (AUOP):**
 - In a less dynamic scene ("cow"), methods showed relatively low TD (e.g., D-3DGS: 0.07, STG: 0.30) and AUOP (e.g., D-3DGS: 0.0, STG: 0.7), comparable to the oracle performance (TD: 0.03, AUOP: 0.0).
 - However, in a highly dynamic scene ("box-smoke"), TD and AUOP values significantly increased (e.g., D-3DGS: TD 4.01, AUOP 37.9; 4DGS: TD 6.00, AUOP 68.8), indicating poor alignment with ground-truth physical flows. The oracle achieved TD 0.84 and AUOP 0.3, highlighting the substantial gap.
- **Qualitative Observations of Flow:** Reconstructed flows frequently deviated from ground truth. Gaussian primitives often merely fluctuated near the surface rather than following actual physical motion. Outlier percentages increased rapidly over time in dynamic scenes, underscoring the inability of current methods to track physically plausible flows.

#### 4.3. Analysis on Inverse-physics Problems

- **Underestimation of Material Stiffness:** PhysGaia was used to evaluate inverse physics problems, specifically the estimation of Young's modulus (E) and Poisson's ratio (ν). Both PAC-NeRF and GIC methods were tested on a "bouncing balls" scene.
- **Limitations in Multi-Object Scenarios:** Both methods significantly underestimated Young's modulus (PAC-NeRF: 1.7 × 10³ vs. GT 100.0 × 10³; GIC: 23.4 × 10³ vs. GT 100.0 × 10³). This behavior is attributed to these methods being designed for single-object scenarios. When applied to multi-object scenes with complex interactions, the algorithms favored lower E values, allowing greater deformation to fit observations. This finding suggests that PhysGaia exposes underexplored challenges in multi-object physics parameter estimation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

PhysGaia introduces a physics-aware benchmark designed to address key limitations in Dynamic Novel View Synthesis (DyNVS) and related fields. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Advancing Physics-Consistent DyNVS:** By providing a dataset with rich multi-body interactions across diverse materials (liquids, gases, rheological materials, textiles) and comprehensive ground-truth physical information (3D trajectories, material parameters), PhysGaia enables the development and rigorous evaluation of DyNVS models that aim for physical realism in addition to photorealism. This moves the field beyond purely appearance-driven reconstruction towards models that understand and predict physical behavior.
- **Facilitating Research in Physics-Based Scene Understanding:** The ground-truth physics parameters (e.g., viscosity, Young's modulus, temperature fields) allow for the quantitative evaluation of inverse physics estimation methods. This supports research in inferring material properties from visual observations, which is crucial for building intelligent systems that interact with dynamic environments.
- **Enabling Multi-Body Interaction Modeling:** Current physics-aware DyNVS often simplifies scenes to single objects or materials. PhysGaia's focus on complex multi-object interactions, including force exchange and deformation during contact, is expected to drive research in multi-physics modeling and adaptive representations for hybrid scenes.
- **Guiding Integration of Material-Specific Physics Solvers:** The benchmark's construction using distinct, material-specific physics solvers (FLIP, Pyro, MPM, Vellum) highlights that different physical phenomena are best captured by specialized approaches. This can guide researchers in integrating more appropriate solvers into particle-based frameworks like Gaussian splatting, encouraging exploration of volumetric solvers for phenomena like smoke.
- **Exposing Limitations of Current DyNVS Methods:** The empirical analysis on PhysGaia reveals that existing state-of-the-art DyNVS methods, while capable of photorealism in simpler settings, demonstrate fundamental limitations in capturing physical realism and handling complex multi-object interactions. This identification of weaknesses provides clear directions for future research and model development.
- **Promoting Robust Evaluation Metrics:** The introduction of Trajectory Distance (TD) and Area Under the Outlier Percentages (AUOP) provides new, quantitative metrics specifically tailored to evaluate the physical plausibility of reconstructed flows, complementing traditional image quality metrics.
- **Supporting Diverse Downstream Tasks:** The customizability of the dataset, with provided source files and the ability to generate various modalities (depth, normals, relighted scenes), offers flexibility for researchers to adapt PhysGaia to a broad range of downstream tasks, including high-resolution imagery generation.
- **Foundation for Future AR/VR Applications:** Progress in physically plausible 4D reconstruction, spurred by PhysGaia, is relevant for advancements in immersive and interactive AR/VR applications that require realistic object behavior and user interaction.

While PhysGaia relies on physics simulators, inheriting their inherent approximations, it represents a structured approach to providing physically consistent supervision.
