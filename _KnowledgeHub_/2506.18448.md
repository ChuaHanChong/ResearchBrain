# GraspMAS: Zero-Shot Language-driven Grasp Detection with Multi-Agent System

> [!summary] Summary
> GraspMAS introduces a multi-agent system for zero-shot language-driven robotic grasp detection that interprets complex natural language queries and achieves precise grasp poses without task-specific training. The framework leverages pre-trained Large Language Models and Vision-Language Models within a collaborative architecture, outperforming state-of-the-art baselines on benchmark datasets and demonstrating robust performance in simulated and real-world robotic environments.

## Problem

- Existing language-driven grasp detection methods often struggle with compositional reasoning needed for complex natural language instructions and operate in cluttered environments.
- Most approaches require extensive, resource-intensive training on domain-specific datasets, limiting their zero-shot generalization to novel objects or unseen scenarios.
- Monolithic neural architectures lack transparency and adaptability for nuanced decision-making, sometimes proposing infeasible grasp poses without considering practical constraints.

## Method

- **GraspMAS** employs a multi-agent system framework consisting of a Planner (GPT-4) for strategic reasoning, a Coder for converting plans to executable Python code, and an Observer (GPT-4o) for monitoring execution and providing feedback.
- The system utilizes a minimal toolset of nine Python functions, each powered by off-the-shelf foundation models (e.g., GrDINO, VLPart, RAGT-3/3, BLIP2, SAM) for specific perception and action tasks.
- An iterative, closed-loop interaction among the agents allows for dynamic adaptation and self-correction, enabling the Planner to refine strategies based on Observer feedback until a feasible grasp pose is determined.

## Results

- GraspMAS achieved success rates of 0.62 on OCID-VLG and 0.68 on GraspAnything++, surpassing all baseline zero-shot and even several supervised methods by significant margins (e.g., +0.19 over the next best compositional baseline on OCID-VLG).
- In simulated robot experiments (Maniskill), GraspMAS recorded success rates of 0.82 for single objects and 0.72 in cluttered scenarios, outperforming baselines.
- Real-world robot experiments with a Kinova Gen3 robot demonstrated success rates of 0.80 for single objects and 0.76 in cluttered setups, proving practical robustness for complex language queries and environments.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A modular, plug-and-play multi-agent architecture effectively integrates the distinct capabilities of various foundation models for complex, multi-step robotic tasks.
> - Iterative reasoning and feedback loops, facilitated by a multi-modal Observer, enable the system to robustly handle ambiguities, verify physical feasibility, and prevent risky actions.
> - Leveraging pre-trained foundation models allows for powerful zero-shot generalization, enabling language-driven grasp detection in novel environments and with unseen objects without additional training.
