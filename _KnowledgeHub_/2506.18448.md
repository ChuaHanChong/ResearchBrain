# GraspMAS: Zero-Shot Language-driven Grasp Detection with Multi-Agent System

> [!summary] Summary
> GraspMAS introduces a multi-agent system for zero-shot language-driven robotic grasp detection that interprets complex natural language queries and achieves precise grasp poses without task-specific training. The framework leverages pre-trained Large Language Models and Vision-Language Models within a collaborative architecture, outperforming state-of-the-art baselines on benchmark datasets and demonstrating robust performance in simulated and real-world robotic environments.

## Problem

- Existing language-driven grasp detection methods often struggle with compositional reasoning needed for complex natural language instructions and operate in cluttered environments.
- Most approaches require extensive, resource-intensive training on domain-specific datasets, limiting their zero-shot generalization to novel objects or unseen scenarios.
- Monolithic neural architectures lack transparency and adaptability for nuanced decision-making, sometimes proposing infeasible grasp poses without considering practical constraints.

## Method

- **GraspMAS** employs a multi-agent system framework consisting of a Planner (GPT-4) for strategic reasoning, a Coder for converting plans to executable Python code, and an Observer (GPT-4o) for monitoring execution and providing feedback.
- The system utilizes a minimal toolset of nine Python functions, each powered by off-the-shelf foundation models (e.g., GrDINO, VLPart, RAGT-3/3, BLIP2, SAM) for specific perception and action tasks.
- An iterative, closed-loop interaction among the agents allows for dynamic adaptation and self-correction, enabling the Planner to refine strategies based on Observer feedback until a feasible grasp pose is determined.

## Results

- GraspMAS achieved success rates of 0.62 on OCID-VLG and 0.68 on GraspAnything++, surpassing all baseline zero-shot and even several supervised methods by significant margins (e.g., +0.19 over the next best compositional baseline on OCID-VLG).
- In simulated robot experiments (Maniskill), GraspMAS recorded success rates of 0.82 for single objects and 0.72 in cluttered scenarios, outperforming baselines.
- Real-world robot experiments with a Kinova Gen3 robot demonstrated success rates of 0.80 for single objects and 0.76 in cluttered setups, proving practical robustness for complex language queries and environments.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A modular, plug-and-play multi-agent architecture effectively integrates the distinct capabilities of various foundation models for complex, multi-step robotic tasks.
> - Iterative reasoning and feedback loops, facilitated by a multi-modal Observer, enable the system to robustly handle ambiguities, verify physical feasibility, and prevent risky actions.
> - Leveraging pre-trained foundation models allows for powerful zero-shot generalization, enabling language-driven grasp detection in novel environments and with unseen objects without additional training.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Grasp detection is a fundamental challenge in robotic manipulation, traditionally focusing on identifying stable grasp configurations based on object geometry and physical properties. Recent advancements have shifted towards integrating natural language understanding into grasp detection systems to facilitate more intuitive and flexible human-robot interaction. This paradigm shift enables robots to execute tasks based on high-level, context-aware instructions rather than predefined visual targets.

The emergence of large-scale foundation models, including Large Language Models (LLMs) and Vision-Language Models (VLMs), has significantly propelled research in this area, fostering zero-shot approaches for various robotic tasks such as planning, manipulation, and navigation. However, existing language-driven grasp detection methods often encounter limitations. Many rely on end-to-end neural architectures that require extensive, resource-intensive training on large, domain-specific datasets. This training dependency limits their adaptability and generalization capabilities when confronted with unseen objects or novel real-world scenarios. Furthermore, these monolithic models often struggle with complex linguistic queries that demand compositional reasoning, such as interpreting spatial relationships (e.g., "second bottle from the left") or inferring functional object properties (e.g., "something to cut").

To address these challenges, multi-agent systems have gained traction, offering a modular and compositional approach to reasoning and decision-making. By integrating LLMs as planners or reasoning engines and VLMs for robust visual perception, these systems can perform complex tasks with minimal fine-tuning, thereby enhancing cross-domain generalization. This paper positions GraspMAS within this evolving landscape as a zero-shot, training-free, and plug-and-play multi-agent framework. It aims to bridge the gap between semantic language understanding and precise physical robotic execution by leveraging the strengths of existing foundation models within a collaborative agent architecture, thereby improving robustness and adaptability in diverse real-world settings.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this work is to develop a robust and adaptable system for language-driven grasp detection that can interpret complex natural language commands and execute grasping tasks in diverse, potentially cluttered, real-world environments without requiring domain-specific training or fine-tuning.

This overarching objective is motivated by several key challenges identified in the existing research landscape:

1. **Interpretation of Complex Language and Cluttered Environments:** Current language-driven grasp detection methods frequently struggle with the compositional reasoning required to interpret nuanced or ambiguous natural language instructions. Commands involving spatial relationships, ordinal positions, directional references, or inferences about object functionality often lead to ineffective operation. This difficulty is exacerbated in densely cluttered environments, where differentiating and localizing specific objects based on linguistic cues becomes more complex. The motivation is to enable robots to understand and execute commands like "Grasp the second bottle from the left" or "I need something to cut," which require advanced semantic and spatial reasoning.

2. **Lack of Zero-Shot Generalization:** Most existing approaches necessitate a training or fine-tuning phase to adapt to new domains or unseen data. This reliance on extensive datasets and computational resources limits their scalability and practical applicability in dynamic real-world scenarios where robots encounter novel objects or environments frequently. A core motivation is to develop a system that can immediately operate in new situations without prior exposure or additional learning, thus overcoming the limitations of resource-intensive, dataset-dependent training.

3. **Enhancing Decision-Making and Robustness:** Existing end-to-end monolithic neural architectures often lack the transparency and adaptability needed for nuanced decision-making. They may produce grasp poses without considering practical feasibility constraints, such as the robot's workspace or the potential for object damage. The motivation is to introduce a framework that can reason through ambiguities, evaluate intermediate outcomes, and refine its strategy iteratively, thereby improving the reliability and safety of grasp detection in real-world applications. This includes ensuring that the robot grasps a plant by its pot rather than its stem to avoid damage, or verifying an object's reachability before attempting a grasp.

4. **Leveraging Foundation Models:** Recent advances in large language and vision models have demonstrated significant capabilities in understanding and generating human-like text and interpreting visual information. The motivation is to effectively integrate these powerful pre-trained models as modular tools within a system, rather than training an entirely new architecture. This approach aims to capitalize on the inherent knowledge and reasoning abilities of foundation models to enhance grasp detection performance and flexibility.

By addressing these motivations, GraspMAS seeks to advance human-robot interaction by providing robots with a more intelligent, adaptable, and practical means of understanding and executing grasping commands.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

GraspMAS is proposed as a multi-agent system framework for zero-shot language-driven grasp detection. It operates on an input RGB image and a free-form text query, aiming to identify a feasible grasping pose represented by five parameters: center coordinates (x, y), width (w), height (h), and rotational angle ($\theta$). The framework is built on a closed-loop interaction among three specialized agents: the Planner, the Coder, and the Observer.

**A. Overview of the GraspMAS Framework**

The system workflow begins with the **Planner** agent, which processes the user's query and visual information. The **Coder** agent then translates the Planner's instructions into executable code. During execution, intermediate results and potential errors are monitored by the **Observer** agent. The Observer provides feedback to the Planner, initiating an iterative refinement loop until a robust and feasible grasp pose is determined.

**B. Planner Agent**

The Planner agent, implemented using GPT-4, functions as the central reasoning component of GraspMAS. Its primary responsibility is to develop a detailed grasping strategy. It receives three types of input:
- **Instructions:** These define the Planner's role as an expert planning agent, specify its objective (locate the query object, compute grasp pose, verify feasibility), provide descriptions of available tools, and include in-context examples to guide plan generation.
- **User Inputs:** Both an RGB image and a text query are provided to the Planner. This multimodal input allows the Planner to identify key visual features and specific elements relevant to the query.
- **Feedback from Observer Agent:** Textual feedback from the Observer, summarizing intermediate results (e.g., object crops, proposed grasp poses) and error logs, informs the Planner. This feedback is integrated with the initial instructions to refine subsequent plans.

The Planner handles two main subtasks:
1. **Determining the grasp pose:** This involves resolving spatial relationships and text ambiguities through multiple iterations. The Planner ensures the generated grasp pose is physically feasible, preventing actions that could damage objects (e.g., grasping the pot of a plant instead of its stem).
2. **Verifying workspace feasibility:** Unlike methods that only compute grasp poses, the Planner explicitly confirms that the target object is within the robot's physical reach before execution, enhancing practical applicability.

**C. Coder Agent**

The Coder agent serves as the operational component, converting the Planner's high-level instructions into executable Python code. It receives detailed plan instructions from the Planner, embedded within a structured prompt that also provides guiding text, a set of predefined Python functional tools, and examples of their correct usage.

The generated Python code is executed by a Python interpreter. Any variable values, processed images, and error logs are transmitted to the Observer. A notable design aspect is that code execution errors do not halt the process; instead, error logs are passed to the Observer for summarization and feedback to the Planner, allowing for dynamic adaptation and self-correction.

The system employs a minimal yet sufficient toolset, comprising nine core Python functions, each leveraging existing foundation models for specific tasks:
- `find(object_name)`: Detects objects based on their name, utilizing GrDINO.
- `find_part(object_name, part_name)`: Detects a specific part of an object, using VLPart.
- `grasp_detection(object_patch)`: Calculates a grasp pose for a target object patch, using RAGT-3/3.
- `exists(image, text)`: Checks for the presence of an object in an image, utilizing BLIP2.
- `verify_property(image, text)`: Verifies object properties (e.g., color, shape), using BLIP2.
- `best_image_match(option_list)`: Returns the image patch most likely to contain specified content, using BLIP2.
- `compute_depth(image)`: Returns the median depth of an image, using MiDaS.
- `masks(object_name)`: Returns the mask of an object, using SAM.
- `llm_query(object_name, part_name)`: Queries external information about the image, using GPT-4.

**D. Observer Agent**

The Observer agent, implemented using GPT-4o, enhances the system's robustness by managing execution results and providing critical feedback to the Planner. It is a multi-modal agent that processes two types of input:
- **Visualized Images:** Intermediate outputs from the Coder, such as cropped object images or visualized grasp pose rectangles.
- **Code Execution Error Logs:** Any errors encountered during the Coder's execution.

Based on these inputs, the Observer performs reasoning to generate textual feedback for the Planner. This feedback explicitly evaluates the generated grasp pose for potential risks, such as object damage, human harm, or collision likelihood. This iterative feedback mechanism allows the Planner to adjust its plans, leading to the generation of valid and feasible grasp poses over one or more reasoning loops.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The effectiveness of GraspMAS was evaluated through extensive experiments across benchmark datasets, simulated environments, and real-world robotic setups.

**A. Zero-shot Language-driven Grasp Detection**
- **Datasets:** Experiments were conducted on GraspAnything++ (1 million images, 3 million objects) and OCID-VLG (1,763 scenes, 31 graspable objects in cluttered environments).
- **Metrics:** Success rate (IoU > 0.25, orientation difference < 30 degrees), Harmonic Mean (for GraspAnything++), and inference time.
- **Baselines:** End-to-end models (GroundingDINO + RAGT, OWLv2 + RAGT, QWEN2-VL + RAGT) and compositional approaches (OWG, ViperGPT). ViperGPT was equipped with the same toolset as GraspMAS for a fair comparison.
- **Results:** GraspMAS demonstrated a notable performance improvement, achieving success rates of 0.62 on OCID-VLG and 0.68 on GraspAnything++. This significantly outperformed all baseline approaches, with the highest margins being +0.19 and +0.11 respectively compared to the next best compositional baseline (ViperGPT). The study also observed that compositional approaches generally yielded higher performance than end-to-end methods. GraspMAS exhibited a longer inference time (2.12 seconds) compared to end-to-end methods (e.g., 0.32-1.13 seconds) and some compositional methods, attributed to the iterative reasoning across multiple agents.

**B. Comparison with Supervised Methods**
- **Baselines:** Supervised methods trained on GraspAnything++ were included: CLIP-Fusion, CLIPort, LGD, MaskGrasp, LLGD, GraspMamba, and GraspSAM.
- **Results:** Despite being a zero-shot method, GraspMAS achieved a success rate of 0.68 on GraspAnything++, surpassing all supervised baselines. The closest supervised method, GraspSAM, achieved 0.63. This indicates the strong generalization capability of GraspMAS without explicit task-specific training.

**C. Qualitative Results**
- GraspMAS successfully generated plausible grasp poses even in cluttered scenes and when interpreting complex text prompts, as illustrated by visual comparisons. In contrast, end-to-end detection models (OWLv2, GroundingDINO, QWEN2-VL) struggled with complex prompts, and other compositional methods (OWG, ViperGPT) exhibited limitations in reasoning and semantic understanding.
- **In-the-Wild Results:** GraspMAS demonstrated the ability to handle ambiguous text queries (e.g., reinterpreting "Kleenex" as "blue box" using external knowledge) and performed task-oriented grasp detection by aligning grasp poses with specified actions (e.g., grasping a knife by the handle for cutting, or by the blade for safe hand-over).

**D. Robot Experiments in Simulation**
- **Setup:** Experiments were conducted in the Maniskill simulator using a Franka Emika Panda robot with 65 YCB dataset objects in both single-object and cluttered scenarios (5-15 objects).
- **Results:** GraspMAS achieved success rates of 0.82 in single-object scenarios and 0.72 in cluttered scenarios, outperforming all baseline approaches. The closest compositional baseline, ViperGPT, achieved 0.70 and 0.62 in respective scenarios.

**E. Real Robot Experiment**
- **Setup:** A Kinova Gen3 7-DoF robot equipped with an Intel RealSense D410 camera was used. The system estimated 5-DoF grasp poses, converted them to 6-DoF by assuming flat surfaces, and guided the robot via trajectory optimization. Experiments involved single objects and cluttered arrangements, with 25 repetitions per experiment.
- **Results:** GraspMAS achieved success rates of 0.80 in single-object setups and 0.76 in cluttered setups. This performance significantly surpassed state-of-the-art end-to-end methods (e.g., GraspMamba at 0.54 and 0.52) and compositional methods (ViperGPT at 0.64 and 0.52) in real-world scenarios.

**F. Discussion of Limitations**
- **Inference Time:** The multi-agent reasoning process of GraspMAS results in a relatively slow inference time, which may not be suitable for industrial or time-critical applications requiring rapid responses.
- **Densely Cluttered Environments:** The method encountered difficulties in environments with significant object overlap, where ambiguities become challenging to resolve effectively.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

GraspMAS represents a significant contribution to the field of language-driven robotic grasp detection. Its introduction of a multi-agent system framework addresses several limitations of prior approaches, particularly concerning compositional reasoning and generalization capabilities.

The primary significance lies in its **zero-shot ability** to interpret complex natural language queries and derive precise grasp poses without requiring task-specific training or fine-tuning. This enhances the adaptability and scalability of robotic systems, enabling them to operate in diverse, unseen environments and interact with novel objects immediately. The ability to grasp objects based on indirect, spatial, or functional language commands signifies a step towards more intuitive and flexible **human-robot interaction**, moving beyond simple object recognition to true contextual understanding.

The **modular and compositional design** of GraspMAS, which leverages existing foundation models as plug-and-play tools (e.g., GrDINO for detection, VLPart for part segmentation, RAGT-3/3 for grasp detection, GPT-4 for planning), is a key strength. This architecture allows for seamless integration of future advancements in individual foundation models, meaning improvements in underlying tools directly enhance the overall performance of the GraspMAS pipeline. This reduces the need for constant re-training of an entire system, promoting efficiency in research and development.

The extensive validation across **benchmark datasets, simulation, and real-world robotic experiments** provides empirical evidence of GraspMAS's effectiveness and robustness. Outperforming both zero-shot baselines and several supervised methods on large-scale datasets underscores its strong generalization ability. Its success in physical robot experiments, particularly in cluttered environments, indicates its potential for practical deployment in complex and dynamic real-world scenarios, such as manufacturing, logistics, or assistive robotics.

While limitations such as inference speed and performance in extremely dense clutter are acknowledged, the GraspMAS framework provides a clear direction for future research. Potential impacts include accelerating the development of more intelligent and autonomous robotic manipulation systems, fostering greater accessibility of advanced robotic capabilities for new applications, and setting a precedent for leveraging collaborative AI agents in complex perception and action tasks in robotics.
