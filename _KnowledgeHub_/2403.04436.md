# Learning Human-to-Humanoid Real-Time Whole-Body Teleoperation

> [!summary] Summary
> The Human to Humanoid (H2O) framework introduces a method for real-time whole-body teleoperation of full-sized humanoid robots using only RGB camera input, enabling operators to control complex dynamic movements through natural human actions. This system demonstrates robust replication of diverse motions like walking, jumping, and kicking on physical robots.

## Problem

- Controlling humanoid robots is challenging due to their high degrees of freedom, dynamic instability, and the complexity of coordinating whole-body movements.
- Existing teleoperation systems for humanoids often require specialized hardware or are limited to upper-body control.
- A lack of physically feasible motion datasets for humanoid robots and difficulties in transferring simulated policies to real-world hardware hinder robust whole-body control.

## Method

- ==The H2O== framework develops a "sim-to-data" pipeline to create feasible humanoid motion datasets by filtering human motion capture data.
- It trains a reinforcement learning policy using a carefully designed state, reward function, and extensive domain randomization to enable accurate motion tracking and robust sim-to-real transfer.
- Real-time teleoperation is achieved by feeding estimated human pose from an off-the-shelf RGB camera into the trained policy, which then generates joint commands for the robot.

## Results

- The H2O framework successfully enables real-time teleoperation of a physical humanoid robot for diverse whole-body motions, including walking, jumping, turning, kicking, and boxing.
- In simulation, H2O achieves higher success rates and improved motion tracking accuracy compared to baseline methods.
- The system operates effectively with only standard RGB camera input, confirming its practicality and accessibility for controlling complex dynamic robot movements.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Generating physically feasible motion data for humanoid robots through a "sim-to-data" filtering process is crucial and effective for training robust controllers.
> - Combining reinforcement learning with extensive domain randomization can produce control policies capable of replicating dynamic whole-body human movements on physical humanoid robots from simple RGB camera inputs.
> - Accessible hardware like a standard RGB camera can be sufficient for real-time, whole-body teleoperation of complex humanoid robots, removing the need for specialized motion capture systems.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Humanoid robots hold significant promise for integrating human cognitive capabilities with robotic versatility. Their human-like form factor makes them well-suited for environments designed for humans, potentially enabling them to perform complex tasks from household chores to emergency response operations. However, controlling humanoid robots presents substantial challenges due to their high degrees of freedom, dynamic instability, and the complexity of coordinating whole-body movements.

This paper introduces Human to Humanoid (H2O), a framework that enables real-time whole-body teleoperation of humanoid robots using only RGB camera input. The system allows human operators to control full-sized humanoid robots through natural movements, addressing a significant gap in existing teleoperation approaches that typically focus on upper-body control or require specialized equipment.

#### 1.1. Research Context

Traditional approaches to humanoid teleoperation have relied on simplified dynamic models or external sensors, limiting their ability to reproduce dynamic human motions. More recent learning-based methods have shown promise but typically focus on upper-body movements or offline motion replication rather than real-time whole-body control.

The authors position their work within several interconnected research areas:
- Humanoid robotics and the challenges of high-dimensional control
- Teleoperation systems for complex robots
- Reinforcement learning for motion generation
- Motion retargeting from humans to robots
- Sim-to-real transfer for robotic control policies

What distinguishes this research is its integration of these areas to create a comprehensive solution for real-time, whole-body humanoid teleoperation using accessible hardware (a single RGB camera) rather than specialized motion capture systems or exoskeletons.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The H2O framework addresses three key challenges in humanoid teleoperation:

1. **The Data Challenge**: Lack of suitable humanoid motion datasets for training robust controllers
2. **The Reality Gap**: Difficulties in transferring policies trained in simulation to real robots
3. **The Real-Time Control Challenge**: Enabling responsive teleoperation with minimal latency

To overcome these challenges, the authors develop a multi-stage approach that combines:
- A novel "sim-to-data" process to create feasible motion datasets
- Extensive domain randomization for sim-to-real transfer
- A reinforcement learning framework optimized for real-time control

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The H2O framework consists of three main components:

1. **Motion Retargeting and Sim-to-Data**: Converting human motion data into feasible humanoid motion sequences
2. **Control Policy Training**: Using reinforcement learning to train a policy that can track retargeted motions
3. **Real-Time Teleoperation**: Deploying the trained policy to enable real-time control by a human operator

This comprehensive approach enables the generation of high-quality training data, robust policy learning, and effective deployment on physical humanoid robots.

#### 3.1. Motion Retargeting and Sim to Data

The authors introduce a novel "sim-to-data" approach to address the lack of suitable humanoid motion datasets. This process involves:

1. Starting with the AMASS dataset, which contains diverse human motions
2. Retargeting these motions to the humanoid robot using inverse kinematics and shape fitting
3. Training a "privileged" motion imitator in simulation with access to complete state information
4. Testing each retargeted motion sequence with this privileged imitator
5. Filtering out motions that the privileged imitator fails to track

This process generates a dataset of motions that are feasible for the humanoid robot, addressing the fundamental challenge of the mismatch between human and robot capabilities. The authors formalize this as:

```
D_filtered = {τ ∈ D_retargeted | TrackingError(τ) < threshold}
```

Where `D_filtered` represents the filtered dataset, `D_retargeted` is the initial retargeted motion dataset, and `TrackingError(τ)` measures how well the privileged imitator can track motion sequence τ.

#### 3.2. Training the Control Policy

With the filtered motion dataset, the authors train a reinforcement learning policy to imitate human motions. The training process includes:

1. **State Design**: Carefully designing a state space that includes information available in the real world (joint positions, velocities, and global body pose)
2. **Reward Function**: Creating a reward function that encourages accurate motion tracking while maintaining physical feasibility
3. **Domain Randomization**: Incorporating extensive randomization of physical parameters to improve sim-to-real transfer

The policy is trained using Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) with a reward function that balances motion tracking accuracy and stability:

```
r = w_1 * r_joint_pos + w_2 * r_joint_vel + w_3 * r_root_pos + w_4 * r_root_ori + w_5 * r_action_reg
```

Where each `r` term represents a specific reward component (joint position matching, velocity matching, etc.) and `w` values are weighting coefficients.

#### 3.3. Real Time Teleoperation

For real-time teleoperation, the H2O framework:

1. Captures the human operator using a standard RGB camera
2. Processes the video feed through an off-the-shelf human pose estimator (HybrIK)
3. Feeds the estimated human pose into the trained RL policy
4. Converts policy outputs to joint commands for the humanoid robot

This pipeline enables responsive control with minimal latency, allowing the operator to perform dynamic movements that the robot can replicate in real-time. Importantly, the system requires only a standard camera rather than specialized motion capture equipment, making it accessible and practical.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The authors evaluate H2O through both simulation experiments and real-world demonstrations:

**Simulation Results:**
- H2O significantly outperforms baseline approaches in terms of success rate, motion tracking accuracy, and physical realism
- The sim-to-data process proves effective even with smaller training datasets
- Performance improves with larger motion datasets, highlighting the importance of diverse training examples

**Real-World Demonstrations:**
- The system successfully enables real-time teleoperation for diverse whole-body motions including walking, jumping, turning, kicking, and boxing
- The control policy demonstrates robustness to external disturbances
- The framework functions effectively with only RGB camera input, validating its practicality

The results confirm that H2O achieves its primary objective: enabling responsive, natural, whole-body teleoperation of humanoid robots using accessible hardware.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

Despite its achievements, the authors acknowledge several limitations and areas for future work:

1. **Representation Gap**: Differences between human and humanoid kinematics limit the fidelity of motion transfer
2. **Embodiment Gap**: Physical differences between humans and robots constrain which motions can be successfully replicated
3. **Sim-to-Real Challenges**: While domain randomization helps, further improvements in sim-to-real transfer could enhance performance
4. **Multimodal Interaction**: Incorporating additional interaction modalities like force feedback could improve the operator experience

Future research directions include addressing these limitations and exploring applications in areas such as remote assistance, healthcare, manufacturing, and data collection for autonomous robotics.

#### 5.1. Conclusion

The H2O framework represents a significant advance in humanoid teleoperation by enabling real-time whole-body control using only RGB camera input. By combining motion retargeting, reinforcement learning, and sim-to-real transfer techniques, the authors demonstrate a practical approach to controlling complex humanoid robots through natural human movements.

The system's ability to replicate dynamic whole-body motions in real-time opens up new possibilities for human-robot collaboration, remote task execution, and data collection for autonomous robotics. The accessibility of the approach—requiring only standard camera equipment rather than specialized sensors—further enhances its practical impact.

H2O establishes a foundation for future research in humanoid teleoperation while providing immediate practical utility for controlling humanoid robots in diverse applications ranging from disaster response to assisted living.
