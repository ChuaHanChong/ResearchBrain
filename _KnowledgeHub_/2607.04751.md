# Trust Region Policy Distillation

> [!summary] Summary
> Trust Region Policy Distillation (TOP-D) is a new method for post-training Large Language Models, which addresses the optimization instability and low sample efficiency of traditional On-Policy Distillation. By introducing a proximal teacher to bound distillation reward signals and internal trust region iterations for data reuse, TOP-D achieved significant accuracy improvements, such as a 25.84% absolute gain on AIME24 for a Qwen3-8B-Base model, on mathematical reasoning tasks without additional computational overhead.

## Problem

- Standard On-Policy Distillation (OPD) for LLM post-training suffers from severe optimization instability due to the unbounded logarithmic probability ratio (reward signal) caused by the teacher-student capacity gap.
- OPD typically exhibits low sample efficiency because it adheres to a strictly on-policy regime, discarding generated trajectories after each policy update.
- Many existing empirical fixes for OPD instability lack theoretical guarantees and do not address the fundamental underlying causes of fragility.

## Method

- An ==external proximal teacher==, constructed by interpolating between the target teacher and the current student, redefines the distillation reward signal to be strictly lower-bounded, preventing divergence and stabilizing optimization.
- ==Internal trust region iterations==, inspired by ==PPO==, decouple the behavior policy from the target policy, enabling efficient data reuse across multiple gradient updates from the same collected trajectories.
- The framework casts autoregressive language modeling as a deterministic ==Markov Decision Process (MDP)== to provide a rigorous theoretical foundation for the proposed stable optimization approach.

## Results

- TOP-D consistently outperformed all evaluated baselines, including standard OPD and RLVR methods, demonstrating substantial performance gains on mathematical reasoning tasks (e.g., a **25.84%** absolute improvement on AIME24 for a Qwen3-8B-Base student).
- Ablation studies confirmed that both the external proximal teacher and internal trust region iterations are indispensable for achieving optimization stability and rapid performance improvement, with removing either leading to significant degradation.
- The proposed method introduces **zero** additional computational overhead, making it a highly practical and plug-and-play solution compatible with existing LLM training infrastructures.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The unbounded nature of the logarithmic probability ratio in standard OPD is the root cause of its optimization instability, and dynamically bounding this reward signal is crucial for stable training.
> - Decoupling the data collection process from the optimization steps, akin to trust region reinforcement learning, significantly improves sample efficiency in policy distillation by allowing data reuse.
> - A principled approach with strong theoretical guarantees (e.g., bounded gradient variance, global convergence, monotonic improvement) is essential for developing robust and reliable LLM post-training methods, surpassing empirical heuristics.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in "Trust Region Policy Distillation" (TOP-D) addresses challenges in the post-training phase of Large Language Models (LLMs), specifically within the paradigm of On-Policy Distillation (OPD). LLM post-training aims to refine pre-trained models for better performance on specific tasks, often involving alignment with human preferences or stronger teacher models.

Historically, Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) has been a common method for post-training, but it can suffer from catastrophic forgetting, where the model loses previously acquired knowledge. Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) emerged as an alternative, offering on-policy training that mitigates catastrophic forgetting and uses sparse, often human-provided, rewards. However, RLVR is typically characterized by low sample efficiency and training instability due to the sparsity of its reward signals.

On-Policy Distillation (OPD) represents a more recent development that seeks to combine the benefits of on-policy training with dense reward signals. In OPD, a smaller "student" model learns from token-level signals provided by a larger, more capable "teacher" model. The reward is often defined as the logarithmic probability ratio between the teacher and student policies for generated tokens. This approach aims to prevent catastrophic forgetting (like RLVR) while providing dense rewards to improve sample efficiency and stability compared to RLVR (as summarized in Table 1 of the paper).

Despite its theoretical advantages, standard OPD has been observed to suffer from significant practical optimization fragility. This instability is attributed to the inherent capacity gap between the teacher and student models. When the student's probability for a generated token is very low, but the teacher's is higher, the logarithmic probability ratio can become unbounded, leading to highly variable and unstable gradients. Previous research has attempted to mitigate this instability through various empirical techniques, such as teacher-student mixed sampling, reward clipping, top-p sampling, off-policy cold starts, and full-vocabulary supervision. However, these methods often lack theoretical guarantees and do not fundamentally address the underlying cause of the instability, which is the unbounded nature of the reward signal.

TOP-D aims to systematically resolve these two core issues of standard OPD: optimization instability and low sample efficiency, by introducing a theoretically grounded framework that builds upon trust region concepts from reinforcement learning. It positions itself as an alternative to existing OPD and RLVR paradigms by offering both empirical effectiveness and strong theoretical guarantees without introducing additional computational overhead.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a robust and stable post-training paradigm for Large Language Models that overcomes the inherent fragilities of standard On-Policy Distillation (OPD) while maintaining its benefits. The specific key objectives and motivations are detailed below:

1. **Resolve Optimization Instability in Standard OPD:**
 - **Motivation:** Standard OPD suffers from severe optimization fragility in practice. This is primarily due to the "capacity gap" between the target teacher and the student model. The distillation objective relies on the logarithmic probability ratio (ρ_k = π_*(y_k|x,y_<k) / π_θ(y_k|x,y_<k)), which acts as the immediate reward. When the student policy (π_θ) assigns a near-zero probability to a token that the teacher policy (π_*) assigns a non-zero probability to, the probability ratio (ρ_k) can diverge, causing the logarithmic reward (r_k = log ρ_k) to approach negative infinity. This unbounded penalty destabilizes the policy gradient and makes the optimization process highly vulnerable.
 - **Objective:** To systematically address this unbounded reward signal by transforming it into a smooth, strictly lower-bounded signal, thereby stabilizing the optimization dynamics.

2. **Improve Sample Efficiency and Address Strict On-Policy Data-Reuse Limitations:**
 - **Motivation:** Standard OPD operates in a strictly on-policy manner, meaning that once the student policy is updated, the previously generated trajectories are typically discarded. This leads to low sample efficiency, as valuable data from rollouts is not reused across multiple optimization steps.
 - **Objective:** To introduce mechanisms that allow for more efficient data reuse, decoupling the behavior policy used for data collection from the target policy being optimized, akin to modern off-policy or trust region reinforcement learning algorithms.

3. **Establish Rigorous Theoretical Guarantees:**
 - **Motivation:** Many existing mitigation strategies for OPD instability are empirical heuristics that lack formal theoretical backing. A deeper understanding of the underlying mechanisms and guarantees for reliable optimization is needed.
 - **Objective:** To develop a rigorous theoretical framework for the proposed method, demonstrating inherent control over gradient variance, providing a global convergence analysis, and establishing monotonic improvement bounds. This aims to mathematically formalize the reliability and stability of the training dynamics.

4. **Achieve Enhanced Performance on Complex Tasks:**
 - **Motivation:** The instability and inefficiency of standard OPD limit the final performance of post-trained LLMs, especially on challenging tasks requiring robust reasoning capabilities.
 - **Objective:** To empirically demonstrate that the proposed method not only stabilizes training and improves sample efficiency but also leads to dramatic enhancements in final performance on tasks like mathematical reasoning.

5. **Maintain Computational Efficiency:**
 - **Motivation:** Introducing new algorithmic complexities can often lead to increased computational overhead, hindering practical adoption.
 - **Objective:** To design a solution that introduces zero additional computational overhead, making it a "plug-and-play" module compatible with existing distributed training and high-throughput inference infrastructures.

In summary, the motivation behind TOP-D is to move beyond empirical fixes for OPD's practical shortcomings by offering a principled, theoretically grounded framework that systematically addresses instability and inefficiency, leading to more robust, performant, and computationally feasible LLM post-training.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The Trust Region Policy Distillation (TOP-D) methodology integrates two primary components to address the instability and sample inefficiency of standard On-Policy Distillation (OPD): an external proximal teacher and internal trust region iterations. The overall approach is formalized within a reinforcement learning framework.

#### 3.1. Reinforcement Learning Formulation

To provide a rigorous theoretical foundation, autoregressive language modeling is cast as a deterministic Markov Decision Process (MDP) with states (s_t = (x, y_<t)), actions (A = V, vocabulary), deterministic dynamics, and a reward function. The immediate reward `r(s, a)` is defined as the distillation signal `log(π_*(a|s) / π(a|s))`. The objective is to maximize the expected cumulative reward `η(π)`.

#### 3.2. External Proximal Teacher

This component addresses the optimization instability caused by the unbounded logarithmic probability ratio (`r_k = log ρ_k`).
- **Problem:** When the student policy `π_θ` assigns very low probability to a token `y_k` that the teacher `π_*` prefers, `ρ_k` becomes very large, causing `r_k` to diverge negatively. This destabilizes gradient updates.
- **Solution:** Instead of direct supervision from the rigid `π_*`, a dynamic, intermediate "proximal teacher" `˜π_*` is constructed. This `˜π_*` is an interpolation between the target teacher `π_*` and the current student `π_θ`:
 `˜π_*(y_k | x, y_<k) = απ_*(y_k | x, y_<k) + (1-α)π_θ(y_k | x, y_<k)`
 where `α ∈ (0, 1)` is an interpolation coefficient.
- **Proximal Reward (`˜r_k`):** The new token-level reward `˜r_k` is derived from this proximal teacher:
 `˜r_k = log (˜π_*(y_k | x, y_<k) / π_θ(y_k | x, y_<k)) = log (αρ_k + 1 - α)`
- **Stabilization Mechanism:** As `ρ_k → 0` (student assigns low probability), `˜r_k` approaches `log(1 - α)`, which is a strictly lower-bounded finite value. This prevents the reward from diverging to negative infinity, thereby stabilizing the optimization process (visualized in Figure 3).
- **Computational Efficiency:** The authors highlight that this `˜r_k` can be computed directly from `ρ_k` and `α` without explicitly constructing `˜π_*`, thus introducing zero additional computational overhead. They also justify interpolating in the probability space rather than log-probability space, as the latter would simply scale the original reward without bounding it.

#### 3.3. Internal Trust Region Iterations

This component addresses the low sample efficiency inherent in strictly on-policy OPD.
- **Problem:** Standard OPD discards trajectories after each policy update, leading to inefficient data usage.
- **Solution:** TOP-D adopts principles from modern trust region algorithms (e.g., PPO) by decoupling the behavior policy (`π_θ_old`) from the target policy (`π_θ`).
- **Objective Function:** The student policy is optimized using a clipped importance-weighted objective, similar to Proximal Policy Optimization:
 `J(θ) = E[ min(p_t * Â_t, clip(p_t, 1-ϵ, 1+ϵ) * Â_t) ]`
 where `p_t = π_θ(y_t|x,y_<t) / π_θ_old(y_t|x,y_<t)` is the importance sampling ratio, and `Â_t` is the advantage function.
- **Advantage Estimation:** To leverage the dense reward signals, a fine-grained token-level normalized advantage `Â_t` is employed. This advantage is calculated using `˜R_k = ˜r_k + (1/(|y_i|-k)) * Σ_{j=k+1}^{|y_i|} ˜r_j`, combining the immediate proximal reward with a length-normalized future return. The advantage `Â_k` is then `˜R_k` normalized by the mean and standard deviation within a group of responses for a given prompt (Figure 4). This helps prevent the model from generating overly short or long responses.
- **Data Reuse:** By using `π_θ_old` for rollouts and `π_θ` for multiple gradient updates (internal epochs), the algorithm reuses collected data, significantly improving sample efficiency.

#### 3.4. Trust Region Policy Distillation Algorithm (Algorithm 1)

The complete TOP-D algorithm outlines an iterative process:
1. Initialize `π_θ_old` with `π_θ`.
2. Sample a batch of prompts `X`.
3. For each prompt, perform rollouts to sample `G` responses using `π_θ_old`.
4. Compute TOP-D rewards `˜r_k` using the formula `log(α * ρ_k + 1 - α)`.
5. Compute token-level returns `˜R_k` and normalized advantages `Â_k`.
6. For `E` internal epochs, update `θ` by maximizing the clipped importance-weighted objective `J(θ)` using the collected data.
7. Repeat until convergence.

#### 3.5. Theoretical Analysis

The paper provides three key theoretical results:
- **Bounded Variance (Theorem 4.2):** The proximal teacher `˜π_*` ensures that the variance of the TOP-D gradient estimator `˜g_k` is strictly bounded by `M^2|V|max((log(1-α))^2, C*α)`. This theoretically confirms the stability introduced by the proximal teacher, showing how `α` directly controls variance. As `α → 1`, the bound diverges, recovering the instability of standard OPD.
- **Convergence Analysis (Theorem 4.4):** The iterative process with a proximal teacher converges. The L1 distance between the student `π_{k+1}` and the target `π_*` is bounded by `(1-α)^{k+1}d(π_0, π_*) + Σ (1-α)^{k-i}||ϵ_i||_1`, where `ϵ_i` is the optimization error. Asymptotically, `lim sup d(π_{k+1}, π_*) ≤ ϵ_∞/α`. This highlights a trade-off: a smaller `α` (more stability) can lead to a larger asymptotic error if `ϵ_∞` is not minimized, motivating the need for robust internal optimization.
- **Monotonic Improvement (Theorem 4.9):** The internal trust region iterations guarantee monotonic policy improvement. By optimizing a lower bound of the true objective `η(˜π)`, defined as `ζ_π(˜π) - 2ξT_maxℓ_π E[D_TV(˜π, π)]`, the method ensures continuous reduction of the reverse KL divergence between the student and the proximal teacher. This, coupled with Pinsker's inequality, rigorously guarantees the minimization of the single-step optimization error `ϵ_k`, thereby closing the asymptotic convergence gap identified in Theorem 4.4.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The empirical evaluation of TOP-D focuses on mathematical reasoning tasks using various LLM configurations, comparing its performance against established baselines.

#### 4.1. Overall Performance Superiority

TOP-D consistently outperforms all evaluated baselines, including Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) methods like GRPO and DAPO, and standard On-Policy Distillation (OPD), across multiple mathematical reasoning benchmarks (AIME24, AIME25, AIME26, AMC23, MATH-500, Olympiad) and student model scales (Qwen3-1.7B-Base and Qwen3-8B-Base).

#### 4.2. Significant Improvements Over Standard OPD

- **Qwen3-8B-Base Student:** Using Qwen3-30B-A3B-Instruct-2507 as the teacher, TOP-D achieved an avg@32 accuracy of 50.42% on AIME24, marking a 25.84% absolute improvement over standard OPD (24.58%). Similar substantial gains were observed on AIME25 (+10.73%) and AIME26 (+18.64%), as well as other benchmarks like AMC23 (+11.25%), MATH-500 (+3.25%), and Olympiad (+5.38%). This demonstrates that bounding the variance of the distillation reward effectively stabilizes training and enhances the student's reasoning capabilities.
- **Qwen3-1.7B-Base Student:** Standard OPD exhibited significant performance degradation when applied to the smaller 1.7B student model, even falling behind some RLVR baselines. This fragility highlights its vulnerability when the teacher-student capacity gap is pronounced. In contrast, TOP-D maintained its performance advantage, surpassing standard OPD by over 10 percentage points on AIME24 (+11.35%) and AIME25 (+10.21%). This indicates TOP-D's robustness across different student model capacities.

#### 4.3. Dominance Over RLVR Baselines

TOP-D also consistently outperformed competitive RLVR baselines. For the Qwen3-8B-Base student, TOP-D exceeded DAPO by 17.5% absolute accuracy on AIME24, underscoring its superiority over traditional RLVR approaches for these tasks.

#### 4.4. Ablation Study Insights (Figure 5)

An ablation study on the Qwen3-1.7B-Base student model with the Qwen3-30B-A3B-Instruct-2507 teacher provided insights into TOP-D's core components:
- **External Proximal Teacher (TOP-D with α = 1.0):** Setting `α = 1.0` effectively removes the proximal teacher, reverting the objective to the unbounded standard OPD reward. This variant resulted in highly unstable training dynamics and a substantial decrease in overall performance, confirming the critical role of the proximal teacher in stabilizing optimization.
- **Internal Trust Region Iterations (TOP-D w/o off-policy):** Disabling the internal trust region iterations forced the optimization into a strictly on-policy regime. This led to a drastic reduction in sample efficiency, requiring significantly more training steps to converge and achieve comparable performance, demonstrating the importance of data reuse for efficient learning.
- **Conclusion:** Both the external proximal teacher and the internal trust region iterations are identified as indispensable components for achieving optimization stability and rapid performance improvement.

#### 4.5. Sensitivity Analysis to Interpolation Coefficient (α)

The study evaluated TOP-D's sensitivity to the interpolation coefficient `α` across values `{0.1, 0.2, 0.3}`. The empirical results showed only marginal differences in training stability, convergence speed, and final performance across this range. This indicates that TOP-D is robust to the choice of `α`, potentially reducing the need for extensive hyperparameter tuning.

#### 4.6. Computational Overhead

A significant finding is that TOP-D introduces zero additional computational overhead, functioning as a plug-and-play module. This makes it highly compatible with existing distributed training infrastructures and high-throughput inference engines.

In summary, TOP-D demonstrates strong empirical evidence of effectively resolving the challenges of OPD, leading to substantial performance gains and robust training, particularly on complex mathematical reasoning tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of Trust Region Policy Distillation (TOP-D) presents a significant contribution to the field of Large Language Model (LLM) post-training, with several key areas of significance and potential impact:

1. **Systematic Resolution of OPD Instability and Inefficiency:** TOP-D offers a principled and systematic solution to the long-standing problems of optimization fragility and low sample efficiency in On-Policy Distillation (OPD). Unlike previous empirical heuristics, TOP-D addresses the root causes—unbounded reward signals and strict on-policy data limitations—through theoretically grounded mechanisms. This transition from heuristic mitigation to systematic resolution enhances the reliability and predictability of LLM post-training.

2. **Enhanced Training Stability and Robustness:** By dynamically constructing a proximal teacher that inherently bounds gradient variance, TOP-D transforms notoriously unstable OPD into a stable training paradigm. This increased stability is critical for reliably training and aligning LLMs, especially when dealing with a significant capacity gap between the teacher and student models, or when the student model is smaller and more prone to divergence. The empirical results on mathematical reasoning tasks validate this stability, showing consistent performance gains where standard OPD falters.

3. **Improved Sample Efficiency:** The incorporation of internal trust region iterations enables the reuse of collected trajectories across multiple optimization steps, breaking the strict on-policy data-reuse barrier of standard OPD. This leads to significantly improved sample efficiency, which is a crucial advantage in the context of LLM training, where data collection (especially via expensive teacher model inference or human labeling) can be a bottleneck.

4. **Strong Theoretical Guarantees:** The paper establishes a rigorous theoretical framework, including proofs for bounded gradient variance, global convergence, and monotonic policy improvement. These guarantees provide a deeper understanding of TOP-D's mechanics and build confidence in its performance and robustness. The theoretical analysis also highlights a key trade-off between the interpolation coefficient `α` and the asymptotic error, motivating the integrated design of TOP-D.

5. **Significant Performance Gains on Complex Tasks:** Empirically, TOP-D demonstrates dramatic improvements in reasoning capabilities on challenging mathematical reasoning benchmarks, consistently outperforming both standard OPD and competitive RLVR baselines. This suggests that TOP-D can unlock higher performance ceilings for LLMs on tasks requiring precise and coherent logical deductions.

6. **Practical Applicability and Accessibility:** A key advantage of TOP-D is its design as a "plug-and-play" module that introduces zero additional computational overhead. This means it can be readily integrated into existing state-of-the-art distributed training infrastructures and high-throughput inference engines without requiring costly hardware upgrades or complex system modifications. This practicality enhances its potential for widespread adoption in research and industrial settings.

7. **Foundation for Future LLM Alignment Research:** TOP-D's success in stabilizing and improving policy distillation provides a more reliable foundation for future research in LLM alignment and fine-tuning. The principled approach and theoretical backing could inspire further advancements in areas such as robust reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF), knowledge distillation for specialized models, and continual learning for LLMs.

While the paper acknowledges limitations, such as the current validation being up to 8B parameter student models and limited training steps, the observed robustness and significant gains suggest that TOP-D's benefits are likely to transfer to larger scales and longer training horizons. Overall, TOP-D offers a highly reliable, sample-efficient, and robust paradigm for aligning foundation models, contributing to the development of more capable and stable LLMs.
