# $ω$-0: A Latent Predictive World Action Model for Concurrent Humanoid Loco-Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers introduce ω-0, a latent predictive world action model that enables humanoid robots to perform concurrent locomotion and manipulation directly from language instructions and observations. The model demonstrates an 81.8% success rate on 11 diverse real-world household tasks, outperforming existing baselines, and is supported by the new 40-hour multimodal ω-HOME dataset.

## Problem

- Current robot learning systems often decompose locomotion, balance, and manipulation, limiting performance in complex tasks requiring simultaneous whole-body coordination.
- Existing whole-body World Action Models (WAMs) frequently rely on pixel-level video dynamics for supervision, which can be sensitive to noisy observations and computationally expensive.
- A scarcity of real-world humanoid data makes it challenging to train robust policies, and leveraging abundant human demonstration data for robot-specific action spaces remains difficult.

## Method

- Developed **ω-0**, a latent predictive world-action model that generates controller-compatible whole-body action latents and compact future observation embeddings, rather than pixel-space video.
- Implemented a ==three-stage training pipeline== involving Vision-Language Model (VLM) pre-training, human-to-humanoid action-latent pre-training using a ==SONIC simulation replay== mechanism, and fine-tuning on real-world data.
- Employed a ==diffusion-based Action DiT== for whole-body action generation, conditioned on fused features from a VLM, a current visual encoder, a text encoder, and a proprioceptive state encoder.

## Results

- ω-0 achieved an **81.8%** success rate and **90.3%** task progress across 11 diverse real-world household loco-manipulation tasks, consistently outperforming state-of-the-art baselines (e.g., DiT4DiT at **43.6%** success rate).
- Ablation studies confirmed the importance of robot state conditioning, the whole-body VLM prefix for semantic context, and the auxiliary future visual latent prediction objective.
- The model demonstrated generalization capabilities to novel object instances and unseen scene layouts, and successfully transferred human demonstration data to complete target tasks on the real humanoid.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Directly learning whole-body coordination as a unified behavior significantly enhances performance for complex concurrent loco-manipulation tasks compared to decomposed approaches.
> - Compact future observation embeddings, when used as a reconstruction-free predictive signal, provide valuable task-progress cues for action learning without the overhead of explicit video generation.
> - Leveraging human motion priors by replaying trajectories through a robot controller in simulation effectively bridges the data gap, enabling the use of scalable human data for robot action learning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of humanoid robots capable of performing complex household tasks is a significant challenge in robotics. Such tasks often necessitate concurrent loco-manipulation, wherein the robot must simultaneously execute locomotion, posture adjustments, balance maintenance, and object manipulation as an integrated behavior. This research addresses a gap in current robot learning systems, which frequently decompose these capabilities or exhibit limitations in their scope.

Prior approaches often fall into several categories:
- **Arm-centric Manipulation Policies:** Many vision-language-action (VLA) policies primarily focus on arm-based manipulation, predicting end-effector commands or arm actions from visual observations and language instructions. These are typically insufficient for tasks requiring full-body coordination.
- **Mobile Manipulation Systems:** While these systems extend the workspace using a wheeled base, the base and arms are frequently treated as separate functional components, leading to suboptimal coordination for integrated tasks.
- **Humanoid VLAs:** Recent advances in humanoid VLAs have made progress in loco-manipulation. However, many of these systems still rely on practical decompositions of locomotion, balance, and manipulation. Such decompositions can be effective for sequential tasks (e.g., move, then manipulate), but they limit performance in scenarios where continuous, simultaneous stepping, torso adjustment, reaching, and contact maintenance are essential for stable execution.
- **World Action Models (WAMs):** WAMs aim to learn coordinated behaviors by using future visual dynamics to supervise task progress and action consequences. Most existing WAMs are designed for arm-centric manipulation. While some recent humanoid WAMs extend this concept to whole-body control, they often rely on video dynamics as a central intermediate representation for action prediction. This video-centered approach can be sensitive to noisy or occluded visual observations in real-world humanoid tasks, potentially leading to temporal inconsistencies and unstable whole-body coordination. Furthermore, the computational overhead of improving pixel-level video fidelity may not directly translate to improved control performance.

This work specifically addresses these limitations by proposing a model that learns whole-body coordination directly, rather than through decomposition. It utilizes future prediction as a compact signal for action learning, aiming to circumvent the challenges associated with video generation in WAMs. The research also tackles the scarcity of real-world humanoid data by incorporating human and public visual-motion priors through a simulation-based replay mechanism.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a latent predictive whole-body world-action model, named $\omega$-0, capable of real-world humanoid concurrent loco-manipulation. This model aims to enable humanoids to perform complex household tasks that necessitate coordinated movement and manipulation, directly from language instructions, visual observations, and proprioceptive states.

The motivation for this objective stems from several identified challenges and gaps in the existing research landscape:

1. **Direct Whole-Body Coordination Learning:** Humanoid household tasks, such as wiping a large table or mopping a floor, inherently require continuous adaptation and coordination among the lower body, torso, arms, and hands. Current systems often decompose these behaviors into separate modules (e.g., locomotion, balance, manipulation). This decomposition can be limiting when stable execution depends on simultaneous actions, motivating the development of a policy representation where whole-body coordination is learned intrinsically as a unified behavior.

2. **Efficient Future Prediction for Action Learning:** While World Action Models (WAMs) offer benefits by incorporating future visual dynamics, many rely on generating future video trajectories. In real-world humanoid environments, visual observations can be noisy, occluded, and dynamic due to robot movement. Basing action generation on pixel-level video prediction can lead to instabilities or computational inefficiency. The motivation here is to explore whether future prediction can serve as a compact, reconstruction-free predictive signal for whole-body action learning, avoiding reliance on large video generators or test-time video-to-action inversion. The objective is to learn compact future observation embeddings that provide task-progress and scene-evolution cues without the overhead of full video reconstruction.

3. **Addressing Real-World Data Scarcity:** Collecting large-scale, high-quality real-world humanoid demonstrations is resource-intensive. Human demonstrations and public video-action datasets contain rich visual-motion priors, but these are not directly compatible with robot-specific action spaces or controllers. A key motivation is to bridge this "human-to-humanoid" data gap, enabling the leverage of scalable human data for robot action learning while ensuring real-robot executability. This involves translating human motion into robot-executable action latents through simulation and filtering.

4. **Flexible Visual Conditioning:** Robot tasks can benefit from different visual perspectives. Egocentric observations are crucial for real-time deployment, providing direct feedback from the robot's viewpoint. However, exocentric (third-person) observations can offer complementary supervision during training, providing a broader view of whole-body motion, object-scene relations, and task progress. The objective is to design a model that supports both egocentric and exocentric inputs, allowing the model to benefit from informative third-person views during training while enabling first-person execution.

5. **Unified Model for Diverse Tasks:** Rather than training task-specific policies, the research aims to develop a single, unified $\omega$-0 model capable of autonomously executing a diverse set of household tasks. This promotes generalizability and reduces the need for extensive retraining for each new task.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The $\omega$-0 model is a latent predictive whole-body world-action model designed for concurrent humanoid loco-manipulation. It takes a language instruction, current visual observation (egocentric or exocentric), and robot proprioceptive state as input. Its primary outputs are a future chunk of controller-compatible whole-body action latents for real-robot execution via a low-level SONIC controller, and compact future observation embeddings as an auxiliary predictive objective.

The methodology involves a three-stage training pipeline and a novel data collection effort:

#### 3.1. Model Architecture Overview

$\omega$-0 comprises three main components:
1. **Whole-Body Action VLM:** Maps language-conditioned visual observations to action-aware semantic features.
2. **Joint Video-Action Latent Predictor:** Fuses features from the VLM, a T5 text encoder, a V-JEPA visual encoder, view tokens, motion queries, and video queries. Motion queries are for action generation, and video queries predict future visual latents.
3. **Action DiT (Diffusion Transformer):** Denoises whole-body action latents conditioned on fused features from the latent predictor.

#### 3.2. Three-Stage Training Pipeline

**Stage 1: Whole-Body Action VLM Pre-training**
- **Objective:** To integrate whole-body action semantics into a vision-language backbone.
- **Action Tokenization:** Continuous whole-body action trajectories are discretized into tokens using a whole-body FAST tokenizer. This tokenizer is optimized by minimizing the reconstruction error between continuous and de-tokenized actions.
- **VLM Fine-tuning:** A Qwen3-VL-2B-Instruct model is fine-tuned to autoregressively predict these discrete action tokens. Input includes a language instruction, current visual observation (egocentric or exocentric), and a learnable view token to distinguish camera perspectives. The training objective is a standard next-token prediction loss. The VLM's hidden representation serves as an action-aware semantic prior for subsequent stages.

**Stage 2: Human-to-Humanoid Action-Latent Pre-training**
- **Objective:** To align future visual latent prediction with diffusion-based whole-body action generation, leveraging scalable human data.
- **Future Visual Latent Prediction:** A lightweight, reconstruction-free objective predicts compact future observation embeddings. Ground-truth future visual latents are extracted using a frozen Wan encoder. This serves as an auxiliary predictive objective, avoiding pixel-space video generation.
- **Data Bridging (SONIC Replay):** Public human video-action datasets typically lack robot-specific action latents. To address this, human motion trajectories are replayed in simulation using the SONIC controller. SONIC tracks these trajectories and generates corresponding robot-executable whole-body action latents and proprioceptive states. Physically infeasible or unreliable motions are filtered out.
- **Robot State Representation:** The robot state $s_t$ includes body joint positions ($q_{pos}$), dexterous hand joint positions ($q_{hand}$), and a 6D rotation representation of the torso orientation ($r_{6D}^{torso}$) converted from the IMU quaternion to ensure continuity.
- **Feature Fusion:** Visual features ($f_v^t$) are extracted from the current observation using a frozen V-JEPA 2.1 image encoder. Language instructions ($\ell$) are encoded into text features ($f_{\ell}$) by a pretrained T5 encoder. A learnable view token ($r_v$) is also included. These, along with the action-aware VLM feature ($f_{vlm}$), form a prefix condition ($p$).
- **Joint Predictor:** Two sets of learnable queries are used: motion queries ($q_m$) for action generation (matched to action chunk size) and video queries ($q_v$) for future visual latent prediction. The predictor uses Prefix-Guided Dual-Query Attention with token-specific Rotary Position Encodings (RoPE): 2D RoPE for visual prefix tokens, 3D RoPE for future video queries, and 1D temporal RoPE for action queries. Motion queries attend to video queries, injecting predicted visual dynamics into the action representation.
- **Loss Functions:** The video output ($h_v$) is supervised with an $L_{video}$ loss (MSE) against the Wan-encoded future visual latents. The motion feature ($h_m$), text feature ($f_{\ell}$), and robot state feature ($f_s$) are fused to condition an Action DiT. The DiT denoises noisy action latents ($z_{\tau}$) into clean latents ($\hat{z}_0$), using an $x_0$-prediction objective ($L_{action}$). The overall Stage-2 objective is $L_{stage2} = L_{action} + \lambda_{video} L_{video}$.

**Stage 3: Fine-tuning on Real-World Data**
- **Objective:** To refine the model on real-world humanoid loco-manipulation data for deployment.
- **Data:** The $\omega$-HOME dataset, a 40-hour real-world dataset of multimodal household humanoid demonstrations, provides synchronized multi-view observations, robot states, and whole-body action latents.
- **Components Fine-tuned:** The joint video-action latent predictor, state encoder, condition-fusion module, and action DiT are fine-tuned. The V-JEPA encoder, Wan encoder, and pretrained VLM are kept frozen.
- **Training-time Real-Time Chunking (RTC):** To improve temporal continuity during receding-horizon deployment, the model is trained to denoise action latents while a random prefix of the noisy sequence is replaced with ground-truth clean latents. The denoising loss ($L_{RTC}$) is computed only on the non-prefix portion.
- **Overall Loss:** $L_{stage3} = L_{RTC} + \lambda_{video} L_{video}$.

#### 3.3. Real-World Deployment

- $\omega$-0 is deployed on the real humanoid robot with SONIC as the low-level controller in a receding-horizon manner.
- At each step, the model predicts an action chunk (length H=25). Only the first K=8 actions are executed, then new observations are acquired.
- RTC-style warm-starting is used: the last few frames of the previous predicted chunk serve as a clean prefix for the current denoising process.
- Overlap blending is applied to reduce discontinuities at chunk boundaries, linearly blending overlapping actions.
- The generated and processed action latents are denormalized and sent to SONIC for execution.

#### 3.4. $\omega$-HOME Dataset

- A 40.3-hour, 4,827-episode, 24-task real-world household humanoid dataset recorded at 30 Hz.
- Modalities: Synchronized egocentric RGB, exocentric RGB-D, robot proprioceptive states, whole-body SMPL motions, and controller-compatible action latents.
- Tasks cover object retrieval, surface cleaning, appliance interaction, container transfer, cloth handling, storage arrangement, mobile manipulation, and tool-based floor operation.
- **Teleoperation Setup:** Pico VR headset, foot-mounted Pico trackers for head and lower-body motion, handheld triggers for grasping. Robot equipped with ZED Mini camera (egocentric) and Inspire DexHands. Room contains ZED depth camera for exocentric RGB-D.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research provides both quantitative and qualitative results demonstrating the performance of $\omega$-0, as well as ablation studies to highlight the contribution of individual components.

#### 4.1. Overall Real-World Performance

$\omega$-0 was evaluated on 11 diverse real-world household loco-manipulation tasks against a range of baselines, including classical imitation learning (ACT, Diffusion Policy), VLA models ($\pi$-0.5, InternVLA-M1, EgoVLA, GR00T-N1.7), and humanoid/WAM-specific models ($\psi$-0, Fast-WAM, DiT4DiT). All methods were trained or fine-tuned on the real-world dataset as a single multi-task model.

- **Consistent Outperformance:** $\omega$-0 consistently achieved higher success rates, subtask scores, and task progress compared to all baselines.
 - **$\omega$-0_Ego:** Achieved a 79.1% success rate, an average score of 35.8 (out of max 41), and 88.7% task progress.
 - **$\omega$-0_Omni:** Achieved a slightly higher 81.8% success rate, 36.7 average score, and 90.3% task progress.
- **Comparison with Baselines:** The performance of $\omega$-0 demonstrated a substantial improvement over the best baselines. For instance, DiT4DiT, a strong video-generation-based WAM baseline, achieved 43.6% success rate, while $\psi$-0, an arm-centric humanoid VLA, reached 44.5%. Classical imitation learning methods and most VLA models exhibited significantly lower performance.

#### 4.2. Effect of Omni-View Training

The $\omega$-0_Omni variant, which supports both egocentric and exocentric visual inputs (using exocentric for locomotion-heavy tasks), showed better overall performance than $\omega$-0_Ego (which uses only egocentric observations). This improvement was particularly noted on tasks requiring substantial locomotion or body repositioning, suggesting that exocentric observations provide beneficial information for global body motion and scene understanding that complements first-person views.

#### 4.3. Effectiveness of $\omega$-HOME Dataset

The integration of non-overlapping $\omega$-HOME data during Stage 2 pre-training (in combination with public human data) led to improved real-world performance after fine-tuning on the 11 downstream tasks. This indicates that $\omega$-HOME provides useful real-world whole-body visual-action priors beyond just the direct downstream task demonstrations.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies

Controlled ablations were conducted to analyze the contribution of various components of $\omega$-0:

- **Robot State Conditioning:** Removing the robot's proprioceptive state input reduced success rate, score, and task progress (e.g., SR from 79.1% to 60.9% for $\omega$-0_Ego). This suggests the importance of direct information about the robot's physical configuration for grounding action latents, especially in tasks involving dynamic body movements.
- **Whole-Body VLM Prefix:** Eliminating the VLM feature (which carries task-conditioned action semantics) from the joint predictor resulted in weaker action generation and less accurate future visual latent prediction (e.g., SR from 79.1% to 66.4% for $\omega$-0_Ego). This indicates the prefix's role as a high-level semantic condition.
- **Future Visual Latent Prediction:** Removing the video queries and the associated future visual latent prediction loss, thus converting the model into a direct language-vision-state-to-action diffusion policy, led to a clear performance drop (e.g., SR from 79.1% to 64.5% for $\omega$-0_Ego). This supports the premise that latent future visual prediction provides valuable task-progress and scene-evolution cues for action generation, particularly for long-horizon, coordinated tasks.
- **Training-time RTC:** Training without Real-Time Chunking resulted in executed trajectories with increased hesitation, discontinuity, and corrective motions (e.g., SR from 79.1% to 71.8% for $\omega$-0_Ego). RTC training was shown to improve temporal continuity and smoothness during receding-horizon execution.
- **Current-Image Encoder:** Using the Wan encoder for the current image (instead of V-JEPA) resulted in reduced performance (e.g., SR from 79.1% to 63.6% for $\omega$-0_Ego). While Wan is video-oriented, V-JEPA provides stronger single-frame semantic features more relevant for real-time action generation, despite potentially creating a latent-space gap with future visual supervision. This suggests that the quality of the current-image representation for action decision-making is critical.

#### 4.5. Generalization

$\omega$-0 demonstrated generalization capabilities:
- **Cross-Object:** The model could localize, approach, and manipulate novel object instances and appearances not seen during training, suggesting it did not merely memorize object features.
- **Cross-Scene:** The model adapted its whole-body motion and interaction strategies to new room layouts and household configurations, indicating the learning of reusable visual-action priors.
- **Human Data Transfer:** Fine-tuning $\omega$-0 with human demonstration data allowed the model to complete target tasks on the real humanoid after grounding into the robot action-latent space, highlighting the potential for leveraging scalable human data.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents several contributions with potential impacts on the field of humanoid robotics:

1. **Advancement in Concurrent Loco-Manipulation:** The introduction of $\omega$-0 provides a framework for humanoid robots to perform concurrent loco-manipulation. By learning coordinated whole-body behaviors directly, rather than relying on decoupled modules, it addresses a fundamental challenge in enabling humanoids to operate effectively in complex, dynamic household environments. This approach is positioned to enhance the fluency and stability of robot execution for tasks requiring simultaneous movement and manipulation.

2. **Novel Latent Predictive World-Action Modeling:** The methodology of coupling future visual latent prediction with diffusion-based whole-body action generation offers an alternative to video-centered world models. By predicting compact future observation embeddings instead of pixel-space video, $\omega$-0 aims to achieve the benefits of visual foresight without the computational burden or potential instabilities associated with explicit video generation. This could make world-action modeling more practical and efficient for real-time robotic control.

3. **Creation of a Real-World Humanoid Dataset ($\omega$-HOME):** The collection of $\omega$-HOME, a 40-hour multimodal dataset of household humanoid demonstrations, addresses a significant data scarcity problem in humanoid robotics. This dataset, with its synchronized multi-view observations, robot states, and action latents, provides a valuable resource for training and evaluating whole-body world-action models, promoting further research in real-world humanoid loco-manipulation.

4. **Improved Performance in Diverse Household Tasks:** The demonstrated consistent outperformance of $\omega$-0 against various baselines across 11 real-world household tasks indicates its effectiveness. This suggests a step towards more capable and generalist humanoid robots that can autonomously execute a wide range of domestic activities, covering both short- and long-horizon tasks involving contact-rich interactions, tool use, and articulated object manipulation.

5. **Leveraging Human Motion Priors:** The method of grounding human and public visual-motion priors into robot-executable action latents through SONIC-based simulation replay offers a pathway to utilize abundant human demonstration data for humanoid policy learning. This strategy can help scale the training data available for robots, potentially accelerating the development of more robust and diverse behaviors.

6. **Insights from Ablation Studies:** The detailed ablation studies provide empirical evidence for the importance of specific architectural choices, such as robot state conditioning, a whole-body VLM prefix, future visual latent prediction, and training-time RTC. These insights can guide future research and model design in humanoid control and world modeling.

In summary, this work contributes to the development of more intelligent and versatile humanoid robots by offering an integrated approach to concurrent loco-manipulation, supported by a novel predictive modeling framework and a dedicated real-world dataset. The potential impact lies in advancing the capabilities of humanoids for practical deployment in complex, human-centered environments.
