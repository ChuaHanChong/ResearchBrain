# A Short Note on the Kinetics-700-2020 Human Action Dataset

> [!summary] Summary
> Kinetics-700-2020 is a replenished edition of the DeepMind Kinetics human action video dataset, keeping 700 YouTube-sourced classes but topping up the 123 rarest ones so every class holds at least 700 training clips. Collection widened text queries across four languages, clustered and removed duplicate clips, and re-filtered class labels. An I3D RGB baseline trained from scratch reaches 58.2% top-1 on test, above Kinetics-700.

## Problem

- YouTube-sourced video datasets decay as users delete or privatize clips, leaving Kinetics-400 retaining only 89% of its originally released training clips.
- Kinetics-700 was class-imbalanced: rarest classes held as few as 532 clips versus a 906 average, skewing training and evaluation across the 700 categories.
- Rare action classes yield poorly under existing query strategies, so naive re-collection returns few candidate clips that human annotators actually rate positive.

## Method

- Targeted replenishment pipeline collecting new clips only for the 123 rarest classes, topping each up to a minimum of 700 clips rather than adding new classes.
- ==Query-space augmentation==: infinitive and gerund verb forms, stop-word and article removal, and synonyms, issued in English, French, Spanish and Portuguese to raise rare-class yield.
- ==Deduplication== by clustering candidate clips and inspecting cluster gifs to drop re-uploads and compilation repeats, followed by a final class-correctness filter.

## Results

- **I3D** with RGB inputs trained on the full dataset reaches **59.3%** top-1 / **82.0%** top-5 on validation and **58.2%** / **80.9%** on test.
- Accuracy rises monotonically with clips per class: **38.8%** top-1 validation at 100 examples versus **59.3%** using all available examples per class.
- The rebalanced set beats Kinetics-700 under the same I3D protocol, **58.2%** versus **57.6%** test top-1, with all classes now at least **700** clips.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Web-sourced video benchmarks are perishable assets; periodic replenishment, not one-off release, is required to keep clip counts and class balance stable.
> - Multilingual, morphologically varied text queries measurably raise candidate yield for rare categories, where per-class positive rates fall as low as 2%.
> - Even a small 12M-parameter model like I3D keeps gaining from more clips per class, so data quantity remains unsaturated independent of capacity.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of computer vision, particularly human action recognition in videos, relies heavily on large-scale, high-quality datasets to train and evaluate deep learning models. Datasets like Kinetics have become foundational benchmarks for developing and testing spatio-temporal neural network architectures. These datasets provide a diverse collection of human actions, enabling models to learn robust representations of movement and context over time.

The Kinetics family of datasets (Kinetics-400, Kinetics-600, Kinetics-700) are prominent examples within this landscape. They are distinguished by their scale, the diversity of action classes, and their compilation from real-world YouTube videos. This sourcing method, while providing realism and scale, inherently introduces a challenge: video disappearance. Over time, videos are removed or made private by their creators, leading to dataset degradation where the originally available clips become inaccessible. This degradation impacts the reproducibility of research and the completeness of the dataset for training new models.

This work addresses the issue of dataset degradation specifically for the Kinetics-700 dataset. By replenishing missing clips and ensuring a minimum number of examples per class, it aims to maintain the dataset's utility and relevance. This effort is crucial for the continued progress of action recognition research, ensuring that researchers have access to a stable and comprehensive benchmark. Furthermore, the provision of baseline results with a standard architecture (I3D) facilitates comparisons and demonstrates the benefits of the updated dataset. This paper contributes to the ongoing maintenance and evolution of critical research infrastructure in the computer vision community.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this work was to replenish and extend the existing Kinetics-700 dataset, resulting in a new edition termed Kinetics-700-2020. This update was specifically designed to address the issue of video clip disappearance and to improve the class balance within the dataset.

The motivation behind this endeavor stems from several factors:
- **Dataset Degradation:** As highlighted in the introduction and Table 2, a significant proportion of video clips from previous Kinetics releases (Kinetics-400, Kinetics-600, and Kinetics-700) had become unavailable over time. For Kinetics-700, approximately 2-4% of clips were lost across its train, validation, and test splits. This degradation directly impacts the integrity and utility of the dataset for training and evaluating machine learning models, as models trained on a progressively incomplete dataset may not be directly comparable to those trained on the original, larger set.
- **Ensuring Minimum Class Representation:** The original Kinetics-700 dataset, while large, exhibited variability in the number of clips per class. The goal for Kinetics-700-2020 was to establish a minimum threshold of at least 700 video clips for each of the 700 human action classes. This aimed to improve the statistical robustness of each class, ensuring that models have sufficient examples to learn distinct features for even the rarer actions.
- **Maintaining Research Utility:** The Kinetics datasets were originally introduced to facilitate the development of novel neural network architectures for spatio-temporal modeling and for pre-training models for downstream tasks. By providing a revitalized and more balanced dataset, the authors aimed to sustain its role as a fundamental benchmark, allowing researchers to continue leveraging its scale and diversity for advanced action recognition research.
- **Unlike previous editions (Kinetics-400 to Kinetics-600, and Kinetics-600 to Kinetics-700), the motivation for Kinetics-700-2020 was not to increase the number of action classes but rather to enhance the quality and completeness of the existing 700 classes.** This strategic focus on replenishment rather than expansion indicates a mature stage of dataset development, emphasizing maintenance over continuous growth in scope.

In summary, the core objective was to create a more robust and complete version of Kinetics-700 by addressing clip disappearance and class imbalance, thereby ensuring its continued relevance and reliability as a foundational resource for computer vision research.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed for creating the Kinetics-700-2020 dataset involved a multi-pronged approach focused on replenishing rare classes, refining the data collection process, and conducting baseline performance evaluations.

#### 3.1. Data Collection and Replenishment

The data collection process primarily targeted the 123 classes that had fewer than 700 video clips in the original Kinetics-700 dataset. For these "rare" classes, new clips were collected until each class reached a minimum of 700 examples. This selective replenishment contrasts with a full re-collection, allowing for an efficient update.

##### 3.1.1. Query Augmentation for Candidate Clip Discovery

To improve the acquisition of relevant video clips for rare classes, the authors enhanced the text queries used to search YouTube video IDs. Techniques included:
- **Linguistic Variations:** Utilizing verbs in both infinitive and gerund forms (e.g., "to run" and "running").
- **Text Preprocessing:** Removing stop words (e.g., "the," "a") and articles from queries to focus on core action terms.
- **Synonym Expansion:** Incorporating synonyms to broaden the search space for specific actions.
- **Multilingual Querying:** The refined query procedure was applied across four languages: English, French, Spanish, and Portuguese. This multilingual approach was a continuation from previous Kinetics editions (Portuguese added for K-600, Spanish for K-700) and was noted to increase both yield and geographical diversity.

The augmentation of the query space aimed to yield more candidate YouTube videos that were closely related to the target action classes, addressing the historically poor yield rates for rare classes.

##### 3.1.2. Duplicate Removal

A critical step in dataset curation was the identification and removal of duplicate clips. Duplication can occur due to:
- **Multiple Uploads:** The same video being uploaded to YouTube multiple times by different users or accounts.
- **Compilation Videos:** Different YouTube videos containing identical segments (e.g., in instructional videos or compilations for actions like 'pouring milk' or 'tasting wine').
To address this, candidate clips were clustered, and individual clusters were reviewed using animated GIFs to visually identify and remove redundant entries.

##### 3.1.3. Final Filtering

After duplicate removal, a final filtering step was performed to ensure that all selected clips accurately depicted the assigned action class, maintaining the dataset's integrity and label accuracy.

#### 3.2. Geographical Diversity Analysis

The authors performed an analysis of the geographical distribution of video uploads in the final dataset. Video locations were assigned based on upload origin, categorizing them by continent. This analysis tracked changes in geographical diversity across Kinetics-400, Kinetics-600, Kinetics-700, and Kinetics-700-2020, assessing the impact of multilingual querying strategies on representation from different global regions.

#### 3.3. Benchmark Performance Evaluation

To provide baseline performance figures and demonstrate the benefits of the replenished dataset, the authors evaluated a standard action recognition model.

##### 3.3.1. Model Architecture

The Inception-3D (I3D) network was chosen as the baseline model. It was configured to process RGB video inputs only, without incorporating optical flow data. This is a common and established architecture in video action recognition.

##### 3.3.2. Training Strategy

The I3D model was trained from scratch exclusively on the Kinetics-700-2020 training set. To analyze the impact of data quantity, training was performed using varying numbers of examples per class: 100, 200, 300, 400, 500, 600 clips, and finally, using all available clips (where some classes reached up to 1000 examples).

##### 3.3.3. Implementation Details

- **Hardware:** Training utilized a 32-device TPU pod.
- **Batch Size:** A batch size of 8 videos per device was used.
- **Clip Duration:** Training clips were 32 frames long.
- **Optimization:** Stochastic Gradient Descent (SGD) with a momentum of 0.9 and a weight decay of 1e-5 was employed.
- **Epochs:** The model was trained for 135 epochs.
- **Learning Rate Schedule:** The initial learning rate was set to 0.5 and decreased by a factor of 10 at epochs 90, 105, 115, and 120.

##### 3.3.4. Evaluation Metrics

Performance was reported using Top-1 and Top-5 accuracy on both the validation and test sets of Kinetics-700-2020. During evaluation, 8 linearly spaced 32-frame clips were extracted from each video.

This systematic approach allowed the authors to not only update and improve the dataset but also to quantitatively assess the benefits of increased and more balanced training data on model performance using a standard benchmark.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research paper presents several key findings related to the updated Kinetics-700-2020 dataset and its impact on baseline action recognition performance.

#### 4.1. Dataset Statistics and Class Balance

The primary finding is the successful replenishment and rebalancing of the Kinetics-700 dataset.
- As detailed in Table 1, the Kinetics-700-2020 dataset now guarantees a minimum of 700 video clips for each of its 700 action classes. This significantly improves upon the previous Kinetics-700 version, which had a minimum of 532 clips per class, and other earlier versions.
- The average number of clips per class increased from 906 in Kinetics-700 to 926 in Kinetics-700-2020. This indicates not only reaching the minimum threshold but also a slight overall expansion of the dataset's size.
- Table 2 illustrates the extent of video disappearance. For example, Kinetics-400 retained only 89-90% of its original clips, and Kinetics-700 retained 96-98%. The Kinetics-700-2020 update directly counteracts this degradation, ensuring a robust and replenished set of available clips.
- Appendix A provides detailed yield rates for the 123 rare classes that received new clips, showing the probability of a candidate clip being rated positive and subsequently published after deduplication and filtering. These rates varied significantly, from "stacking dice" at 37.93% published yield to "twiddling fingers" at 2.02%, indicating the varying difficulty of collecting high-quality clips for different actions.

#### 4.2. Geographical Diversity

The analysis of geographical data distribution (Table 3) revealed trends in the origin of video uploads:
- North America consistently represented the largest fraction of videos across all Kinetics editions, comprising 56.6% in Kinetics-700-2020. This is attributed to early English querying and potentially YouTube's popularity in the region.
- There was a slight increase in geographical diversity over the years. Notably, Latin America's share rose from 3.4% in Kinetics-400 to 7.7% in Kinetics-700-2020, likely due to the introduction of Portuguese (Kinetics-600) and Spanish (Kinetics-700) queries.
- Other continents like Asia and Europe maintained relatively stable proportions, while Oceania showed a significant increase in Kinetics-600 before stabilizing. Africa's representation remained consistently low.

#### 4.3. Benchmark Performance with I3D Model

The I3D model's performance demonstrated a clear correlation between the number of training examples per class and classification accuracy (Table 4, Figure 1):
- Both Top-1 and Top-5 accuracy on the validation and test sets consistently improved as the number of training examples per class increased from 100 to 600. For instance, Top-1 validation accuracy rose from 38.8% with 100 examples to 58.1% with 600 examples.
- When trained on the full Kinetics-700-2020 dataset (with all classes having at least 700 examples), the I3D model achieved Top-1 accuracies of 59.3% on the validation set and 58.2% on the test set. Top-5 accuracies reached 82.0% (validation) and 80.9% (test).
- Crucially, the replenished Kinetics-700-2020 dataset yielded slightly higher performance compared to the original Kinetics-700 on an I3D model. The Top-1 test accuracy increased from 57.6% (Kinetics-700) to 58.2% (Kinetics-700-2020), and Top-5 test accuracy from 80.7% to 80.9%. This empirically validates the benefit of having a more balanced and complete dataset.
- The results indicate that even for a relatively compact model like I3D (approx. 12M parameters), an increased quantity of high-quality, balanced training data translates to tangible performance improvements in action recognition tasks.

These findings collectively demonstrate the successful achievement of the dataset replenishment objectives and underscore the importance of dataset maintenance and quality for advancing deep learning in video understanding.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The release of the Kinetics-700-2020 dataset and its accompanying analysis carries several significant implications and potential impacts for the field of human action recognition and broader computer vision research:

- **Addressing Dataset Decay:** The most immediate significance is the direct confrontation with the problem of dataset degradation, a pervasive issue for large-scale datasets derived from dynamic web sources like YouTube. By explicitly replenishing missing video clips, Kinetics-700-2020 ensures the continued viability and reproducibility of research conducted using this benchmark. This proactive maintenance sets an important precedent for the sustainable development of web-sourced datasets.
- **Enhanced Dataset Quality and Balance:** The commitment to a minimum of 700 clips per class significantly improves the statistical balance and robustness of the dataset. This ensures that models trained on Kinetics-700-2020 have a more equitable representation of all action categories, potentially leading to more generalized and less biased recognition systems, especially for previously "rare" classes. Improved balance can also mitigate issues of overfitting to dominant classes.
- **Reliable Benchmark for Model Development:** Kinetics-700-2020 provides a fresh, robust, and stable benchmark for the development and evaluation of new spatio-temporal action recognition models. Researchers can confidently compare novel architectures against established baselines, knowing they are working with a consistently available and well-curated data resource. The provided I3D baseline results offer a current performance reference point.
- **Validation of Data Quantity for Performance:** The benchmark results clearly demonstrate that increasing the number of training examples per class, even for a relatively simple I3D model, leads to consistent improvements in classification accuracy. This reinforces the long-standing understanding in deep learning that data quantity and quality are critical factors for model performance, even as architectural innovations progress. This quantitative evidence supports further investment in dataset curation.
- **Guidance for Future Dataset Curation:** The detailed methodology, including query augmentation, duplicate removal strategies, and multilingual considerations, offers valuable insights for future efforts in building and maintaining large-scale video datasets. The analysis of geographical diversity also highlights the impact of collection strategies on global representation, prompting considerations for broader and more inclusive data sourcing.
- **Foundation for Pre-training and Downstream Tasks:** The Kinetics datasets are frequently used for pre-training models that are then fine-tuned for various downstream tasks (e.g., untrimmed video understanding, video retrieval, self-supervised learning). A replenished and reliable Kinetics-700-2020 strengthens this foundation, ensuring that pre-trained models are based on the most current and comprehensive data available, which can positively impact performance across diverse applications.
- **Potential for Self-Supervised Learning:** The conclusion section suggests a forward-looking perspective, proposing that Kinetics, with the evolution of self-supervised learning techniques, could eventually become a downstream task itself. This indicates a potential shift in how such datasets are utilized, moving beyond purely supervised training to serve as evaluation platforms for models trained without explicit labels, thereby expanding the dataset's long-term utility.

In summary, Kinetics-700-2020 is a critical update that not only rectifies the practical challenges of web-sourced video datasets but also reinforces its role as a cornerstone for innovation in video action recognition. Its significance lies in ensuring the continued progress of research through reliable data, robust benchmarks, and insights into effective dataset curation.
