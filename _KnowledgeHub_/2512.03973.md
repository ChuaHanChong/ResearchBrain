# Guided Flow Policy: Learning from High-Value Actions in Offline Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Inria, DI-ENS, and Sorbonne Université developed Guided Flow Policy (GFP), an offline reinforcement learning algorithm that achieves state-of-the-art performance by using value-aware behavior cloning to selectively learn from high-value actions in datasets. It demonstrated substantial gains on tasks with suboptimal and noisy data across OGBench, D4RL, and Minari benchmarks.

## Problem

- Offline Reinforcement Learning (RL) policies often suffer from extrapolation error when querying actions outside the training data distribution, leading to unreliable Q-value estimates.
- Traditional behavior regularization methods in BRAC (Behavior-Regularized Actor–Critic) often treat all state-action pairs in a dataset equally, hindering the policy from identifying and exploiting high-value actions in suboptimal or noisy data.
- Integrating expressive generative models like flow and diffusion models into RL frameworks typically incurs high computational costs due to slow inference and the complexities of backpropagation through time.

## Method

- GFP employs a dual-policy ==BRAC== framework consisting of a multi-step Value-aware Behavior Cloning (VaBC) flow-matching policy and a distilled one-step actor.
- The VaBC policy is trained using a weighted flow-matching behavior cloning loss, where a guidance function prioritizes high-value actions from the dataset based on their estimated Q-values.
- The one-step actor policy is regularized (distilled) towards the value-aware actions generated by VaBC, avoiding the computational overhead of iterative sampling while benefiting from value-aware guidance.

## Results

- GFP achieved state-of-the-art performance across **144** tasks from OGBench, D4RL, and Minari benchmarks, with average normalized scores of **53.2** (OGBench), **63.0** (D4RL), and **74.1** (Minari).
- It demonstrated substantial gains on challenging datasets, for instance, scoring **63** on OGBench cube-double-noisy tasks compared to **38** for FQL and **20** for ReBRAC.
- The guidance function's temperature parameter η showed that moderate values yield the best performance, striking a balance between prioritizing high-value actions and maintaining sufficient action diversity for stable training.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Incorporating value-awareness directly into behavior regularization allows for selective learning from beneficial data trajectories, which is crucial for improving performance on suboptimal or noisy offline datasets.
> - A bidirectional guidance mechanism, where the generative VaBC policy regularizes the actor and the actor/critic refines VaBC's understanding of high-value actions, enables more stable and effective learning.
> - Expressive generative policies can be effectively integrated into offline RL by distilling them into efficient one-step actors, mitigating high computational costs without sacrificing the capacity to capture complex action distributions.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Offline Reinforcement Learning (RL) aims to derive effective policies from static datasets without further interaction with the environment. This paradigm is crucial for applications where online exploration is costly or unsafe, such as in robotics or logistics. A primary challenge in offline RL is the **extrapolation error**, which arises when an agent selects actions outside the data distribution, leading to unreliable Q-value estimates.

Existing research largely addresses this challenge through two main lines:
1. **Critic Learning without Out-of-Distribution Queries:** Methods like Implicit Q-Learning (IQL) focus on learning a critic that can robustly evaluate actions without relying on out-of-distribution value estimates.
2. **Behavior-Regularized Actor–Critic (BRAC) Methods:** This family of approaches mitigates extrapolation errors by constraining the learned policy to remain "close" to the behavior policy that generated the dataset. Minimalist BRAC variants often incorporate a behavior cloning (BC) loss, which encourages the policy to imitate actions present in the dataset. While this improves stability, it introduces a trade-off: strict regularization to a potentially suboptimal dataset can prevent the policy from discovering higher-reward actions, even if those actions are present within the dataset. Standard BC does not differentiate between high-value and low-value actions within the regularization component.

Recent advancements in generative models, particularly flow and diffusion models, have introduced more expressive policy representations, capable of capturing complex and multimodal action distributions. However, integrating these iterative generative models into RL frameworks often incurs high computational costs due to slow inference and the complexities of backpropagation through time (BPTT) for direct value optimization. Previous work, such as Flow Q-Learning (FQL), addressed some of these limitations by distilling a flow-matching BC model into a one-step policy, thereby enabling expressive policy learning without BPTT or iterative sampling at inference. However, a limitation of FQL and similar approaches is that their flow-based BC component, like standard BC, does not explicitly incorporate reward information to prioritize high-value actions during regularization.

The presented work, Guided Flow Policy (GFP), positions itself within the BRAC framework, specifically building upon and extending methods like FQL. It seeks to combine the expressiveness of flow models with value-aware regularization, addressing the limitation of indiscriminately imitating all state-action pairs in the dataset.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind the development of Guided Flow Policy (GFP) are:

1. **Address the limitations of value-agnostic behavior regularization in offline RL:** Traditional BRAC methods often regularize the learned policy by forcing it to stay close to the dataset's action distribution. However, this regularization typically treats all state-action pairs in the dataset equally, regardless of their associated reward or value. In datasets that are suboptimal or contain noisy and low-value demonstrations, this indiscriminate imitation can hinder the policy from identifying and exploiting more promising actions, even if those high-value actions exist within the dataset. The authors aim to introduce a mechanism that can distinguish and prioritize high-value actions for regularization.
2. **Integrate value awareness into expressive generative policies without computational overhead:** While flow and diffusion models offer greater expressivity for representing complex action distributions compared to traditional Gaussian policies, their iterative nature often leads to high computational costs during training (due to BPTT for value optimization) and inference. Previous work like FQL attempted to mitigate this by distilling the flow policy into a one-step actor. The authors' motivation is to incorporate reward or value information directly into the flow-matching behavior cloning process itself, thereby creating a "value-aware" generative policy, without reintroducing the BPTT challenges associated with directly optimizing the Q-values of iterative policy outputs.
3. **Develop a robust and efficient offline RL algorithm capable of state-of-the-art performance across diverse and challenging benchmarks:** The motivation is to create an algorithm that can effectively handle various types of offline RL tasks, including those with suboptimal or noisy data, and demonstrate strong performance compared to existing methods. This involves a design that balances stability, exploration (within the dataset's valuable support), and exploitation of high-reward actions.
4. **Investigate the role of hyperparameter choices and implementation details:** The authors also aim to highlight the critical impact of hyperparameter tuning and subtle implementation details on the performance of offline RL algorithms, by re-evaluating existing state-of-the-art methods under carefully optimized settings. This provides a more robust context for comparing GFP's performance.

In essence, GFP is motivated by the need for offline RL policies to learn selectively from beneficial data trajectories while leveraging the modeling capacity of generative policies, all within a computationally practical framework.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Guided Flow Policy (GFP) is a dual-policy BRAC framework that employs a bidirectional guidance mechanism between a multi-step flow-matching policy, termed Value-aware Behavior Cloning (VaBC), and a distilled one-step actor. The overall architecture includes a critic, a one-step actor, and the VaBC policy. The training process involves three interconnected steps:

**1. Learning the Critic Qϕ:** The critic network Qϕ is trained to approximate the state-action value function. It utilizes a standard Bellman mean-squared error (MSE) loss: LC (ϕ) = E (s,a,r,s′)∼D, a′∼πθ(·|s′) [Qϕ (s, a) − (r + γQ ¯ϕ (s′, a′))]2 where Q ¯ϕ is a target Q-network updated slowly via Polyak averaging for stability.

The authors also considered an optional, more conservative variant of the Bellman target, yVaBC (s, r, s′), which averages Q-value estimates from both the actor's action (μθ (s′, z)) and the VaBC policy's action (μω (s′, z)): yVaBC (s, r, s′) = r +γ2 [Q ¯ϕ (s′, μθ (s′, z)) + Q ¯ϕ (s′, μω (s′, z))]

This alternative target is specifically used in environments where it demonstrates performance improvements (e.g., cube and humanoidmaze-medium tasks).

**2. Learning the Distilled One-Step Actor πθ:** The actor policy πθ is designed as a one-step policy, meaning it produces an action directly without iterative sampling, making inference efficient. It is trained using behavior-regularized policy gradients to maximize the Q-function while being regularized (distilled) towards the value-aware actions generated by the VaBC policy. The actor loss is defined as: LA (θ) = E s∼D,z∼N (0,Id) [ − λQϕ (s, μθ (s, z)) + α∥μθ (s, z) − μω (s, z)∥22] Here, μθ (s, z) is an action sampled from the actor and μω (s, z) is an action sampled from the VaBC policy (which is obtained by integrating a flow from noise z). The term `λ` is a normalization factor based on the average absolute Q-value to ensure consistent scaling. The hyperparameter `α` controls the balance between maximizing Q-values and staying close to the VaBC policy. This distillation encourages the actor to select actions that are both high-value and aligned with the support of high-value behaviors within the dataset, as identified by VaBC. Crucially, this approach avoids BPTT for the actor, as it directly produces actions.

**3. Learning the Value-aware Behavior Cloning (VaBC) Policy πω:** The VaBC policy πω is a multi-step flow-matching policy. It is trained using a *weighted* flow-matching behavior cloning loss, where the weights are determined by a "guidance function" g η. This mechanism allows VaBC to selectively prioritize cloning high-value actions from the dataset. The loss is: LVaBC (ω) = E (s,a)∼D,ϵ∼N (0,Id),t∼U [g η (s, a) ∥vω (t, s, at) − (a − ϵ)∥22] where vω is the velocity field learned by flow matching, at is a linear interpolation between noise ϵ and dataset action a, and (a - ϵ) is the target velocity. The **guidance function g η (s, a)** is the core of VaBC's value awareness: g η (s, a) := exp(λ ηQϕ (s, a)) exp(λ ηQϕ (s, a))
- exp(λ ηQϕ (s, μθ (s, z))) This function performs a soft-max comparison between the Q-value of a dataset action (Qϕ (s, a)) and the Q-value of an action proposed by the actor (Qϕ (s, μθ (s, z))). If the dataset action `a` has a higher Q-value, g η (s, a) will be greater than 0.5, giving more weight to cloning it. Conversely, if the actor's proposed action is better, the weight for the dataset action is reduced. This ensures that VaBC focuses on learning from the more promising transitions within the dataset. The parameter `η` is a temperature hyperparameter controlling the sharpness of this weighting. Smaller `η` values make the filtering more selective (stronger filtering of low-value actions), while larger `η` values smooth the weighting, including more diverse actions. Because g η (s, a) is bounded between 0 and 1, it prevents degeneracy during early training when the critic might be unreliable.

**Bidirectional Guidance:** The key aspect of GFP is the bidirectional guidance:
- VaBC regularizes the actor (πθ) by providing a distribution of *value-aware* high-quality actions to stay close to, without requiring the actor to perform iterative sampling.
- The actor (πθ) and the critic (Qϕ) in turn guide VaBC by providing the Q-values used in the g η weighting function, ensuring that VaBC focuses its cloning efforts on the most promising actions according to the current value estimates. This joint training ensures a dynamic adaptation between the actor's exploitation drive and VaBC's value-aware regularization.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The authors conducted extensive experiments across 144 state-based and pixel-based tasks from three benchmarks: OGBench, Minari, and D4RL. The evaluation involved comparisons against 10 prior methods for a subset of tasks and detailed comparisons against three strong baselines (FQL, ReBRAC, and IQL) across the full suite of tasks.

**1. Overall Performance and State-of-the-Art Results:**
- GFP consistently achieved state-of-the-art performance across the 144 tasks.
- On the 50 OGBench tasks previously evaluated by FQL, GFP demonstrated improved performance compared to a wide range of prior works.
- Across the larger set of 105 OGBench tasks, 18 D4RL tasks, and 21 Minari tasks, GFP's actor policy (πθ) yielded superior or competitive results. The average normalized scores across benchmarks were: 53.2 for OGBench, 63.0 for D4RL, and 74.1 for Minari.

**2. Gains on Suboptimal and Challenging Datasets:**
- GFP demonstrated substantial performance gains on tasks characterized by suboptimal, noisy, or challenging data. For instance:
 - On OGBench cube-double-noisy datasets, GFP achieved an average score of 63, compared to 38 for FQL and 20 for ReBRAC.
 - On cube-triple-noisy datasets, GFP scored 24, while FQL achieved 4 and ReBRAC scored 5.
 - Significant improvements were also observed in locomotion tasks like humanoidmaze-large-navigate (GFP: 18 vs. FQL: 7, ReBRAC: 13) and manipulation tasks such as cube-triple-play (GFP: 16 vs. FQL: 4, ReBRAC: 3).
- These results suggest that the value-aware regularization mechanism of GFP is particularly effective when the dataset contains a mix of high and low-quality demonstrations.

**3. Value-Aware Behavior Cloning (VaBC) Policy Performance:**
- The VaBC policy (πω), which serves as the regularization component, also exhibited strong performance across benchmarks, even though it is not the primary policy for deployment. Its average normalized scores were 33.1 on OGBench, 56.5 on D4RL, and 71.6 on Minari. This outcome confirms its role as an effective, in-sample, value-aware regularizer for the main actor.

**4. Analysis of the Temperature Parameter (η):**
- Experiments on challenging OGBench tasks (e.g., puzzle-4x4-noisy, cube-double-noisy) investigated the impact of the temperature parameter `η` in the guidance function.
- Moderate values of `η` were found to yield the best performance, striking a balance between prioritizing high-value actions and maintaining sufficient regularization and action diversity.
- Very low `η` values led to over-concentration on narrow action sets, causing training instability and potential critic degradation by pushing the actor out of the distribution.
- Very high `η` values failed to provide adequate filtering of suboptimal actions, resulting in reduced performance.

**5. Re-evaluation of Prior Works:**
- The authors re-evaluated ReBRAC and FQL on OGBench tasks and found that task-specific hyperparameter tuning (e.g., discount factor γ, minibatch size B, critic aggregation scheme) significantly improved their reported scores. This re-evaluation served to establish a fair and optimized baseline for comparison, emphasizing the importance of detailed implementation and tuning.

**6. Sensitivity Analysis to α and η:**
- A sensitivity analysis across 12 tasks indicated that the BC coefficient `α` remains the most critical hyperparameter for GFP, similar to other BRAC methods. GFP was found to be less sensitive to the precise tuning of the temperature parameter `η`, which justifies the authors' hyperparameter search methodology.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of Guided Flow Policy (GFP) carries several significances and potential impacts on the field of offline Reinforcement Learning:

1. **Advancement in Behavior Regularization:** GFP demonstrates a significant advancement in behavior regularization strategies for offline RL. By introducing value-awareness directly into the regularization component through Weighted Flow-Matching Behavior Cloning (VaBC), it addresses a fundamental limitation of traditional BRAC approaches that indiscriminately regularize towards all dataset actions. This is particularly impactful for suboptimal or noisy datasets, where distinguishing between high-value and low-value behaviors is crucial for learning effective policies.
2. **Effective Integration of Generative Models:** The work effectively leverages the expressiveness of flow models to capture complex, multimodal action distributions without incurring the high computational costs associated with backpropagation through time (BPTT) or slow inference typical of iterative generative policies. This is achieved through the distillation of the multi-step VaBC policy into an efficient one-step actor, while still allowing the generative model to influence the learning process through value-aware regularization. This methodology can inform future research on how to practically incorporate powerful generative models into RL pipelines.
3. **Robustness on Challenging Datasets:** GFP's superior performance on suboptimal, noisy, and challenging environments, as demonstrated across extensive benchmarks, indicates its potential for real-world applications where data quality can be heterogeneous. This robustness is critical for domains like robotics and logistics, where collecting perfectly optimal and comprehensive datasets is often impractical.
4. **Bidirectional Guidance as a Novel Interaction Mechanism:** The proposed bidirectional guidance mechanism between the VaBC policy and the one-step actor is a novel contribution. It establishes a synergistic relationship where VaBC provides targeted regularization based on value, and the actor/critic, in turn, refines VaBC's understanding of high-value actions. This dynamic interaction leads to more stable and effective learning than a unidirectional approach.
5. **Emphasis on Implementation Details:** The retrospective analysis and re-evaluation of prior state-of-the-art algorithms highlight the critical role of hyperparameters and subtle implementation choices in achieving strong offline RL performance. This contributes to the broader understanding and reproducibility within the research community, encouraging more rigorous evaluation practices.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Enhanced Offline Policy Learning:** GFP's ability to selectively learn from high-value actions suggests that policies trained with this method could exhibit higher performance and reliability, especially in scenarios with imperfect data.
- **Broader Applicability:** The method's robustness to suboptimal data could broaden the applicability of offline RL to real-world problems where expert demonstrations are scarce, expensive, or imperfect.
- **Inspiration for Future Research:** The framework provides a foundation for future research exploring alternative guidance functions, different generative models, or extensions to settings with weaker or sparse reward signals. Reducing reliance on a perfectly accurate critic, especially in very challenging environments, remains an area for further investigation.

In conclusion, GFP represents a step towards more intelligent and data-efficient offline RL algorithms by integrating value-aware learning into expressive policy representations, offering a robust solution for extracting high-performance policies from diverse and often imperfect static datasets.
