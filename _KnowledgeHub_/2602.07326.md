# Why Look at It at All?: Vision-Free Multifingered Blind Grasping Using Uniaxial Fingertip Force Sensing

> [!summary] Summary
> Sogang University researchers present a vision-free multifingered blind grasping policy that operates using only uniaxial fingertip force sensing and joint proprioception. The policy, trained through a teacher-student framework in simulation, achieved a 98.3% real-world success rate across 18 diverse objects, demonstrating robust generalization to novel items.

## Problem

- Advanced robotic manipulation systems often rely on complex, costly, fragile, and difficult-to-integrate multimodal sensors, limiting their scalability and widespread adoption in industrial settings.
- Vision-based systems are vulnerable to environmental challenges like occlusions, lighting variations, and object properties (e.g., transparent or reflective materials).
- Existing force sensing solutions are either expensive and bulky (multi-axis) or lack precision and repeatability (low-cost FSRs), while complex tactile arrays are hard to miniaturize.

## Method

- A ==teacher-student training== framework is developed in simulation: an ==RL teacher== uses privileged observations and a custom ==force-incentive reward== to learn blind grasping behaviors, while an ==IL student== distills this knowledge.
- The student policy, implemented as a ==Transformer network==, operates solely on minimal, deployable observations: ==9-DoF joint positions== and ==3 uniaxial fingertip forces==.
- The system uses a custom three-fingered gripper with robust, low-cost ==uniaxial force sensors== embedded in compliant 3D-printed fingertips, integrated with an embedded policy for real-world deployment.

## Results

- The proposed policy achieved an overall **98.3%** grasp success rate on real hardware across **18** diverse objects, including **100.0%** on **6** in-distribution objects and **97.5%** on **12** out-of-distribution objects.
- The method substantially outperformed an RL baseline trained directly with partial observations (**37.2%** success rate) and closely approached the performance of an IL policy derived from human teleoperation (**100%** success).
- Ablation studies confirmed the critical roles of the force-incentive reward, stochastic teacher, and demonstration curation, demonstrating significant performance degradation when these components were omitted.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Reliable multifingered blind grasping is achievable with an extremely minimal sensing suite (uniaxial force feedback and joint proprioception), significantly reducing hardware complexity, cost, and fragility.
> - A teacher-student framework, utilizing a privileged-observation RL teacher and a partial-observation IL student, effectively facilitates robust sim-to-real transfer for complex manipulation tasks without real-world demonstrations.
> - Key design choices, including a stochastic teacher for trajectory diversity, meticulous demonstration curation, and a sensor-guided incentive reward in the teacher's training, are crucial for generating a robust and generalized student policy.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Recent advancements in robotic manipulation have largely leveraged diverse sensing modalities, including vision, tactile, force, visual-tactile, and proprioceptive sensors, to enable dexterous and adaptive control for multifingered grippers. While these multimodal systems offer high dexterity, their scalability and adoption in real-world industrial settings are often constrained by cost, fragility, and integration complexity.

To mitigate these limitations, some research efforts have explored simplified sensor modalities. However, vision-based systems remain vulnerable to occlusions, lighting variations, and inconsistent visibility. Tactile sensors, particularly visual-tactile variants, often face challenges with miniaturization, sensitivity to illumination, and geometric constraints. Multi-axis force sensors are typically expensive and bulky, while low-cost force-sensing resistors (FSRs) suffer from non-linearity and poor repeatability. Attempts at multimodal fusion to enhance robustness often inherit the bulkiness and fragility of their components.

This paper positions itself within the domain of vision-free (blind) manipulation, which seeks to perform robotic tasks relying solely on proprioceptive or force feedback. Prior work in this area has explored various forms of contact or force sensing, but often utilized more complex tactile arrays or model-dependent frameworks that limit scalability and generalization. The current work specifically addresses the need for a truly minimal sensing paradigm that is low-cost, robust, simple, geometry-flexible, and unaffected by visual conditions, which is particularly relevant for industrial automation. It distinguishes itself by demonstrating reliable multifingered grasping using only uniaxial fingertip force feedback and joint proprioception, representing a significant reduction in sensory requirements compared to much of the existing research.

Furthermore, from a learning perspective, the paper employs a teacher-student framework, which is a method designed to bridge the gap between privileged (simulation-only) and partial (real-world deployable) observations. This approach, where a reinforcement-learned teacher provides demonstrations for an imitation-learned student, builds upon recent trends in imitation learning and sim-to-real transfer, particularly those using transformer architectures to capture temporal dependencies in sequential data. By focusing on minimal, deployable sensing, this research aims to overcome some of the practical challenges associated with complex sensor integration and the sim-to-real gap, offering a more streamlined and cost-effective solution for robust grasping.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the inherent challenges associated with deploying advanced multimodal sensing systems in real-world robotic manipulation tasks. While sensor fusion capabilities have enabled dexterous manipulation, the associated costs, fragility, and complexities of integration often limit their scalability and widespread adoption, particularly in industrial contexts. Current simplified sensing approaches, such as vision-only systems, struggle with environmental variability like occlusions and lighting changes. Tactile sensors, while providing rich local contact information, are often difficult to miniaturize, sensitive to illumination, or suffer from fabrication complexities. Multi-axis force sensors are expensive and bulky, while their low-cost alternatives (e.g., FSRs) lack precision and repeatability. The paper identifies a clear need for a sensing paradigm that is simultaneously low-cost, robust, simple, adaptable to various geometries, and entirely independent of visual conditions, specifically for vision-free and contact-based grasping tasks.

Against this background, the key objectives of this work are articulated as follows:
1. **Demonstrate Reliable Multifingered Grasping with Minimal Sensing:** The central objective is to establish that robust and reliable multifingered grasping can be achieved by exclusively utilizing uniaxial fingertip force feedback and joint proprioception. This explicitly excludes reliance on vision, multi-axis tactile sensing, or other high-resolution sensor arrays, thereby aiming to significantly reduce sensing and integration requirements for real-world robotic deployment.
2. **Develop an Efficient Teacher-Student Training Pipeline for Blind Grasping:** The research aims to introduce a novel and efficient training framework tailored for blind grasping scenarios. This pipeline involves:
 - Training a reinforcement-learned (RL) teacher policy that leverages privileged, simulation-only observations and a carefully designed reward function to encourage behaviors dependent on deployable partial observations.
 - Distilling the learned behaviors from the teacher into a transformer-based student policy through imitation learning (IL). The student policy is designed to operate solely on the minimal sensing modalities available during real-world deployment (uniaxial force and proprioception). The pipeline aims to curate demonstrations from successful grasps exhibiting rich uniaxial force interactions.
3. **Validate Real-World Robustness and Generalization:** A critical objective is to rigorously validate the proposed approach on physical hardware. This involves evaluating the policy's performance across a diverse set of objects, encompassing both in-distribution (ID) and out-of-distribution (OOD) cases, to demonstrate strong robustness and generalization capabilities beyond the initial simulation training distribution. This validation serves to confirm the practical viability and efficacy of the minimal sensing approach in real-world scenarios.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology employs a teacher-student training framework to enable vision-free multifingered blind grasping. All policy training is conducted in the IsaacLab simulation environment, which supports large-scale parallelization.

**Hardware Setup:** The real-world system utilizes a custom three-fingered gripper, an adaptation of the open-source D’Claw manipulator. This gripper is mounted on a stationary frame, featuring three 3-DoF fingers (total 9-DoF) actuated by servo motors. Each fingertip is equipped with a custom uniaxial force sensor, designed for precise and continuous force estimation while being robust, low-cost, and compact. The fingertips themselves are 3D-printed with elastic material to ensure compliance and stable contact.

**A. Teacher Policy Training:**
The teacher policy ($\pi_t$) is trained using Reinforcement Learning (RL) in simulation. Its objective is to execute a blind grasp by initiating contact, establishing a stable grasp, and smoothly lifting an object from an initial non-contact state.
- **Training Objects:** The teacher is trained on a set of 18 geometric objects (one sphere, two cuboids, one capsule, two cylinders), each in three distinct sizes, designed to fit within the gripper's workspace.
- **Privileged Observations ($o_{priv_t}$):** To facilitate sample-efficient and stable RL, the teacher receives a high-dimensional (95-vector) "privileged" observation. This includes joint positions and velocities, fingertip pose, object pose and velocities, three-axis fingertip contact forces, planar distance between the object and gripper center, uniaxial fingertip forces (derived by projecting three-axis forces onto the fingertip's local z-axis and thresholding), 6D fingertip wrench, and the previous action. Gaussian noise is added to joint angles and fingertip forces to enhance robustness and sim-to-real transfer.
- **Actions:** The policy outputs relative joint position changes ($\Delta\theta_t \in R^9$) at 20 Hz, clipped to a specified range to prevent unrealistic magnitudes.
- **Episode Setup:** Episodes begin from a fixed initial joint configuration, with randomized joint offsets and object xy positions to encourage exploration and generalization. Episodes terminate after 10 seconds or if the object moves significantly from the gripper center.
- **Reward Function:** A comprehensive reward function guides the teacher's learning, comprising:
 - **Task Reward ($r_t$):** Promotes contact initiation and steady lifting, increasing as the object's height approaches a target and its planar distance to the gripper center decreases. It is only active when at least one fingertip is in contact and the object remains within the workspace.
 - **Incentive Reward ($r_i$):** A binary signal that encourages simultaneous contact of all three fingertips, leveraging uniaxial force feedback.
 - **Penalty Terms:** Penalties are applied for joint-limit violations, large action magnitudes, and rapid action-rate changes to promote safe and smooth control.
- **Domain Randomization:** To further enhance robustness and sim-to-real transfer, various parameters are randomized during training, including initial joint offsets, object positions, object and robot friction, object mass, actuator gains (P/D), and external disturbance forces applied to the object.
- **RL Algorithm:** The teacher policy is trained using Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) with MLP networks for both actor and critic. Training is performed in 9000 parallel environments over approximately 3 hours.

**B. Student Policy Training:**
The student policy ($\pi_s$) is trained via Imitation Learning (IL), specifically behavioral cloning (BC), to distill the teacher's knowledge into a policy that operates on minimal, deployable observations.
- **Partial Observations ($o_t$):** The student's observation space is limited to joint positions (9-DoF) and uniaxial fingertip forces (3 sensors), totaling a 12-dimensional vector. This excludes all privileged information used by the teacher.
- **Demonstration Collection:** High-quality demonstrations are collected by deploying the trained teacher policy in the simulation, with external disturbance forces disabled. Teacher actions and corresponding student observations are logged. A crucial step is **demonstration curation**, where only "successful" episodes are selected. Success is defined by sustained task reward above a threshold for a minimum duration and a cumulative uniaxial fingertip contact force exceeding a specific value. This process yielded 89,500 curated demonstrations.
- **Distillation Architecture:** The student policy is implemented as a 6-layer Transformer network, incorporating an embedding size of 512 and 8 attention heads, followed by an MLP head to predict actions. A context length of 30 past observations is used to capture temporal dependencies.
- **Loss Function:** BC minimizes the Mean Squared Error (MSE) between the student's predicted actions and the recorded teacher actions. Training completes in approximately 3 hours.

**C. Experimental Evaluation:**
The proposed blind grasping policy is evaluated on real hardware. An ablation study analyzes the impact of key design choices (stochastic teacher, demonstration curation, incentive reward) on 6 ID objects. A comprehensive evaluation is then performed on 18 real-world objects (6 ID, 12 OOD), encompassing diverse shapes, materials, and textures. The system operates at 20 Hz, mapping uniaxial force and proprioceptive inputs to joint angle changes, without any additional calibration or zero-shot vision. For comparative analysis, the proposed method is benchmarked against a lower-bound RL policy trained directly on partial observations (RL w/ $o_t$) and an upper-bound IL policy trained from human teleoperated demonstrations (IL Human), collected using a Manus Quantum Metaglove.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several findings derived from ablation studies, real-world grasping experiments, and comparative analyses.

**A. Ablation Study:**
An ablation study on six in-distribution (ID) objects provided insights into the efficacy of specific components of the proposed learning framework:
- **Observation Richness:** The RL policy trained directly with partial observations (RL w/ $o_t$) exhibited an average success rate of 36.7%, significantly lower than the proposed student policy (100%). This suggests that partial observations alone are insufficient for direct RL convergence in this complex task and that the distillation of knowledge from a teacher trained with privileged observations is effective for transferring benefits.
- **Reward Design:** The student policy trained from a teacher without the force-incentive reward ($r_i$) achieved an average success rate of 23.3%, performing worse than the direct RL baseline. This indicates that the incentive reward ($r_i$) is critical for the teacher to generate meaningful force-interaction behaviors suitable for distillation, underscoring the importance of sensor-guided reward design.
- **Object Geometry Influence:** Success rates varied across different geometries, with objects like Cylinder A (88%) and Cuboid A (82%) showing higher robustness, while Capsule (30%) and Sphere (58%) proved more challenging. This highlights the impact of geometric factors such as aspect ratio and surface curvature on grasping difficulty.
- **Stochastic Teacher and Curation:** The proposed method, which combines a stochastic teacher with demonstration curation, achieved a 100% success rate on the ID objects in the ablation study. Variants lacking stochasticity (Student w/o Stochastic Teacher, 91.7%) or curation (Student w/o Curation, 81.7%) showed performance degradation, particularly on difficult objects. This confirms that the stochastic teacher provides trajectory diversity, while curation ensures high-quality force interactions, both contributing to a generalized and robust student policy.

**B. Real-World Grasping Experiments:**
The proposed blind grasping policy was evaluated on real hardware across 18 objects (6 ID and 12 OOD), demonstrating robust performance:
- **Overall Success Rate:** The policy achieved an overall grasp success rate of 98.3%.
- **In-Distribution Performance:** It achieved a 100.0% success rate on all 6 ID objects, which were 3D-printed replicas of the medium-sized training shapes.
- **Out-of-Distribution Generalization:** The policy maintained a high success rate of 97.5% on the 12 OOD objects, which included real-world items with diverse shapes, materials, and textures (e.g., wine glass, foam brick, Rubik’s Cube).
- **Specific Challenges:** The only observed exception was the mustard bottle, which had a 70% success rate. This was attributed to its length (21 cm) exceeding the object size range (10 cm) encountered during simulation training.
- **Factors Contributing to Success:** The authors attribute the robust performance to the low-dimensional observation space, which inherently reduces the sim-to-real gap, enabling the agent to learn robust patterns. Additionally, continuous force feedback allowed for dynamic adaptation to changing contact conditions, and the use of soft elastic fingertips enhanced friction and contact area for stable grasping.

**C. Comparison Study:**
A comparative analysis benchmarked the proposed method against an RL policy with partial observations (RL w/ $o_t$) as a lower bound and an IL policy derived from human teleoperation (IL Human) as an upper bound:
- **Lower Bound Performance:** The RL w/ $o_t$ policy achieved a significantly lower average success rate of 37.2% across the 18 objects, indicating poor generalization across diverse shapes and materials when trained solely on partial observations without privileged information. It recorded 0% success on several objects.
- **Upper Bound Performance:** The IL Human policy achieved a 100% success rate on all 18 objects, demonstrating the potential for optimal performance with high-quality, human-generated demonstrations and full visual feedback. However, this approach entails practical challenges such as sensor drift, labor-intensive manual curation, and the need for iterative data collection.
- **Proposed Method Performance:** The proposed teacher-student framework achieved an average success rate of 98.3%. This performance substantially exceeded the RL w/ $o_t$ baseline and closely approached the IL Human upper bound. The authors highlight that this near-human performance was achieved despite being trained entirely in simulation, thereby avoiding the costs and scalability limitations of manual teleoperation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a framework for multifingered blind grasping that could significantly influence the field of robotic manipulation, particularly in industrial and practical deployment scenarios.

**Reduced Sensing and Integration Complexity:** The work demonstrates that reliable multifingered grasping can be achieved with an extremely minimal sensing suite, relying solely on uniaxial fingertip force feedback and joint proprioception. This eliminates the need for expensive, fragile, and complex vision systems or multi-axis/high-resolution tactile sensors. This simplification can drastically reduce the cost, manufacturing complexity, and integration challenges associated with robotic grippers, making advanced manipulation capabilities more accessible for a broader range of applications.

**Enhanced Robustness and Generalization:** The high real-world success rate of 98.3% across a diverse set of 18 objects, including both in-distribution and out-of-distribution cases, highlights the strong robustness and generalization capabilities of the proposed policy. This implies that the system can adapt to novel objects with varying shapes, materials, and textures without explicit prior knowledge or specific calibration, which is a critical requirement for flexible automation. The vision-free nature of the operation renders it insensitive to common environmental challenges such as lighting variations, occlusions, and the presence of transparent or reflective objects, which often hinder vision-based systems.

**Effective Sim-to-Real Transfer and Learning Paradigm:** The teacher-student framework, leveraging a privileged-observation RL teacher to distill knowledge into a partial-observation IL student, proves to be highly effective for robust sim-to-real transfer. The findings underscore the importance of a stochastic teacher for trajectory diversity, careful demonstration curation for quality, and a sensor-guided incentive reward in the teacher's training for generating meaningful contact behaviors. This methodology provides a blueprint for efficiently training complex manipulation skills in simulation and deploying them reliably in the real world with minimal sensing requirements.

**Practical Implications for Industrial Automation:** The proposed blind grasping policy is well-suited for general-purpose pick-and-place and bin-picking tasks in logistics and manufacturing. Its ability to grasp diverse objects, including irregular and deformable ones, using only onboard proprioception and uniaxial force, makes it highly adaptable to various industrial scenarios where object variety is high and external sensing conditions might be challenging. The gripper's design as a self-contained module with an embedded policy allows for straightforward integration with different robot arms, potentially broadening the applicability of dexterous manipulation beyond highly specialized systems.

**Foundation for Future Research:** While demonstrating robust performance, the work also identifies limitations, such as sensitivity to initial object position and current restriction to single-object scenes. These point towards future research avenues, including integrating arm-hand coordination for object re-centering and higher-level vision for target-conditioned grasping in cluttered environments. This research thus establishes a strong foundation for developing more advanced and comprehensive robotic manipulation systems by addressing the fundamental challenge of robust, minimal-sensing grasping.
