# MolmoAct2: Action Reasoning Models for Real-world Deployment

> [!summary] Summary
> MolmoAct2, developed by the Allen Institute for AI and the University of Washington, is a fully open-source action reasoning model designed for real-world robot deployment, enhancing generalizability and efficiency. It incorporates an embodied reasoning VLM backbone and a continuous action expert, achieving up to 87.1% success on real-world DROID tasks with unseen objects and a 2.42x speedup in control rate compared to unoptimized inference.

## Problem

- Existing frontier vision-language-action (VLA) models are often closed systems, hindering scientific reproduction and adaptation due to proprietary data and weights.
- Current general-purpose VLMs lack specialized embodied skills for robotic control, such as accurate spatial understanding and cross-view object tracking.
- Reasoning-augmented policies for robots suffer from prohibitive inference latency, making them unsuitable for real-time, closed-loop control in dynamic environments.

## Method

- Developed **Molmo2-ER**, an ==embodied reasoning VLM backbone==, specifically optimized for robot perception skills, and curated diverse, open robot demonstration datasets, including large-scale bimanual data.
- Created ==OpenFAST Tokenizer== for efficient discrete action representation and integrated a continuous action expert with a novel ==per-layer KV connection== to the VLM's visual-language attention state.
- Introduced **MolmoAct2**-Think, an adaptive-depth reasoning variant that regenerates depth codes only for dynamic scene regions, alongside inference optimizations like CUDA Graphs to reduce latency.

## Results

- Molmo2-ER achieved an average score of **63.8%** on embodied reasoning benchmarks, outperforming baseline VLMs on 9 out of 13 tasks and improving **17%** over its base Molmo2.
- MolmoAct2-DROID demonstrated **87.1%** average success on five real-world manipulation tasks with unseen objects (a 38.7 percentage point gain over baseline) and MolmoAct2-SO100/101 achieved **56.7%** success on novel pick-and-place tasks.
- Inference optimizations, including caching and CUDA Graphs, resulted in up to a **2.42x** speedup for MolmoAct2, boosting its control rate to 55.79 Hz, and MolmoAct2-Think saw a **1.58x** speedup to 12.71 Hz.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Specializing a VLM backbone for embodied perception skills, through a 'specialize-then-rehearse' approach on a curated corpus, significantly enhances the model's ability to learn robot actions.
> - A per-layer KV connection effectively conditions a continuous action expert on the VLM's deep, hierarchical visual-language context, enabling more grounded and precise robot control without replacing the backbone.
> - Adaptive depth reasoning, which selectively updates depth information only for changing scene areas, provides geometric grounding and interpretability while substantially reducing inference latency for real-time operation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of generalist robot controllers capable of performing diverse tasks in varied real-world environments is a central goal in robotics research. Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models represent a prominent paradigm towards achieving this, integrating perception, language understanding, and physical action within a unified framework. These models typically leverage large vision-language models (VLMs), pre-trained on vast multimodal web data, and then adapt them to robotic datasets to enable visuomotor control.

However, several limitations exist in the current landscape of VLA models, impeding their practical deployment. Many frontier VLA models are proprietary, meaning their training data, architectures, and weights are not publicly accessible. This lack of openness restricts scientific reproducibility, hinders community-driven development, and limits their adaptability by practitioners. Open-weight alternatives, while available, often require specialized or expensive hardware platforms, rendering them inaccessible to many academic labs and independent researchers. Furthermore, models that incorporate reasoning processes to enhance performance, such as those predicting intermediate spatial representations or goal images, frequently incur substantial inference latency. This latency can make them unsuitable for real-time closed-loop control, where prompt action is critical. Even after task-specific fine-tuning, the achieved success rates often remain below the threshold required for dependable real-world use.

MolmoAct2 builds upon prior work in this domain, including its predecessor MolmoAct, which explored the integration of grounded spatial reasoning. It aims to address the identified limitations by focusing on five specific axes: developing a robust and specialized VLM backbone for embodied reasoning, introducing comprehensive open-source robot datasets, creating an open and multi-embodiment action tokenizer, designing a VLA architecture that efficiently bridges discrete VLM reasoning with continuous robot control, and implementing an adaptive reasoning mechanism to reduce inference latency. By providing a fully open-source solution that emphasizes performance, efficiency, and accessibility across diverse robot platforms, MolmoAct2 seeks to advance the field towards deployable, general-purpose robot intelligence.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of this research is to develop and release MolmoAct2, an action reasoning model designed for practical, real-world robotic deployment. This model aims to be fully open-source, deployable across multiple embodiments, achieve high performance, and incorporate efficient, interpretable reasoning capabilities. The motivation for these objectives stems directly from the limitations prevalent in existing VLA models, as identified in the broader research landscape.

Specifically, the key objectives and their underlying motivations are detailed along five axes:

1. **Develop a Stronger Open Embodied-Reasoning VLM Backbone:**
 - **Motivation:** Existing general-purpose VLMs are primarily optimized for semantic image understanding. However, robotic control necessitates a nuanced understanding of metric distances, free space, object tracking across views, and scene geometry. These embodied reasoning skills are often not adequately covered by standard VLM training.
 - **Objective:** To train and release Molmo2-ER, a VLM specialized for spatial and embodied reasoning. This involves curating a 3.3 million-sample corpus focused on embodied skills and utilizing a "specialize-then-rehearse" training recipe to inject these capabilities into a base VLM (Molmo2).

2. **Release New Open-Source Training Datasets:**
 - **Motivation:** The availability of large-scale, high-quality, and diverse robot datasets is crucial for training generalist VLA models. Current open robot datasets are often fragmented across embodiments, inconsistent in quality, or insufficient in scale and annotation fidelity to support reliable multi-embodiment action learning for real-world deployment. Crucially, a gap existed for high-quality bimanual manipulation data on accessible platforms.
 - **Objective:** To collect, filter, and curate three new action datasets targeting low-to-medium cost robot platforms:
 - **MolmoAct2-BimanualYAM Dataset:** A large dataset (720 hours) of teleoperated bimanual trajectories on a custom YAM setup, designed for task repeatability and diversity.
 - **MolmoAct2-DROID Dataset:** A quality-filtered subset of the DROID dataset for Franka robots.
 - **MolmoAct2-SO100/101 Dataset:** A quality-filtered subset of community-sourced SO-100/101 data.
 - An additional objective for the filtered datasets is to re-annotate language instructions to improve diversity and accuracy.

3. **Train and Release an Open-Source Multi-Embodiment Action Tokenizer:**
 - **Motivation:** Continuous robot actions, varying across embodiments and control rates, cannot be directly integrated into language model training streams. Existing action tokenizers are often closed-source, tied to specific action spaces, or lack comprehensive documentation, hindering reproducibility and multi-embodiment VLA training.
 - **Objective:** To introduce MolmoAct2-FAST Tokenizer, an open-weight and open-data implementation following the FAST framework. This tokenizer compresses one second of 32-dimensional continuous actions into a compact discrete sequence, trained on millions of trajectories across five diverse embodiments.

4. **Design a New VLA Architecture:**
 - **Motivation:** While discrete-token VLMs provide strong reasoning grounding, their output space is not directly suited for the continuous, high-frequency trajectories required for robot control. A new architecture is needed to effectively connect the discrete reasoning capacity of the VLM with the generation of smooth continuous actions.
 - **Objective:** To design a novel VLA architecture that integrates a DiT-style flow-matching continuous-action expert with the discrete-token VLM backbone. The key innovation is using *per-layer Key-Value (KV) conditioning*, where each layer of the action expert conditions on the keys and values from the corresponding VLM layer, providing dense access to visual-language attention states.

5. **Propose a New Adaptive Reasoning Paradigm for Efficient Inference:**
 - **Motivation:** Prior reasoning-based VLAs, such as MolmoAct, improve action quality by generating dense intermediate representations (e.g., depth maps) at every timestep. However, this often involves redundant computation in largely static scenes, leading to high inference latency that prevents real-time control.
 - **Objective:** To introduce MolmoAct2-Think, an adaptive-depth reasoning variant. This variant re-predicts depth tokens only for scene regions that change between timesteps, exploiting temporal redundancy to reduce latency while retaining the geometric grounding that enhances performance.

By achieving these objectives, MolmoAct2 aims to set a new standard for open-source, deployable, and efficient action reasoning models in robotics, enabling broader adoption and further research.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

MolmoAct2 is constructed and trained through a multi-faceted approach involving specialized VLM backbone development, extensive data collection and curation, a novel action tokenization scheme, a new VLA architecture, and an adaptive reasoning mechanism. The overall training proceeds in a three-stage pipeline: pre-training, post-training, and embodiment-specific fine-tuning.

#### 3.1. Molmo2-ER: The Embodied Reasoning VLM Backbone

The foundation of MolmoAct2 is Molmo2-ER, a Vision-Language Model (VLM) specifically developed for embodied and spatial reasoning tasks crucial for robot control.
- **Specialization:** Unlike general-purpose VLMs, Molmo2-ER is designed to understand metric distances, free space, cross-view object tracking, and scene geometry.
- **Training Data:** It is trained on a 3.3 million-sample "spatial–embodied corpus." This corpus comprises diverse data sources across six capability pillars: single-image embodied QA (e.g., SAT, RoboPoint-QA, RefSpatial, VST-P, VSI-590K), video embodied QA (e.g., SIMS-VSI, RoboVQA), pixel-accurate pointing and object detection (e.g., RoboPoint, LVIS), multi-image and ego-exo correspondence (e.g., SenseNova-SI, VST-P cross-view split), and abstract embodied reasoning (e.g., CLEVR, GRiD-3D). These datasets offer varied supervision, including simulator ground truth, 3D annotations, template-generated questions, and LLM-generated chain-of-thought.
- **Training Recipe:** A two-stage "specialize-then-rehearse" approach is used.
 1. **Embodied Specialization:** Molmo2 (a general VLM checkpoint) is fine-tuned for 20K steps on the Molmo2-ER corpus, augmented with 8% text-only data to maintain language competence.
 2. **Joint Refinement:** Training continues for an additional 1.5K steps on a mixture interleaving the embodied corpus with Molmo2’s original multimodal mid-training data, balancing embodied and general VLM capabilities.

#### 3.2. Data Collection and Curation

To support the training of a generalist VLA, the project emphasizes large-scale, diverse, and high-quality robot datasets.
- **MolmoAct2-BimanualYAM Dataset:** A newly collected, open-source dataset comprising over 720 hours (34.5K trajectories) of teleoperated bimanual manipulation data on a custom YAM platform. It covers 28 diverse tasks in household, factory, and coffee-shop settings, with significant variation in scene configuration, objects, and placement.
- **MolmoAct2-DROID Dataset:** A quality-filtered subset of the publicly available DROID dataset, focusing on Franka robot manipulations. Filtering removes idle segments, failed attempts, and repetitive instructions, leveraging supplementary annotations for quality and extended language labels.
- **MolmoAct2-SO100/101 Dataset:** Curated from 1,222 public LeRobot datasets (38,059 episodes, 184 hours) collected by the community for SO-100/101 robots. A four-stage filtering pipeline (structural validity, eval-style dataset removal, license checks, TOPReward quality gate) ensures high quality.
- **Language Annotation Pipeline:** For improved diversity and accuracy, all collected and filtered robotics datasets undergo re-annotation. An open-source VLM (Qwen3.5-27B) is prompted with sample frames and original instructions to generate new, varied, and accurate task descriptions, doubling the unique labels.
- **Academic & Multimodal Data:** The training mixture also incorporates targeted subsets of academic robotics datasets (e.g., Open X-Embodiment, MolmoAct Dataset) for broader embodiment coverage, alongside Molmo2 and Molmo2-ER multimodal data to preserve VLM capabilities.

#### 3.3. MolmoAct2-FAST Tokenizer

An open-weight, open-data action tokenizer, following the FAST framework, is developed to convert continuous robot actions into discrete tokens suitable for VLM processing.
- **Mechanism:** It maps a one-second action trajectory into a discrete token sequence by representing trajectories in the frequency domain, quantizing coefficients, and applying byte-pair encoding from a 2048-token vocabulary.
- **Training Data:** Trained on one million subsampled action sequences across five embodiments (MolmoAct2-BimanualYAM, MolmoAct2-SO100/101, MolmoAct2-DROID Franka, Fractal, BC-Z, Bridge, RT-1, MolmoAct Dataset), supporting both absolute joint and delta end-effector control, padded to 32 dimensions.

#### 3.4. MolmoAct2 Architecture and Training Pipeline

MolmoAct2 follows a three-stage training pipeline (Figure 4 in paper):
- **Stage 1: Pre-training (MolmoAct2-Pretrain):**
 - **Objective:** Adapt Molmo2-ER into a discrete autoregressive robot policy.
 - **Architecture:** Initializes from Molmo2-ER. Visual observations (single resized crops, max 8 frames for video) are encoded by SigLIP2 ViT, pooled by a vision-language connector, and passed to the LLM with language instructions and robot-specific text. Robot examples extend this sequence with state tokens (256 discrete tokens per dimension) and action tokens (from MolmoAct2-FAST Tokenizer).
 - **Objective:** Next-token prediction across text, vision-language, state, and action targets.
 - **Data:** 10% multimodal data, 90% robot trajectories (YAM, SO-100/101, DROID each 30%, rest from academic datasets). Image augmentation and prompt formatting (e.g., `<setup_start>`, `<control_start>`) are applied.
- **Stage 2: Post-training (MolmoAct2-Post):**
 - **Objective:** Attach a continuous flow-matching action expert to the pre-trained VLM backbone, enabling continuous control.
 - **Action Expert:** A DiT-style transformer expert (36 layers, same depth as VLM) is added. It receives noisy action chunks and VLM context to predict a target velocity field.
 - **Per-Layer KV Conditioning:** The expert’s key innovation. Each expert block cross-attends to the keys and values from the *corresponding* VLM layer (after learned linear projections), providing dense, hierarchical visual-semantic context. The conditioning path is initially detached from the VLM to insulate knowledge.
 - **Training Objectives:** Combines the autoregressive loss (for discrete action tokens) from pre-training with a flow-matching loss (for continuous action chunks). Multiple flow samples (K=4) are evaluated per action chunk.
- **Stage 3: Deployment (Embodiment-specific Fine-tuning):**
 - **Objective:** Adapt the MolmoAct2-Post checkpoint to specific embodiments and tasks.
 - **Recipe:** Continues co-training discrete and continuous action supervision. Increases flow samples to K=8. Critically, knowledge insulation is *removed*, allowing flow loss gradients to update the VLM through the conditioning path. Specific fine-tuning runs are conducted for Bimanual YAM, DROID, SO-100/101, and LIBERO, adjusting camera order, action representation, and control rates as appropriate.
 - **Inference Optimization:** Implements caching of reusable action-expert intermediates and uses CUDA Graphs to reduce overhead in the flow-matching loop, enhancing control rates.

#### 3.5. MolmoAct2-Think: Adaptive Depth Reasoning

MolmoAct2-Think is a variant designed to integrate efficient, interpretable geometric reasoning.
- **Depth Representation:** For every robotics dataset, depth annotations are attached using monocular depth estimation (Depth Anything V2) quantized into a 10x10 grid of 128 learned depth-code values. These codes are treated as autoregressive tokens.
- **Adaptive Depth Prediction:** Instead of re-predicting all 100 depth codes at every step, MolmoAct2-Think reuses cached codes for static scene regions. Only regions whose RGB evidence changes (determined by cosine similarity of 32x32 patches) have their depth codes regenerated autoregressively. This exploits temporal redundancy.
- **Training:** Fine-tuned from the depth-aware post-trained checkpoint. It samples action prediction, depth prediction, and depth-and-action prediction tasks. During depth-and-action tasks, the model first predicts depth tokens, which then condition the continuous action expert via per-layer KV conditioning. Depth-token noise (10% random replacement) and a learned per-layer gate on the depth portion of the conditioning path are introduced during fine-tuning to improve robustness to imperfect inference-time depth predictions.
- **Inference:** At inference, if no depth cache is available, the full depth sequence is predicted. Otherwise, only changed regions are generated, and static regions are replayed from the cache. The generated/replayed depth prefix then conditions the action expert for continuous action generation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of MolmoAct2 encompassed a comprehensive suite of benchmarks across embodied reasoning, out-of-the-box deployment, efficient fine-tuning, robustness to out-of-distribution shifts, trajectory quality, and systematic ablations.

#### 4.1. Molmo2-ER Embodied Reasoning Performance

Molmo2-ER, the VLM backbone, demonstrated strong performance on 13 established embodied reasoning benchmarks (Point-Bench, RefSpatial, RoboSpatial-Poi, Where2Place, BLINK, CV-Bench, ERQA, EmbSpatial, MindCube, RoboSpatial-VQ, SAT, OpenEQA, VSI-Bench). It achieved an overall average score of **63.8%**, outperforming all baseline VLMs on 9 of the 13 benchmarks. This score surpassed GPT-5 (57.9%) and Gemini Robotics ER-1.5 Thinking (61.3%), and marked a **17-point improvement** over its base model, Molmo2 (46.8%). This indicates that the embodied reasoning specialization effectively enhanced the VLM's capabilities relevant for robotic tasks.

#### 4.2. Out-of-the-Box Deployment Capabilities

MolmoAct2-DROID demonstrated zero-shot performance in both simulation and real-world scenarios.
- **Simulation:** On MolmoSpaces, MolmoAct2-DROID achieved an average success rate of **37.7%**, surpassing the previous best, π
0.5
-DROID (34.5%), by 3.2 percentage points. On MolmoBot, MolmoAct2-DROID achieved **20.6%** average success, an absolute gain of 10.6 percentage points over π
0.5
-DROID (10.0%).
- **Real-world (DROID setup):** Under challenging out-of-distribution conditions (random camera poses, unseen objects, novel environments), MolmoAct2-DROID achieved **87.1%** average success across five manipulation tasks, substantially outperforming MolmoBot (48.4%) by 38.7 percentage points and π
0.5
-DROID (45.2%) by 41.9 percentage points.
- **Real-world (SO-100/101 setup):** MolmoAct2-SO100/101 attained **56.7%** average success on five pick-and-place tasks with novel objects and camera poses, representing an 11.4 percentage point gain over the open-source π
0
-SO100/101 (45.3%) and significantly exceeding SmolVLA (2.3%). These results demonstrate MolmoAct2's capability for deployment on low-to-medium cost robot platforms.

#### 4.3. Effective Fine-tuning

MolmoAct2 exhibited efficient adaptation to new tasks and embodiments through fine-tuning.
- **LIBERO Benchmark:** MolmoAct2 achieved the highest overall average success rate of **97.2%** across four task categories (Spatial, Object, Goal, Long), surpassing state-of-the-art VLAs like GR00T N1.7 (97.0%) and π
0.5
(96.9%). Notably, it achieved 100% on LIBERO-Object tasks and improved over its predecessor, MolmoAct-7B-D, by 10.6 percentage points.
- **RoboEval Benchmark:** MolmoAct2 attained a **44.3%** average success rate on 8 bimanual manipulation tasks, outperforming the second-best model, π
0.5, by 3.8 percentage points.
- **Real-world Bimanual YAM:** On 8 real-world bimanual YAM tasks (including static and mobile manipulation in diverse environments), MolmoAct2 fine-tuned on task-specific data achieved an average success rate of **50.1%**, which was 15 percentage points higher than the runner-up, OpenVLA-OFT (35.5%).

#### 4.4. Performance of MolmoAct2-Think

MolmoAct2-Think, incorporating adaptive depth perception, demonstrated further performance gains. On the LIBERO benchmark, MolmoAct2-Think achieved an average success rate of **98.1%**, a +0.9 percentage point improvement over MolmoAct2 (97.2%). This gain was most pronounced on the more challenging Long-horizon suite (+2.2%), indicating that adaptive depth reasoning provides tangible benefits beyond merely reaching performance ceilings.

#### 4.5. Robustness to Out-of-Distribution Shifts

MolmoAct2-Think showed enhanced robustness to environmental perturbations. When evaluated on 4 real-world bimanual tasks under 4 out-of-distribution variants (spatial, lighting, language rephrasing, and distractors), MolmoAct2-Think achieved a **50.7%** average success rate. This represented a 10.8 percentage point absolute improvement over the next-best model, OpenVLA-OFT (39.9%). MolmoAct2-Think demonstrated leadership across all perturbation categories, though its lowest absolute score was on spatial variance (26.25%), suggesting an area for potential improvement in fine-grained spatial generalization.

#### 4.6. Quality of MolmoAct2 Trajectories

Beyond success rates, MolmoAct2 demonstrated improvements in trajectory quality on the RoboEval benchmark. It consistently produced more efficient, stable, and smoother trajectories compared to baselines. For instance, on "Stack Two Blocks," MolmoAct2 reduced completion time from 5.87s (π
0.5
) to 4.70s and joint path length by approximately 2x. Similar trends were observed for longer-horizon tasks like "Rotate Valve." These metrics are crucial for practical real-world deployment.

#### 4.7. Systematic Ablation Analysis

Ablation studies on LIBERO identified key contributors to MolmoAct2's performance:
- **Embodied-Reasoning Backbone:** Replacing Molmo2 with Molmo2-ER as the VLM backbone yielded a **6.0-point gain** in discrete action prediction (from 77.6% to 83.6%).
- **VLM-to-Expert Conditioning:** The chosen *per-layer KV conditioning* for the continuous action expert achieved 95.9% average success, outperforming hidden-state conditioning (94.0%) and per-head per-layer KV conditioning (94.8%).
- **Multiple Flow Samples:** Increasing the number of flow samples (K) during training generally improved performance, with K=8 yielding the best average of **95.90%** compared to K=1 (94.15%).
- **Fine-tuning Design:** The final recipe of full-model adaptation with discrete and continuous action co-training, without knowledge insulation, provided the best average outcome (97.20%).
- **Depth-aware Fine-tuning (MolmoAct2-Think):** The inclusion of depth-token noise injection and a learned per-layer depth gate, along with mixed training examples, contributed to the performance of MolmoAct2-Think.

#### 4.8. Inference Speed

Inference speed optimizations significantly improved control rates. On LIBERO with a single H100 GPU:
- MolmoAct2 achieved **55.79 Hz** (2.42x speedup over original implementation) through caching and CUDA Graphs.
- MolmoAct2-Think reached **12.71 Hz** (1.58x speedup).
The continuous action path was considerably faster than the discrete action path, which ran at 14.17 Hz for MolmoAct2 and 6.82 Hz for MolmoAct2-Think.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

MolmoAct2 represents a significant contribution to the field of robotic manipulation, particularly concerning the development of generalist, deployable, and open-source Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized in several key areas:

1. **Democratization of Robotic AI Research:** By releasing all model weights, training code, and comprehensive datasets, MolmoAct2 directly addresses the prevalent issue of opacity in frontier VLA models. This full openness allows the broader academic and practitioner community to reproduce the research, understand the underlying mechanisms, build upon the work, and adapt the models to their specific needs. This fosters scientific progress and accelerates innovation in robot learning.

2. **Advancement in VLA Model Capabilities:** MolmoAct2 establishes new performance benchmarks across a diverse array of simulation and real-world tasks. Its VLM backbone, Molmo2-ER, demonstrates leading capabilities in embodied reasoning, an area critical for robust robotic perception. The model's out-of-the-box deployment success on low-to-medium cost hardware, coupled with its efficient fine-tuning capabilities, showcases a notable step forward in making advanced robot control widely accessible and practical.

3. **Facilitating Real-World Deployment:** The explicit focus on "real-world deployment" drives several key design choices. The support for low-to-medium cost platforms (e.g., YAM, SO-100/101) makes the technology accessible beyond well-funded research labs. The demonstrated robustness against out-of-distribution shifts (spatial, lighting, language, distractors) and the generation of high-quality, stable, and efficient trajectories are critical for practical applications where reliability and safety are paramount.

4. **Architectural and Methodological Innovations:**
 - **Molmo2-ER Backbone:** The specialization-then-rehearse training recipe for embodied reasoning is a methodological contribution for developing more robot-centric VLMs.
 - **Per-Layer KV Conditioning:** This novel architectural design for connecting the VLM backbone with the continuous action expert provides a more granular and effective way to leverage hierarchical visual-language features, outperforming simpler conditioning methods.
 - **MolmoAct2-FAST Tokenizer:** An open and transparent action tokenizer for diverse embodiments fills a crucial gap, ensuring reproducibility and flexibility in discrete action representations.
 - **Adaptive Depth Reasoning (MolmoAct2-Think):** This approach offers a practical solution to the latency issue of explicit reasoning by dynamically adjusting the computational cost based on scene changes, enabling geometric grounding without prohibitive overhead.

5. **Enrichment of the Open Data Ecosystem:** The release of new, high-quality datasets, including MolmoAct2-BimanualYAM (the largest open bimanual manipulation dataset to date) and carefully filtered versions of DROID and SO-100/101 datasets, provides invaluable resources for future research. These datasets contribute to addressing the data scarcity and quality issues that often hinder progress in generalist robot learning.

In conclusion, MolmoAct2 contributes not only a performant action reasoning model but also a comprehensive, open ecosystem of models, code, and data. This collective effort aims to lower the barrier to entry for developing and deploying advanced robotic capabilities, moving towards a future where sophisticated robotic intelligence is more broadly accessible and practically applicable across diverse real-world workflows.
