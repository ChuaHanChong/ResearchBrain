# EvoAgent: Self-evolving Agent with Continual World Model for Long-Horizon Tasks

> [!summary] Summary
> EvoAgent introduces a framework for embodied agents to autonomously tackle complex, long-horizon tasks in open-ended environments using a continual world model. The system achieved a 105% average success rate improvement and reduced ineffective actions by over 6x compared to baselines on Minecraft tasks, learning from scratch and mitigating catastrophic forgetting.

## Problem

- Embodied AI agents struggle to autonomously acquire, update, and strategically select multimodal experiences in open-ended environments due to heavy reliance on pre-collected data or pre-defined curricula.
- Current methods for representing world knowledge often rely on static Large Language Models or fixed knowledge graphs, leading to catastrophic forgetting and a lack of adaptability when learning new long-horizon tasks.

## Method

- **EvoAgent** proposes a self-evolving architecture centered around a continual World Model, operating through a closed-loop dynamic of self-planning, self-control, and self-reflection.
- An Experience-Driven Task Planner, based on an LLM, converts long-horizon tasks into sub-tasks and uses ==Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA)== for lightweight fine-tuning on failed experiences to mitigate repeated errors.
- A CL-Based Reflector continuously updates the World Model by selecting relevant experiences from a ==Multimodal Experience Pool== using a two-stage ==Curriculum Learning== algorithm, incorporating ==regularization== to mitigate catastrophic forgetting.

## Results

- On a Minecraft benchmark of 67 long-horizon tasks, EvoAgent achieved a **105%** average success rate improvement and reduced ineffective actions by over **6x** compared to composite baselines like Jarvis-1, DreamerV3, and Optimus-1.
- The agent demonstrated significantly higher success rates on advanced tasks (Iron, Gold, Diamond tiers), for example, achieving **21.69%** success rate on Gold tasks compared to Optimus-1's **10.62%**.
- An ablation study confirmed that the Continual World Model contributed **72%** of the overall performance gain, underscoring its critical role in coherent knowledge integration and mitigating catastrophic forgetting.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Autonomous, continual updating of world knowledge and strategic selection of multimodal experiences are essential for embodied agents to perform long-horizon tasks effectively in dynamic, open-ended environments.
> - A closed-loop system of self-planning, self-control, and self-reflection, coupled with adaptive knowledge refinement, enables agents to learn from scratch and continuously evolve their understanding of the world without human intervention.
> - Employing a continual World Model with curriculum learning and importance-aware updating is crucial for mitigating catastrophic forgetting, allowing agents to retain past knowledge while stably integrating new information.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of embodied AI has seen progress in enabling agents to complete complex tasks, particularly Long-Horizon (LH) tasks in open-ended environments. LH tasks are characterized by their multi-step nature, requiring sustained planning, sequential decision-making, and prolonged execution, often in scenarios with sparse rewards and procedural diversity.

Current approaches to LH tasks typically fall into categories such as Model-Based Reinforcement Learning (MBRL) and Vision-Language Model (VLM) based planning. MBRL approaches, like those utilizing predictive world dynamics, and VLM-based methods, which decompose tasks into hierarchical sub-goals and refine plans, have shown capabilities. However, these methods often rely on assumptions of perfect environmental knowledge, oracle feedback, or perfect execution of low-level policies. This reliance on human-created data or curricula limits their ability to adapt to novel tasks or environments encountered after deployment and to autonomously accumulate multimodal experiences and update world knowledge.

A significant challenge for existing world models (WMs), which build internal representations and make future predictions, is catastrophic forgetting. This issue arises when agents lose previously acquired knowledge while attempting to learn new tasks, hindering the autonomous update and transfer of world knowledge across various LH tasks and environments. Some LLM-based agents (e.g., Voyager, Jarvis-1) represent world knowledge by sampling historical experiences or using sparse graphs, often requiring human intervention and struggling with autonomous updates.

Curriculum Learning (CL) is a strategy that involves organizing training from easier to harder examples, often adaptively. While CL has been applied in multimedia settings, such as visual grounding, video understanding, and human pose estimation, to stabilize adaptation and improve learning signals, its application to solving embodied LH tasks, particularly in achieving autonomous experience updating, has been limited.

This research addresses these gaps by proposing EvolvingAgent, an agent designed to autonomously learn and adapt in open-ended worlds. It integrates concepts from WMs, LLMs, and CL within a self-evolving framework to overcome the dependency on human intervention, manage catastrophic forgetting, and enable continuous, autonomous updates of both multimodal experiences and world knowledge. The work aims to combine the strengths of these areas to create a more robust and adaptable embodied agent for complex, long-horizon challenges.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an embodied agent, termed EvolvingAgent, capable of autonomously completing diverse Long-Horizon (LH) tasks across various environments without human intervention. This overarching goal is driven by two key challenges identified in existing approaches to LH tasks in open-ended worlds:

1. **Failure to autonomously update and select multimodal experiences:** Many contemporary embodied agents, particularly those based on Imitation Learning (IL) or Large Language Models (LLMs), depend heavily on pre-existing human-created data or domain-specific curricula. This dependency means they assume all necessary training data is available from the outset. However, in dynamic, open-ended environments, agents frequently encounter novel tasks or unexpected scenarios after deployment. Existing systems lack the inherent mechanisms to autonomously acquire, process, and select new multimodal experiences to adapt to these unforeseen challenges, thereby limiting their scalability and real-world applicability. The motivation here is to enable agents to learn and grow their experience base autonomously, similar to how humans accumulate knowledge from interactions.

2. **Failure to autonomously update world knowledge and mitigate catastrophic forgetting:** Current methods struggle to maintain a coherent and up-to-date understanding of the world. Approaches using LLMs to represent world knowledge often rely on sampling historical experiences or employing sparse graph representations, which can be difficult to update autonomously and often necessitate human intervention. Furthermore, a significant issue is catastrophic forgetting, where agents tend to overwrite or lose previously acquired knowledge when learning new tasks. This hinders the ability to transfer world knowledge effectively across different LH tasks and environments, leading to inefficiencies and reduced performance on previously mastered skills. The motivation is to design a system that can continually integrate new information into its world model while preserving existing knowledge, thereby enabling robust and cumulative learning.

To address these motivations, EvolvingAgent aims to establish a self-evolving mechanism through self-planning, self-control, and self-reflection, driven by a continual World Model (WM). This structure is intended to allow the agent to autonomously update its multimodal experiences and world knowledge, filtering out invalid explorations and mitigating historical forgetting, ultimately leading to improved performance on complex LH tasks in open-ended settings.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

EvolvingAgent is designed as a curriculum self-evolving agent with a continual World Model (WM) operating within an online Model-Based Reinforcement Learning (MBRL) framework. It establishes a closed-loop dynamic among three core modules: an experience-driven task planner, a WM-guided action controller, and a Curriculum Learning (CL)-based reflector. The continual WM, comprising a Multimodal Experience Pool (MEP) and a world model (M_w), is autonomously updated through this dynamic.

**Overall Architecture:**
The agent's operation involves an iterative process: The planner decomposes a Long-Horizon (LH) task into subtasks, which the controller attempts to execute using the WM. The controller's interactions generate new experiences, which are then used by the reflector to update the WM and the MEP, allowing the agent to continually learn and improve.

**Modules:**

1. **Experience-driven Task Planner (Ψ_plan):**
 - **Function:** This module is responsible for converting a given LH task (T) into a sequence of executable subtasks (G), taking into account the agent’s current state (S) and accumulated multimodal experiences (D_MEP).
 - **State Representation:** The agent’s state (S) is comprehensive, including first-person observations (O_obs), the agent's internal self-state (S_self, e.g., health, hunger), and its asset library (S_assets, e.g., tools).
 - **Mechanism:** It utilizes a Large Language Model (LLM), specifically GPT-4o, augmented with multimodal inputs. Visual components (O_obs, S_self, S_assets) are encoded via an image tokenizer (VQ-GAN) into token embeddings and then projected into the same embedding space as textual inputs (T). The LLM processes these fused embeddings to generate subtasks.
 - **Self-planning:** The planner features a self-updating mechanism to improve its efficacy without human intervention.
 - **Initialization:** At the start, the planner leverages either accumulated experiences from D_MEP or, if empty, the baseline capabilities of GPT-4o.
 - **Dynamic Fine-tuning:** Upon subtask failure, relevant experience trajectories are extracted from D_MEP. These are used to create input-output pairs for lightweight fine-tuning of the LLM planner using Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA). This process allows the planner to learn from failure patterns, improving its robustness and reducing repetitive errors.
 - **Reinitialization:** If the agent "dies" (e.g., health reaches zero), it is reinitialized.

2. **WM-Guided Action Controller (Π_act):**
 - **Function:** This module is responsible for generating low-level actions (a_t:t+H) for a given subtask (g_i) by leveraging the world model (M_w) to predict future states and optimize action sequences over a defined horizon (H).
 - **Action Selection:** The controller employs an RSSM-based WM structure (inspired by DreamerV3) to simulate future outcomes. It samples multiple action sequences and, for each sequence, predicts future states and computes the expected cumulative reward using a goal-aligned reward function. The action sequence that yields the highest predicted reward is selected, and its first action is executed in the environment. The reward function integrates current observation states (s_τ), actions (a_τ), and a goal embedding derived from the subtask (g_i).
 - **Self-control:** A self-verification mechanism is integrated to determine when a subtask is terminated, minimizing inefficient exploration.
 - **Termination Criteria:** A subtask (g_i) terminates if the cosine similarity between the WM-encoded latent representation of the current state (Emb_st) and the subtask goal embedding (Emb_gi) exceeds an empirical threshold (σ), or if the maximum allowed steps (T_max) for the episode are reached. Goal attainment is measured using a contrastive video-language model pretrained on the D_MEP.
 - **Experience Accumulation:** Upon subtask termination (whether successful or due to exceeding T_max), the entire trajectory of experiences—comprising states, actions, rewards, next states, and subtask completion percentage—is added to the Multimodal Experience Pool (D_MEP).

3. **CL-based Reflector (Φ_reflect):**
 - **Function:** This module orchestrates the autonomous update of the world model (M_w) by selecting pertinent experiences from D_MEP using a two-stage Curriculum Learning (CL) algorithm.
 - **Stage 1: Curriculum Subtask Selection:**
 - **Indicators:** For a set of candidate subtasks, a priority score τ(g_i) is calculated based on four indicators: (1) Relevance to the overall target task (cosine similarity of embeddings), (2) Exploration Efficiency (ratio of successful to total steps), (3) Importance (KL divergence between the impact of the subtask on the new and old WM), and (4) Average Completion Ratio.
 - **Selection:** Subtasks with a priority score exceeding a dynamically decaying threshold (ρ_k) are selected for further experience extraction.
 - **Stage 2: Curriculum Experience Selection:**
 - **Indicators:** From the experiences within the selected subtasks, individual experiences (h_j) are scored (ϵ(h_j)) using three indicators: (1) Temporal Difference Error (TD-Error), which prioritizes experiences with high prediction mismatch, (2) Gradient Norm, favoring experiences that maximally influence WM parameter updates, and (3) Information Gain, measuring how much an experience changes the WM's belief distribution (KL divergence between new and old WM predictions).
 - **Selection:** Experiences with a score greater than a dynamically decaying threshold (ρ_k) are chosen for updating the world model.
 - **Self-reflection (WM Update):** The world model (M_w) is updated using the selected experiences (D_exp_k). This update incorporates importance-aware weights (w_j) for critical experiences and includes a regularization term (Ω) to penalize significant shifts in parameters crucial for past tasks, thereby mitigating catastrophic forgetting.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted primarily in Minecraft, and subsequently generalized to Atari, demonstrate EvolvingAgent's performance in Long-Horizon (LH) tasks.

**Quantitative Results and Analysis (Minecraft):**
EvolvingAgent achieved significant improvements across various LH tasks in Minecraft, categorized by resource tiers (Wood, Stone, Iron, Gold, Diamond).
- **Overall Performance:** The agent demonstrated an average success rate improvement of 111.74% and reduced ineffective actions by more than 6x compared to existing methods.
- **Basic Tasks (Wood, Stone):** EvolvingAgent performed comparably to or slightly better than top baselines like Optimus-1. For instance, on "Wood" tasks, it achieved a 97.47% success rate compared to Optimus-1's 96.39%. Exploration efficiency (EE) also showed marginal gains (98.43% vs 97.82%).
- **Advanced Tasks (Iron, Gold, Diamond):** The agent showed substantial advantages in more complex tasks.
 - **Iron:** Achieved 51.82% SR, outperforming Optimus-1 (45.48%). Its EE was 58.54%, significantly higher than Optimus-1's 46.16%.
 - **Gold:** Demonstrated 21.69% SR, which is approximately 1.5 times higher than Optimus-1 (10.62%). Its EE was 30.48%, about 3.8 times higher than Optimus-1's (8.03%).
 - **Diamond:** Achieved 17.36% SR, nearly twice that of Optimus-1 (9.30%). The EE was 26.83%, approximately 3.7 times higher than Optimus-1's (7.31%).
- **Baseline Comparison:**
 - Model-free methods (PPO) and pure vision-language models (GPT-4V) consistently failed (0% SR/EE) on tasks requiring tool hierarchies (Iron+), indicating their limitation in modeling latent state transitions.
 - LLM-driven agents like Jarvis-1 and WM-based agents like DreamerV3 showed partial success on intermediate tasks (e.g., 42.38% SR for Jarvis-1 on Iron) but experienced performance collapse on higher tiers (Gold/Diamond) due to error accumulation in long action sequences.
 - LS-Imagine, while outperforming DreamerV3 in basic tasks, showed slow performance growth on complex tasks due to accumulated errors.
- **Efficiency:** The high EE scores for EvolvingAgent, particularly in advanced tasks, suggest that its CL-based experience selection effectively mitigates exploration bottlenecks in sparse-reward environments, leading to fewer invalid actions.

**Ablation Studies:**
The ablation studies clarified the contributions of EvolvingAgent's components.
- **Individual Component Impact:** Using only PPO as a baseline resulted in failure on complex tasks. Introducing the Planner alone improved basic task performance but did not unlock advanced tasks. The addition of the Controller (P+C) significantly boosted Wood and Stone success rates (92.42% and 85.31%) and provided initial progress on Iron (31.59%), underscoring the role of structured exploration. However, performance still declined on Gold and Diamond tasks.
- **Reflector's Contribution:** Integrating the full Reflector module (P+C+R) achieved near-perfect success on Wood/Stone (96.69%/93.82%) and substantially improved Iron (42.61%), Gold (17.53%), and Diamond (10.09%), demonstrating its role in distilling exploration experiences for world model refinement.
- **LoRA for Planner:** Fine-tuning the Planner with LoRA improved convergence speed and reduced invalid subtasks compared to a planner without LoRA.
- **Two-Stage Reflector:** Combining both stages of curriculum subtask and experience selection in the Reflector yielded better average performance than using either stage in isolation.
- **CL-based Reflector's role in path selection:** Illustrated by an example ("Craft a stone axe"), the CL-based reflector allowed EvolvingAgent to complete subtasks in fewer steps compared to DreamerV3, by efficiently updating the world model and reducing redundant exploration.

**Sensitivity Analysis:**
Analysis of the indicators within the CL-based reflector and self-reflection mechanisms revealed their individual importance.
- **Curriculum Subtask Selection (Eq. 21):** The gradual addition of indicators (relevance, efficiency, importance, completion rate) consistently improved success rates (from 40.16% to 49.37% on Iron tasks), indicating that effective subtask prioritization requires a multi-faceted evaluation.
- **Curriculum Experience Selection (Eq. 23):** Combining TD-Error, gradient norm, and information gain progressively improved success rates (from 42.47% to 50.43%), confirming the need for selecting informative experiences based on prediction discrepancy, update influence, and knowledge increment.
- **Self-reflection (Eq. 25):** Including regularization with curriculum loss further improved success rates (from 48.61% to 50.92%), suggesting that stable world model updating is important for reliable long-horizon task completion.

**Generalization Analysis (Atari100k):**
EvolvingAgent's generalization capabilities were evaluated on Atari100k tasks after training in Minecraft.
- **Cross-Environment Performance:** EvolvingAgent consistently outperformed DreamerV3 across most Atari tasks within the same 400K interaction steps (e.g., Alien, Assault, Asterix, Ms Pacman).
- **Human-Level Performance:** It achieved or exceeded human-level performance in several games, including Boxing, Krull, and Kung Fu Master.
- **Transferability:** These results indicate that the continual world model learned by EvolvingAgent captures transferable dynamics and control priors, rather than being confined to environment-specific patterns, demonstrating robustness under significant environmental shifts.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents EvolvingAgent as a contribution to the field of embodied AI, particularly in addressing the challenges of Long-Horizon (LH) tasks in open-ended environments. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Addressing Fundamental Limitations:** EvolvingAgent directly confronts key limitations of existing embodied agents, specifically their reliance on human-curated data and curricula, and the problem of catastrophic forgetting. By implementing a self-evolving framework, the system aims to move beyond these dependencies.
- **Enabling Autonomous Learning and Adaptation:** The core contribution is the establishment of a planner-controller-reflector closed-loop dynamic that facilitates autonomous multimodal experience accumulation and world knowledge updates. This design allows the agent to continuously learn, refine its understanding of the environment, and adapt to new challenges without requiring constant human intervention.
- **Improved Performance in Complex Environments:** The quantitative results in Minecraft demonstrate a substantial improvement in both success rate and exploration efficiency, particularly for advanced, multi-step tasks. This suggests that the integrated approach of self-planning, self-control, and curriculum-based reflection is effective in managing the complexities of LH tasks in sparse-reward settings.
- **Enhanced Robustness and Resilience:** By learning from failure patterns and selectively updating its world model, EvolvingAgent exhibits enhanced robustness. The self-verification mechanism reduces inefficient exploration, while the CL-based reflector helps distill pertinent experiences and mitigate historical forgetting, allowing for a more stable and effective learning trajectory.
- **Demonstrated Generalization Capabilities:** The ability of EvolvingAgent to generalize its learned dynamics and control priors to a distinctly different environment like Atari100k underscores the transferability of its internal world model. This suggests that the agent learns more fundamental principles rather than merely memorizing environment-specific patterns, which is a critical step towards more broadly intelligent agents.
- **Advancement in Embodied AI Paradigms:** This work contributes to the ongoing evolution of embodied AI by integrating Large Language Models with World Models and Curriculum Learning in a novel, self-evolving architecture. It provides a framework for developing agents that can operate more autonomously and intelligently in complex, dynamic, and partially observable real-world settings.
- **Potential for Real-World Applications:** The research explicitly aims for future application in real-robot scenarios, such as logistics, surgical, or rescue robotics. The capabilities demonstrated by EvolvingAgent—autonomous task completion, adaptation, and continuous learning—are crucial for deploying intelligent agents in practical, real-world operational environments where human supervision might be limited or impractical.
