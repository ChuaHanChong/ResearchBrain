# EvoAgent: Self-evolving Agent with Continual World Model for Long-Horizon Tasks

> [!summary] Summary
> EvoAgent introduces a framework for embodied agents to autonomously tackle complex, long-horizon tasks in open-ended environments using a continual world model. The system achieved a 105% average success rate improvement and reduced ineffective actions by over 6x compared to baselines on Minecraft tasks, learning from scratch and mitigating catastrophic forgetting.

## Problem

- Embodied AI agents struggle to autonomously acquire, update, and strategically select multimodal experiences in open-ended environments due to heavy reliance on pre-collected data or pre-defined curricula.
- Current methods for representing world knowledge often rely on static Large Language Models or fixed knowledge graphs, leading to catastrophic forgetting and a lack of adaptability when learning new long-horizon tasks.

## Method

- **EvoAgent** proposes a self-evolving architecture centered around a continual World Model, operating through a closed-loop dynamic of self-planning, self-control, and self-reflection.
- An Experience-Driven Task Planner, based on an LLM, converts long-horizon tasks into sub-tasks and uses ==Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA)== for lightweight fine-tuning on failed experiences to mitigate repeated errors.
- A CL-Based Reflector continuously updates the World Model by selecting relevant experiences from a ==Multimodal Experience Pool== using a two-stage ==Curriculum Learning== algorithm, incorporating ==regularization== to mitigate catastrophic forgetting.

## Results

- On a Minecraft benchmark of 67 long-horizon tasks, EvoAgent achieved a **105%** average success rate improvement and reduced ineffective actions by over **6x** compared to composite baselines like Jarvis-1, DreamerV3, and Optimus-1.
- The agent demonstrated significantly higher success rates on advanced tasks (Iron, Gold, Diamond tiers), for example, achieving **21.69%** success rate on Gold tasks compared to Optimus-1's **10.62%**.
- An ablation study confirmed that the Continual World Model contributed **72%** of the overall performance gain, underscoring its critical role in coherent knowledge integration and mitigating catastrophic forgetting.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Autonomous, continual updating of world knowledge and strategic selection of multimodal experiences are essential for embodied agents to perform long-horizon tasks effectively in dynamic, open-ended environments.
> - A closed-loop system of self-planning, self-control, and self-reflection, coupled with adaptive knowledge refinement, enables agents to learn from scratch and continuously evolve their understanding of the world without human intervention.
> - Employing a continual World Model with curriculum learning and importance-aware updating is crucial for mitigating catastrophic forgetting, allowing agents to retain past knowledge while stably integrating new information.
