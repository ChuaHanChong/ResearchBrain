# Reuse Before You Retrieve: Diagnosing Headroom and Complementarity for Test-Time Augmentation of Embodied Multimodal Policies

> [!summary] Summary
> A diagnostic framework deciding whether a frozen VLA policy needs test-time sampling or retrieval. Two measurable factors, recoverable headroom (pass@N minus pass@1) and retrieval complementarity (demonstration-fit error), predict which intervention helps. A training-free episode-level retry selector, scoring rollouts by proximity to a demonstration manifold in frozen DINOv2 plus SigLIP embedding space, gains up to 21.0 success-rate points on LIBERO, tracking headroom at r=0.954.

## Problem

- Test-time interventions help inconsistently: the same retrieval mechanism improves one frozen policy but barely moves another, with no guidance on which policy needs which.
- Prior retrieval work studies how external experience is represented and injected, never whether the frozen policy already represents that action prior itself.
- Test-time scaling work designs selection rules but never measures how much better behavior actually exists in the candidate set to select from.

## Method

- Recoverable headroom H_N = pass@N minus pass@1, estimated from repeated stochastic rollouts, upper-bounds any selector's gain over that fixed candidate set.
- Demonstration-fit error: distance from a demonstrated action chunk to the nearest of K policy samples at the same demonstration state, probing the action-prior gap without downstream success.
- Episode-level retry selector scores each rollout by mean cosine similarity to nearest demonstration frame in a frozen encoder, plus event-schema prospective retrieval warm-starting the flow-matching sampler.

## Results

- Selector improves all 12 backbone-by-suite LIBERO cells: +21.0 for one flow-matching backbone, +19.9 and +7.8 for the others; capture ratio 0.90 mean, correlation r=0.954.
- Retrieval helps only the backbone with largest demonstration-fit error (0.042 versus 0.021 and 0.003), gaining +6.4 while others gain +0.3 and +0.5 despite matched baselines.
- Random-of-3 stays at baseline (64.5 versus 64.2), combining retrieval plus selection reaches +23.3, and cross-simulator transfer gives +12.5 recovering 69% of oracle headroom.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Baseline success rate is a poor predictor of test-time opportunity; two policies at equal pass@1 can need completely different interventions.
> - Extra sampling needs two conditions: the candidate set must contain better outcomes and the ranking signal must find them, which the autoregressive case separates starkly.
> - Headroom is not a fixed backbone property; degrading observations raises it and correspondingly raises realized selector gain, so diagnose per deployment condition.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models represent a significant advancement in robotic control, enabling pre-trained policies to interpret language instructions and execute a variety of manipulation tasks. However, when these frozen policies are deployed in new or challenging environments, or under suboptimal observation conditions, their performance often falls short of requirements. This limitation has spurred the development of various test-time augmentation (TTA) methods aimed at improving policy performance without the costly process of retraining the entire model.

Current TTA approaches generally fall into two categories: those that retrieve external demonstrations or memories to guide action generation, and those that leverage additional test-time computation to sample and select from multiple policy outputs. A challenge within this landscape is the inconsistent effectiveness of these interventions. A particular retrieval mechanism might enhance one policy but show minimal impact on another. Similarly, additional sampling only proves beneficial if the policy inherently produces superior behaviors that can be reliably identified. This variability creates a practical decision problem: how to determine whether a deployed frozen VLA policy would benefit more from recovering existing capabilities through sampling and selection, or from integrating external behavioral priors via retrieval.

This paper addresses this gap by investigating measurable properties of frozen policy behavior that can distinguish between these two forms of TTA opportunities. The work aims to provide guidance for applying appropriate test-time interventions, thereby optimizing resource utilization and avoiding the introduction of redundant priors.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the observation that test-time interventions for frozen VLA policies do not consistently yield improvements. Policies can fail for fundamentally different reasons that necessitate distinct augmentation strategies. For instance, a policy might produce successful behavior stochastically across multiple rollouts but fail to execute it reliably in a single attempt. In such cases, repeated execution and selection of the best outcome could be beneficial, assuming retryable or parallel execution is available. Conversely, if repeated rollouts consistently reproduce the same systematic failure, additional sampling offers limited opportunity, suggesting a need for an external behavioral prior. Both scenarios might present similar single-rollout performance but demand different test-time interventions.

The core objective of this study is to determine if quantifiable characteristics of a frozen policy's behavior can differentiate the opportunities targeted by these two types of test-time augmentation. To achieve this, the authors introduce and investigate two measurable factors:

1. **Recoverable Headroom (H_N):** This factor quantifies the latent capability already present within a policy's stochastic behavior. It measures the potential for improvement if better outcomes, already achievable through repeated stochastic executions, could be consistently realized. The authors define recoverable headroom as the difference between `pass@N` (the success rate achievable if the best among N stochastic rollouts is chosen) and `pass@1` (the single-rollout success rate). A larger `H_N` indicates more latent successful behaviors that could be recovered through an effective selection mechanism.

2. **Retrieval Complementarity:** This factor assesses whether an external action prior, such as a retrieved demonstration, supplies a behavioral gap that the frozen policy does not reliably represent on its own. The authors operationalize this by measuring how closely the policy's sampled actions align with demonstrated actions. If the policy's actions diverge significantly from demonstrated behaviors, a retrieved prior might be complementary and beneficial.

By characterizing these two factors, the research aims to provide an empirical basis for understanding when to attempt to "reuse" existing policy capabilities (via sampling and selection) and when to "retrieve" external knowledge (via demonstration augmentation) to improve a deployed VLA policy.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research investigates the distinct opportunities for test-time augmentation using two representative interventions: an episode-level retry selector and event-schema retrieval.

**Problem Setting:**
The study focuses on a frozen VLA policy, denoted as π, whose parameters remain fixed during all test-time interventions. The policy generates stochastic rollouts `τ ∼ π(· | s_0)` from an initial state `s_0`. Task success is binary, `succ(τ) ∈ {0, 1}`. The single-rollout success rate is defined as `pass@1 = E[succ(τ)]`, while the best-of-N success rate is `pass@N = E[max_{i≤N} succ(τ_i)]`. Recoverable headroom is then quantified as `H_N = pass@N - pass@1`. A shared, `ℓ2`-normalized visual encoder `ϕ` (concatenating DINOv2 and SigLIP features) is used across various components, and a demonstration library `D` containing successful task examples is employed.

**Measuring Retrieval Complementarity:**
Retrieval complementarity is assessed using the Demonstration-Fit Error (DFE). For a given demonstration observation `o_d` and its corresponding action chunk `a*` from the demonstration library, `K` candidate action chunks (`a_k`) are sampled from the frozen policy conditioned on `o_d`. The DFE is defined as the expected minimum `ℓ2` distance between a demonstrated action and the closest action among the `K` policy samples: `DFE = E[min_{k≤K} ||a_k - a*||^2]`. A lower DFE suggests a greater overlap between the policy's sampled action distribution and demonstrated behavior, implying less complementarity from an external prior. The DFE is interpreted comparatively across policies evaluated under the same protocol, rather than as an absolute measure.

**Episode-Level Retry Selection:**
This intervention operates under the assumption of retryable or parallel execution. For a given episode, the frozen policy is executed `N` times from the same initial condition, generating `N` candidate trajectories. Each trajectory `τ` is scored based on its average proximity to the demonstration manifold, calculated as `s(τ) = (1/T) * Σ_{t=1 to T} max_j cos(ϕ(o_t), ϕ(o_d_j))`. Here, `o_t` is an observation from the candidate rollout, `o_d_j` are observations from the demonstration library, and `cos` denotes cosine similarity in the embedding space. The rollout with the highest score is selected. This selector is training-free, does not use success labels for candidate rollouts, and relies solely on the frozen visual encoder and nearest-neighbor comparisons to demonstrations. The selection is episode-level, meaning complete rollouts are chosen. The execution cost involves `N` rollouts plus scoring.

**Retrieval Augmentation:**
The study employs an event-schema prospective retrieval mechanism. Event-start frames in the demonstration library are identified based on episode starts, gripper state transitions, or significant changes in end-effector motion direction. Retrieval compares the current event (query) with candidate events from the demonstration library using a weighted sum of standardized distances across the image embedding `ϕ(o)`, the policy's predicted action chunk, and the proprioceptive state. The retrieval is "prospective," meaning that a matched candidate provides the action chunk associated with its *next* event. For flow-matching policies, this retrieved action chunk `a_retr` is used to warm-start the sampler: `a_init = α a_retr + (1-α) ϵ`, where `ϵ` is sampling noise and `α` controls retrieval strength. Multiple retrieval variants were evaluated, with the strongest configuration and hyperparameters selected once on a held-out dataset and kept fixed across all backbone comparisons.

**Experimental Setup:**
The methodology was evaluated across various frozen VLA backbones and benchmarks:
- **Backbones:** Three flow-matching VLA models (π0, SmolVLA, π0.5) and one autoregressive VLA (OpenVLA-7B) were used.
- **Benchmarks:**
 - **LIBERO:** Four suites (Spatial, Object, Goal, LIBERO-10) with the Franka robot, evaluated with `n=500×3` rollouts (N=3 for selection).
 - **SimplerEnv-Bridge:** Three tasks (carrot, spoon, stack) with the WidowX robot, `n=24×3` rollouts.
 - **SimplerEnv coke-can:** For OpenVLA, 25 initial states with 3 sampled rollouts per state.
 - **LIBERO-Occ:** An occlusion-oriented extension of LIBERO, `n=250×2` rollouts, to test under degraded observations.
- **Implementation Details:** The shared encoder `ϕ` used concatenated DINOv2 and SigLIP features (1536-d). The demonstration library for LIBERO tasks contained 50 demos per task, with custom libraries for Bridge and coke-can. The selector used `N=3` candidates for LIBERO, and retrieval strength `α` was set to 0.3. DFE was calculated with `K=3` policy samples over 400 demonstration states.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments revealed distinct patterns in how selection and retrieval augment frozen VLA policies, providing empirical support for the proposed diagnostics.

**Selection and Retrieval Expose Different Opportunities:**
- **Selector Performance:** The episode-level retry selector consistently improved performance across all three tested flow-matching backbones and all 12 backbone×suite combinations on LIBERO. Gains ranged from +7.8 to +21.0 success-rate points. This indicates substantial recoverable capability within the policies' stochastic rollouts.
- **Retrieval Performance:** Retrieval augmentation exhibited more selective benefits. It provided consistent positive gains across all four LIBERO suites exclusively for π0. For SmolVLA and π0.5, retrieval yielded only small and inconsistent gains. This differential impact was notable, as π0 and SmolVLA had similar mean `pass@1` performance (64.2% and 65.6%, respectively).
- **DFE as a Diagnostic for Retrieval Complementarity:** The Demonstration-Fit Error (DFE) provided an explanation for retrieval's selective effectiveness. For π0, DFE was 0.042 (indicating the largest mismatch with demonstrated actions), while for π0.5 it was 0.021, and for SmolVLA, it was 0.003 (indicating the closest match). This quantitative diagnostic indicated that π0 was farthest from the demonstrated action prior, aligning with its consistent improvement from retrieval. This suggests that baseline success rate alone is insufficient to predict retrieval benefits; agreement with the demonstrated action prior, as measured by DFE, offers a complementary diagnostic.
- **Composition of Reuse and Retrieval:** When retrieval and selection were combined (applying the selector to retrieval-warm-started rollouts), they provided additive gains. For π0 on LIBERO, the combined method achieved a mean gain of +23.3 points over the baseline, surpassing selection alone (+21.0) and retrieval alone (+6.4). This indicates that the two interventions address different sources of opportunity—retrieval modifies the candidate distribution, while selection optimizes within that distribution.

**The Headroom Relation:**
- **Selector Gain Tracks Recoverable Headroom:** The selector gain closely tracked the recoverable headroom (`H_N`). Across the 12 LIBERO backbone×suite cells, the mean capture ratio (selector gain divided by `H_3`) was 0.90, with a range of [0.61, 1.00]. The correlation `r` between selector gain and recoverable headroom was 0.954 (Spearman ρ=0.958), which remained robust (r=0.91±0.04) even when evaluated on disjoint episode splits. This strong relationship suggests that `H_N` is a reliable indicator of the opportunity available for selection.
- **Selection vs. Resampling:** Experiments showed that the gains resulted from effective selection, not merely from generating additional rollouts. Randomly retaining one of three rollouts yielded performance close to `pass@1`, whereas demonstration-manifold selection consistently improved performance, with gains increasing from `N=2` to `N=3`. An aggregate AUC of 0.96 for the manifold score also indicated a strong association between manifold proximity and task success.
- **Headroom and Retrieval Complementarity are Distinct:** Recoverable headroom showed a weak correlation with retrieval gain (r=0.32). Retrieval often provided negligible gains in scenarios with substantial headroom, reaffirming that these two factors represent independent sources of improvement.

**Generalization Across Shifts:**
- **Cross-Simulator Transfer:** On SimplerEnv-Bridge tasks, the selector demonstrated effective transfer, improving overall success by +12.5 points and recovering 69% of the available oracle headroom. In contrast, retrieval did not transfer effectively, resulting in a mean change of -0.9 points.
- **Cross-Architecture Evaluation:** With the autoregressive OpenVLA-7B on the SimplerEnv coke-can task, the episode-level selector achieved a modest gain of +0.04 points over the baseline. However, the oracle `pass@3` was 0.72, indicating that substantial better behavior was present among sampled candidates. The disparity between realized selection and oracle headroom highlighted that large recoverable headroom does not guarantee large realized gains if the ranking signal is insufficient to reliably identify successful rollouts.
- **Cross-Condition Evaluation (Degraded Observations):** Under scene-induced occlusion (LIBERO-Occ), the `pass@1` performance of π0.5 decreased, and its recoverable headroom increased. Correspondingly, its selector gain rose from +7.8 (clean condition) to +11.2 (occlusion). This illustrated that the opportunity for test-time selection is dynamic and changes with observation conditions. Retrieval gains remained comparatively low under occlusion.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of embodied AI by providing a framework for diagnosing test-time augmentation opportunities for frozen Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Empirical Basis for TTA Decisions:** The work establishes an empirical foundation for distinguishing between two fundamental types of test-time augmentation. It clarifies that some performance issues can be addressed by recovering latent capabilities already present in the policy's stochastic behavior (quantified by recoverable headroom), while others necessitate the introduction of external behavioral priors (quantified by retrieval complementarity). This separation offers a more nuanced understanding compared to treating TTA interventions as interchangeable.

- **Guidance for Deployment and Resource Allocation:** The measurable diagnostics (recoverable headroom and Demonstration-Fit Error) provide practical guidance for practitioners. Before deploying TTA, one can estimate whether significant headroom exists to warrant a retry-based selection strategy, or if a substantial action-prior gap indicates a greater potential benefit from retrieval. This can lead to more efficient allocation of computational resources by applying the most suitable intervention, avoiding wasted effort on methods that offer little benefit for a given policy or task.

- **Refinement of Test-Time Scaling Concepts:** The findings refine the understanding of test-time scaling, particularly in VLA policies. The research demonstrates that merely generating additional samples is insufficient; two conditions must be met: 1) the candidate set must contain better outcomes (headroom), and 2) the selection mechanism must reliably identify these better outcomes. The experiments with OpenVLA notably highlight that substantial headroom does not automatically translate into large realized gains if the ranking signal is inadequate. This insight encourages further development of robust ranking and verification mechanisms for sampled trajectories.

- **Composability of Interventions:** The demonstration that selection and retrieval can provide additional gains when combined suggests a pathway for multi-modal augmentation strategies. When both recoverable headroom and retrieval complementarity are present, policies can potentially benefit from a synergistic application of both techniques.

- **Demonstrated Generalizability:** The generalization experiments across different robots, simulators, and under degraded observation conditions (occlusion) indicate that the concepts of recoverable headroom and the effectiveness of the episode-level selector are not confined to a single experimental setup. This broadens the potential applicability of the findings to a wider range of robotic deployment scenarios, including those with environmental challenges.

In summary, this research offers a systematic way to characterize test-time augmentation opportunities, moving beyond a one-size-fits-all approach. By providing tools to diagnose policy shortcomings, it can inform the development and deployment of more effective, targeted, and resource-efficient augmentation strategies for embodied AI systems.
