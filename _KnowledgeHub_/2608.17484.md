# Reuse Before You Retrieve: Diagnosing Headroom and Complementarity for Test-Time Augmentation of Embodied Multimodal Policies

> [!summary] Summary
> A diagnostic framework deciding whether a frozen VLA policy needs test-time sampling or retrieval. Two measurable factors, recoverable headroom (pass@N minus pass@1) and retrieval complementarity (demonstration-fit error), predict which intervention helps. A training-free episode-level retry selector, scoring rollouts by proximity to a demonstration manifold in frozen DINOv2 plus SigLIP embedding space, gains up to 21.0 success-rate points on LIBERO, tracking headroom at r=0.954.

## Problem

- Test-time interventions help inconsistently: the same retrieval mechanism improves one frozen policy but barely moves another, with no guidance on which policy needs which.
- Prior retrieval work studies how external experience is represented and injected, never whether the frozen policy already represents that action prior itself.
- Test-time scaling work designs selection rules but never measures how much better behavior actually exists in the candidate set to select from.

## Method

- Recoverable headroom H_N = pass@N minus pass@1, estimated from repeated stochastic rollouts, upper-bounds any selector's gain over that fixed candidate set.
- Demonstration-fit error: distance from a demonstrated action chunk to the nearest of K policy samples at the same demonstration state, probing the action-prior gap without downstream success.
- Episode-level retry selector scores each rollout by mean cosine similarity to nearest demonstration frame in a frozen encoder, plus event-schema prospective retrieval warm-starting the flow-matching sampler.

## Results

- Selector improves all 12 backbone-by-suite LIBERO cells: +21.0 for one flow-matching backbone, +19.9 and +7.8 for the others; capture ratio 0.90 mean, correlation r=0.954.
- Retrieval helps only the backbone with largest demonstration-fit error (0.042 versus 0.021 and 0.003), gaining +6.4 while others gain +0.3 and +0.5 despite matched baselines.
- Random-of-3 stays at baseline (64.5 versus 64.2), combining retrieval plus selection reaches +23.3, and cross-simulator transfer gives +12.5 recovering 69% of oracle headroom.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Baseline success rate is a poor predictor of test-time opportunity; two policies at equal pass@1 can need completely different interventions.
> - Extra sampling needs two conditions: the candidate set must contain better outcomes and the ranking signal must find them, which the autoregressive case separates starkly.
> - Headroom is not a fixed backbone property; degrading observations raises it and correspondingly raises realized selector gain, so diagnose per deployment condition.
