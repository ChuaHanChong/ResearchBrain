# S3Gym: Can LLMs Turn Self-Testing and Self-Judging into Self-Improvement?

> [!summary] Summary
> S3Gym is an interactive benchmark that tests whether LLM agents turn their own experience into better behavior, decomposing self-improvement into Self-Testing, Self-Judging, and Self-Improvement. Seven verifier-backed text games pair permissive exploration with strict held-out evaluation, and three reuse pathways, raw History ICL, score-conditioned Summary Memory, and supervised parameter training, are compared under one protocol. Gains prove task-dependent, unstable, and sometimes negative.

## Problem

- Existing agent benchmarks such as AgentBench and AgentBoard evaluate models as fixed policies, measuring task competence at one moment rather than whether past interaction improves later behavior.
- Prior experience-reuse methods like Reflexion and Voyager each study one mechanism in one setting, so improvement cannot be attributed to experience quality, judgment reliability, or integration pathway.
- Adaptive-agent benchmarks assess evolutionary outcomes or single adaptation artifacts, and none couples agent self-testing with explicit self-judging under executable environment verification.

## Method

- ==Five-stage cycle== Explore-Judge-Consolidate-Update-Evaluate across seven verifier-backed text games, with permissive exploration configurations and disjoint stricter evaluation seeds, so experience must transfer rather than be memorized.
- **Self-Judging** contract: at each step the agent emits an action plus its self-judged immediate reward, while verifier step rewards stay benchmark-side, enabling direct judgment-versus-ground-truth comparison.
- Three experience pathways: **History ICL** serializing score-annotated trajectories, **Summary Memory** compressing them into retain/avoid/next-direction rules, and parameter training that builds filtered supervised fine-tuning data from high-scoring actions.

## Results

- Summary Memory is not uniformly better: Gemini-2.5-Flash's Plants-vs-Zombies AUC+ rose from **24.4** to **238.5**, while GPT-5.5's fell from **548.5** to **33.2**.
- Qwen3-8B training reached score **30** on Trust Evolution with AUC+ **163.5**, but showed persistent negative transfer on Plants-vs-Zombies, **23** down to **6** at all **19** updated checkpoints.
- Across **98** runs and **116,117** transitions, judgment-improvement coupling was near zero (correlation **-0.010** for agreement, **-0.018** for calibration); Chess agreement was **0.496** with **0.365** over-confidence.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Recognizing good actions is insufficient: without converting feedback into executable state-transferable rules, accurate local self-judgment produces no measurable downstream gain.
> - Compress experience only when the task admits stable policy rules; state-contingent control tasks need raw history, because summaries discard the exact geometry needed to act.
> - Parameter updates on self-judged trajectories can overwrite working behavior, so trajectory filtering and judgment calibration matter more than collecting more interaction data.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper positions itself within the evolving landscape of large language model (LLM) agents, which are increasingly deployed in autonomous roles involving tool use, code execution, and interaction with external environments. Existing benchmarks for LLM agents, such as AgentBench, AgentBoard, TextWorld, ALFWorld, and ScienceWorld, primarily evaluate models as fixed policies. These benchmarks focus on static capabilities like multi-turn decision-making, environment exploration, or task completion at a specific point in time. While they diagnose failures within predefined settings, they offer limited insight into an agent's ability to leverage its past interactions for future behavioral improvement.

A growing body of research explores experience-based agent improvement. This includes context-level methods like Reflexion, which uses verbal reinforcement, and external-memory approaches such as Voyager, ReasoningBank, and Tree-of-Experience, which compress trajectories into reusable skills or strategies. Additionally, parameter-training methods like STaR, ReST, Re-ReST, and Self-Rewarding Language Models incorporate experience through model updates. However, these studies often investigate specific improvement mechanisms within particular tasks or training settings, making it challenging to disentangle the sources of improvement or failure (e.g., quality of experience, interpretation reliability, or incorporation mechanism).

Recent benchmarks have started to evaluate adaptive and self-improving systems, including PostTrainBench (automated post-training pipelines), SEA-Eval (long-term agent evolution), SEAGym (evolution of agent harnesses), PAST-Bench (retained experience in personal agents), ContinualSkillBench (in-context continual learning), FinEvo-Bench (longitudinal financial workflows), Social Gym (multi-agent social reasoning), and AI4AI-Bench (design of training algorithms for recursive self-improvement). Analyses of these systems have revealed challenges such as high variance, sensitivity to task order, and potential for skill contamination or non-compounding gains.

S3Gym distinguishes itself by providing a unified framework that couples "Self-Testing" and "Self-Judging" with subsequent "Self-Improvement" under executable environment verification. It explicitly separates model-generated judgments from ground-truth outcomes and systematically compares different experience incorporation strategies (History ICL, Summary Memory, parameter training) within the same interactive environments and interaction budgets. This approach aims to diagnose bottlenecks in the self-improvement process, moving beyond evaluating merely the outcome of adaptation to analyzing the underlying mechanisms.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this work is to evaluate whether large language models can autonomously improve their future behavior through active engagement with environments, specifically by testing strategies, judging outcomes, and incorporating learned experience. The paper introduces S3Gym as a benchmark designed to operationalize this evaluation.

The motivation for this research stems from several observations:

1. **Limited Scope of Existing Benchmarks**: Current LLM agent benchmarks primarily assess fixed policies, meaning they evaluate what a model *can* do at a given moment, rather than its capacity to *learn and improve* from its own interactions over time. This overlooks a critical aspect of intelligence and autonomy.
2. **Theoretical Underpinnings of Learning**: Theories of experiential learning (e.g., Dewey and Kolb) and reflective inquiry (e.g., Popper) emphasize that raw experience is not inherently useful. It must be actively examined, abstracted, tested, and transformed into knowledge. This principle is extended to LLM agents: merely collecting trajectories does not guarantee learning.
3. **Decomposition of Experiential Learning**: To address this, the authors decompose experience-driven learning into three interdependent capabilities:
 - **Self-Testing**: The agent explores strategies and gathers diagnostic evidence through interaction.
 - **Self-Judging**: The agent evaluates its actions and outcomes, assessing their utility and reusability.
 - **Self-Improvement**: The agent modifies its future decisions based on the experience generated and judged.
4. **Variability of Experience Incorporation Pathways**: Experience can be incorporated at different levels of persistence:
 - **Short-term (In-Context Learning - ICL)**: Direct conditioning on past observations, actions, and scores.
 - **Intermediate (External Memory)**: Compression of long trajectories into rules, patterns, or skills (e.g., summaries).
 - **Long-term (Parameter Training)**: Internalization of selected trajectories through model parameter updates.
 Each pathway involves distinct trade-offs regarding detail preservation, context consumption, judgment dependence, and risk of amplification of incorrect experience. A unified evaluation protocol is needed to compare these under consistent interaction and testing conditions.
5. **Practical Relevance to Agent Deployment**: The paper draws a parallel to "forward-deployed engineers" (FDEs) in industry, who bridge the gap between capable models and working systems by defining objectives, constructing feedback signals, and building execution systems in deployment environments. S3Gym focuses on the second layer of FDE work: enabling agents to generate, interpret, and use feedback to improve themselves, thereby closing the "experience-to-improvement loop."

Thus, the key objectives are to formalize and benchmark LLM self-improvement, systematically compare different experience integration pathways, explicitly evaluate the reliability of self-judging, and provide comprehensive empirical and diagnostic analyses of the self-improvement process and its bottlenecks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

S3Gym evaluates LLM self-improvement through an iterative process connecting Self-Testing, Self-Judging, and behavioral change. Each self-improvement cycle comprises five stages: Explore → Judge → Consolidate → Update → Evaluate.

**Interaction Protocol and Formalization:**
Each game consists of multiple independent episodes. For an episode $x$ at step $i$:
- $O_{x,i}$: Current observation.
- $H_{x,i}$: Within-episode interaction history.
- $a_{x,i}$: Agent action.
- $s_{x,i}$: Agent's self-judged immediate score.
- $F_{x,i}$: Observable environment feedback.
- $r_{x,i}$: Verifier-computed step reward.
- $y_x$: Final environment score.

An episode is initialized with an observation $O_{x,0}$ based on a seed and configuration $c_x$. At each step, the agent, using its current parameters $\pi_{\theta_t}$ and pathway-specific experience state $Z_t^{(p)}$, generates an action $a_{x,i}$ and a self-judged score $s_{x,i}$. The environment executes the action, returning the next observation, observable feedback, verifier-computed reward, and termination signal. Crucially, during exploration, verifier-computed rewards ($r_{x,i}$, $y_x$) are *not* exposed to the agent. This allows for independent assessment of self-judgment ($s_{x,i}$) against ground truth. The agent's visible transition, including its self-judged score, is appended to the within-episode history.

**Self-Judging and Experience Consolidation:**
Self-Judging provides an internal signal for identifying useful or harmful experience. S3Gym organizes this judged experience in two context-level forms:
- **History Context**: Score-annotated trajectories from previous exploration episodes are directly serialized and appended to the context for future decisions.
- **Summary Memory**: The model compresses judged trajectories into reusable experience, including "strategies to retain," "mistakes to avoid," and "concrete directions for the next interaction cycle." This compression occurs via a separate call over game rules and truncated history.

**Exploration and Evaluation Phases:**
Each game provides distinct exploration ($C_{exp}$) and evaluation ($C_{eval}$) configurations.
- **Exploration Phase**: The agent interacts with more permissive environments (e.g., smaller state spaces, weaker penalties, more trials) to generate diverse experience.
- **Evaluation Phase**: The updated agent is evaluated on stricter and held-out configurations, requiring transferable knowledge rather than rote memorization. This phase uses disjoint random seeds, and its trajectories are not used for further improvement.
A self-improvement cycle involves $N_{exp}$ exploration episodes followed by $N_{eval}$ evaluation episodes. Performance is measured by the average score $Y_{t,g}^{(p)}$ and improvement $\Delta_{t,g}^{(p)}$ over the original model.

**Self-Improving Pathways:**
S3Gym evaluates three mechanisms for incorporating exploration experience:
1. **History ICL**: Raw, score-annotated trajectories are directly inserted into the context of later episodes. This retains detailed evidence but is limited by context length.
2. **Summary Memory**: The agent receives a consolidated summary of past experience instead of the complete trajectory. This tests the model's ability to abstract transferable strategies.
3. **Parameter Training**: Exploration trajectories and self-judgments are converted into supervised fine-tuning examples. High-scoring actions are positive examples, while low-scoring ones are filtered or corrected. This leads to parameter updates ($\theta_{t+1}$), offering persistent internalization but risking negative transfer from incorrectly judged experience.

**Benchmark Games:**
S3Gym includes seven text-based games covering complementary reasoning forms:
- **Chess**: Latent-rule induction.
- **Minesweeper**: Constraint satisfaction.
- **Nullify**: Numerical transformation.
- **Tetris**: Spatial planning.
- **Snake**: Long-horizon control.
- **Plants-vs-Zombies (PvZ)**: Resource allocation and survival.
- **Trust Evolution**: Multi-agent strategy.
Each game has distinct exploration (more permissive) and evaluation (stricter/more complex) configurations.

**Experimental Setup:**
- **Models**: Seven proprietary LLMs (GPT-4o, GPT-4.1, o3-mini, Gemini-2.5-Flash, Gemini-2.5-Pro, GPT-5.5, Gemini-3.5-Flash) were evaluated.
- **Context-level Pathways**: History ICL and Summary Memory were compared.
- **Runtime Configuration**: Defined limits for history ICL input, output generation, controller interaction steps, exploration budget (30 episodes), and evaluation interval (every 3 exploration episodes).
- **Metrics**:
 - **Avg.**: Mean score over all evaluation checkpoints.
 - **Max.**: Maximum score observed across checkpoints.
 - **AUC+**: Positive area above the initial-score baseline, measuring sustained improvement.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments reveal that self-improvement in LLMs is neither automatic nor uniformly effective, depending significantly on the base model, the experience pathway, and the task structure.

**Overall Context-Level Performance:**
- Self-improvement potential varied across models, pathways, and environments. No single model or pathway consistently outperformed others across all games.
- For example, Gemini-3.5-Flash excelled in Chess, Snake, and Trust Evolution under History ICL, while GPT-5.5 led in Minesweeper, Nullify, and Tetris.
- Under Summary Memory, GPT-5.5 became strong in Chess, Nullify, Tetris, and Snake. These findings indicate that high base capability alone does not guarantee self-improvement; the ability to retrieve, compress, and apply experience is also critical.

**History ICL versus Summary Memory:**
- Summary Memory was not universally superior. It proved beneficial for models and games where long trajectories are difficult to process directly. For instance, Gemini-2.5-Flash showed substantial AUC+ increases in Minesweeper and PvZ with Summary Memory. GPT-5.5 also improved significantly in Chess via summarization.
- Conversely, summarization could degrade performance by discarding state-specific evidence or preserving incorrect judgments. GPT-5.5's PvZ AUC+ and Gemini-3.5-Flash's Chess and Trust Evolution AUC+ decreased with Summary Memory. This suggests that Summary Memory functions as a selective compression mechanism, effective when experience can be abstracted into reusable strategic rules, but potentially detrimental when success relies on precise, state-contingent information.

**Cross-Game Consistency:**
- Games exhibited different self-improvement regimes. Sparse-reward games like Chess, Minesweeper, and Nullify showed isolated capability breakthroughs (high Max.) but smaller average or sustained gains (Avg., AUC+).
- Games with denser feedback, such as Tetris, Snake, PvZ, and Trust Evolution, revealed clearer differences in sustained adaptation, where AUC+ was more indicative. This highlights the complementary nature of the chosen metrics for different task structures.

**RQ1: Can Parameter Training Enable Self-Improvement?**
- Using Qwen3-8B, parameter training showed mixed results:
 - **Substantial Improvement**: On Trust Evolution, scores rose significantly and remained above the baseline for most checkpoints, demonstrating internalization of interaction-derived strategies.
 - **Limited/Transient Gains**: Chess showed sparse but positive gains, while Snake exhibited intermittent successes that were not consistently retained.
 - **No Measurable Improvement**: Minesweeper, Nullify, and Tetris showed no score increase, potentially due to insufficient successful exploration trajectories.
 - **Negative Transfer**: On Plants-vs-Zombies, performance consistently degraded, indicating that parameter updates could overwrite effective behavior. Possible causes include overfitting to exploration, mismatch between exploration and evaluation conditions, or unreliable self-judging.
- Overall, parameter training enabled self-improvement on some tasks but not consistently, emphasizing the importance of experience quality, diversity, and correctness for effective internalization.

**RQ2: Is Agent Self-Judging Reliable?**
- **Step-Level Reliability**: Self-Judging was found to be only partially reliable.
 - PvZ, Snake, Tetris, Minesweeper, and Nullify showed relatively high event agreement (binary agreement on positive outcomes), but this was sometimes influenced by a high proportion of zero-reward transitions.
 - Normalized Mean Absolute Error (NMAE) indicated calibration issues. PvZ had the largest calibration error despite high binary agreement, meaning models could identify useful actions but misestimate their value.
 - Chess and Trust Evolution were "clearer failure cases" with near-random event agreement, high NMAE, and significant over-confidence, suggesting reduced reliability when rewards depend on hidden dynamics, opponent strategy, or exact multi-object state prediction.
- **Judgment–Improvement Coupling**: A weak or negligible correlation was observed between local judgment quality (event agreement, calibration error) and subsequent normalized strict-mode score change across the benchmark. This indicates that accurate local self-judging alone is insufficient for self-improvement; agents must also transform feedback into reusable state abstractions, decision rules, and exploration strategies.

**RQ3: Do Score-Conditioned Summaries Produce Effective Iteration Directions?**
- **Summary Memory vs. Direct History**: Summary Memory improved average NABA (normalized above-baseline area) on Nullify, Tetris, and Trust Evolution. These environments allowed for compact, reusable abstractions (e.g., backward planning, stable board surfaces, opponent strategy adaptation). Summaries effectively removed redundant details while retaining relevant information.
- Conversely, direct history performed better on average in Minesweeper, PvZ, and Snake. These games rely heavily on local board configurations, lane timing, and partially observed risks. Summaries, in these cases, might preserve high-level objectives but discard crucial state-contingent details.
- **Trajectory-Level Evidence**: Successful summaries converted payoff feedback into explicit, executable policy rules (e.g., Trust, Tetris, Minesweeper, Nullify). Failure summaries, however, provided advice that was too coarse or generic to capture precise details required for effective execution (e.g., PvZ lane timing, Chess multi-piece localization, Snake precise geometry). This highlights that effective summary-based improvement requires summaries to identify meaningful failure modes and translate signals into precise, actionable rules.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of S3Gym represents a development in the evaluation of LLM agents, shifting the focus from static capability assessment to dynamic, experience-driven self-improvement. By disentangling Self-Testing, Self-Judging, and various experience incorporation pathways within a controlled, verifiable environment, S3Gym provides a unified framework for diagnosing the complex process of LLM adaptation.

The findings have several implications for future research and development of autonomous agents:

1. **Diagnostic Framework**: S3Gym serves as a diagnostic tool to identify specific bottlenecks in the self-improvement loop. It allows researchers to pinpoint whether failures originate from an inability to generate informative experience, to accurately judge that experience, or to effectively transform correctly identified feedback into transferable behavioral changes.
2. **Nuanced Understanding of Improvement Pathways**: The comparative analysis of History ICL, Summary Memory, and Parameter Training clarifies the conditions under which each pathway is most effective. This suggests that there is no universal "best" approach; the optimal strategy for incorporating experience depends on the task structure and the nature of the required knowledge (e.g., compact rules vs. detailed state-contingent information).
3. **Challenges in Self-Judging**: The finding that self-judging is only partially reliable and often poorly correlated with actual improvement is significant. It underscores a fundamental challenge: LLMs may not accurately assess the causal importance or reusability of their actions, particularly in complex environments with hidden dynamics or delayed rewards. This indicates a need for improved internal reward mechanisms or more robust credit assignment methods.
4. **Instability of Parameter Training**: The observed instability and negative transfer in parameter training highlight the risks associated with internalizing potentially noisy or incorrectly judged experience. This suggests that for training-level self-improvement, advanced techniques for trajectory filtering, curriculum design, and meta-learning may be necessary to ensure robust and compounding gains.
5. **Design Implications for Future Agents**: The research indicates that future self-improving agents will require advancements beyond simply accumulating more interaction data. Specific areas for improvement include:
 - **Better Judgment Calibration**: Enhancing the agent's ability to accurately estimate the value and reusability of its actions.
 - **Intelligent Memory Selection**: Developing mechanisms to selectively retain and retrieve relevant experience, distinguishing between generalizable rules and context-specific details.
 - **Effective Trajectory Filtering**: Implementing strategies to filter out harmful or misleading experiences before they negatively impact future behavior or model parameters.
 - **Executable Abstractions**: Improving the agent's capacity to convert abstract feedback into precise, actionable strategies.

In summary, S3Gym contributes to the understanding of LLM self-improvement by providing a rigorous benchmark and empirical insights into the capabilities and limitations of current models. It lays groundwork for developing more robust, adaptive, and genuinely self-improving LLM agents.
