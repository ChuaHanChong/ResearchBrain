# ProPhy: Progressive Physical Alignment for Dynamic World Simulation

> [!summary] Summary
> The ProPhy framework enhances latent video diffusion models by integrating a progressive physical alignment strategy, leveraging a two-stage Mixture-of-Physics-Experts and VLM-guided supervision. This approach enables the generation of physically consistent videos, improving the Joint metric on the VideoPhy2 benchmark by 19.7% with the CogVideoX backbone.

## Problem

- Current video generation models often synthesize visually realistic but physically inconsistent content, violating fundamental physical laws.
- Existing methods struggle to provide fine-grained physical guidance, leading to isotropic responses to prompts and insufficient capture of localized physical interactions.
- Implicit physical prior learning or global-level explicit guidance often fails to enforce physical plausibility in complex, dynamic scenarios during inference.

## Method

- A Progressive Physical Alignment (**ProPhy**) framework modifies latent video diffusion models by adding a "Physical Branch" that complements the ==denoising== backbone.
- This branch incorporates a two-stage Mixture-of-Physics-Experts (**MoPE**): a Semantic Expert Block (SEB) for global physical principles and a Refinement Expert Block (REB) for token-level physical dynamics.
- The framework utilizes specific loss functions, including VLM-guided fine-grained alignment and load-balancing, to progressively inject and refine physics-aware priors.

## Results

- ProPhy, when applied to the CogVideoX backbone, achieved a 19.7% improvement in the Joint metric (from 22.3 to 26.7) on the VideoPhy2 benchmark, demonstrating enhanced physical plausibility and semantic consistency.
- Qualitative evaluations showed ProPhy generated videos with fewer physical inconsistencies in complex scenarios, such as accurate dust emissions during discus throws and correct momentum conservation in object collisions.
- Ablation studies confirmed the necessity of both the Semantic Expert Block and Refinement Expert Block, along with the effectiveness of the proposed loss functions, in achieving superior physical consistency and overall video quality.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A hierarchical MoPE structure, with global semantic and token-level refinement, effectively integrates explicit and anisotropic physical guidance into generative models.
> - Vision-Language Models can effectively provide fine-grained, spatially localized supervision signals for token-level physical alignment within video generation.
> - Explicitly conditioning generative models on specialized physical experts can lead to more discriminative representations and fine-grained control over specific physical attributes in generated videos.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated within the field of artificial intelligence, specifically focusing on video generation and its application in creating "world simulators." Recent advancements in video generation models, particularly those leveraging diffusion models and transformers, have enabled the synthesis of visually realistic video content. These models show potential for various applications, including creative media production and robotic simulation.

However, a recognized limitation of existing video generation models is their frequent inability to produce physically consistent or plausible results. While capable of generating aesthetically convincing visuals, these models often fail to accurately reproduce fundamental physical dynamics, leading to logically inconsistent videos. This deficiency restricts their utility as robust world simulators that can accurately replicate real-world physical interactions.

Prior efforts to enhance the physical plausibility of generated videos can be broadly categorized:
- **Implicit Physical Prior Learning:** Methods such as VideoREPA, PhysMaster, PISA, and PhysHPO aim to internalize physical knowledge during training. However, they typically lack explicit physical guidance during inference, which can lead to deviations from physical laws in complex scenarios.
- **Explicit Physical Guidance (Global Level):** Approaches like WISA introduce explicit guidance by employing a Mixture-of-Physics-Experts (MoPE) structure. Nevertheless, this guidance is often applied at a global or video level, making it insufficient for capturing fine-grained physical processes that occur locally or when multiple physical phenomena interact within a single scene.
- **Physics Simulation Methods:** Models like PhysGen and PhysMotion utilize explicit physics engines or methods (e.g., rigid-body dynamics, Material Point Method). These require predefined dynamics parameters and explicit rules, which can limit their generalization to diverse, unconstrained scenarios.
- **Learning-based Approaches:** VideoREPA, for instance, extracts inter-frame visual feature relationships to guide diffusion models.
- **External Physics Prior Integration:** Some methods, like PhysT2V, WISA, and NewtonGen, leverage large vision-language models (VLMs) or language models (LLMs) to extract structured physical information. While beneficial, this guidance often remains at a sample level, lacking fine-grained spatial alignment with localized physical cues.

ProPhy addresses these limitations by introducing a framework that provides explicit, progressive, and fine-grained physical alignment. It aims to overcome the isotropic response of prior models to physical prompts and their inability to precisely align generated content with localized physical cues, thus moving closer to creating video generators that function as accurate world simulators. The paper positions ProPhy as an advancement that combines explicit physical guidance with token-level physical dynamics and anisotropic generation, thereby enhancing the discriminative power of physical representations within generative models.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to advance video generation models beyond producing merely visually appealing content to generating videos that are physically consistent and plausible, particularly in scenarios involving large-scale or complex dynamics. This enhancement is intended to improve their capability as reliable world simulators.

The authors identify several key challenges and limitations in existing video generation models that motivate their work:
- **Struggles with Physical Consistency:** Current models frequently generate videos that are visually realistic but violate fundamental physical laws (e.g., incorrect fluid dynamics, momentum conservation, or object interactions). This limits their utility in applications requiring accurate physical simulations.
- **Isotropic Response to Physical Prompts:** Existing approaches tend to respond uniformly to physical prompts, failing to account for localized physical cues. This means that even if a model is "aware" of a physical phenomenon, it might apply that understanding broadly across the video rather than precisely to the specific regions or objects involved.
- **Lack of Explicit Physical Guidance During Inference:** Many prior methods rely on internalizing physical priors implicitly during the training phase. While this can lead to some improvements, it often results in models deviating from physical laws when faced with novel or complex situations during inference, as they lack an explicit mechanism to enforce physical rules.
- **Coarse-grained Alignment:** Even methods that incorporate explicit physical guidance, such as those employing Mixture-of-Physics-Experts (MoPE) structures (e.g., WISA), typically apply this guidance at a global or video level. This coarse granularity makes them inadequate for scenarios where multiple distinct physical phenomena coexist or occur within localized regions, leading to a diffused rather than focused physical awareness. For example, a model might know that "combustion" is occurring but fail to accurately localize its effects or distinguish it from other nearby physical events.

To overcome these limitations, ProPhy sets forth two core objectives:
- **Explicit Physical Guidance:** The framework aims to enable representations of different physical laws to become more discriminative, allowing the model to capture distinct physical characteristics for various phenomena.
- **Fine-grained Physical Alignment:** The approach seeks to ensure that different spatial regions within a video respond accurately and anisotropically to localized physical cues. This means that a specific physical law (e.g., fluid dynamics, collision physics, combustion) should be applied precisely to the relevant tokens or spatial areas where that phenomenon is occurring, rather than uniformly across the entire video.

By achieving these objectives, ProPhy endeavors to develop a framework that can adaptively extract physics-specific priors from textual descriptions and then refine and inject these priors into the exact spatial regions corresponding to particular physical phenomena, leading to physically consistent and spatially anisotropic video generation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

ProPhy (Progressive Physical Alignment Framework) is built upon existing latent video diffusion models (VDMs), specifically WAN2.1 and CogVideoX, and aims to enhance their physical plausibility. The framework introduces a "Physical Branch" that complements the VDM's denoising backbone through a progressive physical alignment strategy.

#### 3.1. Overall Framework Design

ProPhy modifies the standard VDM pipeline by integrating a dedicated Physical Branch. This branch comprises a Semantic Expert Block (SEB), multiple Physical Blocks (PB), and a Refinement Expert Block (REB). The workflow begins with a text prompt, from which the Physical Branch extracts underlying physical priors. These priors are then progressively refined and injected into the VDM's latent video representations during the denoising process, facilitating spatially anisotropic responses to physical phenomena. To preserve the backbone's pre-trained capabilities, each PB shares the architecture and initialized weights of its corresponding transformer block in the VDM and sequentially injects its outputs into the video latent.

#### 3.2. Progressive Physical Routing

The core of ProPhy's physical guidance is a two-stage Mixture-of-Physics-Experts (MoPE) mechanism:

- **Semantic Expert Block (SEB):**
 - **Function:** Operates at the video level, inferring global physical principles from textual prompts.
 - **Components:** Contains a set of learnable physical basis maps (`B_e`), which act as physical experts, each representing a distinct aspect of physical knowledge. A "semantic router" analyzes the input text embedding (`y`) to identify implicit physical cues and dynamically assigns normalized weights (`ρ_p`) to control each expert's contribution.
 - **Output:** The experts' weighted contributions are added to the backbone's visual latent (`X`), producing a "physics-enhanced latent" (`˜X`) that serves as a global physical prior.
 - **Training Consideration:** To mitigate mode collapse issues often encountered with standard sparse MoE in small batch sizes, the SEB employs a continuous weighted formulation.

- **Refinement Expert Block (REB):**
 - **Function:** Operates at the token level, refining the global physical priors into fine-grained, token-level physical dynamics.
 - **Components:** Consists of a set of experts, each implemented by a linear layer. A "refinement router" predicts the underlying physical law associated with each individual token (`˜x`) from the physics-enhanced latent. It then selects the `top-k` most relevant experts for that token, applying a standard MoE strategy (due to the large number of tokens reducing mode collapse risk).
 - **Output:** Generates `˜x'`, which represents a refined, token-wise physical prior.

#### 3.3. Physical Alignment Objectives

ProPhy introduces specific loss functions to guide the learning of its two-stage MoPE mechanism:

- **Semantic Alignment (`L_coarse`):**
 - **Purpose:** Aligns the SEB's routing weights (`ρ_p`) with global physical semantics.
 - **Supervision:** Utilizes per-video physical category vectors (`q_s`) from the WISA-80K dataset. The router's output (`ρ_s`) is mapped to the same dimension as `q_s` via a linear layer.
 - **Mechanism:** It computes a cosine-similarity-based pairwise matrix (`P_s`) for the predicted routing weights and a corresponding label matrix (`Q_s`) for the ground truth `q_s` within a batch. The loss minimizes the squared L2 norm between `P_s` and `Q_s`. This encourages samples with similar physical categories to have similar routing weights and vice-versa.

- **Fine-grained Alignment (`L_fine-align`):**
 - **Purpose:** Enables the Refinement Router to infer token-level physical attributes and activate appropriate experts, leading to anisotropic responses to physical laws.
 - **Supervision Signal Acquisition:** Leverages Vision-Language Models (VLMs), specifically Qwen2.5-VL-32B, which are observed to have superior spatial understanding of physical dynamics compared to generative models.
 - A pipeline is designed to query the VLM with a video and a specific question about a physical phenomenon (e.g., "Describe the combustion phenomenon").
 - Attention scores between relevant text tokens (query) and video tokens (key) are extracted.
 - A "background attention map" is generated using a generic prompt and subtracted from the phenomenon-specific map to reduce noise and enhance sharpness, yielding `Q_r` (token-level alignment targets).
 - A mask `M` is created to focus the loss on high-salience regions and marked categories.
 - **Mechanism:** The loss minimizes the squared L2 norm between the refinement router's output (after passing through an MLP to match dimensions and reduce training conflict) and the VLM-derived `Q_r`, specifically for the masked regions where `M` is 1.

- **Load-Balancing Auxiliary Loss (`L_fine-balance`):**
 - **Purpose:** Applied to the REB's outputs to encourage specialization among refinement experts and prevent mode collapse.

- **Overall Training Objective:** The final loss function is a weighted sum of the standard diffusion loss (`L_diffusion`) and the three proposed alignment losses:
 `L = L_diffusion + λ_1 L_coarse + λ_2 L_fine-align + λ_3 L_fine-balance`
 Fixed coefficients (`λ_1=0.1, λ_2=0.02, λ_3=0.01`) are used.

#### 3.4. Implementation Details

- **Backbones:** Wan2.1-1.3B and CogVideoX-5B are used for fine-tuning.
- **Training Data:** 20K videos sampled from the WISA-80K dataset.
- **VLM for Annotation:** Qwen2.5-VL-32B is employed for generating token-level physical annotations.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of ProPhy was conducted through quantitative comparisons on established benchmarks and qualitative assessments of generated video content, supplemented by ablation studies and analyses of learned expert behaviors.

#### 4.1. Quantitative Comparisons on VideoPhy2 Benchmark

ProPhy was evaluated on the VideoPhy2 benchmark, which assesses physical commonsense (PC) and semantic adherence (SA), along with their joint pass rate (Joint). Comparisons were made against base models (Wan2.1, CogVideoX), other large T2V models (HunyuanVideo, Cosmos), and physics-enhanced approaches (WISA, VideoREPA).
- **Overall Performance (ALL metric):** When applied to the CogVideoX backbone, ProPhy achieved a 19.7% improvement in the Joint metric (from 22.3 to 26.7), demonstrating its ability to simultaneously improve both physical plausibility and semantic consistency. It also showed consistent gains in PC and SA.
- **Challenging Scenarios (HARD subset):** ProPhy exhibited stronger semantic consistency and more faithful physical behaviors on the more difficult subset of the benchmark, outperforming other methods.
- **Ranking:** ProPhy, particularly when integrated with CogVideoX, yielded either the best or second-best results across all evaluated metrics on VideoPhy2.

#### 4.2. Quantitative Comparisons on VBench Quality Metrics

To ensure that physical consistency improvements did not compromise visual quality, ProPhy was also evaluated using VBench.
- **Overall Quality:** ProPhy maintained strong generation capabilities and achieved a higher aggregated Quality Score compared to baseline models and other physics-aware methods.
- **Dynamic Degree:** A notable improvement was observed in the Dynamic Degree dimension, indicating that ProPhy enhances the model's ability to capture and reproduce highly dynamic physical behaviors.

#### 4.3. Qualitative Comparison

Qualitative comparisons in Figure 5 showcased ProPhy's superiority in complex physical scenarios:
- **Discus-throw Scenario:** While prior models incorrectly depicted dust plumes spreading from the discus's center before ground contact, ProPhy accurately triggered dust emission only upon the discus's impact with the ground.
- **Iron-ball Collision Scenario:** Earlier methods exhibited violations of momentum conservation, such as object penetration or incorrect rebound dynamics. ProPhy generated collisions that adhered to momentum conservation, with kinetic energy transfer causing the smaller ball to move realistically after impact.
- **General Observations:** ProPhy consistently produced videos with fewer physical inconsistencies (e.g., water flowing from sealed containers, objects defying gravity, abnormal liquid levels, missing components) observed in other models.

#### 4.4. Ablation Study

Ablation studies using Wan2.1-1.3B as the baseline investigated the contributions of different components:
- **Physical Branch vs. LoRA:** The dedicated Physical Branch provided greater gains in PC, SA, and Joint metrics compared to simply applying LoRA fine-tuning to the backbone, indicating the value of explicit physical conditioning.
- **SEB and REB Contribution:** The full ProPhy framework, incorporating both the SEB and REB, achieved the best performance. Removing either block led to reduced scores, confirming their complementary roles in progressive physical alignment.
- **Loss Function Effectiveness:**
 - The relative-distance loss for the SEB (`L_coarse`) proved more effective than a BCE loss, which improved SA but weakened PC and Joint.
 - Both the fine-grained alignment loss (`L_fine-align`) and the load-balancing loss (`L_fine-balance`) in the REB were found to be crucial. Using only one of them resulted in degraded performance or an imbalance between PC and SA, validating the design choice of combining them.

#### 4.5. Physics Learned by Experts

Analysis of the MoPE structure provided insights into the learned physical knowledge:
- **Semantic Expert Block (SEB):** The semantic router demonstrated a meaningful structure. Physically related categories (e.g., combustion and explosion) showed high Pearson correlation in their router logits, while unrelated categories (e.g., explosion vs. refraction) exhibited low similarity. This indicates that the SEB successfully learns coherent physical semantics.
- **Refinement Expert Block (REB):** Visualizations of the refinement router's projected logits revealed that high-activation regions accurately localized the corresponding physical events in the video. This confirmed the REB's capability for fine-grained physical alignment.
- **Expert Inversion:** By manually manipulating the refinement router's logits to assign "incorrect" physical experts to objects during inference, the authors demonstrated counter-intuitive physical behaviors (e.g., a rigid car door fluttering like cloth). This experiment suggests that individual experts within ProPhy indeed encode distinct physical priors, indicating physically-grounded internal representations with potential for explicit attribute control.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The ProPhy framework represents a significant advancement in the field of video generation by addressing critical limitations related to physical consistency and fine-grained spatial alignment. Its contributions have several implications:

- **Enhanced Physical Plausibility:** By explicitly injecting and progressively refining physics-aware priors, ProPhy enables video generation models to produce content that adheres more closely to fundamental physical laws. This moves generative models closer to becoming true "world simulators" capable of replicating realistic physical dynamics, rather than merely synthesizing visually convincing but logically inconsistent scenes.

- **Novel Methodology for Physical Reasoning:** The introduction of a two-stage Mixture-of-Physics-Experts (MoPE) mechanism for hierarchical physical prior extraction, coupled with an innovative VLM-guided fine-grained physical alignment strategy, provides a novel approach to integrate complex physical reasoning into generative models. This method explicitly tackles the challenge of localized physical phenomena, which was a weakness in prior global-level guidance strategies.

- **Generality and Robustness:** The validation of ProPhy across multiple distinct backbone architectures (Wan2.1 and CogVideoX) demonstrates the framework's generality and robustness. This suggests that the core principles of progressive physical alignment can be broadly applied to improve various video generation models.

- **Improved Quality and Utility of Generated Content:** Beyond physical consistency, ProPhy also maintains or improves overall visual quality metrics, notably enhancing the "Dynamic Degree" of generated videos. This makes the synthetic content more realistic, believable, and ultimately more useful for a wider range of applications.

- **Potential for Advanced Applications:**
 - **Robotic Simulation:** More physically accurate environments can lead to more effective training and testing of robotic agents, reducing the sim-to-real gap.
 - **Creative Media and Special Effects:** Artists and designers could generate more complex and believable animations or visual effects with less manual intervention to correct physical inaccuracies.
 - **Scientific Visualization and Research:** Researchers could potentially use such simulators to model and study physical phenomena in controlled virtual environments.
 - **Explicit Control over Physical Attributes:** The expert inversion experiment hints at the future potential for users to exert fine-grained, explicit control over specific physical attributes of objects within generated videos, opening new avenues for interactive content creation and simulation.

- **Future Research Directions:** The authors acknowledge limitations such as noise in VLM-annotated regions and the coarse nature of region-level physical categorization. This opens pathways for future work to integrate aligned regions with governing physical differential equations, potentially leading to more interpretable and principled physical knowledge injection into video generation models. This could further solidify the role of generative models in high-fidelity scientific and engineering simulations.
