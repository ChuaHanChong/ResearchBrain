# R5DGS: Semantic-Aware 4D Gaussian Splatting with Rigid Body Constraints for Efficient Dynamic Scene Reconstruction

> [!summary] Summary
> R5DGS augments a physics-informed 4D Gaussian Splatting representation with 16-dimensional learnable Identity Encodings that group Gaussians into object instances, a rigid-body inference constraint that integrates dynamics only for per-object centroid representatives and propagates motion via relative transforms, and an offline CLIP-based lookup table for open-vocabulary text queries. Extrapolation runs 11 FPS faster with near-unchanged segmentation accuracy.

## Problem

- Deformation-based dynamic Gaussian methods optimize pixel-level correlations without physical laws, causing motion drift and outright failure when extrapolating beyond the training horizon.
- Physics-informed neural fields and explicit simulators need high training cost, explicit boundary conditions, object masks, or predefined material categories, limiting generality on multi-part dynamics.
- Per-particle physical dynamics formulations incur heavy inference overhead and lack semantic coherence, so independently evolving Gaussians cannot be grouped into objects for perception or control.

## Method

- Identity-Augmented Representation: each Gaussian carries a learnable 16-dim ==identity vector==, alpha-blended and supervised by a 2D ==cross-entropy== identity loss plus a ==KL-divergence== 3D spatial regularization over k-nearest neighbors.
- Rigid-body constrained extrapolation: integrate ==Translation-Rotation Dynamics== only for K per-object representative Gaussians nearest the centroid, propagating position and quaternion offsets rigidly, cutting queries from O(N) to O(K).
- Extra training losses plus querying: a ==rigid distance-preservation loss== activated after iteration 10,000 and a ==semantic majority-consistency MSE loss==, with an offline ==CLIP==-style lookup table for cosine-similarity text retrieval.

## Results

- Rigid-body inference lifts extrapolation speed from **62.1** to **73.6** overall FPS on an RTX 4090, an **11 FPS** gain, using only **9-12** representative Gaussians per object instead of roughly **40,000**.
- Segmentation quality is essentially preserved under rigid propagation: overall mIoU stays at **0.59**, with per-scene values moving only marginally, for example **0.78** to **0.77** on Dining Table.
- Photometric fidelity drops as the trade-off, with PSNR falling from **35.428** to **28.844** on Dining Table versus the semantic-only variant, and the physics baseline reaching **35.580** there.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Enforcing group-level rigidity converts per-particle physics integration into an O(number-of-objects) operation, showing structural priors can buy large inference speedups without destroying trajectory plausibility.
> - Compact learnable identity vectors attached to primitives are enough to bridge geometry and language, enabling open-vocabulary retrieval offline without retraining the underlying dynamic representation.
> - Rigid assumptions cost photometric detail at object boundaries, shadows, and deformable regions, and upstream mask-tracking identity switches under occlusion cap downstream segmentation quality.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The reconstruction and prediction of dynamic 3D scenes from multi-view video constitutes a foundational task with applications in robotics, autonomous navigation, augmented/virtual reality (AR/VR), and digital twins. Historically, 3D reconstruction methods have progressed from explicit discretized representations (e.g., voxels, point clouds, meshes) to implicit continuous fields such as signed distance functions and Neural Radiance Fields (NeRFs). While NeRFs demonstrate high fidelity in novel view synthesis, their volumetric rendering process is computationally intensive.

The emergence of 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) has provided an explicit, particle-based alternative, enabling high-quality rendering at real-time speeds. To address dynamic scenes, initial efforts extended NeRF by learning time-dependent deformation fields or scene flows. These concepts were subsequently adapted to the 3DGS paradigm, resulting in methods like 4D Gaussian Splatting and Deformable 3DGS, which achieve interpolation within observed video durations. However, these deformation-based approaches primarily optimize pixel-level correlations without explicitly encoding physical laws, which can lead to motion drift and limited effectiveness when extrapolating beyond the training data.

To enable predictive modeling and mitigate these limitations, recent research has sought to integrate physical priors into 3D reconstruction. Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINNs) incorporate partial differential equations as regularization terms to model physical phenomena. Despite their theoretical basis, PINNs often entail high training costs, require explicit boundary conditions or object masks, and may struggle with complex, multi-part dynamics. Alternatively, explicit physics simulators utilize models like spring-mass systems or graph networks to simulate specific material behaviors. While effective in targeted domains, these methods typically lack generality and depend on predefined object categories or material properties.

TRACE represents a recent development that integrates a generalized translation-rotation dynamics system for each Gaussian particle. This approach enables label-free future extrapolation through classical mechanics and Runge-Kutta integration. However, the per-particle formulation of TRACE leads to substantial computational overhead during inference. Furthermore, by treating each Gaussian independently, TRACE inherently lacks semantic coherence, making it challenging to group Gaussians into meaningful objects for higher-level perception or control tasks.

R5DGS positions itself within this landscape by addressing the limitations of physics-informed 4D Gaussian Splatting, specifically TRACE. It aims to bridge the gap by combining physical dynamics with semantic awareness and improving inference efficiency. The work introduces instance-level semantic grouping and a rigid-body optimization scheme to enhance the utility and performance of dynamic scene extrapolation, particularly for applications requiring object-level understanding and real-time interaction.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a unified framework that enhances physics-driven 4D Gaussian representations by integrating instance-level semantic grouping and an efficient rigid-body optimization scheme for extrapolation. This framework, named R5DGS, seeks to bridge the existing gaps among high-fidelity physical dynamics, semantic controllability, and real-time inference efficiency in dynamic 3D scene reconstruction.

The motivation for this work stems from several challenges and requirements in various applications:
- **Need for Spatiotemporal Modeling in Embodied Applications**: Robotics, autonomous navigation, augmented/virtual reality, and digital twins necessitate accurate and predictive spatiotemporal 3D models. These applications require the ability to not only reconstruct scenes but also to extrapolate future states from limited observations for tasks such as trajectory planning, proactive interaction, immersive editing, and predictive simulation.
- **Limitations of Deformation-Based Dynamic 3DGS**: While approaches extending 3DGS with deformation fields achieve state-of-the-art interpolation within observed video durations, they primarily optimize pixel-level correlations. They do not encode physical laws, which leads to motion drift and failures when extrapolating beyond the training horizon, limiting their utility for predictive tasks.
- **Computational and Generality Issues of Existing Physics-Informed Methods**: Previous physics-informed methods, such as PINNs and explicit physics simulators, have significant drawbacks. PINNs incur high training costs, require explicit boundary conditions or object masks, and struggle with complex, multi-part dynamics. Explicit simulators, while effective in specific domains, lack generality and depend on predefined object categories or material properties.
- **Inefficiency and Lack of Semantic Coherence in TRACE**: TRACE, a physics-informed 4D Gaussian framework, models translation-rotation dynamics for each Gaussian particle. While it enables future extrapolation, this per-particle formulation results in substantial computational overhead during inference. Crucially, TRACE lacks semantic awareness, meaning independently evolving Gaussians cannot be easily grouped into meaningful objects. This absence of object-level understanding hinders downstream perception, manipulation, and control tasks.

To address these motivations, the authors established the following specific objectives:
- To develop a semantics-augmented, physics-informed 4D Gaussian framework capable of precise Gaussian-to-object grouping through compact Identity Encodings.
- To implement a centroid-driven rigid-body inference strategy that accelerates future prediction while maintaining trajectory plausibility, thereby reconciling high-fidelity physical extrapolation with the requirements of real-time interactive deployment.
- To create an open-vocabulary querying mechanism, supported by an offline CLIP-based lookup table, which enables text-driven retrieval and rendering of specific dynamic objects across arbitrary timestamps and viewpoints.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The R5DGS framework builds upon TRACE, a physics-informed 4D Gaussian framework designed for dynamic scene extrapolation. TRACE represents a scene at a canonical timestamp as a set of 3D Gaussians, each characterized by its center, rotation, scaling, opacity, and SH-encoded color. It models temporal evolution using two modules: an auxiliary deformation field for interpolation within observed time and a Translation-Rotation Dynamics (TRD) module that learns physical parameters (velocity, acceleration, angular velocity, angular acceleration) for future frame extrapolation via 2nd-order Runge-Kutta integration. R5DGS extends this foundation with semantic awareness and an optimized inference strategy.

#### 3.1. A. Identity-Augmented Representation

To enable object-level reasoning, R5DGS augments each 3D Gaussian with a compact, learnable 16-dimensional identity vector. During differentiable rendering, these identity features are α-blended to produce an identity map at the image plane. This identity map is supervised by two mechanisms:
- **2D Identity Loss**: A classifier maps the rendered identity map to *K* object classes. This is optimized using cross-entropy against coherent multi-view masks, which are generated offline using tools like SAM2 and DEVA for temporal and multi-view consistency.
- **3D Spatial Regularization**: A KL-divergence penalty is applied to enforce feature consistency among *k*-nearest spatial neighbors in the canonical space. This helps mitigate supervision gaps caused by occlusions and promotes coherent semantic grouping in 3D.

These semantic priors are integrated with standard photometric reconstruction. The total objective function, `L_5DGS`, combines the rendering loss (a blend of L1 loss and SSIM), the 2D Identity Loss (`L_obj`), and the 3D Spatial Regularization (`L_3d`), weighted by scalar coefficients. This approach facilitates a discrete, by-design grouping of Gaussians into object instances while aiming to preserve reconstruction quality.

#### 3.2. B. Rigid-Body Constrained Extrapolation

The TRD module in TRACE predicts and integrates dynamics for all scene Gaussians independently, leading to high computational expense during extrapolation (future frames beyond training data). R5DGS introduces a group-level rigid-body constraint during inference to address this. The core principle is that the motion of particles within a rigid body is governed by maintaining constant relative distances.

For each identified object group *G_k*, a representative Gaussian *r_k* (typically the one closest to the geometric centroid) is selected. Canonical offsets (*o_i*) are precomputed for all Gaussians *i* in *G_k* relative to *r_k*. During extrapolation:
1. All Gaussians are first propagated to the maximum observed timestamp (*t_max*) using the auxiliary deformation field (`f_def`).
2. The TRD dynamics are then integrated *only* for the *K* representative Gaussians, yielding their future positions (`x_vel_r_k`) and orientations (`q_vel_r_k`).
3. The motion is then rigidly propagated to all other Gaussians within each object group using the representative's motion and the precomputed canonical offsets. This is achieved via specific formulas that calculate the change in position (`∆x_i`) and orientation (`q_out_i`), ensuring inter-point distances and relative orientations are preserved.

This strategy reduces the number of MLP and integrator queries from `O(N)` (total Gaussians) to `O(K)` (total objects), where `K ≪ N`, thereby enhancing inference efficiency.

#### 3.3. C. Additional Loss Components

To further enhance reconstruction quality and semantic coherence, R5DGS introduces two additional loss components during training:
- **Rigid Distance Preservation (`L_rigid`)**: This loss encourages rigid motion during training by penalizing changes in the distance between each Gaussian and its object representative before and after deformation. It acts as a soft constraint, complementing the hard rigid propagation applied during inference. This loss is activated after the deformation field has stabilized.
- **Semantic Majority Consistency (`L_major`)**: To maintain semantic coherence, a Mean Squared Error (MSE) penalty is applied. This loss minimizes the difference between a query Gaussian's predicted class distribution and the mean distribution of its *k* spatial neighbors.

These additional losses are computed periodically during training, with `L_rigid` activating after a specified iteration count (`t_rigid`).

#### 3.4. D. CLIP-like Open-Vocabulary Querying

For text-driven scene interaction, R5DGS constructs an offline lookup table. For each object group, a representative masked view is extracted and encoded using a CLIP-like model (specifically, Perception Encoder) to generate a text-aligned embedding. During inference, a natural language query is encoded by the same text encoder. Object groups are then retrieved by maximizing cosine similarity between the query embedding and the stored object embeddings. This mechanism allows for independent rendering, isolation, editing, and selective visualization of specific object subsets at arbitrary timestamps and camera poses without requiring retraining.

#### 3.5. E. Implementation Details

The methodology employs SAM2 combined with DEVA tracking for offline generation of consistent object masks across views and timestamps, which is crucial for training the identity encoding vectors. Benchmarking is conducted on the Dynamic Indoor Scene dataset, comprising four scenes with multiple independently moving rigid objects. Specific hyperparameters are detailed for the 3D semantic regularization (*k*=5 neighbors, `λ_3d`=2.0, computed every 2 iterations on 1000 points), majority consistency loss (*k*=5 neighbors, `λ_maj`=0.5, computed on 1000 sampled points), and rigid distance loss (`λ_rigid`=0.5, activated after 10,000 iterations).

The paper defines three method variants for evaluation:
- **5DGS**: Uses `L_5DGS` for training and the standard per-Gaussian dynamics prediction (as in TRACE) for inference.
- **R5DGS**: Uses `L_5DGS` for training and the proposed rigid-body group-level dynamics prediction for inference.
- **R5DGS w/ extra loss**: Uses `L_5DGS` augmented with `L_rigid` and `L_major` for training, and the rigid-body inference strategy.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments evaluate R5DGS across rendering quality (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS), segmentation accuracy (mIoU), and novel view synthesis speed (FPS) during extrapolation on unseen timestamps.

#### 4.1. A. Performance Characteristics

- **Inference Speedup**: The rigid-body inference strategy, which restricts physics prediction to a small set of representative Gaussians per object (typically 9-12) instead of the full set (approximately 40,000 Gaussians), results in a consistent 11 FPS speedup during extrapolation novel view synthesis. For instance, the overall FPS for R5DGS is 73.6, compared to 62.1 for 5DGS.
- **Photometric Fidelity**: A reduction in photometric fidelity (lower PSNR, SSIM, higher LPIPS) is observed for R5DGS variants compared to TRACE and 5DGS. For example, TRACE typically achieves PSNR values in the range of 34-37, whereas R5DGS variants are in the 28-31 range. This fidelity reduction is attributed to a small number of misclassified Gaussians near object boundaries and the exclusion of shadows from rigid trajectory propagation, as shadows are not classified as part of the rigid object.
- **Trajectory Plausibility and Structural Coherence**: Despite the reduction in photometric fidelity, the rigid-body constraint maintains physically plausible motion trajectories and preserves structural coherence across future frames, as qualitatively illustrated in Figure 1.
- **Segmentation Accuracy**: Segmentation accuracy (mIoU) remains largely unaffected by the rigid propagation strategy. For example, the overall mIoU for both 5DGS and R5DGS is 0.59.
- **Impact of Additional Losses**: The `R5DGS w/ extra loss` variant, which incorporates `L_rigid` and `L_major` during training, exhibits marginal improvements in some photometric metrics (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS) across specific scenes (e.g., Chessboard, Darkroom, Factory) compared to the baseline R5DGS, while maintaining similar FPS. This suggests that the additional regularization aids in reconstruction quality without compromising efficiency.

#### 4.2. B. Segmentation Failure Analysis

Lower mIoU scores were observed specifically on the Darkroom and Factory scenes. This is primarily attributed to tracking errors during the offline mask preparation stage using SAM2+DEVA. In these challenging scenes, occlusions and rapid object motions reportedly caused identity switches in the tracking output, leading to inconsistencies in the ground truth semantic masks used for training. Figure 1 visually corroborates instances where the tracker loses object consistency.

#### 4.3. C. Open-Vocabulary Grounding

The framework successfully demonstrates semantic interaction through its open-vocabulary grounding capability. By utilizing an offline CLIP-based lookup table that maps object Gaussian groups to text embeddings, the system can retrieve and render specific dynamic objects based on natural language prompts. Figure 2 illustrates successful grounding for queries such as "donut", "lemon", and "apple," where the corresponding Gaussian subsets are accurately retrieved and rendered at arbitrary timestamps and viewpoints.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The R5DGS framework represents an advancement in dynamic 3D scene reconstruction by integrating semantic awareness and computational efficiency into physics-informed 4D Gaussian Splatting. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Enhanced Efficiency for Extrapolation**: The introduction of a rigid-body inference constraint, which selectively applies physics prediction to object centroids rather than individual Gaussians, yields a substantial 11 FPS speedup during future frame extrapolation. This efficiency gain is critical for transitioning dynamic scene reconstruction from computationally intensive offline processes to real-time interactive applications, such as those required in AR/VR and certain robotics tasks.
- **Semantic Understanding and Grouping**: By augmenting 4D Gaussians with Identity Encoding vectors, R5DGS facilitates precise Gaussian-to-object association. This instance-level semantic grouping is a feature largely absent in prior physics-informed Gaussian Splatting methods like TRACE, which treat Gaussians as independent entities. Semantic grouping is fundamental for higher-level scene understanding, object manipulation, and interaction.
- **Open-Vocabulary Controllability**: The framework enables open-vocabulary querying through an offline CLIP-based lookup table. This capability allows users or autonomous agents to retrieve and selectively render specific dynamic objects using natural language prompts. Such text-driven interaction offers new paradigms for intuitive scene editing, selective visualization, and more natural human-robot interaction or robot instruction following.
- **Plausible Dynamics with Practical Trade-offs**: While the rigid-body assumption introduces a moderate trade-off in photometric fidelity, particularly near object boundaries or for scenes with complex shadow interactions, it effectively preserves physically plausible motion trajectories and structural coherence during extrapolation. This balance between physical accuracy and computational practicality is valuable for applications where object integrity and motion realism are paramount.
- **Foundation for Embodied AI and Advanced Applications**: The combination of efficient, physics-informed dynamic scene prediction with semantic awareness and interactive control lays a foundation for more sophisticated embodied artificial intelligence applications. This includes improving robotic navigation (e.g., predicting moving obstacles), enhancing AR/VR experiences (e.g., realistic predictive simulation of virtual objects interacting with real dynamic scenes), and creating more capable digital twins.
- **Direction for Future Research**: The identified limitations, such as the trade-off in photometric fidelity, reliance on robust offline mask tracking, and the current restriction to rigid bodies, provide clear directions for future research. Expanding the rigid-body formulation to handle articulated and deformable objects, alongside improving semantic supervision and mask generation robustness, would further extend the applicability and fidelity of this approach.
