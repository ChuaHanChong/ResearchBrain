# Dense Policy: Bidirectional Autoregressive Learning of Actions

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Shanghai Jiao Tong University introduce Dense Policy, a bidirectional autoregressive learning framework for robotic manipulation that generates precise action sequences through coarse-to-fine prediction, achieving superior performance across simulated and real-world tasks while requiring half the parameters and 10x faster inference compared to existing approaches.

## Problem

- Traditional autoregressive policies for robotic manipulation struggle to capture long-term dependencies in action sequences
- Current next-token prediction approaches perform worse than holistic generative policies despite success in other domains

## Method

- Developed **Dense Policy** framework using bidirectional expansion learning without action tokenization
- Implemented coarse-to-fine action generation starting from single frames and recursively expanding through upsampling
- Created lightweight encoder-only architecture for hierarchical action prediction from observations

## Results

- Outperformed existing generative policies across 2D and 3D manipulation tasks
- Achieved comparable inference speed to ACT with <**50%** parameters
- Demonstrated successful real-world performance on diverse tasks like drawer opening and pouring
- Showed superior learning efficiency on challenging tasks with shorter training time

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Bidirectional modeling of action sequences enables smoother and more coherent actions
> - Coarse-to-fine generation mimics human manipulation strategies of envisioning keyframes
> - Encoder-only architecture provides practical advantages in training and inference speed

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

- **Imitation Learning (IL) and Robotic Manipulation:** The paper situates itself within the field of Imitation Learning, a prominent paradigm in robotic manipulation, where robots learn from expert demonstrations. This field has seen significant progress, particularly with the integration of generative models.
- **Generative Policies vs. Autoregressive Policies:** The paper identifies a prevailing trend in robotic manipulation towards "holistic generative policies" that directly predict entire action sequences. The research then highlights the emerging interest in autoregressive policies, known for their sequence modeling capabilities, but notes their suboptimal performance in action prediction compared to holistic methods.
- **Addressing the Limitations of Autoregressive Policies:** The authors address the challenges faced by traditional next-token autoregressive models in capturing long-term dependencies within action sequences, leading to inferior performance compared to holistic generative policies. They recognize existing efforts to mitigate these limitations, such as multi-scale prediction, but argue that the full potential of autoregressive models for action generation remains untapped.
- **Inspiration from Human Cognition:** The paper draws inspiration from human manipulation, suggesting that humans often envision keyframes and refine actions in a coarse-to-fine manner, mirroring the receptive field concept in vision.
- **Novel Bidirectional Approach:** The research introduces a new paradigm with the Bidirectional expansion learning dubbed Dense Policy. This allows for continuous raw action space without specially designed action tokenization. The key process is to iteratively unfold the action sequence from an initial single frame into a targeted sequence.
- **Connection to Other Fields:** The work connects to research in Natural Language Processing (NLP) through the adoption of the BERT architecture, and to image generation through the exploration of non-raster order autoregressive models. This shows a cross-disciplinary approach to solving the problem of action prediction.
- **Contribution to a Specific Niche:** This research specifically targets the niche of improving autoregressive models for robotic manipulation, aiming to surpass the performance of existing generative policies.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

- **Objective:** To develop a more effective learning method that unlocks the potential of autoregressive policies for robotic manipulation, specifically achieving superior performance compared to existing holistic generative policies.
- **Motivation:**
 - The perceived underperformance of current autoregressive policies in action prediction, despite their success in other domains like language and vision.
 - The limitations of traditional next-token prediction approaches in capturing long-term dependencies in action sequences.
 - The desire to mimic human manipulation strategies, which involve envisioning keyframes and refining actions in a coarse-to-fine manner.
 - The need for lightweight, rapid inference, and easy-to-train models for real-world robotic applications.
- **Key Contributions:**
 - Introducing a novel autoregressive policy (Dense Policy) that outperforms existing generative policies in both simulated and real-world tasks.
 - Presenting the first bidirectional autoregressive learning approach for actions (Dense Process) and demonstrating its superior task execution capabilities.
 - Adopting an encoder-only architecture and demonstrating its advantages in terms of lightweight, rapid inference, and ease of training.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

- **Dense Policy Framework:** The research proposes Dense Policy, an autoregressive policy model featuring bidirectional expansion learning.
- **Coarse-to-Fine Action Generation:** The model generates precise predictions in raw continuous action spaces without relying on action tokenization.
- **Encoder-Only Architecture:** The model employs a lightweight encoder-only architecture to achieve hierarchical action prediction from observations.
- **Dense Process:** The core of the methodology is the "Dense Process," which involves:
 - Starting with a constant vector representing the initial single-frame action.
 - Deriving sparse keyframe actions via cross-attention with observation features embedded by the encoder.
 - Recursively expanding the sparse action sequence bidirectionally through upsampling.
 - Refining the upsampled actions via cross-attention with observation features, yielding a more fine-grained and precise action representation at the next level.
 - Iterating this process until the length of the generated action sequence reaches the predefined horizon.
- **Visual Backbone:** The model uses ResNet18 (with GroupNorm) as the default 2D visual encoder and a sparse convolutional network as the default 3D encoder.
- **Proprioceptive Encoding:** Proprioceptive information is encoded into the feature space using an MLP, with random masking during training to enhance generalization.
- **Loss Function:** L2 loss is used to supervise action predictions against ground-truth actions.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

- **Superior Performance:** Dense Policy consistently outperforms existing generative policies in both simulated and real-world tasks, encompassing both 2D and 3D scenarios.
- **Significant Performance Gains:** The most significant performance gains are observed in tasks involving object pose estimation, high-DoF dexterous manipulation, and contact-rich manipulation.
- **Smoother and More Coherent Actions:** The bidirectional modeling of action sequences enables smoother and more coherent actions, thereby substantially increasing the success rate.
- **Efficient Learning:** Ablation studies demonstrate that bidirectional prediction exhibits superior learning efficiency on challenging tasks, achieving favorable performance within a shorter time frame and demonstrating a higher ceiling for success rates.
- **Improved Trainability:** Diffusion models necessitate iterative steps, and VAE-based policies have extensive optimization. Results demonstrated that Dense Policy offers improved trainability with generative policies.
- **Lightweight and Rapid Inference:** Dense Policy achieves comparable inference speed to ACT with less than half the number of parameters and achieves an inference speed nearly ten times faster than DP.
- **Real-World Validation:** Real-world experiments across four diverse tasks (Put Bread into Pot, Open Drawer, Pour Balls, and Flower Arrangement) further validate the effectiveness and robustness of Dense Policy.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

- **Advancement in Autoregressive Policies for Robotics:** This work significantly advances the field of autoregressive policies for robotic manipulation, demonstrating their potential to surpass existing generative policies.
- **New Paradigm for Action Prediction:** The introduction of the Dense Process and bidirectional autoregressive learning provides a new paradigm for action prediction in robotics.
- **Improved Task Execution:** The superior task execution capabilities of Dense Policy can lead to more robust and reliable robotic systems for various applications.
- **Practical Advantages:** The lightweight architecture, rapid inference, and ease of training make Dense Policy a practical solution for real-world robotic applications.
- **Potential for Generalization:** The modular design of Dense Policy and its compatibility with different visual backbones suggest potential for generalization to other robotic tasks and environments.
- **Future Research Directions:** The authors identify future research directions, including extending Dense Policy into more general-purpose autoregressive VLAs and scaling it up to larger foundation models. This indicates the potential for further advancements and impact in the field.
