# Learning Multi-View Spatial Reasoning from Cross-View Relations

> [!summary] Summary
> The KAIST and Config research team introduces Cross-View Relations (XVR), a large-scale dataset providing explicit geometric supervision across multiple camera views. Fine-tuning vision-language models on XVR improves multi-view spatial reasoning accuracy by 1.8x and enhances robotic manipulation task success rates by an average of 13% in simulated environments.

## Problem

- Existing vision-language models primarily focus on single-view spatial reasoning, limiting their understanding of complex 3D environments due to occlusions and incomplete information.
- Current multi-view datasets often lack explicit supervision for geometric relationships between different viewpoints, leading to spatially inconsistent predictions in vision-language models.
- Embodied AI systems, such as robots, require robust geometric understanding from multi-camera setups for effective manipulation and navigation, a capability not adequately supported by prior work.

## Method

- Developed ==Cross-View Relations (XVR)==, a large-scale multi-view vision-question-answer dataset with 100,000 samples explicitly modeling ==geometric relationships== across views.
- Designed XVR tasks around three core categories -- ==Correspondence==, ==Verification==, and ==Localization== -- inspired by ==Structure-from-Motion== principles, to teach models to link features, check consistency, and infer camera positions.
- Integrated data from both general calibrated multi-view captures and robotic manipulation trajectories to provide diverse and contextually rich ==cross-view supervision==.

## Results

- A Vision-Language Model fine-tuned on XVR (**Qwen3-VL-2B-XVR**) achieved **68.06%** accuracy on the XVR-Eval benchmark, representing a **1.8x** relative improvement over its baseline and surpassing models like GPT-5 (**61.74%**).
- XVR-trained models exhibited super-human performance in Point Correspondence (**94.32%**) and Spatial Verification (**84.85%**) tasks, while showing consistent gains across other geometric reasoning categories.
- Using XVR-trained Vision-Language Models as backbones for Vision-Language-Action models led to an average absolute increase of **13%** in success rates on robotic manipulation tasks in simulated environments.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicit supervision on cross-view geometric relationships, as provided by the XVR dataset, is crucial for developing robust multi-view spatial reasoning capabilities in vision-language models.
> - Vision-language models fine-tuned on cross-view relations can develop 3D-aware representations that generalize effectively to external multi-view and certain single-view spatial reasoning benchmarks.
> - Enhanced multi-view spatial reasoning learned from XVR directly translates to improved performance in embodied AI tasks, serving as a more effective backbone for Vision-Language-Action models in robotic manipulation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Vision-Language Models (VLMs) have demonstrated proficiency in various single-view vision tasks, including optical character recognition, image captioning, and video understanding. Recent efforts have extended these capabilities to single-view spatial reasoning, allowing models to interpret object locations, relations, and motion within a single visual frame.

However, existing spatial reasoning research has largely concentrated on single-view settings. Many Vision Question Answering (VQA) datasets and spatial reasoning benchmarks provide only a single viewpoint, which can limit spatial information and lead to frequent occlusions. This limitation is particularly relevant for embodied AI systems, especially in robotics, where multi-camera setups are standard. In such applications, understanding geometric relationships between viewpoints is essential for tasks like manipulation and navigation.

While some multi-view datasets have been introduced, these often focus on identifying objects present in each view rather than explicitly modeling how different viewpoints geometrically relate to one another. Consequently, VLMs trained on such data may produce predictions that appear plausible within individual views but are spatially inconsistent across multiple perspectives. The lack of explicit supervision on cross-view geometric relationships hinders VLMs from developing a robust understanding of 3D environments.

This paper addresses this gap by introducing Cross-View Relations (XVR), a large-scale dataset specifically designed to provide explicit supervision on geometric relationships across multiple viewpoints. By focusing on fundamental spatial reasoning tasks that parallel concepts from Structure-from-Motion (SfM), this work aims to enhance VLMs' ability to reason about 3D environments from diverse camera perspectives, thereby improving their utility for embodied AI applications.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the multi-view spatial reasoning capabilities of Vision-Language Models (VLMs), which is identified as a critical requirement for embodied AI systems operating in 3D environments.

The motivation stems from several observed limitations in current VLM capabilities:
- **Single-View Bias:** Existing spatial reasoning research largely focuses on single-view settings. These provide limited information, suffer from occlusions, and do not adequately represent the multi-camera setups common in real-world robotic applications.
- **Lack of Cross-View Geometric Understanding:** While some multi-view datasets exist, they primarily address what objects are present in different views, rather than how these views geometrically relate to each other. This absence of explicit supervision on cross-view spatial relationships leads VLMs to generate predictions that may be visually coherent within a single view but spatially inconsistent across multiple viewpoints.
- **Necessity for Embodied AI:** For robots and other embodied agents to effectively understand 3D environments, perform manipulation tasks, and navigate, they require a robust understanding of geometric relationships between various camera perspectives. This includes identifying corresponding features, verifying spatial consistency, and localizing viewpoints.

To address these motivations, the authors established the following key objectives:
1. **Develop a Large-Scale Dataset (XVR):** Create a comprehensive dataset, Cross-View Relations (XVR), comprising 100,000 multi-view vision-question-answer (VQA) samples. This dataset is designed to provide explicit supervision on geometric relationships across views.
2. **Define Core Cross-View Reasoning Tasks:** Structure XVR around three fundamental spatial reasoning task categories—Correspondence, Verification, and Localization—inspired by the stages of Structure-from-Motion (SfM). These categories aim to teach models to link visual features, validate geometric plausibility, and infer camera positions, respectively.
3. **Integrate Diverse Data Sources:** Construct XVR using complementary data sources:
 - **General Domain:** Calibrated multi-view captures providing dense geometric supervision and accurate camera parameters.
 - **Robotic Domain:** Robotic manipulation trajectories offering temporal continuity and diverse viewpoint transitions from embodied interactions.
4. **Demonstrate VLM Enhancement:** Evaluate whether VLMs fine-tuned on XVR exhibit substantial improvements on both newly introduced and existing multi-view and robotic spatial reasoning benchmarks.
5. **Assess Transfer to Embodied Control:** Investigate the utility of XVR-trained representations as backbones in Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, assessing their ability to improve success rates on real-world robotic manipulation tasks in simulated environments.

The overarching aim is to establish that explicit training on cross-view spatial relations significantly enhances multi-view reasoning and effectively transfers to practical robotic control scenarios.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology centers on the creation and utilization of the Cross-View Relations (XVR) dataset, a large-scale multi-view VQA dataset.

#### 3.1. XVR Dataset Design

XVR consists of 100,000 vision-question-answer samples, derived from 18,000 diverse 3D scenes and 70,000 robotic manipulation trajectories. The dataset spans three task categories, drawing inspiration from Structure-from-Motion (SfM) principles:

- **Correspondence:** Tasks involve identifying matching elements (points, directions) across different views that represent the same physical entity in 3D space. This category aims to teach models to link visual features despite viewpoint changes.
 - *Point Correspondence:* Matching 3D points across views.
 - *Directional Correspondence:* Aligning 3D orientations (arrows) across views.
- **Verification:** Tasks assess geometric or temporal consistency across multi-view observations. Models learn to detect spatial or temporal inconsistencies.
 - *Spatial Verification:* Identifying points that violate 3D spatial consistency.
 - *Temporal Verification:* Identifying temporally misaligned frames within a sequence.
- **Localization:** Tasks require determining relative camera positions and identifying which viewpoint corresponds to specific spatial conditions. This aims to enable reasoning about relative camera perspectives.
 - *Viewpoint Localization:* Matching a view to a specific 3D camera location.
 - *Directional View Localization:* Identifying a view based on its direction relative to a reference camera (e.g., left, right).
 - *Cross-Scenario Localization:* Matching corresponding viewpoints across structurally similar but distinct scenes.
 - *Language-Conditioned Localization:* Selecting a camera view that matches a natural language spatial description.

#### 3.2. Data Generation Pipeline

A unified generation framework, G: (I, P, X, T, M) → (Q, A), transforms raw multi-view data into question-answer (QA) pairs. Inputs include images (I), camera parameters (P), 3D geometry (X), temporal indices (T), and metadata (M).

- **General Domain Pipeline (Geometry-Based):**
 - *Data Source:* WildRGB-D, providing calibrated multi-view RGB-D captures of diverse scenes. Samples with dense point clouds (at least 1M points) are retained.
 - *Tasks:* Point Correspondence, Directional Correspondence, Spatial Verification, and Viewpoint Localization.
 - *Process:* 3D primitives (points, camera positions) visible across multiple views are sampled from the 3D geometry. Using camera parameters, these primitives are projected onto 2D image planes. Distractors (spatially separated for correspondence, inconsistent for verification) are generated to create multiple-choice questions.
- **Robotic Domain Pipeline (Metadata-Based):**
 - *Data Sources:* OXE (specifically DROID, MobileAloha, RoboSet, FMB) and AgiBot-World datasets, which provide robotic trajectories with spatio-temporal metadata.
 - *Tasks:* Temporal Verification, Directional View Localization, Cross-Scenario Localization, and Language-Conditioned Localization.
 - *Process:* Utilizes spatio-temporal metadata (robot poses, camera identifiers, action sequences). For Temporal Verification, SSIM-based filtering combined with action-based heuristics ensures generated questions involve perceptually meaningful temporal changes. Strict filtering ensures at least three distinct camera views, consistent camera identifiers, and sufficient motion dynamics (e.g., trajectory duration, end-effector displacement).

#### 3.3. Experimental Setup and Evaluation

Three complementary experiments were conducted:

- **Benchmarking on XVR-Eval:**
 - *Benchmark:* XVR-Eval, a held-out set of 1,866 samples constructed from data sources unseen during XVR training (e.g., WildRGB-D boat category, MobileAloha trajectories).
 - *Models:* Evaluated a range of open-source (Eagle2-2B, Paligemma-3B, InternVL-3.5-4B, Qwen3-VL-Instruct 2B/4B) and closed-source VLMs (Claude-4.5-Sonnet, GPT-5, Gemini-2.5-Flash/Pro, Gemini-Robotics-ER-1.5). Qwen3-VL-2B-XVR denotes Qwen3-VL-2B-Instruct fine-tuned on XVR. A human baseline was also established.
- **Evaluation on External Spatial Benchmarks:**
 - *Benchmarks:* MindCube-Tiny (evaluates scene imagination from limited views: Around, Rotation, Among) and RoboSpatial-Home (evaluates spatial understanding for robotic manipulation: Compatibility, Configuration).
 - *Models:* Compared baseline Qwen3-VL-2B-Instruct against Qwen3-VL-2B-XVR.
- **Transfer to Vision-Language-Action (VLA) Models:**
 - *Setup:* XVR-trained VLMs served as backbones for VLA models. A diffusion action head was added to VLM representations, following the GR00T-N1.5 architecture.
 - *Dataset:* NVIDIA GR00T-X-Embodiment-Sim dataset from the RoboCasa simulator.
 - *Tasks:* Three manipulation scenarios (CoffeePressButton, TurnOffMicrowave, PnPCabToCounter) requiring different forms of cross-view spatial reasoning.
 - *Metric:* Average success rates across 1,000 rollouts.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded several findings regarding the impact of XVR training on multi-view spatial reasoning and embodied AI.

#### 4.1. Benchmarking on XVR-Eval

- **Overall Performance Improvement:** Qwen3-VL-2B-XVR achieved an overall accuracy of 68.06% on XVR-Eval, representing a 1.8x relative gain over its base model, Qwen3-VL-2B-Instruct (36.82%). This performance surpassed all other evaluated open-source and closed-source models, including GPT-5 (61.74%).
- **Task-Specific Gains:** Significant improvements were observed in geometric reasoning tasks. Qwen3-VL-2B-XVR demonstrated super-human performance on Point Correspondence (94.32% vs. human 92.31%) and Spatial Verification (84.85% vs. human 88.46%). Localization tasks also showed consistent gains, with Viewpoint Localization approaching human levels.
- **Temporal Reasoning Limitation:** Temporal Verification was the only task where performance declined after XVR training (from 45.29% to 41.18%). This suggests a trade-off where the emphasis on synchronized spatial reasoning in XVR reduced the model's sensitivity to temporal dynamics.
- **Closed-Source Model Analysis:** Despite their scale, closed-source models exhibited inconsistent performance across tasks, indicating that large model capacity alone does not guarantee robust cross-view spatial reasoning without explicit supervision. For instance, Gemini-Robotics-ER-1.5 performed below random chance on Viewpoint Localization.
- **Scaling Behavior (from supplementary):** XVR training consistently improved performance across different Qwen3-VL model scales (2B and 8B). Furthermore, scaling the XVR training data beyond 100K samples (to 300K and 500K) led to continued improvements, particularly in geometry-intensive tasks like Point Correspondence and Viewpoint Localization.

#### 4.2. Evaluation on External Benchmarks

- **Generalization to External Benchmarks:** XVR training improved Qwen3-VL-2B performance across various subtasks in both MindCube-Tiny and RoboSpatial-Home, despite significant distribution shifts between the training and evaluation data (e.g., different camera configurations, multi-view training transferring to single-view tasks).
- **MindCube-Tiny:** Notable gains were observed in the "Among" task (+7.0 percentage points), which requires object localization by integrating information across views, aligning with XVR's multi-view training. Tasks requiring continuous camera motion ("Around," "Rotation") showed minimal improvement, consistent with the observed temporal reasoning limitations.
- **RoboSpatial-Home:** The "Compatibility" task, which assesses 3D spatial fit from 2D observations, showed the largest improvement (+7.62 percentage points). This suggests that XVR training fosters 3D-aware representations transferable to single-view 3D reasoning. The "Configuration" task also saw a modest gain (+1.80 percentage points).

#### 4.3. Transfer to Vision-Language-Action (VLA) Models

- **Enhanced Robotic Manipulation:** When XVR-trained VLMs served as backbones for Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, they consistently improved manipulation success rates on RoboCasa simulated tasks. The VLA model with Qwen3-VL-2B-XVR backbone achieved an average absolute improvement of 13% over the baseline VLM (Qwen3-VL-2B-Instruct).
- **Task-Specific VLA Gains:** The largest gains were observed on the "TurnOffMicrowave" task, which critically depends on cross-view spatial disambiguation. Improvements were also evident in "CoffeePressButton" (precise relative distance estimation under partial observability) and "PnPCabToCounter" (generalizable multi-view pose estimation).
- **Mechanism of Transfer:** The improvements are attributed to XVR's ability to teach point-level alignment (Correspondence), explicit camera-pose understanding (Localization), and geometric consistency checking (Verification), which are foundational for accurate 3D representations and robust pose estimation required for robotic manipulation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work contributes to the advancement of multi-view spatial reasoning in Vision-Language Models and embodied AI through the introduction of the Cross-View Relations (XVR) dataset.

#### 5.1. Advancement in Multi-View Spatial Reasoning

The development of XVR directly addresses a gap in existing datasets by providing explicit supervision for geometric relationships *between* views, rather than solely on object properties within individual views. This focus on cross-view relations, inspired by Structure-from-Motion, provides a structured framework for teaching VLMs how different camera perspectives geometrically coalesce to form a coherent 3D understanding. The observed performance gains demonstrate that this targeted supervision is more effective than generic training or larger model scales alone for certain spatial reasoning tasks.

#### 5.2. Enhanced VLM Capabilities for 3D Environments

The research indicates that training VLMs on XVR significantly improves their ability to reason about 3D environments from multiple perspectives. The substantial gains on XVR-Eval, particularly in geometric correspondence and spatial verification, suggest that models can develop more robust and consistent 3D representations. This enhanced geometric understanding is a foundational capability for tasks requiring navigation, object interaction, and scene comprehension in complex 3D settings.

#### 5.3. Practical Impact on Embodied AI and Robotics

A key significance of this work is its demonstrated transferability to embodied tasks. The improvements in robotic manipulation success rates on RoboCasa, when XVR-trained VLMs are used as backbones for Vision-Language-Action models, highlight the practical utility of this approach. For robotic systems that increasingly rely on multi-camera setups, the ability to accurately integrate information across views, disambiguate spatial locations, and estimate poses from diverse viewpoints is critical. This work suggests a method for developing more capable and reliable perception systems for robots.

#### 5.4. Broader Applications

Beyond robotics, the methodology and findings have implications for other applications requiring sophisticated spatial understanding across multiple viewpoints. This includes fields such as autonomous navigation, augmented reality (AR), virtual reality (VR), and 3D scene reconstruction, where maintaining geometric consistency and understanding relative perspectives are essential for system performance and user experience.

#### 5.5. Future Research Directions

The identified limitation in temporal reasoning suggests a promising direction for future work: extending cross-view relation reasoning to explicitly incorporate dynamic temporal relationships. This could lead to models that understand both static spatial configurations and continuous camera movements. Additionally, the evaluation of VLA transfer in simulated environments underscores the need for future validation on real robot platforms to fully assess the real-world impact of XVR-trained models.
