# FBI: Learning Dexterous In-hand Manipulation with Dynamic Visuotactile Shortcut Policy

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Shanghai Jiao Tong University developed FBI, a framework for dexterous in-hand manipulation that employs a dynamic visuotactile shortcut policy, establishing a causal link between tactile interactions and object motion. This approach achieved up to 66.5% average success in simulation and 35.0% in real-world tasks, improving performance by 16-18% over prior state-of-the-art methods.

## Problem

- Robotic dexterous in-hand manipulation struggles due to limitations of single-modal sensing (e.g., vision's occlusion, tactile's lack of global context).
- Existing visuotactile fusion often uses bulky hardware or static feature encoding, failing to model the causal, dynamic relationship between touch and object motion.
- Many generative policy learning methods are too slow for real-time robotic control due to multi-step inference.

## Method

- A dynamic visuotactile shortcut policy models the causal relationship between tactile signals and object motion via a dynamics-aware latent model.
- ==The Flow2Tactile== module predicts dense contact states from visual flow, enabling a "Vision-Only" mode without physical tactile sensors and a "Visuotactile" mode for refinement.
- Utilizes a one-step flow-matching-based shortcut model for action prediction, enabling real-time policy generation.

## Results

- Achieved an average success rate of **66.5%** in simulation and **35.0%** in real-world tasks, demonstrating a 16-**18%** improvement over prior state-of-the-art methods.
- The Vision-Only mode performed strongly, while the Visuotactile mode with physical sensors provided a slight, consistent improvement, validating the efficacy of both operational modes.
- Dense tactile information, whether inferred or measured, was critical for performance, with the Flow2Tactile module's predictions closely matching ground truth in efficacy.
- Achieved real-time inference speeds (34.4ms for Vision-Only, 17.4ms for Visuotactile) significantly faster than previous diffusion policies, enabling practical deployment.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Dynamically modeling the causal link between tactile interactions and object state changes is crucial for robust and precise contact-rich manipulation.
> - Dense tactile information can be effectively inferred from visual object motion flow, enabling advanced manipulation capabilities even without physical tactile sensors.
> - One-step generative models (shortcut policies) can provide both high-quality action generation and real-time inference speeds essential for practical robotic control.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Dexterous in-hand manipulation, the ability to reposition an object within a single robotic hand, is a long-standing grand challenge in robotics. This field is characterized by complex contact dynamics, friction, and partial observability, making it difficult for robots to match human dexterity.

Historically, robotic approaches have often focused on single modalities:
- **Vision-only methods** leverage camera data to track global object states. While powerful for global planning, they inherently struggle with occlusions and the precise force control required during intimate contact.
- **Tactile-only methods** excel at sensing local contact forces, slip, and subtle object properties. However, they lack global spatial awareness and are typically restricted to localized interactions, limiting their scope for complex reorientation tasks.

The limitations of single-modal approaches have spurred research into **multimodal sensing**, particularly **visuotactile fusion**. Recent advances have demonstrated improved manipulation stability by combining vision and touch. However, existing visuotactile methods face their own set of challenges:
- **Hardware constraints:** Many rely on bulky optical tactile sensors that are difficult to integrate across full-hand articulations, especially in compact dexterous hands. Distributed tactile arrays, while more practical for full-palm coverage, remain understudied in policy learning frameworks.
- **Static fusion:** Most current visuotactile fusion methods process multimodal inputs through static encoding or augmentation of features from a single frame. These approaches often overlook the fundamental causal relationship between tactile interactions and object motion dynamics—that tactile forces *drive* object state changes.

Furthermore, within the broader context of **imitation learning (IL)** for dexterous manipulation, there's a trend towards generative models, including diffusion models and flow-matching models. These methods learn policies from expert demonstrations, mitigating the challenges of reward design inherent in reinforcement learning. While effective, many of these advanced IL techniques still primarily operate on single modalities or employ static fusion.

The "Flow Before Imitation (FBI)" framework directly addresses these gaps by:
1. **Introducing dynamic visuotactile fusion:** Unlike static fusion, FBI establishes a causal link between tactile signals and object motion via a dynamics-aware latent model. This is a significant conceptual shift.
2. **Enhancing practicality and deployability:** By inferring tactile cues from object motion flow, FBI enables a "vision-only" mode that does not require physical tactile sensors, thus greatly expanding application scenarios and lowering hardware barriers. When sensors are available, a "visuotactile" mode refines predictions.
3. **Leveraging efficient policy learning:** It employs a one-step shortcut diffusion policy for real-time execution, addressing a common bottleneck in generative models for control.
4. **Enabling full-hand dexterity:** It explicitly considers interactions across the entire palm and fingers, rather than just fingertip contacts or restricted object rotations.

In summary, FBI advances the state-of-the-art by developing a more intelligent, dynamic, and practical approach to multimodal sensory fusion for dexterous in-hand manipulation, pushing the boundaries of what's achievable with current robotic hardware and learning paradigms.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind the FBI framework are:

1. **To overcome the limitations of single-modal sensing (vision-only or tactile-only) in dexterous in-hand manipulation.** The authors observe that while humans naturally synergize vision and touch, robotic systems often struggle when relying on one modality alone due to challenges like occlusion (for vision) or lack of global context (for touch).
2. **To develop a visuotactile fusion method that dynamically leverages the causal relationship between tactile interactions and object motion.** Existing static fusion methods are deemed insufficient because they neglect how tactile forces directly influence object state transitions during dynamic manipulation. The motivation is to create a more robust and adaptable system by modeling this intrinsic dynamic link.
3. **To enhance the practical deployability of dexterous manipulation systems by addressing hardware constraints.** Recognizing that high-precision, distributed tactile sensors can be bulky, unavailable, or cost-prohibitive in real-world settings, a key objective is to enable the system to operate effectively even without physical tactile sensors (Vision-Only mode), while still benefiting from them when available (Visuotactile mode). This aims to lower the barrier for adoption in various research and industrial environments.
4. **To integrate these dynamic visuotactile cues into an efficient, real-time policy learning framework.** The goal is to achieve robust manipulation while maintaining real-time execution, which is critical for practical robotic tasks. This motivates the use of one-step generative models.
5. **To achieve superior performance in complex, contact-rich in-hand manipulation tasks** compared to existing state-of-the-art methods, both in simulation and in the real world, and demonstrate good generalization capabilities to novel objects.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

FBI (Flow Before Imitation) is an imitation learning framework that integrates tactile and visual cues through motion dynamics. Its core methodology can be broken down into several key components:

**A. Multimodal Input Processing:**
The system takes three main inputs:
- **Robot States ($s_{t-1}, s_t$):** Consecutive proprioceptive readings of the robot's joint positions.
- **Partially-Observed Point Clouds ($P_{t-1}, P_t$):** Two consecutive frames of 3D point cloud data representing the hand and the object.
- **Tactile Readings ($R_t$):** Contact force readings from tactile sensors (or predicted values).

These inputs are processed by dedicated encoders:
- **Robot State Encoder:** A 3-layer MLP converts robot states into a compact state feature.
- **Visual Encoder:** A lightweight MLP processes downsampled two-frame point clouds to extract visual features.
- **Tactile Encoder:** A 4-layer MLP encodes tactile readings into a tactile feature. Crucially, the source of these tactile readings depends on the operational mode.

**B. Flow2Tactile Module (for Vision-Only Mode):**
This module is a central innovation for enabling the system to operate without physical tactile sensors.
- **Mechanism:** It predicts dense contact states (tactile readings) by analyzing object state flows.
 1. An **object state flow** ($f_{t-1 \to t}$) is first predicted by a shortcut model using the two-frame point clouds ($P_{t-1}, P_t$). This flow represents how $P_{t-1}$ transforms into $P_t$.
 2. A pre-trained **transformer searching model** then takes this predicted flow ($f_{t-1 \to t}$) and the current coordinates of pre-defined contact keypoints ($K_t$) to predict the tactile readings ($R_t$) on these keypoints.
- **Contact Keypoints:** A comprehensive layout of keypoints covers the hand's palm and fingers (e.g., 456 points for the Shadow Hand), designed to correspond to potential physical sensor locations.
- **Training:** The Flow2Tactile module is trained using a combination of flow matching loss and self-consistency loss for flow generation (using Chamfer Distance for point cloud reconstruction), and MSE loss for tactile prediction against ground-truth tactile readings. Ground-truth tactile readings in simulation are obtained from ZeMa, a finite element method (FEM) based simulator known for accurate contact modeling.

**C. Visuotactile Policy Generation:**
This module is responsible for predicting action sequences based on the fused multimodal conditions.
- **Feature Fusion:** The encoded visual feature ($F_v$) and tactile feature ($F_{tac}$) are fed into a **Transformer Fusion Module**. This module treats $F_v$ as a template and $F_{tac}$ as a searching feature, outputting a visuotactile feature. This fused feature is then concatenated with the original visual feature to form the final fused feature ($F_{fuse}$).
- **Action Prediction with Shortcut Model:** The policy generation uses a **shortcut model**, a flow-matching-based generative model designed for high-quality one-step sampling.
 1. Given the combined condition ($C = \{F_{fuse}, F_s\}$), the model directly predicts an action series ($A_t$) in a single step (i.e., $t=0, dt=1$). This significantly reduces inference time compared to multi-step diffusion models.
 2. The model uses a U-Net as a velocity predicting network, which, in the one-step shortcut model, acts as a direct mapping from noisy input to the target action.
- **Training Objective:** The policy is trained with both flow matching loss and self-consistency loss, similar to the Flow2Tactile module, to enable efficient one-step generation.

**D. Sim-to-Real Transfer:**
To facilitate deployment in the real world:
- **Visual:** A glove is used on the Shadow Hand to mitigate metallic reflections, improving real-world point cloud quality for direct transfer.
- **Tactile:** A custom-designed tactile sensor array is attached to the real Shadow Hand. A mapping is established between the physical sensors and the abstract contact keypoints used in simulation, allowing real sensor readings to be converted into the binary contact keypoint readings compatible with the trained model.
- **Action:** Exponential moving average and action interpolation are applied to the predicted joint poses to ensure smoother robot behavior.

**E. Experimental Setup:**
- **Robot:** Shadow Hand (24 DoF) is used in both simulation (Isaac Sim and MuJoCo) and real-world experiments.
- **Tasks:** Two custom in-hand manipulation tasks (Push and Reorientation) with diverse objects, and three standard Adroit benchmark tasks (Door, Hammer, Pen).
- **Baselines:** DP3, ManiCM, Ada-Flow, and Consistency Policy are used for comparison, emphasizing point cloud and image-based diffusion/flow matching policies.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The FBI framework demonstrates significant advancements in dexterous in-hand manipulation, outperforming existing baselines in both simulation and real-world experiments.

**A. Overall Performance Superiority:**
- **Simulation:** FBI achieves an average success rate of 64.7% (Vision-Only) to 66.5% (Visuotactile) across all tasks, representing a 16.6% to 18.4% improvement over the previous state-of-the-art (SOTA) method, DP3 (48.1%). Notably, in the challenging in-hand reorientation tasks, FBI shows even larger gains, 19.3% (Vision-Only) to 21.4% (Visuotactile) higher than DP3.
- **Real-World:** Similar gains are observed in real-world tests, with FBI achieving 33.5% (Vision-Only) to 35.0% (Visuotactile) average success, compared to the SOTA baseline ManiCM at 18.5%. This represents a 15.0% to 16.5% improvement. These results confirm the effectiveness of the sim-to-real transfer and the robustness of FBI in practical settings.

**B. Efficacy of Dual Operational Modes:**
- **Vision-Only Mode:** The system performs exceptionally well even without physical tactile sensors, demonstrating that the Flow2Tactile module effectively infers crucial contact information from visual flow. This greatly expands the applicability of the method.
- **Visuotactile Mode:** While the Vision-Only mode is strong, the inclusion of physical tactile sensors (Visuotactile mode) consistently yields slightly higher success rates, confirming the complementary benefit of direct tactile feedback for precision and robustness, especially in hard manipulation tasks.

**C. Ablation Study Insights:**
- **Importance of Flow2Tactile:** Comparing different tactile generation methods, Flow2Tactile (Ours) achieved 64.7% average SR, significantly outperforming "PC to Tactile" (57.6%), and comparable to "TOCH" (63.8%). "Ground Truth" tactile readings, as an upper bound, showed 67.2% SR, indicating that Flow2Tactile's predictions are highly accurate and effective.
- **Value of Dense Tactile Information:** Dense contact information (binary or continuous) is critical. "Dense Binary Contact" (Ours) achieved 64.7% average SR, while "Dense Continuous Contact" yielded 65.6%. In stark contrast, "Sparse Contact" (link-wise) only reached 50.1%, and "W/o Contact" (no tactile input) achieved 48.1%. This confirms that detailed, dense tactile feedback is crucial for dexterous tasks, and binary representation is a good practical trade-off for sim-to-real.
- **Effectiveness of Transformer Fusion:** The proposed "Transformer Fusion" module for visuotactile features demonstrated superior performance (64.7% average SR) compared to simpler "MLP Fusion" (58.2%) or direct "Add" fusion (44.3%), highlighting its ability to effectively integrate multimodal data.
- **Generalization to Unseen Objects:** FBI (Vision-Only) showed promising generalization to unseen object sizes and chamfered shapes, outperforming DP3 in zero-shot tests. This is attributed to the robustness of the contact modeling.
- **Real-time Inference Speed:** The use of a one-step shortcut model is crucial for real-time operation. FBI (Vision-Only) achieved an average model inference time of 34.4ms, and FBI (Visuotactile) was even faster at 17.4ms. This is significantly faster than DP3 (137.9ms) and underscores the necessity of the shortcut model for practical, real-time control (target 20 Hz).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The FBI framework represents a substantial leap forward in dexterous in-hand manipulation, offering both significant technical innovations and practical implications for the field of robotics.

**A. Technical Innovations:**
- **Dynamic Visuotactile Fusion:** The most significant technical contribution is the paradigm shift from static to dynamic fusion of visuotactile information. By establishing a causal link between tactile signals and object motion through a dynamics-aware latent model, FBI captures the intrinsic relationship crucial for contact-rich tasks. This deepens our understanding and modeling of multimodal perception in robotics.
- **Flow2Tactile Module:** This novel module addresses a fundamental hardware limitation by enabling the prediction of dense tactile information from visual flow alone. It showcases an intelligent way to "simulate" or infer a missing modality, making advanced manipulation techniques accessible even without expensive or bulky tactile sensors.
- **One-Step Shortcut Policy:** The integration of a one-step flow-matching-based shortcut model for policy generation is key for real-time control. This addresses a common challenge in generative models, where multiple denoising steps can lead to high latency, thus making these powerful models viable for fast, dynamic robotic tasks.

**B. Practicality and Deployability:**
- **Flexible Operational Modes:** The ability to function robustly in both "Vision-Only" and "Visuotactile" modes dramatically increases the practical applicability and deployability of dexterous manipulation systems. Researchers and industries can deploy advanced manipulation capabilities without the prohibitive costs or technical complexities of integrating full tactile sensor arrays.
- **Enhanced Real-World Performance and Sim-to-Real Transfer:** The demonstrated superior performance in real-world experiments, coupled with the careful strategies for sim-to-real transfer (e.g., glove for point cloud quality, tactile mapping, action smoothing), highlights FBI's robustness and readiness for practical applications.
- **Real-time Control:** The low inference latency achieved by the shortcut policy ensures that FBI can operate at control frequencies suitable for dynamic manipulation, crucial for responsive and fluid robotic interaction.

**C. Potential Impact:**
- **Advancing Dexterous Manipulation:** FBI pushes the boundaries of what is achievable in complex in-hand manipulation tasks, particularly for reorientation and precise pushing of diverse objects, including those with irregular shapes or challenging properties.
- **Lowering Barriers to Entry:** By mitigating the need for specialized tactile hardware, FBI can democratize access to advanced dexterous manipulation research and applications, enabling more labs and industries to explore and implement these capabilities.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The dynamic fusion approach and the Flow2Tactile concept provide a strong foundation for future research into multisensory integration, robot learning, and embodied AI, particularly in understanding how robots can infer and utilize complex physical interactions with their environment.
- **Robustness and Adaptability:** The system's ability to maintain high performance under partial sensor unavailability or failure (as demonstrated by the Vision-Only mode) significantly enhances the robustness and adaptability of robotic systems in unpredictable real-world scenarios.

In conclusion, FBI is a highly impactful work that not only introduces novel technical solutions for visuotactile fusion and efficient policy learning but also critically addresses the practical challenges of hardware integration and real-world deployment, setting a new benchmark for dexterous in-hand manipulation.
