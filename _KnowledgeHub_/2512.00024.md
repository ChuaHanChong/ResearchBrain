# Learning from Watching: Scalable Extraction of Manipulation Trajectories from Human Videos

> [!summary] Summary
> A two-stage pipeline extracts dense, temporally consistent trajectories of hands, tools, and objects from in-the-wild human manipulation videos: a foundation model proposes semantic keypoints from one frame, then a dense point-tracking network (LocoTrack) recovers trajectories with bidirectional consistency filtering, enabling scalable robot data extraction without hardware or manual annotation.

## Problem

- Teleoperation and scripted demonstration data collection for robot learning is labor-intensive, costly, and hard to scale to Internet-sized volumes.
- Prior human-video methods focus narrowly on hand detection or object pose, underusing the rich interaction cues present in manipulation videos.
- Existing approaches like ViViDex struggle under occlusion, DexMV needs custom hardware, and VidBot omits object dynamics, missing temporal coherence or key entities.

## Method

- ==Semantic Keypoint Proposal==: a pretrained foundation model (GPT-4o, chosen after comparison with Gemini 1.5, Grok V, Claude 3 Opus) labels hand, tool, and object keypoints from the first clear-hand frame using category-specific prompts.
- ==Dense trajectory recovery==: a pretrained ==LocoTrack== point-tracking network propagates proposed keypoints across frames (stride 1, 64-frame window, 256x256 input) to produce spatially aligned trajectories.
- ==Tracking stabilization==: ==bidirectional forward-backward tracking== with ==consistency filtering== discards unreliable tracks, plus interpolation fills gaps from short-term occlusion or missed detection.

## Results

- Qualitative comparison table shows the method uniquely supports multi-object tracking, occlusion robustness, trajectory prediction, and arbitrary-video generalization simultaneously, unlike ViViDex, VidBot, or DexMV individually.
- GPT-4o was selected over Gemini 1.5, Grok V, and Claude 3 Opus specifically for superior keypoint-recognition accuracy on publicly available annotated datasets.
- Experiments across diverse real-world manipulation videos and viewpoints show accurate keypoint tracking with high temporal consistency throughout full manipulation sequences, no numeric benchmark scores reported.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Combining foundation-model semantic reasoning with pixel-level point tracking lets a system inherit both high-level task understanding and low-level motion fidelity from unlabeled video.
> - Mapping human wrist trajectories to robot end-effector motion is a simple abstraction that enables cross-embodiment policy training without per-robot re-annotation.
> - Bidirectional tracking plus consistency filtering is a lightweight way to harden pixel trackers against occlusion and motion blur without extra model capacity.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation learning critically relies on the availability of large volumes of high-quality interaction data. Traditionally, this data has been acquired through two primary methods: teleoperation and scripted demonstrations. Teleoperation involves real-time human control of a robot, which, while allowing for expressive task execution, necessitates dedicated interfaces and significant human effort. Scripted demonstrations, conversely, involve pre-programmed or carefully orchestrated robot movements. Both approaches are characterized by high costs, labor intensity, and inherent difficulties in scaling data collection to the vast quantities often required for training robust and generalizable robotic models. This bottleneck in data acquisition limits the complexity and diversity of tasks robots can learn and the environments in which they can operate effectively.

In response to these limitations, a growing area of research focuses on leveraging readily available, in-the-wild human manipulation videos found online. These videos, depicting a wide array of tasks such as cooking, tool use, and assembly, represent a rich, yet often underutilized, resource for robotic learning. Prior works in this domain have explored various strategies to extract useful information from such video data. For instance, ViViDex utilizes reinforcement learning to learn from videos without explicit object states but has demonstrated limitations in scenarios involving occlusions. DexMV aims to translate human actions into robot policies; however, its methodology often relies on custom hardware, which restricts its scalability and generalizability across different robotic platforms. VidBot focuses on extracting 3D hand motions from 2D videos but does not adequately capture object dynamics, which are crucial for executing fine-grained manipulation tasks. A common challenge across many of these existing methods is their tendency to miss temporal coherence across video frames or to fail in capturing all key entities (e.g., multiple interacting objects, tools, and hands) involved in complex manipulation sequences.

This current work positions itself within this landscape by addressing these identified shortcomings. It aims to develop a more comprehensive and scalable approach to extracting manipulation data from human videos. By integrating advanced large foundation models for semantic video understanding with dense point tracking techniques, the proposed method seeks to overcome the limitations of prior approaches, such as difficulties with occlusion, lack of multi-object tracking, and reliance on specialized hardware. The objective is to more fully exploit the rich interaction cues embedded in unconstrained human videos, thereby providing a more robust and scalable solution for generating training data for robotic systems. This approach represents a step towards bridging the gap between passive human video demonstrations and active robotic imitation by providing fine-grained, temporally consistent trajectory data of all task-relevant components.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The motivation for this research stems from the recognized challenges in scaling up robotic manipulation learning. The conventional methods of data collection—teleoperation and scripted demonstrations—are inherently expensive and labor-intensive. Teleoperation demands real-time human control and the development of specialized interfaces for each robotic platform, making it difficult to expand to diverse tasks or robotic embodiments. Scripted demonstrations, while offering precision, are typically non-generalizable and require extensive programming for each specific task, impeding rapid deployment and learning. These constraints severely limit the volume and variety of high-quality interaction data that can be practically acquired, thereby bottlenecking the training of large-scale, generalizable robotic models.

The Internet, however, hosts a vast repository of human manipulation videos across various domains, representing an abundant and largely untapped resource. While prior research has attempted to leverage these "in-the-wild" videos, existing approaches often exhibit limitations. These include difficulties in tracking objects under occlusion, an inability to comprehensively track multiple interacting entities (hands, tools, and objects simultaneously), a lack of temporal consistency in extracted information, or a dependence on custom hardware, which compromises scalability. These limitations prevent the full exploitation of the rich interaction cues present in human demonstration videos.

Given this context, the key objectives of this work are articulated as follows:

1. **To develop a novel and scalable framework for extracting high-quality interaction data from human manipulation videos.** The primary goal is to overcome the cost and labor intensity associated with traditional data collection methods, offering a more efficient alternative.
2. **To fully leverage Internet-scale human demonstration videos** by extracting comprehensive and fine-grained information that includes all task-relevant keypoints during manipulation.
3. **To extract dense and temporally consistent trajectories of keypoints**, specifically focusing on hands, tools, and manipulated objects, from raw, unconstrained human manipulation videos. This aims to capture the full dynamics of manipulation actions, which is often missed by methods focusing only on hands or objects in isolation.
4. **To achieve this extraction without relying on manual annotations or additional specialized hardware.** This objective is crucial for ensuring the method's scalability and broad applicability to diverse datasets and robotic platforms.
5. **To bridge the gap between high-level semantic understanding and low-level pixel tracking.** This involves integrating the capabilities of large foundation models for interpreting semantic cues in videos with precise point tracking techniques to reconstruct manipulation sequences.
6. **To validate the framework's ability to accurately recover complex motion trajectories** with high temporal consistency and robustness across a diverse collection of real-world human manipulation videos, spanning various tasks and viewpoints.

By achieving these objectives, the research aims to provide a foundation for more data-efficient robot learning and to facilitate the creation of large-scale datasets for training advanced robotic manipulation policies.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology is structured as a two-stage trajectory extraction framework designed to recover dense and temporally consistent trajectories of keypoints (hands, tools, and manipulated objects) from unconstrained human manipulation videos. This framework operates without requiring manual annotations or additional hardware, integrating high-level semantic reasoning with low-level pixel-based tracking.

#### 3.1. A. Semantic Keypoint Proposal via Foundation Models

The initial stage involves leveraging the semantic understanding capabilities of large-scale foundation models.
1. **Input Selection**: Given a human manipulation video, a specific frame is chosen as the input for the foundation model. This frame is typically the first one in the sequence where a clear presence of hands and relevant objects can be observed, allowing the model to establish a strong initial context.
2. **Foundation Model Application**: A pretrained large-scale foundation model is applied to this selected frame. Through an evaluation of several state-of-the-art models (including GPT-4o, Gemini 1.5, Grok V, and Claude 3 Opus), GPT-4o was selected for its demonstrated accuracy in recognizing and proposing keypoints within publicly available datasets.
3. **Prompt Engineering**: To enhance the accuracy and specificity of keypoint identification, category-specific prompts are manually designed. These prompts guide the foundation model in identifying semantically meaningful keypoints pertinent to the manipulation task, such as specific hand joints (e.g., fingertips), tool tips, and critical points on manipulated objects.
4. **Output**: The foundation model outputs initial semantic keypoints, which serve as "seeds" for the subsequent tracking stage. These keypoints represent the spatial locations of relevant entities in the initial frame.

#### 3.2. B. Trajectory Generation via Point Tracking

Following the identification of initial semantic keypoints, the second stage focuses on generating their temporal trajectories across the entire video sequence.
1. **Dense Point Tracking Network**: A pretrained dense point tracking network, specifically LocoTrack, is employed for this purpose. This module takes the entire video sequence along with the semantically proposed initial keypoints as its input.
2. **Trajectory Recovery**: The point tracking network processes the video frames to output spatially aligned trajectories for each keypoint. This process allows for the continuous tracking of entities like fingertips, tool tips, and object contours, even when faced with challenges such as occlusions, motion blur, and changes in viewpoint. The system is configured with a frame stride of 1, meaning every frame is processed, and a tracking window size of 64 frames. Input videos are resized to a resolution of 256x256 pixels to ensure efficient processing.
3. **Enhancements for Stability and Robustness**: To improve the robustness and temporal consistency of the extracted trajectories, several techniques are applied:
 - **Bidirectional Tracking**: Both forward and backward tracking are performed. This helps in verifying the consistency of tracks and mitigating errors that might accumulate in a single-direction tracking approach.
 - **Consistency Filtering**: A filtering mechanism is applied to discard unreliable tracks. This helps in removing erroneous detections or tracks that lose coherence over time.
 - **Trajectory Interpolation**: For segments where short-term occlusions or temporary missing detections occur, trajectory interpolation is performed to maintain continuous and smooth keypoint paths.
4. **Motion Standardization**: To facilitate the transfer of learned manipulation skills to heterogeneous robotic platforms, a standardization step is implemented. The extracted human wrist keypoint trajectories are mapped to represent robot end-effector motion. This abstraction aims to ensure data compatibility and consistent policy training across different robot embodiments, decoupling the learning process from specific human anatomical details and mapping it to a more generalizable robot control space.

The overall approach integrates the high-level reasoning of foundation models, which can understand the semantic meaning of keypoints, with the low-level precision of point tracking networks to robustly reconstruct detailed manipulation sequences from unconstrained human videos.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluations conducted by the authors demonstrate several key findings regarding the proposed framework's capabilities:

1. **Accurate and Comprehensive Keypoint Tracking**: The method is shown to accurately track keypoints throughout the entire manipulation process. This includes not only hands but also task-relevant tools and manipulated objects. This comprehensive tracking capability addresses a limitation of prior works that often focused solely on hands or lacked robust object tracking.
2. **Recovery of Complex Motion Trajectories**: The framework effectively recovers complex motion trajectories involving multiple interacting keypoints. This indicates its ability to capture the intricate dynamics of human manipulation actions, which is crucial for learning dexterous robotic behaviors.
3. **High Temporal Consistency and Robustness**: Experimental results highlight the high temporal consistency and robustness of the extracted trajectories. The use of bidirectional tracking, consistency filtering, and trajectory interpolation contributes to maintaining smooth and reliable tracks, even in the presence of common video challenges such as occlusions and motion blur.
4. **Foundation Model Selection**: Through comparative evaluation of several large foundation models (GPT-4o, Gemini 1.5, Grok V, and Claude 3 Opus), GPT-4o was selected as the most effective for semantic keypoint proposal. This decision was based on its "outstanding accuracy in recognizing keypoints annotated in publicly available datasets," indicating its superior performance in the initial semantic understanding stage.
5. **Comparative Performance with Existing Methods**: As summarized in Table I, the proposed method exhibits advantages over several existing approaches in critical aspects:
 - **Multiple Objects (Multi-obj.)**: Unlike ViViDex and VidBot, this method supports the tracking and analysis of multiple objects simultaneously.
 - **Occlusion Robustness**: The framework demonstrates robustness to occlusions, a common challenge that ViViDex and VidBot struggled with. DexMV also showed robustness in this area.
 - **Trajectory Prediction (Trajectory)**: The method is capable of performing trajectory prediction, a feature shared with ViViDex and DexMV, but not explicitly supported by VidBot according to the table. This refers to the system's ability to output continuous motion paths.
 - **Arbitrary Videos (Arbitrary Vid.)**: Crucially, the approach generalizes to arbitrary videos, a capability shared with ViViDex and VidBot, but not DexMV, which often relies on custom hardware, thus limiting its application to specific video types or capture setups.
6. **Annotation-Free and Hardware-Independent Operation**: A significant outcome is the ability to achieve temporally coherent trajectory recovery without the need for manual annotations or custom hardware. This contributes to the method's scalability and broad applicability, distinguishing it from methods that incur high annotation costs or require specialized equipment.
7. **Motion Standardization for Transfer**: The successful implementation of mapping human wrist keypoint trajectories to robot end-effector motion demonstrates a practical approach to standardize extracted movements. This facilitates cross-domain transfer and enables consistent policy training across diverse robotic platforms, addressing the heterogeneity challenge in robot learning.

In summary, the findings indicate that the proposed framework effectively addresses several limitations of previous works by providing a robust, scalable, and comprehensive method for extracting fine-grained manipulation trajectories from unconstrained human videos.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research presented in this paper holds several significant implications and potential impacts across robotic manipulation and broader AI domains:

1. **Scalable and Cost-Effective Data Collection**: The most immediate significance is the provision of a scalable and low-cost alternative to traditional robotic data collection pipelines. By leveraging the vast, readily available resource of in-the-wild human manipulation videos, the method bypasses the expensive, labor-intensive, and time-consuming processes of teleoperation or scripted demonstrations. This drastically reduces the barrier to acquiring large volumes of diverse, high-quality interaction data, which is crucial for the development of robust and generalizable robotic systems.
2. **Enhanced Data Utilization for Robot Learning**: The framework enables a more comprehensive utilization of Internet-scale human demonstration videos. By extracting dense and temporally consistent trajectories of not just hands, but also tools and manipulated objects, the method captures a richer set of interaction cues compared to prior approaches. This granular detail can significantly improve the quality and richness of training data, leading to more capable and dexterous robot policies.
3. **Bridge Between Passive Observation and Active Imitation**: This work offers a viable bridge between passive video demonstrations and active robotic imitation. By translating observational data (human videos) into actionable, structured trajectories, it provides the necessary input for robots to learn and replicate complex manipulation skills. This could accelerate the development of imitation learning techniques, allowing robots to quickly acquire new skills by simply observing human experts.
4. **Advancement in Multimodal Robot Learning**: The ability to extract fine-grained manipulation trajectories from videos, combined with semantic understanding, lays a foundation for advancing multimodal robot learning frameworks. Such rich data can be integrated with other modalities (e.g., force feedback, tactile sensing) to develop more sophisticated and context-aware robot behaviors.
5. **Facilitating Cross-Domain Transfer**: The proposed motion standardization, which maps human wrist trajectories to robot end-effector motion, directly addresses the challenge of transferring skills across heterogeneous robotic platforms. This abstraction allows for consistent policy training regardless of the underlying robot embodiment, thus increasing the generalizability and applicability of learned skills.
6. **Addressing Limitations of Prior Work**: The method effectively overcomes several critical limitations identified in previous research, including issues with occlusion robustness, the ability to track multiple interacting objects, and the reliance on custom hardware or extensive manual annotations. By providing a solution that is robust, comprehensive, and independent of specialized equipment, it expands the practical utility of learning from human videos.
7. **Potential for Large-Scale Dataset Construction**: The scalable nature of this approach has the potential to facilitate the construction of large-scale datasets specifically designed for pretraining robot policies. Such datasets, automatically generated from human videos, could serve as foundational resources for a wide range of robotic tasks, much like large language and vision datasets have propelled progress in other AI domains.

In conclusion, this research presents a method that could significantly impact the scalability, efficiency, and capability of robotic manipulation learning by providing a robust, generalizable, and cost-effective means of extracting high-quality interaction data from readily available human videos.
