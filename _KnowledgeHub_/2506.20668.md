# DemoDiffusion: One-Shot Human Imitation using pre-trained Diffusion Policy

> [!summary] Summary
> Carnegie Mellon University researchers developed DemoDiffusion, a method that allows robots to execute manipulation tasks by imitating a single human demonstration. It integrates kinematically retargeted human movements with a pre-trained generalist diffusion policy, achieving an 83.8% average success rate across eight real-world tasks, outperforming direct policy deployment (13.8%) and open-loop retargeting (52.5%).

## Problem

- Existing generalist robot policies struggle with zero-shot deployment to novel environments or unseen tasks, often requiring costly robot-specific fine-tuning.
- Current one-shot imitation from human demonstrations often fails due to embodiment mismatches or necessitates extensive online interaction for adaptation.
- There is a need for a practical, user-friendly method to program robots for new manipulation tasks in unstructured environments.

## Method

- A methodology is proposed that combines kinematic retargeting of human hand motions with a pre-trained diffusion policy.
- 3D human hand poses from a single demonstration are extracted and geometrically mapped to an initial open-loop robot end-effector trajectory.
- This trajectory is then refined through a closed-loop denoising process by a pre-trained generalist diffusion policy, starting from an intermediate noise level, to generate feasible robot actions.

## Results

- Achieved an average **83.8%** success rate across eight real-world manipulation tasks with a two-finger gripper, significantly exceeding direct generalist policy deployment (**13.8%**) and open-loop kinematic retargeting (**52.5%**).
- Demonstrated superior performance in dexterous grasping simulation for unseen objects with a 16-DOF hand, yielding **31.0%** success compared to **1.6%** for retargeting and **26.5%** for the base policy.
- Exhibited robustness to noisy human motion capture data and variations in kinematic retargeting, while also generalizing to new objects within the same category.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Human hand trajectories provide a useful approximate prior for robot end-effector motions, which can be extracted via kinematic retargeting.
> - Pre-trained generalist diffusion policies are capable of refining these approximate trajectories to overcome embodiment mismatches and generate robust, closed-loop robot actions.
> - Initiating the diffusion policy's denoising process from an intermediate noise level applied to the retargeted trajectory effectively blends human guidance with the policy's learned behavior manifold.
