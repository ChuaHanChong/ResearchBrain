# Efficient Physics Simulation for 3D Scenes via MLLM-Guided Gaussian Splatting

> [!summary] Summary
> PhysSplat enables efficient, physics-based dynamic interactions for 3D scenes represented by Gaussian Splatting. It utilizes Multi-modal Large Language Models for zero-shot prediction of physical properties, coupled with an adaptive sampling strategy, achieving realistic motion generation within two minutes on a single GPU.

## Problem

- Existing 3D representations like NeRF and 3DGS excel at rendering static scenes but lack inherent mechanisms for physical interaction and dynamics.
- Accurately estimating physical properties (e.g., density, Young's modulus) for 3D objects is a labor-intensive manual process or computationally expensive when learned from multi-view videos or video diffusion models.
- Prior physics-integrated 3D methods often struggle with efficiency for complex scenes, are restricted to specific object types, or produce physically inconsistent results.

## Method

- The pipeline begins with 3D open-vocabulary segmentation of ==Gaussian Splatting== scenes by leveraging 2D foundation models (RAM, Grounding **DINO**, ==SAM==) to identify and isolate individual objects.
- A Multi-modal Large Language Model (**MLLM-P3**) module, using GPT-4V, **BLIP**, and ==CLIP==, performs zero-shot prediction of mean physical properties (density, Young's modulus, Poisson's ratio) for each object from a rendered canonical view and generated text description.
- Physical simulation is optimized through Material Property Distribution Prediction (MPDP) for local property variations and Physical-Geometric Adaptive Sampling (PGAS), which dynamically adjusts particle density for MLS-==MPM== simulation based on material stiffness and geometric curvature.

## Results

- PhysSplat generates highly realistic and physically plausible 3D dynamics, with qualitative comparisons showing motion patterns that closely match real captured videos and maintaining good motion consistency on synthesized datasets.
- The method achieves unprecedented efficiency, completing physics-based 4D generation in approximately 2 minutes on a single NVIDIA 3090 GPU, significantly outperforming state-of-the-art baselines that take 0.1 to 1.5 hours.
- Quantitatively, PhysSplat consistently leads or matches state-of-the-art methods across various datasets in user-study-based Realism Scores (e.g., RS 4.66 vs. 4.62) and AI-predicted Aesthetic Scores (e.g., AS 7.89 vs. 7.83), demonstrating superior perceived quality and applicability to multi-object open-world scenes.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Multi-modal Large Language Models can effectively perform zero-shot inference of an object's physical properties directly from visual cues, mimicking human intuitive physics perception.
> - Representing physical properties as a probability distribution across an object's geometry, rather than a single global value, allows for more accurate and nuanced simulation of deformations.
> - An adaptive sampling strategy that prioritizes softer materials and high-curvature regions during physics simulation drastically reduces computational cost while preserving fine-grained details and overall visual fidelity.
