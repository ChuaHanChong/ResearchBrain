# When Does LeJEPA Learn a World Model?

> [!summary] Summary
> Research by Klindt, LeCun, and Balestriero formally defines and proves the conditions under which a Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA), specifically LeJEPA, learns a "World Model" by recovering the true underlying latent variables. It demonstrates that LeJEPA achieves linear identifiability if and only if the latent variables follow an isotropic Gaussian distribution, which then enables optimal latent-space planning.

## Problem

- Lack of formal guarantees that representations learned by Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs) genuinely reflect the underlying degrees of freedom of the world.
- The absence of a clear definition and specific conditions for when a learned representation qualifies as a "World Model" capable of supporting reliable planning and generalization.
- Learned representations that entangle unrelated latent variables (e.g., an object's position with its color) may hinder generalization to novel compositions or robust planning.

## Method

- Developed a theoretical framework defining a "Gaussian world" where independent, stationary, standard Gaussian latent variables evolve under Ornstein–Uhlenbeck transitions.
- Analyzed LeJEPA's objective function, which minimizes an alignment loss while explicitly regularizing the embedding distribution towards an isotropic Gaussian (Sketched Isotropic Gaussian Regularization, SIGReg).
- Employed spectral analysis, Hermite polynomials, and Sturm–Liouville theory for theoretical proofs, formally verified using the Lean 4 theorem prover, alongside empirical validation in 2D simulations, high-dimensional scaling, and pixel-based robotic control.

## Results

- For a Gaussian world, LeJEPA provably recovers latent variables up to an orthogonal transformation, with empirical validation showing linear identifiability (R² > 0.999) up to 1024 latent dimensions.
- Experiments with varied latent distributions confirm that linear recovery peaks sharply only when the true latents are Gaussian, establishing Gaussianity as a necessary condition for LeJEPA's linear identifiability.
- LeJEPA-trained encoders exhibiting high linear identifiability enable latent-space planning in a robotic control environment with control costs statistically indistinguishable from an oracle controller operating in the true joint space.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - LeJEPA achieves linear identifiability—recovering true latent variables up to an orthogonal transformation—if and only if the underlying latent variables follow an isotropic Gaussian distribution.
> - The necessity of Gaussian latents for identifiability in this nonlinear self-supervised setting contrasts with classical linear Independent Component Analysis (ICA), where Gaussianity typically hinders source separation.
> - Orthogonal identifiability of learned representations enables optimal latent-space planning for tasks with rotation-invariant cost functions, thereby forming a functional "World Model" for control.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work is situated within the field of self-supervised learning (SSL) and representation learning, specifically addressing the question of when a learned representation can be considered a "World Model."

Self-supervised learning aims to acquire useful data representations without explicit labels by leveraging the intrinsic structure of the data, often through predictive tasks. Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs) represent a category of SSL models that train representations to produce similar embeddings for related views of an input, while employing mechanisms to prevent representational collapse. These JEPAs have demonstrated empirical effectiveness across various data modalities, including images, video, and for latent-space planning.

The concept of a "World Model" has roots in cognitive science, control theory, and modern machine learning, referring to an internal model that can simulate and predict system dynamics. In machine learning, neural world models have evolved from recurrent controller-model pairs to architectures that learn latent dynamics directly from pixels. JEPAs aim to learn representations that predict in a latent space rather than a pixel space, positing that this avoids expending capacity on irrelevant detail.

A critical challenge in representation learning is identifiability – the guarantee that a learned representation faithfully recovers the underlying latent variables of a data-generating process. In the context of nonlinear independent component analysis (ICA), identifiability is generally unachievable without additional structural assumptions on the data or learning process, such as non-stationarity, temporal dependence, or auxiliary variables. Previous identifiability results often impose constraints on the representation, such as smoothness. Prior JEPAs, which frequently rely on implicit collapse-prevention mechanisms, have not been accompanied by formal identifiability guarantees. LeJEPA, a variant of JEPA, introduces an explicit regularization towards an isotropic Gaussian distribution (Sketched Isotropic Gaussian Regularization, SIGReg) to prevent collapse, a design choice that the authors investigate for its implications on identifiability.

This paper positions itself by addressing the absence of identifiability results for JEPAs, specifically focusing on how the explicit Gaussian regularization in LeJEPA influences the recovery of underlying world structure. It builds upon spectral analysis methods previously applied to SSL objectives and connects to Slow Feature Analysis (SFA) theory, which seeks to identify slowly varying features in time-series data.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to formally determine the conditions under which a representation learned by a Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA), specifically LeJEPA, constitutes a "World Model." The authors define a World Model as a representation that provides a faithful map of the world’s latent structure.

The primary motivation for this inquiry stems from the observation that while JEPAs have shown empirical success in various applications, the fundamental question of whether the learned representations genuinely reflect the underlying degrees of freedom of the world remains open. A representation that merely performs well on specific tasks but "entangles unrelated latent variables," such as an object's position with its color or velocity with texture, may fail to generalize reliably when the environment changes or for tasks requiring compositional reasoning.

To address this, the authors propose "linear identifiability" as a necessary criterion for a representation to be considered a faithful World Model. Linear identifiability implies a mathematical guarantee that the learned representation recovers the true underlying latent variables up to simple symmetries, such as rotation. This property is considered crucial for several reasons:
- **Reliable Planning:** Representations that scramble the world's true degrees of freedom cannot reliably support planning algorithms, which often depend on disentangled, interpretable latent states.
- **Compositional Generalization:** To generalize effectively to novel compositions of familiar elements, representations must maintain the distinctness of fundamental latent factors.
- **Linear Probing:** The common practice of evaluating representations using linear probes implicitly queries whether the model has learned a linear projection of the latent variables. Without linear identifiability, linear probes cannot perfectly recover these variables.

The specific focus on LeJEPA is motivated by its architectural choice to explicitly regularize the embedding distribution towards an isotropic Gaussian using SIGReg. This explicit regularization, unlike the implicit collapse-prevention mechanisms in other SSL methods, is hypothesized to provide a clearer pathway for establishing formal identifiability guarantees.

Ultimately, the paper aims to provide a theoretical foundation for LeJEPA, transforming it from an empirically successful technique into one with mathematical guarantees for recovering the structure of the world. This goal is to contribute to the development of World Models that are not only effective but also provably faithful to the underlying reality.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research combines theoretical proofs with empirical validation to investigate the conditions for linear identifiability in LeJEPA.

#### 3.1. Theoretical Framework

The authors define a "World Model" in terms of latent variables `z ∈ R^n` that generate observations `x = g(z)` through an unknown, potentially nonlinear, mixing process `g`. The goal of the learner is to train a representation `f` such that the composed map `h = f ◦ g` recovers `z` up to symmetries.

The theoretical analysis is based on a broad class of worlds defined by three core assumptions for the latent variables `z`:
1. **Independence:** Latent components `z_i` are independent, and their transitions `p(z'_i | z_i)` are also independent for all `i ≠ j`.
2. **Stationarity:** Both views (initial `z` and subsequent `z'`) share the same marginal distribution: `p(z) = p(z')`.
3. **Additive noise:** Transitions are modeled as `z'_i = m_i(z_i) + η_i`, where `η_i` is independent of `z_i`.

Within this framework, a specific distributional choice, the "Gaussian World," is made for the forward result: `z ∼ N(0, I_n)`. This choice, combined with the stationarity and additive noise assumptions, implies Ornstein–Uhlenbeck (OU) transitions of the form `z' = ρz + √(1 − ρ²)η`, where `η ∼ N(0, I_n)` is independent noise and `ρ ∈ (0, 1)` controls correlation.

The "Learner" is LeJEPA, characterized by an objective function `L(h)` that combines an invariance loss and a Gaussianity regularizer:
`min_h L(h) = E[||h(z') - h(z)||²]` (Alignment)
`s.t. h(z) ∼ N(0, I_n)` (Gaussianity, enforced by SIGReg)
The authors demonstrate that, with the Gaussianity constraint, minimizing the alignment loss is equivalent to maximizing the correlation `Σ_i E[h_i(z')h_i(z)]`.

#### 3.2. Mathematical Tools

The theoretical proofs extensively utilize spectral analysis of the transition operator, which describes how functions of `z` evolve to `z'`.
- **Hermite Polynomials:** For the Gaussian world, the eigenfunctions of the transition operator are the Hermite polynomials. A key property is that the transition operator attenuates higher-degree Hermite polynomials more than lower-degree ones (`E_η[He_k(z')] = ρ^k He_k(z)`). Furthermore, different Hermite components do not interact across views (`E[H_α(z')H_β(z)] = ρ^|α| δ_αβ`, Mehler’s formula), implying that nonlinearity strictly reduces correlation between `h(z)` and `h(z')`.
- **Sturm–Liouville Theory:** For the converse result, Sturm–Liouville theory is employed to characterize the eigenfunctions of the transition operator for general latent distributions. This theory shows that the first non-constant eigenfunction is always monotonic, but only affine if the latent distribution is Gaussian.

All formal proofs for the theorems were verified using the Lean 4 theorem prover, ensuring logical correctness from axiomatized premises.

#### 3.3. Empirical Validation

The theoretical results are validated through a series of experiments across varying complexities:
- **2D Simulations:** Controlled experiments use Gaussian latent variables `z ∼ N(0, I_2)` subjected to four distinct nonlinear mixing functions (spiral, sinusoidal shear, parabolic shear, RealNVP coupling layer). A 4-layer MLP encoder is trained with the LeJEPA loss.
- **High-Dimensional Scaling:** Experiments are extended to latent dimensions `N` from 2 to 1024, using a RealNVP mixing function and a matched inverse-coupling-layer encoder. This setup compares SIGReg, VICReg, and InfoNCE to assess their performance at scale.
- **Latent-Distribution Ablations:** The latent variable distribution is varied through the generalized normal family (with shape parameter `α`) to test the uniqueness result, observing how linear recovery changes with deviation from Gaussianity.
- **Pixel-Based Robotic Control:** The DeepMind Control Suite Reacher environment is used. Encoders are trained on images generated from two conditions: (i) Gaussian OU-process latent states, and (ii) real latent trajectories derived from a trained reinforcement learning policy. This evaluates identifiability in a more realistic, non-Gaussian setting.
- **Approximate Bound Verification:** For all experimental runs, metrics related to the approximate identifiability bound (covariance deviation `ε`, alignment gap `δ`, and actual recovery error) are calculated and compared.
- **Latent-Space Planning:** Using the Reacher environment, goal-reaching tasks are performed by interpolating in the learned latent space. The quality of these plans is evaluated against optimal trajectories in the true joint space, especially when costs are rotation-invariant.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents four main theorems supported by empirical evidence, characterizing LeJEPA's ability to learn a World Model.

#### 4.1. Forward Direction: LeJEPA Learns the World Model (Theorem 1)

- **Theoretical Finding:** For a "Gaussian world" (where independent latent variables `z` are standard Gaussian and evolve under Ornstein–Uhlenbeck transitions), any measurable map `h: R^n → R^n` that results in `h(z) ∼ N(0, I_n)` minimizes the LeJEPA alignment loss `L(h)` if and only if `h(z) = Qz` for some orthogonal matrix `Q ∈ O(n)`. Furthermore, at this optimum, the conditional transition `h(z') | h(z)` is identical to `z' | z`, specifically `N(ρ h(z), (1 − ρ²) I_n)`.
- **Interpretation:** This theorem provides a mathematical guarantee that LeJEPA, under the specified conditions, compels the learned representation to recover the true latent variables and their transition dynamics, up to an inherent global rotation. Any nonlinearity in the representation would strictly increase the loss, preventing it from reaching the optimum.
- **Empirical Support:** Experiments with 2D nonlinear mixing functions (e.g., spiral, sinusoidal shear) demonstrate that LeJEPA successfully recovers the isotropic Gaussian structure of the latents up to rotation (Figure 3). Scaling experiments up to 1024 dimensions confirm that SIGReg and VICReg (which also enforce whitening) maintain linear identifiability with `R² > 0.999` across a wide range of latent dimensions (Table 1, Tables 5-7).

#### 4.2. Converse Direction: The Gaussian is Unique (Theorem 2)

- **Theoretical Finding:** Considering any world satisfying the independence, stationarity, and additive noise assumptions (Section 3.1), if every minimizer of the LeJEPA objective (with `Cov(h(z)) = I_n`) is a linear map `h(z) = Qz`, then the true latent variables `z` must be Gaussian.
- **Interpretation:** This result establishes that Gaussianity of the latent variables is not merely a sufficient condition for linear identifiability with LeJEPA but also a necessary one within the considered class of worlds. Deviations from Gaussianity in the true latents prevent the learning objective from universally yielding linear representations.
- **Empirical Support:** A sweep through the generalized normal distribution family for latent variables shows that linear recovery `R²(h→z)` peaks sharply when the latent distribution is Gaussian (`α = 2`). For non-Gaussian latent distributions, `R²` values are significantly lower, illustrating that linear identifiability breaks (Figure 4b, Figure 7). Experiments with pixel-based robotic control further support this, showing that Gaussian-distributed replay data (OU condition) achieve high `R²` (`0.95`), while non-Gaussian latent trajectories from an RL policy yield substantially lower and anisotropic `R²` values (Table 2, Figure 15).

#### 4.3. Approximate Identifiability (Theorem 3)

- **Theoretical Finding:** In the Gaussian world, if the LeJEPA objectives are only approximately satisfied (i.e., approximate alignment `L(h) ≤ 2(1 − ρ) tr(Cov(h(z))) + δ` and approximate whitening `||Cov(h(z)) − I_n||_F ≤ ε`), the expected recovery error `E[||h(z) − Qz||²]` is bounded by `D + (ε + D)²`, where `D = δ / (2ρ(1 − ρ))`.
- **Interpretation:** This theorem indicates that the identifiability guarantee degrades gracefully with imperfect optimization. The bound consists of a nonlinearity term `D` (related to the alignment gap `δ`) and a distortion term `(ε + D)²` (related to the whitening error `ε`).
- **Empirical Support:** The empirical validation across various experimental settings (grid search, 2D mixings, scaling, gennorm) shows that the observed recovery errors consistently fall within the predicted bound (Figure 4a). Analysis of the individual terms reveals that the alignment gap `δ` is a stronger predictor of recovery error than the covariance deviation `ε`, suggesting that achieving good alignment is the more critical and challenging aspect for identifiability (Figure 9).

#### 4.4. Optimal Latent Planning (Theorem 4)

- **Theoretical Finding:** If the learned representation `h(z) = Qz` for an orthogonal `Q`, and the stage and terminal costs `ℓ, ℓ_T` of a finite-horizon optimal control problem are `O(n)`-invariant (i.e., `ℓ(Rz, a) = ℓ(z, a)` and `ℓ_T(Rz) = ℓ_T(z)` for any `R ∈ O(n)`), then optimal value functions and optimal plans in the learned `ˆz`-space are identical to those in the true `z`-space.
- **Interpretation:** Linear identifiability, specifically orthogonal recovery, directly translates the learned representation into a functional World Model for planning. Control policies derived in the learned latent space (e.g., by interpolating between states) directly correspond to optimal policies in the true latent space for a broad class of relevant cost functions.
- **Empirical Support:** In the DMC Reacher environment, a Gaussian encoder (which achieves high linear identifiability) enables latent-space planning that results in control costs statistically indistinguishable from an oracle controller operating in the true joint space. In contrast, the Trajectory encoder (with lower linear identifiability) shows inflated control costs. Furthermore, empirical results demonstrate a monotonic relationship between linear identifiability `R²` and lower control cost (Figure 4c,d and Figure 5).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the understanding of self-supervised learning by providing theoretical guarantees for the structural properties of representations learned by JEPAs, specifically LeJEPA.

**Theoretical Advancements:**
- **Formal Foundation for JEPAs:** This work presents the first identifiability result for JEPAs, mathematically formalizing the conditions under which these models provably learn a faithful representation of the world's latent structure. It translates an empirically successful approach into a mathematically grounded method.
- **Definition of a "World Model":** By establishing linear identifiability as a criterion, the paper offers a concrete, testable definition for when a learned representation qualifies as a "World Model" that can recover the true degrees of freedom.
- **Inversion of ICA Narrative:** The finding that Gaussian latents enable identifiability in this nonlinear setting contrasts with classical linear ICA, where Gaussianity is known to hinder source separation. This provides a novel perspective on the role of Gaussianity in representation learning.

**Practical Implications:**
- **Data Generation Strategies:** The empirical results from the robotic control experiments indicate that for robust linear identifiability, the data used for self-supervised pretraining should ideally approximate an isotropic random walk, rather than reflecting highly structured, goal-directed policies. This suggests practical guidelines for collecting or augmenting data for optimal representation learning.
- **Objective Function Design:** The comparative analysis of SIGReg, VICReg, and InfoNCE highlights their differing behaviors and failure modes at scale or under non-Gaussian latent distributions. This informs the selection and potential refinement of regularization techniques in SSL. For instance, InfoNCE's sensitivity to kernel width at high dimensions points to a need for dynamic kernel tuning or alternative contrastive mechanisms.
- **Enhanced Latent-Space Planning:** The proof that orthogonal identifiability enables optimal latent-space planning provides a direct link between the structural quality of a learned representation and its utility for downstream control tasks. This means that classical control theory tools can be applied directly in the learned latent space, provided costs are rotation-invariant, without loss of optimality or the need for re-translation. This simplifies the development and deployment of robust, data-driven control systems based on learned World Models.

**Future Directions:**
The paper acknowledges limitations and suggests future research, including extending identifiability to action-conditioned transitions (connecting to interventional causal representation learning), exploring scenarios with mismatched latent and output dimensions, and investigating the scaling behavior of identifiability with finite sample sizes and specific optimization dynamics.

In summary, this research provides a rigorous theoretical framework and empirical validation for understanding when and why LeJEPA learns representations that are linearly identifiable with the true latent variables of a system, thereby furnishing a foundational piece for constructing provably effective World Models.
