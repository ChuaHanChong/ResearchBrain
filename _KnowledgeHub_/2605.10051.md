# Guided Streaming Stochastic Interpolant Policy

> [!summary] Summary
> The Guided Streaming Stochastic Interpolant Policy (SSIP) framework introduces a principled inference-time guidance mechanism for streaming generative robot policies, enabling real-time adaptation and smooth control in dynamic environments. It substantially improves reactivity and task success rates across various obstacle scenarios and real-world robot tasks, significantly outperforming conventional chunk-based policies.

## Problem

- Generative robot policies based on Diffusion Models or Flow Matching struggle with real-time adaptation to dynamic, unmodeled environmental constraints or evolving user preferences without costly retraining.
- Current chunk-based architectures for generative policies introduce latency and discontinuous motion by generating entire action sequences before execution, limiting adaptation to objectives that change mid-execution.
- A rigorous, principled framework for inference-time guidance within the general Stochastic Interpolant (SI) framework, particularly for streaming policies, was underexplored.

## Method

- A formal, optimal guidance law for general conditional Stochastic Interpolants was derived by analyzing the value function's time evolution through the Backward Kolmogorov Equation.
- The Streaming Stochastic Interpolant Policy (SSIP) was introduced, generalizing deterministic Streaming Flow Policies (SFP) to a Stochastic Differential Equation (SDE) formulation under the SI framework for execution-time-aligned action generation.
- Two complementary guidance mechanisms were developed: Stochastic Trajectory Ensemble Guidance (STEG) for training-free, zero-shot adaptation, and Conditional Critic Guidance (CCG) for amortized, low-latency inference using learned critic networks.

## Results

- Guided SSIP methods achieved higher task success rates and lower per-step inference latency in static environments (e.g., CCG-P 74.3% SR at 14.75ms latency vs. DP 58.5% SR at 91.52ms) and demonstrated a more favorable safety-reward trade-off.
- In dynamic obstacle scenarios (e.g., 'Chase'), SSIP methods maintained substantially higher success rates (30-36%) compared to chunk-based baselines (8-12%), with STEG performing best in highly adversarial regimes.
- Empirical evaluations on 3D Robomimic tasks and a real Franka Panda robot showed SSIP's ability to handle unmodeled static and moving obstacles effectively, support diverse objectives like position preference grasping (SSIP-STEG 95% SR), and produce smoother trajectories compared to chunk-based generation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The optimal guidance for Stochastic Interpolants can be precisely derived as a drift modification proportional to the gradient of the log desirability function, enabling principled steering towards target path distributions.
> - Generalizing to an SDE-based Streaming Stochastic Interpolant Policy allows for mathematical compatibility with the derived guidance law, addressing the commitment problem and latency issues of chunk-based generative control.
> - The combination of on-the-fly gradient estimation (STEG) and amortized learned utility functions (CCG) provides a versatile solution for guiding streaming policies, adaptable to both novel, unstructured and known, structured dynamic environments.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robot policy learning has seen advancements through generative models such as Diffusion Models and Flow Matching, which are capable of capturing complex, multi-modal behaviors from demonstrations. However, a challenge arises when deploying these policies in real-world environments, as they often need to adapt to dynamic, unmodeled constraints (e.g., obstacles) or evolving user preferences not present in the original training data. This adaptation capability, without requiring costly retraining, is known as inference-time guidance.

Existing inference-time guidance methods, which originated in controlled image generation, have been adapted for robot control to enforce safety and align with preferences. A limitation of many current approaches, particularly those based on Diffusion Policy, is their reliance on "chunk-based" architectures. These architectures generate entire sequences of actions (chunks) before execution begins, which introduces latency and can lead to discontinuous motion. Furthermore, once a chunk is committed, open-loop execution over that chunk's horizon prevents adaptation to objectives that change during execution, such as moving obstacles.

Recent work on Streaming Flow Policies (SFP) has begun to address the limitations of chunk-based policies by modeling action trajectories as flow trajectories and aligning generative evolution time with physical execution time. This enables incremental action generation and reduces latency.

This paper extends the capabilities of generative robot policies by developing a principled inference-time guidance framework specifically for *streaming* architectures. It builds upon the framework of Stochastic Interpolants (SI), which unifies score-based diffusion models and flow matching methods. While guidance mechanisms are established for standard diffusion models, a rigorous treatment within the more general SI framework, particularly for streaming policies, has been underexplored. This work generalizes the deterministic Streaming Flow Policy (SFP) to a stochastic differential equation (SDE) formulation under the Stochastic Interpolant framework, thereby enabling a principled derivation of inference-time guidance. The presented approach aims to enhance reactivity and provide physically valid guidance in dynamic, unstructured environments, addressing the fundamental commitment problem inherent in chunk-based policies.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the inherent limitations of conventional generative robot policies when deployed in dynamic, unstructured real-world environments. While these policies excel at capturing complex behaviors, their inability to adapt rapidly to unforeseen circumstances or changing objectives at inference time without retraining significantly restricts their practical utility.

The key objectives of this work are detailed as follows:

- **Derive Optimal Guidance Law for Stochastic Interpolants:** The first objective is to establish a formal and general theoretical framework for inference-time guidance within the Stochastic Interpolant (SI) framework. This involves analyzing the value function's time evolution through the Backward Kolmogorov Equation to derive a modified drift term. This derivation aims to theoretically guarantee sampling from a specified target distribution, thereby providing a principled basis for steering the policy. This goal seeks to generalize existing guidance methods, which are often specific to diffusion models, to a broader class of generative processes.

- **Introduce Streaming Stochastic Interpolant Policy (SSIP):** The second objective is to develop a novel streaming generative control framework, termed the Streaming Stochastic Interpolant Policy (SSIP). This framework is designed to address the latency and motion smoothness issues prevalent in chunk-based policies. SSIP generalizes the deterministic Streaming Flow Policy (SFP) by instantiating the more general Stochastic Interpolant framework, effectively changing the underlying dynamics from a deterministic Ordinary Differential Equation (ODE) to a Stochastic Differential Equation (SDE). This SDE formulation is intended to make the streaming policy mathematically compatible with the optimal inference-time guidance derived for SI.

- **Develop Complementary Guidance Mechanisms:** A crucial objective is to provide practical methods for computing the guidance gradient efficiently at inference time, catering to diverse deployment needs. Two complementary mechanisms are proposed:
 - **Stochastic Trajectory Ensemble Guidance (STEG):** This mechanism is designed as a training-free approach. Its goal is to compute guidance gradients on-the-fly by leveraging parallel inference-time rollouts and backpropagating through a differentiable trajectory ensemble. This aims to enable zero-shot adaptation to novel objectives or unmodeled environmental changes without prior training for those specific conditions.
 - **Conditional Critic Guidance (CCG):** This mechanism is designed as a training-based approach. Its objective is to amortize the utility gradient into a learned critic network. This approach aims to offer efficient, low-latency inference for scenarios where obstacle classes or cost functions are known and can be modeled during training.

- **Empirical Validation of Reactivity and Performance:** The final objective is to empirically evaluate the proposed guided streaming approach. This includes demonstrating that SSIP, with its integrated guidance mechanisms, significantly outperforms conventional chunk-based policies in terms of reactivity and provides superior, physically valid guidance in dynamic, unstructured environments. The evaluation aims to cover scenarios with static, dynamic, and adversarial obstacles, as well as tasks requiring alignment with specific preferences, both in simulation and on a physical robot.

In summary, the motivation is to overcome the limitations of current generative robot policies in real-world deployment by enabling real-time, adaptive, and smooth control through a principled streaming framework and flexible guidance mechanisms.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology centers on deriving a principled inference-time guidance law for Stochastic Interpolants (SI) and integrating it with a streaming policy architecture.

**A. Optimal Guidance for Stochastic Interpolants**
The work formally derives an exact guidance law for a general conditional Stochastic Interpolant, represented by a diffusion process: `dx_t = b(x_t, t, ξ) dt + sqrt(2ϵ(t)) dW_t`. The objective is to sample from a target path distribution `p_target(τ_0:T | ξ)` which is a reweighting of a base trajectory measure `p_base(τ_0:T | ξ)` by an exponentiated trajectory-level cost `e^(-J(τ_0:T, ξ))`. The trajectory cost `J` is assumed to be time-additive.

The derivation leverages the concept of desirability `u(x, t, ξ)`, defined as the expected exponentiated future cost from time `t` onwards, which satisfies the (backward) Feynman–Kac equation. By applying Itô's lemma to a global likelihood-ratio martingale and utilizing Girsanov's theorem, the authors show that the dynamics under the tilted (target) measure remain a diffusion with the same diffusion coefficient but a shifted drift. The optimal guidance modification `∆b(x, t, ξ)` to the base drift `b` is derived as `2ϵ(t) ∇_x log u(x, t, ξ)`. This modification represents the minimum (in relative entropy) drift correction needed to sample from the target distribution.

**B. Streaming Stochastic Interpolant Policy (SSIP)**
The paper proposes the Streaming Stochastic Interpolant Policy (SSIP) as the architectural backbone for guided streaming control. SSIP is an execution-time-aligned diffusion policy where time-indexed marginals follow a stochastic interpolant path. This formulation is a generalization of the deterministic Streaming Flow Policy (SFP) and operates as an SDE.
The SSIP sampling dynamics are defined by: `da_t = [v_θ(a_t, t, h) + (ϵ(t) - γ(t)γ̇(t))s_θ(a_t, t, h)] dt + sqrt(2ϵ(t)) dW_t`. Here, `a_t` is the action at time `t`, `h` is the policy context (e.g., observation history), `v_θ` is a learned transport/velocity field, and `s_θ` is a learned score function. `ϵ(t)` is a tunable diffusion coefficient, and `γ(t)` is an interpolant noise schedule. This formulation separates "structured variation" (learned policy diversity) from "sampling diffusion" (exploration noise), allowing for principled guidance. The deterministic SFP can be recovered as a special case by setting `ϵ(t) = 0` and `γ(t) = 0`.

**C. Derivation of the Target Conditional for Streaming Policies**
To apply the trajectory-level guidance to a streaming setting where actions are generated incrementally, the paper derives the induced target conditional over the current action `a_t` given the realized past `τ_0:t-1`. This derivation shows that the target conditional `p_target(a_t | τ_0:t-1)` is proportional to `p_base(a_t | τ_0:t-1) u(a_t, t, τ_0:t-1)`, where `u(a_t, t, τ_0:t-1)` is the streaming desirability (expected exponentiated future cost from `a_t` onwards). This formulation justifies applying the derived martingale-based guidance law directly to the streaming context.

**D. Guiding Streaming Policies**
The optimal guidance correction `∆b = 2ϵ(t)∇_a log u` is directly added to the SSIP base drift `b_θ(a, t, h)`. The resulting guided SSIP dynamics become: `da_t = [b_θ(a_t, t, h) + 2ϵ(t)∇_a log u(a_t, t, h)] dt + sqrt(2ϵ(t)) dW_t`. Receding-horizon execution ensures reactivity by recomputing the utility and guidance at each timestep based on the instantaneous future trajectory cost.

**E. Methods for Estimating the Guidance Gradient (∇_a log u)**
Two complementary strategies are proposed to address the computational challenge of estimating `∇_a log u`:

1. **Stochastic Trajectory Ensemble Guidance (STEG):**
 - **Approach:** Training-free method for zero-shot adaptation.
 - **Mechanism:** At inference time, it simulates `N` parallel stochastic rollouts of the base SSIP SDE for `K` steps.
 - **Utility Estimation:** The log-expected utility `log E[e^(-J)]` is approximated using a Log-Sum-Exp (LSE) estimator on the accumulated costs `J(τ^(i))` from the ensemble.
 - **Gradient Computation:** Automatic differentiation is used to backpropagate through the unrolled graph, computing `∇_a_t log u` for the current action `a_t`.
 - **Design Principles:** "Large-N, Small-K" strategy (large ensemble for coverage, short horizon for latency) to manage distributional shift and dynamics mismatch errors.

2. **Conditional Critic Guidance (CCG):**
 - **Approach:** Training-based method for amortized inference.
 - **Mechanism:** A parameterized critic network `V_ψ(a_t, t, h, ϕ)` is trained offline to directly approximate the log-desirability `log u(a_t, t, h)`. `ϕ` represents cost-defining parameters (e.g., obstacle positions).
 - **Training:** The critic is trained via regression using Monte Carlo rollouts from the base policy, minimizing an L2 loss against target values `y_target`.
 - **Cost Variants:** Two variants are explored:
 - **CCG-D (Distance Potential Cost):** `y_target` approximates `log E[e^(-J)]` using cumulative distance costs.
 - **CCG-P (Collision Proxy Cost):** `y_target` is a probabilistic collision risk derived from ensemble statistics (e.g., minimum distance, approach angle). This relaxes the strict additive-cost assumption.
 - **Benefit:** Enables low-latency inference when obstacle classes and cost functions are known during training.

The overall approach involves instantiating the streaming SI sampler, estimating `log u` using either STEG or CCG, and applying the derived guidance correction in a receding-horizon manner.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluations aimed to assess the performance of Guided SSIP in comparison to chunk-based baselines, its adaptability to dynamic obstacles, and the trade-offs between the STEG and CCG guidance mechanisms.

**A. Push-T with Static Obstacles (Q1: Static Environment Comparison)**
- **Performance:** Guided SSIP methods generally achieved higher task success rates (SR) with lower *per-step* inference latency compared to conventional chunk-based diffusion policy (DP) and flow policy (FP) baselines. For instance, CCG-P achieved 74.3% SR at 14.75ms latency, while DP achieved 58.5% SR at 91.52ms latency (which is amortized over a chunk of 8 actions, implying a higher effective control latency).
- **Trade-offs:** SSIP demonstrated a more favorable safety-reward trade-off, with Pareto frontiers positioned closer to higher safety and reward values. Among SSIP variants, CCG-P generally dominated across guidance scales, while STEG outperformed CCG-D. Simple repulsion also performed strongly in this static setting (68.6% SR, 11.37ms latency), suggesting that local distance-based avoidance can be effective when obstacles are fixed.

**B. Push-T with Dynamic Obstacles (Q2: Adaptability to Dynamic Obstacles)**
- **Reactivity:** Dynamic obstacles significantly highlighted the limitations of chunk-based policies.
- **Intercept (predictable):** DP and FP remained competitive (72-76%), with SSIP-CCG-P matching the best baseline (76%).
- **Oscillate (continuous change):** CCG-P achieved 68% SR, outperforming DP (38%) and FP (50%), indicating the benefit of continuous online guidance.
- **Chase (adversarial following):** Chunk-based baselines collapsed (DP 12%, FP 8%), while SSIP methods maintained substantially higher success (30-36%). Notably, STEG performed best in this highly adversarial regime (36% SR), demonstrating the advantage of training-free, on-the-fly gradient estimation for novel and rapidly changing obstacle behaviors.
- **Conclusion:** SSIP enables substantially higher success under rapidly changing constraints compared to brittle chunk-based guidance.

**C. Simulation Robot Experiment (Robomimic Tasks)**
- **Overall Performance:** In 3D manipulation tasks (Lift, Can, Square) with unmodeled static obstacles intersecting nominal trajectories, SSIP with CCG-P guidance achieved the strongest performance, outperforming all baselines. For example, in the Square task, CCG-P achieved 79% SR compared to DP's 58% and FP's 48%.
- **Guidance Efficacy:** This result highlighted the benefit of amortized, history-aware guidance when reliable prior structure is available (for CCG-P). STEG consistently improved over simple repulsion but underperformed CCG-P, possibly due to the limitations of finite-horizon stochastic rollouts in discovering long-horizon detours. Simple repulsion performed poorly across these tasks, indicating its insufficiency for complex obstacle interference.
- **Failure Modes:** Analysis revealed failures due to insufficient avoidance (collisions) or task interference (non-completion), often stemming from distributional constraints of the base policy or conflicting guidance gradients.

**D. Real Robot Experiment (Franka Panda Robot)**
- **Moving Obstacles:** Guided DP struggled with fast-moving obstacles (5% SR), often responding late or with abrupt motions. SSIP-STEG and SSIP-CCG showed improved performance (e.g., 55-65% for Fast obstacles), significantly outperforming naive guidance.
- **Position Preference Grasping:** The framework successfully supported inference-time objectives beyond obstacle avoidance, enabling the robot to grasp specific parts of a mug according to a specified preference (SSIP-STEG 95%, DP 85%).
- **Smoothness:** Streaming policies consistently produced smoother trajectories compared to chunk-based generation.

**E. Impact of Stochasticity (SDE vs. ODE)**
- A supplementary finding indicated that while SDEs offer theoretical exploration benefits, the injected noise in high-frequency streaming control (20 Hz) could disrupt temporal coherence and introduce "jitter," degrading performance in fine manipulation tasks. Consequently, the noise-free ODE formulation often served as a more robust backbone for reactive guidance, with main results primarily reported for ODE-based inference.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces a significant advancement in the field of generative robot policies by offering a principled and practical framework for inference-time guidance in streaming control architectures.

**Theoretical Significance:**
- The work formally derives the optimal guidance law for general Stochastic Interpolants by analyzing the value function's time evolution via the Backward Kolmogorov Equation. This provides a rigorous mathematical foundation for steering any SI-based generative process towards a target distribution, extending beyond specific diffusion or flow models.
- By generalizing the deterministic Streaming Flow Policy (SFP) to a Streaming Stochastic Interpolant Policy (SSIP) under an SDE framework, the paper establishes the necessary mathematical compatibility for principled inference-time guidance in streaming settings. This unifies robust generative modeling with reactive control.

**Practical Impact and Benefits:**
- **Enhanced Reactivity and Safety:** The core contribution enables robots to adapt in real-time to dynamic and unforeseen environmental changes (e.g., moving obstacles, shifting human preferences) without the need for retraining. This significantly improves success rates in challenging dynamic scenarios where conventional chunk-based policies fail due to latency and open-loop execution. This capability is critical for deploying robots safely and effectively in unstructured, human-centric environments.
- **Reduced Latency and Smoother Motion:** By generating actions incrementally rather than in full chunks, the SSIP architecture inherently reduces control latency and eliminates the "wait-then-act" bottleneck. This leads to smoother, more continuous robot motions, which is beneficial for physical systems and interaction with the environment.
- **Flexible and Adaptable Guidance Mechanisms:** The introduction of two complementary guidance mechanisms—Stochastic Trajectory Ensemble Guidance (STEG) and Conditional Critic Guidance (CCG)—provides flexibility for diverse deployment needs:
 - **STEG:** Offers training-free, zero-shot adaptation, making it suitable for novel or highly adversarial conditions where prior training data for specific constraints is unavailable. Its ability to estimate gradients on-the-fly provides robustness to distribution shifts.
 - **CCG:** Provides low-latency, amortized guidance for structured environments where obstacle classes or cost functions can be learned offline. This is crucial for real-time performance in repetitive or well-defined scenarios.
- **Broad Applicability:** The empirical evaluations across diverse tasks (2D Push-T, 3D Robomimic manipulation, real-world Franka Panda tasks involving moving obstacle avoidance and position-preference grasping) demonstrate the framework's versatility and applicability beyond specific domains or robot platforms.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The identified limitations, such as the support constraints of the training trajectory distribution and the finite prediction horizon of STEG, suggest clear avenues for future work, including extending look-ahead capabilities and integrating with hierarchical planning.

In conclusion, this research significantly advances the capabilities of generative robot policies by bridging the gap between expressive generative modeling and real-time reactive control. The proposed Guided SSIP framework offers a robust and flexible solution for enabling robots to operate more effectively, safely, and adaptively in complex, dynamic environments, thereby contributing to the broader development of autonomous systems.
