# Guided Streaming Stochastic Interpolant Policy

> [!summary] Summary
> The Guided Streaming Stochastic Interpolant Policy (SSIP) framework introduces a principled inference-time guidance mechanism for streaming generative robot policies, enabling real-time adaptation and smooth control in dynamic environments. It substantially improves reactivity and task success rates across various obstacle scenarios and real-world robot tasks, significantly outperforming conventional chunk-based policies.

## Problem

- Generative robot policies based on Diffusion Models or Flow Matching struggle with real-time adaptation to dynamic, unmodeled environmental constraints or evolving user preferences without costly retraining.
- Current chunk-based architectures for generative policies introduce latency and discontinuous motion by generating entire action sequences before execution, limiting adaptation to objectives that change mid-execution.
- A rigorous, principled framework for inference-time guidance within the general Stochastic Interpolant (SI) framework, particularly for streaming policies, was underexplored.

## Method

- A formal, optimal guidance law for general conditional Stochastic Interpolants was derived by analyzing the value function's time evolution through the Backward Kolmogorov Equation.
- The Streaming Stochastic Interpolant Policy (SSIP) was introduced, generalizing deterministic Streaming Flow Policies (SFP) to a Stochastic Differential Equation (SDE) formulation under the SI framework for execution-time-aligned action generation.
- Two complementary guidance mechanisms were developed: Stochastic Trajectory Ensemble Guidance (STEG) for training-free, zero-shot adaptation, and Conditional Critic Guidance (CCG) for amortized, low-latency inference using learned critic networks.

## Results

- Guided SSIP methods achieved higher task success rates and lower per-step inference latency in static environments (e.g., CCG-P 74.3% SR at 14.75ms latency vs. DP 58.5% SR at 91.52ms) and demonstrated a more favorable safety-reward trade-off.
- In dynamic obstacle scenarios (e.g., 'Chase'), SSIP methods maintained substantially higher success rates (30-36%) compared to chunk-based baselines (8-12%), with STEG performing best in highly adversarial regimes.
- Empirical evaluations on 3D Robomimic tasks and a real Franka Panda robot showed SSIP's ability to handle unmodeled static and moving obstacles effectively, support diverse objectives like position preference grasping (SSIP-STEG 95% SR), and produce smoother trajectories compared to chunk-based generation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The optimal guidance for Stochastic Interpolants can be precisely derived as a drift modification proportional to the gradient of the log desirability function, enabling principled steering towards target path distributions.
> - Generalizing to an SDE-based Streaming Stochastic Interpolant Policy allows for mathematical compatibility with the derived guidance law, addressing the commitment problem and latency issues of chunk-based generative control.
> - The combination of on-the-fly gradient estimation (STEG) and amortized learned utility functions (CCG) provides a versatile solution for guiding streaming policies, adaptable to both novel, unstructured and known, structured dynamic environments.
