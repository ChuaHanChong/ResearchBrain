# Improving LLM General Preference Alignment via Optimistic Online Mirror Descent

> [!summary] Summary
> Optimistic Nash Policy Optimization (ONPO) is presented as an online learning algorithm for aligning Large Language Models (LLMs) with general human preferences, extending beyond the Bradley-Terry model. By integrating optimistic online mirror descent into a self-play framework, ONPO achieves an O(T⁻¹) duality gap bound and empirically demonstrates superior performance on benchmarks such as AlpacaEval 2.0, showing a 21.2% relative improvement for Mistral-Instruct over baselines, while preserving academic reasoning abilities.

## Problem

- Existing LLM alignment methods like RLHF and DPO often rely on the restrictive Bradley-Terry (BT) model, which assumes transitive preferences and a single reward function, failing to capture the complexity and potential non-transitivity of human preferences.
- Current online alignment algorithms for general preferences typically achieve an O(T⁻¹/²) duality gap convergence rate, indicating slower theoretical convergence.
- PPO-based RLHF approaches are known for high computational costs and instability, prompting a need for more stable and efficient alignment methods.

## Method

- The problem of LLM alignment under general preferences is reformulated as a ==two-player zero-sum game==, aiming to find a Nash policy that ensures a win rate of at least 50% against any opponent.
- Optimistic Nash Policy Optimization (**ONPO**) is introduced, an online learning algorithm that integrates optimistic online mirror descent (OMD) into a self-play framework to approximate the Nash policy.
- ONPO iteratively updates the LLM policy by minimizing a derived loss function on sampled preference data, utilizing the previous iteration's reward as an optimistic predictor to improve convergence.

## Results

- ONPO achieves an improved theoretical duality gap bound of O(T⁻¹) for general preference alignment, indicating faster convergence compared to prior OMD-based self-play algorithms.
- Empirically, ONPO consistently outperforms state-of-the-art general preference alignment algorithms, achieving a **21.2%** relative improvement in AlpacaEval 2.0 LC win rate for Mistral-Instruct (**42.8%**) over the strongest baseline (INPO at **35.3%**).
- ONPO-aligned **8B** parameter models demonstrate competitive performance against significantly larger models on AlpacaEval 2.0 and effectively preserve academic abilities across various benchmarks, mitigating the 'alignment tax'.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Abandoning the restrictive Bradley-Terry model for general preferences allows for a more accurate and flexible modeling of human feedback, accommodating non-transitive decision-making.
> - Leveraging optimistic online mirror descent within a self-play game-theoretic formulation provides an improved theoretical convergence rate of O(T⁻¹) for the duality gap, surpassing the O(T⁻¹/²) of standard OMD methods.
> - A computationally lightweight and stable alignment algorithm can be achieved by directly optimizing a loss function derived from OMD updates, bypassing the need for explicit reward models or complex policy gradient methods.
