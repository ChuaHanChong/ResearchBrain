# Improving LLM General Preference Alignment via Optimistic Online Mirror Descent

> [!summary] Summary
> Optimistic Nash Policy Optimization (ONPO) is presented as an online learning algorithm for aligning Large Language Models (LLMs) with general human preferences, extending beyond the Bradley-Terry model. By integrating optimistic online mirror descent into a self-play framework, ONPO achieves an O(T⁻¹) duality gap bound and empirically demonstrates superior performance on benchmarks such as AlpacaEval 2.0, showing a 21.2% relative improvement for Mistral-Instruct over baselines, while preserving academic reasoning abilities.

## Problem

- Existing LLM alignment methods like RLHF and DPO often rely on the restrictive Bradley-Terry (BT) model, which assumes transitive preferences and a single reward function, failing to capture the complexity and potential non-transitivity of human preferences.
- Current online alignment algorithms for general preferences typically achieve an O(T⁻¹/²) duality gap convergence rate, indicating slower theoretical convergence.
- PPO-based RLHF approaches are known for high computational costs and instability, prompting a need for more stable and efficient alignment methods.

## Method

- The problem of LLM alignment under general preferences is reformulated as a ==two-player zero-sum game==, aiming to find a Nash policy that ensures a win rate of at least 50% against any opponent.
- Optimistic Nash Policy Optimization (**ONPO**) is introduced, an online learning algorithm that integrates optimistic online mirror descent (OMD) into a self-play framework to approximate the Nash policy.
- ONPO iteratively updates the LLM policy by minimizing a derived loss function on sampled preference data, utilizing the previous iteration's reward as an optimistic predictor to improve convergence.

## Results

- ONPO achieves an improved theoretical duality gap bound of O(T⁻¹) for general preference alignment, indicating faster convergence compared to prior OMD-based self-play algorithms.
- Empirically, ONPO consistently outperforms state-of-the-art general preference alignment algorithms, achieving a **21.2%** relative improvement in AlpacaEval 2.0 LC win rate for Mistral-Instruct (**42.8%**) over the strongest baseline (INPO at **35.3%**).
- ONPO-aligned **8B** parameter models demonstrate competitive performance against significantly larger models on AlpacaEval 2.0 and effectively preserve academic abilities across various benchmarks, mitigating the 'alignment tax'.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Abandoning the restrictive Bradley-Terry model for general preferences allows for a more accurate and flexible modeling of human feedback, accommodating non-transitive decision-making.
> - Leveraging optimistic online mirror descent within a self-play game-theoretic formulation provides an improved theoretical convergence rate of O(T⁻¹) for the duality gap, surpassing the O(T⁻¹/²) of standard OMD methods.
> - A computationally lightweight and stable alignment algorithm can be achieved by directly optimizing a loss function derived from OMD updates, bypassing the need for explicit reward models or complex policy gradient methods.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work is situated within the field of aligning Large Language Models (LLMs) with human preferences, primarily through Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF). RLHF has been a foundational technique for developing instruction-following models such as Instruct-GPT and Claude.

Traditional RLHF pipelines often involve training a reward model, followed by optimizing a policy using algorithms like Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO). This approach, however, has been noted for potential instability and high computational costs. Subsequently, methods like Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) emerged, aiming to directly fine-tune LLMs on preference datasets without an explicit reward model, offering a more stable and computationally efficient alternative. A common characteristic of many existing RLHF and DPO-based approaches is their reliance on the Bradley-Terry (BT) model assumption. The BT model posits the existence of a single, transitive reward function for any prompt-response pair, determining the probability of one response being preferred over another.

This paper addresses a perceived limitation of the BT model: its assumption of transitive preferences and the existence of a ground-truth reward function may not adequately capture the complexity of human decision-making, especially at a population level where preferences can be non-transitive. Empirical observations in prior research have also indicated that preference models not constrained by the BT assumption can outperform BT reward models.

In response to these limitations, a line of research has begun to explore LLM alignment under "general preferences," which do not adhere to the BT model's restrictive assumptions. Initial efforts in this area include offline algorithms like IPO (Azar et al., 2024) and online approaches that frame the problem as a two-player zero-sum game, seeking a Nash policy. This particular paper builds upon the game-theoretic formulation of general preference alignment, specifically leveraging techniques from online learning and self-play algorithms to approximate Nash equilibria. It distinguishes itself from prior self-play methods (e.g., INPO by Zhang et al., 2024) by incorporating optimistic online mirror descent, a strategy known in game theory for achieving faster convergence rates.

The work contributes to the advancement of LLM alignment techniques by proposing a method that operates under less restrictive assumptions about human preferences, potentially enabling more accurate and nuanced alignment.

### 2. Key objectives and motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the general preference alignment of Large Language Models (LLMs) by addressing the limitations of existing methods that rely on the Bradley-Terry (BT) model assumption.

The key motivations are detailed as follows:

- **Addressing the Restrictiveness of the Bradley-Terry (BT) Model:** Many current LLM alignment techniques, including prominent RLHF frameworks and Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) algorithms, assume that human preferences can be modeled by the BT model. This model posits the existence of a single ground-truth reward for each prompt-response pair and implies that preferences are transitive (if A is preferred to B, and B to C, then A must be preferred to C). The authors contend that this assumption is overly restrictive for modeling the multifaceted and sometimes inconsistent nature of human preferences, particularly at a population level. They cite existing literature demonstrating non-transitive human decision-making and empirical evidence where preference models, not bound by the BT assumption, exhibit superior performance over BT reward models. This motivates the need to move beyond the BT framework to accommodate more general preference structures.

- **Formulating Alignment as a Two-Player Game:** Given the limitations of the BT model, the authors propose to drop this assumption and study LLM alignment under general preferences. To achieve this, they reformulate the problem as a two-player zero-sum game. In this game, one policy (the max-player) aims to maximize its win rate against another policy (the min-player), which seeks to minimize it. This formulation aligns with practical scenarios where human feedback often involves comparing two responses rather than assigning an absolute score to a single response.

- **Approximating the Nash Policy:** The objective within this game-theoretic framework is to find the Nash equilibrium, specifically the Nash policy. A Nash policy ensures a win rate of at least 50% against any other policy, implying it cannot be outperformed by any single opponent. This provides a robust theoretical guarantee for the aligned LLM's performance. The authors aim to develop an algorithm that can effectively approximate this Nash policy.

- **Leveraging Theoretical Insights from Learning in Games:** The literature on learning in games and self-play algorithms has demonstrated effectiveness in approximating Nash policies. The authors are specifically motivated by recent advancements in game theory that show optimistic online mirror descent (OMD) can achieve faster convergence rates (O(T⁻¹) compared to O(T⁻¹/²)) when both players adopt this update strategy. By integrating optimistic OMD into their self-play alignment framework, the goal is to develop an algorithm with improved theoretical guarantees for convergence of the duality gap.

- **Developing a Computationally Lightweight and Stable Algorithm:** The paper aims to develop an algorithm that is computationally efficient and stable in practice. This is contrasted with PPO-based RLHF, which is known for high variance and instability, and some other online alignment methods that may require complex sampling or policy gradient techniques. The proposed approach seeks to directly minimize a loss objective on preference data, making its implementation computationally lightweight.

In summary, the core objectives are to overcome the restrictive BT model assumption, formulate LLM alignment as a robust two-player game, theoretically guarantee faster convergence to a Nash policy through optimistic online mirror descent, and demonstrate practical, stable, and superior performance compared to existing state-of-the-art methods.

### 3. Methodology and approach

The methodology presented in this paper involves reformulating LLM general preference alignment as a two-player zero-sum game and solving it using a novel online learning algorithm called Optimistic Nash Policy Optimization (ONPO).

**Problem Formulation:**

1. **Contextual Setup:** The problem is framed in a contextual setting where a prompt `x` is sampled from a distribution `d_1`, and an LLM policy `π` generates responses `y` from a response space `Y`.
2. **General Preferences:** The Bradley-Terry (BT) model assumption is removed. Instead, a general preference oracle `P: X × Y × Y →` is assumed, which provides a binary signal indicating whether `y_1` is preferred over `y_2` given context `x`. This setup directly models pairwise comparisons without assuming an underlying reward function.
3. **Two-Player Zero-Sum Game:** Since preferences involve two responses, the LLM alignment problem is formulated as a two-player zero-sum game. The objective function `J(π_1, π_2)` represents the expected win rate of policy `π_1` (max-player) against policy `π_2` (min-player) over sampled prompts and responses.
4. **Nash Policies and Duality Gap:** The learning goal is to find the Nash equilibrium `(π*1, π*2)`. Due to the symmetric nature, `π*1 = π*2 = π*`, and the game value is `J(π*, π*) = 0.5`. A Nash policy `π*` guarantees a win rate of at least 0.5 against any other policy `π`. The performance of an approximate policy `π` is quantified by its duality gap: `DualGap(π) := max_π1 J(π1, π) - min_π2 J(π, π2)`. The objective is to minimize this duality gap.

**Algorithm Development: Optimistic Nash Policy Optimization (ONPO):**

The algorithm is inspired by self-play methods and advancements in learning in games, particularly the properties of optimistic online mirror descent (OMD).

1. **Review of Self-play with OMD (Baseline):** The paper first reviews a standard self-play algorithm using OMD updates. At each iteration `t`, the policy `π_t+1` is updated by maximizing `⟨π, r_t⟩ - (1/η) KL(π∥π_t)`, where `r_t(y)` is the expected win rate of response `y` against the current policy `π_t`. The KL divergence term ensures stability by keeping `π_t+1` close to `π_t`. This approach is known to achieve an `O(T^-1/2)` duality gap bound for the uniform mixture of policies `π_1:T`.

2. **Integrating Optimistic OMD (ONPO's Core Idea):** ONPO enhances the OMD update by incorporating an "optimistic predictor." Instead of solely relying on the current reward `r_t`, optimistic OMD uses a reward predictor `m_t` for the first step of policy computation and then observes the actual reward `r_t` for an auxiliary policy update.
 - **Predictor `m_t`:** In ONPO, the reward information from the previous iteration is used as the predictor, i.e., `m_t = r_t-1 = E_y'~π_t-1 [P(y ≻ y')]`. This assumes that the previous reward provides a reasonable approximation for the current one due to the stability induced by KL regularization.
 - **Two-step Update:** ONPO employs a two-step update strategy for its policies `π_t` and `π'_t+1`:
 - `π_t = argmax_π ⟨π, m_t⟩ - (1/η) KL(π∥π'_t)`
 - `π'_t+1 = argmax_π ⟨π, r_t⟩ - (1/η) KL(π∥π'_t)`
 This structure is designed to leverage the "regret bounded by variation in utilities" (RVU) property of optimistic OMD, which allows for faster convergence.

3. **Theoretical Guarantees:** The integration of optimistic OMD enables ONPO to achieve an improved duality gap bound of `O(T^-1)` for the uniform mixture of policies `π_1:T`, assuming a suitable learning rate `η`. This represents a theoretical improvement over the `O(T^-1/2)` bound of standard OMD self-play. The improvement stems from the cancellation of stability terms `∥r_t - r_t-1∥^2_∞` by negative terms `−(1/4η)∥π_t - π_t-1∥^2_1` due to the self-play mechanism. The authors also note that, unlike some previous works, explicit KL regularization in the game objective is not necessary because the update rule already incorporates stability.

**Implementation of ONPO (Algorithm 1):**

The practical implementation of ONPO addresses the challenge of directly computing `r_t(y)` (expected win rate over `π_t`).
1. **Loss Function Derivation:** The closed-form solution for the OMD update (e.g., for `π_t`) relates the log-ratio of probabilities `log(π_t(y)/π_t(y')) - log(π'_t(y)/π'_t(y'))` to the difference in win rates `η(P(y ≻ π_t-1) - P(y' ≻ π_t-1))`. This relationship allows formulating the policy update as minimizing a loss function:
 `E_{y,y'~π_t-1, y_w,y_l~λ_p(y,y')} [(g_t(π, y_w, y_l) - η/2)^2]`, where `g_t(π, y_w, y_l) = log(π(y_w)/π(y_l)) - log(π'_t(y_w)/π'_t(y_l))` and `λ_p` is a preference distribution. This formulation only requires sampling responses from the current policy `π_t` and querying the preference oracle for binary feedback.
2. **Iterative Procedure:**
 - Initialize `π'_1` and `π_1` with a supervised fine-tuned (SFT) policy `π_SFT`.
 - For each iteration `t`:
 - Sample response pairs `{y(i)1, y(i)2}` from the current policy `π_t`.
 - Construct a preference dataset `D_t` by querying the preference oracle `P` for feedback (identifying `y_w` (winner) and `y_l` (loser) from the sampled pairs). A tournament approach is used where multiple generated responses are pairwise compared.
 - Minimize the derived loss functions on `D_t` to calculate `π'_t+1` and `π_t+1`.
 - The final output policy is `π_T`.

**Extension to Multi-Turn Setting (Conceptual):**

The paper outlines a conceptual extension to the multi-turn setting using a contextual Markov Decision Process (CMDP) formulation. This would involve using Q-value functions to capture long-term outcomes and updating policies based on these Q-values. However, the efficient estimation of Q-values in this setting presents a practical challenge, and this extension is noted as future work.

### 4. Main findings and results

The research presents several key findings and results, encompassing both theoretical guarantees and empirical performance.

**Theoretical Findings:**

- **Improved Duality Gap Bound:** The primary theoretical finding is that ONPO achieves an `O(T⁻¹)` bound on the duality gap. This is an improvement over the `O(T⁻¹/²)` result obtained by previous self-play algorithms employing standard Online Mirror Descent (OMD) for general preference alignment. This faster convergence rate is attributed to the integration of optimistic OMD, which leverages a reward predictor (the previous iteration's reward) and the specific properties of regret bounded by variation in utilities (RVU) in self-play.

**Empirical Findings:**

The empirical evaluation involved comparing ONPO against state-of-the-art general preference alignment algorithms, including iterative DPO, SPPO, and INPO, across multiple benchmarks and using two different base models (Llama-3-SFT and Mistral-Instruct-v0.3).

- **Superior Performance on Preference Benchmarks:**
 - **AlpacaEval 2.0:** ONPO demonstrated substantial improvements in length-controlled (LC) win rates. When using Mistral-Instruct as the base model, ONPO achieved a 42.8% win rate, representing a 21.2% relative improvement over the strongest baseline (INPO at 35.3%). With Llama-3-SFT as the base model, ONPO achieved a 48.6% win rate, a 9.9% relative improvement over INPO (44.2%).
 - **Arena-Hard:** ONPO also showed strong performance on this benchmark. For Mistral-Instruct, ONPO achieved a 29.7% win rate compared to INPO's 25.3%. For Llama-3-SFT, ONPO achieved 36.4%, slightly lower than INPO's 37.0% but still comparable.
 - **MT-Bench:** ONPO generally achieved comparable or slightly better average ratings. For Mistral-Instruct, ONPO scored 7.68, marginally outperforming INPO (7.46). For Llama-3-SFT, ONPO scored 8.40, again slightly higher than INPO (8.28).
 - **Overall Consistency:** ONPO consistently outperformed or achieved comparable performance to the baselines across all three representative benchmarks and both base models.

- **Competitive with Larger Models:** Notably, ONPO-aligned models demonstrated competitive performance even when compared to significantly larger proprietary and open-source models. For instance, ONPO-aligned Llama-3-SFT (8B parameters) achieved an AlpacaEval 2.0 LC win rate of 48.6%, surpassing models like Llama-3-70B-it (34.4%), Mixtral-8x22B-it (30.9%), and GPT-4-0613 (30.2%), which have substantially more parameters.

- **Preservation of Academic Abilities ("No Alignment Tax"):**
 - Evaluation on six academic benchmarks (GPQA, Hellaswag, MMLU-Pro, Winogrande, TruthfulQA, GSM8K) using Mistral-Instruct-v0.3 as the base model showed that ONPO achieved a slightly higher average performance (55.4%) compared to the base model itself (54.6%) and other alignment baselines.
 - This indicates that ONPO effectively preserves the model's intrinsic knowledge and reasoning capabilities developed during pre-training, mitigating the "alignment tax" phenomenon where alignment can sometimes degrade these abilities.

- **Robustness to Hyperparameter Variations:**
 - A sensitivity analysis of the learning rate `η` (varying from 200/3 to 200) demonstrated that ONPO maintains strong performance across the tested range. The algorithm consistently outperformed baselines on Arena-Hard and AlpacaEval 2.0, indicating its robustness to variations in this key hyperparameter.

In summary, the main findings highlight ONPO's theoretical advantage in convergence speed and its empirical superiority in aligning LLMs with general human preferences across various evaluation metrics, while also maintaining intrinsic model capabilities.

### 5. Significance and potential impact

The research on Optimistic Nash Policy Optimization (ONPO) presents several points of significance and potential impact within the field of Large Language Model (LLM) alignment.

**1. Advancement in Theoretical Understanding of LLM Alignment:**
- **Relaxation of BT Assumption:** By successfully modeling LLM alignment under general preferences, the work moves beyond the restrictive Bradley-Terry (BT) model assumption. This is significant because the BT model's assumption of transitive preferences does not always align with observed human decision-making, especially when preferences are aggregated or involve nuanced trade-offs. This broader formulation allows for a more accurate and flexible representation of human preferences.
- **Improved Convergence Guarantees:** The theoretical demonstration that ONPO achieves an `O(T⁻¹)` duality gap bound, improving upon the `O(T⁻¹/²)` rate of previous Online Mirror Descent (OMD) based self-play algorithms, contributes to the theoretical understanding of learning in games and its application to LLM alignment. Faster convergence rates are generally desirable as they suggest more efficient learning.

**2. Practical Improvements in LLM Alignment Methodology:**
- **Computationally Lightweight and Stable:** ONPO offers a computationally lightweight implementation by directly minimizing a loss objective on preference data, circumventing the need for a reward model or policy gradient methods (like PPO). PPO is known for its high variance and instability during training, which can lead to increased computational costs and complexity. ONPO's direct optimization approach can potentially lead to more stable and efficient fine-tuning processes.
- **Empirical Performance Superiority:** The consistent outperformance of ONPO over state-of-the-art general preference alignment algorithms (e.g., iterative DPO, SPPO, INPO) on multiple representative benchmarks (AlpacaEval 2.0, Arena-Hard, MT-Bench) demonstrates its practical effectiveness. The significant relative improvements observed on AlpacaEval 2.0 suggest that ONPO can achieve better alignment quality.
- **Efficiency with Smaller Models:** The finding that ONPO-aligned models, particularly 8B parameter models, can outperform much larger models (e.g., Llama-3-70B-it, Mixtral-8x22B-it, GPT-4-0613) on certain benchmarks indicates that more efficient alignment techniques can yield high-performing models even without resorting to excessively large architectures. This could have implications for resource efficiency and accessibility in LLM development.

**3. Mitigation of the "Alignment Tax":**
- The empirical results showing that ONPO-aligned models preserve or slightly improve performance on academic reasoning and knowledge-based tasks (e.g., GPQA, MMLU-Pro) indicate that the method avoids the "alignment tax"—a phenomenon where alignment processes can sometimes degrade a model's intrinsic capabilities. This is significant for developing LLMs that are both helpful and retain their foundational knowledge and reasoning abilities.

**4. Future Directions for LLM Research:**
- The conceptual extension of ONPO to multi-turn settings suggests a path for applying this approach to more complex, interactive LLM applications, like chatbots, which involve sequential decision-making.
- The stated plan for designing strategies for actively selecting preference data points to further enhance alignment performance points towards research into more data-efficient and targeted feedback collection, which could reduce the human labeling effort required for alignment.

In summary, ONPO represents a step towards more robust, theoretically grounded, and empirically effective methods for aligning LLMs with complex human preferences. Its potential impact includes fostering the development of more capable, stable, and resource-efficient LLMs that better reflect diverse human preferences while retaining their core intellectual abilities.
