# Understanding the Role of Hallucination in Reinforcement Post-Training of Multimodal Reasoning Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from UNC Chapel Hill and Honda Research Institute investigated how reinforcement learning impacts multimodal reasoning models, revealing that post-training can improve performance even with corrupted visual input by strengthening textual reasoning patterns. This 'hallucinated visual reasoning' particularly benefited larger models, often achieving performance on par with or exceeding standard training methods.

## Problem

- The specific mechanism by which RL-based post-training improves multimodal reasoning in MLLMs remains unclear, particularly whether it genuinely leverages visual information or primarily strengthens internal text-based reasoning.
- A systematic investigation into the impact and role of hallucination *during the training process* of reasoning MLLMs was lacking.
- Current RL reward designs are often final-answer-based and modality-agnostic, raising questions about whether visual information is effectively utilized during training.

## Method

- Introduced the "**Hallucination-as-Cue** Framework," an analytical approach to intentionally induce and diagnose hallucination during both RL-based training and inference of MLLMs.
- Applied three modality-specific corruption strategies: Blank Image Replacement (BI), Random Image Replacement (RI), and Textual Information Removal (TR) to create hallucination-inductive training and inference conditions.
- Utilized the GRPO algorithm for RL-based post-training on MLLMs (Qwen2.5-VL-3B/7B) using corrupted data and evaluated performance across various benchmarks under different corruption settings.

## Results

- RL-based post-training consistently improved MLLM reasoning performance despite training with severely corrupted visual data (e.g., Blank Image Replacement), challenging assumptions about the necessity of intact visual input.
- Hallucination-inductive training (e.g., GRPO-RI) for larger models (Qwen2.5-VL-**7B**) achieved average performance comparable to, or even surpassing, standard GRPO training on clean data.
- Modality-specific corruptions during inference revealed general reasoning improvements in RL-trained models, and for some smaller models, visual information removal surprisingly led to higher accuracy on certain benchmarks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - RL-based post-training can enhance MLLM reasoning by amplifying the underlying language model's inherent textual reasoning capabilities and priors, even when visual information is severely compromised.
> - Hallucination can serve as a diagnostic signal for understanding MLLM learning mechanisms, revealing how models adapt and reason when confronted with incomplete or misleading multimodal input.
> - Larger model capacities enable more effective generation and learning from positive hallucinated reasoning trajectories, indicating a critical role for model scale in leveraging such signals.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of Large Language Models (LLMs) has seen significant advancements in reasoning capabilities, particularly through the application of reinforcement learning (RL) for post-training. This success has inspired researchers to extend RL-based post-training to Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) to improve their visual reasoning abilities. While numerous studies have reported performance gains in MLLMs post-trained with RL, a foundational question regarding the mechanism of these improvements remains largely unanswered. Specifically, it is unclear whether RL training for MLLMs genuinely enables models to learn from visual information and ground their reasoning in it, or if it primarily strengthens the internal, text-based reasoning patterns already present in the underlying LLM components.

Existing research on MLLM reasoning has explored various approaches, including visual instruction tuning, long chain-of-thought generation, Monte Carlo tree search, and latent-space reasoning. Concurrently, hallucination in MLLMs has been a subject of investigation, with most studies focusing on mitigation strategies, benchmark evaluations where accuracy degradation implicitly indicates hallucination, or detection methods using larger MLLMs. A recent concurrent work also indicates that MLLMs can maintain high accuracy on multimodal benchmarks even without direct access to images. However, a systematic investigation into the impact and role of hallucination *during the training process* of reasoning MLLMs has not been previously conducted. This paper addresses this gap by proposing a framework to explicitly analyze how hallucination influences RL-based post-training for MLLMs, thereby contributing to a mechanistic understanding of these models.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to systematically investigate the effects of RL-based post-training on multimodal reasoning models from the perspective of model hallucination. The authors aim to clarify a fundamental question: Does RL-based training in MLLMs truly leverage visual information to enhance visual reasoning, or does it primarily amplify existing textual reasoning patterns within the language model component, potentially leading to increased reliance on self-generated or "hallucinated" textual priors?

The motivation for this investigation stems from several observations and challenges in the field:
- **Ambiguity in RL's Role:** Despite reported performance gains, the specific way RL improves multimodal reasoning in MLLMs is not well understood. The reward designs in current RL approaches are often final-answer-based and modality-agnostic, raising questions about whether visual information is genuinely utilized during training.
- **The Nature of Hallucination:** While hallucination is typically viewed as an undesirable behavior leading to performance degradation, the authors observe that hallucinated reasoning can, in some instances, coincidentally or due to inherent model biases, lead to correct answers in visual reasoning tasks (as illustrated in Figure 1). This phenomenon transforms hallucination from a mere error into a potential diagnostic signal.
- **Need for Modality-Grounded Intelligence:** Clarifying how MLLMs learn is crucial for developing future multimodal reasoning frameworks that achieve genuinely modality-grounded intelligence. This understanding is particularly important for high-stakes applications like medical imaging diagnosis systems, where accurate and visually grounded reasoning is paramount.
- **Challenging Prevailing Assumptions:** The study aims to challenge the common assumption that RL post-training inherently and effectively utilizes visual information. By revealing the extent to which models rely on textual priors, the research seeks to motivate the development of more modality-aware RL-based training designs.
- **Diagnostic Framework:** The authors intend to introduce an analytical framework that enables a systematic diagnosis of RL training dynamics, the behavior of reasoning models, and the intrinsic properties of existing multimodal reasoning datasets under conditions that induce hallucination.

By systematically examining hallucination during both training and evaluation, the research seeks to provide new insights into the mechanisms and limitations of current RL-based multimodal post-training approaches.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors introduce the "Hallucination-as-Cue Framework," an analytical approach designed to investigate the role of hallucination in RL-based post-training of MLLMs. The core idea is to intentionally induce hallucination during both training and inference to diagnose model behavior and learning dynamics.

**Scope of Study:** The study focuses on current RL-based MLLM post-training methods, specifically using the Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) algorithm, which is prevalent in visual mathematical reasoning tasks. The chosen tasks involve diagrams with textual descriptions and questions, requiring multi-step reasoning.

**Modality-Specific Corruptions:** To encourage hallucination, three distinct modality-specific corruption strategies are defined and applied:
- **Blank Image Replacement (BI):** All training images are replaced with blank images, effectively removing all visual information. This forces the model to generate or "hallucinate" the required visual content internally for reasoning.
- **Random Image Replacement (RI):** Each training image is replaced with a randomly selected image from the dataset. This creates mismatched text-image pairs, compelling the model to reason based on incorrect or irrelevant visual information.
- **Textual Information Removal (TR):** Essential textual conditions and queries are removed from the input prompt using rule-based matching. If matching fails, the entire text is removed, leaving only a generic template. This forces the model to rely primarily on visual inputs.

**Hallucination-Inductive Training:**
- During this phase, RL-based post-training (using GRPO) is applied to models where the input data has been subjected to one of the modality-specific corruptions.
- The reward scheme, which is rule-based and typically assesses the correctness of the final answer, can positively reinforce certain "hallucinated" responses if they coincidentally lead to correct answers despite corrupted inputs. These are termed "hallucination-inductive rewards."
- This process encourages the model to learn reasoning patterns that might be derived from these hallucination-dominant trajectories. Models are trained on datasets with diverse characteristics (Geometry3K, MMR1-V0, CLEVR) to observe how RL learns under such conditions.

**Hallucination-Inductive Inference:**
- At test time, models (both normally trained and hallucination-inductive trained) are evaluated under the same modality-specific corruptions.
- The primary metric is accuracy. While accuracy is expected to drop due to information removal, the persistence of non-zero accuracy indicates the model's ability to reason through hallucination or chance.
- Comparing models under various corruption settings at inference time helps to understand their hallucination behaviors.

**Experimental Setup:**
- **Base Models:** The Qwen2.5-VL-3B and Qwen2.5-VL-7B models are used as the foundational MLLMs.
- **RL Algorithm Implementation:** GRPO, as implemented in the EasyR1 training framework, is utilized.
- **Training Details:** Models are trained for 15 episodes using the AdamW optimizer with a constant learning rate of 1 × 10⁻⁶, weight decay of 1 × 10⁻², and gradient clipping. GRPO-specific hyperparameters include a rollout size of 5, sampling temperature of 0.7, and a KL-divergence weight of 0.01.
- **Training Datasets:** Geometry3K, MMR1-V0, and CLEVR are used for training.
- **Evaluation Benchmarks:** Models are evaluated on Geometry3K's test split, MathVision, MathVerse, MathVista, and WeMath, which represent various types and difficulties of visual mathematical reasoning problems.
- **Evaluation Settings (S1-S8):** A comprehensive set of eight evaluation settings is defined to analyze standard performance, performance on corrupted data with normally trained models, and performance on normal and corrupted data with hallucination-inductive trained models. These settings provide a structured comparison to understand the effects of corruption during both training and inference.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded several findings regarding the impact of hallucination on RL-based post-training of MLLMs:

- **Hallucination-Inductive RL Training Improves Performance:** Despite being trained with severely corrupted data (Blank Image Replacement (BI), Random Image Replacement (RI), Textual Information Removal (TR)), RL-based post-training consistently improved the models' reasoning performance. In some cases, such as with BI training, performance improvements were observed even in early training stages and continued throughout. This finding suggests that current RL-based multimodal reasoning methods can learn from "hallucinated visual reasoning" even without access to actual visual content, challenging the assumption that models require intact visual information to learn effectively. For example, BI training, where images are removed, still improved both training and test accuracy, indicating a strong reliance on textual signals. RI training also showed substantial accuracy increases after an initial drop, demonstrating that models can benefit from RL even with mismatched visual inputs. TR training, despite potentially providing more "normal" visual cues, did not consistently outperform BI or RI training, suggesting that current RL methods may not efficiently leverage visual information during training.

- **Hallucination-Inductive Inference Reveals General Reasoning:** When modality-specific corruptions were applied during inference, both standard GRPO-trained and hallucination-inductive trained models exhibited performance improvements over training steps compared to the baseline, despite reduced absolute accuracy. This indicates that enhanced reasoning ability generally increases the likelihood of producing correct answers even under corrupted inputs. Importantly, hallucination-inductive training did not show a clear advantage over standard GRPO training for corrupted inference. This suggests that the models were learning a more general reasoning capability rather than overfitting to the specific hallucinated content encountered during training.

- **Larger Models Benefit More from Hallucinated Inputs:** When comparing the Qwen2.5-VL-3B and Qwen2.5-VL-7B models across benchmarks, it was observed that all hallucination-inductive trained variants of the 3B model improved over the baseline, with some achieving performance comparable to normal GRPO training on specific benchmarks like MathVista. More significantly, for the larger Qwen2.5-VL-7B model, hallucination-inductive training, particularly with Random Image Replacement (GRPO-RI), achieved average performance that was on par with, or even surpassed, standard GRPO training conducted on clean data. This finding suggests that RL training may primarily amplify the inherent reasoning capabilities of the underlying language model rather than fully relying on multimodal grounding signals. Furthermore, larger models demonstrated a stronger ability to generate and learn from positive hallucinated trajectories, indicating that model capacity plays a critical role in leveraging these types of training signals.

- **Generalizability Across Post-training Datasets:** The findings regarding hallucination-inductive training were consistent across different post-training datasets, including compositional mathematical datasets (MMR1-V0) and traditional visual reasoning datasets (CLEVR). This indicates that the observed effects are not specific to a particular training distribution but generalize across different types of training data. As with Geometry3K, increased model size further reinforced the observation that hallucination-inductive training can achieve performance comparable to or superior to standard GRPO training.

- **Benchmark Evaluation Properties and Modality Reliance:** Applying BI corruption during benchmark evaluation led to substantial performance drops in most cases. However, an notable exception was observed with the Qwen2.5-VL-3B model on the MathVision benchmark, where BI-corrupted inference yielded *higher* accuracy than normal inference. This suggests that for smaller or non-reasoning-oriented models, visual information can sometimes act as a distraction, and its removal can simplify the reasoning process. This anomaly diminished with larger models or RL-trained models, which are better equipped to integrate visual input effectively. Fine-grained analysis on MathVerse showed that performance on "Text Dominant" questions dropped the least under BI-corrupted inference, consistent with MLLMs' reliance on textual cues. Moreover, BI-corrupted training resulted in smaller performance degradation on both "Vision Dominant" and "Text Dominant" questions under BI-corrupted inference compared to normal RL post-training, implying that BI training might encourage a more balanced reliance across modalities.

- **Discussion on Mechanisms:** The authors discuss that corrupted training still improves performance because negative hallucinated trajectories are mostly ignored or discouraged, and RL training is resilient to catastrophic forgetting compared to supervised fine-tuning. The rewarding of positive hallucinated textual reasoning patterns also contributes. The observation that hallucination-inductive training does not lead to higher hallucination-inductive testing accuracy compared to standard training indicates that the model learns general reasoning capability rather than merely mimicking hallucinated content. Larger models show more improvement because they possess a greater capacity to generate a higher quantity and quality of positive hallucinated trajectories and are better equipped to learn from them.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a significant contribution to the understanding of reinforcement learning in multimodal large language models by challenging fundamental assumptions and introducing a novel diagnostic framework.

The primary significance lies in the finding that RL-based post-training for MLLMs can significantly improve reasoning performance even when trained on severely corrupted or absent visual information. The surprising observation that hallucination-inductive training can sometimes match or even surpass standard training, particularly with larger models, directly questions the prevailing belief that current RL methods effectively utilize visual inputs for genuine multimodal grounding. Instead, the study reveals that RL-based training might primarily amplify the inherent reasoning capabilities of the underlying language model by strengthening its reliance on textual priors, including self-generated "hallucinated" textual reasoning chains.

The "Hallucination-as-Cue Framework" itself is a notable methodological contribution. By systematically introducing modality-specific corruptions, it provides a unique lens for diagnosing the intricate dynamics of RL training, understanding the intrinsic properties of reasoning datasets, and analyzing model behavior under conditions that induce hallucination. This framework offers a valuable tool for future research aiming to dissect MLLM learning processes.

The implications of these findings are substantial. For model development, they suggest that a more principled and genuinely modality-aware approach to RL-based post-training is required. Current methods, while yielding performance gains, may not be optimizing for true visual understanding but rather for enhanced textual inference that happens to align with correct answers. This could lead to models that perform well on benchmarks but lack robust grounding in real-world visual data.

From a broader scientific perspective, this work provides critical insights into the limitations and mechanisms of current MLLM training paradigms. It encourages a re-evaluation of how "multimodal reasoning" is achieved in these models and highlights the often-underestimated role of internal language model capabilities and hallucination.

The potential impact extends to practical applications, especially in domains where accurate visual grounding is critical, such as medical imaging diagnosis. If MLLMs in these sensitive areas primarily rely on textual priors or hallucinated content rather than actual visual data, it could lead to misinterpretations or unreliable diagnoses. This research motivates the development of MLLMs that are verifiably grounded in visual information, fostering trust and effectiveness in high-stakes scenarios.
