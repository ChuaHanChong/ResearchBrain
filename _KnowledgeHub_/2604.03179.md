# Understanding the Role of Hallucination in Reinforcement Post-Training of Multimodal Reasoning Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from UNC Chapel Hill and Honda Research Institute investigated how reinforcement learning impacts multimodal reasoning models, revealing that post-training can improve performance even with corrupted visual input by strengthening textual reasoning patterns. This 'hallucinated visual reasoning' particularly benefited larger models, often achieving performance on par with or exceeding standard training methods.

## Problem

- The specific mechanism by which RL-based post-training improves multimodal reasoning in MLLMs remains unclear, particularly whether it genuinely leverages visual information or primarily strengthens internal text-based reasoning.
- A systematic investigation into the impact and role of hallucination *during the training process* of reasoning MLLMs was lacking.
- Current RL reward designs are often final-answer-based and modality-agnostic, raising questions about whether visual information is effectively utilized during training.

## Method

- Introduced the "**Hallucination-as-Cue** Framework," an analytical approach to intentionally induce and diagnose hallucination during both RL-based training and inference of MLLMs.
- Applied three modality-specific corruption strategies: Blank Image Replacement (BI), Random Image Replacement (RI), and Textual Information Removal (TR) to create hallucination-inductive training and inference conditions.
- Utilized the GRPO algorithm for RL-based post-training on MLLMs (Qwen2.5-VL-3B/7B) using corrupted data and evaluated performance across various benchmarks under different corruption settings.

## Results

- RL-based post-training consistently improved MLLM reasoning performance despite training with severely corrupted visual data (e.g., Blank Image Replacement), challenging assumptions about the necessity of intact visual input.
- Hallucination-inductive training (e.g., GRPO-RI) for larger models (Qwen2.5-VL-**7B**) achieved average performance comparable to, or even surpassing, standard GRPO training on clean data.
- Modality-specific corruptions during inference revealed general reasoning improvements in RL-trained models, and for some smaller models, visual information removal surprisingly led to higher accuracy on certain benchmarks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - RL-based post-training can enhance MLLM reasoning by amplifying the underlying language model's inherent textual reasoning capabilities and priors, even when visual information is severely compromised.
> - Hallucination can serve as a diagnostic signal for understanding MLLM learning mechanisms, revealing how models adapt and reason when confronted with incomplete or misleading multimodal input.
> - Larger model capacities enable more effective generation and learning from positive hallucinated reasoning trajectories, indicating a critical role for model scale in leveraging such signals.
