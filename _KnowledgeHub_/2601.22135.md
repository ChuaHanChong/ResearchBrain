# PI-Light: Physics-Inspired Diffusion for Full-Image Relighting

> [!summary] Summary
> S-Lab at Nanyang Technological University and Tencent developed PI-Light, a physics-inspired diffusion framework for full-image relighting. This two-stage model achieves state-of-the-art performance in intrinsic decomposition and generates physically plausible relit images, demonstrating robust generalization to diverse real-world scenes.

## Problem

- Existing full-image relighting methods struggle with physical plausibility and often exhibit poor generalization due to data scarcity and insufficient incorporation of light transport principles.
- Many data-driven relighting solutions fail to maintain albedo consistency, precisely control lighting, or effectively handle self-luminous objects.
- Prior diffusion-based inverse rendering pipelines often generate intrinsic components inefficiently and with potential inconsistencies.

## Method

- PI-Light introduces a two-stage, physics-inspired diffusion framework for full-image relighting: inverse neural rendering for intrinsic decomposition and neural forward rendering for relighting.
- A meticulously curated dataset of over 10,000 objects and 300 scenes with ground-truth intrinsics and controlled lighting is developed to address data scarcity.
- The framework incorporates novel architectural elements like batch-aware attention and integrates physics-inspired loss functions to enforce physical plausibility and enhance generalization.

## Results

- PI-Light achieves state-of-the-art quantitative performance in intrinsic decomposition, outperforming prior methods on custom object and scene datasets across PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS metrics.
- The model demonstrates superior qualitative performance in neural forward rendering, synthesizing accurate specular highlights and diffuse reflections with precise lighting control, maintaining background consistency, and generalizing robustly to real-world scenes.
- Ablation studies confirm that both the two-stage decomposition approach and the physics-inspired loss functions significantly enhance model performance, realism, and convergence speed, especially for out-of-distribution inputs.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decomposing images into intrinsic physical properties (albedo, normal, roughness, metallic) before relighting is crucial for achieving physically plausible and controllable results, outperforming end-to-end approaches.
> - Integrating explicit physics-based loss functions (Diffuse Shading Loss, Physical-based Shading Loss) during neural forward rendering guides the diffusion model to learn accurate light transport principles, improving realism and generalization.
> - Representing target lighting conditions using only the front hemisphere of a rendered gray ball provides an intuitive and precise control mechanism that minimizes interference from self-luminous objects or built-in scene lighting.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of image relighting, which aims to alter the illumination of a captured image, is crucial for applications spanning film production, augmented reality (AR), virtual reality (VR), and digital content creation. This paper positions itself within the broader landscape of **neural rendering**, specifically tackling the challenging problem of **full-image relighting**.

Historically, relighting research has largely fallen into two categories:
1. **Object-centric approaches:** These methods typically focus on relighting foreground objects, often simplifying or replacing the background. While achieving impressive results (e.g., Zhang et al., 2025; Mei et al., 2023), they frequently struggle with maintaining albedo consistency and precise lighting control, as highlighted by issues like altered carpet colors or incorrect light directions in comparisons with IC-Light (Fig. 2).
2. **Scene-level methods:** These attempts at full-image relighting often remain heavily data-driven (e.g., Choi et al., 2024; Kocsis et al., 2024a). Their primary limitations include a struggle to generalize beyond training data without extensive and diverse datasets, an inability to handle self-luminous objects effectively, and difficulties in ensuring physical plausibility.

Recent advancements have seen the rise of **diffusion models** in image synthesis (Rombach et al., 2022). Works like RGB↔X (Zeng et al., 2024b) and DiffusionRenderer (Liang et al., 2025) have successfully employed diffusion models for both inverse and forward rendering. However, these often suffer from limitations such as imprecise lighting control (e.g., using irradiance instead of environment maps) or restricted applicability (e.g., limited to indoor scenes).

PI-Light (π-Light) differentiates itself by directly addressing these core limitations. It operates within a **two-stage framework** inspired by PBR: inverse neural rendering (decomposing an image into intrinsic physical properties like albedo, normal, roughness, and metallic) followed by neural forward rendering (synthesizing a relit image based on these intrinsics and a new lighting condition). This approach leverages the powerful generative capabilities of diffusion models while embedding strong **physical priors** to ensure plausibility and controllability, a crucial gap in many purely data-driven methods. By introducing a carefully curated dataset and novel physics-inspired losses, PI-Light aims to bridge the synthetic-to-real gap more effectively and achieve superior generalization compared to its predecessors. The reliance on models like Wonder3D (Long et al., 2024) and GeoWizard (Fu et al., 2024) for architectural insights (e.g., batch-aware attention) positions PI-Light as an evolution that synthesizes and improves upon state-of-the-art techniques in neural rendering and diffusion-based image generation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to achieve **physically-aware, controllable, and generalizable full-image relighting** using a novel diffusion-based framework. The authors are motivated by several critical challenges inherent in existing approaches to image relighting:

1. **Data Scarcity for Full-Image Relighting:** Collecting large-scale, structured paired datasets that capture diverse scenes under varied and controlled lighting conditions is technically challenging and resource-intensive. Real-world datasets often lack the same scene under multiple lighting setups, hindering purely data-driven model training.
2. **Maintaining Physical Plausibility:** Many data-driven methods struggle to generate relit images that adhere to fundamental light transport principles. This can lead to unnatural artifacts, incorrect shadows, and inconsistent reflections, reducing the realism and utility of the relit output. Existing foreground relighting solutions, for instance, often fail to maintain consistent albedo or accurately control lighting direction.
3. **Limited Generalizability of Data-Driven Priors:** Models trained on limited datasets often perform poorly when encountering unseen materials, novel lighting conditions, or complex real-world scene compositions. This restricts their robustness and practical applicability.
4. **Inefficient and Inconsistent Intrinsic Decomposition:** Prior diffusion-based inverse rendering methods often generate intrinsic components one at a time, leading to inefficiencies and potential inconsistencies between predicted properties.
5. **Lack of Precise Lighting Control:** Existing forward rendering approaches may use abstract lighting representations (e.g., irradiance), making fine-grained control over light direction and intensity difficult for users. They also sometimes interfere with self-luminous objects or built-in scene lighting.

To address these motivations, PI-Light aims to:
- Develop a two-stage pipeline for robust inverse and forward neural rendering.
- Incorporate novel architectural elements, such as batch-aware attention, to improve efficiency and consistency in intrinsic predictions.
- Design a physics-guided neural rendering module with explicit physics-inspired losses to enforce plausible light transport and enhance generalizability.
- Create a new, high-quality dataset of diverse objects and scenes with ground-truth intrinsics and controlled lighting to alleviate data scarcity and serve as a benchmark.
- Introduce an intuitive and effective lighting representation that enables precise user control while preserving scene consistency.

Ultimately, the goal is to produce a full-image relighting solution that is not only visually compelling but also physically accurate, highly controllable, and robustly generalizable to various real-world scenarios.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

PI-Light proposes a two-stage, physics-inspired diffusion framework for full-image relighting. The methodology is built upon the Latent Diffusion Model (LDM) and incorporates several key innovations.

#### 3.1. Dataset Construction

To overcome data scarcity, the authors meticulously curate a new dataset featuring diverse objects and scenes under controlled lighting conditions.
- **Object Data:** Over 10,000 objects with BRDF material properties are selected from Objaverse (Deitke et al., 2023b;a). Each object is rendered 100 times (10 views, 10 lighting conditions), with a bias towards upper hemisphere lighting. Lighting includes point lights and HDRI maps from Poly Haven. Critically, intrinsics (albedo, normal, roughness, metallic) are extracted directly from Blender's Principled BRDF nodes, along with a mask for non-Principled regions, addressing inaccuracies observed with Blender's composition layer.
- **Scene Data:** 300 high-quality indoor and outdoor scenes are curated from BlenderKit (BlenderKit, 2024). Camera views are sampled with constrained perturbations, and lighting is augmented with a point light behind the camera to introduce diverse shadow patterns. Blender versions are fixed per scene to ensure normal rendering consistency.
- **Novel Lighting Representation:** Unlike prior methods, PI-Light represents lighting using only the **front hemisphere of a rendered gray ball** derived from the HDRI. This provides a precise and intuitive control mechanism, mitigating interference from self-luminous objects or built-in scene lighting.

#### 3.2. Stage 1: Inverse Neural Rendering (Image Intrinsic Decomposition)

This stage aims to decompose an input RGB image into its intrinsic physical properties: albedo (A), normal (N), roughness (R), and metallic (M).
- **Pipeline:** A pretrained image diffusion model (initialized from GeoWizard) is repurposed. It takes four batchwise concatenated input images as conditions, along with noise. The model simultaneously predicts the four intrinsic components. CLIP image embeddings of the inputs are used in cross-attention layers.
- **Batch-Aware Attention:** Inspired by Wonder3D, standard self-attention layers are extended to be "global-aware," allowing communication across different batches. This design enhances efficiency and ensures structural consistency among the predicted intrinsic components. One-hot labels control which batch outputs which intrinsic.
- **Training Objective:** A masked latent loss (L_stage1 = MSE(v_pred ⋅ m_z, v_target ⋅ m_z)) is applied in the V-prediction space. This uses a downsampled mask (m_z) to exclude unreliable regions (e.g., translucent objects, sky, non-Principled BRDF materials) during loss calculation, addressing data collection inaccuracies.

#### 3.3. Stage 2: Neural Forward Rendering (Relighting)

This stage leverages the decomposed intrinsics from Stage 1 and a target lighting condition to generate a relit image.
- **Physics-Inspired Pipeline:** The model is designed based on the Surface Reflection Model (I_rendered = A ⊙ D + S), where D is diffuse and S is specular. The model outputs three components: the final relit image (I_relit), predicted diffuse (D_pred), and predicted specular (S_pred).
- **Conditional Inputs:** To guide the prediction of diffuse and specular components, three batchwise concatenated input conditions are used:
 - I_in1 = (I_in, A) → I_relit (guides overall color and final image)
 - I_in2 = (N, L, m) → D_pred (focuses on diffuse, dependent on normal and lighting)
 - I_in3 = (N, L, M, R, m) → S_pred (focuses on specular, dependent on normal, lighting, metallic, roughness)
 - This design ensures that the model learns the specific dependencies for diffuse and specular reflections accurately.
- **Training Objectives (Physics-Based Losses):** Crucially, physical laws are enforced in the RGB space after VAE decoding.
 - **Diffuse Shading Loss (L_DS):** A manually computed loss (MSE(D_calculated, D_pred)) based on the Lambertian model. This avoids reliance on potentially inaccurate ground-truth diffuse maps and directly encourages the model to learn the relationship between surface normals and lighting conditions (using a gray ball environment map).
 - **Physical-based Shading Loss (L_PS):** An MSE loss (MSE(I_relit, A ⊙ D_pred + S_pred)) that ensures the final rendered image is physically consistent with the predicted albedo, diffuse, and specular components. This guides the output to adhere to the PBR formulation.
 - **Reconstruction Loss (L_rec):** A perceptual loss using DINO features (L_rec = ||ϕ(I_relit) - ϕ(I_input)||₂²). This loss helps maintain the overall content and structure of the image before and after relighting, while being largely invariant to lighting changes, thereby reducing artifacts and preserving scene consistency.
 - **Total Loss:** L_stage2 = L_V-pred + (1/t) * (λ_1 * L_DS + λ_2 * L_PS + λ_3 * L_rec), where 1/t acts as a weighting factor across diffusion timesteps.

#### 3.4. Implementation Details

The model is trained for 80k (Stage 1) and 90k (Stage 2) iterations on eight 40GB A100 GPUs. The U-Net weights are initialized from GeoWizard. DeepSpeed with ZeRO stage 2 and mixed precision is used to optimize memory consumption during Stage 2 training.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted by the authors demonstrate the superiority of PI-Light across both inverse neural rendering (intrinsic decomposition) and neural forward rendering (relighting) tasks.

#### 4.1. Inverse Neural Rendering

- **Quantitative Performance:** On custom Object50 and Scene200 test datasets, PI-Light consistently outperforms state-of-the-art inverse rendering methods such as IntrinsicAnything (Chen et al., 2024), Zhu et al. (2022), Kocsis et al. (2024b), and RGB↔X (Zeng et al., 2024b) across most PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS metrics (Table 1). The improved metrics are maintained on larger Object500 and Scene36 datasets (Table 8).
- **Qualitative Results:** PI-Light produces more detailed and accurate intrinsic predictions (albedo, normal, roughness, metallic) compared to competitors (Fig. 5, 17, 18, 21-24). For instance, it correctly predicts albedo for objects reflected in a mirror and captures high metallicity for materials like iron baskets, which other methods may misinterpret. The normal maps are also more reasonable and detailed, even compared to specialized normal estimation methods like DSINE and GeoWizard (Fig. 20), as confirmed by lower Mean Angular Error (Table 7).

#### 4.2. Neural Forward Rendering

- **Quantitative Performance:** PI-Light achieves superior performance in object relighting, outperforming RGB↔X, Neural Gaffer (Jin et al., 2024), and DiLightNet (Zeng et al., 2024a) on the Object50 test dataset, both in standard relighting and reconstruction settings (Table 2).
- **Qualitative Results:**
 - **Realism and Control:** The model successfully synthesizes accurate specular highlights and diffuse reflections across a wide variety of materials (Fig. 6, 7, 15, 25-28). It demonstrates precise control over lighting direction and intensity, unlike data-driven methods like IC-Light (Zhang et al., 2025) which often fail to preserve object albedo or follow specified light directions (Fig. 2, 7, 15).
 - **Generalization to Real-World Scenes:** PI-Light shows strong generalization capability to real images, effectively handling metallic objects, glass, and complex indoor/outdoor scenes, producing plausible shadows and highlights (Fig. 7, 15, 25-28).
 - **Background Consistency:** Unlike some foreground-focused methods, PI-Light maintains background consistency and avoids issues like shadow baking (Fig. 6).

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

- **Necessity of Decomposition:** An ablation study shows that an end-to-end training approach without intrinsic decomposition (directly relighting from input and lighting) leads to unrealistic shading effects and albedo variations, confirming the crucial role of the two-stage inverse rendering (Fig. 8, Table 3).
- **Effectiveness of Physics-Inspired Losses:** Both the Diffuse Shading Loss (L_DS) and Physical-based Shading Loss (L_PS) significantly enhance model performance (Table 3). Qualitatively, the physics-inspired shading loss effectively corrects highlight and shadow directions, improving realism and generalization, especially on out-of-distribution inputs (Fig. 8, 14). This also contributes to faster convergence.
- **Batch-Aware Attention:** The cross-batch self-attention contributes to stronger consistency among predicted intrinsic components (Appendix A.7).
- **Influence of CFG:** Ablations on Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG) show that while higher CFG increases alignment with conditional input, excessive values can bake shading into albedo or introduce artifacts in normal maps, guiding the choice of CFG=1.0 for stage 1 and 1.5 for stage 2 (Table 4, Fig. 10).
- **Methodological Contribution:** Retraining existing methods like RGB↔X and Neural Gaffer on PI-Light's dataset still shows PI-Light's superior performance, attributing gains not only to the dataset but also to the novel methodological design, particularly the physics-inspired modules and losses (Table 5, 6).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

PI-Light represents a significant advancement in the field of full-image relighting, offering a robust, controllable, and physically plausible solution built upon the powerful framework of diffusion models.

#### 5.1. Scientific Significance

- **Addressing Core Challenges:** The work directly tackles long-standing challenges in relighting: data scarcity, maintaining physical plausibility, and enhancing generalizability. By synergistically combining data-driven priors from diffusion models with explicit physics-based regularization, it demonstrates a powerful hybrid approach.
- **Novel Framework Design:** The two-stage, physics-inspired diffusion framework, coupled with innovations like batch-aware attention and a novel gray ball-based lighting representation, provides a robust pipeline for handling complex scenes and diverse materials.
- **Physics-Guided Learning:** The introduction of unique physics-inspired loss functions (Diffuse Shading Loss, Physical-based Shading Loss) is a key scientific contribution. These losses guide the model towards learning correct light transport principles more efficiently and with less data, establishing a physically meaningful learning landscape.
- **Dataset Contribution:** The meticulously curated high-quality dataset of objects and scenes under controlled lighting conditions is a valuable resource for future research, addressing a critical bottleneck in the field and providing a solid benchmark.

#### 5.2. Technological Impact and Potential Applications

- **Enhanced Digital Content Creation:** PI-Light can revolutionize workflows in film production, video games, and advertising by allowing artists and designers to easily adjust lighting conditions in existing images and scenes, producing more realistic and impactful visual content.
- **Advanced Augmented and Virtual Reality (AR/VR):** The ability to relight real-world scenes accurately means AR/VR applications can seamlessly integrate virtual objects with realistic lighting that matches the physical environment, enhancing immersion and believability.
- **Interactive Scene Editing and Manipulation:** Users can gain precise control over scene illumination, enabling novel forms of image editing, visual storytelling, and even forensic analysis (e.g., studying lighting conditions in photographs).
- **Visual Restoration and Data Augmentation:** The robust decomposition and relighting capabilities can aid in restoring old photographs or augmenting training datasets for other computer vision tasks by generating diverse lighting variations of existing images.

#### 5.3. Broader Impacts (as stated by authors)

- **Positive Impacts:** The technology can facilitate more realistic rendering, improve lighting consistency in AR/VR applications, and support visual restoration or data augmentation.
- **Potential Negative Impacts:** As with many advanced image synthesis technologies, there is a risk of misuse. This includes manipulating visual evidence or contributing to deceptive media by altering real-world scenes to imply different environments or conditions.
- **Mitigation:** The authors plan to release their code and dataset under a research-only license with appropriate usage guidelines, signaling a responsible approach to technological dissemination.

In conclusion, PI-Light represents a significant leap forward in full-image relighting, offering a blend of diffusion model power and physical accuracy that promises to impact both academic research and practical applications across various industries. While acknowledging limitations, such as spatial misalignment in latent representations and the scope of material modeling, the work lays a strong foundation for future advancements in physically-inspired neural rendering.
