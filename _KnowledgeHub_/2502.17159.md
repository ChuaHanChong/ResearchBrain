# RobustMerge: Parameter-Efficient Model Merging for MLLMs with Direction Robustness

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers developed RobustMerge, a training-free, data-free, and storage-free model merging method specifically for parameter-efficient tuning (PEFT) modules in Multimodal Large Language Models. This approach significantly enhances knowledge consolidation, leading to a 3.4% average performance improvement on seen generative tasks and a 4.5% gain on unseen tasks on a new multimodal benchmark compared to previous merging techniques.

## Problem

- Existing model merging methods, primarily designed for full fine-tuning, perform poorly when directly applied to parameter-efficient tuning (PEFT) modules.
- Advanced model merging techniques often rely on validation data or test-time adaptation, limiting their generalization to unseen tasks and practical applicability in data-agnostic scenarios.
- Efficiently consolidating knowledge from multiple task-specific PEFT-tuned MLLMs into a single model without extensive retraining or data access remains a challenge.

## Method

- Introduced a method combining ==magnitude-based parameter pruning== with ==complementary parameter scaling== for ==LoRA matrices== to preserve critical directions and reduce interference.
- Developed a ==cross-task normalization== mechanism for scaling coefficients to balance the influence of different tasks and improve generalization across diverse domains.
- The final merged PEFT module is formed by adaptively summing these pruned and scaled low-rank matrices, requiring no training, additional data, or extra storage.

## Results

- Achieved an average performance of **57.33%** on eight seen multimodal generative tasks and **37.99%** on four unseen tasks on the MM-MergeBench, representing a **3.4%** and **4.5%** average improvement respectively over adapted baseline methods.
- Demonstrated superior performance on general MLLM benchmarks (POPE, MME, MMBench) and for vision tasks using CLIP-ViT-B-32 and CLIP-ViT-L-14, with up to a **4.4%** average accuracy increase over previous merging techniques.
- Ablation studies confirmed that both parameter pruning with complementary scaling and cross-task normalization are vital components, significantly contributing to direction robustness and overall performance gains.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Parameter-efficient tuning (PEFT) modules, such as LoRA, exhibit distinct singular value distributions and wider parameter ranges compared to full fine-tuning, rendering existing merging methods unsuitable.
> - Maintaining "direction robustness" by preserving the singular vector directions, particularly for smaller singular values, is essential for minimizing task interference and effective merging of PEFT modules.
> - Compensating for significant singular value gaps through adaptive inter-parameter scaling, combined with cross-task normalization, enhances stability and generalization to unseen tasks without explicit singular value decomposition.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper "RobustMerge" addresses a critical and rapidly evolving challenge within the broader landscape of large language models (LLMs) and multimodal large language models (MLLMs): the efficient aggregation of specialized knowledge. The rise of powerful foundation models has led to a common paradigm where these models are fine-tuned on custom, task-specific datasets to develop "expert models." While highly effective for individual tasks, this approach results in a proliferation of specialized models. The need to consolidate these diverse capabilities into a single, universal model without the prohibitive costs of retraining or encountering issues like catastrophic forgetting or data privacy concerns is paramount.

Historically, this problem has been tackled by **multi-task learning**, where a single model is trained to perform multiple tasks simultaneously. However, multi-task learning can be resource-intensive, requires access to all task data (which might pose privacy or logistical issues), and can still suffer from performance degradation or catastrophic forgetting when new tasks are introduced incrementally.

**Model merging** emerged as an attractive alternative. This paradigm aims to integrate knowledge from several pre-trained or fine-tuned expert models into a single model, typically in a "training-free" manner, meaning without needing to retrain the models or access their original training data. Early model merging methods, such as Task Arithmetic, Ties-merging, DARE, and PCB-merging, primarily focused on merging models that had undergone **full fine-tuning (FFT)**. These methods generally operate by performing arithmetic operations or sophisticated parameter adjustments on the entire weight matrices of the models.

However, the landscape has significantly shifted with the widespread adoption of **parameter-efficient tuning (PEFT)** techniques, most notably LoRA (Low-Rank Adaptation). PEFT methods fine-tune only a small fraction of a large model's parameters (e.g., by introducing low-rank adapters), significantly reducing computational costs, memory footprint, and storage requirements. While PEFT has revolutionized single-task adaptation, the research on *merging models fine-tuned with PEFT* has lagged. Existing FFT-based merging methods often perform poorly when directly applied to PEFT modules due to fundamental differences in parameter distributions and update mechanisms, as highlighted by RobustMerge. Furthermore, many high-performing merging methods (e.g., AdaMerging, EMR-Merging, LoraHub) require additional information like validation data, extra storage, or test-time adaptation, which restricts their generalizability to unseen tasks and practical applicability in real-world, data-agnostic scenarios. LoraHub, while focusing on LoRA composition, still relies on test-time adaptation for coefficient optimization.

RobustMerge critically identifies this gap. It positions itself as a pioneering work in **parameter-efficient model merging for MLLMs** that is **training-free, data-free, and storage-free**, while also explicitly targeting **generalization to unseen tasks**. By analyzing the unique characteristics of PEFT modules (specifically LoRA) through the lens of low-rank decomposition and singular values, the paper uncovers that "direction robustness" is key. This new perspective differentiates RobustMerge from prior work that focused on issues like sign conflicts or simple arithmetic operations on full models. In essence, RobustMerge pushes the frontier of model merging to accommodate the dominant PEFT paradigm, making the integration of diverse AI capabilities more efficient, robust, and broadly applicable.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of RobustMerge is to develop a highly effective and generally applicable model merging algorithm specifically designed for **parameter-efficient modules** (e.g., LoRA) of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs). This algorithm aims to create a single, multi-task capable model from several task-specific expert models, while crucially being **training-free, free from additional data access (e.g., validation data), and free from extra storage requirements.** A central goal is to enhance performance on both previously seen tasks and, importantly, generalize robustly to **unseen tasks**.

The motivations for this work stem from several critical observations and identified shortcomings in the current model merging landscape:

1. **Inefficiency of Full Fine-Tuning (FFT) Merging for PEFT Modules:** With the increasing size of MLLMs and datasets, Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (PEFT) techniques like LoRA have become the standard for creating task-specific models due to their efficiency. However, existing model merging methods were primarily designed for models obtained through full fine-tuning (FFT). The authors observed that directly applying these FFT-based merging methods to PEFT modules leads to a significant performance drop, indicating a fundamental incompatibility (illustrated in Fig. 1).
2. **Lack of Generalizability in Existing High-Performance Methods:** Current advanced model merging methods often rely on additional information (such as validation data from seen tasks or extra storage) or require test-time adaptation to boost performance. This dependency severely limits their robustness and extensibility to real-world scenarios where data access might be restricted, or generalization to entirely unseen tasks is required (Table 1).
3. **Unique Characteristics of PEFT Modules:** Through in-depth analysis from the perspective of low-rank decomposition, the authors made several key observations about PEFT modules (specifically LoRA):
 - **Stark Singular Values and Wider Distributions:** Unlike FFT parameters, efficient parameters in PEFT modules exhibit "stark singular values" (a large difference between the magnitudes of head and tail singular values) and a distinctly wider parameter distribution (Fig. 3a, 3c).
 - **Crucial Role of Direction Robustness:** The core insight is that for efficient merging, maintaining "direction robustness"—i.e., preserving the directions of singular vectors during the merging process—is vital to reduce task interference (Fig. 2). When merging, the directions corresponding to smaller singular values are particularly prone to instability, impacting performance.
 - **Mitigating Singular Value Gaps:** The analysis revealed that compensating for the significant gap between singular values (by adaptively scaling tail singular values) can effectively contribute to maintaining direction robustness (Fig. 3a, 3b).
 - **Asymmetric Roles of LoRA Matrices:** The two low-rank matrices (A and B) in LoRA have asymmetric functions and distinct parameter distributions (Fig. 3c), suggesting that targeted adaptation directly on these components might be effective without requiring explicit and computationally expensive singular value decomposition.

Motivated by these insights, RobustMerge aims to overcome the limitations of prior merging approaches by explicitly addressing the unique challenges of PEFT module merging, particularly the critical aspect of direction robustness, to enable robust multi-task learning and generalization without compromising efficiency or data privacy.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

RobustMerge is a training-free, parameter-efficient merging method for MLLMs that introduces complementary parameter adaptation to maintain direction robustness during the merging of LoRA modules. The approach is designed to be free from additional data or storage requirements and does not necessitate explicit low-rank decomposition.

The methodology is structured around two key components:

**1. Pruning and Complementary Parameter Scaling:**

- **Motivation:** The authors observed that PEFT modules exhibit stark singular values and wide parameter distributions. They hypothesize that smaller magnitude parameters are less critical for preserving the main directions of task-specific knowledge but can introduce noise and interference during merging. To address this and implicitly handle singular value magnitudes without explicit decomposition, RobustMerge proposes a two-step adaptation.
- **Pruning Ineffective Parameters:** Instead of complex sign-based conflict resolution common in FFT merging (e.g., Ties-merging), RobustMerge simplifies the definition of "ineffective parameters" to those with small values in magnitude. A binary mask is created for each LoRA matrix (A and B) by setting a certain percentage (`k`) of the smallest magnitude parameters to zero. This operation is formulated as:
 $\tilde{A} = M_A(k) \odot A$
 $\tilde{B} = M_B(k) \odot B$
 where $\odot$ denotes element-wise multiplication, and $M_A(k), M_B(k)$ are binary masks. The rationale is that pruning small-magnitude parameters reduces task interference while retaining the principal directions of task knowledge (governed by larger parameters).
- **Complementary Parameter Scaling:** After pruning, the remaining parameters are refined to implicitly scale tail singular values and compensate for performance deficiencies caused by task interference. Recognizing the asymmetric roles of LoRA's A and B matrices (A having an approximately uniform distribution, B a Gaussian distribution, and B being more crucial in PEFT), the method constructs scaling coefficients from the statistical characteristics of the pruned $\tilde{A}$ matrix and applies them to the pruned $\tilde{B}$ matrix. This avoids time-consuming explicit SVD.
 Specifically, a diagonal scaling matrix $S$ is constructed where each diagonal element $S_i$ is calculated as the ratio of the sum of absolute values of the $i$-th row of the original matrix $A$ to the sum of absolute values of the $i$-th row of the *pruned* matrix $\tilde{A}$:
 $S_i = \frac{\sum_{j=1}^{d_i} \text{abs}(A_{[i,j]})}{\sum_{j=1}^{d_i} \text{abs}(M_{A[i,j]} \odot A_{[i,j]})}$, for $i = 1, \dots, r$.
 This adaptive scaling mechanism (larger scale for smaller sums) effectively increases smaller "singular values" (implicitly) by a larger proportion, thereby contributing to minimizing task conflicts arising from direction instability. The scaled $\tilde{B}$ matrix for each task $n$ becomes $\tilde{B}_n \cdot S_n$.

**2. Cross-Task Normalization:**

- **Motivation:** The scaling coefficients $S_n$ generated for each task $n$ are initially task-independent. However, tasks can have varying data scales, leading to imbalances (overfitting for data-abundant tasks, underfitting for data-scarce tasks) and negatively impacting generalization to unseen tasks.
- **Normalization:** To mitigate this imbalance, the scaling matrices $S_n$ are normalized across all $N$ tasks for each singular value component $i$:
 $\tilde{S}_{i}^n = S_{i}^n / \sum_{k=1}^N S_{i}^k$, for $n=1, \dots, N$.
 This cross-task normalization aims to balance the influence of different tasks and lead to more stable performance and enhanced unseen task generalization.

**Final Merging Step:**

The final merged parameter-efficient module $\Delta_f W$ is then constructed by summing the adaptively scaled $\tilde{B}$ matrices and multiplying by the sum of the pruned $\tilde{A}$ matrices, scaled by a hyperparameter $\lambda$:
$\Delta_f W = \lambda \sum_{n=1}^N (\tilde{B}_n \cdot \tilde{S}_n) \cdot \sum_{n=1}^N \tilde{A}_n$
The merged model weights are obtained by adding $\Delta_f W$ to the original pre-trained model weights $W_0$.

This methodology uniquely combines pruning and an implicit, inter-parameter scaling strategy, followed by cross-task normalization, to effectively manage the singular value distribution and direction robustness of PEFT modules.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

RobustMerge demonstrates superior performance and generalizability across a wide range of experiments involving multimodal generative tasks and vision tasks, outperforming existing model merging methods tailored for full fine-tuning when applied to parameter-efficient modules.

#### 4.1. Experiments on MLLM with Generative Tasks (MM-MergeBench)

- **Effectiveness on Seen Tasks:** On the proposed MultiModal Merging Benchmark (MM-MergeBench), comprising eight diverse multimodal generative tasks (e.g., ScienceQA, ImageNet, VQAv2, OCRVQA), RobustMerge achieves an average performance of 57.33%, substantially outperforming adapted FFT-based baselines like Task Arithmetic (53.93%), DARE (53.84%), Ties-merging (53.09%), and PCB-merging (53.70%). This represents a significant 3.4% average improvement over the best baseline. Remarkably, RobustMerge even achieves performance comparable to or better than traditional multi-task learning (63.62% individual vs. 57.33% RobustMerge merged, while multi-task is 63.62%), showcasing its effectiveness in consolidating knowledge without data access.
- **Enhanced Unseen Task Generalization:** Evaluating on four diverse unseen tasks (ImageNet-R, AOKVQA, Screen2Word, TabMWP), RobustMerge yields an average accuracy of 37.99%, significantly outperforming all baselines (e.g., Task Arithmetic at 33.31%, DARE at 33.15%, Ties-merging at 33.14%). This 4.5% average improvement over baselines highlights RobustMerge's superior generalizability to new, unencountered domains, a critical capability for practical AI systems. It even surpasses multi-task learning's average of 36.06% on unseen tasks.
- **Performance on General Multimodal Benchmarks:** On general MLLM benchmarks like POPE (for object hallucination), MME, and MMBench, RobustMerge achieves the best results (87.2, 1494.9, 68.1 respectively), surpassing zero-shot, multi-task learning, and other merging methods. This indicates that RobustMerge not only improves task-specific performance but also maintains or enhances the general common knowledge and capabilities of the merged model.

#### 4.2. Experiments on VLM with Vision Tasks

- **Effectiveness on Vision Tasks (CLIP-ViT-B-32):** When tested on eight vision classification tasks using CLIP-ViT-B-32 as the foundation model, RobustMerge achieves an average accuracy of 55.9%, representing a 7.9% promotion against zero-shot performance and a substantial 4.4% improvement over previous merging methods like Task Arithmetic (51.1%) and Ties-merging (51.5%). This confirms its applicability beyond MLLMs to pure vision tasks.
- **Scalability to Larger VLM Models (CLIP-ViT-L-14):** RobustMerge maintains its superiority when applied to a larger foundation model, CLIP-ViT-L-14. It achieves an average accuracy of 70.9%, demonstrating a consistent 2.0% average improvement over the next best baseline (Ties-merging at 68.9%), affirming its scalability to larger model architectures.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies and Further Analysis

- **Component Contribution:** Both "Pruning and Complementary Parameter Scaling" and "Cross-Task Normalization" are essential. Pruning and scaling alone contribute significantly (2.21% average improvement), and adding cross-task normalization further boosts performance by another 1.19%, leading to the full 3.40% gain observed.
- **Influence of Rank:** RobustMerge consistently outperforms baselines across various LoRA ranks (e.g., 16 to 128), validating its scalability and robustness to different efficiency configurations.
- **Pruning Strategy:** Magnitude-based parameter pruning is shown to be superior to random pruning and aggregated sign-based pruning (Fig. 5b). This supports the authors' hypothesis that direction instability, rather than sign conflict, is the primary issue in PEFT merging due to wider parameter distributions.
- **Complementary Parameter Scaling:** The proposed inter-parameter relation-based scaling (derived from the original A and pruned A to scale B) proves more effective than scaling mechanisms solely dependent on individual A, B, or both simultaneously (Table 7).
- **Cross-Task Normalization Impact:** Normalization not only improves average performance but also significantly reduces the standard deviation across tasks (Fig. 5c), leading to more stable and reliable multi-task capabilities.
- **Direction Robustness Validation:** Direct analysis of singular vectors (Fig. 7) shows that RobustMerge substantially improves the similarity of the "remaining" (smaller) singular vectors between merged and task-specific models, and effectively strengthens smaller singular values. This provides strong empirical evidence supporting the core hypothesis that enhancing direction robustness of small singular values is key to improved merging performance.
- **Extensibility to Other PEFT Methods:** RobustMerge is demonstrated to work effectively with other advanced LoRA-based PEFT techniques, such as DoRA, showing consistent improvements over baselines, further highlighting its general applicability to the PEFT paradigm.

In summary, the findings robustly validate RobustMerge as an innovative and highly effective solution for parameter-efficient model merging, addressing critical shortcomings of previous methods and achieving state-of-the-art results across diverse multimodal and vision tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

RobustMerge presents a significant advancement in the field of large model adaptation and multi-task learning, with substantial potential impact across research and practical applications.

1. **Addressing a Critical Gap in Model Merging:** The most significant contribution of RobustMerge is its pioneering focus on **parameter-efficient model merging**, specifically for popular techniques like LoRA. Prior work largely overlooked the unique challenges of merging PEFT modules, leading to suboptimal performance when traditional FFT merging methods were misapplied. RobustMerge fills this crucial gap, acknowledging the widespread adoption of PEFT in modern AI.
2. **Novel Perspective on Direction Robustness:** By introducing the concept of "direction robustness" of singular values in low-rank decomposition as a core problem in efficient merging, the paper offers a novel theoretical and empirical lens. This shifts the focus from parameter-level conflicts (like sign conflicts) to a more fundamental understanding of how task-specific knowledge (represented by singular vectors and their magnitudes) interacts during merging. This perspective could inspire new lines of research in designing PEFT methods and merging algorithms.
3. **High Practical Utility and Efficiency:** The method is **training-free, data-free, and storage-free**. This characteristic is immensely valuable for real-world deployment, where access to original training data might be restricted due to privacy concerns, regulatory compliance, or proprietary data issues. It also eliminates the computational overhead of retraining or continuous learning, making the consolidation of diverse model capabilities much more efficient and scalable.
4. **Enhanced Generalization to Unseen Tasks:** A standout feature of RobustMerge is its superior performance on unseen tasks. The ability to generalize to new, unencountered domains without prior exposure or adaptation is a highly desirable quality for any robust AI system. This makes RobustMerge particularly useful for scenarios with dynamic task requirements or varying data distributions.
5. **Facilitating Universal Multi-Task Models:** RobustMerge enables the creation of a single, unified "universal model" that can handle multiple tasks effectively, alleviating the need to deploy and manage numerous specialized models. This consolidation can lead to simplified model management, reduced inference costs, and better resource utilization, especially for large MLLMs. It offers a pathway to gracefully integrate new capabilities into existing models without catastrophic forgetting.
6. **Broad Applicability and Scalability:** Demonstrated effectiveness across different types of tasks (multimodal generative, vision classification), various model scales (CLIP-ViT-B-32 to L-14, different LoRA ranks), and even different PEFT variants (LoRA and DoRA) highlights the method's robustness and broad applicability.
7. **Economic Impact:** By significantly reducing the computational resources (GPU hours, memory) and development time required to create multi-task AI systems, RobustMerge has the potential to lower the barrier to entry for many organizations, making advanced AI capabilities more accessible and cost-effective.

In conclusion, RobustMerge offers an elegant and effective solution to a pressing problem in the era of large foundation models. Its principled approach based on direction robustness, combined with its practical benefits of being training-free and data-agnostic, positions it as a significant contribution that will likely influence the development of future parameter-efficient merging and multi-task learning strategies for MLLMs and beyond.
