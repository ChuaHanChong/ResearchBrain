# CORE4D: A 4D Human-Object-Human Interaction Dataset for Collaborative Object REarrangement

> [!summary] Summary
> CORE4D is a large-scale 4D human-object-human interaction dataset for collaborative object rearrangement, combining 1K real mocap-captured sequences with 10K synthetic sequences generated via an iterative human-centric collaboration retargeting algorithm, spanning 3K object shapes; benchmarks on motion forecasting, interaction synthesis, and humanoid skill learning expose new generalization challenges.

## Problem

- Existing human-object interaction datasets mostly capture individual behaviors or two-person handovers, lacking multi-person collaborative rearrangement motions across diverse object categories.
- Vision-based motion tracking suffers low fidelity under severe occlusion common in multi-human collaboration, while mocap capture is too expensive to scale across many object instances.
- Prior object-centric interaction retargeting methods rely on contact correspondence between similar-topology objects, failing when source and target object geometries vary substantially.

## Method

- **CORE4D-Real**: a ==hybrid inertial-optical mocap== and multi-camera capture system recording 1K human-object-human sequences with allocentric RGB-D, egocentric RGB, SMPL-X meshes, and 2D masks across 37 objects.
- Collaboration retargeting pipeline: object-centric contact retargeting via ==DeepSDF latent interpolation== transfers contact points from source to novel target object geometries across six categories.
- Human-centric contact selection: a ==GAN-inspired human pose discriminator== with ==ranking loss== and ==beam search== iteratively refines contact-guided interaction retargeting to produce natural, low-penetration motions.

## Results

- Motion forecasting and interaction synthesis benchmarks (MDM, InterDiff, CAHMP, CHOIS, OMOMO) show consistent performance drops on unseen objects (S2) versus seen objects (S1), e.g. human joint error rising from **170.8** to **186.4mm**.
- Ablations confirm each retargeting component matters: removing the discriminator raises penetration distance from **0.24** to **0.55-0.68**, and users preferred the full method's motion naturalness in **84-88%** of comparisons.
- Adding CORE4D-Synthetic to training cuts human joint forecasting error from **127.7** to **116.2**, and humanoid box-lifting success rate rises from **0.0%** (RL) to **26.5%** (imitation learning with CORE4D data).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Human-centric contact selection, not just object-surface correspondence, is key to generalizing interaction retargeting across large geometric variation between objects.
> - Synthetic retargeted data can substitute for costly multi-object mocap capture, with users rating 43% of synthetic sequences as comparable to real and 14% even preferable.
> - Collaborative (multi-person) interaction generation remains substantially harder than single-person interaction synthesis, with all tested baselines showing degraded naturalness (higher FID) versus real motion.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of human-object interaction (HOI) is a cornerstone for advancements in virtual reality (VR), augmented reality (AR), human-robot interaction (HRI), and dexterous/humanoid manipulation. Understanding how humans interact with objects, especially in complex, collaborative scenarios, is crucial for developing intelligent systems that can perceive, predict, and participate in human-centric environments. However, the authors identify a significant gap in the existing research landscape: the scarcity of large-scale, richly annotated datasets specifically designed for *multi-person, collaborative human-object interaction*, particularly for tasks like *object rearrangement*.

Previous HOI datasets have made substantial contributions but often exhibit limitations that CORE4D aims to overcome. Many datasets focus on individual human interactions with static or partially deformable objects, providing valuable data for single-person pose estimation or object manipulation without considering multi-person dynamics (e.g., [2, 4, 18, 19, 30–33, 38, 42, 43, 55, 86, 95, 106, 107, 123, 124, 130, 137, 139]). While some datasets capture dynamic human-object interactions (e.g., GRAB, Li et al., BEHAVE, CHAIRS), they typically involve a limited number of object instances or focus on specific, often individual, actions like grasping or simple human-human handovers. Critically, these datasets often struggle to support generalized interaction understanding across a wide variety of object shapes and complex multi-person collaboration modes.

The challenge of scaling up precise HOI data is formidable due to the expense of motion capture (mocap) systems and the fidelity issues (e.g., occlusions) inherent in vision-based tracking methods, especially in multi-human scenarios. This paper addresses these limitations by introducing CORE4D, a dataset uniquely positioned to fill this void. It distinguishes itself by:
- **Focusing on multi-person and object collaborative motions:** Moving beyond individual or simple handover interactions.
- **Providing category-level interactions:** Enabling models to generalize across diverse object shapes within categories.
- **Offering comprehensive visual signals:** Including both egocentric and allocentric views for a richer perception context.
- **Employing a novel hybrid data acquisition strategy:** Combining real-world mocap with synthetic retargeting to achieve unprecedented scale and diversity cost-effectively.

Furthermore, the work also relates to the field of **human interaction retargeting** and **human-object interaction generation**. Existing retargeting methods are predominantly object-centric, struggling to transfer interactions across objects with significant geometric or scale variations. CORE4D proposes a novel human-centric approach to overcome this. For interaction generation, CORE4D provides a critical benchmark for developing advanced models that can synthesize realistic and collaborative human-object motions, pushing the boundaries for applications in animation, robotics, and virtual environments. By addressing these gaps, CORE4D aims to become a foundational resource, analogous to benchmark datasets in other areas of computer vision, but specifically for the challenging domain of collaborative 4D human-object interaction.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core motivation behind CORE4D stems from the recognized importance of understanding and modeling how humans cooperatively interact with and rearrange objects in their environment. Such behaviors are ubiquitous in daily life, from moving furniture to setting up a workspace. The ability to analyze and synthesize these complex collaborative motions holds transformative potential for various advanced technologies:
- **VR/AR applications:** Enabling more realistic and immersive virtual avatars and interactive environments that respond authentically to multi-person collaboration.
- **Human-Robot Interaction (HRI):** Developing robots that can intelligently and seamlessly participate in collaborative tasks with humans, anticipating actions and adapting to dynamic interaction patterns.
- **Dexterous and Humanoid Manipulation:** Training robotic systems, especially humanoid robots, to perform complex manipulation tasks that require coordination and interaction with objects and other agents.

However, the authors identify a critical bottleneck impeding progress in these areas: the severe lack of large-scale, richly annotated datasets specifically capturing multi-person, collaborative human-object interactions. Existing datasets, while valuable, are insufficient due to their limited scope in terms of object diversity, collaboration modes, or the scale of interaction sequences.

Given this motivation, the **key objectives** of the CORE4D project are:

1. **To present CORE4D, a novel large-scale 4D human-object-human (HOH) interaction dataset:** This dataset is designed to specifically address collaborative object rearrangement, incorporating diverse object geometries, collaboration modes, and 3D scenes.
2. **To introduce a novel hybrid data acquisition method:** This method combines precise real-world motion capture with an innovative synthetic collaboration retargeting strategy. The aim is to overcome the limitations of traditional data collection by cost-effectively scaling the dataset to cover a vast array of object instances while maintaining high quality.
3. **To benchmark two crucial tasks for collaboration generation:** Human-object motion forecasting and interaction synthesis. By applying existing state-of-the-art methods to CORE4D, the authors aim to quantitatively reveal new challenges and research opportunities in modeling human behaviors, enhancing motion naturalness, and adapting to novel object geometries in collaborative contexts.
4. **To demonstrate the practical value of CORE4D:** Specifically, by showing its utility in training humanoid robots for complex interaction skills like collaborative box-lifting, thereby promoting advancements in embodied AI.

In essence, CORE4D seeks to provide the foundational data infrastructure necessary to unlock deeper research into collaborative human behaviors and their applications in advanced intelligent systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core innovation of CORE4D's methodology lies in its **hybrid data acquisition scheme**, which combines meticulous real-world capture with a scalable synthetic generation strategy. This dual approach allows the dataset to achieve both high fidelity and extensive diversity, addressing the limitations of prior datasets.

#### 3.1. CORE4D-Real Data Capture and Annotation

The real-world component, CORE4D-Real, focuses on capturing authentic collaborative scenarios with high precision and rich annotations:

- **Data Capture System:** A hybrid inertial-optical mocap system is employed, leveraging 12 infrared cameras, mocap suits with 8 inertial-optical trackers, and data gloves per person. This system is crucial for robust tracking of human and object motions, especially in multi-person scenarios where occlusions are frequent. Visual signals are captured by four allocentric RGB-D cameras (Kinect Azure DK) and an egocentric camera (Osmo Action3) worn by one participant, providing comprehensive multi-view and first-person perspectives. All sensors operate at 15 FPS and are meticulously calibrated and synchronized.
- **Object Model Acquisition:** 37 rigid 3D models across six household categories (box, board, barrel, stick, chair, desk) are included. These models are generated using industrial 3D scanners and manually refined for accuracy.
- **Privacy Protection:** Faces in RGB videos are blurred, and all participants provided consent, emphasizing ethical data collection practices.
- **Data Annotation:**
 - **Object Pose Tracking:** 6D poses of objects are precisely tracked using markers attached to their surfaces, forming a virtual rigid body.
 - **Human Mesh Acquisition:** Captured BVH (Biovision Hierarchy) human skeletons are retargeted to the expressive SMPL-X model. This process involves optimizing body shape parameters (β) and full-body pose parameters (θ) using a comprehensive loss function. This loss function incorporates terms for regularization, 3D joint positions, joint orientations, temporal smoothness, and realistic contact between hands and objects, ensuring high-fidelity and natural human mesh sequences.
 - **2D Mask Annotation:** Automatic 2D segmentations for humans and manipulated objects are provided by combining DEVA for instance segmentation with mesh rendering and Intersection-over-Union (IoU) selection.

#### 3.2. CORE4D-Synthetic Data Generation (Collaboration Retargeting)

To overcome the limitations of real-world capture in terms of object diversity and scale, the authors introduce an innovative synthetic data augmentation pipeline called collaboration retargeting. This method transfers real interactions from CORE4D-Real to 3,000 diverse virtual object shapes (from ShapeNet), resulting in 10,000 synthetic collaboration sequences. The key insight is that while temporal collaboration patterns are diverse, spatial human-object relations show homogeneity within object categories, allowing for algorithmic transfer.

The pipeline comprises three main components:

1. **Object-centric Contact Retargeting:** This step focuses on accurately transferring contact information from source objects (in CORE4D-Real) to novel target objects. It uses a DeepSDF-based approach, interpolating latent vectors between source and target objects to generate intermediate 3D meshes. Contact positions are then successively transferred across these meshes via nearest-neighbor search, creating a pool of potential "contact constraints" for the target object.
2. **Contact-guided Interaction Retargeting:** Given the target object's geometry and selected contact constraints, this module adapts a source human-object-human motion sequence from CORE4D-Real to the new object. It involves a two-step optimization:
 - First, the target object's 6D motion (rotation and translation) is optimized to maintain fidelity to the source motion, avoid ground penetration, and ensure temporal smoothness.
 - Second, the two humans' SMPL-X poses, translations, and orientations are optimized. This optimization minimizes differences in human joint positions (with specialized handling for arm joints relative to parent joints), ensures temporal smoothness, prevents human-ground interpenetration, and, critically, minimizes the Chamfer Distance between human fingertips and the propagated contact regions on the target object, ensuring realistic contact.
3. **Human-centric Contact Selection:** A novel aspect is selecting the *most plausible* contact constraints from the generated pool, recognizing that not all geometrically transferred contacts are humanly natural. This is achieved via a beam search algorithm guided by two mechanisms:
 - **Human Pose Discriminator:** A discriminator, inspired by GANs, is trained on positive (real) and negative (noise-augmented and retargeted) human pose samples. It learns to score the naturalness of human poses.
 - **Interpenetration Penalty:** A penalty for excessive human-object interpenetration further filters out unrealistic interaction candidates.
 This iterative mechanism refines interaction motions, ensuring realism and adaptability to diverse object geometries.

This methodology yields a dataset (1K real-world, 10K synthetic sequences) that offers unprecedented scale and diversity for collaborative HOI research, addressing the core challenges identified in the broader research landscape.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The CORE4D paper presents a wealth of findings demonstrating the dataset's characteristics, its utility in benchmarking existing methods, and the effectiveness of its novel data augmentation strategy.

#### 4.1. Dataset Characteristics and Diversity

CORE4D is designed with intrinsic diversity across three key dimensions:

- **Object Geometries:** The dataset includes 37 real-world and 3,000 virtual 3D object models spanning six household categories: box, board, barrel, stick, chair, and desk. These categories represent a range of geometric complexities and symmetries, ensuring that interactions can be studied across varied object forms.
- **Collaboration Modes:** Five distinct human-human collaboration modes are defined, each representing a unique interaction pattern: "Move1" (both participants aware of goal), "Move2" (only one aware), "Pass" (handover/relay), "Join" (one joins another), and "Leave" (one leaves during joint transport). This diversity allows for modeling a wide array of cooperative behaviors.
- **3D Scenes:** Interactions are captured in scenes with varying levels of environmental complexity—no obstacles, a single obstacle, or multiple obstacles. This encourages participants to exhibit diverse navigation behaviors (bypassing, stepping over, moving obstacles), adding realism and challenge.

#### 4.2. Benchmarking Human-object Motion Forecasting

The task involves predicting subsequent human-object poses for 15 frames given the previous 15 frames. Three state-of-the-art motion forecasting methods (MDM, InterDiff, CAHMP) were benchmarked on CORE4D.

- **Key Finding:** A consistent performance drop was observed for unseen objects (Test Set S2) compared to seen objects (Test Set S1) in human pose prediction. In contrast, object pose prediction errors remained similar across both test sets.
- **Implication:** This highlights a significant challenge for existing methods in generalizing *human collaborative motion* to novel object shapes, even if they can predict the object's trajectory relatively well. This suggests that models struggle to adapt human interaction strategies to new geometries within a collaborative context.

#### 4.3. Benchmarking Interaction Synthesis

This task aims to generate two-person collaboration motions conditioned on an object geometry sequence. Three advanced diffusion models (MDM, OMOMO, CHOIS) were used as baselines.

- **Key Finding:** Quantitative evaluations showed larger errors for multi-human collaboration (OMOMO) compared to its reported performance on individual interaction synthesis. Furthermore, the Fréchet Inception Distance (FID) scores for synthesized results were higher than for real motion data, indicating a shortfall in motion naturalness.
- **Implication:** Generating natural and plausible multi-human collaborative interactions, especially those involving dynamic objects, remains a significant challenge, both in terms of accuracy and perceptual realism.

#### 4.4. Collaboration Retargeting Ablation Studies and User Preferences

Extensive ablation studies and user studies were conducted to validate the effectiveness and quality of the proposed collaboration retargeting strategy.

- **Effectiveness of Hybrid Strategy:** Ablations demonstrated that removing key components of the retargeting pipeline (e.g., using only contact points from a source trajectory instead of a pool of candidates, omitting the human pose discriminator, or removing the contact candidate update) led to sharp declines in physical plausibility (e.g., increased penetration distance) and user preferences for contact realism and motion naturalness. This strongly validates the necessity of the proposed human-centric contact selection and iterative refinement.
- **Quality of CORE4D-Synthetic:** User studies comparing CORE4D-Synthetic with CORE4D-Real showed that in 43% of cases, users perceived the synthetic data quality as comparable to real data. Remarkably, in 14% of cases, users even preferred the synthetic data. This is a strong indicator that the synthetic data closely approximates the quality of real data and successfully augments the dataset.

#### 4.5. Dataset Applications

The paper showcases two practical applications of CORE4D:

- **Enhancing Human-object Motion Forecasting Quality:** Experiments showed that training with CORE4D-Synthetic data, especially when augmented to a larger scale (e.g., 4K synthetic sequences combined with 1K real), significantly improved the motion forecasting performance of a lightweight model (CAHMP), particularly in object motion forecasting and overall human-object interaction. This demonstrates the tangible value of the synthetic data in improving generalization and model performance.
- **Supporting Humanoid Skill Learning:** CORE4D human-box interaction data was retargeted onto the Unitree H1 humanoid robot for a box-lifting task in an Isaac Gym simulation. Imitation learning methods (HumanPlus, HST + ACT) trained on this data achieved substantial success rates (21.0% and 26.5% respectively) for lifting unseen boxes. This contrasts sharply with a demonstration-free reinforcement learning approach (PPO), which achieved a 0% success rate. This application vividly illustrates CORE4D's potential to facilitate the learning of complex, human-like interaction skills for humanoid robots.

In summary, CORE4D not only provides a unique and large-scale dataset for collaborative human-object interaction but also effectively benchmarks the limitations of current AI models and demonstrates its practical utility in advancing robotic skill learning.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

CORE4D represents a significant step forward in the fields of computer vision, robotics, and human-computer interaction, with a profound potential impact across several domains.

#### 5.1. Addressing a Critical Data Gap and Enabling New Research Avenues

The primary significance of CORE4D lies in its direct response to a long-standing challenge: the scarcity of large-scale, high-quality 4D datasets for *multi-person, collaborative object rearrangement*. By providing 1K real-world and 10K synthetic human-object-human interaction sequences, it fills a crucial gap that previously hindered in-depth studies of complex cooperative behaviors. This dataset will serve as a foundational resource, enabling researchers to:
- Develop more sophisticated models for understanding and predicting multi-person social dynamics and intentions during collaboration.
- Explore how humans adapt their interactions to diverse object geometries and environmental constraints.
- Advance research into generalizable human-object interaction understanding, moving beyond single-instance or object-specific learning.

#### 5.2. Advancing Human-Robot Interaction (HRI) and Embodied AI

One of the most impactful applications demonstrated is the training of humanoid robot skills. CORE4D provides rich, human-like demonstration data that can significantly accelerate the development of:
- **Collaborative Robotics:** Robots capable of engaging in fluid, intelligent, and safe cooperative tasks with humans, understanding shared goals and adapting to dynamic interaction patterns. This is crucial for real-world scenarios in manufacturing, logistics, and domestic assistance.
- **Humanoid Skill Learning:** Empowering humanoid robots to learn a broader range of complex manipulation skills, such as collaborative lifting, carrying, and rearrangement, directly from human examples. This bridges the gap between human capabilities and robotic embodiment, fostering more dexterous and versatile robots.
- **Embodied AI:** Creating AI agents that can operate effectively within physical environments, understanding and acting upon the rich nuances of human interaction and physical world constraints.

#### 5.3. Enhancing VR/AR and Computer Graphics

The detailed 4D human-object interaction data, including egocentric and allocentric views, holds immense value for:
- **Realistic Avatar Animation:** Generating more lifelike and context-aware animations for virtual characters in VR/AR experiences, especially in multi-user collaborative environments.
- **Interactive Virtual Environments:** Creating dynamic virtual worlds where avatars and virtual objects interact plausibly, enhancing immersion and realism.
- **Content Creation:** Providing a rich source of motion primitives and interaction patterns for animators and game developers, reducing manual effort and increasing the naturalness of virtual characters.

#### 5.4. Novel Methodology for Data Acquisition

The proposed hybrid data acquisition method, particularly the human-centric collaboration retargeting strategy, is a methodological contribution in itself. This approach offers a cost-efficient and scalable paradigm for generating large-scale, high-quality 4D interaction data. Its demonstrated effectiveness in transferring interactions across diverse object geometries while preserving human-like qualities will likely inspire similar hybrid data generation techniques in other domains where real-world data collection is expensive and challenging.

#### 5.5. Benchmarking and Future Research Directions

By benchmarking state-of-the-art motion forecasting and interaction synthesis methods, CORE4D has explicitly identified new challenges, such as the difficulty of generalizing human collaborative motion to novel objects and generating perceptually natural multi-human interactions. This acts as a clear call to action for the research community, guiding future efforts toward developing more robust, generalizable, and natural interaction generation models.

#### 5.6. Limitations and Future Work

The authors acknowledge certain limitations, such as the exclusion of outdoor scenes (due to mocap system constraints) and visual signals in CORE4D-Synthetic. These limitations naturally point to future research directions, including extending data capture to outdoor environments and integrating photorealistic rendering or visual signal transfer into synthetic data generation. These areas represent exciting opportunities to further enhance the realism and utility of such datasets.

In conclusion, CORE4D is not merely a dataset; it is a catalyst for new research paradigms in understanding and synthesizing complex human collaboration. Its comprehensive nature, innovative methodology, and demonstrated impact across critical application areas position it as a benchmark resource that will significantly accelerate progress in human-robot interaction, embodied AI, and immersive technologies.
