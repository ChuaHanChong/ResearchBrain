# DiVE-k: Differential Visual Reasoning for Fine-grained Image Recognition

> [!summary] Summary
> The DIVE-K framework enhances Large Vision Language Models' (LVLMs) ability for zero-shot fine-grained image recognition by training them to perform differential visual reasoning using their own top-k predictions in a multiple-choice question format. This approach yielded a 6.2% improvement in Harmonic Mean over prior methods in zero-shot base-to-novel generalization and significantly improved few-shot classification accuracy.

## Problem

- Large Vision Language Models (LVLMs) struggle to differentiate between visually similar categories in fine-grained image recognition, particularly in zero-shot settings.
- Existing reinforcement learning (RL) fine-tuning methods for LVLMs often use brittle exact string-match rewards, which lead to memorization of training categories and poor generalization to unseen data.
- Current methods fail to elicit attribute-level, discriminative reasoning necessary for distinguishing subtle visual differences among plausible, yet similar, categories.

## Method

- The **DiVE-k** framework employs a two-step strategy, first mining the LVLM's own ==top-k predictions== to construct a set of plausible, visually similar options for each image.
- The fine-grained recognition task is reframed into a Multiple-Choice Question (MCQ) format, compelling the model to engage in differential reasoning among these generated options, with the ground-truth category explicitly included.
- Reinforcement learning is applied using the GRPO algorithm with an easily verifiable, binary reward signal (based on MCQ correctness and format adherence), designed to mitigate memorization and encourage robust reasoning.

## Results

- DiVE-k achieved an average Harmonic Mean of **79.8%** in zero-shot base-to-novel generalization, representing a +**6.2%** improvement over ViRFT and outperforming other baselines.
- The framework demonstrated robust generalization in mixed-dataset zero-shot settings (**78.7%** HM) and improved performance in 4-shot classification (**74.75%** HM), showing effectiveness with limited data and across multiple domains.
- Qualitative analysis confirmed that DiVE-k generates attribute-grounded, differential reasoning traces, enabling the model to accurately distinguish between fine-grained labels based on specific visual cues.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Utilizing the model's own top-k generations to create MCQs is critical for eliciting effective differential visual reasoning in fine-grained classification tasks.
> - A simple, verifiable MCQ-based reward system in RL training is more effective than exact string-matching rewards, as it reduces memorization and significantly improves generalization capabilities.
> - Joint optimization of both vision and text components within the LVLM is necessary to maximize the model's differential reasoning capacity and achieve strong generalization across diverse fine-grained domains.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in "DiVE-K: Differential Visual Reasoning for Fine-Grained Image Recognition" operates within the domain of Large Vision Language Models (LVLMs) and their application to fine-grained image recognition, particularly in zero-shot and few-shot learning settings. Early approaches to zero-shot image recognition, such as CLIP, focused on matching visual embeddings with text embeddings of class names. With the advent of LVLMs like QWEN2-VL, which integrate Large Language Models (LLMs) with visual encoders through multimodal pre-training, capabilities in zero-shot recognition have expanded, leveraging extensive linguistic knowledge.

Despite these advancements, LVLMs often exhibit limitations in differentiating between visually similar categories in fine-grained recognition tasks. Existing methods to improve these models, particularly those employing Reinforcement Learning (RL) for fine-tuning, often face challenges. For instance, approaches like ViRFT extend Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning to vision tasks, using RL with a verifiable reward based on exact string matching. However, this exact string-match reward mechanism has been identified as brittle, susceptible to memorization of training categories, and inadequate for eliciting the attribute-level, discriminative reasoning necessary for generalization to unseen classes. This leads to a tendency for models to over-rely on coarse, salient attributes rather than fine-grained distinctions, even when the correct answer may be present within a broader set of model predictions (Pass@K performance).

This research positions itself as an effort to address these identified shortcomings by developing a framework that encourages LVLMs to engage in differential reasoning. It builds upon the success of RL-based reasoning in pure language tasks and its recent extension to multimodal contexts, but seeks to refine the reward mechanisms and training paradigms to better suit the nuances of fine-grained visual discrimination. By formulating the problem as a multiple-choice question (MCQ) task using the model’s own top-k predictions, DiVE-k aims to compel the model to perform the specific type of differential analysis required to distinguish among plausible, yet visually similar, categories. This approach represents a shift from simple string-matching rewards to a more structured, context-aware training signal that leverages the model's inherent knowledge for improved generalization and reduced memorization.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the capability of Large Vision Language Models (LVLMs) in performing zero-shot fine-grained image recognition. This involves improving their accuracy in differentiating between visually similar categories, a task where current LVLMs often exhibit limitations despite their extensive text knowledge.

The motivation for this work stems from several key observations and deficiencies identified in existing LVLM training paradigms:

1. **Limited Fine-Grained Differentiation in LVLMs:** While LVLMs demonstrate impressive zero-shot recognition capabilities, their performance for fine-grained classification remains constrained. They frequently struggle to distinguish between subtle visual differences that define distinct, yet semantically close, categories. This often manifests as a significant gap between the model's top-1 accuracy (Pass@1) and its Pass@K performance, where the correct label is often present among multiple sampled responses but not selected as the primary prediction. This indicates an over-reliance on generic visual cues, rather than fine-grained, differential analysis.

2. **Deficiencies of Existing Fine-Tuning Methods:**
 - **Brittle Reward Signals:** Existing reinforcement learning (RL) fine-tuning methods, particularly those used for visual reasoning tasks like ViRFT, often employ an exact string-match reward mechanism. This mechanism is identified as brittle due to several factors: it requires arbitrary string post-processing, cannot accommodate variations in category names (e.g., scientific vs. common names), and provides a binary, non-discriminative signal.
 - **Encouragement of Memorization:** The exact string-match reward incentivizes models to memorize training category names rather than developing robust reasoning capabilities. This leads to poor generalization performance, especially when evaluated on "novel" categories that were not part of the training (base) set.
 - **Failure to Elicit Differential Reasoning:** Such reward structures do not adequately encourage models to perform attribute-level, discriminative reasoning. Consequently, when presented with ambiguous visual evidence, models may fail to leverage their internal text knowledge to identify the specific features that distinguish one plausible category from another. This hinders their ability to resolve confusion among similar options.

3. **Untapped Knowledge within LVLMs:** The researchers observe that LVLMs possess detailed knowledge about the parts and attributes of various categories, including both seen (base) and unseen (novel) classes. This inherent knowledge is not fully utilized by current methods to drive differential analysis.

To address these issues, the key objectives are:
- **Develop a training framework (DiVE-k) that leverages the model's own top-k predictions as a training signal.** This approach aims to transform the fine-grained recognition problem into a differential visual reasoning task.
- **Formulate the problem as a Multiple-Choice Question (MCQ) interface using the model's top-k outputs as options.** This design is intended to compel the model to engage in fine-grained reasoning among plausible alternatives.
- **Create an easily verifiable reward signal that mitigates memorization and improves generalization.** By framing the task as selecting the correct option from a given set, the reward becomes unambiguous and less prone to the brittleness of exact string matching.
- **Demonstrate improved generalization to unseen categories (base-to-novel setting), mixed-domain scenarios, and few-shot learning.**

In essence, the motivation is to move beyond superficial recognition and memorization towards a deeper, attribute-based understanding that enables LVLMs to accurately distinguish between fine-grained visual categories, thereby enhancing their generalization capabilities across various challenging scenarios.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The DiVE-k (Differential Visual rEasoning using top-k generations) framework employs a two-step strategy designed to elicit differential reasoning in Large Vision Language Models (LVLMs). This approach utilizes the model’s own top-k generations as a training signal.

#### 3.1. Top-k as Hypotheses Set (Offline Option Mining)

The first step of the DiVE-k framework involves an offline process to construct a set of potential hypotheses, which are subsequently used to create Multiple Choice Questions (MCQs).

1. **Response Generation:** For each training image `I` and a given text query `q`, the base policy model `πθ` is used to generate `K` sampled responses, `Y = (y1, y2..., yK)`. This sampling is performed using a decoding strategy such as top-p nucleus sampling (with parameters like `max_new_tokens=1024`, `temperature=1.0`, `top_p=0.95`, `do_sample=True`, `num_return_sequences=20`, `repetition_penalty=1.1`).
2. **Category Extraction and Frequency Counting:** Each response `yi` consists of a reasoning trace `ri` and a final predicted category name `ci`. From the `K` generated responses, a set `C` of unique category names is identified. The frequencies of these unique categories within `C` are then counted.
3. **Option Set Construction:** An option set `Otop-k` is constructed by selecting the `k` most frequent categories from `C`. The parameter `m` is set to 5, meaning `k = min(m, |C|)`.
4. **Ground-Truth Inclusion:** To ensure that the correct answer is always available during training, the ground-truth category name `ˆc` is explicitly included in `Otop-k`. If `ˆc` is not initially among the `k` most frequent categories, the least frequent candidate in `Otop-k` is replaced with `ˆc`.
5. **MCQ Formatting:** The final option set `Otop-k` is formatted into a standard MCQ, with options enumerated (e.g., A, B, C...). These options are randomly shuffled to mitigate order bias. The ground-truth label `ˆa` is assigned to the option corresponding to `ˆc`.
6. **Dataset Formation and Hard-Negative Mining:** Each sample in the final dataset `D` is a tuple `(I, q, Oenum, ˆa)`, comprising the image, query, enumerated options, and the correct label. To focus on challenging examples, trivial cases where the base model generates only a single, correct category prediction (i.e., `|C| = 1` and `C = {ˆc}`) are filtered out. The use of the same base model for option generation is emphasized as crucial, ensuring options are drawn from the model’s own distribution.

#### 3.2. RL Training using GRPO

The second step involves training the LVLM using the constructed MCQ dataset `D` and the GRPO (Group Reinforcement Policy Optimization) algorithm.

1. **Policy Model Training:** The LVLM acts as the policy model `πθ`, trained to generate a sequence comprising intermediate reasoning tokens `s` and a final answer `a`.
2. **GRPO Algorithm:** During training, for each data sample `di` from `D`, the current policy model `πθ` generates `N` rollouts (`O0, O1..., ON`).
3. **Reward Computation:** A reward `(r0, r1..., rN)` is computed for each of these responses. The reward function is composed of two parts:
 - **MCQ Reward (`rmcq`):** This binary reward checks for the correctness of the model's selected option. It yields `1.0` if the model predicts the correct option (`ˆa = a`) and `0.0` otherwise.
 - **Format Reward (`rformat`):** This reward encourages the model to adhere to the expected output format, specifically using `<think>` and `<answer>` tags for reasoning and the final answer, respectively.
 - **Final Reward (`r`):** The total reward is a weighted sum: `r = λf rformat + λm rmcq`, where `λf` and `λm` are hyperparameters (set to `1` in this work).
4. **Group Advantage Estimation:** The computed rewards for each group of rollouts are used to estimate the group advantage `Ai` using the formula: `Ai = (ri - mean{r1..., rN}) / (std{r1..., rN} + δ)`, where `δ` is a small constant.
5. **Objective Function:** The training objective `JGRPO(θ)` aims to maximize the expected reward while maintaining the updated policy `πθ` close to a reference policy `πref` (typically the pre-trained model) to ensure stable learning. This is achieved using a clipped surrogate objective with a KL divergence penalty, as specified by the GRPO algorithm.
 - `JGRPO(θ) = (1/N) Σ [min(si Ai, clip(si, 1 - ϵ, 1 + ϵ)Ai) - β DKL(πθ || πref)]`
 - Here, `ϵ` and `β` are hyperparameters, and `si` is the ratio of probabilities under the new and old policies.

#### 3.3. Inference

The inference pipeline for DiVE-k is also a two-step process, mirroring the training phase:

1. **Option Generation:** The trained policy model is first used to generate a set of potential options by performing top-k generations (K rollouts) for an input image and query. Unlike training, the ground-truth category is not added if it fails to appear in these generated options.
2. **Option Selection:** The same trained policy model is then re-prompted with the generated options in an MCQ format, requiring it to select the correct answer among them.

#### 3.4. Experimental Setup

- **Baselines:** The method is compared against pre-trained QWEN2.5-VL-7B, Supervised Fine Tuning (SFT) with full model and LoRA, ViRFT, and proprietary models (Gemini2.5-flash-light, GPT-5-mini, Grok4-fast). Additionally, baselines are evaluated using DiVE-k's two-step inference pipeline (marked with *). A "Consistency" baseline (most consistent prediction in top-k) is also included.
- **Datasets:** Five standard fine-grained datasets are used: OxfordFlowers-102, CUB-200, OxfordPets-37, StanfordCars-196, and FGVC Aircraft-100. Categories are split into equal halves for base (training) and novel (testing) classes.
- **Evaluation Settings:** Experiments are conducted for standard zero-shot base-to-novel generalization, mixed-dataset base-to-novel generalization (training a single model on base classes from all datasets), and 4-shot classification.
- **Metrics:** Classification accuracy is reported for base classes, novel classes, and their Harmonic Mean (HM).
- **Answer Verification:** To account for variations in category names (e.g., scientific vs. common), an LLM (Gemini 2.5 flash lite) is used to semantically verify if the model's predicted category matches the ground truth.
- **Implementation:** Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct is the base model. K=20 rollouts for offline option generation. Training for 400 steps (base-to-novel) or 1 epoch (mixed-data), 200 steps (few-shot). Trained on three A6000 GPUs. A critical fix was applied to ViRFT's string-matching logic to prevent shortcut learning.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The DiVE-k framework was evaluated across several fine-grained image recognition settings, demonstrating improvements over existing methodologies.

#### 4.1. Zero-Shot Base-to-Novel Generalization

In the standard base-to-novel generalization setting, DiVE-k demonstrated superior performance.
- **Overall Performance:** DiVE-k achieved the highest average Harmonic Mean (HM) of 79.8%, with average base and novel accuracies of 80.8% and 78.8%, respectively.
- **Comparison to ViRFT:** This represents gains of +7.8% on base categories, +4.6% on novel categories, and +6.2% on HM compared to ViRFT. Even when ViRFT was run using DiVE-k's two-step inference pipeline (ViRFT*), DiVE-k maintained improvements of +4.0% on base and +1.8% on novel accuracies. The paper notes that ViRFT's gains on novel categories were marginal (+0.1%) when using the two-step inference compared to QWEN2.5-VL-7B, suggesting its reward primarily reinforced base categories. DiVE-k, in contrast, showed a more substantive boost on novel classes.
- **Dataset-Specific Gains:** Significant improvements were observed on CUB (+14.9% HM) and Oxford Flowers (+8.5% HM), with consistent gains on Stanford Cars (+6.1% HM) and FGVC Aircraft (+3.2% HM). A small regression was noted on Oxford Pets, potentially attributed to an increased number of options in the second step leading to more errors.
- **Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT):** SFT, while showing strong base accuracy, exhibited a sharp decline in novel category performance (average drop of 33.5% relative to base model, 19.9% HM reduction), indicating poor generalization.
- **Proprietary Models:** DiVE-k surpassed Grok4-fast and performed comparably to Gemini2.5-flash-light, though GPT-5-mini achieved higher scores.

#### 4.2. Mixed-Dataset Base-to-Novel Generalization

For the mixed-dataset setting, where a single model is trained on base categories from all five datasets, DiVE-k also achieved notable results.
- **Overall Performance:** DiVE-k attained the highest average HM of 78.7%.
- **Comparison to Baselines:** This represented a +9.0% HM improvement over pre-trained QWEN2.5-VL-7B and a +4.0% HM improvement over ViRFT, with average base/novel gains of +5.1%/+3.5%.
- **Generalization Robustness:** ViRFT exhibited poorer performance on novel classes in this setting compared to the pre-trained QWEN2.5-VL-7B (76.2% vs. 77.0%), suggesting difficulties in cross-domain transfer. DiVE-k, however, achieved 78.8% accuracy on novel classes, indicating more robust generalization.

#### 4.3. Few-Shot Classification

In a 4-shot classification setting, DiVE-k demonstrated improved performance.
- **Overall Performance:** DiVE-k achieved an average HM classification accuracy of 74.75%.
- **Comparison to Baselines:** This represented a 7.73% improvement over ViRFT and a 10.85% improvement over QWEN2.5-VL-7B, indicating the method's effectiveness even with limited training data.

#### 4.4. Visualization

Qualitative comparisons illustrated DiVE-k's ability to perform differential reasoning.
- **ViRFT:** Tended to make coarse judgments, leading to incorrect classifications and generic rationales.
- **DiVE-k:** Generated a top-k shortlist and then applied attribute-grounded differential reasoning (e.g., comparing specific visual cues like tail configuration, body shape, or grille design) to select the correct fine-grained label, providing justifications aligned with the final choice.

#### 4.5. Ablation Studies

- **Efficacy of Top-K for MCQ Option Generation:** Generating MCQ options directly from the base model’s own top-k predictions was found to be critical. This strategy yielded substantial gains (12% on base, 6.9% on novel accuracy for CUB dataset) compared to randomly selected options or options generated using text embeddings, demonstrating its optimality for training.
- **Role of Top-K Generations During Inference:** Performance generally improved as K (number of rollouts for top-k generation) increased, saturating around K=10-15 for most datasets, indicating diminishing returns beyond this range. DiVE-k consistently outperformed baselines across nearly all K values. An exception was the Pet dataset, which peaked at K=2 and declined with larger K, suggesting that too many options could sometimes introduce confusion for specific datasets.
- **Roles of Vision and Text Components:** Full model training (fine-tuning both vision and text components) yielded the best performance. Fine-tuning only vision components improved base accuracy but not novel accuracy, while fine-tuning only text components improved both but less effectively than full model training. This indicated that joint optimization of both modalities is necessary for maximizing reasoning capabilities and generalization.
- **Accuracy Analysis of the Two Steps:**
 - **Step 1 (Top-k accuracy):** The base QWEN2.5-VL model already exhibited high top-k recall. DiVE-k training further improved this recall, ensuring more consistent retrieval of correct candidates.
 - **Step 2 (MCQ accuracy/Differential Reasoning):** DiVE-k achieved average improvements of 4.3% on base and 1.6% on novel categories in MCQ accuracy. For most datasets, the primary performance bottleneck shifted to this differential reasoning stage, while for CUB, there remained room for improvement in candidate generation.

#### 4.6. Additional Backbone Results

DiVE-k also demonstrated generalizability when using Gemma3-12B as the backbone, showing consistent and substantial improvements over the baseline on CUB and Flowers datasets.

#### 4.7. Computational Cost Comparison

The two-step inference pipeline incurred increased computational cost, with an average per-sample inference time of 12.95 seconds compared to 2.50 seconds for one-step inference. However, the study noted that performance gains were not solely due to increased computation, as DiVE-k still outperformed baselines even with K=1 (a single forward pass), indicating the inherent value of the formulation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The DiVE-k framework introduces a novel approach to address limitations in fine-grained image recognition by Large Vision Language Models (LVLMs), specifically their challenges in differentiating between visually similar categories and generalizing to unseen data.

The significance of this work lies in several areas:

1. **Enhanced Differential Reasoning:** By leveraging the model's own top-k predictions to create multiple-choice questions, DiVE-k compels the LVLM to perform explicit differential reasoning. This moves beyond simple pattern recognition or reliance on coarse features, encouraging the model to identify specific attributes that distinguish fine-grained categories. This approach directly addresses the identified weakness of LVLMs in making nuanced visual discriminations.
2. **Improved Generalization:** The framework demonstrates significant improvements in generalization capabilities across various challenging settings:
 - **Base-to-Novel Generalization:** DiVE-k substantially outperforms existing methods like ViRFT on novel (unseen) categories, indicating its effectiveness in transferring learned reasoning skills to new data.
 - **Mixed-Domain Generalization:** The gains observed in the mixed-dataset setting suggest that the method fosters more robust and broadly applicable reasoning strategies, enabling a single model to perform across diverse fine-grained domains.
 - **Few-Shot Learning:** The improvements in few-shot classification highlight its data efficiency and ability to learn effectively even with limited examples per class.
3. **Mitigation of Memorization:** The use of an easily verifiable, MCQ-based reward signal, as opposed to brittle exact string-matching, reduces the tendency for models to merely memorize category names. This encourages the development of more fundamental reasoning abilities that are transferable.
4. **Leveraging Intrinsic Model Knowledge:** The core idea of constructing training signals from the model's own top-k generations is a significant contribution. It demonstrates that the inherent knowledge distribution within a pre-trained LVLM can be effectively leveraged to refine its reasoning capabilities, leading to optimal learning.
5. **Enhanced Interpretability:** The framework encourages the generation of attribute-grounded, differential reasoning traces, which can make the model's decision-making process more transparent and understandable, as illustrated by the qualitative comparisons. This can be valuable in applications requiring explainable AI.
6. **Model Agnostic Potential:** The consistent performance gains observed when DiVE-k was applied to a different backbone model (Gemma3-12B) suggest that the framework's principles are broadly applicable and not specific to a single LVLM architecture.

The potential impact of DiVE-k includes advancing the capabilities of LVLMs for real-world applications requiring high-precision visual identification, such as medical image analysis, species classification, quality control in manufacturing, and detailed content moderation. While acknowledging increased computational cost during inference, the work also suggests avenues for future research, such as integrating Pass@k accuracy as a direct reward signal to further optimize the trade-off between performance and efficiency, and verifying the factual correctness of generated reasoning traces. This research establishes a direction for training LVLMs to move beyond superficial recognition towards a more nuanced and context-aware visual understanding.
