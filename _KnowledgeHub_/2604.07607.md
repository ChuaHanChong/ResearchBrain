# EgoVerse: An Egocentric Human Dataset for Robot Learning from Around the World

> [!summary] Summary
> EgoVerse provides a continuously growing egocentric human dataset, currently totaling 1,362 hours of demonstrations, designed to scale robot learning and foster collaboration. A multi-robot study demonstrated that co-training robot policies with this human data consistently improved robot performance and generalization by up to 30% relative gains, with domain-aligned data crucial for effective scaling.

## Problem

- Robot learning faces a data scalability bottleneck, as collecting robot-specific data is expensive, slow, and limits generalization.
- Existing human activity datasets are often not designed for robot learning, lacking manipulation-relevant annotations or extensibility.
- Understanding and effectively addressing the human-to-robot embodiment gap and scaling behaviors for transfer remains an open research challenge.

## Method

- Introduces **EgoVerse**, a large-scale, continuously growing ==egocentric human dataset== comprising ==EgoVerse-A== (standardized academic data) and ==EgoVerse-I== (diverse industry data) with manipulation-relevant annotations.
- Develops **EgoDB**, a scalable ==cloud-based data management system== supporting continuous ingestion, processing, and access of heterogeneous human and robot data.
- Conducts a consortium-scale, multi-lab, multi-robot experimental study using a ==transformer-based architecture== with a ==flow matching objective==.

## Results

- The current **EgoVerse** release contains **1,362** hours (**80,000** episodes) of human demonstrations, covering **1,965** tasks, **240** scenes, and involving **2,087** unique demonstrators.
- Co-training robot policies with EgoVerse-A human data resulted in up to **30%** relative performance improvements in ID and OOD settings across various robots and tasks.
- Controlled experiments showed that increasing demonstrator diversity improved generalization to unseen demonstrators, while increasing scene diversity consistently enhanced generalization to unseen scenes.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Co-training robot policies with egocentric human data consistently improves robot manipulation performance and generalization in both in-domain and out-of-domain settings across different robots and tasks.
> - The effectiveness of scaling with diverse human data relies on the presence of a small amount of 'domain-aligned' human data, which acts as an anchor for transfer.
> - Both demonstrator and scene diversity contribute to generalization, with scene diversity playing a particularly important role in improving performance on unseen environments, especially under limited data budgets.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robot learning is increasingly reliant on large and diverse datasets to achieve generalization, a trend observed in successful vision and language models. However, robot data collection presents a significant bottleneck. It typically requires specialized physical hardware, expert teleoperation, and controlled environments, making it expensive, slow, and difficult to scale in terms of both volume and diversity.

Egocentric human data has emerged as a promising alternative. Humans routinely perform a wide array of manipulation and loco-manipulation tasks in diverse, everyday environments, generating a rich and abundant source of behavioral data. This data offers a unifying abstraction for the robot learning community, allowing researchers to focus on curating real-world experience without immediate constraints of a specific robot embodiment. Recent advancements in wearable sensors and large-scale data capture systems have further enabled the collection of such human data.

Despite its potential, several challenges persist in leveraging human data for robotics. A primary issue is the open research problem of effective human-robot transfer, which involves addressing the embodiment gap and understanding scaling behavior. Furthermore, most existing human datasets are typically static, one-off releases tailored for specific studies, which limits their extensibility and cumulative growth. While many large-scale human activity datasets exist (e.g., Ego4D, Epic-Kitchens), they are generally not designed for robot learning, often lacking manipulation-relevant annotations or containing tasks beyond current robot capabilities. More recent datasets have introduced manipulation-relevant sensing but remain static and lack systematic evaluation for robot learning transfer.

This work addresses these limitations by introducing a collaborative framework and a continuously growing dataset. It aims to establish a foundational resource that can evolve with new contributions and systematically investigate the factors influencing human-to-robot transfer, thereby contributing to the development of more generalizable robot policies.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind EgoVerse is to overcome the data scalability bottleneck in robot learning by systematically leveraging egocentric human data. The research identifies two major challenges: the difficulty of scaling existing human datasets and the unresolved questions surrounding effective human-to-robot transfer. To address these, the paper sets forth three key objectives:

1. **Develop a Large-Scale, Robot-Learning-Oriented Human Dataset**: The objective is to create EgoVerse, a comprehensive dataset of egocentric human demonstrations specifically designed for robot learning. This dataset is envisioned as a continuous and evolving resource, not a static release. It is structured into two complementary components:
 - **EgoVerse-A**: Collected under standardized, controlled protocols across academic labs to enable reproducible studies and systematic analysis of factors affecting transfer.
 - **EgoVerse-I**: Sourced from industry partners, focusing on maximizing scale, diversity, and richness of real-world annotations. This component aims to facilitate growth beyond academic constraints and align with practical deployment interests.
 The dataset aims to provide standardized formats and manipulation-relevant annotations, such as 3D hand and head poses and subtask descriptions, across a diverse range of tasks, scenes, and demonstrators.

2. **Establish a Collaborative Data Ecosystem**: A key objective is to build EgoVerse as a collaborative platform that supports ongoing contributions from individual researchers, academic labs, and industry partners. This involves developing EgoDB, a scalable, cloud-based data management and access system capable of continuous data ingestion and processing from diverse sources. The goal is to unify data into shared formats, provide tools for downstream learning, and lower the barrier to participation (e.g., through a phone-based data collection pipeline). This framework is designed to enable the concept of a "living dataset" that evolves over time.

3. **Conduct a Comprehensive Study on Human-to-Robot Transfer**: The research aims to conduct a large-scale, systematic study to identify the critical factors that enable effective cross-embodiment transfer from diverse human data to robot manipulation policies. This study is designed for reproducibility, with experiments replicated across multiple independent labs, tasks, and distinct robot embodiments using shared protocols and evaluation criteria. The objective is to derive conclusions that are robust across different hardware and sensing configurations and to understand how embodiment-specific differences influence transfer.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed in this research encompasses the design and collection of the EgoVerse dataset, the development of the EgoDB data management system, and the execution of a consortium-scale experimental study on human-to-robot transfer.

#### 3.1. EgoVerse Dataset Collection and Annotation

The dataset consists of two primary components, EgoVerse-A and EgoVerse-I, supplemented by a community-accessible phone-based capture system.

- **EgoVerse-A (Academic)**:
 - **Hardware**: Primarily collected using Project Aria glasses (Gen 1), lightweight head-worn devices equipped with a wide-FoV RGB camera and synchronized monochrome scene cameras for SLAM and hand tracking.
 - **Protocol**: Data collection adhered to standardized protocols across participating academic labs, organized into "dataset units" (approx. 5 minutes/5-10 demonstrations per task). Quality constraints ensured hand and object visibility.
 - **Flagship Tasks**: Six manipulation tasks (e.g., `object-in-container`, `cup-on-saucer`, `bag-grocery`, `fold-clothes`) were defined to span various manipulation regimes (single-arm, bimanual, fine-grained, long-horizon) while remaining feasible for common robot platforms.
 - **Diversity**: Data was structured along task, scenario/object, and demonstrator axes. Scenes (8-12 per lab) and objects (up to 30 per task per lab) were varied, and 1-8 demonstrators per lab contributed to motion and viewpoint diversity.
 - **Controlled-Diversity Subset**: A specific subset for `cup-on-saucer` and `fold-clothes` involved 16 demonstrators and 16 scenes, allowing for independent scaling and study of demonstrator and scene diversity under controlled conditions.

- **EgoVerse-I (Industry)**:
 - **Hardware**: Data was aggregated from industry partners using custom wearable sensor platforms, typically head-mounted cameras (often stereo fisheye RGB) with synchronized inertial sensing.
 - **Scale and Diversity**: This component focuses on broad task diversity and scale, comprising nearly 1,400 hours across almost 2,000 tasks, 240 scenes, and over 2,000 demonstrators. Collection emphasized manipulation-heavy tasks, visible hands, and decisive actions.

- **Phone-based Capture System**:
 - **Hardware**: Utilizes commodity smartphones (e.g., iPhone) mounted on a head strap, recording egocentric RGB video via the ultrawide camera.
 - **Processing**: Captured videos are uploaded to a cloud pipeline that recovers 6-DoF head pose via visual tracking and estimates 3D hand poses (21 keypoints per hand).

- **Annotation**:
 - **Manipulation-Relevant Signals**: For all data, 3D hand pose (21 keypoints/hand) and 6-DoF head pose (from visual-inertial SLAM) are estimated per frame. These serve as proxies for human end-effector motion.
 - **Pose Estimation**: Project Aria's Machine Perception Service (MPS) is used for EgoVerse-A. Industry datasets combine partner SLAM, model-based pose estimation, and post-processing.
 - **Granularity**: EgoVerse-A uses lightweight per-episode metadata (task, scene, objects, demonstrator). EgoVerse-I provides denser annotations, including fine-grained (1-2s) language descriptions, active-hand indicators, and static/mobile manipulation flags.

#### 3.2. EgoDB: Scalable Data Management System

EgoDB is a cloud-based system (using S3-backed storage and a Postgres SQL database) designed for continuous ingestion, processing, and access of heterogeneous human and robot data.
- **Ingestion**: Raw data from distributed sources are uploaded, hashed, and metadata are registered in the SQL database.
- **Processing**: Nightly Ray processing daemons convert raw files (e.g., Aria .vrs, robot-specific formats) into a unified, training-ready format. This includes running MPS for Aria data, converting to processed formats, and generating visualization videos.
- **Access**: The SQL database enables structured queries over various metadata. A web-based interface allows browsing. For local training, users can synchronize filtered subsets via configuration files using `s5cmd`.
- **Temporal Alignment**: Action trajectories are post-processed to align human and robot execution speeds (1-second human window, 1.5-second robot window, both resampled to T=100 steps).

#### 3.3. Consortium-Scale Study on Human-to-Robot Transfer

- **Robot Platforms**: Experiments were conducted on three distinct robot systems across different labs (Fig. 6):
 - **Robot A**: Dual 6-DoF ARX5 arms with parallel jaw grippers, Project Aria glasses (egocentric), and two wrist-mounted Intel RealSense D405 cameras. Teleoperated via Meta Oculus 3 headset.
 - **Robot B**: Dual ARX5 arms side-mounted for human-like workspace, head-mounted Aria glass, and wrist-mounted Logitech webcams. Teleoperated via customized GELLO device.
 - **Robot C**: Unitree G1 robot with 7-DoF arms and 6-DoF Dexterous Inspire Hands, ZED 2 stereo camera (egocentric). Teleoperated via Apple Vision Pro.
- **Data Alignment**:
 - **Action Representation**: Robot actions were represented as base-frame SE(3) actions (x, y, z, orientation) with a gripper state, projected into the egocentric camera frame. Human actions were constructed as camera-centered stable reference frames by projecting future hand poses relative to the current device frame.
 - **Normalization**: Quantile normalization was applied to proprioception and action features to map them to [-1, 1], making it robust to outliers. Random image crop and color jittering were used during training to account for sensor variations.
- **Learning Architecture**:
 - A transformer-based encoder-decoder architecture was adopted for joint training across embodiments (Fig. 7).
 - **Encoder**: Image observations (egocentric RGB, robot wrist cameras) were processed by ResNet-18 backbones, and proprioceptive inputs by an MLP. These were tokenized via learned query attention into a shared space. A shared transformer encoder then processed these tokens.
 - **Decoder**: A multi-block transformer decoder, trained with a flow matching objective, predicted action sequences.
 - **Training Objective**: Behavior Cloning (BC) co-training loss, specifically a Conditional Flow Matching (CFM) loss, was applied to aggregate human and robot datasets (`L_BC-cotrain = L_robot_CFM + L_human_CFM`).
- **Evaluation Setup**:
 - **Tasks**: Four flagship tasks were used: `object-in-container`, `cup-on-saucer`, `bag-grocery`, and `fold-clothes`.
 - **Settings**: Both in-domain (ID, matching robot training data layouts) and out-of-domain (OOD, unseen objects/environments) settings were evaluated.
 - **Metrics**: 20 ID and 20 OOD rollouts per task with randomized initial conditions. Performance was measured using task-specific sub-task metrics (grasps, placements, full task completion), aggregated into a normalized score.
 - **Robot Data**: Approximately 150-300 demonstrations per task were collected per robot, with randomized object placements.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded several findings regarding the EgoVerse dataset's characteristics and its efficacy in scaling robot learning.

#### 4.1. Dataset Composition and Diversity

The current EgoVerse release comprises 1,362 hours of human demonstrations (80,000 episodes), covering 1,965 tasks, 240 scenes, and involving 2,087 unique demonstrators.
- **EgoVerse-A** (academic contributions) accounts for 75 hours (5.5%) across the six flagship tasks. It emphasizes controlled, standardized collection for systematic analysis.
- **EgoVerse-I** (industry contributions) constitutes the bulk, with nearly 1,400 hours (over 94%) across diverse open-ended tasks. A visual diversity case study using UMAP on DINOv3 embeddings for the `fold-clothes` task demonstrated that EgoVerse-I significantly expands visual coverage compared to single-lab robot data or EgoVerse-A, indicating its potential for learning policies that generalize beyond specific lab appearances.

#### 4.2. Impact of Human Data on Robot Performance

A large-scale study investigated the effect of human egocentric data on robot learning performance and generalization.

- **Co-training Improves Transfer**: Co-training robot policies with EgoVerse-A human data consistently led to performance improvements in both in-domain (ID) and out-of-domain (OOD) settings. These improvements were observed across various robots and tasks, with relative gains of up to 30%. This marked the first validation of this effect in a standardized, multi-lab, multi-robot experimental setup. An exception was noted for Robot B on the `bag-grocery` task, where performance decreased. This was hypothesized to stem from a significant strategy mismatch between human demonstrations and Robot B's specific embodiment limitations, forcing robot demonstrations to deviate from human strategies.

- **Domain-Aligned Data Enables Scaling**: The effectiveness of scaling with diverse human data was found to be critically dependent on the availability of "aligned" human-robot data, where human and robot demonstrations share task semantics and scene context.
 - Neither 8 hours of diverse EgoVerse-A data nor 2 hours of domain-aligned human data alone produced substantial performance gains.
 - However, when a small amount (e.g., 2 hours) of domain-aligned human data was included, it acted as an "anchor" for the learning process. This enabled policies to effectively transfer knowledge from larger volumes of diverse EgoVerse-A data (e.g., 2 hours to 8 hours), leading to positive scaling behavior.

#### 4.3. Influence of Human Data Diversity on Generalization

Controlled studies using a specific EgoVerse-A subset examined the isolated effects of scene and demonstrator diversity on generalization, primarily using the offline Avg-MSE metric for human open-loop action prediction.

- **Demonstrator Diversity Scaling**:
 - Increasing the number of demonstrators in a single scene (under a fixed 2-hour budget) consistently improved generalization to unseen demonstrators in the same scene.
 - Similarly, increasing demonstrator diversity across eight fixed scenes (under a fixed 8-hour budget) also improved generalization to unseen demonstrators within those environments.
 - UMAP visualizations of encoded features showed increased overlap between training and validation demonstrators as demonstrator diversity grew, suggesting better coverage of the state-action distribution.

- **Scene Diversity Scaling**: Increasing the number of training scenes (from 1 to 16, with a fixed demonstrator pool) consistently improved generalization to unseen scenes, regardless of the data budget. This effect was particularly pronounced under limited data budgets, indicating that environmental diversity plays a significant role in generalization, especially when data quantity is constrained. Beyond a certain data scale, expanding scene coverage provided more substantial gains than merely increasing data density within existing scenes.

- **Mixed Diversity Scaling**: Jointly scaling scene and demonstrator diversity (e.g., varying from 4 to 8 scenes and 4 to 8 demonstrators under a fixed 4-hour budget) showed complementary improvements. Increasing scene diversity consistently improved generalization under both demonstrator budgets. The marginal benefit of additional demonstrators was observed to decrease as scene coverage expanded for some tasks. For `fold-clothes`, more demonstrators consistently helped, while for `cup-on-saucer`, 4 demonstrators sometimes slightly outperformed 8, suggesting task-dependent impacts of demonstrator-induced behavioral noise.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The EgoVerse project presents a multi-faceted contribution with implications for the scalability and generalization capabilities of robot learning systems.

**Addressing the Data Bottleneck**: The primary significance lies in providing a scalable and sustainable approach to collecting diverse, manipulation-relevant data for robot learning. By moving beyond robot-exclusive data collection, EgoVerse helps alleviate a fundamental constraint that has historically limited the progress of data-driven robotics, similar to the role of large datasets in vision and language models.

**Foundation for Continuous Research and Collaboration**: EgoVerse is designed as a "living dataset" and a collaborative platform (EgoDB), rather than a static release. This framework is intended to foster ongoing contributions from academic institutions, industry partners, and individual researchers globally. This collaborative model has the potential to accelerate research by providing a continuously growing, standardized resource that can evolve with the community's needs and technological advancements.

**Systematic Understanding of Human-to-Robot Transfer**: The consortium-scale study is significant for its systematic and reproducible examination of human-to-robot transfer. By replicating experiments across multiple distinct robot embodiments, labs, and tasks under shared protocols, the research provides robust empirical evidence and insights that extend beyond single-system validations.
- **Empirical Validation**: It empirically validates that co-training robot policies with human egocentric data leads to reliable performance improvements and enhanced generalization for robot manipulation.
- **Key Transfer Mechanisms**: The identification of "domain-aligned" human data as a critical anchor for effectively leveraging larger, diverse human datasets offers a crucial guiding principle for data collection strategies and curriculum learning in human-to-robot transfer.
- **Diversity Insights**: The detailed analysis of demonstrator and scene diversity highlights their distinct and complementary roles in promoting generalization. The finding that scene diversity plays a dominant role in generalization to novel environments, particularly under limited data budgets, informs how data collection efforts should be prioritized and structured.

**Democratization of Data Collection**: The introduction of a phone-based data capture system significantly lowers the barrier to participation in data collection. This initiative broadens accessibility for researchers and enthusiasts with limited resources, potentially leading to an even greater diversity of data sources and collection environments, further enriching the EgoVerse ecosystem.

**Enabling Generalist Robot Policies**: By providing an unprecedented scale and diversity of manipulation-relevant human data, combined with a framework for systematic evaluation, EgoVerse lays a foundation for the development of more generalizable robot policies. This work supports the trajectory towards creating robots capable of operating effectively in varied, unstructured real-world environments and contributes to the ongoing development of foundation models for robotics.
