# AdaReP:Adaptive Re-Planning under Model Mismatch for Neural World-Model Predictive Control

> [!summary] Summary
> AdaReP introduces an adaptive re-planning framework for neural world-model predictive control, dynamically adjusting the replanning frequency based on online observable signals. The method significantly reduces planner-side computation, achieving over 80% fewer world-model queries in real-world robotic tasks, while maintaining task performance comparable to continuous replanning.

## Problem

- Neural world-model predictive control systems commonly replan at every environment step to mitigate prediction error accumulation, leading to substantial computational overhead.
- The increasing size and complexity of modern neural world models make frequent replanning computationally expensive, hindering their deployment in real-time or resource-constrained environments.
- Optimal replanning frequency is not static, varying across tasks, control backbones, and even different phases of a single task, rendering fixed schedules suboptimal.

## Method

- **AdaReP** proposes a training-free, model-agnostic wrapper that dynamically adjusts the ==replanning frequency== for existing neural world-model MPC systems.
- It leverages two online observable signals: the current deviation between actual and predicted representations, and a finite-difference estimate of the local dynamics sensitivity.
- These signals are used to set a time-varying reuse tolerance via an exponential decay rule, triggering replanning only when the observed deviation exceeds this adaptive threshold.

## Results

- AdaReP achieved significant reductions in planner-side computation (Number of Function Evaluations) in simulations, ranging from **34.9%** to **59.2%** across various image-space and latent-space world models.
- In real-world robotic tasks using a Franka Emika Panda arm, AdaReP reduced world-model queries by over **80%** while maintaining comparable or slightly improved task success rates.
- The framework demonstrated adaptive behavior, dynamically increasing replanning frequency during phases of higher prediction discrepancy or greater dynamics sensitivity within a task, ensuring robust performance under varying conditions.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Theoretical analysis demonstrates that dynamic regret in predictive control under model mismatch scales with reuse length, accumulated prediction mismatch, and an exponentially growing multi-step sensitivity factor.
> - An adaptive replanning strategy, sensitive to both execution deviation and local dynamics sensitivity, can effectively balance computational cost and control quality.
> - Monitoring real-time signals provides a robust, online mechanism to infer the local dynamics' sensitivity and prediction accuracy, enabling dynamic adjustment of replanning cadence.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work operates within the field of robotic control, specifically focusing on systems that employ neural world models for prediction and Model Predictive Control (MPC) for action planning. Neural world models, which learn to predict future states in various representations (image space, latent space, learned state spaces), have demonstrated efficacy in complex control tasks. However, a common practice in these systems is to replan at every environment step to mitigate the accumulation of prediction errors. This frequent replanning strategy, while bounding error, introduces substantial computational overhead, especially with the increasing size and complexity of modern neural world models.

Previous research in this domain has largely concentrated on improving the predictive capabilities of the world model backbone, enhancing representation learning, or refining the planning objective. This paper, in contrast, addresses the *computational efficiency* aspect of deploying such controllers. It diverges from approaches that modify the learned world model, retrain policies, or redesign MPC solvers. Instead, it focuses on the *execution-time policy* that determines *when* replanning is necessary, aiming to reduce the frequency of computationally expensive world model queries without significantly compromising control performance. This places the work at the intersection of online control theory, regret analysis, and practical neurorobotics, specifically targeting the trade-off between computational cost and control quality in learned predictive control systems. It builds upon insights from event-triggered and self-triggered control, as well as perturbation-based regret analyses in predictive control under model mismatch.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an adaptive mechanism for determining the optimal replanning frequency in neural world-model predictive control, thereby reducing computational overhead while maintaining task performance.

The motivation for this objective stems from several key observations:

1. **Computational Overhead of Frequent Replanning:** Neural world models, especially large-scale ones, require numerous queries for each MPC replanning call. Standard MPC approaches replan at every environment step, which leads to substantial computational costs. This overhead is a critical barrier to deploying these advanced control systems in real-time or resource-constrained environments.
2. **Trade-off between Computation and Performance:** While frequent replanning helps bound accumulated prediction error and maintains control quality, reusing a cached plan can save computation. However, the effectiveness of plan reuse depends on how prediction mismatch propagates through the local dynamics. Continued reuse risks performance degradation if execution deviates significantly from the predicted rollout or if the system's local dynamics are highly sensitive.
3. **Variability of Optimal Replanning Cadence:** The paper hypothesizes that a single fixed replanning schedule (e.g., replanning every 'm' steps or when deviation exceeds a fixed 'ϵ' threshold) is often suboptimal. The ideal replanning frequency is likely to vary not only across different tasks and control backbones but also within different phases of the same task, depending on the prevailing prediction accuracy and dynamic sensitivity. Fixed schedules fail to adapt to these varying conditions, leading to either excessive computation or unnecessary performance degradation.
4. **Desire for a Training-Free and Model-Agnostic Solution:** The authors aim to create a solution that can be applied as a "wrapper" around existing neural world models and MPC planners without requiring modifications to their internal structure or retraining. This increases the practical applicability and ease of integration of the proposed method into diverse existing systems.

To address these motivations, the research seeks to:
- Analyze the relationship between replanning frequency, prediction mismatch, local dynamics sensitivity, and control performance using a perturbation-based dynamic-regret framework.
- Identify the key quantities that govern the penalties incurred by reusing a stale plan.
- Propose an adaptive replanning rule that leverages online, observable signals to dynamically adjust the reuse tolerance.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology adopted in this paper involves a theoretical analysis of replanning under model mismatch, followed by the design and empirical evaluation of an adaptive replanning algorithm.

#### 3.1. Problem Setup and Baselines

The problem is formulated as a finite-horizon control task with true dynamics $x_{t+1} = g_t(x_t, u_t)$ and a cumulative cost function. The controller uses a learned predictive model $\hat{g}_t$ and an MPC solver over a horizon $k$ to generate a planned rollout $(y_{t:t+k}, v_{t:t+k-1})$. The core concept is that planning and execution operate on a monitored representation $z_t = \phi(x_t)$, where $\phi$ can be an identity mapping for state-space control, an image-feature embedding for image-space planning, or a latent state for latent-space control. The key monitored quantity for triggering replanning is the discrepancy $d_t = \|z_t - \hat{z}_t\|_2$, where $\hat{z}_t = \phi(\hat{y}_t)$ is the predicted representation from the cached rollout.

The proposed adaptive method, AdaReP, is compared against three baselines:
- **MPC$_{1k}$ (Step-wise Replanning):** The standard approach where the controller replans at every environment step.
- **MPC$_{mk}$ (Fixed-step Reuse):** Replans every $m$ steps, executing a cached plan suffix in between refreshes.
- **MPC$_{k,\epsilon}$ (Fixed-threshold Reuse):** Replans whenever the discrepancy $d_t$ exceeds a constant tolerance $\epsilon$ or the cached plan expires.

#### 3.2. Theoretical Analysis: Regret Structure under Model Mismatch

The authors employ a perturbation-based dynamic-regret framework, adapting existing analyses of predictive control under model mismatch. This analysis aims to quantify the "stale-plan penalties" incurred when a cached plan is reused. Key findings from this theoretical derivation include:
- **Fixed-step Reuse (MPC$_{mk}$):** The regret penalty scales with the reuse length $m$, the accumulated prediction mismatch, and a multi-step sensitivity factor $L^{(m)}$, which can grow exponentially with $m$. This highlights how mismatch accumulates and is amplified.
- **Fixed-threshold Reuse (MPC$_{k,\epsilon}$):** The regret includes terms proportional to $\epsilon L^2 E + \epsilon^2 L^2 T$, where $E$ is the total cumulative prediction error and $L$ is the worst-case single-step dynamics sensitivity. This indicates that the chosen threshold $\epsilon$ interacts with both the accumulated mismatch and the dynamics' sensitivity. The analysis suggests that a constant $\epsilon$ is brittle because mismatch and sensitivity can vary over time. The local dynamics sensitivity $L_t$ is defined as the Lipschitz constant of the true dynamics, quantifying how strongly perturbations in state or action are amplified.

#### 3.3. AdaReP: Adaptive Replanning Algorithm

Motivated by the theoretical insights, AdaReP is designed as a training-free wrapper around existing MPC controllers (Algorithm 1). It dynamically adjusts the replanning tolerance online based on two real-time, observable signals:
1. **Current Deviation ($d_{t+1}$):** The Euclidean distance between the actual observed representation $z_{t+1}$ and the cached predicted representation $\hat{z}_{t+1}$. This directly measures how far the execution has drifted from the cached rollout.
2. **Local Sensitivity Estimate ($\hat{L}_t$):** A finite-difference proxy for local dynamics sensitivity, calculated as $\hat{L}_t = \frac{\|z_{t+1} - z_t\|_2}{\|u_t\|_2 + \gamma}$, where $\gamma$ is a small stabilizer. This estimate quantifies how strongly one-step state changes are induced by the executed action, serving as an online proxy for the amplification factor $L_t$.

These two signals are then used to set a time-varying reuse tolerance $\epsilon_{t+1}$ through an exponential decay rule:
$\epsilon_{t+1} = \epsilon_0 \exp(-\alpha_\delta \bar{d}_{t+1}) \exp(-\alpha_L \bar{L}_t)$
Here, $\epsilon_0$ is a base threshold, $\alpha_\delta$ and $\alpha_L$ are weights, and $\bar{d}_{t+1}$, $\bar{L}_t$ are sliding-window averages of the raw deviation and sensitivity estimates for numerical stability. The exponential form is justified by the theoretical analysis, as it provides a way to bound the sensitivity-amplified regret terms $L_t^2 \tilde{\epsilon}_t^2$ and $L_t^2 \tilde{\epsilon}_t s_t$ by constants controlled by ($\epsilon_0, \alpha_\delta, \alpha_L$). AdaReP then replans only when $d_{t+1} > \epsilon_{t+1}$ or the cached plan reaches its end.

#### 3.4. Theoretical Guarantees for AdaReP

The paper provides a theoretical guarantee (Theorem 2) showing that AdaReP's dynamic regret is bounded, with explicit dependence on the parameters ($\epsilon_0, \alpha_\delta, \alpha_L$). Specifically, larger $\alpha_L$ more strongly suppresses the quadratic sensitivity term, while larger $\alpha_\delta$ more strongly suppresses the mixed mismatch term. This formalizes how the adaptive threshold helps manage the regret contributions.

#### 3.5. Experimental Evaluation

AdaReP is evaluated across three distinct domains:
1. **Simulation with Image-space World Models:** RoboDesk manipulation environment using VP2 benchmark with SVG and Struct-VRNN world models.
2. **Simulation with Latent-space World Models:** DeepMind Control Suite with TD-MPC2, a state-of-the-art model-based RL agent.
3. **Physical Real-world Deployment:** A Franka Emika Panda arm performing door opening and T-block rearrangement tasks, using a state-based world model.

The evaluation focuses on measuring planner-side computation (Number of Function Evaluations, NFE) and task performance (success rates, normalized scores). Hyperparameters for all methods are tuned to ensure comparable task performance to the full-frequency MPC$_{1k}$ baseline.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of AdaReP across various domains consistently demonstrates its ability to substantially reduce planner-side computation while maintaining task performance comparable to continuous replanning.

#### 4.1. Quantitative Performance Across Domains

- **VP2/RoboDesk (Image-space World Models):** Under a matched-performance protocol (where success rates drop no more than 0.02 compared to MPC$_{1k}$), AdaReP achieved the lowest NFE.
 - With the **SVG** backbone, NFE was reduced by 59.2%, corresponding to a 57.8% reduction in wall-clock time.
 - With the **Struct-VRNN** backbone, NFE was reduced by 34.9%, with a 31.5% reduction in wall-clock time.
 These results indicate significant computational savings in vision-based simulation tasks.

- **DeepMind Control Suite (Latent-space World Models with TD-MPC2):** Across 30 continuous control tasks, AdaReP reduced average NFE by 54.5% and wall-clock time by 50.3% while closely matching the mean normalized score of step-wise replanning. This demonstrates its effectiveness in latent-space planning for a broader range of control problems.

- **Real-world Franka Manipulation:** On physical hardware tasks (door opening and T-block rearrangement), AdaReP achieved a success rate of 36/50 trials compared to 34/50 for step-wise replanning, while cutting planner-side world-model queries by over 80%. The higher NFE gains in the real robot experiments compared to simulation are attributed to the more explicit nature of state-based world models versus vision-based ones.

#### 4.2. Analysis and Discussion of Adaptive Behavior

- **Adaptation to Prediction Accuracy:** Experiments with controlled Gaussian noise added to state components in a disturbed simulator showed that as prediction discrepancy ($d_t$) increased with noise, AdaReP's sensitivity estimate ($\hat{L}_t$) adapted accordingly, leading to more frequent replanning. This mechanism ensures that AdaReP gracefully falls back towards step-wise replanning in conditions of high mismatch, maintaining success rates across all disturbance levels without degrading below the baseline.

- **Adaptation to Intra-trajectory Dynamics Sensitivity:** The behavior of AdaReP was observed to dynamically adjust replanning frequency based on varying dynamics sensitivity within a single episode. In a door-opening task, AdaReP shortened reuse intervals near the hinge (where dynamics are highly sensitive, and small end-effector motions cause large angular changes) and extended reuse in smoother phases farther from the hinge. This demonstrates the algorithm's ability to adapt its replanning cadence to the local dynamics, a capability that fixed-cadence methods lack. This observation aligns with the theoretical prediction that replanning decisions should depend on local sensitivity.

- **Robustness to Worst-case Conditions:** AdaReP maintained its success rate even under significant state disturbances, automatically reducing its reuse tolerance and increasing replanning frequency. This graceful degradation prevents performance drops in challenging conditions, although at the cost of reduced NFE savings.

- **Impact of Robust Visual Features:** In image-space tasks, corrupting images with Gaussian noise or blur reduced AdaReP's NFE advantage. This occurred because visual corruption degraded the DINO-based feature signal, causing the discrepancy monitor to trigger unnecessary replanning. This finding highlights the importance of robust feature extractors for maximizing AdaReP's benefits in noisy vision-based settings.

- **Robustness to Smoothing Window:** A sensitivity analysis on the smoothing window parameter $W$ indicated that AdaReP performs stably across a relatively wide range of values ($W \in$). Small $W$ values (1–5) led to erratic replanning, while large values (>15) made the system sluggish.

Overall, the findings confirm that fixed reuse schedules are difficult to tune universally, as the preferred replanning cadence varies considerably across tasks, backbones, and even different stages of a single trajectory. AdaReP's adaptive approach addresses this variability effectively.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The AdaReP framework represents a significant contribution to the field of neural world-model predictive control, particularly in addressing the practical challenge of computational efficiency.

### 6. Significance

1. **Bridging Theory and Practice:** The work provides a theoretical foundation by deriving dynamic-regret bounds that explicitly link replanning penalties to reuse tolerance, accumulated model mismatch, and local dynamics sensitivity. This analysis directly informs the design of the adaptive rule, making AdaReP's practical efficacy grounded in formal understanding.
2. **Computational Efficiency for Advanced Control:** AdaReP offers a substantial reduction in planner-side computational overhead (up to over 80% in real-world settings) without sacrificing task performance. This efficiency gain is crucial for the deployment of large, complex neural world models in real-time robotic systems, where computational resources are often limited.
3. **Training-Free and Model-Agnostic Approach:** As a training-free wrapper, AdaReP can be readily integrated into existing neural world-model MPC systems across various modalities (image-space, latent-space, state-space) and backbones (SVG, Struct-VRNN, TD-MPC2). This broad applicability lowers the barrier to adoption and leverages current advancements in world modeling without requiring their redesign.
4. **Adaptive and Robust Performance:** The adaptive nature of AdaReP allows it to dynamically adjust replanning frequency based on online signals, such as execution deviation and local dynamics sensitivity. This makes the controller more robust to varying task conditions, environmental uncertainties, and phases within a single trajectory, gracefully degrading performance (by increasing replanning) rather than failing catastrophically under high mismatch or sensitivity.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Enabling Real-World Deployment:** By mitigating the computational burden, AdaReP can accelerate the transition of computationally intensive neural world-model controllers from simulation to real-world robotic applications, fostering more agile and responsive autonomous systems.
- **Scalability of Large Models:** As world models grow in complexity and parameter count, efficient deployment strategies become critical. AdaReP provides a mechanism to scale these models to scenarios where frequent, full-horizon replanning is infeasible.
- **New Design Paradigm for Control Systems:** This work highlights replanning cadence as a dynamic control variable rather than a static parameter. This paradigm shift could inspire further research into other adaptive, online strategies for managing computational resources in complex AI systems.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The identified challenge of robust visual feature extractors under corruption opens a clear avenue for future work aimed at improving the reliability of discrepancy monitoring in noisy, vision-based environments, further enhancing the benefits of adaptive replanning.
