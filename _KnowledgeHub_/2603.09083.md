# Provably Safe Trajectory Generation for Manipulators Under Motion and Environmental Uncertainties

> [!summary] Summary
> A framework for robot manipulators generates provably safe and efficient trajectories in uncertain, non-convex environments by combining a learned deep stochastic Koopman operator model with hierarchical, formally verifiable collision risk checks. This approach achieved a 94% success rate in simulation and 90% in real-world human-robot collaboration tasks, maintaining collision probability below a user-defined threshold while improving navigation efficiency.

## Problem

- Existing motion planning methods for uncertain robots often rely on simplifying assumptions like Gaussian uncertainty or convex obstacles, limiting their real-world applicability.
- Many approaches struggle to simultaneously address both robot motion inaccuracies and uncertainties in complex, non-convex environments for high-dimensional manipulators.
- Achieving formal, provable collision risk guarantees for real-time applications with complex robots and environments remains computationally challenging.

## Method

- Develops a Rigid Manipulator Deep Stochastic ==Koopman== Operator (RM-DeSKO) model to learn and robustly predict robot dynamics and state distributions under motion uncertainty.
- Integrates a hierarchical collision risk verification into a Model Predictive Path Integral (MPPI) controller, using parallelized physics simulation for initial cost and Sum-of-Squares (SOS) programming for formal safety certification.
- Approximates robot links as ellipsoids and defines risk contours for uncertain, non-convex obstacles, allowing provable collision probability bounds even with non-Gaussian environmental uncertainties.

## Results

- The framework achieved a **94%** success rate in complex simulation environments with uncertain, non-convex obstacles, significantly improving over baseline methods and yielding more efficient trajectories.
- Successfully demonstrated zero-shot sim-to-real transfer on a UR5e robot, maintaining a **90%** success rate in human-robot collaboration tasks across various noise levels.
- The RM-DeSKO model provided the lowest maximum prediction error among tested baselines, enabling stable and effective stochastic optimization for navigating cluttered and uncertain spaces.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A data-driven Deep Stochastic Koopman Operator can effectively learn and robustly predict high-dimensional robot dynamics, including uncertainty, crucial for sampling-based control.
> - Hierarchical safety verification, using rapid approximate checks (physics simulation) followed by formal guarantees (SOS programming), enables real-time, provably safe motion planning for complex systems.
> - Formal collision risk bounds can be established for non-Gaussian uncertainties and non-convex obstacles by representing robot parts as ellipsoids and utilizing moment-based Sum-of-Squares programming.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotics is increasingly focused on deploying manipulators in complex, uncertain, and human-inhabited environments. A long-standing challenge is the generation of safe and optimal motion plans for high-dimensional robots, particularly when faced with both the robot's own motion inaccuracies and uncertainties in the environment.

Traditional approaches to motion planning under uncertainty often rely on simplifying assumptions. Many methods assume Gaussian uncertainty distributions and convex obstacles, which limits their applicability in real-world scenarios where uncertainties can be non-Gaussian and environments feature complex, non-convex geometries. While techniques exist to convexify non-convex obstacles, this can negatively impact the feasibility of generated solutions, especially in cluttered spaces. Additionally, much of the prior work tends to focus on either obstacle uncertainty or robot state uncertainty, with fewer studies addressing both simultaneously, particularly for high-dimensional manipulators. Recent efforts have begun to tackle non-Gaussian uncertainties and non-convex obstacles, but these often involve computationally intensive collision risk checks or are limited to mobile robots approximated as single convex bodies. Learning-based methods have emerged to generate risk-bounded paths, but often require extensive expert data and primarily produce geometric paths rather than kinodynamic solutions.

Model Predictive Path Integral (MPPI) control is a sampling-based stochastic algorithm demonstrating efficacy for motion planning of high-dimensional robot arms. However, its performance can degrade significantly when substantial tracking errors are present in joint space. While some extensions address system dynamics with neural networks or propagate uncertainty, they frequently focus on deterministic systems with noisy inputs or are restricted to mobile robots in deterministic environments.

This paper addresses these gaps by proposing a framework that specifically targets risk-bounded trajectory generation for uncertain robot manipulators operating in uncertain, non-convex environments. It moves beyond simplified assumptions, providing a method for formally certified collision risk guarantees under general uncertainty distributions and complex geometries, while aiming for computational efficiency suitable for real-time applications like human-robot collaboration. The work integrates a learned, robust predictive model with a hierarchical, formally verifiable safety mechanism within a sampling-based control paradigm, thereby contributing a more comprehensive and practical solution to the problem.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a novel risk-bounded motion planning framework that can generate provably safe and efficient trajectories for robot manipulators. This framework is specifically designed to operate effectively in environments characterized by significant motion uncertainty in the robot's dynamics and environmental uncertainty concerning the geometry, size, and location of non-convex obstacles.

The key motivations driving this work are:

- **Addressing the Limitations of Existing Methods:** Current motion planning solutions for uncertain robots often struggle with a combination of factors. They may impose overly conservative safety margins, which compromises operational efficiency, or they may lack formal guarantees regarding collision risk, making them unsuitable for safety-critical applications. Furthermore, many methods make simplifying assumptions, such as Gaussian uncertainty or convex obstacles, which do not reflect the complexities of real-world scenarios.
- **Enabling Safe Human-Robot Collaboration (HRC):** As robot manipulators are increasingly deployed in shared workspaces with humans (e.g., for assembly, packaging), ensuring their safety is paramount. Imprecise robot motions and dynamic, cluttered environments necessitate robust safety mechanisms that can mathematically guarantee low collision probabilities without sacrificing task efficiency. The paper explicitly highlights the need for a solution that allows efficient motion while keeping the robot's collision probability below a user-defined threshold.
- **Improving Predictive Accuracy for Stochastic Systems:** The success of sampling-based control algorithms like MPPI hinges on accurate forward prediction of robot states. However, real robots exhibit tracking errors and are subject to unknown time-varying process noise. Developing a robust dynamic model that can reliably predict the robot's state distribution under such uncertainties is crucial for generating effective control policies.
- **Achieving Efficient and Formally Certified Safety:** Providing formal certification of collision risk for complex, high-dimensional manipulators in uncertain, non-convex environments is computationally challenging. The objective is to achieve this certification efficiently, avoiding the prohibitive computational costs of directly applying formal methods like Sum-of-Squares (SOS) programming to every sampled trajectory in a receding-horizon control loop. This necessitates a hierarchical approach that combines fast, approximate checks with rigorous, formal verification.
- **Facilitating Sim-to-Real Transfer:** Developing models and control strategies in simulation and successfully transferring them to real robotic systems is a significant challenge. A motivated objective is to create a learning-based predictive model that can generalize well from simulation data to real-world deployment, reducing the need for extensive real-world data collection.

In summary, the authors are motivated to develop a comprehensive, robust, and computationally efficient motion planning framework that offers mathematical guarantees for collision safety in complex, uncertain environments, thereby enabling safer and more effective deployment of robotic manipulators, particularly in human-robot collaboration settings.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology integrates a deep stochastic Koopman operator model for robust state prediction with a hierarchical collision risk verification method, embedded within a Model Predictive Path Integral (MPPI) controller. The overall framework aims to generate risk-bounded trajectories for manipulators in uncertain, non-convex environments.

**A. Overview of the Receding-Horizon Framework:**
The system operates in a receding-horizon manner, continuously replanning actions. At each time step, it samples potential control sequences, predicts future states, assesses collision risks, optimizes its policy, and executes the first optimal control action. This loop is closed with noisy state feedback.

**B. Rigid Manipulator Deep Stochastic Koopman Operator (RM-DeSKO) Model:**
To address the challenges of inaccurate forward rollouts in MPPI due to unknown motion uncertainties, the authors develop a data-driven RM-DeSKO model. This neural network model learns the dynamics of rigid manipulators under tracking errors, providing robust predictions of batched state-control pairs at low computational cost.
1. **Observable Encoding:** For a batch of *N* current noised states `x_t`, an encoder (parameterized by `θ1` and `θ2`) lifts these states to a lifted linear space, representing them as probabilistic distributions of observables `ψ_t`. Each observable is assumed to follow a Gaussian distribution, with `ψ_t = λ_θ1(x_t) + εσ_θ2(x_t)`, where `ε` is a Gaussian noise term.
2. **Koopman Propagation:** In this lifted space, a linear Koopman operator propagates the inferred distribution forward in time. This is achieved through linear transformations `ψ_{t+k} = Aψ_{t+k-1} + Bu_{t+k-1}`, where `A` is the Koopman matrix and `B` is the control matrix, both represented by neural networks of affine linear transformations. This linear dynamics in the lifted space simplifies prediction over the planning horizon `H`.
3. **State Decoding:** A decoder (parameterized by `θ3`) converts the propagated observables `ψ_{t+k}` back to the predicted states `x_{t+k}` in the original state space: `x_{t+k} = C_θ3 ψ_{t+k}`.
4. **Training:** The RM-DeSKO model is trained to minimize the `H`-step mean squared error (MSE) loss, with an additional constraint on the entropy of `ψ` to regularize the latent space distributions.

**C. Model Predictive Path Integral (MPPI) Control:**
The RM-DeSKO model is integrated into an MPPI controller, a sampling-based stochastic control algorithm.
1. **Control Sampling:** At each iteration, `N` control sequences of length `H` are sampled. B-splines are used to ensure smoothness and improve exploration of the action space.
2. **State Rollout and Cost Computation:** The sampled control sequences `U_N,H` are fed into the RM-DeSKO model to predict `N` batches of future states `X_N,H` over the horizon `H`. For each rolled-out state, an associated cost `c(x, u)` is computed. The cost function is a weighted combination of terms (e.g., robot-to-goal position, orientation, collision). The overall cost for a sequence `C_n(x_t, u_t)` is discounted over the horizon.
3. **Policy Update:** The MPPI controller updates its policy parameters (mean `μ` and covariance `Σ`) using a sample-based gradient of weighted sampled control sequences. Importance weights `w_i` are derived from the computed costs.
4. **Execution:** The first control input from the mean sequence `u*` is executed. The policy is then shifted forward to warm-start the next optimization cycle.

**D. Hierarchical Collision Risk Verification:**
This is a core component ensuring formal safety guarantees efficiently.
1. **Problem Formulation:** The environment includes static but uncertain 3D obstacles `O_i` described by polynomial representations, with random variables `ω_i` for their geometry, size, or location (not necessarily Gaussian). Risk contours `C_Δr` define the acceptable safe subspace where the probability of collision with any obstacle is below a user-defined tolerance `Δ`. The robot's rigid links `L(q_t)` are approximated by a union of ellipsoids `E_j(q_t)`. The goal is to ensure `Prob(p_t ∈ O_i | ∀p_t ∈ L(q_t)) ≤ Δ`.
2. **Initial Cost Calculation (IsaacGym):** Due to the computational expense of formal methods for all `N × H` rollouts, collision costs `c_col = α * P F_contact` for the MPPI controller's initial cost computation are quickly obtained using contact forces from parallelizable physics simulations in IsaacGym. This leverages offline-scanned meshes of the environment (e.g., via Polycam).
3. **Formal Verification Filter (Sum-of-Squares Programming):** After the MPPI controller optimizes and proposes a command `u*`, the RM-DeSKO model predicts the next state `x_{t+1}`. *Before execution*, a formal check is performed on the predicted robot configuration `q_{t+1}`.
 - **SOS Condition:** The ellipsoidal volumes `E_j(q_{t+1})` representing the robot links are checked against the risk contours using Sum-of-Squares (SOS) programming. This involves verifying if specific polynomial expressions (derived from the risk contour definition and ellipsoid representation, as per Theorem 1) can be decomposed into a sum of squares, which guarantees the links are within the risk-bounded region with probability `Δ_o`. This method handles any probability distribution for `ω_i` via moment-based approximations.
 - **Decision and Re-optimization:** If any ellipsoid fails the SOS condition (meaning the collision probability is greater than `Δ`), the corresponding cost `c(x_t, u*)` is updated (e.g., set to a high penalty), and the MPPI controller is prompted to re-optimize its policy using this new cost information. This iterative process leverages the gradient-free nature of MPPI.
4. **Theoretical Guarantee:** The paper provides a theoretical proof (Appendix A) that the overall probability of collision for the uncertain arm over the entire planning horizon is bounded by `Δ_o + zΔ_ell ≤ Δ`, where `z` is the number of planning steps and `Δ_ell` is the probability of the robot's true occupancy falling outside its approximating ellipsoids.

**E. Data Collection and Training:**
Nominal trajectories are first collected in an obstacle-free environment using a baseline MPPI controller. Around each waypoint of these trajectories, `N` states are sampled and propagated for `H` steps with added time-varying tracking errors `ω_d`. This dataset is then used to train the RM-DeSKO neural network model to minimize prediction errors.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research paper presents extensive experiments in both simulation and real-world environments to validate the proposed framework. The main findings are detailed across state prediction performance, simulation-based motion planning, and sim-to-real transfer experiments.

**A. State Prediction Performance Comparison:**
- **Setup:** Experiments were conducted on a Franka Emika Panda robot (7 DOF) to evaluate the state prediction accuracy of the RM-DeSKO model against several neural network baselines: Deep Koopman operator with control (DKU), Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM), Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP), Transformer, and variants of RM-DeSKO (RM-DeSKO-N, RM-DeSKO-A). State and motion uncertainties were introduced (`x_t ∼ U[−0.2, 0.2] × U[−0.05, 0.05]`, `ω_d ∼ N(0.2, 0.2)`).
- **Results:**
 - The Transformer model achieved the lowest mean prediction error.
 - The RM-DeSKO model generated the lowest maximum prediction error.
 - LSTM, RM-DeSKO, RM-DeSKO-N, and RM-DeSKO-A models exhibited similar, slightly higher mean errors compared to Transformer.
 - DKU and MLP showed comparatively poorer prediction performance.
- **Conclusion:** While the Transformer excelled in mean error, RM-DeSKO demonstrated competitive performance, particularly in mitigating unknown noise, which is considered critical for MPPI control under uncertainty. Its less complex structure was noted as being more practical than its variants.

**B. Simulation Experiments for Motion Planning Problem:**
- **Setup:** Ten tasks were tested on the Franka robot in IsaacGym, each with random start and goal configurations and two heart-shaped, uncertain, non-convex obstacles with probabilistic parameters (`ω_1 ∼ U[−0.1, 0.1]`, `ω_2 ∼ N(0.05, 0.01)`). The MPPI controller used `N=1000` samples, tracking error `ω_d ∼ N(0.2, 0.2)`, and specific cost weights. Collision risk verification considered the end three ellipsoids of the robot for a risk tolerance `Δ=30%`. The proposed method (Ours, using RM-DeSKO) was compared against a baseline (original IsaacGym dynamics), Transformer-based MPPI, and LSTM-based MPPI.
- **Results:**
 - **Success Rate:** Our method achieved a 94% success rate, significantly higher than the baseline (89%). Transformer and LSTM-based methods had a 0% success rate, failing to navigate to the goal.
 - **Time to Goal (TTG):** Our method required 34.587 seconds on average, which was faster than the baseline (47.249 seconds).
 - **Trajectory Length:** Our method generated shorter trajectories (1.168 units) compared to the baseline (2.273 units).
 - **Rollout Behavior:** Snapshots showed that the baseline's rollouts were transient, leading to inefficient policy updates. Transformer and LSTM models failed to provide effective rollouts, causing the robot to wander or fail. In contrast, our method's stable rollouts (benefiting from RM-DeSKO) accurately guided the stochastic optimization, enabling the robot to navigate narrow spaces between obstacles.
 - **Robustness Analysis:**
 - **Tracking Errors:** Our method demonstrated robustness to varying tracking errors (`ω_d ∼ N(0.22, 0.22)` and `N(0.25, 0.25)`), maintaining a high success rate (91% and 83% respectively), albeit with increased TTG and length.
 - **Number of Samples:** Our method consistently found solutions even with fewer samples (`N=200`, `N=500`), outperforming the baseline which failed in these cases.
 - **Cost Weights:** Our method efficiently found shorter trajectories across different pairs of position and orientation cost weights compared to the baseline. However, its success rate was lower than the baseline under specific low position or high orientation cost weights, attributed to training data influenced by reasonable importance sampling weights.
- **Conclusion:** The proposed framework significantly improved motion planning performance in terms of success rate, efficiency (TTG, length), and ability to navigate complex, uncertain environments. The RM-DeSKO model provided correct guiding rollouts, which was crucial for successful policy updates.

**C. Sim-to-Real Transferred Physical Experiments:**
- **Setup:** A human-robot collaboration task was designed using a UR5e manipulator (6 DOF). The robot's end-effector, equipped with a rebar tying tool, had to approach and tie two vertical rebars held by a worker. Obstacles included the worker's horizontal arms, modeled with probabilistic locations due to fatigue. The eccentric payload of the tying machine (due to consumed tie wires) introduced joint state noise. The human body was scanned via Polycam for collision meshes. `ω_d ∼ N(0, σ)` with `σ = {0.15, 0.20, 0.25}` were tested. Collision risk verification was performed between the tying gun nozzle and human arm ellipsoids with `Δ=10%`.
- **Results:**
 - **Baseline Failure:** The baseline method consistently failed to converge to the target across all three noise levels within the limited time budget, attributed to high noised state variance.
 - **Our Method Success:** Our method achieved a consistent 90% success rate (9 out of 10 trials) under all tested noise levels. Average TTG ranged from 8.32 s to 10.21 s.
 - **Sim-to-Real Transfer:** The method demonstrated successful zero-shot sim-to-real transfer, using the same weight file trained in simulation directly on the real UR5e.
 - **Real-world Behavior:** The real robot continuously adjusted its configuration to avoid the worker's arms, successfully reaching the goal and performing the tying action while adhering to the acceptable risk tolerance. The screen showed stable rollouts predicted by RM-DeSKO.
- **Conclusion:** The physical experiments validated the robustness, efficiency, and real-world applicability of the proposed framework, confirming its ability to generate safe trajectories and transfer policies from simulation to real robots in challenging uncertain HRC scenarios.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research presented in this paper offers several significant contributions and has substantial potential impact on the field of robotics, particularly in applications involving human-robot interaction and operation in complex, dynamic environments.

**A. Enhanced Safety and Reliability in Uncertain Environments:**
The most direct significance lies in providing a robust framework that offers **provable safety guarantees** for robot manipulators. By integrating formal certification (Sum-of-Squares programming) with efficient sampling-based control, the method ensures that the probability of collision remains below a user-defined threshold, even in the presence of non-Gaussian motion uncertainties and non-convex, probabilistically defined environmental obstacles. This moves beyond overly conservative safety margins or heuristic collision avoidance, leading to more reliable and predictable robot behavior in safety-critical applications.

**B. Improved Efficiency and Performance:**
The hierarchical collision risk verification method, which combines fast physics simulations (IsaacGym) for initial cost calculation and SOS programming for fine-grained verification of the optimized trajectory, addresses the computational bottleneck of formal methods. This approach enables the system to operate with a sufficient replanning frequency (6 Hz demonstrated in real-world experiments) for real-time tasks, including dynamic human-robot collaboration. The reported improvements in success rate, time to goal, and trajectory length compared to baseline methods highlight the practical efficiency gained.

**C. Robust Prediction for Complex Dynamics:**
The development and integration of the Rigid Manipulator Deep Stochastic Koopman Operator (RM-DeSKO) model is a key technical contribution. This data-driven neural network effectively learns and predicts the complex, high-dimensional dynamics of robot arms under significant tracking errors and unknown process noise. The ability to robustly propagate state distributions is crucial for the success of sampling-based controllers like MPPI, which rely heavily on accurate forward models. This capability is particularly important for systems with non-linear dynamics where analytical uncertainty propagation can be challenging.

**D. Advancing Human-Robot Collaboration (HRC):**
The successful demonstration in a challenging human-robot collaboration (HRC) scenario with a UR5e manipulator highlights the framework's practical applicability. By accounting for probabilistic human arm locations (due to fatigue) and robot payload uncertainties, the system can generate risk-bounded trajectories that allow robots to operate in close proximity to humans without requiring strict separation, thereby improving efficiency and flexibility in shared workspaces. This direct sim-to-real transfer further underscores its potential for real-world deployment.

**E. Generalizability to Diverse Uncertainties:**
The methodology's ability to handle non-Gaussian uncertainty distributions (via moment-based approximations in SOS) and non-convex obstacle geometries represents a notable advancement over methods restricted by simpler assumptions. This broadens the scope of environments and types of uncertainty that can be safely managed by autonomous systems.

**F. Foundation for Future Research:**
The paper acknowledges limitations, such as the requirement for example paths for training and polynomial equations of obstacles, and suggests future work on reducing setup costs and improving computational speed. The mention of integrating Gaussian splatting renderers for perception-aware planning and dynamic risk contour integration also indicates a clear path for future research building upon this robust foundation.

In conclusion, this work significantly contributes to the state-of-the-art in motion planning under uncertainty by providing a practical, efficient, and formally certified solution that can enable safer and more effective deployment of robot manipulators in complex, uncertain, and human-inhabited environments.
