# Provably Safe Trajectory Generation for Manipulators Under Motion and Environmental Uncertainties

> [!summary] Summary
> A framework for robot manipulators generates provably safe and efficient trajectories in uncertain, non-convex environments by combining a learned deep stochastic Koopman operator model with hierarchical, formally verifiable collision risk checks. This approach achieved a 94% success rate in simulation and 90% in real-world human-robot collaboration tasks, maintaining collision probability below a user-defined threshold while improving navigation efficiency.

## Problem

- Existing motion planning methods for uncertain robots often rely on simplifying assumptions like Gaussian uncertainty or convex obstacles, limiting their real-world applicability.
- Many approaches struggle to simultaneously address both robot motion inaccuracies and uncertainties in complex, non-convex environments for high-dimensional manipulators.
- Achieving formal, provable collision risk guarantees for real-time applications with complex robots and environments remains computationally challenging.

## Method

- Develops a Rigid Manipulator Deep Stochastic ==Koopman== Operator (RM-DeSKO) model to learn and robustly predict robot dynamics and state distributions under motion uncertainty.
- Integrates a hierarchical collision risk verification into a Model Predictive Path Integral (MPPI) controller, using parallelized physics simulation for initial cost and Sum-of-Squares (SOS) programming for formal safety certification.
- Approximates robot links as ellipsoids and defines risk contours for uncertain, non-convex obstacles, allowing provable collision probability bounds even with non-Gaussian environmental uncertainties.

## Results

- The framework achieved a **94%** success rate in complex simulation environments with uncertain, non-convex obstacles, significantly improving over baseline methods and yielding more efficient trajectories.
- Successfully demonstrated zero-shot sim-to-real transfer on a UR5e robot, maintaining a **90%** success rate in human-robot collaboration tasks across various noise levels.
- The RM-DeSKO model provided the lowest maximum prediction error among tested baselines, enabling stable and effective stochastic optimization for navigating cluttered and uncertain spaces.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A data-driven Deep Stochastic Koopman Operator can effectively learn and robustly predict high-dimensional robot dynamics, including uncertainty, crucial for sampling-based control.
> - Hierarchical safety verification, using rapid approximate checks (physics simulation) followed by formal guarantees (SOS programming), enables real-time, provably safe motion planning for complex systems.
> - Formal collision risk bounds can be established for non-Gaussian uncertainties and non-convex obstacles by representing robot parts as ellipsoids and utilizing moment-based Sum-of-Squares programming.
