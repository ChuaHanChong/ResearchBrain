# 4M-21: An Any-to-Any Vision Model for Tens of Tasks and Modalities

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at EPFL and Apple introduce 4M-21, a generalist vision model that processes and generates 21 diverse visual modalities using a unified Transformer architecture. The model achieves competitive out-of-the-box performance across various tasks, often matching or surpassing specialized pseudo-labeling networks, and enables fine-grained, steerable multimodal generation.

## Problem

- Computer vision traditionally relies on specialized models for individual tasks, leading to computational inefficiency and limited ability for complex cross-modal reasoning.
- Existing multimodal foundation models are restricted by the number and diversity of modalities and tasks they can effectively integrate.
- Scaling multitask learning often encounters issues like negative transfer and performance degradation compared to single-task expert models.

## Method

- Developed **4M-21**, a single 3-billion-parameter ==Transformer encoder-decoder== model that unifies 21 diverse visual modalities into a common representation of ==discrete tokens==.
- Employed modality-specific tokenizers (e.g., ==ViT-based VQ-VAE==, ==MLP-based DVAE==, ==WordPiece==) to convert disparate data types into token sequences for a unified ==masked modeling== objective.
- Utilized a multi-stage training approach involving extensive ==pseudo-labeling== from strong specialist models on large datasets (CC12M, COYO700M) and co-training with text corpora (C4).

## Results

- 4M-21 demonstrates strong performance across **21** diverse tasks, frequently matching or outperforming specialist pseudo-labeling networks and other multitask baselines like Unified-IO.
- The model exhibits robust 'any-to-any' multimodal generation, allowing unprecedented fine-grained and steerable control over outputs using varied inputs such as human poses, SAM instances, and metadata.
- It maintains comparable unimodal transfer performance to previous models while showing improved performance on novel multimodal transfer tasks, such as 3D object detection on ARKitScenes.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A single generalist vision model can scale effectively to encompass tens of diverse modalities (21 in 4M-21) without sacrificing out-of-the-box performance relative to specialist models.
> - Unifying diverse visual inputs and outputs through discrete tokenization and a masked modeling objective within a Transformer architecture allows for robust 'any-to-any' prediction and generation.
> - Massive pseudo-labeling combined with strategic multi-dataset co-training is a viable method for distilling knowledge from numerous specialized models into a single, comprehensive foundation model.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of "4M-21" is a significant stride in the rapidly evolving landscape of generalist artificial intelligence, specifically in the domain of multimodal and multitask learning for computer vision. For decades, the dominant paradigm in computer vision involved developing specialized models for individual tasks (e.g., object detection, semantic segmentation, depth estimation) or modalities. While highly effective, this approach leads to a proliferation of models, computational inefficiency, and limited ability for complex cross-modal reasoning.

The past few years have seen a major shift towards "foundation models" – large, pre-trained models capable of performing a wide array of tasks by learning rich, generalized representations from vast datasets. In language, models like BERT and GPT series exemplify this trend, leading to remarkable emergent capabilities. In vision and multimodal AI, similar efforts have emerged with models like Gato, OFA, Pix2Seq, UnifiedIO, and the authors' own previous work, 4M. These models attempt to unify different modalities and tasks by transforming them into a common representation, typically sequences of discrete tokens, and training large Transformer architectures.

However, a critical challenge identified by the authors is that existing multimodal foundation models, while promising, are still limited by the *number* and *diversity* of modalities and tasks they are trained on. Prior attempts to scale multitask learning often faced difficulties such as negative transfer (where learning one task negatively impacts another), performance degradation compared to single-task experts, and complex strategies for balancing different loss functions or gradients. The original 4M model, while pioneering "any-to-any" prediction on 7 modalities, still represented a relatively small subset of possible vision tasks.

"4M-21" directly addresses this limitation. By significantly expanding the repertoire of a single model to tens of diverse modalities (21 in this iteration) without sacrificing performance, this work pushes the boundaries of what a unified vision model can achieve. It demonstrates that the masked modeling paradigm, particularly with sophisticated tokenization strategies, can be effectively scaled to encompass a much broader spectrum of visual understanding and generation tasks. This positions 4M-21 as a leading exemplar in the quest for truly generalist AI agents in the vision domain, one that can seamlessly integrate and reason across a multitude of visual data types.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of this research is to advance the field of generalist AI by developing a single, unified neural network capable of handling an exceptionally wide and diverse range of visual tasks and modalities. The authors explicitly aim for an "any-to-any" model, meaning it can accept any combination of modalities as input and generate any other combination as output.

The specific objectives and motivations underpinning this work can be detailed as follows:

- **Massive Modality Expansion:** The primary goal is to significantly increase the number of modalities a single model can process and generate. Building on their prior 4M model which handled 7 modalities, 4M-21 aims to incorporate at least three times more tasks/modalities (reaching 21 in this paper), encompassing a much richer set of semantic, geometric, and abstract visual information.
- **Performance Preservation:** A critical motivation is to achieve this massive expansion *without degradation in performance* compared to specialized single-task models or previous, less comprehensive unified models. Overcoming negative transfer and maintaining high accuracy across all tasks is a key challenge in multitask learning that 4M-21 seeks to surmount.
- **Enhanced Controllability and Interaction:** By integrating a greater variety of modalities, especially structured data like 3D human poses, SAM instances, color palettes, and various metadata, the authors aim to enable more fine-grained, intuitive, and steerable control over multimodal generation. This allows for novel ways to interact with and guide the model's creative outputs.
- **Cross-Modal Retrieval Capabilities:** The inclusion of global feature embeddings from models like DINOv2 and ImageBind is motivated by the desire to enable robust cross-modal retrieval, allowing users to query using one modality (e.g., text, depth map) and retrieve information in another (e.g., RGB images, surface normals).
- **Unified Knowledge Distillation:** The work is motivated by the potential to study how knowledge from numerous specialist models (used for pseudo-labeling diverse modalities) can be effectively distilled into one unified model, creating an efficient and compact representation of diverse visual intelligence.
- **Scalability Demonstration:** The authors aim to demonstrate the scalability of their approach by training a model with 3 billion parameters on a dataset comprising 0.5 billion samples, proving the feasibility of handling large-scale multimodal data and model sizes.
- **Co-training Vision and Language:** A specific objective is to simultaneously co-train the model on both vision and large text corpora, which is crucial for improving text understanding and multimodal reasoning capabilities.

Ultimately, the motivations stem from the inherent advantages of a truly generalist model: computational efficiency (a single model instead of many), reduced model size, simplified deployment, and the enablement of novel modality fusion and intricate interactions that are impossible with fragmented, task-specific architectures.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology of 4M-21 builds fundamentally upon the multimodal masking pre-training scheme introduced in the original 4M paper, while significantly expanding its scope and capabilities. The core idea is to unify diverse modalities into a common representation space: sequences of discrete tokens, processed by a Transformer encoder-decoder architecture.

#### 3.1. Modality Expansion and Diversity (from 7 to 21+ modalities)

The paper introduces 21 highly diverse modalities, categorized as follows:
- **RGB:** Original RGB images (pixel and tokenized) and extracted Color Palettes for artistic control.
- **Geometric:** Surface Normals, Depth maps (from Omnidata), and 3D Human Poses & Shapes (from 4DHumans).
- **Semantic:** Semantic Segmentation (from Mask2Former), Bounding Boxes (from ViTDet), and SAM Instances (from Segment Anything Model).
- **Edges:** Canny Edges (from OpenCV) and SAM Edges (extracted from SAM instances), providing scene layout information.
- **Feature Maps:** Dense feature maps and global embeddings from state-of-the-art self-supervised models like CLIP, DINOv2, and ImageBind, enabling robust transfer and retrieval. T5-XXL text embeddings are also included as input.
- **Metadata:** Structured numerical and textual metadata describing semantic aspects (crowdedness, clutter score, walkability, semantic/instance diversity), geometric properties (geometric complexity, occlusion score), and image processing characteristics (brightness, contrast, saturation, colorfulness, original resolution).

#### 3.2. Discrete Tokenization

A crucial step is converting all these diverse modalities into discrete tokens. This unification allows for training with a single pre-training objective (per-token classification via cross-entropy loss), full parameter sharing, and eliminates the need for task-specific heads or complex loss balancing. Three main types of modality-specific tokenizers are employed:
- **ViT-based VQ-VAE (with optional Diffusion Decoder):** Used for image-like modalities (RGB, normals, depth, edges, dense feature maps, SAM instances). This maps inputs into a small grid of discrete tokens. Diffusion decoders are optionally used for detail-rich modalities like RGB and edges to improve visual plausibility.
- **MLP-based Discrete VAE (with Memcodes quantization):** Applied to non-spatial, continuous modalities such as 3D human poses and global embeddings (DINOv2, ImageBind). This compresses them into a fixed, small number of discrete tokens.
- **WordPiece Tokenizer:** Employed for text (captions, web text) and structured data like bounding boxes, color palettes, and metadata. Special tokens are introduced to encode the type and values of these structured modalities, effectively converting them into sequences of text.

#### 3.3. Training Details

- **Architecture:** The model adopts the encoder-decoder Transformer architecture from 4M, incorporating additional modality embeddings. The encoder accepts both tokenized modalities and raw RGB pixel inputs (with a learnable patch-wise projection) for transfer learning.
- **Datasets:** Training is performed in two stages. First, a 4M pre-training stage on a significantly larger image dataset. Second, a fine-tuning phase on a smaller dataset containing a larger number of modalities. To mitigate overfitting, the models are co-trained on:
 - **CC12M:** ~10M text-image samples, fully pseudo-labeled with all 21 modalities (60% of training samples).
 - **COYO700M:** ~500M text-image samples, pseudo-labeled with the original 7 modalities of 4M (20% of training samples).
 - **C4 (Colossal Clean Crawled Corpus):** A large text-only corpus used for language modeling co-training (20% of training samples).
- **Pseudo-labeling:** A cornerstone of the approach, leveraging strong specialized models (e.g., Omnidata, Mask2Former, SAM, 4DHumans, DINOv2, ImageBind) to generate dense, aligned labels for a massive dataset.
- **Masking Strategy:** A combination of multimodal random masking and span masking is used. The Dirichlet distribution (parameter α) controls the sampling of input and target tokens, balancing between modality-dominated and more uniform token distributions. Chunk-based masking is introduced for grouped metadata to improve training efficiency and specific conditioning.
- **Scale:** The training scales to models of up to 3 billion parameters, with training lengths up to 500 billion tokens.
- **Computational Resources:** Training involved extensive use of Nvidia A100 GPUs, with the largest model requiring 11 days on 128 A100s, totaling approximately 120,000 A100-hours for primary experiments.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents compelling evidence that "4M-21" successfully achieves its ambitious objectives, demonstrating state-of-the-art performance across a vastly expanded range of tasks and modalities.

#### 4.1. Out-of-the-Box Capabilities and Performance

- **Expanded Task Solvability:** 4M-21 demonstrates the possibility of training a single model to solve at least 3x more tasks/modalities than previous best any-to-any models (e.g., 4M-7 or UnifiedIO) *without* sacrificing performance.
- **Competitive with Specialists:** The model achieves strong out-of-the-box (zero-shot) performance on common vision tasks such as surface normal and depth estimation (on DIODE), semantic and instance segmentation (on COCO), and 3D human pose estimation (on 3DPW). Crucially, it often matches or even outperforms the specialist pseudo-labeling networks and other strong baselines, despite being a single, generalist model.
- **Superior to Other Multitask Models:** Comparisons to other multitask models like Unified-IO and Unified-IO 2 show a significant performance gap, with 4M-21 consistently achieving better metrics across evaluated tasks.
- **Scalability of Performance:** Performance generally improves with larger model sizes (B, L, XL), indicating promising scaling trends for the approach. The 4M-21 XL model notably matches the performance of 4M-7 XL while solving three times more tasks.
- **Robust Retrieval:** Achieves high kNN retrieval performance on ImageNet-1K using predicted DINOv2 global tokens, approaching the theoretical tokenizer bound.

#### 4.2. Multimodal Generation Capabilities

- **Any-to-Any Generation:** The model exhibits robust any-to-any generation, capable of predicting all 21 modalities from any given input modality or subset of modalities, maintaining high consistency across generated outputs (e.g., generating RGB, depth, normals, segmentation, etc., from a single text prompt).
- **Fine-Grained and Steerable Control:** The rich set of input modalities enables unprecedented steerable generation. Users can influence outputs by providing human poses, SAM polygon instances, specific color palettes, various semantic or geometric metadata (e.g., walkability score, occlusion score, brightness, contrast), or Canny/SAM edges. This allows for multimodal edits, grounded generation, and probing of learned representations.
- **Improved Text Understanding:** By co-training with large text corpora and incorporating T5-XXL embeddings, 4M-21 demonstrates improved text understanding, leading to more geometrically and semantically plausible RGB image generations from captions compared to prior models like 4M-7.

#### 4.3. Multimodal Retrieval

- **Flexible Cross-Modal Retrieval:** The model can perform multimodal retrieval by predicting global embeddings (e.g., DINOv2 or ImageBind) from any input modality (e.g., RGB, depth, segmentation, edges, text, color palette). Retrieval is then performed by comparing cosine distances to a retrieval set.
- **Combined Modality Queries:** It allows combining multiple modalities as query inputs, offering better control and precision over retrieval results.

#### 4.4. Transfer Learning

- **Preserved Unimodal Transfer:** For unimodal transfer tasks similar to those handled by 4M-7 (e.g., ImageNet-1K classification, ADE20K semantic segmentation, NYUv2 depth estimation), 4M-21 maintains comparable performance, demonstrating that adding many more modalities does not lead to performance degradation on these established tasks.
- **Improved Novel Task Performance:** Critically, 4M-21 shows *improved* performance on novel transfer tasks that are sufficiently different from the original 4M modalities, such as 3D object detection on ARKitScenes. This suggests that the broader and more diverse pre-training helps the model learn more generalizable and useful representations.
- **Effective Multimodal Transfer:** In multimodal transfer tasks (e.g., NYUv2, Hypersim semantic segmentation, ARKitScenes 3D object detection using RGB+Depth), 4M-21 makes strong use of optionally available depth inputs, significantly improving upon baselines and demonstrating the utility of its multimodal understanding.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The work presented in "4M-21" represents a significant leap forward in the pursuit of generalist AI for vision, with profound implications and potential impacts across several dimensions:

#### 5.1. Advancement Towards Generalist AI

This paper demonstrates that a single foundation model can effectively integrate and process a vast array of visual data types and tasks, moving beyond the fragmented approach of specialized models. It validates the "any-to-any" paradigm at an unprecedented scale, bringing the vision community closer to realizing a truly unified AI agent capable of broad visual intelligence.

#### 5.2. Scalability and Efficiency Breakthrough

A central challenge in multitask learning has been scaling to a large number of diverse tasks without suffering performance degradation or negative transfer. 4M-21 definitively shows that this is achievable, even with 3x more modalities than previous leading models. This implies significant computational efficiency gains (fewer models to train and deploy) and a simplified model management lifecycle. The ability to distill knowledge from many specialized models into one unified model is also a major step towards efficient knowledge representation.

#### 5.3. Enhanced User Interaction and Creative Applications

The fine-grained and steerable multimodal generation capabilities unlocked by 4M-21 have immense potential for creative applications. Artists, designers, and content creators could leverage the model for highly controlled image synthesis, editing, and style transfer, guided by semantic inputs (e.g., human poses, specific objects), geometric properties (depth, normals), or even abstract metadata (colorfulness, walkability). This opens new avenues for human-AI collaboration in creative fields.

#### 5.4. Robust Multimodal Understanding and Retrieval

The model's ability to perform cross-modal retrieval from any input modality enhances how users can interact with and query large visual datasets. This could revolutionize search engines, content recommendation systems, and data analytics tools, allowing for richer, more contextualized queries and more relevant results across modalities.

#### 5.5. Foundation for Future Research

By open-sourcing the code and pre-trained models, the authors provide a powerful new benchmark and foundation for future research. This will accelerate exploration into:
- **Emergent Capabilities:** While the authors note that emergent capabilities (beyond direct transfer) are still largely untapped in vision compared to LLMs, 4M-21 offers a robust platform to investigate if and how such capabilities emerge from increased modality diversity and scale.
- **Tokenizer Design:** The paper highlights the crucial role of modality-specific discrete tokenization. Future research can build on this to develop even more efficient and higher-fidelity tokenizers for increasingly complex data types.
- **Co-training Strategies:** The multi-dataset co-training approach offers a blueprint for integrating diverse, partially-aligned datasets, a key challenge in scaling multimodal AI.
- **Robotics and Embodied AI:** A model that seamlessly understands and generates various sensory inputs (vision, depth, poses) could be invaluable for developing more intelligent and adaptive robotic systems capable of complex interactions with the physical world.
