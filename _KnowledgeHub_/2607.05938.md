# Prior-First, Condition-Second: Scalable and Controllable Hand Motion Completion

> [!summary] Summary
> This work introduces a "prior-first, condition-second" framework for scalable and controllable hand motion completion, which first learns a generic kinematic prior from unlabeled body motion data and then applies lightweight, semantically-layered adapters for control. The approach generates high-fidelity, kinematically consistent hand motions in real time (450 FPS), outperforming end-to-end methods in plausibility and data efficiency for semantic conditioning.

## Problem

- Synthesizing realistic and controllable hand motion is challenging due to hands' high degrees of freedom (DoF).
- There is a scarcity of high-quality, semantically-labeled hand motion data, and existing annotations are often inconsistent across datasets, hindering generalizable model training.
- Many learning-based methods struggle to maintain kinematic plausibility, frequently producing unnatural or unstable hand movements, especially in end-to-end conditional generation.

## Method

- A **"prior-first, condition-second"** paradigm is proposed, decoupling kinematic learning from semantic control. A streaming, ==autoregressive Transformer-based diffusion model== learns a generic body-hand kinematic prior from large, unlabeled data.
- ==Kinematic Chain Cascading Attention (KCCA)== is introduced within the Transformer to explicitly model biomechanical energy transfer from the body to the hand, ensuring stable wrist-forearm coupling and accurate finger articulation.
- Lightweight, ==semantically-layered adapters== are applied on top of the frozen kinematic prior, injecting condition embeddings (e.g., text, attributes) at appropriate kinematic levels to enable data-efficient control with minimal labeled data.

## Results

- The autoregressive kinematic prior achieved the lowest FID (**3.691**), highest Diversity (**1.742**), and superior kinematic consistency (MPJPE **1.310 cm**, Root Error **5.436 deg**) compared to baselines, while operating in real-time at **450 FPS**.
- Semantically-layered adapters demonstrated strong conditional generation, achieving the highest Text–Motion Consistency (**4.21**) and improved kinematic metrics (MPJPE **0.863 cm**) using only a few hours of labeled data.
- The framework showed robust cross-dataset generalization, with the prior-adapter approach outperforming end-to-end models trained from scratch under various labeled data budgets, especially in data-scarce scenarios.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decoupling the learning of intrinsic kinematic structure from semantic conditioning allows for robust prior learning from abundant unlabeled data and highly data-efficient adaptation for specific control tasks.
> - Explicitly modeling the biomechanical hierarchy with Kinematic Chain Cascading Attention (KCCA) is crucial for generating kinematically plausible and stable hand motions, particularly for wrist-forearm coupling.
> - Layer-specific injection of conditioning signals, rather than uniform application, enables more effective and disentangled semantic control by aligning control inputs with relevant kinematic levels.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The generation of realistic and controllable human motion, particularly hand articulation, represents an ongoing challenge in computer graphics and animation. Existing research in data-driven human motion generation has primarily focused on full-body movements, often neglecting the complexities of hand motion due to its high degrees of freedom (DoF) and the scarcity of high-quality, semantically-labeled datasets.

Previous approaches to hand motion synthesis have included physics-based simulations, which require intricate constraint design, and retrieval-based methods, such as motion graphs, which face limitations in scalability, real-time performance, and often introduce visible artifacts at motion segment boundaries. More recent learning-based methods have leveraged generative models to model continuous distributions of hand motions. These often fall into two categories: hand-only generation, which can produce high-fidelity finger articulation but lacks full-body context, and body-conditioned hand completion, where hand motion is derived from body dynamics. Examples in the latter category include deterministic regression models like Body2Hands and diffusion models like BOTH2Hands, which are typically trained end-to-end on scenario-specific, paired body-hand datasets.

A recurring challenge across these learning-based approaches is the reliance on extensive, high-quality, and semantically consistent labeled data. Motion capture data is increasingly available, but associated semantic labels (e.g., text descriptions, attributes) are expensive to collect at scale, often imbalanced, and rarely interoperable across diverse datasets due to variations in capture protocols, skeleton definitions, and annotation schemes. This fragmentation limits the scalability and generalizability of end-to-end conditional motion generation models.

This paper positions itself within the evolving landscape of leveraging pre-trained motion generative models as robust priors. Building upon advancements in diffusion models for motion synthesis (e.g., CAMDM, AMDM) and methods for adapting pre-trained priors with lightweight conditioning (e.g., PriorMDM), this work proposes a "prior-first, condition-second" paradigm specifically for body-conditioned hand motion completion. The core contribution is the concrete instantiation of this paradigm, aiming to overcome the limitations of data scarcity and cross-dataset inconsistencies. It separates the learning of general kinematic structure from specific semantic control, arguing that kinematic regularities are invariant across datasets, allowing semantic control to be a lightweight, dataset-specific conditioning on a shared, pre-learned prior. This approach addresses the scalability, plausibility, and controllability issues inherent in synthesizing complex hand movements by decoupling the underlying kinematic model from the high-level semantic instructions.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to synthesize high-fidelity hand motions that are kinematically consistent with full body dynamics and can be controlled by optional semantic labels. The authors identify several motivations and challenges that drive their proposed framework:

1. **High Degrees of Freedom in Hands:** Hands possess a large number of joints and degrees of freedom, making their animation computationally intensive and artistically challenging. Traditional animation tools often require extensive manual authoring or specialized capture setups for nuanced hand movements.
2. **Scarcity and Cost of Semantic Supervision:** While motion capture data is becoming more accessible, obtaining paired semantic supervision (e.g., text descriptions, audio cues, high-level attributes) for hand motion is expensive and difficult to scale. Existing annotations are often imbalanced, focusing on common actions while neglecting subtle yet important variations.
3. **Cross-Dataset Inconsistency of Semantic Labels:** Semantic labels and annotations are often dataset-dependent, reflecting variations in capture protocols, skeleton definitions, and cultural contexts. This lack of interoperability hinders the transfer and reuse of supervision across different datasets, making it difficult to train a single, generalized, controllable model.
4. **Maintaining Kinematic Plausibility:** End-to-end generative models that attempt to learn both kinematic structure and semantic mapping from sparse data can frequently produce physically implausible or kinematically inconsistent hand movements, such as "floating" hands or unnatural joint rotations.
5. **Need for Scalable and Real-time Solutions:** Production animation pipelines require efficient methods capable of real-time inference and interactive authoring for practical application, which many existing data-driven methods struggle to achieve.

To address these motivations, the paper sets out to achieve the following specific objectives:
- To develop a framework that can learn a generic body–hand kinematic prior from large-scale, unstructured, and unlabeled motion data, capturing the intrinsic coordination between global body dynamics and hand articulation.
- To enable semantic control through lightweight adaptation layers applied on top of a frozen kinematic prior, thereby eliminating the need to re-learn kinematic structure for each new control interface or dataset.
- To design a streaming, autoregressive body–hand prior that generates temporally coherent and kinematically consistent hand motion from body dynamics in real time, incorporating structured kinematic modeling to ensure mechanical body–hand coupling.
- To introduce semantically-layered adapters that inject conditioning signals at appropriate kinematic levels, facilitating data-efficient control, including self-supervised attribute control and weakly supervised text-driven control, with minimal labeled data.
- To demonstrate improved kinematic plausibility, robustness, and controllability compared to end-to-end conditioned baselines, particularly in scenarios with limited resources or across different datasets.
- To showcase the framework's real-time inference capabilities and its utility in an interactive authoring workflow for production animation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework operates on a "prior-first, condition-second" paradigm, dividing the problem into two distinct stages: learning a generic kinematic prior and then applying lightweight, semantically-layered adaptation.

#### 3.1. Kinematic Representation

The framework explicitly decouples body conditioning signals from hand motion targets.
- **Body State Input ($\mathbf{b_n}$):** A hybrid representation comprising global 3D joint positions, velocities, and 6D continuous local rotations for body joints. Hand joint positions are included, but hand local rotations, which are the prediction target, are excluded. Positions and velocities are normalized, while rotations maintain their raw format.
- **Hand State Target ($\mathbf{x_n}$):** This concatenates the 6D rotations of both hands. Wrist rotations are predicted in global orientation, while finger joint rotations are predicted in their local coordinate frames. During inference, the predicted global wrist orientation is converted back to the local frame of its parent joint (the forearm) using an inverse transformation to maintain kinematic consistency.

#### 3.2. Autoregressive Prior with Transformer-based Diffusion

To balance temporal coherence, fidelity, and diversity, the system employs a clip-level autoregressive formulation using a Transformer-based diffusion model.
- **Formulation:** At each time step $t$, the model synthesizes a fixed-length window of hand motion ($\mathbf{x_{seq}}$) of length $L=45$ frames. This generation is conditioned on the corresponding body motion window ($\mathbf{b_{seq}}$) and a history buffer of past hand motions ($\mathbf{h_{past}}$) of length $P=10$ frames. This ensures continuity and supports online inference.
- **Model Architecture:** The core is a Transformer-based diffusion model ($\mathbf{\epsilon_\theta}$) that directly predicts the clean hand motion signal ($\mathbf{\hat{x}_0}$) from a noise vector ($\mathbf{z}$), $\mathbf{b_{seq}}$, and $\mathbf{h_{past}}$. All input data streams are projected into a unified latent space using MLP encoders and linear projections, then concatenated with sinusoidal positional embeddings. The body motion is tokenized into 6 tokens per frame (one frame-level full-body token and five kinematic tokens along the root-to-wrist chain).
- **Attention Mechanisms:** The Transformer employs two interleaved attention mechanisms:
 1. **Temporal Self-Attention:** Models dependencies along the time axis within the prediction window.
 2. **Kinematic Chain Cascading Attention (KCCA):** Models spatial coupling between hand and body tokens along the root-to-wrist kinematic chain.

#### 3.3. Kinematic Chain Cascading Attention (KCCA)

KCCA is introduced to explicitly model the biomechanical energy transfer along the human kinematic chain, from the root to the wrist.
- **Dynamic Gating:** Instead of indiscriminately attending to all body parts, the hand query ($\mathbf{Q_n}$) dynamically distributes its attention along an ordered set of kinematic tokens representing the root-to-wrist chain. A lightweight gating MLP conditions on both the local hand query and the global root momentum to determine the dominant kinematic level for each frame. This mechanism maintains stable kinematic focus even when the hand query is noisy, preventing random correlations.
- **Hierarchical Aggregation:** The final kinematic context is aggregated via a hierarchical gated summation, where weights are determined by the dynamic gating. This ensures generated hand motions remain mechanically coupled to upstream body dynamics, mitigating artifacts like phase drift or implausible wrist behavior.

#### 3.4. Training and Inference

- **Training Objectives:** The diffusion Transformer is trained by minimizing a composite loss function:
 - **Rotation and Velocity Reconstruction Loss ($\mathcal{L}_{rec}$):** L1 loss on per-frame joint rotations and their finite-difference velocities.
 - **FK-based Geometric Consistency Loss ($\mathcal{L}_{geo}$):** L2 loss on the global 3D joint positions of hand end-effectors, computed via forward kinematics, to ensure spatial accuracy.
 - **Dual-hand Relative Distance Loss ($\mathcal{L}_{sym}$):** L1 loss on the relative distances between corresponding left and right end-effector joints (e.g., fingertips, wrists) to ensure realistic inter-hand coordination.
- **Classifier-Free Guidance:** During training, conditioning signals (body context and history buffer) are randomly discarded with a probability of 0.1, replaced by a learnable null embedding. This allows the model to learn the intrinsic unconditional hand motion manifold and handle cold-start scenarios. During inference, a guidance scale ($s > 1.0$) is used to extrapolate predictions, enhancing conditional consistency.
- **Implementation Details:** A DDPM sampler with 50 denoising steps and a cosine noise schedule is used. The latent dimension is 256. The system operates at over 450 frames per second (FPS) on an RTX 4090 GPU, achieving real-time performance.

#### 3.5. Semantically-Layered Conditional Adaptation

After the kinematic prior is trained and frozen, lightweight adapters are introduced for conditional generation.
- **Design Principle:** Instead of uniform signal injection, layer-specific learnable gates modulate the injection of condition embeddings into specific cascading stages of the frozen prior. This exploits the observation that semantic commands naturally operate at different kinematic levels (e.g., global style vs. local articulation).
- **Data Efficiency:** This layered design enables data-efficient adaptation, requiring only a few hours of labeled data to train the adapters, as they only learn minimal residuals for targeted control rather than reconstructing kinematics from scratch.
- **Sampling Strategy:** To maximize manifold coverage, a clustering-based sampling strategy (K-Means on body motion features) is employed to ensure the sparse labeled training set spans the full range of body dynamics.
- **Attribute-Driven Control:** Self-supervised scalar attribute functions (e.g., fist tightness, derived algorithmically) are used. Injection of these attributes is constrained to the last two layers of the prior, focusing on fine-grained hand and finger kinematic levels while preserving global body consistency.
- **Text-Driven Control:** For intuitive control via text prompts, motions are rendered into videos and captioned using a Vision-Language Model (QWEN-VL) with a structured prompting template and a predefined hand verb vocabulary. Text embeddings are injected across all layers of the kinematic hierarchy, allowing the gates to dynamically activate for global action verbs (upstream layers) or specific noun descriptors (downstream layers), disentangling complex textual intents.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation assesses the framework's ability to produce plausible, controllable hand motion, its data efficiency, and its generalization capabilities.

#### 4.1. Setup

- **Datasets:**
 - **Dataset A (Prior Training):** 100 hours (200 hours with mirroring) of diverse, unlabeled motion data from various public and in-house sources (MotionPersona, InterAct, BEAT). Used exclusively for training the kinematic prior.
 - **Dataset B (Adapter Training):** A 3-hour subset of Dataset A with text labels, used for training conditional adapters, along with a 30-minute test set.
 - **Dataset C (In-the-Wild Evaluation):** 1,000 clips from AMASS and HunyuanMotion (unseen during training, no ground-truth hands) for qualitative assessment of robustness.
- **Metrics:** A comprehensive protocol including:
 - **Statistical Metrics:** FID (Fréchet Inception Distance), Diversity (variance of generated samples), FPS (inference speed).
 - **Kinematic & Geometric Consistency:** MPJPE (Mean Per-Joint Position Error for hand end-effectors), Root Error (orientation error of wrist relative to forearm), Velocity Smoothness (mean jerk).
 - **Perceptual Scoring:** Human evaluations on a Likert scale (1-5) for Naturalness, Dynamic Richness, and Text–Motion Consistency (for conditional tasks).

#### 4.2. Evaluation on Prior Learning

The core kinematic prior, trained without semantic supervision, was evaluated against four baselines (MDM, PriorMDM, Body2Hands, BOTH2Hands) on Dataset A's held-out test split, receiving only body motion as input.
- **Performance:** The proposed AR-based Prior outperformed all baselines across all quantitative and qualitative metrics (Table 2):
 - **Statistical:** Achieved the lowest FID (3.691) and highest Diversity (1.742), indicating better distributional quality and variety of outputs. Inference speed was significantly higher (450 FPS vs. 12-65 FPS for baselines).
 - **Kinematic & Geometric:** Demonstrated the lowest MPJPE (1.310 cm), Root Error (5.436 deg), and Velocity Smoothness (0.035), indicating superior kinematic accuracy, wrist-forearm coupling, and temporal continuity.
 - **Perceptual:** Received the highest scores for Naturalness (4.18) and Dynamic Richness (4.38), suggesting more plausible and varied hand motions.
- **Qualitative Observations:** The prior produced stable wrist attachments and plausible finger configurations, which baselines often struggled with, exhibiting unstable wrist behavior or unnatural joint rotations (Figure 5).

#### 4.3. Evaluation on Conditional Generator Learning

The lightweight adapters were evaluated for their ability to add semantic control without degrading kinematic quality, using Dataset B.
- **Performance:** The Adapter-based approach achieved the best trade-off, outperforming end-to-end baselines (MDM-Fullbody, BOTH2Hands) and even an end-to-end trained version of the proposed architecture ("Ours (end-to-end)") (Table 3):
 - **Statistical:** Lowest FID (2.531) and competitive Diversity (0.897).
 - **Kinematic & Geometric:** Lowest MPJPE (0.863 cm), Root Error (4.021 deg), and Velocity Smoothness (0.087), indicating that semantic control was added without compromising fundamental kinematic quality.
 - **Perceptual:** Achieved the highest scores for Naturalness (3.98), Dynamic Richness (3.54), and critically, Text–Motion Consistency (4.21).
- **Generalization to In-the-Wild Motions:** The prior demonstrated robustness by generating plausible hand motions consistent with arm dynamics on unseen body motions from Dataset C (Figure 8), suggesting generalization beyond its training distribution.

#### 4.4. Cross-Dataset Generalization

A controlled transfer experiment using the BOTH2Hands dataset (Dataset B') as a separate target domain was conducted to test generalization across datasets under varying labeled data budgets ($K$).
- **Performance:** "Ours A→B'" (prior trained on Dataset A, adapted on B') consistently outperformed models trained from scratch on B' alone (MDM, BOTH2Hands, "Ours B'-only") across all data budgets, especially when labeled data was scarce (Table 4).
- **Data Scarcity Impact:** At $K=0.25h$, end-to-end baselines suffered sharp degradation in FID and MPJPE, with diversity collapsing to near-zero. In contrast, "Ours A→B'" degraded gracefully, maintaining strong diversity (0.810) and superior FID/MPJPE. Even "Ours B'-only" (prior-adapter factorization trained on B' only) outperformed end-to-end baselines, suggesting the architectural factorization itself is beneficial.

#### 4.5. Ablation Studies

- **Data Efficiency for Adapter:** The adapter's accuracy (MPJPE) and controllability (Text–Motion Consistency) largely saturated after only a few hours (e.g., 3 hours) of labeled data from Dataset B. This contrasts with BOTH2Hands, which showed gradual improvement with more data, confirming the adapter's data efficiency (Figure 9).
- **Module Effectiveness:**
 - KCCA significantly improved kinematic accuracy (MPJPE, Root Error) by strengthening wrist-forearm coupling.
 - Semantically-layered injection was identified as the primary driver for Text–Motion Consistency, highlighting the benefit of injecting conditions at appropriate kinematic levels.
 - The full model (KCCA + Layered) yielded the best overall performance.
 - The dual-hand distance loss was found to be a useful regularizer for hand position, reducing MPJPE.
- **Lower-Body Features:** Removing lower-body features from the body conditioning degraded all metrics, indicating that global momentum cues from the entire body contribute to upper-limb dynamics and hand completion.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a framework for hand motion completion that addresses several persistent challenges in character animation and human motion synthesis. The "prior-first, condition-second" paradigm offers a modular and efficient approach with notable implications:

- **Enhanced Kinematic Plausibility and Robustness:** By learning a comprehensive kinematic prior from large-scale, unlabeled motion data, the framework generates hand movements that are consistently plausible, mechanically coupled to body dynamics, and robust even when confronted with diverse or unseen body motions. The Kinematic Chain Cascading Attention (KCCA) mechanism is a specific contribution to achieving this biomechanical fidelity.
- **Data-Efficient Semantic Control:** The separation of kinematic learning from semantic conditioning significantly mitigates the problem of scarce and inconsistent labeled data. The lightweight, semantically-layered adapters can achieve competitive control over hand motion using substantially less labeled data (a few hours) compared to end-to-end approaches. This makes the system more practical for real-world application where extensive labeling is often prohibitive.
- **Improved Cross-Dataset Generalization:** The pre-trained kinematic prior captures motion regularities that are invariant across different datasets, skeletons, and labeling conventions. This enables the framework to generalize more effectively to new domains, reducing the need for extensive retraining and fostering greater reusability of learned models.
- **Real-time Performance and Practical Application:** The streaming, autoregressive nature of the prior, combined with an optimized diffusion model implementation, allows for real-time inference (over 450 FPS). This capability is crucial for interactive authoring workflows, as demonstrated by the Blender add-on, making it directly applicable to production animation pipelines.
- **Guidance for Future Research:** The work highlights the benefits of specialized priors for sub-problems within full-body generation, suggesting that a single monolithic model may not be optimal for all aspects of human motion. It also lays groundwork for future extensions, such as integrating explicit hand-object interaction, contact modeling, and developing more granular, time-varying control interfaces.

In summary, this research contributes a practical and robust solution for controllable hand motion completion, offering a pathway to overcome limitations posed by data scarcity and computational complexity, thereby impacting fields requiring high-fidelity human animation.
