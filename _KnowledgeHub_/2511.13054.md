# ViSS-R1: Self-Supervised Reinforcement Video Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> ViSS-R1 introduces a self-supervised reinforcement learning framework to enhance visual understanding in Multimodal Large Language Models for video reasoning. This approach achieved state-of-the-art performance on four out of six video benchmarks, including a 7.2% gain on VSI-Bench, while improving model robustness to visual transformations.

## Problem

- Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) for video reasoning often exhibit a "text-centric bias," underutilizing rich spatio-temporal visual information.
- This bias leads to shortcut learning, where models rely on sparse cues rather than deep visual understanding.
- The over-reliance on textual reasoning increases the susceptibility of MLLMs to generating hallucinated or factually incorrect responses in video tasks.

## Method

- Developed ==Pretext-GRPO==, a self-supervised reinforcement learning method that provides a "warm start" by training the policy model on ==pretext tasks== involving various visual transformations (e.g., rotation, reversal, shuffle).
- Introduced the ViSS-R1 framework, which integrates pretext-task-based self-supervised learning (SSL) directly into a unified Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) and reinforcement learning (RL) pipeline.
- ViSS-R1 utilizes a novel training approach where models simultaneously reason about both visual transformations (identified via new tags) and real user queries, guided by a ==composite reward function==.

## Results

- ViSS-R1 achieved state-of-the-art performance on **4** out of **6** widely-used video reasoning and understanding benchmarks, including VSI-Bench, VideoMMMU, TempCompass, and VideoMME.
- The framework delivered substantial performance improvements, with ViSS-R1 showing gains up to **+7.2%** on VSI-Bench and **+6.1%** on MMVU compared to the base Qwen2.5-VL-7B model.
- ViSS-R1 models demonstrated enhanced robustness to various visual transformations, incurring minimal accuracy loss (e.g., **-1.1%** on VSI-Bench) compared to prior R1 models which saw drops of **-3.2%** to **-6.0%**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating self-supervised visual pretext tasks directly into MLLM reinforcement learning shifts the reasoning paradigm from text-centric to a more robust, visual-centric approach.
> - Training models to identify various spatio-temporal visual transformations provides a strong "warm start" and enhances the MLLM's ability to deeply process and understand video content.
> - A unified framework (ViSS-R1) with simultaneous reasoning for pretext tasks and user queries, guided by a multi-component reward, is effective in improving both reasoning and perceptual understanding.
