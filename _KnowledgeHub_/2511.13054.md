# ViSS-R1: Self-Supervised Reinforcement Video Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> ViSS-R1 introduces a self-supervised reinforcement learning framework to enhance visual understanding in Multimodal Large Language Models for video reasoning. This approach achieved state-of-the-art performance on four out of six video benchmarks, including a 7.2% gain on VSI-Bench, while improving model robustness to visual transformations.

## Problem

- Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) for video reasoning often exhibit a "text-centric bias," underutilizing rich spatio-temporal visual information.
- This bias leads to shortcut learning, where models rely on sparse cues rather than deep visual understanding.
- The over-reliance on textual reasoning increases the susceptibility of MLLMs to generating hallucinated or factually incorrect responses in video tasks.

## Method

- Developed ==Pretext-GRPO==, a self-supervised reinforcement learning method that provides a "warm start" by training the policy model on ==pretext tasks== involving various visual transformations (e.g., rotation, reversal, shuffle).
- Introduced the ViSS-R1 framework, which integrates pretext-task-based self-supervised learning (SSL) directly into a unified Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) and reinforcement learning (RL) pipeline.
- ViSS-R1 utilizes a novel training approach where models simultaneously reason about both visual transformations (identified via new tags) and real user queries, guided by a ==composite reward function==.

## Results

- ViSS-R1 achieved state-of-the-art performance on **4** out of **6** widely-used video reasoning and understanding benchmarks, including VSI-Bench, VideoMMMU, TempCompass, and VideoMME.
- The framework delivered substantial performance improvements, with ViSS-R1 showing gains up to **+7.2%** on VSI-Bench and **+6.1%** on MMVU compared to the base Qwen2.5-VL-7B model.
- ViSS-R1 models demonstrated enhanced robustness to various visual transformations, incurring minimal accuracy loss (e.g., **-1.1%** on VSI-Bench) compared to prior R1 models which saw drops of **-3.2%** to **-6.0%**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating self-supervised visual pretext tasks directly into MLLM reinforcement learning shifts the reasoning paradigm from text-centric to a more robust, visual-centric approach.
> - Training models to identify various spatio-temporal visual transformations provides a strong "warm start" and enhances the MLLM's ability to deeply process and understand video content.
> - A unified framework (ViSS-R1) with simultaneous reasoning for pretext tasks and user queries, guided by a multi-component reward, is effective in improving both reasoning and perceptual understanding.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in "ViSS-R1" is situated at the intersection of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs), video understanding, and reinforcement learning (RL). It addresses a critical limitation in the current paradigm of MLLM-based video reasoning.

**Current Challenges in MLLM Video Reasoning:**
Recent years have seen a surge in MLLMs capable of processing both text and images/videos. Inspired by the success of text-based Large Language Models (LLMs) in complex reasoning tasks, particularly with techniques like Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting and Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Reward (RLVR), researchers have extended these approaches to MLLMs for image and video reasoning. The prevalent "R1 paradigm" typically involves Supervised Finetuning (SFT) with CoT datasets, followed by RL refinement (e.g., PPO, GRPO).

However, as the authors highlight, current R1-based methodologies, especially in the video domain, often suffer from several issues:
- **Text-centric Bias**: Many existing MLLMs for video reasoning primarily treat dense visual information as sparse contextual evidence, relying heavily on text-based reasoning (similar to their LLM counterparts). This leads to underutilization of the rich spatio-temporal information present in videos.
- **Shortcut Learning**: Models may exploit superficial cues or focus on single frames, rather than engaging in deep, comprehensive video understanding, akin to "shortcut learning."
- **Hallucination**: The over-reliance on text-centric reasoning, while overlooking visual information, increases the susceptibility to generating "hallucinated" or factually incorrect responses.

**Role of Self-Supervised Learning (SSL):**
Self-supervised learning has a long history in computer vision for learning robust representations by defining "pretext tasks" that don't require manual annotations. Examples include predicting image rotation, solving jigsaw puzzles, or predicting temporal order in videos. These techniques have been highly effective for pre-training models that are then fine-tuned for downstream tasks.

**Integration of SSL and RL:**
This paper proposes to bridge the gap between robust visual representation learning (via SSL) and advanced reasoning capabilities (via RL). While concurrent works (Jigsaw-R1, VisualJigsaw) have explored self-supervised RL for image-based jigsaw tasks, ViSS-R1 distinguishes itself by focusing on video reasoning and incorporating a broader range of spatio-temporal pretext transformations. It systematically investigates a streamlined and effective integration of visual-centric SSL directly into the R1 post-training framework, moving beyond simple combinations of pretext RL tasks. By compelling MLLMs to process and understand transformed visual inputs, ViSS-R1 aims to foster a more robust, visual-centric understanding that mitigates the aforementioned issues of shortcut learning and hallucination.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation for ViSS-R1 stems from the observed limitations of current MLLMs in truly leveraging the rich visual information in videos for complex reasoning tasks. The authors identify that existing R1-based methods for video often prioritize text-centric reasoning, underutilizing dense visual content and consequently leading to shortcut learning and increased hallucination.

Against this backdrop, the **key objectives** of this research are:

1. **Enhance Visual-Centric Video Understanding**: To shift the paradigm from text-centric to a more robust, visual-centric approach for MLLM video reasoning. This involves compelling models to process and reason about visual inputs more deeply, rather than merely extracting sparse cues.
2. **Introduce Self-Supervised Reinforcement Learning for Video**: To develop novel algorithms that integrate pretext-task-based self-supervised learning (SSL) directly into the Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Reward (R1) post-training framework for MLLMs.
3. **Develop Pretext-GRPO**: To propose a self-supervised reinforcement learning algorithm, Pretext-GRPO, which serves as a "warm start" for the RL policy model by training it to solve pretext tasks on transformed visual inputs, thereby non-trivially processing visual information.
4. **Establish the ViSS-R1 Framework**: To design a unified framework, ViSS-R1, that seamlessly integrates pretext-task-based SSL directly into the MLLM’s R1 post-training paradigm. This framework aims to make models simultaneously reason about both the applied visual transformations and the true user queries.
5. **Demonstrate Effectiveness and Superiority**: To empirically validate the effectiveness and superiority of both Pretext-GRPO and ViSS-R1 on various widely-used video reasoning and understanding benchmarks, showcasing improved performance and robustness.

Essentially, the authors want to teach MLLMs to "see" and "understand" videos more comprehensively before attempting to reason about them, by leveraging the self-supervision inherent in visual transformations.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors propose two main approaches: **Pretext-GRPO** and the integrated **ViSS-R1 framework**, both built upon the standard R1 paradigm for MLLMs.

**Background: Standard R1 Paradigm**
The R1 paradigm for MLLMs typically involves two stages:
1. **Supervised Finetuning (SFT)**: The MLLM is finetuned on carefully curated Chain-of-Thought (CoT) datasets, teaching it to generate structured responses with explicit thinking steps (e.g., `<think>...</think><answer>...</answer>`).
2. **Reinforcement Learning (RL) Refinement**: A GRPO (Group Relative Policy Optimization) algorithm, a variant of PPO, is often used. GRPO generates multiple responses, assigns rewards based on ground truth, and computes advantages to update the policy model, encouraging exploration and reasoning.

#### 3.1. Vision-Oriented Pretext-GRPO

Pretext-GRPO introduces an intermediate, self-supervised RL stage *before* the standard RL refinement on real user questions. Its goal is to provide a "warm start" for the policy model, enabling it to better leverage rich, vision-oriented information.

- **Self-Supervised Transformations (SSTs)**: Instead of real user questions, Pretext-GRPO uses handcrafted pretext questions (`Q_p`) related to various visual transformations applied to the input image or video (`Tr(V)`). All `Q_p` are Multiple Choice Questions (MCQs) for straightforward reward assignment.
 - **Image SSTs**: Rotate (0, 90, 180, 270 degrees), Flip (vertical, horizontal, none), Puzzle (swap 2 of 4 patches). These teach spatial semantics.
 - **Video SSTs**: 3D Rotate (all frames rotated uniformly), Reverse (video played normally or reversed), Shuffle (2 of 4 consecutive clips shuffled). These capture spatio-temporal representations.
- **Reward Assignment**: A reward of 1 is assigned if the pretext question is answered correctly, and 0 otherwise.
- **Optimization**: The GRPO objective is used to update the policy model, but based on `Tr(V)` and `Q_p`. This phase focuses solely on visual-centric tasks without external annotations.

After this Pretext-GRPO stage, the model proceeds with vanilla GRPO optimization using real user questions and raw visual inputs. This combined approach is denoted as Pretext-GRPO+.

#### 3.2. Vision-Integrated ViSS-R1

Recognizing the practical complexity of a two-stage RL process, ViSS-R1 streamlines the integration of SSL directly into a single, unified SFT and RL pipeline, where the model *simultaneously* reasons about both pretext tasks and real user queries.

- **SFT with Transform-Tagged CoT**:
 - **Simultaneous Questions**: The model is prompted with both the pretext question (`Q_p`) (e.g., "Has the video been reversed?") and the actual user query (`Q`) (e.g., "Why does the person extend one arm?"). The visual input (`Tr(V)`) is transformed.
 - **Structured Output**: To handle two questions, a new `<transform></transform>` tag is introduced in the CoT. The model is trained via MLE loss to generate responses that encapsulate the inferred transformation and the answer to the user query within predefined tags. Ground-truth CoT rationales are reconstructed by distilling answers from a powerful MLLM (Qwen2.5-VL-72B) on these transformed inputs.
- **RL Reasoning on Transformed Inputs**:
 - **Joint Optimization**: The transformation-acquainted SFT model then undergoes RL using the GRPO algorithm. The key difference from vanilla GRPO is that the policy samples two types of responses: `o_t` (transformation identification) and `o_q` (user question answer).
 - **Reward Manipulation**: ViSS-R1 incorporates three types of rewards:
 1. **Transformation Reward (R_t)**: 0.5 for correctly identifying the applied transformation (0 otherwise).
 2. **Accuracy Reward (R_a)**: Task-specific accuracy metric (e.g., Exact Match, ROUGE) for the user query.
 3. **Format Reward (R_f)**: Reward for adhering to the predefined output structure (`<think>`, `<transform>`, `<answer>` tags).
 - The total reward `R = R_t + R_a + R_f` drives the policy update.
- **Inference**: During inference, pretext questions are removed, and models reason on untransformed, raw videos. This implies the model implicitly "recovers" the original video or understands its properties by having trained to identify transformations.

The codebase is built on Open-R1, and models are trained on Qwen2.5-VL-7B using mixed image and video datasets (Video-R1-CoT-165k and Video-R1-260k).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The comprehensive evaluations across six widely-used video reasoning and understanding benchmarks consistently demonstrate the effectiveness and superiority of Pretext-GRPO and ViSS-R1.

#### 4.1. Performance Comparisons (Table 1)

- **State-of-the-Art (SOTA) Performance**: Both Pretext-GRPO+ and ViSS-R1 achieve SOTA performance on 4 out of 6 benchmarks:
 - **VSI-Bench (Reasoning)**: Pretext-GRPO+ scores 39.2%.
 - **VideoMMMU (Reasoning)**: Pretext-GRPO+ scores 53.9%.
 - **TempCompass (Understanding)**: ViSS-R1 scores 75.3%.
 - **VideoMME (Understanding)**: ViSS-R1 scores 60.5%.
- **Significant Improvements over Baselines**:
 - **Compared to Qwen2.5-VL-7B (Base Model)**: ViSS-R1 shows substantial gains, e.g., +7.2% on VSI-Bench, +4.3% on VideoMMMU, and +6.1% on MMVU. This highlights the value of the proposed post-training techniques.
 - **Compared to Video-R1 (Existing R1 Model)**: Pretext-GRPO+ yields +3.4% on VSI-Bench and +1.6% on VideoMMMU, while ViSS-R1 achieves +2.1% on TempCompass and +1.2% on VideoMME.
- **Competitive Results**: Even on benchmarks where they aren't leading (MVBench), the models deliver competitive performance, particularly against models that specifically emphasize temporal grounding alignment.

#### 4.2. Ablation Studies (Table 2)

- **Training Data**:
 - Image-only training (with 2D pretext tasks) provides a reasonable baseline (avg. 57.7%), suggesting it learns sufficient spatial knowledge.
 - Video-only training (with spatio-temporal pretext tasks) consistently outperforms image-only training (avg. 59.1%), underscoring the critical role of spatio-temporal representation learning for video.
- **Training Paradigm**:
 - **Pretext-GRPO (warm-start)**: Figure 2(b) shows that Pretext-GRPO alone (without real questions) achieves notable results on reasoning benchmarks, validating the acquisition of vision-centric knowledge.
 - **SFT only (ViSS-R1 context)**: Achieves 54.7% average, indicating it learns instruction following and structured responses but lacks deep reasoning.
 - **RL only (ViSS-R1 context)**: Shows stronger reasoning on most benchmarks (avg. 58.0%) but struggles significantly on VSI-Bench (24.0%), particularly with regression tasks, highlighting the necessity of an SFT cold start for format generation.
 - **Full ViSS-R1 (SFT + RL)**: Achieves the best overall robust and generalizable improvements (avg. 59.4%).
- **Reward Modeling**:
 - `R = R_f + R_t` (format + transformation reward): Yields solid improvements over SFT only (avg. 57.7% vs. 54.7%), demonstrating the standalone benefit of self-supervised transformation identification.
 - `R = R_f + R_a` (format + accuracy reward): Provides strong results (avg. 58.4%), similar to previous R1 methods.
 - **Full ViSS-R1 (R = R_f + R_t + R_a)**: The combination of all three rewards leads to the comprehensively enhanced and superior model (avg. 59.4%).
- **Scaling Transformation Reward (Figure 4)**: Setting `R_t = 0.5` (when `R_a = 1.0`) yields the highest average score and best results across most benchmarks. Higher `R_t` values (e.g., 0.9) lead to a performance drop, suggesting that over-emphasizing pretext tasks diminishes the model's ability to distinguish between pretext and real questions.

#### 4.3. Comparisons on Transformed Videos (Table 3)

- **Robustness to Transformations**: While prior R1 models (Video-R1, VideoRFT) show significant performance drops on transformed videos (e.g., -3.2% to -6.0% on VSI-Bench), Pretext-GRPO+ and ViSS-R1 demonstrate strong robustness, with minimal accuracy loss (e.g., -1.1% to -2.5%).
- **Efficacy of Pretext Task**: The fact that accuracy on untransformed videos (`V`) is better than on transformed videos (`Tr(V)`) for their models confirms that processing transformed videos in the pretext task encourages useful aggregation of information that transfers to untransformed scenarios, enhancing real-world performance.

#### 4.4. Qualitative Results (Figure 5)

- ViSS-R1 exhibits a distinct reasoning pattern: it first analyzes the video content, then answers the question, and finally verifies the answer against options. This contrasts with previous Video-R1 models that often directly attempt to solve the problem by analyzing options without a thorough visual review, demonstrating ViSS-R1's ability to achieve visual-centric reasoning.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "ViSS-R1: Self-Supervised Reinforcement Video Reasoning" paper makes several significant contributions with substantial potential impact on the field of multimodal AI, particularly for video understanding and reasoning.

1. **Addressing the Visual-Centric Gap**: The most critical impact is directly tackling the "text-centric bias" prevalent in current MLLMs for video. By compelling models to deeply engage with spatio-temporal visual information through self-supervised pretext tasks, ViSS-R1 fosters a more genuine and robust visual understanding. This is crucial for tasks requiring fine-grained visual reasoning, where sparse visual cues are insufficient.
2. **Enhancing Robustness and Reducing Hallucination**: The visual-centric approach is explicitly designed to mitigate shortcut learning and reduce hallucination. When a model is trained to process and interpret visual transformations, it develops a more fundamental grasp of visual properties, making its reasoning less susceptible to superficial textual correlations or fabricated visual details. This leads to more reliable and trustworthy MLLM outputs.
3. **Novel Integration of SSL and RL**: The paper introduces a novel and effective method for integrating self-supervised learning with reinforcement learning within the MLLM R1 paradigm. This systematic integration, especially through the ViSS-R1 framework, provides a blueprint for future research in combining these powerful learning paradigms to unlock new capabilities in foundation models.
4. **Annotation-Free Visual Pre-training for Reasoning**: Pretext tasks offer "free" supervision, meaning they don't require expensive human annotations. This significantly reduces the data annotation burden for training MLLMs capable of complex visual reasoning, making the development process more scalable and accessible. The warm-start Pretext-GRPO and the integrated ViSS-R1 both leverage this advantage effectively.
5. **Improved Performance and Generalizability**: The consistent state-of-the-art results across various video reasoning and understanding benchmarks demonstrate the practical efficacy of the proposed methods. The robustness against transformed video inputs further highlights the generalizability and deeper understanding achieved by ViSS-R1, suggesting better performance in diverse and challenging real-world scenarios.
6. **Paving the Way for More Perceptually Intelligent MLLMs**: This work indicates a promising direction for developing future intelligent multimodal models that are not just good at "talking about" visual content but truly "understand" it at a deeper perceptual level. It suggests that by teaching MLLMs to process the fundamental properties of visual data, we can empower them with more sophisticated and human-like reasoning abilities.
7. **Open-Source Contribution**: The commitment to publicly releasing codes and models will significantly accelerate research in this area, allowing other researchers to build upon and further explore the potential of self-supervised reinforcement learning for multimodal reasoning.

In essence, ViSS-R1 represents a significant step towards creating MLLMs that are not only powerful in language generation but are also deeply grounded in visual perception, leading to more intelligent, robust, and less fallible AI systems for complex video analysis.
