# Reactive Diffusion Policy: Slow-Fast Visual-Tactile Policy Learning for Contact-Rich Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> This research introduces the Reactive Diffusion Policy (RDP), a novel slow-fast visual-tactile learning approach, paired with TactAR, a low-cost augmented reality teleoperation system, to enable precise and reactive robot manipulation in contact-rich environments. RDP achieves over 35% improvement in performance across tasks like peeling and wiping compared to existing baselines, demonstrating human-like reactive behaviors and immediate adaptation to disturbances.

## Problem

- Existing visual imitation learning policies execute action chunks in an open-loop manner, limiting real-time reaction to environmental changes or precise contact dynamics in complex tasks.
- Current teleoperation systems often lack real-time, fine-grained tactile/force feedback, hindering the collection of high-quality human demonstrations for contact-rich manipulation.
- Robots struggle to perform dexterous, contact-rich tasks (e.g., peeling, wiping) with human-like precision and adaptability due to insufficient reactive capabilities.

## Method

- Introduces **TactAR**, a low-cost, ==AR-based teleoperation system== for collecting ==visual-tactile demonstration data== with real-time, intuitive ==3D deformation field feedback==.
- Proposes **Reactive Diffusion Policy (RDP)**, a ==slow-fast hierarchical imitation learning== algorithm that uses a ==Latent Diffusion Policy== for high-level planning and an ==Asymmetric Tokenizer== for high-frequency, closed-loop reactive control based on tactile/force input.
- Utilizes a ==low-dimensional PCA representation== of tactile data and employs ==relative trajectory prediction== combined with ==latency matching== to enhance policy generalizability and robustness.

## Results

- **Reactive Diffusion Policy (RDP)** outperforms Diffusion Policy baselines by over **35%** in overall scores across peeling, wiping, and bimanual lifting tasks, achieving success rates of **0.90–0.95** in peeling and **0.77–0.87** in wiping.
- RDP demonstrates immediate reaction to external disturbances, vastly outperforming baselines (e.g., **0.8** vs. **0.15** in perturbation scenarios), by dynamically adjusting trajectories at sub-millimeter scales.
- The TactAR teleoperation system improves data quality, leading to significantly better normalized peeling length (**0.91** vs. **0.72**) and a higher ratio of stable contact force (**0.87** vs. **0.58**) in collected demonstrations.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating tactile signals directly into action prediction via a high-frequency "fast policy" is crucial for real-time reactivity in contact-rich tasks, unlike merely adding them to observations.
> - A slow-fast hierarchical policy architecture effectively balances long-horizon action planning with immediate, fine-grained reactive control using tactile feedback.
> - High-quality, stable tactile/force demonstration data, facilitated by intuitive teleoperation systems, is essential for learning robust and human-like reactive robot behaviors.
