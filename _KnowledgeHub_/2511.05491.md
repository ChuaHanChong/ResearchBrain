# Visual Spatial Tuning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from HKU, ByteDance Seed, and Tsinghua University developed Visual Spatial Tuning (VST), a framework that cultivates human-like visuospatial abilities in general Vision-Language Models without architectural modifications, utilizing extensive spatial datasets and a progressive training pipeline. The approach achieves state-of-the-art spatial understanding across various benchmarks and improves Vision-Language-Action models by 8.6% on robotic manipulation tasks, while preserving general multi-modal capabilities.

## Problem

- Large Vision-Language Models (VLMs) demonstrate limitations in capturing precise spatial relationships and understanding 3D environments from visual inputs.
- Existing methods to improve VLM spatial awareness often involve additional expert encoders, which introduce computational overhead and can degrade general capabilities.
- Prior spatial datasets for VLMs typically focus on narrow aspects of spatial understanding or are limited to supervised fine-tuning, lacking diversity and comprehensive training strategies.

## Method

- A comprehensive ==Visual Spatial Tuning (VST)== framework integrates spatial perception and reasoning into general VLM architectures, built on two large-scale datasets, ==VST-Perception (VST-P)== and ==VST-Reasoning (VST-R)==, covering single-image, multi-image, and video spatial tasks.
- A ==three-stage progressive training pipeline== is employed, involving Supervised Fine-tuning (SFT), Chain-of-Thought (CoT) cold start, and Reinforcement Learning (RL) using the GRPO algorithm.
- The spatial-enhanced VST model is adapted for ==Vision-Language-Action (VLA)== tasks by discretizing the action space and fine-tuning the VLM for robotic manipulation.

## Results

- VST models consistently achieved state-of-the-art performance across various spatial benchmarks, with VST-7B-RL reaching an average spatial score (S-AVG) of **61.2%**.
- The VST-7B-RL model demonstrated superior 3D object detection, achieving **44.2%** AP@15 on SUN RGB-D without a specialized 3D encoder, outperforming existing general and specialized models.
- The framework successfully preserved general multi-modal capabilities, and VST-enhanced Vision-Language-Action models showed an **8.6%** improvement in average success rate on the LIBERO robotic manipulation benchmark.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Comprehensive spatial intelligence, encompassing both perception and reasoning, can be effectively cultivated in general VLM architectures without relying on specialized 3D encoders.
> - Constructing large-scale, diverse datasets that cover single-image, multi-image, and video-based spatial tasks is essential for holistic visuospatial understanding.
> - A progressive training pipeline, starting with supervised fine-tuning for perception and advancing to reinforcement learning for reasoning, proves effective for instilling complex spatial abilities.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

"Visual Spatial Tuning" (VST) is situated within the rapidly evolving field of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) and their capabilities in understanding and interacting with the physical world. While VLMs have demonstrated impressive success in various domains like visual question answering and document understanding, the authors highlight a critical limitation: their deficiency in capturing spatial relationships from visual inputs. This "spatial understanding ability" is deemed a "foundational component of general intelligence" essential for applications in robotics, autonomous driving, and augmented/virtual reality.

Previous research efforts to enhance spatial awareness in VLMs have largely fallen into two categories:
1. **Adding Extra Expert Encoders:** Studies like those incorporating specialized 3D encoders aim to provide models with explicit 3D inductive biases. However, the authors argue this approach "brings extra overhead and usually harms general capabilities" by increasing complexity and potentially degrading performance on broader tasks.
2. **Developing Specialized Datasets:** Other works have focused on creating datasets tailored for spatial understanding. The paper notes that these efforts "have typically concentrated on limited or isolated aspects of spatial understanding," often restricted to single scenarios or only supervised fine-tuning, lacking the diversity of visual input or the comprehensive training strategies required for true spatial reasoning.

VST distinguishes itself by proposing a "comprehensive and integrated framework" that aims to **holistically cultivate human-like visuospatial abilities in VLMs without relying on additional expert encoders or sacrificing general capabilities**. It addresses the shortcomings of previous approaches by:
- **Integrating Perception and Reasoning:** Deconstructing spatial ability into "spatial perception" (discerning relationships) and "spatial reasoning" (mentally manipulating environments), aligning with cognitive science principles.
- **Building Large-Scale, Diverse Datasets:** Constructing VST-P (perception) and VST-R (reasoning) datasets that span single images, multiple images, and videos, covering a wide array of spatial tasks.
- **Employing a Progressive Training Pipeline:** Utilizing both supervised fine-tuning (SFT) for foundational knowledge and reinforcement learning (RL) for advanced reasoning, mirroring human cognitive development.
- **Maintaining Generality:** Explicitly designing the training to preserve and enhance general multi-modal understanding, rather than creating specialized, narrow models.

By focusing on general architectures and a holistic data-driven approach, VST positions itself as a significant step towards developing more robust and versatile VLMs that are "physically grounded," bridging the gap between pixel-level understanding and real-world 3D spatial intelligence. ### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core objectives and motivations behind the Visual Spatial Tuning (VST) framework are multifaceted, aiming to address fundamental limitations in current Vision-Language Models (VLMs) and advance the field towards more human-like general intelligence:

- **Cultivate Human-like Visuospatial Abilities:** The primary goal is to endow VLMs with a comprehensive suite of spatial capabilities, ranging from basic spatial perception (e.g., discerning object depth and relationships) to advanced spatial reasoning (e.g., mentally reconstructing and manipulating spatial layouts from multiple viewpoints). This is explicitly motivated by the observation that "capturing spatial relationships from visual inputs is a cornerstone of human-like general intelligence."
- **Enhance Spatial Ability in General Architectures Without Side-Effects:** A key objective is to achieve this enhanced spatial understanding *without* introducing extra computational overhead or negatively impacting the VLM's existing general capabilities. This directly contrasts with previous approaches that relied on additional "expert encoders" which often brought complexity and performance trade-offs. The motivation is to create truly versatile foundation models.
- **Bridge the Gap Between 2D Pixel Space and 3D Physical Space:** The authors note that while VLMs excel at 2D object recognition and localization, their ability to determine object positions in 3D space is limited. VST aims to address this by training models to discern spatial relationships "beyond the pixel level," moving towards a more accurate understanding of objects in a 3D environment.
- **Enable More Physically Grounded AI:** Ultimately, the enhanced spatial intelligence is motivated by the need for AI systems that can effectively interact with and understand the physical world. This has direct implications for critical applications such as robotics, autonomous driving, and augmented/virtual reality, where a robust understanding of 3D space is paramount for successful operation.
- **Develop a Comprehensive and Scalable Framework:** The project seeks to create an "integrated framework" (VST) that is both general and scalable, allowing foundational spatial knowledge to be systematically injected and refined in VLMs. This includes developing large-scale, diverse datasets and a progressive training pipeline that mimics human cognitive development (perception preceding reasoning).

In essence, VST is driven by the ambition to move VLMs beyond superficial visual understanding to a deeper, more actionable comprehension of space, paving the way for AI agents that can perceive, reason, and act in complex 3D environments with human-like proficiency.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The Visual Spatial Tuning (VST) framework adopts a multi-pronged approach encompassing extensive dataset construction and a progressive, multi-stage training pipeline.

#### 3.1. VST Dataset Construction

The VST framework introduces two large-scale, carefully curated datasets: VST-Perception (VST-P) and VST-Reasoning (VST-R).

- **VST-Perception (VST-P):**
 - **Scale and Scope:** Comprises 4.1 million samples across 19 diverse tasks, designed to equip VLMs with foundational spatial knowledge. It covers three primary vision scenarios: single-image (64.8%), multi-image (33.1%), and video (2.1%).
 - **Single-Image Data (2.5D & 3D Perception):**
 - **Tasks:** Focuses on relative depth estimation (2.5D), 3D object detection, and distance estimation to bridge 2D pixel coordinates and 3D physical space.
 - **Data Collection:** Uses dedicated data engines. Depth data from public datasets (ScanNet++, Hypersim) and synthetic data. Pseudo-labels for wild images (COCO) generated by a depth expert model. 3D data from open-source datasets (ScanNet, ARKitScenes, Hypersim, SUN-RGBD, Matterport3D, Objectron) with standardized 3D bounding boxes to a unified camera coordinate system, and synthetic data from Isaac Sim (using GUTopia scenes).
 - **Innovations:** Introduces a **Field of View (FoV) unification strategy** to normalize input data by projecting images onto a virtual camera with a uniform FoV, mitigating geometric inconsistencies from disparate camera intrinsics for 3D object detection. Mixes single-turn and multi-turn instruction formats for 3D object detection to aid in learning layout information. Generates **scene captions** (beyond general image captions) that focus on layout and spatial relationships, prompted by a large VLM using ground-truth 3D bounding boxes and scene graph relationships.
 - **Multi-Image Data:**
 - **Tasks:** Supports multi-view 3D object detection, multi-view correspondence, object-object relationship understanding, and camera motion analysis.
 - **Data Collection:** Samples from RGB-D scans (ScanNet, ScanNet++, ARKitScenes). Utilizes point clouds and depth maps for correspondence tasks. Standardizes object information and camera poses to the first image's coordinate system.
 - **Video Data:**
 - **Tasks:** Enables capturing spatiotemporal relationships (e.g., appearance order, object counting).
 - **Data Collection:** Uses the same engine as multi-image data, adding object appearance times. Enhances diversity by reorganizing data from VLM-3R into a multi-turn format.

- **VST-Reasoning (VST-R):**
 - **Scale and Scope:** Contains 135K samples with two components: Chain-of-Thought (CoT) processes and rule-checkable answers for online Reinforcement Learning (RL). Includes both spatial and general reasoning data.
 - **CoT Process Generation:** Employs a data engine that samples template-based QA pairs and uses a large VLM as a teacher to generate detailed CoT steps.
 - **Prompting with Bird’s-Eye View (BEV) Annotation:** A novel strategy introduced to address the limitations of current VLMs in multi-view spatial understanding. Ground-truth 3D bounding boxes are visualized as BEV images, which are provided as an auxiliary spatial prompt during CoT generation. This helps the teacher VLM better capture spatial relationships, leading to more coherent and accurate reasoning processes. The CoT adopts a textual representation for generality, first reconstructing spatial layout by text, then inferring the answer.

#### 3.2. Progressive Training Pipeline

VST employs a three-stage progressive training pipeline, mirroring human cognitive development. The base model used is Qwen2.5-VL, leveraging its "ViT-MLP-LLM" architecture.

1. **Stage 1: Supervised Fine-tuning (SFT):**
 - **Objective:** Injects foundational spatial understanding from the VST-P dataset.
 - **Data:** VST-P data combined with a portion of general multi-modal data (from LLaVA-OneVision) to prevent catastrophic forgetting.
 - **Method:** Standard auto-regressive objective for text prediction, conditioned on visual tokens.

2. **Stage 2: CoT Cold Start:**
 - **Objective:** Instructs the model in reasoning patterns using Chain-of-Thought (CoT) data.
 - **Data:** Spatial reasoning data from VST-R, along with general multi-modal reasoning data to preserve general abilities.
 - **Method:** Continues SFT with the same auto-regressive objective. The model from this stage serves as the initial RL actor.

3. **Stage 3: Reinforcement Learning (RL):**
 - **Objective:** Further enhances spatial reasoning capabilities.
 - **Methodology:** Utilizes the Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) algorithm, which bypasses the need for a value model by comparing responses within a group.
 - **Reward Function:** Employs a mixed rule-based reward `R(y, ŷ) = R_acc(y, ŷ) + R_format(y, ŷ)`.
 - `R_acc`: Accuracy reward for correctness (standard protocols for multiple-choice, open-ended, OCR). For 3D object detection, it's a linear combination of 3D Intersection over Union (IoU) and F1 score, defined by `αR_iou + (1-α)R_F1`.
 - `R_format`: Incentivizes adherence to specified output format.
 - **Data:** A curated verification dataset comprising spatial understanding, 3D object detection, and general multi-modal understanding tasks (multiple-choice, open-ended, OCR, 3D detection).

#### 3.3. Expanding to Vision-Language-Action (VLA) Model

The spatial-enhanced VST model is adapted for robotic manipulation tasks.
- **Method:** Formulates action prediction as a VLM task. The action space is discretized into 256 bins, each mapped to a special token in the language tokenizer.
- **Training:** The VLM (and its action embeddings) is fine-tuned from scratch on robotic datasets (e.g., LIBERO) using the same auto-regressive objective.

This comprehensive methodology, integrating bespoke data generation with a sophisticated multi-stage training paradigm, is central to VST's ability to imbue VLMs with robust spatial intelligence.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The "Visual Spatial Tuning" (VST) framework demonstrates significant advancements in endowing Vision-Language Models (VLMs) with spatial understanding and reasoning capabilities, while notably preserving their general multi-modal intelligence.

#### 4.1. State-of-the-Art Spatial Understanding and Reasoning

- **Overall Performance:** VST models consistently achieve state-of-the-art (SOTA) results on several spatial benchmarks (Table 2). The VST-7B-RL model, for instance, achieves an average spatial score (S-AVG) of 61.2%, outperforming comparable VLMs.
- **Single-Image Spatial Ability:**
 - **CVBench:** VST-7B-SFT scores 85.5%, surpassing proprietary models like Seed1.5-VL (85.2%).
 - **3D Object Detection (SUN RGB-D, ARKitScenes):** VST-7B-RL achieves 44.2% AP@15 on SUN RGB-D, ranking first among general VLMs (e.g., Gemini-2.0-Pro 32.5%, Seed1.5-VL 33.5%) and outperforming specialized 3D methods (e.g., Implicit3D 24.1%, Total3DU 14.3%), crucially *without* any specialized 3D encoder (Table 4). This highlights VST's ability to derive 3D understanding from purely visual tuning.
- **Multi-Image Spatial Ability:**
 - **MMSI-Bench:** VST-7B-RL achieves 34.8%, outperforming GPT-4o (30.3%) and approaching Gemini-2.5-Pro (36.9%).
 - **BLINK:** VST-7B-SFT scores 62.1%, exceeding Qwen2.5-VL-7B (56.4%).
- **Video-based Spatial Ability:**
 - **VSIBench:** VST-7B-SFT achieves 60.6%, and VST-7B-RL reaches 61.2%, significantly outperforming GPT-4o (34.0%) and even specialized models like VLM-3R-7B (60.9%) which uses an expert 3D encoder (Table 3). VST shows particular strengths in Object Size and Room Size estimation, and strong performance in Relative Direction and Appearance Order tasks.

#### 4.2. Preservation of General Multi-Modal Capabilities

Despite significant spatial enhancements, VST models maintain strong competitiveness on general multi-modal benchmarks (MM-AVG in Table 2). While VST-SFT models show moderate results compared to top proprietary models (e.g., VST-7B-SFT scored 83.3% on MMBench), they still offer a "well-balanced overall performance," demonstrating that the spatial tuning does not incur significant catastrophic forgetting.

#### 4.3. Effectiveness of the Progressive Training Pipeline and Dataset Components

- **Data Ablations (Tables 5, 6, 7):** Incremental introduction of different VST-P data types (3D Object Detection, Scene Captioning, Measurement, Depth) progressively improves spatial perception, with depth data providing significant gains on CVBench-3D and VSIBench. Multi-image data specifically boosts MMSI-Bench and BLINK, while VST-video data yields a substantial 16.6% improvement on VSI-Bench.
- **Model and Data Scaling (Tables 8, 9):**
 - **Model Size:** Scaling from 3B to 7B and then to 32B consistently improves S-AVG on spatial benchmarks (1.3% and 1.7% gains respectively). However, 3D object detection performance saturates at the 7B model, suggesting foundational perception tasks might be sufficiently handled by smaller models.
 - **Data Scale:** Tripling the VST dataset size leads to consistent improvements across all model sizes (1.1% to 1.7% on S-AVG), indicating the value of larger, diverse spatial data.
- **Reasoning Ablations (Tables 10, 11):**
 - **Prompting with BEV Annotation:** The "RT-CoT w/ BEV" method significantly improves spatial reasoning on MMSI-Bench compared to numerical CoT or text-only CoT, showing that explicit spatial grounding (BEV images) enhances the clarity and accuracy of generated reasoning traces.
 - **Reinforcement Learning:** RL (Stage 3) is crucial for refining spatial reasoning. Post-RL, the model's performance on MMSI-Bench improves from 31.7% (CoT cold start with CoT inference) to 35.3% (RL with CoT inference), confirming RL's role in developing stronger reasoning abilities.
- **3D Object Detection Settings (Tables 12, 13):** FoV unification is critical for consistent 3D detection across varied datasets. Mixing single-turn and multi-turn data formats proves beneficial, and the combination of 3D IoU and F1 score as an RL reward function is more effective than IoU+Recall for detection tasks.

#### 4.4. Enhancement of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) Models

- **LIBERO Benchmark:** A VLA model based on VST-tuned Qwen2.5-VL-3B achieves an 8.6% average success rate improvement over the non-VST-tuned baseline on the LIBERO benchmark (Table 14), even without pre-training on robotic data. This demonstrates that integrating spatial knowledge from VST directly enhances robotic manipulation capabilities, leading to more "grounded interaction with the physical world."

In summary, VST successfully demonstrates that a data-centric and progressive training approach can imbue general VLMs with superior spatial intelligence, enabling them to perceive and reason in 3D environments, all while preserving their broad multi-modal understanding and significantly enhancing their utility in real-world action tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "Visual Spatial Tuning" (VST) framework presents a highly significant contribution to the field of artificial intelligence, particularly for Vision-Language Models (VLMs), with profound potential impacts across various domains.

#### 5.1. Advancing General-Purpose AI with Human-like Spatial Intelligence

The most significant impact of VST lies in its demonstration that human-like spatial perception and reasoning can be cultivated in **general-purpose VLMs without requiring specialized architectural modifications or additional expert encoders**. This is a paradigm shift, as it avoids the trade-offs (increased overhead, potential harm to general capabilities) associated with prior "expert encoder" approaches. By showing that sophisticated spatial intelligence can emerge purely through data and a progressive training pipeline within existing VLM architectures, VST paves the way for truly unified, multi-talented AI models that possess a deeper understanding of the physical world. This is crucial for building Artificial General Intelligence (AGI) that can interact intuitively with 3D environments.

#### 5.2. Enabling More Physically Grounded AI Systems

The paper explicitly states its goal of "paving the way for more physically grounded AI." The significant performance gains on spatial benchmarks and, crucially, the demonstrated improvement in Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models on robotic manipulation tasks (LIBERO benchmark) underscore this potential. Robots and autonomous systems require robust spatial understanding to perceive their surroundings, plan movements, and interact with objects. VST's ability to enhance these foundational spatial skills in VLMs means that future robotic agents can be trained more effectively, leading to:
- **Improved Robotics:** More intelligent robots capable of complex manipulation, navigation, and interaction in unstructured environments.
- **Enhanced Autonomous Driving:** Better perception of object distances, relative positions, and scene layouts for safer and more reliable self-driving vehicles.
- **Advanced AR/VR:** More realistic and immersive experiences where AI can accurately understand and place virtual objects within real-world spatial contexts.

#### 5.3. Scalability and Generalizability of Spatial Abilities

The framework's generality and scalability are key. The VST datasets, spanning single images, multi-images, and videos across a wide range of tasks, provide a rich foundation. The progressive training approach (SFT followed by RL) mirrors human learning, offering a robust method to instill complex cognitive abilities. The ablation studies confirm the incremental benefits of each data type and training stage, highlighting a systematic path to scaling spatial intelligence. This suggests that as VLMs and datasets grow, their spatial capabilities will continue to improve without fundamental architectural redesigns.

#### 5.4. Open Science Contribution

The release of the code (https://github.com/Yangr116/VST) is a valuable contribution to the research community. It allows other researchers to replicate the findings, build upon the VST framework, and integrate its spatial tuning paradigm into their own VLM development, accelerating progress in spatial AI.

In conclusion, "Visual Spatial Tuning" marks a significant leap towards imbuing VLMs with human-like spatial intelligence. By providing a comprehensive, data-driven, and architecturally-agnostic solution, VST not only addresses a critical limitation of current AI but also sets a strong foundation for the development of more intelligent, adaptable, and physically grounded AI systems that can truly perceive, reason, and act in the complex 3D world.
