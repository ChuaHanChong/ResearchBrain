# Inference-time Scaling of Diffusion Models through Classical Search

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Helixon US Inc., Stanford University, and Harvard University introduce a unified framework that scales diffusion model inference through classical search algorithms. This approach jointly leverages global and local search strategies, including an adaptive Depth-First Search (DFS) and annealed Langevin MCMC, to efficiently generate higher-quality outputs and achieve state-of-the-art performance across diverse tasks without retraining.

## Problem

- Diffusion model outputs often lack precise alignment with specific real-world objectives, physical laws, or task-specific constraints.
- Existing inference-time control methods are either computationally expensive, prone to generating out-of-distribution samples, or limited to exploring modes within the base model's distribution.
- Efficiently controlling and adapting generated samples to meet diverse test-time objectives in continuous, high-dimensional generative spaces remains a significant challenge.

## Method

- The work formulates diffusion model inference as a ==search problem==, aiming to sample from a compositional distribution that combines the original model's distribution with a verifier function.
- It introduces a dual search strategy: global search using classical tree-search algorithms (BFS and an adaptive DFS) for efficient mode identification, and local search via annealed Langevin MCMC for optimizing samples beyond the base model's capabilities.
- A double-verifier strategy is proposed to mitigate reward hacking, using distinct verifiers for local and global search to reduce overestimation and improve robustness.

## Results

- An improved BFS design, using 'Increase' tempering, 'Max' scoring, and 'SSP' resampling, consistently outperformed prior particle-based methods on image generation tasks.
- Adaptive DFS demonstrated superior efficiency, achieving comparable or better performance with up to **2x** less computational cost on text-to-image generation while dynamically allocating compute based on prompt difficulty.
- The Test-Time Search (TTS) method achieved state-of-the-art performance in offline reinforcement learning on D4RL tasks without model retraining, and showed that fine-tuning the base model on TTS-generated actions can outperform existing online RL methods.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Classical search algorithms, specifically an adaptive DFS with backtracking, can effectively navigate continuous generative spaces, enabling dynamic compute allocation based on task difficulty.
> - Recurrent training-free guidance in diffusion models is theoretically unified with annealed Langevin MCMC, providing a principled understanding of its ability to pull samples back to the data manifold and prevent out-of-distribution samples.
> - Jointly scaling both global and local search strategies provides a more efficient and effective Pareto frontier for diffusion model performance, surpassing methods that scale these components in isolation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Diffusion models have emerged as a leading paradigm in generative AI, demonstrating impressive capabilities across continuous domains such as image and video synthesis, and increasingly in robotics and decision-making for diverse action generation. However, a significant challenge remains: the generated outputs may not always precisely align with specific real-world objectives, such as physical laws, human intent, or task-specific constraints. Achieving "inference-time control"—adapting generated samples to meet diverse test-time objectives—often requires multiple trials and significant computational resources, as the generative space is vast and complex.

The existing landscape of addressing this challenge includes several lines of work:
- **Particle-based and Tree-search methods:** These approaches, often inspired by Sequential Monte Carlo (SMC) or best-first search, denoise multiple "particles" or paths simultaneously, evaluating intermediate states to prioritize promising candidates. Examples include SVDD, DAS, and FK-steering. While these methods improve efficiency over naive Best-of-N sampling, they typically employ fixed schedules and are generally limited to exploring modes within the base diffusion model's distribution.
- **Gradient-based guidance:** This category includes classifier guidance (which requires training a noise-dependent classifier) and training-free guidance (which approximates conditional probabilities using denoised outputs). While powerful, training-free methods can suffer from inherent biases due to their approximations, and gradient updates can sometimes lead to out-of-distribution (OOD) or "adversarial" samples if not carefully managed.
- **Classical Search Algorithms:** Algorithms like Breadth-First Search (BFS) and Depth-First Search (DFS) are foundational to Artificial Intelligence for navigating discrete state spaces. Their application to the continuous, high-dimensional generative spaces of diffusion models, particularly for dynamic compute allocation and exploration, is a relatively nascent area.

This paper bridges these existing lines of research by proposing a unified and principled framework. It reinterprets diffusion model inference through the lens of classical search algorithms, applying BFS and DFS for *global exploration* of diverse modes. Crucially, it then integrates a novel *local search* mechanism based on annealed Langevin MCMC. This dual approach addresses key limitations of prior works:
- It moves beyond fixed-schedule, linear search by introducing adaptive DFS.
- It overcomes the limitation of existing global search methods that are confined to the base model's distribution by using local search to explore high-reward regions *beyond* the pretrained model.
- It provides a theoretical underpinning for the connection between recurrent training-free guidance and asymptotically exact Langevin MCMC sampling, shedding light on the effectiveness and robustness of recurrent guidance.

By jointly scaling local and global search for the first time, this work aims to establish a new Pareto frontier in inference-time scaling, offering superior performance and efficiency across diverse and challenging decision-making domains, thereby significantly advancing the field of controllable and robust generative AI.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a general and principled framework for "inference-time scaling" in diffusion models. This scaling aims to efficiently control and adapt the generated outputs to meet diverse, task-specific objectives defined at test-time.

The core motivations driving this work are:
1. **Bridging the Gap between Generation and Utility:** While diffusion models excel at generating diverse samples, these samples often do not inherently optimize for specific criteria (e.g., physical validity in planning, high reward in reinforcement learning, or adherence to complex constraints in image generation). The authors seek to develop methods that can systematically steer the generation process towards higher-quality, more desirable outputs.
2. **Addressing Limitations of Current Inference-Time Control:** Existing methods for enhancing diffusion model inference have shortcomings. Particle-based and tree-search methods, while improvements over naive sampling, often operate with fixed computational budgets and are generally restricted to the manifold of the pretrained base model. Gradient-based guidance methods, though powerful, can be computationally expensive (requiring additional training) or prone to generating out-of-distribution (OOD) or adversarial samples due to approximation biases.
3. **Leveraging Classical AI Principles for Modern Generative Models:** The authors are motivated by the foundational role of classical search algorithms in AI. They aim to demonstrate that these well-established principles can provide a robust and practical framework for navigating the continuous, high-dimensional generative spaces of diffusion models, which has largely been underexplored in a unified manner.
4. **Enabling Exploration Beyond the Base Model:** A critical motivation is to go beyond merely identifying high-score modes within the base model's distribution. The authors want to enable the diffusion model to *generate* samples that are qualitatively better than what the original model could produce, by exploring high-reward regions in the sample space, even if these are slightly off the original data manifold.
5. **Improving Efficiency and Adaptivity:** The goal is to design search strategies that are not only effective but also computationally efficient and adaptive. Specifically, they aim for methods that can dynamically allocate compute based on the difficulty of a specific generation task or the quality of intermediate samples, avoiding wasted computation on easier instances.
6. **Providing Theoretical Grounding:** For existing practical but less understood techniques, such as recurrent training-free guidance, a key objective is to provide a theoretical unification with established MCMC methods, offering deeper insights into their behavior and facilitating principled extensions.

In essence, the paper strives to create a flexible, efficient, and theoretically sound framework that significantly enhances the ability to control and optimize diffusion model outputs at inference time, pushing the boundaries of what these powerful generative models can achieve for practical applications.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core methodology revolves around formulating inference-time control in diffusion models as a search problem aimed at sampling from a compositional distribution `p̃0(x0) ∝ p0(x0)f(x0)^λ`. This objective combines the original model's distribution `p0(x0)` with a "verifier function" `f(x0)` that quantifies sample quality. To achieve this, the authors propose a unified framework that orchestrates two complementary search strategies: **global search** for efficient mode identification and **local search** for optimizing samples beyond the base model's capabilities.

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation

The problem is defined as biasing the sampling process towards regions where `f(x0)` is high, effectively sampling from `p̃0(x0) ∝ p0(x0)f(x0)^λ`, where `λ` controls the verifier's influence. This requires navigating the generative manifold to find high-quality samples.

#### 3.2. Global Search for Mode Identification

Global search aims to efficiently explore the diverse modes of the diffusion model's generative landscape. The denoising process is abstracted as a fixed-depth tree, allowing the application of classical tree-search heuristics. Intermediate states `xt` are evaluated using estimates of the verifier score `f(x0|t)`, facilitating dynamic compute allocation.

- **Unified BFS-style Linear Search:** This approach processes multiple particles in parallel at each denoising step. It generalizes existing particle-based and tree-search methods (e.g., SVDD, DAS, FK-steering) by exploring a comprehensive design space for:
 - **Tempering:** Re-weighting intermediate scores (`τt f(x0|t)`) to mitigate bias in early steps (Constant, Increase, Inf schedules).
 - **Scoring:** Defining how particles are evaluated based on their estimated rewards (`f(x0|s)` for `s ≥ t`), including Current, Difference, and Max scoring functions.
 - **Resampling:** Strategies for allocating children to high-scoring nodes (Multinomial or Srinivasan Sampling Process (SSP) resampling).
 The authors propose an improved BFS design based on "Increase" tempering, "Max" scoring, and "SSP" resampling, demonstrating its superiority over prior methods.

- **DFS-style Non-linear Search:** This novel approach explores a single branch of the search tree as deeply as possible. If a particle's estimated verifier score `f(x0|t)` drops below a predefined threshold `δt`, the algorithm "backtracks" by reintroducing noise (`q(xt_next | xt)`) to a higher noise level `t_next = t + ∆T`. This allows for adaptive compute allocation: difficult instances naturally trigger more backtracking and exploration, while easier instances are solved more directly. The threshold `δt` acts as a control knob for balancing output quality and computational cost.

#### 3.3. Scaling Local Search via Langevin MCMC with Verifier Gradient

Global search identifies high-scoring modes within the base model's distribution, but cannot generate samples that fundamentally *exceed* the base model's capabilities. To achieve this, local search is introduced, aiming to optimize the compositional objective `p̃0(x0)` and sample from high-reward regions beyond the model.

- **Annealed Langevin MCMC:** The method frames the sampling problem as compositional optimization in measure space. It constructs a sequence of annealed distributions `˜qt(xt) ∝ qt(xt)f̂t(xt)` and draws samples using Langevin dynamics (`x_i+1 = x_i + η∇x log ˜q_t(x_i) + √2ηϵ_i`). The key is that the score of `˜qt` can be directly computed by composing the base diffusion model's score function (`∇x log qt(xt)`) with the gradient of the verifier's annealed function (`∇x log f̂t(xt)`).
- **Theoretical Unification with Recurrent Training-Free Guidance:** The paper provides a significant theoretical contribution by proving that, in the continuous limit, training-free guidance with recurrence (a practical method for improving guidance) is equivalent to running annealed Langevin MCMC. This establishes a strong theoretical foundation for recurrent guidance, interpreting the recurrence steps as MCMC iterations that pull samples back to the data manifold, preventing OOD samples often seen with naive gradient ascent.
- **Implementation:** Local search is implemented by parameterizing the reverse transition kernel as a sequence of Langevin MCMC steps followed by a denoising step (DDIM or DDPM). The number of recurrence steps directly corresponds to the number of local search steps.

#### 3.4. Joint Scaling and Double Verifier Strategy

The framework explicitly combines both global and local search. Furthermore, to mitigate the "reward hacking" problem (where gradient-guided methods can over-optimize a verifier, leading to OOD samples), a **double-verifier strategy** is proposed. This involves using distinct verifiers for local and global search, similar to double-Q learning in RL, to reduce overestimation and improve robustness.

In summary, the methodology is a sophisticated integration of classical search algorithms for efficient high-level exploration, and theoretically grounded MCMC for fine-grained, model-transcending optimization, unified under a compositional sampling objective and enhanced with robustness mechanisms.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation validates the effectiveness, efficiency, and adaptivity of the proposed framework across a diverse set of challenging domains.

#### 4.1. Elucidating the Design Space of BFS

- **Improved BFS:** Through systematic ablation studies on image generation tasks (using SD v1.5 and SDXL models with ImageReward verifier), the authors identified optimal design choices for BFS. They found that **SSP (Srinivasan Sampling Process) resampling** significantly outperforms naive multinomial resampling, while "Max" scoring and "Increase" tempering schedules provide modest but consistent improvements.
- **Superior Performance:** The resulting improved BFS design (Increase, Max, SSP) consistently outperforms prior state-of-the-art particle-based or tree-search methods (FK-steering, DAS, TreeG, SVDD) across different compute budgets (number of particles N=4, 8) and diffusion models (SD v1.5, SDXL). This establishes a stronger baseline for global search.

#### 4.2. Adaptive and Efficient Inference-Scaling with DFS

- **Outperformance and Efficiency:** Evaluated on the CompBench text-to-image dataset using an SSD-1B model and a VLM verifier, DFS consistently outperforms both Best-of-N sampling and the improved BFS design. It achieves comparable or better performance with up to **2x less computational cost**, demonstrating superior efficiency.
- **Adaptive Compute Allocation:** DFS exhibits remarkable adaptivity. It dynamically adjusts compute allocation based on the difficulty of the prompt: harder prompts (defined by lower average scores of random samples) naturally consume more compute (more backtracking and exploration) without requiring prior knowledge of difficulty. This is a significant advantage over linear-search methods with fixed schedules.
- **Robustness to Thresholds:** DFS performs robustly across a wide range of practical threshold values, indicating its stability and user-friendliness.

#### 4.3. Joint Scaling Local and Global Search

This section highlights the critical importance of jointly scaling both local and global search, especially in challenging decision-making domains.

- **Long Horizon Planning (PointMaze Environment):**
 - **Local Search Effectiveness:** Scaling up local search steps (denoted BoN-i, where 'i' is the number of local search steps) significantly improves the overall Pareto frontier for trajectory success rate versus computational cost (NFEs). It dramatically outperforms baselines like TFG (which struggles to scale with high compute) and DAS (which underperforms BoN with increased local search). Visualizations confirm that local search helps overcome maze violations, leading to successful plans.
 - **Joint Scaling Benefits:** Combining local search with global search techniques like BFS and DFS further boosts scaling efficiency, demonstrating the flexibility and power of the unified framework.

- **Offline Reinforcement Learning (D4RL Locomotion Tasks):**
 - **SOTA Performance without Training:** The proposed Test-Time Search (TTS) method achieves performance comparable to, and often exceeding, state-of-the-art *training-based* offline RL baselines (e.g., IQL, SfBC, Diffuser, QGPO). This is a strong result, as TTS operates purely at inference time, composing off-the-shelf diffusion policies with Q-functions without retraining.
 - **Outperformance on Challenging Datasets:** TTS particularly excels on "Medium" and "Medium-Replay" datasets where the base model's capabilities are more limited, outperforming TFG and DAS, which struggle in these scenarios.
 - **Advancing Online Learning via Policy Distillation:** A novel finding is that fine-tuning the base diffusion model on actions generated by TTS (offline distillation) can outperform SOTA diffusion-based *online* RL methods (e.g., DPPO). This "expert iteration" paradigm, leveraging inference scaling to generate high-quality data for model improvement, is identified as a promising new research direction.

#### 4.4. Mitigating Reward Hacking with Double Verifier

- **Improved Robustness and Quality:** On the ImageNet conditional generation task, the double-verifier strategy (using separate verifiers for local and global search) significantly improves FID (Fréchet Inception Distance) and class accuracy compared to a single verifier.
- **Reduced OOD Samples:** The double-verifier approach also leads to higher MSP (Maximum Softmax Probability) scores, indicating a reduction in out-of-distribution (OOD) samples and enhanced robustness. This directly addresses the known vulnerability of gradient-based guidance to adversarial reward hacking, where over-optimization can lead to OOD samples that exploit verifier weaknesses.

Overall, the results demonstrate that the proposed framework not only provides a principled approach to inference-time scaling but also delivers concrete, measurable improvements in performance, efficiency, and robustness across a variety of demanding generative AI tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work presents a significant advancement in the field of controllable generative AI, particularly for diffusion models. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **A Principled and Unified Framework:** The paper establishes classical search algorithms as a robust and principled foundation for inference-time scaling of diffusion models. By explicitly unifying local and global search within this classical AI paradigm, it offers a coherent framework that simplifies the understanding and development of advanced control strategies for diffusion models.
2. **Unlocking New Performance Ceilings:** By demonstrating the power of *jointly* scaling local and global search, the research significantly pushes the Pareto frontier for diffusion model inference across complex decision-making and generation tasks. Previous methods often scaled these components in isolation, leading to suboptimal performance. This integrated approach allows diffusion models to consistently produce higher-quality outputs with greater efficiency.
3. **Enhanced Adaptivity and Efficiency:** The introduction of adaptive DFS is a notable contribution, offering superior efficiency and the ability to dynamically allocate computational resources based on task difficulty. This is highly impactful for real-world applications where task complexity can vary, allowing for more intelligent and resource-aware generation.
4. **Theoretical Clarity for Practical Methods:** The theoretical unification of annealed Langevin MCMC with recurrent training-free guidance provides crucial insights into the inner workings and robustness of a widely used practical technique. This grounding can guide future research in developing more theoretically sound and empirically effective guidance strategies.
5. **Broad Applicability Across Domains:** The demonstration of significant gains across diverse domains—including text-to-image generation, long-horizon planning, and offline reinforcement learning—underscores the generalizability and versatility of the proposed framework. This broad applicability suggests its potential to impact various subfields of AI where controllable and high-quality generation is critical.
6. **Advancing Decision-Making Capabilities:** In the context of robotics and reinforcement learning, the ability to generate high-value actions and robust plans without requiring extensive retraining of the diffusion model is transformative. The discovery that inference-time scaling can facilitate "expert iteration" through policy distillation, outperforming SOTA online RL methods, opens exciting new avenues for improving agent capabilities.
7. **Addressing Robustness Challenges:** The double-verifier strategy is a practical and effective solution to the critical problem of reward hacking and OOD sample generation in gradient-guided diffusion models. This enhances the reliability and trustworthiness of controlled generation, which is paramount for sensitive applications.

In conclusion, this research provides a comprehensive and impactful framework for inference-time scaling in diffusion models. By creatively re-imagining classical search within the context of modern generative AI, and by offering both theoretical insights and strong empirical results, it lays a solid foundation for more intelligent, efficient, and controllable AI generation, with widespread implications for future research and practical deployment across many domains. The identified limitation of hyperparameter tuning also hints at future work on more automated search strategies, such as evolutionary search, to further enhance accessibility and performance.
