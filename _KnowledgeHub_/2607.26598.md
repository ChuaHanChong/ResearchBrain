# Living-Harness Is an Interactive-Agent Evolver

> [!summary] Summary
> Living-Harness is a self-evolving agent harness. After each evaluated episode, a domain-level Evolution-SOP turns the trajectory and evaluator signals into bounded, gated updates of two stores: episodic memory (triggers, failures, recoveries) and a state graph (states, repair edges, transitions). Tools and base context stay frozen. With GPT-5.2 it beats the strongest interactive baselines by about 10 Pass@1 points on τ²-Bench and MultiWOZ-2.4.

## Problem

- Static agent harnesses fix prompts, tools, memory and workflows before deployment, so they cannot absorb failure patterns or recovery actions observed afterward.
- Reflection and experience-memory methods store textual lessons that help one retry but do not specify the trigger condition, required tool action or workflow transition that would prevent recurrence.
- Self-evolving agent systems usually evolve a single artifact, such as memory, skills or workflows, instead of coordinating experiential and workflow knowledge from evaluator-grounded failure evidence.

## Method

- **Evolution-SOP**: a fixed domain-level ==post-episode protocol==. It abstracts each trajectory, extracts candidate repairs, and commits them only after checking ==evidential support==, task scope and consistency with tool constraints.
- ==Dual harness state==: ==episodic memory== stores trigger conditions, failure patterns and recovery actions; a ==state graph== stores state nodes, ==repair edges== and transition rules. Both are retrieved top-3 per task as ==procedural context==.
- **Living-Harness** runs a ==rollout-evaluate-update loop== with frozen tools and base context, framed as a ==program-state POMDP==. A proposition shows informative procedural context cannot increase the ==Bayes prediction error==.

## Results

- On τ²-Bench with GPT-5.2, average Pass@1 reaches **83.09**, versus **73.02** for Reflexion and **82.92** for Gemini 3 Pro. On MultiWOZ-2.4 it reaches **65.50**, versus **55.59** for ReasoningBank.
- In the τ²-Bench ablation, removing the Evolution-SOP drops the average from **83.09** to **73.38**. Removing memory gives **77.34** and removing the state graph gives **79.50**. The largest gains come in the first evolution cycle.
- A frozen harness state evolved with GPT-5.2 transfers, retrieval-only, to Gemini 3 Pro, GLM-5, Qwen3-max and Kimi-k2, improving every MultiWOZ domain. On Taxi, **three** of these backbones rise from **0.00** to over **43**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Recognizing a failure is not the same as repairing it: Reflexion knew to transfer the user but still skipped the tool call. Storing state-conditioned, executable repairs closes this gap.
> - The update protocol matters more than the storage containers. A generic extractor writing to the same memory and graph loses most of the gain.
> - Procedural knowledge kept outside model weights is portable across backbones, so a harness state learned once with one model can be reused by other models.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work is situated within the field of artificial intelligence, specifically focusing on the development and improvement of large language model (LLM) agents for interactive tasks. The broader research landscape for LLM agents involves their deployment as decision-making interfaces in environments requiring task-oriented dialogue and tool mediation.

Existing research has explored various aspects of LLM agents:
- **Interactive LLM Agents:** Efforts have been made to enable language models to act over multiple turns by conditioning on observations, user feedback, tool outputs, and task constraints. This includes developments in task-oriented dialogue systems (e.g., MultiWOZ, Schema-Guided Dialogue) and tool-agent-user benchmarks (e.g., τ²-Bench). Systems such as WebGPT, ReAct, Toolformer, AutoGen, and Reflexion represent advancements in reasoning, tool use, coordination, and trial-level adaptation within individual interactions. However, the paper identifies a limitation in these approaches: while agents may recognize errors or recover locally, the corrective actions often do not persist beyond the completed episode, leading to recurring failures.
- **Agent Harnesses:** As LLM agents integrate more complex functionalities like tool use, memory, and multi-step execution, external "harnesses" have emerged as control layers. These harnesses organize prompts, tools, context, memory, workflows, and evaluation interfaces around the base LLM, aiming to improve reliability. However, existing harness systems are typically static, designed or optimized before deployment, and thus unable to incorporate new observations or evolve their procedural structures over time.
- **Self-Evolving Agent Systems:** Another line of research investigates systems that convert interaction experience into persistent artifacts such as memory, prompts, skills, workflows, or policies. Methods like Reflexion store critiques or trajectories as reusable lessons, and skill-centered systems acquire and evolve capabilities. However, these often focus on evolving a particular artifact in isolation.

The "Living-Harness" work distinguishes itself by addressing a specific gap: the absence of a mechanism to convert *evaluator-grounded post-episode failure evidence into persistent, reusable procedural repairs* that modify the agent's guiding harness. Instead of merely providing task-local feedback or evolving a single component, Living-Harness focuses on a coordinated, bounded evolution of the harness state itself, comprising both experiential memory and structured workflow. This approach aims to enable agents to learn not just from individual responses or retries, but from cumulative experience to improve their fundamental operational procedures across multiple interactions.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from an identified limitation in current LLM agent systems: while an agent might successfully recover from a failure within a single episode or after a task-local retry, the underlying cause of the execution failure can frequently recur in subsequent, similar tasks. This recurrence happens because the corrective feedback or successful recovery action is typically ephemeral, disappearing with the completion of the episode, and rarely leads to a revision of the persistent "harness" that dictates how the agent interacts in future scenarios.

Existing "static harnesses" provide a predefined structure for prompts, tools, context, memory, and workflows, which enhances reliability. However, they are fundamentally fixed after deployment, preventing them from incorporating newly observed failure patterns or effective recovery actions into their future procedural guidance. This leads to a concrete research question: How can an agent convert post-episode failures into persistent, structural procedural repairs?

To address this, the authors establish several requirements for an effective procedural-repair mechanism:
1. **Grounded in Evidence:** Any update must be based on a completed trajectory and its corresponding evaluation signals, ensuring that repairs are validated externally rather than solely on an unverified self-critique.
2. **Persistence and Scope:** The repair must persist across multiple episodes and be appropriately scoped to the relevant task family or specific failure condition, ensuring its applicability without overgeneralization.
3. **Bounded Adaptation:** Adaptation should exclusively modify the stateful components of the harness, specifically episodic memory and the state graph. The available tools and core context should remain frozen, preventing accumulated experience from altering the agent’s fundamental operational boundaries.
4. **Complementary Knowledge Representation:** The persistent state needs to capture two distinct but complementary forms of knowledge:
 - **Experiential Knowledge:** Recording the "why" and "under what condition" a failure occurred and "how" it was successfully recovered.
 - **Workflow Knowledge:** Documenting "which state-conditioned action or transition" needs to be introduced or revised within the agent's procedural flow.

The overarching objective of this work is to introduce **Living-Harness**, a self-evolving agent harness designed to meet these requirements. Living-Harness aims to transform evaluated interaction trajectories into persistent procedural repairs, thereby enabling agents to progressively improve their future interactions by learning from accumulated experience and systematically addressing recurring failures.

The specific contributions of this paper, as outlined by the authors, are:
- **Identification of the gap:** Formulating the distinction between task-local correction and cross-episode adaptation, emphasizing the need for evaluator-grounded procedural repairs that persist beyond the immediate episode.
- **Introduction of the Living-Harness framework:** Proposing a "rollout–evaluate–update" framework guided by a domain-level Evolution-SOP, which updates episodic memory and a state graph to store complementary experiential and workflow knowledge, all while keeping tools and base context frozen. A program-state POMDP interpretation is provided for this cross-episode update process.
- **Empirical evaluation:** Demonstrating the effectiveness of Living-Harness on interactive environments derived from τ²-Bench and MultiWOZ-2.4, showcasing improved task performance, recovery accumulation across evolution cycles, and retrieval-only reuse of the evolved harness state across different model backbones.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Living-Harness employs a "rollout–evaluate–update" loop to enable self-evolution of an agent's procedural guidance. This framework distinguishes between fixed execution resources and an evolving procedural state.

#### 3.1. Harness State (H^(n)_d)

The harness at episode *n* in domain *d* is defined as \(H^{(n)}_d = \langle C_{act_d}, \psi_d, S^{(n)}_d \rangle\).
- **Fixed Execution Resources (C_act_d):** This component remains constant and includes the agent's available tools, base context, and fundamental domain rules.
- **Fixed Evolution-SOP (\(\psi_d\)):** This is a static, domain-level protocol that governs how post-episode updates are performed. It does not change during the agent's evolution.
- **Evolving Harness State (S^(n)_d):** This is the adaptive component, represented as \(S^{(n)}_d = \langle R^{(n)}_d, G^{(n)}_d \rangle\).
 - **Episodic Memory (R^(n)_d):** Stores scoped records of trigger conditions, observed failure patterns, and successful recovery actions. This captures experiential knowledge.
 - **State Graph (G^(n)_d):** Comprises state nodes, transition rules, and repair edges. These edges link observed conditions to missing or revised actions within the agent's workflow, representing structured procedural knowledge.

#### 3.2. Initialization

The process begins with an initial harness state \(S^{(0)}_d = (\emptyset, G_{d,scaf})\), where there is no episodic memory and only a coarse, domain-specific state graph scaffold, which provides basic roots and task-family structure without fine-grained repairs.

#### 3.3. Rollout–Evaluate–Update Loop

For each episode *n*, the following sequence of operations occurs:

1. **Task-Conditioned Query:** A query \(q_n = Q(x_n, f_n)\) is constructed based on the current task \(x_n\) and its scope \(f_n\).
2. **Context Retrieval:** Relevant entries from the current episodic memory \(R^{(n)}_d\) and state graph \(G^{(n)}_d\) are retrieved using \(Render(\text{Retrieve}_R(R^{(n)}, q_n), \text{Retrieve}_G(G^{(n)}, q_n))\). This selective projection provides procedural context relevant to the current task.
3. **Interaction:** The LLM agent interacts with the environment, generating a trajectory \(\tau_n\), conditioned on the fixed execution resources \(C_{act_d}\) and the retrieved context \(\kappa_n\). Crucially, the harness state \(S^{(n)}_d\) remains fixed throughout this rollout phase.
4. **Evaluation:** After the interaction is complete, an evaluator produces signals \(y_n = E(x_n, \tau_n)\) that describe the outcome of the agent's execution.
5. **Episode Abstraction:** The fixed Evolution-SOP (\(\psi_d\)) processes the task, trajectory, evaluation signals, and fixed execution resources to produce an episode abstraction \(e_n\). This abstraction identifies the task objective, verified interaction facts, execution outcome, and critical failure or recovery points. It aims to distill useful procedural information.
6. **Evidence Extraction:** The episode abstraction \(e_n\) is then converted into structured evidence \(u_n = \langle u_{R_n}, u_{G_n} \rangle\), where \(u_{R_n}\) is for episodic memory and \(u_{G_n}\) is for the state graph.
7. **Bounded Harness Update:** The extracted evidence \(u_n\) is treated as a candidate repair. Before being committed, it undergoes checks for evidential support, task scope, and consistency with fixed domain and tool constraints. Accepted candidates update the persistent harness state: \(S^{(n+1)} = \text{Update}_{\psi_d}(S^{(n)}, u_n; C_{act_d})\). This process creates or strengthens memory entries and state-conditioned repair edges. If no candidate repair is accepted (e.g., due to lack of support or conflict), the state remains unchanged. The updated state is only available for subsequent interactions, ensuring that evaluation of an episode is independent of the update it triggers.

#### 3.4. Program-State Analysis

The framework is formalized through a Partially Observable Markov Decision Process (POMDP) perspective, augmented with an episode-level program state \(z^{(n)}\). This distinguishes the environment state (\(s_t\)) that evolves within an episode from the procedural information (\(z^{(n)}\)) derived from the evolving harness state \(S^{(n)}\) which changes only cross-episode. A proposition (Proposition 1 and A.1 in supplementary material) provides an information-theoretic justification, showing that augmenting the belief with the program state can reduce the optimal prediction error by providing refined conditional information.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of Living-Harness was conducted across eight interactive environments derived from two benchmarks: τ²-Bench (Retail, Airline, Telecom domains) and MultiWOZ-2.4 (Restaurant, Hotel, Train, Attraction, Taxi domains). The primary metric used was Pass@1, indicating the task success rate.

#### 4.1. Strong Overall Performance Gains

Living-Harness consistently demonstrated performance improvements across both benchmarks:
- On **τ²-Bench**, Living-Harness achieved an average Pass@1 of 83.09%, which marginally surpassed the average of the strongest flagship model (Gemini 3 Pro, 82.92%), despite using GPT-5.2 medium as its base model. This result suggests that the evolving harness provides benefits beyond the raw capabilities of a powerful base LLM.
- Compared to GPT-5.2-based interactive baselines, Living-Harness showed substantial improvements. For example, on τ²-Bench, it improved the average Pass@1 from 73.02% (Reflexion) to 83.09%. On **MultiWOZ-2.4**, it improved over the strongest interactive baseline (ReasoningBank, 55.59%) by nearly 10 percentage points, reaching an average of 65.50%.
- Notably, Living-Harness exhibited strong performance in multi-domain tasks on MultiWOZ-2.4, achieving the best scores in the two- and three-domain groups. This indicates that its procedural repairs can effectively transfer and be reused across different domain boundaries within complex dialogues.

#### 4.2. Consistent Self-Evolution and Recovery Accumulation

Analysis of per-cycle performance (Table 2) showed that Living-Harness’s performance improved consistently over evolution cycles:
- On τ²-Bench, all three domains (Retail, Airline, Telecom) demonstrated substantial Pass@1 increases from Cycle 0 (initial state) to later cycles. For example, Retail increased from 57.02% to 85.96%, and Telecom from 57.39% to 78.07%.
- MultiWOZ-2.4 domains also showed strong gains, with final-cycle improvements of +13.96 points for Restaurant, +24.11 for Hotel, +22.83 for Train, +25.25 for Attraction, and +28.71 for Taxi.
- The most significant gains were often observed after the first evolution cycle (Cycle 0 to Cycle 1), with subsequent cycles typically yielding smaller, refining improvements. This pattern aligns with the concept of bounded program evolution, where initial updates address major missing workflow steps, and later cycles fine-tune the accumulated harness state.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

Component ablation experiments (Figure 3) revealed the contributions of Living-Harness’s key elements:
- **Evolution-SOP:** Removing the Evolution-SOP caused the largest performance drop (average Pass@1 fell from 83.09% to 73.38% on τ²-Bench). This indicates that the gains are not merely from adding memory or a workflow graph, but from the structured posterior interpretation and bounded state evolution guided by the Evolution-SOP.
- **Episodic Memory and State Graph:** Disabling either episodic memory or the state graph individually also degraded performance (average scores of 77.34% and 79.50% respectively). This suggests that both components provide complementary benefits—reusable recovery lessons from memory and structured workflow transitions from the state graph. The full Living-Harness system consistently performed best across all domains.

#### 4.4. Cross-Model Harness Transfer

A significant finding was the ability to freeze and transfer a Living-Harness state, evolved using GPT-5.2, to other LLM backbones (Gemini 3 Pro, GLM-5, Qwen3-max, Kimi-k2) for retrieval-only reuse (Table 3):
- The transferred, frozen harness state improved every reported domain score for all tested target backbones.
- The gains were particularly notable in domains where the base models initially struggled; for instance, GLM-5, Qwen3-max, and Kimi-k2 improved from 0.00% to 43.08%, 45.13%, and 45.13% respectively in the Taxi domain.
- The harness also improved the performance of stronger backbones like Gemini 3 Pro. This indicates that the learned memory and state graph encode reusable, model-agnostic procedural knowledge that different LLMs can effectively retrieve and leverage.

#### 4.5. Executable Procedural Repair (Case Study)

A detailed case study (Figure 4) illustrated the mechanism of persistent procedural repair:
- In an initial failure cycle, an agent using Reflexion repeatedly identified the correct high-level action ("transfer to human") but failed to execute the specific `transfer_to_human_agents()` tool call.
- Living-Harness converted this evaluated failure into an executable repair, stored both as an episodic memory entry and a state graph edge linking "terminal suspension detection" to the required tool call.
- In a subsequent cycle, the repaired harness retrieved this procedural knowledge, successfully executed the tool call, and completed the task. This demonstrates Living-Harness's ability to convert vague advice into state-conditioned, executable repairs that directly influence future agent behavior.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of Living-Harness presents several significant contributions and potential impacts on the field of interactive LLM agents:

#### 5.1. Paradigm Shift in Agent Adaptation

The work represents a shift from task-local or response-level correction towards persistent, cross-episode procedural repair. Traditional methods often focus on improving the immediate response or allowing a local retry. Living-Harness, conversely, enables agents to learn from cumulative failures and successes by systematically updating their foundational procedural guidance, thereby enhancing long-term reliability. This shifts the focus from merely answering "what to answer next" to learning "how future tasks should be procedurally handled."

#### 5.2. Enhanced Reliability and Robustness

By converting recurring interaction failures into bounded updates of an external executable program state, Living-Harness provides a mechanism for agents to become more robust. Instead of repeatedly making the same mistakes, the agent's harness evolves to incorporate specific trigger conditions, failure patterns, and executable recovery actions. This structured adaptation is crucial for deploying LLM agents in real-world scenarios where reliability is paramount.

#### 5.3. Decoupling Model Capabilities from Procedural Guidance

Living-Harness demonstrates that improved agent performance does not solely depend on stronger single-step generation or model-side updates. By allowing the external harness (episodic memory and state graph) to evolve independently while the base LLM and its core tools remain frozen, the framework offers a modular approach to agent development. This separation of concerns can simplify the development and maintenance of agent systems.

#### 5.4. Reusability and Transferability of Procedural Knowledge

A key finding is the ability to freeze an evolved Living-Harness state and reuse it across different LLM backbones. This suggests that the procedural knowledge accumulated in the harness is generalizable and not tightly coupled to the specific base model used for its initial evolution. Such transferability can significantly reduce the effort required to bootstrap new agents or improve existing ones, as procedural lessons learned with one model could benefit others.

#### 5.5. Foundations for More Autonomous and Intelligent Agents

The concept of a self-evolving harness moves LLM agents closer to exhibiting more autonomous and intelligent behavior. By learning and refining their own operational procedures, agents can adapt to new challenges and evolving environments in a more systematic and grounded manner. The program-state POMDP interpretation provides a theoretical basis for understanding how explicitly modeling procedural knowledge can refine an agent's information and reduce prediction error.

#### 5.6. Future Research Directions

The work also opens several avenues for future research:
- **Robustness to Diverse Conditions:** Evaluating Living-Harness against arbitrary interaction stream orders, held-out task families, policy perturbations, and less structured, open-ended real-world deployments.
- **State Reliability Mechanisms:** Implementing more sophisticated mechanisms for harness state management, such as full rollback capabilities, systematic removal of stale entries, and regression testing over previously solved tasks to guarantee monotonic improvement.
- **Automation of Evolution-SOP Design:** Currently, domain-level Evolution-SOPs are manually specified. Automating the generation or adaptation of these protocols could enhance the framework's scalability and generality to unseen domains.
- **Cost and Ethical Considerations:** Addressing the computational cost of post-episode processing and potential privacy implications of persistently storing interaction histories in practical deployments.

In summary, Living-Harness offers a principled approach to overcoming a critical limitation of current LLM agents, enabling them to accumulate and leverage procedural knowledge for persistent self-improvement. This work contributes to the development of more reliable, adaptable, and robust AI agents.
