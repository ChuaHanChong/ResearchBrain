# See, Think, Confirm: Interactive Prompting Between Vision and Language Models for Knowledge-based Visual Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from MIT-IBM Watson AI Lab introduce the Interactive Prompting Visual Reasoner (IPVR) framework, which leverages iterative interaction between pre-trained vision and language models for few-shot knowledge-based visual reasoning. The framework achieves superior performance on A-OKVQA (46.0%) and OK-VQA while providing step-by-step transparent reasoning traces and higher quality rationales compared to baseline methods.

## Problem

- Knowledge-based visual question answering (KB-VQA) requires complex reasoning that integrates both visual understanding and external world knowledge.
- Existing fine-tuning methods for large Vision-Language Models (VLMs) are computationally intensive and lack transparency in their reasoning processes.
- Prior prompt-based visual reasoning approaches often generate generic image descriptions, failing to provide question-specific visual grounding, and offer limited insight into the LLM's decision-making.

## Method

- The ==Interactive Prompting Visual Reasoner (IPVR)== framework mimics human "See, Think, Confirm" reasoning through an iterative process utilizing existing large pre-trained vision and language models in a few-shot setup.
- A "See" module extracts visual information via object detection and global captioning, while a =="Think" module== adaptively attends to question-relevant visual concepts, generates regional descriptions, and predicts answers using an LLM.
- A =="Confirm" module== generates supporting rationales, verifies their visual consistency against the image using ==CLIP==, and iteratively self-corrects the reasoning until a stable answer or maximum iterations are met.

## Results

- IPVR consistently outperforms strong few-shot baselines like PICa and Chain-of-Thought on KB-VQA datasets, achieving **46.0%** accuracy on the A-OKVQA test set with OPT-66B and **57.5%** with GPT-3, surpassing some fully-supervised methods.
- The framework provides enhanced transparency by generating complete reasoning traces and produces higher quality rationales (BLEU score of **14.34**) compared to Chain-of-Thought baselines.
- Ablation studies confirm that all IPVR modules, particularly the adaptive "attend" module, contribute positively to overall performance and demonstrate the computational efficiency of the interactive prompting approach.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The interactive "See, Think, Confirm" paradigm enables total transparency by generating explicit, step-by-step reasoning traces and supporting rationales, addressing the "black-box" problem in multimodal AI.
> - Adaptive, question-specific visual grounding, combined with iterative rationale generation and verification, significantly enhances both the accuracy and consistency of answers in few-shot KB-VQA.
> - The framework's transparency provides clear diagnostic capabilities, allowing researchers to pinpoint whether reasoning failures originate from visual perception limitations or language model reasoning/knowledge recall.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research directly addresses the challenging problem of **knowledge-based visual reasoning (KB-VQA)**, a domain within AI that demands not only comprehensive image understanding but also the ability to recall and logically apply external world knowledge to answer complex questions. KB-VQA is a step beyond traditional visual question answering (VQA), which often relies solely on image content. The field is increasingly vital for applications like intelligent chatbots, assistive robots, and educational tools, where AI systems need to reason like humans in open-world scenarios.

The paper situates its contributions by highlighting the limitations of current dominant approaches:

- **Fine-tuning Large Pre-trained Vision and Language Models (VLMs):** Recent advancements have seen VLMs (e.g., Flamingo) achieve remarkable performance by fine-tuning massive models on enormous vision-language datasets. However, this approach is **computationally intensive and time-consuming**, often requiring specialized hardware and weeks of training. Furthermore, these models can operate as "black boxes," making their reasoning opaque.
- **Prompt-based Methods for Visual Reasoning:** An alternative paradigm leverages the few-shot learning capabilities of Large Language Models (LLMs) through prompting. Methods like PICa convert images into captions or tags, which then serve as textual prompts for LLMs (e.g., GPT-3). While more computationally efficient and capable of leveraging LLMs' vast general knowledge, these methods often suffer from two key limitations:
 1. **Lack of Question-Specific Visual Grounding:** The generated captions might be general descriptions of the image, failing to focus on visual concepts most relevant to the specific question asked.
 2. **Lack of Transparency:** The LLM's reasoning process remains largely hidden, offering little insight into how an answer was derived.
- **Neural-Symbolic Visual Reasoning:** This approach aims for transparency by translating questions into symbolic programs executed by network modules. While offering step-by-step reasoning, these methods have largely been confined to domains with simpler visual primitives (e.g., synthetic scenes) or struggle to scale effectively to complex, open-world knowledge-based scenarios, often requiring manual design of symbolic logic.

The IPVR framework emerges as a hybrid solution, aiming to reconcile the strengths of these paradigms while mitigating their weaknesses. It specifically targets the few-shot learning setting, aligning with the trend of making powerful LLMs accessible without extensive retraining. By introducing an interactive and iterative process, IPVR seeks to infuse the transparency and question-specific reasoning of neural-symbolic methods into the efficiency and broad knowledge base of prompt-based LLM approaches. The work contributes to the broader push for more **interpretable, trustworthy, and computationally accessible AI systems**, particularly in complex multimodal reasoning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core objectives and motivations behind the development of the Interactive Prompting Visual Reasoner (IPVR) are multifaceted, driven by the desire to overcome the limitations of existing approaches to knowledge-based visual reasoning (KB-VQA):

1. **Achieve High Accuracy in Few-Shot KB-VQA:** The primary objective is to develop a framework that can effectively solve challenging KB-VQA tasks with limited training examples. This is crucial for real-world scenarios where extensive labeled data for every specific task might not be available.
2. **Enhance Transparency and Trustworthiness:** A significant motivation is to move beyond "black-box" reasoning. The authors aim to provide a **total transparency and trustworthiness** of the entire reasoning process. This involves generating explicit, step-by-step reasoning traces and supporting rationales for the predicted answers, making the model's decision-making understandable to humans. This is directly inspired by how humans approach such problems ("See, Think, Confirm").
3. **Ensure Computational Efficiency:** While leveraging large pre-trained models, the framework is designed to be **computationally efficient** compared to fine-tuning massive vision-language models for every new task. This makes the approach more practical and accessible for wider deployment and research.
4. **Enable Adaptive, Question-Specific Visual Grounding:** A key motivation is to address the limitation of prior prompt-based methods that often generate generic image captions. IPVR aims to enable the model to **adaptively attend to key visual concepts** in the image that are semantically relevant to the specific question being asked, thereby providing more focused and useful visual context for the LLM.
5. **Promote Consistency in Reasoning:** The framework aims to ensure that the generated rationales are not only plausible but also **consistent** with both the visual input and the predicted answer. This iterative self-correction mechanism is intended to refine the reasoning process and improve the reliability of the output.
6. **Bridge Modalities Interactively:** The paper is motivated by the potential of designing a system that facilitates **frequent and interactive prompting** between vision models (for perception) and language models (for reasoning and knowledge recall), moving beyond simple one-way information flow.

In essence, the authors are motivated to build a KB-VQA system that mirrors human-like analytical thinking—perceiving, deliberating, and verifying—in a way that is effective, interpretable, and resource-conscious.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework, **Interactive Prompting Visual Reasoner (IPVR)**, is designed to mimic human reasoning by breaking down knowledge-based visual question answering into three iterative stages: **See, Think, and Confirm**. The entire process leverages existing large pre-trained vision and language models in a few-shot, interactive prompting setup.

**Overall Workflow:**
Given an image and a question, IPVR iteratively refines its understanding and answer. It starts by perceiving visual concepts, then uses an LLM to reason and predict an answer, and finally confirms the answer by generating and verifying rationales. This "Think-Confirm" loop continues until a stable answer is reached or a maximum iteration count is met.

**Detailed Breakdown of Modules:**

1. **See Module:**
 - **Purpose:** To extract comprehensive visual information from the input image, grounding potential visual concepts.
 - **Components:**
 - **Object Detector:** A Faster-RCNN model ( used by Yang et al.), pre-trained on Visual Genome with 1,595 diverse classes, detects object candidates within the image. It provides their bounding box locations and categorical labels (e.g., "knife", "plate", "napkin").
 - **Global Captioner:** An image captioning model (BLIP) generates a descriptive caption for the entire image, providing a general contextual understanding.
 - **Output:** A list of detected object categories and a global caption of the image.

2. **Think Module:**
 - **Purpose:** To process the visual information and external knowledge to predict an answer, adaptively focusing on relevant visual concepts.
 - **Steps:**
 - **Attend:** An LLM (OPT-66B) is prompted (as shown in Fig. 3A) to select the most relevant visual concepts from the list provided by the "See" module, based on their semantic relationship to the query question. This ensures that the reasoning focuses on task-relevant parts of the image.
 - **Describe:** For each selected visual concept, the corresponding region in the image (cropped bounding box, expanded 1.5 times for more context) is fed to a regional image captioner (BLIP). This generates a natural language description (regional caption) that is more informative than just the object's class name, often capturing relationships or attributes (e.g., "some pieces of meat on the plate"). Guided decoding is used to make captions more consistent with the question.
 - **Predict:** The LLM (OPT-66B) predicts the answer to the question. This prediction is made based on an accumulated textual context which includes:
 - Few-shot in-context examples (8 examples selected based on CLIP feature similarity).
 - The global image caption.
 - All previously generated regional descriptions from attended concepts.
 - Any verified rationales from prior "Confirm" iterations. (Prompt example in Fig. 3B).

3. **Confirm Module:**
 - **Purpose:** To enhance transparency and trustworthiness by generating and verifying supporting rationales for the predicted answer.
 - **Steps:**
 - **Explain (Generate Rationale):** Immediately after predicting an answer, the LLM is prompted to generate a supporting rationale for that answer (e.g., "Sofa and coffee table are usually located in the living room").
 - **Verify Visual Consistency:** The generated textual rationale is checked against the original image for visual consistency. This is done using a large pre-trained cross-modality classifier (CLIP ViT-B/16), which calculates the matching similarity between the rationale text and the image. Only rationales with similarity above a set threshold (empirically set to 0) are accepted.
 - **Verify Answer Consistency (Iterative Refinement):** If accepted, the rationale is appended to the LLM's prompt context for the *next* iteration of the "Think" module. This creates a self-correction loop: the LLM must consistently produce the same answer when prompted with its own rationale. The "Think-Confirm" loop continues until two consecutive answers are identical, or a maximum of 5 iterations is reached (Algorithm 1, Lines 5-17).

**Key Implementation Details:**
- **LLM:** OPT-66B is used due to its public availability and efficiency for experiments.
- **Few-shot Learning:** 8 in-context examples are selected from the training set based on CLIP similarity. A multi-query ensemble (5 times) is used to improve robustness.
- **Regional Captioning:** BLIP is used, with bounding boxes expanded by 1.5x, and constrained decoding to guide caption generation towards question relevance.

This modular, interactive, and iterative design allows IPVR to dynamically gather and refine visual and textual context, ensuring that both perception and reasoning are closely aligned with the task at hand and transparently explained.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted on challenging knowledge-based visual reasoning benchmarks, OK-VQA and A-OKVQA, demonstrate the effectiveness, transparency, and computational efficiency of the IPVR framework.

**1. Superior Performance in Few-Shot KB-VQA:**
- **Outperformance over Baselines:** IPVR consistently achieves better performance than strong few-shot learning-in-context baselines, including PICa and Chain-of-Thought (CoT), on both A-OKVQA and OK-VQA datasets (Table 1). For instance, on the A-OKVQA test set, IPVR (46.0%) significantly surpasses PICa (43.8%) and CoT (43.7%).
- **Competitive with Fully-Supervised Models:** Notably, IPVR, despite being a few-shot method using OPT-66B, even outperforms some previous fully-supervised pre-trained methods like GPV-2 on the A-OKVQA test split (46.0% vs. 40.7%), showcasing its strong capability.
- **Scalability with Stronger LLMs:** When IPVR is equipped with a more powerful LLM like GPT-3 (175B) for the multiple-choice A-OKVQA setting, it achieves an accuracy of 57.5% on the test split (Table 5). This not only surpasses all OPT-66B-based baselines but also outperforms previous fully-supervised methods like GPV-2 (53.7%), demonstrating the immense potential when combined with state-of-the-art LLMs.

**2. Enhanced Transparency and Interpretability:**
- **Step-by-Step Reasoning Trace:** A key finding is IPVR's ability to provide a complete, step-by-step reasoning trace, including attended visual concepts, their regional descriptions, and the final supporting rationales. This is visually demonstrated in qualitative results (Fig. 4, Fig. 9), offering unprecedented transparency into the model's decision-making process.
- **Higher Quality Rationales:** A quantitative evaluation of rationale quality on the A-OKVQA validation set (Table 2) shows that IPVR generates rationales that are closer to the ground truth compared to CoT, as measured by both BLEU scores (14.34 vs. 14.19) and CLIP sentence similarity (81.22 vs. 80.92). This confirms that IPVR's rationales are not only present but also more accurate and relevant.

**3. Computational Efficiency:**
- **Efficient compared to Fine-tuning:** The paper states that IPVR is computationally efficient compared to fine-tuning methods, which typically demand vast resources.
- **Efficiency over Prompting Baselines:** Even when baselines like PICa and CoT are computationally "aligned" (i.e., given more queries to match IPVR's iterative cost), IPVR still significantly outperforms them (Table 3). This indicates that IPVR's interactive and iterative nature is not just interpretable but also a more effective use of computational resources for generating high-quality answers.

**4. Contribution of Individual Modules (Ablation Study):**
- The ablation study (Fig. 6) confirms that every component of IPVR—the "attend" module, rationale generation, and rationale verification—contributes positively to the overall performance.
- The "attend" module shows the most significant impact, highlighting the critical role of question-adaptive visual grounding. This substantiates the idea that dynamically selecting and describing relevant visual concepts is crucial for accurate knowledge-based reasoning.
- The "w/o rationale" and "w/o verify" ablations confirm that generating and verifying rationales not only increases transparency but also improves answer accuracy, demonstrating the benefit of the iterative self-correction mechanism.

**5. Diagnostic Failure Analysis:**
- The transparency of IPVR allows for clear analysis of failure cases (Fig. 5, Fig. 10). Failures can be attributed to either:
 - **LLM limitations:** The LLM's inability to connect certain visual cues (e.g., "colorful balloons") to specific external knowledge (e.g., "LGBTQ social movement").
 - **Vision model limitations:** The underlying scene parser or captioner failing to detect key objects (e.g., "pacifier") or capture essential visual attributes (e.g., the shape of a donut).
- This diagnostic capability is a significant finding in itself, providing clear directions for future improvements by pinpointing whether the bottleneck lies in visual perception or language reasoning/knowledge recall.

In summary, the main findings establish IPVR as an effective, transparent, and efficient framework that advances the state-of-the-art in few-shot knowledge-based visual reasoning by cleverly integrating pre-trained vision and language models in a human-inspired interactive loop.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The IPVR framework represents a significant advancement in the field of knowledge-based visual question answering and multimodal AI, with several key areas of significance and potential impact:

1. **Advancing Interpretable AI:** Perhaps the most profound impact of IPVR is its contribution to **interpretable AI**. By providing a step-by-step reasoning trace, including attended visual concepts, their detailed descriptions, and explicit supporting rationales, IPVR addresses the critical "black-box" problem prevalent in many deep learning models. This transparency is vital for building trust in AI systems, especially in sensitive real-world applications such as healthcare diagnostics, autonomous systems, or intelligent tutoring, where understanding *why* a decision was made is as important as the decision itself.

2. **Effective Few-Shot Learning for Complex Reasoning:** IPVR demonstrates that high performance on complex, open-world KB-VQA tasks can be achieved using few-shot learning with readily available large pre-trained models. This significantly reduces the need for massive, task-specific fine-tuning datasets and computational resources, making advanced multimodal reasoning more accessible to a broader research community and for various niche applications.

3. **Novel Interactive Multimodal Prompting:** The "See, Think, Confirm" paradigm introduces a novel and highly effective interactive prompting strategy between vision and language models. Unlike previous methods that often treat vision and language models as separate, sequential modules, IPVR's iterative loop with adaptive visual grounding and rationale verification establishes a deeper, more consistent dialogue between modalities. This interactive approach can inspire new architectures for multimodal reasoning.

4. **Robustness and Consistency through Self-Correction:** The iterative "Confirm" module, which generates and verifies rationales for consistency with both the visual input and the predicted answer, introduces a powerful self-correction mechanism. This mechanism not only enhances the quality and reliability of the generated answers and explanations but also makes the model's reasoning more robust.

5. **Diagnostic Capability for System Improvement:** The inherent transparency of IPVR allows for a clear diagnosis of failure points—whether they stem from limitations in visual perception (e.g., object detection, regional captioning) or from the LLM's reasoning and knowledge recall abilities. This diagnostic clarity is invaluable for guiding future research efforts and targeted improvements in both vision and language model components.

6. **Inspiration for General Commonsense Reasoning:** The human-inspired "See, Think, Confirm" framework offers a promising blueprint for building more general-purpose commonsense reasoning systems. The ability to ground abstract knowledge in concrete visual observations and iteratively refine understanding is a hallmark of intelligent agents, and IPVR moves closer to achieving this in an artificial system.

In conclusion, IPVR not only pushes the performance boundaries for few-shot knowledge-based visual reasoning but, more importantly, provides a blueprint for constructing AI systems that are both powerful and understandable. Its emphasis on transparency, interactivity, and efficiency paves the way for a new generation of trustworthy and practical multimodal AI applications.
