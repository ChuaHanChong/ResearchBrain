# VESSA: Video-based objEct-centric Self-Supervised Adaptation for Visual Foundation Models

> [!summary] Summary
> VESSA enables visual foundation models to specialize in new, label-scarce domains through a self-supervised adaptation strategy that utilizes object-centric videos. This method, a collaboration between UFMG, UNICAMP, and Google DeepMind, integrates parameter-efficient fine-tuning and uncertainty-weighted self-distillation, yielding consistent improvements in downstream classification performance.

## Problem

- Visual Foundation Models (VFMs) often face performance degradation when applied to specialized target domains due to distribution shifts and the scarcity of labeled data.
- Existing supervised fine-tuning methods for VFMs are resource-intensive and impractical in label-scarce environments, while naive self-supervised adaptation for vision encoders is ineffective and can lead to catastrophic forgetting.
- A gap exists for an effective self-supervised fine-tuning strategy that can directly specialize pre-trained VFMs to unlabeled target domains for downstream tasks.

## Method

- ==VESSA== introduces a ==video-based object-centric self-supervised adaptation== strategy for VFMs, using short multi-view videos as the sole source of unlabeled adaptation data.
- The method employs an ==Uncertainty-Weighted Self-Distillation (UWSD) loss==, which assigns higher importance to 'harder' examples based on teacher model entropy, to guide the student network.
- It integrates ==Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (PEFT) using LoRA== with a careful, staged unfreezing schedule: initial projection head adaptation followed by gradual fine-tuning of early and late backbone layers.

## Results

- VESSA consistently improved kNN classification accuracy across DINO, DINOv2, and TIPS models on the MVImageNet and CO3D datasets, with DINOv2 showing a **4%** absolute gain on CO3D (from **87.86%** to **91.85%**).
- The approach significantly outperformed image-based self-supervised adaptation methods and other video-based baselines like ExPLoRA in most comparative experiments.
- The adaptation process is computationally efficient, requiring only **1.97 hours** of training for the CO3D dataset with a ViT-Base backbone, though it entails an expected trade-off in catastrophic forgetting of original general-purpose capabilities.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Object-centric videos, with their inherent spatio-temporal consistency and diverse viewpoints, provide rich implicit supervisory signals for effective self-supervised adaptation of VFMs.
> - A carefully designed training schedule, including initial adaptation of the prediction head and selective unfreezing of backbone layers via LoRA, is crucial to stabilize fine-tuning and prevent catastrophic forgetting of pre-trained knowledge.
> - Uncertainty weighting in the self-distillation loss effectively guides the model to focus on more challenging examples, leading to more robust and discriminative feature learning in the target domain.
