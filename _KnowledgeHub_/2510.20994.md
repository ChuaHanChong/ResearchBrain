# VESSA: Video-based objEct-centric Self-Supervised Adaptation for Visual Foundation Models

> [!summary] Summary
> VESSA enables visual foundation models to specialize in new, label-scarce domains through a self-supervised adaptation strategy that utilizes object-centric videos. This method, a collaboration between UFMG, UNICAMP, and Google DeepMind, integrates parameter-efficient fine-tuning and uncertainty-weighted self-distillation, yielding consistent improvements in downstream classification performance.

## Problem

- Visual Foundation Models (VFMs) often face performance degradation when applied to specialized target domains due to distribution shifts and the scarcity of labeled data.
- Existing supervised fine-tuning methods for VFMs are resource-intensive and impractical in label-scarce environments, while naive self-supervised adaptation for vision encoders is ineffective and can lead to catastrophic forgetting.
- A gap exists for an effective self-supervised fine-tuning strategy that can directly specialize pre-trained VFMs to unlabeled target domains for downstream tasks.

## Method

- ==VESSA== introduces a ==video-based object-centric self-supervised adaptation== strategy for VFMs, using short multi-view videos as the sole source of unlabeled adaptation data.
- The method employs an ==Uncertainty-Weighted Self-Distillation (UWSD) loss==, which assigns higher importance to 'harder' examples based on teacher model entropy, to guide the student network.
- It integrates ==Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (PEFT) using LoRA== with a careful, staged unfreezing schedule: initial projection head adaptation followed by gradual fine-tuning of early and late backbone layers.

## Results

- VESSA consistently improved kNN classification accuracy across DINO, DINOv2, and TIPS models on the MVImageNet and CO3D datasets, with DINOv2 showing a **4%** absolute gain on CO3D (from **87.86%** to **91.85%**).
- The approach significantly outperformed image-based self-supervised adaptation methods and other video-based baselines like ExPLoRA in most comparative experiments.
- The adaptation process is computationally efficient, requiring only **1.97 hours** of training for the CO3D dataset with a ViT-Base backbone, though it entails an expected trade-off in catastrophic forgetting of original general-purpose capabilities.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Object-centric videos, with their inherent spatio-temporal consistency and diverse viewpoints, provide rich implicit supervisory signals for effective self-supervised adaptation of VFMs.
> - A carefully designed training schedule, including initial adaptation of the prediction head and selective unfreezing of backbone layers via LoRA, is crucial to stabilize fine-tuning and prevent catastrophic forgetting of pre-trained knowledge.
> - Uncertainty weighting in the self-distillation loss effectively guides the model to focus on more challenging examples, leading to more robust and discriminative feature learning in the target domain.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The VESSA paper situates itself at the intersection of several rapidly evolving areas in computer vision and machine learning, particularly concerning the practical deployment of large **Visual Foundation Models (VFMs)**.

- **Rise of Foundation Models:** The advent of foundation models, exemplified by large language models (LLMs) and VFMs (e.g., DINO, DINOv2, MAE), has dramatically reshaped AI. These models, pre-trained on vast datasets, demonstrate impressive generalizability across diverse tasks. However, their "foundation" nature means they often require **fine-tuning** for optimal performance in specialized downstream applications.
- **Challenge of Domain Shift and Data Scarcity:** A critical limitation of VFMs arises when they are applied to new domains characterized by **distribution shifts** (data characteristics differ from pre-training) and **scarce labeled data**. Traditional **supervised fine-tuning** provides excellent performance but is contingent on the availability of extensive, high-quality annotations, which are often expensive, time-consuming, or even impossible to acquire in many real-world scenarios (e.g., medical imaging, specific industrial inspections, niche scientific data).
- **The Gap in Self-Supervised Adaptation for Vision:** The natural language processing (NLP) community has successfully adopted **unsupervised fine-tuning** or "continued pretraining" for adapting LLMs to new domains using unlabeled in-domain text. This strategy has proven highly effective for generative language models. However, its counterpart for **vision-centric encoder models** (like those in VFMs) remains largely underexplored and presents significant challenges. Existing self-supervised learning methods for vision primarily focus on initial pre-training from scratch or domain adaptation between *labeled source* and *unlabeled target* domains, rather than directly specializing a *pre-trained VFM* to an *unlabeled target domain for downstream tasks*.
- **Leveraging Video Data:** The research also builds on the understanding that **video data** offers rich supervisory signals due to its inherent spatio-temporal consistency, natural motion, and diverse object transformations. While previous works have explored video-to-image knowledge transfer, these often involve complex architectures, hybrid loss functions, or are tailored for pixel-level tasks (e.g., segmentation), showing limited direct benefit for frame-level classification without further supervision.
- **Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (PEFT):** The use of LoRA (Low-Rank Adaptation) places VESSA within the broader trend of PEFT techniques. These methods are crucial for adapting increasingly large foundation models without incurring the prohibitive computational costs of full fine-tuning, thus making adaptation more accessible and sustainable.

VESSA directly addresses the unmet need for an effective and efficient **self-supervised adaptation strategy for vision foundation models** that can bridge the performance gap in specialized, label-scarce domains. By harnessing the unique properties of object-centric videos and carefully engineered self-distillation, it carves out a novel niche within the broader landscape of VFM deployment and domain adaptation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind VESSA stems from the limitations of existing methods for deploying Visual Foundation Models (VFMs) in real-world, specialized scenarios. While VFMs offer unparalleled generalizability due to large-scale pretraining, their performance can significantly degrade when encountering **distribution shifts** in target domains, especially when accompanied by **scarce or absent labeled data**.

The paper identifies a critical gap:
1. **Reliance on Supervised Fine-tuning:** Current state-of-the-art VFM adaptation predominantly relies on supervised fine-tuning, which is resource-intensive and often impractical given the high cost and labor involved in acquiring domain-specific labels.
2. **Ineffectiveness of Naive Self-Supervised Adaptation in Vision:** Unlike the success seen in NLP with continued self-supervised pretraining for language models, a straightforward application of self-supervised learning for adapting vision *encoder* models has not proven effective; it often leads to catastrophic forgetting or performance degradation.

Against this backdrop, the **key objectives** of VESSA are:

- **To develop a novel and effective self-supervised fine-tuning method for vision foundation models.** The core aim is to adapt a pre-trained VFM to a new domain **without requiring any annotations**.
- **To leverage short multi-view object-centric videos as the sole source of unlabeled data for adaptation.** This addresses the question of what form of unlabeled visual data is best suited for this task, positing that temporal consistency and diverse viewpoints from videos can provide rich supervisory signals implicitly.
- **To introduce a carefully formulated self-distillation paradigm tailored for fine-tuning.** The authors recognize that a naive application of self-distillation can lead to model degradation; thus, a critical objective is to design specific training adjustments (e.g., prediction head tuning, parameter-efficient adaptation, uncertainty weighting) to preserve pre-trained knowledge while specializing the model.
- **To demonstrate consistent improvements in downstream classification tasks.** The method must empirically prove its ability to enhance representations in the target domain, leading to better performance compared to base models and other adaptation strategies.
- **To ensure the adaptation process is lightweight and efficient.** This involves employing parameter-efficient techniques like LoRA to make the adaptation of large foundation models practical in resource-constrained environments.

In essence, VESSA seeks to answer fundamental questions about unsupervised VFM adaptation: how to adapt models without supervision, what unlabeled data is optimal, and what learning techniques are effective under these constraints, thereby expanding the applicability of foundation models to a broader range of real-world scenarios.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

VESSA proposes a novel, video-based object-centric self-supervised adaptation strategy for visual foundation models, built upon a foundation of self-distillation and parameter-efficient fine-tuning.

#### 3.1. Background

The methodology of VESSA is rooted in two core techniques:

- **DINO (Self-Distillation with Vision Transformers):** DINO (Self-DIstillation with NO labels) is a self-supervised learning framework where a student network is trained to match the output distribution of a teacher network. Both networks receive different augmented views of the same image, and the teacher's parameters are updated via an Exponential Moving Average (EMA) of the student's weights. This process encourages the student to learn robust, meaningful representations without manual labels, minimizing a cross-entropy loss between their normalized output distributions. VESSA adapts this paradigm for fine-tuning.
- **LoRA (Low-Rank Adaptation):** LoRA is a parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) technique designed to adapt large pre-trained models. Instead of updating all weights of a linear layer, LoRA freezes the original weight matrix and injects small, trainable low-rank matrices (∆W = AB) in parallel. This significantly reduces the number of trainable parameters and memory footprint, making fine-tuning large models more efficient while preserving performance. VESSA employs LoRA to gently tune the backbone of the VFM.

#### 3.2. Video-based objEct-centric Self-Supervised Adaptation (VESSA) Pipeline

The VESSA pipeline is composed of three main modules, as illustrated in Figure 2:

1. **Frame Selection:**
 - **Input:** Videos (V) with T frames {F_i}.
 - **Process:** For each video, `n` pairs of frames are sampled. A starting frame index `t` is randomly chosen from `U(1, T - δ_max)`, and a temporal offset `δ` is sampled from `U(1, δ_max)`.
 - **Output:** A batch of frame pairs `(F_t_k, F_t_k+δ_k)` from different videos. This randomized temporal sampling ensures diverse viewpoints and temporal relationships between frames, crucial for learning robust representations.

2. **Preprocessing and Augmentation:**
 - **Process:** Each frame in a selected pair undergoes independent, distinct augmentation pipelines.
 - **Global Views:** Two globally augmented views, `F^(a)_t` and `F^(b)_t+δ`, are generated from the pair `(F_t, F_t+δ)`. These views are critical for the self-distillation process.
 - **Local Crop Pairs:** Inspired by DINO, VESSA also generates `u` pairs of local crops, `(F^(c_i)_t, F^(c_i)_t+δ)`, sampled from the same original frames. These local crops maintain temporal consistency while introducing local variability, aiding stability.
 - **Output:** A batch containing global view pairs and multiple local crop pairs.

3. **Model Fine-tuning:**
 - **Architecture:** Both student and teacher networks are initialized from the same pre-trained Visual Foundation Model. LoRA is integrated into their architectures for parameter-efficient tuning.
 - **Input Flow:** The globally augmented frame pair `(F^(a)_t, F^(b)_t+δ)` is fed into both student and teacher networks. The local crop pairs are fed *only* into the student network.
 - **Uncertainty-Weighted Self-Distillation (UWSD) Loss:** The core training objective is a modified DINO loss. It introduces an uncertainty weighting term `w(q) = 1 + γ · H(q)` to the standard DINO cross-entropy loss `L_DINO(q, s, s_lc_i)`. Here, `H(q)` is the entropy of the teacher's output distribution `q`. This mechanism assigns higher weights to "harder" (more uncertain) examples predicted by the teacher, prioritizing challenging learning instances. The overall loss `L_UWSD` sums these weighted losses over global and local crop representations.

#### 3.3. Critical Optimization Considerations

The authors highlight that a naive application of self-distillation during fine-tuning can lead to gradient instabilities and catastrophic forgetting of pre-trained knowledge. To mitigate this, VESSA employs:

- **Staged Unfreezing and Head Adaptation:**
 1. **Projection Head Adaptation:** Initially, the VFM backbone is frozen, and only the randomly initialized projection head of the student network is trained for a few epochs. This allows the head to adapt to the existing embedding space without disrupting the robust pre-trained features.
 2. **Gradual Backbone Unfreezing:** After head adaptation, the backbone is gradually unfrozen with a differentiated strategy:
 - **Early Layers (H layers):** The first `H` layers are fine-tuned using LoRA. Specifically, LoRA matrices are injected into the Query, Key, and Value projections of each self-attention layer, while normalization layers remain fully trainable. This preserves low-level features (edges, textures) which are generally transferable.
 - **Late Layers (L layers):** The last `L` layers of the backbone are fully unfrozen and updated normally. This allows the model to adapt high-level semantic representations, which are typically more domain-specific, to the new data distribution.

This sophisticated training schedule and parameter-efficient adaptation strategy are crucial for enabling stable and effective specialization of VFMs in a self-supervised manner, preventing representational drift while enhancing domain relevance.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

VESSA's effectiveness is rigorously demonstrated through comprehensive experiments on two large-scale video datasets and with three prominent visual foundation models, employing both quantitative and qualitative analyses.

#### 4.1. Experimental Setup

- **Datasets:** **MVImageNet** (6.5M frames, 219k videos, 238 categories) and **CO3D** (1.5M frames, 19k videos, 50 categories) are used, both offering multi-view object videos captured under real-world conditions. A 75%-25% split for training/testing is used, with one frame per video for evaluation.
- **Evaluation Metric:** k-Nearest Neighbors (KNN) classification accuracy on learned embeddings.
- **Models:** DINO, DINOv2, and TIPS (Vision Transformers with ViT-Base architecture for balance).
- **Baselines:** Pretrained models (DINO, DINOv2, TIPS), a "Static-baseline" (image-based self-supervised continual learning), and an extended version of ExPLoRA (a recent PEFT method for domain adaptation) adapted to video.
- **Training Details:** 10 epochs for head adaptation, 10 for full model training, batch size 256, 224x224 input. 3 frame pairs sampled per video. Uncertainty weight `γ=1`.

#### 4.2. Ablation Study (Table 1)

The ablation study on MVImageNet using ViT-S/16 highlights critical components:
- **Video vs. Image Input:** Training DINO from scratch with video (39.39%) significantly outperforms image input (33.86%), showing the inherent value of temporal information even with limited data.
- **Pretrained Baseline:** The pretrained DINO model achieves a strong 89.69% accuracy.
- **Naive Image-based Adaptation:** A naive continuation of self-supervised training using only images leads to performance degradation (88.54%).
- **Video without Local Crops:** Switching to video input even without local crops (90.53%) yields better results than image-based adaptation, underscoring the strong role of temporal data.
- **Importance of Head Training:** **Carefully training the prediction head before fine-tuning is paramount.** Without it, performance drops significantly (e.g., from 91.87% to 80.87%), indicating its role in adapting to the new embedding space.
- **Optimal Layer Unfreezing:** Unfreezing the last 2 layers achieves the best performance (91.87%), representing a 2.18 p.p. improvement over the pretrained baseline, validating the architectural and training schedule choices of VESSA.

#### 4.3. Impact of Frame Distance (Table 2)

Experiments on CO3D showed that the temporal gap (`δ`) between sampled frames influences performance. Randomly sampling `δ` from achieves the highest accuracy (85.03% for DINO, 91.85% for DINOv2), suggesting that diverse temporal relationships between views contribute to more robust representation learning.

#### 4.4. Comparative Analysis (Table 3)

VESSA consistently demonstrates superior performance across both datasets and all tested backbone models:
- **CO3D:** VESSA with DINOv2 achieves 91.85% ± 0.56, significantly outperforming ExPLoRA + video (89.64% ± 0.47) and the pretrained DINOv2 (87.86%).
- **MVImageNet:** VESSA achieves 96.01% ± 1.08 with DINOv2. While ExPLoRA + video reaches 96.15% ± 0.87, the difference is not statistically significant for DINOv2 on this dataset, but VESSA still provides strong performance. For DINO, VESSA clearly outperforms ExPLoRA + video (92.51% vs 87.74%).
- **Static-baseline Failure:** The "Static-baseline" (image-based unsupervised continual learning) often leads to performance degradation or minimal gains, further confirming the necessity of video data and VESSA's specific optimization strategies.
- **TIPS Improvement:** VESSA also significantly improves TIPS (Text-Image Pretraining with Spatial Awareness) model performance (e.g., from 60.02% to 70.56% on CO3D), demonstrating its applicability beyond self-supervised VFMs.

#### 4.5. Qualitative Observations (Figure 3)

Qualitative examples of KNN retrieval on CO3D show that VESSA learns more **object-centric and semantically meaningful representations**. While a pretrained DINOv2 might focus on background or broad scene structures, VESSA's embeddings enable retrieval based on the actual object of interest, even with variations in texture or color.

#### 4.6. Statistical Significance and Other Experiments (Appendices)

- **Confidence Intervals (Tables 4 & 5):** Statistical significance tests confirm VESSA's improvements over other video-based methods like ExPLoRA on CO3D (DINO and DINOv2). On MVImageNet, VESSA consistently improves DINO and TIPS, though the improvement over DINOv2 against ExPLoRA is less statistically pronounced due to overlapping confidence intervals.
- **Approximate Video-like Image Transformations (Table 6):** Applying stronger, motion-inspired augmentations to static images did not yield performance comparable to real video data, emphasizing that true temporal consistency and diverse views from videos provide unique benefits.
- **Catastrophic Forgetting (Table 7):** VESSA's specialization comes at the cost of **catastrophic forgetting**. Performance on the original ImageNet classification task drops drastically after adaptation (e.g., DINO from 76.10% to 15.46% on CO3D adaptation). This is an expected trade-off, as VESSA's goal is domain-specific specialization, not maintaining general-purpose performance.
- **Cross-Dataset Generalization (Table 8):** Training on MVImageNet and testing on CO3D also shows a performance drop (e.g., DINOv2 from 87.86% to 80.36%), indicating limited generalization between different target domains after specialization.
- **Computational Efficiency:** VESSA is computationally efficient, requiring only 1.97 hours of training (7.04 core-hours) for the CO3D dataset adaptation using a ViT-Base backbone. This contrasts sharply with the thousands of GPU-hours needed for full VFM pretraining.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

VESSA represents a significant step forward in making visual foundation models more adaptable and practical for real-world applications, particularly in scenarios traditionally hindered by data annotation challenges.

- **Novelty in Unsupervised Visual Adaptation:** VESSA introduces a genuinely novel and effective formulation for **self-supervised fine-tuning of vision encoder models**. It fills a critical void where existing methods either rely on supervision or are not well-suited for direct, label-free specialization of pre-trained VFMs to new domains. This mirrors the successful paradigm of continued pretraining in NLP, bringing a similar level of flexibility to the vision domain.
- **Leveraging Inexpensive Data Sources:** By demonstrating the efficacy of short, object-centric videos as the sole source of adaptation data, VESSA taps into an abundant and relatively inexpensive data modality. Unlike curated image datasets requiring extensive labeling, multi-view videos can be easily captured, making the adaptation process more accessible and scalable for diverse applications.
- **Enhanced Robustness and Representation Learning:** The method's ability to learn from varied viewpoints and temporal coherence within videos leads to more robust and semantically meaningful object-centric representations. This is crucial for tasks where invariance to capture conditions (e.g., camera angle, lighting) is important, improving downstream task performance.
- **Practical Applicability in Label-Scarce Domains:** VESSA has a substantial potential impact on fields where acquiring labeled data is a major bottleneck, such as medical imaging, industrial inspection, remote sensing, robotics, and specialized surveillance. It empowers practitioners to deploy powerful VFMs in niche domains without the prohibitive costs of manual annotation.
- **Efficient Adaptation of Large Models:** The integration of parameter-efficient fine-tuning (LoRA) is key to VESSA's practicality. It allows for the adaptation of massive foundation models without requiring vast computational resources, making advanced AI capabilities more attainable for a wider range of research groups and industries.
- **Advancing VFM Understanding:** The research provides valuable insights into the behavior of VFMs during adaptation, particularly highlighting the importance of tailored training schedules (e.g., projection head adaptation, staged unfreezing) and the role of uncertainty weighting in self-distillation to prevent catastrophic forgetting and maintain stability.

**Limitations and Future Work:** The paper openly acknowledges its primary limitation: **catastrophic forgetting**. While VESSA excels at domain specialization, the adapted model loses much of its original general-purpose capability. Future work could explore strategies to mitigate this, perhaps through selective regularization or hybrid approaches that balance specialization with knowledge retention. Another limitation is the reliance on video data offering *multiple viewpoints of the same object*, which might not be universally available in all real-world scenarios. Expanding VESSA to work with more unstructured or less "object-centric" video data could broaden its applicability. Despite these limitations, VESSA establishes a strong foundation for future research in unsupervised visual foundation model adaptation, pushing the boundaries of what is possible with label-free learning in computer vision.
