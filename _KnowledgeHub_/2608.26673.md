# PredVLA: A Sub-Million-Parameter Predictive-Coding Policy for Robot Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> PredVLA is a language-conditioned predictive-coding robot policy with only 0.68M trainable parameters and no robot-data pretraining. A frozen vision-language front end feeds a hierarchical multi-timescale recurrent generative model that predicts visual features, proprioception, and actions; observations enter only via online prediction-error latent inference. It reaches 86.9% on LIBERO short-horizon suites, 75.4% across all four, at 21.6 Hz.

## Problem

- Strongest vision-language-action policies hold billions of parameters, making training, adaptation, and deployment expensive for language-conditioned manipulation across objects and embodiments.
- Efficient variants like TinyVLA, SmolVLA, and Evo-1 merely compress the same paradigm, still sitting in the hundreds-of-millions-parameter regime rather than changing the control architecture.
- Predictive-coding recurrent models handle sensorimotor dynamics but were never evaluated on standardized language-conditioned benchmarks nor compared against modern policies at matched parameter budgets.

## Method

- ==Hierarchical predictive-coding== recurrent controller: a top module conditioned on frozen sentence embeddings feeds visual and two-level action modules, each with module-specific time constants forming a ==temporal hierarchy==.
- Two lateral pathways carrying no observations: a low-dimensional visual-to-action bottleneck inspired by ==parametric bias==, and an ==efference-copy== pathway returning the model's own predicted action and proprioception.
- **PredVLA**'s ==test-time error regression== minimizes a deterministic ==free energy== (visual plus proprioceptive accuracy, quadratic prior-complexity) over a 40-step sliding window, updating only posterior latent variables with frozen weights.

## Results

- On LIBERO across 14 seeds: **83.19** SPATIAL, **88.40** GOAL, **89.24** OBJECT, **40.57** LONG, giving **86.94** three-suite and **75.35** four-suite mean at **675,732** parameters.
- Parameter-matched baselines under identical front end and protocol reach only **19.73** (Transformer) and **10.26** (LSTM) four-suite mean; larger action-chunking variants score **16.70** and **3.44**.
- Ablations: flattening the temporal hierarchy costs **17-29** points; disabling online inference costs **6.44-12.57** points; proprioceptive corruption at **25%** drops OBJECT by **49.74** points versus mild visual sensitivity.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Architectural choice can substitute for scale: a sub-million-parameter recurrent generative controller matched 7B-parameter published results on this benchmark under its own protocol.
> - Routing observations exclusively through prediction-error inference yields an exact open-loop control condition by setting iterations to zero, isolating feedback contribution without retraining.
> - Which sensory error channel drives correction is task-dependent, and partial correction can underperform fully open-loop execution, so aggregate success rates hide the mechanism.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of general-purpose robot control has seen significant advancements with the emergence of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. These models, such as RT-1, RT-2, Octo, OpenVLA, and π₀, integrate visual observations and language instructions with large-scale robot demonstration data to achieve broad task coverage and transferability across objects, environments, and robot embodiments. This progress has established large-scale pretraining as an effective method for language-conditioned robot manipulation, with top reported performance on benchmarks like LIBERO exceeding 95%.

However, the success of these VLA systems often comes with substantial computational requirements, as many of the leading models incorporate billions of parameters. This scale leads to high costs in training, adaptation, and deployment. Consequently, there has been a growing emphasis on developing more compact and efficient VLA policies. Recent efforts, including TinyVLA, SmolVLA, and Evo-1, have demonstrated that strong manipulation performance can be maintained at reduced scales, typically in the hundreds-of-millions parameter range. These models generally achieve efficiency through architectural simplification or by reducing the computational cost of existing VLA paradigms.

This work addresses a different facet of efficiency by exploring whether a fundamental change in the control architecture can yield even greater parameter reductions while maintaining competitive performance. It investigates an alternative approach using recurrent generative controllers based on predictive coding, operating with fewer than one million trainable network parameters. Predictive coding models propose that internal generative dynamics predict sensory trajectories, and latent states are inferred online by minimizing discrepancies between predicted and observed signals, rather than through direct observation input to recurrent dynamics. This approach offers a framework where action generation and sensory feedback are mediated by learned generative dynamics and error-driven latent-state inference.

Previous work in predictive coding for sensorimotor control has demonstrated its capacity to represent high-dimensional dynamics and perform online inference from prediction errors. However, these models have not typically been evaluated on standardized language-conditioned manipulation benchmarks like LIBERO or compared against contemporary policy architectures under matched parameter budgets and shared protocols. PredVLA aims to bridge this gap by applying a predictive-coding recurrent inference architecture to the modern VLA evaluation regime, specifically focusing on a sub-million-parameter design. This positions PredVLA as an investigation into an alternative, compact architectural paradigm for language-conditioned robot manipulation, distinct from the compression strategies applied to larger VLA models.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the computational cost associated with large-scale Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models currently dominating robot manipulation benchmarks. While these models have demonstrated high performance and broad capabilities, their parameter counts, often in the billions, lead to expensive training, adaptation, and deployment processes. This creates a barrier to wider adoption and efficient development in robotics.

Existing efforts to enhance efficiency in VLA policies, such as TinyVLA, SmolVLA, and Evo-1, typically focus on reducing the scale or computational cost of architectures derived from the prevailing VLA paradigm. These models, while more compact, still operate within the hundreds-of-millions parameter regime. The authors question whether further efficiency gains can be achieved by fundamentally altering the control architecture itself, rather than solely compressing existing large VLA policies.

Specifically, the key objectives of this work are:

1. **Investigate the feasibility of a highly compact control architecture:** To determine if a recurrent generative controller with fewer than one million trainable network parameters can achieve strong language-conditioned manipulation performance on modern benchmarks. This challenges the prevailing assumption that large model scale is necessary for robust language-conditioned control.
2. **Develop a predictive-coding based policy (PredVLA):** To design and implement a language-conditioned predictive-coding policy that incorporates a hierarchical recurrent generative model. This architecture is intended to predict visual features and proprioception, where observations influence latent state exclusively through online inference from sensory prediction errors, rather than direct input.
3. **Evaluate PredVLA on a standardized benchmark under controlled conditions:** To assess PredVLA's performance on the LIBERO benchmark using official demonstrations. A critical objective is to conduct a controlled comparison against parameter-matched Transformer and LSTM policies, ensuring identical frozen front ends, demonstrations, action decoders, and evaluation protocols. This aims to isolate the performance benefits attributable to the predictive-coding architecture itself.
4. **Mechanistically analyze the contributions of architectural components and online inference:** To utilize the predictive-coding formulation to directly measure the contribution of observation-driven correction. By enabling an exact open-loop control condition (disabling prediction-error-based inference), the work aims to quantify the impact of online state correction. Further ablations investigate the roles of hierarchical timescales, lateral pathways, and individual sensory error channels (visual and proprioceptive) in overall policy performance.

In essence, the research is motivated by the need for more efficient robot control policies and aims to demonstrate that an alternative architectural paradigm, specifically predictive coding, can offer a viable path to achieving competitive performance at significantly reduced parameter budgets, while also providing mechanistic insights into how sensory feedback drives control.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

PredVLA is designed as a language-conditioned predictive-coding policy, characterized by a trainable hierarchical recurrent generative network coupled with a frozen multimodal front end. A core principle of the architecture is that sensory observations do not directly input into the recurrent dynamics; instead, their influence is mediated solely through prediction-error-driven inference over latent free variables.

#### 3.1. Frozen Front End

The system utilizes fixed preprocessing for three input streams: language, vision, and proprioception.
- **Language:** Language instructions are embedded using the pretrained `all-MiniLM-L6-v2` sentence encoder and then projected through a fixed PCA basis to yield a compact language representation (`l`). This representation remains constant for the duration of an episode.
- **Vision:** Two camera views (third-person agentview and wrist-mounted eye-in-hand) are independently encoded by a frozen ImageNet-pretrained ResNet18. Each visual feature is then projected through a fixed per-camera PCA basis, and the resulting representations are concatenated to form the visual observation (`v`). Visual observations are encoded at a fixed interval during control.
- **Proprioception:** Joint angles and gripper state (`q`) are used directly without an encoder.

The visual and language encoders are not pretrained on robot-specific data. PCA bases are fitted on the training demonstrations prior to policy training and then remain fixed.

#### 3.2. Hierarchical Dynamics

The trainable component of PredVLA is a recurrent network comprising hierarchical modules: a shared top module (T), a visual branch (V), and an action branch split into `A_top` and `A_bottom`. The top module feeds `V` and `A_top`, and `A_top` subsequently feeds `A_bottom`. A lateral connection from `V` to `A_top` couples the branches within each time step.

Each module maintains a deterministic state (`h_l_t`), an output (`d_l_t`), and a latent free variable (`c_l_t`). A deterministic variant of the prior-posterior free-variable formulation, adapted from PV-RNN, is employed alongside multiple-timescale recurrent dynamics.
- **Prior Generation:** A top-down prior (`c_l_p,t`) for each non-root module's free variable is generated from its parent module's output. For the root module (T), the prior is generated recurrently from its own previous output.
- **Deterministic State Update:** Deterministic states are updated using module-specific time constants (`τ_l`), which define the temporal hierarchy. Larger `τ_l` values result in slower state dynamics. The top module also receives the episode-level language signal (`l`).
- **Language Conditioning:** The language vector `l` conditions the root module T, propagating an episode-level signal throughout the hierarchy.
- **Lateral Pathways:** Two lateral pathways couple the visual and action branches without directly carrying observations:
 - **Visual-to-Action Bottleneck:** A low-dimensional bottleneck (`z_VA_t`) from `V` to `A_top` is dynamically computed from the visual state at each time step.
 - **Efference Copy:** The model's own previous action (`ˆa_t-1`) and proprioceptive predictions (`ˆq_t-1`) are fed back to the visual branch, ensuring sensory measurements do not directly influence forward dynamics through this pathway.

#### 3.3. Decoders

Two-layer MLP heads decode predictions:
- Visual prediction (`ˆv_t`) from `d_V_t`.
- Proprioceptive prediction (`ˆq_t`) and a K-component diagonal Gaussian mixture action distribution from `d_A_bottom_t`. The mean of the highest-weight mixture component is emitted as the action (`ˆa_t`).

#### 3.4. Free Energy

Latent-state inference minimizes a deterministic free-energy objective, combining accuracy and complexity terms:
- **Accuracy Terms:** Visual (`ε_v`) and proprioceptive (`ε_q`) prediction errors, calculated as squared L2 norms between observed and predicted signals, averaged per dimension.
- **Complexity Term:** A quadratic penalty for the deviation of each posterior free variable (`c_l_q,t`) from its corresponding generative prior (`c_l_p,t`).
The free energy `E(I)` sums these terms over a defined temporal index set `I`.

#### 3.5. Training

During training, the posterior free variables (`c_l_q,t`) are treated as model parameters, optimized jointly with network weights via backpropagation through time. The training objective `L` augments the free-energy terms with the negative log-likelihood (NLL) of the demonstrated action, which is excluded during test-time inference. Posterior free variables are discarded after training.

#### 3.6. Inference by Error Regression

At test time, network weights are frozen, and only the posterior free variables are updated. Inference minimizes the free energy over a sliding temporal window of recent observations:
1. The current observation is appended to the inference window, and new posterior free variables are initialized from their priors.
2. `n_itr` optimization steps are performed on the posterior free variables using gradient descent, regenerating deterministic recurrent states over the window after each update.
3. The current action is decoded from `d_A_bottom_t`.

#### 3.7. Exact Open-Loop Control

A key feature of PredVLA is the ability to achieve exact open-loop control by setting `n_itr = 0`. In this configuration, posterior free variables remain equal to their priors, which are generated solely from the recurrent hierarchy. Sensory observations are thus completely disengaged from the recurrent forward computation, allowing for direct measurement of the contribution of online inference without model retraining or language conditioning alteration.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents quantitative results for PredVLA's performance on the LIBERO benchmark, along with controlled comparisons and ablation studies.

#### 4.1. Main Results

PredVLA, with 675,732 trainable network parameters and no robot-data pretraining, achieved the following mean success rates (± standard deviation across 14 seeds) on the LIBERO suites:
- **SPATIAL:** 83.19% ± 6.10
- **GOAL:** 88.40% ± 3.83
- **OBJECT:** 89.24% ± 6.13
- **LONG:** 40.57% ± 6.44

The mean success rate across the three short-horizon suites (SPATIAL, GOAL, OBJECT) was 86.94%. Including the long-horizon suite, the overall mean success rate across all four suites was 75.35%. PredVLA demonstrated an end-to-end control latency of 46 ms per step (21.6 Hz) on an RTX 5090, which includes the frozen front end and online error regression.

#### 4.2. Comparison with Controlled Baselines

A controlled comparison was conducted against behavior-cloning policies (Transformer and LSTM) using the same frozen front end, demonstrations, action representation, and evaluation protocol.
- **PredVLA (675,732 parameters):** 75.35% mean success rate across four suites.
- **Parameter-matched BC-Transformer (646,628 parameters, 0.957× PredVLA):** 19.73% mean success rate.
- **Parameter-matched BC-RNN (LSTM) (674,276 parameters, 0.998× PredVLA):** 10.26% mean success rate.

Action-chunking Transformer variants, which had larger parameter counts (1.14× and 1.36× PredVLA), performed lower:
- **BC-TF, chunk 8 (772,558 parameters):** 16.70% mean success rate.
- **BC-TF, chunk 16 (916,478 parameters):** 3.44% mean success rate.

These comparisons indicate that PredVLA achieved approximately 3.7× and 7.4× higher mean success rates than the parameter-matched Transformer and LSTM baselines, respectively, and substantially outperformed the larger action-chunking Transformer variants.

#### 4.3. Ablation Study

Ablation experiments, comparing against the intact PredVLA model using matched seeds, provided insights into the contributions of various architectural and inference components:

##### 4.3.1. Architectural Components (requiring retraining)

- **Removing Temporal Hierarchy (all `τ` set to mean):** Resulted in substantial performance degradation across short-horizon suites: -18.66 percentage points on SPATIAL, -16.99 on GOAL, and -29.03 on OBJECT. This was the only architectural change that consistently degraded performance across all three.
- **Removing Visual-to-Action Bottleneck:** Reduced success by -24.23 points on SPATIAL and -16.06 points on GOAL. It showed no significant effect on OBJECT (+0.04 points).
- **Removing Efference Copy (A→V pathway):** Had a weaker, suite-dependent effect: -6.51 points on SPATIAL, -11.84 on GOAL, and -3.31 on OBJECT.

These findings suggest that the temporal hierarchy is broadly important, while the lateral pathways provide more specialized, suite-dependent benefits.

##### 4.3.2. Online Error Regression (inference-time changes)

- **Disabling Online Error Regression (n_itr = 0):** This created an exact open-loop control condition. Performance decreased by -6.44 points on SPATIAL, -9.56 on GOAL, -12.17 on OBJECT, and -12.57 on LONG. The resulting open-loop success rates were 73.79%, 78.50%, 78.14%, and 28.00% respectively. This indicates that online error regression contributes to improved closed-loop execution across all suites.

##### 4.3.3. Sensory Error Channels (inference-time changes)

- **Removing Visual Error in Inference (λ_v = 0):** Reduced success by -3.51 points on SPATIAL, -4.99 on GOAL, and -2.96 on OBJECT.
- **Removing Proprioceptive Error in Inference (λ_q = 0):** Reduced success by -2.80 points on SPATIAL, -1.41 on GOAL, and -14.17 on OBJECT.

The strongest asymmetry was observed on OBJECT, where removing the proprioceptive term had a much larger impact than removing the visual term. Task-level analysis further revealed that while suite-level averages might be similar, the specific tasks affected by visual versus proprioceptive error term removal differed substantially. For instance, on OBJECT, removing the visual term sometimes improved performance on some tasks, whereas removing the proprioceptive term caused large drops on several tasks.

##### 4.3.4. Sensitivity to Corrupted Observations

- **Visual Corruption:** Partial corruption of visual observations (up to α = 0.50) led to changes of less than five percentage points on all short-horizon suites.
- **Proprioceptive Corruption:** Even at α = 0.25, proprioceptive corruption caused significant reductions: -5.71 points on SPATIAL, -4.41 on GOAL, and -49.74 points on OBJECT. Proprioceptive corruption consistently demonstrated a more detrimental effect than visual corruption as the corruption level increased.

This indicates that online inference relies differently on visual and proprioceptive error signals depending on the task and suite, with proprioceptive feedback being critically important for certain tasks, particularly within the OBJECT suite.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents PredVLA, a language-conditioned predictive-coding policy, demonstrating that strong robot manipulation performance can be achieved with a compact architecture comprising fewer than one million trainable network parameters and no robot-data pretraining. This finding challenges the current trend of requiring billions of parameters in VLA models for robust control.

The significance of PredVLA lies in several aspects:

1. **Efficiency and Resource Optimization:** By achieving an 86.94% mean success rate across short-horizon LIBERO suites and 75.35% across all four suites with only 0.68M trainable parameters, PredVLA offers a computationally efficient alternative to large VLA models. This efficiency has implications for reducing training, adaptation, and deployment costs, potentially making advanced robot control more accessible and practical for diverse applications and hardware constraints. The measured control latency of 46 ms (21.6 Hz) further highlights its practical applicability.

2. **Architectural Paradigm Shift:** PredVLA demonstrates that a fundamentally different control architecture, based on predictive coding, can be competitive. The controlled comparison against parameter-matched Transformer and LSTM policies, which achieved substantially lower success rates, indicates that PredVLA's performance is not merely due to the chosen frozen front end or parameter budget. Instead, it suggests that the predictive-coding framework, with its error-driven latent-state inference, provides an effective mechanism for robust control.

3. **Mechanistic Insights into Control:** The predictive-coding formulation allows for explicit measurement of the contribution of observation-driven correction. By disabling online error regression, the researchers were able to quantify the performance difference between closed-loop and exact open-loop control using the same learned model. This provides clear evidence for the importance of online inference in improving execution, particularly for longer-horizon tasks like those in the LONG suite.

4. **Role of Hierarchical Dynamics and Sensory Feedback:** Ablation studies highlight the importance of both hierarchical time constants and online error regression. The non-uniform contribution of visual and proprioceptive prediction errors across different tasks and suites, as well as their differential sensitivity to corruption, reveals that the policy intelligently leverages various sensory signals based on task requirements. For instance, the OBJECT suite's high sensitivity to proprioceptive error suggests tasks where precise self-perception or interaction forces are critical.

**Potential Impact:**

- **Democratization of Robotics:** More efficient models could lower the barrier to entry for robotics research and development, allowing smaller teams or institutions with limited computational resources to develop and deploy advanced robotic systems.
- **Edge Computing and Embedded Systems:** The compact nature of PredVLA could enable the deployment of sophisticated control policies on edge devices and embedded systems, where computational and power budgets are severely constrained.
- **Robustness and Adaptability:** The mechanism of online prediction-error-driven inference offers a pathway for inherent adaptability to unexpected environmental changes by continually updating the internal state based on sensory discrepancies.
- **Neuroscience-Inspired AI:** As a predictive-coding model, this work contributes to bridging neuroscience theories of brain function with practical AI applications, potentially leading to more biologically plausible and interpretable control systems.

The study acknowledges limitations, including evaluation in simulation only, a single robot embodiment, and separate training for each LIBERO suite, alongside the relatively lower performance on long-horizon tasks. Future work aims to address these by focusing on physical robot deployment, robustness to perturbations, enhanced long-horizon capabilities, and broader joint training, all while maintaining the small parameter budget. This ongoing research direction could further solidify the position of predictive-coding architectures as a viable and efficient paradigm for next-generation robot manipulation.
