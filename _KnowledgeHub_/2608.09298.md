# WorldSimProbe: Diagnosing Simulator Faithfulness in Action-Conditioned World Models for Embodied Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> WorldSimProbe is a diagnostic benchmark testing whether action-conditioned world models behave as physical simulators. It formalizes an Observable Simulator Contract linking supplied action to realized agent motion to grounded environment response, then probes that chain with five controlled suites and dedicated evaluators over 18,608 instances in RoboTwin, ManiSkill, and LIBERO, exposing systematic action-realization, grounding, and dynamics failures in six open-source models.

## Problem

- Prevailing world-model evaluations emphasize visual quality, semantic alignment, or task success, so a rollout can look plausible while never executing the supplied action.
- Action-following is judged at rollout level inside task-specific control distributions, leaving counterfactual, recovery, and physically executable out-of-distribution controls essentially untested.
- Prior benchmarks rarely check whether predicted environment responses are causally supported by realized motion, so hallucinated contact and wrong interaction dynamics stay unmeasured.

## Method

- **Observable Simulator Contract** formalizes two links: ==action-realization consistency==, where generated motion matches the supplied action, and ==interaction-response consistency==, where environment change is mediated by that motion.
- Five suites operationalize it: ==Local Action Calibration==, ==Global Trajectory Coverage== by donor-receiver replay, ==Action-Source Behavior Preservation== over human and policy-checkpoint streams, ==Interaction Grounding==, and eight-primitive ==Interaction Dynamics==.
- Suite-specific evaluators: oracle-relative ==MSE response-ratio calibration==, ==Robot-Masked Flow Alignment== combining ==RobotSeg== masks with ==DPFlow==, ==TAPNext++== displacement gating for false contact, and a ==Qwen3-VL== primitive judge.

## Results

- Six models score **35.6** to **63.5** overall across three platforms with cross-platform ranking consistency of Spearman **0.695**; strength at one chain stage never implies overall fidelity.
- Grounding falls from **75.0** on distractors to **38.6** under appearance-induced false contact; shake dynamics collapse to **0.0** to **1.2** while tap peaks at **40.8** to **56.9**.
- Flow alignment tracks human ratings at Spearman **0.750** on diverse and OOD controls versus **0.284** for an inverse dynamics baseline; synthetic-data policies split **21** to **53** percent OOD despite **78** to **89** percent standard success.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Reacting to an action change is not calibrated response: models compress perturbation magnitude, so simulated failure onset arrives many times later than the physical simulator's.
> - Fidelity decays monotonically with distance from training control distributions, so benchmarks confined to task-directed trajectories systematically overstate a world model's simulator reliability.
> - Hallucinated contact, not missed contact, dominates grounding errors, since visual contact cues override supplied controls; check no-contact cases before trusting any generative simulator.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The research on action-conditioned world models (ACWMs) is situated within the broader field of embodied AI and Physical AI, which aims to enable intelligent agents to predict future scene evolution and interact with their environments. ACWMs specifically extend this capability to robotic manipulation by predicting visual futures given a stream of actions. The utility of ACWMs is envisioned in applications such as planning, policy evaluation, and synthetic data generation.

Prior evaluation methodologies for ACWMs have primarily focused on metrics such as visual quality, task outcomes (e.g., success rates), or general responsiveness at a rollout level. Some benchmarks have moved towards assessing action reliability or physical plausibility. However, the authors identify a critical gap: existing evaluations often fail to directly test the *fidelity* of the simulator. They argue that generating merely "plausible" outputs is insufficient; instead, ACWMs must generate the "particular future physically induced by the supplied action." Current diagnostic capabilities are limited, often assessing action-following at a coarse rollout level or within task-specific control distributions, thus providing incomplete coverage of physically valid action variations. Furthermore, whether predicted environment responses are causally supported by the induced motion is rarely examined. This can lead to situations where a rollout appears plausible or action-aware but lacks faithful action execution or physically grounded interactions.

This paper positions itself as a response to this limitation by introducing a framework designed to diagnose simulator faithfulness directly, moving beyond visual fidelity or aggregate task success. It contrasts with approaches that infer actions from generated videos, assess language-level interventions, or focus solely on downstream utility without granular failure localization. Table 1 in the paper summarizes this by comparing WorldSimProbe's capabilities (explicit action, local/global/source-diverse testing, causal probe, interaction decomposition) against existing benchmarks, highlighting its unique comprehensive diagnostic approach.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to develop a robust and diagnostic framework for evaluating the simulator faithfulness of action-conditioned world models (ACWMs) in embodied manipulation. The authors aim to move beyond conventional evaluations that prioritize visual plausibility or task success, seeking to establish whether ACWMs can function as precise, action-conditioned physical simulators.

The motivation for this work is multi-faceted:

- **Necessity for Precise Transitions:** For ACWMs to reliably support downstream applications like planning, policy evaluation, and data generation in embodied AI, they must produce precise action-conditioned transitions, not just visually plausible outcomes. Realizing the promise of ACWMs requires a high degree of fidelity in how supplied actions translate to agent motion and subsequent environment responses.
- **Limitations of Existing Evaluations:** Current evaluation paradigms are identified as insufficient for truly diagnosing simulator fidelity.
 - They often emphasize visual quality or aggregate task success, which can mask underlying failures in action execution or interaction grounding.
 - Action-following is typically assessed at a coarse rollout level or within task-specific control distributions, failing to cover the full spectrum of physically valid action variations (e.g., counterfactuals, subtle perturbations, diverse execution styles).
 - They frequently neglect to verify if predicted environment responses are causally supported by the agent's realized motion, leading to potential "hallucinated" interactions.
- **Formalizing Simulator Requirements:** There is a need for a clear, formal definition of what constitutes a "faithful physical simulator" in the context of ACWMs. This includes establishing minimal observable requirements for action-to-motion correspondence and grounded environment responses.
- **Enabling Failure Localization:** Aggregate success metrics provide limited diagnostic information. The authors are motivated to create a framework that can localize specific failure modes along the simulator chain, from action realization to interaction dynamics. This granular understanding is crucial for guiding targeted improvements in ACWM design.
- **Broadening Action Space Coverage:** ACWMs are typically trained and evaluated on task-associated action subsets. The research aims to evaluate ACWMs across a broader distribution of feasible actions, including local perturbations, globally divergent but executable trajectories, and actions from diverse sources (e.g., human teleoperators, different policy checkpoints), to test robustness beyond familiar task protocols.

To address these motivations, the authors define the **Observable Simulator Contract**, a minimal set of requirements for physical simulators, and introduce **WorldSimProbe**, a diagnostic benchmark comprising five controlled evaluation suites designed to operationalize this contract.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology centers around **WorldSimProbe**, a diagnostic benchmark designed to evaluate Action-Conditioned World Models (ACWMs) against a formal definition of "simulator faithfulness" called the **Observable Simulator Contract**. This contract posits two linked consistency conditions:

1. **Action-Realization Consistency:** The generated agent motion ($\hat{r}_{t:t+H}$) must correspond to the supplied action stream ($a_{t:t+H}$) given the initial scene state ($r_t, e_t$).
2. **Interaction-Response Consistency:** The generated environment response ($\hat{e}_{t:t+H}$) must be physically supported by the realized agent motion ($\hat{r}_{t:t+H}$) and initial environment state ($e_t$).

WorldSimProbe operationalizes this contract through five diagnostic suites, each targeting specific aspects of the simulator chain from supplied action to realized agent motion and environment response. The benchmark uses over 18,000 instances across three distinct simulation platforms: RoboTwin, ManiSkill, and LIBERO, spanning single- and dual-arm manipulation tasks and diverse embodiments. Six open-source ACWMs, representing both action-injection and unified action-video architectures, were evaluated.

**The Five Diagnostic Suites:**

1. **Task 1: Local Action Calibration**
 - **Objective:** To assess an ACWM's sensitivity and calibrated response to fine-grained action perturbations, even when these do not alter task semantics or success.
 - **Intervention:** Starting from a successful action trajectory ($x_0$), two variants ($x_s, x_l$) are created by perturbing one non-gripper action dimension over a fixed window with two different magnitudes (small and large).
 - **Evaluation:** The score ($S_i$) is an oracle-relative metric comparing the ratio of generated video MSEs (original-to-small vs. original-to-large) against the corresponding ratio from the simulator references. This measures whether the model reproduces the simulator's relative response scaling.

2. **Task 2: Global Trajectory Coverage**
 - **Objective:** To evaluate action realization beyond task-associated trajectories, testing coverage across physically feasible but potentially counterfactual action streams.
 - **Intervention:** For a given "receiver" scene, an action stream from a different "donor" task is executed, creating a reference "counterfactual" rollout. The ACWM receives the receiver observation and the donor action stream.
 - **Evaluation:** **Robot-Masked Flow Alignment (RMFA)** is used. This metric estimates dense optical flow for both generated and simulator-reference rollouts. It then computes the Root Mean Squared Error (RMSE) between these flow fields only within the active motion region of the robot arm (identified by segmentation and motion magnitude in the simulator reference). The score ($S_{i,w}$) normalizes this error by the reference motion magnitude, penalizing errors in robot-motion direction, magnitude, and spatial alignment.

3. **Task 3: Action-Source Behavior Preservation**
 - **Objective:** To determine if ACWMs preserve source-specific behavioral nuances (e.g., speed, smoothness, corrective behavior) from different control distributions (human, policy checkpoints).
 - **Intervention:** Successful and unsuccessful task-directed trajectories are collected from multiple human teleoperators and early/late-training policy checkpoints. Each trajectory, along with its simulator reference, constitutes an instance.
 - **Evaluation:** Similar to Task 2, **RMFA** is applied to compare the generated rollouts against their source-specific simulator references.

4. **Task 4: Interaction Grounding**
 - **Objective:** To assess whether environment responses are correctly grounded in physically supported interactions, specifically identifying "contact hallucination" (i.e., generating an interaction when no valid contact occurs).
 - **Intervention:** Controlled no-contact action-scene pairs are constructed via three methods:
 - **Distractor Object:** A plausible object is inserted, but the action stream does not interact with it.
 - **Appearance-Induced False Contact:** Grippers are clamped open while replaying an original arm trajectory that might visually suggest contact.
 - **Spatial Proximity:** The target object is relocated beyond the robot's swept region, but visual cues suggestive of interaction remain.
 - **Evaluation:** **TAPNext++** (a point tracker) tracks the evaluated object in the generated rollout. The score ($S_i$) is binary: 100 if the object's displacement remains below a threshold (indicating no unsupported interaction) and a robot-motion gate is passed; 0 otherwise.

5. **Task 5: Interaction Dynamics**
 - **Objective:** To evaluate whether, when an interaction occurs, the ACWM accurately models its dynamics according to predefined primitive behaviors.
 - **Intervention:** Instances begin from successful simulator trajectories, but near initial contact, the remaining controls are replaced with scripted "interaction primitives" (e.g., push, pull, drag, rotate, shake, tap, knock-over, drop).
 - **Evaluation:** A **Visual Language Model (VLM)** (Qwen3-VL-8B) acts as a deterministic judge. Given the generated agent-environment rollout and primitive specifications, the VLM predicts the realized primitive. The score ($S_{T5}$) is the percentage of instances where the VLM's prediction matches the intended primitive, provided both agent and object motion judgments from the VLM also pass. Human annotators validate the simulator-defined primitive labels.

**Experimental Setup:**
- The three platforms (RoboTwin, ManiSkill, LIBERO) offer diverse tasks and embodiments.
- Models are trained on official splits using their released recipes, with minimal interface adaptations.
- For each instance, models receive identical initial observations, action streams, simulator seeds, and three shared diffusion seeds, with scores averaging the resulting stochastic generations.
- Evaluator thresholds are fixed from reference data across models.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of six open-source ACWMs across 18,000+ instances revealed several systematic findings:

#### 4.1. Overall Simulator Faithfulness (RQ1)

- **Cross-platform Consistency:** The paper observed substantial consistency in model rankings across RoboTwin, ManiSkill, and LIBERO (mean pairwise Spearman ρ = 0.695). LingBot-VA generally performed well, leading on RoboTwin and ManiSkill, while Ctrl-World led on LIBERO and was second overall.
- **Architectural Diversity:** Both action-injection and unified action-video architectural families spanned higher and lower ranks, indicating that architecture alone did not exclusively determine simulator faithfulness.
- **Suite-Level Variation:** Model strengths varied by suite. For example, Ctrl-World led in Interaction Grounding on two platforms, but LingBot-VA led overall on ManiSkill. This stage-specific variation highlighted that strong performance at one stage of the simulator chain does not guarantee overall fidelity, enabling targeted failure localization.

#### 4.2. Robustness of Action-to-Motion Correspondence (RQ2)

- **Local Action Calibration:** ACWMs exhibited attenuated responses to action perturbations compared to the simulator oracle (Figure 4a). While the simulator responded progressively, models showed varying degrees of compression, indicating that they might detect action changes but fail to reproduce the calibrated response scaling. This weak local calibration was linked to earlier onset of action-induced failures, meaning models broke down at smaller perturbations than the simulator (Figure 4b).
- **Global Trajectory Coverage:** Action-realization fidelity systematically declined for all models as the mismatch between the "donor" action and the "receiver" task's typical motion increased (mean Spearman correlation ρ = -0.433) (Figure 5a). This suggests that models rely increasingly on scene- or task-associated motion priors when confronted with unfamiliar trajectories, compromising their ability to generalize to physically valid but out-of-distribution actions.
- **Action-Source Behavior Preservation:** Fidelity, as measured by RMFA, degraded not only for global counterfactuals but also for task-directed trajectories with unfamiliar execution characteristics. Models scored notably higher on late-training policy checkpoints compared to early ones (10.8–16.1 points), and human trajectories showed consistent operator-dependent variation (Figure 5b). This indicates a limitation in preserving the supplied behavior across diverse execution styles.

#### 4.3. Structure of Agent–Environment Interaction Failures (RQ3)

- **Interaction Grounding:** Qualitative analysis revealed that interaction-grounding failures were dominated by **contact hallucination** (false positives), where models generated interactions despite lack of physical support. No false negatives (missed responses to supported contact) were observed in the audit. Grounding was strongest for distractor objects (75.0%) and spatial proximity (54.5%) but significantly lower for appearance-induced false contact (38.6%) (Table 3). Models were more susceptible to hallucinating contact when visual cues were present but lacked actual control support.
- **Interaction Dynamics:** Fidelity varied more across interaction primitives than across models (Figure 6). "Tap" was the strongest primitive (40.8–56.9%), followed by "drag," "drop," and "push." "Pull," "rotate," and "knock-over" were weaker, and "shake" was nearly absent (0.0–1.2%). This indicated shared primitive-specific biases among ACWMs, exposing interaction failures that task outcomes might miss.

#### 4.4. Benchmark Validity and Downstream Relevance (RQ4)

- **Agreement with Human Judgments:** WorldSimProbe's RMFA metric demonstrated strong alignment with human judgments of action-following (Spearman ρ = 0.750 on diverse/OOD controls). In contrast, a VLM-based judgment showed lower agreement (0.450), and an inverse dynamics model (IDM) exhibited poor alignment (ρ = 0.284) under diverse/OOD controls (Table 4a). IDM's high error on simulator references suggested it measures inverse-model decoding failure rather than ACWM action-realization fidelity (Table 4b).
- **Synthetic-Data Utility:** A controlled downstream study on a RoboTwin task revealed that policies trained on synthetic data generated by higher-fidelity ACWMs (as ranked by WorldSimProbe) achieved significantly better success rates under out-of-distribution (OOD) controls (e.g., Ctrl-World and LingBot-VA at 53%) compared to lower-fidelity ACWMs (e.g., Cosmos-3-Nano at 21%). Standard control success rates were relatively similar across models (78–89%) (Figure 7). This suggests that while success-oriented training might mask fidelity differences, WorldSimProbe's diagnostics are relevant for real-world applications requiring robustness beyond familiar controls.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces **WorldSimProbe**, a diagnostic framework that reorients the evaluation of Action-Conditioned World Models (ACWMs) from assessing plausible or task-successful rollouts to directly testing the capabilities expected of physical simulators. This shift provides a more rigorous and granular understanding of ACWM performance.

The key significance and potential impact include:

- **Standardized Diagnostic Paradigm:** By formalizing the **Observable Simulator Contract** and implementing it through five controlled suites, WorldSimProbe establishes a transparent and standardized methodology for evaluating ACWM simulator fidelity. This moves beyond coarse, task-directed evaluations, providing a common ground for comparative analysis.
- **Failure Localization:** The suite-specific evaluators enable the localization of failures along the "simulator chain"—from supplied action to agent motion and grounded environment response. This capability to identify *where*, *when*, and *how* simulator fidelity breaks down is a critical advancement over aggregate performance metrics. It provides actionable insights for model developers to target specific weaknesses.
- **Revealing Hidden Weaknesses:** The systematic study using WorldSimProbe uncovered systematic failures in ACWMs that were previously obscured by conventional evaluations. These include:
 - **Miscalibrated action realization:** ACWMs often attenuate responses to fine-grained action perturbations, leading to premature failure onset.
 - **Degraded generalization:** Fidelity systematically declines when presented with trajectories outside of typical task distributions or with unfamiliar execution characteristics (e.g., human vs. policy-generated actions).
 - **Structured interaction failures:** A tendency towards contact hallucination (generating unsupported interactions) was observed, and primitive-level interaction dynamics showed distinct and shared biases across models.
- **Empirical Validation and Downstream Relevance:** The benchmark's findings are supported by agreement with human judgments on action-following and demonstrate a correlation with downstream policy performance, particularly under out-of-distribution (OOD) controls. This validates WorldSimProbe's relevance for developing robust and reliable ACWMs for embodied AI applications.
- **Foundation for Improvement:** By providing a clear diagnostic tool, WorldSimProbe lays a foundation for future improvements in ACWMs. It can guide targeted data collection, model architecture design, and training strategies to address specific fidelity gaps.

Future work could extend WorldSimProbe to real-world rollouts incorporating physical measurements, longer-horizon and closed-loop predictions, and more complex interactions involving deformable or multi-object scenarios. This framework could also inform curriculum learning for ACWMs and the design of more effective synthetic data generation for robot policy training.
