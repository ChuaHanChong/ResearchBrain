# ReTouch: Empowering Contact-Rich Dexterous Manipulation with Online-Refined Tactile Prediction

> [!summary] Summary
> ReTouch is a Vision-Language-Action framework that enhances contact-rich dexterous manipulation by integrating a structured tactile representation with an online refinement mechanism for tactile prediction. The system achieved an 83.6% macro-average success rate across seven real-robot tasks, surpassing the strongest baseline by 18.4 percentage points, and demonstrated significantly improved robustness to various perturbations.

## Problem

- Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models often lack detailed contact information (location, force, stability) for dexterous manipulation due to visual occlusions, particularly in multi-finger scenarios.
- Dense tactile observations from dexterous hands are frequently flattened or compressed by existing methods, obscuring finger-wise spatial structure and local contact topology crucial for precise finger adjustments.
- Future tactile predictions generated at the beginning of an action sequence become stale due to dynamic contact states (e.g., slippage, errors), leading to misguidance for subsequent online action refinement.

## Method

- A ==Tactile-Patch Encoder== is introduced to partition taxels into functional patches per finger, aggregating local responses into compact, finger-identity-preserving tactile tokens.
- Future tactile latents, crucial for action, are learned by aligning predictions from a deployable ==Foresight Action Expert (FAE)== with ground-truth targets from a ==Hindsight Action Expert (HAE)== during training.
- **ReTouch**'s online refinement mechanism asynchronously updates future tactile latents and action chunks using high-frequency real-time tactile feedback, maintaining alignment with evolving physical interactions during execution.

## Results

- ReTouch achieved an **83.6%** macro-average success rate on seven real-robot manipulation tasks, outperforming the strongest single baseline by **18.4** percentage points.
- The framework demonstrated enhanced robustness, achieving a **73.1%** average success rate under perturbations (e.g., object shifts, lighting changes, physical disturbances), surpassing the strongest baseline by **23.8** percentage points.
- Ablation studies confirmed that intra-chunk refinement (tactile predictions and actions) contributed the largest performance gain (**23.6** percentage points), and the structured tactile encoding provided a **14.5** percentage point improvement over a flattened representation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Preserving the finger-wise spatial structure and local contact topology within tactile representations is critical for effective fine-grained dexterous control in complex manipulation tasks.
> - For dynamic contact-rich tasks, future tactile prediction is most effective when treated as a continuously refined control state rather than a static forecast, adapting to real-time physical interactions.
> - Timely online refinement of both tactile predictions and corresponding action chunks, driven by immediate execution feedback, is essential for maintaining accuracy and robustness in evolving contact situations.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Dexterous manipulation in robotics, particularly tasks involving significant physical contact, presents challenges for autonomous systems. While Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models offer instruction understanding and scene-level perception, they often face a perceptual gap because visual input alone cannot reliably provide detailed information about contact locations, forces, or grasp stability. This limitation is particularly evident in multi-finger manipulation where hand–object occlusions can obscure contact regions. Tactile sensing is recognized as a modality that can provide local feedback necessary for fine-grained action adjustments.

The integration of tactile feedback into robotic policies has progressed through several approaches:
- **Direct-fusion methods** incorporate current tactile signals as an additional sensory input to enhance contact awareness.
- **Reactive methods** utilize high-frequency tactile or force feedback to revise actions online, addressing events such as slippage or contact shifts.
- **Predictive methods** forecast future tactile states or contact forces to guide action generation.
- **Predictive-reactive methods** combine future forecasts with online action refinement based on incoming feedback.

Despite these advancements, the application of tactile-aware policies to dexterous manipulation encounters two specific challenges that ReTouch addresses. First, dexterous hands generate a large volume of tactile data, while task-relevant contacts are typically sparse and localized to specific fingers and fingertip regions. Existing methods frequently flatten tactile signals or compress temporal force histories, which can obscure the finger-wise spatial structure of contact. This loss of structure can impede a policy's ability to correlate local contact changes with precise finger adjustments. Second, contact states in multi-finger manipulation can evolve rapidly due to slippage, contact displacement, or execution errors. This dynamism can cause future tactile predictions, generated at the beginning of an action sequence, to become outdated during execution. While some predictive-reactive methods refine actions with fast feedback, the tactile prediction itself, which guides these actions, is often not continually re-estimated using the latest high-rate tactile updates. This can lead to the predicted tactile state becoming stale and potentially misguiding subsequent action refinement.

ReTouch positions itself within the landscape of predictive-reactive VLA models by introducing innovations designed to mitigate these specific challenges. It proposes a structured tactile representation that preserves crucial spatial contact information and an online mechanism for continuously refining tactile predictions, thereby improving their alignment with dynamic physical interactions.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enable robust and effective contact-rich dexterous manipulation by robots, leveraging tactile feedback in a more integrated and dynamic manner than previous approaches. The work is motivated by two key limitations identified in existing tactile-aware manipulation policies.

The first motivation stems from the nature of tactile observations produced by dexterous hands. Such hands generate dense tactile signals, yet the information critical for manipulation is often sparse and localized to specific fingers and regions of the fingertips. Current methods frequently process these dense signals by flattening them into generic tokens or compressing each finger's temporal force history. This processing often compromises the preservation of the finger-wise spatial structure of contact. Consequently, the policy may struggle to associate observed local contact changes with the specific fine-grained adjustments required from individual fingers. The objective is therefore to develop a tactile representation that retains this essential spatial and identity information, allowing the model to accurately perceive and respond to localized contact cues for precise control.

The second motivation addresses the dynamic and often unpredictable nature of contact states during multi-finger manipulation. Factors such as slippage, contact displacement, and execution errors can cause rapid changes in hand-object interaction. Policies that rely on future tactile predictions, especially those generated at the commencement of an action chunk, risk these predictions becoming stale as the physical interaction evolves. While some advanced predictive-reactive methods use immediate tactile feedback to revise actions, the underlying predicted tactile state that guides these action revisions is not always continually updated or re-estimated within each high-rate feedback loop. This can result in a mismatch between the guiding prediction and the actual evolving contact state, potentially leading to suboptimal or incorrect action corrections. The objective is to establish an online refinement mechanism that continually updates future tactile predictions using real-time execution feedback, ensuring these predictions remain accurate and aligned with the ongoing physical interaction, thereby providing reliable guidance for responsive action generation.

In summary, ReTouch aims to:
1. **Develop a structured tactile representation** capable of preserving finger identity and local contact topology to support fine-grained dexterous control.
2. **Implement an online tactile prediction refinement mechanism** that jointly updates future tactile states and action chunks, using incoming tactile feedback to maintain alignment with evolving physical interactions.
3. **Demonstrate the effectiveness and robustness** of this integrated approach across a range of real-world contact-rich dexterous manipulation tasks, including scenarios with significant perturbations.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

ReTouch is presented as a Vision-Language-Action (VLA) framework designed for dexterous manipulation, featuring online-refined tactile prediction. Its methodology involves a decoupled execution scheme, separating low-frequency vision-language reasoning from high-frequency tactile prediction and action updates.

#### 3.1. Overview of the Framework

At each low-frequency update (9 Hz), a VLM caches semantic context ($c_k$) and the chunk-start robot state ($s_k$). This cached information then conditions a high-frequency (36 Hz) Foresight Action Expert (FAE). At each high-frequency call, the FAE takes the current tactile context ($T_t$), comprising current and historical tactile observations, along with future tactile latents carried over from the previous call. It then refines the predicted future tactile latents ($\hat{Z}^{+}_t$) and regenerates the action chunk ($\hat{A}_t$).

#### 3.2. Structured Tactile Patch Encoding

To address the challenge of dense tactile observations and preserve local contact topology, ReTouch introduces a **Tactile-Patch Encoder**.
- **Patch Partitioning:** The encoder partitions the 120 3D force taxels on each of the five fingers into five functional patches: tip, center, base, left, and right regions.
- **Local Response Aggregation:** For each patch `p` on finger `i` at time `t`, the encoder computes soft contact weights from force magnitudes and aggregates local tactile responses. This aggregated feature, $u_{i,p}^t$, includes contact-gated mean 3D force, component-wise maximum absolute force, contact area, and contact strength.
- **Patch-Informed Tokenization:** These local responses are projected and augmented with finger-identity and patch-position embeddings. The five patch features for each finger are then aggregated into a compact patch-informed finger token. The output of this stage is $z_{tac}^t = [z_1^t, \dots, z_5^t]$, representing structured tactile patch features for the five fingers. This design provides a compact representation (five tokens per frame) while explicitly preserving finger-wise local contact topology.
- **Pretraining:** The Tactile-Patch Encoder is pretrained with three prediction heads that predict: (1) relative contact-strength distribution across patches, (2) reconstruction of the contact-gated mean 3D force of each patch, and (3) contact state. These heads are trained with cross-entropy, force regression, and binary cross-entropy, respectively, to ensure that the compact finger tokens retain recoverable contact information. After pretraining, these heads are discarded, and the encoder weights are used to initialize and jointly optimize the policy model.

#### 3.3. Learning Action-Relevant Tactile Latents

ReTouch employs two Action Experts to learn future tactile latents that capture action-relevant contact information:
- **Hindsight Action Expert (HAE):** This is a training-only branch with access to ground-truth future tactile signals ($\tau^+_{t:t+H}$). It uses the Tactile-Patch Encoder to convert these signals into future tactile patch features, which then condition the HAE during action generation. The representations extracted from a specific alignment layer ($\ell$) of the HAE, $Z^{+,hid}_{t,\ell}$, serve as supervision targets, as they are intrinsically relevant to subsequent actions due to HAE's optimization objective.
- **Foresight Action Expert (FAE):** This is the deployable branch. It learns to predict corresponding future tactile latents, $\hat{Z}^{+}_{t,\ell}$, solely from current observations: the cached semantic context ($c_k$), chunk-start robot state ($s_k$), observed tactile context ($T_t$), and learnable Tactile Foresight Queries ($Q_{fore}$).
- **Latent Alignment:** A cosine distance loss ($L_{align}$) aligns the FAE's predicted latents with the HAE's hindsight targets. A stop-gradient is applied to the hindsight targets to prevent backpropagation through the HAE. During deployment, the HAE and alignment projector are removed, and only the FAE is used for inference.

#### 3.4. Online Tactile Prediction Refinement

To address the issue of stale predictions, ReTouch treats future tactile latents as recursively maintained control states that are continuously refined during execution.
- **Asynchronous Execution:** The VLM updates at 9 Hz, providing low-frequency semantic context, while the FAE runs at 36 Hz, performing high-frequency tactile prediction and action revision.
- **Refinement Process:** At each high-frequency call `t` (after the initial prediction), the FAE reuses the cached $c_k$ and $s_k$ and incorporates the updated tactile context $T_t$. The current Tactile Foresight Queries ($\tilde{Q}_{fore}^t$) are constructed from a latent prefix carried over from the previous call and learnable queries for the remaining future segments. The FAE then jointly predicts the refined future tactile latents ($\hat{Z}^{+}_t$) and updates the action chunk ($\hat{A}_t$). The latest tactile feedback is appended to $T_t$ for the next call.
- **Directional Attention Mask:** Within the FAE, a directional attention mask ensures that Tactile Foresight Queries can attend to context and carried-over latents but not action tokens. Action tokens can attend to the updated future tactile latents, guaranteeing that tactile predictions are formed before generating corresponding actions.
- **Training Alignment:** Training mirrors the asynchronous refinement scheme by randomly sampling offsets within action chunks, allowing the model to learn to update its future tactile latents and remaining action chunk from intermediate execution states.

#### 3.5. Training Objectives

ReTouch is trained in two stages:
1. **Tactile-Patch Encoder Pretraining:** Using $L_{TPE}$ (Equation 2).
2. **Policy Training:** Jointly optimizes action flow matching for both HAE ($L_{hid\_act}$) and FAE ($L_{fore\_act}$), future tactile latent alignment ($\lambda_{align}L_{align}$), and the random-offset update objective ($L_{update}$). Stop-gradient is applied to HAE targets.

#### 3.6. XHT-Dataset

A real-world dataset, XHT-Dataset, was collected on an XHand–UR7e platform. It comprises 900 demonstrations across seven contact-rich dexterous manipulation tasks, including visual observations, language instructions, robot states, robot actions, and dense tactile streams.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of ReTouch was conducted through closed-loop real-robot experiments across seven contact-rich dexterous manipulation tasks, using an XHand–UR7e platform. Performance was measured by normalized task score (%), averaged over 20 trials per method per task.

#### 4.1. Overall Task Performance (Table 1)

- **Superior Performance:** ReTouch achieved a macro-average success rate of **83.6%**, outperforming Tactile-VLA, the strongest single baseline, by **18.4 percentage points**.
- **Task-Specific Gains:** ReTouch ranked first in five tasks and tied for first in one (Button Press). Notable improvements were observed in Sponge Wipe and Liquid Transfer, where ReTouch surpassed the strongest task-specific baselines by 25.0 and 19.0 percentage points, respectively. These tasks involve sustained or dynamically changing contact, aligning with ReTouch's design motivations.
- **Inconsistent Baseline Effects:** Directly adding tactile observations to pretrained VLA baselines ($\pi_0$ and $\pi_{0.5}$) yielded inconsistent results, sometimes leading to a decrease in average success rate ($\pi_0$). Existing tactile policies demonstrated task dependence, with ViTacFormer performing better than ReTouch in the Cabinet Retrieval task (67.5% vs. 62.5%).

#### 4.2. Structured Tactile Encoding (Figure 4 and Appendix Table 5)

- **Representation Fidelity:** Visualizations demonstrated that patch-wise force reconstructions from the Tactile Patch Features closely matched ground-truth values. This indicates that the Tactile-Patch Encoder effectively compresses dense taxel measurements into compact finger-level tactile tokens while retaining finger identity, local contact location, and contact strength.
- **Pretraining Effectiveness:** Diagnostic comparisons of Tactile-Patch Encoder pretraining configurations (Appendix Table 5) showed that a balanced three-head configuration significantly improved contact F1 score, reduced canonical patch-distribution KL divergence, improved active-force vector L2, active-force direction cosine, and force-strength correlation compared to a single-head force-reconstruction configuration.

#### 4.3. Refinement of Tactile Prediction (Figure 5 and Appendix Table 6)

- **Impact of Online Refinement:** A comparison between ReTouch's online refinement and a "one-shot" variant (which re-predicts actions but keeps tactile predictions fixed) demonstrated the benefit of continuous updates.
- **Pre-contact Phase:** Both strategies performed similarly before contact was established.
- **In-contact Phase:** After contact, ReTouch's recursive refinement led to an increase of **0.908%** in mean latent cosine similarity (with target tactile latents) and a reduction of **2.139%** in overall action error (physical-space MSE). The reduction in action error became more pronounced with successive updates within an action chunk. This finding suggests that initial fixed predictions become misaligned with evolving tactile states post-contact, and feedback-driven updates are crucial for maintaining prediction accuracy and improving subsequent action generation.

#### 4.4. Robustness to Perturbations (Table 2)

- **Enhanced Robustness:** ReTouch achieved an average success rate of **73.1%** under challenging settings (height variations, object-position shifts, lighting changes, external pulling disturbances), outperforming the strongest baseline ($\pi_{0.5}$+tactile, 49.3%) by **23.8 percentage points**.
- **Specific Perturbation Resistance:**
 - **Contact Geometry:** ReTouch achieved the highest success rates under height variations and object-position shifts.
 - **Visual Changes:** It outperformed the strongest baseline by 20.0 percentage points under lighting changes, suggesting that tactile feedback provides complementary local contact information when visual observations are perturbed.
 - **Physical Disturbances:** The largest gain was observed under post-grasp pulling disturbances, where ReTouch achieved 85.0%, exceeding the strongest baseline by 30.0 percentage points. This aligns with the design to continually refine tactile predictions in response to physical interactions.

#### 4.5. Ablation Studies (Table 3)

- **Importance of Intra-Chunk Refinement:** The removal of intra-chunk refinement (both tactile predictions and actions) resulted in the largest degradation, reducing the average success rate by **23.6 percentage points**. This indicates that predictions and actions generated only at the beginning of a chunk are insufficient for adapting to evolving contact states.
- **Value of Tactile Prediction Refinement:** Retaining action updates while fixing the initial tactile prediction resulted in a 15.2 percentage point degradation. Removing future tactile prediction entirely caused a 16.0 percentage point degradation. The small difference (0.8 pp) suggests that a fixed one-shot tactile prediction offers limited benefit, with the main improvement stemming from continuous refinement of predictions.
- **Timeliness of Updates:** A non-blocking variant, where updates were delayed by approximately one action step, showed a 7.8 percentage point degradation, indicating the importance of timely updates for effective online refinement.
- **Role of Structured Encoding:** Replacing the Tactile-Patch Encoder with an MLP encoding flattened raw tactile signals (removing explicit finger-wise and local-region structure) reduced the average success rate by 14.5 percentage points, confirming the benefit of preserving this structural information in tactile representations.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The ReTouch framework presents an advancement in the field of contact-rich dexterous manipulation, particularly through its novel integration of structured tactile sensing and online prediction refinement.

**Advancement in Dexterous Manipulation:** ReTouch demonstrates improved performance and robustness over existing baselines, achieving a substantial increase in average success rates across a diverse set of real-world contact-rich tasks. This indicates progress toward more capable and adaptable robotic manipulation.

**Innovative Tactile Representation:** The Tactile-Patch Encoder introduces a method for structuring dense tactile observations from dexterous hands. By preserving finger identity and local contact topology, it provides a compact yet physically grounded representation that enables fine-grained control. This approach to tactile data processing could be adopted in other systems seeking to leverage dense tactile arrays effectively.

**Online Prediction Refinement Paradigm:** The core contribution of continually refining future tactile predictions and action chunks using execution-time feedback addresses a fundamental limitation in predictive control: the problem of stale predictions in dynamic environments. By treating tactile prediction as a recursively maintained control state rather than a fixed forecast, ReTouch offers a paradigm that can enhance responsiveness and robustness. This methodology has the potential to influence the design of future closed-loop control architectures in robotics.

**Enhanced Robustness to Real-World Variability:** The strong performance of ReTouch under various challenging conditions, including out-of-distribution object properties, lighting changes, and physical disturbances, highlights its potential for practical deployment. Such robustness is a critical factor for robotic systems operating in unstructured and unpredictable environments outside of laboratory settings.

**Contribution to VLA Models:** ReTouch demonstrates an effective strategy for integrating high-bandwidth, physically grounded tactile feedback into Vision-Language-Action models. This integration helps bridge the gap between high-level semantic understanding and low-level physical interaction control, enhancing the overall capability of VLA models in complex manipulation tasks.

**Dataset and Open Research:** The introduction of the XHT-Dataset, comprising 900 real-world demonstrations, provides a valuable resource for the research community. This dataset can facilitate further development and benchmarking of tactile-aware dexterous manipulation algorithms.

The findings from ReTouch suggest that for dynamic, contact-rich tasks, tactile prediction is most effective when treated as a control state that is continually updated by execution feedback, rather than a static, initial forecast. This understanding could guide future research in multi-modal sensor fusion, adaptive control strategies, and the co-design of tactile hardware and algorithms for advanced robotic manipulation.
