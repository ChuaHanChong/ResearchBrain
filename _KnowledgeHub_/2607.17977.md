# RynnBrain 1.1: Towards More Capable and Generalizable Embodied Foundation Model

> [!summary] Summary
> RynnBrain 1.1, an upgraded family of embodied foundation models from DAMO Academy, Alibaba Group, and Hupan Lab, enhances physical world understanding and generalizability for robot control. It achieves precise 3D grounding and contact point prediction capabilities, leading to significantly improved real-robot manipulation performance across diverse platforms compared to previous models.

## Problem

- Existing embodied models, like RynnBrain 1.0, provided less precise outputs (e.g., broad bounding boxes) that were not functionally aligned with specific robot manipulation requirements.
- Integrating explicit 3D structure understanding into embodied foundation models for robust physical world interaction was challenging.
- Effectively initializing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies for heterogeneous robot embodiments with diverse action spaces presented a limitation for generalizability.

## Method

- Introduced new pretraining tasks: ==Contact Point Prediction== to provide precise grasp locations and ==Native 3D Grounding== for explicit 3D object modeling from single images.
- Developed a ==unified cross-embodiment action space== with ==embodiment-specific masking==, enabling joint training of VLA policies across diverse robots like humanoids and dexterous hands.
- Scaled **RynnBrain 1.1** as a family of models (2B, 9B, 122B-A10B) based on the Qwen3.5 architecture, incorporating spatio-temporal modeling techniques like ==DeepStack== and ==Interleaved MRoPE==.

## Results

- RynnBrain 1.1 (122B-A10B) achieved leading performance on several embodied cognition benchmarks (e.g., VSI-Bench, MMSI, RefSpatial-Bench) and spatial grounding tasks (e.g., RefSpatial-Bench **79.1**, RynnBrain-Grounding **86.6**).
- The model demonstrated native 3D grounding, with RynnBrain 1.1-9B achieving **41.12 AP@15** on SUN RGB-D and outperforming a specialized WildDet3D detector (**23.44 AP3D** vs **22.6**).
- RynnBrain-VLA achieved stable and superior real-world manipulation performance across diverse robots, improving the average process score from **68.33%** (Qwen-based VLA) to **91.28%** and increasing the final success rate from **60.00%** to **86.67%**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicit embodied supervision with 3D grounding and precise contact point prediction is essential for transforming reasoning-intensive embodied cognition tasks and improving real-world robot manipulation.
> - General VLM scaling alone without specific embodied pretraining can be insufficient or detrimental for complex spatio-temporal reasoning tasks, highlighting the complementary nature of data-driven spatial supervision and model capacity.
> - Joint multi-task and multi-embodiment training allows different robot embodiments to provide complementary supervision, reinforcing a common visual-action representation and enhancing cross-embodiment generalization.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Embodied intelligence, requiring agents to operate in the physical world, extends beyond traditional visual recognition and language understanding. Robotic systems need to perceive objects, reason about spatial relationships, identify actionable regions, and translate this understanding into executable behaviors. While multimodal large language models (MLLMs) have demonstrated performance in image and video question answering, real-world robotics necessitates understanding 3D structure, reasoning across viewpoints, grounding language instructions to physical locations, and supporting downstream manipulation policies. The central challenge for embodied foundation models is to ensure visual understanding is physically grounded and transferable to real-robot actions.

Recent embodied foundation models, including RynnBrain 1.0, have advanced capabilities in areas such as object and region localization, spatio-temporal understanding, and robot-oriented planning. RynnBrain 1.0 specifically introduced a unified embodied multimodal model for egocentric understanding, spatial grounding, physically grounded reasoning, and planning, demonstrating the feasibility of combining general and robot-oriented multimodal understanding. This current work, RynnBrain 1.1, builds upon RynnBrain 1.0, addressing identified limitations to further align model outputs with robot manipulation requirements and enhance its utility as an initialization for Vision-Language-Action (VLA) post-training. The paper aims to contribute to the development of more capable and generalizable foundation models for embodied AI, specifically by integrating more direct physical world understanding into the model's pretraining and by enabling cross-embodiment action generalization.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this work is to develop RynnBrain 1.1, an upgraded family of embodied foundation models, and systematically investigate how model capabilities evolve with scale under a unified training framework. This development is motivated by two specific questions arising from the limitations of RynnBrain 1.0:

1. **Alignment of Representations and Outputs with Robot Manipulation:** RynnBrain 1.0's representations, particularly its grasp rectangle detection, were found to be less precise and functionally aligned with actual robot manipulation needs. Robots require more specific information, such as the exact contact point and gripper orientation, rather than a broad bounding box. This motivated the objective to learn representations and outputs that are more directly meaningful and usable for robot control. To achieve this, RynnBrain 1.1 introduces two new training tasks during embodied pretraining:
 - **Contact Point Prediction:** This task requires the model to locate the center point and the rotation angle of the gripper for grasping. This is intended to provide more precise and relevant features for downstream robot policies compared to the previous grasp rectangle detection.
 - **Native 3D Grounding:** Recognizing that robots operate in three-dimensional environments, the work aims to equip embodied foundation models with explicit 3D modeling capabilities. This is intended to provide richer 3D representations and directly usable 3D outputs for manipulation tasks, enabling the model to infer object position, dimensions, and orientation in real-world 3D space from a single image.

2. **Effectiveness of RynnBrain as Initialization for Downstream VLA Post-training:** The second motivation concerns how effectively RynnBrain can serve as a strong base model for fine-tuning Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies for heterogeneous robot embodiments and control interfaces. This requires a robust framework for action space generalization across diverse robots. To address this, the authors introduce:
 - **Unified Cross-Embodiment Action Space:** This involves organizing embodiment-specific actions into semantically aligned body-part groups within a unified action space.
 - **Embodiment-Specific Masking:** This mechanism allows only the dimensions available on each robot to be activated, enabling joint training of data from different embodiments within a single policy without requiring explicit alignment of incompatible action spaces.

By addressing these motivations, RynnBrain 1.1 seeks to create a more practical and generalizable embodied foundation model capable of advanced physical world understanding and effective control across a variety of robotic platforms.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

RynnBrain 1.1 is developed as a family of embodied foundation models, built upon the Qwen3.5 architecture and extending the principles of RynnBrain 1.0.

#### 3.1. Model Architecture

RynnBrain 1.1 employs a decoder-only vision-language architecture, initializing its large language model (LLM) backbone from Qwen3.5 variants. The family includes three scales: 2B, 9B, and 122B-A10B, all sharing a consistent overall architecture and training formulation to facilitate systematic study of embodied scaling. The architecture integrates a vision encoder, a vision-language projector, and the LLM backbone. To effectively process multimodal information and support long-context spatio-temporal modeling over images, videos, and embodied observations, techniques such as DeepStack and Interleaved MRoPE are adopted. The 122B-A10B model specifically utilizes sparse Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) training.

#### 3.2. Training Infrastructure

The training pipeline for RynnBrain 1.1 follows the design of RynnBrain 1.0, which is optimized for large-scale, heterogeneous embodied multimodal data. Key features include online sequence-length-aware load balancing, per-sample loss reduction, memory-efficient training with ZeRO and gradient checkpointing, and optimized MoE execution with expert parallelism and efficient token dispatching. This infrastructure aims to mitigate straggler effects from long-tail sequence length distributions in mixed image, video, text, and spatial-grounding data. For RynnBrain 1.1, this infrastructure is extended to the Qwen3.5-based model family to ensure that performance differences primarily reflect model scaling and data updates.

#### 3.3. Pretraining Framework

The embodied pretraining framework is built on two principles: **spatio-temporal memory** and **physical-world grounding**.
- **Unified Spatio-temporal Representation:** Visual observations (images or videos) are represented as ordered sequences of frames. Each frame is encoded into visual tokens, with temporal position information appended to preserve frame order and associate observations over time. This allows the model to capture object persistence, motion evolution, and long-range visual dependencies.
- **Physically Grounded Output Space:** The model's generation vocabulary is extended beyond natural language tokens to include discrete representations of bounding boxes (B), points (P), and trajectory waypoints (T). Continuous image coordinates are mapped to integers and predicted autoregressively alongside text tokens, enabling joint learning of semantic reasoning and spatial localization.

**New Pretraining Tasks:**
- **3D Grounding:** For the 2B and 9B models, explicit 3D modeling is introduced. The model takes a natural language instruction and camera intrinsics as input and predicts a 3D bounding box in the camera coordinate system. This box is parameterized by its center position (cx, cy, cz), dimensions (w, l, h), and orientation (pitch, yaw, roll) in physical units. These continuous 3D quantities are discretized into integer tokens for consistency with the autoregressive framework.
- **Contact Point Prediction:** This task aims to provide more precise guidance for robot manipulation. Instead of grasp rectangles, the model predicts a compact contact-centered representation (p, θ), where p=(x, y) is the center of the grasp and θ is the in-plane angle of the gripper. This representation simplifies the output space and focuses on action-relevant information.

**Optimization:** All RynnBrain 1.1 variants are optimized end-to-end using a causal autoregressive objective to minimize the negative log-likelihood of target sequences containing both language and spatial tokens. Scale-specific hyperparameters are selected through preliminary experiments.

#### 3.4. Pretraining Data

RynnBrain 1.1 utilizes a capability-oriented data organization scheme, categorized into:
- **General MLLM Data:** A broad corpus including LLaVA-OV-SI, LLaVA-Video, ShareGPT-4o-video, VideoGPT-plus, FineVideo, CinePile, ActivityNet, YouCook2, LLaVA-SFT, VL3, and newly incorporated self-collected RynnBrain-Thinking data to align with Qwen3.5-Thinking style.
- **Multi-dimensional Cognition Data:** Covers object understanding (RynnBrain-Object, RefCOCO, Google Refexp, Osprey-724K, DAM, VideoRefer-700K), spatial understanding (SenseNova-SI-800K, VSI-590K, VLM-3R, RynnBrain-Spatial), counting (RynnBrain-Counting, Molmo2), OCR (RynnBrain-OCR, Llama-Nemotron-VLM OCR), and egocentric task understanding (EgoRe-5M, EgoTaskQA, Env-QA, QAEgo4D, RoboVQA, Robo2VLM, ShareRobot).
- **Spatio-temporal Localization Data:** Includes object localization (ADE20K, COCOStuff, Mapillary, PACO-LVIS, PASCAL-Part, VG, RoboAfford-Object, RynnBrain-Grounding), area localization (RefSpatial, RoboAfford-Area, Molmo2, RynnBrain-Area, RoboRefer), affordance localization (RynnBrain-Affordance, RoboAfford-Affordance), and trajectory prediction (RynnBrain-Trajectory, FSD, MolmoAct).
- **3D-grounded Data:** A multi-source corpus is constructed for 3D grounding. This includes WildDet3D-Data (Essential and Synthetic subsets), which lifts 2D annotations to 3D and uses Qwen-VL to generate open-ended referring queries with spatial and attribute descriptions. Additionally, FoundationPose, a synthetic dataset with noise-free 3D bounding boxes from scanned objects, provides physically exact supervision.
- **Contact Point Data:** Simulation-native, multidimensional grasp annotations from sources like Grasp-Anything, Jacquard V2, GraspFactory, GraspNet-1B, and GraspClutter6D are converted into 2.6M training samples. A view-conditioned rendering and projection pipeline is used to transform these into 2D (p, θ) representations, filtering out unobservable or ambiguous views.
- **Physics-aware Planning Data:** High-level task instructions are paired with grounded sub-task descriptions. This includes AgibotWorld, Open X-Embodiment, RynnBrain-Planning, and the newly added Galaxea-G0, for which global task descriptions are generated using Gemini 3.1 Pro.

#### 3.5. Post-training for Vision-Language-Action (VLA) Model (RynnBrain-VLA)

- **Model Architecture:** RynnBrain-VLA utilizes the RynnBrain base model as a single-stream Diffusion Transformer (DiT) within a flow matching framework. It processes language instructions, multi-camera observations, robot state, and noisy action chunks to predict an action chunk.
- **Unified Cross-Embodiment Action Space:** Actions from heterogeneous robots are mapped to a shared 81-dimensional action space, partitioned into semantically aligned body-part groups (Arm-Joint, Arm-EEF, Gripper, Hand, Torso, Head). Embodiment-specific masks activate only physically available dimensions, allowing joint training across diverse robots. For example, Unitree G1 uses 14D Hand group plus a 64D SONIC latent token; Tianji-Wuji uses 14D Arm-Joint and 40D Hand; Astribot S1 uses 14D Arm-Joint, 2D Gripper, 3D Head, and 4D Torso.
- **Cross-Embodiment and Cross-Driver Deployment:** A three-component framework (Model layer, Control layer, Embodiment layer) decouples the VLA policy from embodiment-specific execution. This allows for adapting the same RynnBrain-VLA recipe to heterogeneous robots by implementing only the embodiment layer.
- **Real-Time Chunking (RTC):** To ensure smooth, low-latency control, RTC is adopted. The model predicts a 32-step action chunk, but new inference is triggered every 5 steps. The unexecuted portion of the previous chunk guides the denoising of the new chunk, maintaining continuity while allowing adaptation to new observations.
- **Fine-tuning Setup:** Teleoperation demonstrations are collected on Unitree G1, Astribot-S1, and Tianji-Wuji, covering pick-and-place and manipulation tasks. Demonstrations include synchronized visual observations, robot states, action trajectories, and task instructions. Policies are fine-tuned for 60k steps with a learning rate of 2x10^-5 and a batch size of 32.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

#### 4.1. Embodied Cognition Capability

RynnBrain 1.1 demonstrated improvements over previous RynnBrain models and other contemporary models across various embodied cognition benchmarks.
- **Scaling Trends:** At the 2B scale, improvements were task-dependent (e.g., MMSI from 34.1 to 40.5, MindCube from 50.1 to 61.7, RynnBrain-Spatial from 57.2 to 63.8). At the 9B scale, gains were more systematic, with RynnBrain 1.1 outperforming RynnBrain-8B on eight of ten shared benchmarks (e.g., MindCube from 56.6 to 86.9, MMSI from 39.6 to 47.0). The 122B-A10B model further extended this advantage, showing strong benefits from model scaling for reasoning-intensive embodied cognition.
- **Competitive Performance:** RynnBrain 1.1 achieved leading results on multiple cognition benchmarks at both 2B and 9B scales compared to other models in similar scale groups. The 122B-A10B model surpassed all evaluated proprietary and open-source models on VSI-Bench, MMSI, and RefSpatial-Bench.

#### 4.2. Embodied Localization Capability

RynnBrain 1.1 showed improved performance on spatial grounding tasks compared to RynnBrain 1.0 and other models.
- **Scaling Trends:** At 2B, RefSpatial-Bench improved from 52.7 to 58.5 and RynnBrain-Grounding from 79.1 to 84.1. At 9B, gains were larger for relational and motion-oriented tasks (e.g., RefSpatial-Bench from 59.2 to 67.2, RynnBrain-Trajectory from 64.5 to 70.0). The 122B-A10B model achieved leading performance on RefSpatial-Bench (79.1), RynnBrain-Grounding (86.6), and RynnBrain-Affordance (91.3), indicating that relational spatial grounding benefits significantly from increased model capacity.

#### 4.3. Scaling Analysis (RynnBrain 1.1 vs. Qwen3.5)

A systematic comparison between RynnBrain 1.1 and raw Qwen3.5 baselines revealed distinct scaling behaviors across embodied capabilities:
- **General Embodied Cognition:** Both RynnBrain 1.1 and Qwen3.5 improved with scale, with the performance gap narrowing, reflecting conventional MLLM scaling for recognition and comprehension tasks.
- **Reasoning-intensive Cognition:** RynnBrain 1.1 showed steady improvement (+38.6%), while Qwen3.5 exhibited negative scaling (−39.2%), with the performance gap widening from 18.2 to 50.8. This indicates that multi-view and temporal reasoning require explicit embodied supervision; larger general models without such grounding may produce less accurate responses.
- **Embodied Localization:** Both models improved, but Qwen3.5 at its largest scale still underperformed RynnBrain 1.1 at its smallest scale. This suggests that explicit coordinate supervision in data is more impactful than model scaling alone for spatio-temporal pointing tasks.
- **Embodied Pretraining as an Enabler:** The analysis concluded that embodied pretraining consistently establishes a higher performance floor and transforms reasoning-intensive tasks from degrading with pure VLM scaling to benefiting from it. Data-driven spatial supervision and model scaling are complementary.

#### 4.4. 3D Grounding

RynnBrain 1.1 demonstrated native 3D grounding capabilities.
- **Quantitative Performance:** On SUN RGB-D, RynnBrain 1.1-2B achieved 34.28 AP@15, surpassing general-purpose VLMs like Seed1.5-VL (33.5) and Gemini 2.0 Pro (32.5). Scaling to 9B further improved this to 41.12 AP@15, narrowing the gap to specialized systems like Gemini Robotics-ER (48.3). On WildDet3D-Bench, RynnBrain 1.1-9B (23.44 AP3D) surpassed a specialized WildDet3D detector (22.6).
- **Capability:** These results indicate that explicit 3D-grounded training enables even compact models to achieve metric spatial understanding, with larger model capacity further enhancing depth, size, and orientation reasoning. Qualitative examples showed the model's ability to localize, estimate extent, and orient objects from a single image across diverse scenes.

#### 4.5. Contact Point Prediction

Qualitative evaluations indicated RynnBrain 1.1's ability to identify task-relevant contact locations and orientations. The model demonstrated reasoning about object identity, geometry, state, and intended interaction, for instance, by selecting the handle of a mug for grasping, adapting to an overturned mug, and predicting appropriate contact for various object shapes in cluttered scenes.

#### 4.6. Real-Robot Evaluation (RynnBrain-VLA)

RynnBrain-VLA was deployed on three robot embodiments for real-world manipulation tasks.
- **Stable Real-World Performance:** RynnBrain-VLA achieved stable performance across Unitree G1 humanoid, Astribot-S1 bimanual robot, and Tianji-Wuji dexterous-hand system. For example, on "Pull the Chair" with Unitree G1, it achieved a 90% success rate compared to 75% for GR00T N1.7 with the same controller. Tasks requiring active viewpoint changes and continuous visual feedback (Astribot tasks) also showed robust performance.
- **Stronger Embodied Base:** RynnBrain-VLA consistently outperformed Qwen-Based-VLA (initialized from the base Qwen model with the same post-training recipe). The average process score improved from 68.33% to 91.28%, and the average final success rate increased from 60.00% to 86.67%, demonstrating that embodied foundation model training effectively transfers to downstream VLA policies.
- **Cross-Embodiment Generalization:** The consistent performance across diverse robot morphologies (humanoid, bimanual, dexterous hand) and control interfaces suggests that RynnBrain learns task- and interaction-level knowledge that generalizes beyond specific robot embodiments.
- **Benefits of Joint Multi-task and Multi-embodiment Training:** The RynnBrain-VLA_Generalist, trained jointly on multi-task and multi-embodiment data, improved the average process score from 91.28% to 94.14% and the final success rate from 86.67% to 91.67% compared to separately fine-tuned single-task policies. This indicates that different embodiments can provide complementary supervision, reinforcing a common visual-action representation while maintaining embodiment-specific control.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents RynnBrain 1.1, a family of embodied foundation models that advances the field of embodied AI through several contributions.

Firstly, the introduction of **contact point prediction and native 3D grounding** directly addresses a critical gap in previous embodied models, making their outputs more precise and actionable for robot manipulation. This closer alignment with physical robot requirements has the potential to simplify the development of downstream manipulation policies and improve their effectiveness in real-world scenarios.

Secondly, the work provides a **systematic scaling analysis** under a unified training framework, offering insights into how embodied capabilities evolve with model size. The finding that explicit embodied supervision is crucial for reasoning-intensive tasks, and that general VLM scaling alone can be insufficient or even detrimental in such cases, carries significant implications for future research directions in designing and training embodied AI models. It highlights the complementary nature of data-driven spatial supervision and model scaling.

Thirdly, the development of **RynnBrain-VLA with a unified cross-embodiment action space and embodiment-specific masking** demonstrates significant progress towards generalist robot policies. The robust performance in real-robot evaluations across diverse platforms (humanoid, bimanual, dexterous-hand systems) indicates a practical capability for heterogeneous robot control. The observed benefits of joint multi-task and multi-embodiment training suggest a pathway for leveraging diverse robot data to build more capable and adaptable shared policies, potentially accelerating skill acquisition across robotic platforms.

The superior performance of RynnBrain-VLA over Qwen-based and other generalist VLAs in long-horizon manipulation tasks further establishes RynnBrain 1.1 as a strong foundation for real-world robot control. This work contributes to bridging the gap between embodied perception/reasoning and actual robot action, moving towards more autonomous and versatile intelligent agents.

Future impacts could include more sophisticated robot autonomy in complex, unstructured environments, reduced development effort for new robotic applications, and the acceleration of research into advanced cognitive capabilities such as long-term memory, world modeling, and active perception in embodied systems. The open release of RynnBrain 1.1 components also fosters community-wide advancements in embodied AI.
