# PhyX: Does Your Model Have the "Wits" for Physical Reasoning?

> [!summary] Summary
> A collaborative research team introduces PHYX, a large-scale multimodal benchmark featuring 3,000 university-level physics questions grounded in visual scenarios. The benchmark reveals a performance gap exceeding 29% between state-of-the-art AI models and human experts in complex physical reasoning tasks.

## Problem

- Existing AI benchmarks primarily evaluate disciplinary knowledge recall or abstract mathematical problem-solving, lacking a robust assessment of 'physical reasoning' capabilities in multimodal contexts.
- Physical problem-solving requires models to decode implicit conditions from realistic visual scenarios, maintain physical consistency, and ground abstract formulas in real-world contexts, which prior benchmarks do not adequately capture.
- Current physics-specific benchmarks often rely on text-based problems or simplified, schematic visuals, limiting their capacity to evaluate the integration of visual perception with implicit physical constraints.

## Method

- The authors introduce **PHYX**, a large-scale multimodal benchmark with 3,000 visually-grounded university-level physics questions spanning six core domains (Mechanics, Electromagnetism, Thermodynamics, Wave/Acoustics, Optics, Modern Physics).
- Each question is available in multiple-choice and open-ended formats, and in three text variations (Full-Text, Text-DeRedundancy, Text-Minimal) to assess model reliance on explicit textual information versus visual cues.
- Questions are categorized into six distinct physical reasoning types, such as Physical Model Grounding and Implicit Condition Reasoning, to enable fine-grained diagnostic evaluation of model strengths and weaknesses.

## Results

- The best-performing MLLM (GPT-o4-mini) achieved **45.8%** accuracy on open-ended questions with de-redundant text, trailing behind human experts (**75.6%**) by over 29 percentage points.
- Error analysis of GPT-4o revealed a high prevalence of visual reasoning errors (**39.6%**) and a lack of deep domain knowledge (**38.5%**), indicating models struggle with interpreting visual contexts and applying fundamental physical principles.
- LLMs augmented with high-quality image captions demonstrated competitive performance with many MLLMs, suggesting current MLLMs face limitations in effectively processing raw visual signals for physical reasoning.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Physical reasoning, particularly when requiring visual interpretation and inference of implicit conditions, presents a more substantial challenge for current AI models than purely mathematical or general reasoning tasks.
> - State-of-the-art MLLMs exhibit significant over-reliance on detailed textual descriptions, struggling to leverage raw visual information effectively when textual cues are reduced or absent.
> - Dedicated reasoning-oriented models outperform general-purpose MLLMs, but a considerable performance gap of over 29% persists between the best AI models and human experts on physical reasoning tasks.
