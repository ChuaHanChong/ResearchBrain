# GATO: GPU-Accelerated and Batched Trajectory Optimization for Scalable Edge Model Predictive Control

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Columbia University developed GATO, an open-source, GPU-accelerated and batched trajectory optimization solver, designed for real-time performance with moderate batch sizes in advanced Model Predictive Control applications. This approach demonstrated up to 16x speedup over prior GPU solvers and achieved 10kHz control rates for complex tasks, leading to improved task success rates and robustness against disturbances in both simulation and hardware experiments.

## Problem

- Model Predictive Control (MPC) applications rely on computationally intensive nonlinear Trajectory Optimization (TO) problems, which are difficult to solve in real-time.
- Existing GPU-accelerated TO solvers are either optimized for single-problem parallelism, handle very large batches at sub-real-time rates, or sacrifice problem generality, creating a performance gap for real-time moderate batch processing.
- Advanced MPC strategies, such as online hyperparameter tuning, disturbance rejection, and planning under uncertainty, could greatly benefit from solving batches of TO problems, but lack an efficient, general-purpose solver.
- solution
- Developed GATO, an open-source, GPU-accelerated, batched trajectory optimization solver specifically optimized for moderate batch sizes (tens to low-hundreds) and time horizons.
- Implemented a Sequential Quadratic Programming (SQP) framework with an algorithm-software-hardware co-design approach to maximize GPU parallelism across multiple levels (block, warp, thread) for batched KKT system solutions and line searches.
- Utilized a preconditioned conjugate gradient (PCG) iterative method with a symmetric stair preconditioner for efficiently solving the Schur Complement of the KKT system, with optimized memory access and linear algebra operations for the GPU architecture.
- keyInsights
- A dedicated algorithm-software-hardware co-design for GPUs can significantly enhance the real-time performance of batched trajectory optimization, effectively closing a performance gap for moderate batch sizes.
- Distributing individual TO problems to separate CUDA thread blocks, combined with warp-optimized linear algebra within blocks, effectively minimizes synchronization overheads and maximizes throughput for batched PCG solves.
- Real-time processing of moderate batches of TO problems enables more sophisticated MPC capabilities, including faster online hyperparameter tuning, effective "hypothesize-and-test" strategies for disturbance rejection, and robust planning under uncertainty.
- results
- GATO achieved speedups of 1.4x to 16x over the GPU-based MPCGPU solver and 18x to 21x over the CPU-based OSQP solver for a 6-DoF manipulator tracking task.
- The solver demonstrated 10kHz control rates for problems with up to 512 total knot points (e.g., M=64, N=8), surpassing the 1kHz scaling reported for previous GPU-accelerated TO methods.
- Improved task success rates in simulation (from 33% to 99.2% for an unmodeled suspended load with increased batch size) and enabled successful hardware deployment for a 7-DoF manipulator under unmodeled loads, where single-solve methods failed.

## Method

- Developed **GATO**, an open-source, GPU-accelerated, batched trajectory optimization solver specifically optimized for moderate batch sizes (tens to low-hundreds) and time horizons.
- Implemented a Sequential Quadratic Programming (SQP) framework with an algorithm-software-hardware co-design approach to maximize GPU parallelism across multiple levels (block, warp, thread) for batched KKT system solutions and line searches.
- Utilized a preconditioned conjugate gradient (PCG) iterative method with a symmetric stair preconditioner for efficiently solving the Schur Complement of the KKT system, with optimized memory access and linear algebra operations for the GPU architecture.

## Results

- GATO achieved speedups of **1.4x** to **16x** over the GPU-based MPCGPU solver and **18x** to **21x** over the CPU-based OSQP solver for a 6-DoF manipulator tracking task.
- The solver demonstrated 10kHz control rates for problems with up to 512 total knot points (e.g., M=64, N=8), surpassing the 1kHz scaling reported for previous GPU-accelerated TO methods.
- Improved task success rates in simulation (from **33%** to **99.2%** for an unmodeled suspended load with increased batch size) and enabled successful hardware deployment for a 7-DoF manipulator under unmodeled loads, where single-solve methods failed.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A dedicated algorithm-software-hardware co-design for GPUs can significantly enhance the real-time performance of batched trajectory optimization, effectively closing a performance gap for moderate batch sizes.
> - Distributing individual TO problems to separate CUDA thread blocks, combined with warp-optimized linear algebra within blocks, effectively minimizes synchronization overheads and maximizes throughput for batched PCG solves.
> - Real-time processing of moderate batches of TO problems enables more sophisticated MPC capabilities, including faster online hyperparameter tuning, effective "hypothesize-and-test" strategies for disturbance rejection, and robust planning under uncertainty.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Model Predictive Control (MPC) is a prominent feedback control strategy employed across a range of robotic applications. A fundamental component of most nonlinear MPC implementations involves solving underlying optimal control problems, often formulated as Trajectory Optimization (TO) problems. These TO problems are typically addressed using first- or second-order optimization methods, but they are known to be computationally intensive and yield only locally optimal solutions.

To mitigate these computational burdens, researchers have pursued two primary avenues: employing careful approximations and simplifications of the optimal control problem, and leveraging hardware acceleration, particularly Graphics Processing Units (GPUs). GPU-accelerated efforts include the development of zeroth-order methods that construct sample-based approximate gradients, as well as hybrid, first-order, and second-order methods targeting both full solvers and their underlying numerical linear algebra and physics kernels. This body of work has demonstrated the real-time, real-world applicability of GPU-accelerated solvers on various robot hardware platforms.

Concurrently, there has been increasing interest in MPC applications that benefit from solving batches of trajectory optimization problems, often involving tens to low-hundreds of solves. While some of these applications utilize GPU parallelism, existing GPU-accelerated solvers generally fall into distinct categories: those designed to parallelize a single solve across the entire GPU, those capable of processing very large batches (e.g., over 1000 solves) but often at sub-real-time rates, or specialized solvers that achieve high speed by restricting problem generality (e.g., to simple point-mass models or single linearizations). This landscape creates a performance gap for applications requiring real-time execution of moderate batches (tens to low-hundreds) of general trajectory optimization problems, where current solutions exhibit trade-offs between latency, throughput, and model generality. This paper directly addresses this identified gap by developing a solver specifically optimized for this regime.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop GATO, an open-source, GPU-accelerated, batched trajectory optimization (TO) solver. GATO is specifically designed to achieve real-time throughput for moderate batch sizes, typically ranging from tens to low-hundreds of solves, each containing tens to low-hundreds of knot points.

The motivation for this work stems from several observations within the field of robotic control:
- **Computational Demand of MPC**: Model Predictive Control, despite its strong performance in robotics, inherently relies on solving computationally demanding nonlinear TO problems online. The real-time requirements of many robotic applications necessitate efficient and fast solvers.
- **Emergence of Batched TO in Advanced MPC**: Recent advancements in MPC have demonstrated that solving batches of TO problems can significantly enhance performance. This includes applications such as online hyperparameter tuning, advanced disturbance rejection strategies, and planning under uncertainty, where evaluating multiple hypotheses or exploring different parameters simultaneously can lead to improved control outcomes.
- **Limitations of Existing GPU-Accelerated Solvers**: Current GPU-accelerated TO solvers exhibit limitations in addressing the specific needs of moderate batch sizes. Solvers optimized for single-solve performance do not scale efficiently when multiple problems need to be solved in parallel. Conversely, solvers built for very large batches may not meet real-time requirements for smaller, but still substantial, batches. Furthermore, some high-speed GPU solvers achieve their performance by sacrificing the generality of the robot model or problem formulation, which restricts their applicability. This leaves a significant gap for a general-purpose solver that can handle moderate batch sizes in real-time without compromising model fidelity.
- **Algorithm-Software-Hardware Co-design Potential**: The authors are inspired by prior work like MPCGPU, which demonstrated the benefits of GPU acceleration through careful co-design of algorithms, software, and computational hardware for single-solve performance. The motivation is to extend this co-design philosophy to simultaneously solve multiple problems efficiently on a GPU.

By developing GATO, the researchers aim to provide a tool that enables state-of-the-art MPC applications requiring real-time, batched TO, thereby overcoming existing computational bottlenecks and broadening the scope of deployable autonomous systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

GATO's methodology is predicated on an algorithm-software-hardware co-design approach to maximize GPU parallelism for batched trajectory optimization. The solver is designed to handle batches of `M` trajectory optimization problems, each with `N` timesteps. The overall architecture is inspired by MPCGPU, adopting a three-step design flow common in direct methods for trajectory optimization: problem formation, linear system solve, and line search.

**Core Algorithmic Framework (Direct Trajectory Optimization):**
GATO employs a Sequential Quadratic Programming (SQP) approach. Each iteration involves:
1. **Quadratic Program (QP) Formation**: A quadratic approximation of the original nonlinear optimal control problem is formed via a second-order Taylor expansion around a nominal trajectory. This involves computing gradients and Hessians of cost and dynamics functions.
2. **KKT System Solution**: The Karush-Kuhn-Tucker (KKT) system corresponding to the QP is solved to obtain optimal primal-dual step directions (δX*, δU*).
3. **Line Search Update**: A line search procedure is performed to determine a step size (α*) that ensures sufficient descent on a merit function (L1 merit function is used) before updating the nominal trajectory.

**Batched KKT System Solution:**
The KKT system is solved by forming the Schur Complement, `S`, and then using a preconditioned conjugate gradient (PCG) iterative method to solve `Sλ* = γ`. A symmetric stair preconditioner, `Φ⁻¹`, is applied to improve the convergence rate of PCG. The formulation of `S`, `γ`, and `Φ⁻¹` leverages block-tridiagonal structures, which are exploited for efficient parallel computation.

**GPU-First Design and Parallelism Strategy:**
GATO's implementation incorporates parallelism at multiple levels:
- **Block-level Parallelism**: Discrete, naturally parallel components within each step of the batched solve are distributed among CUDA thread blocks. This applies to both timestep-level computations (e.g., forming elements of `S`, `γ`, `Φ⁻¹`, and merit function calculations) and problem-level computations (each thread block can be assigned a distinct TO problem for the PCG solve).
- **Warp-level Parallelism**: Within each thread block, warps (groups of 32 threads) distribute work over knot points or specific linear algebra operations, leveraging the native implicit synchronization of NVIDIA GPU hardware.
- **Thread-level Parallelism**: Individual threads within warps operate on rows or columns of state/control blocks, performing fine-grained linear algebra operations.

**Implementation Details:**
1. **Batched Problem Setup and Line Search**:
 - **Problem Formation**: For forming `S`, `γ`, and `Φ⁻¹`, GATO exploits block-based parallelism for each of the `N * M` total timesteps. The GRiD library is utilized for efficient dynamics gradient computations. Thread-based parallelism is used within each block for small-scale linear algebra.
 - **Merit Function and Line Search**: Similar block-based parallelism is used for computing merit function values across all `N * M * A` (A = number of line search iterates) total timesteps. A block-level reduction computes the merit function for each line search iterate across all batches.
 - **Memory Management**: Temporary variables are computed in fast shared memory. Final matrices and vectors are arranged densely and contiguously in global memory to maximize coalesced loads and stores for downstream solvers. The device's L2 cache is reserved to minimize global memory access during PCG. Data transfer overhead between CPU and GPU is minimized by only transferring current system states, goals, and final optimized trajectories.
2. **Batched PCG**:
 - **Problem-per-Block Assignment**: Each CUDA thread block is responsible for solving one linear system (corresponding to one TO problem in the batch). This design eliminates inter-block coordination and associated synchronization overheads.
 - **Warp-Optimized Linear Algebra**: Within a block, warps distribute computations over knot points, and threads handle row/column operations. Matrices and vectors are packed contiguously in row-major order by batch and knot points, exploiting the block tridiagonal structure of `S` and `Φ⁻¹`. This enables efficient warp-strided accesses and warp shuffle intrinsics for reductions.
 - **Block Tridiagonal Matrix-Vector Multiplication**: A warp-optimized routine uses shared memory tiles to stage blocks, performs thread-parallel fused multiply-adds, pipelines loads with `cudaMemcpyAsync`, and avoids atomics or grid-wide barriers.
 - **Kernel Tuning**: Shared-memory footprint, block dimensions, and register usage are tuned to maintain high occupancy and prevent register spilling.
 - **Compiler Optimizations**: Inner loops over small, compile-time dimensions are partially unrolled, and aggressive optimization flags (`-O3`, `-use_fast_math`) are used during compilation.

**Evaluation Approach:**
The effectiveness of GATO is demonstrated through:
- **Simulated Benchmarks**: Comparison against OSQP (CPU QP solver) and MPCGPU (state-of-the-art GPU solver) on a 6-DoF manipulator tracking task, evaluating speedups and scalability across varying batch sizes and time horizons.
- **Case Studies**:
 - **Online Hyperparameter Optimization**: Using a 7-DoF manipulator to adjust a damping parameter ρ to improve convergence.
 - **Fixed Disturbance Rejection**: A 6-DoF manipulator tracking a trajectory under unmodeled external force, using a "hypothesize-and-test" strategy with batched solves.
 - **Planning Under Uncertainty**: A 7-DoF manipulator performing a pick-and-place task with an unmodeled suspended load, evaluating robustness and success rates in simulation and on hardware.
- **Hardware Validation**: Deployment on a physical KUKA iiwa LBR14 manipulator for real-world performance assessment.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of GATO involved a series of simulated benchmarks, case studies, and a hardware demonstration.

**Scalability Benchmarks:**
- **Performance Comparison**: GATO demonstrated significant speedups over baseline solvers. For a 6-DoF manipulator tracking task with a horizon of N=64, GATO achieved speedups of 18−21× over the CPU-based OSQP solver and 1.4−16× over the GPU-based MPCGPU solver as the batch size (M) increased from 1 to 128.
- **Latency and Scaling**: While MPCGPU was 1.4× slower than GATO for a single problem (M=1), its latency scaled nearly linearly with batch size, resulting in GATO being 16× faster at M=128. OSQP consistently exhibited higher latency than both GPU methods.
- **Batch Size and Time Horizon Flexibility**: A heat map analysis showed GATO reaching kHz control rates for large batches of short-horizon trajectories (e.g., M=512, N=8) and smaller batches of longer-horizon trajectories (e.g., M=32, N=128). The solver demonstrated efficient GPU resource utilization up to hardware limits, with performance scaling approximately linearly with the total number of knot points (`N * M`). For instance, problems with up to 512 total knot points (e.g., (N, M) = (64, 8), (8, 64), (16, 32)) could achieve real-time iterations at approximately 10kHz (100μs). This performance metric surpasses the 1kHz scaling reported for MPCGPU and other CPU-based state-of-the-art methods.

**Case Study 1: Online Hyperparameter Optimization:**
- **Convergence Improvement**: When optimizing a damping parameter `ρ` for a 7-DoF KUKA iiwa manipulator, larger batch sizes consistently led to a faster reduction in merit function values, indicating accelerated convergence to an optimal solution. Batches larger than `M=16` outperformed the minimum merit achieved by a single-solve baseline within 20 SQP iterations. Batches of `M=32` to `M=128` achieved nearly half the initial merit after only a single SQP iteration.
- **Saturation Effects**: The gains in convergence rate began to saturate beyond `M ≈ 32`, suggesting an optimal range for batch sizes that aligns with the "tens to low-hundreds" target.

**Case Study 2: Fixed Disturbance Rejection:**
- **"Online Hypothesize-and-Test" Effectiveness**: Employing GATO to evaluate multiple candidate disturbance models in parallel proved effective for a 6-DoF manipulator tracking a figure-8 trajectory under an unmodeled external force.
- **Optimal Batch Size for Disturbance Rejection**: Both tracking error and joint velocities decreased with increasing batch size, reaching an optimal point around `M = 32`. Beyond this, the increased solve times associated with larger batches began to offset the benefits of finer hypothesis granularity, leading to an increase in closed-loop error. For a 50 N disturbance, `M=32` substantially improved tracking compared to a single-solve baseline.

**Case Study 3: Planning Under Uncertainty (Unmodeled Suspended Load):**
- **Simulation Studies**: For a 7-DoF KUKA iiwa manipulator performing a pick-and-place task with an unmodeled suspended load:
 - **Success Rate**: The task success rate increased significantly with batch size, from 33.0% for `M=1` to 91.8% for `M=8`, and further to 99.2% for `M=128`.
 - **Task Completion Time**: Mean task completion time also improved, decreasing from 20.1s for `M=1` to 6.7s for `M=128`.
 - **Solve Time Distribution**: Larger batch sizes (`M=64`, `M=128`) enabled faster problem solving across almost all disturbance scenarios, indicating more accurate unmodeled disturbance rejection.
- **Hardware Deployment**: Experiments on a physical KUKA iiwa LBR14 robot confirmed these findings:
 - **No Load (100Hz)**: A batch size of `M=32` completed 5/5 targets in 9.49s, outperforming `M=1` which also completed 5/5 but took 16.93s.
 - **4kg Unmodeled Load (1000Hz)**: The batched solver (`M=32`) successfully completed 3/3 targets in 6.71s. In contrast, the single-solve approach (`M=1`) failed to complete any targets (0/3), timing out at 24.00s. This demonstrated the batched solver's robustness to unmodeled forces, control loop delays, system identification errors, and sensor noise.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

GATO represents an advancement in the field of real-time optimal control for robotics, particularly by addressing a specific, underserved segment of the computational landscape.

**Addressing a Research Gap:** The work directly targets the gap in existing GPU-accelerated solvers by providing a real-time, general-purpose solution for moderate batch sizes (tens to low-hundreds) of trajectory optimization problems. This is crucial for state-of-the-art MPC applications that benefit from parallel hypothesis testing, hyperparameter exploration, or robust planning under uncertainty.

**Significant Performance Improvements:** GATO's demonstrated speedups, reaching 18−21× over CPU baselines and 1.4−16× over GPU baselines, enable significantly higher control rates or more complex decision-making within stringent real-time constraints. Its ability to achieve 10kHz control rates for total knot points up to 512, even with batches, surpasses previous benchmarks for general GPU-accelerated TO. This performance is a direct result of the co-design across algorithm, software, and computational hardware, effectively utilizing multi-level GPU parallelism.

**Enhanced MPC Capabilities and Robustness:**
- **Faster Convergence and Robustness**: The case study on online hyperparameter optimization indicates that GATO can accelerate the convergence of MPC policies, potentially leading to more optimal and robust controllers in deployed systems.
- **Improved Disturbance Rejection**: The "online hypothesize-and-test" strategy, made feasible by GATO's batched processing, significantly improves the ability of MPC to reject fixed and time-varying unmodeled disturbances. This enhances the operational reliability of robots in dynamic and uncertain environments.
- **Planning Under Uncertainty**: The success in both simulated and physical hardware deployments with unmodeled loads highlights GATO's capability to improve planning under uncertainty, leading to higher task success rates and reduced completion times in challenging scenarios. This improved robustness to real-world complexities like model errors, sensor noise, and control delays is particularly impactful for practical robotic deployments.

**Enabling New Applications:** The real-time processing of moderate batches of TO problems can enable new classes of advanced MPC strategies. Potential future impacts include:
- **Reinforcement Learning Integration**: Guiding agent exploration in actor-critic reinforcement learning algorithms by evaluating multiple prospective actions.
- **Complex Contact Dynamics**: Facilitating branch-and-bound-based methods for contact-implicit trajectory optimization, where numerous contact sequences might need to be evaluated.
- **Edge Computing Deployment**: Its efficiency and GPU-first design make it suitable for deployment on low-power GPU platforms like NVIDIA Jetson, extending advanced MPC capabilities to mobile robots and resource-constrained edge systems.

**Open-Source Contribution:** Releasing GATO as open-source software supports reproducibility, encourages adoption within the research community, and fosters further development and integration into diverse robotic platforms and applications.
