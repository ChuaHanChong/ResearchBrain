# GATO: GPU-Accelerated and Batched Trajectory Optimization for Scalable Edge Model Predictive Control

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Columbia University developed GATO, an open-source, GPU-accelerated and batched trajectory optimization solver, designed for real-time performance with moderate batch sizes in advanced Model Predictive Control applications. This approach demonstrated up to 16x speedup over prior GPU solvers and achieved 10kHz control rates for complex tasks, leading to improved task success rates and robustness against disturbances in both simulation and hardware experiments.

## Problem

- Model Predictive Control (MPC) applications rely on computationally intensive nonlinear Trajectory Optimization (TO) problems, which are difficult to solve in real-time.
- Existing GPU-accelerated TO solvers are either optimized for single-problem parallelism, handle very large batches at sub-real-time rates, or sacrifice problem generality, creating a performance gap for real-time moderate batch processing.
- Advanced MPC strategies, such as online hyperparameter tuning, disturbance rejection, and planning under uncertainty, could greatly benefit from solving batches of TO problems, but lack an efficient, general-purpose solver.
- solution
- Developed GATO, an open-source, GPU-accelerated, batched trajectory optimization solver specifically optimized for moderate batch sizes (tens to low-hundreds) and time horizons.
- Implemented a Sequential Quadratic Programming (SQP) framework with an algorithm-software-hardware co-design approach to maximize GPU parallelism across multiple levels (block, warp, thread) for batched KKT system solutions and line searches.
- Utilized a preconditioned conjugate gradient (PCG) iterative method with a symmetric stair preconditioner for efficiently solving the Schur Complement of the KKT system, with optimized memory access and linear algebra operations for the GPU architecture.
- keyInsights
- A dedicated algorithm-software-hardware co-design for GPUs can significantly enhance the real-time performance of batched trajectory optimization, effectively closing a performance gap for moderate batch sizes.
- Distributing individual TO problems to separate CUDA thread blocks, combined with warp-optimized linear algebra within blocks, effectively minimizes synchronization overheads and maximizes throughput for batched PCG solves.
- Real-time processing of moderate batches of TO problems enables more sophisticated MPC capabilities, including faster online hyperparameter tuning, effective "hypothesize-and-test" strategies for disturbance rejection, and robust planning under uncertainty.
- results
- GATO achieved speedups of 1.4x to 16x over the GPU-based MPCGPU solver and 18x to 21x over the CPU-based OSQP solver for a 6-DoF manipulator tracking task.
- The solver demonstrated 10kHz control rates for problems with up to 512 total knot points (e.g., M=64, N=8), surpassing the 1kHz scaling reported for previous GPU-accelerated TO methods.
- Improved task success rates in simulation (from 33% to 99.2% for an unmodeled suspended load with increased batch size) and enabled successful hardware deployment for a 7-DoF manipulator under unmodeled loads, where single-solve methods failed.

## Method

- Developed **GATO**, an open-source, GPU-accelerated, batched trajectory optimization solver specifically optimized for moderate batch sizes (tens to low-hundreds) and time horizons.
- Implemented a Sequential Quadratic Programming (SQP) framework with an algorithm-software-hardware co-design approach to maximize GPU parallelism across multiple levels (block, warp, thread) for batched KKT system solutions and line searches.
- Utilized a preconditioned conjugate gradient (PCG) iterative method with a symmetric stair preconditioner for efficiently solving the Schur Complement of the KKT system, with optimized memory access and linear algebra operations for the GPU architecture.

## Results

- GATO achieved speedups of **1.4x** to **16x** over the GPU-based MPCGPU solver and **18x** to **21x** over the CPU-based OSQP solver for a 6-DoF manipulator tracking task.
- The solver demonstrated 10kHz control rates for problems with up to 512 total knot points (e.g., M=64, N=8), surpassing the 1kHz scaling reported for previous GPU-accelerated TO methods.
- Improved task success rates in simulation (from **33%** to **99.2%** for an unmodeled suspended load with increased batch size) and enabled successful hardware deployment for a 7-DoF manipulator under unmodeled loads, where single-solve methods failed.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A dedicated algorithm-software-hardware co-design for GPUs can significantly enhance the real-time performance of batched trajectory optimization, effectively closing a performance gap for moderate batch sizes.
> - Distributing individual TO problems to separate CUDA thread blocks, combined with warp-optimized linear algebra within blocks, effectively minimizes synchronization overheads and maximizes throughput for batched PCG solves.
> - Real-time processing of moderate batches of TO problems enables more sophisticated MPC capabilities, including faster online hyperparameter tuning, effective "hypothesize-and-test" strategies for disturbance rejection, and robust planning under uncertainty.
