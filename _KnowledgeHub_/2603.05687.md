# Contact-Grounded Policy: Dexterous Visuotactile Policy with Generative Contact Grounding

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers developed the Contact-Grounded Policy (CGP), a dexterous visuotactile framework that explicitly grounds multi-point contacts by predicting coupled robot state and tactile feedback trajectories, then translating these into controller-executable targets. CGP consistently outperformed visuomotor and visuotactile diffusion-policy baselines across various complex manipulation tasks, demonstrating effective contact grounding and maintaining real-time inference efficiency.

## Problem

- Dexterous manipulation requires continuous, fine-grained regulation of complex, high-dimensional, and rapidly evolving multi-point contact interactions across multiple fingers.
- Current methods (grasp-centric, RL, IL) often predict kinematic trajectories without explicit contact semantics, struggling to represent and control evolving multi-point contacts effectively.
- Existing tactile-informed policies typically treat tactile signals as auxiliary observations, failing to produce controller-compatible targets that reflect actual contact evolution and ensure physical consistency.

## Method

- The **Contact-Grounded Policy (CGP)** framework is introduced, which models dexterous manipulation as a ==contact-grounding problem== to explicitly manage multi-point interactions.
- It employs a ==conditional trajectory generator (diffusion model)== to predict coupled future trajectories of both actual robot state and expected ==tactile feedback==.
- A learned, controller-specific ==contact-consistency mapping== translates these predicted coupled trajectories into physically consistent, executable target robot states for a low-level ==compliance controller==. A ==KL-regularized VAE== compresses tactile observations into a compact latent space for stable long-horizon forecasts.

## Results

- **CGP** consistently outperformed visuomotor and visuotactile diffusion-policy baselines across **five** complex simulation and real-robot dexterous manipulation tasks, including jar opening and in-hand box flipping.
- The framework effectively grounded contacts, evidenced by **close alignment** between predicted and observed tactile signals in rollouts, demonstrating reliable execution of contact evolution.
- The contact-consistency mapping proved crucial, requiring **both robot state and tactile input** for accurate target predictions, and maintained **real-time inference latency** comparable to simpler baselines.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly modeling and grounding multi-point contacts by predicting coupled trajectories of robot state and tactile feedback leads to more robust and fine-grained dexterous manipulation.
> - A learned, data-driven contact-consistency mapping effectively translates high-level contact predictions into controller-executable target robot states, ensuring physical consistency during deployment.
> - Employing a KL-regularized VAE to compress tactile observations into a compact latent space is crucial for stable and high-fidelity long-horizon tactile forecasts by the diffusion model.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Dexterous manipulation using multi-finger robotic hands presents a significant challenge in robotics due to the complex, high-dimensional, and continuously evolving multi-point contact interactions. These interactions are non-linear, partially observable, and highly sensitive to factors such as object geometry, friction, and slip. Existing robotic manipulation paradigms encounter various limitations when addressing these intricacies.

Grasp-centric pipelines prioritize establishing stable grasp configurations, which are effective for pick-and-place tasks but often constrain subsequent finger motion, limiting their utility for contact-rich behaviors like in-hand manipulation or tool use. Reinforcement learning (RL) offers the potential to discover complex contact strategies; however, RL policies frequently face difficulties with sim-to-real transfer, particularly with high-dimensional visual and tactile observations, and often necessitate extensive reward engineering for delicate tasks.

Imitation learning from human demonstrations has emerged as a promising approach for contact-rich dexterous manipulation. Humans naturally regulate distributed, time-varying contacts through coordinated finger movements and compliant interactions, making human demonstrations a rich source of data. This paradigm bypasses complex reward design and can scale with additional data. Despite its advantages, many visuomotor imitation policies have demonstrated limitations in complex, contact-rich scenarios. A core issue is that these policies often predict only kinematic trajectories, lacking explicit contact semantics, which hinders their ability to represent and control evolving multi-point contacts effectively.

To overcome these limitations, policies require advancements beyond kinematics-only prediction. This includes the integration of multi-modal perception, leveraging tactile feedback alongside vision to mitigate ambiguity under partial observability. Furthermore, policies need to move beyond merely treating tactile signals as additional observations. Instead, they must model the contact state explicitly and consider how their action outputs interact with the low-level controller dynamics. Specifically, a policy should generate controller-compatible targets that reflect contact evolution and can be faithfully executed by a compliance controller during deployment. Without this grounding, learned outputs can become physically inconsistent, leading to issues such as slip or overly stiff interactions.

This paper introduces Contact-Grounded Policy (CGP) as a framework to address this gap. CGP distinguishes itself by explicitly grounding multi-point contacts. While prior work has explored tactile-informed policy learning and methods for predicting control-relevant interaction targets (such as adaptive compliance references or sparse fingertip forces), many of these approaches either treat tactile signals as auxiliary, or rely on simplified contact abstractions (e.g., single arm end-effector force vectors or single force vectors per gripper finger). These simplifications limit their extensibility to the distributed contact points and high degrees of freedom inherent in multi-finger dexterous hands. CGP aims to provide a comprehensive solution by modeling dense, multi-patch contacts across the entire hand and leveraging this detailed contact information to produce executable control targets, thereby moving beyond previous limitations in representing and controlling complex contact interactions.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for developing the Contact-Grounded Policy (CGP) framework stems from the inherent difficulties associated with dexterous manipulation using multi-finger robotic hands. These difficulties arise from the need to continuously regulate rich, high-dimensional contact interactions that are distributed across multiple contact points, evolve rapidly, and are highly sensitive to factors like object geometry, frictional transitions, and slip. The core challenge is that current robotic policies often fail to adequately account for these dynamic and complex contact mechanics.

The specific limitations of existing paradigms that motivated this work include:
- **Inadequate Contact Representation in Grasp-Centric Methods:** While effective for rigid pick-and-place, these methods typically constrain subsequent finger motion once a grasp is established, limiting their ability to perform continuous reconfiguration and active contact regulation necessary for in-hand manipulation or tool use.
- **Challenges in Reinforcement Learning (RL):** RL can discover complex contact strategies, but often struggles with sim-to-real transfer, particularly with visual and tactile observations, and requires extensive, task-specific reward engineering for delicate manipulation.
- **Kinematics-Only Limitations in Imitation Learning:** Although imitation learning from human demonstrations is well-suited for contact-rich tasks, many proposed visuomotor policies predict only kinematic trajectories. This lack of explicit contact semantics makes it difficult to represent and control evolving multi-point contacts effectively.
- **Loose Coupling of Tactile Signals in Existing Tactile-Informed Policies:** Many tactile-informed approaches treat tactile signals as additional observations or auxiliary prediction targets. They often do not explicitly model contact state or how action outputs interact with low-level controller dynamics. This can lead to a disconnect where models predict tactile patterns, but the robot's low-level controller cannot reliably reproduce those predicted contacts during execution, resulting in physically inconsistent interactions.

To address these motivations, the key objectives of the Contact-Grounded Policy (CGP) framework are:
1. **Ground Evolving Multi-Point Contacts:** To develop a visuotactile policy that explicitly grounds multi-point contacts, rather than merely using tactile signals as additional observations. This involves understanding and predicting the full range of distributed and evolving contact interactions across a high-DOF hand.
2. **Predict Coupled Trajectories:** To forecast coupled trajectories of the actual robot state (kinematics) and tactile feedback in a compressed latent space. This structured prediction aims to provide a comprehensive description of the intended contact evolution.
3. **Learn a Contact-Consistency Mapping:** To develop a learned mapping that converts these predicted coupled state-tactile pairs into controller-executable target robot states. This mapping is designed to account for the dynamics of a compliance controller, ensuring that the predicted contacts can be faithfully realized by the robot.
4. **Enable Real-Time Execution:** To facilitate real-time execution of the intended contact evolution by producing concrete, contact-consistent targets that a low-level compliance controller can track effectively.
5. **Achieve Efficient Tactile Prediction:** To integrate an efficient latent tactile prediction model that delivers high-fidelity tactile forecasts. This involves compressing high-dimensional raw tactile observations into a compact, well-behaved latent space to make long-horizon generation computationally feasible.

In summary, CGP is motivated by the need for robotic manipulation policies to move beyond kinematic control and auxiliary tactile sensing, towards a paradigm where the policy explicitly understands, predicts, and grounds complex contact interactions through controller-compatible action outputs.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The Contact-Grounded Policy (CGP) is a supervised policy learning framework designed for dexterous visuotactile manipulation, conceptualizing the problem as contact grounding. This methodology involves predicting future actual robot states and corresponding tactile feedback, and then translating these predictions into executable targets for a compliance controller via a learned mapping.

**A. Problem Setup**
The robot system comprises an arm and a multi-finger hand equipped with tactile sensing. At each timestep `t`, the robot receives multi-modal observations `o_t = {i_t, u_t, x_t}`.
- `i_t`: Vision observation, consisting of `N_i` RGB views (e.g., agent-view, wrist-view).
- `u_t`: Tactile observation, which can be `N_u` RGB images from vision-based tactile sensors or `N_tac` tactile units with `d_tac` features per unit (dense tactile arrays).
- `x_t`: Robot state, including end-effector pose (translation and quaternion rotation) and hand joint angles.
The policy outputs a target robot state `a_t`, which serves as a reference for a low-level tracking controller (e.g., an operational space impedance controller for the arm and a joint space proportional-derivative (PD) controller for the hand). The target state includes arm translation, 6D continuous rotation (rot6D) for arm orientation, and hand joint targets.

**B. Contact Grounding**
CGP represents contact not by explicit locations or modes, but through the measurable triplet `(x_t, u_t, a_t)`: actual robot state, tactile feedback, and compliance controller reference (target robot state). This representation implicitly captures complex, distributed, and evolving multi-point contacts. The interaction is understood as a discrepancy between `a_t` and `x_t`, with `u_t` reflecting the resulting contact outcome.

A controller-specific **contact-consistency mapping** `M_ϕ` is learned: `a_t = M_ϕ(x_t, u_t)`. This mapping infers the target state `a_t` that, if tracked, would reproduce the observed interaction `(x_t, u_t)` under the specific compliance controller. This data-driven approach avoids explicit modeling of complex contact dynamics or fixed parameterizations, remaining flexible to varying contact patterns.

**C. Policy Pipeline Overview**
The CGP pipeline consists of two coupled components:
1. **Contact-Consistency Mapping (M_ϕ):** This component infers the controller reference (target robot state) that is consistent with the current actual robot state and tactile interaction.
2. **Conditional Trajectory Generator (π_θ):** Conditioned on a short history of observations `O_t`, this component samples coupled trajectories of future actual robot states and tactile feedback `(ˆX_t, ˆU_t)`. This generator is implemented as a diffusion model parameterized by a U-Net denoiser.

This factorization is designed to first model contact evolution as paired state-tactile trajectories (`π_θ`), and then translate these desired interactions into executable low-level controller references (`M_ϕ`).

- **Residual Mapping:** The `M_ϕ` mapping is formulated in a residual form, predicting an offset from the current actual robot state rather than regressing the target state directly. This improves conditioning and robustness.
- **Inference and Execution:** At test time, `π_θ` samples future actual robot state and tactile feedback trajectories. The `M_ϕ` mapping converts these step-wise into target robot states (`ˆa_t+k = M_ϕ(ˆx_t+k, ˆu_t+k)`). The compliance controller tracks the next target state, and the policy replans in a receding-horizon manner.

**D. Latent Tactile Generation**
To handle high-dimensional tactile observations and enable efficient real-time generation over a prediction horizon, CGP generates tactile trajectories in a learned latent space.
- **Tactile Compression with VAE:** A variational autoencoder (VAE) compresses raw tactile observations `u_t` into a compact latent representation `h_t`. KL regularization is applied to encourage a compact and well-structured latent space, which is beneficial for stable diffusion-based generation. The VAE backbone is adapted for different tactile modalities (arrays or images).
- **Coupled Diffusion over State and Tactile Latent:** The future trajectory `Y_t = (x_t+1:t+T, h_t+1:t+T)` (concatenating future actual robot states and tactile latent states) is modeled using a diffusion model. A U-Net denoiser `π_θ` is trained to predict the noise injected into a perturbed trajectory, conditioned on the observation history `O_t`.

**E. Implementation Details**
- **Tactile Encoders and Decoders:** Different architectures are used for tactile arrays (1D ResNet with convolutions along the tactile-unit dimension) and vision-based tactile sensors (shared 2D ResNet for each RGB image). Encoder weights are not shared across components.
- **Visual Encoder and Diffusion:** Follows a U-Net-based conditional diffusion model with DDIM sampling. Visual, tactile, and robot state features are concatenated and injected into the U-Net via FiLM conditioning. For real hardware, per-sensor tactile features are aggregated via cross-sensor self-attention before concatenation.
- **Contact-Consistency Mapping:** A lightweight network. For tactile arrays, it decodes the predicted tactile latent to raw tactile, re-encodes it, and concatenates the feature with the actual robot state, then uses an MLP to predict the target state. For vision-based tactile sensors (Digit360), to reduce inference cost, the diffusion-predicted tactile latent states are directly concatenated with actual robot states and fed to an MLP.

**F. System Overview**
- **Simulation:** Uses a UR5 arm with an operational space impedance controller and a Tesollo DG-5F five-finger 20-DoF hand with a joint space PD controller. Tactile sensing is provided by a dense tactile array of 748 points (3D force vectors). Visual observations come from two RGB cameras. Demonstrations are collected via VR teleoperation using a Meta Quest 3, with retargeting via RelaxedIK.
- **Real-Robot:** Employs a Franka Panda arm with an operational space impedance controller and an Allegro V5 four-finger 16-DoF hand with a joint space PD controller. Tactile sensing is provided by four Digit360 vision-based sensors (RGB tactile images). Visual sensing uses two RGB cameras. Demonstrations are collected via OptiTrack motion capture and an instrumented glove, with retargeting via RelaxedIK.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of CGP encompassed performance on contact-rich dexterous manipulation tasks, validation of the contact-consistency mapping, analysis of tactile reconstruction and compression, and assessment of time efficiency and robustness.

**A. Performance on Contact-Rich Dexterous Manipulation**
CGP was evaluated in closed-loop execution across five challenging tasks: In-Hand Box Flipping, Fragile Egg Grasping, and Dish Wiping in simulation, and Jar Opening and Real In-Hand Box Flipping on a physical robot.
- **Superior Performance:** CGP consistently outperformed both visuomotor and visuotactile diffusion-policy baselines across all tasks. For instance, in real-world Jar Opening, CGP achieved a 93.3% success rate, compared to 66.7% for Visuotactile DP and 73.3% for Visuomotor DP. Similar improvements were observed in simulation, such as in-hand box flipping (66.0% for CGP vs. 58.0% and 53.2% for baselines).
- **Effectiveness in Delicate/Sustained Contact:** The improvements were most pronounced in tasks requiring sustained or delicate contact, where the evolution of contacts plays a central role (e.g., dish wiping, in-hand box flipping).
- **Contact Grounding Verification:** Visualizations of rollouts (Fig. 5) demonstrated time-aligned predicted and observed tactile feedback. The close match between these signals indicated that CGP effectively executes contact-grounded targets and realizes the predicted contact evolution. This suggests that tactile prediction in CGP is tightly coupled to execution through contact-consistent targets, rather than being an auxiliary objective.
- **Generalization Across Interaction Types:** The analysis of target-actual pose offsets (Fig. 5) showed that most interactions in in-hand box flipping and egg grasping were hand-dominated. In contrast, dish wiping exhibited a clear target-actual pose offset when the sponge contacted the plate, indicating arm-driven force application and demonstrating the formulation's generalization to both hand- and arm-driven contact interactions.

**B. Hand Configuration Prediction**
A controlled experiment was designed to validate the contact-consistency mapping in isolation, predicting the target robot state from actual robot state and tactile feedback.
- **Necessity of Multi-Modal Input:** Accurate prediction of the target robot state required both actual robot state and tactile feedback. Removing either modality substantially increased error (e.g., in terms of joint-angle MAE), supporting the hypothesis that together they capture contact-consistent structure.
- **Mapping Design Choices:** A ResNet-style tactile encoder demonstrated better performance than MLP and Transformer variants for this task. Additionally, predicting residual targets (offsets from the current actual state) yielded lower errors compared to predicting absolute target states, indicating that contact grounding is effectively modeled as contact-conditioned corrections.
- **Generalization:** Qualitative results (Fig. 6) illustrated the model's ability to accurately predict complex and previously unseen target robot states given informative tactile feedback, suggesting that the learned mapping generalizes beyond memorized grasp poses.

**C. Tactile Reconstruction, Compression, and Design Impact**
The effectiveness of the latent tactile representation was evaluated through reconstruction and compression performance.
- **Reconstruction Quality:** ResNet-based encoders achieved high-fidelity tactile reconstruction across various tasks and sensor types (tactile arrays in simulation, Digit360 images on real robots). Reconstructions preserved key contact structures, including active unit locations, contact region extent, and relative force magnitudes (Fig. 9, Fig. 10).
- **Importance of KL Regularization:** While removing KL regularization sometimes resulted in slightly lower reconstruction error, it produced a less structured latent space with a much higher KL divergence. This negatively impacted downstream policy performance (Fig. 7), suggesting that KL regularization is crucial for generating a compact, well-conditioned latent space that stabilizes diffusion-based prediction and improves the fidelity of long-horizon tactile forecasts.
- **Latent Dimension Analysis:** For Digit360 sensors, increasing the latent dimension under KL regularization maintained reconstruction quality while modestly decreasing KL loss, indicating improved latent organization. An 80-dimensional latent (20 per sensor) was selected for CGP to balance quality, regularity, and runtime efficiency.

**D. Time Efficiency**
Inference efficiency was compared across CGP, visuomotor, and visuotactile diffusion-policy baselines.
- **Real-Time Capability:** Despite the added complexity of modeling future tactile feedback and contact-consistent targets, CGP achieved inference latency comparable to both baselines (Fig. 8). This suggests that CGP is suitable for real-time deployment.

**E. Robustness to Tactile Noise**
The contact-consistency mapping's robustness to simulated tactile prediction errors was evaluated by injecting Gaussian noise into tactile inputs.
- **Stable Performance:** The prediction error of the mapping remained stable across a wide range of noise levels (Fig. 13). This indicates that the mapping is robust to moderate tactile perturbations, and that errors in tactile prediction do not catastrophically propagate to the mapped robot state, further supporting the design choices of conditioning on proprioceptive state and residual mapping.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Contact-Grounded Policy (CGP) framework presents advancements in dexterous manipulation, addressing limitations of prior approaches by explicitly grounding multi-point contacts through a visuotactile policy. This work contributes to the field in several ways and suggests various potential impacts.

**Advancement in Dexterous Manipulation:** CGP moves beyond kinematics-only prediction by generating coupled trajectories of actual robot state and tactile feedback. This approach directly tackles the complexity of continuous, high-dimensional, and sensitive multi-point contacts, which is a fundamental challenge in robotic dexterous manipulation. By doing so, it provides a more robust and capable method for tasks requiring intricate finger-object interactions.

**Improved Control Fidelity and Reliability:** A key contribution is the learned contact-consistency mapping that translates predicted state-tactile pairs into *controller-executable target robot states* for a compliance controller. This mechanism ensures that the policy's outputs are physically consistent and can be reliably realized by the robot. This contrasts with methods where tactile predictions are auxiliary or loosely coupled to the control stack, which can lead to a disconnect between predicted and actual contact outcomes. The ability to faithfully execute intended contact evolution enhances the robot's ability to perform delicate and sustained contact tasks without slip or overly stiff interactions.

**Data-Driven and Flexible Contact Representation:** The framework's implicit representation of contact through coupled state and tactile signals, without relying on hand-designed contact parameterizations, offers flexibility. This data-driven approach allows the system to adapt to diverse contact patterns and complex object geometries. The demonstrated generalization of the contact-consistency mapping across unseen grasp configurations and its robustness to tactile noise further highlight its adaptability.

**Efficient Multi-Modal Integration:** The use of a KL-regularized variational autoencoder for latent tactile generation enables efficient processing of high-dimensional tactile data. By compressing raw tactile observations into a compact, well-behaved latent space, CGP can perform long-horizon tactile forecasts in real-time. This efficiency is crucial for practical deployment in dynamic environments.

**Broad Applicability:** The successful evaluation across various dexterous tasks—including in-hand manipulation, delicate grasping, and tool use—and in both simulated and real-world environments, using different tactile sensor modalities (dense tactile arrays and vision-based Digit360 sensors), indicates the versatility and potential broad applicability of the CGP framework. This suggests that the principles of contact grounding can be extended to a wide range of complex manipulation scenarios.

**Potential Impact on Future Research and Applications:**
- **Enabling Complex Robotic Skills:** By improving the robot's ability to manage and control contact, CGP contributes to the development of more advanced, human-like dexterous manipulation capabilities. This could enable robots to perform tasks previously limited to human operators, such as intricate assembly, delicate handling of fragile items, or skilled tool use.
- **Bridging High-Level Intent and Low-Level Control:** The framework provides a structured approach to bridge high-level task intent (through predicted state-tactile trajectories) and low-level contact-rich control (through executable targets), which is a significant step towards more autonomous and versatile robotic systems.
- **Future Generalization:** While the current work acknowledges limitations such as specificity to sensing and control configurations, and a focus on single-task training, it lays a foundation for future research into cross-sensor and cross-controller co-training, conditioning on system parameters, and scaling to broader task distributions. Addressing these limitations could lead to even more generalized and transferable contact-grounded policies, reducing the need for extensive re-training for new environments or hardware.
- **Sim-to-Real Transfer:** The methodology provides a strong basis for future work on sim-to-real transfer, potentially through co-training or domain randomization, by offering a principled way to represent and control contacts that are critical for realistic physical interactions.

In conclusion, CGP offers a foundational framework for robust, contact-rich dexterous manipulation, providing a pathway towards more intelligent and adaptable robotic systems capable of performing a wider array of complex, contact-dependent tasks in diverse environments.
