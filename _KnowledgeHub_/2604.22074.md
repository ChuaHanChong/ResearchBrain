# Outcome Rewards Do Not Guarantee Verifiable or Causally Important Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> Research introduces Causal Importance of Reasoning (CIR) and Sufficiency of Reasoning (SR) metrics to evaluate if large language models' generated reasoning chains are genuinely used and independently verifiable. The study finds that standard Reinforcement Learning from Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) often fails to improve these reasoning qualities, but methods like Supervised Fine-Tuning on expert traces or integrating auxiliary CIR/SR rewards can significantly enhance reasoning fidelity and verifiability while preserving task accuracy.

## Problem

- Current Reinforcement Learning from Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) for LLMs assumes generated reasoning chains are faithful to the model's internal process and verifiable by external agents.
- It is unclear if optimizing for final answer correctness via RLVR inherently leads to genuinely causally important or sufficiently verifiable reasoning steps.
- Reasoning chains could be merely plausible-looking post-hoc rationalizations or incomplete, hindering transparency and auditability.

## Method

- Developed two novel metrics: ==Causal Importance of Reasoning (CIR)== to measure if the final answer depends on the reasoning, and ==Sufficiency of Reasoning (SR)== to assess external verifiability.
- Experimentally evaluated standard outcome-based ==RLVR== on these metrics across diverse tasks using Qwen2.5 and Llama3.2 models.
- Explored two improvement methods: ==Supervised Fine-Tuning== on expert reasoning traces and ==auxiliary CIR/SR rewards== integrated into the RLVR objective.

## Results

- Standard RLVR often failed to improve CIR or SR, with **19 tasks** showing decreased CIR and **17** showing decreased SR on ReasoningGym.
- SFT with **64-512 expert reasoning examples** boosted CIR to approximately **0.4** and SR to **0.65-0.75** by the end of RL training.
- Training with auxiliary CIR/SR rewards improved both reasoning metrics while preserving task accuracy comparable to outcome-only RLVR.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Standard outcome-based RLVR does not reliably improve the causal importance or verifiability of reasoning chains, and can even degrade these qualities for many tasks.
> - Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) on even a small number of expert reasoning traces can significantly bootstrap and improve both CIR and SR.
> - Integrating auxiliary rewards directly incentivizing CIR or SR into the RLVR objective can foster genuinely better reasoning while maintaining task accuracy.
