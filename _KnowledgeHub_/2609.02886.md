# SolarWM: Open Data and Scalable Training for Long-Horizon Video World Models

> [!summary] Summary
> SolarWM is a fully open foundation for interactive video world models, pairing a reconfigurable multi-source data engine that normalizes 1.43M clips from 10 datasets into one frame-aligned contract with backbone-native adaptation of four 5B-33B video generators. A shared three-stage recipe of bidirectional camera-conditioned training, teacher-forced AnyFlow autoregressive initialization, and distribution matching distillation yields causal models that roll out for hours after training only on 5-second clips.

## Problem

- Existing world-model datasets differ in temporal scale, camera convention, metric scale, caption style and quality, so naive mixing gives inconsistent supervision that degrades multi-source training.
- Prior open systems release checkpoints or partial code but omit processed data, selection records, source-to-training pipelines and exact recipes, making reproduction and controlled comparison impractical.
- Backbone-specific adaptation ties each system to one video generator, while prior causal-conversion recipes need extra ODE or consistency-distillation initialization stages and long-video finetuning.

## Method

- **SolarWM**'s open data engine annotates every clip before selection, emitting a unified schema of video, metric camera poses, per-frame intrinsics, dense captions, quality metrics and provenance, with frozen per-owner filtering policies.
- ==Fused-PRoPE== injects camera control by applying ==projective rotations== to query, key and value tensors inside the existing ==self-attention== path, requiring no separate control branch or extra attention pass.
- Three-stage recipe: bidirectional ==flow-matching== adaptation, teacher-forced ==AnyFlow== objective that produces a few-step autoregressive initializer directly, then ==distribution matching distillation== causal training on self-rollouts against a frozen bidirectional teacher.

## Results

- Corpus totals **1,425,694** canonical clips across **14** dataset owners and **25.85 TB**: **471,798** high, **404,795** xhigh, and **549,101** fully annotated rejected clips retained with failure reasons.
- Four instantiated routes (Wan2.2-**5B**, Wan2.2-**14B**, LTX-2.5-**22B** retaining **14.7B** video parameters, MiniMax-H3-**33B**) share one data and camera interface and generate at **16 fps** with four sampling steps.
- Causal students trained only on **5-second** sequences sustain uninterrupted minute-scale and **60-minute** rollouts, on held-out and externally synthesized OOD first frames, without attention sinks or clip splicing.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decoupling expensive source annotation from mixture construction makes a corpus reconfigurable: thresholds, weights and temporal views change without rerunning camera estimation or captioning.
> - Long-horizon rollout can emerge from short-clip training when data, camera conditioning and stages align, so long-video finetuning and attention sinks may be avoidable.
> - Front-loading optimization into bidirectional training lets causal adaptation and distillation converge in few steps, dropping specialized ODE or consistency-distillation initialization entirely.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in "SolarWM" addresses several challenges within the field of interactive video world models. Interactive video world models aim to move beyond passive video generation, enabling the imagination and exploration of future visual observations conditioned on factors such as camera motion, user actions, or semantic instructions. This capability has implications for simulation, embodied learning, and interactive content creation.

Existing research in this area has shown increasing realism in game environments and open visual domains. However, a significant transition challenge lies in extending these capabilities from short-clip generation to interactive long-horizon rollouts, which requires models to maintain visual quality and temporal coherence over extended periods while remaining responsive to dynamic control.

Two primary issues are identified in the broader landscape:
1. **Heterogeneous Data Sources:** Current datasets for world modeling vary considerably in temporal scale, visual quality, motion distribution, captioning styles, and camera conventions. Naive mixing of these diverse sources can introduce inconsistent supervision, leading to degraded model performance even if individual models perform well on specific sources. The processing and integration of these sources into a coherent training contract is a recognized challenge.
2. **Diverse Video Generation Backbones:** Video generators, which form the core of world models, exhibit substantial differences in architecture, including latent representations, attention structures, and conditioning mechanisms. While a shared adaptation strategy is desirable for scalability and comparison, overlooking these backbone-specific characteristics can compromise their pre-trained capabilities. Conversely, model-specific adaptations limit systematic comparison and extensibility across different architectures.

Previous open systems and frameworks often either rely on restricted data sources or employ largely model-specific implementations. This has resulted in a lack of a unified and reproducible foundation that coordinates multi-source data construction and backbone adaptation, or provides a standardized and efficient training protocol applicable across various backbone architectures. "SolarWM" positions itself as a comprehensive, open foundation designed to bridge these gaps by providing a unified approach to data preparation, backbone adaptation, and training for long-horizon interactive video world models.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of SolarWM is to establish a fully open and unified foundation for building interactive video world models. This foundation is intended to cover the entire pipeline, from data preparation through long-horizon inference, and address the complexities arising from heterogeneous data sources and diverse video backbone architectures.

The motivation for this work stems from several challenges observed in the current state of interactive video world modeling:

- **Inconsistent Supervision from Heterogeneous Data:** Different video datasets possess distinct characteristics in terms of temporal duration, visual fidelity, motion dynamics, descriptive styles of captions, and camera calibration conventions. Directly combining these without proper harmonization leads to conflicting training signals, potentially hindering model performance and reproducibility. A core motivation is to standardize this data to provide consistent supervision.
- **Difficulty in Adapting Diverse Video Generators:** Various video generators employ unique internal representations and architectural designs. Adapting these diverse backbones for interactive world modeling, particularly for causal long-horizon prediction and camera control, is challenging. Existing adaptation strategies are often model-specific, limiting generalization and direct comparison across different architectures. The project aims to provide a scalable framework that supports backbone-native adaptation while maintaining shared interfaces.
- **Lack of Reproducible and Unified Open Foundations:** The research landscape, despite advancements in video generation, lacks a comprehensive open-source system that integrates multi-source data processing, backbone adaptation, and efficient training protocols in a unified and reproducible manner. Many systems release only checkpoints or inference code, making it difficult for other researchers to reproduce results, adapt models to new data, or extend capabilities to different backbones. SolarWM seeks to fill this gap by releasing a complete stack, including data, pipelines, recipes, weights, and framework.
- **Enabling Long-Horizon, Real-Time Interaction:** A significant goal is to enable world models to perform real-time, interactive rollouts over extended durations (minutes to hours) from short training sequences (e.g., 5 seconds). This requires models to maintain temporal coherence and visual quality while being responsive to user or environmental control, a capability that is crucial for practical applications but remains a substantial technical hurdle.

In summary, the key objectives are to provide an open, unified, and reproducible foundation that addresses data heterogeneity and backbone diversity, enabling the scalable training of interactive video world models capable of long-horizon, real-time inference.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

SolarWM employs a multi-component methodology comprising a reconfigurable multi-source data engine, a scalable backbone-native adaptation framework, and a unified three-stage training recipe.

#### 3.1. Open Data Engine

The data engine is designed to be reconfigurable and comprehensive, decoupling computationally intensive source preprocessing from training-mixture construction.
- **Corpus:** It processes approximately 1.43 million canonical clips from 10 source datasets, corresponding to over 25 TB of data. These sources span real-world, synthetic, and game environments, with diverse scene layouts, camera motions, and temporal scales.
- **Unified Data Representation:** Every source is normalized into a frame-aligned sample representation `S_i = (V_i, P_i, K_i, C_i, m_i, q_i, π_i)`. This schema includes video (`V_i`), metric camera-to-world transforms (`P_i`), per-frame intrinsics (`K_i`), dense captions (`C_i`), metadata (`m_i`), quality metrics (`q_i`), and provenance (`π_i`). This standardization makes samples from different sources interchangeable for training while preserving traceability.
- **Metric-Scale Camera Annotation:** A robust camera-annotation pipeline is utilized. For video-only sources, Pi3X and MoGe-2 estimate scene structure and metric depth, which are then aligned and fused before being passed to a modified VIPE SLAM backend for 6-DoF camera trajectory estimation and bundle adjustment with per-frame intrinsics. For sources with ground-truth or COLMAP poses, these are preserved, and Pi3X is used to connect scene structure to the trajectory’s metric gauge. This ensures consistent, metric-scale camera geometry across all samples.
- **LTX Clean Plate Processing:** To reduce ambiguity from dynamic entities (people, vehicles), three derivative "Clean Plate" owners are created using the LTX-2.3 Clean Plate IC-LoRA pipeline. This process removes dynamic elements while preserving static scene layout and camera trajectory, providing cleaner supervision.
- **Dense Captioning and Multi-Axis Annotation:** All 1.43 million canonical clips are captioned by the Kimi-K2.6 production pipeline. The prompt instructs the model to describe only persistent environment and stable scene content, excluding dynamic entities, actions, and camera motion. Alongside captions, a vector of complementary measurements is retained, including camera integrity, visual quality (DOVER scores), motion (VMAF, UniMatch), temporal consistency (PySceneDetect), and semantic suitability (Kimi-K2.6 quality, entity density, scene type, reject flags).
- **Source-Aware Filtering and Quality Tiers:** A versioned policy per dataset owner produces three disjoint physical labels: `xhigh`, `high`, and `rejected`, based on specific thresholds for various metrics (e.g., frame count, Kimi-K2.6 quality, DOVER scores, motion, camera integrity). Rejected samples are retained with their metadata and reasons, allowing researchers to define alternative training recipes by modifying filtering criteria, sampling ratios, and source weights without rerunning preprocessing.

#### 3.2. SolarWM Model Family and Backbone-Native Adaptation

SolarWM instantiates a family of four interactive video world models based on existing video generators: SolarWM-wan2.2-5B, SolarWM-wan2.2-14B, SolarWM-ltx-2.5-22B, and SolarWM-minimax-h3-33B, with sizes ranging from 5B to 33B parameters.
- **Unified Camera Conditioning (Fused-PRoPE):** All models use fused projective rotary positional embeddings (fused-PRoPE) to inject camera control. Camera poses and intrinsics determine projective rotations applied directly to the query, key, and value tensors in the self-attention path. This integrates camera motion into the attention computation without separate control branches or additional attention passes.
- **Backbone-Specific Adaptations:** The framework preserves each backbone’s native temporal grid, text representation, attention mask, and training objective.
 - **SolarWM-wan2.2-5B:** Uses its native TI2V latent path; the first latent is a clean image anchor, excluded from video loss.
 - **SolarWM-wan2.2-14B:** Initializes from the high-noise expert of Wan 2.2 I2V-A14B and is trained as one dense model. It uses the official `y` interface for image conditioning (first-frame mask + image latent).
 - **SolarWM-ltx-2.5-22B:** Derived from an audio-visual checkpoint, its 3.7B audio stream and 2.6B audio-video cross-attention components are removed. It retains the 13.1B video core and loads a frozen 1.6B Gemma video connector, preserving LTX’s native first-frame latent convention and rectified-flow objective.
 - **SolarWM-minimax-h3-33B:** Retains MiniMax-H3’s multimodal packing, with Qwen caption/image conditions, VisualVAE image anchor, audio rows (filled with silence and zero loss), and supervised target-video rows. Camera injection preserves the pretrained head split, with PRoPE applied to specific dimensions.

#### 3.3. Unified Three-Stage Training Recipe

The models are trained using a simple yet efficient three-stage procedure:
1. **Bidirectional Adaptation:** The pretrained video generator is adapted to camera-conditioned world data using bidirectional attention over the complete training window. This stage minimizes a standard denoising objective (e.g., `L_bid = E[||f_theta(z_t, t, c) - u_t||^2_2]`) and adapts the visual prior to camera control (via fused-PRoPE). This checkpoint serves as initialization for causal training and a fixed teacher for distillation.
2. **Teacher-Forced AnyFlow Initialization (AR Adaptation):** Causality is introduced by training with causal attention, where the model attends only to the current noisy state and clean ground-truth history. The AnyFlow loss (`L_TF-AF = E[sum_k l_AF(f_phi; z^t_k, t, r, z^0_<k, c)]`) supervises the flow map between arbitrary noise levels, enabling few-step autoregressive generation directly. Teacher forcing provides a stable clean history, accelerating convergence compared to methods requiring separate Causal ODE or Causal Consistency Distillation (CD) stages.
3. **DMD-based Causal Training (Distribution Matching Distillation):** To address the exposure gap between teacher-forced training (clean history) and inference (model’s own predictions), Distribution Matching Distillation (DMD) is employed. A causal student generates trajectories, and its distribution is aligned with a frozen bidirectional teacher using a trainable fake-distribution model. The DMD update (`∇_psi L_DMD = E[J^T_G_psi w(t) (s_fake(z_t, t, c) - s_real(z_t, t, c))]`) guides the student, maintaining a stable quality reference while tracking the student's evolving rollout distribution.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted within SolarWM demonstrate several findings regarding the proposed framework and the resulting models:

- **State-of-the-Art Performance without Specialized Initialization:** The models achieve competitive performance for interactive world modeling without requiring specialized initialization techniques such as ODE or consistency distillation (CD) methods, which are often used in other approaches (e.g., Causal Forcing, Causal Forcing++). This indicates the efficiency and effectiveness of the three-stage training recipe.
- **Efficiency of the Three-Stage Training Procedure:**
 - Most of the optimization occurs during the **bidirectional training** stage, which effectively adapts the pre-trained video generators to camera-conditioned world data.
 - The **autoregressive (AR) adaptation** (Teacher-Forced AnyFlow Initialization) converges rapidly, primarily by activating causal prediction mechanisms in the already well-trained bidirectional model, rather than relearning fundamental appearance or motion representations.
 - The **Distribution Matching Distillation (DMD)** stage requires even fewer optimization steps, indicating its efficiency in aligning the causal generator with its inference-time distribution.
 These observations suggest that the proper alignment of data construction, backbone adaptation, and training stages can lead to scalable long-horizon generation without computationally intensive training on long videos or complex training pipelines.
- **Long-Horizon Rollout Capability from Short Sequence Training:** Despite being trained solely on 5-second sequences, the resulting causal models support open-ended rollouts over minutes-to-hours horizons. This capability is achieved without requiring additional long-sequence fine-tuning or specialized attention-sink mechanisms, demonstrating significant temporal generalization.
- **Qualitative Generalization and Coherence:**
 - **Bidirectional Pretrained Models (OOD):** Evaluation of the bidirectional models on out-of-domain (OOD) first frames (e.g., GPT Image 2 or Krea generated images) showed that the unified data and camera-conditioning contract generalizes beyond training domains, while largely preserving the visual prior of each native backbone. Models could generate 10-second videos consistent with diverse OOD inputs under various camera paths.
 - **In-Domain Causal Generation:** The causal student models (e.g., SolarWM-wan2.2-5B-fast) demonstrated the ability to follow prescribed camera trajectories in held-out in-domain scenarios. This included maintaining subject consistency and responding accurately to camera movements in 10-second third-person rollouts, and producing coherent minute-scale rollouts across various real-world, synthetic, and game environments.
 - **Out-of-Domain Initialization for Causal Models:** The models showed robust generalization from externally synthesized initial images (GPT Image 2 or Krea) covering stylized, surreal, or unusual scenes. For 10-second OOD rollouts, the models largely retained the initial style, palette, and dominant scene elements, inferring new content consistently as the camera moved into previously unseen regions.
 - **Sustained Long Video Generation (Minute- and Hour-Scale):** Uninterrupted minute-scale rollouts from synthesized first frames demonstrated the model's ability to navigate visually distinct OOD scenes, preserving principal layout and appearance while responding to compound camera trajectories. Crucially, hour-scale rollouts from real first frames also showed that the models could sustain diverse scenes through continuous generation, maintaining visual coherence and responsiveness to camera control for up to 60 minutes.

Overall, the main findings highlight that SolarWM provides an effective and efficient framework for training interactive world models capable of robust, long-horizon generation, addressing key challenges related to data heterogeneity, backbone diversity, and the need for open, reproducible research stacks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The SolarWM project presents a significant contribution to the field of interactive video world models through its emphasis on openness, unification, and reproducibility. Its potential impact spans several areas:

- **Advancing Interactive World Modeling Research:** By providing a fully open foundation, SolarWM directly addresses the existing fragmentation in research where model-specific implementations and restricted data sources limit systematic progress. Researchers can now leverage a unified framework to compare different backbones, experiment with various data mixtures, and develop new algorithms within a standardized context. This will likely accelerate innovation in areas such as visual simulation, embodied AI, and interactive content creation.
- **Enhancing Reproducibility and Extensibility:** The comprehensive release of processed data (1.43 million clips from 10 datasets, 25.85 TB), the end-to-end data-processing pipeline, detailed training recipes, model weights, and the full training and inference framework provides an unprecedented level of transparency and reproducibility. This allows other researchers to verify findings, replicate models, and extend the work to new datasets or architectures with reduced effort. The reconfigurable data engine, which decouples source preprocessing from mixture construction, particularly enables flexible data design without costly re-computation.
- **Scalable Training for Long-Horizon Generation:** The demonstrated ability to train models on short (5-second) sequences and achieve long-horizon (minutes-to-hours) rollouts without additional fine-tuning or complex mechanisms is a practical advancement. This efficiency reduces the computational burden associated with training models for extended temporal coherence, making long-horizon interactive world models more accessible for research and development.
- **Unified Approach to Data and Backbones:** The framework's ability to seamlessly integrate heterogeneous data sources into a consistent training contract and adapt diverse video generation backbones (Wan2.2, LTX-2.5, MiniMax-H3) under a shared interface simplifies the development process. This unified approach facilitates the exploration of different architectural strengths and weaknesses under comparable conditions, fostering a deeper understanding of effective world model design.
- **Foundation for Future Applications:** Interactive video world models have broad potential applications in creating dynamic virtual environments for training AI agents, developing new forms of interactive entertainment, and enabling more intuitive human-computer interaction in visual domains. SolarWM's contribution of robust, camera-controllable, and long-horizon generative models lays a strong foundation for the practical realization of these applications. The ability to generalize to out-of-domain initial images further increases the utility and applicability of these models.

In essence, SolarWM offers a robust, transparent, and flexible infrastructure that lowers barriers to entry for interactive world model research, fosters collaborative development, and pushes the boundaries of what is achievable in long-horizon visual imagination and control.
