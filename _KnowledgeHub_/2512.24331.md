# Spatial-aware Vision Language Model for Autonomous Driving

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Motional and the University of Amsterdam developed LVLDrive, a framework that integrates LiDAR point clouds into Vision-Language Models (VLMs) to enhance 3D metric spatial understanding for autonomous driving. This system employs a Gradual Fusion Q-Former and a specialized Spatial-Aware Question-Answering (SA-QA) dataset, leading to improved open-loop planning performance on nuScenes with a reduced collision rate of 0.25%.

## Problem

- Existing Vision-Language Models (VLMs) for autonomous driving predominantly rely on 2D imagery, leading to a lack of robust 3D metric spatial understanding essential for safe navigation.
- The inability to accurately infer 3D geometry results in unreliable estimations of distances, object dimensions, and collision risks, which directly impacts the safety and effectiveness of driving policies.
- Directly incorporating disparate 3D LiDAR data into pre-trained VLMs can cause "catastrophic disturbance," disrupting their learned visual-linguistic alignments and degrading performance.

## Method

- LVLDrive integrates LiDAR point clouds, camera images, and text prompts into a VLM using a novel ==Gradual Fusion Q-Former== architecture.
- A ==zero-initialized multi-head gating mechanism== is implemented within the Gradual Fusion Q-Former to enable stable and adaptive integration of LiDAR features, preserving the VLM's pre-trained knowledge.
- The ==SA-QA dataset== was created from nuScenes and OpenLane ground-truth annotations to explicitly teach the model advanced 3D spatial perception and reasoning through structured question-answering tasks.

## Results

- LVLDrive achieved an average L2 displacement error of **0.29 meters**, an average collision rate of **0.25%**, and an average road boundary intersection rate of **2.59%** on the nuScenes open-loop planning benchmark, outperforming existing LLM-based driving models.
- On the DriveLM dataset, LVLDrive obtained a CIDEr score of **21.65**, demonstrating superior content relevance and more accurate spatial and semantic understanding in its generated answers compared to baselines.
- Ablation studies confirmed that the zero-initialized multi-head gate is critical for stable LiDAR integration and robust planning, while the SA-QA dataset's spatial perception and reasoning components are essential for achieving the best overall 3D grounding and planning performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly providing 3D metric spatial understanding and reasoning capabilities to Vision-Language Models is crucial for improving their performance and safety in autonomous driving applications.
> - A controlled, gradual integration mechanism, like the zero-initialized multi-head gate within a unified Q-Former, can effectively incorporate LiDAR data into pre-trained VLMs without disrupting their existing knowledge.
> - A targeted dataset that includes both fundamental 3D spatial perception and complex spatial reasoning questions is vital for training VLMs to leverage multimodal 3D data comprehensively.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

**Context and Problem:**
Vision-Language Models (VLMs) have garnered attention as a foundation for end-to-end autonomous driving systems, primarily due to their capacity for language-native reasoning and the generation of human-interpretable explanations for decisions. However, a limitation of contemporary VLMs in this domain is their predominant reliance on 2D imagery. This reliance restricts their ability to perform accurate *metric spatial understanding*, which encompasses precise inference of distances, object extents, occlusions, and 3D interactions. Such capabilities are critical for safe and reliable planning in complex urban driving environments. Image-only models often struggle to differentiate between visual appearance and true metric structure, leading to unreliable estimations of range, free space, and collision risks. Benchmarks designed to test spatial queries have indicated that general-purpose VLMs exhibit suboptimal performance in driving-critical spatial reasoning tasks.

LiDAR technology offers a complementary solution by providing direct, calibrated 3D point measurements. These measurements supply long-range and lighting-invariant geometric cues, which are beneficial for understanding scene layout, identifying drivable areas, and discerning inter-agent relationships. Previous research has demonstrated that combining LiDAR with image data can enhance perception in autonomous driving scenarios. However, directly integrating LiDAR data into pre-trained VLMs presents a challenge. These VLMs are typically optimized on extensive image-text datasets, not on multimodal LiDAR-image-text data. Consequently, a naive injection of a disparate 3D representation can disrupt the VLM's established alignments between visual and linguistic modalities, potentially compromising its linguistic competence and visual grounding.

**Related Work Categories:**
The paper positions its work within several related areas:
- **Language-Driving Datasets:** Early datasets like Talk2Car, BDD-X, and BDD-OIA focused on grounding natural language commands and explanations in driving scenes. More recent efforts, such as nuScenes-QA, LingoQA, and DriveLM, have scaled up Visual Question Answering (VQA) for driving, incorporating multi-view contexts and structured reasoning. Bird's-Eye-View (BEV) centric benchmarks (e.g., Talk2BEV-Bench, BEV-TSR, ChatBEV-QA) and instruction-style datasets (NuInstruct, OmniDrive) have further emphasized global scene reasoning and comprehensive language-driving tasks. The authors note that while nuScenes-SpatialQA specifically benchmarks spatial reasoning, their proposed SA-QA dataset shares this motivation but focuses more on planning-oriented spatial reasoning and cross-modal interaction between LiDAR and vision.
- **VLMs for Autonomous Driving:** Numerous VLM-based agents have been developed, primarily relying on image inputs for scene understanding and trajectory planning. Examples include DriveLM, LMDrive, VLP, LLaDA, GPT-Driver, DriveGPT4, DriveVLM, and DriVLMe. Some models, like BEV-InMLLM and V2X-VLM, have incorporated BEV-aware or V2X contexts. More recent developments, such as OpenDriveVLA and AutoVLA, aim to unify perception, reasoning, and action. While most of these approaches are image-based, LiDAR-LLM explores the use of LLMs directly with LiDAR inputs. The current work distinguishes itself by integrating the complementary strengths of both image and LiDAR within a unified framework, leveraging pre-trained VLM reasoning alongside 3D metric information from LiDAR.
- **Spatial Understanding in VLMs:** While generic VLMs have made progress in open-ended reasoning, several studies (e.g., Jia et al., Liu et al., Ma et al.) indicate that their spatial understanding capabilities remain insufficient, even with enhancements like chain-of-thought prompting. In autonomous driving, where precise spatial reasoning is vital, benchmarks like NuScenes-SpatialQA also show current VLMs' limitations. These findings highlight a need for more capable approaches to effectively enhance spatial understanding and reasoning in complex driving scenarios.

The current work addresses the aforementioned challenges by proposing a framework that augments existing VLMs with robust 3D metric spatial understanding through LiDAR integration, while preserving the VLM's learned knowledge and using a specifically designed spatial-aware dataset.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The motivation for this research stems from the identified limitations of existing Vision-Language Models (VLMs) in the context of autonomous driving, despite their inherent promise.
- **Leveraging VLM Capabilities:** VLMs offer significant potential for end-to-end autonomous driving systems. They can provide human-interpretable scene descriptions, justify decisions, and expose intermediate rationales, moving beyond the opaque nature of earlier autonomous driving pipelines. This capability is valuable for trustworthiness and transparency.
- **Addressing the 2D Bottleneck:** A critical limitation of contemporary VLMs in autonomous driving is their primary reliance on 2D image cues. This reliance creates a bottleneck for accurate metric spatial reasoning and geometric inference. Essential capabilities for safe driving, such as understanding precise distances, object extents, occlusions, and complex 3D interactions, are often deficient in image-based methods. This deficiency can lead to unreliable driving policies and is a significant safety concern.
- **Integrating 3D Metric Data:** LiDAR point clouds provide direct, robust, and lighting-invariant 3D metric information that is crucial for perceiving scene layout, identifying drivable areas, and understanding relationships between agents. Integrating this explicit 3D data is expected to enhance the spatial understanding capabilities necessary for reliable autonomous driving.
- **Mitigating Disruption to Pre-trained VLMs:** A key technical challenge is that existing VLMs are pre-trained extensively on image-text corpora. Naively introducing a disparate 3D data modality like LiDAR can catastrophically disturb their well-established visual-linguistic alignments and learned knowledge base. A method is needed to incorporate 3D data without compromising the VLM's existing competencies.
- **Explicit Spatial Education:** General-purpose VLMs often lack explicit training signals for advanced 3D perception and spatial reasoning. Therefore, there is a motivation to develop targeted datasets that can specifically teach these critical capabilities, aligning language-based reasoning with precise 3D geometric understanding.

Based on these motivations, the key objectives of this work are:
1. To develop a novel framework, LVLDrive, that equips existing VLMs with robust 3D metric spatial understanding by incorporating LiDAR point clouds as an additional input modality.
2. To design a Gradual Fusion Q-Former that incrementally injects LiDAR features into the VLM, ensuring the stability and preservation of the VLM's pre-trained knowledge base by mitigating catastrophic disturbance.
3. To construct a spatial-aware question-answering (SA-QA) dataset that explicitly trains the model in advanced 3D perception and reasoning capabilities relevant to autonomous driving.
4. To demonstrate through extensive experimentation that the proposed framework achieves superior performance in scene understanding, metric spatial perception, and reliable driving decision-making compared to vision-only VLM counterparts.
5. To highlight the necessity of explicit 3D metric data for building trustworthy VLM-based autonomous systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors propose LVLDrive (LiDAR-Vision-Language), a framework designed to enhance Vision-Language Models (VLMs) with robust 3D metric spatial understanding for autonomous driving. The core components of LVLDrive are a specialized fusion module and a custom spatial-aware dataset.

**Framework Overview:**
LVLDrive accepts text prompts, images, and LiDAR point clouds as multimodal inputs. Its architecture consists of three pre-trained encoders (for text, image, and point cloud), a Large Language Model (LLM), and a Gradual Fusion Q-Former. The Gradual Fusion Q-Former acts as a projector, bridging the visual and linguistic representations to enable the LLM to generate task-specific responses. The text tokenizer, image encoder, LLM, and Q-Former were initially pre-trained without point cloud input, ensuring strong alignment between text and image modalities. The primary challenge addressed is the effective integration of "out-of-domain" point cloud features into this pre-aligned VLM.

**Gradual Fusion Q-Former:**
This module is designed to incrementally and stably integrate LiDAR features. It builds upon the Q-Former 3D block, which uses learnable instance tokens associated with 3D reference points, 3D positional embeddings (3D PE), memory tokens for temporal context, and image embeddings.
- **Challenge:** Directly injecting new point cloud features into a VLM pre-trained without them can lead to training instability and suboptimal performance.
- **Solution: Zero-initialized Gating Mechanism:** The Gradual Fusion Q-Former incorporates point cloud features through a zero-initialized learnable gate, denoted `g`.
 - Input modalities (point cloud, image, text) are processed by their respective encoders to produce features (F_P, F_I, T).
 - Learnable tokens (L) and memory tokens (M) are processed by a first multi-head attention (MHA) layer.
 - In the second MHA layer, image features (F_I) and point cloud features (F_P) are transformed into keys (K) and values (V).
 - The crucial step is applying the `tanh(g)` gate to the attention scores corresponding to the point cloud values. Since `g` is initialized to zero, `tanh(g)` starts near zero, effectively suppressing the influence of the unaligned point features at the beginning of training. This allows for their gradual integration as `g` is learned, preserving the stability of the pre-trained components.
 - 3D positional embeddings are added to queries and keys for spatial alignment across modalities, ensuring all reference points reside in the same 3D physical space.
 - The authors employ independent gates for each attention head ("multi-head gate") to encourage diverse and complementary feature learning.

**SA-QA Dataset:**
To foster the model's spatial understanding and adaptation to LiDAR data, the authors constructed the Spatial-Aware Question-Answering (SA-QA) dataset. This dataset is built on the nuScenes dataset, enriched with ground-truth 3D annotations from nuScenes and lane information from the OpenLane dataset. It focuses on both fundamental spatial perception and complex spatial reasoning tasks.
- **Spatial Perception Tasks:**
 - **Drivable Area Judgment:** Questions inquire whether a specific BEV point lies within a drivable area.
 - **3D Attribute Identification:** Tasks require the model to identify an object (indicated by bounding box or arrow) and describe its 3D attributes (coordinates, size, orientation, velocity).
 - **Cross-Modal Encouragement:** The dataset incorporates specific designs to encourage LiDAR-image feature fusion. For instance, visual arrows in 2D images cue objects, prompting cross-modal reasoning. Crucially, masking the corresponding region in the 2D image forces the model to extract information directly from the LiDAR point cloud.
- **Spatial Reasoning Tasks:**
 - **Object-on-Lane Identification:** Given lane centerline points, the model identifies objects located on that lane.
 - **Nearest Object in Direction:** The model determines the nearest object in a specified direction relative to the ego vehicle.
 - **3D Distance Computation:** For two indicated objects (potentially across different camera views), the model computes their 3D metric distance, necessitating true 3D spatial reasoning rather than 2D pixel-based comparisons.
 - **Temporal Reasoning:** Tasks involve predicting an object's future position after a given time interval.
- **Generation:** All questions are generated from templates, and answers are derived from high-precision human-annotated ground-truth labels. The generation process is fully automated and rule-based, supporting dynamic augmentation. The SA-QA dataset is specifically designed for large-scale instruction tuning on the nuScenes training split (850 scenes), contrasting with evaluation-focused benchmarks like NuScenes-SpatialQA.

**Implementation Details:**
LVLDrive uses EVA-02-L as the vision encoder and LLaVA v1.5 (initialized with LLaVA-665K weights) as the LLM, applying LoRA to LLM components. FSDv2, pre-trained on nuScenes, serves as the point cloud encoder. The entire LVLDrive framework is fine-tuned jointly on the OmniDrive dataset and the proposed SA-QA dataset. An AdamW optimizer is used with a batch size of 16 and a cosine annealing learning rate policy over 12 epochs. The learning rate for the Gradual Fusion Q-Former is set higher than for other components (4 × 10⁻⁴ vs. 2 × 10⁻⁵).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluates LVLDrive across open-loop planning, language-based driving question answering, and through ablation studies.

**Open-loop Planning on nuScenes:**
LVLDrive's performance was compared against state-of-the-art end-to-end planning models and other LLM-based planning approaches on the nuScenes open-loop planning benchmark.
- LVLDrive achieved an average L2 displacement error of 0.29 meters, a collision rate of 0.25%, and an intersection rate of 2.59% over a 3-second horizon.
- It demonstrated superior performance compared to existing LLM-based approaches such as OmniReason, Orion, and OmniDrive-Agent across all average metrics (L2, Collision Rate, Intersection Rate). For instance, against its base model, OmniDrive-Agent, LVLDrive showed consistent improvements.
- The model exhibited a higher Intersection Rate than some end-to-end models (e.g., UniAD, VAD-Base, Ego-MLP, BEV-Planner). However, the authors note that LLM-based approaches offer broader functionality, including scene-level question answering.
- These results suggest that accurate spatial understanding and reasoning, enhanced by the proposed methodology, contribute to more robust and safer planning.

**Results on DriveLM Dataset:**
LVLDrive was evaluated on the DriveLM dataset to assess its ability to generate contextually appropriate and accurate answers to driving-related questions.
- The model achieved competitive scores on BLEU-4 and ROUGE-L metrics, which measure surface-level phrasing and syntactic overlap, indicating its generated answers were comparable to prior vision-language driving models in linguistic similarity.
- Notably, LVLDrive attained a CIDEr score of 21.65, significantly surpassing all baseline models (e.g., OmniDrive-Agent scored 15.44, MPDrive scored 3.56). As CIDEr emphasizes content relevance, informativeness, and agreement with human-consensus keywords, this substantial margin suggests that LVLDrive generates answers that more accurately capture critical spatial cues, object attributes, and driving-relevant semantics.
- This improvement is interpreted as reflecting stronger grounding in 3D geometry and more precise references to spatial relationships, which are essential for safe driving. The findings validate the effectiveness of the LiDAR injection strategy and spatial-aware fine-tuning in producing semantically faithful and geometrically grounded language generation.

**Ablation Study:**

- **Ablation on Q-Former Configuration, Input Modalities, and Gate Variants:**
 - **Modality Comparison:** Using separate Q-Formers for camera (C) and LiDAR (L) inputs showed that adding LiDAR (C+L) yielded moderate improvements in planning (e.29% collision rate vs. 0.33% for C-only) and 3D grounding (0.19 mIoU vs. 0.18 for C-only) compared to vision-only or LiDAR-only configurations. However, this incurred additional computational cost.
 - **Unified Q-Former and Gating:** In a unified Q-Former setting, directly incorporating LiDAR features without the proposed gate ("Unified C+L No") led to degraded performance (e.g., 0.40% collision rate, 0.17 mIoU), confirming the instability of naive LiDAR injection.
 - The introduction of a single zero-initialized gate ("Unified C+L Single") improved performance significantly (0.30% collision rate, 0.21 mIoU).
 - The multi-head gate variant ("Unified C+L Multi-head") achieved the best overall performance, with the lowest collision rate (0.28%) and intersection rate (2.93%), and improved mIoU (0.21).
 - These results indicate that the zero-initialized multi-head gate mechanism enhances training stability, leading to more robust planning and grounding. Furthermore, the lightweight unified Q-Former with this gate outperformed the more computationally intensive separate Q-Former design.

- **Ablation on Dataset Composition:**
 - **OmniDrive Only:** When trained solely on the OmniDrive dataset, LVLDrive failed to produce valid 3D bounding box information for grounding tasks, resulting in an mIoU of 0.00. This suggests OmniDrive alone does not provide sufficient supervision for precise 3D spatial attribute generation.
 - **OmniDrive + Perception QAs (P.):** Incorporating only the perception-oriented questions from SA-QA significantly improved all metrics, reducing the collision rate from 0.39% to 0.29% and improving mIoU to 0.20. This highlights the importance of explicit perception QAs for learning robust instruction-following behavior.
 - **OmniDrive + Reasoning QAs (R.):** While adding reasoning QAs alone improved performance over OmniDrive-only, its collision rate (0.33%) and mIoU (0.11) were still inferior to those achieved with perception QAs, suggesting reasoning questions are less effective for learning precise individual object 3D information.
 - **OmniDrive + Perception QAs + Reasoning QAs (P.+R.):** The best overall results were achieved when both perception and reasoning QAs were utilized during training, yielding the lowest collision rate (0.27%), lowest intersection rate (2.83%), and highest mIoU (0.22).
 - These findings demonstrate that perception and reasoning QAs provide complementary supervision signals that jointly enhance spatial understanding and decision-making capabilities.

### 5. Significance

This work addresses a critical limitation in current Vision-Language Models (VLMs) applied to autonomous driving: their inadequate 3D metric spatial understanding derived from primarily 2D visual inputs. The research establishes that explicit 3D metric data is necessary for building reliable VLM-based autonomous systems.
- **Stable Multimodal Integration:** The introduction of the Gradual Fusion Q-Former with its zero-initialized gating mechanism provides a method for stably integrating a new and disparate modality (LiDAR) into pre-trained VLMs. This approach effectively fuses LiDAR and image inputs while preserving the valuable linguistic and visual reasoning capabilities already present in the VLM backbone, mitigating the risk of catastrophic interference.
- **Targeted Spatial Learning:** The development of the SA-QA dataset signifies a directed approach to teaching advanced 3D perception and reasoning. By leveraging ground-truth annotations and designing specific question-answering pairs that demand 3D grounding, spatial reasoning, and future state prediction, the dataset explicitly guides the model to interpret and utilize LiDAR point clouds effectively. This contrasts with more general-purpose language-driving datasets and addresses the identified gap in spatial understanding.
- **Improved Driving Performance:** The experimental results consistently demonstrate that LVLDrive achieves superior performance in crucial autonomous driving metrics, including scene understanding, metric spatial perception, and reliable driving decision-making, compared to vision-only counterparts. This validates the effectiveness of integrating explicit 3D spatial information into VLMs for practical applications.

**Potential Impact:**
The contributions of this research have several potential impacts:
- **Enhanced Safety and Reliability in Autonomous Driving:** By improving 3D metric spatial understanding, LVLDrive can contribute to more accurate perception of the environment, leading to safer and more robust decision-making in autonomous vehicles. This can reduce collision risks and ensure trajectories remain within drivable areas, directly impacting the trustworthiness and public acceptance of autonomous systems.
- **Advancement in End-to-End Driving Systems:** This work provides a framework for building more capable end-to-end VLM-based driving agents. Such agents can leverage both the qualitative common sense and explanatory power of language models with the quantitative precision of 3D sensors, potentially leading to more adaptable and interpretable autonomous driving systems.
- **Guidance for Multimodal AI Development:** The methodology for gradually fusing new, disparate modalities into pre-trained multimodal models is applicable beyond autonomous driving. It offers a paradigm for addressing similar integration challenges in broader multimodal AI research where new sensor data or input types need to be incorporated without retraining foundational models from scratch.
- **Influence on Dataset Design:** The SA-QA dataset and its detailed construction (e.g., cross-modal encouragement, explicit 3D grounding, cross-view reasoning, automated generation) can serve as a model for future dataset development aimed at enhancing spatial reasoning and multimodal alignment in real-world scenarios.

**Limitations and Future Work:**
The authors acknowledge that LVLDrive is currently constrained by the limited availability of large-scale, naturally paired text–LiDAR data. Ideally, a unified model would be pre-trained on extensive, aligned language–LiDAR samples, allowing the representation space for 3D geometry and linguistic concepts to be co-optimized from initial stages. The construction of such comprehensive datasets is identified as an important and challenging direction for future research, aiming to bridge this data gap.
