# STAR-VLM: Spatiotemporal Grounding Vision-Language Models for Motion and Velocity Estimation via Automotive Radar Supervision

> [!summary] Summary
> The STAR-VLM framework enhances vision-language models with metric spatiotemporal reasoning for motion and velocity estimation in autonomous driving by leveraging automotive radar data as a scalable, annotation-free supervision source. This approach achieved improved performance in motion classification and radial velocity estimation compared to existing methods.

## Problem

- Vision-language models (VLMs) lack robust metric spatiotemporal understanding, particularly in quantifying object motion and velocity in real-world physical units.
- Existing methods for improving VLM spatiotemporal reasoning often rely on costly human annotations, complex preprocessing, or synthetic data, leading to scalability and sim-to-real generalization challenges.
- A gap exists in effectively leveraging readily available, physically-grounded sensor data like automotive radar for direct, label-free metric temporal supervision in VLMs.

## Method

- The **STAR-VLM** framework integrates automotive radar data as a label-free supervision source to enhance VLM training for motion and velocity estimation.
- A new dataset, ==STAR-Bench==, was curated from nuScenes, projecting radar measurements onto image planes with an ==occlusion filter== and designing specific pixel-level question-answer prompts.
- A ==cross-task supervised fine-tuning== strategy was implemented, jointly training the VLM on motion classification, radial velocity, and ego-velocity estimation to improve motion disentanglement.

## Results

- STAR-VLM achieved a motion classification accuracy of **0.80** on human-annotated data and **0.94** on radar-derived data, surpassing general VLMs and task-specific models like SegAnyMo.
- The framework demonstrated a radial velocity Mean Absolute Error (MAE) of **1.94 m/s** on human-annotated data and **1.37 m/s** on radar-derived data, outperforming baselines including Any4D.
- Ablation studies confirmed that jointly training with ego-velocity estimation significantly improves both motion classification accuracy and radial velocity estimation MAE compared to single-task or limited joint training.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Automotive radar's direct Doppler measurements provide an effective, annotation-free source of physically grounded supervision for metric motion and velocity estimation in VLMs.
> - Joint training with ego-velocity estimation significantly improves the VLM's ability to disentangle ego-motion from object motion, enhancing the robustness of overall spatiotemporal reasoning.
> - Combining both visual markers (rendered arrows) and textual pixel coordinates in prompts strengthens pixel-level grounding, leading to more accurate localization and query responses.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Vision-language models (VLMs) are increasingly recognized as foundational components for embodied intelligence, with applications extending to autonomous driving and robotic control. Initial VLM developments primarily focused on static visual understanding and language-based reasoning. However, the deployment of such systems in dynamic environments, like autonomous vehicles operating amidst moving objects and complex interactions, necessitates robust spatiotemporal understanding. This includes the ability to reason about object motion, temporal events, and overall scene dynamics.

Prior research has attempted to extend VLMs to spatiotemporal reasoning. These approaches often involve complex preprocessing, extensive human annotations, or synthetic data generation. Such methods introduce scalability challenges and potential sim-to-real gaps, where models trained on simulated data may not generalize effectively to real-world scenarios. Furthermore, while these methods improve spatiotemporal understanding, they frequently lack strong metric reasoning capabilities, particularly for dynamic elements such, as estimating object motion in real-world physical units (e.g., meters per second).

A related line of work has focused on enhancing metric spatial perception in VLMs. For instance, methods like DepthLM have utilized LiDAR or depth-based supervision to improve metric depth estimation. While effective for spatial grounding, these approaches do not directly address temporal motion reasoning. Consequently, the domain of metric temporal reasoning—specifically, estimating object velocity within a metric space—remains underexplored in VLMs, particularly in the context of autonomous driving.

This paper addresses this gap by proposing automotive radar as a supervision source for enhancing metric spatiotemporal reasoning in VLMs. Radar is a low-cost and widely deployed sensor in automotive platforms. It offers direct measurements of range and Doppler shifts, providing rich spatiotemporal information, including radial velocity, which is directly relevant to temporal motion. The integration of radar supervision offers a scalable, annotation-free, and real-world-grounded alternative to human annotations or synthetic data, positioning this work at the intersection of spatiotemporal VLMs, metric reasoning, and sensor-based supervision for autonomous systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the spatiotemporal reasoning capabilities of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) with strong metric understanding, specifically for motion and velocity estimation in autonomous driving scenarios. This overall objective is driven by several motivations related to the limitations of existing VLM approaches:

1. **Addressing Scalability and Data Dependency:** Current methods for improving spatiotemporal reasoning in VLMs often rely on labor-intensive and expensive human annotations, complex preprocessing pipelines, or synthetic data. These dependencies introduce significant scalability challenges and can lead to potential discrepancies between simulated and real-world performance (sim-to-real gaps). The authors seek a scalable and cost-effective supervision source that can bypass these limitations.

2. **Improving Metric Reasoning for Dynamic Scenes:** While some progress has been made in enhancing VLMs' spatial understanding (e.g., metric depth estimation using LiDAR supervision), a significant gap exists in their capacity for metric temporal reasoning. Specifically, VLMs struggle to estimate object motion in physically meaningful units, such as real-world velocities, which are crucial for safe and effective autonomous driving. The motivation is to equip VLMs with the ability to quantify motion beyond qualitative descriptions.

3. **Leveraging Automotive Radar as a Novel Supervision Source:** The paper identifies automotive radar as a largely untapped and naturally suitable supervision source for metric spatiotemporal learning. Radar is already widely deployed in vehicles, providing inherent advantages:
 - **Low Cost and Ubiquitous Deployment:** Its widespread presence makes it a practical and readily available data source.
 - **Direct Motion Measurement:** Radar's Doppler measurements directly provide radial velocity information, which is a key physical attribute for temporal reasoning and motion estimation. This offers physically grounded training signals without requiring manual labeling.
 - **Complementary Spatiotemporal Cues:** Beyond velocity, radar also provides range information, complementing visual data for enhanced spatial grounding.

4. **Developing a Framework for Radar-Supervised VLM Enhancement:** Motivated by the advantages of radar, the authors aim to introduce a framework, STAR-VLM, that systematically leverages radar signals during training to improve both spatial grounding and temporal motion reasoning in VLMs. This framework is designed to utilize radar range and Doppler cues as label-free ground truth.

5. **Establishing Performance Benchmarks:** A further objective is to evaluate the effectiveness of STAR-VLM on motion understanding tasks within driving environments. This involves demonstrating its performance on both motion classification and metric velocity estimation, and comparing it against both general VLMs and existing task-specific methods. The goal is to show that radar supervision can lead to state-of-the-art performance in these areas, even surpassing models explicitly designed for these individual tasks.

In summary, the core motivation is to overcome the limitations of existing VLM training paradigms by harnessing the inherent spatiotemporal and metric measurement capabilities of automotive radar, thereby building more capable and reliable VLMs for real-world autonomous driving applications.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The STAR-VLM framework is designed to enhance spatiotemporal reasoning in Vision-Language Models (VLMs) by leveraging automotive radar data as a label-free supervision source. The methodology involves several key steps: data curation, prompt engineering, and a tailored training strategy.

**A. STAR-Bench Dataset Curation**
The authors curated STAR-Bench using the public nuScenes dataset, which provides synchronized camera-radar measurements and human annotations for autonomous driving scenes. The dataset includes front-view images and front radar data from the training and validation splits (excluding the mini split) for training, totaling approximately 34K images. The mini split (around 400 images) is used for evaluation. Two variants of STAR-Bench were created:

- **STAR-Bench-radar:** This variant is constructed by projecting radar measurements onto the image plane. It provides point-level radial velocity, motion status, and distance cues. A critical component here is an **occlusion filter**. Given that radar can penetrate objects, radar points may correspond to objects occluded in the camera view. To prevent incorrect supervision, the filter removes radar points deemed occluded. For each projected radar point, it searches for a nearest neighbor in image coordinates. If the neighbor is within a 30-pixel distance and closer in depth by more than 0.3 meters, the radar point is filtered out.
- **STAR-Bench-anno:** This variant utilizes human-labeled 3D bounding boxes from nuScenes, projecting them onto the image plane to provide object-level annotations, including full velocity, motion status, and distance. Similar to the radar data, human annotations are also filtered for occlusion by sorting 3D bounding boxes from near to far and removing those occluded in the image plane with an IoU greater than 0.3. The center of the bounding box at 0.3m height serves as the target point for VLM queries.

**B. Prompt Design**
The prompt design for STAR-VLM incorporates both visual and textual elements to ensure accurate pixel-level grounding and effective querying for motion and velocity.

- **Pixel Reference:** Unlike approaches that rely solely on textual coordinates (x, y) or visual markers, STAR-VLM combines both. A small arrow is rendered directly onto the input image to visually mark the queried pixel. Simultaneously, the pixel coordinates (x, y) are included in the textual prompt. This dual approach is hypothesized to provide stronger pixel-level grounding, improving localization accuracy and generalization across different prompt formats.
- **Question-Answer (QA) Format:** For training and evaluation, the VLM is asked specific questions:
 - "What is the radial velocity (in m/s) of the point indicated by the red arrow at pixel (x, y) relative to the camera?"
 - "What is the motion state of the point indicated by the red arrow at pixel (x, y) relative to the camera?"
 The corresponding answer templates are "The point has motion state X." (where X is 'moving' or 'static') and "The point has radial velocity Y m/s relative to the camera." (where Y is velocity rounded to two decimal places). This structured format helps the VLM learn to extract precise metric information.

**C. Training Strategy**

- **Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT):** The models are fine-tuned using the SFT approach, which has been shown to achieve performance comparable to reinforcement learning but with greater computational efficiency. The training follows a next-token prediction paradigm with a standard cross-entropy loss applied to the text tokens. No additional regression or regularization losses are specifically used, consistent with practices in related VLM work.
- **Radial and Ego-Velocity Cross-Task Training:** A key aspect of the training strategy is the joint training of the model to predict both object radial velocity and ego-vehicle velocity. The authors observe that this cross-task training improves metric velocity estimation and enhances motion classification accuracy. The hypothesis is that ego-velocity supervision helps the model disentangle ego-motion from object motion, allowing it to infer motion states in a global coordinate frame. Ego-velocity supervision is obtained from ego-pose estimation, requiring no human annotation. This strategy increases the amount of available metric supervision.
- **Architecture and Training Details:** The base VLM selected for experiments is the 4B Qwen3-VL model. Training is performed on 34K images with approximately 900K radar points from STAR-Bench-radar (25-30 radar points per image) using 4 A100 GPUs for 12-30 hours. The camera focal length is unified to enable scaling to different camera types, following prior work.

**D. Final Method (STAR-VLM)**
The complete STAR-VLM integrates these components:
1. **Joint Pixel Reference:** Combines textual pixel coordinates and rendered visual markers in the input image.
2. **Architecture and Training:** Fine-tunes pretrained VLMs (e.g., Qwen3-VL) with SFT using cross-entropy loss on text tokens, trained primarily on STAR-Bench-radar. A model trained on STAR-Bench-anno is also used for comparative analysis of supervision sources.
3. **Camera Ambiguity Resolution:** Unifies camera focal length to generalize across different camera intrinsics.
4. **Cross-Task Training:** Jointly trains on motion classification, radial velocity estimation, and ego-velocity estimation QA sets. This multi-task approach is shown to improve overall performance, particularly in disentangling motion.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrate STAR-VLM's performance on motion classification and metric radial velocity estimation, comparing it against various VLM baselines and task-specific models.

**A. Performance of General VLMs and Physical/Metric-Depth VLMs**
- **General VLMs (Qwen2.5-VL, Qwen3-VL):** These models, without specific spatiotemporal grounding, exhibit limited performance on both motion classification and radial velocity estimation. For instance, Qwen2.5-VL 3B achieved 0.35 accuracy for motion classification on annotations and 0.50 on radar data, with radial velocity MAE of 25.03 m/s and 27.17 m/s, respectively. Qwen3-VL 4B performed slightly better on radar motion classification (0.83) but failed to output for radial velocity.
- **Cosmos-Reason (Physical VLM):** While developed for enhanced physical understanding, Cosmos-Reason 1 7B showed limited improvement, with motion classification accuracy of 0.44 (anno) / 0.63 (radar) and radial velocity MAE of 10.42 m/s (anno) / 11.77 m/s (radar).
- **DepthLM (Metric-Depth VLM):** Tailored for metric depth estimation, DepthLM models (3B and Pixtral 12B) struggled with motion-related tasks. They failed to produce meaningful motion classification results and yielded inaccurate radial velocity predictions (e.g., DepthLM 3B with MAE of 23.16 m/s on annotations and 26.33 m/s on radar). This suggests that depth-centric training does not directly translate to robust temporal reasoning.

**B. Performance Against Task-Specific Models**
STAR-VLM was compared against dedicated models for motion classification and radial velocity estimation.

- **Motion Classification (vs. SegAnyMo):** SegAnyMo, a state-of-the-art motion segmentation method, was used as a task-specific baseline. While it produces dense motion masks, it sometimes failed to accurately classify motion at queried pixels, especially in complex scenes with multiple overlapping moving objects. STAR-VLM achieved a motion classification accuracy of 0.80 on annotated data and 0.94 on radar data, outperforming SegAnyMo's 0.73 and 0.77, respectively. This indicates STAR-VLM's ability to reliably predict motion status at specific locations.
- **Radial Velocity Estimation (vs. Any4D):** Any4D, which predicts dense pixel-level scene flow, served as a baseline for radial velocity estimation. Although Any4D captured relative motion patterns, its metric velocity estimates were less accurate compared to STAR-VLM. STAR-VLM achieved a radial velocity MAE of 1.94 m/s on annotated data and 1.37 m/s on radar data. In contrast, Any4D's MAE was 2.31 m/s (using anno data for comparison, likely meaning it's compared to the ground truth from anno, but radar for its own measurement) / 3.60 m/s on radar data, highlighting STAR-VLM's superior metric accuracy.

**C. STAR-VLM Performance**
STAR-VLM (4B Qwen3-VL base) demonstrated the following key results:
- **Motion Classification Accuracy:** 0.80 (on STAR-Bench-anno) and 0.94 (on STAR-Bench-radar).
- **Radial Velocity MAE:** 1.94 m/s (on STAR-Bench-anno) and 1.37 m/s (on STAR-Bench-radar).
- **TADBench Evaluation:** On the public TADBench, STAR-VLM achieved 0.81 VQA accuracy and 0.72 MCQ accuracy, indicating good generalization to benchmarks without explicit pixel-level grounding.

**D. Cross-Task Ablation Studies**
Ablation studies investigated the impact of different training tasks:
- **Single-Task Training:** Training solely for motion classification resulted in 0.79 (anno) / 0.93 (radar) accuracy. Training only for radial velocity yielded 2.23 m/s (anno) / 1.42 m/s (radar) MAE.
- **Joint Motion Classification and Radial Velocity Training:** Simply combining these two tasks led to worse performance (0.62 / 0.81 accuracy; 4.91 / 3.51 MAE), suggesting increased optimization difficulty without additional cues.
- **Joint Training with Ego-Velocity Estimation:** Crucially, adding ego-velocity estimation to the joint training of motion classification and radial velocity significantly improved performance across all metrics. This configuration (STAR-VLM's final method) achieved 0.80 (anno) / 0.94 (radar) accuracy for motion classification and 1.89 m/s (anno) / 1.20 m/s (radar) MAE for radial velocity. This indicates that ego-velocity supervision aids in disentangling ego-motion from object motion, leading to more robust overall spatiotemporal reasoning.

The effectiveness of combining both pixel coordinates and visual markers in the prompt design was also confirmed, yielding the best performance across different Qwen base models.

Overall, the findings indicate that automotive radar, when strategically integrated as a supervision source within the STAR-VLM framework, enables VLMs to achieve state-of-the-art performance in metric motion classification and radial velocity estimation, surpassing both general VLMs and specialized models designed for these specific tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of STAR-VLM and its radar-supervised framework presents several points of significance and potential impact within the field of Vision-Language Models and autonomous driving:

1. **Scalable and Annotation-Free Supervision:** A primary contribution is the demonstration of automotive radar as a scalable, cost-effective, and annotation-free source of supervision for enhancing VLMs. Unlike methods relying on expensive human annotations or complex synthetic data generation, radar data is inherently available from deployed automotive platforms and provides physically grounded measurements (range, Doppler) without requiring manual labeling. This significantly reduces the data acquisition and preparation burden, making the development of advanced VLMs more efficient and practical for real-world deployment.

2. **Enhanced Metric Spatiotemporal Reasoning:** STAR-VLM addresses the recognized limitation of existing VLMs in performing strong metric temporal reasoning. By leveraging radar's Doppler measurements, the framework endows VLMs with the ability to estimate object motion in real-world units (e.g., m/s), rather than just qualitative descriptions. This capability is critical for safety-sensitive applications like autonomous driving, where precise understanding of object velocities is essential for prediction, planning, and control.

3. **State-of-the-Art Performance in Motion Understanding:** The experimental results show that STAR-VLM achieves state-of-the-art performance on both motion classification and metric radial velocity estimation, outperforming general VLMs and even task-specific methods. This indicates that a VLM, when appropriately grounded with sensor data, can not only interpret scenes but also quantify dynamic properties with high accuracy, suggesting a path towards more unified and capable perception systems.

4. **Improved Generalization and Robustness:** The cross-task training, particularly the inclusion of ego-velocity estimation, is shown to improve the model's ability to disentangle ego-motion from object motion. This leads to more robust motion classification and velocity estimation in various driving scenarios, contributing to better generalization and reliability of the VLM in dynamic environments. The joint pixel reference design also strengthens pixel-level grounding, enhancing the VLM's ability to accurately localize queried points.

5. **Implications for Embodied AI and Autonomous Driving:** The success of STAR-VLM in metric spatiotemporal grounding positions VLMs as more viable backbones for embodied intelligence in autonomous driving. Such models could be instrumental in:
 - **Auto-labeling:** Generating high-quality, metric-aware labels for large-scale datasets, further reducing reliance on human annotators.
 - **End-to-end Autonomous Driving:** Providing comprehensive scene understanding—interpreting "what is where" and "what is doing what, how fast"—which is foundational for higher-level decision-making and planning, potentially leading to more interpretable and adaptable autonomous systems.
 - **Sensor Fusion Paradigms:** This work demonstrates a novel form of sensor supervision, where radar acts as a "teacher" for visual understanding, rather than solely a complementary input during inference. This paradigm could be extended to other sensor modalities and tasks.

6. **Future Research Directions:** The paper also outlines future work, such as extending the method from relative radial velocity to full or global velocity estimation, and combining it with Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. This suggests that STAR-VLM is a foundational step towards VLMs that can not only perceive and reason about dynamic scenes but also directly inform and execute control actions in autonomous systems.

In conclusion, STAR-VLM represents a significant advancement in grounding VLMs with real-world metric spatiotemporal understanding through an efficient and scalable radar-supervised approach, holding implications for the robustness and capabilities of future autonomous systems.
