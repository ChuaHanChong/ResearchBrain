# PICO: Reconstructing 3D People In Contact with Objects

> [!summary] Summary
> A framework combines optimization-based fitting with dense contact modeling to reconstruct 3D human-object interactions from single images, introducing a dataset of 4,123 images with 3D contact annotations across 44 object categories and demonstrating improved generalization to unconstrained scenarios compared to existing approaches.

## Problem

- Existing methods for 3D human-object interaction reconstruction struggle with unconstrained images and arbitrary object categories
- Lack of datasets with dense 3D contact annotations between humans and objects limits development of robust solutions
- Unlike humans, there is no single well-established statistical shape model for representing arbitrary objects, complicating 3D object reconstruction

## Method

- **PICO**-db: New dataset with 4,123 images containing 3D contact annotations for both humans and objects
- PICO-fit: Three-stage optimization method using contact correspondences and image evidence to refine human and object poses
- Initializes body shape/pose with OSX and object shape via **OpenShape** foundation-model retrieval before the three-stage contact-based optimization refines poses

## Results

- Successfully reconstructs 3D interactions with previously untackled object classes in unconstrained settings
- Outperforms existing methods on perceptual realism in real-world scenarios
- Demonstrates competitive performance on unseen InterCap dataset without requiring retraining

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Contact information serves as a crucial cue for accurate 3D reconstruction of human-object interactions
> - Combining OpenShape foundation model for object retrieval with contact-based optimization enables handling of diverse object categories
> - Multi-stage optimization separating object registration, alignment and human pose refinement improves reconstruction quality

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work falls squarely within the field of **3D Human-Object Interaction (HOI) reconstruction**. This is a rapidly evolving area within computer vision that aims to understand and model how humans interact with objects in 3D space from visual input, typically images or videos. The significance of 3D HOI stems from its numerous potential applications, including:

- **Robotics:** Enabling robots to understand and assist humans in complex tasks.
- **Augmented and Virtual Reality:** Creating realistic and interactive virtual environments.
- **Smart Homes:** Developing intelligent systems that can understand and respond to human actions.
- **Human Activity Recognition:** Analyzing and interpreting human behavior for security, healthcare, or entertainment purposes.

The paper addresses several key challenges that have limited the progress in 3D HOI:

- **Lack of Robust Object Shape Models:** Unlike humans, there isn't a single, well-established statistical model for representing the shapes of arbitrary objects.
- **Occlusion and Ambiguity:** Humans and objects often occlude each other in images, making it difficult to infer their 3D pose and arrangement accurately.
- **Contact Ambiguity:** Determining the precise points of contact between humans and objects from 2D images is challenging.
- **Generalization to In-the-Wild Images:** Most existing methods struggle to generalize to real-world images with uncontrolled viewpoints, lighting conditions, and object diversity.

The paper attempts to push the boundaries of the field by:

- **Focusing on Unconstrained, Natural Images:** Addressing the need for methods that can work in real-world scenarios.
- **Supporting Interaction with Arbitrary Object Classes:** Moving beyond the limitations of existing methods that are restricted to a small set of known object categories.
- **Explicitly Modeling Contact:** Recognizing the importance of contact information as a key cue for 3D HOI reconstruction.

The paper positions itself against previous works by highlighting their limitations in handling unconstrained images and arbitrary object classes. It builds upon existing research on 3D human pose estimation, 3D object reconstruction, and contact estimation, but it proposes a novel framework that integrates these components in a more robust and generalizable manner. The integration with the DAMON dataset, OpenShape foundation model, and the use of Large Language Models for reasoning are key novelties.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of the research is to develop a novel framework, called PICO ("People In Contact with Objects"), for **reconstructing accurate 3D Human-Object Interaction (HOI) from natural color images.** This framework aims to overcome the limitations of existing methods and enable 3D HOI reasoning in unconstrained environments with diverse object classes.

Specifically, the key objectives are:

- **Create a new dataset (PICO-db):** This dataset should contain natural images paired with dense 3D contact annotations on both the human body and the interacting object. The contact annotations should establish rich point correspondences between the human and the object.
- **Develop a method (PICO-fit) for projecting existing body contacts onto objects:** This method should be efficient, scalable, and require minimal human effort. The projection should also preserve contact information and establish bijective body-object contact correspondences.
- **Build a method (PICO-fit) that recovers 3D HOI from an image:** This method should be able to scale to previously untackled object classes and handle images with varying viewpoints, occlusions, and body poses.

The motivation behind this research is driven by the increasing importance of 3D HOI understanding for various applications. As mentioned earlier, robotics, AR/VR, and smart homes all require a comprehensive understanding of how humans interact with objects in 3D space. The paper argues that existing methods are insufficient for these applications due to their limitations in handling unconstrained images and arbitrary object classes. The development of PICO aims to address these limitations and pave the way for more robust and generalizable 3D HOI understanding.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The PICO framework consists of two main components: **PICO-db (dataset)** and **PICO-fit (method)**.

**PICO-db Dataset Collection:**

1. **Leveraging Existing Data (DAMON):** The authors build upon the DAMON dataset, which contains images paired with 3D contacts on the human body.
2. **Object Shape Retrieval:** To obtain 3D object shapes for the images in DAMON, the authors leverage the OpenShape foundation model and a large-scale database of 3D object meshes (Objaverse). They embed images and 3D shapes into a joint latent space using OpenShape and retrieve the nearest-neighbor mesh for each image.
3. **Contact Projection:** They develop a novel method for projecting the body contacts from DAMON onto the retrieved object meshes. This method is based on the "ContactEdit" approach, but it simplifies the annotation process by automatically generating an axis per contact patch via Principal Component Analysis (PCA). This allows annotators to transfer contact patches by simply clicking two points on the object mesh.
4. **Crowdsourcing Annotations:** The authors integrate their contact projection method into an online tool and use Amazon Mechanical Turk (AMT) to crowdsource 3D object annotations for DAMON's images.

**PICO-fit Method for 3D HOI Reconstruction:**

1. **Initialization:**
 - **Body Shape and Pose:** The authors initialize 3D body shape and pose using the OSX model, a state-of-the-art human pose estimator.
 - **Object Shape:** Object shape is initialized using the OpenShape-based database retrieval method described above.
 - **Object Pose:** The object pose is initialized by exploiting the contact correspondences between the body and the object.

2. **Optimization-Based Fitting:** PICO-fit employs an optimization-based render-and-compare fitting method to refine the 3D human and object meshes. The optimization process is divided into three stages:
 - **Stage 1: Registering Object to Body via Contact:** In this stage, the human is fixed, and the object pose is optimized based on the contact correspondences.
 - **Stage 2: Aligning the Object to the Image:** In this stage, the object pose, rotation and scale are refined to align with the image evidence. This stage also incorporates a penetration loss to prevent human-object interpenetration.
 - **Stage 3: Refining the Human Pose:** In this stage, the human pose is refined to maintain contact with the object while also aligning with the image evidence.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The paper presents both quantitative and qualitative results to demonstrate the effectiveness of the PICO framework:

- **PICO-db Dataset:** The authors collected a new dataset of 4123 images with 3D contact annotations for both humans and objects, spanning 44 object categories. This dataset is the first of its kind and provides a valuable resource for training and evaluating 3D HOI methods.
- **Generalization to OOD Datasets:** PICO-fit performs competitively on the InterCap dataset, an in-lab dataset that is unseen during training, demonstrating its generalizability.
- **Perceptual Study on In-the-Wild Images:** A perceptual study on real-world images showed that PICO-fit reconstructions are perceived as much more realistic than those produced by state-of-the-art methods.
- **Handling Previously Untackled Object Classes:** PICO-fit is able to handle a wider range of object classes than existing methods, demonstrating its ability to scale to new and unseen scenarios.
- **Ablation Study:** Ablation studies showed that each stage of the PICO-fit optimization process contributes meaningfully to the overall accuracy of the reconstruction.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The PICO framework represents a significant advancement in the field of 3D Human-Object Interaction (HOI) reconstruction. Its significance and potential impact stem from several key factors:

- **Novel Dataset:** PICO-db provides a unique and valuable resource for the research community. The availability of dense 3D contact annotations on both humans and objects will enable the development of more accurate and robust 3D HOI methods.
- **Improved Generalization:** PICO-fit demonstrates superior generalization performance compared to existing methods, particularly on in-the-wild images. This is crucial for deploying 3D HOI systems in real-world applications.
- **Scalability to New Object Classes:** PICO-fit's ability to handle a wider range of object classes opens up new possibilities for 3D HOI understanding in diverse environments.
- **Contact as a Key Cue:** The paper emphasizes the importance of contact information as a key cue for 3D HOI reconstruction. This insight can guide future research in the field and lead to the development of more effective methods.
- **Integration of Foundation Models:** The use of OpenShape and GPT-4V demonstrates the potential of leveraging foundation models for 3D HOI understanding. This approach can be extended to other foundation models and modalities to further improve the accuracy and robustness of 3D HOI reconstruction.

The research has the potential to impact a wide range of applications, including:

- **Robotics:** Enabling robots to understand and assist humans in complex tasks.
- **Augmented and Virtual Reality:** Creating realistic and interactive virtual environments.
- **Smart Homes:** Developing intelligent systems that can understand and respond to human actions.
- **Human Activity Recognition:** Analyzing and interpreting human behavior for security, healthcare, or entertainment purposes.

In conclusion, the PICO framework represents a significant step towards more robust, generalizable, and scalable 3D Human-Object Interaction understanding. The authors have made valuable contributions to the field through the creation of a novel dataset, the development of a novel method for contact projection, and the demonstration of a 3D HOI reconstruction method that can handle a wider range of object classes and real-world scenarios.
